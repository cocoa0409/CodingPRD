# LMS Shell & Term Rollover API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Campus learning management systems live or die on the quality of their plumbing. Every semester the registrar's student information system pushes a fresh catalogue of sections into the LMS; every semester instructors want last term's material back without last term's due dates, submissions or grades; and every semester the same three or four things go wrong — a cross-listed section is merged twice, a waitlist promotion silently overfills a room, a vendor ships a new tool build the day after the privacy office approved the old one, or a grade dispute lands after the registrar has already closed the term. This document specifies the API for a campus LMS administration service that treats each of those failure modes as a first-class, testable rule rather than an operational accident.

The service governs six intertwined concerns. It provisions **course shells** from the SIS section feed and lets administrators cross-list sections by absorbing one shell's roster into another, with a compensating reversal path. It synchronizes **enrollments** in ordered batches of adds and drops, assigns LMS roles, and runs a waitlist whose capacity depends on the term's academic-continuity posture. It performs **term rollover**, copying content from a concluded shell into a new-term shell while stripping dates, submissions and grades according to per-job switches. It gates **third-party tool integrations** behind two independent reviews — privacy and accessibility — that must be decided by different people and that combine with a computed risk score to produce a single approval verdict. It passes **grades** back to the registrar and manages the dispute-and-amendment cycle that follows. And it handles **archival, retention and post-term student access**, together with the outage and extension windows that keep a finals period running when infrastructure fails.

Two design commitments run through every section below. First, aggregates are conserved: a term's active student headcount, a shell pair's stored megabytes, and a passback's points ledger each have a stated invariant that must hold exactly — not approximately — after every operation, including after a compensating reversal. Second, the resource topology is flat: every collection is mounted directly at the server root, and every relationship travels in a request body or a query parameter rather than in a nested URL. Readers should therefore expect relationships to be described as actions ("the payload names the shell") rather than as schema declarations.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply to every endpoint in the service: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, record identity, precision, stale-view rejection, and the fixed order in which validations are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Six roles are recognized, spelled exactly as follows: `Registrar`, `LmsAdmin`, `Instructor`, `PrivacyReviewer`, `AccessibilityReviewer`, and `Student`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those six, or that is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

The `Registrar` owns the registrar calendar — terms, people, the SIS enrollment feed, and the archival tail of shells — and decides submitted grade passbacks. The `LmsAdmin` operates the LMS itself: shell provisioning, cross-list merges, term rollover, tool registration and approval, continuity windows, the term's continuity posture, and the logical clock. The `Instructor` is the instructor of record for a shell and authors its content, requests tool placements, stages and submits passbacks, and resolves disputes. `PrivacyReviewer` and `AccessibilityReviewer` are peer reviewer roles from the campus privacy and accessibility offices respectively. The `Student` reads their own enrollments and course material and files grade disputes.

Role identity is interpreted differently across those six names, and the distinction matters because ownership is enforced on `X-User-ID`:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Instructor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Person`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CourseShell` compare that shell's `owner_person_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Person`; ownership checks compare the relevant `Enrollment`'s `person_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Registrar`, `LmsAdmin`, `PrivacyReviewer`, or `AccessibilityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says so — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a `MergeRequest`, and as `reviewer_user_id` on a `ToolReview` — and compares it to other recorded operator identifiers for segregation-of-duties purposes, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Ownership enforcement produces `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) and applies to exactly these operations: `POST /content-items`, `PATCH /content-items/{id}`, `DELETE /content-items/{id}`, `POST /tool-placements`, `POST /tool-placements/{id}/transitions`, `POST /grade-passbacks`, `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/transitions`, `POST /grade-disputes`, `POST /grade-disputes/{id}/resolve`, and `GET /enrollments`. An `Instructor` acting on a shell whose `owner_person_id` differs from their `X-User-ID` is rejected there, as is a `Student` acting on an enrollment whose `person_id` differs from their `X-User-ID`. `GET /enrollments` carries only the `Student` half of that rule: an `Instructor` listing enrollments is never narrowed to the shells they own, while a `Student` is judged against the `person_id` query parameter itself, so omitting that parameter altogether is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` exactly as naming somebody else's is.

One further role rule is specific to tool review. The two reviewer roles have disjoint duties, so on `POST /tool-reviews` the `dimension` in the body must match the acting role: a `PrivacyReviewer` may file only `PRIVACY` and an `AccessibilityReviewer` only `ACCESSIBILITY`. A mismatch is a role failure, not a request-shape failure, and is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` names how many records to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0. Both values are echoed back in the `meta` object of the envelope, alongside `total_count` (the number of records matching the filter before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current window). A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, a `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with the server's monotonic insertion sequence breaking exact ties. Exactly one endpoint departs from that default: the term collection is ordered by `term_start_at` descending so that the newest calendar sits first, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break between terms that begin at the same instant.

Because the topology is flat, every collection that carries a foreign key also accepts that foreign key as an optional filter, which is how the reverse direction of each relationship is reachable. Filters compose: supplying two of them narrows to records matching both. Filter values are also the state fields themselves, so a caller can ask for one lifecycle stage at a time. One filter has soft-delete semantics rather than plain filter semantics, and it is called out in §3.3.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties and Mutation Bookkeeping

Every persisted record in the service carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply it. Every record also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written, read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.

Almost every record additionally carries `updated_at` — the same timestamp format, likewise read from real wall-clock time and refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that record. One request advances a given record's `version` by at most one however many of that record's fields it changes, and a record created by a request stays at `version` 1 even when that same request goes on to re-derive its figures. An update endpoint counts as a mutation even when the values it supplies are identical to the stored ones, so a no-op `PATCH` still refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version`. Recomputing a derived figure counts as a mutation: when a shell's `seat_utilization_pct` is re-derived, when a term's `rollover_completion_pct` is recomputed and persisted, or when a tool's `risk_score` is re-scored, that record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances.

There is one exception. `ToolReview` records are immutable review verdicts: they carry `id` and `created_at` only. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `ToolReview` response, there is no update operation for one, and a review is never rewritten — a re-review round produces new review records rather than editing the old ones.

Path identifiers are validated left to right. A request whose path names a record that does not exist is rejected with the 404 code specific to that record type — `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERSON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MERGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROLLOVER_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PASSBACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved the same way and return the same record-specific codes, checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, so the first unresolvable reference decides the response. A body foreign key present as `null` or as an empty string is treated as absent and falls through to the request-shape band instead; any other supplied value that is not the `id` of an existing row of the referenced type — including a value that is not a string at all — produces that type's 404.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, expiry, window boundary and retention horizon in this service is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock** rather than against wall-clock time. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An `LmsAdmin` may move it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability; that operation mutates no entity, but every subsequent request evaluates `add_drop_deadline_at`, `sandbox_expires_at`, `student_access_end_at`, `retention_purge_at`, `access_revoked_at` and continuity window bounds against the new instant. Timestamps the server stamps itself — `dropped_at`, `resolved_at`, `closed_at` — are taken from the logical clock at the moment of the transition, while `created_at` and `updated_at` are not. The clock-setting capability answers `200` with a `data` object carrying `to_iso8601` and `current_utc`, both rendering the instant just set, and it is the only endpoint whose `data` is not a persisted resource. Every record instant other than `created_at` and `updated_at` — `term_start_at`, `student_access_end_at`, `retention_purge_at`, `sandbox_expires_at`, `due_at`, `dropped_at`, `resolved_at`, `closed_at` and the rest — is rendered without any fractional part when it falls on a whole second, and with three fractional digits only when it does not.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Unless a rule states otherwise, every stored decimal figure in this service is carried to exactly two decimal places and rounded **half up**. That covers percentages (`seat_utilization_pct`, `provisional_grade_pct`, `final_grade_pct`, `rollover_completion_pct`), point totals and ledger buckets (`earned_points_total`, `graded_points_possible`, `total_points_possible`, `submitted_points_total`, `accepted_points_total`, `disputed_points_held`, `held_points`, `adjusted_earned_points`, `claimed_earned_points`), storage figures (`storage_mb`, `copied_storage_mb`), the monetary `sandbox_storage_charge_usd`, and the tool `risk_score`. One supplied figure escapes the rule: a tool's `vpat_score` is stored exactly as the client wrote it rather than quantised, so a `vpat_score` of `88.375` is stored and returned as `88.375` and enters the risk arithmetic at that precision, the rounding happening once on the resulting `risk_score`.

Three rules about *when* rounding happens are load-bearing and are stated with their calculations rather than here in the abstract: the storage chargeback rounds once per shell and never on the accumulated sum (§3.3), grade percentages carry the division at full precision and round exactly once at the end (§3.8), and the proportional due-date projection carries its ratio at full precision and rounds the scaled product to whole seconds half up rather than truncating it (§3.6).

Counts are integers and are never rounded: `active_enrollment_count`, `waitlisted_enrollment_count`, `absorbed_child_count`, `content_item_count`, `blocked_placement_count`, `published_shell_count`, `overflow_shell_count`, `total_active_enrollments`, `open_amendment_count`, `sis_sync_epoch`, `reinstatement_count`, `amendment_round`, `open_dispute_count`, `staged_enrollment_count`, `active_placement_count`, `affected_enrollment_count`, `extension_days_granted`, `waitlist_position`, `base_seat_cap`, `effective_seat_cap`, `batch_seq`, `pii_tier`, `access_grace_days`, and every per-operation or per-item tally on a batch or rollover job.

### 2.7. Stale-View Rejection on Cross-List, Rollover and Continuity Writes

Three creates in this service supersede rather than conflict: a new cross-listing request may legitimately target the same parent shell as an existing one, a second rollover job may legitimately supersede an earlier one for the same target shell, and a superseding continuity window may legitimately be opened over the same term. Because none of those cases can be caught by a state precondition, each of the three creates instead requires the client to prove it is acting on the picture it last read, by echoing the parent record's `version`.

- `POST /merge-requests` requires `expected_parent_shell_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `CourseShell` named by `parent_shell_id`. If it does not equal that shell's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments the parent shell's `version` by one, so a superseding request must re-read the parent shell first.
- `POST /rollover-jobs` requires `expected_target_shell_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `CourseShell` named by `target_shell_id`. A mismatch with that shell's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments the target shell's `version` by one.
- `POST /continuity-windows` requires `expected_term_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `AcademicTerm` named by `term_id`. A mismatch with the term's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments the term's `version` by one, so a superseding window must re-read the term.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Endpoints that are not one of those three ignore any version-shaped key a client sends. Of the three guards, only the rollover job's is echoed back on the record it created: `expected_target_shell_version` is part of a `RolloverJob`'s response shape, whereas a `MergeRequest` and a `ContinuityWindow` consume `expected_parent_shell_version` and `expected_term_version` for the check alone and never return them.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Aggregates

Many operations here touch more than one record. Three rules make those cascades reproducible. First, whenever a cascade walks a set of child records, it walks them in `created_at` ascending order, never in identifier order — this applies to shells concluded by a term closing, enrollments completed by a shell concluding, enrollments relocated by a cross-list merge, content items copied by a rollover, placements blocked by a tool suspension, and placements removed by a shell archival. Second, every record a cascade touches is mutated in its own right: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, including when the only change is a recomputed count. Third, cascades run to completion inside the request that triggered them; there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation, so a caller reading any affected record immediately afterwards sees its settled figures.

Three named invariants must hold exactly, with zero drift, after every operation:

- **The term seat ledger.** A term's `total_active_enrollments` always equals the sum of `active_enrollment_count` across that term's shells whose `provisioning_state` is neither `REJECTED` nor `PURGED`. Only enrollments whose `role` is `STUDENT` contribute. A shell leaving the sum zeroes its own `active_enrollment_count` and decrements the term by the same amount in the same request, so the identity is never left broken even momentarily.
- **Stored megabytes across a rollover pair.** Compensating a failed rollover restores the target shell's `storage_mb` to exactly its pre-execution value and leaves the source shell's `storage_mb` untouched, so the sum of stored megabytes across the source/target pair returns to its pre-execution total.
- **The passback points ledger.** Once a passback has been submitted at least once, its `accepted_points_total` plus its `disputed_points_held` always equals its `submitted_points_total`. Each bucket stores an already-rounded two-decimal value, so the identity is exact rather than approximate.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three duty pairs must be performed by different people, and each is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` values rather than roles. The `LmsAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` created a merge request may not be the `X-User-ID` that approves or declines it. The reviewer who decided one dimension of a tool's review may not decide the other dimension of the same tool. And the instructor who submitted a grade passback may not be the operator who accepts or returns it on the registrar side. Any of those three collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409), which applies to `POST /merge-requests/{id}/transitions` when the action is `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`, to `POST /tool-reviews`, and to `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/registrar-decisions`.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When several rules could reject the same request, exactly one of them decides the response, and the order is fixed. The shape of the ordering follows campus priorities: authentication and authorization first, then existence, then request shape, then uniqueness, then declared state transitions and stale-view rejection, then student-privacy and compliance safety, then segregation of duties, then referential and linkage integrity, then temporal windows, then cross-entity state dependencies, then aggregate ceilings, and finally fiscal capacity — seat caps and sandbox quotas — last. Enumerated in full, lowest number evaluated first:

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and is authorized for the target endpoint; and on `POST /tool-reviews` the body's `dimension` matches the acting reviewer role — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Ownership for the two person-backed roles, on `POST /content-items`, `PATCH /content-items/{id}`, `DELETE /content-items/{id}`, `POST /tool-placements`, `POST /tool-placements/{id}/transitions`, `POST /grade-passbacks`, `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/transitions`, `POST /grade-disputes`, `POST /grade-disputes/{id}/resolve` and `GET /enrollments` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Ownership can only be judged against a record that resolves, so when the shell or enrollment the check would read does not exist at all, the record-specific 404 of the next two bands decides instead.
4. Every identifier embedded in the path exists, checked left to right, on every path containing an identifier — otherwise the record-specific 404 code from §2.4.
5. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, on every create and update carrying foreign keys — otherwise the same record-specific 404 codes.
6. Request shape, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This band covers: every required input field present; every enum value a declared member; every number inside its declared range; every datetime parseable as ISO 8601 UTC; `term_end_at` strictly after `term_start_at`; `finals_window_end_at` equal to or before `term_end_at`; `window_end` strictly after `window_start`; a merge request's `child_shell_id` different from its `parent_shell_id`; `points_possible` equal to 0 on a `PAGE` or `FILE` item; `conditions_note` present when a review `decision` is `CONDITIONS`; and `adjusted_earned_points` present and at least 0 when a dispute resolution `outcome` is `CHANGED`. Unknown keys in a payload are ignored rather than rejected.
7. `term_code` is unique across all terms, on `POST /academic-terms` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERM_CODE`.
8. `sis_section_code` is unique per term among that term's `OFFICIAL` shells whose `provisioning_state` is neither `REJECTED` nor `PURGED`; `SANDBOX` and `DEV` shells are exempt from the uniqueness rule entirely. On `POST /course-shells` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SECTION_CODE`.
9. The pair of `vendor_name` and `tool_name` is unique across tools, on `POST /tool-integrations` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TOOL`.
10. At most one passback exists per `shell_id`, on `POST /grade-passbacks` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PASSBACK`.
11. The target record's own state field permits the requested transition or mutation, including the `action` enum on every transition endpoint, terminal-state lockdown, and re-issuing a transition that has already been taken. Applies to every transition, lifecycle-transition, posture-change, provisioning-decision, publish and unpublish endpoint, plus `POST /tool-reviews`, `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/registrar-decisions`, `POST /grade-disputes`, `POST /grade-disputes/{id}/resolve` and every update endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
12. `expected_parent_shell_version`, `expected_target_shell_version` or `expected_term_version` matches the referenced parent's current `version`, on `POST /merge-requests`, `POST /rollover-jobs` and `POST /continuity-windows` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.
13. No person whose `ferpa_block` is true is exposed by the operation, on `POST /tool-placements/{id}/transitions` with action `ACTIVATE` and on `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/transitions` with action `SUBMIT` — otherwise `ERR_FERPA_BLOCK_ACTIVE`.
14. A student read or dispute filing happens at or before the term's `student_access_end_at`, and the acting person's `access_revoked_at` is either null or still in the future. On `GET /enrollments`, `GET /course-shells/{id}` and `POST /grade-disputes` — otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_ACCESS_EXPIRED`.
15. The tool behind a placement being activated has an `approval_state` of `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, on `POST /tool-placements/{id}/transitions` with action `ACTIVATE` — otherwise `ERR_TOOL_NOT_APPROVED`.
16. That tool's `approved_version` still equals its `tool_version`, on the same activate action — otherwise `ERR_TOOL_VERSION_DRIFT`.
17. Segregation of duties as described in §2.9, on `POST /merge-requests/{id}/transitions` with action `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`, on `POST /tool-reviews`, and on `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/registrar-decisions` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
18. A tool holds at most one review per dimension within one review round, on `POST /tool-reviews` — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_DIMENSION_CONFLICT`.
19. Finalizing a review requires both `privacy_review_state` and `accessibility_review_state` to have left `PENDING`, on `POST /tool-integrations/{id}/transitions` with action `FINALIZE_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE`.
20. The child shell of a merge is not already absorbed — its `merged_into_shell_id` is null and its `provisioning_state` is not `MERGED`. On `POST /merge-requests` and on `POST /merge-requests/{id}/transitions` with action `APPROVE` or `APPLY` — otherwise `ERR_SHELL_ALREADY_MERGED`.
21. Both shells of a merge share the same `term_id` and both have `shell_type` `OFFICIAL`, on `POST /merge-requests` and on `POST /merge-requests/{id}/transitions` with action `APPROVE` — otherwise `ERR_CROSS_TERM_MERGE`.
22. No merge chains: the parent shell's own `merged_into_shell_id` is null and the child's `absorbed_child_count` is 0. On `POST /merge-requests` and on `POST /merge-requests/{id}/transitions` with action `APPROVE` — otherwise `ERR_MERGE_CHAIN_FORBIDDEN`.
23. Reinstating a dropped enrollment happens at or before the term's `add_drop_deadline_at`, on `POST /enrollments/{id}/transitions` with action `REINSTATE` — otherwise `ERR_ADD_DROP_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
24. A shell-scoped continuity window does not overlap another open window of the same shell and kind under half-open interval arithmetic, on `POST /continuity-windows` — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
25. Cancelling a continuity window happens strictly before its `window_start`, on `POST /continuity-windows/{id}/transitions` with action `CANCEL` — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_ALREADY_STARTED`.
26. A `SANDBOX` or `DEV` shell receives no new content, placements or rollover output after its `sandbox_expires_at`, on `POST /content-items`, `POST /tool-placements` and `POST /rollover-jobs` — otherwise `ERR_SANDBOX_EXPIRED`.
27. A term whose `retention_class` is `LEGAL_HOLD` forbids purge outright, regardless of elapsed time. On `POST /course-shells/{id}/lifecycle-transitions` targeting `PURGED` — otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_HOLD_ACTIVE`. This band deliberately outranks the next one, so a shell under legal hold reports the hold rather than an unmet horizon even though its `retention_purge_at` is null.
28. Purging an archived shell whose term is not under legal hold requires `retention_purge_at` to be non-null and at or before the logical clock, on the same purge target — otherwise `ERR_PURGE_NOT_DUE`.
29. While the shell's term has `continuity_posture` `CONTINUITY_MODE`, passback submission and term lifecycle advancement out of `FINALS` are frozen, on `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/transitions` with action `SUBMIT` and on `POST /academic-terms/{id}/lifecycle-transitions` — otherwise `ERR_TERM_POSTURE_FROZEN`.
30. Publishing requires `publish_readiness` to be true, on `POST /course-shells/{id}/publish` — otherwise `ERR_SHELL_NOT_PUBLISH_READY`.
31. The shell's `provisioning_state` permits the operation: `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED` for enrollment and placement, `PUBLISHED` or `CONCLUDED` for passback staging. `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, `MERGED`, `ARCHIVED` and `PURGED` shells reject all three. On `POST /enrollments`, `POST /tool-placements` and `POST /grade-passbacks` — otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`.
32. A `SANDBOX` or `DEV` shell can never be published and can never carry an enrollment whose `role` is `STUDENT`; such shells remain legal rollover targets. On `POST /course-shells/{id}/publish` and `POST /enrollments` — otherwise `ERR_SANDBOX_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`.
33. A rollover job's source shell has a `provisioning_state` of `CONCLUDED` or `ARCHIVED` and belongs to a term other than the target term, on `POST /rollover-jobs` and on `POST /rollover-jobs/{id}/transitions` with action `VALIDATE` — otherwise `ERR_ROLLOVER_SOURCE_INELIGIBLE`.
34. A rollover job's target shell has a `provisioning_state` of `REQUESTED` or `PROVISIONED`, a `content_item_count` of 0, and no enrollments, on `POST /rollover-jobs` and on `POST /rollover-jobs/{id}/transitions` with action `VALIDATE` — otherwise `ERR_ROLLOVER_TARGET_NOT_EMPTY`.
35. Role-assignment integrity, on `POST /enrollments`, `POST /enrollment-sync-batches/{id}/transitions` and `POST /grade-disputes` — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_CONFLICT`. A person holds at most one enrollment per shell whose `enrollment_state` is `ACTIVE`, `WAITLISTED` or `DROPPED`; a dropped row must be reinstated rather than re-created, and only a `COMPLETED` row frees the pair for a fresh enrollment. A `STUDENT` role may not coexist with a `TEACHER` role for the same person and shell, the shell's `owner_person_id` may not hold a `STUDENT` enrollment in their own shell, and a student may hold at most one open dispute per enrollment. Each of the three endpoints carries only the forms of the rule its own workflow describes: the one-live-row-per-pair rule — role-agnostic, which is what subsumes the `STUDENT`-versus-`TEACHER` clause — is what the enrollment create and the sync batch enforce, the batch adding the bar on two `ADD` operations claiming one pair; the owner-as-their-own-student bar belongs to `POST /enrollments` alone; and the one-open-dispute-per-enrollment bar to `POST /grade-disputes` alone.
36. A sync batch may be applied only when its `batch_seq` equals the term's `sis_sync_epoch` plus one, on `POST /enrollment-sync-batches/{id}/transitions` with action `APPLY` — otherwise `ERR_SYNC_EPOCH_OUT_OF_ORDER`.
37. Submitting a passback requires at least one staged enrollment whose `graded_points_possible` is greater than 0, on `POST /grade-passbacks/{id}/transitions` with action `SUBMIT` — otherwise `ERR_NO_GRADES_STAGED`.
38. Closing a term requires its `open_amendment_count` to be 0, meaning no passback of the term sits in `DISPUTED` or `AMENDED`. On `POST /academic-terms/{id}/lifecycle-transitions` targeting `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTES_BLOCK_CLOSE`.
39. Opening registration requires the freshly recomputed `rollover_completion_pct` to be at least 100.00, on `POST /academic-terms/{id}/lifecycle-transitions` targeting `REGISTRATION_OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_ROLLOVER_INCOMPLETE`.
40. The continuity concurrency ceiling: at most two of a term's open outage windows may cover any single instant, on `POST /continuity-windows` — otherwise `ERR_CONTINUITY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Each window is individually legal; only the collective is not.
41. A content item whose `has_submissions` is true may not be deleted, on `DELETE /content-items/{id}` — otherwise `ERR_ITEM_HAS_SUBMISSIONS`.
42. A person owns at most three shells whose `shell_type` is `SANDBOX` or `DEV` and whose `provisioning_state` is neither `REJECTED` nor `PURGED`, on `POST /course-shells` — otherwise `ERR_SANDBOX_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`.
43. A student enrollment may occupy a seat only while at least one seat is open under the shell's effective capacity, on `POST /enrollments` and on `POST /enrollments/{id}/transitions` with action `PROMOTE` or `REINSTATE` — otherwise `ERR_SEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering the Term and Its Retention Calendar

Everything in this service hangs off an **academic term**. A `Registrar` registers one by supplying a `term_code` — the registrar's own code for the term, such as `2026FA`, which must be unique across every term ever registered; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERM_CODE`. The two mandatory calendar anchors are `term_start_at`, the first instructional instant, and `term_end_at`, the last; the end must fall strictly after the start.

Four further calendar fields are optional and have derived defaults. `add_drop_deadline_at` is the last instant at which a dropped enrollment may be reinstated, and defaults to fourteen days after `term_start_at`. `finals_window_start_at` defaults to seven days before `term_end_at`, and `finals_window_end_at` defaults to `term_end_at` itself; the latter may equal `term_end_at` but may never fall after it. `finals_window_start_at` carries no constraint of its own — it is checked neither against `term_start_at`, nor against `term_end_at`, nor against `finals_window_end_at`. `access_grace_days` is the number of days of read access students keep after the term ends; it defaults to 30 and must be at least 0 and at most 365. Finally, `retention_class` selects the retention schedule and defaults to `STANDARD`, the alternatives being `EXTENDED` and `LEGAL_HOLD`.

A newly registered term opens with `lifecycle_state` `PLANNING` and `continuity_posture` `NORMAL`, and with every aggregate counter at its floor: `published_shell_count`, `overflow_shell_count`, `total_active_enrollments`, `open_amendment_count` and `sis_sync_epoch` all read 0, while `rollover_completion_pct` reads 100.00 for the reason explained in §3.2.

Registration also computes the term's two retention instants, and a `Registrar` amending the term's `access_grace_days`, `retention_class` or `add_drop_deadline_at` recomputes them. Those three are the only fields a term update accepts, the pair is recomputed on every such call whether or not any value actually moved, and the update is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` once the term's `lifecycle_state` has reached `CLOSED` or `ARCHIVED`. The computation runs in two steps. First, take the calendar date reached by advancing `term_end_at` forward by `access_grace_days` whole days, and set `student_access_end_at` to that date at 23:59:59 UTC — a fixed wall time with no sub-second component. Second, choose a retention horizon from `retention_class`: 365 days for `STANDARD`, 1825 days for `EXTENDED`, and no horizon at all for `LEGAL_HOLD`. When there is no horizon, `retention_purge_at` is null and the shell purge path is closed permanently. Otherwise `retention_purge_at` is `student_access_end_at` advanced by that many whole days, keeping the same 23:59:59 wall time. For a term ending `2026-12-11T00:00:00Z` with 30 grace days, `student_access_end_at` is `2027-01-10T23:59:59Z`; under `STANDARD` the purge horizon is `2028-01-10T23:59:59Z`, and under `EXTENDED` it is `2032-01-09T23:59:59Z`, the day-of-month shifting because 1825 days spans a leap day. A grace period of 0 days is legal and collapses `student_access_end_at` onto the term's own end date at 23:59:59, so a student read at exactly that instant still succeeds while one instant later fails with `ERR_STUDENT_ACCESS_EXPIRED`.

That expiry gate is what protects post-term privacy. A `Student` reading their own enrollments, reading a single shell, or filing a dispute must act at or before the term's `student_access_end_at`, and the acting person's `access_revoked_at` must be either null or still in the future; otherwise the read or filing is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_ACCESS_EXPIRED`. The two halves treat their boundary instant differently: the calendar half admits it, so a student acting at exactly `student_access_end_at` still succeeds, while the revocation half does not, so a student acting at exactly their own `access_revoked_at` is already refused. Because an enrollment list can span several terms, that read evaluates the calendar half of the gate once per term represented in the rows it matched, and a single expired term among them refuses the whole page; the `access_revoked_at` half is evaluated even when the page comes back empty.

### 3.2. The Term Lifecycle and Its Closing Cascades

The term's `lifecycle_state` dimension moves under a `Registrar`'s explicit direction, driven by a `target_state` value and an optional `reason`. The `reason` must be a non-empty string whenever it is supplied, but it is not persisted anywhere on the term — and the same holds for the optional `reason` on the posture change of §3.9 and on a shell's own lifecycle transition. Its declared path runs `PLANNING` → `REGISTRATION_OPEN` → `IN_SESSION` → `FINALS` → `GRADING` → `CLOSED` → `ARCHIVED`, with one reversal — `FINALS` back to `IN_SESSION` — and `ARCHIVED` as the terminal state. Every one of these transitions increments the term's `version` by one.

Opening registration is gated on rollover readiness. Before the gate is evaluated the term's `rollover_completion_pct` is recomputed and persisted, and the transition proceeds only when the recomputed value is at least 100.00; below that it is refused with `ERR_ROLLOVER_INCOMPLETE`. The percentage itself is defined over a term's **eligible shells**: those belonging to the term whose `shell_type` is `OFFICIAL` and whose `provisioning_state` is none of `REJECTED`, `MERGED` or `PURGED`. Of those, a shell counts as completed when it is the target shell of at least one rollover job whose `job_state` is `COMPLETED`. When the eligible set is empty the percentage is 100.00 by convention — which is precisely why a brand-new term with no shells at all can open registration. Otherwise the percentage is the completed count divided by the eligible count, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places: three eligible shells with two completed reads 66.67 and the gate refuses the transition. Sandbox and development shells are governance artifacts and never enter either count, and a shell absorbed by a cross-list merge leaves the eligible set, which can move the percentage without any rollover activity at all. The figure is recomputed whenever a rollover job settles into `COMPLETED`, whenever a shell in the term is created, rejected or purged, as the first step of the registration gate, and once more at the end of every successful term lifecycle transition. Compensating a failed rollover job, applying a cross-list merge and reversing one do **not** recompute it, so a merge that lifts an incomplete shell out of the eligible set leaves the stored figure standing until one of those triggers fires again — harmlessly, because the registration gate recomputes before it reads. The gate's own recomputation is persisted only when the gate passes: a refusal with `ERR_ROLLOVER_INCOMPLETE` leaves the stored `rollover_completion_pct` exactly as the previous recomputation left it.

`REGISTRATION_OPEN` advances to `IN_SESSION`, and `IN_SESSION` to `FINALS`, with no precondition beyond the term already being in the state the transition departs from. Leaving `FINALS` forks two ways, and the fork is decided by the term's other, independent state dimension. Advancing `FINALS` → `GRADING` requires the term to currently be in `FINALS` and requires `continuity_posture` to be anything other than `CONTINUITY_MODE`; attempting it during continuity mode is refused with `ERR_TERM_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Reversing `FINALS` → `IN_SESSION` is the mirror image: it is legal **only** while `continuity_posture` is `CONTINUITY_MODE`, and outside continuity mode it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. That reversal is the academic-continuity escape hatch — finals are suspended and instruction resumes — and it is a genuine cross-dimension guard on one row.

Closing the term is the largest cascade in the service. `GRADING` → `CLOSED` first requires the term's `open_amendment_count` to be 0, meaning no passback of the term is sitting in `DISPUTED` or `AMENDED`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTES_BLOCK_CLOSE`. Once past the gate, three cascade hops run in order. Hop one: every shell of the term whose `provisioning_state` is `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED` and whose `merged_into_shell_id` is null transitions to `CONCLUDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Hop two: each of those conclusions moves that shell's enrollments whose `enrollment_state` is `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED`. Hop three: each of those completions zeroes the shell's `active_enrollment_count`, `seat_utilization_pct` and `overflow_flag`, and decrements the term's `total_active_enrollments` by the number of rows that shell completed. The term's `published_shell_count` is then set to 0. Every successful term lifecycle transition, and every posture change of §3.9, finishes by re-deriving all four of the term's aggregate counters from the current population rather than by trusting an accumulated delta — `published_shell_count` from the shells still in `PUBLISHED`, `overflow_shell_count` from the shells whose `overflow_flag` is true, `total_active_enrollments` from the `active_enrollment_count` of the term's shells outside `REJECTED` and `PURGED`, and `open_amendment_count` from the term's passbacks sitting in `DISPUTED` or `AMENDED` — so the per-event deltas stated elsewhere and the re-derived figures always agree.

Archiving is the last step. `CLOSED` → `ARCHIVED` requires the logical clock to have reached or passed the term's `student_access_end_at` — an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, because students still hold read rights. The transition then walks every shell of the term whose `provisioning_state` is `CONCLUDED`, in `created_at` ascending order, and transitions each to `ARCHIVED`.

### 3.3. People, Shell Provisioning, Sandbox Governance and the Archival Tail

People arrive from the SIS person feed. A `Registrar` registers one with a `sis_person_id` (the SIS identifier, which the registrar's own feed keeps unique across all people, though the service enforces no uniqueness constraint on it and holds no error code for a collision), a `full_name`, and a `primary_affiliation` of `STUDENT`, `FACULTY` or `STAFF`, which is what nominally makes someone eligible to own a shell. Two compliance switches may be set at registration or adjusted later, together with the display name: `ferpa_block`, which defaults to false and when true forbids exporting that person's records to a third-party tool or to the registrar, and `access_revoked_at`, the instant at which LMS access was withdrawn, which stays null while access is live. Those three — `full_name`, `ferpa_block` and `access_revoked_at` — are the only fields a person update accepts, and supplying `access_revoked_at` explicitly as `null` clears it and restores live access. A person's `sandbox_storage_charge_usd` starts at 0.00 and is only ever written by the chargeback computation described later in this subsection.

An `LmsAdmin` requests a **course shell** by naming the term it belongs to through `term_id`, the instructor of record through `owner_person_id` — who is expected to have a `primary_affiliation` of `FACULTY` or `STAFF`, although the service does not verify the affiliation and accepts any registered person as an owner — the SIS section code as `sis_section_code`, and the governance class as `shell_type`, which is `OFFICIAL` for a real section, `SANDBOX` for an instructor's scratch space, or `DEV` for a development copy. An optional `base_seat_cap` declares the SIS seat cap before any overflow allowance; it defaults to 25 and must be at least 1 and at most 2000. The new shell opens in `provisioning_state` `REQUESTED`, its `effective_seat_cap` seeded from the term's current `continuity_posture` by the capacity rule of §3.4, its `sandbox_expires_at` set to sixty days after the term's `term_end_at` when the class is `SANDBOX` or `DEV` and left null for `OFFICIAL`, and the term's `rollover_completion_pct` recomputed to account for the new eligible shell. A shell may be requested against a term in any `lifecycle_state` — the calendar gate applies to the approval, not the request — and while still `REQUESTED` the shell already reads `publish_readiness` true and `blocked_placement_count` 0. Two limits bite at request time: section codes must be unique per term among live `OFFICIAL` shells (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SECTION_CODE`), and one person may own at most three live sandbox or development shells (`ERR_SANDBOX_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`).

A requested shell then goes to an `LmsAdmin` for a provisioning decision, carried as a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `reason`. The two branches diverge sharply. `APPROVE` moves `REQUESTED` → `PROVISIONED`, and requires the shell's term to be in `PLANNING`, `REGISTRATION_OPEN` or `IN_SESSION` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`) and the owner's `access_revoked_at` to be null (otherwise `ERR_FERPA_BLOCK_ACTIVE`). On approval `effective_seat_cap` is set equal to `base_seat_cap`, `publish_readiness` is set to true, and `sandbox_expires_at` is set to sixty days after the term's `term_end_at` for a `SANDBOX` or `DEV` shell while remaining null for an `OFFICIAL` one. `REJECT` moves `REQUESTED` → `REJECTED`, which is terminal: it requires only that the shell still be `REQUESTED`, records the supplied reason into `reject_reason`, sets the shell's `storage_mb` to 0.00, and recomputes the owner's `sandbox_storage_charge_usd` — necessarily downward, since a rejected shell stops being billable. Rejection sets no seat cap and no sandbox expiry.

Publishing a provisioned shell to students is available to both the owning `Instructor` and an `LmsAdmin`, and it carries five preconditions, each with its own refusal. The shell's `shell_type` must be `OFFICIAL`, since sandbox and development shells are never student-visible (`ERR_SANDBOX_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`); its `merged_into_shell_id` must be null, since an absorbed shell has no independent existence (`ERR_SHELL_ALREADY_MERGED`); the term's `lifecycle_state` must be `REGISTRATION_OPEN` or `IN_SESSION` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`); the term's `continuity_posture` must be anything other than `CONTINUITY_MODE` (`ERR_TERM_POSTURE_FROZEN`); and `publish_readiness` must be true (`ERR_SHELL_NOT_PUBLISH_READY`). A successful publish moves `PROVISIONED` → `PUBLISHED` and increments the term's `published_shell_count` by one.

The reverse move, `PUBLISHED` → `PROVISIONED`, exists in two forms. When an operator drives it, an optional `reason` is recorded into `unpublish_reason` and the posture gate applies exactly as it does for publishing, so unpublishing during `CONTINUITY_MODE` is refused with `ERR_TERM_POSTURE_FROZEN`. When the server drives it as a safety response to a tool placement becoming `BLOCKED`, `unpublish_reason` is recorded as `tool placement blocked` and the posture gate is deliberately bypassed — pulling a shell offline because a tool was blocked is a protective act, not an authoring action. Either form decrements the term's `published_shell_count` by one, and a shell may cycle between `PROVISIONED` and `PUBLISHED` any number of times.

Sandbox and development shells are not free. Whenever a content item is added to or removed from such a shell, whenever such a shell's `storage_mb` changes because a rollover copied into it or a compensation unwound the copy, and whenever a shell is purged, the owner's `sandbox_storage_charge_usd` is recomputed from scratch. The billable set is that person's shells whose `shell_type` is `SANDBOX` or `DEV` and whose `provisioning_state` is neither `REJECTED` nor `PURGED`; `OFFICIAL` shells are never charged. Walk that set in `created_at` ascending order — never in identifier order, so the accumulated total is reproducible. For each shell, subtract a free allowance of 100.00 megabytes from its `storage_mb` and treat any negative result as zero; multiply the remainder by a rate of 0.0150 US dollars per megabyte; multiply again by the class multiplier, which is 1.00 for a `SANDBOX` shell and 0.50 for a `DEV` shell; then round that single shell's charge half up to two decimal places. The person's total is the sum of those already-rounded per-shell charges. **The rounding order is pinned and observable:** for an owner holding a `SANDBOX` shell at 210.00 megabytes, a `DEV` shell at 175.00 megabytes and a second `SANDBOX` shell at 187.50 megabytes, rounding per shell first yields 1.65 plus 0.56 plus 1.31, and the stored total must be 3.52 — never the 3.53 that summing the unrounded 1.65, 0.5625 and 1.3125 and rounding once at the end would produce.

Sandbox shells also expire. Once the logical clock passes a shell's `sandbox_expires_at`, that shell accepts no new content items, no new tool placements and no new rollover jobs targeting it; each such attempt is refused with `ERR_SANDBOX_EXPIRED`.

The archival tail is driven by a `Registrar` supplying a `target_state`, and it can also arrive as the term-closing cascade of §3.2. Any of the eight `provisioning_state` values is accepted as a `target_state`; the five that are not `CONCLUDED`, `ARCHIVED` or `PURGED` are refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than as a request-shape failure. `PROVISIONED` → `CONCLUDED` and `PUBLISHED` → `CONCLUDED` both require the shell's `merged_into_shell_id` to be null (`ERR_SHELL_ALREADY_MERGED`), and both move every enrollment of the shell whose `enrollment_state` is `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED` in `created_at` ascending order, zero the shell's `active_enrollment_count`, set its `seat_utilization_pct` to 0.00 and its `overflow_flag` to false, and decrement the term's `total_active_enrollments` by the number of rows completed. The only difference between the two is that concluding a shell that was `PUBLISHED` additionally decrements the term's `published_shell_count` by one, while concluding a `PROVISIONED` shell does not, because it was never counted there.

`CONCLUDED` → `ARCHIVED` requires the shell's term to have reached `CLOSED` or `ARCHIVED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`). It then walks every tool placement of the shell whose `placement_state` is `REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE` or `BLOCKED`, in `created_at` ascending order, and moves each to `REMOVED`; each removal that took an `ACTIVE` placement away decrements that tool's `active_placement_count` and re-scores that tool's `risk_score`. Afterwards the shell's `blocked_placement_count` reads 0 and its `publish_readiness` reads true.

`ARCHIVED` → `PURGED` is terminal and doubly gated. First, the term's `retention_class` must not be `LEGAL_HOLD`; a shell under legal hold is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_HOLD_ACTIVE`, and because that check outranks the horizon check, it is the code reported even though such a term's `retention_purge_at` is null. Second, the logical clock must have reached or passed the term's `retention_purge_at`; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_PURGE_NOT_DUE`. Purging sets every content item of the shell to `copy_state` `SKIPPED` with `storage_mb` 0.00, sets the shell's own `content_item_count` to 0, its `total_points_possible` to 0.00 and its `storage_mb` to 0.00, and recomputes the owner's `sandbox_storage_charge_usd`. A purged shell is soft-deleted rather than erased: it disappears from shell listings unless the caller explicitly filters on a `provisioning_state` of `PURGED`, in which case it is returned as normal, and it remains readable by identifier.

### 3.4. Enrollment, Seats, the Waitlist and the SIS Feed

A `Registrar` enrols a person in a shell by naming the shell through `shell_id` and the person through `person_id`, and by assigning an LMS `role` of `STUDENT`, `TEACHER`, `TA`, `DESIGNER` or `OBSERVER`. An optional `requested_state` decides which door the row comes in through: omitted or `ACTIVE` asks for a live seat, while `WAITLISTED` asks explicitly for a queue slot. A manually created enrollment leaves `batch_id` null — that field is only stamped when the SIS feed creates or last mutates the row — and leaves `merged_from_shell_id` null, which is the cross-list origin marker described in §3.5.

Asking for a live seat requires three things: the shell's `provisioning_state` is `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED` (otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`); no other enrollment exists for that same shell-and-person pair whose `enrollment_state` is `ACTIVE`, `WAITLISTED` or `DROPPED` (otherwise `ERR_ROLE_CONFLICT`); and, when the `role` is `STUDENT`, the shell's `active_enrollment_count` is still below its `effective_seat_cap` (otherwise `ERR_SEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED`). The new row opens in `enrollment_state` `ACTIVE`, with `earned_points_total`, `graded_points_possible`, `provisional_grade_pct` and `disputed_points_held` at 0.00, `final_grade_pct` null, `reinstatement_count` 0 and `dropped_at` null; when its `role` is `STUDENT` the shell's `active_enrollment_count` and the term's `total_active_enrollments` each rise by one, and the shell's `seat_utilization_pct` and `overflow_flag` are re-derived. Asking explicitly for the queue is narrower: only a `STUDENT` may be waitlisted, and any other role asking for it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The shell-state and duplicate-pair checks apply identically. A waitlisted row's `waitlist_position` is set to one more than the shell's current `waitlisted_enrollment_count`, and that count then rises by one; a waitlisted row consumes no seat and moves neither the shell's active count nor the term's total.

**Seat capacity is computed in a pinned order**, and the order matters because the dependency only looks circular. Step one derives the overflow ratio from the *term's* `continuity_posture` and from nothing else: 0.00 under `NORMAL`, 0.10 under `ELEVATED`, and 0.20 under `CONTINUITY_MODE`. The overflow allowance is the shell's `base_seat_cap` multiplied by that ratio and rounded **down** to a whole seat. Step two freezes `effective_seat_cap` at `base_seat_cap` plus that allowance, and that frozen value governs the entire run — it is never recomputed between individual promotions inside one run. The freeze is derived afresh from the term's current posture every time the rule runs, and it is that freshly derived number, never whatever sits in the stored `effective_seat_cap` field, that the seat gates of `POST /enrollments`, `PROMOTE` and `REINSTATE` compare against; the two readings can genuinely differ, because a provisioning approval writes `base_seat_cap` straight into `effective_seat_cap` (§3.3) without consulting the posture. Because the promotion gate freezes the cap into the stored field before it tests the promotable count, a `PROMOTE` refused with `ERR_SEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED` still leaves the shell's `effective_seat_cap` at the freshly frozen number while the shell's `version` stays put, whereas the create and reinstate gates derive the same number without writing it. Step three derives the open seats as `effective_seat_cap` minus `active_enrollment_count`, treated as zero if that difference would be negative, and derives the promotable head count as the smaller of the open seats and the shell's `waitlisted_enrollment_count`. Step four promotes exactly that many queued rows, taken in `waitlist_position` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and renumbers the surviving queue from 1 upward. Only in step five are the observables recomputed: `seat_utilization_pct` becomes `active_enrollment_count` divided by `base_seat_cap`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `overflow_flag` becomes true exactly when `active_enrollment_count` is greater than `base_seat_cap`. The utilization denominator is always `base_seat_cap` and never `effective_seat_cap`, and `overflow_flag` is strictly an output of step five: it must never feed back into the ratio chosen in step one.

Rounding the allowance down has visible consequences at small caps. Under `ELEVATED`, a shell with `base_seat_cap` 9 gets an allowance of 0 and an `effective_seat_cap` of 9, while a shell with `base_seat_cap` 10 gets an allowance of 1 and an `effective_seat_cap` of 11. An overflowing shell legitimately reports above full utilization: eleven active students against a base cap of 10 reads a `seat_utilization_pct` of 110.00 with `overflow_flag` true. Only rows whose `role` is `STUDENT` participate in any of this — `TEACHER`, `TA`, `DESIGNER` and `OBSERVER` rows never consume a seat and never appear on a waitlist. The capacity rule is evaluated on every enrollment create, on every drop that frees a seat, on every applied SIS operation in array order, and whenever the term's `continuity_posture` changes, since the ratio moves with it. Step four's promotion, though, fires only where a seat is actually freed — an individual drop, a drop from the SIS feed, and a posture change on a `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED` shell — so an enrollment create, a reinstatement and a completion run the rule without promoting anyone, and a row that asked explicitly for the queue stays queued even when the shell has seats to spare. Both directions of a cross-list merge sit outside the rule altogether: they re-derive step five's observables on the two shells, freeze no new `effective_seat_cap` and promote nobody.

Individual enrollments are driven by a `Registrar` through an `action` of `DROP`, `REINSTATE`, `PROMOTE` or `COMPLETE`, with an optional `reason` that must be a non-empty string whenever it is supplied and is not persisted on the enrollment. **Promotion** takes `WAITLISTED` → `ACTIVE`; it requires the capacity rule to report at least one promotable slot (otherwise `ERR_SEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED`) and the shell to be `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED` (otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`). The promoted row's `waitlist_position` becomes null and the shell's remaining queued rows are renumbered from 1 in `waitlist_position` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; the shell's `waitlisted_enrollment_count` falls by one, its `active_enrollment_count` rises by one, its `effective_seat_cap`, `seat_utilization_pct` and `overflow_flag` are re-derived, and the term's `total_active_enrollments` rises by one. If the shell's `overflow_flag` flips from false to true in the process, the term's `overflow_shell_count` rises by one.

**Dropping** takes `ACTIVE` → `DROPPED` and needs only that the shell be `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED` (otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`). It stamps `dropped_at` from the logical clock and then cascades in four hops: the shell's `active_enrollment_count` and the term's `total_active_enrollments` each fall by one; the freed seat re-runs the capacity rule, and while it reports at least one promotable slot the head of the shell's waitlist — chosen by `waitlist_position` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — moves `WAITLISTED` → `ACTIVE`; each such promotion re-derives the shell's `effective_seat_cap`, `seat_utilization_pct` and `overflow_flag`; and any change in `overflow_flag` bubbles up to the term's `overflow_shell_count`. A drop whose freed seat is immediately consumed by a promotion therefore nets to zero at the term level: minus one for the drop, plus one for the promotion.

**Reinstatement** takes `DROPPED` → `ACTIVE` and closes that cycle, bounded by the calendar rather than by any loop counter. It requires the logical clock to be at or before the term's `add_drop_deadline_at` (otherwise `ERR_ADD_DROP_WINDOW_CLOSED`), the shell to be `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED` (otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`), and — for a `STUDENT` row — the shell's `active_enrollment_count` to still be below its `effective_seat_cap` (otherwise `ERR_SEAT_CAP_EXCEEDED`). It increments `reinstatement_count` by one, sets `dropped_at` back to null, and for a `STUDENT` row raises the shell's `active_enrollment_count` and the term's `total_active_enrollments` by one each while re-deriving `seat_utilization_pct` and `overflow_flag`. **Completion** takes `ACTIVE` → `COMPLETED`, the terminal state, and requires the shell to be `PUBLISHED` or `CONCLUDED` (otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`); it lowers the shell's `active_enrollment_count` and the term's `total_active_enrollments` by one each and re-derives the shell's `seat_utilization_pct` and `overflow_flag`. Only a `COMPLETED` row frees a shell-and-person pair for a brand-new enrollment; a `DROPPED` row must be reinstated instead.

The seat ledger of §2.8 is maintained by a fixed set of per-event deltas over `STUDENT` rows: a row created `ACTIVE` adds one to both the shell and the term; a row created `WAITLISTED` adds nothing to either; `ACTIVE` → `DROPPED` subtracts one from each; a waitlist promotion adds one to each; a reinstatement adds one to each; `ACTIVE` → `COMPLETED` subtracts one from each; and a cross-list merge moves N rows from child to parent, subtracting N from the child and adding N to the parent while leaving the term unchanged, with the reversal doing the exact opposite. Zero is a live boundary: a shell whose only active row drops reads `active_enrollment_count` 0, `seat_utilization_pct` 0.00 and `overflow_flag` false, and the term falls by exactly one.

The SIS feed arrives as an ordered batch rather than as individual calls. A `Registrar` stages one against a term through `term_id`, supplying `operations` — an ordered array of between 1 and 200 objects, each carrying an `op` of either `ADD` or `DROP` together with a `shell_id`, a `person_id` and a `role` — and optionally a `batch_seq`, which defaults to the term's `sis_sync_epoch` plus one. Staging inspects an operation only as far as its shape: the `shell_id` and `person_id` inside one are checked to be non-empty strings and are deliberately *not* resolved, so an identifier naming nothing at all is neither a record-specific 404 nor a request-shape failure here, merely an operation that validation will go on to count as rejected. A supplied `batch_seq` is likewise held to nothing beyond being an integer — no range is enforced at staging, because a value out of feed order is caught at apply time and nowhere earlier. A staged batch opens in `batch_state` `DRAFT` with every tally at zero, `failure_reason` null, and its `operations` echoed back as objects carrying `op`, `shell_id`, `person_id` and `role` alone, any further key of a supplied operation having been dropped.

Validating a batch (`VALIDATE`) takes `DRAFT` → `VALIDATED` and requires the term's `lifecycle_state` to be `REGISTRATION_OPEN` or `IN_SESSION`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Each operation is examined in array order and counts as valid when its `shell_id` resolves to a shell belonging to this batch's term whose `provisioning_state` is `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED`, and its `person_id` resolves to a person whose `access_revoked_at` is null. The pass writes `validated_operation_count` and `rejected_operation_count` and touches no enrollment whatsoever. A `DRAFT` batch may instead be abandoned (`CANCEL`), which takes it to the terminal `CANCELLED` with no other effect.

Applying a validated batch (`APPLY`) forks two ways on the same action, and both branches are ordinary 200 responses carrying the batch — the failure branch is not an error envelope. The batch takes `VALIDATED` → `APPLIED` when its `rejected_operation_count` is 0 and every operation's shell is still `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED`; it additionally requires `batch_seq` to equal the term's `sis_sync_epoch` plus one, and a batch arriving out of feed order is refused outright with `ERR_SYNC_EPOCH_OUT_OF_ORDER`. Before either of those is weighed, the batch's `ADD` operations are swept in array order for role conflicts: an `ADD` naming a shell-and-person pair that already holds an `ACTIVE`, `WAITLISTED` or `DROPPED` enrollment is refused with `ERR_ROLE_CONFLICT`, and so is a second `ADD` naming a pair an earlier `ADD` of the same batch has already claimed. That sweep outranks the feed-order check and the failure fork alike, so a batch that would otherwise have failed quietly answers with the `ERR_ROLE_CONFLICT` envelope instead; the owner-may-not-be-their-own-student bar and the sandbox-shell bar of `POST /enrollments` are **not** re-applied on this path. On the applied branch the operations execute in array order: an `ADD` creates an enrollment with `batch_id` set to this batch, live when the capacity rule reports a free seat and `WAITLISTED` when it does not — that graceful degradation reaching a `STUDENT` role alone, every other role being created `ACTIVE` however full the shell is — and a `DROP` moves the pair's `ACTIVE` row to `DROPPED`, contributing nothing to `applied_drop_count` when the pair holds no such row while still running the freed-seat promotion pass over that operation's shell. The batch records `applied_add_count` and `applied_drop_count`, each drop's freed seat cascades waitlist promotions exactly as an individual drop does, `promoted_from_waitlist_count` records how many promotions fired across the whole batch, and the term's `sis_sync_epoch` advances by one. The batch instead takes `VALIDATED` → `FAILED` when its `rejected_operation_count` is greater than 0, or when at least one operation's shell has left `PROVISIONED` and `PUBLISHED` since validation; on that branch `failure_reason` is recorded as `rejected operations present` when the rejected tally is what failed the batch and as `shell state changed since validation` when drift alone is — the rejected tally taking precedence when both are true — not a single enrollment is created or mutated — the batch applies all-or-nothing — and the term's `sis_sync_epoch` is left exactly as it was. Both `APPLIED` and `FAILED` are terminal. Note that batch `ADD` is the one place in the service where a full shell degrades gracefully onto the waitlist instead of erroring, precisely because a batch must apply as a whole.

### 3.5. Cross-Listing Two Sections Through a Merge Request

Two sections that meet as one class are cross-listed by merging a child shell into a parent shell. An `LmsAdmin` opens a merge request by naming `parent_shell_id` and `child_shell_id`, optionally supplying a `justification` (which defaults to "cross-listed section") and mandatorily supplying `expected_parent_shell_version`. That last field is the stale-view guard of §2.7: cross-listing is a supersede-style create, because a second request may legitimately target the same parent as an earlier one, so a client working from an out-of-date picture of the parent shell can only be caught by comparing its `expected_parent_shell_version` against the parent's current `version`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. The request opens in `merge_state` `DRAFT` with `submitted_by_user_id` recorded from the acting user, `decided_by_user_id` null, both relocation tallies at zero, and both seat-ledger readings null.

The lifecycle runs `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` → `APPLIED` → `REVERSED`, with a divergent decision branch to the terminal `DECLINED`. **Submitting** (`SUBMIT`) requires both the parent and the child shell to be `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. **Approving** (`APPROVE`) is the segregation-of-duties gate: the acting user must not be the `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. It further re-applies all three of the create's linkage gates in the same order: the child's `merged_into_shell_id` must still be null and its `provisioning_state` must not be `MERGED` (otherwise `ERR_SHELL_ALREADY_MERGED`), the parent and child must share the same `term_id` and both be `OFFICIAL` (otherwise `ERR_CROSS_TERM_MERGE`), and the parent's own `merged_into_shell_id` must be null with the child's `absorbed_child_count` at 0 (otherwise `ERR_MERGE_CHAIN_FORBIDDEN`), which is what forbids merge chains — a shell that has itself absorbed another section cannot become someone else's child. Approving records `decided_by_user_id` from the acting user and arms the apply step. **Declining** (`DECLINE`) shares the same segregation-of-duties gate and also records `decided_by_user_id`, but it is terminal and touches no shell and no enrollment at all: the two branches of the decision fork differ only in the action word, and one arms a relocation while the other leaves the world exactly as it found it.

**Applying** (`APPLY`) is the relocation, and it requires once more that the child's `merged_into_shell_id` still be null, otherwise `ERR_SHELL_ALREADY_MERGED`. It first records `seat_ledger_before` as the term's `total_active_enrollments` read immediately before any mutation. Then every enrollment of the child shell whose `enrollment_state` is `ACTIVE` is relocated in `created_at` ascending order: its `shell_id` is repointed at the parent and its `merged_from_shell_id` is stamped with the child's identifier, and `moved_enrollment_count` records how many rows moved. The child shell itself moves `PROVISIONED` → `MERGED`, or `PUBLISHED` → `MERGED` if it was live, taking `merged_into_shell_id` to the parent's identifier, zeroing its `active_enrollment_count` and `waitlisted_enrollment_count`, setting its `seat_utilization_pct` to 0.00 and its `overflow_flag` to false, and — in the published case only — lowering the term's `published_shell_count` by one. A `WAITLISTED` row of the child does not travel: it stays on the child with its `waitlist_position` intact and is merely uncounted for as long as the child sits in `MERGED`, which is why the reversal below can hand the child its queue back. The parent's `active_enrollment_count`, `seat_utilization_pct` and `overflow_flag` are re-derived and its `absorbed_child_count` rises by one. **No waitlist promotion is triggered on the parent by this transition**: an absorbing parent that ends up over its cap simply reports `overflow_flag` true rather than pulling its own queue forward. Finally `seat_ledger_after` records the term's `total_active_enrollments` after the relocation, and the seat ledger requires that it equal `seat_ledger_before` exactly — a merge moves students between shells and never changes how many of them the term is carrying. A parent holding 8 active students and a child holding 5, in a term carrying 13, therefore ends with the parent at 13, the child at 0 and the term still at 13.

**Reversing** (`REVERSE`) is the compensating transition and requires the parent to be `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Every enrollment sitting on the parent whose `merged_from_shell_id` points at this child is sent home in `created_at` ascending order: its `shell_id` returns to the child and its `merged_from_shell_id` returns to null, with `reversed_enrollment_count` recording how many rows returned. The child moves `MERGED` → `PROVISIONED`, its `merged_into_shell_id` returns to null and its seat observables are recomputed from the returned rows; the parent's `absorbed_child_count` falls by one and its own seat observables are recomputed too. A child that was `PUBLISHED` before the merge comes back as `PROVISIONED`, **not** as `PUBLISHED`, and must be published again explicitly. The term's `total_active_enrollments` is left at its pre-apply value, so the round trip through apply and reverse restores the parent to 8, the child to 5 and the term to 13 with zero drift. `REVERSED` and `DECLINED` are the two terminal states.

A merged child is out of play for everything else. Because concluding a shell requires its `merged_into_shell_id` to be null (§3.3), a child that has been folded into a parent cannot be concluded on its own, and because publishing carries the same requirement, it cannot be published either; both refuse with `ERR_SHELL_ALREADY_MERGED`. The child must be released by a reversal first.

### 3.6. Course Content and Rolling It Over Into the Next Term

Content lives on a shell. An `Instructor` or an `LmsAdmin` adds an item by naming `shell_id`, an `item_type` of `ASSIGNMENT`, `QUIZ`, `PAGE` or `FILE`, and a `title`, optionally with a `due_at`, a `points_possible` and a `storage_mb`; the latter two each default to 0.00 and must be at least 0. Authoring an item is gated only by the ownership rule of §2.1 and, on a `SANDBOX` or `DEV` shell, by the expiry gate of §3.3 — the shell's own `provisioning_state` is never consulted, so content may be added to or edited on a shell that is still `REQUESTED` or already `CONCLUDED`, `ARCHIVED` or `MERGED`. Reads are open to every authorized role including a `Student`, with no check that the caller is enrolled in the shell and no post-term access gate. A new item opens with `copy_state` `SOURCE`, `date_stripped` false, `submission_count` 0, `has_submissions` false and `source_item_id` null — the last field is the copy-provenance self-reference, filled in only on rollover copies. Creating, editing or removing an item re-derives the owning shell's `storage_mb` (the sum of its items' `storage_mb`), its `content_item_count` (the number of its items whose `copy_state` is not `SKIPPED`) and its `total_points_possible` (the sum of `points_possible` over its `ASSIGNMENT` and `QUIZ` items whose `copy_state` is not `SKIPPED`), and re-derives the shell owner's `sandbox_storage_charge_usd` for `SANDBOX` and `DEV` shells. `PAGE` and `FILE` items carry `points_possible` 0.00 and never contribute points. An item may be edited in place — `title`, `due_at`, `points_possible`, `storage_mb`, with `due_at` cleared by supplying it explicitly as `null` — and may be removed only while its `has_submissions` is false; removing an item that has submissions is refused with `ERR_ITEM_HAS_SUBMISSIONS`, because the gradebook must keep them. A removal deletes the row outright rather than soft-deleting it, so a later read of that identifier answers `ERR_CONTENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`. A submission is recorded against an item by naming the `enrollment_id` it belongs to; the item's `has_submissions` becomes true and its `submission_count` rises by one. That call answers `201` carrying the updated content item, and beyond requiring the `enrollment_id` to resolve it checks neither that the enrollment sits on the same shell as the item nor that the item is of a gradable type. That single flag is what later makes an item ineligible for a submissions-preserving rollover copy.

A rollover copies a finished shell's content into a fresh shell in a later term. An `LmsAdmin` stages one by naming `source_shell_id`, `target_shell_id` and `target_term_id`, supplying the mandatory `expected_target_shell_version`, and optionally overriding four switches: `strip_dates` (default true), `strip_submissions` (default true), `strip_grades` (default true) and `date_shift_mode` (default `ANCHOR_PROPORTIONAL`, the alternative being `FIXED_OFFSET`). The `expected_target_shell_version` is the stale-view guard: a second job legitimately supersedes an earlier one for the same target, so only the target shell's `version` can tell the caller that the picture moved underneath them, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. The job opens in `job_state` `DRAFT` with every tally at zero.

**Validating** (`VALIDATE`) takes `DRAFT` → `VALIDATED` and requires four things: the source shell's `provisioning_state` is `CONCLUDED` or `ARCHIVED` and its own `term_id` is not the job's `target_term_id`, otherwise `ERR_ROLLOVER_SOURCE_INELIGIBLE`; the target shell's `provisioning_state` is exactly `PROVISIONED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the target shell's `content_item_count` is 0 and it carries no enrollment at all, otherwise `ERR_ROLLOVER_TARGET_NOT_EMPTY`; and `target_term_id` matches the target shell's own `term_id`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — that last one sits in the request-shape band and is therefore decided ahead of the `DRAFT` precondition itself. Validation sets `eligible_item_count` to the number of the source shell's items whose `copy_state` is not `SKIPPED`, and copies nothing.

**Executing** (`EXECUTE`) takes `VALIDATED` → `COPYING` and requires the target shell to still be `PROVISIONED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Each source item whose `copy_state` is not `SKIPPED` is processed in `created_at` ascending order. When `strip_submissions` is false and the source item's `has_submissions` is true the item is passed over and `skipped_item_count` rises by one — a submissions-preserving rollover cannot carry an item whose submissions belong to last term's students. Otherwise a copy is created in the target shell with `copy_state` `COPIED` and `source_item_id` set to the source item's identifier, carrying the source item's `item_type`, `title`, `points_possible` and `storage_mb` across unchanged; when `strip_submissions` is true the copy opens with `submission_count` 0 and `has_submissions` false. The job accumulates `copied_item_count` and `copied_storage_mb`, and the target shell's `storage_mb`, `content_item_count` and `total_points_possible` are re-derived. No grade record itself is ever carried across, whichever way `strip_grades` is set; the switch decides only whether the matching target rows are touched at all. With `strip_grades` true they are left alone. With it false the source shell's enrollments are walked in `created_at` ascending order and each one whose `person_id` matches an enrollment of the target shell hands that row its `earned_points_total`, which is then immediately re-derived — along with the row's `graded_points_possible` and `provisional_grade_pct` — from the target enrollment's *own* grade records, so a target row holding no grade records of its own settles straight back at 0.00 and the switch costs it a `version` bump rather than last term's points.

Dates are handled by the switch pair. When `strip_dates` is true the copy's `due_at` is set to null, its `date_stripped` is set to true, and `date_stripped_count` rises by one. When `strip_dates` is false the copy's `due_at` is **projected**, its `date_stripped` stays false, and `date_shifted_count` rises by one. Items whose source `due_at` is null — typically `PAGE` and `FILE` items — are copied with a null `due_at` and are counted in neither tally. Projection works from the source shell's term and the term named by `target_term_id`: the source span is the source term's `term_end_at` minus its `term_start_at` in seconds, the target span is the same difference on the target term, and the item's offset is its `due_at` minus the source term's `term_start_at`.

Under **`ANCHOR_PROPORTIONAL`** the item keeps its fractional position inside the instructional span, so the calendar stretches or compresses with the target term's length. The raw ratio is the offset divided by the source span; that ratio is then clamped into the range from 0.0 to 1.0 **before** it is scaled, never afterwards. The projected instant is the target term's `term_start_at` plus the ratio multiplied by the target span, rounded half up to a whole number of seconds. The ratio itself is carried at full precision and must never be pre-rounded, and the final product must be rounded half up rather than truncated, because the difference shows up in the stored value: an offset of 2,591,940 seconds against a 100-day source term and a 109-day target term gives 2,591,940 multiplied by 1.09, which is 2,825,214.6 seconds — half up yields 2,825,215 seconds, truncation would yield 2,825,214, and the copied `due_at` differs by one second.

Under **`FIXED_OFFSET`** the item keeps its day of the week instead. The gap between the two term starts is converted to a whole number of weeks by dividing by 604,800 seconds and rounding **down**; the item's `due_at` is shifted forward by exactly that many weeks; and the result is then clamped into the closed range from the target term's `term_start_at` to its `term_end_at`. The two variants genuinely diverge. With a source term running from 2026-01-12T00:00:00Z to 2026-04-22T00:00:00Z (100 days), a target term running from 2026-08-24T00:00:00Z to 2026-12-11T00:00:00Z (109 days), and a source item due at 2026-03-03T00:00:00Z — an offset of 50 days, a ratio of exactly 0.50 — `ANCHOR_PROPORTIONAL` projects 2026-10-17T12:00:00Z, while `FIXED_OFFSET` shifts by 224 days divided by 7, that is 32 whole weeks or 224 days, and projects 2026-10-13T00:00:00Z: four days and twelve hours apart from the same input.

The execute step then forks on storage, automatically and inside the same request. The job takes `COPYING` → `COMPLETED` once `copied_item_count` plus `skipped_item_count` equals `eligible_item_count` and the target shell is within its storage quota — meaning that for a `SANDBOX` or `DEV` target its `storage_mb` is at most the 500.00-megabyte sandbox quota — and completion re-derives the target term's `rollover_completion_pct` and advances that term's own `version` whether or not the figure moved. The job instead takes `COPYING` → `FAILED` when the copy pushed a `SANDBOX` or `DEV` target above that quota. The quota fork is weighed first and the tally equality only afterwards, so a job that stayed inside its quota but whose tallies no longer settle — a source item added or skipped between validation and execution is enough — comes to rest in `COPYING` with its copies in place and `failure_reason` null, and stays there, since no declared action departs from `COPYING`. On the failed branch `failure_reason` is recorded as "sandbox storage quota exceeded", **the copies are deliberately left in place** so the target shell is observable in its over-quota state, and the target term's `rollover_completion_pct` is not advanced.

**Compensating** (`COMPENSATE`) takes `FAILED` → `COMPENSATED` and is the undo. Every item of the target shell whose `copy_state` is `COPIED` and whose `source_item_id` points at an item of the source shell has its `copy_state` set to `SKIPPED` and its `storage_mb` set to 0.00; the target shell's `storage_mb` is reduced by `copied_storage_mb`, restoring its pre-execute value exactly, and its `content_item_count` and `total_points_possible` are re-derived. The source shell's `storage_mb` is left untouched, so the megabytes stored across the source-and-target pair return to their pre-execute sum. The job's `copied_item_count`, `copied_storage_mb`, `date_stripped_count` and `date_shifted_count` are deliberately left as the audit record of what was undone. `COMPLETED` and `COMPENSATED` are the two terminal states.

### 3.7. Third-Party Tool Governance and Placement

A third-party learning tool is registered by an `LmsAdmin` with a `vendor_name`, a `tool_name`, a `tool_version` and a `data_sharing_scope` of `NONE`, `ROSTER_ONLY`, `ROSTER_AND_GRADES` or `FULL_PROFILE`, optionally with a `pii_tier` between 0 and 3 inclusive, a `vpat_score` between 0.00 and 100.00 (default 100.00) and a `wcag_level` of `NONE`, `A`, `AA` (the default) or `AAA`. Those five governance attributes — `tool_version`, `data_sharing_scope`, `pii_tier`, `vpat_score` and `wcag_level` — remain editable while the tool is registered, whatever `approval_state` it currently holds, and an update accepts nothing else: `vendor_name` and `tool_name` are fixed at registration. A new tool opens with `approval_state` `DRAFT`, `privacy_review_state` `PENDING`, `accessibility_review_state` `PENDING`, `risk_score` null, `approved_version` null, `active_placement_count` 0 and `suspension_reason` null.

Every tool therefore carries **three state dimensions at once**: a privacy verdict, an accessibility verdict, and an overall `approval_state` that is a function of the other two together with the tool's computed risk. The two verdict dimensions move only through reviews; the overall dimension moves only through administrative actions.

An `LmsAdmin` opens the review by an `action` of `OPEN_REVIEW`, which takes `approval_state` `DRAFT` → `UNDER_REVIEW` and requires both verdict dimensions to be `PENDING`. A `PrivacyReviewer` then files the privacy verdict and an `AccessibilityReviewer` the accessibility verdict, each naming `tool_id`, a `dimension` of `PRIVACY` or `ACCESSIBILITY`, a `decision` of `CLEAR`, `CONDITIONS` or `BLOCK`, and optionally a `conditions_note`. A review row is a permanent record: it carries only its own identifier and its creation timestamp, it is never edited, never deleted and carries no row version of its own — the audit trail is append-only. Filing a review requires the tool's `approval_state` to be `UNDER_REVIEW` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`) and requires that no review already exist for that tool in that same `dimension` (otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_DIMENSION_CONFLICT`); each dimension is decided exactly once per review round. A `CONDITIONS` decision must carry a non-empty `conditions_note`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two dimensions must also be decided by two different people: the acting user may not be the `reviewer_user_id` recorded on this tool's review in the *other* dimension **of the current review round**, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; a verdict filed in an earlier round no longer binds, so the person who decided privacy in round one may decide accessibility in round two. A `CLEAR` decision moves that dimension `PENDING` → `CLEARED`, `CONDITIONS` moves it `PENDING` → `CONDITIONAL`, and `BLOCK` moves it `PENDING` → `BLOCKED`.

The tool's `risk_score` is a weighted composite of its governance attributes and its live exposure, computed from five addends. The PII points are the `pii_tier` multiplied by 10, so 0 through 30. The scope points are 0 for `NONE`, 10 for `ROSTER_ONLY`, 25 for `ROSTER_AND_GRADES` and 40 for `FULL_PROFILE`. The accessibility-gap points are 100 minus the `vpat_score`, multiplied by 0.30. The conformance points are 20 for `NONE`, 10 for `A`, 0 for `AA` and **minus 5** for `AAA` — the top conformance level earns a credit, which is why the raw total can go below zero. The exposure points are the `active_placement_count` multiplied by 3, capped at 15. The raw total is the sum of those five, and the stored `risk_score` is that total clamped into the range from 0.00 to 100.00 and then rounded half up to two decimal places — rounded **once**, after clamping, never per addend. Review rows never enter the arithmetic: a `BLOCK` vetoes the outcome from outside the number, and `CONDITIONS` forces at least a conditional outcome without changing it. Both clamps are reachable: a tool with `pii_tier` 3, `FULL_PROFILE`, a `vpat_score` of 0.00, `wcag_level` `NONE` and five active placements totals 30 plus 40 plus 30.00 plus 20 plus 15, that is 135.00, and stores 100.00; a tool with `pii_tier` 0, `NONE`, a `vpat_score` of 100.00, `wcag_level` `AAA` and no placements totals minus 5.00 and stores 0.00. The score is recomputed and persisted whenever the review is finalized, whenever a placement becomes active, whenever an `ACTIVE` placement is removed, and whenever the tool is suspended or rejected. Nothing else re-scores it: editing the governance attributes through the update endpoint leaves the stored `risk_score` exactly as the last scoring event left it — so a tool can carry a score its own attributes no longer produce until the next scoring event — filing a review does not re-score, and removing a `REQUESTED` or a `BLOCKED` placement leaves the tool untouched altogether.

Finalizing the review is a single `action` of `FINALIZE_REVIEW` that **forks three ways**. All three branches first require both verdict dimensions to have left `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE`, and all three recompute and persist `risk_score`. The tool takes `UNDER_REVIEW` → `APPROVED` when neither dimension is `BLOCKED`, both dimensions are `CLEARED`, and the freshly computed score is below 40.00; `approved_version` is set to the tool's current `tool_version`. The tool takes `UNDER_REVIEW` → `CONDITIONAL` when neither dimension is `BLOCKED` and either at least one dimension is `CONDITIONAL` or the score falls in the range from 40.00 up to but not including 70.00; `approved_version` is likewise set to the current `tool_version`, and a conditional tool may still be placed. The tool takes `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REJECTED` when either dimension is `BLOCKED` or the score is 70.00 or above; `approved_version` is left null, and this is the only branch that cascades into placements — every placement of the tool whose `placement_state` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `BLOCKED` in `created_at` ascending order with `blocked_reason` "tool rejected". That sweep then re-derives the tool's `active_placement_count` and re-scores it once more, after the last placement has been blocked; because activating a placement demands a tool that is already `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, a tool being finalized never holds an active placement, so the exposure addend contributes nothing to the deciding score, the count reads 0 both before and after the cascade, and the post-cascade re-score restates the same figure. `REJECTED` is terminal: no declared action leaves it, so a rejected tool is re-registered rather than re-reviewed. Both thresholds are inclusive on their lower edge, and the boundaries are live: `pii_tier` 0 with `ROSTER_AND_GRADES`, a `vpat_score` of 50.00, `wcag_level` `AA` and no placements scores exactly 40.00 and is therefore `CONDITIONAL` rather than `APPROVED`, while the same tool with a `vpat_score` of 51.00 scores 39.70 and is `APPROVED`; `pii_tier` 3 with `ROSTER_AND_GRADES`, a `vpat_score` of 50.00, `wcag_level` `AA` and no placements scores exactly 70.00 and is `REJECTED`.

An approved or conditional tool can be **suspended** (`SUSPEND`), which records `suspension_reason` from the request `reason` (defaulting to "privacy incident") and runs the service's deepest cascade in four hops. Every placement of the tool whose `placement_state` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `BLOCKED` in `created_at` ascending order; each of those blockings raises its shell's `blocked_placement_count` by one and sets that shell's `publish_readiness` to false; each shell whose `publish_readiness` just became false and whose `provisioning_state` is `PUBLISHED` is unpublished back to `PROVISIONED` with `unpublish_reason` "tool placement blocked"; and each of those unpublishings lowers the term's `published_shell_count` by one. The tool's `active_placement_count` is re-derived as each placement is blocked and so lands at 0, while its `risk_score` is recomputed **once**, after the whole sweep has finished, rather than once per blocked placement; `approved_version` is left standing, so a suspended tool still reports the version it was approved at. The system-driven unpublishing deliberately ignores the frozen-posture gate of §3.9, because it is a safety response rather than an authoring action.

A suspended tool can be **reopened** (`REOPEN_REVIEW`), which takes `approval_state` `SUSPENDED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` and resets both verdict dimensions to `PENDING` — a `CLEARED` dimension returns to `PENDING`, and so does a `CONDITIONAL` one. The tool's existing review rows are retained as history but no longer decide either dimension, `approved_version` returns to null, and `suspension_reason` is left standing as history. Re-review therefore requires two fresh review rows, one per dimension, and the cycle from `UNDER_REVIEW` through `APPROVED` and `SUSPENDED` back to `UNDER_REVIEW` can be walked repeatedly.

A tool is put in front of students by placing it in a shell: an `Instructor` or `LmsAdmin` names a `tool_id` and a `shell_id`, and the placement opens in `placement_state` `REQUESTED` with `pinned_tool_version` null. **Activating** (`ACTIVATE`) runs from `REQUESTED` and from nowhere else — a placement that has reached `BLOCKED` can never be activated, only removed — and requires the tool's `approval_state` to be `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, otherwise `ERR_TOOL_NOT_APPROVED`; the tool's `approved_version` to equal its current `tool_version`, otherwise `ERR_TOOL_VERSION_DRIFT`, which is what stops a vendor upgrade from slipping into classrooms on the strength of an older approval; the shell's `provisioning_state` to be `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`; and, when the tool's `data_sharing_scope` is anything other than `NONE`, that no enrollment of the shell whose `enrollment_state` is `ACTIVE` or `WAITLISTED` belongs to a person whose `ferpa_block` is true, otherwise `ERR_FERPA_BLOCK_ACTIVE`. Activation pins `pinned_tool_version` to the tool's `approved_version`, raises the tool's `active_placement_count` by one, re-scores the tool, and re-derives the shell's `publish_readiness`.

A placement may also be driven into `BLOCKED` from either `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` by a tool rejection or suspension; each such blocking records `blocked_reason`, raises the shell's `blocked_placement_count` and sets its `publish_readiness` to false, and blocking an already-active placement additionally lowers the tool's `active_placement_count` by one. **Removal** (`REMOVE`) reaches the terminal `REMOVED` from all three earlier states, and can also arrive as the shell-archival cascade of §3.3, but its bookkeeping differs by origin state: removing an `ACTIVE` placement lowers the tool's `active_placement_count` by one, re-scores the tool and re-derives the shell's `publish_readiness`; removing a `BLOCKED` placement lowers the shell's `blocked_placement_count` by one and re-derives its `publish_readiness`; removing a `REQUESTED` placement only re-derives `publish_readiness`. A shell is publish-ready exactly when its `blocked_placement_count` is 0. A removal never clears `blocked_reason`, so a placement removed out of `BLOCKED` carries the reason it was blocked for into its terminal state as history; and a shell that regains `publish_readiness` because its last blocked placement was removed is not republished automatically, since publishing is always an explicit act.

### 3.8. Grades, Passback to the Registrar, and Grade Disputes

Grades enter the system one record at a time. An `Instructor` records — or overwrites — one enrollment's earned points for one content item by naming `enrollment_id`, `content_item_id` and `earned_points`; this is the only way points reach a gradebook. Each write re-derives the enrollment's `earned_points_total` as the sum of its grade records and its `graded_points_possible` as the sum of the `points_possible` of the items it now carries a record for, and re-derives that enrollment's `provisional_grade_pct`. A grade record outlives the deletion of its content item, and the two sums part company when that happens: the record goes on contributing its `earned_points` to `earned_points_total` while the vanished item contributes nothing to `graded_points_possible`, which puts a `provisional_grade_pct` above 100.00 within reach. Deleting an item does not itself re-derive the enrollments graded on it; the shift lands the next time a grade record is written for one of them. The write answers `201` carrying the updated enrollment. `earned_points` carries no lower bound, the two references resolve in the order just given, and neither the enrollment's `role` and `enrollment_state` nor the question of whether the item belongs to the enrollment's own shell is checked here — the `STUDENT`-and-`ACTIVE`-or-`COMPLETED` restriction stated below bites at staging, not at the point of recording.

A **grade percentage** is always the enrollment's `earned_points_total` divided by a denominator, multiplied by 100, with the division carried at full precision and the result rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once at the end; when the denominator is zero the percentage is 0.00. The two variants differ only in which denominator they use, and that difference is the entire point. The **provisional** variant divides by the enrollment's own `graded_points_possible` — the work graded so far — which flatters an incomplete gradebook, and it writes `provisional_grade_pct`. The **final** variant divides by the shell's `total_points_possible` — every gradable point in the shell, graded or not — so missing work drags the figure down, and it writes `final_grade_pct`. On identical stored grades the two disagree: an `earned_points_total` of 50.00 against a `graded_points_possible` of 60.00 gives 83.33, while the same 50.00 against a shell `total_points_possible` of 80.00 gives 62.50, and that 20.83-point gap is exactly what the registrar sees between the staged and the submitted figures. Only rows whose `role` is `STUDENT` and whose `enrollment_state` is `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` are graded at all; `WAITLISTED` and `DROPPED` rows are excluded from staging entirely, so no passback ever writes a percentage onto one, and a row that has never carried a grade record of its own keeps a `provisional_grade_pct` of 0.00.

An `Instructor` stages a passback for a shell by naming `shell_id`. Staging requires the shell's `provisioning_state` to be `PUBLISHED` or `CONCLUDED` (otherwise `ERR_SHELL_STATE_BLOCKS_ENROLLMENT`), the shell's term to be in `lifecycle_state` `FINALS` or `GRADING` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`), and no other passback to exist for that shell (otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PASSBACK`) — one shell, one passback, for its whole amendment history. The passback opens in `passback_state` `STAGED` with `term_id` copied from the shell, `variant_applied` `PROVISIONAL`, `staged_enrollment_count` set to the number of gradable rows described above, and every provisional percentage computed. That percentage is re-derived from each enrollment's **stored** `earned_points_total` and `graded_points_possible` as they stand at staging; staging never revisits the underlying grade records, so it restates a figure above 100.00 left behind by a deleted content item rather than correcting it, and it recomputes no point total of its own. `staged_enrollment_count` is likewise re-derived from the gradable rows as they stand at that moment, and again on every submission and every restaging, so a row that completes or drops between staging and submission changes the count. Its three point buckets — `submitted_points_total`, `accepted_points_total` and `disputed_points_held` — all start at 0.00, as do `open_dispute_count` and `amendment_round`.

**Submitting** (`SUBMIT`) takes `STAGED` → `SUBMITTED` and carries three gates: the shell's term must not be in `continuity_posture` `CONTINUITY_MODE`, otherwise `ERR_TERM_POSTURE_FROZEN`; no staged enrollment may belong to a person whose `ferpa_block` is true, otherwise `ERR_FERPA_BLOCK_ACTIVE`; and at least one staged enrollment must have a `graded_points_possible` above 0, otherwise `ERR_NO_GRADES_STAGED`. Submitting flips `variant_applied` to `FINAL`, recomputes every staged enrollment's `final_grade_pct` under the final variant, sets `submitted_points_total` to the sum of the staged enrollments' `earned_points_total`, and sets `accepted_points_total` and `disputed_points_held` to 0.00.

The registrar then decides, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` and an optional `note` that is stored as `registrar_note`; a decision that supplies no `note` leaves whatever `registrar_note` an earlier decision recorded standing rather than clearing it. The duty-segregation check of §2.9 compares the acting registrar against the user recorded by the most recent `SUBMIT`, so on a resubmitted passback it is the latest submitter, never an earlier one, who is barred from deciding. **Accepting** takes `SUBMITTED` → `ACCEPTED` and sets `accepted_points_total` equal to `submitted_points_total`, leaving `disputed_points_held` at 0.00. **Returning** takes `SUBMITTED` → `RETURNED` and leaves `accepted_points_total` at 0.00 — the two branches of the same decision fork send the passback into two different cycles, one toward disputes and one back to authoring. A returned passback is **restaged** by the instructor (`RESTAGE`), which takes `RETURNED` → `STAGED`, flips `variant_applied` back to `PROVISIONAL`, recomputes every staged `provisional_grade_pct`, sets every `final_grade_pct` back to null, and returns `submitted_points_total` to 0.00, closing the staged-submitted-returned cycle.

The passback's three buckets obey one invariant once the passback has been submitted at least once: **`accepted_points_total` plus `disputed_points_held` equals `submitted_points_total`**. Every bucket write stores an already-rounded two-decimal figure, so the invariant is exact rather than approximate. The invariant is deliberately not asserted between submission and the registrar's decision, because the submitted total is in flight. The bucket events are these: submission **out of `STAGED`** sets the submitted total from the staged grades and zeroes the other two, while a resubmission out of `AMENDED` writes no bucket at all and leaves all three exactly as the resolved dispute round left them — which is safe precisely because that round already restored the identity; acceptance moves the whole submitted total into accepted; a return zeroes all three; filing a dispute moves that dispute's `held_points` out of accepted and into disputed; upholding a dispute moves the same `held_points` back out of disputed and into accepted; and resolving a dispute in the student's favour moves `held_points` out of disputed, adds the `adjusted_earned_points` to accepted, and adds the difference between the adjusted figure and the held figure to submitted, which is precisely what keeps the invariant true when the total itself changes. Filing followed by upholding is the compensating pair and must restore all three buckets to their pre-filing values with zero drift. Worked through: a passback submitting 250.00 reads 250.00 accepted and 0.00 disputed after acceptance; a dispute holding 90.00 takes it to 160.00 accepted, 90.00 disputed and 250.00 submitted; upholding returns it to 250.00 accepted, 0.00 disputed and 250.00 submitted; while resolving that same dispute with an adjusted figure of 120.00 instead leaves 280.00 accepted, 0.00 disputed and 280.00 submitted.

A `Student` files a dispute by naming `passback_id`, `enrollment_id`, `content_item_id` and `claimed_earned_points`. Filing requires the passback to be in `passback_state` `ACCEPTED` or `DISPUTED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`), the acting user to be the very person the enrollment belongs to (otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), the logical clock to be at or before the `student_access_end_at` of the passback's own term (otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_ACCESS_EXPIRED`), the referenced content item to belong to the same shell as the enrollment (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), and no other dispute for that same enrollment to be `OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_ROLE_CONFLICT`). Those are the whole of the gate: the enrollment is not required to belong to the passback's own shell, nor to be one of the rows that passback staged, nor to carry any particular `role` or `enrollment_state`. `claimed_earned_points` is recorded exactly as supplied, rounded to two decimals, with no bound of its own in either direction, and it never enters the ledger arithmetic — every figure the ledger moves comes from `held_points` and from the resolution instead. The dispute opens in `dispute_state` `OPEN` with `held_points` set to the enrollment's current `earned_points_total`, the enrollment's own `disputed_points_held` set to the same amount, and `filed_by_user_id` recorded from the acting user. The **first** dispute against an accepted passback drives that passback `ACCEPTED` → `DISPUTED`, raising its `open_dispute_count` by one, moving the held points from accepted into disputed, and raising the term's `open_amendment_count` by one; a subsequent dispute against an already-disputed passback simply raises `open_dispute_count` and repeats the bucket move.

The owning instructor resolves a dispute with an `outcome` of `UPHELD` or `CHANGED`, plus `adjusted_earned_points` and an optional `note` — which is validated as a non-empty string when supplied but is persisted nowhere, the dispute carrying no note field of its own; the acting user must be the shell's `owner_person_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. **Upholding** takes `OPEN` → `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock, leaves `adjusted_earned_points` null, leaves the enrollment's `earned_points_total` untouched while zeroing its `disputed_points_held`, and returns the held points from the passback's disputed bucket to its accepted bucket, restoring the accepted total to its pre-filing value with zero drift. **Changing** takes `OPEN` → `RESOLVED_CHANGED` and requires `adjusted_earned_points` to be present and not negative, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It cascades in three hops: `resolved_at` and `adjusted_earned_points` are recorded while the enrollment's `earned_points_total` is set to the adjusted figure and its `disputed_points_held` is zeroed; that new total re-runs the final grade percentage and rewrites the enrollment's `final_grade_pct` while leaving `graded_points_possible` and `provisional_grade_pct` standing at their pre-resolution figures, so the two percentages of an adjusted enrollment are deliberately out of step; and the passback ledger absorbs the delta as described above. The adjustment lands on the enrollment rather than on any grade record, so a later grade record written for that enrollment re-derives `earned_points_total` from the records again and silently supersedes it. Both resolved states are terminal.

How the passback leaves `DISPUTED` depends on what the round decided. When the last open dispute resolves and **every** resolution in that round was upheld, the passback returns `DISPUTED` → `ACCEPTED` and the term's `open_amendment_count` falls by one — the round is ledger-neutral and costs the term nothing. When the last open dispute resolves and **at least one** resolution changed a grade, the passback takes `DISPUTED` → `AMENDED`, its `amendment_round` rises by one, and the term's `open_amendment_count` stays raised, which is what blocks the term from closing with `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTES_BLOCK_CLOSE`. An amended passback must be resubmitted by the instructor (`SUBMIT`, taking `AMENDED` → `SUBMITTED`), which requires the term not to be in `CONTINUITY_MODE` (otherwise `ERR_TERM_POSTURE_FROZEN`) and, read only after that freeze has passed, `open_dispute_count` to be 0 (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`) — so a frozen term still carrying open disputes reports the freeze, the band-11 lockdown of §2.10 having already settled the passback's own `passback_state` before either of the two; it recomputes every staged `final_grade_pct` under the final variant and lowers the term's `open_amendment_count` by one. Three of this workflow's writes reach the owning term in their own right: a passback submission, a dispute filing, and the resolution that closes a dispute round each re-derive the term's aggregates and refresh its `updated_at` and `version` even when no counter actually moves, while staging, restaging and the registrar's decision leave the term untouched — so a client holding an `expected_term_version` for §2.7 must re-read the term after any of the three. That is the second cycle on this entity — amended, submitted, accepted, disputed, amended — and it is what makes the amendment count a real gate on term closure rather than a cosmetic tally.

### 3.9. Academic Continuity: Windows and the Term's Posture

When a campus loses power, a building closes or an instructor needs to extend a deadline, the disruption is recorded as a continuity window. An `LmsAdmin` opens one by naming `term_id`, a `kind` of `OUTAGE`, `EXTENSION` or `MAINTENANCE`, a `window_start`, a `window_end` and the mandatory `expected_term_version`, optionally narrowing the window to a single shell with `shell_id`. A window with no `shell_id` is term-wide, and a `shell_id` supplied as `null` or as an empty string is treated as absent, so it too yields a term-wide window. A supplied `shell_id` must resolve to an existing shell, but the service does not require that shell to belong to the named term.

Five conditions gate the creation. The term's `lifecycle_state` must be `IN_SESSION`, `FINALS` or `GRADING`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The `window_end` must be strictly after the `window_start`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a zero-length window is not a window. The `expected_term_version` must match the term's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`: an operator opens a window against a posture and an enrollment picture they have just read, and because a superseding window is perfectly legitimate, only the term's version can tell them the picture moved underneath them. Then two placement rules apply, and they use **deliberately different interval conventions**.

The first is a per-shell exclusion. When `shell_id` is present, no other window of the same term with the same `shell_id`, the same `kind` and `window_state` `OPEN` may intersect the new interval **half-open**, treating the interval as including its start and excluding its end; a collision is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Two windows that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant the next begins — therefore do not collide, and both may be open at once.

The second is a term-wide capacity rule that applies only when `kind` is `OUTAGE`. Counting the new window itself, no more than two open outage windows of one term may cover any single instant, and scope is irrelevant to the count: a term-wide outage and a shell-scoped one sit in the same pool, and two shell-scoped outages count against each other even when they name different shells. Equivalently: at most **one** existing open outage window of that term may intersect the new interval treated as **closed** — including both of its endpoints, so windows that only touch at an endpoint *do* count as sharing that instant. A third overlapping outage is refused with `ERR_CONTINUITY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Each of the three windows is individually legal; only the collective is not, which is why this is a term-level check and not a property of any one window.

A newly opened window records `affected_enrollment_count` — the shell's `active_enrollment_count` for a shell-scoped window, or the term's `total_active_enrollments` for a term-wide one — and, for a window whose `kind` is `EXTENSION`, records `extension_days_granted` as the window's length in seconds divided by 86,400 and rounded **up** to a whole day, so that any partial day counts as a full granted day; every other kind records 0. Creating a window increments the **term's** `version` by one, which is what makes two clients racing to open windows against the same term visible to each other.

A window is **closed** (`CLOSE`) from `OPEN`, which stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, or **cancelled** (`CANCEL`) from `OPEN`, which requires the logical clock to still be strictly before its `window_start`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_ALREADY_STARTED` — a window that has already begun affecting students can be closed but never cancelled. Cancelling also resets `affected_enrollment_count` and `extension_days_granted` to 0, since nothing was in fact affected or granted, and it leaves `closed_at` null: that stamp belongs to `CLOSE` alone, and a cancelled window never carries one. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal. Neither transition touches the term — within this workflow it is the open alone that advances the term's `version` — so closing or cancelling a window leaves a client's last-read term version still good.

The term's `continuity_posture` is the second, independent dimension of the term row, and an `LmsAdmin` moves it by naming a `target_posture` and an optional `reason`. It escalates `NORMAL` → `ELEVATED`, which requires the term's `lifecycle_state` to be `IN_SESSION`, `FINALS` or `GRADING` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`). It escalates again `ELEVATED` → `CONTINUITY_MODE`, which requires at least one window of that term whose `kind` is `OUTAGE` and whose `window_state` is `OPEN` to exist, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — the highest posture must be justified by a live outage on the record. It de-escalates `CONTINUITY_MODE` → `ELEVATED`, which requires the mirror image: **no** window of that term with `kind` `OUTAGE` and `window_state` `OPEN` may remain, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. And it de-escalates `ELEVATED` → `NORMAL` unconditionally. Jumping straight from `NORMAL` to `CONTINUITY_MODE` is **not** a declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the escalation must be walked one step at a time.

Closing the last open outage window does not by itself change the posture. The posture dimension is always walked back explicitly, and the de-escalation out of `CONTINUITY_MODE` is precisely what is gated on there being no open outage window left — so the operator must close the outage first and then step the posture down, in that order.

The posture matters because it reaches across the whole service in three different ways. It **freezes authoring**: while the term is in `CONTINUITY_MODE`, publishing a shell, unpublishing a shell by hand, and submitting a passback all refuse with `ERR_TERM_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and so does advancing the term from `FINALS` to `GRADING`. It **unlocks the reversal**: the `FINALS` back to `IN_SESSION` transition of §3.2 is legal only during `CONTINUITY_MODE`. And it **relaxes seat capacity**: the overflow ratio of §3.4 is read straight off the posture, so escalating a term to `ELEVATED` or `CONTINUITY_MODE` widens the `effective_seat_cap` of every shell in it, and de-escalating narrows it again. That sweep walks the term's shells in `created_at` ascending order and covers every shell whose `provisioning_state` is neither `REJECTED` nor `PURGED`, but it pulls the waitlist forward only on the shells that are `PROVISIONED` or `PUBLISHED`; a `REQUESTED`, `MERGED`, `CONCLUDED` or `ARCHIVED` shell has its cap and its seat observables re-derived without any promotion firing. A shell already over its base cap when the posture drops is not truncated — no enrollment is ever dropped by a posture change — it simply reports `overflow_flag` true until enrollments leave by their own transitions. Note also that the system-driven unpublishing that follows a tool suspension (§3.7) is exempt from the freeze: it is a safety response, not an authoring action.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every collection sits directly at the server root: the service has a flat topology, so no resource is nested under another and every relationship travels in a request body or as a query parameter. Successful creates answer with `201 Created`, successful reads, updates, transitions and decisions with `200 OK`, and the single delete with `204 No Content` and an empty body. List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` along with the filters named in their descriptions, and every foreign-key field of a listable entity is accepted as an optional equality filter on that entity's list endpoint. The two endpoints in the underscore namespace exist so that time-dependent and grade-dependent behaviour is drivable from an empty database.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every failing request answers with the error envelope of §2.2, whose `error_id` is one of the identifiers below and whose HTTP status is the one paired with it here. When more than one of these conditions is true of a single request, the one reported is decided by the precedence order of §2.10.

Two framework-level failures sit outside that pairing and are decided before any endpoint rule runs, authentication included: a request whose path matches no declared endpoint answers `404`, and a request whose method is not declared for an otherwise existing path answers `405`. Both carry the error envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even though the table pairs that identifier with `400`.
