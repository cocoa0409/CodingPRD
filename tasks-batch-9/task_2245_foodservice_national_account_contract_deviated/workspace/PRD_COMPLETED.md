# Foodservice National Account Contract & Deviated Pricing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A broadline foodservice distributor sells to national restaurant chains under negotiated contracts rather than at list price, and almost every dollar of margin on those accounts is decided by paperwork rather than by the warehouse. Each contract fixes cost-plus markup tiers per product class, names the distribution centers allowed to ship, and commits the operator to buying a stated share of its volume from contracted items. On top of that sits deviated (off-invoice) pricing: the distributor invoices the operator below its own landed cost on selected items and recovers the difference from the manufacturer through bill-back claims. When a manufacturer remits less than the claim, when an out-of-stock item is filled with a substitute, when a market-indexed cost moves, or when an invoice is simply wrong, the money involved is real and the audit trail is contractual.

This document specifies the National Account Contract service, mounted under `/nac`, which administers that machinery end to end: contract construction and amendment, deviated pricing negotiation with the manufacturer, distribution center coverage and out-of-market service fees, ranked product substitution, price change notices with contractual notice periods and market indexing, contracted-item compliance reporting, rebate accrual and settlement split between distributor and manufacturer funding, manufacturer bill-back recovery with cash application, and the dispute-and-audit path that reprices an invoice line and issues an operator credit.

Two properties dominate the design. First, every monetary figure is derived, never asserted: prices come out of the tier tables and the deviation agreements, rebates come out of measured compliance, credits come out of a recomputed audit price. Second, corrections are first-class — settlements can be reversed, remittances can be unwound, closed rebate periods can be reopened by an audit credit — and each compensating action must restore the ledgers exactly, with no drift. The rules below are therefore written to be arithmetically reconstructible rather than merely descriptive.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role it acts in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the role is not authorized for the endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `SystemAdmin`, `NationalAccountManager`, `PricingAnalyst`, `OperatorBuyer`, `ManufacturerRep`, `RebateAccountant`, and `ContractAuditor`. `SystemAdmin` owns reference data — operators, manufacturers, distribution centers, products — and the logical clock. `NationalAccountManager` constructs contracts, markup tiers, coverage mappings and substitution rules, activates deviated prices, and invoices and reprices order lines. `PricingAnalyst` drafts and submits deviated pricing agreements and issues, revises, withdraws and activates cost change notices. `OperatorBuyer` acts for the operator: signing or returning contracts, placing and substituting order lines, acknowledging or objecting to notices, and opening or withdrawing pricing disputes. `ManufacturerRep` acts for the manufacturer: deciding on submitted deviations and disputing submitted bill-back claims. `RebateAccountant` opens, closes and settles rebate periods and drives bill-back accrual, submission and cash application. `ContractAuditor` audits invoice pricing accuracy: running and resolving dispute audits, issuing credits, writing off claims and reversing settlements.

Two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `OperatorBuyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Operator`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Operator` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — concretely, the governing `Contract`'s `operator_id` must equal `X-User-ID` for signing, returning to draft, placing or substituting order lines, acknowledging, objecting to or (through the line's contract) disputing prices, and withdrawing a dispute. When `X-User-Role` is `ManufacturerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Manufacturer`; a manufacturer decision requires `X-User-ID` to equal the target `DeviationAgreement`'s `manufacturer_id`, and disputing a claim requires it to equal the target `BillBackClaim`'s `manufacturer_id`. A mismatch on either is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The remaining five roles — `SystemAdmin`, `NationalAccountManager`, `PricingAnalyst`, `RebateAccountant` and `ContractAuditor` — carry an opaque `X-User-ID`: an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on a `DeviationAgreement` when a `PricingAnalyst` submits, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a `BillBackClaim` and `settled_by_user_id` on a `RebatePeriod` when a `RebateAccountant` acts, or as `audited_by_user_id` on a `PricingDispute` when a `ContractAuditor` audits) and it may compare two such recorded values against each other for segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`. The `OperatorBuyer` identifiers recorded as `signed_by_user_id` on a `Contract` and `opened_by_user_id` on a `PricingDispute` are, by contrast, `Operator` ids.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filtering

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0 and no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, a negative `offset`, or a value for either that is not an integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Results are always sorted by `created_at` ascending, with insertion order breaking a tie. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Every collection is also filterable, and all filters are optional and combine conjunctively; each filter is an exact equality match on the named field, a filter naming a status or other enumerated value rejects a value outside that enumeration with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an id-valued filter that matches nothing simply returns an empty window. Products may be filtered by `manufacturer_id`, `product_class_code`, `substitution_group_code`, `stock_status` and `status`. Contracts may be filtered by `operator_id`, `rebate_funding_manufacturer_id`, `amends_contract_id` and `status`. Markup tiers accept `contract_id`, `product_class_code` and `status`; coverage mappings accept `contract_id`, `distribution_center_id`, `coverage_type` and `status`; deviation agreements accept `contract_id`, `product_id`, `manufacturer_id` and `status`; substitution rules accept `contract_id`, `primary_product_id`, `substitute_product_id` and `status`; price change notices accept `contract_id`, `product_id` and `status`; rebate periods accept `contract_id` and `status`; bill-back claims accept `deviation_agreement_id`, `manufacturer_id` and `status`; remittances accept `manufacturer_id` and `status`; pricing disputes accept `contract_id`, `order_line_id` and `status`. Order lines are the widest: they accept `contract_id`, `product_id`, `distribution_center_id`, `rebate_period_id`, `deviation_agreement_id`, `substituted_from_product_id`, `substitution_rule_id`, `applied_tier_id`, `bill_back_claim_id`, `pricing_status` and `billback_status`.

### 2.4. Resource Addressing and Common Properties

The entity model is flat: every resource is addressed at a top-level collection under `/nac`, and relationships are expressed exclusively through foreign-key fields in request bodies, never through nested URL paths. Where a path does carry a parameter, it is validated left to right before any body field is examined; the path parameters in use are `operator_id`, `manufacturer_id`, `distribution_center_id`, `product_id`, `contract_id`, `contract_tier_id`, `dc_coverage_id`, `deviation_agreement_id`, `substitution_rule_id`, `price_change_notice_id`, `order_line_id`, `bill_back_claim_id`, `bill_back_remittance_id`, `rebate_period_id` and `pricing_dispute_id`. Each has its own not-found code: `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MANUFACTURER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DC_COVERAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILL_BACK_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILL_BACK_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REBATE_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PRICING_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. The same codes cover body foreign keys, which are resolved after the path parameters in the order they are declared in the endpoint's input list; `rebate_funding_manufacturer_id` resolves under `ERR_MANUFACTURER_NOT_FOUND`, and `primary_product_id` and `substitute_product_id` resolve under `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`.

Every resource in this service carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same mutation. Every entity in this service — `Operator`, `Manufacturer`, `DistributionCenter`, `Product`, `Contract`, `ContractTier`, `DcCoverage`, `DeviationAgreement`, `SubstitutionRule`, `PriceChangeNotice`, `RebatePeriod`, `OrderLine`, `BillBackClaim`, `BillBackRemittance` and `PricingDispute` — carries all four; none of them is version-free. Because a cascade counts as a mutation, a row touched only as a side effect of somebody else's request still has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. The one exception is the manufacturer's `open_claim_exposure_cents`: when a bill-back claim is created, when a `ManufacturerRep` disputes a claim, and when the dispute-credit side branch pushes a claim back to `DISPUTED`, that counter is recomputed in place and the manufacturer's `version` and `updated_at` are left alone; claim submit, claim write-off, remittance application and remittance reversal do increment the manufacturer's `version`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Interval Conventions

The service does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. It keeps a logical UTC clock whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and a `SystemAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock endpoint — any parseable instant is accepted, including one earlier than the clock's current value, and the sweep simply runs against the new value — which answers with the resulting `current_utc` together with `expired_contract_ids` and `expired_deviation_ids` — the rows the move swept out of their live states, whose exact membership §3.3 pins. That clock stamps `order_date`, `submitted_at`, `closed_at`, `settled_at`, `received_at`, `signed_at` and `cost_effective_at`, and it is the value compared against every contractual window.

Interval conventions differ by entity and are deliberately not uniform, so each must be read literally. A `Contract` term `[effective_from, effective_to]` is **closed** at both ends: an order dated exactly at `effective_to` is still inside the term. A `DcCoverage` window `[coverage_from, coverage_to]` is likewise **closed**. A `DeviationAgreement` window `[effective_from, effective_to)` is **half-open**: the deviated price applies from `effective_from` inclusive up to but excluding `effective_to`. A `RebatePeriod` window `[period_start, period_end)`, a `BillBackClaim` window `[claim_period_start, claim_period_end)`, and a `ContractTier` case-volume band `[min_case_volume, max_case_volume)` are all half-open in the same way, and a null `max_case_volume` means the band is unbounded above. In every one of these, the upper bound must be strictly after the lower bound; `effective_to` must be strictly after `effective_from`, `coverage_to` strictly after `coverage_from`, `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, `claim_period_end` strictly after `claim_period_start`, and `max_case_volume`, when supplied, strictly greater than `min_case_volume`. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding

All money in this service is an integer count of cents; there are no fractional cents and no floating-point currency. Wherever a money value is produced by a multiplication or a division, it is rounded to whole cents with round half up **immediately after** that operation rather than at the end of a chain, and the pinned order of operations in each calculation below is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Percentages — `markup_value` under `PERCENT_OF_COST`, `contracted_purchase_pct_target`, `max_price_delta_pct`, `compliance_pct`, `rebate_rate_pct`, `delta_pct`, `variance_pct`, `index_baseline_value` — carry two decimal places, are likewise rounded half up, and travel over the wire as JSON numbers rather than strings, so a two-decimal value may serialize with its trailing zeros dropped. Where a threshold comparison is inclusive or strict, the text below says so explicitly; those boundaries are load-bearing and no tolerance is applied around them.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Contract and Deviation Versions

Two write paths take a client-observed parent version as a mandatory input, because both build a money figure out of a parent whose terms may have moved.

Creating a deviation agreement (`POST /nac/deviation-agreements`) requires `expected_contract_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `Contract`. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because the contract's tier table or posture may already have moved on — a tier was added, an amendment was opened, a rebate period was closed — so a deviated price computed from the client's stale view of the contract would supersede or contradict the current terms. A successful create increments the `Contract`'s `version` by 1, which means a second create built on the same observed version is itself stale.

Creating a bill-back claim (`POST /nac/bill-back-claims`) requires `expected_deviation_agreement_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `DeviationAgreement`. A mismatch against that agreement's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEVIATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412): a new claim supersedes the accrual view the client held, and an activated price change notice may have re-derived `deviated_price_cents` in the meantime, which would change the recoverable delta the claim is built from. A successful create increments the `DeviationAgreement`'s `version` by 1.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to any endpoint outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and supplying an unknown field is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the following priority ladder. Checks are evaluated in ascending priority order and the first failure wins; a later, more specific failure never masks an earlier one.

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Role authorization, including entity-backed ownership — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. Existence of every referenced id, path parameters first (left to right), then body foreign keys in declaration order — the matching `*_NOT_FOUND` code (404).
4. Body parsing, declared types, enum membership, range constraints, and rejection of unknown fields — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. The target resource is in a declared from-state of the requested transition — `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEVIATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_NOTICE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REBATE_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_DISPUTE_INVALID_STATE` (409), on every action endpoint.
6. The governing contract is `ACTIVE` — `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). This applies to deviation create, deviation submit, deviation activate, tier create, coverage create, substitution-rule create, notice create, notice revise, notice activate, order-line create, order-line substitute, order-line invoice, order-line reprice and rebate-period create. On the three contract-construction endpoints — tier create, coverage create and substitution-rule create — a contract in `DRAFT` is accepted as well as one in `ACTIVE`, so that the first tier and the first coverage can exist before signature; any status other than those two still raises this error on those three endpoints.
7. When the contract being signed carries `amends_contract_id`, its parent must be in `AMENDING` — `ERR_PARENT_CONTRACT_NOT_AMENDING` (409).
8. `expected_contract_version` matches the referenced contract's `version` — `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (412), on deviation create.
9. `expected_deviation_agreement_version` matches the referenced agreement's `version` — `ERR_STALE_DEVIATION_VERSION` (412), on claim create.
10. The deviation's `manufacturer_id` equals the deviated product's `manufacturer_id` — `ERR_DEVIATION_MANUFACTURER_MISMATCH` (422), on deviation create.
11. Substitute and primary product compatibility — `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_GROUP_MISMATCH` (422), on substitution-rule create.
12. The product has a non-null cost on the requested cost basis and the contract has an `ACTIVE` tier covering that product's class — `ERR_NO_TIER_FOR_PRODUCT_CLASS` (422), on notice create, order-line create, order-line substitute, order-line reprice and deviation create.
13. The activating manager is not the analyst who submitted — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (409), on deviation activate.
14. The writing-off auditor is not the accountant who submitted — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (409), on claim write-off.
15. Contract posture is not `RESTRICTED` — `ERR_CONTRACT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (409), on deviation activate, amendment open and rebate-period settle.
16. Manufacturer status is `ACTIVE` — `ERR_MANUFACTURER_SUSPENDED` (409), on claim submit and manufacturer decision.
17. The rebate period covering a credited line is not `SETTLED` — `ERR_SETTLED_PERIOD_IMMUTABLE` (409), on dispute credit issue.
18. Deviation window non-overlap — `ERR_DEVIATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on deviation create.
19. Coverage changeover buffer — `ERR_DC_COVERAGE_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (409), on coverage create.
20. Rolling notice-window conflict — `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on notice create.
21. `effective_at` is at or after `earliest_effective_at` — `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT` (422), on notice create and notice revise.
22. The clock has reached `effective_at` — `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE` (409), on notice activate.
23. The order date lies inside the contract term — `ERR_ORDER_DATE_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM` (422), on order-line create.
24. Rebate period non-overlap — `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on rebate-period create.
25. Claim window non-overlap — `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on claim create.
26. The clock has reached `period_end` — `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (409), on rebate-period close.
27. Tier band non-overlap — `ERR_TIER_BAND_OVERLAP` (409), on tier create.
28. Index-value / proposed-cost exclusivity — `ERR_INDEX_VALUE_REQUIRED` (422), on notice create and notice revise.
29. `revision_round` is below the revision cap — `ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` (409), on deviation submit.
30. A live coverage row covers the shipping DC at the order date — `ERR_DC_NOT_COVERED` (409), on order-line create and order-line substitute.
31. Out-of-market coverage is permitted by the contract — `ERR_OUT_OF_MARKET_NOT_PERMITTED` (409), on coverage create.
32. Exactly one `OPEN` rebate period of the contract contains the clock — `ERR_NO_OPEN_REBATE_PERIOD` (409), on order-line create.
33. The referenced product is out of stock — `ERR_PRODUCT_IN_STOCK` (409), on order-line substitute.
34. The substitution candidate set is non-empty — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUBSTITUTE` (409), on order-line substitute.
35. The claim sweeps at least one line — `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_LINES` (422), on claim create.
36. No live dispute blocks settlement — `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTES_BLOCK_SETTLEMENT` (409), on rebate-period settle.
37. The disputed line's period is final — `ERR_REBATE_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (409), on dispute start-audit.
38. No live dispute already exists for the line — `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_EXISTS_FOR_LINE` (409), on dispute create.
39. The disputed line has been invoiced — `ERR_ORDER_LINE_INVALID_STATE` (409), on dispute create.
40. The resolved deviated price is within the operator ceiling — `ERR_DEVIATED_PRICE_ABOVE_CEILING` (422), on deviation create and deviation activate.
41. An upheld resolution clears the audit tolerance — `ERR_VARIANCE_BELOW_AUDIT_TOLERANCE` (422), on dispute resolve.
42. No rebate remains payable before termination — `ERR_REBATE_PAYABLE_OUTSTANDING` (409), on contract terminate.

Two consequences are worth stating outright. Because posture gating sits at priority 15 and window conflicts sit at 18 and later, a `RESTRICTED` contract surfaces as `ERR_CONTRACT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` even when the request also overlaps an existing deviation window. And because the stale-version checks sit at 8 and 9, above every business rule, a client holding an out-of-date parent version is told so before it is told that its deviated price breaches the ceiling.

### 2.9. Cascades and Version Bookkeeping

Many operations here touch more than one row, and a cascade is never optional or best-effort: it happens inside the same request, and every row it touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. Three cascades are deep enough to be named as such in the narrative below — the four-hop cost cascade of an activated price change notice, the four-hop credit cascade of an issued dispute credit, and the recovery cascade that a manufacturer remittance and its reversal drive through claims and order lines. Where a parent leaves its live state, its dependent configuration rows follow: contract termination supersedes markup tiers and ends coverage rows, contract expiry ends coverage rows, and both terminate non-terminal deviation agreements. Superseded tiers are permanently invisible to later pricing lookups, and ended coverage rows make the corresponding distribution center un-shippable for that contract.

### 2.10. Ledger Conservation

Two equalities must hold before and after every request, including after every compensating action, with exact integer-cent equality and no tolerance.

**Rebate liability conservation.** For any contract, `rebate_payable_cents` plus `rebate_settled_total_cents` equals the sum of `operator_rebate_cents` over that contract's rebate periods whose status is `CLOSED` or `SETTLED`. Periods still `OPEN` contribute nothing, because their `operator_rebate_cents` is null. Settling a period moves cents from `rebate_payable_cents` into `rebate_settled_total_cents` and reversing a settlement moves the same cents back; neither may change the sum, and after a reversal the manufacturer-side mirror `rebate_funding_payable_cents` must equal its value from before the settlement.

**Manufacturer recovery conservation.** For any manufacturer, `total_remitted_cents` equals the sum of `recovered_amount_cents` across all of that manufacturer's bill-back claims regardless of status, plus `unapplied_credit_cents`. Applying a remittance and reversing that remittance must each leave the equality true, and the reversal must restore every term — `total_remitted_cents`, each claim's `recovered_amount_cents`, and `unapplied_credit_cents` — to its pre-application value. Cash that cannot be applied to an open claim is never discarded; it is parked on `unapplied_credit_cents` and carried forward.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Posture Gating

Contract administration is split so that no single person can both create and bless the same money movement. The `NationalAccountManager` who activates a deviated price must not be the `PricingAnalyst` who submitted it: the activating `X-User-ID` is compared against the agreement's `submitted_by_user_id`, and equality is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`. The `ContractAuditor` who writes off a bill-back claim must not be the `RebateAccountant` who submitted it: the writing-off `X-User-ID` is compared against the claim's `submitted_by_user_id`, and equality raises the same error. Both comparisons are on the recorded identifier alone, not on role.

Layered on top is the contract's own `compliance_posture`, a derived gate with three values — `OPEN`, `WATCH` and `RESTRICTED`. `RESTRICTED` blocks exactly three operations: activating a deviation agreement, opening an amendment, and settling a rebate period, each rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. `WATCH` is advisory and blocks nothing. The posture is never supplied by a client; it is recomputed from measured compliance and from breach history whenever a rebate period is closed, settled or reverse-settled, whenever a contract is signed or reinstated, and at the tail of both the price-notice cascade and the dispute-credit cascade.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Trading Partners, Distribution Centers and the Product Catalogue

Everything begins with reference data, which only a `SystemAdmin` may author. A national account operator is registered with its `legal_name` and a short trading `operator_code` — unique across all operators, 2 to 16 characters of uppercase alphanumerics or hyphens. Every uniqueness constraint in this service — this one, `manufacturer_code`, `dc_code`, `sku`, `contract_code` and a remittance's per-manufacturer `remittance_reference` — rejects a duplicate with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Its `status` is `ACTIVE` by default and may also be `SUSPENDED`; a suspended operator may not sign a contract. `rebate_remit_method` records how settled rebates reach the operator and is one of `CHECK`, `ACH` or `CREDIT_MEMO`, defaulting to `CREDIT_MEMO`.

A manufacturer is registered with its legal `name` and a `manufacturer_code`, unique across all manufacturers and subject to the same 2-to-16-character uppercase-alphanumeric-or-hyphen shape. Its `status` is `ACTIVE` by default, with `SUSPENDED` as the alternative, and a suspended manufacturer can neither decide on a submitted deviation nor receive a submitted claim. `bill_back_terms_days` records the contractual days the manufacturer has to remit against a submitted claim; it defaults to 45 and must be at least 1. Four counters on the manufacturer row are computed by the service and never supplied: `open_claim_exposure_cents`, the sum over that manufacturer's claims in `SUBMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` or `DISPUTED` status of the claimed amount less the amount recovered so far, recomputed on every claim create, submit, dispute and write-off and on every remittance application or reversal; `unapplied_credit_cents`, remitted cash the allocation waterfall could not place on any open claim; `total_remitted_cents`, the lifetime sum of `remitted_amount_cents` over that manufacturer's remittances in `APPLIED` status; and `rebate_funding_payable_cents`, the accrued-but-unsettled manufacturer-funded share of operator rebates on every contract naming this manufacturer as its rebate funder. All four start at 0.

A distribution center is registered with a `dc_code` — unique, 2 to 16 uppercase alphanumeric or hyphen characters — a display `name`, a non-empty `in_market_state_codes` array of two-letter uppercase US state codes describing the territory it treats as in-market, and an `out_of_market_fee_per_case_cents` of zero or more, the per-case service fee it charges when it ships outside that territory. Its `status` is `ACTIVE` by default, with `SUSPENDED` as the alternative; only an active center may be mapped into new coverage, and an attempt to map a `SUSPENDED` center is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, judged with the body fields rather than as a state conflict.

The product catalogue is the richest piece of reference data because pricing reads so much of it. A product is registered with a unique `sku`, an invoice `description`, a `product_class_code` drawn from `PROTEIN`, `PRODUCE`, `FROZEN`, `DRY_GROCERY`, `DISPOSABLES`, `BEVERAGE` and `SPECIALTY`, a `manufacturer_id` naming the manufacturer that funds deviations on the item, and a `pack_size` of at least 1 giving units per case. It carries three independent landed-cost figures per case, each zero or more and all three mandatory at registration: `last_cost_cents`, `avg_cost_cents` and `replacement_cost_cents`. A fourth, system-maintained field `cost_effective_at` records the logical instant at which the most recent activated price change notice last rewrote one of those cost fields, and is null until the first such activation. `stock_status` is `IN_STOCK` by default and may be `OUT_OF_STOCK`; `status` is `ACTIVE` by default and may be `DISCONTINUED`, and only an active item may be ordered or chosen as a substitute. `substitution_group_code` is an optional free-form non-empty label naming an interchangeability group — a substitute must share the primary item's group code — and an omitted group code means the item is simply not substitutable. Items whose cost moves with a published market index set `is_market_indexed` to true (it defaults to false) and must then also carry `index_baseline_value`, the published index value, greater than zero and carrying two decimals, together with `index_baseline_cost_cents`, the cost per case that corresponds to that index value, greater than zero. Both baseline fields must be absent on a non-indexed item. A `SystemAdmin` later adjusts availability by supplying `stock_status`, `status`, or both — each is optional, an omitted field is left as it stands, and a request supplying neither is still accepted; that write increments the product's `version` by 1 and is exactly how an item is pushed out of stock so a substitution can be exercised.

### 3.2. Contract Construction: Markup Tier Bands, Coverage Mapping and Substitution Rules

A `NationalAccountManager` opens a contract for an operator by supplying `operator_id`, a globally unique `contract_code`, the `rebate_funding_manufacturer_id` that will fund the manufacturer share of the operator's rebate, and the term bounds `effective_from` and `effective_to`. Four optional terms may be set at creation and otherwise take their defaults: `notice_period_days`, at least 1 and defaulting to 30, the advance notice the operator is owed for a major cost change; `contracted_purchase_pct_target`, between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive with two decimals and defaulting to 85.00, the contracted-item purchase share the operator commits to; `compliance_basis`, either `DOLLAR_WEIGHTED` (the default) or `CASE_WEIGHTED`, selecting which variant of the compliance percentage the contract is measured on; and `allow_out_of_market`, defaulting to false, which decides whether out-of-market coverage may be mapped at all. A new contract lands in `DRAFT` with `compliance_posture` `OPEN`, `deviation_breach_count` 0, `rebate_payable_cents` 0 and `rebate_settled_total_cents` 0, and with `amends_contract_id`, `signed_at` and `signed_by_user_id` all null. `amends_contract_id` is never set by hand on an original contract; it is written only on an amendment draft.

Markup tiers are what turn landed cost into a contract price. Each tier belongs to a contract via `contract_id` and prices exactly one `product_class_code`. It names the `cost_basis` the markup is applied to — `LAST_COST`, `AVG_COST` or `REPLACEMENT_COST` — and a `markup_type` of either `PERCENT_OF_COST`, where `markup_value` is a percentage with two decimals, or `DOLLARS_PER_CASE`, where `markup_value` is cents added to each case. `markup_value` may not be negative and carries at most two decimal places in either mode; integrality is not enforced under `DOLLARS_PER_CASE`, a fractional-cent value there simply being rounded half up when the price is formed. The tier applies only inside a contract-year case-volume band running from `min_case_volume` inclusive, which must be zero or more, up to `max_case_volume` exclusive; a null `max_case_volume` leaves the band unbounded above, and a supplied one must exceed the lower bound. `counts_as_contracted`, true by default, decides whether lines priced from this tier count toward the operator's contracted-item purchase share. A tier begins life `ACTIVE` and can only ever become `SUPERSEDED`, at which point it is excluded from every subsequent pricing lookup. Two `ACTIVE` tiers of the same contract and the same product class may not have overlapping bands: an attempt to create one whose half-open band intersects an existing active band for that class is rejected with `ERR_TIER_BAND_OVERLAP`.

Coverage mapping decides which distribution centers may ship, and on what commercial terms. A coverage row names the `contract_id` and the `distribution_center_id`, a `coverage_type` of `IN_MARKET` or `OUT_OF_MARKET`, and the closed window `coverage_from` to `coverage_to` over which it is valid. `IN_MARKET` waives the per-case service fee outright; `OUT_OF_MARKET` makes it chargeable and is permitted only when the contract's `allow_out_of_market` is true — otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_OUT_OF_MARKET_NOT_PERMITTED`. Even under out-of-market coverage the parties may negotiate a waiver for that specific mapping by setting `service_fee_waived`, which defaults to false. Coverage rows begin `ACTIVE` and can only become `ENDED`. Unlike deviation windows, coverage windows may not merely avoid overlapping — a distribution center needs a changeover buffer. For every existing `ACTIVE` coverage row on the same contract and the same center, the two closed intervals must be disjoint **and** the clear gap between them, measured as the later row's `coverage_from` minus the earlier row's `coverage_to`, must be strictly greater than fourteen days. Abutting or merely adjacent intervals are rejected with `ERR_DC_COVERAGE_BUFFER_CONFLICT`.

Substitution rules are declared up front, one per candidate replacement. Each names the `contract_id`, the `primary_product_id` that may go out of stock, the `substitute_product_id` offered in its place, and a `rank` of at least 1 giving preference order, where 1 is considered first. `price_protection_mode` is the commercial heart of the rule: `HOLD_CONTRACT_PRICE` invoices the primary item's contract price and keeps the line counting as a contracted purchase, while `SUBSTITUTE_OWN_PRICE` invoices the substitute's own contract price and makes the line non-contracted. `max_price_delta_pct`, zero or more with two decimals, is the largest absolute percentage difference between the substitute's and the primary's contract list price the operator will tolerate; beyond it the rule is ineligible. A rule begins `ACTIVE` and may become `RETIRED`, and only `ACTIVE` rules ever enter a candidate set. At declaration time the two products must be genuinely interchangeable: they must share a non-null `substitution_group_code` and the same `product_class_code`, and `primary_product_id` must differ from `substitute_product_id`. Any failure is `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_GROUP_MISMATCH`. All three of these construction operations accept a contract in `DRAFT` as well as one in `ACTIVE`, precisely so that the first tier and the first coverage row can exist before the operator signs; any other contract status raises `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

### 3.3. The Contract Lifecycle: Signature, Amendment, Suspension and Termination

A contract in `DRAFT` is put in front of the operator by a `NationalAccountManager`, moving `DRAFT → PENDING_SIGNATURE`. The move is refused unless the contract is currently in `DRAFT` and the configuration is complete: at least one markup tier of that contract must be `ACTIVE`, and at least one coverage row must be `ACTIVE`. Either failure — wrong from-state or incomplete configuration — is `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`. The transition refreshes `updated_at` and increments the contract's `version` by 1, and nothing else changes.

The operator has two answers. Returning the contract for revision moves `PENDING_SIGNATURE → DRAFT`, optionally carrying a `note`, and is available only to the `OperatorBuyer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the contract's `operator_id`; the manager may then resubmit, so `PENDING_SIGNATURE → DRAFT → PENDING_SIGNATURE` is an ordinary revision cycle rather than an error path. Signing moves `PENDING_SIGNATURE → ACTIVE` and additionally requires the acting operator's own `status` to be `ACTIVE`, a `SUSPENDED` operator being refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` — and, on an amendment draft, the parent-amending check of priority 7 is evaluated before that operator-status test. Signature stamps `signed_at` from the logical clock and `signed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, recomputes `compliance_posture` (which on a fresh contract with no closed period resolves to `OPEN`), and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

Amendments are how live terms change. Opening one moves the live contract `ACTIVE → AMENDING` and creates a **new** contract row in `DRAFT` whose `amends_contract_id` points at the live contract and whose terms come from the amendment payload — a new `contract_code`, `effective_from` and `effective_to`, plus optionally `notice_period_days`, `contracted_purchase_pct_target`, `compliance_basis` and `allow_out_of_market`, each defaulting exactly as it does on an original contract rather than inheriting the parent's value. The draft copies the parent's `operator_id` and `rebate_funding_manufacturer_id`, and like any fresh draft it starts with `compliance_posture` `OPEN`, `deviation_breach_count` 0, `rebate_payable_cents` 0, `rebate_settled_total_cents` 0 and `signed_at` and `signed_by_user_id` null; it inherits none of the parent's counters, tiers, coverages or deviations. The live contract's `version` is incremented by 1. An amendment may be opened only from `ACTIVE`, only while `compliance_posture` is not `RESTRICTED`, and only when no contract row already exists that amends this one and sits in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_SIGNATURE` — one outstanding amendment at a time, a second attempt being `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`.

The amendment then resolves in one of two symmetric ways, and the parent's fate differs between them. If the amendment draft is **signed**, the parent moves `AMENDING → EXPIRED` as a system cascade: every `ACTIVE` markup tier of the parent becomes `SUPERSEDED`, every `ACTIVE` coverage row of the parent becomes `ENDED`, and the parent's `version` is incremented by 1. Signing an amendment draft is refused with `ERR_PARENT_CONTRACT_NOT_AMENDING` unless the parent is in `AMENDING` at that moment. If instead the amendment is **abandoned** — the draft, in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, is terminated while its parent is in `AMENDING` — the draft becomes `TERMINATED` and, because the draft terminated, the parent moves `AMENDING → ACTIVE` with its `version` incremented by 1, closing the `ACTIVE → AMENDING → ACTIVE` cycle with the parent's tiers and coverages untouched.

Suspension is available from either live state: `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and `AMENDING → SUSPENDED`, each optionally carrying a `note` and each incrementing the contract's `version` by 1. Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` unless the logical clock is at or before `effective_to`, recomputes `compliance_posture`, and increments `version` by 1. Suspending a parent in mid-amendment deliberately strands the outstanding draft: signing it now fails the parent-amending check, because the parent is no longer in `AMENDING`. Nothing deadlocks — reinstating returns the parent to `ACTIVE`, and the stranded draft can still be terminated.

Termination is permitted from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, optionally carrying a `reason`, and is refused while the contract still owes money: `rebate_payable_cents` must be exactly 0, otherwise `ERR_REBATE_PAYABLE_OUTSTANDING`. A termination moves the contract to `TERMINATED` and cascades in four directions: every deviation agreement of the contract in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `MANUFACTURER_APPROVED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE` or `PRICE_CEILING_BREACHED` becomes `TERMINATED`; every `ACTIVE` markup tier becomes `SUPERSEDED`; every `ACTIVE` coverage row becomes `ENDED`; and when the terminated row is itself an amendment draft whose parent is `AMENDING`, the parent returns to `ACTIVE`. The contract's `version` is incremented by 1, as is each cascaded row's.

Expiry, by contrast, is driven by the clock rather than by a person. When a `SystemAdmin` advances the logical clock past a contract's `effective_to` while that contract is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, the contract moves to `EXPIRED`; its deviation agreements in any of the six non-terminal states listed above become `TERMINATED`, and its `ACTIVE` coverage rows become `ENDED`, but — unlike a termination — its markup tiers are left as they are. The same clock move separately expires deviation agreements: an agreement in `ACTIVE` or `PRICE_CEILING_BREACHED` whose `effective_to` the new clock has reached or passed becomes `EXPIRED`. `EXPIRED` and `TERMINATED` are the contract's two terminal states, and the sweep reports what it touched through `expired_contract_ids` and `expired_deviation_ids`: `expired_contract_ids` carries the contracts this clock move itself drove to `EXPIRED`, and `expired_deviation_ids` carries every deviation agreement the same move drove out of a non-terminal state — both the ones an expiring contract's cascade moved to `TERMINATED` and the ones the window comparison moved to `EXPIRED`. No id is reported twice, because an agreement the contract cascade has already terminated no longer matches the window sweep's state filter.

Alongside the raw contract record sits a computed compliance read, available to the manager, the operator, the accountant and the auditor. It answers with the `contract_id`, an `evaluated_at` equal to the logical clock, a `closed_period_count` giving the number of that contract's rebate periods in `CLOSED` or `SETTLED` status, the `latest_period_id` of the most recent such period (ordered by `period_start` descending, then `created_at` descending), that period's `latest_compliance_pct` and `latest_rebate_rate_pct`, the contract's own `contracted_purchase_pct_target`, a `shortfall_pct` equal to the target minus `latest_compliance_pct` rounded half up to two decimals — negative when the operator has beaten its commitment — and the contract's `compliance_basis` and `compliance_posture`. When the contract has no `CLOSED` or `SETTLED` period at all, `closed_period_count` is 0 and `latest_period_id`, `latest_compliance_pct`, `latest_rebate_rate_pct` and `shortfall_pct` are all null.

### 3.4. Deviated Pricing: Drafting, Manufacturer Negotiation and Activation

A deviated price is an off-invoice net price on a single contracted item, funded by the manufacturer. A `PricingAnalyst` drafts one against a live contract by supplying `contract_id`, `product_id`, the `manufacturer_id` that will be billed back, the `bill_back_basis`, the operator's ceiling `max_operator_price_cents` (which must be greater than zero), the half-open window `effective_from` to `effective_to`, and `expected_contract_version`. The named manufacturer must be the item's own funder: if the agreement's `manufacturer_id` differs from the product's `manufacturer_id`, the draft is refused with `ERR_DEVIATION_MANUFACTURER_MISMATCH`. The contract must also already have an `ACTIVE` tier covering the item's product class, or the draft fails with `ERR_NO_TIER_FOR_PRODUCT_CLASS`.

The basis decides which of two mutually exclusive price inputs is required, and how the effective price behaves over time. Under `FIXED_NET_PRICE` the analyst supplies `requested_net_price_cents`, greater than zero, and must leave `protected_discount_cents` null; the resulting `deviated_price_cents` is simply that requested net price, pinned at creation and never re-derived afterwards. Under `COST_PLUS_PROTECTED` the analyst supplies `protected_discount_cents`, greater than zero, and must leave `requested_net_price_cents` null; `deviated_price_cents` is then the item's current cost on the relevant basis minus that protected discount, where the cost basis is the one named by the contract's entry-band tier — the single `ACTIVE` tier of this contract covering the product's class at case volume zero, that is, the tier whose `min_case_volume` is 0. Supplying the wrong field for the basis, or both, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at ladder priority 4, and so is a resolved `deviated_price_cents` of zero or less — except that the latter, being derived rather than supplied, can only be judged once the tier lookup has succeeded, so it is evaluated immediately after the priority-12 tier check and therefore before the window-overlap and ceiling tests. Once resolved, the price is tested against the ceiling: a `deviated_price_cents` strictly greater than `max_operator_price_cents` at creation is rejected with `ERR_DEVIATED_PRICE_ABOVE_CEILING`. The window itself must not collide with an existing agreement: the new half-open window may not intersect the window of any other agreement on the same contract and product whose status is not `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`. Touching endpoints are explicitly fine — a previous agreement whose `effective_to` equals the new `effective_from` is not an overlap — while any true intersection is `ERR_DEVIATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A successful draft lands in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, `price_revision_count` 0, and `submitted_by_user_id` and `manufacturer_decision_note` both null, and it increments the parent contract's `version` by 1.

Submitting the agreement moves it to `SUBMITTED` from either `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, requires `revision_round` to be strictly below three, and records the acting analyst's `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`. The two submit paths differ in exactly one respect: the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` path leaves `revision_round` alone, while the `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` path increments `revision_round` by 1 before the agreement is submitted again. Both increment the agreement's `version` by 1. A fourth submission attempt — one made while `revision_round` has already reached three — is rejected with `ERR_MAX_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`, which is what bounds the `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` negotiation loop.

Out of `SUBMITTED` the manufacturer's own representative decides, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_REVISION` or `REJECT` and optionally a `decision_note`. All three branches require the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the agreement's `manufacturer_id`, all three require the manufacturer's `status` to be `ACTIVE` (a suspended counterparty cannot decide at all, and is told so with `ERR_MANUFACTURER_SUSPENDED`), all three write the supplied note into `manufacturer_decision_note`, and all three increment the agreement's `version` by 1. They differ only in the destination state: `APPROVE` moves `SUBMITTED → MANUFACTURER_APPROVED`, `REQUEST_REVISION` moves `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and hands the agreement back to the analyst, and `REJECT` moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal.

Activation is the distributor's own final act and belongs to a `NationalAccountManager`. It moves `MANUFACTURER_APPROVED → ACTIVE` and carries four preconditions beyond the from-state: the contract must be `ACTIVE`; the contract's `compliance_posture` must not be `RESTRICTED`; the activating `X-User-ID` must differ from the agreement's `submitted_by_user_id`; and the resolved `deviated_price_cents` must still be at or below `max_operator_price_cents`, since an intervening cost change may have moved it. Resolving it at activation means re-deriving it: on the `COST_PLUS_PROTECTED` basis the price is recomputed as the entry-band tier's current cost on that tier's `cost_basis` minus `protected_discount_cents` and the recomputed figure is written back onto the agreement, while a `FIXED_NET_PRICE` agreement keeps the price pinned at creation; if no entry-band tier exists any more, or its cost field is null, the stored price is used as it stands rather than raising an error. Once `ACTIVE`, the agreement rewrites the invoiced price of every later order line on that contract and product whose order date falls inside `[effective_from, effective_to)`. The agreement's `version` is incremented by 1. `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` and `TERMINATED` are the three terminal states; `PRICE_CEILING_BREACHED` is not terminal, and an agreement in it can still expire with the clock or be terminated with its contract.

### 3.5. The Order Line: Tier Pricing, Service Fees, Substitution, Invoicing and Repricing

An `OperatorBuyer` records an order line against its own contract by supplying just four things — `contract_id`, `product_id`, `distribution_center_id` and `quantity_cases` (at least 1) — and the server derives everything else. The ordered item must be `ACTIVE`: a `DISCONTINUED` product is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, judged with the body fields and so ahead of the contract-active check. The logical clock at the moment of the request becomes the line's `order_date`, which must fall inside the contract's inclusive term from `effective_from` to `effective_to`; outside it, the line is refused with `ERR_ORDER_DATE_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM`. Exactly one `OPEN` rebate period of the contract must contain that instant in its half-open window, and its id is recorded as `rebate_period_id`; if none does, the line is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_REBATE_PERIOD`. The line lands with `pricing_status` `DRAFT_PRICED` and `billback_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, with `credited_amount_cents` 0 and with `substituted_from_product_id`, `substitution_rule_id` and `bill_back_claim_id` all null.

**Tier selection.** The applicable markup tier depends on how much of the same product class the operator has already bought under this contract. The server sums `quantity_cases` over the contract's other order lines whose product shares this line's `product_class_code`, whose `pricing_status` is `DRAFT_PRICED`, `INVOICED` or `REPRICED` — lines that have gone to `CREDITED` are excluded — and whose `order_date` is at or after the contract's `effective_from` and strictly earlier than this line's own `order_date`, excluding the line itself; the sweep runs in `order_date` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending order so that a replay is deterministic. Because the accumulated volume counts only strictly earlier lines, an early line keeps the low-volume band's higher markup for the rest of the period rather than being retroactively repriced. The tier is then the single `ACTIVE` tier of this contract for that product class whose `min_case_volume` is at most the accumulated volume and whose `max_case_volume` is either null or strictly greater than that volume; its id is recorded as `applied_tier_id`. If no such tier exists, the line is refused with `ERR_NO_TIER_FOR_PRODUCT_CLASS`.

**Cost basis and markup.** The tier's `cost_basis` names which of the product's three cost fields to read — `LAST_COST` reads `last_cost_cents`, `AVG_COST` reads `avg_cost_cents`, `REPLACEMENT_COST` reads `replacement_cost_cents` — and that value is frozen onto the line as `cost_basis_snapshot_cents`, because later audits reprice from the frozen figure rather than from whatever the catalogue says at audit time. From it comes `list_unit_price_cents`, the cost-plus contract price per case before any deviation. Under `PERCENT_OF_COST`, `list_unit_price_cents` is the frozen cost multiplied by one hundred plus `markup_value`, divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents. Under `DOLLARS_PER_CASE`, it is the frozen cost plus `markup_value` rounded half up to whole cents, `markup_value` being integral cents in that mode.

**The invoiced price.** Three cases are tried in a fixed order. First, the server looks for the single deviation agreement on this contract and this product whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose half-open window `[effective_from, effective_to)` contains the line's `order_date`. If one exists, `invoiced_unit_price_cents` is that agreement's `deviated_price_cents` and the agreement's id is recorded as `deviation_agreement_id`. Otherwise, if the line carries a `substitution_rule_id` whose `price_protection_mode` is `HOLD_CONTRACT_PRICE`, `invoiced_unit_price_cents` is the list price that the markup step would produce for the **originally ordered** item named by `substituted_from_product_id`, computed with the tier already selected above. Otherwise `invoiced_unit_price_cents` equals `list_unit_price_cents`. In every case `extended_price_cents` is `invoiced_unit_price_cents` multiplied by `quantity_cases`.

**The contracted-item flag.** A line with no `substitution_rule_id` takes `is_contracted_item` straight from the applied tier's `counts_as_contracted`. A substituted line ignores the tier and instead sets `is_contracted_item` to true when the chosen rule's `price_protection_mode` is `HOLD_CONTRACT_PRICE` and false when it is `SUBSTITUTE_OWN_PRICE`.

**Out-of-market service fees.** Shipping is only legitimate where coverage exists. The server looks for the single `ACTIVE` coverage row on this contract and this `distribution_center_id` whose closed window from `coverage_from` to `coverage_to` contains the line's `order_date`; if there is none — including the case where a contract-level cascade has already moved the row to `ENDED` — the request fails with `ERR_DC_NOT_COVERED`. The waiver test then runs against the pre-fee line value, and its evaluation order is pinned. `service_fee_cents` is 0 when the coverage row's `coverage_type` is `IN_MARKET`, or when its `service_fee_waived` is true, or when `extended_price_cents` is at or above five thousand dollars — 500,000 cents. Otherwise `service_fee_cents` is the shipping center's `out_of_market_fee_per_case_cents` multiplied by `quantity_cases`. Only after the fee is settled is the total formed: `invoice_total_cents` is `extended_price_cents` plus `service_fee_cents`. The threshold is tested against `extended_price_cents` and never against `invoice_total_cents`, even though `invoice_total_cents` is what the operator ultimately pays; testing the total would let the fee decide whether the fee applies, and that reading is forbidden. Both operands are integers, so no rounding occurs here.

**Filling an out-of-stock item.** When the contracted item on a draft-priced line turns out to be unavailable, the operator asks for a fill and the **server** — not the client — picks the replacement. The request optionally carries a `reason` and nothing else. It is available only to the `OperatorBuyer` owning the contract, only while `pricing_status` is `DRAFT_PRICED`, and only when the currently referenced product's `stock_status` is `OUT_OF_STOCK`; asking to substitute an item that is in stock is `ERR_PRODUCT_IN_STOCK`.

The candidate set is built from the contract's substitution rules whose `primary_product_id` is the line's current product and whose own `status` is `ACTIVE`, keeping only those whose substitute product is itself `ACTIVE` and `IN_STOCK`, whose substitute shares the primary's `substitution_group_code`, and whose price gap is inside tolerance. The gap is computed from the two list prices the markup step produces for the primary item and for the substitute under the same tier lookup and the same `order_date`: it is the absolute difference between the substitute's list price and the primary's list price, multiplied by one hundred, divided by the primary's list price, rounded half up to two decimals, and the rule survives when that figure is less than or equal to its `max_price_delta_pct` — the comparison is inclusive. Two further candidates are dropped silently rather than raising: one whose substitute has no cost value on the selected tier's `cost_basis`, and — since the percentage is undefined when the primary's list price is zero — every candidate whose own list price is not also zero, a zero-against-zero pair being treated as a gap of `0.00` and therefore surviving. Surviving candidates are ordered by `rank` ascending and, when two rules share a rank, by `created_at` ascending and, should that match too, by insertion order; ties are never broken by id. The first candidate after that ordering wins. An empty candidate set is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUBSTITUTE`.

The fill rewrites the line in place: `substituted_from_product_id` takes the previous `product_id`, `product_id` becomes the selected substitute, and `substitution_rule_id` records the chosen rule. Everything derived is then recomputed for the substitute — `applied_tier_id`, `cost_basis_snapshot_cents`, `list_unit_price_cents`, `invoiced_unit_price_cents`, `extended_price_cents`, `service_fee_cents`, `invoice_total_cents`, `is_contracted_item` and `deviation_agreement_id` — and the line's `version` is incremented by 1. Note that the line does not leave `DRAFT_PRICED`: substitution is a self-loop on the pricing dimension, and a coverage check still applies, so a fill on a line whose distribution center has lost coverage fails with `ERR_DC_NOT_COVERED`.

Invoicing is the `NationalAccountManager`'s act and requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`. It moves `pricing_status` `DRAFT_PRICED → INVOICED`, and it is also the only entry point to the line's second, entirely independent status dimension: when `deviation_agreement_id` is not null, `billback_status` moves `NOT_APPLICABLE → ACCRUED` in the same request, and when it is null `billback_status` stays `NOT_APPLICABLE`. An invoiced line becomes eligible both for rebate-period close aggregation and for bill-back claim sweeps. The line's `version` is incremented by 1.

Repricing exists for the case where the tier table or the underlying cost has moved while the money is still open. It moves `pricing_status` `INVOICED → REPRICED`, requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, and additionally requires the rebate period named by the line's `rebate_period_id` to still be `OPEN` — a period in any other status is `ERR_REBATE_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`, tested as a state conflict and therefore before the contract-active check. It recomputes `list_unit_price_cents`, `invoiced_unit_price_cents`, `extended_price_cents`, `service_fee_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` from the current cost basis and the currently `ACTIVE` tier, and increments the line's `version` by 1. It recomputes those five fields and no others: `applied_tier_id`, `cost_basis_snapshot_cents`, `deviation_agreement_id` and `is_contracted_item` are left exactly as they stood, so a reprice reads the newly selected tier and today's cost without refreshing the frozen snapshot, and picks up a deviated price now in force without recording that agreement's id. The coverage lookup runs again as part of the fee step, so a reprice on a line whose distribution center has lost coverage at the line's `order_date` fails with `ERR_DC_NOT_COVERED`, just as a create or a fill would. Invoicing a repriced line moves it `REPRICED → INVOICED` again, so `INVOICED → REPRICED → INVOICED` is a legitimate cycle inside the pricing dimension. Neither dimension constrains the other: the bill-back dimension advances on its own triggers, so a line that has reached `CREDITED` on the pricing side may still be `CLAIMED` on the bill-back side, and a line whose recovery has reached `RECOVERED` may still be repriced.

### 3.6. Cost Change Notices, Market Indexing and the Activation Cascade

Costs move, and the operator is contractually owed warning before they do. A `PricingAnalyst` issues a notice against a live contract by naming the `contract_id`, the `product_id` whose cost is changing, the `cost_basis` that activation will overwrite — `LAST_COST`, `AVG_COST` or `REPLACEMENT_COST` — and the `effective_at` instant on which the new cost is to take hold. Exactly one of two price inputs is required and it is dictated by the item: a market-indexed product's notice must supply `published_index_value` (greater than zero, two decimals) and must not supply `proposed_cost_cents`, while a non-indexed product's notice must supply `proposed_cost_cents` (greater than zero) and must not supply `published_index_value`. Either mistake — in either direction — is `ERR_INDEX_VALUE_REQUIRED`, but the two halves sit at different points in the ladder: the omission of the input the item demands is caught as soon as the priority-12 cost-and-tier check has passed, and so precedes the rolling-window and notice-period tests, whereas supplying the input the item forbids is caught only after them, at priority 28. The item must also already carry a non-null cost value on the requested `cost_basis`, and the contract must have an `ACTIVE` tier covering that item's `product_class_code`; otherwise the notice is refused with `ERR_NO_TIER_FOR_PRODUCT_CLASS`.

From those inputs the server derives four fields. `new_cost_cents` for an indexed item is the product's `index_baseline_cost_cents` multiplied by the supplied `published_index_value`, divided by the product's `index_baseline_value`, rounded half up to whole cents; for a non-indexed item it is simply `proposed_cost_cents`. `delta_pct` is the signed percentage change measured against the item's **current** cost on the requested basis, not against its baseline cost: the new cost minus the current cost, multiplied by one hundred, divided by the current cost, rounded half up to two decimals; when that current cost is zero the ratio is undefined and `delta_pct` is exactly `0.00`, which makes the change minor. `notice_required_days` follows from that delta: when the magnitude of `delta_pct` is at or above ten percent the change is major and the contract's own `notice_period_days` applies; below that magnitude, the minor-change window of seven days applies. Only then is the window anchored — `submitted_at` is set to the logical clock and `earliest_effective_at` is `submitted_at` advanced by `notice_required_days` days. A requested `effective_at` earlier than `earliest_effective_at` is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`. One further guard protects the operator from notice churn on the same item: if any other notice on the same product in `PENDING` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` status has an `effective_at` whose absolute distance from the new notice's `effective_at` is strictly less than thirty days, the new notice is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. That is a count over the rolling window, not a pairwise interval intersection. A new notice lands in `PENDING` with `dispute_note` null.

The operator answers a notice by acknowledging it — `PENDING → ACKNOWLEDGED`, and equally `DISPUTED → ACKNOWLEDGED` — or by objecting to it, `PENDING → DISPUTED`, which requires a `note` that is stored as `dispute_note`. Both are restricted to the `OperatorBuyer` whose `X-User-ID` matches the contract's `operator_id`, and both increment the notice's `version` by 1. The analyst answers an objection by revising the notice, `DISPUTED → PENDING`, which requires the contract to be `ACTIVE` and takes a fresh `cost_basis` and `effective_at` plus whichever of `published_index_value` or `proposed_cost_cents` the item demands. A revision restarts the notice clock: `submitted_at` is reset to the logical clock and `new_cost_cents`, `delta_pct`, `notice_required_days` and `earliest_effective_at` are all recomputed from the revised inputs under the same rules, `dispute_note` is cleared to null, and the notice's `version` is incremented by 1 — so `PENDING → DISPUTED → PENDING` is a renegotiation loop in which each round buys the operator a fresh advance-notice window. The analyst may instead withdraw a notice from `PENDING`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DISPUTED`, optionally supplying a `reason`; the notice becomes `WITHDRAWN`, its `version` is incremented by 1, and it stops occupying the rolling thirty-day slot for its product, freeing the analyst to issue a replacement.

Activating an acknowledged notice is where the cost actually moves, and it drives a four-hop cascade. Activation belongs to the `PricingAnalyst`, requires the notice to be `ACKNOWLEDGED`, requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, and requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `effective_at` — an early activation is `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE`. The notice moves `ACKNOWLEDGED → EFFECTIVE`, which is terminal, and its `version` is incremented by 1. **Hop one:** the product's cost field named by `cost_basis` is overwritten with `new_cost_cents`, `cost_effective_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the product's `version` is incremented by 1. **Hop two,** caused by hop one: every deviation agreement on that product whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose `bill_back_basis` is `COST_PLUS_PROTECTED` — on this contract or on any other — re-derives `deviated_price_cents` from the new cost minus its `protected_discount_cents`, increments `price_revision_count` by 1 (unconditionally, even when the re-derived figure is unchanged) and increments its own `version` by 1. The cost the re-derivation reads is the one named by the `cost_basis` of that agreement's own entry-band tier, falling back to the notice's `cost_basis` when its contract no longer has an entry-band tier for the class; if that cost field is null the agreement keeps the price it already had, and `price_revision_count` and `version` still move. Agreements on the `FIXED_NET_PRICE` basis are untouched — their price was pinned at creation. **Hop three,** caused by hop two: any such agreement whose re-derived `deviated_price_cents` now exceeds its `max_operator_price_cents` transitions `ACTIVE → PRICE_CEILING_BREACHED`, and because the ceiling was breached the owning contract's `deviation_breach_count` is incremented by 1 and the contract's `version` is incremented by 1. **Hop four,** caused by hop three: once `deviation_breach_count` reaches one — a single breach is enough — the contract's `compliance_posture` is recomputed to `RESTRICTED`, which from that moment blocks activating any further deviation agreement and blocks opening any amendment, both with `ERR_CONTRACT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`.

### 3.7. Compliance Measurement, Rebate Accrual and Settlement

Rebates are earned by buying contracted items, and they are measured over periods a `RebateAccountant` opens against a live contract by supplying `contract_id`, `period_start` and `period_end`. A new period's half-open window may not intersect any existing period of the same contract, whatever that period's status; an intersection is `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A period opens in `OPEN` with `settled_amount_cents` 0, `reopen_count` 0, `closed_at`, `settled_at` and `settled_by_user_id` null, and all of `gross_purchases_cents`, `contracted_purchases_cents`, `qualifying_cases`, `contracted_cases`, `compliance_pct`, `rebate_rate_pct`, `operator_rebate_cents`, `manufacturer_funded_cents` and `distributor_funded_cents` null — an open period has no measured figures at all.

Closing the period is what computes them. It is refused unless the period is `OPEN`, unless the governing contract is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` — a contract in any other status raises `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`, since a period may legitimately be closed after the term has run out or while the account is suspended — and unless the logical clock has reached or passed `period_end` (otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`). Those three tests are evaluated in that order, so a wrong contract status is reported ahead of a period that has not yet ended.

**Measuring compliance.** The qualifying line set is the contract's order lines whose `pricing_status` is `INVOICED` or `REPRICED` — lines still `DRAFT_PRICED` and lines that have gone to `CREDITED` are both excluded — and whose `order_date` lies at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. The contracted subset is those qualifying lines whose `is_contracted_item` is true. Four totals are recorded: `gross_purchases_cents` is the sum of `extended_price_cents` over the qualifying set, `contracted_purchases_cents` the same sum over the contracted subset, `qualifying_cases` the sum of `quantity_cases` over the qualifying set, and `contracted_cases` the same sum over the contracted subset. Service fees never enter either the numerator or the denominator; only `extended_price_cents` does. The contract's `compliance_basis` then chooses the ratio: under `DOLLAR_WEIGHTED` the numerator is `contracted_purchases_cents` and the denominator `gross_purchases_cents`, so an expensive non-contracted substitution depresses the percentage more than a cheap one; under `CASE_WEIGHTED` the numerator is `contracted_cases` and the denominator `qualifying_cases`, so the very same lines yield a different percentage whenever contracted and non-contracted lines differ in price per case. `compliance_pct` is the numerator multiplied by one hundred, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimals — and when the denominator is zero, because the qualifying set was empty, `compliance_pct` is exactly `0.00`, never null and never an error.

**Accruing the rebate.** `rebate_rate_pct` comes from a four-band table read against `compliance_pct` with inclusive lower bounds: 4.00 percent at 95.00 or above, 3.00 percent at 90.00 or above, 1.50 percent at 80.00 or above, and 0.00 percent below that. `operator_rebate_cents` is then accumulated **per contracted line and rounded per line**, not summed and rounded once: for each line in the contracted subset, iterated in `order_date` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending order, the line's `extended_price_cents` is multiplied by `rebate_rate_pct`, divided by one hundred, and rounded half up to whole cents, and those per-line amounts are added together. An empty contracted subset gives `operator_rebate_cents` 0. The funding split is then applied once to that already-rounded total: `manufacturer_funded_cents` is `operator_rebate_cents` multiplied by sixty, divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents — the manufacturer funds sixty percent of the operator rebate — and `distributor_funded_cents` is `operator_rebate_cents` minus `manufacturer_funded_cents`, which absorbs the rounding remainder.

**What closing touches.** The period moves `OPEN → CLOSED`, `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the period's `version` is incremented by 1. The contract's `rebate_payable_cents` is increased by `operator_rebate_cents` and its `version` incremented by 1. The manufacturer named by the contract's `rebate_funding_manufacturer_id` has its `rebate_funding_payable_cents` increased by `manufacturer_funded_cents` and its `version` incremented by 1. Finally the contract's `compliance_posture` is recomputed from this newly closed period.

**How posture is derived.** The posture reads the single most recent rebate period of the contract whose status is `CLOSED` or `SETTLED`, ordered by `period_start` descending and then `created_at` descending; periods that are `OPEN`, including ones an audit credit has reopened, are ignored entirely. If the contract's `deviation_breach_count` is at least one, the posture is `RESTRICTED` regardless of compliance. Otherwise, if there is no closed or settled period at all, the posture is `OPEN`. Otherwise, if that period's `compliance_pct` is strictly below the contractual floor of 80.00 percent, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; if it is strictly below the contract's own `contracted_purchase_pct_target` but not below the floor, the posture is `WATCH`; and otherwise it is `OPEN`. Both comparisons are strict, so a period at exactly 80.00 is not `RESTRICTED` and a period exactly at the target is not `WATCH`.

**Settling.** A `RebateAccountant` settles a `CLOSED` period by supplying a `settlement_reference`. Settlement is refused while any pricing dispute in `OPEN` or `UNDER_AUDIT` status exists on a line assigned to this period — `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTES_BLOCK_SETTLEMENT` — and refused while the contract's `compliance_posture` is `RESTRICTED` — `ERR_CONTRACT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. On success the period moves `CLOSED → SETTLED`, `settled_amount_cents` is set to `operator_rebate_cents`, `settled_at` to the logical clock and `settled_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`. The contract's `rebate_payable_cents` is decreased by `operator_rebate_cents` while `rebate_settled_total_cents` is increased by the same amount, and the contract's `version` is incremented by 1; the funding manufacturer's `rebate_funding_payable_cents` is decreased by `manufacturer_funded_cents` and its `version` incremented by 1. The period's `version` is incremented by 1, and the rebate liability equality of §2.10 holds both before and after.

**Reversing a settlement.** A `ContractAuditor` may unwind a settlement by supplying a `reason`, moving the period `SETTLED → CLOSED`. `settled_amount_cents` is reset to 0 and `settled_at` and `settled_by_user_id` back to null. The contract's `rebate_payable_cents` is increased by `operator_rebate_cents` and `rebate_settled_total_cents` decreased by the same amount, with the contract's `version` incremented by 1; the funding manufacturer's `rebate_funding_payable_cents` is increased by `manufacturer_funded_cents` with its `version` incremented by 1; the period's `version` is incremented by 1; and the same conservation equality holds before and after. Because a reversal recomputes nothing, `CLOSED → SETTLED → CLOSED` is a clean compensating cycle. The period has no terminal state at all: it can also be pushed back from `CLOSED` to `OPEN` by an audit credit, as described in §3.9 below.

### 3.8. Manufacturer Bill-Back Recovery and Cash Application

Every case invoiced below its contract list price under a deviation is money the distributor expects back from the manufacturer. A `RebateAccountant` accrues that expectation as a claim by naming the `deviation_agreement_id`, the half-open sweep window `claim_period_start` to `claim_period_end`, and `expected_deviation_agreement_version`. The window may not intersect any existing claim on the same agreement unless that claim has been written off; an intersection is `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The claim copies the agreement's `manufacturer_id` onto itself at creation, starts in `ACCRUED` with `recovered_amount_cents` 0 and `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_at` and `write_off_reason` all null, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1.

The sweep gathers the agreement's order lines whose `billback_status` is `ACCRUED` or `CLAIMED`, whose `pricing_status` is `INVOICED`, `REPRICED` or `CREDITED`, and whose `order_date` lies at or after `claim_period_start` and strictly before `claim_period_end`. `claimed_cases` is the sum of `quantity_cases` over those lines. `claimed_amount_cents` accumulates, over the same lines iterated in `order_date` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending order, the greater of zero and the difference between the line's `list_unit_price_cents` and its `invoiced_unit_price_cents`, multiplied by that line's `quantity_cases`; clamping each per-line delta at zero means a held contract price above list can never produce a negative claim. A sweep that gathers no cases at all — `claimed_cases` of zero — is rejected with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_LINES`. Each line the sweep gathers moves `billback_status` `ACCRUED → CLAIMED`, records the new claim's id as its `bill_back_claim_id`, and has its `version` incremented by 1; a gathered line already in `CLAIMED` keeps that status but is still re-pointed at the new claim and still version-bumped.

Submitting the claim to the manufacturer moves it to `SUBMITTED` from either `ACCRUED` or `DISPUTED`, requires the manufacturer's `status` to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_MANUFACTURER_SUSPENDED` otherwise), and then requires `claimed_amount_cents` to be at least 1 cent — a claim with nothing recoverable is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, reported only once the suspension test has passed. It records the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id` and the logical clock as `submitted_at`, recomputes the manufacturer's `open_claim_exposure_cents` and increments the manufacturer's `version` by 1, and increments the claim's own `version` by 1.

The manufacturer's representative may dispute a claim in `SUBMITTED` or `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` status, supplying a `note`. The dispute recomputes `claimed_amount_cents` over the lines as they currently stand — which matters because an operator credit may have shrunk a line's contribution since the claim was raised — refreshes the manufacturer's `open_claim_exposure_cents`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1. A disputed claim can be submitted again once the parties agree, so `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → SUBMITTED` is a recovery-reopening cycle. A `ContractAuditor` may instead write the claim off from `SUBMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` or `DISPUTED`, supplying a `write_off_reason` and provided the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `submitted_by_user_id`. A write-off is terminal: the claim becomes `WRITTEN_OFF`, every order line carrying its id whose `billback_status` is `CLAIMED` moves to `WRITTEN_OFF` with its `version` incremented by 1, the manufacturer's `open_claim_exposure_cents` is recomputed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Applying cash.** A remittance records what the manufacturer actually paid: a `RebateAccountant` supplies the `manufacturer_id`, a `remitted_amount_cents` of at least 1, and a `remittance_reference` that must be unique for that manufacturer. `received_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the remittance starts in `APPLIED`. The cash is then poured through a waterfall over that manufacturer's claims whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` — claims that are `ACCRUED`, `DISPUTED`, `RECOVERED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` are skipped — taken in `claim_period_start` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending order, never by id, so the oldest claim period is satisfied first. Beginning with the whole remitted amount as the remaining balance, each claim in turn receives the lesser of the remaining balance and its own shortfall, that shortfall being `claimed_amount_cents` minus `recovered_amount_cents`. Where that slice is greater than zero, the claim's `recovered_amount_cents` is increased by the slice, the claim's id is appended to the remittance's `applied_claim_ids` in application order, the remaining balance is reduced by the slice, and the claim's status becomes `RECOVERED` when `recovered_amount_cents` now equals `claimed_amount_cents` and `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` when it is still short. The walk stops as soon as the remaining balance reaches zero. `applied_amount_cents` is the remitted amount less whatever balance remains, and `unapplied_amount_cents` is that remaining balance; the manufacturer's `unapplied_credit_cents` is increased by `unapplied_amount_cents` and its `total_remitted_cents` by the full `remitted_amount_cents`, and `open_claim_exposure_cents` is recomputed. Every claim the waterfall drives all the way to `RECOVERED` also moves each of its order lines from `billback_status` `CLAIMED` to `RECOVERED`. No rounding occurs anywhere in the waterfall, all operands being integer cents, and surplus cash is parked rather than discarded.

**Unwinding cash.** A remittance in `APPLIED` may be reversed by a `RebateAccountant` supplying a `reason`, provided every claim listed in `applied_claim_ids` is still in `SUBMITTED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`, `RECOVERED` or `DISPUTED` status; a claim that has since been written off blocks the reversal with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, tested after the remittance's own from-state. Reversal moves the remittance `APPLIED → REVERSED`, which is terminal, and increments its `version` by 1. Each claim in `applied_claim_ids` has its `recovered_amount_cents` reduced by exactly the slice this remittance applied to it and its status recomputed — back to `SUBMITTED` when `recovered_amount_cents` returns to zero, and to `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` when it remains positive. Every order line under those claims whose `billback_status` is `RECOVERED` reverts to `CLAIMED`. The manufacturer's `unapplied_credit_cents` is reduced by this remittance's `unapplied_amount_cents`, its `total_remitted_cents` by the full `remitted_amount_cents`, its `open_claim_exposure_cents` recomputed, and its `version` incremented by 1. Attempting to reverse a remittance that is already `REVERSED` is `ERR_REMITTANCE_INVALID_STATE`. The manufacturer recovery equality of §2.10 holds both before and after, and the reversal restores each term to the value it had before the application.

### 3.9. Invoice Pricing Disputes and Audit

An operator that believes it was overcharged opens a dispute on the offending line, naming the `order_line_id`, the `claimed_correct_unit_price_cents` it asserts should have been invoiced (zero or more), and a `dispute_reason` drawn from `TIER_MISAPPLIED`, `DEVIATION_NOT_APPLIED`, `COST_BASIS_WRONG` and `SERVICE_FEE_WRONG`. The line must actually have been invoiced — its `pricing_status` must be `INVOICED` or `REPRICED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_LINE_INVALID_STATE` — and no dispute in `OPEN`, `UNDER_AUDIT` or `UPHELD` status may already exist for that line, otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_EXISTS_FOR_LINE`. The dispute copies the line's `contract_id` onto itself, stamps `opened_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, opens in `OPEN`, and leaves `audited_correct_unit_price_cents`, `variance_cents_per_case`, `variance_pct`, `credit_amount_cents`, `audited_by_user_id` and `audit_note` null. The operator may withdraw its own dispute from `OPEN` or `UNDER_AUDIT`, optionally supplying a `reason`; the dispute becomes `WITHDRAWN`, its `version` is incremented by 1, and the period it was blocking becomes settleable again.

A `ContractAuditor` starts the audit, moving `OPEN → UNDER_AUDIT`. The audit may only begin once the retroactive volume is final: the rebate period named by the disputed line's `rebate_period_id` must be `CLOSED` or `SETTLED`, otherwise `ERR_REBATE_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. The audit recomputes what the line should have cost, and it deliberately differs from the original pricing in two ways. First, the cost it starts from is the line's frozen `cost_basis_snapshot_cents`, not the product's cost today. Second, tier selection is **retroactive**: instead of counting only lines strictly earlier than the disputed line, the accumulated class volume counts every line of the same contract and product class whose `order_date` is strictly before the period's `period_end`, the disputed line itself included — but otherwise under the prospective sweep's own two filters, so a line is counted only when its `pricing_status` is `DRAFT_PRICED`, `INVOICED` or `REPRICED` (an already `CREDITED` line drops out) and its `order_date` is at or after the contract's `effective_from`. Because the full period volume now selects the band, the same inputs can land in a higher-volume, lower-markup tier and therefore produce a lower unit price than the line was originally charged — which is exactly the overcharge the operator is complaining about. Should no `ACTIVE` tier cover that retroactive volume, the audit falls back to the tier the line itself recorded in `applied_tier_id`, and only when that row is unavailable too is the audit refused with `ERR_NO_TIER_FOR_PRODUCT_CLASS`. The deviated-price and substitution-protection overrides are then re-applied exactly as they stood on the line: when the line carries a `deviation_agreement_id` the audited price is that agreement's `deviated_price_cents` as it reads now, with no fresh window lookup, and the substitution-protection branch is consulted only when the line carries no such id. The result is stored as `audited_correct_unit_price_cents`; `variance_cents_per_case` is the line's `invoiced_unit_price_cents` minus that audited price, so a positive variance means the operator was overcharged; and `variance_pct` is the absolute value of that variance multiplied by one hundred, divided by the line's `invoiced_unit_price_cents`, rounded half up to two decimals — or exactly `0.00` when that invoiced price is zero and the ratio is undefined. `audited_by_user_id` records the acting auditor and the dispute's `version` is incremented by 1.

An auditor who needs more information sends the dispute back, moving `UNDER_AUDIT → OPEN` with a required `note` stored as `audit_note`, and resets `audited_correct_unit_price_cents`, `variance_cents_per_case` and `variance_pct` to null; the dispute's `version` is incremented by 1. Restarting the audit re-derives all three from the then-current period volume, so `OPEN → UNDER_AUDIT → OPEN` is a genuine loop rather than a dead end.

Resolution takes a `resolution` of `UPHELD` or `REJECTED` and an optional `note`; either branch writes `audit_note` — an omitted `note` writes null, clearing whatever an earlier request-info round had left there — records `audited_by_user_id`, and increments the dispute's `version` by 1. The `REJECTED` branch is unconditional from `UNDER_AUDIT`. The `UPHELD` branch is gated twice: `variance_pct` must be at or above the audit tolerance of 0.50 percent — the comparison is inclusive — and `variance_cents_per_case` must be strictly greater than zero, meaning the operator really was overcharged rather than favoured. A resolution that fails either gate is rejected with `ERR_VARIANCE_BELOW_AUDIT_TOLERANCE`.

Issuing the credit for an `UPHELD` dispute is the deepest cascade in the service, and it is refused outright when the rebate period covering the credited line has already reached `SETTLED` — a settled period may not be reopened by a credit, and the attempt is `ERR_SETTLED_PERIOD_IMMUTABLE`. **Hop one:** the dispute moves `UPHELD → CREDIT_ISSUED`, `credit_amount_cents` is set to `variance_cents_per_case` multiplied by the line's `quantity_cases`, and the dispute's `version` is incremented by 1. **Hop two,** caused by hop one: the order line moves `pricing_status` from `INVOICED` or `REPRICED` to `CREDITED`, its `invoiced_unit_price_cents` is rewritten to `audited_correct_unit_price_cents`, `extended_price_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` are recomputed from that rewritten price — `service_fee_cents` is carried across untouched, so the five-thousand-dollar waiver threshold is never re-tested against the reduced extended value — `credited_amount_cents` is increased by `credit_amount_cents`, and the line's `version` is incremented by 1. `CREDITED` is terminal on the pricing dimension. **Hop three,** caused by hop two: the rebate period named by that line's `rebate_period_id`, if it is `CLOSED`, moves `CLOSED → OPEN`; `gross_purchases_cents`, `contracted_purchases_cents`, `qualifying_cases`, `contracted_cases`, `compliance_pct`, `rebate_rate_pct`, `operator_rebate_cents`, `manufacturer_funded_cents` and `distributor_funded_cents` are all reset to null, `closed_at` is reset to null, `reopen_count` is incremented by 1 and the period's `version` is incremented by 1. **Hop four,** caused by hop three: the contract's `rebate_payable_cents` is decreased by that period's `operator_rebate_cents` and the funding manufacturer's `rebate_funding_payable_cents` by that period's `manufacturer_funded_cents`, the contract's `compliance_posture` is recomputed, and the `version` of both the contract and the manufacturer is incremented by 1. Because the reopened period is `OPEN`, it no longer counts as the latest closed period for posture purposes, so posture falls back to the next most recent closed or settled period — or to `OPEN` when none remains and no breach has been recorded. **And a side branch off hop two:** when the credited line's `billback_status` is `CLAIMED`, the bill-back claim it belongs to moves `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` and its `claimed_amount_cents` is recomputed over the currently swept lines — a claim sitting in any other status, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` included, is left exactly as it is — since the credit has shrunk — possibly to nothing — that line's recoverable delta. That is the intended serialization point between an operator credit and a manufacturer recovery: the credit is the reason the claim must be renegotiated.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create a new resource return `201 Created`; operations that mutate an existing resource, drive a state transition, or perform a computed read return `200 OK`.
