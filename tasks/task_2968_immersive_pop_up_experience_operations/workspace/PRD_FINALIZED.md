# Immersive Pop-Up Experience Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Touring immersive entertainment is a business of borrowed rooms and rented time. An operator signs a short lease on an empty retail shell, persuades a building authority that the space can legally hold a crowd, installs a licensed fiction inside it, sells timed admission windows against a bottleneck it cannot exceed, and then either extends the run because the city wants more or strikes the set and ships the scenic modules to the next city. Every one of those moves is constrained by somebody else's paperwork: a landlord's extension option, an authority's occupancy grant, a licensor's approval rights over creative execution and merchandise, an inspector's egress measurement. The operator that loses track of which constraint is currently binding either overbooks a room, opens without clearance, or discovers at settlement that it owes a minimum guarantee it never accrued against.

This document specifies the backend service that keeps those constraints reconciled. The service is the single system of record for pop-up venues and their permit rounds, intellectual-property licences and the creative submissions filed under them, experience runs and the themed rooms that define their guest flow, timed-entry windows and the orders sold into them, the cast and crew rostered onto each run, the safety inspections that can suspend a run mid-sale, the reusable build kits that tour between cities, and the royalty statements that close the books with the licensor. It is deliberately opinionated about ordering: capacity is always the tightest of several independently-derived ceilings, money is always allocated through fixed buckets in a fixed sequence, and a request that violates several rules at once always reports the earliest violation on a published ladder rather than whichever check happened to run first.

Two design decisions shape everything that follows. First, time is explicit: the service evaluates every lead-time, admission window, grace period, lease date and licence-term boundary against a server-held **logical clock** that test operators move deliberately, never against wall-clock time. Second, compliance is a separate dimension from operations: a run's operational progress (planned, open, extended, closing, closed) and its compliance standing (uncleared, cleared, suspended) advance independently, driven by different actors, and the service refuses to let one masquerade as the other.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows them. They are contract-level: field names, formats, header names, status codes, error identifiers and evaluation order are all fixed, and an implementation that varies them is wrong even if its business logic is otherwise correct.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` names the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, is blank, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the roles declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the authorisation column in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

Seven roles exist:

- **`VenueOpsManager`** — owns real estate and the touring hardware. Registers venues, drives the assembly-occupancy permit package, activates and ends leases, draws buildout money, and owns the build-kit fleet including refurbishment and localisation for new tour cities.
- **`LicensingManager`** — owns the intellectual-property relationship from the operator's side. Registers, activates and expires licence agreements, and cuts, recomputes, issues and settles royalty statements.
- **`LicensorRep`** — the licensor's own representative, holding approval rights over creative execution and merchandise. Takes submissions into review and decides them.
- **`ExperienceProducer`** — designs and runs the experience. Creates runs, themed rooms and timed-entry windows, files creative submissions, rosters performers, ambassadors and technicians, schedules shifts, and decides extensions and closure.
- **`SafetyOfficer`** — the authority-facing compliance role. Records permit decisions, books and finalises safety inspections, suspends and resumes admission windows, suspends staff, and lifts run suspensions.
- **`GuestServicesAgent`** — front of house. Opens and maintains guest accounts, sells and confirms orders on a guest's behalf, records arrival posture at the door, and settles refunds and experience credit.
- **`Guest`** — a ticket buyer acting for itself.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `VenueOpsManager`, `LicensingManager`, `LicensorRep`, `ExperienceProducer`, `SafetyOfficer` or `GuestServicesAgent`, the `X-User-ID` value is an arbitrary operator-chosen identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server does record such identifiers where a rule needs to remember who acted — `created_by_user_id` on a run, `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a creative submission, `finalized_by_user_id` on a safety inspection, and `licensor_rep_user_id` as supplied on a licence agreement — and the segregation-of-duties comparisons described in §2.10 compare those recorded strings against the caller's `X-User-ID`. They are string comparisons between operator identifiers, not lookups.

The `Guest` role alone is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Guest`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `GuestAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `GuestAccount` compare the resource's `guest_account_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Guest` that places an order for a different account, or cancels an order belonging to a different account, is rejected with `ERR_GUEST_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403. The same operations performed by a `GuestServicesAgent` carry no such restriction, because that role acts on any guest's behalf.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint is paginated by two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending; where two rows share a `created_at`, the order remains stable across repeated identical requests. A list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads carry none of those four fields in `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id`. Alongside it, most resources carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. Both timestamps are ISO 8601 instants in UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z` — for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. `created_at` is fixed at creation. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations caused by a cascade the client did not directly request. `version` is an integer that is 1 at creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, again including cascaded mutations.

One entity is deliberately exempt. A **ThemedRoom** is immutable once created: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field, no `version` field and no lifecycle status field, and the API offers neither an update nor a delete operation for it. Its one derived field, `throughput_per_slot`, is computed and written once at creation from the values supplied then, and the server never writes a room field again afterwards.

Dates supplied to the API — `lease_start_date`, `lease_end_date`, `term_start_date`, `term_end_date`, `scheduled_open_date`, `scheduled_close_date`, `period_start_date`, `period_end_date` — are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, exactly ten characters, interpreted in UTC. Instants supplied to the API — `slot_start_utc`, `slot_end_utc`, `shift_start_utc`, `shift_end_utc`, `scheduled_for_utc` — are ISO 8601 UTC instants ending in `Z`, with either no fractional digits or exactly three.

All temporal reasoning uses a server-held **logical clock**, never wall-clock time. Before any client moves it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. An operator of any of the six operator roles may reposition it by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new position as `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock touches no stored row, advances no `version`, and never reprices, recomputes or re-evaluates anything retroactively — it only changes the answer the next request gets when it asks what time it is. Every field the specification describes as "the logical-clock instant of" some event — `permit_issued_at`, `lease_activated_at`, `review_started_at`, `resubmitted_at`, `decided_at`, `opened_at`, `closing_started_at`, `closed_at`, `suspended_at`, `released_at`, `checked_in_at`, `refund_settled_at`, `finalized_at`, `issued_at` and `settled_at` — is stamped from the clock's position at the moment the transition is accepted.

### 2.5. Money, Precision and Conserved Quantities

Money is carried in whole cents as integers throughout; every field name ending in `_cents` is an integer number of cents, and no monetary field is ever expressed as a decimal currency amount or as a string. Wherever a money computation produces a fraction of a cent, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent, and the rounding happens exactly where the rule specifying the computation says it happens and nowhere else. Two computations in this specification pin their rounding order explicitly — the per-guest ticket price and the two legs of a royalty accrual — because rounding one step earlier or later yields a different number of cents.

Non-monetary derived figures — `cert_coverage_pct`, `readiness_score_preflight`, `readiness_score_audit`, `condition_score`, `initial_condition_score`, `demand_index`, `demand_index_at_extension`, `refund_pct` and `egress_width_cm` — are decimal numbers carried to two decimal places, likewise rounded half up. Capacity figures are integers, and every division that produces a capacity **floors** toward zero rather than rounding, so a derived ceiling can never be overstated by a fraction of an occupant.

Four quantities are conserved, and the service must be able to demonstrate each of them after every accepted request:

- **Buildout money.** On a venue, `buildout_spend_cents` always equals `allowance_drawn_cents` plus `kit_credit_drawn_cents` plus `operator_capex_cents`.
- **Seats on an admission window.** On a timed-entry slot, `released_capacity` always equals `held_capacity` plus `sold_capacity` plus `available_capacity`.
- **Guest money.** For every order that leaves the confirmed state by cancellation, `refunded_cents` plus `credit_issued_cents` equals `net_amount_cents`; and a guest's `experience_credit_cents` always equals the sum of `credit_issued_cents` over that guest's fully refunded orders.
- **Lease flexibility.** On a venue, `lease_extension_days_consumed` always equals the sum of `extension_days_granted` across every run at that venue.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Superseding Creates

Two create operations can silently retire a row the caller has never seen, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on a **parent** entity's `version`. In each case the version field is a required input, not an optional one, and a request that omits it fails request-shape validation.

A request to create a royalty statement must carry `expected_run_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `ExperienceRun` named by `run_id`. Because a statement create legitimately supersedes an earlier draft covering the same period, no state precondition would stop a second create from retiring a statement the caller never inspected; the version guard is what makes that case deterministic. If `expected_run_version` does not equal the run's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_RUN_VERSION_CONFLICT` and HTTP 412. Note that every accepted statement create itself advances the run's `version` (by incrementing `royalty_statement_count`), so a caller intending to supersede must re-read the run first.

A request to create a creative submission must carry `expected_agreement_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `LicenseAgreement` named by `license_agreement_id`. A new submission in an existing family legitimately supersedes the open row, so again nothing in the state machine blocks a caller holding an older view of the agreement. If `expected_agreement_version` does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_VERSION_CONFLICT` and HTTP 412. The agreement's `version` is advanced by every submission create, every submission decision and the agreement's own expiry, so all three of those events invalidate a client's cached view.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. In particular, none of the state-transition endpoints take an expected-version input; their protection comes from the state preconditions in §3, not from optimistic concurrency.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder: Which Error a Bad Request Earns

Most requests that fail could plausibly fail for several reasons at once, and the service must be predictable about which one it reports. Checks are grouped into rungs and evaluated in the ascending order listed below; **the first violated rung decides the response and evaluation stops there.** A request that violates both a segregation-of-duties rule and a lease-budget rule reports the segregation-of-duties error, because identity sits above money on the ladder.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` present and non-blank, and `X-User-Role` present and naming a declared role. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The role named by `X-User-Role` appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the request path resolves to a stored row, validated left to right. The unresolved identifier selects the code: `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KIT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
4. **Request shape.** Every required input field is present; every type matches; every enum value is a member of its declared set; every numeric value lies inside its declared range; dates are ten-character ISO calendar dates; instants are ISO 8601 UTC instants; array-valued inputs contain no duplicates and only declared members. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). Applies to every POST and PATCH endpoint.
5. **Body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, **and resolves within the scope its owning rule declares**. A `themed_room_id` naming a real room that belongs to a different run than the shift's own run counts as unresolved and earns `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND` exactly as if it named nothing at all. The codes available at this rung are `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_KIT_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404. This rung guards the creation of experience runs, themed rooms, timed-entry slots, ticket orders, creative submissions, staff members, staff shifts, safety inspections, build kits and royalty statements.
6. **Uniqueness.** A build kit's `kit_code` is unique across all kits (`ERR_DUPLICATE_KIT_CODE`); a guest account's `email` is unique across all accounts compared case-insensitively (`ERR_DUPLICATE_GUEST_EMAIL`); a themed room's `room_sequence` is unique among the rooms of the same run (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_SEQUENCE`). All three answer HTTP 409, and they guard build-kit creation, guest-account creation and themed-room creation respectively.
7. **Stale parent version.** The two optimistic-concurrency guards of §2.6: `ERR_RUN_VERSION_CONFLICT` on royalty-statement creation and `ERR_AGREEMENT_VERSION_CONFLICT` on creative-submission creation, both at HTTP 412.
8. **Own-state gate.** The resource named in the path is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Otherwise the code named for that entity — `ERR_VENUE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SLOT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_STAFF_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SHIFT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_KIT_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_STATEMENT_INVALID_STATE` — at HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint.
9. **Cross-entity state gate.** A related row is in the wrong state: the run's venue is not `LEASE_ACTIVE` when the run opens (`ERR_VENUE_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE`); the licence agreement is not `ACTIVE` when a run is created or a submission is created or resubmitted (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`); the run is not `CLEARED` when it opens or when an order is confirmed (`ERR_RUN_NOT_CLEARED`); the run is `SUSPENDED` when an admission window is released or a guest is checked in (`ERR_RUN_SUSPENDED`); the guest account is `RESTRICTED` when an order is created or confirmed for it (`ERR_GUEST_RESTRICTED`); the build kit is not `READY` when a run is created against it (`ERR_KIT_NOT_READY`); the staff member is not `CLEARED` when a shift is scheduled for them (`ERR_STAFF_NOT_CLEARED`); or a venue's lease is ended while a run at that venue is still `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `EXTENDED` or `CLOSING` (`ERR_VENUE_HAS_ACTIVE_RUN`). All answer HTTP 409. This rung guards run creation, run opening, submission creation and resubmission, window release, order creation, order confirmation, attendance recording, shift creation and lease ending.
10. **Identity and segregation of duties.** Evaluated *after* state, so that a request which is both mistimed and self-dealt reports the state problem first. The five codes are `ERR_SOD_SUSPENSION_LIFT_SAME_USER`, `ERR_SOD_APPROVAL_SAME_USER`, `ERR_SOD_EXTENSION_SAME_USER`, `ERR_LICENSOR_REP_MISMATCH` and `ERR_GUEST_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`, all at HTTP 403; §2.10 states the rules. This rung guards suspension lifting, submission review and decisions, extension granting, order creation and order cancellation.
11. **Interval and calendar bounds.** `lease_end_date` strictly after `lease_start_date`; `term_end_date` strictly after `term_start_date`; `scheduled_close_date` strictly after `scheduled_open_date`; `slot_end_utc` strictly after `slot_start_utc`; `shift_end_utc` strictly after `shift_start_utc`; `period_end_date` strictly after `period_start_date` — any empty or inverted interval earns `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). An admission window that does not lie wholly inside its run's admission span, or a run whose `scheduled_close_date` falls after its venue's `lease_end_date` at creation, earns `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_RUN_WINDOW` (422). A statement period not wholly inside its agreement's term earns `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422). This rung guards venue creation, agreement creation, run creation, slot creation, shift creation and statement creation.
12. **Clock-position gates.** `ERR_LEASE_START_NOT_REACHED` when a lease is activated before its start date; `ERR_TERM_NOT_ENDED` when an agreement is expired without the term having ended; `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED` when a check-in falls outside the admission window; `ERR_NO_SHOW_TOO_EARLY` when a no-show is recorded too soon; `ERR_CANCEL_WINDOW_CLOSED` when a confirmed order is cancelled too late; `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_ENDED` when a shift is completed before its end instant; `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` when a statement is finalised before its period has closed. All answer HTTP 409, and this rung guards lease activation, agreement expiry, attendance recording, order cancellation, shift status decisions and statement finalisation.
13. **Occupation geometry.** `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP` and `ERR_SHIFT_TOO_LONG` on shift creation, and `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP_CAPACITY` on slot creation and slot release, all at HTTP 409. §3.4 and §3.5 give the geometry.
14. **Accessibility and sensory accommodation.** `ERR_WHEELCHAIR_ROUTE_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_QUIET_ROUTE_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_ASL_NOT_STAFFED`, `ERR_WHEELCHAIR_POSITIONS_EXHAUSTED` and `ERR_SEVERITY_FINDINGS_MISMATCH`, all at HTTP 409. This rung guards order creation, order confirmation and inspection finalisation.
15. **Occupancy and capacity ceilings.** `ERR_OCCUPANCY_ABOVE_EGRESS`, `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_ABOVE_RUN`, `ERR_PARTY_SIZE_ABOVE_GROUP_MAX`, `ERR_BUYOUT_PARTY_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_RUN_HAS_NO_ROOMS` and `ERR_SLOT_HAS_SALES`, all at HTTP 409. This rung guards permit submission, slot creation, slot release, slot status decisions, order creation, order confirmation and themed-room creation.
16. **Loop caps and eligibility.** `ERR_PERMIT_ROUND_CAP`, `ERR_REVISION_ROUND_CAP`, `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_IN_AGREEMENT`, `ERR_CITY_NOT_IN_TERRITORY`, `ERR_STAFF_READINESS_BELOW_MIN`, `ERR_STAFF_INCIDENTS_ABOVE_MAX`, `ERR_KIT_CONDITION_BELOW_THRESHOLD` and `ERR_INSPECTION_PASS_MISSING`, all at HTTP 409. This rung guards permit submission, submission creation, submission resubmission, run creation, staff status decisions, kit localisation and suspension lifting.
17. **Money and lease budgets, checked last.** `ERR_BUILDOUT_CAP_EXCEEDED` on a buildout draw; `ERR_DEMAND_BELOW_THRESHOLD` and `ERR_LEASE_EXTENSION_EXHAUSTED` on an extension request, with the demand floor evaluated **before** the lease budget so that an under-performing run that also lacks lease days reports the demand failure; and `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_ISSUED` on a statement create that would supersede a period already closed out. All answer HTTP 409.

### 2.8. Path Parameters, Filters and Reverse Reachability

No collection in this API is nested inside another collection's URL; every collection sits at the top level and relationships are expressed by identifier fields in request bodies and by query filters on list endpoints. Where a path does carry an identifier, it is named for the entity it addresses: `venueId`, `agreementId`, `submissionId`, `runId`, `roomId`, `slotId`, `guestId`, `orderId`, `staffId`, `shiftId`, `inspectionId`, `kitId` and `statementId`. Path identifiers are resolved left to right and the first one that resolves to nothing decides the 404 code, before any body field is examined.

Because relationships are not walkable through the URL hierarchy, the list endpoints carry the filters that make the graph traversable in reverse. Venues filter on `city_code` and `status`. Licence agreements filter on `status` and `city_code`, the latter matching against the agreement's licensed territory. Creative submissions filter on `license_agreement_id`, `run_id`, `scope`, `status` and `superseded_by_submission_id`. Experience runs filter on `venue_id`, `license_agreement_id`, `build_kit_id`, `suspending_inspection_id`, `run_status` and `clearance_status` — the two status dimensions are independent filters. Themed rooms filter on `run_id`. Timed-entry slots filter on `run_id`, `buyout_order_id` and `status`. Ticket orders filter on `run_id`, `slot_id`, `guest_account_id` and `status`. Staff members filter on `run_id`, `staff_role` and `status`. Staff shifts filter on `staff_member_id`, `run_id`, `themed_room_id` and `status`. Safety inspections filter on `run_id`, `inspection_type` and `status`. Build kits filter on `origin_run_id`, `status` and `localization_status`. Royalty statements filter on `run_id`, `license_agreement_id`, `superseded_by_statement_id` and `status`. All of these are combinable with `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate rows the caller did not name. Those cascades are immediate and complete: by the time a response is returned, every row the cascade touches has its new state, its refreshed `updated_at` and its incremented `version` already persisted, and there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in the service. A cascade never partially applies — if the triggering request is rejected at any rung of §2.7, none of the cascade's effects occur.

Cascades in this specification are described as numbered hops, because several of them are chains where each hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the original request. Closing a run, for example, first closes the run, which then decays the deployed build kit, which then selects the kit's next lifecycle state from the decayed score, which then — on one branch only — resets the kit's localisation posture. Where a hop is conditional, the condition is stated with the hop. Where two hops write the same field, the later hop wins. Two derived states in this system, a run's `clearance_status` and an admission window's sold-out standing, are **re-derived** rather than assigned: any change to the rows they depend on causes the service to recompute the gate and move the state if the answer changed, in the same request.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Ownership

Three named segregation rules protect decisions where reviewer and reviewed must be different people, and two ownership rules bind an actor to the rows it may touch. All five sit on rung 10 of §2.7 and all five answer HTTP 403.

The first rule concerns safety. The officer whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `finalized_by_user_id` on the inspection that suspended a run may not be the officer who lifts that suspension; an attempt is refused with `ERR_SOD_SUSPENSION_LIFT_SAME_USER`. The second concerns creative approval: the `X-User-ID` recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` on a creative submission may not be the `X-User-ID` that issues that submission's approval or rejection, and a self-decision earns `ERR_SOD_APPROVAL_SAME_USER`. The third concerns commercial extension: the `X-User-ID` recorded as `created_by_user_id` on a run may not be the `X-User-ID` that grants that run's extension, and a self-grant earns `ERR_SOD_EXTENSION_SAME_USER`.

Beyond those three, a `LicensorRep` may act only on submissions whose governing agreement names that caller's `X-User-ID` as its `licensor_rep_user_id`; any other representative attempting to start a review or issue a decision is refused with `ERR_LICENSOR_REP_MISMATCH`. And a `Guest` may act only on its own orders, as described in §2.1, under `ERR_GUEST_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Securing a Site: Lease Term, Permit Rounds and Buildout Funding

Everything begins with a **PopupVenue** — a shell the operator has negotiated for but cannot yet legally fill with a crowd. A `VenueOpsManager` registers one by supplying the three-letter uppercase `city_code` the shell sits in, a human-readable `site_name` of between 1 and 120 characters, and the base lease term as `lease_start_date` and `lease_end_date`. The landlord's flexibility is captured as `lease_extension_option_days`, the total number of extra days the option grants beyond the base term; it defaults to 0 when omitted and may not exceed 180. Commercial terms follow: `base_monthly_rent_cents`, the landlord's `buildout_allowance_cents`, an optional `kit_reuse_credit_cents` earned by bringing a touring kit into the space (0 when omitted), and `buildout_cap_cents`, a hard ceiling on cumulative buildout spend that may never be registered below the landlord allowance itself. Finally the physical application: `requested_occupancy_load`, the assembly occupancy the operator is applying for, which must be at least 1; `egress_width_cm`, the total clear egress width in centimetres carried to two decimal places, which must be greater than zero; and `sprinklered`, a boolean that defaults to false and is recorded for the permit file only — no calculation in this system reads it.

At registration the service derives `egress_capacity` from the geometry alone: building code allows one occupant for every 2.03 centimetres of clear egress width, and the venue's `egress_capacity` is the clear width divided by that figure, floored to a whole occupant. This is the code-derived ceiling and it is independent of what the authority later grants. A new venue starts in `DRAFT` with `permitted_occupancy_load` at 0, `permit_submission_round` at 0, `permit_conditions_note`, `permit_denial_reason`, `permit_issued_at` and `lease_activated_at` all null, and all five cumulative counters — `allowance_drawn_cents`, `kit_credit_drawn_cents`, `operator_capex_cents`, `buildout_spend_cents` and `lease_extension_days_consumed` — at 0.

The permit conversation is a loop, not a single exchange. A `VenueOpsManager` files the assembly-occupancy package, optionally attaching a `package_note`, which moves the venue from `DRAFT` to `PERMIT_UNDER_REVIEW`, increments `permit_submission_round` by 1 and clears any `permit_conditions_note` left over from an earlier round. Filing is refused with `ERR_OCCUPANCY_ABOVE_EGRESS` when the `requested_occupancy_load` on the venue exceeds its own `egress_capacity` — there is no point asking an authority for more people than the doors can discharge — and refused with `ERR_PERMIT_ROUND_CAP` once `permit_submission_round` has already reached three, which is the number of packages this system will let a venue file before the operator must renegotiate rather than resubmit.

A `SafetyOfficer` records the authority's answer as a `decision` of `ISSUED`, `CONDITIONS` or `DENIED`, with an optional `note`. On `CONDITIONS` the venue moves to `PERMIT_CONDITIONS`, the authority's correction text is stored as `permit_conditions_note`, and `permitted_occupancy_load` stays at 0 — a conditioned package grants nothing yet. The `note` is mandatory on this branch and must be between 1 and 500 characters. A venue in `PERMIT_CONDITIONS` may be filed again, returning it to `PERMIT_UNDER_REVIEW` with the round counter incremented and the conditions note cleared; this is the `PERMIT_UNDER_REVIEW → PERMIT_CONDITIONS → PERMIT_UNDER_REVIEW` cycle, and the round cap is what terminates it. On `ISSUED` the venue moves to `PERMIT_ISSUED`, `permit_issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock, any conditions note is cleared, and `permitted_occupancy_load` becomes the smaller of `requested_occupancy_load` and `egress_capacity` — the authority never grants more than the geometry supports even when the application asked for more. On `DENIED` the venue moves to the terminal `PERMIT_DENIED`, the mandatory 1-to-500-character `note` is stored as `permit_denial_reason`, and `permitted_occupancy_load` is set to 0.

Once the permit is issued, the lease can be switched on, but not before the calendar says so: activation is admissible only when the logical clock's calendar date is on or after `lease_start_date`, and an early attempt is refused with `ERR_LEASE_START_NOT_REACHED`. Activation moves `PERMIT_ISSUED → LEASE_ACTIVE` and stamps `lease_activated_at`. At the other end of the term, a `VenueOpsManager` ends the lease, moving `LEASE_ACTIVE → LEASE_ENDED`, but only when the site is genuinely empty: if any run at that venue still has an operational status of `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `EXTENDED` or `CLOSING`, the request is refused with `ERR_VENUE_HAS_ACTIVE_RUN`. `PERMIT_DENIED` and `LEASE_ENDED` are terminal; a venue in either state accepts no further venue-level action, and a buildout draw against such a venue is refused with `ERR_VENUE_INVALID_STATE`.

Buildout money is drawn incrementally and allocated through three buckets in a fixed order that never varies with the size of the draw. A `VenueOpsManager` requests a draw by supplying `draw_amount_cents` and an optional `memo`. Before any bucket is touched, the service checks the ceiling: if the venue's existing `buildout_spend_cents` plus the requested `draw_amount_cents` would exceed `buildout_cap_cents`, the draw is refused with `ERR_BUILDOUT_CAP_EXCEEDED` and all four counters are left exactly as they were. Otherwise the amount flows downhill. Bucket one is the landlord allowance: the service takes the smaller of the amount still to be allocated and the allowance headroom, which is `buildout_allowance_cents` less `allowance_drawn_cents`, adds it to `allowance_drawn_cents`, and reduces the amount still to be allocated by that much. Bucket two is the kit reuse credit, handled identically against the headroom `kit_reuse_credit_cents` less `kit_credit_drawn_cents`, adding to `kit_credit_drawn_cents`. Bucket three is the operator's own capital, which is uncapped and absorbs whatever remains: the remainder is added to `operator_capex_cents`. Finally the whole `draw_amount_cents` is added to `buildout_spend_cents`. The arithmetic is exact integer cents with no rounding anywhere, and after every accepted draw the conserved identity of §2.5 holds: spend equals allowance drawn plus credit drawn plus operator capital.

### 3.2. Licensing the Property: Approval Rights, Revision Rounds and Supersession

A **LicenseAgreement** records what the operator is allowed to stage and what the licensor may veto. A `LicensingManager` registers one with the `licensor_name` and the `ip_title` of the licensed property, the `licensor_rep_user_id` of the licensor's representative of record (an opaque operator identifier of 1 to 64 characters, never compared against any entity's `id`), the ticket royalty rate `royalty_rate_bps` and merchandise royalty rate `merch_royalty_rate_bps` in basis points from 0 to 10000, the whole-term `minimum_guarantee_cents`, the `approval_scopes` the licensor holds veto rights over, the `territory_city_codes` licensed for the property, and the licence term as `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`. `approval_scopes` is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `CREATIVE_EXECUTION`, `MERCHANDISE` and `MARKETING`; `territory_city_codes` is a non-empty array of three-character uppercase city codes. A fresh agreement sits in `DRAFT` with `open_submission_count` and `approved_submission_count` at 0.

Activation moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is admissible only while the logical clock's calendar date is on or before `term_end_date`; a licence whose term has already elapsed can never be activated. Only an `ACTIVE` agreement may be named by a new run or by a new creative submission. Expiry moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and is the mirror image: it is admissible only once the logical clock's calendar date is strictly after `term_end_date`, and an early attempt earns `ERR_TERM_NOT_ENDED`. Expiry is one of the larger cascades in the system. Every creative submission on the agreement still in `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is moved to `REJECTED`, with `decision_note` set to the literal string `AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`, `decided_at` stamped from the clock and `decided_by_user_id` set to the expiring caller's `X-User-ID`; each of those rows takes a version increment. `open_submission_count` is then set to 0. Because those rejections strip approval coverage from the runs that depended on them, every run under the agreement whose compliance standing was `CLEARED` drops to `UNCLEARED` as a second hop, each such run taking its own version increment. `EXPIRED` is terminal.

A **CreativeSubmission** is the unit of licensor approval. An `ExperienceProducer` files one against a `license_agreement_id` and a `run_id`, declaring the `scope` the asset satisfies, an operator-assigned `asset_code` of 1 to 40 characters using only uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, a descriptive `title` of 1 to 160 characters, and the `expected_agreement_version` guard of §2.6. The named agreement must be `ACTIVE`, or the create is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the named run must be one staged under that same agreement — a `run_id` naming a real run whose own `license_agreement_id` is some other agreement counts as unresolved at rung 5 of §2.7 and earns `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, decided before the version guard and before the agreement's own state is examined; and the declared `scope` must appear in the agreement's `approval_scopes`, or the create is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_IN_AGREEMENT`. A new row lands in `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at 0, `submitted_by_user_id` copied from the filing `X-User-ID`, `superseded_by_submission_id` null, and `change_note`, `decision_note`, `review_started_at`, `resubmitted_at`, `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` all null.

The triple of `run_id`, `scope` and `asset_code` identifies a **supersede family**. When a new submission joins a family that already holds a row in `SUBMITTED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, that older row is moved to `SUPERSEDED` and its `superseded_by_submission_id` is set to the new row's `id`. Supersession preserves the older row's history rather than erasing it — a row superseded out of `IN_REVIEW` keeps its `review_started_at` exactly as it was. The agreement's `open_submission_count` rises by 1 only when nothing was superseded; when one row closed as another opened, the count is unchanged. Either way the agreement takes a version increment, which is precisely why a client wanting to supersede twice must re-read the agreement between attempts. After the create, the run's clearance gate is re-derived, and a run that was `CLEARED` drops `CLEARED → UNCLEARED` if the gate no longer holds.

Review is the licensor's move and belongs to the representative of record. When the `LicensorRep` whose `X-User-ID` matches the agreement's `licensor_rep_user_id` takes a submitted asset into review, the row advances `SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW` and `review_started_at` is stamped from the clock; the agreement must still be `ACTIVE` at that moment. Any other representative is refused with `ERR_LICENSOR_REP_MISMATCH`. From `IN_REVIEW` the representative records a `decision` of `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` with an optional `note`, and the three branches diverge sharply. On `CHANGES_REQUESTED` the row moves to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, the mandatory 1-to-500-character `note` is stored as `change_note`, and both agreement counters are left untouched — nothing has closed. On `APPROVED` the row moves to the terminal `APPROVED`, `decided_at` is stamped, `decided_by_user_id` records the deciding `X-User-ID`, `decision_note` takes the supplied `note`, the agreement's `open_submission_count` falls by 1 while its `approved_submission_count` rises by 1, and the run's clearance gate is re-derived — this is one of the two calls that can complete the gate and drive a run `UNCLEARED → CLEARED`. On `REJECTED` the row moves to the terminal `REJECTED` with a mandatory 1-to-500-character `note` stored as `decision_note`, `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` recorded the same way, the agreement's `open_submission_count` falls by 1 while `approved_submission_count` is untouched, and a run that was `CLEARED` drops to `UNCLEARED` because the approval gate has lost this scope. Both terminal decisions are barred to the filer: if the deciding `X-User-ID` equals the row's `submitted_by_user_id`, the decision is refused with `ERR_SOD_APPROVAL_SAME_USER`.

A submission sent back for changes can go round again. The producer resubmits it, moving `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, incrementing `revision_round` by 1, stamping `resubmitted_at` and clearing `change_note`. Resubmission requires the agreement to still be `ACTIVE` and requires `revision_round` to be below three; once three revision rounds have been consumed the negotiation is over and further attempts earn `ERR_REVISION_ROUND_CAP`. The full cycle is therefore `SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, bounded by that cap, with `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` as the three terminal exits.

### 3.3. Designing a Run: Rooms, the Capacity Bottleneck and Two Independent Standings

An **ExperienceRun** is one installation of one licensed property into one venue for one span of dates. An `ExperienceProducer` creates it by naming the `venue_id`, the `license_agreement_id` and the `build_kit_id` it deploys, plus a `run_name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `scheduled_open_date` and `scheduled_close_date`, the `base_ticket_price_cents` list price from 1 to 100000, the timed-entry cadence `timed_slot_minutes` from 15 to 120, and optionally `max_group_size`, which defaults to 12 and may run from 1 to 60. Creation is fenced by four cross-entity gates. The venue must be in `PERMIT_ISSUED` or `LEASE_ACTIVE` — a run may be planned as soon as the permit exists, before the lease is switched on. The agreement must be `ACTIVE`, or the create is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the venue's `city_code` must appear in that agreement's `territory_city_codes`, or the create is refused with `ERR_CITY_NOT_IN_TERRITORY`. The kit must be `READY`, or the create is refused with `ERR_KIT_NOT_READY`; a `READY` kit is additionally refused with `ERR_KIT_CONDITION_BELOW_THRESHOLD` when its `condition_score` has fallen below 60.00 or its `localization_status` is not `LOCALIZED`, because a worn or unlocalised kit cannot be deployed even when its status says it is on the shelf. The calendar must line up too: `scheduled_open_date` must be on or after the venue's `lease_start_date`, and `scheduled_close_date` must be strictly after `scheduled_open_date` — an inverted or empty run span earns `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` — and on or before the venue's `lease_end_date` at creation time, a run that would outlive its lease earning `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_RUN_WINDOW`. On success `created_by_user_id` is copied from the creating `X-User-ID` — the identity that segregation of duties will later bar from granting this run's extension — and the deployed kit moves `READY → DEPLOYED`, taking its own version increment.

A new run starts with `effective_capacity` and `effective_capacity_cap` at 0, `royalty_statement_count` and `extension_days_granted` at 0, `demand_index` at 0.00, and `demand_index_at_extension`, `opened_at`, `closing_started_at`, `closed_at`, `suspended_at` and `suspending_inspection_id` all null.

**ThemedRoom** rows describe the guest flow, and they are immutable once written: there is no way to edit or remove a room, so a mistake means a new run. A producer adds a room to a run that is still `PLANNED`, giving its 1-based `room_sequence` from 1 to 12 — unique among the rooms of that run, with a collision refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_SEQUENCE` — a `name` of 1 to 80 characters, the instantaneous `room_occupancy_cap` from 1 to 400, the designed `target_dwell_minutes` from 1 to 120, the `min_dwell_minutes` the scripted content permits which must be at least 1 and no greater than `target_dwell_minutes`, and the `sensory_intensity_level` from 1 (calm) to 5 (extreme). Two flags carry the accessibility promises: `has_quiet_bypass`, false when omitted, records whether a route around the room exists, and `wheelchair_accessible`, true when omitted, records whether the room can be traversed in a chair. Rooms are the one resource in this system with no `version` and no `status`, so every room of a run counts towards capacity from the moment it is written.

Each room's `throughput_per_slot` is how many guests it can pass in one timed slot: its `room_occupancy_cap` multiplied by the run's `timed_slot_minutes`, divided by its own `target_dwell_minutes`, floored to a whole guest. Adding a room therefore recomputes the run's `effective_capacity`, and that figure is the tightest of four heterogeneous ceilings: the venue's `permitted_occupancy_load` as granted by the authority; the code-derived egress ceiling, being the venue's `egress_width_cm` divided by 2.03 centimetres per occupant and floored; the smallest `throughput_per_slot` across every room of the run, which is the bottleneck room; and, when `effective_capacity_cap` is greater than 0, that cap as well. A cap of 0 means no inspection ceiling is in force and the first three terms alone decide. Every division floors rather than rounds, so the admitted headcount can never be overstated. A run with no rooms at all is not a run of capacity zero — it is a design error, and a request that would need to compute capacity for a roomless run is refused with `ERR_RUN_HAS_NO_ROOMS`.

A run carries **two standings at once**, and they move for entirely different reasons. `run_status` is the operational dimension — `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `EXTENDED`, `CLOSING`, terminating at `CLOSED` — and only deliberate producer actions advance it. `clearance_status` is the compliance dimension — `UNCLEARED`, `CLEARED`, `SUSPENDED` — and it is never set directly by an operator command; it is derived from creative approvals and staffing, or forced by a failed safety inspection, and it has no terminal state. A run can be commercially `OPEN` while compliance-wise `SUSPENDED`, and a run can be `CLEARED` while still `PLANNED`.

The clearance gate is a conjunction over two other entity families, re-derived on every change to either. It holds when every scope listed in the run's agreement's `approval_scopes` has at least one creative submission for this run in that scope with status `APPROVED`, **and** the run has at least one staff member with status `CLEARED` in each of the three crafts `PERFORMER`, `AMBASSADOR` and `TECHNICIAN`. Whichever call completes the gate — the approval of the last outstanding scope, or the clearing of the last missing craft — is the call that moves the run `UNCLEARED → CLEARED`; whichever call breaks it moves the run `CLEARED → UNCLEARED`. Neither transition has a dedicated command of its own.

Opening a run is the producer's move and demands that everything line up at once: the run must be `PLANNED`; its venue must by then be `LEASE_ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_VENUE_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE`; its `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RUN_NOT_CLEARED`; its `effective_capacity` must be greater than 0; and at least one timed entry slot of the run must already be `OPEN`, because a run with no released window cannot admit anybody. Opening stamps `opened_at` from the logical clock and moves `PLANNED → OPEN`.

Alongside these actions the system exposes a read-only demand telemetry view of a run: it returns the demand figure the extension calculation would produce at this instant together with every input feeding it. That view persists nothing, writes no field and never advances the run's `version`.

### 3.4. Rostering the Cast: Readiness, Two Score Variants and Shift Geometry

A **StaffMember** is a person staffed to one run. A producer enrols one against a `run_id` whose run is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `EXTENDED`, with a `full_name` of 1 to 80 characters, the `staff_role` craft of `PERFORMER`, `AMBASSADOR` or `TECHNICIAN`, the `certifications` the person already holds — a duplicate-free subset of `SCRIPTED_INTERACTION`, `SENSORY_DEESCALATION`, `ADA_ASSIST`, `EVACUATION_MARSHAL`, `RIGGING` and `ELECTRICAL`, empty when omitted — and an optional opening `incident_count` from 0 to 20, which is 0 when omitted. A new enrolment lands in `CANDIDATE` with `training_score`, `completed_rehearsal_count`, `scheduled_rehearsal_count`, `cert_coverage_pct`, `readiness_score_preflight` and `readiness_score_audit` all at zero.

Training is recorded incrementally. A producer posts a training record carrying the `training_score` just achieved from 0 to 100, optionally the `completed_rehearsal_count` and `scheduled_rehearsal_count` from the latest rehearsal block, and optionally an `incident_delta` charging further on-floor incidents to the person. The stored `training_score` becomes the greater of the score already held and the score just submitted, so a bad day can never erase a good one; the two rehearsal counters accumulate by addition, adding the submitted figures to the running totals; and `incident_count` rises by `incident_delta`, remaining bounded at 20. Every record recomputes `cert_coverage_pct` and both readiness variants, and moves the person `CANDIDATE → TRAINING`, or `TRAINING → TRAINING` on each further block. Records are admissible while the person is `CANDIDATE` or `TRAINING` and the run is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `EXTENDED`.

Readiness is a weighted score out of 100. Certification coverage is the share of the craft's required certifications the person actually holds: `PERFORMER` requires `SCRIPTED_INTERACTION` and `SENSORY_DEESCALATION`; `AMBASSADOR` requires `SCRIPTED_INTERACTION`, `ADA_ASSIST` and `EVACUATION_MARSHAL`; `TECHNICIAN` requires `RIGGING` and `ELECTRICAL`. `cert_coverage_pct` is 100 multiplied by the number of required certifications the person holds, divided by the number required for that craft, rounded half up to two decimals — certifications outside the craft's required set add nothing. Rehearsal completion is 100 multiplied by the counted rehearsals divided by the rehearsal target of 4, rounded half up to two decimals, then capped at 100.00 so extra rehearsals cannot push the term above full marks. The raw score is 0.40 times `training_score`, plus 0.35 times `cert_coverage_pct`, plus 0.25 times the rehearsal percentage, minus 5.00 for each recorded incident. Those four terms are summed at full precision and the total is rounded half up to two decimals exactly **once** at the end — rounding each term first is forbidden — and the result is clamped into 0.00 to 100.00.

The counted-rehearsals term is where the two variants part company. `readiness_score_preflight` counts `completed_rehearsal_count` plus `scheduled_rehearsal_count`, treating a booked but unworked rehearsal as if it were already done; this is the optimistic roster view a producer plans against. `readiness_score_audit` counts `completed_rehearsal_count` only. Whenever `scheduled_rehearsal_count` is above zero the audit figure is strictly the lower of the two, and a person can therefore look clearable on the roster view while the gate still refuses — because the audit variant is the **only** variant the clearing gate ever reads.

A staffing decision names a `decision` of `CLEAR`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `RELEASE` with an optional `note`. `CLEAR` moves a person `TRAINING → CLEARED` and is doubly gated: `readiness_score_audit` must be at least 78.00, or the decision is refused with `ERR_STAFF_READINESS_BELOW_MIN`, and `incident_count` must be no more than 2, or it is refused with `ERR_STAFF_INCIDENTS_ABOVE_MAX`. Clearing then re-derives the run's clearance gate and may complete it. `SUSPEND` moves a cleared person `CLEARED → SUSPENDED`, cancels every one of that person's `SCHEDULED` shifts — each cancelled shift taking its own version increment — and re-derives the run's gate, dropping the run to `UNCLEARED` when that craft no longer has a cleared member. `REINSTATE` moves `SUSPENDED → CLEARED` under exactly the same two readiness gates as `CLEAR`, which is why incidents charged during a suspension can keep the gate shut; the `CLEARED → SUSPENDED → CLEARED` cycle is otherwise unbounded. `RELEASE` moves a person to the terminal `RELEASED` from any of `CANDIDATE`, `TRAINING`, `CLEARED` or `SUSPENDED`, cancelling that person's `SCHEDULED` shifts and re-deriving the run's gate as well.

A **StaffShift** books one cleared person onto one closed time interval. Only a `CLEARED` staff member may be scheduled — anyone else earns `ERR_STAFF_NOT_CLEARED` — and the shift's `run_id` is copied from that person's own `run_id` rather than supplied by the client. The producer gives `shift_start_utc` and `shift_end_utc`, an optional `themed_room_id` for a shift stationed in a specific room, and an optional `station` of `FLOOR`, `BOOTH`, `RIGGING` or `ENTRY`, defaulting to `FLOOR`; `BOOTH` and `ENTRY` shifts normally carry no room. A `themed_room_id` naming a room that belongs to a different run is treated exactly like an identifier that names nothing at all and is refused with `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`.

Shift geometry is deliberately unforgiving. The new shift occupies the **closed** interval from `shift_start_utc` to `shift_end_utc`, so `shift_end_utc` must be strictly after `shift_start_utc` and adjacency is not tolerated. Each of that same person's existing `SCHEDULED` shifts is widened by a rest buffer of 30 minutes at both ends, and the widened interval is also closed at both ends. If the new interval touches or overlaps any widened interval — that is, if the new start is at or before the widened end while the widened start is at or before the new end — the request is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. Touching endpoints collide, and a gap of exactly 30 minutes still collides because the buffered endpoint is inclusive; the first admissible gap is 31 minutes. Existing shifts are examined in ascending `created_at` order and the first collision found decides the rejection. `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` shifts never collide with anything. Only after the collision sweep passes is the length checked: a shift longer than 600 minutes is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_TOO_LONG`.

A shift decision names a `decision` of `COMPLETE` or `CANCEL` with an optional `note`. `COMPLETE` moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and is admissible only once the logical clock has reached `shift_end_utc`; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_ENDED`. `CANCEL` moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. Both are terminal, and a shift may also be cancelled as a side effect of its own staff member being suspended or released.

### 3.5. Putting Windows on Sale: Slot Geometry, Dynamic Pricing and Buyout Holds

A **TimedEntrySlot** is one admission window on one run. A producer creates it against a `run_id` whose run is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `EXTENDED`, giving the window as `slot_start_utc` and `slot_end_utc`, the seats designed into it as `base_capacity`, optionally the `pricing_tier` of `OFFPEAK`, `STANDARD`, `PEAK` or `PREMIUM` (defaulting to `STANDARD`), and optionally `wheelchair_positions`, which defaults to 4 and may not exceed `base_capacity`. The window is **half-open**: `slot_start_utc` is inclusive, `slot_end_utc` is exclusive, and a following window may legitimately start at exactly the instant an earlier one ends. `slot_end_utc` must be strictly after `slot_start_utc`, and the whole window must lie inside the run's scheduled dates — on or after midnight at the start of `scheduled_open_date` and on or before 23:59:59.999 of `scheduled_close_date` — with a violation refused as `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_RUN_WINDOW`. `base_capacity` must be at least 1 and may not exceed the run's `effective_capacity`, nor a non-zero `effective_capacity_cap`; a violation is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_ABOVE_RUN`.

Beyond the single-window ceiling there is an aggregate one, because two windows that overlap in time put their guests inside the same building at the same time. The peers considered are the other slots of the same run with status `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `SOLD_OUT`, `HELD_FOR_BUYOUT` or `SUSPENDED`, examined in ascending `slot_start_utc` order and then ascending `created_at`; `CANCELLED` and `CLOSED_OUT` peers are excluded entirely. A peer overlaps the new window when the new start is strictly before the peer's end **and** the peer's start is strictly before the new end — strict on both sides, so adjacency does not overlap. The admission load is the new `base_capacity` plus the sum of `base_capacity` over every overlapping peer, and when that load exceeds the run's `effective_capacity` the request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP_CAPACITY`. Two overlapping windows are perfectly legal as long as their combined seats fit. A new slot lands in `DRAFT` with `released_capacity`, `held_capacity`, `sold_capacity`, `available_capacity` and `wheelchair_sold` all at 0, `buyout_order_id` and `released_at` null.

Releasing a slot moves it `DRAFT → OPEN`, sets both `released_capacity` and `available_capacity` to `base_capacity` and stamps `released_at` from the clock. Release re-checks everything, because the world has moved since creation: the run must still be `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `EXTENDED`, the run's `clearance_status` must not be `SUSPENDED` — a suspended run may not put seats on sale, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RUN_SUSPENDED` — `base_capacity` must still fit `effective_capacity` and any non-zero `effective_capacity_cap`, and the overlap admission load is evaluated a second time because peers may have been added in the meantime. After release the conserved slot identity of §2.5 holds: `released_capacity` always equals `held_capacity` plus `sold_capacity` plus `available_capacity`.

The per-guest list price of a window is not stored on the slot; it is computed from the clock position at the moment an order is placed, and only then frozen. Lead time is measured from the current logical clock instant to `slot_start_utc` in seconds and floored to whole days. Booking 30 or more days ahead earns the early multiplier 0.90; from 7 up to 29 days the multiplier is 1.00; closer in than 7 days the late multiplier is 1.15. The tier multiplier is 0.80 for `OFFPEAK`, 1.00 for `STANDARD`, 1.30 for `PEAK` and 1.60 for `PREMIUM`. The evaluation order is pinned: the run's `base_ticket_price_cents` is multiplied by the tier multiplier and the lead multiplier in one single product, and that product is rounded half up to whole cents exactly **once**. Rounding after the tier multiplier and again after the lead multiplier is forbidden. The rounded figure is then clamped into the range 1000 to 100000 cents inclusive, and becomes the order's `unit_price_cents`. Because it is frozen at creation, moving the clock afterwards can never reprice an existing order.

The same figure is available without committing to a purchase. A read-only quote on a slot returns `slot_id`, the slot's `pricing_tier`, the computed `lead_days`, the `unit_price_cents`, and the full money breakdown a hypothetical order would produce — `per_guest_after_loyalty_cents`, `gross_amount_cents`, `group_discount_cents`, `companion_waiver_cents`, `discount_cents` and `net_amount_cents` — for the hypothetical shape described by the optional `order_type`, `loyalty_tier`, `party_size` and `wheelchair_party_count`. The quote persists nothing and advances no version.

A **private buyout** takes a whole window out of general sale in two steps that are exact mirror images. When a `PRIVATE_BUYOUT` order is created against an `OPEN` slot that has no sales yet — `sold_capacity` at 0 and `buyout_order_id` null — and whose `base_capacity` exactly equals the order's `party_size`, the slot moves `OPEN → HELD_FOR_BUYOUT`, its `held_capacity` becomes the whole `released_capacity`, its `available_capacity` becomes 0, and `buyout_order_id` records the holding order. Confirming that holding order moves the slot `HELD_FOR_BUYOUT → SOLD_OUT`, moving the whole quantity from `held_capacity` to `sold_capacity`. Cancelling it instead moves the slot `HELD_FOR_BUYOUT → OPEN`, returns `held_capacity` to 0, restores `available_capacity` to `released_capacity` less `sold_capacity`, and clears `buyout_order_id`. The conserved slot identity holds before and after every one of those hops.

Ordinary sales drive the capacity cycle. A confirmation that drives `available_capacity` to 0 moves the slot `OPEN → SOLD_OUT`; a cancellation that returns a seat moves it `SOLD_OUT → OPEN`; `sold_capacity` is the loop variable and the cycle is unbounded.

A producer or safety officer may also move a slot administratively with a `decision` of `SUSPEND`, `RESUME`, `CANCEL` or `CLOSE_OUT` plus an optional `note`. `SUSPEND` moves an `OPEN` or `SOLD_OUT` slot to `SUSPENDED`, preserving `released_capacity`, `held_capacity` and `sold_capacity` untouched — the seats are still sold, they are simply not admissible. `RESUME` moves `SUSPENDED → OPEN`, but only when the run itself is no longer `SUSPENDED` and the slot's `base_capacity` still fits `effective_capacity` and any non-zero `effective_capacity_cap`; if `available_capacity` is 0 at that moment the slot lands in `SOLD_OUT` instead. `CANCEL` moves a `DRAFT` or `OPEN` slot to the terminal `CANCELLED`, zeroing `released_capacity` and `available_capacity`, and is refused with `ERR_SLOT_HAS_SALES` when either `sold_capacity` or `held_capacity` is above 0 — a window carrying money cannot simply be deleted. `CLOSE_OUT` moves an `OPEN`, `SOLD_OUT` or `SUSPENDED` slot to the terminal `CLOSED_OUT`, zeroing `available_capacity` and, where a hold existed, `held_capacity`; sold seats are preserved so the run's takings survive the close-out.

### 3.6. Selling and Serving Guests: Order Money, Attendance and the Refund Ladder

A **GuestAccount** is opened by a `GuestServicesAgent` with an `email` of 5 to 160 characters containing exactly one `@` and unique across all accounts compared case-insensitively — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GUEST_EMAIL` — a `display_name` of 1 to 80 characters, an optional `loyalty_tier` of `VISITOR`, `MEMBER` or `PATRON` defaulting to `VISITOR`, and an optional `accommodation_profile`, a duplicate-free subset of `QUIET_ROUTE`, `WHEELCHAIR`, `ASL_INTERPRETER` and `LOW_STIM_HOURS` that is empty when omitted. The profile is informational only: it is the per-order `sensory_accommodations` array that is actually validated against the run. The account's `id` is the value a `Guest` presents as its `X-User-ID`. A new account is `ACTIVE` with `no_show_strikes`, `visit_count` and `experience_credit_cents` at 0. An agent may later replace any of `loyalty_tier`, `display_name` and `accommodation_profile`; only the supplied fields change, omitted fields keep their stored values, and orders already priced are never repriced by a tier change.

A **TicketOrder** is placed by a `Guest` for its own account, or by a `GuestServicesAgent` for anyone, naming the `slot_id`, the `guest_account_id`, the `party_size`, optionally the `order_type` of `SINGLE`, `GROUP` or `PRIVATE_BUYOUT` (defaulting to `SINGLE`), optionally `wheelchair_party_count` from 0 to `party_size` (0 when omitted), optionally the `sensory_accommodations` requested for this visit, and optionally `merch_amount_cents` from 0 to 500000 (0 when omitted). The order's `run_id` is copied from the slot rather than supplied. The guest account must be `ACTIVE`, or the create is refused with `ERR_GUEST_RESTRICTED`. Party size is bounded by shape: for `SINGLE` and `GROUP` it may not exceed the run's `max_group_size`, a `SINGLE` order may additionally carry no more than 4 guests, both refused with `ERR_PARTY_SIZE_ABOVE_GROUP_MAX`; a `PRIVATE_BUYOUT` must name a `party_size` exactly equal to the slot's `base_capacity`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_BUYOUT_PARTY_MISMATCH`. The hold must fit the window: a party larger than the slot's `available_capacity` is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Accommodation requests are promises about the physical run, so they are checked against its rooms and roster. A `WHEELCHAIR` request requires **every** themed room of the run to be `wheelchair_accessible`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WHEELCHAIR_ROUTE_UNAVAILABLE`. A `QUIET_ROUTE` request requires every room whose `sensory_intensity_level` is 4 or higher to have `has_quiet_bypass` true, or it is refused with `ERR_QUIET_ROUTE_UNAVAILABLE`; rooms rated 1, 2 or 3 need no bypass. An `ASL_INTERPRETER` request requires at least one staff member of the run with status `CLEARED` and `staff_role` of `AMBASSADOR`, or it is refused with `ERR_ASL_NOT_STAFFED`. `LOW_STIM_HOURS` carries no structural requirement. Independently, the slot's `wheelchair_sold` plus this order's `wheelchair_party_count` may not exceed the slot's `wheelchair_positions`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WHEELCHAIR_POSITIONS_EXHAUSTED`. All four checks are applied both when the order is created and again when it is confirmed, because rooms, roster and neighbouring sales can all change in between.

Creation prices the order once and for all. `unit_price_cents` is frozen from the dynamic price of the window, and the breakdown follows. On the `PRIVATE_BUYOUT` branch loyalty never applies: `per_guest_after_loyalty_cents` is the unit price unchanged, `gross_amount_cents` is the unit price multiplied by `party_size` multiplied by the buyout premium of 1.25 and rounded half up once, and both `group_discount_cents` and `companion_waiver_cents` are 0 — a private booking earns no group discount and no companion waiver. On every other branch the **rounding order is pinned**: the per-guest price is rounded to whole cents **first**, and only then multiplied by the party. `per_guest_after_loyalty_cents` is the unit price reduced by the loyalty discount for the guest's tier — 0 percent for `VISITOR`, 5 percent for `MEMBER`, 12 percent for `PATRON` — rounded half up to whole cents. `gross_amount_cents` is that per-guest figure multiplied by `party_size`. Multiplying first and rounding the product afterwards yields a different number of cents and is forbidden. `group_discount_cents` is 5 percent of `gross_amount_cents`, rounded half up once, but only when `order_type` is `GROUP` **and** `party_size` is 6 or more; a `GROUP` order of five guests earns nothing, and a `SINGLE` order never earns it at any size. `companion_waiver_cents` is the per-guest figure multiplied by the smaller of `wheelchair_party_count` and 1, so exactly one companion travels free however many chairs are in the party. `discount_cents` is the sum of the two, and `net_amount_cents` is `gross_amount_cents` less `discount_cents`, floored at 0. `merch_amount_cents` is never discounted and is not part of `net_amount_cents`; it only ever reaches the merchandise leg of the royalty accrual. A new order is `PENDING` with `net_collected_cents`, `refunded_cents` and `credit_issued_cents` at 0 and `refund_pct` at 0.00, and it takes its seats as a hold: the slot's `held_capacity` rises by `party_size` while `available_capacity` falls by the same amount.

Confirmation takes the money. It requires the order to be `PENDING`; the slot to be `OPEN`, or `HELD_FOR_BUYOUT` with `buyout_order_id` naming this very order; the window to still have room for the seats this order is paying for — measured as `released_capacity` less `sold_capacity` being at least `party_size`, because the hold this order itself took at creation is its own and is therefore not counted against it, with a shortfall refused as `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the run's `clearance_status` to be `CLEARED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RUN_NOT_CLEARED`; the guest to still be `ACTIVE`; the wheelchair position count to still hold; and the logical clock to be strictly before `slot_start_utc`. On success the order moves `PENDING → CONFIRMED`, `net_collected_cents` becomes `net_amount_cents`, the seats move from `held_capacity` into `sold_capacity`, `wheelchair_sold` rises by `wheelchair_party_count`, `available_capacity` is recomputed as `released_capacity` less `held_capacity` less `sold_capacity`, and the slot moves to `SOLD_OUT` when that leaves nothing available.

At the door an agent records a `decision` of `CHECK_IN` or `NO_SHOW`. The admission window is generous but bounded: a check-in is admissible from `slot_start_utc` up to and including 15 minutes after `slot_end_utc`, both bounds inclusive, and outside it the request is refused with `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED`; the run must also not be `SUSPENDED`. A check-in moves the order `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN`, stamps `checked_in_at` and raises the guest's `visit_count` by 1. A no-show may only be recorded **strictly after** that same upper bound, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_SHOW_TOO_EARLY`; it moves the order `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW`, raises `no_show_strikes` by 1, and preserves `net_collected_cents` — a no-show forfeits the ticket and refunds nothing. The strike threshold is cumulative and inclusive: once `no_show_strikes` reaches 3 the account moves `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED`, so the third strike restricts and the second does not. An agent may reinstate a restricted account, moving `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` and resetting `no_show_strikes` to 0; `visit_count` is never reset. Both counters are maintained incrementally on the account and must always agree with a full recount of that account's orders.

Cancellation behaves differently either side of payment. A `PENDING` order simply drops its hold: it moves to `CANCELLED` with `refund_pct` 0.00 and all three money legs at 0, and the slot's `held_capacity` falls by `party_size` while `available_capacity` rises again; a cancelled `PRIVATE_BUYOUT` additionally clears `buyout_order_id` and returns the slot to `OPEN`. A `CONFIRMED` order instead becomes `REFUND_DUE`, and it may only be cancelled strictly before `slot_start_utc` — afterwards the request is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The refund tier is a ladder on exact hours remaining, computed as the seconds from the current clock instant to `slot_start_utc` divided by 3600 and **not** floored: 72 hours or more earns `refund_pct` 1.00, 24 hours or more earns 0.50, and anything closer earns 0.00. `refunded_cents` is `net_collected_cents` multiplied by `refund_pct` rounded half up, `credit_issued_cents` is the exact remainder so no cent is lost, and `net_collected_cents` is then set to 0. The seats come back: `sold_capacity` falls by `party_size`, `wheelchair_sold` falls by `wheelchair_party_count`, `available_capacity` is recomputed, and a `SOLD_OUT` slot returns to `OPEN`. A `Guest` may cancel only an order whose `guest_account_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and a `reason` may be supplied.

Settling the refund is a separate act and it recomputes nothing. The split was fixed at the moment the order entered `REFUND_DUE` — whether by the guest's own cancellation or by the failed-inspection cascade — and `net_collected_cents` is already 0. Settling moves `REFUND_DUE → REFUNDED`, stamps `refund_settled_at`, and adds `credit_issued_cents` to the guest's `experience_credit_cents`. Only at that moment does the credit actually reach the guest. Afterwards `refunded_cents` plus `credit_issued_cents` equals `net_amount_cents` for that order, and a guest's `experience_credit_cents` equals the sum of `credit_issued_cents` over all of that guest's `REFUNDED` orders.

### 3.7. Safety Inspections: A Derived Verdict and Three Disjoint Cascades

A **SafetyInspection** is booked by a `SafetyOfficer` against a `run_id` whose run is not yet `CLOSED`, naming the `inspection_type` inspected — `EGRESS`, `CAPACITY`, `ACCESSIBILITY` or `SENSORY` — and the `scheduled_for_utc` instant it is booked for, which is recorded but never enforced. A new inspection is `SCHEDULED` with `outcome` null, `measured_egress_width_cm` at 0, `findings_count` at 0, `severity` at `NONE`, `allowed_occupancy_cap` at 0, and `findings_note`, `finalized_by_user_id` and `finalized_at` all null.

Finalisation is where the inspector's numbers arrive: the `measured_egress_width_cm` actually measured on site, the `severity` of the worst finding, optionally the `findings_count` from 0 to 200, and optionally a narrative `findings_note`. The **`outcome` is never supplied by the client** — it is derived, and the derivation is a first-match-wins ladder evaluated in exactly this order. First, the geometry override: on an `EGRESS` inspection whose `measured_egress_width_cm` is strictly below the egress floor, the outcome is `FAIL`. That floor is the venue's own `egress_width_cm` multiplied by 90 percent, rounded half up to two decimals — a site that has lost more than a tenth of its designed egress width fails whatever the paperwork says, and this branch beats every severity. Second, a `severity` of `CRITICAL` yields `FAIL`. Third, a `severity` of `MAJOR` yields `FAIL` when `findings_count` is greater than 5 and `CONDITIONAL` otherwise. Fourth, a `severity` of `MINOR` yields `CONDITIONAL` when `findings_count` is greater than 2 and `PASS` otherwise. Fifth and last, a `severity` of `NONE` yields `PASS`. A finalisation declaring `severity` of `NONE` while reporting a `findings_count` above 0 is contradictory and is refused with `ERR_SEVERITY_FINDINGS_MISMATCH`; and whenever the derived outcome is anything other than `PASS`, the `findings_note` is mandatory and must be 1 to 500 characters. Note the asymmetry between rungs three and four: five findings at `MAJOR` are conditional while six fail, whereas two findings at `MINOR` still pass and three are conditional.

On every branch, whatever the verdict, finalisation moves the inspection `SCHEDULED → FINALIZED`, records `finalized_by_user_id` from the finalising `X-User-ID` and `finalized_at` from the clock, and writes `allowed_occupancy_cap` as the `measured_egress_width_cm` divided by 2.03 centimetres per occupant and floored. What differs is what happens outside the inspection, and the three cascades are entirely disjoint.

On `PASS` there is no cascade at all. Nothing outside the inspection row changes; in particular the run's `effective_capacity` and `effective_capacity_cap` are left untouched. The inspection is simply recorded — and it is exactly this recorded `PASS` that can later lift a suspension.

On `CONDITIONAL` the authority has capped the room rather than closed it. The run's `effective_capacity_cap` becomes this inspection's `allowed_occupancy_cap`, and the run's `effective_capacity` is recomputed as the tightest of the four ceilings, now including the new cap. Every slot of that run in `OPEN` or `SOLD_OUT` whose `base_capacity` exceeds the new `effective_capacity_cap` moves to `SUSPENDED`, because those windows are now oversold against the permitted headcount; windows that already fit the cap keep selling. The run's `clearance_status` is **not** touched by a conditional outcome.

On `FAIL` the run stops. Hop 1 writes the inspection's own verdict fields as described. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, drives the run's `clearance_status` to `SUSPENDED` from either `UNCLEARED` or `CLEARED`, stamping `suspended_at` from the clock and recording `suspending_inspection_id` as this inspection's `id`. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, moves every slot of the run in `OPEN` or `SOLD_OUT` to `SUSPENDED`, preserving their capacity counters. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, moves every `CONFIRMED` order on those now-suspended slots to `REFUND_DUE` — and this is the operator-fault branch of the refund rule: `refund_pct` is forced to 1.00 **regardless** of how many hours remain before the slot, `refunded_cents` becomes the whole pre-call `net_collected_cents`, `credit_issued_cents` is 0 because nothing is retained as credit when the failure is the operator's, and `net_collected_cents` becomes 0. The slots' `sold_capacity`, `available_capacity` and `wheelchair_sold` are deliberately **preserved** through hop 4, because a suspended window is not resellable and those seats must not be handed to anybody else. The same from-and-to pair as a guest cancellation therefore carries a different refund rule; the branch is chosen by what triggered it, not by anything on the order.

An inspection that will not be carried out is voided instead, moving `SCHEDULED → VOIDED` with the supplied `note` stored as `findings_note`, leaving `outcome` null and `allowed_occupancy_cap` at 0. A voided inspection can never clear a suspension, precisely because it has no outcome.

Lifting a suspension is the officer's move and it is fenced three ways. The run's `clearance_status` must be `SUSPENDED`. The officer must supply a `clearing_inspection_id`, optionally with a `note`, naming a **later** inspection of the **same** `inspection_type` as the suspending one, whose status is `FINALIZED` and whose `outcome` is `PASS`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_PASS_MISSING`. And the lifting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `finalized_by_user_id` of the suspending inspection, or the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_SUSPENSION_LIFT_SAME_USER` — the officer who closed the run may not be the one who reopens it. On success `suspended_at` and `suspending_inspection_id` are both cleared, and every slot of the run still in `SUSPENDED` returns to `OPEN`, landing in `SOLD_OUT` instead where its `available_capacity` is already 0. The destination of the run itself depends on the very same approval-and-staffing gate that governs the uncleared-cleared pair: when the gate holds the run moves `SUSPENDED → CLEARED`, and when it does not the run moves `SUSPENDED → UNCLEARED`. One trigger, two targets, chosen by state rather than by the caller.

### 3.8. Demand, Extension, Closure and the Touring Kit

Extending a run is the only way to buy more days, and demand has to justify it. The `demand_index` is a utilisation figure penalised by refunds. Released windows are those slots of the run whose status is `OPEN`, `SOLD_OUT`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED_OUT` — that is, every window that was ever released; `DRAFT` and `CANCELLED` slots are excluded. Utilisation is 100 multiplied by the sum of `sold_capacity` over those windows divided by the sum of `released_capacity` over them, rounded half up to two decimals, and is 0.00 when nothing has been released at all rather than an error. The refund share counts orders, not money: the counted orders are those of the run with status `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `NO_SHOW`, `REFUND_DUE` or `REFUNDED`, and the refunded ones are those with status `REFUND_DUE` or `REFUNDED`; the share is 100 multiplied by the refunded count divided by the counted count, rounded half up to two decimals, and is 0.00 when no order is counted. `PENDING` and `CANCELLED` orders enter neither term. The `demand_index` is then the utilisation percentage minus half the refund-share percentage, rounded half up to two decimals — both component percentages are rounded before the subtraction. The gate is inclusive: an index of at least 70.00 permits an extension and exactly 70.00 passes, while anything lower is refused with `ERR_DEMAND_BELOW_THRESHOLD`.

An extension request carries the `requested_extension_days`, at least one day, and an optional `memo`. The run must be `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` and its `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED`. The producer granting it must not be the producer who created the run: when the granting `X-User-ID` equals the run's `created_by_user_id` the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_EXTENSION_SAME_USER`. The demand floor is evaluated **before** the lease budget, so a run with weak demand and no remaining lease flexibility reports the demand failure. Only then is the budget checked: the venue's `lease_extension_days_consumed` plus the `requested_extension_days` may not exceed its `lease_extension_option_days`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LEASE_EXTENSION_EXHAUSTED`. On success the freshly computed figure is stored as `demand_index` and also copied into `demand_index_at_extension`, `scheduled_close_date` advances by that many calendar days, `extension_days_granted` rises by the same number, the venue's `lease_extension_days_consumed` rises too — taking a version increment on the venue — and the run moves `OPEN → EXTENDED`, or `EXTENDED → EXTENDED` on each further round. The self-loop is unbounded except by the venue's remaining lease flexibility, which is what eventually terminates it. Note that `demand_index` is written only on a successful extension; the read-only telemetry view computes the same figure and stores nothing.

Closure comes in two deliberate steps. A `decision` of `START_CLOSING`, optionally with a `memo`, moves a run `OPEN → CLOSING` or `EXTENDED → CLOSING`, stamps `closing_started_at`, and closes the shop front: every slot of the run in `OPEN`, `SOLD_OUT`, `HELD_FOR_BUYOUT` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `CLOSED_OUT`, and every `PENDING` order of the run is cancelled with `refunded_cents` and `credit_issued_cents` at 0 — those cancellations happen because their window left the sellable states, not because the run itself moved.

A `decision` of `CLOSE` then moves `CLOSING → CLOSED`, and it is admissible only when the books are clean: no order of the run may still be `CONFIRMED` or `REFUND_DUE`, and no shift of the run may still be `SCHEDULED`. The cascade runs in four causal hops. Hop 1 stamps `closed_at` and sets `run_status` to `CLOSED`. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, strikes the kit: its `condition_score` is decayed, its `reuse_cycle_count` rises by 1, and its `last_city_code` is set to the closing venue's `city_code`. The wear is 4.00 as a base charge for the close, plus 0.30 for each of the kit's `module_count` modules, plus a further 9.00 when the kit has moved city — that is, when `last_city_code` was already set and differs from this venue's `city_code`. The three terms are summed and rounded half up to two decimals once, then subtracted from `condition_score`, that difference rounded half up to two decimals again and floored at 0.00. The first closure of a brand-new kit never pays the city-move charge, because `last_city_code` is still null. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, routes the kit on the **decayed** score: below 60.00 it moves `DEPLOYED → REFURB_REQUIRED`, and at 60.00 or above it moves `DEPLOYED → IN_TRANSIT`. Hop 4 fires only on the refurbishment branch: a kit that needs rebuilding also needs re-localising, so its `localization_status` is reset to `PENDING` and its `localization_cost_cents` is reset to 0. Independently of those hops, every staff member of the run in `CANDIDATE`, `TRAINING`, `CLEARED` or `SUSPENDED` is moved to `RELEASED`.

A **BuildKit** is registered by a `VenueOpsManager` with a `kit_code` of 1 to 40 characters using only uppercase letters, digits and hyphens and unique across all kits — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KIT_CODE` — the `module_count` of shippable scenic modules from 1 to 60, the `localization_language` tag of 2 to 12 characters, optionally an `origin_run_id` naming the earlier run the modules were fabricated for, and optionally an `initial_condition_score` from 0.00 to 100.00 which defaults to 100.00. A new kit is `READY` and already `LOCALIZED` for its first city, with `condition_score` equal to `initial_condition_score`, `reuse_cycle_count` and `refurb_count` at 0, `last_city_code` null and `localization_cost_cents` at 0. Deploying it onto a run moves it `READY → DEPLOYED`, which is admissible only while its `localization_status` is `LOCALIZED` and its `condition_score` is at least 60.00.

Refurbishment restores a struck kit, twice at most. On a kit in `REFURB_REQUIRED` whose `refurb_count` is still below 2, refurbishing adds 30.00 to `condition_score` capped at 100.00, raises `refurb_count` by 1, and moves the kit `REFURB_REQUIRED → IN_TRANSIT`; `localization_status` stays `PENDING` and `reuse_cycle_count` is **not** cleared, so the localisation discount keeps accruing across rebuilds. Once `refurb_count` has reached 2 the same request retires the kit instead: it moves `REFURB_REQUIRED → RETIRED`, its `condition_score` and `refurb_count` are left exactly as they were, and `RETIRED` is terminal. One trigger, two targets, selected by the counter.

Localising prepares a kit in transit for its next city. It takes a replacement `localization_language`, requires the kit to be `IN_TRANSIT` with `localization_status` of `PENDING` and a `condition_score` of at least 60.00, and moves the kit `IN_TRANSIT → READY` with `localization_status` set to `LOCALIZED`. The cost booked into `localization_cost_cents` is a base of 400000 cents plus 3300 cents for each module, less a reuse discount: the kit's `reuse_cycle_count` is first capped at 5 cycles, and the discount is 2.70 percent of the **base alone** for each counted cycle, rounded half up to whole cents once. The per-module component is never discounted, and the cycle cap is applied before the multiplication, so a kit on its ninth tour pays exactly what a kit on its fifth pays.

### 3.9. Royalty Accounting: Two Accrual Variants, True-Up and the Minimum Guarantee

A **RoyaltyStatement** is what the licensor is eventually paid from. A `LicensingManager` opens one against a `run_id` for the accrual period bounded by `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, both inclusive, supplying the `expected_run_version` guard described in §2.6. The statement's `license_agreement_id` is copied from the run rather than named by the client, so a statement can never be pointed at an agreement the run does not stage. `period_end_date` must be strictly after `period_start_date`, and the whole period must lie inside the agreement's own term — `period_start_date` on or after `term_start_date` and `period_end_date` on or before `term_end_date` — with a violation refused as `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM`.

Statements for a period supersede rather than accumulate. When a statement already exists for the same combination of `run_id`, `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` and its status is `DRAFT` or `UNDER_TRUEUP`, the new create moves that older row to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` and sets its `superseded_by_statement_id` to the new row's `id`, preserving its `truedown_cents` as the audit trail. But once a statement for that period has been issued the numbers have left the building: if the existing row is `ISSUED`, `SETTLED` or already `SUPERSEDED`, the create is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_ISSUED`. Every accepted create raises the run's `royalty_statement_count` by 1 and therefore advances the run's `version`, which is exactly why the create carries a stale-version guard: two managers cutting statements for the same run must serialise, and the loser must re-read the run.

The accrual itself always selects the same population of orders — the orders of that run whose slot's `slot_start_utc` falls, by UTC calendar date, on or after `period_start_date` and on or before `period_end_date`. The date that matters is when the guest was due to walk in, not when the order was placed. Within that population the statement is computed from two legs at two different rates: the ticket leg is the ticket base multiplied by the agreement's `royalty_rate_bps` and divided by 10000, and the merchandise leg is the merchandise base multiplied by `merch_royalty_rate_bps` and divided by 10000. **Each leg is rounded half up to whole cents separately and the two rounded figures are then added**; summing the bases first and rounding once is forbidden. Orders are replayed in ascending `created_at` order, and `CANCELLED` orders and `SUPERSEDED` statements never enter either computation.

What the two legs are made of is where the variants diverge. The **provisional** variant counts money the operator *expects*: its ticket base is the sum of `net_amount_cents` and its merchandise base the sum of `merch_amount_cents` over the orders with status `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `NO_SHOW` — unpaid pending orders included at face value. It runs at creation and on every recompute, writing `ticket_base_cents`, `merch_base_cents` and `provisional_royalty_cents`. The **final** variant counts money the operator actually *holds*: its ticket base is the sum of `net_collected_cents` over the orders with status `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `NO_SHOW`, `REFUND_DUE` or `REFUNDED` — a refunded order contributes nothing because its `net_collected_cents` was zeroed when the refund was computed — and its merchandise base is the sum of `merch_amount_cents` over the orders with status `CHECKED_IN` **only**, because merchandise is handed over at the door. On identical order data the final figure is therefore strictly the lower of the two whenever any order is `PENDING`, `REFUND_DUE`, `REFUNDED`, or confirmed-but-not-yet-checked-in while carrying merchandise. A new statement lands in `DRAFT` with `provisional_royalty_cents_prev`, `truedown_cents`, `final_royalty_cents`, `mg_prorated_cents`, `minimum_guarantee_shortfall_cents` and `amount_due_cents` all at 0 and `superseded_by_statement_id` null.

Recomputing the provisional figure is how a draft tracks a moving period, and the comparison against the previous run is what detects a shrinking period. The recompute is declared only out of `DRAFT`: a statement in any other state — including one that a previous recompute has already dropped into `UNDER_TRUEUP` — is refused at the own-state rung with `ERR_STATEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, in contrast with finalisation below, which names `UNDER_TRUEUP` as a source explicitly. The recompute first copies the stored `provisional_royalty_cents` into `provisional_royalty_cents_prev`, then rewrites `ticket_base_cents`, `merch_base_cents` and `provisional_royalty_cents` from the provisional variant. When the new figure is greater than or equal to the previous one the statement stays in `DRAFT` and `truedown_cents` is 0 — the period held or grew. When the new figure is strictly lower, cancellations have eaten into the accrual: the statement moves `DRAFT → UNDER_TRUEUP` and `truedown_cents` records exactly how much was lost, being the previous figure less the new one. One trigger, two destinations, chosen by comparing the recomputed figure with the stored one.

Finalising issues the statement, and it may only happen once the period is genuinely over: the logical clock's calendar date must be **strictly after** `period_end_date`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. The agreement may be `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` by then — an expired licence does not stop its final statements being cut. Finalisation refreshes `ticket_base_cents` and `merch_base_cents` from the final variant's population, and then writes four figures in a **pinned order**. First `final_royalty_cents` takes the earned royalty. Second the minimum guarantee is prorated: the licence term length in days is `term_end_date` minus `term_start_date` plus one, the period length is `period_end_date` minus `period_start_date` plus one — both inclusive counts — and `mg_prorated_cents` is the agreement's `minimum_guarantee_cents` multiplied by the period days, divided by the term days, and rounded half up to whole cents. Third `minimum_guarantee_shortfall_cents` is `mg_prorated_cents` less `final_royalty_cents`, floored at 0 — there is no shortfall when the earned royalty already clears the guarantee. Fourth `amount_due_cents` is `final_royalty_cents` plus `minimum_guarantee_shortfall_cents`. The amount due therefore always equals the greater of the earned royalty and the prorated guarantee, but the four fields must be written in that order so the shortfall is never counted twice. The statement then moves `DRAFT → ISSUED` or `UNDER_TRUEUP → ISSUED` and `issued_at` is stamped from the clock; a statement issued out of true-up keeps its `truedown_cents` as the record of the provisional reduction.

Settling records payment to the licensor: the statement moves `ISSUED → SETTLED` and `settled_at` is stamped from the clock. `SETTLED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the two terminal states of a statement.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are top-level collections with no nesting prefix. Resource-creating operations return HTTP 201 with the created resource in the `data` field; every other successful operation — reads, lists, state transitions and sub-resource posts that modify an existing resource — returns HTTP 200 with the affected resource, the requested resource, or the requested list in the `data` field. List endpoints accept the `limit` and `offset` query parameters of §2.3 and return the pagination fields in `meta`.
