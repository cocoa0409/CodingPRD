# Health System Capital Equipment Planning API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A multi-hospital health system replaces imaging scanners, cath labs, surgical towers and laboratory analysers out of one finite annual capital pool, and today that pool is contested by spreadsheets. Each clinical department argues for its own scanner from its own service log; each facility argues for its own share; the capital committee arrives at its meeting without a defensible ranking; and nobody can say afterwards whether the machine that won actually delivered the volume its business case promised. The system specified here replaces that process with a single auditable pipeline: an equipment inventory that scores remaining useful life from age, service history and vendor support status; a replacement-request intake with clinical justification and volume projections; a weighted prioritization score across safety, growth, regulatory and end-of-support drivers; a capital waterfall that distributes a plan-year pool across facilities with contingency reserves held back; a group-purchasing and vendor-negotiation stage that produces a total cost of ownership; installation project sequencing that respects clinical downtime and construction capacity; and a post-implementation utilization audit that feeds its verdict back into the next cycle's scores.

Two properties drive most of the design. The first is **capital conservation**: every cent distributed to a facility is either still available, or committed against a named request, and the identity that expresses this must hold after every funding, every funding withdrawal, every quote acceptance and every contingency release. The second is **segregation of duties**: the planner who runs an allocation may not draw capital from it, and a committee member who sponsors a request or sits in its department may not vote on it. Both properties are enforced server-side, with explicit error identifiers, rather than left to procedural discipline.

The API is mounted under `/capital`, with one out-of-band administrative capability mounted at the root for driving the server's logical clock during testing. All read endpoints are open to every role; every write endpoint belongs to exactly one role, and the surface is deliberately narrow — a facility director cannot cancel an installation project, an auditor cannot fund, a requester cannot screen.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal acts in. When `X-User-ID` is absent or blank the request is rejected with `ERR_USER_CONTEXT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 401). When the acting role is not among the roles the target endpoint authorizes, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). These two checks precede every other check in the system, in that order.

There are exactly five roles: `CapitalPlanner`, `ClinicalRequester`, `FacilityDirector`, `CommitteeMember` and `ComplianceAuditor`.

Three of the five roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those three `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier that ownership checks compare against:

- When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicalRequester`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Department`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Department` compare the resource's `department_id` to `X-User-ID`. A requester acting on a replacement request whose `department_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityDirector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Facility`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Facility` compare the resource's `facility_id` to `X-User-ID`. A director acting on an installation project whose `facility_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). On project creation the comparison is made against the facility the project will belong to, which is the facility of the funded request's department.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CommitteeMember` row; the acting member's `seat`, facility scope, department conflict and sponsorship exclusion are all resolved from that row.

The remaining two roles are opaque:

- When `X-User-Role` is `CapitalPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque planner identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — it is written to `allocated_by_user_id` on the capital cycle whose allocation the planner ran — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Because funding is barred to the planner identifier that ran the allocation, exercising a full cycle requires two distinct planner identifiers.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque auditor identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server neither records nor compares it; the auditor owns the post-implementation utilization audit and nothing else.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, an `offset` below 0, or either parameter supplied as anything other than an integer is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

Rows are always returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. The `id` field is never a sort key, and no list endpoint offers an alternative ordering. Any filters an endpoint declares are combined with logical AND and applied before sorting, and the sorted result is then windowed by `offset` and `limit`. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before windowing) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

### 2.4. The Server Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Asset age, OEM support expiry, group-purchasing contract expiry, vendor-quote validity, service-event dating, downtime and construction windows, audit windows and go-live timestamps all read a server **logical clock**. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It is moved only by the administrative clock capability described in §4, which takes a single `now` instant and sets the clock to it.

Moving the clock recomputes nothing. Stored remaining-useful-life fields, stored total-cost-of-ownership figures and stored priority scores are refreshed only by the operations that own them; a clock jump followed by a read shows stale derived values until the owning operation runs again. This is deliberate: it makes the pinned nature of every snapshot observable.

### 2.5. Monetary Amounts, Score Precision and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer counts of cents, and every monetary field name ends in `_cents`. No floating-point value is ever persisted for money. Fractional inputs are expressed as decimal fractions rather than percentages — a discount of twelve percent is carried as `0.12` — and each such fraction is limited to at most four decimal places.

All score and percentage fields are decimals carrying exactly two fractional digits. Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the rounding method is **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Rounding is applied at the points named in the individual calculations and nowhere else; in particular, several calculations deliberately round an intermediate value and then consume the rounded value, and one calculation deliberately rounds each of five projected years separately rather than rounding a single five-year sum. Implementations that "clean up" by rounding only at the end will disagree with this specification by whole cents.

### 2.6. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID version 4 string with no type prefix. Identifiers are used for identity only. No selector, ranking, tie-break, allocation order, cascade order or remainder distribution ever reads an `id`; where a final tie-break is required, the rule names `created_at` ascending as the tie-breaker.

Most resources also carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are server-minted ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits; `created_at` is fixed at insertion and `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row, including mutations performed as a side effect of an operation on another resource.

Three resources are append-only records rather than mutable entities, and they carry only `id` and `created_at` — for a `ServiceEvent`, a `PriorityScore` and a `CommitteeVote` both `updated_at` and `version` are absent. A `CommitteeVote` may be marked `superseded`, and a `PriorityScore` may be outranked by a later `sequence`, but neither row is ever rewritten in place.

### 2.7. Nested Path Resolution

Several resources are addressed under a parent path — service events and score histories hang off an asset or a request, quotes, installation projects, votes and audits are created under their request. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to no row terminates the request with that entity's own not-found error: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPARTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GPO_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404.

The same discipline applies to identifiers supplied in a request body rather than a path. A body-borne reference — `facility_id`, `department_id`, `cycle_id`, `asset_id`, `sponsor_member_id`, `gpo_contract_id`, `trade_in_asset_id`, `predecessor_project_id` or `installation_project_id` — that names no existing row yields the referenced entity's own not-found error at HTTP 404, and body references are checked after path references.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations require the client to prove it is acting on the version of a parent record it last observed. Both are non-optional inputs, and both reject a mismatch at HTTP 412.

Casting a committee ballot on a replacement request requires `expected_request_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `ReplacementRequest`. When the value does not match the request's current `version`, the ballot is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A member is otherwise free to cast a second ballot on the same request — the later ballot supersedes the earlier one — so no state precondition blocks the second create; the version check is what prevents a member from voting against a composite score that has already been replaced by a re-score after an asset condition change or a department penalty change, or by another member's ballot.

Running the facility allocation for a capital cycle requires `expected_cycle_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `CapitalCycle`. When the value does not match the cycle's current `version`, the run is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A re-run legitimately supersedes a prior allocation set while nothing has been committed, so no state precondition blocks the second call; the version check is what prevents a run computed from a superseded score set — one where a scoring re-run or a withdrawal has changed the eligible requests — from overwriting a newer allocation.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Operations not listed above do not take one and must not require one.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Capital release is split across two planner identities. The `X-User-ID` recorded in `allocated_by_user_id` when an allocation run completes is barred from funding any request in that cycle; such an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SOD_ALLOCATOR_CANNOT_FUND` (HTTP 403). Re-running the allocation overwrites `allocated_by_user_id` with the identity of the planner who ran it most recently, so the bar follows the latest run.

Committee eligibility is likewise structural rather than procedural. A member is barred from voting on a request when the member is inactive, when the member is scoped to a facility other than the request's facility, when the member is that request's nominated sponsor, or when the member is seated in the request's own department; the ballot is then rejected with `ERR_VOTE_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST` (HTTP 403). Only the safety-officer seat may veto and only the chair seat may close voting; closing voting is a procedural act, so the chair does not itself need to be an eligible voter on the request it closes.

### 2.10. The Conserved Capital Identity

Each facility's slice of a cycle's pool is held on a `FacilityAllocation` row, and for the life of that row the following identity holds exactly: the sum of `allocated_cents` and `released_contingency_cents` equals the sum of `committed_cents` and `available_cents`. Every operation that moves capital — funding a request, withdrawing funding, accepting a vendor quote that changes the capital term, withdrawing an accepted quote, releasing the facility contingency reserve — recomputes `available_cents` from that identity and refreshes `posture` in the same write. An operation whose effect would drive `available_cents` below zero is rejected before anything is written.

Because allocations, cycles, requests and assets all reference one another, a single call can legitimately mutate four rows deep. Every such cascade is specified explicitly in §3, and every row touched by a cascade has its own `version` incremented and its own `updated_at` refreshed. A cascade never silently skips a hop, and a cascade never partially applies: either every hop is written or the whole call is rejected.

### 2.11. Order of Rule Evaluation

When one call violates several rules, exactly one error is returned: the one earliest in the following order. Nothing later in the order is evaluated, and no side effect is written. Implementations that evaluate in a different order will return the wrong identifier on multi-fault requests.

1. `X-User-ID` absent or blank, on every endpoint — `ERR_USER_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`, 401.
2. Acting role not authorized for the endpoint, on every endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403.
3. A templated path identifier that resolves to no row — that entity's not-found error, 404.
4. A body-borne reference that resolves to no row — the referenced entity's not-found error, 404.
5. A `ClinicalRequester` acting on a request whose `department_id` is not the acting `X-User-ID`, on request creation, request patch, request submit and request withdraw — `ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403.
6. A `FacilityDirector` acting on a project whose `facility_id` is not the acting `X-User-ID`, on project creation, project patch, construction advance, equipment advance and go-live — `ERR_FACILITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403.
7. A required input absent, a field of the wrong JSON type, an enum value outside its declared set, a string outside its length bounds or a number outside its declared range, on every write endpoint — `ERR_VALIDATION`, 422.
8. Pagination parameters outside their bounds, on every list endpoint — `ERR_VALIDATION`, 422.
9. A duplicate `code` on facility creation, a duplicate `plan_year` on cycle creation, or a duplicate `contract_code` on group-purchasing-contract creation — `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, 409.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Capital Estate: Facilities, Departments and Equipment Inventory

The estate is registered top-down by the capital planning office. A facility is created with a display `name` of one to one hundred twenty characters that may not be blank, a short `code` of two to eight characters drawn only from uppercase letters A through Z and the digits 0 through 9 and unique across all facilities, and a `region` that is one of `NORTH`, `SOUTH`, `EAST` or `WEST`. A facility also declares `max_concurrent_construction`, the largest number of live installation projects whose construction intervals may overlap at that site at any single instant; it defaults to 1 and must be at least 1 and at most 5. The server maintains `active_asset_count` on the facility, which starts at 0 and always equals the number of equipment assets at that facility whose `status` is anything other than `DECOMMISSIONED`. A planner may later edit a facility's `name`, `region` and `max_concurrent_construction`; lowering the concurrency ceiling does not retroactively invalidate projects that are already live, it only tightens the capacity test applied to later writes. Facilities can be listed filtered by `region` and by `code`.

Departments hang beneath facilities. A department declares the `facility_id` that owns it — immutable once set — a clinical `name` of one to one hundred twenty characters, and a `modality_scope` that is one of `CT`, `MRI`, `CATH_LAB`, `ULTRASOUND`, `LAB` or `SURGICAL`; the modality scope is the axis along which clinical downtime is deconflicted. Two department fields are written only by the system and never by a caller: `utilization_penalty_points`, an integer that starts at 0 and can reach at most 20, and `underperformance_flag`, a boolean that starts false and becomes true the first time any post-implementation audit of that department's requests returns an underperforming verdict. A planner editing a department may change only `name` and `modality_scope`; neither `facility_id` nor `utilization_penalty_points` is writable. Departments can be listed filtered by `facility_id`, by `modality_scope` and by `underperformance_flag`.

Registering an equipment asset requires its `facility_id`, the `department_id` of the clinical department that operates it, a `modality` drawn from the same six values as a department's scope, a `model_name` of one to one hundred twenty characters, the `install_date` at which the machine entered service, an `expected_life_years` between 1 and 30 inclusive, an `acquisition_cost_cents` greater than zero, and the `oem_support_end_date` at which the manufacturer's support lapses. The supplied `facility_id` must equal the owning department's own `facility_id`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_ASSET_FACILITY_MISMATCH`. Creation runs the remaining-useful-life scoring described next, sets `disposition_status` to `NONE` and `trade_in_credit_applied_cents` to 0, and then increments the owning facility's `active_asset_count` and bumps that facility's `version`.

An asset's `status` is one of `IN_SERVICE`, `DERATED`, `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` or `DECOMMISSIONED`, and its `disposition_status` is one of `NONE`, `HELD_FOR_TRADE_IN`, `TRADED_IN` or `SCRAPPED`. A planner may later edit `model_name`, `install_date`, `expected_life_years`, `oem_support_end_date` and `acquisition_cost_cents`; every such edit re-runs the scoring, re-applies the derate gate, and re-scores any replacement request of that asset that is currently `SCORED`, appending a fresh score row whose `reason` is `ASSET_CONDITION_CHANGE`. Once an asset reaches `DECOMMISSIONED` it is closed: edits and service-event appends are both rejected with `ERR_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. Assets can be listed filtered by `facility_id`, `department_id`, `modality`, `status` and `disposition_status`.

### 3.2. Service History, Remaining Useful Life and the Derate Gate

Service history is an append-only log. Each service event carries the `event_date` on which it occurred, which may not be later than the logical clock — a future-dated event is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_FUTURE` — a `category` of `PM`, `CORRECTIVE`, `RECALL` or `UPGRADE`, a `downtime_hours` figure with two decimal places that defaults to 0.00 and must be at least 0 and at most 8760, a `cost_cents` figure that defaults to 0 and may not be negative, a `recall_resolved` boolean that defaults to false and is meaningful only for a recall event (for every other category the value is ignored), and a free-text `notes` field of up to five hundred characters. The event's `asset_id` is taken from the path, never from the body. Service events of an asset can be listed filtered by `category`.

Every asset creation, asset edit and service-event append re-derives the asset's remaining-useful-life picture at the current logical clock, and stores that instant in `ruls_as_of`. The derivation proceeds in a fixed order. First, `age_years` is the whole number of days elapsed between `install_date` and `ruls_as_of`, divided by 365, rounded half up to two decimals — a machine two thousand one hundred ninety days old is exactly 6.00 years old. Second, the fraction of life remaining is the expected life in years less the age in years, divided by the expected life in years. Third, the **nominal** score, stored as `nominal_ruls_score`, is that fraction multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimals and then clamped into the range 0.00 through 100.00; the nominal variant reads nothing but `install_date` and `expected_life_years`, and it is never consulted for eligibility, for derating or for the safety driver.

The adjusted variant then applies service history. Over the closed trailing window of seven hundred thirty days — two years — ending at `ruls_as_of`, the server counts every service event whose `category` is `CORRECTIVE` into `corrective_count_24m`, and sums the `downtime_hours` of events of **every** category into `downtime_hours_24m`. The two aggregates deliberately use different category filters: a planned-maintenance or upgrade event contributes its downtime but not to the corrective count. From those, `service_penalty_points` is three points for each corrective event plus half a point for each downtime hour, rounded half up to two decimals, and then capped at 40.00; the cap applies to this penalty term alone and never to the sum of all penalties. An end-of-support penalty of 20.00 applies when `oem_support_end_date` is at or before `ruls_as_of`; a penalty of 8.00 applies when the support end falls after `ruls_as_of` but at or within three hundred sixty-five days — one year — of it; otherwise the end-of-support penalty is zero. Separately, `unresolved_recall` is true when the most recent recall event of the asset, chosen over the asset's entire history rather than only the trailing window and ordered by `event_date` descending and then `created_at` descending, has `recall_resolved` false; it is false when the asset has no recall event at all. An unresolved recall contributes a further penalty of 10.00.

`adjusted_ruls_score` is the nominal score with the service penalty, the end-of-support penalty and the recall penalty **subtracted** from it, the result rounded half up to two decimals and clamped into the range 0.00 through 100.00. The penalties are never multiplied into the nominal score. Every penalty term and both scores carry two decimals.

Two thresholds then read the adjusted score. The **derate gate** sets the asset's `status` to `DERATED` when `adjusted_ruls_score` is at most 15.00 or when `unresolved_recall` is true, and to `IN_SERVICE` otherwise. On asset creation the gate decides the status the new row is born with, so an asset whose adjusted score is already at or below 15.00, or that is created with an unresolved recall, starts life `DERATED` rather than `IN_SERVICE`. On a service-event append or an asset edit the gate drives the pair of transitions `IN_SERVICE → DERATED` and `DERATED → IN_SERVICE`: the machine falls out of service when the adjusted score drops to 15.00 or below or an unresolved recall appears, and it returns to service only when the adjusted score is strictly above 15.00 and `unresolved_recall` is false — which happens when a later recall event records the resolution, or when an old downtime window rolls out of the trailing two-year span. Assets already in `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` or `DECOMMISSIONED` are never re-gated by this rule.

Whenever the derate gate flips an asset's status, the effect propagates. Every replacement request of that asset that is currently `SCORED` is re-scored, receiving a new score row whose `reason` is `ASSET_CONDITION_CHANGE`, and the request's own `composite_score` and `expedited` are refreshed from it, with the request's `version` incremented. Where such a re-score flips a request's `expedited` flag, the owning cycle's `expedited_count` is refreshed and the cycle's `version` is incremented as well.

The **eligibility gate** is the second threshold, and it is read when a requester submits a replacement request: the asset may back the request when `adjusted_ruls_score` is at most 30.00, or the asset's `status` is `DERATED`, or `unresolved_recall` is true. An asset whose adjusted score is above 30.00 while its status is not `DERATED` and it carries no unresolved recall fails the gate, and the submission is rejected with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_REPLACEMENT_ELIGIBLE`. The boundary is inclusive on the eligible side: an adjusted score of exactly 30.00 is eligible, and 30.01 is not.

### 3.3. The Capital Cycle Stage Pipeline

A capital cycle is the annual competition. Creating one requires a `plan_year` between 2026 and 2035 inclusive and unique across cycles — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` — a `plan_year_start` naming the first instant of that plan year, a `total_capital_cents` greater than zero, a `contingency_pct` between 0.00 and 0.50 inclusive with at most four decimal places, and a `facility_contingency_pct` under the same bounds. The cycle is born in `DRAFT` with `allocation_method` null, every aggregate at zero and `allocated_by_user_id` null. While the cycle is still in `DRAFT` a planner may edit `total_capital_cents`, `contingency_pct`, `facility_contingency_pct` and `plan_year_start`; once the cycle has left `DRAFT` the edit is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`. Cycles can be listed filtered by `status`, `plan_year` and `allocation_method`.

The cycle's `status` is the stage gate for every child operation and walks `DRAFT → INTAKE_OPEN → SCORING → ALLOCATED → COMMITTED`. Opening intake requires the cycle to be in `DRAFT` and moves it to `INTAKE_OPEN`. Closing intake requires `INTAKE_OPEN` and moves the cycle to `SCORING`; requests of that cycle still sitting in `DRAFT`, `RETURNED` or `SUBMITTED` keep whatever status they hold, but they can no longer be submitted or screened forward into this cycle, so only requests that already reached `SCREENED` enter the scoring run. A scoring run requires `SCORING` and leaves the cycle in `SCORING` — the self-transition `SCORING → SCORING` — so it may be repeated. The allocation run requires either `SCORING` — the first run, which moves the cycle to `ALLOCATED` — or `ALLOCATED`, for a re-run that leaves it in `ALLOCATED` as the self-transition `ALLOCATED → ALLOCATED`. Committing requires `ALLOCATED` and moves the cycle to `COMMITTED`. Any operation attempted against a cycle in the wrong stage is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`; the pinned mapping is `DRAFT` to open intake, `INTAKE_OPEN` to submit a request or to close intake, `SCORING` to score or to run the first allocation, and `ALLOCATED` to vote, to close voting, to fund, to re-run the allocation or to commit.

Committing the cycle is a settlement act with a cascade. The cycle moves `ALLOCATED → COMMITTED` and its `version` is incremented, and then every request of that cycle whose status is `SCORED` or `IN_COMMITTEE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, is moved to `DEFERRED` with that request's `version` incremented. `COMMITTED` is sticky: a later funding withdrawal restores capital to the facility allocation but never returns the cycle to `ALLOCATED`, and requests already deferred by the commit are never revived.

The cycle carries the running arithmetic of the plan year, all of it written by the allocation machinery rather than by a caller: `system_contingency_cents` and `distributable_cents` and `unallocated_cents` and `contingency_drawn_cents`, the count `allocation_count` of allocation rows for the cycle whose posture is not `SUPERSEDED`, the count `exhausted_allocation_count` of allocation rows whose posture is `EXHAUSTED`, `scored_count` as the number of the cycle's requests that stand in `SCORED` after the most recent scoring run, and `expedited_count` as the number of the cycle's requests whose `expedited` flag is currently true.

### 3.4. Replacement Request Intake, Revision and Screening

A clinical department raises a replacement request naming the `cycle_id` it competes in, its own `department_id`, the `asset_id` it proposes to replace, a `clinical_justification` narrative of at least twenty and at most two thousand characters, a `current_annual_volume` that may not be negative, a `projected_annual_volume` of at least 1, an `estimated_capital_cents` greater than zero, an `annual_service_contract_cents` that may not be negative, and a `disposable_unit_cents` per-procedure consumable cost that may not be negative. Four inputs are optional: a `sponsor_member_id` nominating a committee sponsor, which defaults to null; a `facility_modification_cents` figure for construction, shielding and power work, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative; a `regulatory_mandate_flag`, which defaults to false; and a `mandate_deadline`, which defaults to null. The acting requester's `X-User-ID` must equal the `department_id` on the request, and the referenced asset's own `department_id` must equal it too — a foreign asset is rejected with `ERR_ASSET_DEPARTMENT_MISMATCH`. The request is born in `DRAFT` with all four priority drivers at 0.00, `composite_score` 0.00, `expedited` false, `revision_count` 0, `return_count` 0 and `funded_amount_cents` 0.

Two shape rules ride along with intake and are re-checked on every edit and again at submission. The facility modification cost is capped at thirty percent of the estimated capital cost, and the comparison is made as an exact integer cross-multiplication: ten times `facility_modification_cents` may not exceed three times `estimated_capital_cents`. When it does, the write is rejected with `ERR_MODIFICATION_COST_EXCEEDS_CAP`. Separately, a request that asserts `regulatory_mandate_flag` true must also carry a `mandate_deadline`; a true flag with a null deadline is rejected with `ERR_MANDATE_DEADLINE_REQUIRED`.

Editing a request is permitted only while it stands in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED`, and only by the owning department. The editable inputs are `clinical_justification`, `current_annual_volume`, `projected_annual_volume`, `estimated_capital_cents`, `facility_modification_cents`, `annual_service_contract_cents`, `disposable_unit_cents`, `sponsor_member_id`, `regulatory_mandate_flag` and `mandate_deadline`. An edit applied to a request in `DRAFT` leaves it in `DRAFT` — the self-transition `DRAFT → DRAFT` — and leaves `revision_count` untouched. An edit applied to a request in `RETURNED` performs the transition `RETURNED → DRAFT` and increments `revision_count` by one. That is the revision loop `RETURNED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and it is bounded: submission requires `revision_count` to be at most 3, so a fourth revision is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Submitting a request performs `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is the densest gate in the intake stage. The request must stand in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED`, the acting requester must own the department, the owning cycle must be in `INTAKE_OPEN`, the revision bound must hold, the asset must not be `DECOMMISSIONED` and must clear the eligibility gate of §3.2, no other request naming the same `asset_id` in the same `cycle_id` may hold a status other than `WITHDRAWN`, `REJECTED` or `DEFERRED` — a live sibling is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_REQUEST` — and both the modification cap and the mandate-deadline rule must hold. On success the request's `version` is incremented and the budgetary total-cost-of-ownership projection is run and stored.

That projection walks five plan years. In the **budgetary** variant the service contract escalates at five percent a year: the service cost of plan year one is `annual_service_contract_cents` itself, and the service cost of each later year is `annual_service_contract_cents` multiplied by one plus five hundredths raised to the power of the year number less one, the product evaluated exactly as a decimal and only then rounded half up to whole cents. No year's service cost is ever waived in the budgetary variant. Disposables are driven by `projected_annual_volume` multiplied by `disposable_unit_cents`; plan year one carries a utilization ramp of eighty-five hundredths, so year one's disposables are that product multiplied by 0.85 and rounded half up to whole cents, while years two through five each carry the full unramped product. Disposables never escalate. Each of the five years is rounded to whole cents **before** the summation, and `budgetary_operating_5y_cents` is the sum over the five years of that year's service cost plus that year's disposables cost. The capital term of the budgetary variant is `estimated_capital_cents` plus `facility_modification_cents`, and `budgetary_tco_cents` is that capital term plus the five-year operating total. The facility modification cost sits in the capital term of both variants and never inside the operating total. Submission also writes `baseline_year1_opex_cents` as the year-one service cost plus the year-one disposables cost of the authoritative variant, which at submission time is the budgetary one; this figure is the business-case baseline the post-implementation audit measures actual operating cost against. The two negotiated-variant fields, `negotiated_operating_5y_cents` and `negotiated_tco_cents`, stay null until a vendor quote is accepted.

Screening is the planner's gate and it forks. A screening call carries a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` and an optional `screening_note`, and it requires the request to stand in `SUBMITTED` with its cycle in either `INTAKE_OPEN` or `SCORING`. A `decision` of `ACCEPT` performs `SUBMITTED → SCREENED` and increments the request's `version`, and nothing else changes. A `decision` of `RETURN` performs `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, increments `return_count` by one and increments the request's `version`. The two branches have disjoint effects and the same guards.

A requester may abandon a request at any point up to scoring. Withdrawal accepts an optional `withdrawal_reason` and performs the transition to `WITHDRAWN` from any of `DRAFT`, `RETURNED`, `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENED` or `SCORED` — so `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, `RETURNED → WITHDRAWN`, `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`, `SCREENED → WITHDRAWN` and `SCORED → WITHDRAWN` are all permitted, incrementing the request's `version`; where the request was `SCORED`, the owning cycle's `scored_count` and `expedited_count` are refreshed and the cycle's `version` is incremented. A request that has reached `FUNDED` or any later status can no longer be withdrawn, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`.

The full request lifecycle is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENED`, `RETURNED`, `SCORED`, `IN_COMMITTEE`, `APPROVED`, `DEFERRED`, `REJECTED`, `FUNDED`, `IMPLEMENTED`, `AUDITED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `AUDITED` terminal. Requests can be listed filtered by `cycle_id`, `department_id`, `asset_id`, `sponsor_member_id`, `status` and `expedited`. The state set each operation requires is pinned: submitting demands `DRAFT` or `RETURNED`; editing demands `DRAFT` or `RETURNED`; screening demands `SUBMITTED`; withdrawing demands `DRAFT`, `RETURNED`, `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENED` or `SCORED`; balloting demands `SCORED` or `IN_COMMITTEE`; closing voting demands `IN_COMMITTEE`; funding demands `APPROVED`; withdrawing funding demands `FUNDED`; raising a vendor quote demands `SCORED`, `IN_COMMITTEE`, `APPROVED` or `FUNDED`; opening an installation project demands `FUNDED`; and scheduling a utilization audit demands `IMPLEMENTED`. Anything else is `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.5. Prioritization Scoring Across the Four Drivers

A scoring run sweeps the cycle's requests in `created_at` ascending order. Requests standing in `SCREENED` are scored and moved `SCREENED → SCORED`; requests already standing in `SCORED` are re-scored and stay `SCORED`. In both cases a new score row is appended with `reason` `CYCLE_SCORING`, the request's `version` is incremented, and after the sweep the cycle's `scored_count` and `expedited_count` are refreshed and the cycle's `version` is incremented.

Scoring reads one request, its asset's stored `adjusted_ruls_score` and `corrective_count_24m` exactly as stored, its department's `utilization_penalty_points`, and its cycle's `plan_year_start`. It produces four drivers.

The **safety driver** is one hundred less the asset's `adjusted_ruls_score`, multiplied by seventy-five hundredths, plus four points for every corrective service event in the asset's trailing two-year window, the sum rounded half up to two decimals and then capped at 100.00. It reads the adjusted variant of the remaining-useful-life score and never the nominal one.

The **growth driver** is a tier lookup on projected volume growth. The growth percentage is the projected annual volume less the current annual volume, divided by the current annual volume or by 1 when the current volume is zero, multiplied by one hundred, and rounded half up to two decimals. That rounding happens **before** the tier lookup, so a raw ratio of 9.995 percent becomes 10.00 and crosses into the next tier. A growth percentage below 0.00 yields a driver of 0.00; from 0.00 up to but not including 10.00 yields 25.00; from 10.00 up to but not including 25.00 yields 50.00; from 25.00 up to but not including 50.00 yields 75.00; and 50.00 or above yields 100.00. Each break is inclusive at its lower edge, so exactly 10.00 belongs to the 50.00 tier, exactly 25.00 to the 75.00 tier and exactly 50.00 to the 100.00 tier.

The **regulatory driver** is 0.00 when `regulatory_mandate_flag` is false. When the flag is true, the driver is 100.00 if `mandate_deadline` falls at or before three hundred sixty-five days — one year — after the cycle's `plan_year_start`, and 70.00 otherwise.

The **end-of-support driver** is 100.00 when the asset's `oem_support_end_date` falls at or before the cycle's `plan_year_start`, 60.00 when it falls at or before seven hundred thirty days — two years — after `plan_year_start`, and 0.00 otherwise. Note the deliberate asymmetry with §3.2: this driver is measured against the cycle's `plan_year_start`, while the end-of-support penalty inside the remaining-useful-life score is measured against the logical clock. The two reference instants are different by design and differ by months in practice.

The weighted sum applies thirty-five hundredths to the safety driver, twenty hundredths to the growth driver, twenty-five hundredths to the regulatory driver and twenty hundredths to the end-of-support driver. The four products are summed exactly and the **total** is rounded half up to two decimals once; the individual products are never rounded. `composite_score` is that weighted sum less the department's `utilization_penalty_points` — an integer point count, subtracted after weighting and never itself weighted — rounded half up to two decimals and clamped into the range 0.00 through 100.00. Finally, `expedited` is true when `composite_score` is at least 85.00, and false below it; the comparison is inclusive at 85.00.

Each scoring produces an immutable score row rather than overwriting history. A score row carries the `request_id` it belongs to, a one-based `sequence` equal to one more than the highest sequence already recorded for that request, a `reason` of `CYCLE_SCORING`, `ASSET_CONDITION_CHANGE` or `DEPARTMENT_PENALTY_CHANGE`, the pinned `asset_adjusted_ruls_snapshot` and `corrective_count_snapshot` taken from the asset at scoring time, the four values `safety_driver`, `growth_driver`, `regulatory_driver` and `eos_driver`, the `department_penalty_points` subtracted in that row, that row's own `composite_score`, and that row's own `expedited`. The request's own `safety_driver`, `growth_driver`, `regulatory_driver`, `eos_driver`, `composite_score` and `expedited` always mirror the row with the highest `sequence`, which is the authoritative score. The score history of a request is readable in `created_at` ascending order and can be filtered by `reason`.

Re-scoring is triggered from three directions, and each stamps its own `reason`. A repeat scoring run stamps `CYCLE_SCORING`. A change in an asset's condition — a service-event append or an asset edit that flips the derate gate — stamps `ASSET_CONDITION_CHANGE` on the `SCORED` requests of that asset. A department penalty change following an underperforming audit stamps `DEPARTMENT_PENALTY_CHANGE` on that department's `SCORED` requests whose cycle is still in `SCORING` or `ALLOCATED`. In every case the request stays `SCORED` — the self-transition `SCORED → SCORED` — its `composite_score` and `expedited` are refreshed from the new row, its `version` is incremented, and the owning cycle's `expedited_count` is refreshed.

### 3.6. Distributing the Pool: The Facility Allocation Waterfall and Contingency Reserves

The allocation run is the moment the plan-year pool becomes real money at named sites. It takes two inputs: an `allocation_method` naming the variant to use, either `PRO_RATA` or `RANK_ORDERED`, and the `expected_cycle_version` described in §2.8. The cycle's stored `allocation_method` is set from that input on every run, and is null before the first run.

Only requests of the cycle whose status is exactly `SCORED` are eligible. Requests in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENED`, `RETURNED`, `IN_COMMITTEE`, `DEFERRED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `REJECTED` are excluded, and a facility none of whose departments has a `SCORED` request receives no allocation row at all. When no request of the cycle stands in `SCORED` the run is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REQUESTS`.

Each eligible request contributes a project cost equal to `estimated_capital_cents` plus `facility_modification_cents`. For each facility holding at least one eligible request, `demand_cents` is the sum of those project costs, and `weighted_score` is the sum over the facility's eligible requests of the request's `composite_score` multiplied by its project cost, divided by the sum of those project costs, rounded half up to two decimals — a capital-weighted mean of composite scores, never a plain average. Separately, `system_contingency_cents` is the cycle's `total_capital_cents` multiplied by `contingency_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, and `distributable_cents` is `total_capital_cents` less `system_contingency_cents`. Only the distributable amount is ever handed out; the system contingency is withheld first.

One ordering governs both variants. Facilities are ranked by `weighted_score` descending, then by `demand_cents` descending, then by the facility's own `created_at` ascending. A facility's `id` is never consulted in that ordering.

Under `PRO_RATA`, total demand is the sum of `demand_cents` across the participating facilities, and each facility's provisional grant is the smaller of its own `demand_cents` and the distributable amount multiplied by that facility's demand divided by total demand, taking the **floor** of the quotient rather than rounding it — flooring under-commits by design so that the shortfall can be redistributed rather than over-committing the pool. The leftover is the distributable amount less the sum of the provisional grants. The remainder is then swept: walking the ranking repeatedly, one additional cent is granted to each facility whose running grant still sits below its own `demand_cents`, and the sweep stops when the leftover reaches zero or every facility has been filled to its demand. A facility's `allocated_cents` is its provisional grant plus whatever the sweep added.

Under `RANK_ORDERED`, the remaining pool starts at the distributable amount and the ranking is walked exactly once. Each facility in turn receives the smaller of its own `demand_cents` and the pool remaining at that point, and the pool is reduced by what it received. The top-ranked facility is filled to its full demand before the next facility receives anything, so a lower-ranked facility can legitimately receive nothing at all. On identical inputs the two variants produce materially different grants, which is why the chosen `allocation_method` is recorded on the cycle.

Both variants then finish each allocation row the same way. `contingency_reserve_cents` is the facility's `allocated_cents` multiplied by the cycle's `facility_contingency_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents; it is drawn from the cycle's system contingency **on top of** the facility's allocated capital, and is never taken out of `allocated_cents`. A new row starts with `contingency_status` `HELD`, `released_contingency_cents` 0 and `committed_cents` 0, so `available_cents` equals `allocated_cents`, and `posture` is `ACTIVE` when `available_cents` is above zero and `EXHAUSTED` when it is zero. Each row also records the `cycle_id` and `facility_id` it belongs to, both written by the system.

Cycle-level aggregates are written from the same run: `unallocated_cents` is the distributable amount less the sum of every `allocated_cents`, `contingency_drawn_cents` is the sum of every `contingency_reserve_cents`, `allocation_count` is the number of rows created, and `exhausted_allocation_count` is set to 0. The drawn contingency may not exceed `system_contingency_cents`; when it would, the entire run is rejected with `ERR_CONTINGENCY_RESERVE_EXCEEDED` and no allocation row is written at all.

A re-run is permitted while the cycle sits in `ALLOCATED`, but only while nothing has been spent: every allocation row of the cycle whose `posture` is not `SUPERSEDED` must have `committed_cents` of zero, and a re-run attempted after any capital has been committed is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_ALREADY_COMMITTED`. A permitted re-run moves every existing allocation row of the cycle to `posture` `SUPERSEDED` with that row's `version` incremented, builds a fresh allocation set from the current composite scores under the newly requested `allocation_method`, and overwrites the cycle's `allocation_method`, `allocated_by_user_id` and every allocation aggregate, incrementing the cycle's `version`. The three postures are therefore `ACTIVE`, `EXHAUSTED` and `SUPERSEDED`, and only rows in the first two are ever drawn against.

Releasing a facility's contingency reserve is a separate planner act. It moves `contingency_status` from `HELD` to `RELEASED`, sets `released_contingency_cents` equal to `contingency_reserve_cents`, and recomputes `available_cents` as `allocated_cents` plus `released_contingency_cents` less `committed_cents`. Where that recomputation lifts `available_cents` above zero on a row whose `posture` was `EXHAUSTED`, the posture flips back to `ACTIVE`, the cycle's `exhausted_allocation_count` is decremented and the cycle's `version` is incremented; the allocation row's own `version` is incremented in every case. A release attempted on a row whose `contingency_status` is already `RELEASED`, or on a row whose `posture` is `SUPERSEDED`, is rejected with `ERR_CONTINGENCY_ALREADY_RELEASED`. Allocation rows can be read individually and listed filtered by `cycle_id`, `facility_id`, `posture` and `contingency_status`.

### 3.7. Committee Deliberation: Ballots, Quorum and the Chair's Decision

The committee roster is maintained by the planning office. A member row carries a `full_name` of one to one hundred twenty characters, a `seat` that is one of `CHAIR`, `FINANCE`, `CLINICAL`, `FACILITIES` or `SAFETY_OFFICER`, an integer `voting_weight` between 1 and 5 inclusive, an optional `facility_id` that when set restricts the member to voting only on that facility's requests and when null denotes a system-wide seat, and an optional `department_id` that when set conflicts the member out of that department's requests. `active` defaults to true and determines whether the seat counts toward quorum; it is settable only by the planner, alongside `full_name`, `seat`, `voting_weight`, `facility_id` and `department_id`. Editing a member never rewrites ballots already cast — those keep the weight pinned on them — but a member who becomes inactive or newly conflicted drops out of both quorum and cast weight the next time a tally runs. Members can be listed filtered by `facility_id`, `department_id`, `seat` and `active`, and every ballot a single member has cast across all requests can be read in `created_at` ascending order filtered by `superseded` and by `vote`.

Casting a ballot carries a `vote` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, `ABSTAIN` or `VETO`, the mandatory `expected_request_version`, and an optional `rationale` of up to five hundred characters. The ballot's `request_id` comes from the path and its `member_id` from `X-User-ID`; neither is accepted in the body. The acting member must be eligible: `active` true, either a null `facility_id` or one equal to the request's facility, an `id` different from the request's `sponsor_member_id`, and either a null `department_id` or one different from the request's `department_id`. An ineligible member is rejected with `ERR_VOTE_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. A `VETO` from any seat other than `SAFETY_OFFICER` is rejected with `ERR_VETO_NOT_PERMITTED`. The cycle must stand in `ALLOCATED`, and an allocation row whose `posture` is not `SUPERSEDED` must exist for the cycle and the request's facility.

The first counted ballot performs `SCORED → IN_COMMITTEE`. Subsequent ballots leave the request in `IN_COMMITTEE`, the self-transition `IN_COMMITTEE → IN_COMMITTEE`. Every ballot increments the request's `version`, and every ballot pins `counted_weight` from the casting member's `voting_weight` at that instant. Where the same member had already cast a ballot on this request, that earlier ballot's `superseded` becomes true, and superseded ballots are excluded from both quorum and tally. A request's ballots can be listed filtered by `member_id` and by `superseded`.

Closing voting is the chair's act and carries only an optional `decision_note`. The acting member's `seat` must be `CHAIR`, otherwise the call is rejected with `ERR_CHAIR_REQUIRED`; presiding is procedural, so the chair does not itself need to be an eligible voter on the request. No ballot is mutated by closing voting and no capital is reserved.

The tally proceeds in a fixed order over integer arithmetic; no percentage is ever materialised, so no rounding mode can shift a boundary. First the **eligible voter set** is derived over every committee member row using exactly the eligibility predicate above, and the quorum weight is the sum of `voting_weight` over that set. Then the **counted ballots** are those ballots of this request whose `superseded` is false and whose `member_id` lies in the eligible set — a ballot from a member who has since been deactivated or newly conflicted is dropped from both sums. Cast weight is the sum of `counted_weight` over the counted ballots, with `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, `ABSTAIN` and `VETO` all contributing; an abstention therefore counts toward quorum and raises the approval bar. Approve weight is the sum of `counted_weight` over the counted ballots whose `vote` is `APPROVE`. In both sums it is the weight pinned on the ballot that counts, never the member's current `voting_weight`. A veto is present when at least one counted ballot has `vote` `VETO` and was cast by a member whose `seat` is `SAFETY_OFFICER`.

The decision is then read off in exactly this sequence. If a veto is present the request moves `IN_COMMITTEE → REJECTED` and quorum is not consulted at all. Otherwise, if the eligible voter set is empty the call is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOTERS`. Otherwise the quorum bar, a sixty-percent threshold expressed as an exact cross-multiplication, is applied: when cast weight multiplied by five is less than quorum weight multiplied by three, the call is rejected with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. Otherwise the two-thirds approval bar is applied: when approve weight multiplied by three is at least cast weight multiplied by two, the request moves `IN_COMMITTEE → APPROVED`. Otherwise the simple-majority defer bar is applied: when approve weight multiplied by two is at least cast weight, the request moves `IN_COMMITTEE → DEFERRED`. Otherwise the request moves `IN_COMMITTEE → REJECTED`. In every branch the request's `version` is incremented. Because the veto branch precedes the quorum branch, a single safety-officer veto on a request far short of quorum still yields a rejection on the merits rather than a quorum failure.

### 3.8. Group Purchasing, Vendor Negotiation and the Negotiated Cost of Ownership

Group purchasing contracts carry the discount table that prices every quote. A contract declares a `contract_code` of two to twenty-four characters and unique across contracts, a `vendor_name` of one to one hundred twenty characters, three discount fractions `small_tier_pct`, `mid_tier_pct` and `large_tier_pct` each between 0.00 and 0.50 inclusive with at most four decimal places, two inclusive tier floors `tier_mid_floor_cents` greater than zero and `tier_large_floor_cents` strictly greater than the mid floor, a `multi_unit_addendum_pct` between 0.00 and 0.20 inclusive with at most four decimals, a `trade_in_cap_pct` between 0.00 and 0.50 inclusive with at most four decimals, and an `expires_on` instant. A planner may later edit `vendor_name`, all three tier fractions, both floors, `multi_unit_addendum_pct`, `trade_in_cap_pct` and `expires_on`; already-priced quotes are **not** repriced by a contract edit, and a quote re-prices only on its own counter or revise. Contracts can be listed filtered by `vendor_name` and `contract_code`.

Raising a quote against a request names the `gpo_contract_id` whose tier table prices it, a `vendor_name`, a `list_price_cents` greater than zero, a `service_escalation_pct` between 0.00 and 0.25 inclusive with at most four decimals, and a `valid_until` instant. Four inputs are optional: `trade_in_asset_id` naming the machine offered in part-exchange, which must equal the request's own `asset_id` and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_TRADE_IN_ASSET_MISMATCH`; `trade_in_offer_cents`, the vendor's pre-cap offer, defaulting to 0 and never negative; `multi_unit_count` between 1 and 10 inclusive, defaulting to 1; and `warranty_months`, which must be exactly 0, 12 or 24 and defaults to 0. The quote's `request_id` is taken from the path. The contract must still be live: an `expires_on` at or before the logical clock is rejected with `ERR_GPO_CONTRACT_EXPIRED`. A new quote is born in `SUBMITTED` with `negotiation_round` 1, and raising it changes nothing on the request — the request's cost of ownership and funded amount move only when a quote is accepted.

Pricing runs in exactly six ordered steps, each consuming the previous step's output, and every money step is integer cents rounded half up. Step one selects `gpo_tier` from the **list** price alone: `LARGE` when `list_price_cents` is at or above `tier_large_floor_cents`, otherwise `MID` when it is at or above `tier_mid_floor_cents`, otherwise `SMALL`. Both floors are inclusive, so a list price exactly equal to the large floor is `LARGE`. Step two takes the fraction of the selected tier — `large_tier_pct`, `mid_tier_pct` or `small_tier_pct` — and sets `gpo_discount_cents` to the list price multiplied by that fraction, rounded half up. Step three sets `multi_unit_addendum_cents`, when `multi_unit_count` is at least 2, to the list price less the group-purchasing discount, multiplied by `multi_unit_addendum_pct`, rounded half up; when the count is 1 the addendum is 0. The addendum applies to the post-discount amount, never to the list price. Step four sets `subtotal_cents` to the list price less the group-purchasing discount less the multi-unit addendum. Step five sets `trade_in_credit_cents`, when a `trade_in_asset_id` is present, to the smaller of `trade_in_offer_cents` and the subtotal multiplied by `trade_in_cap_pct` rounded half up; with no trade-in asset the credit is 0. The cap is a fraction of the subtotal, never of the list price. Step six sets `net_capital_cents` to the subtotal less the trade-in credit. The quote is priced for a single unit even when `multi_unit_count` exceeds 1; the count only unlocks the addendum discount.

Negotiation is cyclic. Countering a quote requires it to stand in `SUBMITTED`, performs `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED`, overwrites `list_price_cents` from the mandatory `counter_list_price_cents` and, when supplied, `trade_in_offer_cents` from `counter_trade_in_offer_cents`; an optional `counter_note` may accompany it. A counter increments `negotiation_round` by one and re-runs the whole six-step pricing over the new list price, rewriting `gpo_tier`, `gpo_discount_cents`, `multi_unit_addendum_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `trade_in_credit_cents` and `net_capital_cents`. Revising a quote requires it to stand in `COUNTERED`, performs `COUNTERED → SUBMITTED`, and overwrites `list_price_cents` from the mandatory `revised_list_price_cents` plus, when supplied, `trade_in_offer_cents` from `revised_trade_in_offer_cents` and `warranty_months` from `revised_warranty_months`. A revise re-runs the pricing but does **not** increment `negotiation_round`. The loop `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED → SUBMITTED` may repeat only while `negotiation_round` is at most 3; a revise attempted once the round counter has passed 3 is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. A quote's own lifecycle values are `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED`, with the last two terminal, and quotes can be listed filtered by `request_id`, `gpo_contract_id`, `trade_in_asset_id` and `status`.

Accepting a quote requires it to stand in `SUBMITTED`, requires the owning request to stand in `APPROVED` or `FUNDED`, and requires the logical clock to be at or before the quote's `valid_until` — validity is evaluated lazily at acceptance and never at creation, counter or revise, so a lapsed quote is rejected with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` only here. A request whose `safety_driver` is at least 90.00 may not be contracted silently: acceptance then also requires the optional `safety_review_ack` input to be present and true, and its absence or a false value is rejected with `ERR_SAFETY_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. Where a `trade_in_asset_id` is set, that asset must equal the request's `asset_id`, must not be `DECOMMISSIONED`, and must have a `disposition_status` of either `NONE` or `HELD_FOR_TRADE_IN`; an asset already held for another request's accepted quote is rejected with `ERR_TRADE_IN_ASSET_UNAVAILABLE`.

Acceptance performs `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and then fans out. Any sibling quote of the same request standing in `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` — `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED`, `COUNTERED → SUPERSEDED` or `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED` — with its own `version` incremented, and where the superseded quote had been accepted its trade-in claim on the asset is released; at most one quote of a request may be accepted at any instant. Where the accepted quote names a trade-in asset, that asset's `trade_in_credit_applied_cents` is set to the quote's `trade_in_credit_cents`, its `disposition_status` becomes `HELD_FOR_TRADE_IN`, and its `version` is incremented. Because a quote is now accepted, the negotiated cost-of-ownership variant runs and rewrites the request's `negotiated_operating_5y_cents`, `negotiated_tco_cents` and `baseline_year1_opex_cents`, the funded amount is recomputed into `funded_amount_cents`, and the request's `version` is incremented. Where the request already stands in `FUNDED`, the change in funded amount is applied as a delta to the facility allocation: `committed_cents` moves by the difference, `available_cents` is recomputed, `posture` is refreshed and the allocation's `version` is incremented; and where that recomputation lifts `available_cents` above zero on a row that had been `EXHAUSTED`, the posture returns to `ACTIVE`, the cycle's `exhausted_allocation_count` is decremented and the cycle's `version` is incremented. The quote's own `version` is incremented too. When no allocation row with `posture` `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` exists for the cycle and the request's facility, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_NO_FACILITY_ALLOCATION`, and when the delta would drive `available_cents` below zero it is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_INSUFFICIENT`.

The **negotiated** cost-of-ownership variant projects the same five plan years as the budgetary one and rounds each year to whole cents before summing, but it substitutes three things. The escalation fraction is the quote's own `service_escalation_pct` rather than the budgetary five percent. The service contract of a year may be waived: year one is waived when `warranty_months` is at least 12, and years one and two are both waived when `warranty_months` is at least 24 — a twelve-month warranty never waives year two. A waived year contributes a service cost of exactly 0, and an unwaived year contributes `annual_service_contract_cents` multiplied by one plus the quote's escalation fraction raised to the power of the year number less one, evaluated exactly and only then rounded half up. The capital term is the accepted quote's `net_capital_cents` plus `facility_modification_cents`. Disposables are identical to the budgetary variant, including the eighty-five-hundredths year-one utilization ramp and the flat unescalated years two through five. `negotiated_operating_5y_cents` is the five-year sum of per-year service plus per-year disposables, and `negotiated_tco_cents` is the negotiated capital term plus that operating total. `baseline_year1_opex_cents` is always taken from the **authoritative** variant — the negotiated one whenever `negotiated_tco_cents` is not null, and the budgetary one otherwise — so accepting a quote moves the audit baseline onto the negotiated year-one figure.

Withdrawing a quote requires it to stand in `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`, and requires the owning request not to have reached `IMPLEMENTED` or `AUDITED`. It accepts an optional `withdrawal_reason` and performs the transition to `WITHDRAWN` — `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`, `COUNTERED → WITHDRAWN` or `ACCEPTED → WITHDRAWN` — with the quote's `version` incremented. Withdrawing a quote that had only been submitted or countered changes nothing but the quote row. Withdrawing an **accepted** quote unwinds the acceptance: the asset's `trade_in_credit_applied_cents` returns to 0 and its `disposition_status` moves from `HELD_FOR_TRADE_IN` back to `NONE` with the asset's `version` incremented; the request's `negotiated_operating_5y_cents` and `negotiated_tco_cents` return to null, `baseline_year1_opex_cents` falls back to the budgetary year-one figure, the funded amount is recomputed and the request's `version` is incremented; and where the request stands in `FUNDED` the allocation's `committed_cents` is adjusted by the delta, `available_cents` recomputed, `posture` refreshed and the allocation's `version` incremented. Because unwinding a quote normally raises the draw back toward the unquoted estimate, a withdrawal that the allocation cannot cover is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_INSUFFICIENT`, and a missing allocation row yields `ERR_NO_FACILITY_ALLOCATION`.

### 3.9. Funding, the Commitment Cascade and Capital Release

Funding is what turns an approved request into committed capital. The amount drawn is deliberately simple: it is the accepted quote's `net_capital_cents` when the request has a quote standing in `ACCEPTED`, and `estimated_capital_cents` when it does not, plus `facility_modification_cents` in either case. The facility modification cost is always added, quoted or not, so the drawn amount is directly comparable with the request's own contribution to the facility's `demand_cents`. A quote in `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` is never treated as the accepted one, and the five-year figures are never used here — the draw is a capital figure, not a cost-of-ownership figure.

Funding requires the request to stand in `APPROVED`, the cycle to stand in `ALLOCATED`, an allocation row with `posture` `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` to exist for the cycle and the request's facility, and that row's `available_cents` to be at least the computed draw. The acting planner's `X-User-ID` must differ from the cycle's `allocated_by_user_id`. On success the request moves `APPROVED → FUNDED`, `funded_amount_cents` is written and the request's `version` is incremented; the allocation's `committed_cents` rises by the funded amount, `available_cents` is recomputed and the allocation's `version` is incremented; and the asset moves from `IN_SERVICE` or `DERATED` to `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` — either `IN_SERVICE → REPLACEMENT_PENDING` or `DERATED → REPLACEMENT_PENDING` — with the asset's `version` incremented.

Funding is also where the deepest cascade in the system fires, and every hop must land. Where the draw brings the allocation's `available_cents` exactly to zero, that row's `posture` moves to `EXHAUSTED`. Because a row became exhausted, the cycle's `exhausted_allocation_count` rises by one; and where that count then equals the cycle's `allocation_count`, the cycle itself moves `ALLOCATED → COMMITTED` with the cycle's `version` incremented. Because the cycle reached `COMMITTED`, every request of that cycle still standing in `SCORED` or `IN_COMMITTEE` is moved to `DEFERRED` — `SCORED → DEFERRED` and `IN_COMMITTEE → DEFERRED` — in `created_at` ascending order, each with its own `version` incremented. That auto-commit is indistinguishable in effect from an explicit commit by the planner.

Funding can be unwound while nothing has been built. Withdrawing funding accepts an optional `withdrawal_reason`, requires the request to stand in `FUNDED`, and requires that no installation project of the request has `record_status` `ACTIVE` — an active project must be cancelled first, and otherwise the withdrawal is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_ACTIVE`. On success the allocation's `committed_cents` falls by the released amount, `available_cents` is recomputed and the allocation's `version` is incremented; where `available_cents` becomes greater than zero on a row whose `posture` was `EXHAUSTED`, the posture returns to `ACTIVE` and the cycle's `exhausted_allocation_count` is decremented, though the cycle's own `status` is never reverted. The request moves `FUNDED → APPROVED`, its `funded_amount_cents` returns to 0 and its `version` is incremented. The asset leaves `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` — `REPLACEMENT_PENDING → DERATED` or `REPLACEMENT_PENDING → IN_SERVICE` — moving to `DERATED` when its `adjusted_ruls_score` is at most 15.00 or `unresolved_recall` is true and to `IN_SERVICE` otherwise, with the asset's `version` incremented. A cycle already in `COMMITTED` stays `COMMITTED` and siblings already deferred are not revived.

### 3.10. Installation Sequencing: Two Axes, Downtime and Go-Live

An installation project is opened by the facility director once the request stands in `FUNDED`. Creation names the mandatory clinical downtime bounds `downtime_start` and `downtime_end`, and optionally `construction_required` (defaulting to false), the construction bounds `construction_start` and `construction_end`, and a `predecessor_project_id` naming a project that must be commissioned before this one may go live. The project's `request_id` comes from the path, and its `facility_id` and `department_id` are copied from the request's department at creation — construction capacity is evaluated per facility while downtime conflicts are evaluated per department. A new project starts with `record_status` `ACTIVE`, `equipment_status` `AWAITING_DELIVERY`, `go_live_at` null, and `construction_status` `DESIGN` when `construction_required` is true or `NOT_REQUIRED` when it is false. The same six inputs are editable afterwards, and turning `construction_required` on sets `construction_status` to `DESIGN` while turning it off sets it to `NOT_REQUIRED` and clears both construction bounds. Projects can be listed filtered by `request_id`, `facility_id`, `department_id`, `predecessor_project_id`, `record_status`, `construction_status` and `equipment_status`.

Four scheduling rules guard creation and every edit. First, intervals must be well-formed: `downtime_end` must be strictly later than `downtime_start` and `construction_end` may not precede `construction_start`, and a violation is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Second, the construction window must be present exactly when it is needed: `construction_required` true with either construction bound null, or `construction_required` false with either bound supplied, is rejected with `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_REQUIRED`. Third, clinical downtime is deconflicted within the department with a buffer. The downtime interval is treated as half-open, running from `downtime_start` inclusive to `downtime_end` exclusive, and the incoming interval is widened by twenty-four hours at each end before comparison while stored intervals are compared raw. A conflict exists when some other project with `record_status` `ACTIVE` in the same `department_id` — excluding the row being edited — has a `downtime_end` strictly later than the incoming start minus twenty-four hours **and** a `downtime_start` strictly earlier than the incoming end plus twenty-four hours; the rejection is `ERR_DOWNTIME_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the test is strict on both sides, an incoming buffered start landing exactly on another window's `downtime_end` does not conflict. Fourth, concurrent construction is capped per facility. Counting projects with `record_status` `ACTIVE` and `construction_required` true in the same `facility_id` — again excluding the row being edited — whose **closed** construction interval intersects the incoming closed interval, meaning the other project's `construction_start` is at or before the incoming end and the incoming start is at or before the other project's `construction_end`, the write is rejected with `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that count plus one exceeds the facility's `max_concurrent_construction`. Unlike the downtime test this interval is closed and unbuffered, so two projects that merely touch at a single instant do occupy the same capacity slot.

Predecessor edges form a directed acyclic graph. Whenever `predecessor_project_id` is supplied, the server follows predecessor links transitively from the incoming value; if that walk reaches the project being written the edge would close a cycle and the write is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_DEPENDENCY_CYCLE`. A project may also not name itself as its own predecessor.

The project carries two independent state dimensions that advance separately. The construction axis walks `DESIGN → PERMIT → BUILD → COMPLETE`, one step per call, and never reads the equipment axis. The equipment axis walks `AWAITING_DELIVERY → DELIVERED → INSTALLED → COMMISSIONED`, one step per call, and never reads the construction axis. Both advances require `record_status` `ACTIVE` and are reserved to the owning facility's director. A project created with `construction_required` false has no construction axis at all, so `construction_status` stands at `NOT_REQUIRED` and an attempt to advance construction is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`; the same rejection covers advancing construction that already reads `COMPLETE`, advancing equipment that already reads `COMMISSIONED`, acting on a project whose `record_status` is `CANCELLED`, and acting on a project whose `go_live_at` is already set.

Going live is the join step where the two axes must have converged. It requires `record_status` `ACTIVE` with `go_live_at` still null, requires `construction_status` to read either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `COMPLETE`, requires `equipment_status` to read `COMMISSIONED`, requires the owning request to stand in `FUNDED`, and — where a `predecessor_project_id` is set — requires the predecessor to be active and its own `equipment_status` to read `COMMISSIONED`. An unconverged axis is a project-state fault and yields `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, whereas a predecessor that is cancelled or not yet commissioned yields `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_COMMISSIONED`. On success `go_live_at` is set to the logical clock instant and the project's `version` is incremented, and the request moves `FUNDED → IMPLEMENTED` with its own `version` incremented.

Cancellation is reserved to the planning office rather than the facility, because cancelling releases capital-relevant windows. It accepts an optional `cancellation_reason`, requires `record_status` `ACTIVE` with `go_live_at` still null, moves `record_status` from `ACTIVE` to `CANCELLED` — the transition `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` on that axis — and increments the project's `version`. A cancelled project's downtime and construction intervals stop occupying their resources immediately, so a later project — or a later edit of an existing project — may claim windows that overlap them.

### 3.11. Post-Implementation Utilization Audit and Asset Disposition

Internal audit closes the loop. Scheduling an audit requires the request to stand in `IMPLEMENTED` and names the `installation_project_id` whose go-live starts the observation window, plus the window bounds `audit_window_start` and `audit_window_end`. The window end must be strictly later than the window start, or the write is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. The referenced project must belong to this same request, must already have a `go_live_at`, and the `audit_window_start` may not precede that `go_live_at`; any of those three failures is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_INVALID`. The audit's `request_id` comes from the path. A new audit is born in `SCHEDULED` with `projected_annual_volume_snapshot` and `baseline_year1_opex_snapshot` pinned from the request's current `projected_annual_volume` and `baseline_year1_opex_cents`, both actuals and both percentages null. Those snapshots are frozen: a later change to the request's projection or baseline never moves an already-scheduled audit's denominators. Audits can be listed filtered by `request_id`, `installation_project_id` and `status`.

Collecting an audit supplies the two observed figures `actual_annual_volume` and `actual_annual_opex_cents`, neither of which may be negative. The audit must stand in `SCHEDULED` — a second collect is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE` — and the request must stand in `IMPLEMENTED`. Two metrics are then computed. `volume_attainment_pct` is `actual_annual_volume` divided by `projected_annual_volume_snapshot`, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimals. `opex_variance_pct` is `actual_annual_opex_cents` less `baseline_year1_opex_snapshot`, divided by `baseline_year1_opex_snapshot`, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimals; a negative variance means the equipment came in under its business-case operating cost. Both comparisons are made **after** the two-decimal rounding.

The verdict branches on those two metrics. The audit moves `SCHEDULED → PASSED` when `volume_attainment_pct` is at least 80.00 **and** `opex_variance_pct` is at most 10.00; both boundaries are inclusive, so an attainment of exactly 80.00 and an overrun of exactly 10.00 both pass. The audit moves `SCHEDULED → UNDERPERFORMING` when the attainment falls below 80.00 or the variance exceeds 10.00. In either branch the actuals and both percentages are written, the audit's `version` is incremented, and the request moves `IMPLEMENTED → AUDITED` with its own `version` incremented.

A passing verdict changes nothing outside the audit row. An underperforming verdict cascades three hops. Because the verdict is underperforming, the owning department's `utilization_penalty_points` becomes the smaller of 20 and its current value plus 10, its `underperformance_flag` becomes true, and the department's `version` is incremented — so the penalty accumulates ten points per underperforming audit and stops at twenty. Because the department penalty changed, every request of that department standing in `SCORED` whose cycle stands in `SCORING` or `ALLOCATED` is re-scored with the new penalty subtracted, receiving a fresh score row whose `reason` is `DEPARTMENT_PENALTY_CHANGE`, with that request's `version` incremented and its cycle's `expedited_count` refreshed. Requests in a cycle that has already reached `COMMITTED`, and requests no longer standing in `SCORED`, are left alone.

Decommissioning closes the asset's life and is a planner act carrying a single `disposition` input of either `TRADED_IN` or `SCRAPPED`. It requires the asset to stand in `REPLACEMENT_PENDING`, and otherwise fails with `ERR_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. It also requires the replacement to be physically in place: the installation project of the funding request whose `record_status` is `ACTIVE` must have `equipment_status` `COMMISSIONED`, and an asset with no such project, or one whose project is not yet commissioned, is rejected with `ERR_INSTALLATION_NOT_COMMISSIONED`. A `disposition` of `TRADED_IN` additionally requires the asset's `disposition_status` to be `HELD_FOR_TRADE_IN`, meaning an accepted quote is claiming it; otherwise the call is rejected with `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_HELD`. On success the asset moves `REPLACEMENT_PENDING → DECOMMISSIONED`, its `disposition_status` becomes `TRADED_IN` or `SCRAPPED` per the input and its `version` is incremented, and the owning facility's `active_asset_count` falls by one with the facility's `version` incremented.
