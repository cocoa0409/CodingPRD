# Union Representation Election Response API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a labor organization files a representation petition with the Board, the employer's labor relations team inherits a case that runs on statutory clocks it did not set. Within days the unit description has to be analyzed classification by classification, the authorization-card showing has to be audited, an election arrangement has to be either stipulated or contested at a hearing, an eligible voter list carrying prescribed content has to be attested and filed against a deadline measured in business days, every front-line supervisor has to be trained on what may lawfully be said, and every campaign message has to survive both a factual-accuracy pass and a legal-risk clearance. Today this work is tracked in spreadsheets and email threads, which is why the two most common employer failures — a voter list that arrives incomplete or late, and a captive-audience speech scheduled inside the statutory pre-election blackout — are both bookkeeping failures rather than judgment failures.

This document specifies the API for a system that carries a representation case from petition receipt through certification. The system is the authoritative record of the case: it holds the classification roster and the community-of-interest arguments made about it, the showing-of-interest arithmetic, the election arrangement and the deadlines it produces, the voter list and its per-voter eligibility findings, supervisor training and conduct history, the campaign message pipeline, the polling sessions and their ballots, the objections filed against the conduct of the election, the unfair labor practice charges that may block or impound it, and finally the certification and the bargaining, election-bar and decertification consequences that flow from it.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, the case record carries **two independent state dimensions** on the same row — a procedural lifecycle that may cycle back through a rerun, and a posture dimension driven only by unfair labor practice charges that can freeze the election without touching the lifecycle. Second, every determination that belongs to the Board is exposed as a Board-actor operation, and every determination that belongs to the employer is exposed separately, so that the audit trail of who decided what is a property of the data rather than of a convention. The API is mounted under the `/labor-relations` namespace, with a single administrative exception described in §2.4.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Case Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check precedes everything else, including the existence of the resource addressed by the path. Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear on the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are:

- **`LaborRelationsDirector`** — the employer-side owner of the case file. The director dockets the petition, opens scope analysis, drafts and delivers campaign messages, attests and files the eligible voter list, seats employer observers, and files employer-side objections and charges.
- **`HRDataAnalyst`** — builds the data behind the case: the classification roster, the voter entries and their statutory content, and the factual-accuracy pass over campaign claims. The analyst never attests a filing and never dispositions anything.
- **`EmploymentCounsel`** — outside or in-house labor counsel. Counsel scores community-of-interest arguments, clears or returns campaign messages in legal review, records substantiated prohibited conduct against a supervisor, and settles unfair labor practice charges.
- **`SupervisorTrainer`** — delivers and records lawful-communication training for front-line supervisors, and may not draft, review or deliver any campaign message.
- **`PlantSupervisor`** — a front-line supervisor acting as themselves, with read access to their own training record and, through the campaign-message *listing* alone, to the campaign messages they are scheduled to present — the single-message read in §4 does not admit the role at all, so a `PlantSupervisor` addressing one message by its own identifier is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` even for a message they present.
- **`BoardAgent`** — the Board's regional agent, who owns every neutral determination: showing-of-interest verification, scope rulings, stipulation and direction of election, voter-list review, poll layout and closing, tally posting and revision, challenged-ballot resolution, objection rulings, charge determinations, rerun scheduling, and certification.
- **`UnionRepresentative`** — the petitioning labor organization's representative, who may demand a hearing, seat union observers, file objections and unfair labor practice charges, and read the public case record.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `LaborRelationsDirector`, `HRDataAnalyst`, `EmploymentCounsel`, `SupervisorTrainer`, `BoardAgent` or `UnionRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a voter list at attestation, as `authored_by_user_id` when a campaign message is drafted, and as `reviewed_by_user_id` when legal review is dispositioned — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

`PlantSupervisor` is the exception: its identity is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supervisor` row. Two reads are consequently self-scoped. A `PlantSupervisor` reading a single supervisor record may only address their own row, and a `PlantSupervisor` listing campaign messages must filter to `presenter_supervisor_id` equal to their own `X-User-ID`; addressing another supervisor's row, or filtering by a presenter identifier that is not their own, is rejected with `ERR_SUPERVISOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Omitting the filter is not itself a violation: a `PlantSupervisor` who supplies no `presenter_supervisor_id` at all is served rather than refused, and the server silently substitutes their own `X-User-ID` as the filter, so the listing they see is identical either way. The substitution applies to that one query parameter only; a `petition_id` or `status` filter supplied alongside it is honoured as given.

One further identity rule cuts across the `EmploymentCounsel` role rather than between roles. A campaign message records the `X-User-ID` of whoever drafted it, and the user who dispositions its legal review may never be that same user — even when both users hold `EmploymentCounsel`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Role membership alone therefore does not authorize a review; the acting user identity must differ from the recorded author.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, and it is the only instant anywhere in a response that is: every instant stored on a resource, or derived from one, comes from the logical clock instead.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, or a negative `offset`, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) rather than being silently clamped, and a `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer at all fails the same way.

Results from every list endpoint are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock advances only when it is set, rows very often share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by insertion order, so of two rows stamped at the same instant the one created first sorts first. A filter whose supplied value falls outside the enumeration that filter names is a schema failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), whereas an identifier-valued filter that matches no row is not an error at all and simply yields an empty page with `total_count` 0. When a list endpoint responds, the `meta` object carries the four pagination fields — `limit` and `offset` echoing the values in force for the request, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any further rows exist beyond the current page. Single-resource reads and action endpoints do not carry pagination fields in `meta`.

### 2.4. Case Record Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted row in the system — petitions, classifications, election agreements, voter lists and their entries, supervisors, campaign communications, election rounds, poll sessions, challenged ballots, objections and unfair labor practice charges — carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. Every *other* instant the server computes and stores — `core_wage_locked_at`, `hearing_requested_at`, `voter_list_due_at`, `certified_at`, `certification_year_end_at`, `election_bar_end_at` and their peers on the other resource types — is instead rendered to whole seconds as `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`, and carries three fractional digits only in the case where the instant being recorded genuinely has a non-zero sub-second component. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There are no exceptions in this domain: all twelve resource types carry `updated_at` and `version`.

The `version` counter is deliberately sensitive. It advances not only when a client writes a row directly but also when a row is mutated as a downstream consequence of some other write. A petition's `version` therefore increases when a classification is added to it, when a scope ruling moves headcount between its buckets, when a blocking charge changes its posture, and when an objection ruling resets its counters — and each of those increments is visible to the concurrency rules described in §2.7. Whenever a row is mutated, `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock instant.

Temporal reasoning in this system never reads the host's wall clock. Instead the server maintains a **logical UTC clock** that every deadline comparison, blackout-window test, cure-window calculation and certification-timeline computation reads. That clock is advanced by an administrative test-driving capability, mounted outside the `/labor-relations` namespace at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which takes a single `current_utc` instant and is open to all seven roles. That field is required and must be an ISO 8601 instant carrying a literal `Z` offset, the capability answers HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object rather than a resource, and it is subject to no other gate whatsoever: the clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards, and no case state is consulted. Before any call to that capability the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time.

### 2.5. The Two Dimensions of a Representation Case

A petition carries two enumerated state fields that move independently of one another. The first, `case_status`, is the procedural lifecycle and takes one of `RECEIVED`, `SCOPE_ANALYSIS`, `SOI_VERIFIED`, `ELECTION_SET`, `VOTING_CLOSED`, `TALLY_POSTED`, `OBJECTIONS_PENDING`, `RERUN_DIRECTED`, `CERTIFIED` or `DISMISSED`. A new petition begins at `RECEIVED`; `CERTIFIED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal. The lifecycle is not a straight line: `TALLY_POSTED` may move to `OBJECTIONS_PENDING` and back again, and a sustained objection sends the case through `RERUN_DIRECTED` back to `ELECTION_SET`, so the whole polling-tally-objection arc can run more than once on one case.

The second dimension, `case_posture`, takes one of `OPEN`, `BLOCKED` or `IMPOUNDED` and defaults to `OPEN`. Nothing a party does to the procedural lifecycle changes the posture, and nothing that changes the posture changes `case_status`. The posture is written only by unfair labor practice charge activity, and its purpose is to act as a gate: while the posture is anything other than `OPEN`, every write that would advance the election is refused with `ERR_CASE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409). Thirteen operations sit behind that gate: opening scope analysis, verifying the showing of interest other than on its dismissal branch, approving a stipulated arrangement, directing an election after a hearing, opening the polls, closing a poll session, posting a tally, revising a tally, submitting a voter list, reviewing a voter list, delivering a campaign communication, scheduling a rerun, and certifying the election. Operations outside that set — drafting a message, adding a voter entry, filing an objection, docketing a further charge, adding a classification, scoring, ruling or reconsidering one, opening an arrangement file, and demanding a representation hearing — remain available while the case is blocked.

### 2.6. Cascade Discipline

Many writes in this domain touch more than one row. When they do, every consequence lands inside the same request, and a client that reads the affected collections immediately afterwards sees the fully settled result — there is no deferred or eventually-consistent tail. A response body likewise reflects its own cascade: an operation that recomputes derived fields on the row it returns returns the recomputed values, not the pre-write ones.

Cascades in this system are frequently **selective** and only **partly reversible**, and both properties matter. Selective means a cascade names the states it acts on and leaves siblings alone: the blocking-charge cascade blocks approved campaign messages but not drafts, whereas the dismissal cascade blocks drafts as well. Partly reversible means the unwinding of a cascade is narrower than the cascade itself: when the last blocking charge on a case goes away, suspended rounds return to `SCHEDULED` and messages blocked for that specific reason return to `APPROVED`, but poll sessions that were cancelled during the block are never restored and must be laid out again. Each cascade's exact hops are stated with the workflow that triggers it in §3.

### 2.7. Read-Consistent Writes: Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two create operations in this system are meaningful only against a parent row the client has actually read, and both therefore require an explicit version assertion. These are the only two endpoints in the API that take a version field; every other endpoint takes none, and supplying one elsewhere is not part of its contract.

Creating an eligible voter list requires `expected_petition_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target petition. The field is not optional: a create request that omits it fails validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). The comparison runs after authentication, role and **both** existence checks — the petition addressed by `petition_id` and the arrangement named by `election_agreement_id` must each resolve first, so a create naming a version that is stale *and* an arrangement identifier that matches no row reports `ERR_ELECTION_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` — and before every later check, so the stale-version rejection outranks case coherence, the supersession sweep and the duplicate-list test alike. If `expected_petition_version` does not equal the petition's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PETITION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing at all is written. The rationale is that a voter list is only meaningful against the scope rulings and the election arrangement that were in force when the director read the case; a reconsidered classification, a blocking charge that changed the posture, or an objection ruling in the interim all invalidate the list about to be filed. On success the petition's `version` increments by exactly 1, so replaying the same create with the same asserted value conflicts.

Creating a poll session requires `expected_election_round_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target election round; again the field is mandatory, and its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). If the asserted value does not equal the round's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROUND_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no session is created. Sessions are laid out against a round whose schedule, suspension state and session counters move underneath the planner, and an overlap check computed against a stale round would either admit two crews at one location or resurrect sessions on a round a blocking charge has just suspended. The location-overlap and observer-capacity checks therefore run only after the version comparison has succeeded. On success the round's `scheduled_session_count` rises by 1 and its `version` increments by exactly 1.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Conserved Quantities

Every percentage and score the system derives is carried to exactly two decimal places, and every rounding step is round-half-up. This governs `showing_of_interest_pct`, `completeness_pct`, `wage_proximity` and `coi_score`; `majority_threshold` is by contrast an exact integer, produced by the halve-discard-add-one rule of §3.8 and carrying no decimal component at all. Wages are held in whole cents as integers: `avg_hourly_wage_cents` is supplied that way, and the derived `core_avg_hourly_wage_cents` is rounded half-up to a whole cent. Counts — card counts, headcounts, entry counts, ballot buckets, `risk_score`, `quiz_score` — are exact integers, and where an integer result is clamped or floored the clamping happens exactly once, at the point the calculation says, never term by term. Two rounding subtleties are load-bearing and are restated with their calculations in §3: the community-of-interest score rounds each weighted term before summing rather than rounding the sum, and every threshold comparison in the system runs on the rounded value rather than on an unrounded intermediate.

Two quantities are **conserved** and are re-checked after every write that could disturb them. On a petition, `included_headcount` plus `excluded_headcount` plus `pending_headcount` must always equal `total_classified_headcount`; a write that would leave those out of balance is rejected with `ERR_HEADCOUNT_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409) and nothing is persisted. This invariant is enforced when a classification is added, when a classification is ruled, when a ruling is reconsidered, and across the cascade that renders classifications moot on dismissal. On an election round, the physical ballots that entered the box are conserved: `provisional_votes_for` plus `challenged_counted_for_count`, plus `provisional_votes_against` plus `challenged_counted_against_count`, plus `provisional_void_count` plus `challenged_voided_count`, plus `challenged_withdrawn_count`, plus `challenged_open_count`, must equal `ballots_cast_total`. A write that would break that equality is rejected with `ERR_BALLOT_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). The invariant is enforced when a poll session is closed, when a challenged ballot is segregated, when one is resolved, when one is reopened, and when the tally is revised. Resolution and reopening are exact inverses of one another: a resolution moves one ballot out of the open bucket and into exactly one other bucket, and a reopen moves that same ballot back.

### 2.9. Statutory Deadlines and the Business-Day Calendar

Several deadlines in this domain are expressed in business days. A business day is Monday through Friday, excluding a fixed holiday set of `2026-09-07`, `2026-11-26`, `2026-11-27` and `2026-12-25`. To add a number of business days to an anchor date, step forward one calendar day at a time and count only business days until the required number have been counted; **the anchor day itself is never counted, even when the anchor day is itself a business day.** Weekends and holidays are skipped without consuming a count. A deadline expressed this way always lands at the end of the resulting day, meaning 23:59:59Z on that date.

Cure deadlines behave differently on purpose. A cure window is expressed in hours and is added to the review instant as exact hour arithmetic with no calendar skipping whatsoever, so a 24-hour cure granted on a Friday afternoon expires on Saturday afternoon. Wherever a deadline is compared against the logical clock, an instant exactly equal to the deadline is still inside the window — the comparisons are inclusive at the deadline. Where the specification instead requires a clock strictly beyond a deadline, as certification does against the objection window, that is stated explicitly at the workflow.

The certification timeline uses a third convention: the 365-day periods it writes are exact day arithmetic applied to the certification instant, preserving the time of day, with no month arithmetic and no leap-year adjustment.

### 2.10. Reference Resolution and Case Coherence

The resource collections in this system are peers under the `/labor-relations` namespace rather than a nested hierarchy: a classification, an election round, a poll session and a challenged ballot are each addressed directly by their own identifier, and no resource identifier is embedded inside another resource's path. Every parent-child relationship is therefore asserted by a foreign-key field in a request body and must be validated from that body rather than from the URL.

Resolution proceeds outside-in and left-to-right. The identifier in the path is resolved first: if no such row exists, the request fails with that resource type's own not-found code — `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOTER_LIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOTER_LIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPERVISOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_COMMUNICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLL_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHALLENGED_BALLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBJECTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ULP_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND` — all with HTTP 404. Only then are the foreign keys in the body resolved, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, each producing the referenced type's own not-found code.

A row that exists but belongs to a different case is a separate and later failure. Every entity named in a request must belong to the same petition as the request's target, and a mismatch is reported as `ERR_CROSS_PETITION_REFERENCE` (HTTP 409). This coherence rule guards six operations: creating a voter list, creating a voter list entry, creating a campaign communication, designating an observer on a poll session, docketing an unfair labor practice charge, and segregating a challenged ballot. Concretely, a voter entry's classification must sit on the same petition as the list it joins; a voter list's election agreement must belong to the list's petition; a campaign message's presenting supervisor must be registered on the message's petition, as must a supervisor named as an observer; and a charge consolidated with an objection must name an objection whose round belongs to the charge's petition.

### 2.11. The Order in Which Rejections Are Reported

A single request can fail several ways at once, and which failure is reported is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Checks run in the following fixed order, and the first failing check determines both the `error_id` and the HTTP status:

1. **Header authentication** — both headers present and the role recognized, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Endpoint authorization** — the acting role appears on the endpoint, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Self-scoped supervisor identity** — a `PlantSupervisor` addressing a supervisor row that is not their own, or filtering campaign messages by a presenter identifier that is not their own, yields `ERR_SUPERVISOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (403). This applies to exactly two reads: the single-supervisor read and the campaign-communication list.
4. **Segregation of duties** — the acting user identity differs from the recorded message author, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This applies to exactly one operation: the legal review disposition on a campaign communication.
5. **Request schema** — the body parses, every required field is present, every enumerated value is in range, integers are integers, community-of-interest factors lie between 0 and 100 inclusive, pagination is in range, and every datetime is ISO 8601 with a `Z` offset. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every endpoint. A missing `expected_petition_version` or `expected_election_round_version` is reported here. A body field the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected, and an absent or empty body is read as an empty JSON object — which is enough for an action endpoint that declares no fields of its own, while a body that is present but is not a JSON object, or does not parse as JSON at all, fails here.
6. **Path existence** — the row addressed by the path identifier exists, else the matching 404 code from §2.10. Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
7. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key in the body resolves, in declared field order, else the referenced type's 404 code. Applies to every create and to every action that names another entity in its body.
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — the asserted parent version matches, else `ERR_STALE_PETITION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ROUND_VERSION` (412). Applies to voter-list creation and poll-session creation.
9. **Case coherence** — every referenced row belongs to the same petition, else `ERR_CROSS_PETITION_REFERENCE` (409). Scope as enumerated in §2.10.
10. **Accepted-list membership** — a challenged ballot naming a voter entry must name one that sits on an `ACCEPTED` voter list for the round's petition, else `ERR_BALLOT_VOTER_NOT_ON_ACCEPTED_LIST` (409). Applies to challenged-ballot creation.
11. **Case posture gate** — the posture is `OPEN`, else `ERR_CASE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (409), across the thirteen election-advancing operations named in §2.5.
12. **Own-state precondition** — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else the state code for that resource type: `ERR_PETITION_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_ALREADY_RULED`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_VOTER_LIST_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_COMMUNICATION_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_ROUND_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_ROUND_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_POLL_SESSION_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_BALLOT_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_OBJECTION_ALREADY_RULED` or `ERR_CHARGE_STATUS_INVALID`, each HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint. Where one request finds both the target row in a state the transition does not allow *and* its parent case in the wrong `case_status`, the target row's own code is reported first — so ruling a classification that has already been ruled on a case that has also moved past scope analysis reports `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_ALREADY_RULED`, not `ERR_PETITION_STATUS_INVALID`.
13. **Cross-entity prerequisite** — the other rows the transition depends on are in the required state, yielding `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_SCORED`, `ERR_VOTER_LIST_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_BALLOTS_IMPOUNDED`, `ERR_CHALLENGES_UNRESOLVED`, `ERR_ROUND_NOT_REVISED`, `ERR_ACCURACY_REVIEW_MISSING` or `ERR_PRESENTER_NOT_TRAINED`, each HTTP 409. Applies to scope rulings, opening the polls, posting a tally, revising a tally, certification, and submitting a message for legal review.
14. **Per-se statutory safety** — a captive-audience meeting inside the pre-poll blackout yields `ERR_PEERLESS_PLYWOOD_WINDOW` (422). This is checked on all three message-pipeline dispositions: submitting for legal review, dispositioning legal review, and confirming delivery. The first two consult the stored `peerless_plywood_violation` flag exactly as the accuracy review left it; confirming delivery consults that flag and, in addition, re-evaluates the rule against the round in force at delivery time, as §3.7 describes.
15. **Campaign risk gates** — `ERR_RISK_SCORE_PROHIBITED` or `ERR_ESCALATION_ACK_REQUIRED` (422), on submitting for legal review and on dispositioning legal review respectively.
16. **Observer eligibility** — a statutory supervisor may never be seated as an observer, else `ERR_SUPERVISOR_OBSERVER_PROHIBITED` (422), on observer designation.
17. **Campaign window** — `ERR_CAMPAIGN_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409), on confirming delivery.
18. **Filing and opening windows** — `ERR_VOTER_LIST_PAST_DUE`, `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` or `ERR_POLLS_NOT_YET_OPEN` (409), on voter-list submission, cure resubmission and opening the polls respectively.
19. **Objection window** — `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` on filing an objection, and `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_OPEN` on certifying, both HTTP 409.
20. **Notice posting** — `ERR_NOTICE_POSTING_ACTIVE` (409), on certifying.
21. **Location occupancy** — `ERR_POLL_SESSION_LOCATION_CONFLICT` (409), on poll-session creation.
22. **Presenter calendar** — `ERR_PRESENTER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409), on campaign-communication creation.
23. **Uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASE_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLASSIFICATION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELECTION_AGREEMENT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTER_LIST`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHALLENGED_BALLOT` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPERVISOR`, all HTTP 409, on the corresponding creates.
24. **Loop limits** — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409), on legal-review revision requests, cure resubmissions and ballot reopens respectively.
25. **Arithmetic coherence of supplied counts** — `ERR_CARD_COUNTS_INCONSISTENT`, `ERR_CLAIM_COUNTS_INCONSISTENT`, `ERR_TALLY_RECONCILIATION_FAILED` and `ERR_UNIT_HEADCOUNT_ZERO` (422), on showing-of-interest verification, accuracy review and tally posting.
26. **Observer capacity** — `ERR_OBSERVER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on observer designation and, as a closing precondition, on closing a poll session whose roster already exceeds the cap for either party.
27. **Conservation invariants** — `ERR_HEADCOUNT_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION` and `ERR_BALLOT_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION` (409), evaluated last of all, on scope rulings, reconsiderations, ballot resolutions and ballot reopens.

Four collisions are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. A captive-audience message whose planned delivery sits inside the blackout, submitted for delivery after the polls have already closed, reports `ERR_PEERLESS_PLYWOOD_WINDOW` (422) rather than `ERR_CAMPAIGN_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409): a per-se statutory violation outranks a scheduling-window failure. A poll session created against a round that a blocking charge has already suspended, at a location and time window that also collide with an existing session, reports `ERR_ROUND_NOT_SCHEDULED` rather than `ERR_POLL_SESSION_LOCATION_CONFLICT` — the round cannot host any session at all, so the overlap question never arises, and note that both are HTTP 409, so the implementation must order the checks rather than infer the order from status codes. An observer designation naming a supervisor from a different petition for a party that already seats three observers reports `ERR_CROSS_PETITION_REFERENCE`, not `ERR_OBSERVER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`: someone who is not part of the case can never occupy a seat, so the seat count is never consulted. And a delivery attempted while the case is blocked by a charge, on a message that also carries a per-se blackout violation, reports `ERR_CASE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (409) rather than `ERR_PEERLESS_PLYWOOD_WINDOW` (422), because the posture gate is a state check on the parent case and runs before any message-level compliance analysis.

One schema-level check deserves separate mention because it applies at two different creates. Wherever a request supplies a pair of instants bounding an interval, the later instant must be strictly after the earlier one: a poll session's `session_end_at` must be strictly after its `session_start_at`, and an election arrangement's `polls_close_at` must be strictly after its `polls_open_at`. A violation is reported as `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). That comparison belongs to step 5 above and runs as soon as the body has been read, ahead of the resolution of the path identifier, so a request that both inverts its window and addresses a row that does not exist reports `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` rather than the 404.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Docketing the Case and Assembling the Classification Roster

A representation case enters the system when a `LaborRelationsDirector` dockets the petition that was served on the employer. The docketing payload carries the Board's docket number as `case_number`, the `petition_type` — `RC` for a union-filed certification petition, `RM` for an employer-filed one, `RD` for a decertification petition — the petitioning labor organization as `union_name`, the calendar date the petition was docketed as `filed_date`, the unit description exactly as the petition words it in `proposed_unit_description`, the headcount the petitioner claims is in the unit as `claimed_employee_count`, and the last day of the payroll period that fixes voter eligibility as `payroll_period_end`. The claimed headcount must be at least 1. The docket number is unique across the entire system, and a second petition asserting a number already in use is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASE_NUMBER`.

A freshly docketed case begins at `case_status` `RECEIVED` with `case_posture` `OPEN`. Every derived counter on it starts at zero — `total_classified_headcount`, `included_headcount`, `excluded_headcount` and `pending_headcount`, and the five card counters `submitted_card_count`, `duplicate_card_count`, `undated_card_count`, `stale_card_count` and `nonunit_card_count` — while `showing_verdict` begins at `PENDING`, `certification_outcome` at `NONE`, `retraining_required` at `false`, and `pending_objection_count` and `blocking_charge_count` at 0. The fields the case will later fill in — `valid_card_count`, `showing_of_interest_pct`, `showing_headcount_basis`, `core_avg_hourly_wage_cents`, `core_wage_locked_at`, `certified_at`, `certified_round_id`, `bargaining_obligation_start_at`, `bargaining_obligation_end_at`, `certification_year_end_at`, `election_bar_end_at`, `decert_window_open_at` and `notice_posting_end_at` — are all absent until the workflow that writes them runs.

Before any scope work may happen, the director opens scope analysis, moving `case_status` from `RECEIVED` to `SCOPE_ANALYSIS`. This is only possible from `RECEIVED` and only while `case_posture` is `OPEN`; from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_PETITION_STATUS_INVALID`. The transition writes nothing except `case_status` itself, along with the usual `updated_at` and `version` refresh.

Either the director or an `HRDataAnalyst` then builds the classification roster. Each classification names its owning case through a payload containing the `petition_id`, along with the employer's job-classification `code`, the payroll `title`, the `headcount` of employees currently in the classification, the average straight-time hourly wage in cents as `avg_hourly_wage_cents`, the primary shift designation as `shift_code` and the primary work location as `work_location`. Both the headcount and the wage must be at least 1. A classification code must be unique within its own case, though two different cases may use the same code; a collision inside one case is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLASSIFICATION_CODE`. The payload also carries the two parties' asserted positions — `employer_position` and `union_position`, each either `INCLUDE` or `EXCLUDE` — and the five community-of-interest factors that the parties argue over: `supervision_overlap`, `skills_overlap`, `functional_integration`, `interchange_rate` and `work_conditions_overlap`, each an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive. An optional `supervisory_authority_count` records how many Section 2(11) indicia of supervisory authority — hiring, firing, discipline, assignment and the rest — the classification exercises; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative.

Adding a classification moves headcount onto the case: the classification's `headcount` is added both to the petition's `total_classified_headcount` and to its `pending_headcount`, which keeps the conservation identity of §2.8 true by construction, and the petition's `version` increments. A new classification starts with `scope_ruling` `PENDING` and no `ruling_rationale`, and its `wage_proximity`, `coi_score`, `scoring_standard` and `coi_recommendation` are all absent until it is scored. Classification listings may be narrowed by `petition_id` or by `scope_ruling`, and case listings by `case_status`, by `case_posture`, or by `certified_round_id` for the narrow question of which case rests on a particular round. Building the roster is not itself gated on either case dimension: a classification may be added while the case is still `RECEIVED` and scope analysis has not yet been opened, and equally after the case has moved on, in any `case_posture`.

### 3.2. Community-of-Interest Argument: Scoring, Rulings, and Reconsideration

Scoring a classification is the analytical heart of the scope dispute, and it is available to a `LaborRelationsDirector` or to `EmploymentCounsel`. The request supplies a single field, `scoring_standard`, choosing among `TRADITIONAL`, `OVERWHELMING` and `ACCRETION`. That choice is a mode selector: it picks both the shape of the arithmetic and the bands that turn the resulting number into a recommendation, and it is echoed onto the classification so a later reader can see which standard produced the stored score. Scoring carries no state precondition of its own: it is available in every `case_status` and in every `case_posture`, and a classification may be scored repeatedly — under the same standard or a different one, and even after it has been ruled or has become `MOOT` — each call simply overwriting `wage_proximity`, `coi_score`, `scoring_standard` and `coi_recommendation` with the fresh result.

The **first** scoring call on any given case does one extra thing before it scores anything: it freezes the case's core wage. The undisputed core group is the set of classifications on the case where `employer_position` and `union_position` are both `INCLUDE` — the classifications nobody is arguing about. For that group, multiply each classification's `headcount` by its `avg_hourly_wage_cents`, sum those products, divide the sum by the total headcount of the group, and round half-up to a whole cent; the result is written to `core_avg_hourly_wage_cents` and the instant of the calculation is written to `core_wage_locked_at`. Freezing the snapshot is a mutation of the petition row in its own right, so the petition's `updated_at` and `version` advance on that first scoring call — and on no later scoring call, which touches the classification alone. The division happens once over the summed products — it is a headcount-weighted average, never the plain mean of the per-classification averages, and taking the plain mean is the classic error here. If no classification on the case has both positions set to `INCLUDE`, there is no core group, the first scoring call fails with `ERR_NO_UNDISPUTED_CORE` (HTTP 422), and nothing at all is frozen.

Once frozen, the core wage is **permanent**. Every later scoring call on the case — including calls made after a scope ruling removed a classification from what would now be the core group, after a reconsideration put one back, and after a rerun was directed — reads the frozen `core_avg_hourly_wage_cents`, and `core_wage_locked_at` is never overwritten while it holds a value. Recomputing the snapshot would produce a different derived wage factor and therefore a different score, which is the single most common way to get this calculation wrong.

Scoring itself proceeds in four steps. **Step one** derives the sixth factor. Take the absolute difference between the classification's own `avg_hourly_wage_cents` and the case's frozen core wage, multiply by 100, divide by the frozen core wage, and round half-up to two decimals; that is the wage gap as a percentage. Subtract the wage gap from 100.00 and floor the result at 0.00 to obtain `wage_proximity`, which therefore never goes negative even when the classification pays more than twice the core wage.

**Step two** combines the factors according to the chosen standard. Under `TRADITIONAL`, each of the six factors is multiplied by its own weight — `supervision_overlap` by 0.325, `skills_overlap` by 0.225, `functional_integration` by 0.175, `interchange_rate` by 0.125, `work_conditions_overlap` by 0.100 and `wage_proximity` by 0.050 — **each product is rounded half-up to two decimals individually**, and only then are the six rounded terms added together to give `coi_score`. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end yields a different score and is wrong. Under `OVERWHELMING`, that same weighted total is computed, then averaged with the single smallest of the six factor values: half the weighted total plus half the minimum factor value, rounded half-up to two decimals. The weakest link is dispositive under this standard, which is the standard used when the petitioner seeks to add a group to an existing unit. Under `ACCRETION`, wages, skills and working conditions are ignored entirely and only operational integration counts: 0.40 times `functional_integration`, plus 0.35 times `interchange_rate`, plus 0.25 times `supervision_overlap`, rounded half-up to two decimals.

**Step three** maps the score onto `coi_recommendation`, and the bands differ by standard. Under `TRADITIONAL`, a score of at least 65 is `SHARED_COMMUNITY`, a score of at least 45 but below 65 is `CONTESTED_COMMUNITY`, and anything lower is `DISTINCT_COMMUNITY`. Under `OVERWHELMING` the two thresholds are 80 and 60. Under `ACCRETION` they are 60 and 40. Every band comparison is inclusive at its lower edge, so under `TRADITIONAL` a score of exactly 65.00 is `SHARED_COMMUNITY` while 64.99 is `CONTESTED_COMMUNITY`.

**Step four** is a statutory override applied after the band mapping has already run. If the classification's `supervisory_authority_count` is 1 or more, `coi_recommendation` becomes `MANDATORY_EXCLUSION` regardless of which band the score fell in — a single indicium of Section 2(11) authority is enough. The computed `coi_score` is still stored in that case; only the recommendation is overridden. A classification with a `supervisory_authority_count` of exactly 0 keeps whatever band mapping produced.

Rulings belong to the Board. A `BoardAgent` rules a classification into or out of the voting unit with a payload carrying `ruling` — either `INCLUDED` or `EXCLUDED` — and the `ruling_rationale` text recorded with the decision. A ruling is only possible while the classification's `scope_ruling` is still `PENDING` and while the case sits in either `SCOPE_ANALYSIS` or `SOI_VERIFIED`; a classification that has already been ruled, or one that has become moot, is refused with `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_ALREADY_RULED`. A classification that has never been scored cannot be ruled at all: `coi_score` must already hold a value, and if it does not the request fails with `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_NOT_SCORED`. When the ruling lands, the classification's `headcount` moves out of the case's `pending_headcount` and into either `included_headcount` or `excluded_headcount` according to the ruling, the rationale is written, the conservation identity is re-checked, and the petition's `version` increments.

Rulings are reversible through a compensating action. Reconsideration takes a ruled classification — `INCLUDED` or `EXCLUDED` — back to `PENDING`, optionally recording a `reconsideration_note`. It reverses the exact headcount movement the ruling made, returning the classification's `headcount` from `included_headcount` or `excluded_headcount` to `pending_headcount`, and it clears `ruling_rationale` back to absent, while leaving the stored `coi_score`, `wage_proximity`, `scoring_standard` and `coi_recommendation` exactly as the scoring left them. Reconsideration is likewise confined to cases in `SCOPE_ANALYSIS` or `SOI_VERIFIED`, and the conservation identity must hold both before and after. A classification that is still `PENDING`, or that has already become `MOOT`, cannot be reconsidered.

There is one further scope state, `MOOT`, and no party writes it directly. It is produced only by the dismissal cascade described in §3.3, and it is terminal. Because a moot classification's headcount stays inside `pending_headcount`, the dismissal cascade moves no headcount at all.

### 3.3. Auditing the Showing of Interest — and the Dismissal Cascade

The showing-of-interest audit is a `BoardAgent` determination. The request records the authorization-card audit in five fields: `submitted_card_count`, the cards the petitioner submitted, and the four struck-card counts — `duplicate_card_count` for duplicate signatures, `undated_card_count` for cards missing a signature date, `stale_card_count` for cards signed more than six months before the petition's `filed_date`, and `nonunit_card_count` for signers sitting in a classification outside the counted unit. `valid_card_count` is the submitted count with all four struck counts subtracted from it, as exact integer arithmetic. If the four struck counts together exceed the submitted count, so that the valid count would be negative, the request fails with `ERR_CARD_COUNTS_INCONSISTENT` (HTTP 422) before anything is written.

The denominator against which those valid cards are measured is **not** supplied by the caller; the system selects it from the state of the scope analysis, and records which one it used in `showing_headcount_basis`. If every classification on the case has been ruled — that is, each one's `scope_ruling` is either `INCLUDED` or `EXCLUDED` — the basis is `AS_RULED` and the denominator is the case's `included_headcount`, because once scope is settled only the classifications actually inside the voting unit form the base. A case carrying no classifications at all satisfies that test vacuously, so its basis is `AS_RULED` as well and its denominator is `included_headcount`. If at least one classification is still `PENDING`, or has become `MOOT`, the basis is `AS_PETITIONED` and the denominator is `total_classified_headcount`, because with scope unresolved the Board tests the showing against the unit as the petition described it — every classified head counts, including the ones the employer wants excluded. The consequence is a genuine oscillation: excluding a large disputed group *raises* the percentage, and reconsidering a ruling so that one classification returns to `PENDING` swings the basis back to `AS_PETITIONED` and lowers it again.

`showing_of_interest_pct` is the valid card count multiplied by 100, divided by the selected denominator, rounded half-up to two decimals. If the selected denominator is zero, that is not a zero-percent showing — the request fails with `ERR_UNIT_HEADCOUNT_ZERO` (HTTP 422) and nothing is written. `showing_verdict` is `SUFFICIENT` when the rounded percentage is at least 30.00, and `INSUFFICIENT` otherwise; exactly 30.00 is sufficient, since the comparison is inclusive at the threshold. For a case with 90 heads in the counted unit, 27 valid cards is exactly 30.00 and sufficient, while 26 valid cards is 28.89 and insufficient.

On a `SUFFICIENT` verdict the case advances from `SCOPE_ANALYSIS` to `SOI_VERIFIED`, writing `valid_card_count`, `showing_of_interest_pct`, `showing_headcount_basis` and `showing_verdict`. The Board may also **re-verify** a case that is already `SOI_VERIFIED`, which is the ordinary response to a scope ruling having changed the denominator; that is a self-loop from `SOI_VERIFIED` to `SOI_VERIFIED` that overwrites all four output fields from the card counts supplied in the fresh request. Both the forward transition and the self-loop require `case_posture` to be `OPEN`.

An `INSUFFICIENT` verdict dismisses the case, and it does so through a cascade that reaches four other resource types inside the same request. The verdict itself is written first, along with the percentage, the selected `showing_headcount_basis` and the five card counts copied from the request. Because the verdict is `INSUFFICIENT`, `case_status` becomes `DISMISSED`, which is terminal. Because the case is dismissed, every classification on it whose `scope_ruling` is still `PENDING` becomes `MOOT` — and since moot headcount stays inside `pending_headcount`, no bucket totals change. Because the case is dismissed, every election agreement on it whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `HEARING_REQUESTED` becomes `VOID` and its `voter_list_due_at` is cleared to absent, while a `hearing_requested_at` already recorded is retained for the record. Because the case is dismissed, every campaign communication on it whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` becomes `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `CASE_DISMISSED`. And because the case is dismissed, every voter list on it still in `DRAFT` becomes `CANCELLED`, keeping its `due_at` for the record. The dismissal branch is reachable from `SCOPE_ANALYSIS` and from `SOI_VERIFIED` alike; unlike the sufficient branch, it does not additionally require the posture to be `OPEN`. Because the branch is chosen from the computed verdict, a request whose counts yield no verdict at all — a negative valid-card count, or a zero denominator — is treated as the non-dismissal branch for gating purposes, so on a case that is not `OPEN` it is refused with `ERR_CASE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` before the arithmetic failure is ever reported.

### 3.4. Fixing the Election: The Stipulated Branch and the Hearing Branch

Once the showing has been verified, the employer opens an election-arrangement file for the case. The `LaborRelationsDirector` creates it with a payload naming the `petition_id`, the `agreement_type` the employer proposes — `STIPULATED` or `CONSENT` — the `proposed_election_date`, and the `proposed_voting_method`, which is `MANUAL`, `MAIL` or `MIXED`. The new arrangement begins with `status` `PROPOSED` and `route` `PENDING`, and its `approved_election_date`, `voting_method`, `polls_open_at`, `polls_close_at`, `hearing_requested_at`, `hearing_requested_by_party`, `decision_date` and `voter_list_due_at` are all still absent. A case may hold only one arrangement that is not `VOID`; attempting to open a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELECTION_AGREEMENT`. Opening the file is itself gated on neither `case_status` nor `case_posture` — the file may be opened before the showing has been verified — and only the branch transitions out of `PROPOSED` test the state of the case. Arrangement listings accept `petition_id` and `status` filters.

From `PROPOSED` the case forks, and the two branches differ in one consequential way: **they anchor the voter-list deadline on different instants.**

On the **stipulated branch**, a `BoardAgent` approves the arrangement, moving `status` from `PROPOSED` to `STIPULATED_APPROVED`. Approval requires the case to be `SOI_VERIFIED` with `case_posture` `OPEN` and `showing_verdict` already `SUFFICIENT`; an arrangement in any status other than `PROPOSED` is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATUS_INVALID`. The approving payload fixes the election, carrying `approved_election_date`, `voting_method`, `polls_open_at` and `polls_close_at`, and the closing instant must be strictly after the opening instant. Approval sets `route` to `STIPULATED` and computes `voter_list_due_at` as the end of the second business day after the date on which the approval itself happened — that is, the date component of the logical clock at approval, advanced by two business days, at 23:59:59Z. A stipulated approval landing on Thursday 2026-08-20 counts Friday the 21st and Monday the 24th, so the list is due at `2026-08-24T23:59:59Z`. Approval also schedules the balloting: an election round is created with `round_number` 1 and `status` `SCHEDULED`, copying `voting_method`, `election_date`, `polls_open_at` and `polls_close_at` from the approved arrangement. Finally, the case's `case_status` becomes `ELECTION_SET` and its `version` increments.

On the **hearing branch**, either the director or the `UnionRepresentative` refuses to stipulate and demands a representation hearing, supplying `hearing_requested_by_party` as `EMPLOYER` or `UNION`. The arrangement moves from `PROPOSED` to `HEARING_REQUESTED`, `hearing_requested_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the party is recorded. Nothing else happens: no election round is created and `voter_list_due_at` stays absent, because on this branch the deadline is deferred to the decision date rather than fixed at the approval instant. The demand requires the case to be `SOI_VERIFIED`.

The hearing branch resolves when a `BoardAgent` issues a Decision and Direction of Election, moving the arrangement from `HEARING_REQUESTED` to `DIRECTED`. The direction is only available once the hearing has actually resolved scope: **every** classification on the case must have a `scope_ruling` other than `PENDING`, and if even one remains pending the request fails with `ERR_SCOPE_RULINGS_INCOMPLETE`. The case must again be `SOI_VERIFIED` with posture `OPEN`. The directing payload carries the `decision_date` of the decision itself alongside the same four election fields — `approved_election_date`, `voting_method`, `polls_open_at` and `polls_close_at` — and again the closing instant must be strictly after the opening one. Direction sets `route` to `DIRECTED` and computes `voter_list_due_at` as the end of the second business day after `decision_date`, which may be earlier or later than the instant the direction was actually recorded. A decision dated Tuesday 2026-08-25 counts Wednesday the 26th and Thursday the 27th, giving `2026-08-27T23:59:59Z`. As on the stipulated branch, an election round with `round_number` 1 and `status` `SCHEDULED` is created, and the case moves to `ELECTION_SET` with its `version` incremented.

Both `PROPOSED` and `HEARING_REQUESTED` are exposed to the dismissal cascade of §3.3, which drives either of them to the terminal `VOID` state.

### 3.5. The Eligible Voter List: Building, Attesting, Grading and Curing

An eligible voter list is opened by a `LaborRelationsDirector` or an `HRDataAnalyst` against a case whose state the client has actually observed. The payload names the `petition_id`, the `election_agreement_id` of the approved or directed arrangement whose deadline governs, and the mandatory `expected_petition_version` described in §2.7. A successful create copies `due_at` from the arrangement's `voter_list_due_at`, starts the list at `status` `DRAFT`, and — this is the point of opening a fresh list — **supersedes** any list on the same case that is still `DRAFT` or `DEFICIENT`, moving each to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state where it keeps its entries and its `cure_deadline_at` for the record but can never be submitted again. A case that already holds a list in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` is a different matter: such a list cannot be superseded, and the create is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTER_LIST` — and because that test is applied across the whole case before any supersession is written, a rejected create leaves every sibling list exactly as it found it, including a `DRAFT` sitting alongside the filed one. Sibling lists already in `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED` neither block the create nor are disturbed by it. Opening a list carries no state precondition of its own: neither `case_status` nor `case_posture` is consulted, and neither is the named arrangement's own `status` — a list may be opened against an arrangement still in `PROPOSED` or already `VOID`, in which case there is no `voter_list_due_at` to copy and the new list's `due_at` is simply absent, which makes the deadline test at submission and the lateness test at grading vacuous rather than failing. Beyond `status` and `due_at`, a new list starts with `total_entry_count`, `complete_entry_count`, `eligible_voter_count` and `resubmission_count` all at 0 and `is_late` false, while `submitted_at`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `attested_by_title`, `completeness_pct`, `list_grade`, `cure_window_hours`, `cure_deadline_at`, `reviewed_at` and `noted_defects` are all absent. The petition is the only other row the create touches — its `version` advances by exactly 1 as §2.7 requires — while the named arrangement is left untouched. Lists may be listed by `petition_id`, by `election_agreement_id`, or by `status`.

Voters go onto the list one at a time, again by the director or the analyst. Each entry names the `voter_list_id` it joins and the `classification_id` giving the voter's job classification, and carries the voter's `full_name` and `hire_date`. The remainder of the payload is optional and is where the statutory content lives: `home_address`, `personal_email`, `personal_phone`, `shift_code` and `work_location`, plus a `termination_date` for a separated employee and an `on_leave_type` recording leave status on the eligibility date, which is one of `NONE`, `SICK`, `FMLA`, `MILITARY` or `PERMANENT_LAYOFF` and defaults to `NONE`. The five statutory content fields are counted: `missing_field_count` is the number of them that are absent, null or empty, so it runs from 0 to 5, and `is_statutory_content_complete` is true exactly when that count is 0. An entry may also be updated later — to cure missing statutory content or to correct employment data — and an update accepts `home_address`, `personal_email`, `personal_phone`, `shift_code`, `work_location`, `termination_date` and `on_leave_type`. Every create and every update re-runs the eligibility rules on the entry and recomputes the parent list's counters, and it matters which counters those are: `total_entry_count`, `complete_entry_count` and `eligible_voter_count` are recomputed from the entry rows as they now stand, while `completeness_pct`, `is_late` and `list_grade` are **not** — those three are written only by a submission or a resubmission, so an entry added to an already-graded list moves the raw counts and leaves the recorded grade stale until the list is filed again. Neither the create nor the update carries a state precondition of its own: an entry may be added to, or amended on, a list in any `status` whatever — `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DEFICIENT`, `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED` alike — on a case in any `case_status` and in any `case_posture`. The parent list is touched on every such write, so its `version` and `updated_at` advance even when no counter value actually changes, while the entry's own `version` stays at 1 through its create and advances only on a later update. On an update, a field the body omits is left exactly as it stands, whereas a field the body supplies as an explicit JSON `null` is **cleared** to absent: clearing one of the five statutory content fields raises `missing_field_count` accordingly, and clearing `on_leave_type` leaves it absent rather than returning it to `NONE`.

Eligibility is decided by six ordered rules, and the **first matching rule wins**; the ordering is not incidental, because a voter can satisfy several at once. First, if the voter's classification has a `scope_ruling` of `EXCLUDED`, the entry is `INELIGIBLE` with `ineligibility_reason` `OUTSIDE_UNIT`. Second, if `hire_date` falls after the case's `payroll_period_end`, the entry is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `HIRED_AFTER_PAYROLL_PERIOD`. Third, if `termination_date` is present and falls before the arrangement's `approved_election_date`, the entry is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `TERMINATED_BEFORE_ELECTION`. Fourth, if `on_leave_type` is `PERMANENT_LAYOFF`, the entry is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `PERMANENT_LAYOFF`. Fifth, if the voter's classification is still `PENDING`, the entry is `CHALLENGED`, meaning the ballot may be cast but is subject to challenge at the polls. Otherwise the entry is `ELIGIBLE` and `ineligibility_reason` is left absent. All three date tests are plain calendar-date comparisons on `YYYY-MM-DD` values with no timezone conversion, and both edges are generous to the voter: a `hire_date` exactly equal to `payroll_period_end` is eligible, because being hired on the cut-off day places the voter inside the period, and a `termination_date` exactly equal to `approved_election_date` is eligible, because the last day worked is election day. Leave of type `SICK`, `FMLA` or `MILITARY` never defeats eligibility. A classification whose `scope_ruling` is `INCLUDED` — or `MOOT`, which is where the dismissal cascade of §3.3 leaves a pending one — matches neither rule one nor rule five and therefore carries no eligibility consequence of its own. Rule three is vacuous whenever the date it compares against has not been written: when the list names an arrangement still in `PROPOSED`, and so carrying no `approved_election_date` yet, no `termination_date` can defeat eligibility however early it falls. Because rule one precedes rule two, a voter who sits in an `EXCLUDED` classification *and* was hired late reports `OUTSIDE_UNIT`, not `HIRED_AFTER_PAYROLL_PERIOD`. Missing statutory content is orthogonal to all of this: an entry missing only its `personal_email` has a `missing_field_count` of 1 and `is_statutory_content_complete` false, yet its eligibility outcome is unaffected. Entry listings may be filtered by `voter_list_id`, by `classification_id`, or by `eligibility_status`.

Filing the list is the director's act, and it is an attestation: the submission payload carries the `attested_by_title` of the attesting official and, when the list is going in late on purpose, an explicit `late_submission_ack`. Submission is only possible from `DRAFT`, requires the case to be in `ELECTION_SET` with posture `OPEN`, and requires `total_entry_count` to be at least 1 — a list with no entries cannot be graded and the attempt fails with `ERR_VOTER_LIST_EMPTY` (HTTP 422), a check that sits after the two state preconditions and ahead of the deadline test below, so an empty list filed past its `due_at` without an acknowledgement reports `ERR_VOTER_LIST_EMPTY` rather than `ERR_VOTER_LIST_PAST_DUE`. Unless the request carries `late_submission_ack` set to true, the logical clock must be at or before `due_at`; a submission past the deadline without that acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_VOTER_LIST_PAST_DUE`. On success the list moves to `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `submitted_by_user_id` records the attesting director's `X-User-ID`, and the title is stored.

Submission grades the list. `completeness_pct` is `complete_entry_count` multiplied by 100, divided by `total_entry_count`, rounded half-up to two decimals — where the complete-entry count is the number of entries whose `is_statutory_content_complete` is true. The base tier follows from the rounded percentage: exactly 100.00 is `COMPLIANT`, at least 90.00 is `MINOR_DEFECT`, at least 75.00 is `MATERIAL_DEFECT`, and anything below 75.00 is `GROSSLY_DEFICIENT`. Exactly 90.00 is a minor defect while 89.99 is a material one, and exactly 75.00 is a material defect. A percentage of 100.00 can only arise when every entry is complete, never from rounding up. `is_late` is true exactly when `submitted_at` falls after `due_at`; equality is on time, and one second later is late. A late list is then graded **one tier worse** than its base tier, where the tiers run in the order `COMPLIANT`, `MINOR_DEFECT`, `MATERIAL_DEFECT`, `GROSSLY_DEFICIENT`, and the outcome is stored in `list_grade`. Nine complete entries out of ten scores 90.00 and grades `MINOR_DEFECT` if on time, but `MATERIAL_DEFECT` if late. Grading also recomputes `eligible_voter_count`, the number of entries whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`.

Board review of a filed list is a `BoardAgent` disposition carrying `disposition` and optional `noted_defects` codes — an array of strings against which no vocabulary is enforced, so any codes the caller chooses are accepted and an empty array is accepted as well — and the three dispositions have different grade prerequisites. `ACCEPT` moves the list to `ACCEPTED` and is only available when `list_grade` is `COMPLIANT` or `MINOR_DEFECT`; it stamps `reviewed_at`, writes `noted_defects` only when the request actually supplies them and otherwise leaves whatever an earlier review recorded in place, and touches neither `cure_window_hours` nor `cure_deadline_at` — both are therefore still absent on a list accepted first time round, while an acceptance that closes a cure cycle **retains** the 48- or 24-hour window the earlier return-for-cure wrote rather than clearing it. `RETURN_FOR_CURE` moves the list to `DEFICIENT` and is only available when `list_grade` is `MINOR_DEFECT` or `MATERIAL_DEFECT`; it stamps `reviewed_at`, writes the noted defect codes unconditionally — so a return-for-cure that omits them clears any codes a previous review had recorded — and opens a cure window whose length is set by the grade — 48 hours for a minor defect, 24 hours for a material one — with `cure_deadline_at` computed as `reviewed_at` plus that many hours by exact hour arithmetic. A material defect reviewed at `2026-08-21T18:00:00Z` therefore expires at `2026-08-22T18:00:00Z`, a Saturday, because the cure clock ignores business days entirely. `REJECT` moves the list to the terminal `REJECTED` state and is only available when `list_grade` is `GROSSLY_DEFICIENT`; it stamps `reviewed_at`, writes the noted defects unconditionally in the same way, sets `cure_window_hours` to 0, and offers no cure at all — a replacement list must be opened. `cure_deadline_at` is left exactly as it stands rather than being cleared, so a list rejected at the end of a cure cycle keeps the deadline the earlier return-for-cure recorded even though its window is now 0. A disposition whose grade prerequisite is unmet, or a list not in `SUBMITTED`, is refused with `ERR_VOTER_LIST_STATUS_INVALID`.

The cure loop closes when the director resubmits, supplying `attested_by_title` again. Resubmission is only possible from `DEFICIENT`, only while the logical clock is at or before `cure_deadline_at` — later is `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` — and only while `resubmission_count` is still below its ceiling of 2, since a third cure attempt is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Those three are its only preconditions: unlike the original filing, a resubmission consults neither `case_status` nor `case_posture`, so a cured list may be re-filed on a case whose lifecycle has moved past `ELECTION_SET` and on a case a blocking charge has driven to `BLOCKED` or `IMPOUNDED`. A successful resubmission increments `resubmission_count`, re-stamps `submitted_at` and overwrites `submitted_by_user_id` and `attested_by_title` from the fresh request, returns the list to `SUBMITTED`, and re-grades `completeness_pct`, `is_late` and `list_grade` from the entry rows as they now stand — so curing entries between the review and the resubmission genuinely improves the grade, while the lateness test is applied afresh against the original `due_at`. `reviewed_at`, `noted_defects`, `cure_window_hours` and `cure_deadline_at` are all left exactly as the review wrote them, which is what keeps one cure window governing every attempt made under it. The `DEFICIENT`-to-`SUBMITTED`-to-`DEFICIENT` loop may therefore repeat up to that ceiling.

### 3.6. Supervisor Training and the Consequences of Prohibited Conduct

Front-line supervisors are the employer's largest legal exposure during a campaign, so the system tracks each of them individually. A `SupervisorTrainer` or a `LaborRelationsDirector` registers a supervisor against a case with a payload naming the `petition_id`, the supervisor's `full_name` and `job_title`, the `span_of_control` giving how many unit employees report to them, and `has_hire_fire_authority` recording whether they hold Section 2(11) hire-and-fire authority, which defaults to false. The span of control may not be negative. No case may hold two supervisors sharing both a name and a job title; the duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPERVISOR`. A newly registered supervisor begins at `training_status` `UNTRAINED`, with `modules_completed` and `quiz_score` absent, `training_attempt_count` and `violation_count` at 0, and no `certified_at` or `last_violation_code`. Supervisors may be listed by `petition_id` or by `training_status`.

Training is recorded by the `SupervisorTrainer` and only by them. Each attempt supplies the `modules_completed` codes covered and the `quiz_score` achieved, a value from 0 to 100. `modules_completed` is schema-checked only as an array of strings: codes beyond the three the system requires are neither rejected nor credited with anything, an unrecognized code is carried on the record exactly as supplied, and an empty array is accepted and simply fails the completeness test. Recording an attempt is gated on neither `case_status` nor `case_posture`. Certification is granted only when the attempt is complete on both counts: `modules_completed` must contain every one of the three required module codes — `TIPS_BOUNDARIES`, `PROHIBITED_CONDUCT` and `SOLICITATION_RULES` — and `quiz_score` must be at least 80. A qualifying attempt increments `training_attempt_count`, records the score and the modules, stamps `certified_at` with the logical clock, and moves `training_status` from `UNTRAINED` to `TRAINED`. An attempt that falls short — either because a required module is missing or because the score is below 80 — is recorded rather than rejected: it is a self-loop that increments `training_attempt_count`, records the `quiz_score` and the `modules_completed` array exactly as a qualifying attempt would, and withholds certification, leaving `training_status` and any `certified_at` already on the record untouched. The attempt counter, the recorded score and the recorded module list are therefore all durable effects of a failed attempt; what a failure never touches is `training_status`, so a failing attempt by a supervisor who is already `TRAINED` does **not** demote them — only a substantiated prohibited-conduct finding does that. A qualifying attempt by an already-`TRAINED` supervisor is likewise a self-loop: it re-stamps `certified_at` with the current logical clock, increments the attempt counter, and leaves the status at `TRAINED`.

Certification can be lost. `EmploymentCounsel` records a prohibited-conduct finding against a supervisor with a payload carrying the `violation_code` — one of `THREAT`, `INTERROGATION`, `PROMISE_OF_BENEFIT`, `SURVEILLANCE` or `SOLICITATION_RULE_DISPARITY` — and a `substantiated` flag, which is required rather than optional. A finding recorded with `substantiated` false is a complete no-op: the call answers HTTP 200 with the supervisor exactly as it stood, `violation_count` and `last_violation_code` are untouched, and the row's `version` and `updated_at` do not advance at all. A substantiated finding against a `TRAINED` supervisor fires a three-hop cascade. First, `violation_count` increments, `last_violation_code` is written, `certified_at` is cleared to absent, and `training_status` returns to `UNTRAINED`. That first hop is not itself conditional on certification: a substantiated finding against a supervisor who is already `UNTRAINED` still increments `violation_count` and still writes `last_violation_code`, but it fires neither of the two hops below, so messages naming that supervisor are left where they are and `retraining_required` is not raised. Second, because the presenter has lost certification, every campaign communication naming this supervisor in `presenter_supervisor_id` whose `status` is `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` becomes `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `PRESENTER_DECERTIFIED`. Third, because at least one message was blocked for decertification, the case's `retraining_required` becomes true — and the petition row is touched, advancing its `version` and `updated_at`, only on that transition, so a later decertification on a case whose `retraining_required` is already true leaves the petition entirely untouched. The supervisor may afterwards train again and return to `TRAINED` — and doing so does **not** unblock the messages, which stay blocked permanently for that reason.

There is one further restriction that follows a supervisor around the case rather than living on their own record: a supervisor holding Section 2(11) hire-and-fire authority may never be seated as an election observer, which §3.8 returns to.

### 3.7. Campaign Communications: Accuracy Review, Risk Scoring and Legal Clearance

Every campaign message is drafted by a `LaborRelationsDirector`, and the draft is where the message's shape is fixed. The payload names the `petition_id`, the `channel` — one of `EMAIL`, `LETTER`, `DIGITAL_POST`, `SMALL_GROUP_MEETING` or `CAPTIVE_AUDIENCE_MEETING` — the `audience_scope`, which is `UNIT_WIDE`, `CLASSIFICATION` or `INDIVIDUAL`, the `planned_delivery_at` instant at which delivery or presentation begins, an optional `duration_minutes` defaulting to 60 and never below 1, and a `message_summary`. A `presenter_supervisor_id` names the presenting supervisor: it is required for the two meeting channels and forbidden otherwise, and both halves of that rule are enforced as schema checks reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) — a meeting channel that omits the field fails, and a non-meeting channel that supplies one fails too rather than having the value silently ignored. Because that pairing belongs to step 5 of §2.11, it is reported ahead of the resolution of `petition_id` and `presenter_supervisor_id` themselves, so a draft naming `EMAIL` with a presenter *and* a `petition_id` matching no row reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND`. The reviewer's characterization of the content also travels with the draft as optional fields: `threat_statement_count`, the number of statements predicting adverse consequences, defaulting to 0; `contains_interrogation`, true when the message solicits employees' union sympathies; `contains_promise_of_benefit`, true when it promises a benefit conditioned on the vote; `contains_surveillance_reference`, true when it references watching protected activity; and the two claim counters `material_claim_count` and `unsupported_material_claim_count`, both defaulting to 0 and neither permitted to be negative. The two are not cross-checked against one another at draft time — a draft whose unsupported count exceeds its material count is accepted, since the consistency test belongs to the accuracy review alone. A draft starts at `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, `risk_score` 0, `peerless_plywood_violation` false and `escalation_ack` false, records the drafting user's `X-User-ID` in `authored_by_user_id`, and leaves `reviewed_by_user_id`, `accuracy_reviewed_at`, `delivered_at` and `blocked_reason` absent.

Drafting a meeting-channel message also reserves the presenter's calendar, and the reservation is padded on both sides by a 60-minute travel buffer. Two meeting-channel messages sharing a `presenter_supervisor_id` may not overlap on the interval running from one hour before `planned_delivery_at` to one hour after the delivery instant plus `duration_minutes`. That interval is **closed at both ends**, so two reservations that merely touch at an endpoint still conflict, and the collision is reported as `ERR_PRESENTER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The slot is held by **every** existing meeting-channel message naming that presenter, whatever its `status`: a `DRAFT`, a `REVISION_REQUIRED`, a `BLOCKED` and even a `DELIVERED` message each still occupy the calendar, and nothing ever releases a slot once taken. Messages on the three non-meeting channels hold no slot and are never consulted. The comparison runs before the new row is written, so a draft is never compared against itself and a rejected draft leaves nothing behind. Message listings may be filtered by `petition_id`, by `presenter_supervisor_id` or by `status`, subject to the self-scoping rule of §2.1.

The factual-accuracy review is performed by the director or by an `HRDataAnalyst`, and it is the step that computes the message's risk. The request supplies `material_claim_count`, the number of material factual claims the reviewer counted, and `unsupported_material_claim_count`, the number the reviewer could not source; if the unsupported count exceeds the material count, the request fails with `ERR_CLAIM_COUNTS_INCONSISTENT` (HTTP 422). Both counts are required on this request rather than optional, and neither may be negative. The review carries no state precondition and no posture gate at all: it may be run against a message in any `status`, including one already `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `BLOCKED` or `DELIVERED`, and it may be run as often as the reviewer likes. The review stamps `accuracy_reviewed_at` with the logical clock and writes `risk_score` and `peerless_plywood_violation`, each call recomputing all three from scratch and overwriting the two stored claim counts, while `status`, `revision_round`, `escalation_ack`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `blocked_reason` are left exactly as they stand. The `polls_open_at` the per-se rule measures against is read from the case's highest-numbered election round; a case holding no round at all offers nothing to measure against, so `peerless_plywood_violation` is written false and `risk_score` takes its ordinary additive value.

`risk_score` is built additively from an exact-integer base rate determined by channel — 5 for `EMAIL`, 5 for `LETTER`, 10 for `DIGITAL_POST`, 20 for `SMALL_GROUP_MEETING` and 30 for `CAPTIVE_AUDIENCE_MEETING` — to which the following are added: 15 for each threat statement counted in `threat_statement_count`; 12 if `contains_interrogation` is true; 10 if `contains_promise_of_benefit` is true; 8 if `contains_surveillance_reference` is true; 6 for each unsupported material claim **beyond the first**, so a single unsupported claim adds nothing while two add 6 and four add 18; and 5 if `audience_scope` is `UNIT_WIDE`. The accumulated total is then clamped to a maximum of 100, once, after every additive term rather than term by term. A small-group meeting with two threat statements, an interrogation, a surveillance reference, classification-level scope and one unsupported claim therefore scores 20 plus 30 plus 12 plus 8, which is 70; a captive-audience meeting with three threats, an interrogation, a promise, a surveillance reference, four unsupported claims and unit-wide scope accumulates 128 and is clamped to 100.

`peerless_plywood_violation` is a per-se rule evaluated independently of the additive score. It is true exactly when the `channel` is `CAPTIVE_AUDIENCE_MEETING` **and** `planned_delivery_at` falls inside the 24 hours immediately before the current round's `polls_open_at` — an interval that is closed at its lower edge and open at its upper, so a delivery planned exactly 24 hours before the polls open is inside the blackout while one planned exactly at the opening instant is not. When the per-se rule fires, `risk_score` is set to 100 outright. A message on a non-meeting channel planned three hours before the polls open carries no per-se violation at all, because the rule turns on the channel.

Two numeric gates then govern the message's onward path. A `risk_score` of 90 or more is **prohibited**: the message may not even enter legal review, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RISK_SCORE_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422). Exactly 90 is prohibited rather than merely elevated. A `risk_score` of 70 or more is **elevated**: the message may enter review, but counsel may only approve it when the approving request carries `escalation_ack` set to true, and approval without it is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_ACK_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Exactly 70 requires the acknowledgement; 69 does not.

Sending a message into legal review is the director's act and moves it from `DRAFT` — or from `REVISION_REQUIRED` — to `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW`. Several prerequisites are checked. The message must have been accuracy-reviewed: `accuracy_reviewed_at` must hold a value, and if it does not the request fails with `ERR_ACCURACY_REVIEW_MISSING`. `risk_score` must hold a value and must be below 90. If the `channel` is `SMALL_GROUP_MEETING` or `CAPTIVE_AUDIENCE_MEETING`, the presenting supervisor's `training_status` must be `TRAINED`, and an untrained presenter is refused with `ERR_PRESENTER_NOT_TRAINED`. When the message is coming back around from `REVISION_REQUIRED`, two further conditions apply: `revision_round` must still be below its ceiling of 3, and the accuracy review must be **fresh** — `accuracy_reviewed_at` must be at or after the instant of the last legal review, since an edited message has to be re-reviewed for accuracy before it can be re-submitted, and a stale review is again `ERR_ACCURACY_REVIEW_MISSING`. Entering review clears `reviewed_by_user_id`; no counter changes.

Legal review is dispositioned by `EmploymentCounsel`, subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1, with a payload carrying `disposition`, an optional `escalation_ack` and an optional `review_note` — the note is schema-checked as a string, may be empty, and is then discarded, appearing nowhere on the stored resource. A disposition of `APPROVE` moves the message to `APPROVED` and records the acting user in `reviewed_by_user_id`; it additionally requires the escalation acknowledgement described above when the score is elevated. That gate reads the `escalation_ack` carried on **this** request and never a value some earlier disposition stored, so an approval that omits the field is refused even when the row's stored `escalation_ack` is already true; and the field is written onto the row only when the request actually supplies it, on either branch, so a `REQUEST_REVISION` carrying `escalation_ack` true records it even though the acknowledgement gates nothing there. A disposition of `REQUEST_REVISION` moves the message to `REVISION_REQUIRED`, increments `revision_round` and records the reviewer; it is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` once `revision_round` has already reached 3. Either way the message must currently be in `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW`, and a message in any other status is refused with `ERR_COMMUNICATION_STATUS_INVALID`. The per-se blackout test belongs to the disposition as a whole rather than to the approval branch: a message whose `peerless_plywood_violation` is true is refused with `ERR_PEERLESS_PLYWOOD_WINDOW` before the requested disposition is consulted at all, so counsel can no more return such a message for revision than approve it. The revision loop therefore runs `DRAFT` to `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW` to `REVISION_REQUIRED` and back to `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW`, up to three completed rounds.

Delivery is confirmed by the director and moves an `APPROVED` message to the terminal `DELIVERED` state, stamping `delivered_at` with the logical clock. Delivery requires the case's `case_posture` to be `OPEN`, requires that the message carry no per-se blackout violation — both the stored `peerless_plywood_violation` flag must be false **and** the per-se rule must not fire when it is re-evaluated at this moment against the round then in force, so that a captive-audience message whose `planned_delivery_at` sits inside that round's blackout is refused with `ERR_PEERLESS_PLYWOOD_WINDOW` even when the flag recorded by the accuracy review is false because no round existed when that review ran — requires — for a `CAPTIVE_AUDIENCE_MEETING` and for that channel alone — that the logical clock not sit inside the current round's blackout, a message on any of the other four channels being free to go out inside that window, and requires the campaign period to still be open — the current round must be `SCHEDULED` or `POLLS_OPEN` and the logical clock must be strictly before that round's `polls_close_at`, an instant exactly equal to `polls_close_at` therefore being outside the window rather than inside it. A delivery attempted after the polls have closed, against a round that has moved past `POLLS_OPEN`, or on a case that holds no election round at all, is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Four distinct events can put a message into `BLOCKED`, and `blocked_reason` records which: `PRESENTER_DECERTIFIED` when the presenting supervisor loses certification, `RERUN_BLACKOUT` when a sustained objection directs a rerun, `POSTURE_BLOCKED` when a blocking charge changes the case posture, and `CASE_DISMISSED` when the showing-of-interest audit dismisses the case. The four are not equally permanent. Only `POSTURE_BLOCKED` is reversible: when the case's posture returns to `OPEN`, a message blocked for that reason returns to `APPROVED` with `blocked_reason` cleared. `RERUN_BLACKOUT`, `PRESENTER_DECERTIFIED` and `CASE_DISMISSED` are permanent for that row. A message blocked out of `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW` keeps its `revision_round` intact.

### 3.8. Running the Election: Sessions, Observers, Ballots and Tallies

An election round is never created directly by a client. Rounds appear as consequences: one is created when an arrangement is stipulated or an election is directed, and another when a rerun is scheduled. Each round records the `petition_id` of its case, the `election_agreement_id` of the arrangement that produced it, its `round_number` — 1 for the original election and one higher for each rerun — and, on a rerun, the `prior_round_id` of the set-aside round it replaces, which is absent on round 1. It also carries the `voting_method`, `election_date`, `polls_open_at` and `polls_close_at` copied from the arrangement or supplied with the rerun. However it comes into existence, a round is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `ballots_impounded` and `determinative_challenges` both false and with `scheduled_session_count`, `closed_session_count`, `ballots_cast_total`, `provisional_votes_for`, `provisional_votes_against`, `provisional_void_count`, `provisional_margin`, `challenged_open_count`, `challenged_counted_for_count`, `challenged_counted_against_count`, `challenged_voided_count` and `challenged_withdrawn_count` all at 0, while `revised_votes_for`, `revised_votes_against`, `revised_void_count`, `revised_margin`, `valid_votes_cast`, `majority_threshold`, `tally_posted_at`, `tally_revised_at` and `objection_window_close_at` are absent until the tally workflow writes them. Rounds move through `SCHEDULED`, `SUSPENDED`, `POLLS_OPEN`, `POLLS_CLOSED`, `TALLY_POSTED` and `TALLY_REVISED`, with `SET_ASIDE` and `ROUND_CERTIFIED` terminal. Rounds may be listed by `petition_id`, by `election_agreement_id`, by `status`, or by `prior_round_id` to find the rerun that replaced a given round.

A `BoardAgent` lays out the polling. Each session names the `election_round_id` it belongs to, the `location_code` of the polling location, the `session_start_at` and `session_end_at` bounding the session — the closing instant strictly after the opening one — the `method` by which ballots are cast at that session, either `MANUAL` or `MAIL`, and the mandatory `expected_election_round_version` of §2.7. Sessions begin at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `votes_for_count`, `votes_against_count`, `void_count`, `challenged_ballot_count`, `employer_observer_count` and `union_observer_count` all at 0, an empty `observer_roster`, and no `closed_at`. A session may only be laid out against a round that is `SCHEDULED`; a round that a blocking charge has suspended, or one whose polls have already opened, is refused with `ERR_ROUND_NOT_SCHEDULED`.

The polling location is a physical resource, and sessions at one location must not collide. Occupancy of a location extends 30 minutes past a session's close to allow for teardown, so two sessions at the same `location_code` on the same round may not overlap on the interval running from `session_start_at` up to but not including `session_end_at` plus 30 minutes. That upper edge is open, which means a session may begin **exactly** 30 minutes after the previous one ends but not one second earlier; a collision is reported as `ERR_POLL_SESSION_LOCATION_CONFLICT`. Only sessions in `SCHEDULED` or `CLOSED` occupy a location: a session that a blocking charge cancelled releases its location immediately, so a replacement session may be laid out over precisely the window the cancelled one held. A successful layout raises the round's `scheduled_session_count` by 1. Sessions may be listed by `election_round_id` or by `status`.

Observers are seated one at a time, and three roles may do the seating — the `LaborRelationsDirector` for the employer, the `UnionRepresentative` for the union, and a `BoardAgent` in either capacity; that pairing describes practice rather than a check, because the `party` an observer serves is read from the request body and is never compared against the acting role, so any of the three authorized roles may in fact seat an observer for either party. The request names the `party` the observer serves, the `observer_name`, and optionally the `supervisor_id` of a registered supervisor being seated. Seating is gated on neither the session's own `status` nor its round's, so an observer may still be designated on a session that has already `CLOSED` or that a blocking charge `CANCELLED`. Two rules bound the roster. A supervisor holding Section 2(11) hire-and-fire authority may never be seated: naming such a supervisor is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISOR_OBSERVER_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422). And each party is capped at three observers per session, counted separately for the employer and the union, so a fourth designation for the same party is refused with `ERR_OBSERVER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful designation increments either `employer_observer_count` or `union_observer_count` according to the party, and appends an entry to `observer_roster` recording the party, the observer name and the supervisor identifier, in designation order.

Opening the polls advances a round from `SCHEDULED` to `POLLS_OPEN` and is a `BoardAgent` act. Four conditions must hold beyond the posture gate: the case must hold a voter list in `ACCEPTED` status, and if it does not the request is refused with `ERR_VOTER_LIST_NOT_ACCEPTED`; the round's `scheduled_session_count` must be at least 1, and a round carrying no scheduled session is refused with `ERR_ROUND_STATUS_INVALID` rather than with a session-specific code — a code that is nevertheless tested here, in the order these four conditions are listed, and so *after* the accepted-list requirement, meaning a round with neither an accepted list nor a scheduled session reports `ERR_VOTER_LIST_NOT_ACCEPTED`; and the logical clock must have reached the round's `polls_open_at` — an instant exactly equal to it counts as reached — since opening early is refused with `ERR_POLLS_NOT_YET_OPEN`. Opening changes no counters, and it moves neither dimension of the case — `case_status` and `case_posture` are both left exactly as they stand — it simply makes the sessions closable with results.

Closing a session is where ballots enter the record. The closing payload carries the unchallenged counts observed at that session: `votes_for_count`, `votes_against_count` and `void_count`. The session must be `SCHEDULED`, its round must be `POLLS_OPEN`, the logical clock must have reached `session_end_at`, and neither party's observer count on the session may exceed three. The posture gate of §2.5 is applied to the case reached through the session's round, since a session names no petition of its own. The four preconditions are then tested in the order just given, which settles two collisions: a session that is not `SCHEDULED` reports its own `ERR_POLL_SESSION_STATUS_INVALID` even when its round has also moved past `POLLS_OPEN` and would have earned `ERR_ROUND_STATUS_INVALID`, and a clock that has not yet reached `session_end_at` is likewise reported as `ERR_POLL_SESSION_STATUS_INVALID` rather than as a distinct window code, while an over-seated party is `ERR_OBSERVER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Closing moves the session to the terminal `CLOSED` state, stamps `closed_at`, records the three counts, raises the round's `closed_session_count` by 1 and lowers its `scheduled_session_count` by 1. A session that is already closed, or one that was cancelled, is refused with `ERR_POLL_SESSION_STATUS_INVALID`.

When the closing of a session takes the round's `scheduled_session_count` to 0 and at least one session has closed, the round itself moves from `POLLS_OPEN` to `POLLS_CLOSED` within that same request, and the case's `case_status` becomes `VOTING_CLOSED`. At that point the round accumulates the closed sessions' figures: `provisional_votes_for`, `provisional_votes_against` and `provisional_void_count` are summed from the `CLOSED` sessions, and `ballots_cast_total` records every ballot that entered the box across the round — precisely, the sum of `votes_for_count`, `votes_against_count` and `void_count` over the round's `CLOSED` sessions, plus `challenged_ballot_count` summed over **every** session on the round whatever its status, so a segregated ballot left on a cancelled session still counts. The three provisional sums and `ballots_cast_total` are re-derived from the sessions at this moment rather than accumulated session by session.

Challenged ballots are segregated as they are cast, by a `BoardAgent`. Each names the `poll_session_id` at which the ballot was challenged, optionally the `voter_list_entry_id` of the voter whose ballot is being set aside, the `challenger_party` — `EMPLOYER`, `UNION` or `BOARD` — and the `basis` for the challenge, which is one of `SUPERVISOR`, `NOT_ON_LIST`, `OUTSIDE_UNIT`, `IDENTITY` or `LAID_OFF`. A segregated ballot begins with `resolution` `OPEN`, `reopen_count` 0 and no `resolved_at`; it raises the session's `challenged_ballot_count` by 1, the round's `challenged_open_count` by 1, and the round's `ballots_cast_total` by 1, because a challenged ballot is a real ballot that entered the box. Segregation is gated on neither the session's `status` nor the round's, so a ballot may still be recorded against a session that has `CLOSED` or been `CANCELLED`, and against a round that has already reached `POLLS_CLOSED`, `TALLY_POSTED` or `TALLY_REVISED`, the counters and the ballot conservation identity moving with it in every case. When a `voter_list_entry_id` is supplied it must name an entry sitting on a voter list in `ACCEPTED` status for the same case as the session's round; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_BALLOT_VOTER_NOT_ON_ACCEPTED_LIST`. The same voter entry may not be challenged twice at the same session, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHALLENGED_BALLOT`; the test considers every ballot already segregated at that session whatever its current `resolution`, so an entry whose earlier challenge was withdrawn or voided still blocks a second one, while ballots that name no `voter_list_entry_id` at all are never compared and may be segregated at one session without limit. Challenged ballots may be listed by `poll_session_id`, by `voter_list_entry_id` or by `resolution`.

Resolving a segregated ballot is possible only while its round is `TALLY_POSTED` or `TALLY_REVISED`, and only from `OPEN`; a ballot that has already been dispositioned is refused with `ERR_BALLOT_STATUS_INVALID`. On both resolving and reopening the ballot's own state is tested before the round's, so a ballot in the wrong state reports `ERR_BALLOT_STATUS_INVALID` rather than the `ERR_ROUND_STATUS_INVALID` its round would also have earned. The payload carries the `resolution` and, for one of the four outcomes, a `withdrawing_party`. `COUNTED_FOR` and `COUNTED_AGAINST` place the ballot in a vote column, `VOIDED` voids it, and `WITHDRAWN` records that the challenging party withdrew the challenge and the ballot was returned unopened — for which the `withdrawing_party` supplied must be the same party recorded in `challenger_party`, a mismatch being reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) and tested only once both state checks have passed, while a `withdrawing_party` sent alongside any of the other three resolutions is checked against the party enumeration and then ignored. Every resolution stamps `resolved_at`, lowers the round's `challenged_open_count` by 1, and raises exactly one of `challenged_counted_for_count`, `challenged_counted_against_count`, `challenged_voided_count` or `challenged_withdrawn_count` by 1.

A resolution can be undone. Reopening a ballot, which may carry an optional `reopen_reason` — accepted and schema-checked but not stored on the ballot and so never returned in any response — returns `resolution` to `OPEN`, clears `resolved_at`, increments `reopen_count`, decrements whichever bucket counter the prior resolution had raised, and raises `challenged_open_count` by 1 — the exact inverse of the resolution, so the ballot conservation identity of §2.8 holds identically before and after. Reopening is only possible while the round is `TALLY_POSTED` or `TALLY_REVISED`, only on a ballot that is not `OPEN`, and only while `reopen_count` is still below its ceiling of 2; a third reopen is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Posting the provisional tally is a `BoardAgent` act on a round in `POLLS_CLOSED`. It is refused with `ERR_BALLOTS_IMPOUNDED` while the round's `ballots_impounded` flag is true, and refused with `ERR_TALLY_RECONCILIATION_FAILED` (HTTP 422) if the sessions' ballot counts do not reconcile with the round's `ballots_cast_total`, that reconciliation total being recomputed by exactly the rule the round's own accumulation used: the three unchallenged counts summed over the `CLOSED` sessions plus `challenged_ballot_count` summed over every session on the round. The posting computes `provisional_margin` as `provisional_votes_for` less `provisional_votes_against`, which may be negative; `valid_votes_cast` as `provisional_votes_for` plus `provisional_votes_against`, excluding void ballots; and `majority_threshold` as the valid votes cast divided by two, discarding any fractional part, then increased by 1 — so 81 valid votes and 80 valid votes both give a threshold of 41. It also computes `determinative_challenges`, which is true exactly when `challenged_open_count` is greater than or equal to the absolute value of `provisional_margin`: an equal count is determinative, and a margin of 0 makes any single open challenge determinative. `tally_posted_at` is stamped, the case's `case_status` becomes `TALLY_POSTED`, and the objection window opens, with `objection_window_close_at` set to the end of the seventh business day after the tally date. A tally posted on Wednesday 2026-09-02 skips the Labor Day holiday on 2026-09-07 and closes the window at `2026-09-14T23:59:59Z`. Because challenged ballots sit inside `ballots_cast_total` but outside `valid_votes_cast`, the provisional threshold is computed on a smaller base than the revised one will be. As a worked case: sessions closing with 30 for, 25 against and 1 void, and 12 for, 14 against and no void, give provisional figures of 42, 39 and 1, a margin of 3, valid votes cast of 81 and a threshold of 41 — and with three challenged ballots still open, three is at least three, so the challenges are determinative.

Revising the tally folds the resolved challenges in, and it may only run when `challenged_open_count` has reached 0; while any ballot is still open the request is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGES_UNRESOLVED`. `revised_votes_for` is `provisional_votes_for` plus `challenged_counted_for_count`; `revised_votes_against` is `provisional_votes_against` plus `challenged_counted_against_count`; `revised_void_count` is `provisional_void_count` plus `challenged_voided_count`. `revised_margin` is the revised votes for less the revised votes against, `valid_votes_cast` is recomputed as the revised votes for plus the revised votes against, and `majority_threshold` is recomputed from that new base by the same halve-discard-add-one rule. Withdrawn challenges never enter any vote column, though they remain inside `ballots_cast_total`. `determinative_challenges` is **not** recomputed — it is a historical finding about the provisional margin and keeps its provisional value. `tally_revised_at` is stamped and the round moves to `TALLY_REVISED`, while the case's own `case_status` is left untouched — unlike the provisional posting, revising writes nothing to the petition, so a case sitting at `TALLY_POSTED` or at `OBJECTIONS_PENDING` stays there. Crucially, `objection_window_close_at` is **not** recomputed either: the objection window continues to run from the provisional posting date. Continuing the worked case, resolving those three open ballots as counted-for, counted-for and voided gives revised figures of 44, 39 and 2, a margin of 5, valid votes cast of 83 and a threshold of 42 — while `ballots_cast_total` stays at 85 throughout every resolution and reopen. Revising is also a self-loop: a round already in `TALLY_REVISED` may be revised again after a ballot has been reopened and re-resolved, overwriting the revised fields and advancing `tally_revised_at`, so a corrected tally can converge. A round in any other status is refused with `ERR_ROUND_STATUS_INVALID`.

### 3.9. Objections, Blocking Charges, Reruns and Certification

Once a provisional tally is posted, either the `LaborRelationsDirector` or the `UnionRepresentative` may object to the conduct of the election. An objection names the `election_round_id` whose conduct is at issue, optionally the `related_supervisor_id` of the supervisor whose conduct is alleged — absent for non-conduct grounds — the `filed_by_party` (`EMPLOYER` or `UNION`), the `ground_code`, which is one of `PROHIBITED_SUPERVISOR_CONDUCT`, `CAPTIVE_AUDIENCE_TIMING`, `LIST_DEFICIENCY`, `POLLING_IRREGULARITY` or `THIRD_PARTY_CONDUCT`, the `conduct_occurred_at` instant, the `alleged_affected_voter_count` of voters the filer says the conduct reached, which may not be negative, and a narrative `description`. The system stamps `filed_at` with the logical clock. Filing is only possible while the objected round is `TALLY_POSTED` or `TALLY_REVISED` and while the logical clock is at or before that round's `objection_window_close_at`; a late filing is refused with `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

A new objection starts at `status` `FILED` with `rerun_directed` false and no `ruled_at` or `disposition_rationale`, and the system immediately computes `affects_results`: it is true exactly when `alleged_affected_voter_count` is greater than or equal to the absolute value of the objected round's `provisional_margin`. Filing raises the case's `pending_objection_count` by 1, and any objection accepted while the case reads `TALLY_POSTED` moves `case_status` to `OBJECTIONS_PENDING` — not merely the first one, since a case that returned to `TALLY_POSTED` after an earlier objection was overruled moves across again on the next filing; a `case_status` reading anything else, `OBJECTIONS_PENDING` included, is left exactly where it stands. Objections may be listed by `election_round_id`, by `related_supervisor_id` or by `status`.

Ruling on an objection belongs to a `BoardAgent`, who supplies a `disposition` of `SUSTAIN` or `OVERRULE` together with the `disposition_rationale`. An objection that has already been ruled is refused with `ERR_OBJECTION_ALREADY_RULED`. An objection may only be sustained when `affects_results` is true — conduct that could not have changed the outcome may not set an election aside, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_OBJECTION_NOT_DETERMINATIVE` — and only while the objected round is `TALLY_POSTED` or `TALLY_REVISED`. Determinativeness is the earlier of those two tests, so an attempt to sustain a non-determinative objection whose round has already been set aside reports `ERR_OBJECTION_NOT_DETERMINATIVE` rather than `ERR_ROUND_STATUS_INVALID`.

Overruling is the quiet path: it stamps `ruled_at` and the rationale, moves the objection to the terminal `OVERRULED` state, and lowers the case's `pending_objection_count` by 1 — though never below 0, so a sibling objection overruled after another on the same round was sustained finds the count already reset to 0 and leaves it at 0. Overruling itself demands no particular round status, so an objection sitting on a round that has since become `SET_ASIDE` may still be overruled. When that count reaches 0 with no objection on the round having been sustained, the case returns from `OBJECTIONS_PENDING` to `TALLY_POSTED`, so certification may proceed.

Sustaining is the loud path, and it fires a four-hop cascade inside the same request. First, the objection moves to the terminal `SUSTAINED` state, `rerun_directed` becomes true, and `ruled_at` and the rationale are written. Second, because the objection is sustained, the objected round moves to the terminal `SET_ASIDE` state; its provisional and revised tallies stay readable for the record but the round can never be certified. Third, because the round has been set aside, the case's `case_status` becomes `RERUN_DIRECTED` and its `pending_objection_count` is reset to 0. Fourth, two collections react to the case entering `RERUN_DIRECTED`: every voter list on the case whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` becomes `SUPERSEDED` — all of them, not merely the most recently accepted one — because a rerun requires a brand-new list, and every campaign communication whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PENDING_LEGAL_REVIEW` becomes `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `RERUN_BLACKOUT`.

Scheduling the rerun closes the cycle. A `BoardAgent` acting on a case in `RERUN_DIRECTED` with posture `OPEN` supplies the `rerun_election_date`, the `voting_method`, and the `polls_open_at` and `polls_close_at` instants for the fresh balloting. The system creates the next election round with `round_number` one higher than the set-aside round's, with `prior_round_id` pointing at the set-aside round, with the same `election_agreement_id` that round named, and with `status` `SCHEDULED`; the round it builds on is the highest-numbered `SET_ASIDE` round on the case, falling back to the highest-numbered round of any status should none have been set aside; the case returns to `ELECTION_SET`, and the whole layout-polling-tally-objection arc runs again from the beginning, including the requirement for a newly built and newly accepted voter list.

Running alongside all of this is the unfair labor practice track, which drives the case's second state dimension. Either the `UnionRepresentative` or the `LaborRelationsDirector` may docket a charge, naming the `petition_id`, optionally the `related_objection_id` of an objection the charge is consolidated with, the `charging_party` (`UNION`, `EMPLOYER` or `INDIVIDUAL`), the `charged_party` (`EMPLOYER` or `UNION`), the `section_code` alleged, which is one of `8A1`, `8A3`, `8A5` or `8B1A`, an `allegation_summary`, the `conduct_occurred_at` instant, and `blocking_requested`, which defaults to false and expresses whether the charging party asks that the election be blocked. A charge starts at `status` `FILED` with `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock and with `remedy_code`, `settlement_type`, `notice_posting_days`, `determined_at` and `settled_at` all absent. Charges may be listed by `petition_id`, by `related_objection_id` or by `status`.

A charge that does **not** request blocking simply sits on the record — and so does one that requests blocking on a case already `DISMISSED` or `CERTIFIED`: `blocking_requested` is still stored as true on the charge, but the charge is never counted as a blocking one, no posture cascade runs, and its later dismissal or settlement therefore leaves `blocking_charge_count` untouched and fires no unblock chain. A charge that **does** request blocking, docketed against a case whose `case_status` is neither `DISMISSED` nor `CERTIFIED`, raises the case's `blocking_charge_count` by 1 and changes the posture — and which posture it produces depends on how far the balloting has got. If the case's highest-numbered round is *not* in `POLLS_CLOSED`, `TALLY_POSTED` or `TALLY_REVISED`, the posture becomes `BLOCKED`, and three further hops follow: because the posture is `BLOCKED`, the highest-numbered round in `SCHEDULED` becomes `SUSPENDED`; because that round is suspended, each of its poll sessions in `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED` and the round's `scheduled_session_count` is recomputed to 0; and, fanning out from the posture change itself, every campaign communication on the case whose `status` is `APPROVED` becomes `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `POSTURE_BLOCKED`. If instead the highest-numbered round *is* in `POLLS_CLOSED`, `TALLY_POSTED` or `TALLY_REVISED` — ballots are already cast — the posture becomes `IMPOUNDED` and that round's `ballots_impounded` flag becomes true, which is what makes a subsequent tally posting fail. In neither case does `case_status` move; the two dimensions are genuinely independent.

Disposing of a charge is what releases the hold. A `BoardAgent` determines the charge with a `determination` of `MERIT` or `DISMISS`, optionally supplying a `remedy_code` and `notice_posting_days`. A determination of `MERIT` requires a `remedy_code` — one of `NOTICE_POSTING`, `REINSTATEMENT`, `BARGAINING_ORDER` or `RERUN_ELECTION` — stamps `determined_at`, records the remedy, and moves the charge to `MERIT_FOUND`. Critically, a merit finding does **not** lower `blocking_charge_count`: a meritorious blocking charge keeps the case `BLOCKED` or `IMPOUNDED` until it is settled. When the remedy is `NOTICE_POSTING` **and** `notice_posting_days` was actually supplied, the case's `notice_posting_end_at` is set to `determined_at` plus that many days — including when the value supplied is 0, which lands the end instant on `determined_at` itself; omitting `notice_posting_days` leaves `notice_posting_end_at` untouched, and no remedy code other than `NOTICE_POSTING` ever writes it. A supplied `notice_posting_days` is recorded on the charge under either determination, whereas `remedy_code` is stored only on the merit path, so a remedy code sent with a dismissal is checked against the enumeration and then discarded. Beyond that one timestamp the remedy code drives nothing further: `RERUN_ELECTION` in particular neither sets a round aside, nor schedules a rerun, nor moves `case_status`. A determination of `DISMISS` stamps `determined_at`, moves the charge to the terminal `DISMISSED` state, and — when the charge had requested blocking — lowers `blocking_charge_count` by 1, potentially firing the unblock chain. A charge that has already been determined or settled is refused with `ERR_CHARGE_STATUS_INVALID`.

Settling is available to `EmploymentCounsel` or to a `BoardAgent` and works from `FILED` or from `MERIT_FOUND`. The request must carry a `settlement_type` of `NON_BOARD` or `FORMAL` and may carry `notice_posting_days`, which may not be negative. Settlement stamps `settled_at`, moves the charge to the terminal `SETTLED` state, lowers `blocking_charge_count` by 1 when the charge was a blocking one, and, when `notice_posting_days` is greater than 0, sets the case's `notice_posting_end_at` to `settled_at` plus that many days.

The unblock chain runs whenever a dismissal or a settlement leaves `blocking_charge_count` at 0, and it is deliberately narrower than the block was. The posture returns to `OPEN`. Every round on the case in `SUSPENDED` returns to `SCHEDULED` — but the poll sessions cancelled during the block are **not** restored, so `scheduled_session_count` stays at 0 until the Board lays out replacement sessions. Every campaign communication that is `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `POSTURE_BLOCKED` returns to `APPROVED` with the reason cleared, while messages blocked for any other reason stay blocked. When the posture had been `IMPOUNDED`, the impounded round's `ballots_impounded` flag returns to false.

Certification is the last act, and it is a `BoardAgent` determination naming the `election_round_id` whose revised tally the certification rests on. Seven conditions must all hold. The case must be in `TALLY_POSTED`, its `case_posture` must be `OPEN`, the named round's `status` must be `TALLY_REVISED` — a round that has not been revised is refused with `ERR_ROUND_NOT_REVISED` — the logical clock must be **strictly after** that round's `objection_window_close_at`, since certifying at or before the window's close is refused with `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_OPEN`, `pending_objection_count` must be 0 or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PENDING_OBJECTIONS`, `blocking_charge_count` must be 0 or it is refused with `ERR_BLOCKING_CHARGE_PENDING`, and `notice_posting_end_at` must be either absent or at or before the logical clock, since a still-running settlement notice period is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_POSTING_ACTIVE`.

The certification itself stamps `certified_at` with the logical clock, records the named round in `certified_round_id`, moves the case to the terminal `CERTIFIED` status, and moves that round to the terminal `ROUND_CERTIFIED` status. Which certification issues turns on one comparison: the petitioning union prevailed when the round's `revised_votes_for` is greater than or equal to its `majority_threshold`. Equality is a union win, because the threshold already embeds the halve-discard-add-one rule, so a genuine tie on the raw ballots is a loss.

The two outcomes write **disjoint** sets of timestamps, and a field belonging to the other outcome must be left absent rather than set to zero or copied across. When the union prevailed, `certification_outcome` becomes `CERTIFICATION_OF_REPRESENTATIVE`; `bargaining_obligation_start_at` equals `certified_at`, because the duty to bargain begins immediately; `certification_year_end_at` is `certified_at` plus 365 days, the insulated certification year; `decert_window_open_at` equals `certification_year_end_at`, since a decertification petition may only be filed once that year closes; and `election_bar_end_at` and `bargaining_obligation_end_at` are both left absent. When the union did not prevail, `certification_outcome` becomes `CERTIFICATION_OF_RESULTS`; `election_bar_end_at` is `certified_at` plus 365 days, the 12-month bar against a further election; `bargaining_obligation_start_at`, `certification_year_end_at` and `decert_window_open_at` are all left absent; and `bargaining_obligation_end_at` equals `certified_at` only when the case's `petition_type` is `RD`, because a decertification petition that ends in a certification of results terminates the pre-existing duty to bargain, and is left absent for every other petition type. All the 365-day arithmetic is exact day arithmetic preserving the time of day: a certification issued at `2026-09-15T09:00:00Z` produces a year-end or bar-end of `2027-09-15T09:00:00Z`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served under the base path shown. Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence — the eleven collection-root creates and the rerun scheduler, which mints the next election round — answer with HTTP `201`; every other successful call, including every state transition on an existing resource and every read, answers with HTTP `200`. Unless a workflow section says otherwise, a create or a transition returns the created or updated resource in the `data` field; the rerun scheduler returns the newly created election round rather than the petition. All list endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` and return the pagination block described in the response-structure section, and all filters named in a list endpoint's description are supplied as query parameters. A request from a role not listed in the final column is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. A request addressed to a path no endpoint serves, or carrying a method that path does not accept, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) rather than with a bare framework 404 or 405; a trailing slash on any path is accepted as equivalent to the path without one.
