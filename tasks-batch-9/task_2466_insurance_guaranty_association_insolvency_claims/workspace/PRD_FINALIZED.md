# Insurance Guaranty Association API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a court enters an order of liquidation against an insurance company, the policyholders of that company do not simply lose their coverage — a statutory safety net takes over. This document specifies the back-office API for a state insurance guaranty association: the statutorily chartered body that steps into the shoes of an insolvent member insurer, pays covered claims up to statutory caps, refunds unearned premium on policies cancelled by the liquidation order, and funds all of it by assessing the surviving solvent insurers that write business in the same statutory account. The association operates in this state, whose two-letter code is `MI`; claims that statutorily belong to another state's association are referred out rather than paid locally.

The central discipline the platform must enforce is that estate money is a closed ledger. Every cent the liquidator's receiver remits to the association is, at any instant, in exactly one of three buckets: already distributed to claimants, refund recipients and contracted vendors; still undistributed and awaiting a distribution run; or returned to the receiver when the insolvency file is finally closed. There is no fourth place for cash to be, and no operation may create or destroy a cent. Layered on top of that ledger are three independent capped money computations — the statutory net payable on a single covered claim, the unearned premium refund on a single cancelled policy, and the allocation of an assessment across member insurers under per-member basis-point caps. Each of the first two is computed twice, once under the elected statutory variant and once under the variant that was not elected, so that association staff and regulators can see what the alternative treatment would have produced on identical facts.

Association work is also work that must be auditable and segregated. The staff member who screens a claim for coverage may not be the committee officer who approves the settlement. Contracted adjusters may recommend settlements only inside the settlement authority delegated to their engagement, and may invoice hours only against an engagement that is still live. Member insurers may pay only their own levies and may file rate surcharges only up to a statutory aggregate. Accordingly the API models eight distinct actors, a priority-ordered validation cascade so that failures are reported in a stable and explainable order, and optimistic concurrency control on the three writes where a client acting on a stale view of a parent record would corrupt the ledger.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being addressed, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Role names are case-sensitive and spelled exactly as follows.

- **`AssociationAdmin`** — association staff who open and administer insolvency files, configure statutory coverage, register member insurers, engage contracted vendors, execute distribution runs and coordinate sister-association referrals.
- **`ReceiverLiaison`** — staff of the liquidator's office who remit estate cash, deliver proof-of-claim extract batches and run the data conversion pass.
- **`CoverageAnalyst`** — association coverage staff who screen claims against the covered-claim definition, track other insurance to resolution and file unearned premium refunds.
- **`ClaimsAdjuster`** — a contracted independent adjuster acting under a vendor engagement of type `ADJUSTER`; recommends settlements within delegated authority and invoices hours.
- **`DefenseCounsel`** — contracted defense counsel acting under a vendor engagement of type `DEFENSE_COUNSEL`; bills defense hours but holds no settlement recommendation authority.
- **`ClaimsCommitteeOfficer`** — an association claims committee member who approves or remands adjuster recommendations, and who is barred from approving a claim that the same principal screened.
- **`AssessmentOfficer`** — association assessment staff who draft and certify member assessments against reported premium bases.
- **`MemberInsurerAgent`** — the authorised agent of an assessed member insurer, who pays its levies, files policyholder rate surcharges and reports recoupment proceeds.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role, and three roles are backed by persisted entities. When `X-User-Role` is `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting vendor engagement row; ownership checks compare that value against the `id` of a vendor engagement on the target claim whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` and whose `vendor_type` is `ADJUSTER`. When `X-User-Role` is `DefenseCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting vendor engagement row, and the ownership check requires an `ASSIGNED` engagement on the target claim with `vendor_type` equal to `DEFENSE_COUNSEL`. A failure of either check is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ADJUSTER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `MemberInsurerAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting member insurer record; ownership checks compare the target member assessment invoice's `member_insurer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_ASSESSED_MEMBER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining five roles carry opaque identifiers. When `X-User-Role` is `AssociationAdmin`, `ReceiverLiaison`, `CoverageAnalyst`, `ClaimsCommitteeOfficer` or `AssessmentOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any row's `id`. The server does, however, record two of these identifiers on the covered claim: the screening analyst's identifier is stored in `screened_by_user_id`, and the approving committee officer's identifier is stored in `approved_by_user_id`. Those two recorded values drive the segregation-of-duties rule: an approval whose acting `X-User-ID` is equal to the claim's stored `screened_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), because the same person may not both certify coverage and authorise payment.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a section of this document explicitly states a different ordering for a particular computation. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` set to true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and a `version` integer that begins at 1 on creation and increases by one on every mutation of that row; whenever a row is mutated, its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant in the same operation that advances `version`.

One resource type is deliberately exempt. The statutory coverage configuration row is a configuration record rather than a lifecycle record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither field should appear in its representation. Every other entity in this system — insolvency files, member insurers, assessments, member assessment invoices, proof-of-claim transfer batches, transferred policies, covered claims, other-insurance layers, vendor engagements, unearned premium refunds and sister-association referrals — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Time in this system is a logical clock, never wall-clock time. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to real time. Every `created_at`, and every domain timestamp — `filed_at_utc` on a covered claim, `triggered_at_utc` on an insolvency file, `certified_at_utc` on an assessment, `recommended_at_utc` on a claim recommendation and `responded_at_utc` on a referral — is stamped from that clock. Rendering follows one convention throughout: every instant-valued field this API emits — `created_at` and `updated_at`, the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`, each of the domain `*_at_utc` fields just listed, the derived `proof_of_claim_deadline_utc` on an insolvency file, and `current_utc` in the clock-setting response — is written in ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits whenever it is non-null, so the startup instant renders as `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. The convention governs instant-valued fields only; the date-valued fields of this system — `liquidation_order_date`, `coverage_termination_date`, a policy's `effective_date`, `expiration_date` and `cancellation_date`, a claim's `loss_date`, and an engagement's `engagement_start` and `engagement_end` — are outside it and are never rewritten into instant form. An `AssociationAdmin` may move the clock forward by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability; the response reports the new `current_utc` together with `delinquent_member_assessment_count`, the number of member assessment invoices swept into delinquency by the move.

### 2.5. Money, Cents and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents. There are no fractional-cent representations anywhere in a stored field, a request body or a response body. Because the inputs to the claim payable computation are already integer cents, that computation performs no rounding step at all: it is exact integer arithmetic throughout.

Where a computation must divide or multiply by a rate, the rounding rule is round half up to whole cents — a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Round half up applies in exactly four places: the per-member assessment cap derived from a basis-point rate; each of the two raw unearned premium refund figures, where the rounding happens exactly once per variant at the very end of that variant's arithmetic; the projected recoupment derived from a filed surcharge rate; and the fee on a single vendor invoice, where hours are multiplied by the contracted hourly rate. Two computations deliberately do **not** use round half up: pro-rata shares inside a distribution run and raw shares inside an assessment allocation are truncated downward to whole cents, with the residual cents then handed out one at a time in a declared deterministic order, so that the sum of the parts equals the whole exactly.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Ledger-Bearing Writes

Three writes take an explicit expected-version field because each of them supersedes a money figure that the client must have observed before writing. In each case the field is a required, non-optional member of the request body, and its value must equal the current `version` of the named parent row at the moment the request is processed. If the parent's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the write is refused and nothing is changed.

- `POST /claims/{id}/other-insurance` requires `expected_claim_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the covered claim named in the path. A mismatch with the claim's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Registering a new other-insurance layer legitimately supersedes the claim's previously computed payable, so a writer holding a stale view of the claim would be posting an offset against a payable that no longer exists.
- `POST /policies/{id}/premium-refunds` requires `expected_policy_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the transferred policy named in the path. A mismatch with the policy's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The refund is computed from the policy's cancellation data and written premium, so a writer holding a stale view of the policy would elect a refund variant against superseded cancellation facts.
- `POST /insolvencies/{id}/distributions` requires `expected_insolvency_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the insolvency file named in the path. A mismatch with the insolvency file's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSOLVENCY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A distribution run supersedes the estate ledger, so a writer holding a stale view of the undistributed balance would allocate cash that an earlier run has already committed.

No other endpoint in this system accepts an expected-version field. A request that supplies one where it is not defined is an unknown field and fails request-shape validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.7. The Closed Estate Ledger and Its Derived Counters

The estate ledger on an insolvency file obeys one identity at all times: `estate_remitted_cents` equals the sum of `estate_distributed_cents`, `estate_undistributed_cents` and `estate_returned_cents`. Cash enters only through remittances from the receiver, which increase `estate_remitted_cents`; cash leaves only through distribution runs, which increase `estate_distributed_cents`; and the residue is returned exactly once, at file closure, which moves the whole of `estate_undistributed_cents` into `estate_returned_cents`. `estate_undistributed_cents` is always recomputed as `estate_remitted_cents` less `estate_distributed_cents` less `estate_returned_cents`, never incremented in place. A new insolvency file starts with all four of these fields at 0, with `administrative_paid_cents` at 0 and with `distribution_sequence` at 0.

Alongside the cash buckets, the insolvency file exposes derived counters that are fully recomputed on every write that could change them — every write touching a covered claim, an unearned premium refund, a vendor engagement, an assessment or the estate cash position. Full recomputation, rather than incremental adjustment, is required so that no arithmetic drift can accumulate. The counters and their definitions are:

- `administrative_expense_cents` — the sum of `fees_accrued_cents` over every vendor engagement whose claim belongs to this insolvency file, counting engagements in every status. This total is class 1 in the distribution waterfall.
- `reserved_claim_cents` — the sum, over covered claims of this insolvency file whose `status` is either `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, of `net_payable_cents` less `paid_amount_cents`. Claims in `DENIED_NOT_COVERED` or `REFERRED_OUT` never contribute to this reserve.
- `pending_up_liability_cents` — the sum, over unearned premium refunds on policies of this insolvency file whose `status` is either `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, of `computed_refund_cents` less `paid_amount_cents`.
- `referral_pending_count` — the count of covered claims of this insolvency file whose `status` is `REFERRAL_PENDING`.
- `assessment_shortfall_cents` — the sum of `shortfall_cents` over assessments of this insolvency file whose `status` is either `CERTIFIED` or `SETTLED`. Assessments still in `DRAFT` never contribute.
- `policy_count` — the count of transferred policies whose `insolvency_id` equals this file's `id`, counting policies in every status.

### 2.8. Path Resolution, Request Shape and Validation Precedence

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. The first path identifier is looked up first, and only if it resolves does the server look up the nested child identifier; the child must in addition belong to the parent named earlier in the path. Failures of the first identifier report the collection-specific not-found code: `ERR_INSOLVENCY_NOT_FOUND` for any path rooted at `/insolvencies/{id}`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for any path rooted at `/claims/{id}`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` for any path rooted at `/policies/{id}`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND` for any path rooted at `/poc-transfers/{id}`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` for any path rooted at `/assessments/{id}`, `ERR_MEMBER_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` for any path rooted at `/member-assessments/{id}`, and `ERR_MEMBER_INSURER_NOT_FOUND` for `GET /member-insurers/{id}` and `PATCH /member-insurers/{id}`. All seven are HTTP 404. A nested child identifier that does not resolve to a row whose parent foreign key equals the first path identifier yields `ERR_CHILD_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); this applies to `PATCH /claims/{id}/other-insurance/{coverageId}`, `POST /claims/{id}/vendor-assignments/{assignmentId}/invoices` and `POST /claims/{id}/referrals/{referralId}/respond`. A foreign key supplied in a request body rather than the path — for example `insolvency_id` or `policy_id` — that does not resolve to an existing row yields `ERR_REFERENCED_ROW_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); this applies to `POST /policies`, `POST /claims`, `POST /assessments` and `POST /insolvencies/{id}/poc-transfers`.

Request bodies are validated for shape on every endpoint: every required field must be present, every value must have the declared type, every enumerated field must carry a declared member, every numeric field must fall in its declared range, and no unknown field is accepted. Any of these failures is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The same code also covers one domain-shaped input error: a reported other-insurance recovery greater than the layer's `stated_limit_cents`.

Because many endpoints carry several checks at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following priority order, and the first failing check is the one reported:

1. Authentication — both headers present and `X-User-Role` a declared role → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint.
2. Role authorisation — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised roles → `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, on every endpoint.
3. Vendor ownership — for `ClaimsAdjuster` and `DefenseCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of an `ASSIGNED` vendor engagement on the target claim whose `vendor_type` matches the role → `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ADJUSTER`, on `POST /claims/{id}/recommend` and `POST /claims/{id}/vendor-assignments/{assignmentId}/invoices`.
4. Member ownership — for `MemberInsurerAgent`, `X-User-ID` equals the target invoice's `member_insurer_id` → `ERR_NOT_ASSESSED_MEMBER`, on `POST /member-assessments/{id}/payments`, `POST /member-assessments/{id}/surcharge-filings` and `POST /member-assessments/{id}/recoupment-remittances`.
5. Segregation of duties — the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `screened_by_user_id` → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on `POST /claims/{id}/approve`.
6. Existence of the insolvency file named first in the path → `ERR_INSOLVENCY_NOT_FOUND`.
7. Existence of the claim named first in the path, evaluated after insolvency rows → `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`.
8. Existence of the policy named first in the path → `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`.
9. Existence of the transfer batch named first in the path → `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`.
10. Existence of the assessment named first in the path → `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
11. Existence of the member assessment invoice named first in the path → `ERR_MEMBER_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`; and, immediately after it, existence of the member insurer named in the path on `GET /member-insurers/{id}` and `PATCH /member-insurers/{id}` → `ERR_MEMBER_INSURER_NOT_FOUND`.
12. Existence and parentage of the nested child identifier → `ERR_CHILD_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
13. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body → `ERR_REFERENCED_ROW_NOT_FOUND`.
14. Request shape, types, enum membership, numeric ranges and rejection of unknown fields → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, on every endpoint.
15. `expected_claim_version` equals the claim's `version` → `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION`, on `POST /claims/{id}/other-insurance`.
16. `expected_policy_version` equals the policy's `version` → `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION`, on `POST /policies/{id}/premium-refunds`.
17. `expected_insolvency_version` equals the insolvency file's `version` → `ERR_STALE_INSOLVENCY_VERSION`, on `POST /insolvencies/{id}/distributions`.
18. No sister-association referral on the claim is still `OPEN` → `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims/{id}/referrals`. This check deliberately outranks the claim-state check that follows, so that a claim already parked with a sister association reports the open referral rather than its parked status.
19. The target claim's `status` is a declared origin state of the requested transition → `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims/{id}/screen`, `POST /claims/{id}/vendor-assignments`, `POST /claims/{id}/recommend`, `POST /claims/{id}/approve`, `POST /claims/{id}/referrals`, `POST /claims/{id}/other-insurance` and `PATCH /claims/{id}/other-insurance/{coverageId}`. For the three engagement and other-insurance writes — `POST /claims/{id}/vendor-assignments`, `POST /claims/{id}/other-insurance` and `PATCH /claims/{id}/other-insurance/{coverageId}` — the admissible claim statuses are instead `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`, `ADJUSTING` and `RECOMMENDED`, and only a claim in `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE` is additionally advanced to `ADJUSTING` by an `ADJUSTER` engagement.
20. The remand is refused once the claim has already been remanded twice → `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims/{id}/remand`.
21. The target transfer batch's `status` is `RECEIVED` → `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on `POST /poc-transfers/{id}/convert`.
22. The target assessment's `status` is `DRAFT` → `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on `POST /assessments/{id}/certify`.
23. The target member assessment invoice is in a declared origin state of the addressed dimension — `billing_status` for payments, `recoupment_status` for surcharge filings → `ERR_MEMBER_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on `POST /member-assessments/{id}/payments` and `POST /member-assessments/{id}/surcharge-filings`.
24. The target referral's `status` is `OPEN` → `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_ANSWERED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims/{id}/referrals/{referralId}/respond`.
25. The governing insolvency file's `status` is `TRIGGERED` → `ERR_INSOLVENCY_NOT_TRIGGERED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims`, `POST /policies/{id}/premium-refunds`, `POST /insolvencies/{id}/estate-remittances`, `POST /insolvencies/{id}/distributions`, `POST /assessments/{id}/certify` and `POST /insolvencies/{id}/close`.
26. The governing insolvency file's `status` is anything other than `CLOSED` → `ERR_INSOLVENCY_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /policies`, `POST /insolvencies/{id}/poc-transfers`, `POST /poc-transfers/{id}/convert`, `POST /insolvencies/{id}/trigger` and `POST /assessments`; and, immediately after it, the triggering event runs only once, requiring `status` to be `DECLARED` → `ERR_INSOLVENCY_ALREADY_TRIGGERED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /insolvencies/{id}/trigger`.
27. The claim's transferred policy has `status` `CANCELLED_BY_ORDER` → `ERR_POLICY_NOT_CANCELLED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims/{id}/screen` and `POST /policies/{id}/premium-refunds`.
28. The logical-clock instant of the request is at or before the insolvency file's `proof_of_claim_deadline_utc`, the boundary instant itself being allowed → `ERR_FILING_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /claims` and `POST /policies/{id}/premium-refunds`.
29. The policyholder's `policyholder_net_worth_cents` is strictly below the line's `net_worth_exclusion_threshold_cents` → `ERR_NET_WORTH_EXCLUSION` (HTTP 422), on `POST /claims/{id}/screen`.
30. A coverage configuration row exists for every line of business the operation must price → `ERR_COVERAGE_PROFILE_MISSING` (HTTP 422), on `POST /insolvencies/{id}/trigger`, `POST /claims/{id}/screen` and `POST /policies/{id}/premium-refunds`.
31. `claim_number` is unique within the target `policy_id` → `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims`.
32. The policy's `up_refund_filed` is still false → `ERR_UP_REFUND_ALREADY_FILED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /policies/{id}/premium-refunds`.
33. The triple of `naic_code`, `assessable_line` and `base_year` is not already registered → `ERR_MEMBER_DUPLICATE_LINE` (HTTP 409), on `POST /member-insurers`.
34. The new policy term does not intersect the term of another policy of the same insolvency file sharing its `policy_number` → `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), on `POST /policies`.
35. The new engagement's date interval does not intersect an `ASSIGNED` engagement of the same `vendor_type` on the same claim → `ERR_ADJUSTER_ENGAGEMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims/{id}/vendor-assignments`.
36. The referral's asserted basis resolves to a state code equal to `target_state_code` → `ERR_REFERRAL_BASIS_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), on `POST /claims/{id}/referrals`.
37. The member's already-filed surcharge rates plus the requested rate stay at or below the statutory aggregate → `ERR_SURCHARGE_AGGREGATE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /member-assessments/{id}/surcharge-filings`.
38. Every other-insurance layer on the claim has reached `EXHAUSTED` or `CARRIER_DENIED` → `ERR_OTHER_INSURANCE_NOT_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /claims/{id}/approve`.
39. `recoupment_status` is `SURCHARGE_FILED` or `RECOUPING` before surcharge proceeds may be remitted → `ERR_RECOUPMENT_NOT_FILED` (HTTP 409), on `POST /member-assessments/{id}/recoupment-remittances`.
40. No covered claim of the insolvency file remains in `RECEIVED`, `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`, `ADJUSTING`, `RECOMMENDED`, `REFERRAL_PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` → `ERR_CLAIMS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409), on `POST /insolvencies/{id}/close`.
41. No unearned premium refund on a policy of the insolvency file remains in `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` → `ERR_REFUNDS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409), on `POST /insolvencies/{id}/close`.
42. `recommended_amount_cents` is at or below the acting engagement's `settlement_authority_cents` → `ERR_ADJUSTER_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /claims/{id}/recommend`.
43. The amount tendered does not exceed the outstanding balance — `recommended_amount_cents` at or below `net_payable_cents` on a recommendation, and `amount_cents` at or below `levied_amount_cents` less `paid_amount_cents` on a member payment → `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` (HTTP 422), on `POST /claims/{id}/recommend` and `POST /member-assessments/{id}/payments`.
44. The claim's stored `net_payable_cents` is at or below the policy's remaining aggregate room → `ERR_AGGREGATE_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /claims/{id}/approve`.
45. `recouped_amount_cents` plus the reported `proceeds_cents` does not exceed `levied_amount_cents` → `ERR_OVER_RECOUPMENT` (HTTP 422), on `POST /member-assessments/{id}/recoupment-remittances`.
46. At least one member insurer satisfies the assessment's eligibility filter with a positive premium base → `ERR_PREMIUM_BASE_EMPTY` (HTTP 422), on `POST /assessments/{id}/certify`.
47. `distribution_amount_cents` is at or below the insolvency file's `estate_undistributed_cents` → `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ESTATE_FUNDS` (HTTP 422), on `POST /insolvencies/{id}/distributions`.

### 2.9. The Statutory Coverage Configuration

Coverage caps in this system are not hard-coded; they are read from a configuration row per statutory line of business, registered by an `AssociationAdmin` before any claim in that line can be priced. `line_of_business` is one of `PERSONAL_AUTO`, `HOMEOWNERS`, `COMMERCIAL_GENERAL_LIABILITY` or `WORKERS_COMPENSATION`, and at most one configuration row may exist per line. The row declares `per_claim_cap_cents`, the statutory maximum payable on a single covered claim in that line, which must be greater than 0; `per_policy_aggregate_cap_cents`, the statutory maximum payable across all covered claims sharing one policy in that line, which must be greater than or equal to `per_claim_cap_cents`; and `unearned_premium_cap_cents`, the statutory maximum refund payable on one policy in that line, which must be greater than 0. It also declares `refund_priority_class`, an integer that must be 1, 2 or 3, which ranks that line's unearned premium refunds inside distribution class 3, with the lower rank paid first.

Four further fields on the configuration row are optional at registration and take defaults when omitted. `statutory_deductible_cents` is subtracted from every covered claim in the line and defaults to 10 000 cents, which is one hundred dollars. `net_worth_exclusion_threshold_cents` is the policyholder net worth at or above which a claim in the line is excluded from coverage altogether, and defaults to 2 500 000 000 cents, which is twenty-five million dollars. `covers_punitive_damages` is a boolean that defaults to false; when it is false, the punitive portion of a demand is left out of the covered loss basis in that line. `cap_order_mode` is an enumerated flag that is either `OFFSET_THEN_CAP` or `CAP_THEN_OFFSET` and defaults to `OFFSET_THEN_CAP`; it selects whether the statutory deductible and other-insurance recovery are subtracted before or after the per-claim cap bites, and it is the single most consequential configuration choice in this system, because the same loss facts produce different payables under the two settings.

Any operation that must price a line — recording the triggering event, screening a claim, or filing an unearned premium refund — first requires that a configuration row exists for the relevant line of business, failing with `ERR_COVERAGE_PROFILE_MISSING` when it does not.

### 2.10. Insolvency-Wide Write Gates

Work on an insolvency file is gated on that file's own lifecycle, which runs `DECLARED` → `TRIGGERED` → `CLOSED`. Two families of gate apply, and they are distinct. The first family demands that the triggering event has already been recorded: presenting a covered claim, filing an unearned premium refund, recording an estate remittance, executing a distribution run, certifying an assessment and closing the file all require `status` to be `TRIGGERED`, and a `DECLARED` file refuses them with `ERR_INSOLVENCY_NOT_TRIGGERED`. The second family merely demands that the file is not yet finished: keying in a policy, accepting a proof-of-claim batch, converting a batch, recording the triggering event and drafting an assessment are all refused on a `CLOSED` file with `ERR_INSOLVENCY_CLOSED`. Recording the triggering event carries an additional single-shot guard — it requires `status` to be exactly `DECLARED`, and a second attempt on a file already in `TRIGGERED` is refused with `ERR_INSOLVENCY_ALREADY_TRIGGERED`.

Consistency in this system is immediate. Every cascade described in this document — a parent counter recomputation, a child state change, a version bump — is visible in the same response that caused it and to any subsequent read, with no deferred or eventual settling. There is no background reconciliation job: if an operation is specified to update six fields across three entities, all six are correct the instant the operation returns 200 or 201.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Insolvency File and Recording the Triggering Event

An `AssociationAdmin` opens an insolvency file as soon as the liquidation court acts. The payload carries the `insurer_name` of the insolvent member insurer, its `naic_code`, the `domiciliary_state_code` of the court that entered the order, and the `liquidation_order_date` on which the order with its finding of insolvency was entered. `naic_code` must be unique across insolvency files, so the same insurer cannot be opened twice. `domiciliary_state_code` must be exactly two uppercase letters, and `liquidation_order_date` must be an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form. The new file lands in `DECLARED`, with `status` defaulting to that value, with `triggered_at_utc` null, with every estate ledger bucket and every derived counter at 0, and with `distribution_sequence` at 0.

Nothing statutory happens until the **triggering event** is recorded, and that recording is a one-way door. Before an `AssociationAdmin` may record it, three conditions must hold: the file must still be in `DECLARED`; at least one transferred policy of the file must exist in `IMPORTED`; and a statutory coverage configuration row must exist for every distinct line of business appearing among the file's transferred policies, without which the operation fails with `ERR_COVERAGE_PROFILE_MISSING` because the association would be unable to price the book it is about to inherit.

Recording the triggering event does five things at once. It stamps `triggered_at_utc` from the logical clock. It computes `coverage_termination_date` as the `liquidation_order_date` advanced by 30 calendar days — the date on which policies still in force are cancelled by operation of law. It computes `proof_of_claim_deadline_utc` as the `liquidation_order_date` advanced by 365 calendar days, at the instant midnight on that date, stored and rendered as `00:00:00.000Z`; filings at exactly that instant are still accepted, and only filings strictly after it are late. It cascades into the policy book, moving the file from `DECLARED` to `TRIGGERED`. And it recomputes `policy_count` and increments the insolvency file's `version` by one.

The policy cascade is a two-way branch, and both branches must be implemented with equal care. Every transferred policy in `IMPORTED` whose `expiration_date` falls strictly after the `liquidation_order_date` moves `IMPORTED → CANCELLED_BY_ORDER`: its `cancellation_date` is set to whichever is earlier of its own `expiration_date` and the file's `coverage_termination_date`, its `unexpired_days` is set to the number of whole calendar days from that `cancellation_date` forward to its `expiration_date`, and its `version` is incremented by one. Every transferred policy in `IMPORTED` whose `expiration_date` falls on or before the `liquidation_order_date` moves `IMPORTED → EXPIRED_BEFORE_ORDER` instead: its `cancellation_date` is left null and its `unexpired_days` stays 0 — which is precisely why no unearned premium refund can ever be filed on such a policy — and its `version` is likewise incremented by one. `CANCELLED_BY_ORDER` and `EXPIRED_BEFORE_ORDER` are both terminal; a policy never leaves either one.

Closing the file is the mirror image and is available only to an `AssociationAdmin` on a file in `TRIGGERED`. Two clearance checks guard it. If any covered claim of the file remains in `RECEIVED`, `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`, `ADJUSTING`, `RECOMMENDED`, `REFERRAL_PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, the close is refused with `ERR_CLAIMS_OUTSTANDING`; claims already in `DENIED_NOT_COVERED`, `REFERRED_OUT` or `PAID` are settled business and do not block. If any unearned premium refund on a policy of the file remains in `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, the close is refused with `ERR_REFUNDS_OUTSTANDING`. When both checks clear, the file moves `TRIGGERED → CLOSED`, the whole of `estate_undistributed_cents` is added into `estate_returned_cents` and `estate_undistributed_cents` is set to 0 — which preserves the closed-ledger identity exactly, since the cash has merely changed bucket — and the file's `version` is incremented by one. `CLOSED` is terminal.

Reading a single insolvency file exposes the estate ledger together with `reserved_claim_cents`, `pending_up_liability_cents`, `referral_pending_count`, `assessment_shortfall_cents`, `administrative_expense_cents`, `administrative_paid_cents`, `policy_count` and `distribution_sequence`, so association staff can see the whole fiscal position of the estate in one read. Coverage configuration rows can also be listed, optionally narrowed to a single `line_of_business`, by every role that has to reason about caps.

### 3.2. Bringing the Receiver's Records Across

Policyholder and claim data reaches the association in bulk from the liquidator's office. A `ReceiverLiaison` delivers a batch by supplying a `receiver_batch_ref`, which the receiver assigns and which must be unique within the one insolvency file it belongs to, together with a `records` array that must contain at least one element. The batch is stored raw and unconverted: it lands in `RECEIVED` with `record_count` set from the length of `records`, with `converted_count` and `rejected_count` at 0, and with `rejection_reasons` empty.

Each element of `records` is an object as delivered by the receiver, and the association does not reshape it on intake. An element carries `record_type`, which is either the string `POLICY` or the string `CLAIM`, and then the fields that type needs: `policy_number`, `line_of_business`, `policyholder_name`, `policyholder_state_code`, `policyholder_net_worth_cents`, `effective_date`, `expiration_date` and `annual_premium_cents` describe a policy, while `claimant_name`, `claimant_state_code`, `loss_date` and `gross_loss_cents` — alongside the `policy_number` that ties the claim to its policy — describe a claim.

The conversion pass is a separate, explicit act by the `ReceiverLiaison`, permitted only while the batch is still in `RECEIVED` and only while the governing insolvency file is in `DECLARED` or `TRIGGERED`. Conversion evaluates each element against three checks, always in this order, and reports only the **first** one that fails for that element. The first check is `MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`: a key that the element's `record_type` needs is absent or empty. The second is `UNKNOWN_LINE_OF_BUSINESS`: no statutory coverage configuration row exists for the element's `line_of_business`. The third is `NO_MATCHING_POLICY`, which applies to `CLAIM` elements only: the element's `policy_number` matches no transferred policy of this insolvency file, counting policies created earlier in the very same conversion pass, so a batch may legitimately carry a policy and its claims together provided the policy element comes first.

The batch's terminal state records the outcome, and the three outcomes — `CONVERTED`, `PARTIALLY_CONVERTED` and `REJECTED` — are exhaustive, mutually exclusive and all terminal. When every element passes, the batch moves `RECEIVED → CONVERTED`: one transferred policy is created per `POLICY` element and one covered claim per `CLAIM` element, each with `source_transfer_id` set to the batch's own `id`, each claim matched to the transferred policy sharing its `policy_number`; `converted_count` is set equal to `record_count`, `rejected_count` to 0 and `rejection_reasons` to an empty array; and the insolvency file's `policy_count` is recomputed. When at least one element passes and at least one fails, the batch moves `RECEIVED → PARTIALLY_CONVERTED`: rows are created only for the passing elements, `converted_count` and `rejected_count` are set to their respective counts, one string per failing element is appended to `rejection_reasons` in `records` order, and `policy_count` is again recomputed. When every element fails, the batch moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED`: no rows at all are created, `converted_count` is 0, `rejected_count` equals `record_count`, and one string per element is appended to `rejection_reasons` in `records` order. Each entry in `rejection_reasons` is formatted as the element's zero-based index inside `records`, then a colon, then the reason code — so the third element of the array failing on an unknown line reads exactly `2:UNKNOWN_LINE_OF_BUSINESS`. Requesting conversion on a batch that is not in `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`, which makes conversion effectively idempotent by refusal rather than by silent replay. Batches may be listed per insolvency file with an optional `status` filter, and read individually to inspect the counters and the per-element rejection detail.

Policies may also be keyed in directly, outside any batch, by a `ReceiverLiaison` or an `AssociationAdmin`. The payload names the owning `insolvency_id` and carries `policy_number`, `line_of_business`, `policyholder_name`, `policyholder_state_code`, `effective_date`, `expiration_date` and `annual_premium_cents`, with `policyholder_net_worth_cents`, `large_deductible_cents`, `self_insured_retention_cents` and `collateral_held_cents` all optional and all defaulting to 0 when omitted. `policy_number` must be unique within the one insolvency file. `line_of_business` must be one of the four statutory lines. `policyholder_state_code` must be exactly two uppercase letters, `expiration_date` must be an ISO 8601 calendar date strictly after `effective_date`, and `annual_premium_cents` must be greater than 0. A directly keyed policy is created in `IMPORTED` with `source_transfer_id` null, with `cancellation_date` null, `unexpired_days` 0, `aggregate_paid_cents` 0 and `up_refund_filed` false, and the insolvency file's `policy_count` is recomputed.

A policy term is the half-open interval running from `effective_date` inclusive to `expiration_date` exclusive. Two policies of the same insolvency file sharing a `policy_number` may not have terms that intersect; an attempt to create one is refused with `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because the interval is half-open, terms that merely touch at a boundary — one ending on the very day the next begins — do not intersect and are accepted, which is exactly how a renewal of the same policy number is expressed. Policies can be listed with optional filters on `insolvency_id`, `source_transfer_id`, `status` and `line_of_business`, and read individually to see the cancellation and aggregate figures.

### 3.3. Refunding Unearned Premium on Cancelled Policies

A policyholder whose coverage was cut short by the liquidation order is owed the unused portion of the premium. Exactly one refund may exist per policy: a `CoverageAnalyst` files it, and the operation is admissible only on a policy whose `status` is `CANCELLED_BY_ORDER` — a policy still in `IMPORTED`, or one that expired before the order and sits in `EXPIRED_BEFORE_ORDER`, is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_CANCELLED` — and only while that policy's `up_refund_filed` is still false, a second attempt being refused with `ERR_UP_REFUND_ALREADY_FILED`. The filing also sits behind the file's `TRIGGERED` gate, behind the proof-of-claim deadline, and behind the requirement that a coverage configuration row exists for the policy's line. Because the computation reads the policy's cancellation data, the payload must carry `expected_policy_version` alongside its `refund_method`, and a stale value is refused as described in the optimistic-concurrency rules.

`refund_method` elects the statutory computation variant and is either `PRO_RATA` — the variant that applies when the policy was cancelled by operation of the liquidation order, refunding the full unexpired fraction of the written premium — or `SHORT_RATE`, the variant for a policyholder-initiated cancellation recorded before the order, which retains a ten percent penalty. Both variants are computed on every filing, and both start from the same two quantities: the **term length**, being the number of whole calendar days from the policy's `effective_date` to its `expiration_date`, and the policy's stored `unexpired_days`.

The pro-rata raw figure is the policy's `annual_premium_cents` multiplied by `unexpired_days`, divided by the term length, then rounded half up to whole cents. The short-rate raw figure is that same product and quotient — `annual_premium_cents` multiplied by `unexpired_days`, divided by the term length — then multiplied by a short-rate factor of nine tenths, and only then rounded half up to whole cents. The rounding step occurs exactly once per variant, at the very end of that variant's arithmetic; in particular the short-rate factor is applied to the unrounded quotient, never to the already-rounded pro-rata figure, because rounding twice can shift the result by a cent.

Whichever variant `refund_method` names becomes the elected raw figure and the other becomes the alternative raw figure. Each is then independently limited by the line's `unearned_premium_cap_cents`: `computed_refund_cents` is the smaller of the elected raw figure and that cap, and `alt_refund_cents` is the smaller of the alternative raw figure and the same cap. `capped` is set to true when the elected raw figure — before the cap was applied — was strictly greater than the cap, and false otherwise. `priority_class` is set to a snapshot of the line's `refund_priority_class`, so a later reconfiguration of the line cannot reorder refunds that have already been filed. The refund is created in `PENDING` with `paid_amount_cents` at 0 and with the next `sequence` value assigned by the server.

Filing a refund reaches back into two other rows. On the policy, `up_refund_filed` is set to true and the policy's `version` is incremented by one — which is exactly why a second, concurrent filer holding the earlier version is turned away. On the insolvency file, `computed_refund_cents` is added into `pending_up_liability_cents`, booking the association's refund exposure the moment it is quantified. Refunds on a given policy may be listed, optionally narrowed by `status`, and the list will hold either zero rows or one.

### 3.4. Presenting and Screening a Covered Claim

A covered claim may be presented by a `ReceiverLiaison` or a `CoverageAnalyst`, but only against a triggered insolvency file and only at or before the file's `proof_of_claim_deadline_utc`; a filing whose logical-clock instant falls strictly after that boundary is refused with `ERR_FILING_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The payload names the `insolvency_id` whose estate the claim is presented against and the `policy_id` the claim arises under, and carries `claim_number`, `claimant_name`, `claimant_state_code`, `loss_date` and `gross_loss_cents`, with `punitive_damages_cents` optional and defaulting to 0. `claim_number` must be unique within the one policy, a collision being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`; `claimant_state_code` must be exactly two uppercase letters; `loss_date` must be an ISO 8601 calendar date; and `gross_loss_cents` must be greater than 0. The claim is created in `RECEIVED` with `filed_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, with `source_transfer_id` null when filed directly, with `paid_amount_cents` and `remand_count` at 0, with `cap_binding` at `NONE`, with `pre_referral_status`, `referred_to_state_code`, `denial_reason`, `screened_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `recommended_amount_cents`, `recommended_by_vendor_name` and `recommended_at_utc` all null, and with the next monotone `sequence` value assigned by the server. That `sequence` is the deterministic tie-break after `created_at` ascending wherever claims are ordered.

Screening is the coverage decision and belongs exclusively to a `CoverageAnalyst`. It is admissible only on a claim in `RECEIVED`. Two substantive coverage gates guard the operation: the claim's parent policy must be in `CANCELLED_BY_ORDER`, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_CANCELLED`; and the policy's `policyholder_net_worth_cents` must be strictly below the governing line's `net_worth_exclusion_threshold_cents`, else `ERR_NET_WORTH_EXCLUSION` — a net worth exactly equal to the threshold is excluded, since the statutory test is exclusion at or above the threshold. A coverage configuration row for the line must also exist, else `ERR_COVERAGE_PROFILE_MISSING`. The payload carries a `decision` and, optionally, a `denial_reason`.

A `decision` of `COVERED` moves the claim `RECEIVED → SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`. The acting analyst's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `screened_by_user_id`, the statutory payable computation described below is run and writes `covered_basis_cents`, `attachment_point_cents`, `covered_layer_cents`, `net_payable_cents`, `alt_net_payable_cents` and `cap_binding`, and the claim's `version` is incremented by one. A `decision` of `DENIED` moves the claim `RECEIVED → DENIED_NOT_COVERED` instead. The supplied reason code is stored in `denial_reason`, `screened_by_user_id` is recorded from the acting `X-User-ID` exactly as on the covered path, `net_payable_cents` is left at 0 so that the claim can never enter the distribution waterfall, every vendor engagement on the claim whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` is closed, and the claim's `version` is incremented by one. `DENIED_NOT_COVERED` is terminal.

The **statutory net payable** computation runs at three distinct moments in a claim's life: when a claim is screened as covered, again every time an other-insurance layer on that claim resolves to exhaustion or carrier denial, and once more when the claims committee approves the claim, so that a recovery reported late still lands in the figure the association actually pays. It proceeds in ordered steps against the coverage configuration row whose `line_of_business` matches the parent policy's `line_of_business`.

First, `covered_basis_cents` is the claim's `gross_loss_cents` plus its `punitive_damages_cents` when the line's `covers_punitive_damages` is true, or `gross_loss_cents` alone when that flag is false. Second, `attachment_point_cents` is whichever is smaller of two figures on the parent policy: the sum of `large_deductible_cents` and `self_insured_retention_cents`, or `collateral_held_cents` on its own. This is the self-funded retention credit rule in force: the insured's retained layer is credited against the association's exposure only up to the collateral actually held by the receiver, because collateral is the only part of the retention the receiver can genuinely realise. Third, `covered_layer_cents` is `covered_basis_cents` less `attachment_point_cents`, floored at 0 so it never goes negative.

Two offsets then apply to that layer: the other-insurance recovery held in `other_insurance_recoverable_cents`, and the line's `statutory_deductible_cents`. The line's `cap_order_mode` decides the order in which they interact with the line's `per_claim_cap_cents`, and it computes both orders on every run so the divergence is always visible. Under `OFFSET_THEN_CAP`, the statutory figure is obtained by subtracting both the other-insurance recovery and the statutory deductible from `covered_layer_cents`, taking whichever is smaller of that difference and `per_claim_cap_cents`, and flooring the result at 0; the alternative figure is obtained the other way round, by first taking whichever is smaller of `covered_layer_cents` and `per_claim_cap_cents`, then subtracting the other-insurance recovery and the statutory deductible, then flooring at 0. Under `CAP_THEN_OFFSET` the two are exchanged exactly: the statutory figure first truncates `covered_layer_cents` to `per_claim_cap_cents` and subtracts the two offsets afterwards, flooring at 0, while the alternative figure subtracts the two offsets first, truncates to `per_claim_cap_cents`, and floors at 0. Offsetting before capping lets the association still pay the full per-claim cap on a very large loss; capping before offsetting produces a strictly lower payable whenever the uncapped layer exceeds the cap, and identical loss data therefore yields different payables under different configuration rows.

The per-policy aggregate then applies. The policy's remaining aggregate room is the line's `per_policy_aggregate_cap_cents` less the policy's `aggregate_paid_cents`, floored at 0. `net_payable_cents` is whichever is smaller of the statutory figure and that remaining room, while `alt_net_payable_cents` is the alternative figure as computed above, without the aggregate step. The policy's `aggregate_paid_cents` is itself the sum of `net_payable_cents` over the sibling covered claims on the same `policy_id` whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID`, excluding the claim currently being priced.

Finally `cap_binding` records which statutory ceiling actually bit. It is `PER_POLICY_AGGREGATE` when `net_payable_cents` came out strictly below the statutory figure, meaning the policy aggregate was the binding constraint. Otherwise it is `PER_CLAIM` when the fully uncapped figure — `covered_layer_cents` less the other-insurance recovery less the statutory deductible, floored at 0 — is strictly greater than `per_claim_cap_cents`. Otherwise it is `NONE`. Because every input to this computation is already an integer number of cents, no rounding step occurs anywhere in it.

A dedicated settlement worksheet read lays the whole computation out term by term for a `CoverageAnalyst`, a `ClaimsCommitteeOfficer` or the assigned `ClaimsAdjuster`. It returns `claim_id`, the governing `cap_order_mode`, `covered_basis_cents`, `attachment_point_cents`, `covered_layer_cents`, `other_insurance_recoverable_cents`, the line's `statutory_deductible_cents` and `per_claim_cap_cents`, `remaining_aggregate_cents` for the policy, `net_payable_cents`, `alt_net_payable_cents`, `variant_delta_cents` as the difference between the two payable variants, and `cap_binding`. Claims can also be listed with optional filters on `insolvency_id`, `policy_id`, `source_transfer_id` and `status`, and read individually with both payable variants exposed.

### 3.5. Other Insurance, Exhaustion and Non-Duplication

The association is a payer of last resort, not a co-payer. Whenever another solvent carrier may owe on the same loss, a `CoverageAnalyst` registers that carrier as a layer against the claim while the claim is in `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`, `ADJUSTING` or `RECOMMENDED`. The payload carries `carrier_name`, the other carrier's `policy_number`, a `coverage_type` that is one of `PRIMARY`, `EXCESS` or `UMBRELLA`, a `stated_limit_cents` greater than 0, and `expected_claim_version`. The layer is created in `UNEXHAUSTED` with `recovered_cents` at 0 and with the next `sequence` value within that claim, and the claim's `version` is incremented by one.

Layers advance through a short chain. Marking a layer as being pursued moves it `UNEXHAUSTED → PURSUING`, is admissible only while the parent claim is in `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`, `ADJUSTING` or `RECOMMENDED`, and deliberately changes no money at all: `recovered_cents` stays 0 and the claim's `other_insurance_recoverable_cents` is unchanged. From `PURSUING` the layer resolves one of two ways, and the two are symmetric in everything except the money. Reporting an exhausted layer moves it `PURSUING → EXHAUSTED` and sets `recovered_cents` to the reported recovery, which must not exceed the layer's `stated_limit_cents` — a larger figure is a bad input and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Reporting a carrier denial moves it `PURSUING → CARRIER_DENIED` and leaves `recovered_cents` at 0, but still marks the layer resolved. Both resolutions recompute the claim's `other_insurance_recoverable_cents`, re-run the statutory net payable computation so that `net_payable_cents`, `alt_net_payable_cents` and `cap_binding` reflect the new offset, and increment the claim's `version` by one. `EXHAUSTED` and `CARRIER_DENIED` are both terminal for the layer.

`other_insurance_recoverable_cents` is the sum of `recovered_cents` over the claim's layers whose `status` is `EXHAUSTED` or `CARRIER_DENIED`; layers still in `UNEXHAUSTED` or `PURSUING` contribute nothing. A carrier denial therefore resolves a layer without reducing the association's payable at all, which is the intended outcome — the association pays because the other carrier will not.

Non-duplication is enforced at the approval gate rather than at registration. So long as any layer on a claim is still in `UNEXHAUSTED` or `PURSUING`, committee approval is refused with `ERR_OTHER_INSURANCE_NOT_EXHAUSTED`, since the association would otherwise be paying money another carrier may yet pay. Layers on a claim may be listed with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.6. Contracted Adjusters, Defense Counsel and Administrative Expense

Claims administration is outsourced. An `AssociationAdmin` engages a contracted vendor on a claim that is in `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`, `ADJUSTING` or `RECOMMENDED`, supplying a `vendor_type` of either `ADJUSTER` or `DEFENSE_COUNSEL`, the firm's `vendor_name`, a `settlement_authority_cents` of at least 0 that bounds what the vendor may recommend without going back to the committee, an `hourly_rate_cents` greater than 0 that the association will accrue against, and the engagement window as `engagement_start` and `engagement_end`. The engagement window is a closed interval: both endpoints belong to it, and `engagement_end` must fall on or after `engagement_start`. The engagement is created in `ASSIGNED` with `hours_logged` and `fees_accrued_cents` at 0.

Two engagements of the same `vendor_type` on the same claim may not overlap in time while both are live. Because the interval is closed at both ends, engagements that merely touch — one ending on the day the next begins — do collide, and the create is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTER_ENGAGEMENT_OVERLAP`; only engagements already in `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED` are ignored by this test. Replacing a vendor is done deliberately rather than by overlap: when a new engagement of a `vendor_type` that already has a live engagement on the claim is created, the older row moves `ASSIGNED → SUPERSEDED`, which freezes its `fees_accrued_cents` so that no further invoice may ever be posted to it, while the accrued total already rolled up into the insolvency file's `administrative_expense_cents` stays there. Engaging an `ADJUSTER` on a claim that is in `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE` additionally advances the claim `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE → ADJUSTING`; an engagement created while the claim is already in `ADJUSTING` or `RECOMMENDED`, or an engagement of type `DEFENSE_COUNSEL` at any point, leaves the claim's own status where it is. Every engagement create increments the claim's `version` by one.

Vendors bill by the hour. Both a `ClaimsAdjuster` and a `DefenseCounsel` may post an invoice against their own engagement, supplying `hours` and an `invoice_ref`; only an engagement still in `ASSIGNED` accepts invoices, and engagements in `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED` reject them. The invoice fee is the submitted `hours` multiplied by the engagement's `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. That fee is added to the engagement's `fees_accrued_cents` and the submitted `hours` are added to its `hours_logged`, after which the insolvency file's `administrative_expense_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `fees_accrued_cents` across every engagement on every claim of the file, in every engagement status. Accrued vendor fees are class 1 in the distribution waterfall and therefore rank ahead of claimants, but they never reduce any claim's `net_payable_cents`: administrative expense and covered loss are separate liabilities of the estate, not offsets against one another.

An engagement also closes automatically. Any claim transition into `DENIED_NOT_COVERED`, `REFERRED_OUT` or `PAID` moves every engagement on that claim whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` to `ASSIGNED → CLOSED`, freezing `fees_accrued_cents` while leaving the accrued total in the insolvency file's `administrative_expense_cents` — the work was done and the estate owes for it even though the claim itself is finished. Engagements on a claim may be listed with optional `vendor_type` and `status` filters. `DefenseCounsel` differs from `ClaimsAdjuster` in exactly one respect that matters here: defense counsel may bill hours and read the claim and its engagements, but has no settlement recommendation authority at all.

### 3.7. Recommendation, Remand and Committee Approval

Once a claim is in `ADJUSTING`, the assigned adjuster proposes a number. Recommending is exclusive to `ClaimsAdjuster`, and three substantive checks apply in addition to the requirement that the claim is in `ADJUSTING`. The acting `X-User-ID` must be the `id` of a vendor engagement on this claim that is in `ASSIGNED` with `vendor_type` equal to `ADJUSTER`, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_ADJUSTER`. The submitted `recommended_amount_cents` must be at or below that engagement's `settlement_authority_cents`, else `ERR_ADJUSTER_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`. And it must be at or below the claim's `net_payable_cents`, else `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` — the association will not recommend more than it is statutorily liable for. The payload may also carry an optional `note`. On success the claim moves `ADJUSTING → RECOMMENDED`, `recommended_amount_cents` is stored, `recommended_by_vendor_name` is set to the `vendor_name` of the acting engagement, `recommended_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock, and the claim's `version` is incremented by one.

The claims committee can send the number back. A `ClaimsCommitteeOfficer` remands a claim that is in `RECOMMENDED` by supplying a `remand_reason`, which moves the claim `RECOMMENDED → ADJUSTING` and increments `remand_count` by one. Remand is permitted at most twice per claim: the check is that `remand_count` is currently below 2, so a first remand is allowed on a claim with `remand_count` 0, a second on a claim with `remand_count` 1, and a third attempt on a claim whose `remand_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_REACHED`. A remand clears `recommended_amount_cents`, `recommended_by_vendor_name` and `recommended_at_utc` back to null, so the next recommendation starts clean. It leaves the insolvency file's `reserved_claim_cents` untouched, because no estate reserve is ever booked before approval — the recommend/remand cycle costs the estate nothing but adjuster hours.

Approval is the moment the estate takes on a hard liability, and it is exclusive to `ClaimsCommitteeOfficer` on a claim in `RECOMMENDED`. The payload carries only an optional `approval_note`. Three business guards apply, in the priority order set out in the validation cascade: first the segregation-of-duties check, which refuses the approval with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `screened_by_user_id`; then the non-duplication check, which refuses with `ERR_OTHER_INSURANCE_NOT_EXHAUSTED` unless every other-insurance layer on the claim has reached `EXHAUSTED` or `CARRIER_DENIED`; then the aggregate-cap check, which requires the claim's currently stored `net_payable_cents` to be at or below the line's `per_policy_aggregate_cap_cents` less the policy's `aggregate_paid_cents`, floored at 0, and refuses with `ERR_AGGREGATE_CAP_EXHAUSTED` otherwise. That last check deliberately reads the value stored at screening time and runs *before* the approval recompute, which means an earlier approval of a sibling claim that consumed the policy aggregate in the meantime will block this approval rather than silently shrinking it.

When all three clear, the claim moves `RECOMMENDED → APPROVED` and four things happen together. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded in `approved_by_user_id`. The statutory net payable computation is re-run, so that any other-insurance recovery reported since screening is reflected in `net_payable_cents`, `alt_net_payable_cents` and `cap_binding`. The resulting `net_payable_cents` is added into the insolvency file's `reserved_claim_cents`, which is where the association's hard claim liability now lives. And the parent policy's `aggregate_paid_cents` is recomputed, so the next sibling claim to reach approval sees a correctly reduced remaining aggregate.

### 3.8. Multistate Coordination with Sister Associations

A policyholder or claimant whose statutory connecting factor points to a different state belongs to that state's guaranty association, not to this one. An `AssociationAdmin` offers such a claim outward while it is in `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE` or `ADJUSTING`, supplying the `target_state_code` of the sister association and the `basis` on which jurisdiction is asserted. `target_state_code` must be exactly two uppercase letters and must not be this association's own state code `MI`. `basis` is one of `POLICYHOLDER_RESIDENCE`, `CLAIMANT_RESIDENCE` or `PROPERTY_LOCATION`, and the server resolves it into `basis_state_code`: `POLICYHOLDER_RESIDENCE` resolves to the parent policy's `policyholder_state_code`, while both `CLAIMANT_RESIDENCE` and `PROPERTY_LOCATION` resolve to the claim's `claimant_state_code`. The resolved `basis_state_code` must equal `target_state_code`, else the offer is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_BASIS_MISMATCH` — the association will not export a claim to a state that the asserted facts do not actually point at.

Only one referral may be in flight per claim. If any referral on the claim is still `OPEN`, a further offer is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_OPEN`, and this check is evaluated ahead of the claim-state check so that a claim already parked with a sister association reports the open referral rather than merely reporting an inadmissible status. A successful offer creates the referral in `OPEN` with `response_note` and `responded_at_utc` null, moves the claim from whichever of `SCREENED_ELIGIBLE` or `ADJUSTING` it was in into `REFERRAL_PENDING`, records the status it came from in `pre_referral_status`, and increments the insolvency file's `referral_pending_count` by one.

The sister association's answer is recorded by an `AssociationAdmin` as a `disposition` of either `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED`, with an optional `response_note`; the target referral must still be `OPEN`, else `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_ANSWERED`. Both dispositions set the referral's `status` accordingly along with `response_note` and a `responded_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, and both decrement the insolvency file's `referral_pending_count` by one. Beyond that the two branches diverge sharply and each must be implemented in full.

On `ACCEPTED` the claim moves `REFERRAL_PENDING → REFERRED_OUT`, which is terminal here. `referred_to_state_code` is set to the referral's `target_state_code`, `pre_referral_status` is cleared to null, every vendor engagement on the claim whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` is closed, and `net_payable_cents` is set to 0 so that the departed claim leaves this association's waterfall entirely and stops contributing to `reserved_claim_cents`. On `DECLINED` the claim is restored to whatever `pre_referral_status` holds and `pre_referral_status` is then cleared to null: a claim that was parked from adjustment moves `REFERRAL_PENDING → ADJUSTING`, and a claim that was parked from screening moves `REFERRAL_PENDING → SCREENED_ELIGIBLE`. A declined referral changes no money, closes no engagement and leaves `net_payable_cents` exactly as it was — the claim simply resumes where it left off. Referrals on a claim may be listed with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.9. Funding the Association: Member Assessments, Caps and Recoupment

The association's money comes from the insurers that are still solvent. An `AssociationAdmin` registers each member insurer's premium base one line at a time, supplying `naic_code`, `insurer_name`, an `assessable_line` drawn from the same four statutory lines, a `base_year` that must be an integer between 2000 and 2100 inclusive, and the `direct_written_premium_cents` written in this state for that line in that year, which must be at least 0. `annual_cap_rate_bps` is optional and defaults to 200; it expresses the per-member annual assessment ceiling in basis points of the member's own premium base and must be an integer between 0 and 200 inclusive, so 200 basis points — two percent of premium — is both the default and the statutory maximum, and a regulator-reduced rate can only be lower. The row is created with `account_status` `ACTIVE` and with the next monotone `sequence` value assigned by the server, that `sequence` being the deterministic tie-break after `created_at` ascending. The triple of `naic_code`, `assessable_line` and `base_year` must be unique across member insurer rows; a repeat registration is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_DUPLICATE_LINE`. A member row may later be amended to change any of `direct_written_premium_cents`, `annual_cap_rate_bps` or `account_status`, each field optional in the amendment payload, and every amendment increments the member row's `version` by one. `account_status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `IMPAIRED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and only `ACTIVE` rows will ever enter an allocation pool.

An `AssessmentOfficer` drafts a levy against a non-closed insolvency file by supplying the `insolvency_id` whose covered-claim liabilities the levy funds, the `assessable_line` to be assessed, the `base_year` whose premium base will be used, and the `needed_amount_cents` the association must raise, which must be greater than 0. The assessment is created in `DRAFT` with `levied_amount_cents`, `shortfall_cents`, `member_count` and `collected_amount_cents` all at 0 and with `certified_at_utc` null. Assessments of an insolvency file may be listed with an optional `status` filter, and read individually to see the levied total, the shortfall and the collected total.

Certification is where the allocation happens, and it is admissible only while the assessment is in `DRAFT` and the governing insolvency file is in `TRIGGERED`, a `DECLARED` file being refused with `ERR_INSOLVENCY_NOT_TRIGGERED`. The eligible pool is the set of member insurer rows whose `account_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `assessable_line` matches the assessment's `assessable_line`, whose `base_year` matches the assessment's `base_year`, and whose `direct_written_premium_cents` is strictly greater than 0. Members in `IMPAIRED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded from the pool entirely and receive no invoice at all. If the pool is empty, certification is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_BASE_EMPTY`.

The allocation itself is a capped redistribution. Before any spreading, each pooled member's ceiling is computed once: its `cap_amount_cents` is its `direct_written_premium_cents` multiplied by its `annual_cap_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents. The outstanding need starts at the assessment's `needed_amount_cents`, and the allocation then proceeds through at most three redistribution rounds. In each round, the total premium base is the sum of `direct_written_premium_cents` across the members still in the pool; if the pool has become empty, or that total base is 0, the rounds stop. Each pooled member's raw share for the round is the outstanding need multiplied by that member's `direct_written_premium_cents`, divided by the total base, truncated downward to whole cents. Because truncation loses cents, the residual — the outstanding need less the sum of the truncated raw shares — is then handed out one cent at a time, walking the pool ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `sequence` ascending, until the residual is exhausted.

The round then asks which members' raw shares exceeded their own ceilings. If none did, every pooled member is allocated its raw share, the pool empties and the allocation is finished. If any did, each such member is instead fixed at exactly its `cap_amount_cents`, taken in `created_at` ascending then `sequence` ascending order, its cap amount is subtracted from the outstanding need, and it is removed from the pool — after which the next round re-spreads the reduced need over the remaining premium base, so the uncapped members necessarily carry a strictly larger share than their first-round raw figure. Should the third round complete with members still in the pool, each remaining member is allocated whichever is smaller of its last computed raw share and its `cap_amount_cents`.

Each pooled member receives one invoice row. Its `premium_base_cents` is a snapshot of the member's `direct_written_premium_cents` at certification time, so a later amendment to the member row cannot retroactively change a levy. Its `cap_amount_cents` is the ceiling computed above and its `levied_amount_cents` is the amount allocated to it. Its `capped` flag is true exactly when its `levied_amount_cents` equals its `cap_amount_cents` *and* its uncapped raw share had exceeded that cap, and false otherwise — a member whose raw share happened to land exactly on its cap without ever exceeding it is not flagged as capped. Each invoice starts with `paid_amount_cents`, `surcharge_rate_bps`, `surcharge_base_cents`, `projected_recoupment_cents` and `recouped_amount_cents` all at 0, with `billing_status` `INVOICED`, with `recoupment_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and with the next monotone `sequence` value within its parent assessment, which is the deterministic tie-break after `created_at` ascending in remainder-cent allocation. On the assessment itself, `levied_amount_cents` becomes the sum of the invoiced amounts, `member_count` becomes the number of invoices created, `shortfall_cents` becomes the assessment's `needed_amount_cents` less its `levied_amount_cents` — positive exactly when the per-member ceilings exhausted the pool before the need was met — and `certified_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock as the assessment moves `DRAFT → CERTIFIED`. The resulting `shortfall_cents` is added into the insolvency file's `assessment_shortfall_cents`. When no member's raw share exceeds its cap in the first round, the levy closes in a single round at exactly `needed_amount_cents` with `shortfall_cents` at 0; when every member caps out, the levy stops short and the whole gap lands in `shortfall_cents`.

A member assessment invoice then advances along **two independent dimensions**, and neither one constrains the other. `billing_status` runs over `INVOICED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID` and `DELINQUENT` and tracks cash collection; `recoupment_status` runs over `NOT_STARTED`, `SURCHARGE_FILED`, `RECOUPING` and `RECOUPED` and tracks recovery from policyholders. An invoice reaching `PAID` on the billing dimension does not lock, close or advance the recoupment dimension, and a fully recouped invoice may still be unpaid. Invoices may be listed with optional filters on `assessment_id`, `member_insurer_id`, `billing_status` and `recoupment_status`, and read individually with both dimensions exposed.

On the billing dimension, a `MemberInsurerAgent` remits cash against its own levy by supplying `amount_cents` and a `payment_ref`. When `paid_amount_cents` plus `amount_cents` is strictly below `levied_amount_cents`, the invoice moves `INVOICED → PARTIALLY_PAID` and `amount_cents` is added to `paid_amount_cents`. When that sum equals `levied_amount_cents` exactly, `paid_amount_cents` is set to `levied_amount_cents` and the invoice moves `INVOICED → PAID`, or `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` when it had already been part-paid. When the sum would exceed `levied_amount_cents`, the payment is refused with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT`. Every payment recomputes the parent assessment's `collected_amount_cents` as the sum of `paid_amount_cents` across its invoices, and leaves `recoupment_status` untouched. When every invoice of an assessment has reached `PAID`, the assessment itself bubbles up `CERTIFIED → SETTLED` and its `collected_amount_cents` is set equal to its `levied_amount_cents`. Delinquency is driven by the clock rather than by a request: whenever the logical clock is advanced, every invoice still in `INVOICED` whose parent assessment's `certified_at_utc` advanced by 30 days falls strictly before the new instant is swept `INVOICED → DELINQUENT`, leaving its `recoupment_status` untouched, and the count of invoices swept is reported back as `delinquent_member_assessment_count`.

On the recoupment dimension, a member recovers its levy from its own policyholders through a filed rate surcharge. The `MemberInsurerAgent` files once per invoice, while `recoupment_status` is still `NOT_STARTED`, supplying `surcharge_rate_bps`, the `surcharge_base_cents` of written premium the surcharge applies to, and a `filing_ref`. There is a statutory aggregate: the sum of `surcharge_rate_bps` this member has already filed across its invoices whose `recoupment_status` is `SURCHARGE_FILED` or `RECOUPING`, plus the rate now requested, must stay at or below 300 basis points — three percent of written premium — and a filing that would push the total past that ceiling is refused with `ERR_SURCHARGE_AGGREGATE_EXCEEDED`. A successful filing moves the invoice `NOT_STARTED → SURCHARGE_FILED`, stores `surcharge_rate_bps` and `surcharge_base_cents` from the filing, leaves `billing_status` untouched, and computes `projected_recoupment_cents` as `surcharge_base_cents` multiplied by `surcharge_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents, and then limited to whichever is smaller of that figure and `levied_amount_cents` — a member may not project recovering more than it was levied.

Surcharge proceeds are then reported as they come in, by supplying `proceeds_cents` and a `remittance_ref`. Remittance requires `recoupment_status` to be `SURCHARGE_FILED` or `RECOUPING`; a remittance while the dimension is still `NOT_STARTED` or already `RECOUPED` is refused with `ERR_RECOUPMENT_NOT_FILED`. When `recouped_amount_cents` plus `proceeds_cents` is strictly below `levied_amount_cents`, the invoice moves `SURCHARGE_FILED → RECOUPING` and `proceeds_cents` is added to `recouped_amount_cents`. When that sum reaches `levied_amount_cents` exactly, the dimension completes — `SURCHARGE_FILED → RECOUPED` when the whole levy arrives in a single remittance, or `RECOUPING → RECOUPED` when it arrives after earlier partial proceeds — and `recouped_amount_cents` is set to `levied_amount_cents`. The boundary is exact: reaching precisely `levied_amount_cents` completes the dimension, while a sum even one cent above `levied_amount_cents` is refused with `ERR_OVER_RECOUPMENT`. `RECOUPED` is terminal on this dimension.

### 3.10. Estate Cash, the Distribution Waterfall and Winding Up

Cash reaches the association only from the receiver, and only on a triggered file. A `ReceiverLiaison` records each early-access payment or dividend by supplying `amount_cents` and a `remittance_ref`; the amount is added to `estate_remitted_cents`, `estate_undistributed_cents` is recomputed from the closed-ledger identity rather than incremented, and the insolvency file's `version` is incremented by one — which is precisely why a distribution run planned against a pre-remittance view of the file will be turned away as stale.

An `AssociationAdmin` then executes a distribution run, supplying `distribution_amount_cents` together with `expected_insolvency_version`. The amount must be at or below the file's current `estate_undistributed_cents`, else the run is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ESTATE_FUNDS`; the association cannot distribute cash it does not hold. The supplied amount is the funds entering the waterfall, and the waterfall pays three classes strictly in order: class 1 is association administrative expense, class 2 is covered claim payments, and class 3 is unearned premium refunds. A class receives only what the classes above it left behind.

Class 1 is a single figure rather than a list of items: the outstanding administrative expense is the file's `administrative_expense_cents` less its `administrative_paid_cents`, and class 1 is paid whichever is smaller of the available funds and that outstanding figure. Whatever is paid increases `administrative_paid_cents` and is deducted from the funds carried forward.

Class 2 comprises the covered claims of this insolvency file whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` — and only those; claims in `RECOMMENDED`, `REFERRED_OUT` or `DENIED_NOT_COVERED` are excluded, and a claim already in `PAID` has nothing outstanding. Each such claim's outstanding amount is its `net_payable_cents` less its `paid_amount_cents`, and the class is ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `sequence` ascending. Class 3 comprises the unearned premium refunds on policies of this insolvency file whose `status` is `PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; each refund's outstanding amount is its `computed_refund_cents` less its `paid_amount_cents`, and the class is ordered by `priority_class` ascending first — so the statutory line the coverage configuration ranked lowest is paid first — then by `created_at` ascending, then by `sequence` ascending.

Within a class the allocation has exactly two modes, and both must be implemented. When the funds reaching the class are greater than or equal to the total outstanding across all its items, every item is paid its full outstanding amount, the surplus flows on to the next class, and no rounding step occurs at all. When the funds reaching the class are less than that total, the class is settled pro rata and nothing flows onward: each item's share is the available funds multiplied by that item's outstanding amount, divided by the class's total outstanding, truncated downward to whole cents. The residual — the available funds less the sum of the truncated shares — is then handed out one cent at a time, walking the items in that class's own declared ordering, until the residual is exhausted. Each item is paid its resulting share, and the funds carried to the next class are 0.

Every payment cascades into the item that received it, and the boundaries are exact. A covered claim in `APPROVED` that receives a positive amount strictly less than its outstanding figure moves `APPROVED → PARTIALLY_PAID` with the allocated amount added to its `paid_amount_cents`. A covered claim in `APPROVED` that receives exactly its outstanding figure moves `APPROVED → PAID` with `paid_amount_cents` set equal to `net_payable_cents`, and a claim already in `PARTIALLY_PAID` that receives exactly its remaining outstanding figure moves `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` the same way. Any claim transition into `PAID` additionally closes every vendor engagement on that claim still in `ASSIGNED`. In every case the allocated amount is subtracted from the insolvency file's `reserved_claim_cents` and added to its `estate_distributed_cents`, so the reserve unwinds exactly as the cash goes out. `PAID` is terminal for a claim.

Unearned premium refunds cascade in the same shape. A refund in `PENDING` that receives a positive amount strictly less than its `computed_refund_cents` moves `PENDING → PARTIALLY_PAID` with the allocated amount added to its `paid_amount_cents`. A refund in `PENDING` that receives exactly its `computed_refund_cents` moves `PENDING → PAID` with `paid_amount_cents` set equal to `computed_refund_cents`, and a refund in `PARTIALLY_PAID` that receives exactly its remaining outstanding figure moves `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` the same way. In every case the allocated amount is subtracted from the insolvency file's `pending_up_liability_cents` and added to its `estate_distributed_cents`. `PAID` is terminal for a refund.

At the end of the run the file's `estate_distributed_cents` has grown by the class 1 payment plus everything paid in classes 2 and 3, `estate_undistributed_cents` has been recomputed from the closed-ledger identity, `distribution_sequence` has been incremented by one, and the file's `version` has been incremented by one. The run reports back the new `distribution_sequence`, the three class totals as `class1_paid_cents`, `class2_paid_cents` and `class3_paid_cents`, the per-item detail as `claim_payments` and `refund_payments`, and the new `estate_distributed_cents` and `estate_undistributed_cents` so the caller can immediately see the ledger it must pass as `expected_insolvency_version` next time. Once every claim and every refund has been settled or otherwise resolved, the file can be closed and the residue returned to the receiver as described at the start of this section's lifecycle narrative.
