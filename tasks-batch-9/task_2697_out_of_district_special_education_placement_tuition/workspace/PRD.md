# Out-of-District Special Education Placement & Tuition API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a district's own continuum of programmes cannot deliver a student's IEP, the placement office must send that student out of district — and from that moment the district owns a problem that is simultaneously legal, clinical and fiscal. The placement team has to document why the least restrictive environment cannot be met in-house; the business office has to contract with a state-approved private school at a state-approved rate, encumber the appropriation before a single instructional day is delivered, and then reconcile every invoice the school sends against attendance the district has independently verified. Meanwhile parents who disagree may place the student unilaterally and file for reimbursement, hearing officers may award or deny that claim on two very different legal bases, and the district must chase back whatever the state high-cost fund and Medicaid will return. Today most districts run these threads in disconnected spreadsheets, which is why over-billing, lapsed encumbrances and unclaimed state recoveries are endemic.

This API consolidates the whole out-of-district lifecycle behind a single mount point, `/ood-placements`. It is built around one non-negotiable fiscal invariant — every cent of an appropriation is at all times either available, encumbered or disbursed, and no operation may break that decomposition — and one non-negotiable documentation invariant: money may not move on an out-of-district placement until the placement team's least-restrictive-environment record is complete, a second reviewer has approved it, and the school's attendance assertion has been independently verified by district staff. Everything else in this document exists to make those two invariants enforceable.

Two structural characteristics shape the design and should be understood before reading further. First, segregation of duties is enforced by *user identity*, not by role: the same role authors and approves a placement decision, and the same role verifies and pays an invoice, so the system compares the acting user's identifier against the recorded author or verifier and refuses when they match. Second, the domain is deeply cascading — terminating a placement touches service addenda, transportation seats, waitlisted riders on a shared route, an in-flight transition plan, unpaid disputed invoices and the district ledger, in that causal order — so implementers should expect a single write to mutate half a dozen resources and to bump each of their version counters.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that applies to every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identity and audit columns, money handling, ledger conservation, stale-view protection, cascade semantics, path resolution, and the strict order in which a request is refused.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). When the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among those authorised for the target operation, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

The seven roles are `PlacementCoordinator`, `TeamChair`, `FiscalOfficer`, `SchoolBillingAgent`, `ParentGuardian`, `HearingAdjudicator` and `SystemAdmin`.

A `PlacementCoordinator` is the district placement-office case manager and owns the operational lifecycle: registering students, drafting decisions, proposing placements, proposing service addenda, requesting and releasing transportation, verifying attendance, accepting progress reports and drafting transition plans. A `TeamChair` chairs the IEP placement team, reviews and approves decisions, and activates return-to-district transition plans. A `FiscalOfficer` works in the district business office and owns every movement of money: executing tuition contracts, authorising addenda, approving invoice payment, and filing state and Medicaid recovery claims. A `SchoolBillingAgent` is the billing contact at an approved private school and submits attendance records, invoices and progress reports. A `ParentGuardian` is the parent or guardian of a student, files unilateral-placement reimbursement claims, and controls Medicaid billing consent. A `HearingAdjudicator` is an independent hearing officer and is the only role that may adjudicate or remand a reimbursement claim. A `SystemAdmin` is the platform operator: it manages reference data — schools, transport routes, fiscal accounts and rate schedules — and the logical clock, and it deliberately holds no authority at all over placements, invoices, decisions or claims, so it cannot be used to sidestep either segregation-of-duties pair.

Two of the seven roles are entity-backed, and for those roles `X-User-ID` is not an opaque string but a resource identifier:

- When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolBillingAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ApprovedPrivateSchool`; ownership checks on any resource that resolves to a school compare that resource's school reference to `X-User-ID`. A `SchoolBillingAgent` may therefore read a placement, an attendance record, an invoice or a progress report only when the placement it hangs from names that agent's own school, and any attempt to reach a row belonging to another school is refused with `ERR_OUT_OF_SCOPE` (403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `ParentGuardian`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student`; ownership checks on any resource that resolves to a student compare that resource's student reference to `X-User-ID`, and a cross-student read or write is likewise refused with `ERR_OUT_OF_SCOPE`.

The remaining five roles — `PlacementCoordinator`, `TeamChair`, `FiscalOfficer`, `HearingAdjudicator` and `SystemAdmin` — carry an opaque operator identifier in `X-User-ID` that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition captures an actor: as `authored_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a placement decision, as `verified_by_user_id` on an attendance record, and as `verified_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a tuition invoice. It never compares those values to any row's `id`.

Read access is broad by design: all thirty-two read operations are open to all seven roles, and the two entity-backed roles are narrowed by the ownership check described above rather than by role exclusion. A `SchoolBillingAgent` may therefore address a placement detail read, but only for placements at its own school.

Segregation of duties is enforced twice, in both cases by comparing `X-User-ID` against a value the system froze earlier, and in both cases within a single role. Approving a placement decision requires an acting user different from the decision's `authored_by_user_id`; approving payment on a tuition invoice requires an acting user different from that invoice's `verified_by_user_id`. Either collision yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Because both pairs live inside one role — `TeamChair` for the decision pair, `FiscalOfficer` for the invoice pair — the constraint cannot be satisfied by handing the second step to a different role; a second human holding the same role is required.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` skips that many rows from the front of the ordering and defaults to 0.

All list reads return rows ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a narrative rule says otherwise. For list responses the `meta` object of the envelope carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`; `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system carries the same four system-managed properties, and all fifteen resource types carry all four — none of them is identity-only.

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix; a placement identifier and an invoice identifier are indistinguishable by shape, so clients must track which collection an identifier came from.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant the row was first written, with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, in the same format. It is refreshed on every change, including changes made to a row as a side effect of a write directed at some other row.
- `version` (integer): a counter that increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row. It is the value the stale-view guards in §2.7 compare against.

Because cascades in this domain mutate many rows at once, one client request routinely advances several `version` counters. A payment approval, for instance, advances the invoice, the fiscal account, the placement, and — when the high-cost threshold is crossed — the newly created funding claim and the student. Each such touched row also gets a fresh `updated_at`.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `SystemAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting operation, which makes activation-grace expiry, billing-window checks and notice-day arithmetic fully deterministic. Every field described in this document as "the instant of" a transition — `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `contract_executed_at`, `activated_at`, `terminated_at`, `promoted_at` — is stamped from the logical clock at the moment the transition is taken, not from the host's system time. Date-only comparisons (start dates, notice dates, billing periods, target return dates) use the calendar date of the logical clock.

### 2.5. Whole-Cent Money and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer in JSON. A monetary amount supplied as a decimal string, or as a floating-point number, is a malformed request and draws `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). There is no currency-formatting layer: an appropriation of two hundred thousand dollars is `20000000`.

Where a computation involves a non-integer factor — a fuel index, a travel differential, a fee percentage, a reduction percentage, a Medicaid participation rate — the intermediate product is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up**, meaning a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero. Two rounding disciplines matter enormously and are stated once here because they are the most common source of a one-cent discrepancy:

- **Round per line, then sum.** Where a total is built from several component lines, each line is rounded to whole cents *before* the lines are added together. Summing the raw products and rounding once at the end produces a different total and is wrong. This governs the four billing lines of a tuition invoice, the per-addendum related-service lines inside the third of those, and the two award components of a reimbursement claim.
- **Round before clamping.** Where a rounded figure is then capped or floored, the rounding happens first and the cap or floor is applied to the rounded integer.

Non-monetary computed numbers have their own precision. A progress report's `report_score` and a return-readiness aggregate are each rounded half up to two decimal places. An invoice's `variance_pct` is rounded half up to four decimal places. Service hours (`authorized_hours_per_week`, `hours_delivered`, `delivered_hours_ytd`, `billable_service_hours`) are decimal numbers in quarter-hour increments; a value that is not an exact multiple of 0.25 is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a rule explicitly states an exclusive comparison, every threshold in this document is inclusive. Where a threshold is exclusive it is called out at the point of use, and there are exactly three such places: the invoice variance tolerance (a variance strictly greater than the tolerance disputes the invoice), the high-cost funding trigger (a paid-to-date figure strictly below the trigger is rejected, so a figure exactly at the trigger qualifies), and the parental notice period (notice strictly fewer than the required number of days ahead is late, so notice exactly that many days ahead is timely).

### 2.6. The District Fiscal Ledger: Encumbrance, Disbursement and Conservation

Money in this system lives on a `DistrictFiscalAccount`, which a `FiscalOfficer` or `SystemAdmin` opens for a `fiscal_year` under a unique `account_code` with an `authorized_cents` appropriation. That appropriation is then decomposed into exactly three buckets at all times: `available_cents` (unencumbered and undisbursed), `encumbered_cents` (committed by a contract, addendum, transport assignment or transition reserve but not yet paid out) and `disbursed_cents` (actually paid out on settled invoices and reimbursement awards). At creation the whole appropriation sits in `available_cents`, with `encumbered_cents` and `disbursed_cents` both zero.

**The conservation identity.** For every account, `authorized_cents` always equals the sum of `available_cents`, `encumbered_cents` and `disbursed_cents`. Every fiscal movement in this document is expressed as a transfer between two of those three buckets, never as a unilateral change to one of them. Booking an encumbrance moves cents from available to encumbered. Releasing an encumbrance moves them back from encumbered to available. Settling an invoice moves them from encumbered to disbursed, leaving available untouched. Paying a reimbursement award moves them from available directly to disbursed, because a parental claim was never encumbered. Reversing an award on remand is the exact compensating action: the same figure moves back from disbursed to available, restoring the pre-adjudication decomposition cent for cent.

**The placement-level mirror.** Each `Placement` also tracks its own share of the account. Its `encumbrance_cents` is defined exactly as the greater of zero and (`contract_encumbrance_cents` minus `paid_total_ytd_cents`), plus `addendum_encumbrance_cents`, plus `transport_encumbrance_cents`, plus `transition_reserve_cents`. The account's `encumbered_cents` always equals the sum of that quantity across every placement naming the account. A placement's `released_encumbrance_cents` accumulates every cent it has ever pushed back into `available_cents` — through termination, return to district, activation-grace expiry, addendum termination or transportation release — and is never decremented.

The four components of a placement's commitment are booked independently and at different moments. `contract_encumbrance_cents` is booked when the tuition contract is executed and is the only component an invoice settlement ever draws against. `addendum_encumbrance_cents` is the running sum of the commitments of that placement's `ACTIVE` service addenda. `transport_encumbrance_cents` is the running sum of the commitments of its `ACTIVE` transportation assignments. `transition_reserve_cents` holds the post-return transition reserve while a return plan is live.

A second conserved quantity governs transportation. On every `TransportRoute`, `seat_capacity` always equals `seats_available` plus `seats_committed`, from the moment the route is created (where `seats_committed` starts at zero) through every assignment, release and waitlist promotion. `seats_committed` is by definition the count of `ACTIVE` assignments on that route.

### 2.7. Stale-View Guards on Rate Publication and Invoice Creation

Two write operations require the client to prove it has observed the current state of a *parent* resource before it may create a child against it. Both are keyed on the parent's `version` counter, and both reject a mismatch rather than retrying.

**Invoice creation.** A `POST /ood-placements/tuition-invoices` request must carry `expected_placement_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `Placement` as the client last read it. If it does not equal that placement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION` (412). Creating an invoice is a perfectly legitimate operation from an unchanged placement — a new invoice for a billing period supersedes a prior voided or disputed one for the same period — so what this guard defends is not the transition but the writer's *view*: the placement's pinned rate schedule, its encumbrance components, its transport status and its paid-to-date figure all change how the invoice will later be verified, and a client that has not seen those changes would be billing against assumptions the district no longer holds.

**Rate-schedule publication.** A `POST /ood-placements/tuition-rate-schedules` request must carry `expected_school_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `ApprovedPrivateSchool` as the client last read it. If it does not equal that school's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCHOOL_VERSION` (412). Publishing a state-approved schedule legitimately supersedes the school's prior state-approved schedule for the same fiscal year, so again the create is valid from an unchanged state; the guard protects the rate-setter whose view of the school's approval standing and active-schedule pointer has gone stale. Note that changing a school's approval status is itself a mutation that advances the school's `version`, and executing a placement contract advances the placement's `version` — either invalidates any expected-version value a client is holding.

No other endpoint in this system takes an expected-version field, and no other write is guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check. Every other transition is guarded only by its own state preconditions.

### 2.8. Cascade Propagation Across the Placement Aggregate

This domain has a flat resource topology — every collection sits directly under the mount point and parentage is carried in body fields rather than in nested URL segments — but its *behaviour* is deeply hierarchical. Two named cascades are referenced throughout §3.

**Cascade A** is fired by terminating an active placement. It walks outward from the placement itself to the fiscal account, then to every active service addendum, then from each terminated addendum back to the fiscal account, then to every transportation assignment, then from each released assignment to its route's seat counters and the fiscal account, then — because a seat has just been freed — to the waitlist on that route, then from the promoted rider back to its own placement and the fiscal account, then to any active return transition plan, and finally to the placement's disputed unpaid invoices. The last four hops matter especially: each is caused by the mutation the previous hop performed, not by the terminate request itself. Releasing a seat is what makes a promotion possible, and the promotion is what books a fresh transport commitment on a *different* placement than the one being terminated.

**Cascade B** is fired by approving payment on a verified invoice. It walks from the invoice to the fiscal account, from the invoice to the placement's paid-to-date counter, then — only because that counter moved across the high-cost threshold — to a newly created state high-cost funding claim, and then, only because that claim now exists, to the student's high-cost flag and projected recovery rollup. If the counter does not cross the threshold, the third and fourth hops simply do not fire.

Collections that are mutated in bulk by a cascade are always processed in ascending `created_at` order of the affected rows, so the outcome of a cascade is deterministic. Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been written directly.

Because cascades create rows the client never asked for, some resources are discoverable only by re-reading a collection. A funding claim created by Cascade B, and a transportation assignment promoted off a waitlist, are both cases where the client's original response body does not mention the new or changed row; the corresponding filtered list read is the only channel through which they surface.

### 2.9. Collection Addressing and Reverse Lookup

Every entity is a peer collection directly beneath the `/ood-placements` mount, so a URL never nests one business resource inside another. Parentage is expressed through identifier fields in request bodies — a placement names its `student_id`, `school_id`, `decision_id`, `rate_schedule_id` and `fiscal_account_id`; an addendum, an assignment, an attendance record, an invoice, a progress report, a transition plan and a funding claim each name a `placement_id`; a rate schedule names its `school_id`; an invoice additionally names its `attendance_record_id`; a reimbursement claim names its `student_id`, its `fiscal_account_id` and optionally the `superseding_placement_id` of the district placement the parent turned down; and a placement may name the `prior_placement_id` of the placement it succeeds, forming a self-referential chain of successive placements for one student.

Where a path does carry identifiers, they are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names no existing row determines the 404 that comes back. Each entity has its own not-found code — `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADDENDUM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTENDANCE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSITION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDING_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FISCAL_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` — and the same code set applies to identifier fields inside request bodies. An unresolvable `prior_placement_id` or `superseding_placement_id` therefore yields `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `service_addendum_id` inside a service-delivery line yields `ERR_ADDENDUM_NOT_FOUND`.

Because nothing nests, every foreign-key relationship is instead traversable in reverse through a filtered collection read. Rate schedules filter by `school_id`, `fiscal_year` and `approval_state`. Placement decisions filter by `student_id` and `status`. Placements filter by `student_id`, `school_id`, `decision_id`, `rate_schedule_id`, `fiscal_account_id`, `prior_placement_id` and `status`. Service addenda filter by `placement_id` and `status`. Transportation assignments filter by `placement_id`, `route_id` and `status`. Attendance records filter by `placement_id` and `status`. Tuition invoices filter by `placement_id`, `attendance_record_id`, `verification_status` and `payment_status`. Reimbursement claims filter by `student_id`, `superseding_placement_id`, `fiscal_account_id` and `status`. Progress reports filter by `placement_id`, `status` and `fiscal_year`. Transition plans filter by `placement_id` and `status`. Funding claims filter by `placement_id`, `claim_type`, `status`, `fiscal_year` and `source`. Fiscal accounts filter by `fiscal_year`, students filter by `high_cost_flag`, private schools filter by `approval_status`, and transport routes take no filter beyond pagination. Every one of these filters is optional and combinable, and all of them accept `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.10. The Order in Which Requests Are Refused

A single request frequently breaks more than one rule, and clients must be able to predict which error they get. The refusal ladder is **total**: the system evaluates rules in the order below and always returns the error belonging to the earliest rung that is violated, never a later one. The domain rationale for the last three rungs in particular is that a placement that is unsafe or non-compliant must be refused even when money is available, and a placement that breaks a structural constraint must be refused before the district is told it is merely broke.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` present and one of the seven declared roles, `X-User-ID` present and non-empty — on every request. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation.** The declared role appears in the target operation's authorised set. Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the path resolves to an existing row, checked left to right, yielding that entity's not-found code (404).
4. **Request shape.** On every creating or modifying request: required fields present, enum values inside their declared sets, integers non-negative where required, dates well-formed ISO 8601 calendar dates or date-times, monetary fields supplied as integer cents rather than decimal strings, and no unknown field names. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. **Body reference existence.** Every identifier-bearing field in the body resolves to an existing row — `student_id`, `school_id`, `rate_schedule_id`, `decision_id`, `placement_id`, `route_id`, `attendance_record_id`, `fiscal_account_id`, `prior_placement_id`, `superseding_placement_id`, and every `service_addendum_id` inside a service-delivery line — each yielding its entity's not-found code (404).
6. **Entity-backed scope.** A `SchoolBillingAgent` may only read or write rows resolving to its own school, and a `ParentGuardian` only rows resolving to its own student. Otherwise `ERR_OUT_OF_SCOPE` (403). This rung applies to every operation either of those two roles can reach.
7. **Stale-view guards.** `expected_placement_version` must equal the placement's current `version` on invoice creation; `expected_school_version` must equal the school's current `version` on rate-schedule creation. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SCHOOL_VERSION` (412).
8. **State legality.** The target row's current state permits the requested transition, per the lifecycles in §3. This rung covers terminal-state lockdown and any second invocation of a transition already taken. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).
9. **Referential consistency.** Four checks, all 409: an invoice's attendance record must belong to the invoice's own placement (`ERR_ATTENDANCE_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH`); a new placement's decision must belong to the named student and its rate schedule to the named school (`ERR_PLACEMENT_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`); every service-delivery line on an attendance record must name an addendum on that same placement (`ERR_ADDENDUM_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH`); and a funding claim's fiscal year must match the fiscal year of the placement's pinned rate schedule, with that placement's contract actually executed (`ERR_FUNDING_CLAIM_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`).
10. **Temporal and window rules.** Six checks, all 409: billing-period shape (`ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_SHAPE`), billing period inside the placement window (`ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_PLACEMENT`), no duplicate or overlapping billing period (`ERR_DUPLICATE_BILLING_PERIOD`), rate-schedule effectivity (`ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`), placement window sanity (`ERR_INVALID_PLACEMENT_WINDOW`) and progress-report period uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRESS_PERIOD`).
11. **Safety and compliance.** Eight checks, all 409: LRE documentation completeness (`ERR_LRE_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`), the two segregation-of-duties comparisons (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), residential authorisation (`ERR_RESIDENTIAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED`), Medicaid consent (`ERR_MEDICAID_CONSENT_MISSING`), attendance-verified-before-money (`ERR_ATTENDANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`), school approval standing at activation (`ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_APPROVED`) and transition-services continuity (`ERR_TRANSITION_SERVICES_INCOMPLETE`).
12. **Structural constraints.** Nine checks, all 409: student placement exclusivity (`ERR_PLACEMENT_DATE_OVERLAP`), transport window exclusivity (`ERR_TRANSPORT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), the return-readiness gate (`ERR_RETURN_READINESS_NOT_MET`), the four round caps (`ERR_MAX_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`), the three uniqueness rules (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`), high-cost eligibility (`ERR_HIGH_COST_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`), the concurrent-placement bar on reimbursement (`ERR_CONCURRENT_DISTRICT_PLACEMENT`), the equitable-basis findings gate (`ERR_EQUITABLE_FINDINGS_REQUIRED`) and transport billing linkage (`ERR_TRANSPORT_NOT_ASSIGNED`).
13. **Fiscal capacity.** Four checks, all 402: the account must be able to cover an encumbrance or an award about to be booked (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FISCAL_AUTHORITY`), the placement's remaining contract commitment must cover an invoice about to be paid (`ERR_ENCUMBRANCE_EXCEEDED`), and a high-cost claim that computes to zero because the student's annual cap is already exhausted is refused (`ERR_HIGH_COST_CAP_EXHAUSTED`).

Two consequences of the ladder are worth stating explicitly because they are easy to get backwards. Rung 11 outranks rung 12, so a residential placement whose decision never recommended residential care is refused as unauthorised even when it *also* overlaps an existing placement. And rung 12 outranks rung 13, so a return transition plan that fails the readiness gate reports the readiness failure even when the district could not have afforded the reserve either.


## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Reference Data: Appropriations, Students, Schools, Rates and Routes

Nothing in this system can happen until the district's reference data is in place, and the four reference collections are deliberately owned by different people.

**The appropriation.** A `FiscalOfficer` or a `SystemAdmin` opens a `DistrictFiscalAccount` by supplying the `fiscal_year` it covers, an `account_code` that must be unique across all accounts, and the `authorized_cents` appropriated for out-of-district placements that year. The three derived ledger columns are seeded at creation: `available_cents` equals `authorized_cents`, while `encumbered_cents` and `disbursed_cents` both start at zero. From that point onward the account is read-only from the client's perspective — no endpoint edits it directly, and every change to its three buckets arrives as a side effect of some placement, addendum, transport, transition, invoice or award operation. `fiscal_year` on this and every other resource that carries one is an integer between 2020 and 2100 inclusive.

**The student.** A `PlacementCoordinator` or `SystemAdmin` registers a student with an active IEP by supplying `state_student_id` (the statewide identifier used on state funding claims, which must be unique across all students and between 1 and 32 characters long), `full_name` as printed on the IEP, `district_of_residence` (the code of the district financially responsible for the placement), the primary IDEA `disability_category`, the `iep_effective_date` as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and the `lre_current_setting` the student occupies today. `disability_category` must be one of `AUTISM`, `EMOTIONAL_DISABILITY`, `INTELLECTUAL_DISABILITY`, `MULTIPLE_DISABILITIES`, `SPECIFIC_LEARNING_DISABILITY` or `OTHER_HEALTH_IMPAIRMENT`. `lre_current_setting` must be one of `GENERAL_ED_80_PLUS`, `RESOURCE_40_79`, `SEPARATE_CLASS_UNDER_40`, `SEPARATE_DAY_SCHOOL` or `RESIDENTIAL_FACILITY`. Two optional booleans may be supplied at registration and both default to false: `medicaid_eligible`, true when the student is Medicaid-enrolled, and `medicaid_consent_on_file`, true when signed parental consent to bill Medicaid has been received. Three further fields are system-derived and always start at their floor: `placement_status` at `NONE`, `high_cost_flag` at false and `projected_state_recovery_cents` at 0.

A student's `placement_status` is a rollup of the out-of-district lifecycle and takes one of `NONE`, `DECISION_APPROVED`, `OOD_ACTIVE`, `RETURNED` or `TERMINATED`. It is never written by a client. It moves to `DECISION_APPROVED` when a placement decision for the student is approved, to `OOD_ACTIVE` when one of the student's placements is activated, to `RETURNED` when a placement closes by returning the student to district, and to `TERMINATED` when a placement is terminated outright. `high_cost_flag` is set true by the cascade link that creates a state high-cost funding claim for any of the student's placements, and `projected_state_recovery_cents` is the running sum of `reimbursable_amount_cents` over every funding claim whose placement belongs to this student and whose status is `ELIGIBLE` or `SUBMITTED`.

Medicaid posture is separately maintainable: a `ParentGuardian`, a `PlacementCoordinator` or a `SystemAdmin` may record or withdraw consent by supplying both `medicaid_eligible` and `medicaid_consent_on_file`, which increments the student's `version`. Withdrawal is prospective only — it does not retroact against funding claims that have already reached `SUBMITTED`.

**The school.** Only a `SystemAdmin` registers an `ApprovedPrivateSchool`, supplying the school's legal `name` and its `state_approval_id`, which must be unique across all schools. Three optional inputs round out the profile: `approval_status`, which defaults to `PROVISIONAL` when omitted and is otherwise one of `APPROVED`, `PROVISIONAL` or `REVOKED`; `residential_capable`, true when the school holds a residential twenty-four-hour programme licence, defaulting to false; and `medicaid_provider_number`, the state Medicaid provider number, which defaults to null for a school that cannot bill Medicaid. The school's `active_rate_schedule_id` is derived rather than supplied and starts as null; it always points at whichever of that school's rate schedules is `STATE_APPROVED` and carries the highest `fiscal_year` among such schedules, and it remains null while the school has no state-approved schedule at all.

Approval standing is separately maintainable, again only by a `SystemAdmin`, by supplying a new `approval_status`. Moving a school to `REVOKED` blocks activation of any placement at that school, but it deliberately does **not** retroactively terminate placements that are already active. Because this write increments the school's `version`, it also invalidates any expected-version value a rate-setter is holding, as described in §2.7.

**The rates.** A `FiscalOfficer` or `SystemAdmin` publishes a `TuitionRateSchedule` for a school and a fiscal year, supplying `school_id`, `fiscal_year`, the three state-approved rates, an effectivity window and an `approval_state`, together with the `expected_school_version` guard from §2.7. State fiscal years run July to June — FY2027 covers 1 July 2026 through 30 June 2027 — and the schedule's own `effective_from` and `effective_to` calendar dates bound the period it covers, both inclusive. `day_rate_cents` is the per-instructional-day tuition rate and must be at least 1 cent; `residential_rate_cents` is the per-night room-and-board rate and may be zero; `service_hourly_rate_cents` is the per-hour related-services rate, may be zero, and is the value later pinned onto a service addendum at authorisation. `approval_state` may be set to `DRAFT` or `STATE_APPROVED` at creation. It may never be set to `SUPERSEDED` by a client — that value is written only by the system, as described next.

Publishing a schedule whose `approval_state` is `STATE_APPROVED` for a school and fiscal year that already has a `STATE_APPROVED` schedule drives the incumbent from `STATE_APPROVED` to `SUPERSEDED`, writes the new schedule's identifier into the incumbent's `superseded_by_schedule_id`, repoints the school's `active_rate_schedule_id` at the new schedule and increments the school's `version`. Critically, placements that have already executed their contract keep the schedule they pinned and are **not** repriced — a mid-year rate revision changes future contracts only. Schedules in `DRAFT` are never superseded by anything; they simply stay `DRAFT`, and a `DRAFT` schedule can never be used to execute a contract. A schedule's `superseded_by_schedule_id` stays null for as long as its `approval_state` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`.

**The routes.** A `PlacementCoordinator` or `SystemAdmin` creates a `TransportRoute` with a `route_code` unique across all routes, a `seat_capacity` between 1 and 24 specialised seats inclusive, a `daily_rate_cents` per-service-day contracted rate of at least 1 cent, and a service window given as `service_window_start_min` and `service_window_end_min`, both expressed as minutes since midnight. `service_window_start_min` lies between 0 and 1439 inclusive, `service_window_end_min` between 1 and 1440 inclusive, and the end must be strictly greater than the start. On creation `seats_committed` is zero and `seats_available` equals `seat_capacity`, so the seat conservation identity of §2.6 holds from the very first moment.

### 3.2. The Placement-Team Decision Record and Its Revision Cycle

Before a district may contract with a private school it must document, on the record, why the least restrictive environment cannot be delivered in district. That record is a `PlacementDecision`, and it moves through four states: `DRAFT`, `TEAM_REVIEW`, `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` and the terminal `APPROVED`.

A `PlacementCoordinator` or a `TeamChair` opens the record in `DRAFT` by naming the `student_id` the team is deciding for and supplying three pieces of substance: an `lre_justification` narrative explaining why the least restrictive environment cannot be met in district, a `continuum_options_considered` array recording each continuum-of-alternative-placements option the team examined and rejected, and a `recommended_placement_type` of either `DAY` or `RESIDENTIAL`. Each entry of `continuum_options_considered` is an object with a `setting` naming the option and a `rejected_reason` explaining why the team rejected it. At creation the record captures the acting user's identifier as `authored_by_user_id` and freezes it forever; `revision_round` starts at 0, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `reviewer_notes` are null, and `submitted_at` and `decided_at` are null.

While the record sits in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, either of those two roles may revise it, supplying any subset of `lre_justification`, `continuum_options_considered` and `recommended_placement_type`; each such revision increments the record's `version`. Revision is refused once the record has entered `TEAM_REVIEW`, and refused permanently once it reaches the terminal `APPROVED` state — in both cases with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Submitting the record for team review advances it from `DRAFT` to `TEAM_REVIEW`, and the same submission action also closes a revision cycle by advancing it from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` back to `TEAM_REVIEW`. Either path is available to a `PlacementCoordinator` or a `TeamChair`, and either path stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock instant and increments `version`. Submission is where the documentation completeness gate bites: the `lre_justification`, once leading and trailing whitespace is stripped, must be at least 40 characters long; `continuum_options_considered` must hold at least three entries, each with a non-empty `setting` and a non-empty `rejected_reason`; and `recommended_placement_type` must be set. Any shortfall is refused with `ERR_LRE_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. The `DRAFT` path carries one additional guard the revision path does not: no other decision record for the same student may already be sitting in `TEAM_REVIEW`. Note also that `revision_round` is **not** touched by submission — it is advanced only by a return for revision.

A `TeamChair`, and only a `TeamChair`, disposes of a record under review, and the two dispositions are siblings out of `TEAM_REVIEW`.

*Returning for revision* moves the record `TEAM_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`. It requires an acting user whose identifier differs from `authored_by_user_id`, and it requires `revision_round` to be strictly below three, since the review loop is bounded at three rounds. The action carries `reviewer_notes`, which are stored on the record. Its side effects are: the status change, `revision_round` incremented by one, `reviewed_by_user_id` set to the acting user's identifier, `reviewer_notes` stored, and `version` incremented. A fourth attempt to return the same record is refused with `ERR_MAX_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`.

*Approving* moves the record `TEAM_REVIEW → APPROVED` and is terminal. It likewise requires an acting user whose identifier differs from `authored_by_user_id` — a violation here is the first of the two segregation-of-duties comparisons and yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. It additionally requires that the student's `placement_status` not already be `OOD_ACTIVE`, since a student already in an active out-of-district placement needs that placement resolved before a new decision can authorise another. Approval accepts an optional `reviewer_notes`. Its side effects are: status set to `APPROVED`, `decided_at` stamped with the logical clock instant, `reviewed_by_user_id` set to the acting user, `reviewer_notes` stored, the student's `placement_status` set to `DECISION_APPROVED` with the student's `version` incremented, and the record's own `version` incremented.


### 3.3. Contracting a Placement: Proposal, Execution, Activation and Lapse

A `Placement` is the contract between the district and the receiving school, and it is the hub every downstream resource hangs from. Its lifecycle runs `PROPOSED` → `CONTRACTED` → `ACTIVE` and then forks into one of three terminal states: `TERMINATED`, `RETURNED_TO_DISTRICT` or `EXPIRED`. Alongside that lifecycle it carries an entirely independent transportation dimension in `transport_status`, which takes one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `ASSIGNED`, `WAITLISTED` or `RELEASED` and evolves on its own schedule.

Only a `PlacementCoordinator` proposes a placement. The payload names the `student_id` being placed, the `school_id` of the receiving approved private school, the `decision_id` of the placement-team decision authorising it, the `rate_schedule_id` whose rates will be pinned onto this placement and used for every downstream invoice line, and the `fiscal_account_id` the placement will encumber and disburse against. When this placement succeeds an earlier one it may also name that earlier placement's identifier in `prior_placement_id`, which chains successive placements for one student; the field is optional and defaults to null for a first placement. The substantive terms are a `placement_type` of either `DAY` or `RESIDENTIAL`, a `start_date` and `end_date` given as inclusive calendar dates, a `contracted_instructional_days` count between 1 and 260 inclusive that the contract commits to fund, an optional `contracted_residential_nights` count between 0 and 366 inclusive that defaults to 0, and an optional `transportation_required` boolean defaulting to false, true when the IEP requires district-funded specialised transportation. A `DAY` placement must carry `contracted_residential_nights` of exactly 0.

Five checks stand between the payload and a `PROPOSED` row, and by §2.10 they are evaluated in that fixed order. First, linkage: the named decision must belong to the named student and the named rate schedule must belong to the named school, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`. Second, rate effectivity: the named schedule's `approval_state` must be `STATE_APPROVED` and the placement's `start_date` must fall inside the schedule's inclusive `effective_from` to `effective_to` window, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` — a `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` schedule can never underwrite a placement. Third, window sanity: `end_date` must be strictly later than `start_date`, and `start_date` must not precede the current logical clock date, or the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_PLACEMENT_WINDOW`. Fourth, residential authorisation: a `RESIDENTIAL` placement requires the named decision's `recommended_placement_type` to be `RESIDENTIAL` **and** the named school's `residential_capable` to be true, or the refusal is `ERR_RESIDENTIAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Fifth, exclusivity: the new placement's closed `start_date`-to-`end_date` interval may not intersect the closed interval of any other placement for the same student in `PROPOSED`, `CONTRACTED` or `ACTIVE` status. Adjacency is permitted with no buffer whatsoever — the prior placement's `end_date` need only be strictly earlier than the new `start_date` — but a single shared day is an intersection and draws `ERR_PLACEMENT_DATE_OVERLAP`.

A freshly proposed placement is fiscally inert. Its `status` is `PROPOSED`, all four encumbrance components — `contract_encumbrance_cents`, `addendum_encumbrance_cents`, `transport_encumbrance_cents` and `transition_reserve_cents` — are zero, so its `encumbrance_cents` and `released_encumbrance_cents` are zero too, `paid_total_ytd_cents` is zero, and `transport_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` regardless of what `transportation_required` says. `contract_executed_at`, `activated_at`, `terminated_at` and `termination_reason` are all null.

**Executing the contract.** A `FiscalOfficer` advances the placement `PROPOSED → CONTRACTED`, and this is the moment the district's money is committed. The transition re-checks the decision's status (it must be `APPROVED`), the school's standing (its `approval_status` must be `APPROVED` or `PROVISIONAL` — a `REVOKED` school cannot be contracted with), the residential pairing (a `RESIDENTIAL` placement additionally requires the school's `residential_capable` to be true *and* its `approval_status` to be exactly `APPROVED`, so a provisionally approved school may take day students but not residential ones), and rate effectivity again — the schedule must still be `STATE_APPROVED`, must still belong to the placement's school, and must still cover the `start_date`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`.

The commitment itself is the **contract encumbrance**, computed from exactly two counters on the placement and two rates on the pinned schedule, and from nothing else. The tuition component is `contracted_instructional_days` multiplied by the schedule's `day_rate_cents`. The residential component is `contracted_residential_nights` multiplied by the schedule's `residential_rate_cents` when `placement_type` is `RESIDENTIAL`, and exactly zero when it is `DAY`. `contract_encumbrance_cents` is the sum of those two components. Both operands of each product are integers, so no rounding arises. Service addenda and transportation are deliberately excluded here — each books its own commitment at its own authorisation moment.

Before that figure is booked, the account must be able to cover it: `available_cents` must be at least the computed contract encumbrance, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FISCAL_AUTHORITY` (402) — and, per §2.10, only after every state, referential, temporal, safety and constraint check has already passed. On success the side effects are: `contract_encumbrance_cents` written, `encumbrance_cents` recomputed from its definition, `status` set to `CONTRACTED`, `contract_executed_at` stamped from the logical clock, `transport_status` flipped to `PENDING` when `transportation_required` is true and left at `NOT_REQUIRED` otherwise, the account's `encumbered_cents` increased and `available_cents` decreased by the contract encumbrance with the account's `version` incremented, and the placement's own `version` incremented — which, as §2.7 warns, invalidates any `expected_placement_version` a billing client is holding.

**Activation.** A `PlacementCoordinator` advances the placement `CONTRACTED → ACTIVE` once the student is actually in the seat. Three conditions gate it. The school's `approval_status` must be exactly `APPROVED` at this instant — both `PROVISIONAL` and `REVOKED` block activation with `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_APPROVED`, so a school revoked between contract execution and activation stops the placement dead even though the contract was legitimately executed against a provisional or approved school. The current logical clock date must be on or after `start_date` and no later than thirty days after `start_date`, that thirty-day span being the contract activation grace window. And when `transportation_required` is true, at least one transportation assignment on this placement must already be `ACTIVE`. Activation attempted before the start date, after the grace window has closed, or without the required transportation in place is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. On success: `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the student's `placement_status` becomes `OOD_ACTIVE` with the student's `version` incremented, and the placement's `version` is incremented.

**Lapse of the grace window.** A contracted placement that is never activated does not linger. Whenever the logical clock is advanced, the system evaluates every placement still in `CONTRACTED` — in ascending `created_at` order — and drives to `EXPIRED` each one whose new clock date is strictly beyond thirty days after its `start_date`. This is a system transition with no actor and no client request against the placement itself. Its side effects: `status` set to `EXPIRED`, `terminated_at` stamped from the logical clock, `termination_reason` set to the literal string `ACTIVATION_GRACE_EXPIRED`, and a release of the placement's remaining contract commitment — the difference between `contract_encumbrance_cents` and `paid_total_ytd_cents` moves from the account's `encumbered_cents` back into its `available_cents`, that same figure is added to the placement's `released_encumbrance_cents`, and `contract_encumbrance_cents` is then set equal to `paid_total_ytd_cents` so the remaining commitment is exactly zero. `encumbrance_cents` is recomputed, and both the account's and the placement's `version` counters advance.

### 3.4. Related Services and Specialised Transportation

Tuition is only part of what the district owes. Two peer resources add to a placement's commitment after the contract is executed, and each books its own money at its own authorisation moment.

**Related-services addenda.** A `PlacementCoordinator` proposes a `ServiceAddendum` against a placement by naming its `placement_id` and a `service_type` — one of `SPEECH`, `OT`, `PT`, `COUNSELING` or `BCBA` — together with `authorized_hours_per_week`, the weekly service hours the district authorises, and `contract_weeks`, the number of service weeks the addendum funds. `authorized_hours_per_week` must be greater than zero and no more than 40, and must be an exact multiple of 0.25. `contract_weeks` is an integer between 1 and 52 inclusive and defaults to 36 when omitted. An optional `medicaid_billable` boolean, defaulting to false, marks the addendum's hours as countable toward a Medicaid recovery claim. A placement may not hold two addenda for the same `service_type` while an existing one is still `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`; a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The addendum's lifecycle is `PROPOSED` → `ACTIVE`, with two terminal states, `TERMINATED` and `TRANSFERRED`, both reached only by cascade. On creation `status` is `PROPOSED`, `hourly_rate_cents` is 0, `encumbrance_cents` is 0 and `delivered_hours_ytd` is 0 — importantly, the rate is **not** pinned at proposal time.

A `FiscalOfficer` authorises the addendum, advancing it `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`. The parent placement must be `CONTRACTED` or `ACTIVE`; from any other placement state the authorisation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. At this moment, and only at this moment, `hourly_rate_cents` is copied from the `service_hourly_rate_cents` of the schedule pinned on the placement's `rate_schedule_id`, and frozen — a later rate revision never reprices an authorised addendum. The addendum's commitment is then `authorized_hours_per_week` multiplied by `contract_weeks` multiplied by that pinned hourly rate, rounded half up to whole cents (rounding matters here precisely because the hours may carry a quarter, half or three-quarter fraction). The account's `available_cents` must be at least that figure or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FISCAL_AUTHORITY`. On success: `encumbrance_cents` written, `status` set to `ACTIVE`, the placement's `addendum_encumbrance_cents` increased by that amount with the placement's `encumbrance_cents` recomputed and its `version` incremented, the account's `available_cents` decreased and `encumbered_cents` increased by the same amount with the account's `version` incremented, and the addendum's own `version` incremented.

An addendum's `delivered_hours_ytd` is a running total, not an input: it accumulates the `hours_delivered` of every service-delivery line naming this addendum on attendance records that have reached `VERIFIED`. Lines on records still `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED` contribute nothing.

**Transportation assignments.** Specialised transportation is modelled as a seat on a shared `TransportRoute`, and this is where the system's only intentionally non-erroring capacity constraint lives. A `PlacementCoordinator` requests transportation by naming the `placement_id` and the `route_id`, a requested pickup window as `pickup_window_start_min` (inclusive, 0 to 1439) and `pickup_window_end_min` (exclusive, 1 to 1440, and strictly greater than the start), a `days_per_week` count between 1 and 7 inclusive, and a `contract_weeks` count between 1 and 52 inclusive that defaults to 36. The placement must be `CONTRACTED` or `ACTIVE` and its `transportation_required` must be true.

The request resolves immediately and synchronously into one of two states, and the branch is decided by the route's seat position — not by an operator choice. Before either branch, the window is checked against the route: an assignment's occupancy is the half-open interval running from `pickup_window_start_min` up to but not including `pickup_window_end_min` plus a ten-minute turnaround buffer, and that interval may not intersect the equivalent interval of any other `ACTIVE` assignment on the same route. The buffer expands only the end side, so two riders separated by a gap of exactly ten minutes are permitted while nine minutes is not. The window must also fall inside the route's own `service_window_start_min` to `service_window_end_min` service window. A collision or an out-of-window request is a hard refusal with `ERR_TRANSPORT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). The same window-exclusivity rule is re-evaluated whenever a seat is released and a waitlisted rider is considered for promotion.

With the window clear, the branch is: if the route's `seats_available` is at least 1, the assignment resolves to `ACTIVE`; if `seats_available` is exactly 0, it resolves to `WAITLISTED`. Seat exhaustion is deliberately **not** an error — the request is parked on the route's waitlist and returns successfully. An assignment's lifecycle is therefore `REQUESTED` at the instant of creation, resolving at once to `ACTIVE` or `WAITLISTED`, with `RELEASED` as the single terminal state.

An assignment that resolves to `ACTIVE` books a **transport commitment** equal to `days_per_week` multiplied by `contract_weeks` multiplied by the route's `daily_rate_cents` multiplied by a fuel index of 1.0325, rounded half up to whole cents. The account's `available_cents` must cover it or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FISCAL_AUTHORITY`. On success the side effects run across four rows: `status` set to `ACTIVE` with `encumbrance_cents` written; the route's `seats_committed` incremented and `seats_available` decremented with the route's `version` incremented; the placement's `transport_status` set to `ASSIGNED`, its `transport_encumbrance_cents` increased by the commitment, its `encumbrance_cents` recomputed and its `version` incremented; and the account's `available_cents` decreased and `encumbered_cents` increased with its `version` incremented.

An assignment that resolves to `WAITLISTED` holds `encumbrance_cents` of exactly 0 — there is no fiscal commitment while waitlisted, and no seat is consumed. Its only side effect beyond its own creation is that the placement's `transport_status` becomes `WAITLISTED` and the placement's `version` is incremented. Its `promoted_at` stays null.

**Releasing a seat, and the promotion that follows.** A `PlacementCoordinator` may release an assignment from either `ACTIVE` or `WAITLISTED`, and the two paths are asymmetric because the two states hold different things. Releasing an `ACTIVE` assignment moves it `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, decrements the route's `seats_committed` and increments its `seats_available` with the route's `version` incremented, and unwinds the money exactly: the assignment's `encumbrance_cents` moves from the account's `encumbered_cents` back to its `available_cents`, is subtracted from the placement's `transport_encumbrance_cents`, is added to the placement's `released_encumbrance_cents`, and the assignment's own `encumbrance_cents` is then set to 0. The placement's `transport_status` becomes `RELEASED` only when no other `ACTIVE` assignment remains for that placement, and its `encumbrance_cents` is recomputed. Releasing a `WAITLISTED` assignment moves it `WAITLISTED → RELEASED` with no seat movement and no money movement at all, since it held neither; the placement's `transport_status` becomes `RELEASED` only when no other `ACTIVE` **or** `WAITLISTED` assignment remains for it.

Freeing a seat immediately runs the **waitlist promotion selector** on that route, and its rules are pinned so the outcome is never ambiguous. The candidate set is every assignment on the same route whose `status` is `WAITLISTED` and whose parent placement is currently `CONTRACTED` or `ACTIVE`. Candidates are ranked by `created_at` ascending; ties are broken by `pickup_window_start_min` ascending and then by `id` ascending, which makes the ordering total. The first candidate wins, and exactly one candidate is promoted per released seat even in the unusual case where the route's `seats_available` has climbed above one. When the candidate set is empty, no promotion occurs, `seats_available` simply stays incremented, and the release still returns 200 — an empty waitlist is not an error condition. A waitlisted assignment whose placement has since gone `TERMINATED`, `RETURNED_TO_DISTRICT` or `EXPIRED` is skipped by the filter and remains `WAITLISTED` indefinitely.

Promotion moves the winner `WAITLISTED → ACTIVE`, stamps its `promoted_at` from the logical clock, and books the full transport commitment for it using the same fuel-indexed formula above. Because the promoted rider may belong to an entirely different placement than the one whose seat was released, the fiscal and rollup effects land on *that* placement: its `transport_encumbrance_cents` grows by the new commitment, its `transport_status` becomes `ASSIGNED`, and its `version` is incremented, while the account's `available_cents` falls and its `encumbered_cents` rises by that same amount. Promotion is the only mechanism that writes `promoted_at`, and because the promoted row is not the row the client addressed, the filtered assignment list is the only channel through which the client can discover it.


### 3.5. Attendance Reporting and Tuition Invoice Verification

The district never pays a school's assertion at face value. Billing is a two-stage reconciliation: the school first asserts what it delivered in a twenty-eight-day cycle, the district verifies that assertion, and only then may an invoice be priced against it.

**The attendance record.** Either a `SchoolBillingAgent` or a `PlacementCoordinator` files an `AttendanceRecord` for a placement, naming the `placement_id` and delimiting the billing cycle with `period_start` and `period_end` as inclusive calendar dates. The cycle length is fixed: the closed interval from `period_start` to `period_end` must span exactly twenty-eight days, which means `period_end` is twenty-seven days after `period_start`. The substance of the record is four counts. `days_enrolled` is how many days the seat was held for the student and is the enrolment-basis billable-day figure; `days_attended` is how many days the student was physically present; `days_absent_unexcused` is the count of unexcused absence days; and `residential_nights` is the number of room-and-board nights delivered, optional and defaulting to 0. Each of the four is an integer between 0 and 28 inclusive. `days_attended` may not exceed `days_enrolled`, and `days_attended` plus `days_absent_unexcused` may not exceed `days_enrolled` either. `residential_nights` must be exactly 0 when the placement's `placement_type` is `DAY`.

Related-service delivery is reported in the same record through an optional `service_delivery_lines` array, defaulting to empty. Each entry is an object pairing a `service_addendum_id` with the `hours_delivered` under it, where `hours_delivered` is a number in quarter-hour increments. Every named addendum must belong to the same placement as the record and must currently be `ACTIVE`; a line naming an addendum on a different placement, or one that is not `ACTIVE`, is refused with `ERR_ADDENDUM_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH`.

Three period rules guard the filing, evaluated in the §2.10 order. A cycle whose closed interval is not exactly twenty-eight days, or where `days_enrolled` plus `days_absent_unexcused` exceeds twenty-eight, or where `residential_nights` exceeds twenty-eight, or where `days_attended` exceeds `days_enrolled`, is refused with `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_SHAPE`. A cycle that is not entirely inside the placement's contracted window — `period_start` on or after the placement's `start_date` and `period_end` on or before its `end_date` — is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_PLACEMENT`. And a cycle that shares a `period_start` with, or whose closed interval overlaps, another attendance record for the same placement in `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `DISPUTED` status is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BILLING_PERIOD`. A record lands in `SUBMITTED` with `amendment_round` at 0 and `verified_by_user_id` null.

**Verifying the attendance record.** A `PlacementCoordinator` disposes of a `SUBMITTED` record by supplying a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `DISPUTE`, with an optional `dispute_reason`. The two dispositions are siblings out of `SUBMITTED` and their side effects differ in one consequential way.

`ACCEPT` moves the record `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`, which is terminal. It requires the parent placement to be `ACTIVE`, `TERMINATED` or `RETURNED_TO_DISTRICT` — attendance may legitimately be verified after a placement has closed, because the last cycle usually lands after the student has left. Its side effects are: `status` set to `VERIFIED`, `verified_by_user_id` set to the acting user's identifier, and, for every entry in `service_delivery_lines`, that entry's `hours_delivered` added to the named addendum's `delivered_hours_ytd` with that addendum's `version` incremented. The record's own `version` is incremented.

`DISPUTE` moves the record `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` and requires nothing of the placement's state. Its side effects are: `status` set to `DISPUTED`, `verified_by_user_id` set to the acting user's identifier, the record's `version` incremented — and, crucially, **no** movement of `delivered_hours_ytd` on any addendum. Disputed hours are not banked.

A `SchoolBillingAgent`, and only a `SchoolBillingAgent`, amends a `DISPUTED` record, moving it `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED` and closing the amendment cycle. The acting agent's identity must resolve to the school on the record's placement, or the request is refused with `ERR_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. The amendment supplies replacement values for `days_enrolled`, `days_attended` and `days_absent_unexcused`, and optionally for `residential_nights` and `service_delivery_lines`; those values wholly replace the previous ones, and the same period-shape, placement-window and overlap validations re-run against them. Side effects: `status` back to `SUBMITTED`, `amendment_round` incremented by one, `verified_by_user_id` reset to null, and `version` incremented. The cycle is bounded at two amendment rounds — once `amendment_round` has reached two, a further amendment is refused with `ERR_MAX_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`.

**Submitting the invoice.** A `SchoolBillingAgent` or a `FiscalOfficer` creates a `TuitionInvoice` naming the `placement_id` being billed and the `attendance_record_id` the invoice bills against, restating the cycle as `billing_period_start` and `billing_period_end` — each of which must equal the corresponding `period_start` and `period_end` on the named attendance record, or the payload is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — plus `submitted_amount_cents`, the amount the school claims for the cycle, which must be a non-negative integer. `transport_days_billed` is optional, defaults to 0, and must be an integer between 0 and 28 inclusive. The create also carries the `expected_placement_version` stale-view guard of §2.7.

The named attendance record must belong to the invoice's own placement, or the create is refused with `ERR_ATTENDANCE_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH`. Only one live invoice may exist per attendance record: a second invoice against the same `attendance_record_id` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` while an existing one has `verification_status` of `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED` and a `payment_status` other than `VOIDED`. That phrasing is what makes a re-bill legitimate — once the incumbent invoice has been voided, or has been driven to `DISPUTED`, a fresh invoice for the same cycle may be filed.

A `TuitionInvoice` carries **two independent status dimensions** that evolve on separate schedules and never constrain each other's transitions except where explicitly stated. `verification_status` takes one of `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `DISPUTED`; `payment_status` takes one of `UNPAID`, `PAID` or `VOIDED`. A new invoice starts at `SUBMITTED` and `UNPAID` with `resubmission_round` at 0, `verification_basis` null, `verified_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null, and every computed figure — the four line fields, `gross_billable_cents`, `volume_credit_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `provisional_amount_cents`, `final_amount_cents`, `verified_amount_cents`, `variance_pct` and `settled_amount_cents` — at 0.

**Pricing the cycle.** A `PlacementCoordinator` or a `FiscalOfficer` verifies a `SUBMITTED`, `UNPAID` invoice by supplying a `basis` of either `ENROLLMENT` or `ATTENDANCE`, which is copied onto the invoice as `verification_basis`. The linked attendance record must already be `VERIFIED`; verification attempted while it is `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_ATTENDANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`. Exactly one attendance record enters the computation — the one named by `attendance_record_id` — together with the placement's pinned rate schedule, the addenda named by that record's service-delivery lines, and a single transportation assignment selected as described below. No other attendance period, addendum or assignment contributes anything.

The computation proceeds in a pinned order. **First**, the billable-day count is derived, and this is the *only* thing the two bases disagree about. Under the `ENROLLMENT` basis the billable-day count is simply `days_enrolled` — every held-seat day is billed regardless of whether the student showed up, which is the reserved-seat contract basis the school bills on while the cycle is open. Under the `ATTENDANCE` basis it is `days_attended` plus the lesser of `days_absent_unexcused` and three, meaning the first three unexcused absence days remain billable as a tolerance and every unexcused absence beyond the third is abated. The attendance basis is therefore strictly lower than the enrolment basis whenever `days_absent_unexcused` exceeds three, or whenever `days_attended` plus three is less than `days_enrolled`.

**Second**, four billing lines are computed, and — per §2.5 — each is rounded to whole cents *before* any of them are added together.

- `tuition_line_cents` is the billable-day count multiplied by the pinned schedule's `day_rate_cents`.
- `residential_line_cents` is the record's `residential_nights` multiplied by the pinned schedule's `residential_rate_cents` when the placement's `placement_type` is `RESIDENTIAL`, and exactly 0 when it is `DAY`.
- `service_lines_total_cents` is built entry by entry. The record's `service_delivery_lines` are processed in ascending `created_at` order of the `ServiceAddendum` each entry names. For each entry, the hours that may be billed are the lesser of that entry's `hours_delivered` and the named addendum's `authorized_hours_per_week` multiplied by four — a twenty-eight-day cycle is treated as four service weeks, so an addendum authorised for six hours a week may bill at most twenty-four hours in one cycle no matter what the school reports. Those capped hours are multiplied by the addendum's frozen `hourly_rate_cents` and by a travel differential of 1.075, and the product is rounded half up to whole cents to give that entry's line. `service_lines_total_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-entry lines, never a single rounding of the combined product.
- `transport_line_cents` is exactly 0 when `transport_days_billed` is 0. Otherwise it is `transport_days_billed` multiplied by the applicable route's `daily_rate_cents` multiplied by the fuel index of 1.0325, rounded half up to whole cents. The applicable route is the route of the placement's `ACTIVE` transportation assignment, and when the placement has more than one such assignment the candidates are ranked by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `pickup_window_start_min` ascending, and the first is taken. If the placement has no `ACTIVE` assignment at all while `transport_days_billed` is greater than 0, verification fails outright with `ERR_TRANSPORT_NOT_ASSIGNED`.

**Third**, `gross_billable_cents` is the sum of those four rounded lines.

**Fourth**, the gross is turned into a net payable by a three-step waterfall whose evaluation order is pinned precisely because the dependency looks circular — the net includes an administrative fee, the fee is a percentage of the post-credit subtotal, and the credit itself is tier-selected. The tier is keyed on `gross_billable_cents` **only**, never on the subtotal and never on the net. The credit rate is 3% when the gross is at least 1 000 000 cents (ten thousand dollars), 1.5% when the gross is at least 700 000 cents (seven thousand dollars) but below the higher floor, and 0% otherwise; both floors are inclusive. `volume_credit_cents` is the gross multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. The subtotal is the gross minus `volume_credit_cents`. `admin_fee_cents` is that subtotal multiplied by 2.25%, rounded half up to whole cents, independently of the credit rounding. The net payable is the subtotal plus `admin_fee_cents` — plain integer addition. The `volume_credit_cents` and `admin_fee_cents` persisted on the invoice are those produced by the run under the requested `verification_basis`.

**Fifth**, and this is the part implementers most often miss: **both bases are always evaluated and both results are always stored**, regardless of which one the caller asked for. `provisional_amount_cents` is the net payable computed with the enrolment billable-day count, and `final_amount_cents` is the net payable computed with the attendance billable-day count. `verified_amount_cents` then equals `provisional_amount_cents` when `verification_basis` is `ENROLLMENT` and `final_amount_cents` when it is `ATTENDANCE`. Because the two bases can produce different gross figures, they can also land in different volume-credit tiers; each variant runs the whole waterfall on its own gross.

**Finally**, the invoice's own verification outcome is decided arithmetically, not by an operator. `variance_pct` is the absolute difference between `submitted_amount_cents` and `verified_amount_cents`, divided by `verified_amount_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places. If `variance_pct` is greater than 0.02 — that is, if the school's claim is off by more than two percent of the district's own figure — the invoice auto-branches `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED`; otherwise it goes `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`, which is terminal for the verification dimension. Both branches write the identical set of line and dual-variant fields and both record `verified_by_user_id` as the acting user. Both branches leave `payment_status` untouched at `UNPAID`, since the two dimensions are independent. The invoice's `version` is incremented either way.

**Correcting a disputed invoice.** A `SchoolBillingAgent` whose identity resolves to the placement's school resubmits a `DISPUTED`, `UNPAID` invoice, supplying a replacement `submitted_amount_cents` and optionally a replacement `transport_days_billed`. This closes the verification cycle by moving `verification_status` back to `SUBMITTED` while `payment_status` remains `UNPAID` throughout. Side effects: the two supplied figures replace their predecessors, `resubmission_round` is incremented by one, and `verification_basis`, `verified_by_user_id`, `verified_amount_cents` and `variance_pct` are all cleared back to their initial values of null, null, 0 and 0 respectively; `version` is incremented. The cycle is bounded at two resubmission rounds, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_MAX_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`.


### 3.6. Payment Approval, Disbursement and State Recovery

**Approving payment.** Only a `FiscalOfficer` may approve payment, and the operation carries no request body — everything it needs is already on the invoice. It moves `payment_status` `UNPAID → PAID` on the second, independent status dimension and requires all of the following: `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, `payment_status` is `UNPAID`, the linked attendance record is *still* `VERIFIED` (a record that has since been driven back to `DISPUTED` blocks payment with `ERR_ATTENDANCE_NOT_VERIFIED`), the acting user's identifier differs from the invoice's `verified_by_user_id` (the second segregation-of-duties pair of §2.1; a self-approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and the placement still holds enough unspent contract commitment — `contract_encumbrance_cents` minus `paid_total_ytd_cents` must be greater than or equal to `verified_amount_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ENCUMBRANCE_EXCEEDED`. Note the ladder consequence: because segregation of duties sits at rung 11 and encumbrance sufficiency at rung 13, an officer who both verified the invoice *and* has exhausted the commitment is told about the duty conflict, not about the money.

Approval fires **Cascade B**, whose five links must be applied in order:

- **Link 0 (the invoice itself).** `payment_status` is set to `PAID`, `settled_amount_cents` is set to `verified_amount_cents`, `approved_by_user_id` is set to the acting user's identifier, and the invoice's `version` is incremented. `verification_status` stays at `VERIFIED`.
- **Link 1 (invoice to fiscal account).** The placement's `DistrictFiscalAccount` has `encumbered_cents` decreased by `settled_amount_cents` and `disbursed_cents` increased by the same figure; its `version` is incremented. `available_cents` and `authorized_cents` are untouched, which is exactly why the conservation identity of §2.6 continues to hold — the money moves from committed to spent, not out of the appropriation.
- **Link 2 (invoice to placement).** The placement's `paid_total_ytd_cents` is increased by `settled_amount_cents`, its `encumbrance_cents` is recomputed from the four components per §2.6 — which mechanically shrinks the contract component, since that component is the contract commitment net of payments to date — and its `version` is incremented.
- **Link 3 (placement to funding claim, caused by Link 2).** If and only if the *new* `paid_total_ytd_cents` has reached the high-cost trigger of 4 000 000 cents (forty thousand dollars) or more, and no `FundingClaim` already exists for this `placement_id` with `claim_type` of `HIGH_COST_FUND`, the placement's fiscal year and a `status` of `ELIGIBLE` or `SUBMITTED`, then a new `FundingClaim` is created by the system with `source` of `AUTO_CASCADE`, `status` of `ELIGIBLE`, `eligible_base_cents` equal to the placement's new `paid_total_ytd_cents`, and `reimbursable_amount_cents` computed by the high-cost rules below. This link is caused by Link 2's counter movement, not by the approval call: an approval that does not push the counter over the trigger, or one where a qualifying claim already exists, creates nothing.
- **Link 4 (funding claim to student, caused by Link 3).** Only if Link 3 actually created a claim, the student's `high_cost_flag` is set to true, `projected_state_recovery_cents` is increased by the new claim's `reimbursable_amount_cents`, and the student's `version` is incremented.

The claim created at Link 3 is returned in no response body — the approval returns the invoice. Per §2.8, the only way a client learns of it is the filtered funding-claim list.

**Filing a funding claim by hand.** A `FiscalOfficer` may also file a claim directly, naming the `placement_id`, the `claim_type` of either `HIGH_COST_FUND` or `MEDICAID`, and the `fiscal_year`. Such a claim carries `source` of `MANUAL`. Two gates precede the arithmetic. At rung 9, the linkage check: the supplied `fiscal_year` must equal the `fiscal_year` of the schedule named by the placement's `rate_schedule_id`, and the placement must have had its contract executed — `contract_executed_at` must not be null — or the request is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_CLAIM_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`. At rung 12, uniqueness: only one claim may exist per `placement_id`, `claim_type` and `fiscal_year` triple among claims in `ELIGIBLE` or `SUBMITTED` status, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

The two claim types then compute completely differently.

For a `HIGH_COST_FUND` claim, `eligible_base_cents` is the placement's `paid_total_ytd_cents`. If that figure is *below* 4 000 000 cents the claim is refused with `ERR_HIGH_COST_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`; the comparison is strict, so a placement that has paid exactly 4 000 000 cents qualifies. The excess is `eligible_base_cents` minus 4 000 000. The `tier_rate` is selected from that excess by inclusive floors: 0.90 when the excess is at least 6 000 000 cents, otherwise 0.75 when the excess is at least 2 000 000 cents, otherwise 0.60. Separately, the prior awarded total is the sum of `reimbursable_amount_cents` over every *other* `FundingClaim` with `claim_type` of `HIGH_COST_FUND`, the same `fiscal_year`, and a placement belonging to the same student, counting only claims in `ELIGIBLE` or `SUBMITTED` status — the cap is per student per fiscal year, not per placement, so a student moved between two schools mid-year shares one ceiling. `reimbursable_amount_cents` is then the greater of 0 and the lesser of two quantities: the excess multiplied by the `tier_rate` and rounded half up to whole cents, and 5 000 000 cents minus the prior awarded total. The rounding happens on the tier product *before* the cap comparison and before the floor at zero, per §2.5. If the trim leaves a figure of 0 — the student's fiscal-year ceiling is already exhausted — the claim is refused with `ERR_HIGH_COST_CAP_EXHAUSTED`; a trim that leaves anything above zero is accepted at the trimmed figure rather than rejected. A high-cost claim leaves `billable_service_hours` at 0.

For a `MEDICAID` claim, the student must have both `medicaid_eligible` and `medicaid_consent_on_file` set to true, or the claim is refused with `ERR_MEDICAID_CONSENT_MISSING`. `billable_service_hours` is the sum of `hours_delivered` across every `service_delivery_lines` entry, on every `AttendanceRecord` for this placement whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, whose named `ServiceAddendum` has `medicaid_billable` set to true. Three exclusions are load-bearing: a line naming an addendum with `medicaid_billable` false contributes zero, a record still in `SUBMITTED` contributes zero, and a record in `DISPUTED` contributes zero — only district-verified delivery is federally billable. `eligible_base_cents` is those hours multiplied by the Medicaid hourly rate of 9 000 cents, rounded half up to whole cents. The `tier_rate` is the federal match percentage of 0.5432, and `reimbursable_amount_cents` is `eligible_base_cents` multiplied by 0.5432, rounded half up to whole cents — a second rounding applied to the already-rounded base, not a single rounding of the combined product. Neither the high-cost trigger nor the per-student cap applies to a Medicaid claim.

Either way the claim lands in `ELIGIBLE`, and the student's `projected_state_recovery_cents` is increased by `reimbursable_amount_cents` with the student's `version` incremented.

**Submitting to the state.** A `FiscalOfficer` submits an `ELIGIBLE` claim to the state, supplying the `state_claim_reference` the state assigned. The transition `ELIGIBLE → SUBMITTED` is terminal and requires `reimbursable_amount_cents` to be at least 1 — a claim worth nothing may not be filed. Medicaid consent is re-checked at this moment, so a student whose `medicaid_consent_on_file` has since been withdrawn blocks submission with `ERR_MEDICAID_CONSENT_MISSING` even though the claim computed successfully at creation. Side effects: `status` set to `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` set to the logical clock's current instant, `state_claim_reference` stored, and `version` incremented.

**Projecting the annual cost.** A computed read on a placement projects its full-year cost without writing anything, and it accepts an optional `variant`. The two variants answer different questions and the caller chooses which.

Under `CONTRACTED_ENCUMBRANCE`, which is the default when `variant` is omitted, `projected_annual_cents` is the plain sum of the placement's three commitment components: `contract_encumbrance_cents` plus `addendum_encumbrance_cents` plus `transport_encumbrance_cents`. This is the promise-based view — what the district signed up for.

Under `ACTUAL_RUN_RATE`, the candidate set is the placement's `TuitionInvoice` rows whose `payment_status` is `PAID`, and nothing else: invoices in `UNPAID` contribute nothing whatever their verification state, and a `VOIDED` invoice contributes nothing even if it had previously been paid, because it is no longer `PAID`. The settled total is the sum of `verified_amount_cents` over that set and `periods_paid` is its row count. When `periods_paid` is 0, `projected_annual_cents` is exactly 0 — no invention of a run rate from no data. Otherwise `projected_annual_cents` is the settled total divided by `periods_paid`, multiplied by thirteen billing periods per year, rounded half up to whole cents once at the end, after both the division and the multiplication.

Both variants then report the same two derived figures. `remaining_commitment_cents` is the greater of 0 and the placement's `encumbrance_cents`. `variance_cents` is `projected_annual_cents` minus `contract_encumbrance_cents`, and it is signed — a negative value means the placement is running under its contracted commitment. The response carries `placement_id`, the `variant` actually applied, `projected_annual_cents`, `remaining_commitment_cents`, `variance_cents` and `periods_paid`, with `periods_paid` reported as 0 under the contracted-encumbrance variant, which does not consult invoices at all.


### 3.7. Parental Unilateral Placement Reimbursement

When a family concludes the district's offer is inadequate, enrols the child privately at its own expense and asks to be repaid, the dispute leaves the contracting track entirely and enters adjudication. A `ReimbursementClaim` never reads a `Placement`, a `TuitionRateSchedule` or an `AttendanceRecord` — there is no district contract to read rates from, which is precisely why a statutory day-rate ceiling substitutes for one.

**Filing.** A `ParentGuardian` or a `PlacementCoordinator` files the claim, naming the `student_id`, the `fiscal_account_id` the award would be charged to, and the `unilateral_school_name` as free text — the private school need not be a state-approved provider, which is the whole point of a unilateral placement. The money claimed is `claimed_tuition_cents` and `claimed_transport_cents`, each a non-negative integer with transport defaulting to 0, and `claimed_instructional_days`, an integer between 1 and 260 inclusive, which is what the statutory ceiling is measured against. The window is `notice_date` — the date the family told the district it was withdrawing the child, which must be on or before `unilateral_start_date` — together with `unilateral_start_date` and `unilateral_end_date`, the latter on or after the former. Two optional fields shape the equitable analysis: `prior_district_offer_date`, the date the district put a written offer on the table, null when it never did, and `parent_cooperation`, a flag that defaults to true when omitted and is set false when the family obstructed the district's own evaluation. `superseding_placement_id`, when supplied, names the district placement the family rejected. The claim lands in `FILED` with `remand_round` at 0, `award_basis` null and every award column at 0.

**Adjudication.** Only a `HearingAdjudicator` adjudicates, and only a claim in `FILED` or `REMANDED` status. The call supplies an `award_basis` of either `strict_statutory` or `equitable_actual_cost`, plus an optional `equitable_findings`. Three gates precede the arithmetic, in ladder order. At rung 12, the equitable-basis gate: an `award_basis` of `equitable_actual_cost` requires `equitable_findings` whose trimmed length is at least 30 characters, or the call is refused with `ERR_EQUITABLE_FINDINGS_REQUIRED`; under `strict_statutory` the field is ignored entirely and may be omitted or blank. Also at rung 12, the concurrent-placement bar: no `Placement` for the same student may be in `ACTIVE` status with a closed interval from its `start_date` to its `end_date` that intersects the claim's closed interval from `unilateral_start_date` to `unilateral_end_date`, or the call is refused with `ERR_CONCURRENT_DISTRICT_PLACEMENT` — the district will not simultaneously pay a contracted school for a seat and reimburse a parent for the same weeks. At rung 13, the disbursement authority: the named `DistrictFiscalAccount` must have `available_cents` greater than or equal to the award about to be booked, or the call is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FISCAL_AUTHORITY`. That last check is skipped entirely on the denial branch, because nothing is disbursed.

The award is computed in three stages.

**Stage one, the eligible base.** The statutory cap is the state-approved ceiling day rate of 28 000 cents (two hundred and eighty dollars) multiplied by `claimed_instructional_days`. Under the `strict_statutory` basis the eligible tuition is the lesser of `claimed_tuition_cents` and that cap, so a claim whose implied per-day cost exceeds the ceiling is trimmed before any reduction runs. Under the `equitable_actual_cost` basis the statutory ceiling is waived and the eligible tuition is the full `claimed_tuition_cents` — the hearing officer has found the district's own offer inadequate. In **both** bases the eligible transport is the lesser of `claimed_transport_cents` and a transport cap of 150 000 cents (fifteen hundred dollars): the transport cap always binds, and waiving the tuition ceiling does not waive it. `eligible_amount_cents` is the sum of the two eligible components. The consequence is that the equitable basis produces a strictly larger award than the strict basis whenever, and only whenever, `claimed_tuition_cents` exceeds the statutory cap; when the claim is already under the ceiling the two bases produce identical figures and differ only in the recorded `award_basis`.

**Stage two, the equitable reduction waterfall.** `equitable_reduction_pct` starts at 0 and three reductions are considered in this pinned order, accumulating **additively** — they are added together, never multiplied or compounded — and the accumulated total is clamped exactly once, at the end.

1. The notice days are `unilateral_start_date` minus `notice_date`, counted in whole days. If that count is *below* ten, 0.25 is added for late notice. The comparison is strict, so notice given exactly ten days before the unilateral start is timely and adds nothing.
2. If `parent_cooperation` is false, 0.20 is added for non-cooperation.
3. If `prior_district_offer_date` is not null **and** falls on or before `notice_date`, 0.15 is added, because the district had made a timely offer the family declined.

The accumulated percentage is then reduced to at most 0.50. Because the three reductions sum to 0.60 when all three fire, the clamp is reachable, and it is inclusive of the maximum: a claim hitting exactly 0.50 is at the ceiling.

**Stage three, the award.** `awarded_tuition_cents` is the eligible tuition multiplied by one minus `equitable_reduction_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. `awarded_transport_cents` is the eligible transport multiplied by the same factor, rounded half up to whole cents independently. `awarded_amount_cents` is the sum of those two already-rounded integers — never a single rounding of the combined base, which would differ by a cent whenever both components round up.

The outcome status is then decided by three mutually exclusive tests, and the three branches are equally weighted siblings out of `FILED` (and, identically, out of `REMANDED`). The claim is `DENIED` when `awarded_amount_cents` is 0 **or** when `equitable_reduction_pct` reached 0.50; on this branch the computed fields are still written, `award_basis` is still recorded, and no money moves at all. The claim is `AWARDED_FULL` when `awarded_amount_cents` equals `claimed_tuition_cents` plus `claimed_transport_cents` — the family got every cent it asked for, which requires both that no reduction applied and that neither cap bit. Otherwise the claim is `AWARDED_PARTIAL`, which is the case whenever a reduction applied or the eligible base came in below the claimed total. On both award branches the side effects are identical: `award_basis`, `equitable_reduction_pct`, `eligible_amount_cents`, `awarded_tuition_cents`, `awarded_transport_cents` and `awarded_amount_cents` are written; `status` is set to the branch's value; the fiscal account has `available_cents` decreased by `awarded_amount_cents` and `disbursed_cents` increased by the same figure, with its `version` incremented; and the claim's `version` is incremented. Note that a reimbursement award never touches `encumbered_cents` — an adjudicated award is paid straight out of uncommitted appropriation, which is why the authority check reads `available_cents` rather than a placement's commitment. `AWARDED_FULL` is terminal.

**Remand and its exact compensation.** A `HearingAdjudicator` may remand a claim in `AWARDED_PARTIAL` or `DENIED` status for re-adjudication, supplying a `remand_reason`. This is the compensating action for the adjudication disbursement and it must restore the ledger cent for cent: the fiscal account has `disbursed_cents` decreased by the claim's `awarded_amount_cents` and `available_cents` increased by exactly the same figure, with its `version` incremented, so the conservation identity of §2.6 returns to its pre-adjudication decomposition rather than merely still summing correctly. Remanding a `DENIED` claim performs no fiscal reversal, because `awarded_amount_cents` was already 0. The claim itself then has `awarded_amount_cents`, `awarded_tuition_cents`, `awarded_transport_cents`, `eligible_amount_cents` and `equitable_reduction_pct` all reset to 0 and `award_basis` cleared to null, `status` set to `REMANDED`, `remand_round` incremented by one, and `version` incremented. Re-adjudicating a `REMANDED` claim runs the identical three branches under identical preconditions and leaves `remand_round` unchanged. The cycle is bounded at two remand rounds; a third remand is refused with `ERR_MAX_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`.


### 3.8. Progress Monitoring and the Return-Readiness Gate

An out-of-district placement is meant to be temporary, and the district may not simply declare a student ready to come home. Readiness is a computed figure built from accepted quarterly progress reports, and it gates the transition plan.

**Quarterly reporting.** A `SchoolBillingAgent` or a `PlacementCoordinator` files a `ProgressReport` for a placement, naming the `placement_id`, the `fiscal_year` it covers as an integer between 2020 and 2100 inclusive, and the `quarter` as an integer from 1 to 4. Four measures follow: `goals_total`, the number of measurable IEP goals monitored, an integer of at least 1; `goals_met`, an integer of at least 0 that may not exceed `goals_total`; `goals_regressed`, an integer of at least 0 defaulting to 0, where `goals_met` plus `goals_regressed` may not exceed `goals_total`; and two numbers each in the closed range 0 to 100, `benchmark_scaled_score` and `attendance_rate_pct`. Only one report may exist per `placement_id`, `fiscal_year` and `quarter` triple among reports in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` status, and a duplicate — or a `quarter` outside 1 through 4 — is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRESS_PERIOD`. A rejected quarter therefore frees the slot for a refiling. The report lands in `SUBMITTED` with its `report_score` already computed.

The per-report score is a weighted composite, identical under both readiness variants. It is one half of the goal-attainment percentage — `goals_met` divided by `goals_total`, multiplied by one hundred — plus three tenths of `benchmark_scaled_score`, plus two tenths of `attendance_rate_pct`, minus four points for every goal counted in `goals_regressed`. The result is rounded half up to two decimal places and stored as `report_score`, **per report, before any aggregation** — the readiness figure is a mean of already-rounded per-report scores, never a mean of unrounded ones. Because the regression penalty is subtracted after the weighted sum, a report with many regressed goals can compute below zero, and a report scoring at the top of every measure with no regressions computes to one hundred.

**Disposition.** A `PlacementCoordinator` or a `TeamChair` disposes of a `SUBMITTED` report by supplying a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`; both resulting states are terminal, and the two branches are siblings. `ACCEPT` requires the parent placement to be `ACTIVE`, `TERMINATED` or `RETURNED_TO_DISTRICT`, sets `status` to `ACCEPTED`, recomputes `report_score` by the per-report formula above, and increments `version`. `REJECT` requires nothing of the placement's state, sets `status` to `REJECTED`, sets `report_score` to 0 — a rejected report never enters the readiness baseline, and zeroing its score makes that irreversible — and increments `version`. Only the accept branch changes the placement's readiness figure.

**Readiness aggregation.** The candidate set for a plan's readiness is exactly the `ProgressReport` rows whose `placement_id` matches the plan's placement and whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Reports in `SUBMITTED` and `REJECTED` are excluded under both variants, and so are reports belonging to any other placement — including the earlier placement named by the placement's own `prior_placement_id`, so a student's history at a previous school does not travel with them. The candidates are ordered newest first: `fiscal_year` descending, then `quarter` descending, then `created_at` descending.

Under the `trailing_two_quarter` variant, which is the default, only the first two rows of that ordering contribute, each with equal weight, and the readiness score is the plain arithmetic mean of their two `report_score` values, rounded half up to two decimal places. If only one accepted report exists, that single report is taken and its score is the mean. Every older quarter is discarded outright, so one early bad quarter cannot depress the figure.

Under the `cumulative_recency_weighted` variant, **every** accepted report contributes, with a geometrically decaying weight: the newest row's weight is 0.7 raised to the power zero, that is 1; the next row's is 0.7; the next 0.49; and so on. The readiness score is the sum of each row's weight multiplied by its `report_score`, divided by the sum of the weights used, rounded half up to two decimal places. Against the trailing variant this scores a student with an early weak quarter strictly lower and a student with an early strong quarter strictly higher, which is why it is the basis a family invokes on appeal.

Under either variant, readiness is met when the score is greater than or equal to 62.50. The comparison is inclusive, so a score of exactly 62.50 passes.

**Drafting and activating the plan.** A `PlacementCoordinator` or a `TeamChair` drafts a `ReturnTransitionPlan` for a placement, naming the `placement_id`, the `target_return_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date, and the `receiving_program` that will take the student in district. Only one non-terminal plan — `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` — may exist per placement, and a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The plan lands in `DRAFT` with `transition_services` empty, `reserve_cents` at 0 and `readiness_score_at_activation` null.

A `TeamChair` then activates the plan, supplying `transition_services` as an array of strings naming the services being committed, and optionally a `readiness_variant` selecting which aggregation to apply, defaulting to `trailing_two_quarter`. The transition `DRAFT → ACTIVE` requires the plan to be `DRAFT`, the placement to be `ACTIVE`, and no other plan on the same placement to be `ACTIVE`. Three substantive gates then apply, in ladder order, and the order matters because a district that is unready in two ways is told about the more fundamental one. At rung 11, the plan-completeness check: `transition_services` may not be empty and no entry may be blank, and `target_return_date` may not fall before the current logical clock date; a violation is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_SERVICES_INCOMPLETE`. At rung 12, the readiness gate: the placement must have at least two `ACCEPTED` progress reports **and** the readiness score under the requested variant must be at least 62.50; either failure is refused with `ERR_RETURN_READINESS_NOT_MET`. At rung 13, the fiscal check: the placement's `DistrictFiscalAccount` must have `available_cents` of at least 250 000 cents (twenty-five hundred dollars), or the activation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FISCAL_AUTHORITY`. A plan can therefore be blocked for incompleteness while also being unready and unfunded, and the caller sees only the completeness error.

Activation's side effects reserve real money. `status` becomes `ACTIVE`; `transition_services` is stored verbatim as supplied; `readiness_score_at_activation` is stamped with the score computed under the applied variant, freezing the figure the decision rested on even though later reports will move the live computation; `reserve_cents` is set to 250 000. The placement's `transition_reserve_cents` is set to 250 000 — the fourth component of its `encumbrance_cents`, which is recomputed — and the placement's `version` is incremented. The fiscal account has `available_cents` decreased by 250 000 and `encumbered_cents` increased by 250 000, with its `version` incremented; `authorized_cents` and `disbursed_cents` do not move, so the conservation identity holds. The plan's `version` is incremented.

**Reading readiness without committing.** A computed read on a plan evaluates readiness under an optional `variant`, defaulting to `trailing_two_quarter`, and mutates nothing at all — in particular it never writes `readiness_score_at_activation`. Its response carries `plan_id`, the `variant` applied, the aggregated `readiness_score`, the `accepted_report_count` of candidate rows found, the boolean `readiness_met` reflecting both the two-report minimum and the 62.50 threshold, and `report_scores`, the array of contributing per-report scores in the newest-first order the variant used. This read is how a district or a family tests whether an activation would clear the gate, and how the two variants can be compared side by side before one is chosen.


### 3.9. Closing a Placement: Termination Versus Return to District

An `ACTIVE` placement can end in two ways, and they are deliberate fork siblings: both are performed by a `PlacementCoordinator`, both close the placement, both release the unspent contract commitment, and both unwind transportation identically. They diverge in exactly three side-effect sets, and every one of those divergences reflects whether the district is walking away from the arrangement or bringing the student home on purpose.

Both closures share one precondition beyond the placement being `ACTIVE`: no `TuitionInvoice` on the placement may still have `verification_status` of `SUBMITTED`. All submitted billing must first be verified or disputed, so the district never closes a placement with an unpriced claim outstanding; a closure attempted with such an invoice standing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

**Termination.** Termination takes a `termination_reason` and moves the placement `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`. It fires **Cascade A**, ten links deep, applied in order:

- **Link 0 (the placement and its student).** `status` is set to `TERMINATED`, `terminated_at` to the logical clock's current instant, `termination_reason` to the supplied reason; the student's `placement_status` is set to `TERMINATED` and the student's `version` is incremented.
- **Link 1 (placement to fiscal account).** The contract release is `contract_encumbrance_cents` minus `paid_total_ytd_cents` — the part of the tuition commitment never spent. The fiscal account has `encumbered_cents` decreased and `available_cents` increased by that release, with its `version` incremented. The placement's `contract_encumbrance_cents` is then set equal to `paid_total_ytd_cents`, driving its remaining contract commitment to exactly zero, and `released_encumbrance_cents` is increased by the release.
- **Link 2 (placement to service addenda).** Every `ServiceAddendum` on the placement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order.
- **Link 3 (each addendum to the fiscal account, caused by Link 2).** Each addendum termination releases its own commitment: the fiscal account has `encumbered_cents` decreased and `available_cents` increased by that addendum's `encumbrance_cents`; the placement's `addendum_encumbrance_cents` is decreased and its `released_encumbrance_cents` increased by the same figure; the addendum's `encumbrance_cents` is zeroed and its `version` incremented.
- **Link 4 (placement to transportation assignments).** Every `TransportationAssignment` on the placement whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `ACTIVE` or `WAITLISTED` moves to `RELEASED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. If at least one such assignment existed, the placement's `transport_status` is set to `RELEASED`.
- **Link 5 (each released assignment to its route, caused by Link 4).** Only for assignments whose status immediately before release was `ACTIVE`: the route's `seats_committed` is decreased by one and `seats_available` increased by one with the route's `version` incremented, and the fiscal account has `encumbered_cents` decreased and `available_cents` increased by that assignment's `encumbrance_cents`, while the placement's `transport_encumbrance_cents` is decreased and `released_encumbrance_cents` increased by the same figure and the assignment's `encumbrance_cents` is zeroed. An assignment released from `REQUESTED` or `WAITLISTED` held no seat and no commitment, so it moves nothing.
- **Link 6 (route back out to another placement's assignment, caused by Link 5).** Because the route's `seats_available` rose, the waitlist-promotion selector of §3.4 runs on that route and promotes at most one `WAITLISTED` assignment to `ACTIVE`, by the candidate set, ordering, tie-break and empty-set fallback pinned there. An empty candidate set promotes nothing and is not an error.
- **Link 7 (the promoted assignment, caused by Link 6).** The promoted assignment's `encumbrance_cents` is computed by the transportation encumbrance rule of §3.4, that amount is added to **its own** placement's `transport_encumbrance_cents`, that placement's `transport_status` becomes `ASSIGNED` and its `version` is incremented, the fiscal account has `available_cents` decreased and `encumbered_cents` increased by the same amount, and the assignment's `promoted_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant. This is the deepest reach of the cascade: a termination on one student's placement books money against a different student's placement, and the only channel through which a client can discover it is the filtered assignment list.
- **Link 8 (placement to transition plan).** A `ReturnTransitionPlan` on the placement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → ABANDONED`, releasing its reserve: the fiscal account has `encumbered_cents` decreased and `available_cents` increased by the plan's `reserve_cents`, the placement's `transition_reserve_cents` is set to 0, the plan's `reserve_cents` is set to 0, and the plan's `version` is incremented.
- **Link 9 (placement to invoices).** Every `TuitionInvoice` on the placement whose `verification_status` is `DISPUTED` and whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID` has `payment_status` set to `VOIDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, with each invoice's `version` incremented. `settled_amount_cents` stays 0 and `verification_status` is left untouched at `DISPUTED`, because the two invoice dimensions move independently. Invoices already `PAID` are untouched, and invoices in `SUBMITTED` cannot exist here by the shared precondition.

Links 4 through 7 form the genuinely transitive chain: each hop is caused by the mutation the previous hop performed, not by the termination call itself. When all links have applied, the placement's `encumbrance_cents` is recomputed from its four components and the placement's `version` is incremented; the fiscal account's `authorized_cents` never moves, so the conservation identity of §2.6 holds throughout, and the placement's remaining commitment reaches exactly zero.

**Return to district.** Return takes a `return_reason` and a `receiving_lre_setting`, and moves the placement `ACTIVE → RETURNED_TO_DISTRICT`. It carries one additional precondition the termination path does not: a `ReturnTransitionPlan` on the placement must be in `ACTIVE` status — a student may only be brought home under an activated plan that has already cleared the readiness gate of §3.8. Absent such a plan the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Its shared side effects mirror termination exactly. The placement's `status` is set to `RETURNED_TO_DISTRICT`, `terminated_at` to the logical clock's current instant, and `termination_reason` to the supplied `return_reason` — the same column carries the reason on both paths. The unspent contract commitment is released by the identical arithmetic of Link 1: the release is `contract_encumbrance_cents` minus `paid_total_ytd_cents`, the fiscal account moves that figure from `encumbered_cents` to `available_cents`, `contract_encumbrance_cents` is set to `paid_total_ytd_cents`, and `released_encumbrance_cents` is increased by the release. Transportation unwinds identically to Links 4 through 7: every assignment on the placement is `RELEASED`, seats return to their routes, commitments are released, the promotion selector runs on each freed route, and any promoted assignment books its own transport encumbrance against its own placement. The student's `placement_status` is set to `RETURNED` and the student's `version` is incremented. The placement's `encumbrance_cents` is recomputed, and both the placement's and the fiscal account's `version` are incremented.

The three divergences are these.

**Divergence one — the addenda's terminal state.** Every `ACTIVE` `ServiceAddendum` on the placement transitions `ACTIVE → TRANSFERRED` rather than `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, processed in the same ascending `created_at` order, and each releases its commitment by exactly the same arithmetic as Link 3 — the fiscal account moves the addendum's `encumbrance_cents` from `encumbered_cents` to `available_cents`, the placement's `addendum_encumbrance_cents` falls and `released_encumbrance_cents` rises by that figure, the addendum's `encumbrance_cents` is zeroed and its `version` is incremented. Only the terminal state differs, and it differs because the services are being handed to the receiving in-district programme rather than stopped.

**Divergence two — the transition reserve is retained.** The `ACTIVE` `ReturnTransitionPlan` transitions `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` rather than `ACTIVE → ABANDONED`, and its reserve is **not** released: the plan's `reserve_cents` and the placement's `transition_reserve_cents` both keep the 250 000 cents encumbered, and the fiscal account's `encumbered_cents` is not reduced by it, so the post-return transition services stay funded. The plan's `version` is incremented. This is the one place where a closed placement legitimately retains a non-zero commitment component.

**Divergence three — disputed invoices survive.** `DISPUTED`, `UNPAID` invoices on the placement are **not** voided. They are left standing in `DISPUTED` with `payment_status` of `UNPAID`, so the school can still resubmit them within its two-round budget and the district can still verify and pay them after the student has come home. Nothing in Link 9's counterpart runs on this path at all.

**The student's recorded setting.** Both paths update the student, but only the return path updates `lre_current_setting`, which is set to the supplied `receiving_lre_setting` — the in-district continuum position the student is re-entering. Termination sets `placement_status` to `TERMINATED` and leaves `lre_current_setting` where it stood, because a terminated placement records no successor setting.
