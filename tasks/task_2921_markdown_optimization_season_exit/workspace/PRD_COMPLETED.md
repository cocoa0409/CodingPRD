# Markdown Optimization & Season Exit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every selling season ends with inventory that did not sell at full price, and the retail pricing team has roughly one quarter to convert that inventory into cash, floor space and vendor funding before the next season lands. Today that work is spread across spreadsheets: one file classifies items by lifecycle stage, another guesses at price elasticity, a third tracks which store put the sale signage up, and a fourth reconciles what the vendor agreed to fund against what the controller actually recognised. The consequences are expensive and invisible until too late — markdowns taken on protected new arrivals, ladders that step down too fast for signage to keep up, clearance bays double-booked across two plans, and a post-season margin number nobody can reproduce.

This service replaces that patchwork with a single system of record for markdown optimization and season exit. It classifies every item into a lifecycle stage from its receipt date, sell-through and remaining weeks to the season's exit target; estimates own-price elasticity either from the item's own price history or by pooling certified estimates from cluster peers; recommends a first markdown depth from that elasticity together with urgency and inventory pressure; and then holds the analyst to a disciplined cadence of depth rungs that respect protected new-arrival periods, minimum spacing between price changes and a buffer before the exit date. Price changes only reach the shelf through a zone execution that has been signed off on both dimensions — the execution itself and its signage compliance — and every applied execution writes an auditable markdown liability line that the vendor allowance machinery can be claimed against.

The system is deliberately opinionated about accounting treatment and about reversal. A permanent markdown writes the ticket down and recognises an inventory reserve at execution time on every unit in scope; a promotional markdown books contra-revenue only on units that actually sell on or after the effective date, and is never claimable against a vendor allowance. Clearance floor space is a finite, chain-level budget allocated across open stores by an explicit waterfall, and its commitment is what tips a season into its exit window. Residual units leave through salvage, jobber or donation, each with its own recovery arithmetic. Finally, two review variants — one on projections, one on realized facts only — close the loop with a reproducible margin and sell-through scorecard, and the final variant is the gate that allows a season to be closed at all.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal acts in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. Emptiness is judged literally, on the header value exactly as sent and with no trimming: only the zero-length value counts as empty, so a header carrying nothing but whitespace is a present, non-empty value and clears authentication — it then fails authorization instead, because no whitespace string is a declared role name. Neither header's value is otherwise constrained; `X-User-ID` in particular is accepted in any non-empty form and its meaning depends only on the role, as described below. If both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names — matched exactly and case-sensitively — or is a declared role that the requested operation does not list as authorized, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Exactly five roles exist, spelled precisely as follows.

- `PricingAnalyst` owns the markdown mechanics. The analyst registers items and their weekly sales history, requests elasticity estimates, opens markdown plans and authors their depth ladders, submits plans for review, reopens plans that came back for revision, schedules zone executions and applies or rolls them back, allocates clearance floor space, opens vendor allowance negotiations, and commits residual units to a disposition channel.
- `MerchandiseDirector` owns the season, price-zone and store registry, reviews submitted plans by either approving them or sending them back for revision, and verifies posted signage — the safety gate that stands between a scheduled price change and a repriced ticket.
- `StoreManager` acts for exactly one store, and its single write capability is recording that markdown signage is physically posted.
- `VendorRep` acts for a vendor across the allowance negotiation table: it counters offers and accepts them, and it reads the agreements, plans and claims that concern its funding.
- `Controller` owns the money. The controller recognises and reverses vendor allowance funding, approves and settles disposition orders, and signs the post-season margin scorecard.

Role identity is not uniform, and the difference matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Store`; ownership checks on any resource that references a store compare that resource's store foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a store manager posts signage for an execution whose `execution_scope` is `STORE`, the execution's `store_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STORE_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403); chain-scope and zone-scope executions carry no `store_id` at all and may be posted by any store manager. When `X-User-Role` is `PricingAnalyst`, `MerchandiseDirector`, `VendorRep` or `Controller`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a plan or a disposition order, as `posted_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on an execution, as `recognized_by_user_id` on a claim, as `last_offer_by_user_id` on an allowance agreement, and as `generated_by_user_id` on an exit review — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

Three separations of duty are enforced on top of role membership, and all three surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The merchandise director who approves a markdown plan must not be the analyst recorded in the plan's `created_by_user_id`. The controller who approves a disposition order must not be the principal recorded in that order's `created_by_user_id`. And the party that accepts an allowance offer must act in a role different from the agreement's `last_offer_by_role` — a party may never accept its own offer.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, so the whole value is 24 characters long. It is minted independently for every response, success or error, and is never derived from anything the caller sent.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is taken from the server's own wall clock, not from the logical clock of §2.5, and is therefore unrelated to the instants stored on resources.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. A create that legitimately yields several rows at once — clearance allocation, which writes one reservation per store the waterfall reaches — returns HTTP 201 with `response_type` `array` and those rows in `data`, and its `meta` carries no pagination fields because the payload is not a page.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and only for them: a single-resource read, a create, a transition and the logical-clock capability all return a `meta` holding only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format, and so does any 5xx a defect might produce — the envelope is never abandoned for a bare framework error page, and `response_type` is `error` for every non-2xx status:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints are paginated with two optional query parameters. `limit` is the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the zero-based index of the first row returned: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a non-integer or negative `offset`, is a payload violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400) rather than silently clamped. "Integer" is read strictly: the parameter's textual value must be an optionally sign-prefixed run of decimal digits, so `limit=` supplied but empty, `limit=abc`, `limit=1.5` and `limit=+5` are all `ERR_VALIDATION`. `offset` carries a lower bound of 0 but no upper bound at all, and an `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error — it returns an empty `data` array with the true `total_count` and `has_more` false.

Unless a collection's description states a different order, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending; because the server mints `created_at` from a strictly increasing counter (§2.4), that order is exactly insertion order and never ambiguous. Every paginated response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, each collection accepts its own optional filters, and a filter value that is not a member of the corresponding enumeration is likewise an `ERR_VALIDATION`. Only enumeration-valued and date-valued filters are validated: a `week_ending_date` filter must be a well-formed `YYYY-MM-DD` date or the request is an `ERR_VALIDATION`, while every identifier-valued or free-text filter (`season_id`, `item_id`, `store_id`, `price_zone_id`, `parent_zone_id`, `markdown_plan_id`, `markdown_step_id`, `elasticity_estimate_id`, `superseded_by_plan_id`, `allowance_agreement_id`, `vendor_code`, `cluster_code` and `region_code`) is compared for exact, case-sensitive equality against the stored value with no existence check at all — a filter naming an identifier that resolves to nothing yields an empty page rather than a 404. Query parameters the collection does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, and every filter is optional, with an omitted filter constraining nothing. Seasons filter on `status` and `category`. A season's allowance agreements filter on `status` and `vendor_code`, and a season's exit reviews on `review_type`; the cross-season exit-review collection additionally accepts `season_id`. Price zones filter on `zone_type`, `parent_zone_id` and `region_code`; stores filter on `price_zone_id`, `status` and `region_code`. Items filter on `season_id`, `cluster_code`, `lifecycle_stage`, `markdown_state`, `vendor_code` and `category`. The cross-item sales-week collection filters on `item_id` and `week_ending_date`, and an item's nested sales-week collection accepts `week_ending_date` as well; elasticity estimates filter on `item_id`, `status` and `confidence`, and an item's nested estimate collection accepts `status` and `confidence`. Markdown plans filter on `season_id`, `item_id`, `elasticity_estimate_id`, `superseded_by_plan_id`, `plan_type` and `status`; ladder rungs filter on `markdown_plan_id` and `status`; zone executions filter on `markdown_plan_id`, `markdown_step_id`, `price_zone_id`, `store_id`, `execution_scope`, `status` and `signage_status`. Clearance reservations filter on `season_id`, `store_id`, `markdown_plan_id` and `status`. Allowance agreements filter on `season_id`, `vendor_code`, `item_category_scope` and `status`; vendor claims on `allowance_agreement_id`, `markdown_plan_id` and `status`; disposition orders on `season_id`, `item_id`, `channel` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and Audit Timestamps

Every resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a random version-4 UUID in the canonical lowercase hyphenated 8-4-4-4-12 form, 36 characters long. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no domain-flavoured encoding; a client may never construct one. A path or foreign-key identifier that is not a string, or is a string no row carries, is simply unresolvable and reports the matching not-found code below — the server never validates an identifier's shape separately from resolving it, so a malformed identifier is a 404 and never an `ERR_VALIDATION`. Timestamps are ISO 8601 instants in UTC rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Server-minted `created_at` and `updated_at` values are drawn from a strictly increasing millisecond counter, so no two rows ever share a `created_at` and ordering by `created_at` is always ordering by insertion. Instants that arrive in a request body are parsed leniently rather than held to the output format: the seconds component, the fractional part and the zone designator are each optional, `T` may be lowercase or a single space, `Z` may be lowercase, an explicit `+HH:MM` or `-HH:MM` offset is accepted and normalised to UTC, a fractional part of any digit length is accepted and truncated to milliseconds, and an omitted zone is read as UTC. Dates that describe a selling calendar rather than a moment — `season_start_date`, `season_end_date`, `exit_target_date`, `first_receipt_date`, `protected_until_date`, `exit_date_target`, `week_ending_date`, `effective_date`, `signage_due_date` and `window_end_date_exclusive` — are date-only values in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and carry no time component.

Most resources carry the full set of common properties: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and an integer `version`. `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation came from a direct request or from a cascade triggered elsewhere; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. Two resources are deliberately append-only and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all: a posted `SalesWeek` and a generated `SeasonExitReview`. A sales week is a historical reading and an exit review is a snapshot; neither is ever edited in place, and a caller must not expect either field to appear on them. A resource's projection is fixed rather than sparse: every field this document attributes to a resource is present as a key on every read and every write response for that resource, carrying `null` where the resource has no value for it yet — an unapproved plan's `approved_by_user_id`, an uncertified estimate's `certified_at`, a `CHAIN` zone's `parent_zone_id`, a live reservation's `released_at`, an unposted execution's `posted_at` and a claim with no rejection its `rejection_reason` are all present-and-null rather than omitted. Nothing beyond that field list is ever returned, so internal bookkeeping is never leaked and a client may treat the key set of a resource as stable.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so the outer segment is validated before the inner one and the error reported is always the one belonging to the leftmost unresolvable segment. The path identifiers this API uses are `season_id`, `exit_review_id`, `price_zone_id`, `store_id`, `item_id`, `sales_week_id`, `estimate_id`, `markdown_plan_id`, `markdown_step_id`, `zone_execution_id`, `reservation_id`, `agreement_id` and `disposition_order_id`, plus `claim_id` for a vendor claim. Each maps to its own not-found code: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STEP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESTIMATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXECUTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_FOUND`, all at HTTP 404. Two of the fourteen path identifiers have no code of their own and borrow their parent's: an unresolvable `sales_week_id` reports `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` and an unresolvable `exit_review_id` reports `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, both at HTTP 404. Requests that address no declared route at all are answered with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and a request that names a declared path with a method that path does not implement is answered with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400); a trailing slash is insignificant, so `/seasons` and `/seasons/` reach the same handler. The same codes govern foreign keys carried in a request body: an `item_id`, `season_id`, `price_zone_id`, `parent_zone_id`, `store_id`, `elasticity_estimate_id`, `markdown_plan_id`, `markdown_step_id` or `allowance_agreement_id` that resolves to nothing produces the matching 404, and a `price_zone_id` or `parent_zone_id` with no matching zone reports `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every date-gated rule — protected new-arrival periods, ladder cadence, signage due dates, effective-date checks, lifecycle aging and weeks-to-exit — reads a server-held logical clock. Before any caller sets it, that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time. Any of the five roles may advance or rewind it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the operation replaces the clock value and returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object rather than a resource.

Advancing the clock is not inert. Because lifecycle classification depends on elapsed weeks, setting the clock re-runs the classification for every item in the system, which can move items out of their protected period into `CORE` or `AGING`, onward into `CLEARANCE`, and — once the governing season is in its exit window — into `EXIT_PENDING`; that reclassification can in turn flip `RESERVED` clearance reservations to `COMMITTED`, and a flip that lifts a season's `committed_clearance_units` to the trigger threshold of §3.7 carries that season `ACTIVE → EXIT_WINDOW` inside the same request, which reclassifies its items once more. Items are swept in `created_at` order and every season's three clearance counters are recomputed afterwards in the same order; an item or a season whose recomputed values all match what is already stored is not written at all, so its `updated_at` and `version` are left alone. Date arithmetic is always in whole days on the date part of the clock in UTC, and where a rule counts weeks it takes the whole number of days and divides by seven, discarding any remainder (six days is zero weeks; fourteen days is exactly two weeks). The remainder is discarded towards zero, not towards minus infinity, so a negative day count truncates the same way in magnitude: minus six days is zero weeks and minus thirteen days is minus one week, not minus two.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Supersede-Style Creates

Two creates in this system silently destroy work that the caller may not have seen, because each legitimately supersedes an earlier row. Both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against a parent resource's `version`, and in both cases the guard field is a required part of the request body, not an optional refinement.

A request that opens a markdown plan must carry `expected_item_version` holding the `version` of the target `Item` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the item's current `version` — because a sales week was posted, an execution was applied or rolled back, a disposition order was created, approved, cancelled or settled, an earlier plan create already bumped it, or a clock advance or a season transition reclassified it and the reclassification actually moved a value — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no plan is written. The guard exists because a new plan for the same season and item supersedes the prior plan for that pair, releasing its clearance reservations and voiding its ladder; without the check a client working from an old read would discard a ladder it never observed.

A request that allocates clearance floor space against a season must carry `expected_season_version` holding the `version` of the target `Season` as the client last read it, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SEASON_VERSION` (HTTP 412) with no reservation created. Allocation first releases every still-live reservation of the same `markdown_plan_id` and then redraws the waterfall from scratch, so a caller working from a stale view of `reserved_clearance_units` and `committed_clearance_units` would be superseding an allocation it never observed. No other write in this API accepts a version field, and none may be rejected for concurrency reasons.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, the response is decided by the lowest-numbered rule in the ordered ladder below. The ordering is normative: a caller must be able to predict which of several simultaneous problems is reported, and a later check may never mask an earlier one.

1. Authentication: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Authorization: `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles and is listed among the authorized roles of the requested operation, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Payload shape: every required field is present with its declared type, enum membership and numeric range — including unit counts of at least 1, a `depth_pct` inside 0.01 through 70.00, and a pagination `limit` inside 1 through 100 — else `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every endpoint. Shape is judged on the parsed JSON strictly. A body that is absent or entirely empty reads as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose fields are all optional accepts a request with no body at all; a body that is not parseable JSON, or that parses to anything other than a JSON object (an array, a bare string, a bare number), is `ERR_VALIDATION`. An explicitly `null` field is treated exactly as an omitted one: optional fields fall back to their documented defaults and required fields report the same "is required" `ERR_VALIDATION` they would report if the key were absent. Types are not coerced: a JSON string is never accepted where a number or integer is expected, a JSON boolean is never accepted where a number or integer is expected, a fractional number is never accepted where an integer is expected, and only a real JSON boolean satisfies a boolean field. Every string field carries an implicit minimum length of 1, so the empty string never satisfies a string field, whether that field is required or optional, and string length bounds are counted in characters. Enumeration and length checks run in the order the endpoint declares its fields, and the first field to fail decides the message.
4. Path existence: every identifier in the URL resolves, checked left to right, else the corresponding 404 code from §2.4. Applies to every endpoint that has a path identifier.
5. Body existence: every foreign key in the request body resolves, else the corresponding 404 code. This applies to item registration, store registration, price-zone creation, elasticity estimation, plan creation, ladder-rung creation, zone-execution scheduling, clearance allocation, allowance-agreement creation, vendor-claim creation, disposition-order creation and sales-week posting.
6. Store ownership on signage posting: for a `STORE`-scope execution the posting store manager's `X-User-ID` must equal the execution's `store_id`, else `ERR_STORE_NOT_OWNED` (403). Chain-scope and zone-scope executions carry no `store_id` and may be posted by any store manager.
7. Stale parent version: `expected_item_version` must equal the item's `version` on plan creation and `expected_season_version` must equal the season's `version` on clearance allocation, else `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SEASON_VERSION` (412).
8. Transition legality: the target row must currently sit in a state that declares the requested transition, including on the signage dimension, where posting requires `PENDING` and verification requires `POSTED`. A transition requested from a state that does not declare it yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); posting signage that is not `PENDING` yields `ERR_SIGNAGE_ALREADY_POSTED` (409) and verifying signage that is not `POSTED` yields `ERR_SIGNAGE_NOT_POSTED` (409). Preconditions of the declared transition that have no dedicated code further down this ladder are reported here as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` too — notably applying an execution before its `effective_date` has arrived, applying an execution whose rung price is not strictly below the item's `current_retail_cents`, and rolling back an execution while a `RECOGNIZED` vendor claim still references its plan. Applies to every transition endpoint.
9. Season status: the governing season must be `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW` to create or submit or approve a plan, to schedule a zone execution, to allocate clearance space, to create a vendor claim and to generate a `PRELIMINARY` exit review — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). The season must be `EXIT_WINDOW` specifically to approve a disposition order, to settle one and to generate a `FINAL` exit review — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_NOT_IN_EXIT_WINDOW` (409). A season whose status is `CLOSED` accepts no write at all, including another exit review, and rejects with `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED` (409). This rule also guards item registration, sales-week posting, elasticity estimation and certification, and disposition-order creation. The season's own transition endpoint is the one exception: it is governed by rule 8 instead, because `CLOSED` declares no outgoing transition, so an attempt to move a `CLOSED` season reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED`.
10. Plan status: the governing markdown plan must be `APPROVED` or `EXECUTING` to schedule a zone execution, to apply one, and to allocate clearance space against it, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
11. Segregation of duties on plan approval, disposition approval and offer acceptance, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
12. Supersede safety on plan creation: a new plan may supersede the prior plan for the same season and item only while no execution of that prior plan has reached `APPLIED`, else `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_EXECUTING` (409).
13. Claim eligibility, on both vendor-claim creation and recognition: the plan's `plan_type` must be `PERMANENT`, else `ERR_PROMO_NOT_CLAIMABLE` (422); the plan's status must be `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETED`, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_CLAIMABLE` (409); and the agreement's `vendor_code` and `item_category_scope` must equal the plan item's `vendor_code` and the plan item's `category` respectively, else `ERR_ALLOWANCE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (422). The agreement's own `season_id` is not part of that comparison, so an agreement opened under one season may legitimately fund a plan belonging to another as long as the vendor and the category line up.
14. Estimate certification on plan submission: the referenced estimate must be `CERTIFIED` and must belong to the plan's item, else `ERR_ELASTICITY_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409).
15. Markdown eligibility on plan submission: the item's `lifecycle_stage` must be `AGING`, `CLEARANCE` or `EXIT_PENDING`, else `ERR_MARKDOWN_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
16. Disposition eligibility on disposition-order creation: the item's `lifecycle_stage` must be `CLEARANCE` or `EXIT_PENDING`, else `ERR_ITEM_NOT_EXIT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
17. Protected new-arrival period on ladder-rung creation: the rung's `effective_date` must be strictly later than the item's `protected_until_date`; a rung falling exactly on that date is a violation, reported as `ERR_PROTECTED_PERIOD_ACTIVE` (422).
18. Cadence on ladder-rung creation: the number of whole days between the previous rung's `effective_date` and this rung's must be at least 14; exactly 14 days is allowed and 13 is rejected with `ERR_CADENCE_TOO_TIGHT` (422).
19. Exit-date framing on ladder-rung creation: the rung's `effective_date` must be no later than the plan's `exit_date_target` minus 7 days, else `ERR_EXIT_DATE_MISSED` (422); and it must be no earlier than the logical clock's current date, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST` (422).
20. Signage lead at scheduling: the scheduled `effective_date` minus the zone's `signage_lead_days` must be no earlier than the logical clock's current date, so the signage window has not already closed, else `ERR_SIGNAGE_LEAD_VIOLATION` (422).
21. Signage compliance at apply time: `signage_status` must be `VERIFIED`; `PENDING`, `POSTED` and `NON_COMPLIANT` all block the apply with `ERR_SIGNAGE_NOT_VERIFIED` (409).
22. Ladder shape on rung creation: `step_index` must equal the plan's current `ladder_step_count` plus 1, else `ERR_STEP_INDEX_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE` (422); `ladder_step_count` must be below 4, else `ERR_MAX_LADDER_STEPS` (422); and `depth_pct` must be strictly greater than the previous rung's `depth_pct`, else `ERR_DEPTH_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422).
23. Store-scope occupancy overlap on execution scheduling, reported as `ERR_STEP_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
24. Zone spacing on execution scheduling, reported as `ERR_ZONE_SIGNAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
25. Zone scope integrity on execution scheduling, reported as `ERR_ZONE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (422).
26. Round ceilings: an agreement's `round_index` must be below 4 before another counter-offer may be placed, else `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409); a plan's `revision_round` must be at most 3 before a reopen, else `ERR_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409).
27. Uniqueness, reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409), over a season's `code`, an item's `sku`, a store's `store_number`, a zone's `zone_code`, the pair of `item_id` and `week_ending_date` for a sales week, and the pair of `season_id` and `review_type` for an exit review. It guards season, item, store, price-zone, sales-week and exit-review creation.
28. Units in scope on execution scheduling, reported as `ERR_ZONE_UNITS_EXCEED_ON_HAND` (409).
29. Chain clearance cap on allocation, reported as `ERR_SEASON_CLEARANCE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409).
30. Store clearance capacity on allocation, reported as `ERR_CLEARANCE_SPACE_EXCEEDED` (409).
31. Allowance cap on claim recognition: the recognition arithmetic must yield at least 1 cent, else `ERR_ALLOWANCE_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (409).
32. Unit availability, reported as `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_UNITS` (409): a disposition order's `units` may not exceed the item's on-hand units less those already committed to disposition, `settled_units` may not exceed the order's `units`, and a posted week's `units_sold` may not exceed the item's on-hand units at posting time.
33. Channel payload coherence on disposition-order creation, reported as `ERR_DISPOSITION_CHANNEL_MISMATCH` (422).
34. Elasticity sample sufficiency on estimation: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PRICE_HISTORY` or `ERR_EMPTY_PEER_POOL` (both 422).
35. Confidence gate on certification: `ERR_LOW_CONFIDENCE_NOT_OVERRIDDEN` (422).
36. Ladder completeness on submission: `ladder_step_count` must be at least 1, else `ERR_EMPTY_LADDER` (409).

The practical consequence of this ordering deserves emphasis. Because season status sits at rule 9 and plan status at rule 10, a request against a `CLOSED` season reports `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED` even when the plan is also unapproved. Because segregation of duties sits at rule 11, an approver who is also the creator is told so before any downstream eligibility check runs. And because the ladder-shape checks sit at rule 22, below the three temporal rules, a rung that is both out of sequence and inside the protected period is reported as `ERR_PROTECTED_PERIOD_ACTIVE`.

### 2.8. Cents, Percentages and Pinned Rounding Orders

All money in this system is expressed as an integer number of cents; no monetary field ever carries a fractional part. Fields whose names end in `_cents` — among them `original_retail_cents`, `current_retail_cents`, `unit_cost_cents`, `price_cents`, `per_unit_markdown_cents`, `line_markdown_cents`, `price_cents_applied`, `permanent_markdown_reserve_cents`, `promo_discount_cents`, `gross_markdown_cents`, `vendor_funded_cents`, `net_markdown_cents`, `current_cap_cents`, `funding_cap_cents`, `remaining_cap_cents`, `recognized_total_cents`, `claimed_cents`, `recognized_cents`, `unit_recovery_cents`, `fmv_cents`, `projected_recovery_cents`, `settled_recovery_cents`, `revenue_cents`, `cogs_cents`, `markdown_reserve_cents`, `vendor_funding_cents`, `disposition_recovery_cents` and `gross_margin_cents` — are integers, and any intermediate value that is not already integral is rounded to a whole cent.

Percentages carry exactly two decimal places: `sell_through_target_pct`, `sell_through_pct`, `sell_through_variance_pct`, `depth_pct`, `recommended_depth_pct`, `price_relative_pct`, `current_share_pct` and `agreed_share_pct`. Weeks of supply is likewise carried at two decimals in `weeks_of_supply`. Elasticity is the one quantity carried at four decimals, in `elasticity_coefficient`. Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the method is round half up: a tie at exactly half a unit rounds away from zero, so 1624.5 cents becomes 1625 and 32.455 percent becomes 32.46. Every one of these quantities — cents, percentages, weeks of supply and elasticity alike — is carried in JSON as a bare number and never as a string, and a request that sends one as a string is an `ERR_VALIDATION`. "Two decimal places" and "four decimal places" describe the precision the value is rounded to, not a textual width: JSON numbers do not preserve trailing zeros, so a percentage of zero is emitted as `0.0` and a coefficient of exactly minus one and a half as `-1.5`, and a client must compare numerically rather than by string form.

Two rounding orders are pinned, because the alternatives are observably different and only one is correct. When a ladder rung's price is computed, the discount amount is rounded to a whole cent first and only then subtracted from the original ticket; the resulting price is never rounded again. When an execution's markdown liability is computed, the per-unit markdown — already an integral number of cents, because the rung price was rounded at authoring time — is established first, then multiplied by the units in scope, and only then are the resulting line amounts summed across executions. Multiplying an unrounded per-unit amount by the unit count and rounding the product is explicitly not how this system computes a markdown line. To make the difference concrete: an item with an original ticket of 4999 cents marked to a depth of 32.50 percent yields a rounded discount of 1625 cents from an exact 1624.675, and across 37 units in scope the line comes to 60125 cents, where rounding the product of the unrounded per-unit amount would have produced 60113.

### 2.9. Cascades, Counters and Compensating Reversals

This system is immediately consistent: when an operation changes a row that other rows summarise, every dependent counter is recomputed inside the same request, and the response reflects the settled state rather than an eventual one. Two long cascades dominate the design. Applying a zone execution propagates five hops — the ticket and the rung, the item's markdown dimension, the item's lifecycle dimension, the clearance reservations of the plan, and finally the season's own status. Recognising a vendor claim propagates five hops as well — the claim's recognised amount, the agreement's running totals, the agreement's status, the sibling claims that the exhausted cap now rejects, and the net markdown of every plan those siblings belonged to. Each hop is described where its business narrative lives in §3; what matters globally is that a hop counts as a write only when it actually changes something. A hop that does change a row refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by exactly 1, exactly as if it had been written directly, however many of the row's fields the hop restates at once. A hop that recomputes a row and reproduces every value it already held writes nothing at all: that row keeps its `updated_at` and its `version`, and the cascade is simply invisible on it. This change-only rule is the default for every row a cascade merely reaches — an item whose reclassification restates nothing, a sibling plan whose totals do not move, a store whose committed total lands back on the number it already held, a season whose three clearance counters all match. It does not govern the row the request is itself about: the item behind a posted sales week, a new plan, a disposition order or an applied execution, the plan behind a ladder rung, a zone execution or a vendor claim, and the season behind a clearance allocation or a reservation release are each written unconditionally, bumping `version` by exactly 1 even when the recomputation restates only values that were already correct. §3.1, §3.3, §3.7 and §3.8 pin the individual cases.

Three quantities are conserved, and every reversal in the system is a compensating action that must restore them exactly. First, an item's markdown exposure is the sum of `permanent_markdown_reserve_cents` and `promo_discount_cents`; applying an execution and then rolling the same execution back must leave that sum, and `current_retail_cents`, at their pre-apply values. Second, an agreement's `funding_cap_cents` always equals its `remaining_cap_cents` plus the sum of `recognized_cents` across its `RECOGNIZED` claims; recognising a claim and then reversing it must restore both sides. Third, a store's `clearance_bay_units` always equals its `available_clearance_units` plus the sum of `reserved_units` across its `RESERVED` and `COMMITTED` reservations, so releasing a reservation gives the space back exactly. An item's available pool obeys the same discipline: its on-hand units less `units_committed_disposition` is restored to its pre-create value when a disposition order is cancelled.

Read paths that nest one collection under another — a season's allowance agreements, a season's exit reviews, an item's sales weeks, an item's elasticity estimates, a plan's ladder rungs — resolve the outer identifier first and return only the children of that parent. "First" is meant relative to selecting rows, not relative to the whole ladder of §2.7: the pagination and filter parameters are still validated ahead of the outer identifier, so a request that both carries a malformed `limit` or an out-of-enumeration filter and names a parent that does not exist reports `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than the parent's 404. A parent that exists but has no children is a successful empty page, never a 404, and no read is ever gated on the parent's status — a `CLOSED` season's children remain readable. Every collection is also exposed as a flat top-level list with the parent identifier available as a filter, so the same rows are reachable both ways; the flat list of exit reviews is ordered by `generated_at` ascending, an item's sales weeks by `week_ending_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and a plan's ladder by `step_index` ascending.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Season, Price-Zone and Store Foundations

A season is the unit of planning, and only a `MerchandiseDirector` may register one. Registration carries a human-readable `code` of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across seasons as an exact, case-sensitive match, the merchandise `category` the season governs (one of `APPAREL`, `FOOTWEAR`, `ACCESSORIES` or `SEASONAL_DECOR`), the calendar bracket formed by `season_start_date` and `season_end_date`, the `exit_target_date` by which residual inventory must be gone, an `exit_units_target` of at least one unit, a `sell_through_target_pct` between 0.01 and 100.00, and the chain-level `clearance_space_units` budget of at least one unit. The calendar must be coherent: `season_end_date` must fall strictly after `season_start_date`, and `exit_target_date` must fall strictly after `season_start_date` while landing on or before `season_end_date`. The submitted `sell_through_target_pct` is stored rounded half up to two decimals, so a value carrying more precision than that is accepted and normalised rather than rejected. The three calendar coherence checks are payload violations and therefore run ahead of the uniqueness check, so a request that is both calendrically incoherent and duplicates an existing `code` reports `ERR_VALIDATION`. A newly registered season opens in `PLANNING`, with `reserved_clearance_units`, `committed_clearance_units` and `projected_exit_units` all at 0, and a duplicate `code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Those three counters are never written by a client. `reserved_clearance_units` is the sum of `reserved_units` across the season's reservations that are still `RESERVED`; `committed_clearance_units` is the same sum across reservations that have reached `COMMITTED`, and it is the quantity the exit-window trigger is measured against; `projected_exit_units` is the sum of `projected_exit_units` across the season's plans whose status is `APPROVED`, `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETED`. Every recomputation restates all three from the underlying rows rather than adjusting them by a delta, and the season row is written only when at least one of the three values actually moves: clearance allocation and reservation release write the season directly and always bump its `version`, while a recomputation reached through a cascade from an item, a plan or an execution refreshes `updated_at` and `version` only on a real change and is otherwise invisible.

The season's own lifecycle runs `PLANNING → ACTIVE → EXIT_WINDOW → CLOSED`, and the director drives it by naming a `to_status`. Moving `PLANNING → ACTIVE` requires that at least one item already belongs to the season, and it recomputes the lifecycle stage of every one of those items. Moving `ACTIVE → EXIT_WINDOW` likewise reclassifies every item of the season, and items whose remaining whole weeks to the exit target are two or fewer land in `EXIT_PENDING`. Moving `EXIT_WINDOW → CLOSED` is the most guarded step: a `FINAL` exit review must already exist for the season, and no disposition order of the season may still be sitting in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED`. Closing then sweeps up everything left open — every allowance agreement of the season still in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `EXPIRED`; every claim still in `SUBMITTED` against an agreement that just expired moves to `REJECTED` with its `rejection_reason` set to `AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`; and every clearance reservation of the season still in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `RELEASED`, giving each store's `available_clearance_units` back. Any `to_status` that the season's current status does not declare — jumping `PLANNING` straight to `EXIT_WINDOW`, or reviving a `CLOSED` season — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Each transition writes the season's own row once, incrementing its `version` by 1, and only then reclassifies; where that reclassification shifts the season's clearance counters the counter write bumps the `version` again, once for each item whose reservations flip, so a move into `EXIT_WINDOW` that commits reservations leaves the season more than one version ahead. The close runs in the order sweep, then reclassification, then counter recomputation, so a closed season reports `reserved_clearance_units` and `committed_clearance_units` of 0 while `projected_exit_units` keeps the roll-up of its approved, executing and completed plans.

Price zones give the chain its execution geometry, and only a director creates them. A zone carries a unique `zone_code` of between 1 and 64 characters, a `zone_type` of `CHAIN`, `REGION` or `STORE`, an optional reporting `region_code` that must be a non-empty string when supplied and is stored as null when omitted, and a `signage_lead_days` count between 1 and 30. Zones nest through a self-referential `parent_zone_id`: a `REGION` zone must name a `CHAIN` zone as its parent, a `STORE` zone must name a `REGION` zone as its parent, and a `CHAIN` zone must name no parent at all. A supplied `parent_zone_id` is resolved before any of those three coherence rules is applied and before the `zone_code` uniqueness check, so an unresolvable `parent_zone_id` always reports `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND` — including on a `CHAIN` zone, where naming a parent at all is the deeper mistake, and including when the `zone_code` is also a duplicate. A `REGION` or `STORE` zone that omits `parent_zone_id` entirely, or whose parent is of the wrong `zone_type`, is an `ERR_VALIDATION`. When `signage_lead_days` is omitted it defaults by breadth — ten days for a `CHAIN` zone, one week for a `REGION` zone, three days for a `STORE` zone — because signage takes longer to reach every door of the chain than to reach one region or one shop. That same number does double duty: it is both the lead time required before an execution's effective date in the zone and the minimum spacing between two executions on the same item in the zone.

Stores hang off zones. A store registration carries a `store_number` of 1 to 16 characters that is unique across stores, the `price_zone_id` whose prices the store executes, an optional `region_code`, a `clearance_bay_units` capacity of zero or more, and optionally an operating `status` drawn from `OPEN`, `REMODEL` and `CLOSING`, defaulting to `OPEN`. On registration `committed_clearance_units` starts at 0 and `available_clearance_units` starts equal to `clearance_bay_units`. Thereafter `committed_clearance_units` is maintained as the sum of `reserved_units` across the store's reservations that are `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`, and `available_clearance_units` is always the bay capacity less that committed sum. A director may later revise a store's `status` or its `clearance_bay_units`; either revision recomputes `available_clearance_units` from the new capacity and bumps the store's `version`. Both fields are optional on that revision, so a request naming neither — or naming values identical to the stored ones — is accepted, still recomputes `available_clearance_units` and still bumps `version` by exactly 1. The recomputation is a plain subtraction and is never clamped: cutting `clearance_bay_units` below the units already committed leaves `available_clearance_units` negative, and the revision is allowed rather than refused, because existing reservations are never silently discarded. A store revision is not gated on any season's status. Only `OPEN` stores are candidates for clearance allocation — a store in `REMODEL` or `CLOSING` is skipped even when its bays are empty — and a duplicate `store_number`, like a duplicate `zone_code`, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

### 3.2. Item Intake, Protected Periods and Weekly Sales History

Items are registered by a `PricingAnalyst` or a `MerchandiseDirector` and are the pivot of everything downstream. An item carries a `sku` unique across items, the `season_id` it belongs to, its own `category`, a `cluster_code` naming the store cluster whose peers it may pool elasticity with, a `vendor_code` identifying the supplier that allowance agreements will be matched against, the `first_receipt_date` on which its first units landed, an `original_retail_cents` ticket of at least one cent, a landed `unit_cost_cents` of zero or more, and `units_received_total` of at least one unit. The item's `category` must equal its season's `category`; a mismatch is a payload violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`, as is a duplicate `sku` being caught later by the uniqueness rule with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Registration derives the protected new-arrival window immediately. `protected_until_date` is `first_receipt_date` advanced by the category's protected period: six weeks for `APPAREL`, eight weeks for `FOOTWEAR`, four weeks for `ACCESSORIES` and three weeks for `SEASONAL_DECOR` — that is, 42, 56, 28 and 21 days respectively. No markdown rung may ever take effect on or before that date. Registration also initialises `current_retail_cents` to `original_retail_cents`, sets `on_hand_units` to `units_received_total`, and leaves `units_sold_total`, `units_committed_disposition`, `units_disposed_total` and `sample_weeks` at 0, `sell_through_pct` at 0.00, `weeks_of_supply` at 999.99, `permanent_markdown_reserve_cents` and `promo_discount_cents` at 0, `lifecycle_stage` at `NEW_ARRIVAL` and `markdown_state` at `FULL_PRICE`. Registration then immediately runs the classification of §3.3 over those initial values, so an item whose `protected_until_date` already lies behind the logical clock is persisted and returned in `CORE`, `AGING` or `CLEARANCE` rather than in `NEW_ARRIVAL`; that opening classification is part of creation rather than a mutation of it, so the created item's `version` is 1 and its `updated_at` still equals its `created_at`. The item's `on_hand_units` is always its `units_received_total` less `units_sold_total` and less `units_disposed_total`, and it is never written directly. The category-equality check against the season runs after the season is resolved but before the CLOSED-season gate and before the `sku` uniqueness check, so a mismatched category is reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` even when the season is closed or the `sku` is a duplicate.

Weekly sales history is posted by the analyst, one reading at a time, each carrying a `week_ending_date`, the `units_sold` in that week and the `ticket_price_cents` that was in effect. A week-ending date must be strictly later than the item's `first_receipt_date` — an earlier or equal date is an `ERR_VALIDATION` — and the pair of item and `week_ending_date` may appear only once, so a re-post is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Units sold must be zero or more and may not exceed the item's on-hand units at the moment of posting, which is where `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_UNITS` guards the ledger; the ticket price must be at least one cent. Each reading stores its own `price_relative_pct`, the posted ticket price as a percentage of the item's original retail, rounded half up to two decimals. Sales weeks are append-only history: they carry an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else.

Posting a week moves several item aggregates at once. `units_sold_total` grows by the week's `units_sold` and the on-hand count falls by the same amount; `sample_weeks` grows by one; `sell_through_pct` is recomputed as units sold as a percentage of units received, rounded half up to two decimals; and `weeks_of_supply` is recomputed as the on-hand units divided by the average weekly sales rate, rounded half up to two decimals, where that average rate is itself `units_sold_total` divided by `sample_weeks` rounded half up to four decimals. Where there is no history to divide by — no sample weeks at all, or a cumulative sales total of zero — `weeks_of_supply` is reported as 999.99 rather than as an error or an absent value. The posting then re-runs lifecycle classification, which can carry the item out of `NEW_ARRIVAL` into `AGING` or out of `CORE` into `AGING`, and it increments the item's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. Lifecycle Classification and the Eligibility Gates

Every item carries two independent state dimensions that never constrain each other's transitions: a `lifecycle_stage` describing where the unit sits in its selling life, and a `markdown_state` describing where its ticket sits. The lifecycle dimension takes the values `NEW_ARRIVAL`, `CORE`, `AGING`, `CLEARANCE`, `EXIT_PENDING` and `DISPOSED`, begins at `NEW_ARRIVAL` and ends at `DISPOSED`; the pricing dimension is described in §3.6.

Classification is a pure function of the item, its season and the logical clock, and it is re-evaluated after every item mutation — registration, a posted sales week, an applied or rolled-back execution, a settled disposition order — and for every item of a season whenever the season's status changes or the clock is set. Three intermediate quantities are computed first. The current date is the date part of the logical clock in UTC. Weeks on hand is the number of whole days from the item's `first_receipt_date` to the current date divided by seven with the remainder discarded. Weeks to exit is the number of whole days from the current date to the season's `exit_target_date` divided by seven with the remainder discarded, and it may be negative once that date has passed. An item counts as a slow seller when its `sell_through_pct` is strictly below 45.00 percent and it has been on hand for at least six weeks. A reclassification that reproduces every value it recomputes is not a mutation at all: it refreshes neither `updated_at` nor `version`, which is why sweeping a whole season or the whole system leaves untouched items untouched. When something does move, the refresh is a single write however much of the item it restates — the aggregates, the `markdown_state` and the `lifecycle_stage` together cost the item exactly one `version` increment, and the reservation and counter side effects it triggers add nothing further to the item's own count.

Six classification rules are then evaluated strictly top to bottom, and the first one that matches decides the stage; no later rule can override an earlier match.

1. The item is `DISPOSED` when its on-hand units have reached 0 and its `units_disposed_total` is greater than 0.
2. Otherwise it is `NEW_ARRIVAL` when the current date is on or before its `protected_until_date`. The boundary day is still protected: an item whose protected date is exactly today has not yet aged out.
3. Otherwise it is `EXIT_PENDING` when the season's status is `EXIT_WINDOW` and weeks to exit is two or fewer.
4. Otherwise it is `CLEARANCE` when the item's `markdown_state` is either `PERM_MARKED` or `EXIT_PRICED`, or when weeks to exit is six or fewer.
5. Otherwise it is `AGING` when weeks on hand is ten or more, or when the item qualifies as a slow seller.
6. Otherwise it is `CORE`.

Read as transitions, the same rules describe a cyclic dimension. An item leaves `NEW_ARRIVAL` for `CORE` once the current date is strictly past its `protected_until_date` and none of the earlier rules match, and it leaves `NEW_ARRIVAL` for `AGING` directly when the protected period has passed and it has either reached ten weeks on hand or become a slow seller; both moves refresh the item's `updated_at` and increment its `version`, and reaching `AGING` is precisely what makes the item markdown-eligible. `CORE → AGING` fires on the same aging-or-slow-selling condition. `AGING → CLEARANCE` fires when the item's `markdown_state` becomes `PERM_MARKED` or when the clock advances far enough that weeks to exit falls to six or fewer, and it carries a side effect of its own: every clearance reservation belonging to the item's non-terminal plans that is still `RESERVED` moves to `COMMITTED`, where a plan counts as non-terminal unless its status is `SUPERSEDED`, so an `EXECUTING` or a `COMPLETED` plan is still in scope. `CORE → CLEARANCE` fires on the same weeks-to-exit condition with the same reservation side effect. The dimension runs backwards too: `CLEARANCE → AGING` fires when the item's `markdown_state` reverts to `FULL_PRICE` after a rolled-back execution, provided weeks to exit is still more than six and the item is still ten weeks on hand or a slow seller, and it reverts that plan's `COMMITTED` reservations back to `RESERVED`, reading the same non-terminal plan set, so a plan that has already reached `COMPLETED` still has its reservations flipped back. Only a move into `AGING` out of `CLEARANCE` or `EXIT_PENDING` flips reservations back that way: a reclassification that instead carries such an item into `CORE`, back into `NEW_ARRIVAL` because the clock was rewound, or on into `DISPOSED` leaves its reservations sitting in `COMMITTED`. Both `CLEARANCE → EXIT_PENDING` and `AGING → EXIT_PENDING` fire when the season has entered `EXIT_WINDOW` and weeks to exit has fallen to two or fewer, and each makes the item eligible for a disposition order. Finally both `EXIT_PENDING → DISPOSED` and `CLEARANCE → DISPOSED` fire after a settlement leaves the item with zero on-hand units and a positive `units_disposed_total`, and each drives the item's `markdown_state` to `SETTLED`.

Two gates read this dimension, and they read it differently. Markdown eligibility, checked when a plan is submitted, is satisfied by exactly `AGING`, `CLEARANCE` and `EXIT_PENDING`; a submission on an item still in `NEW_ARRIVAL` or `CORE`, or already in `DISPOSED`, is refused with `ERR_MARKDOWN_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Disposition eligibility, checked when a disposition order is drafted, is narrower: only `CLEARANCE` and `EXIT_PENDING` qualify, and anything else is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_EXIT_ELIGIBLE`. Note that opening a plan is not gated on the stage at all — a plan may be opened on any item and will still be stamped with a recommended depth — the stage is only enforced at submission.

### 3.4. Elasticity Estimation and Certification

Before an analyst may commit to a depth ladder, the system needs a number for how the item's demand responds to price. An estimate is requested against one `item_id`, optionally naming a `method`: `AUTO`, which is the default when the field is omitted, `OWN_HISTORY`, or `CLUSTER_POOLED`. The estimate that comes back echoes the requested method verbatim in `method` — which therefore reads `AUTO` whenever the field was omitted or sent as `AUTO`, and is never rewritten to the path that ran — and records which path actually ran in `resolved_method` — always either `OWN_HISTORY` or `CLUSTER_POOLED`, never `AUTO` — together with the `elasticity_coefficient` itself, the `sample_weeks_used`, the `peer_items_used`, a `confidence` of `HIGH` or `LOW`, the `estimated_at` instant taken from the logical clock, a null `certified_at`, and a status of `DRAFT`.

The own-history path is available only when the item's price history can actually reveal a slope. Ordering the item's sales weeks by `week_ending_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, the history qualifies when there are at least four weeks, when at least two distinct `ticket_price_cents` values appear across them, when the earliest week's ticket price differs from the latest week's ticket price, and when both the earliest and the latest week sold at least one unit. Where the history qualifies, the coefficient is the two-point log-log slope: the natural logarithm of the latest week's `units_sold` divided by the earliest week's `units_sold`, divided in turn by the natural logarithm of the latest week's `ticket_price_cents` divided by the earliest week's `ticket_price_cents`. Both logarithms are taken on the raw integer cent prices and raw unit counts. On this path `resolved_method` is `OWN_HISTORY`, `sample_weeks_used` is the number of weeks in the history and `peer_items_used` is 0.

The cluster-pooled path never looks at the subject item's own weeks. Its peer set is every other item in the same season with the same `category` and the same `cluster_code`, whose `units_sold_total` is at least 1 and which holds a `CERTIFIED` elasticity estimate — and because certification supersedes an item's other estimates, an item can hold at most one. The pooled coefficient is the units-weighted mean of those peers' coefficients: each peer's coefficient multiplied by that peer's `units_sold_total`, summed across the peers, then divided by the sum of those same `units_sold_total` weights. On this path `resolved_method` is `CLUSTER_POOLED`, `sample_weeks_used` is 0, `peer_items_used` is the number of peers that entered the average, and `confidence` is always `LOW`.

Whichever path runs, the raw coefficient is clamped into the interval from -4.0000 to -0.2000 — a value more negative than -4.0000 is pulled up to -4.0000 and a value closer to zero than -0.2000 is pushed down to -0.2000 — and the clamped result is rounded half up to four decimal places. The clamp is what guarantees the sign rather than merely trimming the tails: a raw slope that comes out at zero or positive — which the two-point formula yields whenever the later week sold at least as many units at the higher price, and which a degenerate pooled average can also produce — is pushed to -0.2000 exactly as a shallow negative slope is, and is never reported as it stands. Elasticity in this domain is always negative. Confidence is `HIGH` only when the resolved path was `OWN_HISTORY` and at least four of the item's own weeks entered the calculation; in every other case, including every pooled estimate, it is `LOW`.

Method resolution and its failure modes deserve care because the two variants return genuinely different numbers from the same stored data. When `method` is `AUTO`, the system uses the own-history path if the qualifying conditions above hold and otherwise falls through to the cluster-pooled path. When `method` is `OWN_HISTORY` explicitly and those conditions do not hold, the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PRICE_HISTORY` rather than silently pooling. When the cluster-pooled path runs — whether requested outright or reached through `AUTO` — and no qualifying peer exists, the request is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_PEER_POOL`. An item that both qualifies for own history and has pooled peers will therefore report a different coefficient depending on which method was asked for, and that difference propagates straight into the recommended first-step depth of any plan built on the estimate.

Certification is what turns a number into a decision input, and either a `PricingAnalyst` or a `MerchandiseDirector` may certify. A certification request applies only to an estimate still in `DRAFT` — attempting it on one already `CERTIFIED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and it requires either that the estimate's `confidence` is `HIGH` or that the request carries `override_low_confidence` set to true. A low-confidence estimate certified without that acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_LOW_CONFIDENCE_NOT_OVERRIDDEN`. Certification moves the estimate `DRAFT → CERTIFIED`, stamps `certified_at` from the logical clock, and then supersedes the field: every other estimate of the same item whose status is `DRAFT` or `CERTIFIED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, whether it was a stale draft (which simply becomes uncertifiable) or the previously certified figure. That second case has teeth — a plan in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` that references a now-superseded estimate can no longer be submitted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ELASTICITY_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Certification increments the estimate's `version`.

### 3.5. Plan Authoring, the Depth Ladder, Review and Approval

A markdown plan is opened by a `PricingAnalyst` and binds four things together: the `season_id` whose inventory is being exited, the `item_id` whose ticket will move, the `elasticity_estimate_id` whose coefficient drives the recommendation, and a `plan_type` of either `PERMANENT` or `PROMOTIONAL`. It also names an `exit_date_target`, the date by which this item must be fully exit-priced, which may not fall after the season's own `exit_target_date`; overshooting it is a payload violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`, and it is tested as soon as the three body identifiers have resolved — they resolve in the order `season_id`, then `item_id`, then `elasticity_estimate_id` — which places it ahead of both the stale-version guard and the season-status gate, so an overshooting `exit_date_target` is reported in preference to a stale `expected_item_version` and in preference to a season that is not `ACTIVE` or is `CLOSED`. Because opening a plan supersedes any earlier plan for the same season and item, the request must additionally carry `expected_item_version` as described in §2.6. On creation the plan stamps `created_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, leaves `approved_by_user_id` null, opens in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0 and `ladder_step_count` 0, computes and stores `recommended_depth_pct`, and increments the item's `version`. Note that `plan_type` is an accounting decision, not just a label: a `PERMANENT` plan writes the ticket down and books an inventory reserve, while a `PROMOTIONAL` plan books contra-revenue on sold units only and can never be claimed against a vendor allowance.

Supersession is the reason the create is guarded. Any earlier plan for the same season and item whose status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`; its `superseded_by_plan_id` is pointed at the new plan; every one of its ladder rungs still in `PLANNED` or `SCHEDULED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which recomputes its `ladder_step_count`; and every clearance reservation it held in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `RELEASED`. Its zone executions are deliberately not touched: one still `SCHEDULED` stays `SCHEDULED` and can never be applied, because its plan is no longer `APPROVED` or `EXECUTING`, yet it goes on occupying the item's on-hand pool, its rung's store window and its zone's signage rhythm for all three scheduling rules of §3.6. One earlier plan cannot be swept away like this: if any execution of the prior plan has already reached `APPLIED`, the ticket has physically moved in a store and the create is refused outright with `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_EXECUTING`.

The recommended depth is advisory but precisely defined, and it is evaluated in a fixed order: base, then urgency, then inventory pressure, then the elasticity multiplier, then the outer clamp. The base depth is keyed to the item's `lifecycle_stage`: 0.00 percent for `NEW_ARRIVAL`, 5.00 for `CORE`, 15.00 for `AGING`, 30.00 for `CLEARANCE`, 50.00 for `EXIT_PENDING` and 0.00 for `DISPOSED` — every stage is keyed, because a plan may be opened on an item at any stage. The urgency addend is selected by tier from the whole weeks remaining to the season's `exit_target_date`: nothing at all when more than twelve weeks remain, 5.00 percent when between eight and twelve weeks remain inclusive, 10.00 percent when between four and seven weeks remain inclusive, and 20.00 percent when fewer than four weeks remain. The inventory-pressure addend charges 1.50 percent for each week of supply above a four-week target: take the item's `weeks_of_supply` less 4.00, floor that difference at zero so a well-covered item contributes nothing, multiply by 1.50, round half up to two decimals, and cap the result at 15.00 percent. The elasticity multiplier is the absolute value of the estimate's `elasticity_coefficient` divided by a pivot of 1.50, clamped into the range 0.60 through 1.40 and rounded half up to four decimals — an item twice as price-responsive as the pivot is pushed deeper, an inelastic one is held back. The three percentage addends are summed first and the sum is multiplied by the elasticity multiplier exactly once; that product is then clamped to no less than 10.00 percent and no more than 70.00 percent and rounded half up to two decimals to give `recommended_depth_pct`. The outer floor is why even a plan opened on a `NEW_ARRIVAL` or `DISPOSED` item, whose base is 0.00, still reports 10.00 percent unless urgency and pressure alone carry it higher. The figure is stamped once, at creation, from the stage, weeks of supply and coefficient as they stand at that moment, and it is never restated afterwards: a later change to the item's `lifecycle_stage` or `weeks_of_supply`, a fresh certified estimate, or an advance of the logical clock all leave the stored `recommended_depth_pct` exactly as it was.

The ladder itself is authored one rung at a time, each rung carrying a `step_index`, an `effective_date` and a cumulative `depth_pct`. Depth is always measured off the original ticket, never off the live one, so the rung's `price_cents` is the item's `original_retail_cents` less the discount, where the discount is `original_retail_cents` multiplied by `depth_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up to a whole cent before the subtraction. That discount is taken from the depth exactly as submitted, while the value stored on the rung is the same depth rounded half up to two decimals, so a depth carrying more precision than two decimals is accepted and normalised rather than rejected — a rung authored at 32.455 percent prices off 32.455 and reports its `depth_pct` as 32.46. Each new rung also fixes the ladder's occupancy calendar: a rung's `window_end_date_exclusive` is set to the plan's `exit_date_target` when it is the last rung, and appending a further rung rewrites the previous rung's `window_end_date_exclusive` to the new rung's `effective_date`, so the rungs tile the plan's calendar as half-open windows — that rewrite is a mutation of the earlier rung and increments its own `version`. Appending a rung increments the plan's `ladder_step_count`, recomputes the plan's `projected_exit_units`, and bumps the plan's `version`. Rungs open in `PLANNED`.

Four families of rule constrain a rung, and their precedence matters when a rung breaks several at once. Temporally, the `effective_date` must be strictly after the item's `protected_until_date` — landing exactly on it is a violation reported as `ERR_PROTECTED_PERIOD_ACTIVE`; it must sit at least two weeks after the previous rung's `effective_date`, where exactly fourteen days is acceptable and thirteen is refused with `ERR_CADENCE_TOO_TIGHT`; it must leave a one-week buffer before the plan's target, so it may be no later than `exit_date_target` less seven days, else `ERR_EXIT_DATE_MISSED`; and it may not be earlier than the logical clock's current date, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST`. Structurally, `step_index` must be exactly one more than the plan's current `ladder_step_count`, else `ERR_STEP_INDEX_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`; a ladder may hold at most four rungs, so a fifth append is refused with `ERR_MAX_LADDER_STEPS`; and `depth_pct` must be strictly greater than the previous rung's `depth_pct`, else `ERR_DEPTH_NOT_MONOTONIC`. The depth itself is bounded by the payload rule at 0.01 through 70.00 percent. As set out in §2.7, the three temporal rules are evaluated before the three structural ones, so a rung that is both inside the protected window and out of sequence reports the protected-period violation. Both comparisons that speak of "the previous rung" — the cadence floor and the monotonic depth — read the plan's last rung that has not been `SUPERSEDED`, taken in `step_index` order, and the same set is what `ladder_step_count` counts: a ladder cleared on reopen therefore constrains neither the cadence nor the depth of the first rung authored after it, which is compared against nothing at all.

Submission hands the plan to the director and is the point where every authoring assumption is re-checked against the world as it now stands. Submitting requires that the plan is in `DRAFT`, that the season is `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW`, that the ladder holds at least one rung — an empty ladder is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_LADDER` — that the referenced estimate is still `CERTIFIED` and still belongs to the plan's item, and that the item's `lifecycle_stage` is one of the three markdown-eligible stages. Submission recomputes the plan's `projected_exit_units` from its scheduled executions, which is 0 while none exist, and moves the plan `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`.

Review is a genuine cycle. A director may send a submitted plan back by supplying a `revision_reason` of between 1 and 1024 characters, which moves it `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and increments `revision_round` by 1. The analyst then reopens it, moving it `REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`; the reopen request may carry `clear_ladder`, and when that flag is true every rung of the plan still in `PLANNED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` and `ladder_step_count` resets to 0, so the analyst re-authors the ladder from `step_index` 1. A reopen is permitted while `revision_round` is at most 3; once it has gone beyond that the plan is out of revisions and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Alternatively the director approves, which requires the plan to be `SUBMITTED`, the season to be `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW`, and the approving `X-User-ID` to differ from the plan's `created_by_user_id`; approval stamps `approved_by_user_id`, moves the plan `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and recomputes the season's `projected_exit_units`.

Two further plan transitions are driven by events rather than by a request. The first execution of an approved plan to reach `APPLIED` carries the plan `APPROVED → EXECUTING` and recomputes `gross_markdown_cents` and `net_markdown_cents` from its applied executions. The plan then moves `EXECUTING → COMPLETED` once every rung that has not been superseded has reached `EXECUTED`, or as soon as the item's `markdown_state` reaches `SETTLED`. A plan's money fields follow from its executions and claims: `gross_markdown_cents` is the sum of `line_markdown_cents` over its `APPLIED` executions accumulated in `created_at` ascending order, `vendor_funded_cents` is the sum of `recognized_cents` over its `RECOGNIZED` claims, `net_markdown_cents` is the gross less the vendor-funded amount, and `projected_exit_units` is the sum of `units_in_scope` over its executions that are `SCHEDULED` or `APPLIED`. Both `EXECUTING` and `COMPLETED` are claimable against a vendor allowance; nothing earlier is.

### 3.6. Zone Execution, Signage Compliance and the Apply Cascade

An approved ladder does not move a single ticket by itself. Each rung reaches the shelf through a zone execution, scheduled by the analyst against a `markdown_plan_id` and one of that plan's rungs named by `markdown_step_id`, aimed at a `price_zone_id`, with an `execution_scope` of `CHAIN`, `ZONE` or `STORE`, an `effective_date` and a count of `units_in_scope` of at least one unit. A store-scope execution additionally names the `store_id` whose shelf changes. Those body identifiers resolve in that same order — plan, then rung, then zone, then the optional store — so where several of them name nothing the 404 reported is the first one in that order. Scheduling requires the plan to be `APPROVED` or `EXECUTING` — anything else is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` — and the season to be `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW`. The new execution opens with `status` `SCHEDULED` and `signage_status` `PENDING`, computes its `signage_due_date` as the `effective_date` less the zone's `signage_lead_days`, leaves `per_unit_markdown_cents`, `line_markdown_cents` and `price_cents_applied` at 0 and `applied_at`, `posted_at`, `posted_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` null, moves the referenced rung `PLANNED → SCHEDULED`, adds its `units_in_scope` to the plan's `projected_exit_units`, and bumps the plan's `version`. The rung's own status is never checked: any rung of the plan may be named, including one already `SCHEDULED` or `EXECUTED` by an earlier execution or one left `SUPERSEDED` by a cleared ladder, and only a rung sitting in `PLANNED` is advanced — the others keep the status they already carry.

Scope integrity is checked strictly, because breadth and signage responsibility travel together. A `CHAIN` execution requires the named zone's `zone_type` to be `CHAIN` and forbids a `store_id`. A `ZONE` execution requires the zone's `zone_type` to be `REGION` and likewise forbids a `store_id`. A `STORE` execution requires the zone's `zone_type` to be either `STORE` or `REGION`, requires a `store_id`, and requires that store's `price_zone_id` to equal the execution's `price_zone_id`. The execution's `effective_date` must also equal the referenced rung's own `effective_date`, and the rung must genuinely belong to the named plan. Any of these disagreements is refused with `ERR_ZONE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

Three further scheduling rules protect the calendar and the stockroom. The signage window must still be open: the `effective_date` less the zone's `signage_lead_days` must not already be in the past relative to the logical clock's current date, else the execution could never be posted in time and is refused with `ERR_SIGNAGE_LEAD_VIOLATION`. Store-scope executions must not double-book a shelf: a store-scope execution occupies the half-open window that runs from its rung's `effective_date` up to, but not including, that rung's `window_end_date_exclusive`, scoped to the pair of item and store, and a new store-scope execution whose window intersects the window of a `SCHEDULED` or `APPLIED` execution for the same item and store is refused with `ERR_STEP_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Windows that merely touch do not intersect — a new execution starting exactly on the day an existing window ends is accepted. Executions on one item in one zone must also respect the signage rhythm: for any two executions on the same `price_zone_id` and item that are `SCHEDULED` or `APPLIED`, the gap between their effective dates, measured in whole days and ignoring direction, must be at least the zone's `signage_lead_days`; a gap exactly equal to that lead is allowed and one day less is refused with `ERR_ZONE_SIGNAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Finally, the physical inventory has to exist: the new execution's `units_in_scope` plus the `units_in_scope` of the item's other `SCHEDULED` and `APPLIED` executions may not exceed the item's on-hand units, else `ERR_ZONE_UNITS_EXCEED_ON_HAND`.

Signage compliance is the execution's second, independent dimension, and it moves through `PENDING`, `POSTED`, `NON_COMPLIANT` and `VERIFIED`. A `StoreManager` records that the signage is physically up; for a store-scope execution the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the execution's `store_id`, while chain-scope and zone-scope executions carry no `store_id` and may be posted by any store manager. If the logical clock's current date is on or before the `signage_due_date`, the dimension moves `PENDING → POSTED`. If the due date has already passed, the same request instead moves it `PENDING → NON_COMPLIANT`: the posting is still recorded — `posted_at`, taken from the logical clock rather than from wall-clock time, and `posted_by_user_id` are stamped in both cases — but `NON_COMPLIANT` is terminal on this dimension. Such an execution can never be verified and can never be applied, and the analyst's only remedy is to schedule a fresh execution. Posting an execution whose `signage_status` is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_SIGNAGE_ALREADY_POSTED`. Verification is a `MerchandiseDirector` responsibility for every scope, including chain-wide ones: it moves the dimension `POSTED → VERIFIED` and stamps `verified_by_user_id`, and attempting it on an execution that is not `POSTED` — still `PENDING`, already `VERIFIED`, or `NON_COMPLIANT` — is refused with `ERR_SIGNAGE_NOT_POSTED`.

Applying the execution is the moment the ticket actually changes, and it is reserved to the `PricingAnalyst`. Apply requires the execution to be `SCHEDULED`, its `signage_status` to be `VERIFIED` — `PENDING`, `POSTED` and `NON_COMPLIANT` all block with `ERR_SIGNAGE_NOT_VERIFIED` — the plan to be `APPROVED` or `EXECUTING`, the item's `markdown_state` to be `FULL_PRICE`, `PROMO_ACTIVE` or `PERM_MARKED`, the clock's current date to have reached the execution's `effective_date`, the `units_in_scope` not to exceed the item's on-hand units, and the rung's `price_cents` to sit strictly below the item's `current_retail_cents`. The last two of those preconditions and the date check carry no dedicated code and are reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; applying an execution twice reports the same code, since `SCHEDULED` is the only state that declares the transition. For this endpoint only the `SCHEDULED` requirement itself occupies rule 8 of §2.7's ladder; the four dependent preconditions — the item's `markdown_state`, the arrival of the `effective_date`, the on-hand pool and the rung price — are evaluated after the season gate, so an apply against a `CLOSED` season reports `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED` even when one of those four is also violated.

The apply itself computes the markdown liability line and freezes it: `per_unit_markdown_cents` is the item's `current_retail_cents` less the rung's `price_cents`, `line_markdown_cents` is that per-unit amount multiplied by `units_in_scope`, and `price_cents_applied` records the rung price written onto the item. The execution moves `SCHEDULED → APPLIED` with `applied_at` stamped from the logical clock, and then five hops run in order. Hop one rewrites the item's `current_retail_cents` to the rung's `price_cents` and moves the rung `SCHEDULED → EXECUTED`. Hop two moves the item's pricing dimension according to the plan's `plan_type` and the rung's depth. Hop three re-runs lifecycle classification, which for a permanently marked item carries the lifecycle dimension into `CLEARANCE`. Hop four follows from that reclassification: every clearance reservation held by any non-terminal plan of the item — not merely by the plan whose execution was applied — that is still `RESERVED` moves to `COMMITTED`, so the season's `reserved_clearance_units` falls and its `committed_clearance_units` rises by the same units. Hop five follows from the season counter: once `committed_clearance_units` reaches or passes the exit-window threshold described in §3.7, the season moves `ACTIVE → EXIT_WINDOW`, which reclassifies every item of the season all over again. Alongside the cascade, the plan's `gross_markdown_cents` grows by `line_markdown_cents`, its `net_markdown_cents` is recomputed, and the plan moves `APPROVED → EXECUTING` on its first applied execution and then, as §3.5 describes, continues on to `COMPLETED` inside this same request once every rung of the plan that has not been superseded has reached `EXECUTED` — which a single-rung ladder reaches on that very first apply, because hop one has just moved its only rung to `EXECUTED`, so the apply response reports the plan as `COMPLETED`. The plan is written twice in that case — once for the ledger recomputation and the move to `EXECUTING`, once again for the completion — so its `version` climbs by 2 where a single apply carries it all the way to `COMPLETED` and by 1 where it does not. Both the execution's and the item's `version` are incremented, the item exactly once however many of the five hops touch it.

Hop two is where accounting treatment bites, and each branch is specified separately. Under a `PROMOTIONAL` plan whose rung depth is below 50.00 percent, an item at `FULL_PRICE` moves `FULL_PRICE → PROMO_ACTIVE`; its `current_retail_cents` becomes the rung price, and its `promo_discount_cents` accrues the per-unit markdown for every unit sold on or after the execution's `effective_date` — measured at the granularity of the posted history, so a sales week whose `week_ending_date` falls on or after that `effective_date` contributes all of its `units_sold` even where the week itself opened before the markdown landed, and a week ending strictly earlier contributes none of them — contra-revenue, recognised only as the units actually move. Under a `PERMANENT` plan whose rung depth is below 50.00 percent, an item at `FULL_PRICE` moves `FULL_PRICE → PERM_MARKED` and an item already at `PROMO_ACTIVE` moves `PROMO_ACTIVE → PERM_MARKED`; in both cases `current_retail_cents` becomes the rung price, the full `line_markdown_cents` is added to the item's `permanent_markdown_reserve_cents` immediately at execution time on every unit in scope, and the item's `lifecycle_stage` is reclassified. Under a `PERMANENT` plan whose rung depth reaches or exceeds 50.00 percent — the exit-pricing threshold — an item at `PERM_MARKED` moves `PERM_MARKED → EXIT_PRICED`, again writing `current_retail_cents` to the rung price and adding `line_markdown_cents` to the permanent reserve. The two combinations those three branches leave out move the pricing dimension nowhere at all, while still rewriting `current_retail_cents` and still accruing their markdown in the ordinary way: a `PROMOTIONAL` rung whose depth reaches or exceeds 50.00 percent leaves the item in whatever pricing state it was already in, and a `PERMANENT` rung at or beyond 50.00 percent applied to an item still at `FULL_PRICE` or `PROMO_ACTIVE` likewise leaves it there rather than jumping it to `PERM_MARKED` or to `EXIT_PRICED` — only an item that has already reached `PERM_MARKED` is carried on to `EXIT_PRICED`. Settlement closes the dimension from either of two states: both `EXIT_PRICED → SETTLED` and `PERM_MARKED → SETTLED` fire as soon as the item's on-hand units reach zero, whatever emptied them — a settled disposition order, or simply a posted sales week that clears the last unit — and no pricing state other than those two is carried to `SETTLED` this way. The accompanying move of `lifecycle_stage` to `DISPOSED` is the narrower one, because it also requires a positive `units_disposed_total`: an item sold out to zero with no disposition behind it reports `markdown_state` `SETTLED` while its `lifecycle_stage` stays whatever the six classification rules give it.

Roll-back is the compensating action, performed by the analyst with a `rollback_reason` of between 1 and 1024 characters, and it must undo every one of those effects. It requires the execution to be `APPLIED` and requires that no `RECOGNIZED` vendor claim references the execution's plan — money already recognised against a markdown may not be un-marked, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The execution moves `APPLIED → ROLLED_BACK`, and `ROLLED_BACK` is terminal. The item's `current_retail_cents` is restored to its `original_retail_cents` when no other `APPLIED` execution remains for the item; where others do remain, it is restored instead to the `price_cents_applied` of the latest remaining applied execution, ordered by `effective_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. The plan's `gross_markdown_cents` is reduced by the rolled-back `line_markdown_cents` and `net_markdown_cents` is recomputed. The plan's own `status` is not part of the reversal: a plan that has reached `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETED` stays in that status, even where the roll-back leaves it with no `APPLIED` execution at all. On the accounting side, a `PERMANENT` plan's reversal subtracts the same `line_markdown_cents` from the item's `permanent_markdown_reserve_cents`, while a `PROMOTIONAL` plan's reversal subtracts the accrued contra-revenue from `promo_discount_cents`; either way the item's total markdown exposure returns to its pre-apply value. The rung moves `EXECUTED → SCHEDULED`, provided the rolled-back execution was the only applied execution of that rung, so a rung can be re-executed later. The item's pricing dimension reverts — `PROMO_ACTIVE → FULL_PRICE` or `PERM_MARKED → FULL_PRICE`, each of which requires that no other execution of the item is still `APPLIED`, while an item that had already reached `EXIT_PRICED` or `SETTLED` keeps that state even when the roll-back has restored its `current_retail_cents` to the original ticket — and lifecycle classification re-runs, which is what carries a `CLEARANCE` item back to `AGING` and flips the `COMMITTED` reservations of every non-terminal plan of that item — not only of the plan being rolled back — to `RESERVED`, shifting the season's counters from committed back to reserved. Both the execution's and the item's `version` are incremented.

### 3.7. Clearance Space Allocation and the Exit-Window Trigger

Clearance floor space is scarce, and the system treats it as a budget rather than as an afterthought. A season declares a chain-level `clearance_space_units` ceiling, each store declares its own `clearance_bay_units` bay capacity, and an allocation draws down both at once. The analyst requests an allocation against a season by naming the `markdown_plan_id` whose exit units need the space, a `total_units` figure, and the `expected_season_version` guard from §2.6. The plan must be `APPROVED` or `EXECUTING` and the season must be `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW`. The named plan is not required to belong to the addressed season: nothing compares the plan's `season_id` with the season in the path, and the reservations that result simply carry the addressed season's identifier alongside the named plan's, drawing on that season's budget.

The allocation runs as a strict sequence, and the ordering is what makes a re-allocation for the same plan safe. First it supersedes: every existing reservation carrying the same `markdown_plan_id` whose status is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `RELEASED`, returning `reserved_units` to each store's `available_clearance_units` and to the season's counters. That release is settled first in the arithmetic and is what every later step measures against, but it is not committed to the stored rows until the whole allocation is known to succeed: the status changes and the counter give-backs land only after both refusals below have been cleared, so a request that trips the chain ceiling or the all-or-nothing rule writes nothing whatsoever and leaves this plan's existing reservations exactly where they were, still `RESERVED` or still `COMMITTED`, with the stores and the season untouched. Only then is the chain ceiling tested, against the post-release figures: the season's `reserved_clearance_units` plus its `committed_clearance_units` plus the requested `total_units` may not exceed `clearance_space_units`, and a breach is refused with `ERR_SEASON_CLEARANCE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Next the candidate stores are drawn — every store in the chain, since a store belongs to a price zone and never to a season, whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose post-release availability is at least 1, which deliberately excludes stores in `REMODEL` or `CLOSING` even when their bays are empty. Post-release availability means the store's stored `available_clearance_units` plus whatever this plan's superseded reservations hand back to that particular store, and it is the figure the whole waterfall works in: a store sitting at 0 available becomes a candidate again on the strength of the space its own superseded reservation returns. Those candidates are ordered by that same post-release availability descending, then by `store_number` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, so the largest free bay is filled first and ties resolve deterministically; `store_number` is a string of 1 to 16 characters rather than a number, so ascending means an ordinary character-by-character comparison and `S10` therefore sorts ahead of `S9`. The waterfall then walks that order, and for each store in turn takes the smaller of that store's post-release availability and the units still unplaced, creating one reservation per filled store with that quantity in `reserved_units`, with `allocation_rank` counting 1, 2, 3 and so on in fill order, and with `status` `RESERVED`; the walk stops as soon as nothing remains to place. Allocation is all-or-nothing: if the ordered candidates cannot absorb the whole `total_units`, the request is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_SPACE_EXCEEDED` and not a single reservation is written. On success each filled store's `committed_clearance_units` grows by its `reserved_units` with `available_clearance_units` recomputed, the season's `reserved_clearance_units` grows by `total_units`, and the season's `version` is incremented. Every store the request touched is restated exactly once, whether it gave space back, took space, or did both, so a single allocation never writes one store twice; and because that store write obeys the change-only rule of §3.1, a store whose committed total lands back on the number it already held — the ordinary outcome when a re-allocation hands the same units to the same store — is not written at all and keeps its `updated_at` and `version`. The response is the array of reservations this allocation created, in `allocation_rank` order, and only those: the reservations it released are not returned.

A reservation's own lifecycle is short but consequential. It begins `RESERVED`, and it moves `RESERVED → COMMITTED` as a consequence of its item's `lifecycle_stage` reaching `CLEARANCE` or `EXIT_PENDING`, which shifts `reserved_units` out of the season's `reserved_clearance_units` and into its `committed_clearance_units`. The flip is driven by the stage change itself and not by the stage's standing value, so a reservation allocated against an item that already sits in `CLEARANCE` or `EXIT_PENDING` opens `RESERVED` and stays `RESERVED` until that item's stage next moves. It moves back `COMMITTED → RESERVED` when that item's stage reverts to `AGING` after a rolled-back execution, shifting the same units back the other way. Either state may be surrendered explicitly by a `PricingAnalyst` or a `MerchandiseDirector`, who may supply an optional `release_reason` of up to 1024 characters: `RESERVED → RELEASED` and `COMMITTED → RELEASED` both stamp `released_at` from the logical clock, as does every other route into `RELEASED` such as a plan supersession or a season close, both reduce the store's `committed_clearance_units` and restore its `available_clearance_units`, and each reduces whichever season counter the reservation was occupying — `reserved_clearance_units` for a reservation that was `RESERVED`, `committed_clearance_units` for one that was `COMMITTED`. A release increments both the reservation's and the season's `version`. `RELEASED` is terminal, so releasing an already-released reservation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An explicit release is not an exit-window operation and is accepted whatever the governing season's status, a `CLOSED` season being the single exception that refuses it as it refuses every other write; and because transition legality outranks season status in §2.7, an already-`RELEASED` reservation whose season has since closed reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED`.

Commitment is also what pulls the season into its exit window without anyone asking. Whenever a reservation entering `COMMITTED` lifts the season's `committed_clearance_units` to at least 60 percent of the season's `exit_units_target` — that is, to at least the `exit_units_target` multiplied by 60.00, divided by 100 and rounded half up to a whole unit — a season still in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → EXIT_WINDOW` on the spot. The comparison is inclusive, so landing exactly on the threshold fires the trigger. The automatic move behaves exactly like the director-driven one: every item of the season is reclassified, and those whose remaining whole weeks to the exit target are two or fewer land in `EXIT_PENDING`, which is what makes them eligible for disposition.

### 3.8. Vendor Allowance Negotiation and Funding Recognition

Vendors normally fund part of a markdown, and the negotiation over how much is modelled as an offer sitting on a table. A `PricingAnalyst` opens the negotiation for a `season_id`, a `vendor_code` and an `item_category_scope`, and puts the retailer's opening terms on the table as `current_share_pct` — the share of gross markdown the vendor would fund, between 0.01 and 100.00 percent — and `current_cap_cents`, the funding ceiling in cents, at least one cent. The submitted share is stored rounded half up to two decimals, so a value carrying more precision than that is accepted and normalised rather than rejected, and opening a negotiation is gated only on the named season not being `CLOSED` — a season still in `PLANNING` may already have terms on the table. The agreement opens in `PROPOSED` with `round_index` at 1, `last_offer_by_role` set to `PricingAnalyst` and `last_offer_by_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, while `agreed_share_pct`, `funding_cap_cents` and `remaining_cap_cents` all stay null and `recognized_total_cents` sits at 0 — nothing is committed until an offer is accepted.

Countering replaces the terms on the table. A counter carries `share_pct` and `cap_cents`, which overwrite `current_share_pct` and `current_cap_cents` — the share rounded half up to two decimals on the way in, exactly as at opening — increments `round_index` by 1, and records the countering party in `last_offer_by_role` and `last_offer_by_user_id`. The two directions are symmetric and both are cyclic: a `VendorRep` counter moves the agreement `PROPOSED → COUNTERED`, and a `PricingAnalyst` counter moves it `COUNTERED → PROPOSED`, so the table can pass back and forth. It cannot pass back and forth forever: a counter is permitted only while `round_index` is still below 4, and once four offers have been placed the next counter is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Countering an agreement that has already reached `AGREED`, `FUNDED` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Countering and accepting both consult the agreement's own season and are blocked by it only when it is `CLOSED`, so a negotiation runs on through `PLANNING`, `ACTIVE` and `EXIT_WINDOW` alike; and because transition legality outranks season status, an agreement already in `AGREED` whose season has closed reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED`, while the round ceiling sits below the season gate, so a counter that is out of rounds inside a `CLOSED` season reports `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED`.

Acceptance freezes the terms, and no party may accept its own offer. A `VendorRep` accepts an agreement in `PROPOSED`, moving it `PROPOSED → AGREED`; a `PricingAnalyst` accepts one in `COUNTERED`, moving it `COUNTERED → AGREED`. In both directions the acting role must differ from `last_offer_by_role`, and an attempt to accept one's own terms is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Those two directions are a consequence of the segregation rule rather than a pair of separately declared transitions: the transition-legality check on acceptance is role-blind and asks only that the agreement currently sits in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`, so `AGREED`, `FUNDED` and `EXPIRED` are the only states that yield `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `PricingAnalyst` reaching for an agreement still in `PROPOSED`, or a `VendorRep` reaching for one in `COUNTERED`, is therefore refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and not with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because in each case the offer standing on the table is that party's own. Acceptance copies `current_share_pct` into `agreed_share_pct` and `current_cap_cents` into `funding_cap_cents`, sets `remaining_cap_cents` equal to that cap, and leaves `recognized_total_cents` at 0.

A claim draws on an agreed allowance. It names the `allowance_agreement_id`, the `markdown_plan_id` whose applied markdown is being claimed and a `claimed_cents` amount of at least one cent, and it opens in `SUBMITTED` with `recognized_cents` at 0, `recognized_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` null. Filing does not inspect the agreement's status at all: a claim may be lodged against an agreement still `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`, or even one already `FUNDED` or `EXPIRED`, and it simply sits in `SUBMITTED` until an agreement in `AGREED` can carry it — the requirement that the agreement be `AGREED` bites at recognition only, where anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Eligibility is checked both when the claim is filed and again when it is recognised: the plan's `plan_type` must be `PERMANENT`, because promotional contra-revenue is never vendor-funded and a promotional plan is refused with `ERR_PROMO_NOT_CLAIMABLE`; the plan's status must be `EXECUTING` or `COMPLETED`, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_CLAIMABLE`; and the agreement's scope must match the claim's subject, meaning the agreement's `vendor_code` equals the plan item's `vendor_code` and the agreement's `item_category_scope` equals that item's `category`, else `ERR_ALLOWANCE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Scope is exactly those two comparisons: the agreement's own `season_id` is deliberately never measured against the plan's, so an agreement negotiated inside a different season is still a valid funding source, and the only season the claim endpoints consult is the plan's own — which must be `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW` for a claim to be filed and merely not `CLOSED` for one to be recognised or reversed.

Recognition belongs to the `Controller`, and its arithmetic has a pinned evaluation order because the dependency looks circular — the vendor share is a share of markdown, and net markdown is markdown less the vendor share. Net markdown is never an input. First the funding-blind gross is taken: the plan's `gross_markdown_cents`, which counts applied executions only. Second, the total the vendor could ever owe on this plan is the agreement's `agreed_share_pct` divided by 100 and multiplied by that gross, rounded half up to a whole cent. Third, the headroom still unclaimed on this plan is that eligible total less the plan's `vendor_funded_cents`. Fourth, the amount actually recognised is the smallest of three quantities in that order: the claim's `claimed_cents`, the plan headroom, and the agreement's `remaining_cap_cents`. Fifth, if that smallest quantity is below one cent there is nothing to recognise and the request is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. Only then is net markdown restated: the recognised amount is added to the plan's `vendor_funded_cents` and the plan's `net_markdown_cents` becomes gross less that funded total, while the agreement's `recognized_total_cents` grows by the same amount and its `remaining_cap_cents` becomes `funding_cap_cents` less `recognized_total_cents`. Where several claims sit on one agreement, they drain the cap in the `created_at` ascending order in which the controller recognises them, and the claim whose turn finds the cap empty is the one that is refused.

Recognition then cascades five hops. Hop one stores `recognized_cents` on the claim, moves it `SUBMITTED → RECOGNIZED` and stamps `recognized_by_user_id`. Hop two updates the agreement's `recognized_total_cents` and `remaining_cap_cents`. Hop three fires when that remaining cap has reached exactly 0: the agreement moves `AGREED → FUNDED`. Hop four follows from the agreement being funded — every other claim of that agreement still in `SUBMITTED` moves to `REJECTED` with its `rejection_reason` set to `CAP_EXHAUSTED` and its `recognized_cents` left at 0. Hop five follows from those rejections: each rejected sibling's plan recomputes its `vendor_funded_cents` and restates `net_markdown_cents` as gross less vendor-funded. That recomputation is arithmetically inert, because a claim still in `SUBMITTED` carried `recognized_cents` of 0 and so had never contributed anything to withdraw; and since a counter recomputation reached through a cascade writes only on a real change — the same discipline §3.1 states for the season's counters — a sibling's plan comes out of hop five entirely untouched, with its `updated_at` and `version` unmoved. A rejected sibling that happens to sit on the very plan the recognised claim belongs to earns no separate write either: that one plan is written exactly once for the whole request, and always, even in the case where its own recomputed counters did not move. Both the claim's and the agreement's `version` are incremented.

Reversal is the compensating action and belongs to the controller as well, carried out with a required `reversal_reason` of between 1 and 1024 characters. It applies to a claim in `RECOGNIZED` whose agreement is either `AGREED` or `FUNDED`, and moves the claim `RECOGNIZED → REVERSED`, and `REVERSED` is terminal. The agreement's `recognized_total_cents` falls by the reversed amount, its `remaining_cap_cents` is restored, and an agreement that had reached `FUNDED` returns to `AGREED`; the plan's `vendor_funded_cents` falls by the same amount with `net_markdown_cents` recomputed; and the claim's own `recognized_cents` is zeroed. The invariant of §2.9 holds exactly across the pair of operations. Note that reversal does not resurrect the sibling claims the exhausted cap rejected — `REJECTED` is terminal, and a new claim must be filed.

The other way an agreement ends is by expiry. When a season is closed, every agreement of that season still in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `EXPIRED`, and every claim still in `SUBMITTED` against one of those agreements moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`. `FUNDED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal: an expired agreement can neither be countered nor accepted, and no claim against it can be recognised.

### 3.9. Residual Disposition, the Exit Scorecard and Season Close

Whatever markdown cannot sell has to leave the building, and it leaves through one of three channels. A `PricingAnalyst` drafts a disposition order naming the `season_id`, the `item_id`, a `channel` of `SALVAGE`, `JOBBER` or `DONATION`, and the count of `units` committed to that channel. Salvage and jobber both recover cash per unit and therefore require `unit_recovery_cents` of at least one cent while forbidding `fmv_cents` entirely; a donation recovers a tax credit rather than cash, and therefore requires `fmv_cents` of at least one cent — the fair market value per unit — while forbidding any non-zero `unit_recovery_cents`. A payload that mixes the two shapes, or omits the money field its channel needs, is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`. Both money fields are optional at the payload level and both accept 0 there, so the one-cent floors are matters of channel coherence rather than of shape: a `SALVAGE` or `JOBBER` order carrying `unit_recovery_cents` of 0, and a `DONATION` order carrying `fmv_cents` of 0, are each `ERR_DISPOSITION_CHANNEL_MISMATCH` (422) at rule 33 and never `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) at rule 3. The prohibitions are not symmetric in strength either: a `DONATION` may state `unit_recovery_cents` explicitly so long as the value is exactly 0, whereas `SALVAGE` and `JOBBER` reject `fmv_cents` on its mere presence, 0 included. Drafting also requires the season to be `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW`, the item's `lifecycle_stage` to be `CLEARANCE` or `EXIT_PENDING`, and the requested `units` to be at least 1 and no more than the item's on-hand units less its `units_committed_disposition` at that moment; over-committing is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_UNITS`. A drafted order stamps `created_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, leaves `approved_by_user_id` null, opens in `DRAFT` with `settled_units` and `settled_recovery_cents` at 0, computes and stores `projected_recovery_cents`, adds its `units` to the item's `units_committed_disposition` — so the item's uncommitted pool shrinks immediately, even before anything ships — and increments the item's `version`.

Recovery arithmetic differs per channel and is applied once to the whole order, never per unit. For `SALVAGE`, recovery is simply the unit count multiplied by `unit_recovery_cents`. For `JOBBER`, the same product is reduced by an eight percent freight deduction — that is, multiplied by 92 and divided by 100 — and rounded half up to a whole cent. For `DONATION`, the tax credit is the unit count multiplied by the lesser of `fmv_cents` and the item's `unit_cost_cents`, because landed cost caps the donation basis, then multiplied by 21 and divided by 100 for the credit rate, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The same three formulas are used twice: once on the drafted `units` to produce `projected_recovery_cents`, and again on the actually shipped quantity to produce `settled_recovery_cents`.

Approval and settlement both belong to the `Controller`, and both are exit-window operations: the season must be in `EXIT_WINDOW`, and an attempt while it is merely `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_IN_EXIT_WINDOW`. Approval moves the order `DRAFT → APPROVED` and stamps `approved_by_user_id`, and the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `created_by_user_id`. Settlement carries a `settled_units` count of at least 1 and no more than the order's `units`, moves the order `APPROVED → SETTLED`, recomputes `settled_recovery_cents` from that quantity, and then reconciles the item: `units_disposed_total` grows by `settled_units`, `units_committed_disposition` falls by the order's full `units`, and the item's on-hand count is recomputed from its received total less units sold less units disposed. When that leaves the item with zero on-hand units its `lifecycle_stage` becomes `DISPOSED`, and its `markdown_state` moves to `SETTLED` only where the item had actually been marked — that is, only out of `PERM_MARKED` or `EXIT_PRICED`; an item disposed of while still `FULL_PRICE` or `PROMO_ACTIVE` reaches `DISPOSED` while keeping the `markdown_state` it already carried. Settlement then re-runs the completion test of §3.5 across every plan of that item, taken in `created_at` order, so a plan in `EXECUTING` whose item has just reached `markdown_state` `SETTLED` is carried `EXECUTING → COMPLETED` inside the same request, which restates the season's `projected_exit_units`. A `PricingAnalyst` or a `MerchandiseDirector` may instead cancel an order with an optional `cancel_reason`, from either `DRAFT → CANCELLED` or `APPROVED → CANCELLED`; cancellation subtracts the order's `units` from the item's `units_committed_disposition`, restoring the uncommitted pool to exactly its pre-draft value. Cancellation is the one operation of the four that is not an exit-window operation: it is accepted while the season is merely `ACTIVE` just as readily as while it is in `EXIT_WINDOW`, and only a `CLOSED` season turns it away. Its `cancel_reason` is optional and may run to 1024 characters. `SETTLED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal, and each of these four operations increments both the order's and the item's `version`.

The season's performance is settled by an exit scorecard, generated by a `MerchandiseDirector` or a `Controller` and immutable once written — it carries an `id`, a `created_at`, the `generated_at` instant taken from the logical clock and the `generated_by_user_id`, and neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`. The request names only a `review_type`: `PRELIMINARY`, which may be generated while the season is `ACTIVE` or `EXIT_WINDOW`, or `FINAL`, which requires the season to be in `EXIT_WINDOW`. A season may hold at most one review of each type, so a second review of the same type is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, and a `CLOSED` season accepts neither.

Both variants aggregate the same population — every item of the season together with that item's posted sales weeks — and stamp the same fields. `units_received_total`, `units_sold_total` and `units_disposed_total` are the sums of those per-item totals. `revenue_cents` is the sum, across every sales week of every item of the season, of that week's `units_sold` multiplied by its `ticket_price_cents`. `cogs_cents` is the sum, across the items, of each item's `units_sold_total` multiplied by its `unit_cost_cents`. `sell_through_pct` is the season's `units_sold_total` as a percentage of its `units_received_total`, rounded half up to two decimals; a season whose `units_received_total` is 0 — one holding no items at all, or none that has taken a receipt — is nonetheless reviewable, and rather than failing it reports a `sell_through_pct` of exactly 0.00, which makes its `sell_through_variance_pct` the plain negation of the season's `sell_through_target_pct`. `gross_margin_cents` is then revenue less cost of goods, less `markdown_reserve_cents`, less `promo_discount_cents`, plus `vendor_funding_cents`, plus `disposition_recovery_cents`. Two variances close the scorecard: `exit_units_variance` is the sum of units sold and units disposed less the season's `exit_units_target`, and may be negative when the season fell short; `sell_through_variance_pct` is the achieved `sell_through_pct` less the season's `sell_through_target_pct`, rounded half up to two decimals.

What separates the two variants is which rows are admitted into the four middle terms, and the difference is deliberate: the preliminary variant scores the season on projections, the final variant on realized facts only. In a `PRELIMINARY` review, `markdown_reserve_cents` sums, over the season's `PERMANENT` plans and over their executions that are either `SCHEDULED` or `APPLIED`, the frozen `line_markdown_cents` for each applied execution and, for each scheduled one, the difference between the item's `current_retail_cents` and the rung's `price_cents` multiplied by that execution's `units_in_scope`; `promo_discount_cents` sums the same per-unit markdown multiplied by `units_in_scope` over the `SCHEDULED` and `APPLIED` executions of `PROMOTIONAL` plans, on the assumption that every unit in scope will sell; `vendor_funding_cents` counts both the `claimed_cents` of claims still `SUBMITTED` and the `recognized_cents` of claims already `RECOGNIZED`; and `disposition_recovery_cents` counts the `projected_recovery_cents` of orders in `DRAFT` and `APPROVED` plus the `settled_recovery_cents` of orders already `SETTLED`. No plan-status filter narrows either of the two markdown terms: every plan of the season is walked whatever its own status, so the still-`SCHEDULED` executions that §3.5 deliberately strands on a `SUPERSEDED` plan are counted into a preliminary scorecard exactly like any other scheduled line. In a `FINAL` review, `markdown_reserve_cents` is the sum of `permanent_markdown_reserve_cents` across the season's items — equivalently the sum of `line_markdown_cents` over the applied executions of permanent plans; `promo_discount_cents` is the sum of the items' own `promo_discount_cents`, which counts only units actually sold on or after each execution's `effective_date`; `vendor_funding_cents` counts `recognized_cents` of `RECOGNIZED` claims only; and `disposition_recovery_cents` counts `settled_recovery_cents` of `SETTLED` orders only. Both variants read identical stored rows, and they return different `gross_margin_cents` whenever any execution is still `SCHEDULED`, any claim still `SUBMITTED`, or any disposition order not yet `SETTLED`.

The final review is the season's gate. A director may only close a season out of `EXIT_WINDOW`, only once a `FINAL` review exists for it, and only once no disposition order of the season remains in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` — unsettled commitments may not be stranded by a close. Closing then expires the open allowance agreements, rejects the claims those agreements were carrying, and releases every reservation the season still held, as described in §3.1 and §3.8. After that, the season is read-only: any further write reports `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED`.
