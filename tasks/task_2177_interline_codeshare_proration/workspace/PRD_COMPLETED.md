# Interline & Codeshare Proration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When two airlines carry one passenger on one ticket, only one of them collected the money. The validating carrier sells a journey — say Lisbon to Osaka via Frankfurt — takes the whole fare into its own bank account, and then owes its partners whatever their sectors were worth. Deciding what those sectors were worth is the business of revenue proration, and it is not a division problem. Some partners have negotiated a *special prorate agreement* that lifts a flat sum or a fixed percentage off the top of the fare before anyone else is paid; the rest share whatever remains, weighted by distance flown and by cabin. The answer changes again once the passenger actually travels, because unflown sectors drop out of the calculation entirely. Meanwhile the money itself has to move through an interline clearing house that charges a handling fee, converts everything into a single settlement currency at a rate nobody controls, and refuses late submissions without a penalty.

This document specifies the service that runs that pipeline end to end. It manages the bilateral agreement book and its sector-level rate cards; it issues interline tickets and their flight coupons; it executes provisional and final proration runs whose per-cent split must reconcile to the fare exactly; it builds clearing-house invoices, converts them twice at two different FX anchors and books the variance; it drives the rejection-memo negotiation cycle through counter-billing, re-rejection, withdrawal and arbitration, with an aging clock that never resets; it accrues frequent-flyer mileage cost to the operating partner at a tiered rate and trues that estimate up at period close; and it supports statistical undercharge audits that extrapolate a sampled shortfall to a population and discount the result by a confidence haircut.

Three properties govern every design decision below. First, **conservation**: the sum of every coupon's allocation plus the ticket's residual slack always equals the fare, and the sum of every carrier's net clearing-house position across the whole membership is always zero. Second, **traceability**: no figure is ever recomputed silently — allocations, conversions and accruals are stamped onto the rows that carry them, and reversals are explicit compensating operations rather than deletions. Third, **determinism**: every ranking, every tie-break, every rounding step and every rejection precedence is pinned, so that two runs over the same data produce byte-identical results. The whole service is mounted under `/interline`, with one out-of-band test-driving hook described in §2.5.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — including reads — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the desk it is acting from. If either header is missing or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is not one of the four role names declared below, or is a declared role that the requested operation does not list among its authorized roles, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

There are exactly four roles, spelled exactly as follows:

- **`ClearinghouseOperator`** — the IATA-clearing-house side. It owns the settlement calendar, the FX quote table, agreement activation and suspension, carrier membership standing, arbitration rulings and the logical clock. It never acts for a carrier's own account. When `X-User-Role` is `ClearinghouseOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may log it but never compares it to any stored row's `id`, and no ownership check is applied to an operator's request.
- **`RevenueAccountant`** — the revenue-accounting desk of one member carrier. It drafts agreements and their sector factors, issues tickets, appends coupons, flies and voids them, runs and voids prorations, and drafts outbound invoices.
- **`BillingAgent`** — the interline billing desk of one member carrier. It submits and recalls its own invoices and drives every side of the rejection-memo cycle that segregation of duties permits it, plus the accept-or-deny decision on an incoming audit recovery claim.
- **`InterlineAuditor`** — the revenue-audit desk of one member carrier. It draws undercharge samples against partner carriers, records findings, and closes samples into recovery claims. It can never decide a claim, not even one raised by a different desk of its own carrier.

For `RevenueAccountant`, `BillingAgent` and `InterlineAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Carrier`; ownership checks on any resource compare the relevant carrier-valued field of that resource to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: a `RevenueAccountant` must match the `validating_carrier_id` of the ticket it is acting on, and — when drafting an agreement or an invoice — the `issuing_carrier_id` or `billing_carrier_id` named in the request body, and — when attaching a sector factor — the `issuing_carrier_id` of the agreement named in the path. A `BillingAgent` must match `billing_carrier_id` when submitting or recalling an invoice, countering a memo or accepting a memo; must match `billed_carrier_id` when raising, re-rejecting or withdrawing a memo; must match either of those two carriers when escalating a memo to arbitration; and must match `audited_carrier_id` when deciding an audit claim. An `InterlineAuditor` must match the sample's `auditing_carrier_id`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403).

This ownership rule is what implements segregation of duties: the billed carrier that raised a memo can never counter or accept it, the billing carrier that must pay a memo can never withdraw or re-reject it, and the auditing carrier can never decide its own recovery claim. Escalation to arbitration is the single memo action either side may take, and only the `ClearinghouseOperator` may then rule on it. Ownership is not evaluated on carrier registration, settlement-period creation, FX-quote publication, agreement activation or suspension, arbitration, or the clock hook, because those are operator-only capabilities with no carrier account behind them. Every read (list or single-resource) endpoint in this service is open to all four roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the server's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning operation is paginated by two optional query-string arguments. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being clamped; so is a `limit` or `offset` that does not parse as an integer at all, and so is a filter query parameter whose value is not a declared member of the enumeration it filters on. An identifier-valued filter naming no stored row is not an error — it simply matches nothing and returns an empty page.

Collections are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. The server realises that ordering as a monotonic per-row insertion sequence rather than by comparing `created_at` values, so a collection is always in insertion order and stays stably ascending even if the logical clock is rewound between two insertions. Where a listing is described elsewhere in this document as "newest last", that is the same rule stated from the other end. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoed back as applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before the window was applied, and `has_more` set true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is still less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Row Versions

Every stored row is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID at creation time. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat them as opaque. Every row also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted. Both `created_at` and the `updated_at` described next are stamped from the server's real wall clock, not from the logical clock of §2.5 — they are deliberately absent from the list of clock-stamped fields there, and moving the logical clock never changes them.

Most rows additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed to the moment of the mutation whenever `version` advances. `Carrier`, `ProrateAgreement`, `ProrateFactor`, `SettlementPeriod`, `Ticket`, `Coupon`, `BillingInvoice`, `RejectionMemo` and `AuditSample` all behave this way.

Two record types are append-only ledger entries and are never mutated after they are written: `ExchangeRate` and `ProrationRun`. These two carry `id` and `created_at` only — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither field should appear in their representations.

Version increments are not incidental bookkeeping. Because two operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency (§2.8), the exact set of writes that bump a row's `version` is part of the contract, and each workflow narrative in §3 states which rows it touches. One deliberate asymmetry is worth flagging up front: marking a coupon flown bumps that `Coupon`'s `version` and the operating `Carrier`'s `version`, but explicitly does **not** touch the owning `Ticket`'s `version`, whereas appending a coupon or voiding one does.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

This service never reads wall-clock time for business purposes. It maintains a single logical UTC clock that stamps `flown_at`, `submitted_at`, `cleared_at`, `raised_at`, `resolved_at`, `closed_at` and `settled_at`, and that drives every window, aging, deadline and time-bar rule described below. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time.

A `ClearinghouseOperator` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the test-driving hook, which is mounted outside the `/interline` tree. The clock may be advanced or rewound freely; doing so mutates no entity and stamps nothing retroactively — already-written timestamps stay where they are. The hook responds 200 with a `data` object carrying that single `current_utc` field; it persists no row and returns no entity.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integers in the minor units of their stated currency; there are no decimal money fields anywhere in this API. All rates, weights, fees, ratios and haircuts are integers in basis points, where 10000 basis points is one whole unit — so a handling fee of 75 basis points is three quarters of one per cent, and a materiality threshold of 200 basis points is two per cent. Foreign-exchange quotes are integers scaled by one million: a rate of 1.085 is stored as `rate_scaled` of 1085000, and converting an amount means multiplying by the scaled rate and dividing by 1000000.

Wherever a division occurs the result is rounded **half up** to a whole integer — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Rounding is applied at each division as it is reached, never deferred: where a calculation below rounds per row and then sums, summing the exact rational values and rounding once at the end produces a different and incorrect answer. The only non-half-up rounding in the system is memo aging, which floors a whole-second difference into whole days.

Four currencies are recognised throughout: `USD`, `EUR`, `GBP` and `JPY`. Any field described as a currency accepts exactly one of those four.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence — the Validation Ladder

When a request violates more than one rule, the response is decided by the **first** rule that fails in the ordering below, and no side effect of the request has been applied when it does. This ladder is global and absolute: a later check never masks an earlier one, and a request that would fail step 30 but also fails step 4 is answered as step 4. Implementations must evaluate in this sequence.

1. **Headers.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every operation.
2. **Role.** `X-User-Role` is one of the four declared roles and that role is authorized for the requested operation — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every operation.
3. **Request shape.** Every required input field is present; every enum-typed field carries a declared member; every integer money, mileage and basis-point field is an integer inside its declared bounds; ISO 8601 datetime fields and `YYYY-MM-DD` date fields parse; `effective_to` is on or after `effective_from`; `period_end` is strictly after `period_start`; `from_currency` differs from `to_currency`; `limit` is between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is at least 0. The declared integer bounds enforced at this step are: `credit_limit_minor`, `correct_amount_minor` and `counter_amount_minor` at least 0; `total_fare_minor`, `tpm_miles`, `rate_scaled`, `flat_amount_minor`, `coupon_number` and a memo's `amount_minor` at least 1; `factor_bp` between 1 and 100000; `percent_bp` between 1 and 10000; `sample_coupon_ids` between 1 and 50 entries with no duplicates, while `coupon_ids` carries no length bound at all and may legitimately be empty. String-typed fields are checked only for being non-empty strings: `iata_code`, `name`, `period_code`, `ticket_number`, `counter_reason` and the `origin` and `destination` station codes carry no length or character-set validation, so the shapes those fields are described with in §3 are business conventions the server does not police. A request body that is present but is not a JSON object is refused here too. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every operation.
4. **Path identifiers.** Every `id` embedded in the request path resolves to a stored row, checked strictly left to right, so an outer segment's failure is reported before an inner segment is even looked at. The code names the missing type: `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUPON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMO_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. Applies to every operation carrying a path identifier.
5. **Body references.** Every foreign-key identifier supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, checked in the order the fields are declared in that operation's input list; for the `coupon_ids` and `sample_coupon_ids` arrays, in the order the elements were supplied. The code again names the missing type — `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUPON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to agreement drafting, ticket issue, coupon append, invoice drafting, memo raise, audit-sample draw and audit finding recording.
6. **Carrier ownership.** For the three carrier-backed roles, `X-User-ID` equals the carrier that owns the operation, as detailed in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_CARRIER_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (403). Applies to every write operation except carrier registration, settlement-period creation, FX-quote publication, agreement activation, agreement suspension, memo arbitration and the clock hook.
7. **Period open.** The referenced settlement period is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to invoice drafting, invoice submission, invoice recall and settlement-period close.
8. **Period closed.** For settlement, the period is `CLOSED`; for an audit-sample draw, the named period is `CLOSED` or `SETTLED` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (409). Applies to settlement-period settle and audit-sample draw.
9. **Invoice state.** Submission requires `DRAFT`; recall requires `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to invoice submission and invoice recall.
10. **Invoice cleared.** The invoice a memo is raised against is `CLEARED` or `PARTIALLY_REJECTED` — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_CLEARED` (409). Applies to memo raise.
11. **Memo state.** Countering and accepting require `RAISED`; re-rejecting requires `COUNTERED`; withdrawing and escalating require `RAISED` or `COUNTERED`; arbitrating requires `ARBITRATION` — otherwise `ERR_MEMO_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to memo counter, re-reject, accept, withdraw, escalate and arbitrate.
12. **Coupon state.** The coupon is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_COUPON_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to coupon fly and coupon void.
13. **Ticket finally prorated.** For every coupon named in `coupon_ids`, the owning ticket's `proration_status` is `FINAL` — otherwise `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FINAL_PRORATED` (409). Applies to invoice drafting.
14. **Agreement state.** Activation requires `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED`; suspension requires `ACTIVE`; attaching a sector factor requires `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to agreement activation, agreement suspension and factor attachment.
15. **Sample state.** Recording a finding requires `DRAWN` or `IN_REVIEW`; closing requires `IN_REVIEW`; a claim decision requires `CLAIM_RAISED` — otherwise `ERR_SAMPLE_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to audit finding recording, audit-sample close and audit claim decision.
16. **Ticket prorated.** The ticket's `proration_status` is `PROVISIONAL`, `FINAL` or `REPRORATION_REQUIRED` — otherwise `ERR_TICKET_NOT_PRORATED` (409). Applies to proration voiding.
17. **Carrier state.** Suspension requires the carrier to be `ACTIVE`; reinstatement requires it to be `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_CARRIER_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to carrier suspend and carrier reinstate.
18. **Acting carrier in good standing.** The carrier acting for its own account is `ACTIVE` — the `validating_carrier_id` on ticket issue and coupon append, the `issuing_carrier_id` on agreement drafting, the `billing_carrier_id` on invoice drafting and invoice submission, the `billed_carrier_id` on memo raise and memo re-reject, and — uniquely, since the operator acts for no carrier there — both the `issuing_carrier_id` and the `partner_carrier_id` on agreement activation. Otherwise `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED` (409).
19. **Operating carrier in good standing.** The coupon's `operating_carrier_id` names an `ACTIVE` carrier — otherwise `ERR_OPERATING_CARRIER_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to coupon fly.
20. **Agreement window overlap.** Closed-closed interval rule, detailed in §3.2 — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to agreement activation.
21. **Period window overlap.** Half-open interval rule, detailed in §3.3 — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to settlement-period creation.
22. **Memo window.** The current logical clock is at most 45 whole days after the invoice's `cleared_at` — otherwise `ERR_MEMO_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to memo raise and memo re-reject.
23. **Arbitration eligibility.** The memo's `stage` is 3, or its `age_days` is at least 90 — otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409). Applies to memo escalation.
24. **Audit time bar.** No coupon in the derived audit population was flown earlier than 730 days before the current logical clock — otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_TIME_BARRED` (409). Applies to audit-sample draw.
25. **Coupon on invoice.** The contested `coupon_id` has a `billing_invoice_id` equal to the `invoice_id` named in the request (for a re-rejection, the memo's own invoice) — otherwise `ERR_COUPON_NOT_ON_INVOICE` (422). Applies to memo raise.
26. **Coupon carrier match.** For every coupon in `coupon_ids`, the coupon's `operating_carrier_id` equals the invoice's `billing_carrier_id` and the owning ticket's `validating_carrier_id` equals the invoice's `billed_carrier_id` — otherwise `ERR_COUPON_CARRIER_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to invoice drafting.
27. **Currency agreement.** Every attached coupon's ticket `sale_currency` equals the invoice's `billing_currency` on an invoice draft; the selected agreement's `settlement_currency` equals the ticket's `sale_currency` during a final proration run; every coupon named in `sample_coupon_ids` belongs to a ticket whose `sale_currency` equals the sample's `claim_currency` on an audit draw. Otherwise `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to invoice drafting, proration runs and audit-sample draw.
28. **Coupon in sample.** The `coupon_id` a finding is recorded against is a member of the sample's `sample_coupon_ids` — otherwise `ERR_COUPON_NOT_IN_SAMPLE` (422). Applies to audit finding recording.
29. **Ticket number uniqueness.** `ticket_number` is not already used by another ticket — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TICKET_NUMBER` (409). Applies to ticket issue.
30. **Coupon sequence.** `coupon_number` equals the ticket's current `coupon_count` plus 1 — otherwise `ERR_COUPON_SEQUENCE_INVALID` (422). Applies to coupon append.
31. **Sector uniqueness.** No other factor of the same agreement already carries the identical combination of `origin`, `destination` and `class_of_service` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACTOR_SECTOR` (409). Applies to factor attachment.
32. **Factor value field.** Exactly the one value field the parent agreement's `agreement_type` calls for is supplied, and the other two are absent — otherwise `ERR_FACTOR_FIELD_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to factor attachment.
33. **FX quote uniqueness.** No other quote already carries the identical combination of `from_currency`, `to_currency` and `effective_date` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXCHANGE_RATE` (409). Applies to FX-quote publication.
34. **Non-empty run input.** The requested run variant's coupon input set is non-empty — otherwise `ERR_NO_PRORATABLE_COUPONS` (422). Applies to proration runs.
35. **Negotiation rounds remaining.** The memo's `stage` is less than 3 — otherwise `ERR_MEMO_STAGE_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to memo re-reject.
36. **Coupons unbilled.** Every coupon in `coupon_ids` has a null `billing_invoice_id` and a `coupon_status` of `FLOWN` or `ADJUSTED` — otherwise `ERR_COUPON_ALREADY_INVOICED` (409). Applies to invoice drafting.
37. **Non-empty audit population.** The derived population contains at least one coupon — otherwise `ERR_EMPTY_AUDIT_POPULATION` (422). Applies to audit-sample draw.
38. **Sample within population.** Every identifier in `sample_coupon_ids` is a member of the derived population — otherwise `ERR_COUPON_NOT_IN_POPULATION` (422). Applies to audit-sample draw.
39. **Special-prorate headroom.** The off-the-top special-prorate amounts sum to no more than the ticket's `total_fare_minor` — otherwise `ERR_SPA_OVERALLOCATION` (422). Applies to proration runs.
40. **Memo within coupon.** The contested `amount_minor` is no larger than the coupon's current `allocated_amount_minor` — otherwise `ERR_MEMO_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_COUPON` (422). Applies to memo raise and memo re-reject.
41. **Memo within invoice.** The invoice's `disputed_amount_minor` plus its `rejected_amount_minor` plus the requested `amount_minor` is no larger than its `total_claim_minor`; on a re-rejection the memo's own previous `amount_minor` is excluded from the running disputed figure before the comparison. Otherwise `ERR_MEMO_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE` (422). Applies to memo raise and memo re-reject.
42. **FX quote available.** A quote exists for the ordered currency pair with an `effective_date` at or before the anchor instant, unless the two currencies are equal — otherwise `ERR_NO_EXCHANGE_RATE` (422). This necessarily precedes the credit check below, which needs the converted figure. Applies to invoice submission, settlement-period close and audit-sample close.
43. **Credit limit.** The billed carrier's `pending_payable_minor` plus `settled_payable_minor` plus the invoice's `provisional_settlement_minor`, less that carrier's `pending_receivable_minor` and `settled_receivable_minor`, is no larger than its `credit_limit_minor` — otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to invoice submission.
44. **Invoice version.** `expected_invoice_version` equals the invoice's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_INVOICE_VERSION` (412). Applies to memo raise, memo re-reject, memo accept and memo withdraw.
45. **Ticket version.** `expected_ticket_version` equals the ticket's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_TICKET_VERSION` (412). Applies to proration runs and proration voiding.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve to be spelled out, because they are easy to get backwards. Existence beats permission-of-account: a request naming a carrier that does not exist is a 404 before it can be a 403 for acting on someone else's carrier, but ownership (step 6) beats every state and business check that follows it. And the optimistic-concurrency checks sit at the very bottom: a stale `expected_invoice_version` on a memo whose amount also exceeds the coupon's allocation is answered as `ERR_MEMO_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_COUPON`, not as a stale-version rejection.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Invoices and Tickets

Two write paths in this service operate against a parent row whose state the client must have observed before it may write. Both are guarded by an explicit expected-version field that is a **required** part of the request body, not an optional hint.

A `POST /interline/rejection-memos` request must carry `expected_invoice_version` holding the `version` of the target `BillingInvoice` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the invoice's current `version`, the memo is not created and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INVOICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because raising a second memo against the same invoice is entirely legitimate business — each memo contests a different coupon and consumes part of the remaining disputable headroom — so a writer holding an out-of-date view of `disputed_amount_minor` would otherwise book a memo that the headroom no longer supports.

A `POST /interline/tickets/{id}/prorate` request must carry `expected_ticket_version` holding the `version` of the target `Ticket` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the ticket's current `version`, no run is created and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TICKET_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new run legitimately supersedes the previous allocation, so a writer whose view of the coupon set predates a void or an adjustment would silently overwrite an allocation it never saw.

Beyond those two creation paths, `expected_invoice_version` is also required — carrying the same meaning and yielding the same `ERR_STALE_INVOICE_VERSION` rejection — on memo re-rejection, memo acceptance and memo withdrawal, and `expected_ticket_version` is likewise required on proration voiding with the same `ERR_STALE_TICKET_VERSION` rejection. Countering a memo, escalating one and arbitrating one take no version field. No other operation in this service accepts an expected-version field of any kind; supplying one elsewhere is simply ignored, and omitting one where it is required is a malformed request under step 3 of the ladder.

### 2.9. Cascades, Version Bumps and Nested Reads

Several operations in this service are not single-row writes but ordered cascades that walk from one entity to the next, each hop firing *because* the previous hop changed something. Two such cascades run five hops deep: closing a settlement period, and resolving a rejection memo in the rejecting carrier's favour. The hops are enumerated in §3.7 and §3.8 respectively, and every hop that mutates a row increments that row's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`, exactly as a direct write would. Cascades are executed to completion within the triggering request; there is no deferred or eventual settling. Where a cascade iterates over a set of rows, it processes them in ascending `created_at`.

Two nested read paths exist, and both are reverse-reachability listings rather than ownership trees: the sector factors of one agreement, and the proration runs of one ticket. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so a request naming a non-existent agreement is answered `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` without the factors ever being examined. Every other resource in this service is a peer addressed directly under `/interline` by its own collection and `id`; the relationships between them live in carrier-valued and row-valued reference fields, not in URL nesting.

### 2.10. Clearing-House Balances and the Zero-Sum Invariant

Each `Carrier` carries four running balances, all expressed in the settlement currency's minor units, all defaulting to 0 at registration. `pending_receivable_minor` is the sum of `provisional_settlement_minor` over the invoices this carrier has billed out that are currently `SUBMITTED`; `pending_payable_minor` is the same sum over the invoices billed *to* this carrier that are currently `SUBMITTED`. `settled_receivable_minor` and `settled_payable_minor` are the cleared balances: both grow at period close, and both shrink when a rejection memo is accepted or upheld. Alongside them, `net_position_minor` is defined as `pending_receivable_minor` plus `settled_receivable_minor`, less `pending_payable_minor` and less `settled_payable_minor`; a positive value means the clearing house owes this carrier money.

`net_position_minor` is recomputed on every balance write, which happens in exactly six places: invoice submission, invoice recall, settlement-period close, memo acceptance, arbitration ruled in the rejecting carrier's favour, and acceptance of an audit recovery claim. After each of those six, the clearing-house invariant must hold: **summed across every carrier row in the system, without any status filter, `net_position_minor` is exactly zero.** Suspended carriers still carry balances and still count toward the sum. The invariant holds because each of the six operations moves one identical figure onto exactly one carrier's receivable side and one carrier's payable side. No rounding is applied at this step — the figures being summed were already rounded when they were converted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Membership: Admitting, Suspending and Reinstating Carriers

Nothing in this system happens outside a membership. A `ClearinghouseOperator` admits a partner airline by registering it with its two-character IATA designator in `iata_code` — exactly two characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits, and deliberately not required to be unique, because historical designators are reused; that two-character shape is a business convention the server does not enforce, so any non-empty string is accepted — together with its legal `name`, its home accounting currency in `base_currency`, and the `credit_limit_minor` beyond which the clearing house will not let its net payable position grow. The credit limit is a non-negative integer. A newly registered carrier opens in `ACTIVE` standing, with all four balances, `net_position_minor`, `period_accrued_miles`, `period_ffp_cost_estimate_minor`, `period_ffp_cost_trued_up_minor`, `ffp_true_up_delta_minor` and `open_reproration_count` at zero, and `audit_flag` false. Carriers may be listed and filtered by `status`.

A carrier's `status` moves between exactly two values, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, and the cycle may be walked any number of times. Suspension is the clearing house's sanction against a defaulting or non-compliant member: it requires the carrier to be `ACTIVE`, sets `status` to `SUSPENDED`, bumps the carrier's `version`, and — this is the part implementers most often miss — sweeps out into the agreement book, moving *every* prorate agreement currently `ACTIVE` that names this carrier on either side, whether as `issuing_carrier_id` or as `partner_carrier_id`, into `SUSPENDED` with a `suspension_reason` of `CARRIER_SUSPENDED`, bumping each agreement's `version` in turn.

Reinstatement is deliberately asymmetric. Returning a `SUSPENDED` carrier to `ACTIVE` bumps its `version` and does nothing else: agreements that were swept out with `suspension_reason` of `CARRIER_SUSPENDED` are **not** reactivated by it. Each one must be brought back into force explicitly, and each one re-runs the full activation checks when it is. The business reason is that a carrier's return to good standing says nothing about whether its bilateral terms are still commercially current.

Suspension has teeth beyond the agreement sweep. A suspended carrier may not issue tickets, may not have coupons appended to its tickets, may not draft an agreement as the issuing side, may not draft or submit an invoice as the billing side, and may not raise or re-reject a rejection memo as the billed side; each of those is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`. Separately, a coupon may not be marked flown while the carrier that actually operates it is suspended, which is refused with the distinct code `ERR_OPERATING_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`. Suspended carriers do, however, keep their balances and continue to be read, listed and counted in the zero-sum invariant.

### 3.2. Bilateral Prorate Agreements and Their Sector Rate Cards

A `RevenueAccountant` drafts a bilateral agreement between its own carrier, named as `issuing_carrier_id`, and a partner named as `partner_carrier_id`. The pair is ordered and directional: an agreement covers coupons that *its* issuing carrier ticketed and *its* partner carrier flew, never the reverse. The drafter states an `agreement_type` of `STRAIGHT_RATE`, `SPA_FLAT` or `SPA_PERCENT`, the `settlement_currency` its amounts are denominated in, and a closed effective window from `effective_from` through `effective_to`, both `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates, with the end date on or after the start date. A fresh draft opens in `DRAFT` status with `suspension_reason` of `NONE` and `factor_count` of zero. Agreements may be listed filtered by `issuing_carrier_id`, by `partner_carrier_id` or by `status`.

The agreement's rate card is a list of sector-level factors, each attached individually while the agreement is still `DRAFT` — once an agreement has been activated its card is frozen, and any attempt to add to it is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. A factor names an `origin` and a `destination`, each either exactly three uppercase letters or the single-character wildcard `*` — a convention the server does not validate, though only the literal `*` is read as a wildcard — and a `class_of_service` of `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM`, `BUSINESS` or `FIRST`, which is never wildcarded. Each factor records the `agreement_id` of the card it belongs to. Exactly one of three value fields must accompany it, chosen by the parent's `agreement_type`: a `factor_bp` between 1 and 100000 inclusive for a `STRAIGHT_RATE` card, a strictly positive `flat_amount_minor` for an `SPA_FLAT` card, or a `percent_bp` between 1 and 10000 inclusive for an `SPA_PERCENT` card. The other two must be omitted entirely; supplying the wrong one, supplying more than one, or omitting all three, is refused with `ERR_FACTOR_FIELD_MISMATCH`. The two unused value fields still appear in the stored factor's representation, each carrying `null`. No two factors of the same agreement may share an identical `origin`, `destination` and `class_of_service` combination, on pain of `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACTOR_SECTOR`. Attaching a factor bumps the parent agreement's `factor_count` and its `version`.

Each factor also carries a computed `specificity_score`, written at attachment time and used later as the sole ranking key when several factors could match one coupon. It counts how many of the two station fields are concrete rather than wildcarded: 2 when neither `origin` nor `destination` is `*`, 1 when exactly one of them is, and 0 when both are.

Segregation of duties splits drafting from commissioning. The `RevenueAccountant` that wrote the agreement can never bring it into force; only a `ClearinghouseOperator` may. Activation is permitted from `DRAFT` and from `SUSPENDED` alike — the `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` pair is a genuine cycle an agreement may traverse any number of times — and it requires three things: the agreement is in one of those two statuses, both the carrier named as `issuing_carrier_id` and the carrier named as `partner_carrier_id` are currently `ACTIVE`, and the effective window does not collide with any other agreement for the same ordered carrier pair. A carrier on either side that is not `ACTIVE` refuses the activation with `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED` (409), tested after the agreement's own status check and before the window check.

The collision test uses a **closed-closed** reading of the window, and this is the strictest interval rule in the system. Considering every other agreement for the same ordered pair whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — drafts are invisible to the test — a collision exists when the candidate's `effective_from` is on or before the other's `effective_to` **and** the other's `effective_from` is on or before the candidate's `effective_to`. Sharing a single endpoint date is therefore a collision: an agreement running through the 30th and one starting on the 30th conflict, and the two windows must be separated by at least one clear calendar day. A collision is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A successful activation sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, resets `suspension_reason` to `NONE` and bumps `version`.

Operator suspension of an `ACTIVE` agreement is the mirror image: `status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, `suspension_reason` becomes `OPERATOR_SUSPENDED` — distinct from the `CARRIER_SUSPENDED` value written by the membership sweep, so the clearing house can tell a commercial suspension from a collateral one — and `version` bumps. The operational consequence is immediate: because the factor selector only ever considers agreements whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, coupons prorated after this point silently fall back to the straight-rate default rather than failing.

**Selecting the governing factor for a coupon.** Before any money is moved, each candidate coupon in a proration run is matched against the agreement book. This is a pure lookup with no side effects, and it returns at most one agreement-and-factor pair. The eligible candidates are every factor whose parent agreement satisfies all four of these conditions — its `issuing_carrier_id` is the ticket's `validating_carrier_id`; its `partner_carrier_id` is the coupon's `operating_carrier_id`; its `status` is `ACTIVE`, so `DRAFT` and `SUSPENDED` agreements are invisible; and the calendar date of the coupon's `flown_at` falls on or after `effective_from` and on or before `effective_to` — and whose own fields satisfy all three of these — its `class_of_service` equals the coupon's `class_of_service`; its `origin` is either the coupon's `origin` or the wildcard `*`; and its `destination` is either the coupon's `destination` or the wildcard `*`. The surviving candidates are ranked by `specificity_score` from highest to lowest, so a fully concrete sector beats a half-wildcarded one and a half-wildcarded one beats a fully wildcarded one. When two candidates tie on `specificity_score`, the one with the earlier `created_at` wins; the row `id` is never consulted as a tie-break.

An empty candidate set is not an error. It means the coupon settles on the straight-rate default: a weight multiplier of 10000 basis points, no off-the-top amount, and an `allocation_basis` of `STRAIGHT_RATE`. What *is* an error is a currency disagreement on a pair that did match — if the selected agreement's `settlement_currency` differs from the ticket's `sale_currency`, the entire run aborts with `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). It does not quietly fall back to the default.

### 3.3. The Clearing-House Calendar and the FX Quote Table

A `ClearinghouseOperator` opens a settlement period with a `period_code` label such as "2026-08-P1", an inclusive `period_start` instant, an exclusive `period_end` instant strictly after it, a `submission_deadline` after which a submitted invoice attracts a penalty, a `rate_fixing_date` naming the instant whose FX quotes will be used at close, and optionally a `settlement_currency` in which every clearing-house balance for the period is expressed, defaulting to `USD`. The period opens in `OPEN` status with `cleared_invoice_count` at zero and both `closed_at` and `settled_at` null.

Periods use a **half-open** window rule, and the contrast with agreements is deliberate. Against every other period whose `status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, a collision exists when the candidate's `period_start` is strictly before the other's `period_end` **and** the other's `period_start` is strictly before the candidate's `period_end`. Sharing a boundary instant is therefore legal — back-to-back periods where one ends exactly where the next begins are the normal clearing-house calendar, not a conflict. A genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

The FX table is a flat, append-only ledger of quotes. Each quote names an ordered pair of `from_currency` and `to_currency`, which must differ, an `effective_date` instant from which it is the prevailing rate, and a strictly positive `rate_scaled` holding the rate multiplied by one million. No two quotes may share the same `from_currency`, `to_currency` and `effective_date` combination; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXCHANGE_RATE`.

Converting an amount always follows the same three-step selection. When the source and target currencies are equal, the scaled rate is taken as exactly 1000000 and no lookup happens at all. Otherwise the eligible quotes are those whose `from_currency` and `to_currency` match the requested ordered pair and whose `effective_date` is at or before the anchor instant; among them the one with the latest `effective_date` wins, with the earlier `created_at` breaking a tie that the uniqueness rule above makes unreachable in practice. If no quote is eligible, the operation that needed the conversion fails with `ERR_NO_EXCHANGE_RATE` (HTTP 422). The converted figure is the amount multiplied by the selected scaled rate, divided by one million, rounded half up to a whole minor unit; the rate itself is never rounded.

Two different anchors are used, and the gap between them is the point of the whole mechanism. At invoice submission the anchor is the current logical clock, giving the *provisional* conversion that touches only the pending balances. At period close, and again when an audit sample is closed, the anchor is the period's `rate_fixing_date`, giving the *fixed* conversion that decides what is actually settled. Because a quote may have landed between the two instants, the two conversions of the same claim routinely differ, and that difference is booked and reported — it can even push a carrier past its credit limit at close although the submit-time credit check passed cleanly.

### 3.4. Issuing Tickets, Appending Coupons, and Flying Them

A `RevenueAccountant` issues an interline ticket for its own carrier by supplying the `ticket_number` (thirteen characters by convention, though the server validates only that it is a non-empty string), the `validating_carrier_id` of the issuing airline, the `sale_currency` the fare was collected in, a strictly positive `total_fare_minor` holding the prorate-able fare in that currency's minor units, and the `issue_date` instant of sale. Ticket numbers are globally unique; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TICKET_NUMBER`. A new ticket opens with `coupon_count` at zero, `last_run_type` of `NONE`, and `unallocated_residual_minor` equal to the whole `total_fare_minor` — the fare starts entirely unallocated and stays conserved from there.

Tickets carry **two independent state axes** which never read each other. The first, `proration_status`, moves among `UNPRORATED`, `PROVISIONAL`, `FINAL` and `REPRORATION_REQUIRED`, and starts at `UNPRORATED`. The second, `billing_status`, moves among `UNBILLED`, `BILLED`, `SETTLED` and `DISPUTED`, and starts at `UNBILLED`. Any combination is reachable — a ticket may sit at `FINAL` and `DISPUTED`, at `REPRORATION_REQUIRED` and `BILLED` where a memo was upheld after the ticket had been recalled out of billing, or even at `UNPRORATED` and `SETTLED`. Tickets may be listed filtered by `validating_carrier_id`, by `proration_status` or by `billing_status`.

Coupons are appended one at a time and strictly in sequence. Each names its `coupon_number`, which must equal the ticket's current `coupon_count` plus one — gaps and repeats are refused with `ERR_COUPON_SEQUENCE_INVALID` — its `origin` and `destination` station codes (three uppercase letters by convention, but validated only as non-empty strings), the `operating_carrier_id` of the airline that will actually fly the sector and bill for it, the `class_of_service` flown from `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM`, `BUSINESS` and `FIRST`, and a strictly positive `tpm_miles` holding the ticketed point mileage of the sector. Each coupon records the `ticket_id` it belongs to. A fresh coupon opens in `coupon_status` `OPEN`, with `weight_units`, `allocated_amount_minor`, `ffp_miles_awarded`, `ffp_accrual_cost_minor` and `adjusted_amount_minor` at zero, `allocation_basis` of `UNALLOCATED`, `ffp_tier` of `NONE`, and both `flown_at` and `billing_invoice_id` null. Appending bumps the ticket's `coupon_count` and its `version`. Coupons may be listed filtered by `ticket_id`, by `operating_carrier_id`, by `billing_invoice_id` or by `coupon_status`.

A coupon's `coupon_status` walks `OPEN` to `FLOWN` when the sector is actually operated, `OPEN` to `VOID` when the sector is cancelled before departure, and `FLOWN` to `ADJUSTED` when a rejection memo strips money out of it. `VOID` is terminal — nothing brings a voided coupon back. Voiding is available only from `OPEN`, is restricted to the `RevenueAccountant` whose `X-User-ID` is the owning ticket's `validating_carrier_id`, and does three things: it sets `coupon_status` to `VOID`, zeroes `allocated_amount_minor` and resets `allocation_basis` to `UNALLOCATED`, then recomputes the ticket's `unallocated_residual_minor` so the fare stays conserved. Both the coupon's `version` and the ticket's `version` bump.

Marking a coupon flown is likewise reserved to the `RevenueAccountant` of the ticket's `validating_carrier_id`, requires the coupon to be `OPEN`, and requires the coupon's `operating_carrier_id` to name an `ACTIVE` carrier. It stamps `flown_at` with the current logical clock and sets `coupon_status` to `FLOWN`. It also books the frequent-flyer mileage cost the validating carrier owes the operating partner, described next. Flying bumps the coupon's `version` and the operating carrier's `version`, and — uniquely among coupon operations — leaves the ticket's `version` untouched.

**Mileage accrual at fly time.** The miles the passenger earns on the sector are `ffp_miles_awarded`, computed as the sector's `tpm_miles` multiplied by the cabin's earning multiplier and divided by 10000, rounded half up. The multipliers are 5000 basis points for `ECONOMY`, 10000 for `PREMIUM`, 15000 for `BUSINESS` and 20000 for `FIRST` — in plain terms, economy earns half a mile per ticketed mile, premium economy earns mile for mile, business earns one and a half, and first earns double.

What those miles *cost* depends on how many miles the operating carrier has already accrued this settlement period, and the tier is read from the carrier's running `period_accrued_miles` **as it stands before this coupon's miles are added**. Below 10000 accumulated miles the coupon is costed at `TIER_A`, 1400 minor units per thousand miles. From 10000 up to but not including 40000 it is costed at `TIER_B`, 1150 per thousand. At 40000 and above it is costed at `TIER_C`, 900 per thousand. The chosen tier is stamped onto the coupon as `ffp_tier`, and `ffp_accrual_cost_minor` is the coupon's `ffp_miles_awarded` multiplied by the tier's per-thousand rate and divided by 1000, rounded half up. Only then are the coupon's miles added to the operating carrier's `period_accrued_miles`, and its `ffp_accrual_cost_minor` added to that carrier's `period_ffp_cost_estimate_minor`. Because the tier is read before the addition, coupons flown early in a period are costed at a rate at least as high as those flown later — the bill gets cheaper as the partner flies more, and the estimate as a whole is therefore systematically conservative until it is trued up at period close.

### 3.5. Coupon-Level Revenue Allocation

A proration run is what converts one fare into a set of coupon allocations, and it exists in two variants that share one algorithm. A `RevenueAccountant` acting for the ticket's `validating_carrier_id` requests a run by naming a `run_type` of either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` together with the `expected_ticket_version` described in §2.8. The result is a `ProrationRun` record — an immutable ledger entry naming its `ticket_id` and `run_type` — and a rewritten allocation across the ticket's coupons. A run may be requested from any of the four `proration_status` values, so a `FINAL` run can be repeated as often as needed, each one superseding the previous allocation entirely; a `PROVISIONAL` run leaves the ticket at `PROVISIONAL` and a `FINAL` run leaves it at `FINAL`. The runs of one ticket can be read back as a list, ordered oldest first.

The two variants differ in exactly two ways. `PROVISIONAL` is the straight-rate settlement taken at ticketing time, before any sector has flown: its input set is the coupons whose `coupon_status` is `OPEN` or `FLOWN`, and it skips special prorate agreements entirely — even active ones — so nothing is taken off the top and every included coupon settles on `STRAIGHT_RATE` with a weight multiplier of exactly 10000 basis points. `FINAL` is the basis actually billed through the clearing house: its input set is the coupons whose `coupon_status` is `FLOWN` or `ADJUSTED`, so unflown coupons drop out and the surviving coupons share a differently-shaped pool, and it does honour special prorate agreements and does bend straight-rate weights by the matched factor. Voided coupons are excluded by both variants. Whichever variant is requested, if its input set is empty the run fails with `ERR_NO_PRORATABLE_COUPONS` (HTTP 422) and nothing is written.

The run proceeds as a waterfall, and the order of its steps is pinned.

The pool entering the run, recorded as `pool_minor`, is always the ticket's full `total_fare_minor` — both variants start from the whole fare, never from a remainder. The number of coupons in the input set is recorded as `included_coupon_count`.

Next come the off-the-top special-prorate amounts, and this step runs only in the `FINAL` variant. Walking the included coupons in ascending `coupon_number`, each is matched against the agreement book by the selector described in §3.2. A coupon whose matched agreement has an `agreement_type` of `SPA_FLAT` takes the factor's `flat_amount_minor` as its `allocated_amount_minor` and records an `allocation_basis` of `SPA_FLAT`. A coupon whose matched agreement has an `agreement_type` of `SPA_PERCENT` takes the pool multiplied by the factor's `percent_bp` and divided by 10000, rounded half up, and records an `allocation_basis` of `SPA_PERCENT`. Every such amount accumulates into `spa_total_minor`, which is always zero for a `PROVISIONAL` run. If `spa_total_minor` ends up larger than the pool, the whole run is refused with `ERR_SPA_OVERALLOCATION` (HTTP 422). What remains, the pool less `spa_total_minor`, is recorded as `straight_rate_pool_minor` and is what the remaining coupons share.

Those remaining coupons — the included coupons that took no off-the-top amount — are weighted next. Each one's `weight_units` is its `tpm_miles`, multiplied by its cabin's weight in basis points, multiplied by the applicable factor in basis points, divided by 10000, and rounded half up. The cabin weights are 10000 basis points for `ECONOMY`, 12500 for `PREMIUM`, 16000 for `BUSINESS` and 20000 for `FIRST`, so a business-class mile counts for 1.6 economy miles and a first-class mile for double. The applicable factor is the matched factor's `factor_bp` when — and only when — an agreement matched and its `agreement_type` is `STRAIGHT_RATE`; in every other case, including an unmatched coupon and every coupon in a `PROVISIONAL` run, it is the default 10000 basis points, which leaves the weight untouched. The sum of these across the sharing coupons is recorded as `total_weight_units`.

Then the money is split, and **the rounding order here is the single most consequential detail in this document**. Walking the sharing coupons in ascending `coupon_number`, each coupon's `allocated_amount_minor` is `straight_rate_pool_minor` multiplied by that coupon's `weight_units`, divided by `total_weight_units`, and rounded half up **on its own, before any summing**, with `allocation_basis` set to `STRAIGHT_RATE`. Summing the exact rational shares and rounding once at the end is wrong and produces a different cent split. Because per-coupon rounding cannot be expected to reconstitute the pool, the difference is measured: `rounding_residual_minor` is `straight_rate_pool_minor` less the sum of those rounded shares, and it may be positive or negative. That entire residual is then granted to exactly one coupon — the one with the highest `weight_units`, with the lowest `coupon_number` breaking a tie — by adding it to that coupon's `allocated_amount_minor`. No other coupon is touched by the residual.

There is one case where no coupon can absorb the residual: when every included coupon took an off-the-top amount and none remained to share. The run then records a `rounding_residual_minor` of zero and reports the entire `straight_rate_pool_minor` as the run's own `unallocated_residual_minor`; otherwise that field is zero.

Finally, every coupon of the ticket that was *not* in the input set is explicitly zeroed — `allocated_amount_minor` set to 0 and `allocation_basis` reset to `UNALLOCATED` — so that no stale figure from an earlier run survives. The run records `allocated_total_minor` as the sum of every `allocated_amount_minor` it wrote, and the ticket's `unallocated_residual_minor` is recomputed as the pool less the sum of `allocated_amount_minor` across *all* the ticket's coupons. The ticket's `last_run_type` is set to the variant just executed, its `proration_status` moves to `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` accordingly, and its `version` bumps. A run also rewrites `weight_units` on every coupon of the ticket — the computed weight on each sharing coupon, and 0 both on a coupon that took an off-the-top amount and on a coupon outside the input set — while touching no coupon's own `version` or `updated_at`: the ticket's row is the only one whose version a run advances. The response is 201 Created carrying the new `ProrationRun` row itself, not the ticket. A worked consequence: a two-coupon ticket where one sector has flown and the other has not will allocate across both coupons under `PROVISIONAL` but only across the flown one under `FINAL`, and the unflown coupon is zeroed back to `UNALLOCATED` by the later run.

**Voiding an allocation.** Proration voiding is the compensating action for a run. It is available to the `RevenueAccountant` of the ticket's `validating_carrier_id` when `proration_status` is `PROVISIONAL`, `FINAL` or `REPRORATION_REQUIRED` — never from `UNPRORATED`, which is refused with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_PRORATED` — and requires `expected_ticket_version`. It sets `allocated_amount_minor` to 0 and `allocation_basis` to `UNALLOCATED` on every coupon of the ticket regardless of status, restores `unallocated_residual_minor` to the full `total_fare_minor`, resets `last_run_type` to `NONE`, moves `proration_status` back to `UNPRORATED` and bumps the ticket's `version`. It leaves `billing_status` completely untouched — the two axes never read each other, and an already-billed ticket stays billed even after its allocation is discarded. It likewise leaves each coupon's `weight_units`, `coupon_status`, `adjusted_amount_minor`, `billing_invoice_id` and `version` exactly where they were, and answers 200 with the updated ticket.

### 3.6. Billing Through the Clearing House

Once a ticket's allocation is final, the operating carrier's share becomes a claim. Drafting the invoice is the `RevenueAccountant`'s job and it happens against the billing carrier's own account: the draft names the `settlement_period_id` of the open clearance window, the `billing_carrier_id` of the operating carrier claiming the money, the `billed_carrier_id` of the validating carrier that must pay, the `billing_currency` the coupons are denominated in, and a `coupon_ids` array listing the coupons being claimed. Every named coupon must belong to a ticket whose `proration_status` is `FINAL`, must be `FLOWN` or `ADJUSTED` and not already attached to another invoice, must have been operated by the `billing_carrier_id` and ticketed by the `billed_carrier_id`, and must belong to a ticket whose `sale_currency` equals the invoice's `billing_currency`. The corresponding rejections are `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FINAL_PRORATED`, `ERR_COUPON_ALREADY_INVOICED`, `ERR_COUPON_CARRIER_MISMATCH` and `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH`, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.7. A successful draft opens in `DRAFT` status, writes the new invoice's identifier into each attached coupon's `billing_invoice_id`, sets the invoice's own `coupon_count`, and leaves every money field at zero — nothing is computed until submission. A repeated identifier inside `coupon_ids` attaches its coupon once and is counted once, and an empty `coupon_ids` array is accepted, yielding a legal draft with a `coupon_count` of zero. Invoices may be listed filtered by `settlement_period_id`, by `billing_carrier_id`, by `billed_carrier_id` or by `status`.

Submission is the `BillingAgent`'s act, and only for its own `billing_carrier_id`. It requires the invoice to be `DRAFT`, the period to be `OPEN`, and the billing carrier to be `ACTIVE`. At submission the claim is built and converted, in that order.

**Building the claim.** The `gross_billed_minor` is the sum of `allocated_amount_minor` over the coupons whose `billing_invoice_id` is this invoice; the coupon set was frozen at draft time and is only ever cleared by a recall. The clearing house's handling-fee rate, `ich_fee_bp`, is then selected from a three-tier scale keyed on that **pre-fee gross** — 75 basis points when the gross is below 500000 minor units, 50 basis points when it is at least 500000 but below 5000000, and 25 basis points when it is 5000000 or more. This evaluation order is pinned and looks circular if read carelessly: the tier is keyed on the gross, never on the claim the carrier ultimately receives, and it is never re-selected after the deductions land. `ich_handling_fee_minor` is the gross multiplied by `ich_fee_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. `late_submission_penalty_minor` is the gross multiplied by 200 basis points, divided by 10000, rounded half up, but only when the current logical clock is strictly after the period's `submission_deadline`; otherwise it is zero. `total_claim_minor` is the gross less the handling fee and less the late penalty, and `net_billed_minor` is `total_claim_minor` less whatever `rejected_amount_minor` already stands on the invoice — zero at first submission. All of these are in `billing_currency` minor units.

**Converting the claim.** The claim is then converted from `billing_currency` into the period's `settlement_currency` at the anchor of the current logical clock, using the selection procedure of §3.3. The chosen scaled rate is stamped as `provisional_rate_scaled` and the converted figure as `provisional_settlement_minor`, being `total_claim_minor` multiplied by `provisional_rate_scaled`, divided by 1000000, rounded half up. If the pair has no eligible quote and the two currencies differ, submission fails with `ERR_NO_EXCHANGE_RATE`.

**The credit check.** Submission is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` when it would push the billed carrier's net payable position past its limit — specifically when that carrier's `pending_payable_minor` plus `settled_payable_minor` plus this invoice's `provisional_settlement_minor`, less its `pending_receivable_minor` and `settled_receivable_minor`, would exceed `credit_limit_minor`. Note that this check necessarily runs after the FX quote has been resolved, because it needs the converted figure.

A successful submission sets `status` to `SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` to the current logical clock, adds `provisional_settlement_minor` to the billing carrier's `pending_receivable_minor` and to the billed carrier's `pending_payable_minor`, recomputes `net_position_minor` on both, and moves every ticket owning one of the attached coupons from `billing_status` `UNBILLED` to `BILLED`. The invoice's `version` bumps.

**Recall** is the compensating action, available to the same `BillingAgent` while the invoice is `SUBMITTED` and the period is still `OPEN`. It reverses the submission exactly: it subtracts `provisional_settlement_minor` from the billing carrier's `pending_receivable_minor` and from the billed carrier's `pending_payable_minor` and recomputes `net_position_minor` on both; resets `gross_billed_minor`, `ich_fee_bp`, `ich_handling_fee_minor`, `late_submission_penalty_minor`, `total_claim_minor`, `net_billed_minor`, `provisional_rate_scaled`, `provisional_settlement_minor` and `submitted_at` to their defaults; detaches every attached coupon by clearing its `billing_invoice_id` back to null and setting the invoice's `coupon_count` to zero; returns the invoice to `DRAFT`; and bumps its `version`. Tickets follow it out of billing: a ticket whose `billing_status` is `BILLED` returns to `UNBILLED` precisely when, after this recall's detachment, none of its coupons remains attached to any invoice in `SUBMITTED`, `CLEARED`, `PARTIALLY_REJECTED`, `FULLY_REJECTED` or `SETTLED` status. A ticket still carried on another live invoice stays `BILLED`.

### 3.7. Closing and Settling a Period

Closing an open period is the heaviest operation in the system: a `ClearinghouseOperator` fires it, and it walks five hops plus an independent mileage true-up. The period must be `OPEN`. Its own row is stamped first — `status` becomes `CLOSED`, `closed_at` becomes the current logical clock, and its `version` bumps. The period row is written once for the whole close, so its `version` advances by exactly 1 however many invoices clear against it and however far `cleared_invoice_count` climbs.

**Hop 2 — invoices clear.** Every invoice belonging to this period whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` is processed in ascending `created_at`. For each, the fixed FX quote is selected at the anchor of the period's `rate_fixing_date` and stamped as `fixed_rate_scaled`; `final_settlement_minor` becomes `total_claim_minor` multiplied by `fixed_rate_scaled`, divided by 1000000, rounded half up; and `fx_variance_minor` becomes `final_settlement_minor` less `provisional_settlement_minor`, which may be negative. The invoice's `cleared_at` is set to the period's `closed_at`, its `status` becomes `CLEARED`, and its `version` bumps. The period's `cleared_invoice_count` increments by one for each. If a required quote is missing, the close fails with `ERR_NO_EXCHANGE_RATE`; every one of those quotes is resolved up front, before hop 1 stamps anything, so a missing quote leaves the period `OPEN` and not one row written.

**Hop 3 — balances move.** Because each of those invoices became `CLEARED`, its billing carrier's `pending_receivable_minor` is reduced by `provisional_settlement_minor` and its `settled_receivable_minor` increased by `final_settlement_minor`, while its billed carrier's `pending_payable_minor` is reduced by `provisional_settlement_minor` and its `settled_payable_minor` increased by `final_settlement_minor`. `net_position_minor` is recomputed on both carriers and both carriers' `version` bumps. This is where the FX variance actually lands on the ledger: the provisional figure leaves the pending side and a different fixed figure arrives on the settled side.

**Hop 4 — over-limit carriers are suspended.** Because a carrier's settled balances just moved, every carrier whose `settled_payable_minor` less its `settled_receivable_minor` is now strictly greater than its `credit_limit_minor`, and whose `status` is still `ACTIVE`, transitions to `SUSPENDED` and bumps its `version`. This is the automatic sanction the submit-time credit check cannot pre-empt, because that check was made against a provisional conversion that the fixed conversion has now displaced.

**Hop 5 — their agreements follow.** Because a carrier became `SUSPENDED` in hop 4, every prorate agreement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and which names that carrier as either `issuing_carrier_id` or `partner_carrier_id` transitions to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `CARRIER_SUSPENDED`, bumping each agreement's `version`.

**The frequent-flyer true-up** runs at close as well, independently of the five hops above. For every carrier that operated at least one coupon whose `coupon_status` is `FLOWN` or `ADJUSTED` and whose `flown_at` falls in the half-open window from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive, the period's total miles are summed from those coupons' `ffp_miles_awarded`. A **single** tier is then chosen from that period total using the same thresholds as fly time — `TIER_A` at 1400 per thousand below 10000 miles, `TIER_B` at 1150 per thousand from 10000 up to but not including 40000, `TIER_C` at 900 per thousand at 40000 and above — and applied retroactively to every mile of the period. The result, the period total multiplied by the chosen per-thousand rate divided by 1000 and rounded half up, is written to that carrier's `period_ffp_cost_trued_up_minor`. The difference between it and the sum of those same coupons' `ffp_accrual_cost_minor` estimates is written to `ffp_true_up_delta_minor`, and it may be negative. The two figures diverge exactly when the carrier's cumulative mileage crossed a tier ceiling mid-period, because the per-coupon estimates were priced tier by tier as the miles accumulated while the true-up prices the whole period at one rate. Writing those two figures bumps that carrier's `version` as any other mutation would — a second bump for a carrier that hop 4 has already suspended — while a carrier with no qualifying coupon in the window is skipped entirely and neither its figures nor its `version` move.

**Settlement** is the second and final step of the calendar, available only from `CLOSED`. It stamps `settled_at` with the current logical clock, sets the period's `status` to `SETTLED` and bumps its `version`. Every invoice of the period whose `status` is `CLEARED`, `PARTIALLY_REJECTED` or `FULLY_REJECTED` transitions to `SETTLED` — the three branches rejoin here and `SETTLED` is terminal for an invoice. Every ticket owning a coupon on one of those invoices whose `billing_status` is `BILLED` transitions to `SETTLED`; a ticket sitting at `DISPUTED` is deliberately left where it is, because its money is still contested. Finally, every carrier in the system has its `period_accrued_miles` and `period_ffp_cost_estimate_minor` reset to zero, so the next period's tiering starts from a clean slate; only a carrier whose two counters were not already both zero is actually written, so only those carriers' `version` bumps, and the period's own row — as at close — bumps by exactly 1. `period_ffp_cost_trued_up_minor` and `ffp_true_up_delta_minor` are not reset — they are the closed period's audit trail. Attempting to settle a period that is not `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`.

### 3.8. The Rejection-Memo Cycle: Dispute, Counter, Arbitration and Aging

Once an invoice has cleared, the carrier that had to pay it may contest individual coupons. The billed carrier's `BillingAgent` raises a rejection memo naming the `invoice_id`, the single `coupon_id` whose allocation is contested, the contested `amount_minor` in the invoice's `billing_currency`, a `reason_code` drawn from `PRORATE_FACTOR_DISPUTE`, `SPA_NOT_APPLIED`, `FARE_BASIS_ERROR`, `DUPLICATE_BILLING` and `CURRENCY_ERROR`, and the `expected_invoice_version` of §2.8. The invoice must be `CLEARED` or `PARTIALLY_REJECTED` — a memo against a `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `FULLY_REJECTED` or `SETTLED` invoice is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_CLEARED`. The contested coupon must actually be attached to that invoice, or the raise is refused with `ERR_COUPON_NOT_ON_INVOICE`.

Two fiscal ceilings guard the raise, and both apply again on every re-rejection. The contested `amount_minor` may not exceed the coupon's current `allocated_amount_minor` (`ERR_MEMO_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_COUPON`), and the invoice's `disputed_amount_minor` plus its `rejected_amount_minor` plus the requested amount may not exceed its `total_claim_minor` (`ERR_MEMO_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE`) — on a re-rejection the memo's own previous `amount_minor` is first removed from the disputed running total, so raising a round from 400 to 500 is tested as an incremental 100, not as an additional 500. A memo must also be raised inside the clearing house's dispute window: the current logical clock may be no more than 45 whole days after the invoice's `cleared_at`, and a late raise or a late re-rejection is refused with `ERR_MEMO_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

A successful raise opens the memo in `RAISED` status at `stage` 1, copies the invoice's `billed_carrier_id` into `raised_by_carrier_id`, stamps `raised_at` with the current logical clock, sets `counter_amount_minor` to zero, adds `amount_minor` to the invoice's `disputed_amount_minor`, and bumps the invoice's `version`. Memos may be listed filtered by `invoice_id`, by `coupon_id`, by `raised_by_carrier_id`, by `status` or by `aging_bucket`.

**The negotiation cycle.** The billing carrier's `BillingAgent` — the one who claimed the money, matched on `billing_carrier_id` — may counter-bill a `RAISED` memo, conceding part of the contested sum. Countering supplies a `counter_amount_minor`, which may not be negative and may not exceed the memo's `amount_minor` — a breach of that ceiling is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), but it is tested only after the memo-state check of step 11, so countering an already-`COUNTERED` memo with an oversized figure answers `ERR_MEMO_STATE_INVALID` — and a free-text `counter_reason`; it sets `status` to `COUNTERED`, records both fields, bumps the memo's `version`, and pointedly leaves the invoice's `disputed_amount_minor` unchanged, because nothing has been conceded in ledger terms yet. The billed carrier's `BillingAgent` — matched on `billed_carrier_id` — may then reject the counter and open the next round, supplying a fresh `amount_minor`, a fresh `reason_code` and `expected_invoice_version`. Re-rejection requires the memo to be `COUNTERED` and its `stage` to be below 3; a fourth round is refused with `ERR_MEMO_STAGE_EXHAUSTED`. It returns `status` to `RAISED`, increments `stage` by one, overwrites `amount_minor` and `reason_code`, resets `counter_amount_minor` to zero, adjusts the invoice's `disputed_amount_minor` by the difference between the old and new amounts, and bumps both the memo's and the invoice's `version`. Critically, it leaves `raised_at` alone — the aging clock keeps running from the original raise across the entire negotiation, which is what stops a carrier from resetting the clock by stalling.

**Aging.** A memo's `age_days` is the whole number of days between `raised_at` and an anchor instant, computed by taking the difference in whole seconds and dividing by 86400 with the remainder discarded. The anchor is the memo's `resolved_at` once it has reached any of the four resolved states, and the current logical clock while it is still live; the value is therefore recomputed on every read of a memo and frozen at resolution. From `age_days` follows `aging_bucket`: `CURRENT` below 30 days, `AGED_30` from 30 up to but not including 60, `AGED_60` from 60 up to but not including 90, and `AGED_90` at 90 days and beyond.

**Exits without a cascade.** The billed carrier may withdraw its own memo from either `RAISED` or `COUNTERED`, supplying `expected_invoice_version`. Withdrawal sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, stamps `resolved_at`, subtracts the memo's `amount_minor` from the invoice's `disputed_amount_minor`, and bumps both versions — no balance, coupon or ticket is touched. Either side's `BillingAgent`, matched on `billing_carrier_id` or on `billed_carrier_id`, may escalate a `RAISED` or `COUNTERED` memo into `ARBITRATION`, but only once it is exhausted or stale: the memo's `stage` must be 3, or its `age_days` must be at least 90. An escalation that meets neither condition is refused with `ERR_ARBITRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Escalation sets `status` to `ARBITRATION` and bumps the memo's `version`; nothing else moves. From `ARBITRATION`, a `ClearinghouseOperator` rules by supplying a `ruling` of either `UPHELD` or `DENIED`. A `DENIED` ruling sets `status` to `RESOLVED_DENIED`, stamps `resolved_at`, subtracts the memo's `amount_minor` from the invoice's `disputed_amount_minor`, bumps both the memo's and the invoice's `version`, and stops there — no balance, coupon or ticket effect at all. `ACCEPTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `RESOLVED_UPHELD` and `RESOLVED_DENIED` are all terminal.

**The five-hop resolution cascade.** Acceptance by the billing carrier's `BillingAgent`, which requires the memo to be `RAISED` and carries `expected_invoice_version`, and an arbitration ruling of `UPHELD`, which requires the memo to be `ARBITRATION`, fire the identical cascade. They differ only in who acts and in the resulting terminal status.

Hop 1 resolves the memo itself: `status` becomes `ACCEPTED` or `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `resolved_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, `age_days` and `aging_bucket` freeze, and the memo's `version` bumps.

Hop 2 moves the invoice, and it forks. The memo's `amount_minor` is added to the invoice's `rejected_amount_minor` and removed from its `disputed_amount_minor`, and `net_billed_minor` is recomputed as `total_claim_minor` less the new `rejected_amount_minor`. The memo's `settlement_impact_minor` is written as its `amount_minor` multiplied by the invoice's `fixed_rate_scaled`, divided by 1000000, rounded half up, and that figure is subtracted from the billing carrier's `settled_receivable_minor` and from the billed carrier's `settled_payable_minor`, with `net_position_minor` recomputed on both. The fork is decided by one comparison: if the invoice's `rejected_amount_minor` before this memo plus the memo's `amount_minor` is strictly less than `total_claim_minor` the invoice moves to `PARTIALLY_REJECTED`; if it is greater than or equal to `total_claim_minor` the invoice moves to `FULLY_REJECTED`. Both branches perform exactly the same field writes and both carry the same continuation into hop 3 — only the resulting `status` differs. An invoice already sitting at `PARTIALLY_REJECTED` re-enters the same fork on each further accepted or upheld memo, staying `PARTIALLY_REJECTED` or tipping over to `FULLY_REJECTED` by the same comparison. The invoice's `version` bumps.

Hop 3 moves the contested coupon, because the invoice's `rejected_amount_minor` increased. The memo's `amount_minor` is subtracted from the coupon's `allocated_amount_minor` and the same figure added to its `adjusted_amount_minor`; `coupon_status` becomes `ADJUSTED`; and the same figure is added to the owning ticket's `unallocated_residual_minor`, which is what preserves the fare-conservation identity through the adjustment. The coupon's `version` bumps. A coupon already `ADJUSTED` can be adjusted again by a second accepted or upheld memo, performing the identical writes, provided the new memo's `amount_minor` is no larger than the coupon's remaining `allocated_amount_minor` — repetition may drive the allocation down to zero but never below it.

Hop 4 moves the owning ticket, because its coupon changed: `proration_status` becomes `REPRORATION_REQUIRED` and `billing_status` becomes `DISPUTED`, both written in the same hop even though the two axes remain independently reachable, and the ticket's `version` bumps.

Hop 5 moves the validating carrier, because the ticket entered `REPRORATION_REQUIRED`: that carrier's `open_reproration_count` increments by one, and once the resulting count reaches 3 or more its `audit_flag` is set to true. The counter increments on every accepted or upheld memo that reaches this hop, including one whose ticket was already sitting at `REPRORATION_REQUIRED`, and the carrier's `version` bumps with it. The flag is a permanent mark — nothing in this system ever clears it back to false.

### 3.9. Sampling, Extrapolation and Undercharge Recovery

Revenue audit is the last line of defence against systematic under-allocation. An `InterlineAuditor` acting for its own carrier draws a sample by naming itself as `auditing_carrier_id`, the partner under examination as `audited_carrier_id`, the `settlement_period_id` of the window being audited, the `claim_currency` the sampled coupons and any resulting claim are denominated in, and an explicit `sample_coupon_ids` array of between 1 and 50 coupon identifiers with no duplicates. The period must already be `CLOSED` or `SETTLED`; drawing from an `OPEN` period is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`.

**Deriving the population.** At draw time the server derives the population the sample is drawn from: every coupon whose `operating_carrier_id` is the `audited_carrier_id`, whose `coupon_status` is `FLOWN` or `ADJUSTED`, whose `flown_at` falls in the half-open window from the period's `period_start` inclusive to its `period_end` exclusive, whose `billing_invoice_id` is not null — unbilled coupons are outside the audit's reach — and whose owning ticket's `sale_currency` equals the sample's `claim_currency`. An empty population is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_AUDIT_POPULATION`. A population containing any coupon flown earlier than 730 days before the current logical clock is time-barred and refused with `ERR_AUDIT_TIME_BARRED`. Separately — at ladder step 27, ahead of both the empty-population and the in-population checks — every coupon named in `sample_coupon_ids` must itself belong to a ticket whose `sale_currency` equals the sample's `claim_currency`, or the draw is refused with `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH`. Every identifier in `sample_coupon_ids` must then be a member of that derived population, or the draw is refused with `ERR_COUPON_NOT_IN_POPULATION`.

A successful draw freezes four figures and never recomputes them: `population_coupon_count` as the number of coupons in the population, `population_allocated_minor` as the sum of their `allocated_amount_minor`, `sampled_coupon_count` as the number of identifiers supplied, and `sampled_allocated_minor` as the sum of `allocated_amount_minor` over the sampled coupons as they stood at draw time. The sample opens in `DRAWN` status with `sampled_undercharge_minor` and `findings_count` at zero. Samples may be listed filtered by `auditing_carrier_id`, by `audited_carrier_id`, by `settlement_period_id` or by `status`.

**Recording findings.** The auditor then works through the sample coupon by coupon, each finding naming the `coupon_id` examined and the `correct_amount_minor` the auditor believes should have been allocated. The `coupon_id` must be a member of `sample_coupon_ids`, or the finding is refused with `ERR_COUPON_NOT_IN_SAMPLE`. The shortfall booked is the `correct_amount_minor` less the coupon's current `allocated_amount_minor`, floored at zero — an overcharge finding contributes nothing rather than a negative — and it accumulates into `sampled_undercharge_minor`. Each accepted finding increments `findings_count` and bumps the sample's `version`; the same `coupon_id` may be recorded more than once, and each recording books its own shortfall into the running total again. The first finding moves the sample from `DRAWN` to `IN_REVIEW`; subsequent findings leave it at `IN_REVIEW`.

**Closing the sample.** Closing requires `IN_REVIEW` and computes the extrapolation in a pinned order. First, `undercharge_ratio_bp` is `sampled_undercharge_minor` multiplied by 10000, divided by `sampled_allocated_minor`, rounded half up — the shortfall as a proportion of what was sampled, in basis points. Second, `coverage_bp` is `sampled_allocated_minor` multiplied by 10000, divided by `population_allocated_minor`, rounded half up — how much of the population by value the sample actually saw. Third, `haircut_bp` is selected from that coverage on a three-tier scale: no haircut at all when `coverage_bp` is 2000 or more, 1500 basis points when it is at least 500 but below 2000, and 3500 basis points when it is below 500. Thin samples are discounted hard. Fourth, `extrapolated_gross_minor` is `sampled_undercharge_minor` multiplied by `population_allocated_minor`, divided by `sampled_allocated_minor`, rounded half up — the sampled shortfall scaled up to the whole population. Where one of those denominators is zero — a `sampled_allocated_minor` or a `population_allocated_minor` of nothing — the quotient is taken as 0 rather than failing the close, so a `sampled_allocated_minor` of nothing yields a zero ratio and an immaterial close.

Materiality decides the branch, and the two close outcomes are separated by exactly one comparison against 200 basis points, or two per cent. When `undercharge_ratio_bp` is strictly below 200 the finding is immaterial: `claim_amount_minor` and `claim_settlement_minor` are both set to zero and the sample closes into `CLOSED_IMMATERIAL`. `extrapolated_gross_minor`, `coverage_bp` and `haircut_bp` are still written in that branch, for the audit trail. When `undercharge_ratio_bp` is 200 or more the sample closes into `CLAIM_RAISED` and the recovery claim is computed: the extrapolated gross is first **capped** at 25 times `sampled_undercharge_minor`, taking whichever of the two is smaller, and only then discounted by the haircut — `claim_amount_minor` is the capped figure multiplied by 10000 less `haircut_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The cap before the haircut is the pinned order; reversing them gives a different answer. Finally `claim_settlement_minor` converts `claim_amount_minor` from `claim_currency` into the audited period's `settlement_currency` using the quote prevailing at that period's `rate_fixing_date`, by the same conversion procedure as everywhere else, and a missing quote fails the close with `ERR_NO_EXCHANGE_RATE`. That quote is demanded by both branches, and is resolved before the extrapolation is computed at all, so a close that would have landed in `CLOSED_IMMATERIAL` is refused just the same when no quote is eligible. Either way `closed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock and the sample's `version` bumps.

**Deciding the claim.** Only the audited carrier's `BillingAgent`, matched on `audited_carrier_id`, may decide a `CLAIM_RAISED` sample, by supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `DENY`. The auditing carrier can never decide the claim it raised. An `ACCEPT` adds `claim_settlement_minor` to the `auditing_carrier_id`'s `settled_receivable_minor` and the same figure to the `audited_carrier_id`'s `settled_payable_minor`, recomputes `net_position_minor` on both, bumps both carriers' `version`, and moves the sample to `CLAIM_ACCEPTED`. A `DENY` moves the sample to `CLAIM_DENIED` and moves no money at all. Both are terminal, as is `CLOSED_IMMATERIAL`, and both bump the sample's `version`.
