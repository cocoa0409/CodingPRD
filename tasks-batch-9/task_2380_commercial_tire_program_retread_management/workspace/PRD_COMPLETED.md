# Commercial Tire Program & Retread Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A commercial tire dealer does not sell tires so much as it rents mileage. The dealer owns the casing program, the retread plant, the road service trucks and the scrap-tire paperwork; the fleet owns the trucks and pays by the mile. Everything that makes that arrangement profitable — position-specific tread and casing specifications, disciplined pressure and tread-depth surveying, casing recovery before a carcass is ruined, retread throughput on a small number of production lines, response-time commitments on breakdown calls — is today tracked in a patchwork of plant whiteboards, dispatch spreadsheets and state-mandated manifest carbon copies. The consequences are predictable and expensive: casings that could have carried two more retreads get scrapped, national accounts are invoiced at the wrong tier, credit is issued twice for the same carcass, and a scrap load leaves a yard on a lapsed permit.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes those gaps. Its organising idea is that a casing is a durable, valuable, individually serialised asset with a **cyclic** life — mounted, collected, graded, queued, cured, mounted again — and that the money attached to that life is a **ledger**, not a field. Every event that changes what a casing is worth writes an immutable entry to the casing credit ledger, and the fleet's spendable balance is nothing more than the sum of those entries. Four otherwise unrelated workflows — casing inspection, retread production, certified scrap destruction and cross-location invoicing — all debit and credit that one conserved quantity, and the specification below is unusually explicit about ordering, rounding and compensation precisely because those four workflows must never leave the ledger drifting by a cent.

A second theme running through the specification is **segregation of duties**. The inspector who grades a casing into a credit accrual is structurally barred from signing the scrap manifest that writes that credit off, and the officer who signs a manifest is barred from certifying its destruction. The scheduler who mints a retreaded tire is not the clerk who bills it. Readers implementing this API should treat those separations as functional requirements with their own error codes, not as advisory policy.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Personnel, Roles and Segregation of Duties

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present and well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `DealerAdmin`, `FleetManager`, `TireTechnician`, `CasingInspector`, `PlantScheduler`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `BillingClerk` and `DispatchCoordinator`. Their responsibilities are:

- **`DealerAdmin`** — dealer-side operations administrator. Builds the location network, fleet accounts, tire policies, national agreements, production lines and technician rosters, and controls the logical clock. Also shares vehicle registration and odometer posting with `FleetManager`, casing and tire-asset registration with `TireTechnician`, and the cost-per-mile analytic with `FleetManager`. Has read access everywhere.
- **`FleetManager`** — customer-side fleet contact. Registers vehicles, posts odometer readings, opens road service calls, disputes invoices, runs the cost-per-mile analytic, and reads its own fleet's policies, vehicles, casings, assets, surveys, calls, jobs, ledger entries and invoices.
- **`TireTechnician`** — field and shop technician. Collects casings, mounts, pulls and surveys tire assets, and records arrival and completion on road service calls. Shares casing and tire-asset registration with `DealerAdmin` and odometer posting with `DealerAdmin` and `FleetManager`.
- **`CasingInspector`** — casing grading authority. Records inspection grades and condemnations, the acts that make casing credit accrue.
- **`PlantScheduler`** — retread plant scheduler. Owns the production schedule and every retread job transition, including the completion that mints the retreaded tire and posts the production-verified credit adjustment.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — scrap tire disposal compliance officer. Opens manifests, adds lines, signs, records transport, certifies destruction and voids.
- **`BillingClerk`** — national account billing clerk. Creates, submits and settles cross-location invoices, and shares the dispute action with `FleetManager`.
- **`DispatchCoordinator`** — road service dispatcher. Opens, dispatches and cancels calls, but never records arrival or completion, so response times and billable labour are attested by the technician in the field rather than by the dispatcher.

**How `X-User-ID` is interpreted per role.** When `X-User-Role` is `FleetManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FleetAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FleetAccount` compare the resource's `fleet_account_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FLEET_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `TireTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; a technician recording arrival or completion on a road service call must be the technician named in that call's `technician_id`, and any other technician is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The technician's identifier is also stamped onto each survey as `surveyed_by_user_id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `DealerAdmin`, `CasingInspector`, `PlantScheduler`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `BillingClerk` or `DispatchCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a `CasingInspector`'s identifier is retained as `inspected_by_user_id` on the casing it graded, and a `ComplianceOfficer`'s identifier is retained as `signed_by_user_id` or `certified_by_user_id` on the manifest they signed or certified — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers exist so that the segregation-of-duties checks described in §3.7 can compare one operator against another.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range from 1 to 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a plain integer literal, a filter value that is not a member of the filtered field's enumeration, and a boolean filter whose value is not one of `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.

Unless a specific endpoint declares otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two collections override that default because their business meaning is a sequence rather than a set: survey history is returned ordered by `surveyed_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and casing credit ledger entries are returned in replay order, meaning `posted_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`. Identifiers are UUIDs and carry no type prefix — a casing id and an invoice id are indistinguishable by shape, so path resolution depends on the collection being addressed rather than on the identifier's spelling. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded at insert time.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Service locations, fleet accounts, national agreements, tire policies, vehicles, casings, tire assets, retread lines, retread jobs, technicians, road service calls, scrap manifests and cross-location invoices all carry the full set.

Two resources are deliberately append-only observations rather than mutable records, and they carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them entirely. Those two are the tire survey and the casing credit ledger entry. A survey records what a technician measured at one instant and is never edited; a ledger entry records a posting and is never edited. Corrections to the ledger take the form of further entries, never mutations of prior ones.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a logical UTC clock rather than reading wall-clock time. Before any request sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability sets the clock to an arbitrary instant supplied as `current_utc`, and its response echoes `current_utc` alongside `expired_agreement_ids`; the echo is character-for-character the string the caller supplied, and `expired_agreement_ids` lists the agreements this call expired in `created_at` ascending order. Wherever the server stamps its own clock onto a row — an agreement's `activated_at`, a ledger entry's `posted_at` — it stores and returns that same supplied string verbatim rather than reformatting it, which means those fields read exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until the first clock set.

The logical clock is the reference for three things: the scrap permit expiry check performed at manifest signing, the `posted_at` stamp written onto every casing credit ledger entry, and the resolution of which national agreement is in force when a casing credit rate is needed. Instants supplied in request bodies — arrival times, completion times, signature times and the like — are taken from the payload and are not overwritten by the clock, and they too are stored and echoed exactly as the caller spelled them; the clock governs only the server's own notion of "now".

Advancing the clock has one substantive side effect, described in §3.2: agreements whose window has closed expire.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reverse Reachability

Collections are flat. Every entity has a top-level collection, and relationships between entities are expressed as identifier fields on the child rather than as nesting in the URL. Creation of a resource whose natural owner is another resource is the one exception: tire policies, vehicles, retread lines, technicians and scrap manifests are created underneath their owning fleet account or service location.

When a path contains more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. Each entity type has its own not-found code — `ERR_SERVICE_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLEET_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NATIONAL_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TIRE_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TIRE_SURVEY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETREAD_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETREAD_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROAD_SERVICE_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCRAP_MANIFEST_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` — all at HTTP 404. The same codes are used when an identifier supplied in a request body fails to resolve; body-supplied references that can fail this way are service locations, fleet accounts, vehicles, casings, tire assets, retread lines and technicians. When one body carries several such references they are resolved in the order this document introduces them for that operation, and the first one that fails determines the error.

Because the topology is flat, every relationship is navigable in reverse through a filter on the child collection. The fleet list accepts `home_location_id`, `status`, `billing_mode` and `account_type`; the location list accepts `state_code` and `is_retread_plant`; agreements filter by `fleet_account_id` and `status`; policies by `fleet_account_id` and `position`; vehicles by `fleet_account_id`, `vehicle_type` and `status`; casings by `fleet_account_id`, `collected_at_location_id`, `scrap_manifest_id`, `status`, `intended_position` and `inspection_grade`; tire assets by `fleet_account_id`, `casing_id`, `vehicle_id`, `replaced_tire_asset_id`, `status`, `position` and `brand`; surveys by `tire_asset_id`, `finding_code` and `pull_recommended`; ledger entries by `fleet_account_id`, `casing_id`, `entry_type` and `source_entity_type`; retread lines by `service_location_id` and `status`; retread jobs by `casing_id`, `retread_line_id`, `produced_tire_asset_id` and `status`; technicians by `service_location_id`, `status` and `certification_level`; road service calls by `fleet_account_id`, `vehicle_id`, `service_location_id`, `technician_id`, `cross_location_invoice_id`, `dispatch_status`, `billing_status` and `priority`; manifests by `service_location_id`, `status` and `hauler_permit_number`; invoices by `fleet_account_id`, `national_agreement_id` and `status`. This matters for resources the API never returns from a creation response — the tire asset minted by a retread completion, for instance, is discoverable only by filtering the job list on `produced_tire_asset_id` or the asset list on `casing_id`.

### 2.7. Money, Measures and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer counts of cents. There are no fractional-cent values anywhere in the system, in storage or in responses. Where a computation produces a fractional intermediate, the result is rounded half up — a value exactly on the half rounds away from zero — and the specification always states where in the sequence that rounding lands, because two arithmetically equivalent orderings can differ by a cent.

Three rounding disciplines are worth stating up front because they are the ones most easily got wrong. First, the casing credit figure is the product of a base rate, a grade factor and a fatigue decay factor, and it is rounded to whole cents **once**, on the final product; the individual factors are never rounded first. Second, road service pricing rounds three quantities separately and in a fixed sequence: the labour charge, then the surcharge, then any service-level credit. Third, the tier discount on a cross-location invoice is rounded per line and the already-rounded per-line figures are summed; rounding the aggregate instead is forbidden and yields a different answer whenever the per-line remainders round up but the aggregate remainder does not.

Non-monetary measures have their own precision rules. Tread depth is an integer count of thirty-seconds of an inch. Weights are integer pounds. Retread slot durations are whole minutes, and a slot boundary that does not land on a whole minute is rejected. Warranty terms are whole months. The measured pressure deviation on a survey is a fraction rounded half up to four decimal places, and that rounding happens **before** the comparison against the policy tolerance, so a deviation that rounds up to exactly the tolerance counts as a deviation. Cost-per-mile figures are expressed in cents per mile to four decimal places, rounded half up once per output group. The negotiated tier discount fraction on an agreement is captured with at most three decimals.

Labour hours are the only quarter-granular quantity: `labor_hours` must be a multiple of 0.25, and any other value is a malformed request.

### 2.8. The Casing Credit Ledger as a Conserved Quantity

A fleet's casing credit is not a stored number that operations increment. It is the arithmetic sum of an append-only ledger. Each `CasingCreditEntry` records a `fleet_account_id`, an optional `casing_id`, an `entry_type`, a signed `amount_cents` where a positive value increases the fleet's balance and a negative value decreases it, a polymorphic pair of `source_entity_type` and `source_entity_id` naming the row that caused the posting, a `posted_at` instant taken from the logical clock, and a machine-generated `memo` describing the posting.

`entry_type` takes one of five values, each tied to a specific business event. `ACCRUAL` is written when an inspection grade is recorded. `REVERSAL` undoes a previously posted amount — either because a casing was re-graded or because an invoice was disputed. `ADJUSTMENT` posts the production-verified delta once a casing survives the cure. `APPLICATION` consumes credit against an invoice. `EXPIRY` writes credit off when scrap destruction is certified.

`source_entity_type` is one of `CASING`, `RETREAD_JOB`, `SCRAP_MANIFEST` or `CROSS_LOCATION_INVOICE`, and `source_entity_id` is resolved against the collection that discriminator names — the casings, retread jobs, scrap manifests or cross-location invoices collection respectively. It is deliberately not a single-target foreign key.

Two derived figures read off that ledger. The fleet account's `casing_credit_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every entry carrying that fleet's `fleet_account_id`, with no filter on entry type, age or status. A casing's `provisional_credit_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over entries carrying that casing's `casing_id` whose `entry_type` is one of `ACCRUAL`, `REVERSAL`, `ADJUSTMENT` or `EXPIRY`; `APPLICATION` entries carry a null `casing_id` and therefore never attach to a casing. Replay order for auditing is `posted_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. That order does not change the sum, but it pins the sequence returned by the ledger read endpoints.

One invariant governs every posting: after the posting, the fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents` must be zero or greater. The check runs *before* anything is persisted, and an operation that would drive the balance below zero is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_BALANCE_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 409) having written no entry at all. Three operations can trip it: submitting a cross-location invoice, recording a casing inspection grade, and certifying scrap destruction.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations snapshot a value they could otherwise silently clobber, and both are guarded by an optimistic concurrency check on the parent row's `version`.

Recording a casing inspection grade requires `expected_casing_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the `version` of the casing as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the casing's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this guard exists is that the product deliberately permits a later inspection to supersede an earlier one on the same casing, reversing the prior accrual and posting a fresh one; without the check, a second grading issued from a stale view would succeed and reverse an amount the client never saw.

Creating a cross-location invoice requires `expected_fleet_account_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the `version` of the fleet account as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the fleet account's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FLEET_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Invoice creation snapshots the fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents` into `credit_applied_cents`, and that balance moves whenever an inspection accrues, a retread completion adjusts, a scrap destruction expires or another invoice applies credit — so a creation carrying a stale fleet version would capture a credit figure the client never observed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Operations not named above take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one is not part of their contract.

### 2.10. Cascade Propagation and Hierarchical Consistency

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. Consistency is immediate: by the time a 2xx response is returned, every cascaded effect described in §3 has already been applied and is visible to a subsequent read. There is no deferred or eventual settlement.

Cascades are described in §3 as numbered hops, and the numbering is meaningful — each hop's precondition is the completion of the hop before it. The deepest chains in the system are retread completion, which walks from the job through the casing to the ledger, the fleet balance and then every open invoice of that fleet; certified scrap destruction, which walks from the manifest through every listed casing to their pulled tire assets, the ledger, the fleet balance and the holding counts of every affected location; and road service completion with a replacement, which walks from the call through the pulled asset to its casing and then to the dispatching location's holding count.

Whenever a cascade mutates a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1, exactly as for a directly requested mutation. A caller that has cached a fleet account or a casing must therefore expect its `version` to have moved after an unrelated-looking operation elsewhere in the system — which is precisely the situation the optimistic concurrency checks in §2.9 are there to catch.

Derived aggregates are recomputed from their definitions rather than incrementally patched. A service location's `casings_in_holding_count` is the number of casings whose `collected_at_location_id` is that location and whose `status` is one of `COLLECTED`, `INSPECTED`, `REJECTED`, `RETREAD_QUEUED` or `IN_RETREAD`. A retread line's `booked_minutes` is the sum, over jobs on that line whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, of that job's whole-minute slot length plus the line's `cure_cooldown_minutes`. A manifest's `tire_count` and `total_weight_lbs` are the count of, and the sum of `scrap_weight_lbs` over, the casings whose `scrap_manifest_id` is that manifest. An invoice's `line_count` is the number of calls whose `cross_location_invoice_id` is that invoice. A technician's `completed_call_count` is the number of calls carrying that technician's identifier in `technician_id` whose `dispatch_status` is `COMPLETED`.

### 2.11. Order of Validation

When more than one rule could reject a request, the outcome must be deterministic. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure is the one reported. This ordering is normative: a request that violates rules at positions 9 and 31 reports the position-9 error.

1. Both auth headers present and `X-User-Role` among the eight declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Every identifier in the URL path resolves, checked left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code from §2.6.
4. A `FleetManager` acts only within its own fleet — otherwise `ERR_FLEET_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. This applies to every endpoint `FleetManager` may call. On a single-resource read or write it compares the resource's fleet against `X-User-ID`; on a collection read it means the window is implicitly narrowed to that fleet's own rows, and an explicit `fleet_account_id` filter naming any other fleet is the violation, while on the two collections that expose no `fleet_account_id` filter — surveys and retread jobs — rows belonging to other fleets are simply omitted from the window.
5. A `TireTechnician` recording arrival at, or completion of, a road service call is the technician assigned to it — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. This applies to exactly those two operations, and a call that carries no `technician_id` yet fails the check for every technician, so an unassigned call reports the scope violation rather than an invalid-state error.
6. Request body and query shape: required fields present, enum members recognised, numbers inside their declared bounds, `labor_hours` a multiple of 0.25, dates and datetimes well-formed ISO 8601, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, `offset` zero or greater — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A body that is unparseable JSON, or that parses to anything other than a JSON object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` as well; a request that carries no body at all is read as an empty object, so it fails only if the operation has required fields.
7. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves — otherwise the matching not-found code.
8. Uniqueness constraints — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOCATION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASING_SERIAL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIRE_POLICY`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VEHICLE_UNIT` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_CODE`. These are enforced on location registration, agreement drafting, casing registration, tire-policy creation, vehicle registration and retread-line creation respectively.
9. The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the entity's invalid-state code: `ERR_CASING_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TIRE_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RETREAD_JOB_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_MANIFEST_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FLEET_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_LINE_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_TECHNICIAN_INVALID_STATE`. This applies to every action endpoint that drives a state transition.
10. The optimistic concurrency check of §2.9 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CASING_VERSION` on casing inspection or `ERR_STALE_FLEET_VERSION` on invoice creation.
11. The signing location's scrap permit is still valid on the logical clock's UTC date — otherwise `ERR_SCRAP_PERMIT_EXPIRED`. Applies to manifest signing.
12. The signing officer is not the inspector of any listed casing — otherwise `ERR_SOD_INSPECTOR_CANNOT_SIGN`. Applies to manifest signing.
13. The certifying officer is not the signer — otherwise `ERR_SOD_SIGNER_CANNOT_CERTIFY`. Applies to destruction certification.
14. Mount-time tread depth clears the policy minimum — otherwise `ERR_TREAD_BELOW_POLICY_MINIMUM`. Applies to mounting.
15. The casing has retread capacity left under its policy — otherwise `ERR_MAX_RETREAD_COUNT_REACHED`. Applies to retread job scheduling.
16. Every casing listed on the manifest is condemned — otherwise `ERR_CASING_NOT_SCRAP_ELIGIBLE`. Applies to both manifest signing and manifest line addition.
17. The manifest being signed is not empty — otherwise `ERR_MANIFEST_EMPTY`. Applies to manifest signing.
18. The casing being added is not already on another manifest — otherwise `ERR_CASING_ALREADY_ON_MANIFEST`. Applies to manifest line addition.
19. A tire policy exists for the fleet-and-position pair the operation needs — otherwise `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_MISSING`. Applies to four operations: mounting, survey recording, casing inspection and retread job scheduling.
20. The brand is approved and the casing specification matches — otherwise `ERR_BRAND_NOT_APPROVED` or `ERR_CASING_SPEC_MISMATCH`, with the brand checked first. Applies to mounting.
21. The casing, vehicle and tire asset named in one request all belong to the same fleet — otherwise `ERR_FLEET_MISMATCH`. Applies to four operations: tire asset registration, mounting, road service call creation and road service call completion.
22. The assigned technician's home location is the call's dispatching location — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_LOCATION_MISMATCH`. Applies to dispatch.
23. The activated agreement's window does not intersect another active window for the same fleet — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Applies to agreement activation.
24. The requested retread slot does not intersect an occupied interval on the line — otherwise `ERR_RETREAD_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Applies to both scheduling and rescheduling.
25. The slot duration is a whole number of minutes within the permitted band and the end is strictly after the start — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_DURATION`. Applies to both scheduling and rescheduling.
26. A job is not started before its booked slot begins — otherwise `ERR_JOB_START_TOO_EARLY`. Applies to job start.
27. Supplied instants do not regress against the instants already recorded on the same row — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TIMELINE`. Applies to six operations: call dispatch, call arrival, call completion, manifest transport, destruction certification and retread job completion.
28. The billing period is well-formed and not overlong — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_PERIOD`. Applies to invoice creation.
29. Odometer readings do not regress — otherwise `ERR_ODOMETER_REGRESSION`. Applies to odometer posting and survey recording.
30. The wheel position is free — otherwise `ERR_POSITION_OCCUPIED`. Applies to mounting.
31. The fleet is not suspended — otherwise `ERR_FLEET_SUSPENDED`. Applies to road service call creation and retread job scheduling.
32. The retread line is not down — otherwise `ERR_LINE_DOWN`. Applies to scheduling, rescheduling and job start.
33. The hauler load stays within capacity — otherwise `ERR_MANIFEST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Applies to manifest line addition.
34. An explicitly named technician is available — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_UNAVAILABLE`. Applies to dispatch.
35. When no technician is named, the selector finds at least one candidate — otherwise `ERR_NO_TECHNICIAN_AVAILABLE`. Applies to dispatch.
36. An active agreement covers the invoice's anchor date — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_AGREEMENT`. Applies to invoice creation.
37. The resolved line set is non-empty — otherwise `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_LINES`. Applies to invoice creation.
38. The invoice is billable at submit time — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_BILLABLE`. Applies to invoice submission.
39. Payment is for the full amount — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Applies to invoice settlement.
40. The ledger stays non-negative — otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_BALANCE_NEGATIVE`. Applies to invoice submission, casing inspection and destruction certification.
41. An agreement with an unfinished invoice cannot be terminated — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_HAS_OPEN_INVOICE`. Applies to agreement termination.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Dealer Network and the Fleet Program

Before any tire moves, a `DealerAdmin` builds the network. Registering a service location captures its `name`, a short dealer code in `location_code` that must be between 3 and 16 characters long and unique across every location, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the state whose scrap tire rules govern the yard, and a baseline road service response commitment in `road_service_response_minutes_committed` expressed in minutes, which must be at least 15 and at most 480 — in other words, from a quarter of an hour up to a full eight-hour shift. Compliance details are captured at the same time: `scrap_permit_number` records the state hauler or transfer permit the location holds, and `scrap_permit_expires_on` is the ISO 8601 calendar date on which that permit lapses, treated as the last day on which it is still valid. A location that hosts retread production lines is flagged with `is_retread_plant`, which defaults to false. `casings_in_holding_count` starts at 0. Revising a location may change `road_service_response_minutes_committed`, `scrap_permit_number`, `scrap_permit_expires_on` or `is_retread_plant`; each revision increments the location's `version`, and it does so even when the payload carries none of those four fields and nothing actually changes. A revised commitment governs only later calls — a call that has already recorded arrival keeps the `committed_response_minutes` it resolved at the time.

A fleet customer is registered with a `name`, an `account_type` of either `SINGLE_LOCATION` or `NATIONAL_ACCOUNT`, a `home_location_id` naming the service location that owns the commercial relationship, and a `billing_mode` of either `DIRECT` or `CROSS_LOCATION`. `DIRECT` means each location bills its own work separately; `CROSS_LOCATION` means every location's work rolls into a single national-account invoice. New accounts start with `status` of `ACTIVE` and a `casing_credit_balance_cents` of 0. A `DealerAdmin` may later toggle standing between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, supplying both the target `status` and a free-text `status_reason` that is retained on the account; each toggle increments the fleet's `version`. Attempting to move an account into the standing it already holds is rejected with `ERR_FLEET_INVALID_STATE`. Suspension is forward-looking only: work already in flight is untouched, but a suspended fleet may not have a new road service call opened for it and may not have a new retread scheduled — both are rejected with `ERR_FLEET_SUSPENDED`.

**Tire policy is the governing reference for almost every technical decision in the system**, and it is scoped to one wheel position. A policy is created under a fleet account for a `position` of `STEER`, `DRIVE` or `TRAILER`, and at most one policy may exist per fleet-and-position pair; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIRE_POLICY`. The policy carries `target_pressure_psi`, the cold inflation target, which must be greater than 0 and at most 150; `pressure_tolerance_pct`, the allowed absolute deviation fraction from that target before a survey raises a pressure finding, which must be greater than 0 and at most 0.50; `min_tread_depth_32nds`, the legal minimum tread depth in thirty-seconds of an inch, which must be at least 2 and strictly less than the pull point; `pull_point_32nds`, the fleet's own pull threshold in thirty-seconds, which must be strictly greater than the legal minimum and at most 20; `max_retread_count`, the maximum number of prior retreads a casing may already carry and still be booked for another retread at this position, which must be at least 0 and at most 5; `approved_brands`, a non-empty array of brand strings explicitly approved for the position; `new_tire_tread_depth_32nds`, the nominal as-new depth in thirty-seconds used as the mount-time baseline for a replacement, which must be strictly greater than the pull point and at most 32; and `casing_spec_code`, the required casing specification code, which defaults to the wildcard string `ANY`. Revising a policy may change any of those values except `position` — a `position` supplied in a revision is ignored rather than rejected — and increments the policy's `version`; every bound above, including the ordering of `min_tread_depth_32nds`, `pull_point_32nds` and `new_tire_tread_depth_32nds`, is re-checked against the merged result of the revision. Revisions are never retroactive: findings already computed on existing surveys are left exactly as they were, and only later evaluations see the new thresholds.

Fleet units are registered under the fleet account by a `DealerAdmin` or the `FleetManager` itself, carrying a `unit_number` that must be unique within the fleet — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VEHICLE_UNIT` — a `vehicle_type` of `TRACTOR`, `TRAILER` or `STRAIGHT_TRUCK` that later serves as a cost-per-mile grouping dimension, and a starting `current_odometer_miles` of 0 or more. A new unit's `status` is `IN_SERVICE`. Odometer readings are posted separately by a `DealerAdmin`, a `FleetManager` or a `TireTechnician`, supplying `odometer_miles` and the `reading_at` instant the reading was taken; the reading updates `current_odometer_miles`, records `reading_at` as `last_odometer_reading_at`, and increments the vehicle's `version`. Odometer history is monotone: a reading below the vehicle's current `current_odometer_miles` is rejected with `ERR_ODOMETER_REGRESSION`. Note carefully that posting an odometer reading does **not** touch any tire asset's `accumulated_miles` — that figure tracks surveys and only surveys.

Two further rosters complete the setup. Technicians are added to a location's roster with a `name` and a `certification_level` of `LEVEL_1`, `LEVEL_2` or `TIA_CERTIFIED`; a new technician starts `AVAILABLE` with `completed_call_count` at 0, and is dispatchable only from the location whose `service_location_id` they were added under. A `DealerAdmin` may set a technician's `status` administratively: a technician who is `AVAILABLE` or `ON_CALL` may be moved to `OFF_DUTY`, and an `OFF_DUTY` technician may be returned to `AVAILABLE`; setting a technician to the status they already hold is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_INVALID_STATE`, and so is a request naming `ON_CALL`, which is a recognised status value but never an administratively reachable one — only dispatch puts a technician `ON_CALL`. An `OFF_DUTY` technician is excluded from the automatic dispatch candidate pool described in §3.6. Retread production lines are added to a plant with a `line_code` unique within that location — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_CODE` — and an optional `cure_cooldown_minutes` that defaults to 30 and must be between 0 and 240 inclusive. That cooldown is the mandatory rest appended to every scheduled job's occupied interval before the line can take the next job. A new line is `AVAILABLE` with `booked_minutes` at 0. A `DealerAdmin` may set a line's `status` to `DOWN` for maintenance and back to `AVAILABLE`; a `DOWN` line rejects new scheduling, rescheduling and job starts with `ERR_LINE_DOWN`, but already-booked jobs keep their slots and the line's `booked_minutes` does not change when the line goes down or comes back up. Setting a line to the status it already holds is rejected with `ERR_LINE_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.2. National Account Pricing Agreements and Their Windows

A national account agreement is the instrument that prices a fleet's work. A `DealerAdmin` drafts one against a `fleet_account_id`, giving it an `agreement_code` unique across all agreements — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_CODE` — and a closed date window running from `effective_from` through `effective_to`, both ISO 8601 dates and both inclusive: work dated on either endpoint is priced by the agreement. The commercial terms are `tier_discount_pct`, the per-line discount fraction applied to each road service call during invoice rollup, which must be at least 0 and at most 0.50 and is captured with at most three decimals; `casing_credit_rate_cents`, the negotiated base casing credit in cents, which must be at least 0 and at most 100000; `emergency_response_minutes`, the contractual response commitment in minutes for emergency-priority calls, which must be at least 15 and at most 480; and `emergency_labor_rate_cents_per_hour`, the labour rate in cents per hour billed on emergency calls under this agreement, which must be at least 0 and at most 100000.

A freshly drafted agreement is `DRAFT`. A `DRAFT` agreement prices nothing and blocks no window — it is inert. Activation moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, sets `activated_at` to the logical clock, and increments the agreement's `version`. Activation is refused unless `effective_to` falls on or after `effective_from` — a reversed window is a malformed request, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), evaluated after the `DRAFT` state check and before the overlap check — and unless the agreement's closed window fails to intersect the closed window of **every other** agreement for the same fleet that is currently `ACTIVE`. Two closed windows intersect when the new window's `effective_from` falls on or before the existing window's `effective_to` **and** the existing window's `effective_from` falls on or before the new window's `effective_to`. The consequence deserves emphasis: two windows that merely touch on a single day — where the new `effective_from` equals an existing `effective_to` — **do** conflict, and the activation is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. To abut cleanly, the new window must begin at least one calendar day after the existing window ends. Only `ACTIVE` agreements participate in this check; agreements in `DRAFT`, `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED` never block an activation. Activating an agreement that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

An `ACTIVE` agreement leaves the active set in one of two ways. A `DealerAdmin` may terminate it, supplying `terminated_reason`, which moves it `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`. Termination is blocked while any cross-location invoice priced by that agreement is still `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED`, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_HAS_OPEN_INVOICE`; the intent is that a fleet's billing cannot be orphaned mid-cycle. When termination succeeds and the fleet is left holding no other `ACTIVE` agreement, the fleet's `billing_mode` reverts to `DIRECT` and the fleet's `version` increments — a national account that has no agreement is, by definition, no longer cross-location billed. That touch is conditional on the mode actually moving: a fleet already billed `DIRECT` is left exactly as it was and its `version` does not change. The second exit is temporal. When the logical clock is advanced, every agreement that is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` falls strictly before the new clock's UTC date transitions `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, and the identifiers of the agreements so expired are returned in the clock response's `expired_agreement_ids`. Expiry deliberately leaves every cross-location invoice exactly as it was — an invoice already created under an agreement is not re-priced or withdrawn when that agreement lapses.

Agreement resolution is used in three places, and the anchor date differs in each. Casing credit accrual resolves the agreement covering the logical clock's date. Road service pricing resolves the agreement covering the UTC date of the call's `reported_at`. Invoice rollup resolves the agreement covering `billing_period_start`. In every case the agreement must be `ACTIVE` and the anchor date must fall on or after `effective_from` and on or before `effective_to`. Because the overlap rule above forbids two `ACTIVE` windows on the same fleet from intersecting, at most one agreement can ever satisfy that description.

### 3.3. The Mounted-Wheel Program: Mounting, Surveying and Pulling

A tire asset is a tread on a casing — a mounted-wheel assembly the dealer tracks as an individual cost-bearing unit. It is registered by a `DealerAdmin` or a `TireTechnician` against a `fleet_account_id`, a `casing_id` naming the carcass it is built on, a `brand` string for the tread, and a `cost_basis_cents` of 0 or more that records what the assembly cost to acquire or retread. That figure is the numerator input to every cost-per-mile calculation. A newly registered asset sits in `IN_INVENTORY`, with `vehicle_id`, `position`, `mounted_at`, `odometer_at_mount` and `tread_depth_at_mount_32nds` all still empty. The casing named must belong to the same fleet as the asset; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FLEET_MISMATCH`.

Mounting is the moment the wear clock starts, and it is the most heavily gated operation in the program. A `TireTechnician` supplies `vehicle_id`, `position`, `mounted_at`, `odometer_at_mount` — 0 or more — and `tread_depth_at_mount_32nds`, a whole number of thirty-seconds between 0 and 32 inclusive, and the asset advances `IN_INVENTORY → MOUNTED`; an asset in any other status is rejected with `ERR_TIRE_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. The named unit must belong to the same fleet as the asset, or `ERR_FLEET_MISMATCH` applies. A tire policy must exist for that fleet and the requested `position`, otherwise `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_MISSING`. The asset's `brand` must appear in the policy's `approved_brands`, otherwise `ERR_BRAND_NOT_APPROVED`; and the underlying casing's `casing_spec_code` must equal the policy's `casing_spec_code` unless the policy's value is the wildcard `ANY`, otherwise `ERR_CASING_SPEC_MISMATCH` — with brand checked before specification, so an asset failing both reports the brand error. The supplied `tread_depth_at_mount_32nds` must be **strictly greater** than the policy's `min_tread_depth_32nds`; a depth equal to the legal minimum is not mountable and is rejected with `ERR_TREAD_BELOW_POLICY_MINIMUM`. Finally, no other asset may already occupy the same wheel: if another asset on that `vehicle_id` at that `position` is `MOUNTED` or `PULL_FLAGGED`, the mount is rejected with `ERR_POSITION_OCCUPIED`. A successful mount stores all five supplied values, increments the asset's `version`, and — when the underlying casing is sitting in `RETREADED` — cascades that casing `RETREADED → ON_WHEEL`, recomputes the `casings_in_holding_count` of the location named in the casing's `collected_at_location_id`, then clears `collected_at_location_id` and increments the casing's `version`. That cascade is what closes the retread cycle: the carcass is back on a truck and eligible for collection and another retread round.

**Surveys** are how the program learns what is happening at the wheel. A `TireTechnician` records a survey against a mounted asset — the asset must be `MOUNTED` or `PULL_FLAGGED`, and one in any other status rejects the survey with `ERR_TIRE_ASSET_INVALID_STATE` — supplying `surveyed_at`, the measured cold inflation `pressure_psi` between 0 and 200 inclusive, the measured `tread_depth_32nds` between 0 and 32 inclusive, and the vehicle's `odometer_miles` at survey time, which must be 0 or more. The server stamps `surveyed_by_user_id` with the acting technician's identifier. Odometer discipline applies here too: the supplied `odometer_miles` must be at least as large as the greater of the asset's `odometer_at_mount` and the `odometer_miles` of the latest survey already recorded against that asset, and a lower value is rejected with `ERR_ODOMETER_REGRESSION`. A survey also needs the governing policy for the asset's fleet and its current `position`; without one, `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_MISSING`.

The survey's verdict is computed, not supplied. First, `pressure_deviation_pct` is the absolute difference between the measured `pressure_psi` and the policy's `target_pressure_psi`, divided by that same `target_pressure_psi`, rounded half up to four decimal places. Then `finding_code` is resolved by walking four conditions strictly from the top and stopping at the **first** one that holds: it is `TREAD_BELOW_MIN` when the measured `tread_depth_32nds` is strictly less than the policy's `min_tread_depth_32nds`; otherwise `TREAD_AT_PULL_POINT` when the measured `tread_depth_32nds` is less than or equal to the policy's `pull_point_32nds`; otherwise `PRESSURE_DEVIATION` when the rounded `pressure_deviation_pct` is greater than or equal to the policy's `pressure_tolerance_pct`; otherwise `COMPLIANT`. Because evaluation stops at the first match, a tire that is both bald and badly under-inflated reports a tread finding, never a pressure one. `pull_recommended` is true when `finding_code` is either `TREAD_BELOW_MIN` or `TREAD_AT_PULL_POINT`, and false otherwise.

Two cascades follow from the finding. When `pull_recommended` is true and the asset is `MOUNTED`, the asset advances `MOUNTED → PULL_FLAGGED`. When `finding_code` is `COMPLIANT` and the asset is `PULL_FLAGGED`, the flag is cleared and the asset reverts `PULL_FLAGGED → MOUNTED`. The asymmetry is deliberate and must be implemented literally: a later `PRESSURE_DEVIATION` survey does **not** clear a pull flag, because low pressure says nothing about whether the tread has come back. Every survey also refreshes two derived figures on the asset. `current_tread_depth_32nds` becomes the `tread_depth_32nds` of the asset's latest survey, where "latest" means the greatest `surveyed_at` and, among ties on that instant, the last row by `created_at` ascending; when the asset has no survey at all, `current_tread_depth_32nds` falls back to `tread_depth_at_mount_32nds`. `accumulated_miles` becomes that latest survey's `odometer_miles` less the asset's `odometer_at_mount`, floored at 0 so it can never go negative, and is 0 when no survey exists.

Pulling is the ordinary end of a mounted life. A `TireTechnician` supplies a `pull_reason` and the asset moves from `MOUNTED` or `PULL_FLAGGED` to `PULLED`, incrementing its `version`. Both `vehicle_id` and `position` are deliberately **retained** after the pull, so the asset continues to group correctly under the vehicle type and position it actually wore out on when cost-per-mile is computed. A `PULLED` asset has exactly one further destination — `PULLED → SCRAPPED`, which happens only as part of certified scrap destruction, described in §3.7. `SCRAPPED` is terminal. An asset can also be pulled automatically by a road service replacement, in which case `pull_reason` is recorded as the fixed string `ROAD_SERVICE_REPLACEMENT` and the replacement asset it gave way to points back at it through `replaced_tire_asset_id`; that chain is described in §3.6.

### 3.4. Casing Recovery, Grading and Provisional Credit

The casing is the durable asset in this business, and its life is a cycle rather than a line. A casing is registered by a `DealerAdmin` or a `TireTechnician` against a `fleet_account_id`, with a `casing_serial` that must be unique across every casing in the system — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASING_SERIAL` — a `brand`, a `casing_spec_code`, an `intended_position` of `STEER`, `DRIVE` or `TRAILER`, a `scrap_weight_lbs` between 1 and 500 inclusive that later drives manifest capacity and weight totals, and an optional `retread_count` recording retreads already on the carcass, which defaults to 0 and must be between 0 and 5 inclusive. A new casing starts `ON_WHEEL`, with `inspection_grade`, `inspected_by_user_id`, `collected_at_location_id`, `scrap_manifest_id` and `rejection_reason` all empty and `provisional_credit_cents` at 0. It is worth being explicit about one design decision: `intended_position` together with `fleet_account_id` is the **sole** key used to resolve the governing tire policy for grading, retread candidacy and credit. The position a casing happens to have been mounted at is not consulted for those decisions.

Collection is the first step of recovery. A `TireTechnician` supplies `collected_at_location_id`, and the casing advances `ON_WHEEL → COLLECTED`; a casing in any other status is rejected with `ERR_CASING_INVALID_STATE`. Collection is refused while the carcass is still in service: every tire asset built on that casing must be in `IN_INVENTORY`, `PULLED` or `SCRAPPED`, a condition that holds vacuously when no asset references the casing at all; an asset still `MOUNTED` or `PULL_FLAGGED` is reported with the same `ERR_CASING_INVALID_STATE`. The location named must exist. On success the casing records `collected_at_location_id`, moves to `COLLECTED`, causes that location's `casings_in_holding_count` to be recomputed, and increments its own `version`.

Grading is where credit is born. A `CasingInspector` records a `grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `REJECT` together with `expected_casing_version`, the casing `version` the inspector last observed. There are three distinct outcomes. A `COLLECTED` casing graded `A`, `B` or `C` advances `COLLECTED → INSPECTED` and posts an `ACCRUAL` ledger entry for the provisional credit. An already-`INSPECTED` casing graded `A`, `B` or `C` stays in `INSPECTED` — a supersede — and posts **two** entries: first a `REVERSAL` whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the casing's `provisional_credit_cents` as read *before* this request, then a fresh `ACCRUAL` computed under the new grade. A `COLLECTED` casing graded `REJECT` advances `COLLECTED → REJECTED` and posts **no ledger entry whatsoever**, on the principle that a carcass that never graded retreadable never accrued anything to reverse. Those three are exhaustive: grading an already-`INSPECTED` casing `REJECT` is not one of them and is rejected with `ERR_CASING_INVALID_STATE`, condemnation after grading being the separate reject operation below. Each entry a grading writes carries a `source_entity_type` of `CASING` with the casing's own identifier as both `source_entity_id` and `casing_id`, and on a supersede the §2.8 non-negative check is applied twice — first to the balance the `REVERSAL` alone would leave, then to the balance after the fresh `ACCRUAL` — so a supersede whose intermediate balance would go negative is refused even if the final one would not. In all three cases the casing's `inspection_grade` is overwritten with the new grade and `inspected_by_user_id` is stamped with the inspector's identifier — the value the manifest segregation-of-duties check later consumes. Grading requires a tire policy for the casing's fleet and `intended_position`, otherwise `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_MISSING`. A supersede is additionally refused while any retread job on that casing is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, because re-grading a carcass whose snapshot has already been taken for production would leave the job's snapshot describing a grade the casing no longer holds; that refusal is reported as `ERR_CASING_INVALID_STATE`. Every successful grading recomputes the casing's `provisional_credit_cents` and the fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents`, increments the fleet's `version`, and increments the casing's `version`. And, as §2.9 spells out, a mismatched `expected_casing_version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASING_VERSION`.

**How the credit figure is computed.** The base rate is the negotiated `casing_credit_rate_cents` of the fleet's `ACTIVE` national agreement whose closed window covers the logical clock's date; when no such agreement exists, the base rate is the dealer default of 12000 cents — one hundred and twenty dollars per casing. That base is multiplied by a grade factor: 1.00 for grade `A`, 0.70 for grade `B`, 0.40 for grade `C`, and zero for `REJECT`, which is why a rejected casing accrues nothing at all. The product is then multiplied by a retread fatigue decay factor, which is the larger of two quantities: a floor of 0.25, and one minus fifteen hundredths multiplied by the retread count in play. In plain terms, each retread already on the carcass knocks fifteen percent off its credit value, but the value never decays below a quarter of its ungraded worth no matter how many rounds it has survived. The resulting product is rounded half up to whole cents **once**, at the end; none of the three factors is rounded on its own.

That formula runs in two variants that differ only in which inputs they read and whether an uplift applies. The **grade-provisional** variant runs at inspection time, uses the casing's `retread_count` as read at that moment, and applies no uplift; its figure is posted as the `ACCRUAL`. The **production-verified** variant runs once a casing has actually survived a cure, and it deliberately reads the values snapshotted onto the retread job at scheduling — `grade_at_scheduling` in place of the grade and `retread_count_at_scheduling` in place of the retread count — rather than the casing's live values. It then multiplies the raw product by one plus a ten-percent uplift before the single half-up rounding to whole cents. The reason the snapshot must be used is arithmetical rather than stylistic: by completion time the casing's `retread_count` has already been incremented and its `inspection_grade` has already been cleared, so reading live values would change the decay factor and make the delta irreproducible. And the entry posted at completion is the **delta**, never the gross: the `ADJUSTMENT` amount is the production-verified figure minus the grade-provisional figure. Both sides of that subtraction are recomputed at completion time from the same two snapshotted inputs and the same base rate — the rate of the agreement in force on the logical clock's date at completion — so the delta is `round(raw × 1.10) − round(raw)` and is never taken against whatever amount the earlier `ACCRUAL` happened to post.

A casing can also be condemned after grading. A `CasingInspector` may reject an `INSPECTED` casing with a `rejection_reason`, moving it `INSPECTED → REJECTED` and incrementing its `version`; the operation is refused while a retread job on that casing is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`. Note what does **not** happen: no ledger entry is posted, and the accrued credit is deliberately left standing on the fleet balance. The fleet keeps the benefit of a graded carcass right up until the moment its destruction is certified, which is the single point at which the credit is written off — see §3.7.

### 3.5. Booking and Running the Retread Plant

Plant capacity is scarce and a cured casing cannot be rushed, so the schedule is treated as a conserved quantity: whatever a `PlantScheduler` books must be given back exactly when the booking is released, with no drift. A retread job is created against a `casing_id`, a `retread_line_id`, a `tread_design_code` naming the tread pattern to be applied, and a half-open slot running from `scheduled_start_at` inclusive to `scheduled_end_at` exclusive. The job begins life in `SCHEDULED`.

Scheduling is refused for several distinct reasons, each with its own code. The owning fleet must not be suspended, or `ERR_FLEET_SUSPENDED` applies. The casing must be sitting in `INSPECTED` carrying an `inspection_grade` of `A`, `B` or `C`; anything else — an `ON_WHEEL`, `COLLECTED`, `REJECTED`, `RETREAD_QUEUED`, `IN_RETREAD`, `RETREADED` or `SCRAPPED` carcass — is rejected with `ERR_CASING_INVALID_STATE`. A tire policy must exist for the casing's fleet and `intended_position`, otherwise `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_MISSING`. The casing's `retread_count` must be **strictly less than** that policy's `max_retread_count`; a carcass whose count already equals or exceeds the cap has no retread capacity left and is rejected with `ERR_MAX_RETREAD_COUNT_REACHED`. The target line must be `AVAILABLE`, or `ERR_LINE_DOWN` applies. The slot's length in whole minutes, exposed on the job as `slot_minutes`, must be a whole number at least 60 and at most 480 — from one hour up to a full eight-hour shift — with `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at`; a sub-minute boundary or an out-of-band duration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_DURATION`.

**Slot feasibility** is the geometry that keeps the line honest, and it is worth stating carefully because the interval shapes are asymmetric. The jobs that occupy a line are those whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`; jobs that are `COMPLETED`, `FAILED_CURE` or `CANCELLED` have released their slot and are ignored. Each occupying job occupies a half-open interval beginning at its `scheduled_start_at` and ending at its `scheduled_end_at` advanced by the line's `cure_cooldown_minutes` — the cooldown is part of what the existing job holds. The candidate, by contrast, occupies only its own half-open interval from `scheduled_start_at` up to but not including `scheduled_end_at`, because its own cooldown lands *after* it and will be accounted for when the next job is booked behind it. The two intervals conflict when the candidate's start falls strictly before the occupied interval's end **and** the occupied interval's start falls strictly before the candidate's end. The practical consequence: a candidate starting at exactly the instant an existing job's `scheduled_end_at` plus `cure_cooldown_minutes` falls is **accepted**, while a candidate starting one minute earlier is rejected with `ERR_RETREAD_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Note that this half-open timestamp geometry is deliberately different from the closed date geometry that governs agreement windows in §3.2, where merely touching counts as an overlap.

A newly booked job opens in `SCHEDULED` with a `reschedule_count` of 0. A successful booking does three things beyond persisting the slot. It snapshots the casing's current grade into `grade_at_scheduling` and its current retread count into `retread_count_at_scheduling` — the pinned inputs the production-verified credit and the warranty term will both read later. It cascades the casing `INSPECTED → RETREAD_QUEUED`. And it adds the job's `slot_minutes` plus the line's `cure_cooldown_minutes` to the line's `booked_minutes`.

Putting a job into production requires `started_at`. The job must be `SCHEDULED`, the line must still be `AVAILABLE` — a line that went down between booking and start rejects the start with `ERR_LINE_DOWN` — and `started_at` must fall on or after the job's `scheduled_start_at`; an earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_JOB_START_TOO_EARLY`. The job advances `SCHEDULED → IN_PRODUCTION` and cascades the casing `RETREAD_QUEUED → IN_RETREAD`. The line's `booked_minutes` is unchanged, because a job in production is still in the booked set.

**Completion is the deepest cascade in the system.** A `PlantScheduler` supplies `completed_at` and the `cost_basis_cents` that the retreaded product cost to produce. The job must be `IN_PRODUCTION`, and `completed_at` must fall on or after the job's `scheduled_start_at` — an earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIMELINE`. Five hops then run in order. In hop 1 the job advances `IN_PRODUCTION → COMPLETED`, records `completed_at`, computes `warranty_months` and `warranty_expires_on`, and mints a brand-new tire asset in `IN_INVENTORY` carrying the casing's fleet as its `fleet_account_id`, the casing's identifier as its `casing_id`, the casing's `brand` as its `brand` and the supplied `cost_basis_cents`; the identifier of that new asset is stored on the job as `produced_tire_asset_id`. In hop 2, because the job completed, the casing advances `IN_RETREAD → RETREADED`, its `retread_count` increases by 1 and its `inspection_grade` is cleared to empty. In hop 3, because the casing's retread count changed, an `ADJUSTMENT` ledger entry is posted for the production-verified figure minus the grade-provisional figure as defined in §3.4 — carrying a `source_entity_type` of `RETREAD_JOB`, this job's identifier as `source_entity_id` and the casing's identifier as `casing_id` — and the casing's `provisional_credit_cents` is recomputed. In hop 4, because a ledger entry was posted, the fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents` is recomputed and the fleet's `version` increases by 1. In hop 5, because the fleet's balance moved, **every** cross-location invoice of that fleet whose `status` is `OPEN` recomputes its `credit_applied_cents` — the smaller of the fleet's new balance and the same 25-percent-of-`net_before_credit_cents` cap, floored at 0 — and its `total_cents` as `net_before_credit_cents` less that figure, and increments its own `version`; those invoices' `subtotal_cents`, `discount_cents` and `net_before_credit_cents` are deliberately left untouched, because the lines and the tier discount have not changed — only the credit available to offset them has. Separately, completion releases the job's slot from the line's `booked_minutes`.

**Warranty on the retreaded product** is a tiered term computed from the snapshot, not from the casing's live state. The base term is 36 months when `grade_at_scheduling` is `A`, 24 months when it is `B`, and 12 months when it is `C`. From that base, six months are deducted for each retread the carcass already carried at scheduling time, as recorded in `retread_count_at_scheduling`. The result is then floored at six months, so no retreaded tire ever carries a warranty shorter than half a year regardless of how many rounds the casing has been through. `warranty_expires_on` is that many calendar months added to the UTC date of `completed_at`; when the target month has fewer days than the source day-of-month, the day is clamped to the last day of the target month, so a thirty-one-month term landing on a thirty-day month expires on the thirtieth.

Cures fail, and the `failure_reason` forks three genuinely different outcomes. Recording a cure failure moves the job `IN_PRODUCTION → FAILED_CURE`, stores the `failure_reason`, accepts an optional free-text `note`, and releases the job's slot from the line's `booked_minutes` — that release happens in **all three** branches. When `failure_reason` is `CURE_DEFECT`, the casing reverts `IN_RETREAD → INSPECTED`, keeping both its grade and its accrued credit so it is immediately re-schedulable, and the line's `status` is untouched. When `failure_reason` is `LINE_FAULT`, the casing likewise reverts `IN_RETREAD → INSPECTED` keeping grade and credit, **and in addition** the line is taken down, moving `AVAILABLE → DOWN`, because the fault was the plant's, not the carcass's; a line that is already `DOWN` is simply left as it is, with no error and no further version bump. When `failure_reason` is `CASING_FAILURE`, the carcass itself is condemned: the casing moves `IN_RETREAD → REJECTED` with its `rejection_reason` set to the fixed string `CASING_FAILURE_IN_PRODUCTION`, no ledger entry is posted — credit is written off only at certified destruction — and the line's `status` is untouched.

`FAILED_CURE` is not terminal. A `PlantScheduler` may re-book the job into a new slot by supplying a fresh `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, and optionally a different `retread_line_id`. The job must be `FAILED_CURE`; the casing must be sitting in `INSPECTED`, which is exactly why a job that failed with `CASING_FAILURE` can never be rescheduled — its carcass is `REJECTED`, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CASING_INVALID_STATE`. The target line must be `AVAILABLE` and the new slot must pass the same duration and feasibility checks described above, with one refinement: the job being rescheduled is excluded from the occupying set, so a job may be re-booked into a slot overlapping its own former one. On success the job returns `FAILED_CURE → SCHEDULED` with the new slot fields, adopts the supplied `retread_line_id` when one was given, increments `reschedule_count` by 1, cascades the casing `INSPECTED → RETREAD_QUEUED`, and adds the new slot's minutes plus the target line's cooldown to that line's `booked_minutes`.

Cancellation ends a job in `CANCELLED`, which together with `COMPLETED` is terminal. A `reason` is supplied. From `SCHEDULED`, cancellation cascades the casing `RETREAD_QUEUED → INSPECTED` — the grade and the accrued credit are untouched, so the carcass simply returns to the queue of retreadable candidates — and releases the job's slot from the line's `booked_minutes`, restoring that figure to exactly its pre-booking value with zero drift. From `FAILED_CURE`, cancellation leaves the casing in whatever state the failure left it (`INSPECTED` after a cure defect or line fault, `REJECTED` after a casing failure) and touches `booked_minutes` not at all, because the failure already released the slot. Cancelling a job that is `IN_PRODUCTION`, `COMPLETED` or already `CANCELLED` is rejected with `ERR_RETREAD_JOB_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.6. Road Service: Dispatch, Arrival, Pricing and Closure

A road service call is unusual in this system in that it advances along **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `dispatch_status` tracks the operational reality of getting a technician to a disabled truck and runs through `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` and then either `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. `billing_status` tracks money and runs through `UNBILLED`, `INVOICED`, `BILLED_TO_NATIONAL` and `SETTLED`, with `NON_BILLABLE` as an alternative outcome. The two dimensions evolve separately and neither implies the other; the single exception is noted at the end of this subsection. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal on the dispatch dimension, and `NON_BILLABLE` and `SETTLED` are terminal on the billing dimension.

A `DispatchCoordinator` or a `FleetManager` opens a call for a disabled unit, supplying `fleet_account_id`, `vehicle_id`, the dispatching `service_location_id`, a `priority` of either `NORMAL` or `EMERGENCY`, a free-text `complaint` describing the reported failure, and `reported_at`, the instant the fleet reported the breakdown — which is where the response clock starts. The fleet must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_FLEET_SUSPENDED`; and the unit must belong to that fleet, otherwise `ERR_FLEET_MISMATCH`. A new call starts with `dispatch_status` of `OPEN` and `billing_status` of `UNBILLED`, with `technician_id`, `dispatched_at`, `arrived_at`, `closed_at`, `dispatch_miles`, `labor_hours`, `committed_response_minutes`, `actual_response_minutes`, `sla_met`, `gross_cents`, `invoice_total_cents`, `cancel_reason` and `cross_location_invoice_id` all empty and `sla_credit_cents` reading 0. Opening the call already takes the unit `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, because the vehicle rule stated under dispatch keys on the existence of a live call rather than on the dispatch step itself.

Dispatching advances the call `OPEN → DISPATCHED` and is reserved to the `DispatchCoordinator`. The coordinator supplies `dispatched_at`, which must fall on or after `reported_at` — an earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIMELINE` — and `dispatch_miles`, the round-trip service mileage, which must be at least 0 and at most 2000. A `technician_id` may be named explicitly, in which case that technician's `service_location_id` must equal the call's `service_location_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_LOCATION_MISMATCH`, and that technician's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_UNAVAILABLE`. On success the call records `technician_id`, `dispatched_at` and `dispatch_miles`, the assigned technician moves `AVAILABLE → ON_CALL`, and the unit's `status` becomes `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — a vehicle reads `OUT_OF_SERVICE` for exactly as long as some call for it has a `dispatch_status` of `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE`. The `billing_status` is untouched by dispatch.

**Automatic technician selection.** When `technician_id` is omitted, the server picks one. The eligible pool is the technicians whose `service_location_id` equals the call's `service_location_id` and whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`; technicians at other locations are never considered, however idle they may be, and an `OFF_DUTY` or `ON_CALL` technician is never eligible. On top of that, when the call's `priority` is `EMERGENCY`, only technicians whose `certification_level` is `TIA_CERTIFIED` remain eligible; on a `NORMAL` call any certification tier qualifies. The surviving pool is ranked by certification descending, treating `TIA_CERTIFIED` as rank 3, `LEVEL_2` as rank 2 and `LEVEL_1` as rank 1, so the most highly certified technician wins. Where two candidates tie on certification, the one with the smaller `completed_call_count` wins — the roster self-levels. Where they still tie, the technician whose `created_at` is earliest wins. If the eligible pool is empty the dispatch is refused with `ERR_NO_TECHNICIAN_AVAILABLE` and the call remains `OPEN`, ready to be retried when someone frees up.

Arrival is recorded by the assigned `TireTechnician` and advances the call `DISPATCHED → ON_SITE`. The technician supplies `arrived_at`, which must fall on or after `dispatched_at`, and which stops the response clock. Three figures are resolved at this moment. `committed_response_minutes` starts from the dispatching location's `road_service_response_minutes_committed`. On an `EMERGENCY` call for which an `ACTIVE` national agreement covers the UTC date of `reported_at`, the commitment is the **smaller** of that location baseline and the agreement's `emergency_response_minutes` — the contract can tighten the promise but never loosen it. On an `EMERGENCY` call with no covering agreement, the commitment is the location baseline unchanged. On a `NORMAL` call, the commitment is the location baseline plus a grace allowance of one hour, that is 60 minutes. `actual_response_minutes` is the elapsed time from `reported_at` to `arrived_at`, taken in seconds, divided by 60 and truncated down to a whole number of minutes. `sla_met` is true when `actual_response_minutes` is less than or equal to `committed_response_minutes`; arriving on exactly the committed minute **counts as met**.

Closing the call is where the money is computed. The assigned `TireTechnician` supplies `closed_at`, which must fall on or after `arrived_at`, and `labor_hours`, the billable labour recorded on site, which must be at least 0, at most 24, and a multiple of 0.25. Pricing then proceeds in exactly this order. The labour rate is the agreement's `emergency_labor_rate_cents_per_hour` when the call's `priority` is `EMERGENCY` **and** a covering `ACTIVE` agreement exists; in every other case it is the standard rate of 11000 cents per hour, that is one hundred and ten dollars an hour. The labour charge is that rate multiplied by `labor_hours`, rounded half up to whole cents. The mileage charge is 285 cents per mile — two dollars and eighty-five cents — multiplied by `dispatch_miles`, which needs no rounding because both operands are whole numbers. Whether the call was after hours is decided by the UTC hour of `dispatched_at`: it is after hours when that hour is earlier than 6 or is 18 or later, meaning the window from six in the evening through to six in the morning. Two multipliers are then in play — 1.75 when the `priority` is `EMERGENCY` and otherwise 1.00, and 1.35 when the call was after hours and otherwise 1.00 — and the multiplier actually used is the **larger** of the two. The multipliers do not stack: an after-hours emergency is priced at 1.75, not at 1.75 times 1.35. The surcharge is one less than that multiplier, multiplied by the sum of the base service call charge, the labour charge and the mileage charge, rounded half up to whole cents, and then capped at 40000 cents — four hundred dollars — taking whichever is smaller. `gross_cents` is the sum of the base service call charge of 9500 cents (ninety-five dollars), the labour charge, the mileage charge and the surcharge. `sla_credit_cents` is 20 percent of `gross_cents` rounded half up to whole cents when `sla_met` is false, and exactly 0 when `sla_met` is true — the fleet is credited a fifth of the call for a missed commitment. `invoice_total_cents` is `gross_cents` less `sla_credit_cents`, and that per-line figure is what invoice rollup consumes.

Closing the call also releases the people and the truck: the call advances `ON_SITE → COMPLETED` with `closed_at` recorded, the assigned technician moves `ON_CALL → AVAILABLE` and has `completed_call_count` recomputed, and the unit returns to `IN_SERVICE`.

A road service call is frequently the moment a failed assembly comes off the truck, and the technician may record that in the same request. When `replaced_tire_asset_id` is supplied, that asset must be `MOUNTED` or `PULL_FLAGGED` and its `vehicle_id` must equal the call's `vehicle_id`; either failure is reported as `ERR_TIRE_ASSET_INVALID_STATE`. A `replacement_casing_id` supplied without a `replaced_tire_asset_id` is ignored, and the remaining `replacement_*` fields with it. Three further hops then run. In hop 1 the replaced asset moves from `MOUNTED` or `PULL_FLAGGED` to `PULLED` with its `pull_reason` set to the fixed string `ROAD_SERVICE_REPLACEMENT`; and if `replacement_casing_id` is also supplied, a fresh tire asset is minted on that casing and mounted immediately on the same `vehicle_id` at the same `position` the failed assembly occupied, taking `replacement_brand` as its `brand` and `replacement_cost_basis_cents` as its `cost_basis_cents`, with `odometer_at_mount` set from the unit's `current_odometer_miles` and `tread_depth_at_mount_32nds` set from the governing policy's `new_tire_tread_depth_32nds`, and carrying `replaced_tire_asset_id` pointing back at the assembly it succeeded. That minted assembly takes the call's `closed_at` as its `mounted_at`; `replacement_brand` and `replacement_cost_basis_cents` are optional and are left empty on it when omitted; and when the replacement casing was itself sitting in `RETREADED` it cascades `RETREADED → ON_WHEEL` with its `collected_at_location_id` cleared, exactly as an ordinary mount does. The governing policy consulted for `new_tire_tread_depth_32nds` is the one for the call's fleet and the failed assembly's `position`, and its absence refuses the whole completion with `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_MISSING` — a check that runs after the `closed_at` timeline check and only when a `replacement_casing_id` was actually supplied. In hop 2, because the replaced asset became `PULLED`, its casing is automatically collected — the casing advances `ON_WHEEL → COLLECTED` with `collected_at_location_id` set to the call's `service_location_id`, on the plain operational logic that the road service technician carries the failed carcass back to the yard they came from. In hop 3, because that casing entered the holding set, the `casings_in_holding_count` of that location is recomputed. Any casing, vehicle and tire asset named across one such request must all belong to the same fleet, or `ERR_FLEET_MISMATCH` applies.

Completion deliberately leaves `billing_status` at `UNBILLED`. Becoming `COMPLETED` makes the call *eligible* for the next invoice rollup; it does not by itself move the billing dimension one step.

Cancellation is the one place the dispatch dimension reaches across into the billing dimension. A `DispatchCoordinator` may cancel a call that is `OPEN` or `DISPATCHED`, supplying `cancel_reason`; a call that has reached `ON_SITE`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` cannot be cancelled and is rejected with `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation sets `dispatch_status` to `CANCELLED`, releases any assigned technician back to `AVAILABLE`, returns the unit to `IN_SERVICE`, and sets `billing_status` to `NON_BILLABLE` — a call that never happened is never billed, and `NON_BILLABLE` is terminal.

### 3.7. Scrap Tire Disposal Compliance

Scrap tires are a regulated waste stream, and the manifest is the document that proves the dealer handed a specific set of carcasses to a permitted hauler who delivered them to a permitted processor. A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a manifest under the location generating the load, supplying the `hauler_permit_number` of the hauler who will take it and the `hauler_capacity_lbs` that the assigned hauler load may gross, which must be at least 1 and at most 40000 pounds. The server mints a document number into `manifest_number` of the form `SM-` followed by six digits, drawn from a single server-wide counter whose first value is `SM-000001`. The manifest starts in `DRAFT` with `tire_count` and `total_weight_lbs` both at 0.

Adding a casing to a `DRAFT` manifest identifies it by `casing_id`. Four things are checked. The manifest must still be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_MANIFEST_INVALID_STATE`. The casing's `status` must be `REJECTED`, because only a condemned carcass is scrap; anything else is rejected with `ERR_CASING_NOT_SCRAP_ELIGIBLE`. The casing's `scrap_manifest_id` must be empty, since a carcass cannot be on two loads at once; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_CASING_ALREADY_ON_MANIFEST`. And the load must stay within the truck: the manifest's current `total_weight_lbs` plus the candidate casing's `scrap_weight_lbs` must not exceed `hauler_capacity_lbs`. The comparison is strict — a load landing on **exactly** the capacity is accepted, and only a load that would push the total strictly above it is rejected with `ERR_MANIFEST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the casing's `scrap_manifest_id` is set and its own `version` increments, the manifest's `tire_count` and `total_weight_lbs` are recomputed from the casings now linked to it, and the manifest's `version` increments; the response body is the updated manifest, returned with HTTP 200 rather than 201, since linking a casing creates no new resource.

Signing is the compliance gate, and it enforces four conditions in a fixed order after the manifest's own state has been checked. First the permit: the signing location's `scrap_permit_expires_on` must fall on or after the logical clock's UTC date — a permit lapsing today is still good today, and one that lapsed yesterday rejects the signature with `ERR_SCRAP_PERMIT_EXPIRED`. Second, segregation of duties: the signing officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `inspected_by_user_id` of **every** casing listed on the manifest, so that the person who graded a carcass into a credit accrual cannot also be the person who signs the paperwork that will write it off; a collision is rejected with `ERR_SOD_INSPECTOR_CANNOT_SIGN`. Third, every listed casing must still be `REJECTED`, otherwise `ERR_CASING_NOT_SCRAP_ELIGIBLE`. Fourth, the manifest must not be empty — `tire_count` must be at least 1, and a manifest with nothing on it is rejected with `ERR_MANIFEST_EMPTY`. Note that the emptiness check comes after the eligibility check, which an empty manifest passes trivially. A successful signature advances the manifest `DRAFT → SIGNED`, records `signed_by_user_id` from the acting officer and `signed_at` from the supplied instant, and increments the manifest's `version`.

Recording hauler pickup advances the manifest `SIGNED → TRANSPORTED`. The officer supplies `transported_at`, which must fall on or after `signed_at`, and `hauler_vehicle_id`, a free-text identifier for the hauler's truck — it is deliberately not a reference to any fleet unit in this system.

**Certifying destruction is the point at which accrued casing credit leaves the fleet balance.** The officer supplies `certified_at`, which must fall on or after `transported_at`, and `destruction_facility_code` identifying the processor. Segregation of duties applies again: the certifying officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the manifest's `signed_by_user_id`, so that two distinct officers are structurally required per load; a collision is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SIGNER_CANNOT_CERTIFY`. Four hops then run. In hop 1 the manifest advances `TRANSPORTED → CERTIFIED_DESTROYED` and records `certified_by_user_id`, `certified_at` and `destruction_facility_code`. In hop 2, because the manifest was certified, every listed casing advances `REJECTED → SCRAPPED`, and every tire asset built on one of those casings whose `status` is `PULLED` advances `PULLED → SCRAPPED` alongside it. In hop 3, for each listed casing carrying a non-zero `provisional_credit_cents` — the figure read before hop 2 ran, so the write-off follows the credit rather than the status change — an `EXPIRY` ledger entry is posted whose `amount_cents` is the negation of that casing's `provisional_credit_cents`, with `source_entity_type` of `SCRAP_MANIFEST`, `source_entity_id` naming this manifest and `casing_id` naming the casing — one entry per such casing, in the casings' `created_at` ascending order. In hop 4, because ledger entries were posted, the fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents` is recomputed and the fleet's `version` increments, and the `casings_in_holding_count` of every affected location is recomputed, since a `SCRAPPED` carcass is no longer in holding. Nothing constrains a load to one customer: when the manifest lists casings of several fleets, both the §2.8 non-negative check and this recomputation run once per fleet that actually carried credit, on that fleet's own summed delta, and a fleet none of whose listed casings carried credit is not touched at all. Both `CERTIFIED_DESTROYED` and `VOIDED` are terminal for a manifest, and `SCRAPPED` is terminal for a casing.

A manifest that has not yet been transported can be abandoned. Voiding a `DRAFT` or `SIGNED` manifest records the supplied `void_reason`, advances the manifest to `VOIDED`, and clears the `scrap_manifest_id` of every casing that was listed, each such casing's `version` incrementing as the link is broken — with the casings' own statuses left completely unchanged, so a condemned carcass returns to the pool of scrap-eligible carcasses rather than being resurrected. Both `tire_count` and `total_weight_lbs` fall to 0 as a consequence of the unlinking. When the manifest being voided was already `SIGNED`, the void additionally clears `signed_by_user_id` and `signed_at`, because the signature no longer attests to anything. Voiding a `TRANSPORTED`, `CERTIFIED_DESTROYED` or already-`VOIDED` manifest is rejected with `ERR_MANIFEST_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.8. Cross-Location Billing and Credit Consumption

A national account expects one bill covering work done at every location, priced under one agreement. A `BillingClerk` creates that bill by supplying `fleet_account_id`, `billing_period_start`, `billing_period_end` and `expected_fleet_account_version`. The billing period is a closed date interval: `billing_period_end` must fall on or after `billing_period_start`, and the inclusive span must be no longer than 92 days — roughly a calendar quarter — with anything else rejected as `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_PERIOD`. The pricing agreement is resolved as the fleet's `ACTIVE` national agreement whose closed window covers `billing_period_start`, and its identifier is stored on the invoice as `national_agreement_id`; when no such agreement exists the creation is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_AGREEMENT`.

The lines the invoice claims are the road service calls of that fleet whose `dispatch_status` is `COMPLETED`, whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED`, and whose `closed_at` falls, by UTC date, on or after `billing_period_start` and on or before `billing_period_end` — both ends inclusive. They are taken in `closed_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Cancelled calls, which sit at `NON_BILLABLE`, are excluded, and so is any call already claimed by another invoice at `INVOICED`, `BILLED_TO_NATIONAL` or `SETTLED`. When that resolution yields nothing, the creation is refused with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_LINES` rather than producing a zero invoice.

The rollup then proceeds in four pinned steps. **First**, for each line, a per-line discount is computed as that call's `invoice_total_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `tier_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. **Second**, the aggregates: `subtotal_cents` is the sum of the lines' `invoice_total_cents`; `discount_cents` is the sum of the **already-rounded** per-line discounts; and `net_before_credit_cents` is `subtotal_cents` less `discount_cents`. Rounding the aggregate instead — taking `subtotal_cents` times `tier_discount_pct` and rounding once — is forbidden, because the two answers differ by a cent whenever the per-line remainders round up but the aggregate remainder does not. **Third**, the credit cap: the cap is 25 percent of `net_before_credit_cents`, truncated down to a whole number of cents, and `credit_applied_cents` is the smaller of the fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents` and that cap. The cap is deliberately keyed on `net_before_credit_cents` and not on `total_cents`; keying it on `total_cents` would be circular, since `total_cents` is itself derived from the credit. **Fourth**, `total_cents` is `net_before_credit_cents` less `credit_applied_cents`. `line_count` records how many calls the invoice claims.

Creation also claims its lines and touches the fleet. Each resolved call advances `billing_status` from `UNBILLED` to `INVOICED` and records the invoice's identifier in its `cross_location_invoice_id`. The fleet's `version` increments by 1. The invoice itself starts in `OPEN`. And, as §2.9 requires, a mismatched `expected_fleet_account_version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FLEET_VERSION` before any of this happens.

Submitting an `OPEN` invoice hands it to the national account and is the moment the credit is actually consumed. The invoice's referenced agreement must still be `ACTIVE` and the invoice's `total_cents` must be greater than 0; either failure is reported as `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_BILLABLE`. The invoice advances `OPEN → SUBMITTED`. When `credit_applied_cents` is greater than 0, an `APPLICATION` ledger entry is posted whose `amount_cents` is the negation of `credit_applied_cents`, carrying a null `casing_id`, a `source_entity_type` of `CROSS_LOCATION_INVOICE` and this invoice's identifier as `source_entity_id`; when `credit_applied_cents` is 0, no entry is posted at all. The fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents` is then recomputed and the fleet's `version` increments — that increment is unconditional, so it happens even on a submission that posted no entry because `credit_applied_cents` was 0. Every claimed call cascades `INVOICED → BILLED_TO_NATIONAL`. If the application would drive the fleet's balance below zero the whole submission is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_BALANCE_NEGATIVE` and nothing is written.

Settlement records payment in full. The clerk supplies `paid_amount_cents`, which must equal the invoice's `total_cents` exactly; a partial or excess amount is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. The invoice advances `SUBMITTED → PAID` and records the amount, and every claimed call cascades `BILLED_TO_NATIONAL → SETTLED`.

Dispute is the **compensating action** for submit, and it is available to either a `FleetManager` or a `BillingClerk`. A `dispute_reason` is supplied and the invoice advances `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED`. When `credit_applied_cents` is greater than 0, a `REVERSAL` ledger entry is posted whose `amount_cents` is the positive value of `credit_applied_cents` — returning to the ledger exactly what the submission took from it — after which the invoice's `credit_applied_cents` is set to 0 and its `total_cents` is set equal to `net_before_credit_cents`. The fleet's `casing_credit_balance_cents` is recomputed and the fleet's `version` increments — again unconditionally, so a dispute of an invoice that had applied no credit still bumps the fleet even though no `REVERSAL` was written. Every claimed call cascades `BILLED_TO_NATIONAL → UNBILLED` and has its `cross_location_invoice_id` cleared, releasing it to be picked up by a later invoice; the calls' `dispatch_status` remains `COMPLETED` throughout, since disputing a bill does not un-repair a truck. The design guarantee here is that the summed ledger returns to its exact pre-submission value with zero drift. Both `PAID` and `DISPUTED` are terminal for an invoice; submitting an invoice that is not `OPEN`, or settling or disputing one that is not `SUBMITTED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.9. Cost-per-Mile Analytics

The question a fleet manager actually asks is not what a tire cost but what a mile costs, broken out by the dimensions the fleet can act on. A `DealerAdmin` or a `FleetManager` requests the analytic for one fleet, supplying a required `variant` of either `HISTORICAL_ACTUAL` or `FORWARD_PROJECTED`, and optionally narrowing the asset set with `brand`, `position` or `vehicle_type` before any grouping happens. The read never mutates a tire asset or a survey.

The candidate assets are that fleet's tire assets whose `status` is `MOUNTED`, `PULL_FLAGGED` or `PULLED`; assets sitting in `IN_INVENTORY` have not run a mile, and `SCRAPPED` assets have left the program, so both are excluded outright. For each candidate, the latest survey is the one with the greatest `surveyed_at`, taking the last row by `created_at` ascending among ties. From it, the miles the asset has run are that survey's `odometer_miles` less the asset's `odometer_at_mount`, floored at 0, and taken as 0 when the asset has no survey at all. The tread it has consumed is the asset's `tread_depth_at_mount_32nds` less that survey's `tread_depth_32nds`. The governing policy is the one for the fleet and the asset's `position`, and the asset's usable tread is its `tread_depth_at_mount_32nds` less that policy's `pull_point_32nds` — the depth the fleet is actually willing to wear away.

One exclusion applies to **both** variants and removes an asset from numerator and denominator alike: an asset whose miles run are 0, or whose consumed tread is 0 or negative, is dropped and counted in `excluded_asset_count`. Those are assets with no usable wear signal — freshly mounted, never surveyed, or surveyed at a depth no shallower than the mount baseline — and including them would either divide by zero or fabricate a wear rate.

Surviving assets are grouped by the triple of `brand`, `position` and the `vehicle_type` of the unit the asset is on, and the groups are emitted sorted by `brand` ascending, then `position` ascending, then `vehicle_type` ascending. Under the `HISTORICAL_ACTUAL` variant, a group's cost per mile is the sum of its assets' `cost_basis_cents` divided by the sum of their miles run, rounded half up to four decimal places. Under the `FORWARD_PROJECTED` variant, an asset whose `position` has no policy for the fleet is dropped and counted in `excluded_asset_count` rather than failing the read with `ERR_TIRE_POLICY_MISSING` — a policy is consulted only under this variant, so the same asset set reads without exclusion under `HISTORICAL_ACTUAL`. Each surviving asset first gets a wear rate per mile, which is its consumed tread divided by its miles run, kept as an exact rational and never rounded; its projected life in miles is its usable tread divided by that wear rate, truncated down to a whole number of miles; and the group's cost per mile is the sum of its assets' `cost_basis_cents` divided by the sum of those projected lives, rounded half up to four decimal places. The two variants answer different questions on the same asset set: the historical figure makes a nearly-new tire look expensive because it has barely earned its cost back yet, while the projected figure divides by the whole life the observed wear rate implies and therefore reads materially lower.

**The rounding order is pinned.** The division is performed exactly once per group, on the summed numerator and the summed denominator. Per-asset cost-per-mile figures are never computed, never rounded and never averaged — averaging rounded per-asset figures gives a different and wrong answer. A group with no included assets is omitted entirely rather than emitted with a zero, and when no group survives at all the response carries a `group_count` of 0 and an empty `groups` array. The response echoes the requested `variant` alongside `group_count`, `excluded_asset_count` and `groups`, `group_count` being the number of groups actually emitted. Each group carries its `brand`, `position` and `vehicle_type` — the last null for an asset on no unit — its `asset_count`, its summed cost basis under both the names `cost_basis_cents` and `total_cost_basis_cents`, its summed miles run under both `miles_run` and `total_miles_run`, its summed projected life under both `projected_life_miles` and `total_projected_life_miles`, and the rounded quotient under both `cpm_cents` and `cost_per_mile_cents`; the paired names are aliases carrying identical values, and the projected-life pair reads 0 under `HISTORICAL_ACTUAL`, where no projection is computed.
