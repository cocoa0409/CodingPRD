# Inland Marine Equipment Floater Underwriting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An inland marine underwriting unit prices and services property that will not sit still. Contractors equipment moves between jobsites; installation work is covered only until the owner accepts it or moves in; cargo travels on conveyances the insured owns and on conveyances it hires; and warehoused stock is insured on a reporting form where the insured, not the underwriter, declares the values at risk each period. Every one of those exposures is priced on a different basis, and the same physical unit can be worth four different amounts depending on whether the policy values it at actual cash value, at replacement cost, at an agreed value, or at a stated amount. The unit currently reconciles all of this in spreadsheets, which means that the answer to "what is this policy worth on a blanket basis instead of a scheduled basis?" takes a day to produce and is wrong as soon as an item is endorsed on.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. The service owns the full underwriting record: the actuarial rate table from which contractors equipment rates are developed by class, territory and age; the policy and its coverage parts; the scheduled units, installation projects, conveyances, shipments and periodic value reports hanging off those parts; the jobsites whose theft controls earn or lose credit; and the claims that consume the aggregate capacity the parts sold. It is a pricing engine first — every write that touches an exposure re-develops the rate, re-rolls the part, and re-rolls the policy — and a temporal system second, because almost every rule in the domain turns on a date comparison: a policy term, a closed coverage interval, a half-open transit window, a reporting grace date, a carrier approval expiry.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. The first is that money is never approximated: every amount is an integer number of cents, every rate and factor is an integer number of basis points, and each formula is evaluated as one exact rational expression that is rounded exactly once, at exactly the point this document names. The second is segregation of duties: the underwriter who refers a policy for authority may not decide that referral, and the adjuster who declares a loss a total loss may not authorise the cheque. Both are enforced on the acting user's identity, not on a flag the client sets.

All resources described here are mounted under the `/inland-marine` prefix, with the sole exception of the administrative clock endpoint documented in §4.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the service: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and versioning, arithmetic precision, stale-version guards, validation precedence, and the conserved quantities that the ledgers and aggregate limits maintain.

### 2.1. Underwriting Roles and Request Authentication

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent or blank, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The service recognises exactly five roles, and `X-User-Role` must name one of them verbatim: `Actuary`, `Underwriter`, `UnderwritingManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `Policyholder`. A role string outside that set of five is also `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), not a permission failure. Once the headers are accepted, the caller's role must appear in the authorised-role list published for the endpoint in §4; a caller whose role is not listed there is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles divide the work as follows. An `Actuary` owns rate development: building and publishing the contractors-equipment rate cells that rating selects from, plus reading those cells and querying the equipment they have priced. An `Underwriter` builds and prices the policy — coverage parts, equipment schedules, installation floaters, conveyances, shipments, jobsite surveys — and drives the policy through quoting, referral, binding and activation, as well as adjudicating value reports. An `UnderwritingManager` holds the authorities the underwriter must not hold: deciding referrals, cancelling policies, authorising claim payments, and setting the service's logical clock. It never rates and never adjusts. A `ClaimsAdjuster` owns loss adjustment — inspection, the choice between repair and total loss, and the salvage disposition — but cannot authorise the payment it recommends. A `Policyholder` is the named insured itself: it files periodic value reports, reports losses on its own policy, and reads its own premium picture.

Four of the five roles are operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `Actuary`, `Underwriter`, `UnderwritingManager` or `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `referred_by_user_id` and `referral_decided_by_user_id` on a policy, or as `inspected_by_user_id`, `declared_by_user_id` and `settled_by_user_id` on a claim) and may compare two such recorded values against each other for segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`. The fifth role is entity-backed: when `X-User-Role` is `Policyholder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting policy, and ownership checks on any resource that references a policy compare the resource's policy — directly, or transitively through its coverage part — to `X-User-ID`. A `Policyholder` acting on a resource that belongs to some other policy is rejected with `ERR_NOT_POLICY_OWNER` (HTTP 403). That ownership check guards exactly seven operations: filing a value report on a coverage part, reporting a loss on a policy, reading a policy, reading a policy's premium summary, listing a policy's coverage parts, listing a policy's ledger entries, and reading a claim.

Segregation of duties is enforced at two points, both with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A referral decision is rejected when the deciding manager's `X-User-ID` equals the `referred_by_user_id` recorded when the referral was raised. A claim settlement is rejected when the settling manager's `X-User-ID` equals the `declared_by_user_id` recorded when the adjustment basis was declared.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and List Filters

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two query-string parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is negative or not an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

List endpoints additionally accept exact-match filter parameters drawn from the fields of the resource being listed. Across the service those filters are `part_type` on a policy's coverage parts; `ledger` and `entry_type` on ledger entries; `status` on equipment items, installation floaters, transit shipments and value reports; `coverage_part_id`, `assigned_jobsite_id` and `applied_rate_rule_id` on equipment items; `coverage_part_id` and `jobsite_id` on installation floaters; `coverage_part_id` and `vehicle_id` on transit shipments; and `policy_id`, `coverage_part_id`, `equipment_item_id`, `transit_shipment_id`, `installation_floater_id` and `adjustment_status` on claims. Every filter is optional, and omitting one means the corresponding dimension is unconstrained.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Policies, coverage parts, rate rules, equipment items, jobsites, installation floaters, vehicles, transit shipments, value reports and claims all carry the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID, with no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant the row was created, with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`).
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, in the same format.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increases as the rules in §2.7 dictate.

Ledger entries are the one exception. A ledger entry is immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its representation.

Whenever a mutation changes a resource's stored fields — including a derived field such as a re-rolled premium or a decremented capacity — that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock instant. Whether the mutation also advances `version` is governed by §2.7, because `version` is a concurrency token in this domain and not merely an audit counter.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Date Arithmetic

The service does not read the host's wall clock. It maintains a logical UTC clock which every date and instant comparison in this specification reads, and which starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Under no circumstances may the service fall back to real time before the clock has been set explicitly. An `UnderwritingManager` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response echoes the new `current_utc` and reports, in `expired_policy_ids` and `expired_floater_ids`, the identifiers of the policies and installation floaters that the move pushed past the end of their coverage.

Calendar dates are ISO 8601 `YYYY-MM-DD` strings; instants are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps. Two interval conventions coexist and must not be confused. Date intervals in this domain are **closed** — both endpoints are inside the interval — and are used for installation-floater coverage, reporting periods, and overlap testing between floaters on one jobsite. Instant windows for transit are **half-open**: the departure instant is inside the window and the arrival instant is not. Wherever a day count is taken between two dates, it is a count of whole days; where a rule needs both endpoints counted, this document says so explicitly with the phrase "plus one".

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and the Single-Rounding Rule

All monetary amounts are integer numbers of cents and every field holding one is named with a `_cents` suffix. All rates, factors, credits and ratios are integer numbers of basis points, where ten thousand basis points is exactly one, and every field holding one is named with a `_bp` suffix. A base rate of 185 basis points is therefore 1.85 percent of insured value, or $1.85 per $100 of insured value.

Each formula in this document is evaluated in exact rational arithmetic from end to end and rounded exactly once, at the single point the formula names, using round-half-up (a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds to the larger integer). No intermediate product or quotient is ever truncated or pre-rounded. Where a calculation genuinely performs two roundings — the transit premium, the reporting penalty, the short-rate cancellation return, and the blanket weighted rate — the order of the two roundings is pinned in the narrative that defines it, and performing them in the other order produces observably different money.

The order of operations matters as much as the rounding. Rolling a policy premium up, for instance, means summing the unloaded part premiums exactly, applying the territory expense load to that single sum, and rounding once; loading each part separately and rounding per part is wrong and diverges by whole cents.

### 2.7. Resource Versions and Stale-Version Guards

The `version` counter on a resource is the client's evidence that it has observed the resource's current state. Three writes advance a version, and they are the only ones that do:

- Scheduling a new equipment item onto a coverage part increments the owning **policy's** `version` by 1, because the item re-develops the part premium and therefore rewrites the policy's premium roll-ups, its total insured value and its referral flag.
- Endorsing a coverage part's terms increments that **coverage part's** `version` by 1.
- Filing a value report on a coverage part, and running the delinquency sweep on one, each increment that **coverage part's** `version` by 1.

Adding a coverage part to a policy explicitly does **not** bump the policy's `version`.

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and on those two the version field is a required input, not an optional one.

`POST /inland-marine/coverage-parts/{id}/equipment-items` requires `expected_policy_version` in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the owning policy. If that value does not equal the policy's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Scheduling another unit is a legitimate repeat operation — nothing in the state machine bars it — so the stale-version test is the only thing that distinguishes a writer that has seen the policy's current premium and total insured value from one whose view predates an earlier accepted schedule change and which may therefore believe it is still under a referral threshold that has already been crossed.

`POST /inland-marine/coverage-parts/{id}/value-reports` requires `expected_coverage_part_version` in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target coverage part. If that value does not equal the part's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COVERAGE_PART_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Filing a report for a period that already has one legitimately supersedes the earlier report, so again no state precondition can bar the operation; the guard exists because a reporter whose view of `latest_report_period_end_date` and `earned_reported_premium_cents` predates an already-accepted filing would be restating a period the part has since moved past.

A policy's current `version` is readable from the policy resource, and a coverage part's from the coverage-part resource. No other endpoint in the service accepts a version field, and none of them performs a concurrency check.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence and Nested Path Resolution

Checks are evaluated in one strict, service-wide order, and the **first** check that fails decides the response; no later check is evaluated and no partial mutation is retained. Where two checks sit at the same rung, they are mutually exclusive in practice because they guard different endpoints. The ladder, from first to last:

1. **Headers present.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-blank, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role recognised.** `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. **Role permitted.** The caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. **Policyholder ownership.** For the seven operations named in §2.1, a `Policyholder` caller's `X-User-ID` equals the owning policy's `id`, else `ERR_NOT_POLICY_OWNER` (403).
5. **Referral segregation of duties.** On a referral decision, the caller is not the underwriter who raised the referral, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
6. **Settlement segregation of duties.** On a settlement, the caller is not the adjuster who declared the basis, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
7. **Path identifiers resolve.** Each identifier embedded in the request path resolves to a live row, checked left to right so that the outermost identifier is validated first. A policy identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`; a coverage part identifier `ERR_COVERAGE_PART_NOT_FOUND`; a rate rule identifier `ERR_RATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`; an equipment item identifier `ERR_EQUIPMENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`; a jobsite identifier `ERR_JOBSITE_NOT_FOUND`; an installation floater identifier `ERR_INSTALLATION_FLOATER_NOT_FOUND`; a vehicle identifier `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`; a transit shipment identifier `ERR_TRANSIT_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a value report identifier `ERR_VALUE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`; and a claim identifier `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. All ten are HTTP 404.
8. **Body-referenced identifiers resolve.** A `jobsite_id` supplied in the body of an equipment-item create, an installation-floater create, or a jobsite assignment must resolve, else `ERR_JOBSITE_NOT_FOUND` (404). A `vehicle_id` supplied on a transit-shipment create must resolve, else `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND` (404). On claim creation, the supplied `coverage_part_id` and the single named loss subject must both resolve, else `ERR_LOSS_SUBJECT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
9. **Request shape.** Every write endpoint validates its payload: all required fields present; integers actually integers; money and limit fields not negative; rate, factor and basis-point fields not negative; every enum field carrying a declared member; dates formatted `YYYY-MM-DD` and instants formatted as ISO 8601 UTC; a policy's `expiration_date` strictly after its `effective_date`; a rate rule's `age_band_min_years` not greater than its `age_band_max_years`; a floater's `coverage_end_date` not earlier than its `coverage_start_date`; a report's `period_end_date` not earlier than its `period_start_date`; a shipment's `arrival_at` strictly after its `departure_at`; and a claim naming exactly one of `equipment_item_id`, `transit_shipment_id` and `installation_floater_id`. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
10. **Pagination shape.** On every list endpoint, `limit` and `offset` satisfy §2.3, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
11. **Target-entity state.** The entity the request targets is in a state that admits the operation. All of these are HTTP 409 and are listed with their workflows in §3: `ERR_POLICY_NOT_QUOTABLE`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_REFERRABLE`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BINDABLE`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVATABLE`, `ERR_POLICY_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_PART_VOID`, `ERR_RATE_RULE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_ITEM_ALREADY_OFF_RISK`, `ERR_FLOATER_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`, `ERR_VEHICLE_ALREADY_APPROVED`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`, `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE` and `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SETTLED`.
12. **Ancestor state.** Only after the target's own state has been cleared does the service test the state of the target's ancestors: `ERR_POLICY_NOT_MUTABLE` and `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE` for the owning policy, and `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_APPROVED` for the conveyance behind a departure. All are HTTP 409.
13. **Stale version.** The optimistic-concurrency comparisons of §2.7, yielding `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_COVERAGE_PART_VERSION` (both HTTP 412).
14. **Jobsite security sufficiency.** `ERR_JOBSITE_SECURITY_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
15. **Theft control.** `ERR_GEO_TRACKING_REQUIRED` (422).
16. **Carrier approval currency at departure.** `ERR_VEHICLE_APPROVAL_EXPIRED` (422).
17. **Carrier approval completeness at approval.** `ERR_CARRIER_APPROVAL_MISSING` (422).
18. **Coverage-interval containment.** `ERR_FLOATER_OUTSIDE_POLICY_TERM`, `ERR_TERMINATION_DATE_OUTSIDE_COVERAGE` and `ERR_LOSS_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM` (all 422).
19. **Report period inside the term.** `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422).
20. **Report period alignment and cancellation date containment.** `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_MISALIGNED` and `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM` (both 422).
21. **Child type matches parent part type.** `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422).
22. **Loss-subject and loss-of-use eligibility.** `ERR_LOSS_SUBJECT_NOT_ON_PART` and `ERR_LOSS_OF_USE_NOT_COVERED` (both 422).
23. **A published rate cell exists.** `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RATE_RULE` (422).
24. **Jobsite floater exclusivity.** `ERR_JOBSITE_FLOATER_OVERLAP` (409).
25. **Conveyance window exclusivity.** `ERR_VEHICLE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
26. **Conveyance daily capacity.** `ERR_VEHICLE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
27. **Something ratable exists.** `ERR_NO_RATABLE_COVERAGE` (422).
28. **Constructive total loss.** `ERR_CONSTRUCTIVE_TOTAL_LOSS` (409).
29. **Reporting delinquency bar on settlement.** `ERR_REPORTING_DELINQUENT` (409).
30. **Fiscal limits, in this order:** referral clearance at bind (`ERR_REFERRAL_REQUIRED`), the per-item value limit (`ERR_ITEM_VALUE_EXCEEDS_PER_ITEM_LIMIT`), the floater limit against remaining part aggregate (`ERR_FLOATER_LIMIT_EXCEEDS_PART_AGGREGATE`), the shipment's declared value against its applicable limit (`ERR_TRANSIT_VALUE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`), the report's declared value against the reporting limit (`ERR_REPORTED_VALUE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`), and the settlement amount against remaining part aggregate (`ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`). All are HTTP 422.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve to be spelled out, because they are the cases where two plausible answers exist and only one is correct.

First, the coverage-part state check at rung 11 tests the **target** entity and therefore runs before the owning policy's state is examined at rung 12. Cancelling a policy and declining a referral both void every coverage part on that policy. So a child create or a part endorsement aimed at a part belonging to a cancelled or declined policy returns `ERR_PART_VOID`, not `ERR_POLICY_NOT_MUTABLE`. Conversely, when a policy expires its parts stay `ACTIVE`, so the same request aimed at an expired policy's part passes the part check and fails the ancestor check with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_MUTABLE`. The one request that has no part target — adding a coverage part to a policy — yields `ERR_POLICY_NOT_MUTABLE` on all three terminal policy statuses.

Second, mutability and being on risk are different requirements. Adding a coverage part, endorsing one, scheduling an equipment item, opening an installation floater, planning a transit shipment, filing a value report, and endorsing an item off the schedule all require the owning policy to be in one of `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `REFERRED`, `BOUND`, `IN_FORCE` or `SUSPENDED`; a `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` policy accepts no new exposure and no endorsement, and the rejection is `ERR_POLICY_NOT_MUTABLE`. Putting exposure on risk is stricter: activating an equipment item, activating an installation floater, dispatching a shipment and running the reporting sweep all require the owning policy to be exactly `IN_FORCE`, and any other status — including `BOUND` and `SUSPENDED` — is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`.

### 2.9. The Premium and Loss Ledgers as Conserved Quantities

Money never moves in this system without a ledger entry. A ledger entry records the `policy_id` whose quantities it moves, an optional `claim_id` (populated only on claim-settlement and salvage-recovery entries), an `entry_type` naming what produced it, a `ledger` naming which of the two books it belongs to, a signed `amount_cents`, a human-readable `memo` naming the triggering action and the subject row, and an `effective_date` taken from the logical clock unless the triggering action supplies its own effective date. Amounts are signed by direction: a debit to the insured is positive, and a return of premium or a recovery is negative.

There are eight entry types, and each belongs to exactly one book. `INITIAL_PREMIUM`, `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM`, `ENDORSEMENT_RETURN`, `REPORTING_ADJUSTMENT`, `LATE_REPORTING_PENALTY` and `CANCELLATION_RETURN` all belong to the `PREMIUM` ledger. `CLAIM_SETTLEMENT` and `SALVAGE_RECOVERY` belong to the `LOSS` ledger. The two conserved quantities on a policy follow directly: `premium_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over that policy's entries in the `PREMIUM` ledger, and `incurred_loss_cents` is the sum over its entries in the `LOSS` ledger. Both are recomputed from the ledger every time an entry is appended, and neither is ever adjusted independently of an entry.

Because `written_premium_cents` is independently re-derived from the rate table while the ledger records only the amounts actually billed, the two views legitimately move by different amounts on the same call. Only `premium_balance_cents` is required to reconcile against the ledger.

### 2.10. Aggregate Capacity as Conserved Capacity

Each coverage part sells a fixed amount of aggregate capacity and tracks how much of it has been consumed. The identity `aggregate_limit_cents` equals `aggregate_available_cents` plus `aggregate_paid_net_cents` holds at every observable moment. On creation the part's `aggregate_available_cents` is initialised to its declared `aggregate_limit_cents` and its `aggregate_paid_net_cents` is 0. A claim settlement adds the amount paid to `aggregate_paid_net_cents` and subtracts the same amount from `aggregate_available_cents`. A salvage recovery makes the compensating move, subtracting the credited salvage from `aggregate_paid_net_cents` and adding it back to `aggregate_available_cents`. Raising a limit by endorsement afterwards does not retroactively restore capacity that paid claims have already consumed.

### 2.11. Re-Rating Cascades and Hierarchical Consistency

Almost every write in this domain has consequences above and beside the row it touches, and those consequences are part of the contract rather than an optimisation. Whenever an equipment item is rated or re-rated, the item's own derived fields are rewritten, then its parent coverage part's `rated_premium_cents` is recomputed from the surviving schedule, then the owning policy's `sum_of_part_premiums_cents`, `written_premium_cents`, `scheduled_basis_premium_cents`, `blanket_basis_premium_cents`, `total_insured_value_cents` and `referral_required` are all re-derived. If the policy is already `IN_FORCE` or `SUSPENDED` at that moment, the premium movement is additionally booked to the ledger as a prorated endorsement entry and `premium_balance_cents` is refreshed.

The same discipline applies downward. When a parent becomes non-operational, its children are resolved rather than left dangling: a declined referral or a cancellation voids every coverage part and every live installation floater on the policy; a cancellation additionally removes every live equipment item and cancels every planned shipment; a policy expiry expires every live floater. Each of those child transitions carries its own further consequences — most notably that a floater leaving the live set decrements its jobsite's live-floater count, which can release the jobsite, which re-prices every item standing on it, which re-rolls those items' parts and policies. Every hop in such a chain is a real mutation: the affected rows have their `updated_at` refreshed, and where §2.7 says a version advances, it advances.

The server performs these reconciliations itself. A client never has to issue a follow-up request to bring a parent into line with its children, and there is no endpoint for doing so.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Policy and Structuring Its Coverage Parts

An `Underwriter` opens the file by creating a policy for a named insured. The submission carries the `policyholder_name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank), the `territory_code` of the garaging address, and the two ends of the term: `effective_date` and `expiration_date`, both ISO 8601 calendar dates, with the expiration strictly later than the effective date. The submission may also carry `minimum_premium_cents`, the policy's premium floor; when omitted it is 250,000 cents, that is $2,500, and it may not be negative. Four rating territories exist and every territory-valued field in the domain draws from the same four: `T1_COASTAL`, `T2_URBAN`, `T3_INLAND` and `T4_REMOTE`.

On creation the service computes `term_days` as the number of whole days from `effective_date` to `expiration_date`. The expiration is exclusive for this purpose — a policy running from the first of January to the first of February has a `term_days` of 31 — and that figure is the pro-rata denominator for every endorsement and every cancellation calculation the policy will ever see. The new policy opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `referral_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, `referral_required` false, `referred_by_user_id` and `referral_decided_by_user_id` both null, `cancellation_reason` and `cancel_effective_date` both null, `quoted_at`, `bound_at` and `in_force_at` all null, `occupancy_termination_count` at 0, and every money roll-up — `sum_of_part_premiums_cents`, `scheduled_basis_premium_cents`, `blanket_basis_premium_cents`, `written_premium_cents`, `premium_balance_cents`, `incurred_loss_cents` and `total_insured_value_cents` — at 0. A policy moves through the states `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `REFERRED`, `DECLINED`, `BOUND`, `IN_FORCE`, `SUSPENDED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED`, of which `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

Coverage is then structured as one or more **coverage parts**, each of which is a distinct inland marine grant with its own limits, its own deductible and its own aggregate. The part's `part_type` selects which rating calculation runs and which child rows may hang off it, and is one of `SCHEDULED_EQUIPMENT`, `BLANKET_EQUIPMENT`, `RENTED_LEASED`, `INSTALLATION_FLOATER`, `TRANSIT` or `REPORTING_FORM`. Its `valuation_basis` sets the default loss-valuation convention for everything on the part and is one of `ACV`, `REPLACEMENT_COST`, `AGREED_VALUE` or `STATED_AMOUNT`. Its `rating_basis` — `SCHEDULED` or `BLANKET`, defaulting to `SCHEDULED` — selects which variant of the equipment roll-up sets the part's charged premium, and it is meaningful only on `SCHEDULED_EQUIPMENT` and `BLANKET_EQUIPMENT` parts; a `RENTED_LEASED` or `REPORTING_FORM` part is always charged on the scheduled sum whatever its `rating_basis` says. Every part declares an `aggregate_limit_cents` greater than zero, and takes a `deductible_cents` that defaults to 100,000 cents ($1,000) and may not be negative. A part is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, and the only other status it can reach is `VOID`; a `VOID` part accepts no children and contributes no premium. Adding a part to a policy is the one exposure-adding write that leaves the policy's `version` alone.

The remaining part fields are conditional on `part_type`, and the shape check of §2.8 enforces them. A part rated on the blanket basis must declare `blanket_limit_cents`, the single limit that applies to every unit on the part and the claim limit for a `BLANKET_EQUIPMENT` loss; it may also declare `coinsurance_pct_bp`, a coinsurance percentage in basis points which, when supplied, must lie between 5,000 and 10,000 inclusive (9,000 basis points being 90 percent coinsurance). A `RENTED_LEASED` part must declare `per_item_limit_cents`, the ceiling on any single rented or leased unit's insured value, together with `loss_of_use_daily_limit_cents` and a `loss_of_use_max_days` of at least 1. A `TRANSIT` part must declare `transit_owned_limit_cents`, `transit_hired_limit_cents` and `catastrophe_limit_cents`. A `REPORTING_FORM` part must declare a `reporting_frequency` of `MONTHLY` or `QUARTERLY`, a `reporting_annual_rate_bp`, and a `reporting_limit_cents`. The part's own derived fields open at their base values: `aggregate_available_cents` equal to `aggregate_limit_cents`, `aggregate_paid_net_cents` at 0, `earned_reported_premium_cents` at 0, `rated_premium_cents` at 0, and `deposit_premium_cents` and `latest_report_period_end_date` both null.

Terms can be endorsed later. An `Underwriter` may revise a part's `rating_basis`, `valuation_basis`, `deductible_cents`, `blanket_limit_cents`, `coinsurance_pct_bp`, `per_item_limit_cents`, `loss_of_use_daily_limit_cents`, `loss_of_use_max_days`, `transit_owned_limit_cents`, `transit_hired_limit_cents`, `catastrophe_limit_cents`, `reporting_annual_rate_bp` or `reporting_limit_cents`, each field optional and only the supplied ones changing. Switching `rating_basis` or `valuation_basis` does not itself restate the charged premium; it changes which variant of the equipment roll-up will produce `rated_premium_cents` at the next quote. Raising a limit does not restore aggregate capacity that settled claims have already consumed, per §2.10. The endorsement advances the part's `version` by 1, and it is refused with `ERR_PART_VOID` on a voided part and with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_MUTABLE` when the owning policy is past mutability.

### 3.2. Rate Development and the Equipment Schedule

Nothing can be rated until an `Actuary` has built the rate table. A rate cell prices exactly one combination of equipment class, territory and age band, and is submitted with an `equipment_type` drawn from `EXCAVATOR`, `CRANE`, `LOADER`, `DOZER`, `GENERATOR`, `COMPRESSOR`, `SCAFFOLDING` and `SURVEY_INSTRUMENT`; a `territory_code`; an `age_band_min_years` of at least 0 and an `age_band_max_years` not less than the minimum, both being inclusive bounds in whole years; a `base_rate_bp` greater than 0; a `theft_hazard_grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`; and optionally a `replacement_cost_index_bp`, which defaults to 11,000 basis points and may not be below 10,000. The cell is created with `status` `DRAFT` and a null `published_at`. Draft cells are invisible to rating.

Publishing a cell moves it `DRAFT` → `PUBLISHED` and stamps `published_at` from the logical clock. Publication also retires competition: any other cell already `PUBLISHED` with the identical `equipment_type`, `territory_code`, `age_band_min_years` and `age_band_max_years` is moved to `RETIRED`. Publishing a cell that is already `PUBLISHED` or `RETIRED` is refused with `ERR_RATE_RULE_NOT_DRAFT`, and only `DRAFT` cells may be published — the three statuses a cell can hold are `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` and `RETIRED`.

**Selecting a cell.** Every time an item is rated, the service picks exactly one cell. It first fixes the item's **rating territory**: the `territory_code` of the jobsite named in `assigned_jobsite_id` when the item has one, and otherwise the owning policy's `territory_code`. It then fixes `age_years_at_rating` as the calendar year of the logical clock minus the item's `model_year`, floored at 0. The candidate set is every rate cell whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, whose `equipment_type` equals the item's `equipment_type`, whose `territory_code` equals the rating territory, and whose age band contains the age — that is, whose `age_band_min_years` is not greater than `age_years_at_rating` and whose `age_band_max_years` is not less than it. `DRAFT` and `RETIRED` cells never enter the candidate set even when their band matches. If the candidate set is empty the rating is rejected with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_RATE_RULE`. Otherwise the candidates are ordered by `published_at` descending — most recently published first — with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first candidate wins. The winner's identifier is stored on the item as `applied_rate_rule_id`, its `base_rate_bp` is copied to the item's `applied_base_rate_bp`, and its `theft_hazard_grade` governs the item's theft loading and its tracker credit. Ordering never consults any identifier.

**Scheduling a unit.** An `Underwriter` schedules a piece of contractors equipment onto a coverage part by supplying a `description` (1 to 160 characters, not blank), an `equipment_type`, a `model_year` between 1950 and 2100, an `original_cost_cents` greater than 0, an `ownership` of `OWNED`, `RENTED` or `LEASED`, and the `expected_policy_version` required by §2.7. The submission may also carry `stated_value_cents`, `geo_tracker_installed`, an item-level `valuation_basis` override, and an `assigned_jobsite_id` if the unit is going straight to a site. Where `geo_tracker_installed` is omitted it is false; where `ownership` is omitted it is `OWNED`; where the item-level `valuation_basis` is omitted the parent part's `valuation_basis` is copied onto the item at creation, so the item always carries an explicit basis afterwards. `stated_value_cents` is required when the effective basis is `AGREED_VALUE` or `STATED_AMOUNT` and is ignored otherwise. Equipment items may be scheduled only on parts whose `part_type` is `SCHEDULED_EQUIPMENT`, `BLANKET_EQUIPMENT`, `RENTED_LEASED` or `REPORTING_FORM`; anywhere else the create fails with `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH`. A unit whose `ownership` is `RENTED` or `LEASED` belongs only on a `RENTED_LEASED` part, and those are the only units the loss-of-use extension will ever pay for.

**Insured value.** The item is rated in the same call that creates it, and the first step is to convert cost into insured value using the effective valuation basis. Under `ACV`, the unit depreciates 800 basis points — 8 percent — of its original cost for each whole year of `age_years_at_rating`; the retained proportion is ten thousand basis points less that depreciation, but never less than a floor of 2,000 basis points, so a unit that is ten years old or older retains exactly 20 percent; `insured_value_cents` is then the original cost multiplied by the retained proportion, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half-up once. Under `REPLACEMENT_COST`, `insured_value_cents` is the original cost multiplied by the selected rate cell's `replacement_cost_index_bp`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half-up once. Under `AGREED_VALUE`, `insured_value_cents` is simply `stated_value_cents`, untouched by age and untouched by the rate cell — the only basis immune to both. Under `STATED_AMOUNT`, `insured_value_cents` is the lesser of `stated_value_cents` and the indexed figure computed exactly as in the replacement-cost branch. If the resulting `insured_value_cents` exceeds a `per_item_limit_cents` declared on the part, the item is refused with `ERR_ITEM_VALUE_EXCEEDS_PER_ITEM_LIMIT`; where the part declares no such limit the check does not apply.

**Item premium.** The unit's `rated_premium_cents` is the product of five independent dimensions, rounded exactly once at the very end. The first is `insured_value_cents`. The second is `applied_base_rate_bp` from the selected cell. The third is a valuation factor keyed on the effective basis: 10,000 basis points for `ACV`, 11,500 for `REPLACEMENT_COST`, 12,500 for `AGREED_VALUE` and 10,800 for `STATED_AMOUNT`. The fourth is a theft factor keyed on the selected cell's `theft_hazard_grade`: 11,800 basis points for grade `A`, 11,000 for `B`, 10,400 for `C` and 10,000 for `D`. The fifth is the net-of-credit factor, being ten thousand basis points less the item's `applied_credit_bp`. Take the exact product of those five quantities, divide it by ten thousand once for each of the four basis-point factors, and round half-up once; the result is `rated_premium_cents`. Because grade `A` both surcharges the unit by 18 percent and qualifies it for the larger tracker credit, the theft grade enters the pricing twice with opposite signs.

**Putting the unit on risk and taking it off.** A newly scheduled item carries `status` `RATED`, a null `removed_at`, and the derived trace `applied_rate_rule_id`, `age_years_at_rating`, `insured_value_cents`, `applied_base_rate_bp`, `applied_credit_bp` and `rated_premium_cents`. Activation moves it `RATED` → `ACTIVE`, and is possible only while the owning policy is `IN_FORCE` and the parent part is still `ACTIVE`; a void part rejects the activation with `ERR_PART_VOID`. An item may be endorsed off the schedule from either `RATED` or `ACTIVE`, moving to `REMOVED` with `removed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Attempting to activate, re-site or remove an item that is already `REMOVED` or `TOTALED` — or to activate one that is already `ACTIVE` — is refused with `ERR_ITEM_ALREADY_OFF_RISK`. The four statuses an item can hold are `RATED`, `ACTIVE`, `REMOVED` and `TOTALED`, and only `RATED` and `ACTIVE` items are on risk for rating and for total-insured-value purposes.

**Mid-term endorsement money.** Adding or removing a unit while the owning policy is `IN_FORCE` or `SUSPENDED` books a prorated premium movement. Count `remaining_days` as the whole days from the logical clock's date to the policy's `expiration_date`; if that count is zero or negative the amount is 0. Otherwise the amount is the item's `rated_premium_cents` multiplied by `remaining_days`, divided by the policy's `term_days`, and rounded half-up once. Adding the unit appends a positive `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM` entry for that amount; removing it appends an `ENDORSEMENT_RETURN` entry for the negative of it. The territory expense load is deliberately not re-applied to a mid-term endorsement, so the ledger records the unloaded prorated item premium while `written_premium_cents` is independently re-derived, loaded and floored. Adding a unit and removing it again on the same logical day is therefore an exact compensating pair: `remaining_days` is identical, so `premium_balance_cents` and `written_premium_cents` both return to their prior values. While the policy is still `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `REFERRED` or `BOUND`, no endorsement entry is written at all — the unit is simply folded into the quote.

### 3.3. Jobsites, Theft Controls and the Release Cascade

Jobsites are registered independently of any policy and are shared across the book. An `Underwriter` registers one with a `name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank) and a `territory_code`. A new site opens with `status` `UNSURVEYED`, `security_score` 0, `security_tier` `TIER_0`, `active_floater_count` 0 and a null `surveyed_at`.

**The security survey.** A site is scored by recording four boolean theft controls: `fenced` for perimeter fencing, `lighting` for night lighting, `alarm_monitored` for central-station alarm monitoring, and `guarded_24h` for a 24-hour guard service. The `security_score` is the sum of their weights: one point each for fencing, lighting and alarm monitoring, and two points for the guard, because a live guard is worth two controls. The `security_tier` follows from the score by inclusive cutoffs: a score of at least 5 is `TIER_3`, otherwise a score of at least 3 is `TIER_2`, otherwise a score of at least 1 is `TIER_1`, and a score of 0 is `TIER_0`. The maximum attainable score is 5, so `TIER_3` is the unique perfect-score tier — a fully controlled site that loses any one of fencing, lighting or alarm monitoring drops to a score of 4 and lands in `TIER_2`, and a site with all three of those but no guard scores 3 and also lands in `TIER_2`. The survey stamps `surveyed_at` from the logical clock and moves the site's `status` to `SURVEYED`, whether it was `UNSURVEYED`, already `SURVEYED` (a re-survey), or `RELEASED` (a re-survey of a released site). Those three are the only statuses a jobsite holds, and none of them is terminal.

**How a site earns money back.** Two credits reduce an equipment item's premium, and their sum is capped. A unit with `geo_tracker_installed` true earns 1,500 basis points when the rate cell selected for it carries theft hazard grade `A` or `B`, and 500 basis points when the grade is `C` or `D`; a unit without a tracker earns nothing. A unit standing on a jobsite earns a jobsite security credit keyed on that site's `security_tier`: nothing at `TIER_0`, 300 basis points at `TIER_1`, 700 at `TIER_2` and 1,200 at `TIER_3`; a unit with no `assigned_jobsite_id` earns no security credit at all. The item's `applied_credit_bp` is the lesser of 2,500 basis points and the sum of the two credits, so the cap binds exactly when a tracker on a high-hazard unit (1,500) meets a perfect site (1,200) and the uncapped sum of 2,700 is trimmed to 2,500.

**Re-siting a unit.** Moving a unit to a jobsite is a re-rating, not a bookkeeping change. The `Underwriter` supplies the `jobsite_id`; a value that does not resolve is `ERR_JOBSITE_NOT_FOUND`. The service sets the item's `assigned_jobsite_id`, re-selects the rate cell against the jobsite's `territory_code` rather than the policy's, re-derives `insured_value_cents`, and re-prices the item with the new site's security credit. A unit that was `RATED` stays `RATED` and a unit that was `ACTIVE` stays `ACTIVE` — the assignment is a self-loop on both — but the money changes, sometimes substantially, because a different territory can swap in a cell with a different `base_rate_bp` *and* a different `theft_hazard_grade`, and the grade change moves the theft factor and the tracker credit band together. The cascade of §2.11 then re-rolls the parent part and the policy, booking a prorated `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM` or `ENDORSEMENT_RETURN` entry for the premium delta and refreshing `premium_balance_cents` when the policy is `IN_FORCE`.

**The grade-A theft control.** A unit rated on a grade `A` cell may not stand on a poorly controlled site without telematics. Concretely: if the rate cell selected for a unit carries `theft_hazard_grade` `A`, and the site the unit would stand on has a `security_tier` of `TIER_0` or `TIER_1`, and the unit's `geo_tracker_installed` is false, the operation is refused with `ERR_GEO_TRACKING_REQUIRED`. That control guards three distinct operations, not one: scheduling a new equipment item (when the create names an `assigned_jobsite_id`), re-siting an existing item, and recording a jobsite security survey. The third is the subtle one — a re-survey that *drops* a site's tier can put a standing grade-A unit into violation, and in that case the whole survey is rejected with `ERR_GEO_TRACKING_REQUIRED` and nothing at all changes: the site keeps its previous `security_score`, `security_tier` and `surveyed_at`, and no item is re-rated.

**The release cascade.** A jobsite's `active_floater_count` is the number of installation floaters attached to it whose status is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`. Opening a floater on a site increments it by one; a floater leaving that live set for any reason decrements it by one. When the count falls to 0 from a positive value on a site that is `SURVEYED`, the site transitions `SURVEYED` → `RELEASED`, and the release is not cosmetic: a released site is no longer treated as secured, so every equipment item still assigned to it whose status is `RATED` or `ACTIVE` is re-rated with a jobsite security credit of zero. Those items keep their `assigned_jobsite_id` — they are still physically there — but they lose the credit, which raises their premium. Each affected item's parent part `rated_premium_cents` is then recomputed, and after that the policy's `written_premium_cents`; where the policy is `IN_FORCE` or `SUSPENDED` the increase is booked as an `ENDORSEMENT_PREMIUM` entry and `premium_balance_cents` is refreshed. A subsequent survey of the released site moves it back to `SURVEYED` and re-prices those items upward or downward again according to its new tier.

### 3.4. From Schedule to Quote: Both Bases, the Expense Load and the Referral Thresholds

The commercial question the unit exists to answer is whether a given schedule is cheaper written as a schedule of individually rated units or as one blanket limit. The service therefore computes **both** answers on every quote, and charges only the one each part's own `rating_basis` names.

**The scheduled variant.** On the scheduled basis a part's `rated_premium_cents` is the plain integer-cent sum of `rated_premium_cents` over the equipment items on the part whose `status` is `RATED` or `ACTIVE`. Items that are `REMOVED` or `TOTALED` are excluded from the moment they leave risk. No further rounding is applied, because each addend was already rounded once when the item was priced. Every unit's insured value, valuation factor, theft factor and credits are honoured individually, so one high-hazard unit drags the whole part upward; the part's `blanket_limit_cents` and `coinsurance_pct_bp` are ignored entirely.

**The blanket variant.** On the blanket basis the schedule collapses into a single insured-value-weighted rate applied to the declared blanket limit, and per-unit theft grades and per-unit credits disappear altogether. First compute the weighted rate: sum, over the items on the part in `RATED` or `ACTIVE` status, the product of each item's `insured_value_cents` and its `applied_base_rate_bp`; divide that total by the sum of those same items' `insured_value_cents`; and round half-up once. That is `weighted_rate_bp`. If the sum of insured values is zero, the weighted rate is zero and so is the blanket premium. Next compute the coinsurance credit: 500 basis points when the part's `coinsurance_pct_bp` is at least 9,000 — that is, when the insured co-insures at 90 percent or better — and zero otherwise. The blanket premium is then the exact product of the part's `blanket_limit_cents`, the `weighted_rate_bp`, the valuation factor of the part's own `valuation_basis` (the same table used for items: 10,000 for `ACV`, 11,500 for `REPLACEMENT_COST`, 12,500 for `AGREED_VALUE`, 10,800 for `STATED_AMOUNT`), a blanket load of 10,600 basis points — a 6 percent loading for writing the coverage blanket — and ten thousand basis points less the coinsurance credit; divide that product by ten thousand once for each of the four basis-point factors and round half-up once. A part that declares no `blanket_limit_cents` has nothing for the blanket form to charge and its blanket-variant premium is zero. So the two bases genuinely diverge on identical units: the blanket form charges the declared limit rather than the schedule, adds the 6 percent load, drops every theft factor and every geo and jobsite credit, and hands back 500 basis points for adequate coinsurance.

**Which parts get two figures.** Both variants are computed for every equipment-bearing part — those whose `part_type` is `SCHEDULED_EQUIPMENT`, `BLANKET_EQUIPMENT`, `RENTED_LEASED` or `REPORTING_FORM`. A `TRANSIT` or `INSTALLATION_FLOATER` part has only its single `rated_premium_cents`, computed as described in §3.6 and §3.7, and it contributes that identical figure to both comparison roll-ups.

**Rolling up to the policy.** The policy's `sum_of_part_premiums_cents` is the sum of `rated_premium_cents` over its coverage parts whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; `VOID` parts contribute nothing. The territory expense load is then applied **once, to that single sum** — never per part: multiply `sum_of_part_premiums_cents` by the expense load of the policy's `territory_code`, divide by ten thousand, and round half-up once. The four loads are 10,800 basis points for `T1_COASTAL`, 10,400 for `T2_URBAN`, 10,200 for `T3_INLAND` and 11,200 for `T4_REMOTE`. Finally the minimum premium floor is applied, strictly **after** the load: `written_premium_cents` is the greater of the loaded figure and the policy's `minimum_premium_cents`. The two comparison roll-ups run the identical load-then-floor pipeline over the corresponding variant premiums, producing `scheduled_basis_premium_cents` and `blanket_basis_premium_cents`. Because the load is applied to the sum rather than per part, two identical `T2_URBAN` parts of 173,318 cents each roll up to 360,501 cents and not 360,502.

`total_insured_value_cents` is the sum of `insured_value_cents` over every equipment item whose coverage part belongs to the policy and whose `status` is `RATED` or `ACTIVE`.

**The referral thresholds.** `referral_required` is recomputed on every quote and is true when either of two tests trips, and the two deliberately differ in strictness. The premium test trips when `written_premium_cents` reaches $50,000 (5,000,000 cents) — at or above, inclusive. The exposure test trips when `total_insured_value_cents` goes above $500,000 (50,000,000 cents) — strictly above, so exactly 50,000,000 cents does not trip it.

**Quoting.** An `Underwriter` quotes a policy that is `DRAFT` or `QUOTED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_QUOTABLE`. The policy must have at least one `ACTIVE` coverage part, else `ERR_NO_RATABLE_COVERAGE`, and each such part must end the call with a `rated_premium_cents` actually set. The quote re-selects the rate cell and re-derives insured value and item premium for every equipment item in `RATED` or `ACTIVE` status; runs both variants of the equipment roll-up on each equipment-bearing part and stores each part's `rated_premium_cents` from its own `rating_basis`; prices `TRANSIT` parts and `INSTALLATION_FLOATER` parts by their own calculations; sets `deposit_premium_cents` on every `REPORTING_FORM` part to that part's freshly computed `rated_premium_cents`, leaving it null on every other part type and on any reporting-form part that has never been quoted; and then runs the policy roll-up, setting `sum_of_part_premiums_cents`, `scheduled_basis_premium_cents`, `blanket_basis_premium_cents`, `written_premium_cents`, `total_insured_value_cents` and `referral_required`. It stamps `quoted_at` from the logical clock. A `DRAFT` policy becomes `QUOTED`; a `QUOTED` policy re-quotes to `QUOTED` again, which is how a policy is re-priced after a schedule or limit endorsement without leaving the quoted state. A re-quote leaves `referral_status` exactly as it was, even when `referral_required` flips.

**Reading the comparison.** The policy's premium summary is a computed read that derives everything on the fly and returns `sum_of_part_premiums_cents`, the `expense_load_bp` actually in force for the policy's territory, `written_premium_cents`, `scheduled_basis_premium_cents`, `blanket_basis_premium_cents`, a `minimum_premium_applied` boolean stating whether the floor rather than the loaded figure produced the written premium, `total_insured_value_cents`, `premium_balance_cents`, `incurred_loss_cents` and `referral_required`.

### 3.5. Referral, Binding, Activation, Cancellation and Expiry

**Referral.** When the thresholds of §3.4 have tripped, the `Underwriter` refers the file upward, optionally attaching a `referral_note` for the manager. Referral is permitted only when the policy is `QUOTED`, only when `referral_required` is true, and only when `referral_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` or `DECLINED`; a referral that is already `PENDING` or `APPROVED`, a policy that is not `QUOTED`, or a policy whose thresholds never tripped is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_REFERRABLE`. A successful referral moves the policy `QUOTED` → `REFERRED`, sets `referral_status` to `PENDING`, and records the calling underwriter's `X-User-ID` in `referred_by_user_id`. The four values `referral_status` can hold are `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `APPROVED` and `DECLINED`.

**The decision.** An `UnderwritingManager` resolves a pending referral by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`. The policy must be `REFERRED` with `referral_status` `PENDING`, else `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_PENDING`, and the deciding manager must not be the referring underwriter, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either way the manager's `X-User-ID` is written to `referral_decided_by_user_id`. An approval sets `referral_status` to `APPROVED` and returns the policy `REFERRED` → `QUOTED`, closing the `QUOTED` → `REFERRED` → `QUOTED` loop so that the file can be re-priced or bound. A decline sets `referral_status` to `DECLINED`, moves the policy `REFERRED` → `DECLINED`, and cascades downward: every coverage part on the policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `VOID`, and every installation floater on those parts whose status is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED` with `termination_trigger` `CANCELLATION`.

**Binding.** Binding requires the policy to be `QUOTED`, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BINDABLE`, and requires a `written_premium_cents` above zero. It also requires referral clearance: if `referral_required` is true then `referral_status` must be `APPROVED`, and otherwise the bind is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_REQUIRED`. A policy whose thresholds never tripped binds without any referral at all. On success the policy moves `QUOTED` → `BOUND`, `bound_at` is stamped from the logical clock, an `INITIAL_PREMIUM` entry is appended to the premium ledger for the full `written_premium_cents` as a positive amount with its `effective_date` set to the policy's own `effective_date`, and `premium_balance_cents` is recomputed from the ledger.

**Activation.** Putting the policy on risk requires it to be `BOUND`, and requires the logical clock's date to have reached the `effective_date` — activation on the effective date itself is permitted — while still being before the `expiration_date`. A policy that is not `BOUND`, or one whose effective date the clock has not yet reached, is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVATABLE`. On success the policy moves `BOUND` → `IN_FORCE` and `in_force_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

**Cancellation.** Only an `UnderwritingManager` cancels, and only from `BOUND`, `IN_FORCE` or `SUSPENDED`; from `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `REFERRED` or from any terminal status the attempt is refused with `ERR_POLICY_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The submission carries a `cancellation_reason` of `INSURED_REQUEST`, `NON_REPORTING` or `UNDERWRITING`, and a `cancel_effective_date` which must fall on or after the policy's `effective_date` and strictly before its `expiration_date`, else `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM`. A cancellation reached from `SUSPENDED` after a delinquency sweep is the `NON_REPORTING` case.

The return of premium is computed from the policy's own term and its `written_premium_cents` as of cancellation; individual parts and items are not re-rated. Count `unearned_days` as the whole days from `cancel_effective_date` to `expiration_date`. The pro-rata return is `written_premium_cents` multiplied by `unearned_days`, divided by `term_days`, rounded half-up once. When the `cancellation_reason` is `INSURED_REQUEST` the insurer keeps a short-rate penalty of 10 percent: the amount actually returned is 9,000 basis points of the already-rounded pro-rata figure, divided by ten thousand and rounded half-up a second time. When the reason is `NON_REPORTING` or `UNDERWRITING` — both insurer-initiated — the full pro-rata figure is returned with no second rounding. The two roundings happen in that pinned order: pro-rata first, short-rate penalty second, applied to the already-rounded pro-rata figure. The returned amount is booked as a `CANCELLATION_RETURN` entry in the premium ledger with a negative `amount_cents`, `premium_balance_cents` is recomputed, and `cancellation_reason` and `cancel_effective_date` are stored on the policy.

Cancellation then cascades, and every branch of that cascade is required. Every equipment item on the policy whose status is `RATED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `REMOVED` with `removed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and — importantly — **no** per-item endorsement entry is written for those removals, because the cancellation return has already accounted for them. Every installation floater whose status is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED` with `termination_trigger` `CANCELLATION`, and each one decrements its jobsite's `active_floater_count` with the ordinary release consequence of §3.3. Every transit shipment still `PLANNED` becomes `CANCELLED`, freeing its conveyance window. Finally every coverage part still `ACTIVE` becomes `VOID`. The policy itself reaches `CANCELLED`, which is terminal.

**Expiry.** Expiry is not requested; it is discovered when the clock moves. When an `UnderwritingManager` advances the logical clock to a date at or past a policy's `expiration_date`, a policy that is `IN_FORCE` or `SUSPENDED` transitions to `EXPIRED` and its identifier is reported in the clock response's `expired_policy_ids`. Every `ACTIVE` installation floater on that policy becomes `EXPIRED` with `termination_trigger` `EXPIRY`, each decrementing its jobsite's `active_floater_count`, and each such floater's identifier is reported in `expired_floater_ids`. An expired policy's coverage parts, unlike a cancelled policy's, remain `ACTIVE` — which is exactly why §2.8 distinguishes `ERR_PART_VOID` from `ERR_POLICY_NOT_MUTABLE`.

### 3.6. Installation Floaters: Coverage That Ends at Acceptance or at Occupancy

**Opening a floater.** An installation floater is opened on an `INSTALLATION_FLOATER` coverage part — any other `part_type` is rejected with `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH` — and names a `jobsite_id`, a `project_name` of one to one hundred twenty non-blank characters, an `installed_value_limit_cents` above zero, and the two dates `coverage_start_date` and `coverage_end_date`, the end strictly after the start. The named jobsite must exist, else `ERR_JOBSITE_NOT_FOUND`.

The coverage interval is closed at both ends: the first covered day is `coverage_start_date` and the last covered day is `coverage_end_date`. Two consequences follow from that single choice. First, the whole closed interval must sit inside the policy term — `coverage_start_date` on or after the policy's `effective_date` and `coverage_end_date` on or before the policy's `expiration_date` — else `ERR_FLOATER_OUTSIDE_POLICY_TERM`. Second, a jobsite may carry at most one live floater at a time: the new floater's closed interval must not intersect the closed interval of any floater on the same jobsite whose status is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, and a single shared endpoint counts as an intersection, so a floater ending on a given day and another beginning on that same day collide. A collision is refused with `ERR_JOBSITE_FLOATER_OVERLAP`. The overlap test looks only at the jobsite, never at the coverage part, so two different policies competing for the same site collide with each other.

**Pricing the floater.** `term_days` counts both endpoints: take the whole days from `coverage_start_date` to `coverage_end_date` and add one. The rate is 140 basis points per annum on the `installed_value_limit_cents`, modified by the jobsite's `security_tier` as read at the moment of creation, and pro-rated over the term. Multiply `installed_value_limit_cents` by 140, by the tier's installation security factor in basis points, and by `term_days`; then divide by ten thousand for the rate, by ten thousand again for the security factor, and by 365 for the annualisation; round the whole exact expression half-up once to obtain `rated_premium_cents`. The installation security factors are 12,500 basis points for `TIER_0`, 11,000 for `TIER_1`, 10,000 for `TIER_2` and 9,500 for `TIER_3`.

Note carefully that on a floater the security tier is a **multiplier**, not the credit it is on equipment: `TIER_0` is a 25 percent surcharge and `TIER_1` a 10 percent surcharge, `TIER_2` is neutral, and only `TIER_3` earns a 5 percent discount. The same jobsite attribute is therefore monetised in two structurally different ways depending on which exposure is looking at it. Because a `TIER_0` site can be priced but cannot be activated, the surcharge branch is only ever observable on a floater that is still `PENDING`.

A newly created floater has status `PENDING`, `earned_premium_cents` zero, `unearned_return_cents` zero, and a null `termination_trigger` and `termination_date`. Creation increments the jobsite's `active_floater_count` by one — a `PENDING` floater already reserves the site — and does not by itself bump any version. The part-level roll-up is refreshed on the next quote.

**Going on risk.** Activation requires the floater to be `PENDING`, else `ERR_FLOATER_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`; requires the owning policy to be `IN_FORCE`, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`; requires the jobsite to be `SURVEYED` with a `security_tier` other than `TIER_0`, else `ERR_JOBSITE_SECURITY_INSUFFICIENT`; and requires `installed_value_limit_cents` to be no greater than the part's `aggregate_available_cents` at that moment, else `ERR_FLOATER_LIMIT_EXCEEDS_PART_AGGREGATE`. This last test is a comparison against remaining capacity, not against the original `aggregate_limit_cents`, so a part that has already paid claims may refuse a floater it would have accepted before. On success the floater moves `PENDING` → `ACTIVE`; the premium is not recomputed, so a jobsite re-survey between creation and activation does not re-price the floater.

**Terminating at acceptance or at occupancy.** Termination requires the floater to be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FLOATER_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`, and carries a `termination_reason` of either `ACCEPTANCE` or `OCCUPANCY` together with a `termination_date` that must lie within the floater's own closed coverage interval, else `ERR_TERMINATION_DATE_OUTSIDE_COVERAGE`. Count `elapsed_days` as the whole days from `coverage_start_date` to `termination_date` plus one, matching the closed-interval convention used for `term_days`.

The two reasons are the same API call with the same date and they produce different money. On `ACCEPTANCE` — the owner has accepted the completed work — the floater moves to `TERMINATED_ACCEPTANCE`, `earned_premium_cents` is `rated_premium_cents` multiplied by `elapsed_days`, divided by `term_days`, rounded half-up once, `unearned_return_cents` is `rated_premium_cents` minus that earned figure, and an `ENDORSEMENT_RETURN` entry is appended to the premium ledger for the negative of `unearned_return_cents`. Because the unearned figure is obtained by subtraction rather than by a second rounding, the earned and unearned amounts always sum exactly to `rated_premium_cents` with no drift. On `OCCUPANCY` — the structure has been occupied, which ends installation coverage on its own terms — the floater moves to `TERMINATED_OCCUPANCY`, `earned_premium_cents` is set to the whole `rated_premium_cents`, `unearned_return_cents` is zero, **no ledger entry is written at all**, and the policy's `occupancy_termination_count` is incremented by one. Apart from the money and that counter the two branches are side-effect identical.

**The release cascade.** Both branches then run the four-hop cascade of §3.3 in full: the floater leaves the live set, so the jobsite's `active_floater_count` is decremented, a count reaching zero moves the jobsite `SURVEYED` → `RELEASED`, every equipment item standing on that released jobsite in status `RATED` or `ACTIVE` is re-rated with a jobsite security credit of zero, and each affected coverage part's `rated_premium_cents` and then each affected policy's `written_premium_cents` and `premium_balance_cents` are recomputed. Terminating one project's floater therefore raises the premium on unrelated equipment sitting on the same site.

**The other two exits.** A floater that is `ACTIVE` when the logical clock passes its `coverage_end_date` — or when its owning policy expires in the same clock call — becomes `EXPIRED` with `termination_trigger` `EXPIRY`, `termination_date` set to `coverage_end_date`, `earned_premium_cents` equal to the full `rated_premium_cents`, `unearned_return_cents` zero and no ledger entry; the jobsite decrement and its release consequence still apply. A floater that is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` when its policy is cancelled or its referral is declined becomes `VOIDED` with `termination_trigger` `CANCELLATION` and `unearned_return_cents` zero, because the policy-level cancellation return has already handled that premium; again the jobsite is decremented with the same release consequence. All four of `TERMINATED_ACCEPTANCE`, `TERMINATED_OCCUPANCY`, `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

**Rolling floaters up to the part.** The `INSTALLATION_FLOATER` part's `rated_premium_cents` is the sum, over **every** floater on the part in **any** status, of that floater's `rated_premium_cents` minus its `unearned_return_cents`. This is the only part-level roll-up whose input set is not filtered by status, and the reason is exactly the acceptance/occupancy divergence: a `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, occupancy-terminated, expired or voided floater has `unearned_return_cents` of zero and therefore contributes its full rated premium, while an acceptance-terminated floater contributes only its `earned_premium_cents`. Filtering terminated floaters out of the sum would silently give back the premium the occupancy branch is supposed to keep. No further rounding is applied to the sum; both addends were already rounded when they were computed.

### 3.7. Transit: Conveyances, Half-Open Windows and the Catastrophe Tier

**Registering a conveyance.** A `Vehicle` is registered independently of any policy and carries a `unit_label` of one to sixty non-blank characters, an `ownership` of `OWNED` or `HIRED`, a `per_conveyance_limit_cents` above zero, and optionally a `carrier_name` and an `approval_expires_on` calendar date. It is created with status `PENDING_APPROVAL` and `in_transit_value_cents` of zero, and `approved_at` null. The `in_transit_value_cents` field is at all times the sum of `declared_value_cents` over the shipments on this vehicle whose status is `IN_TRANSIT`.

**Approval and its lapse.** Approval is permitted from `PENDING_APPROVAL` or from `SUSPENDED`; a vehicle already `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_ALREADY_APPROVED`. The request may supply a fresh `approval_expires_on`, which overwrites whatever was stored. An `OWNED` conveyance needs nothing further. A `HIRED` conveyance is a third party's truck and needs paperwork: it must have a `carrier_name` and an `approval_expires_on` that is strictly after the logical clock's current date, and re-approving a `SUSPENDED` hired conveyance requires the request itself to supply that fresh date; failing either, the approval is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_APPROVAL_MISSING`. On success the status becomes `APPROVED` and `approved_at` is stamped from the logical clock. A vehicle has no terminal status: the `APPROVED` → `SUSPENDED` → `APPROVED` cycle can run any number of times.

**Planning a shipment.** A transit shipment is planned on a `TRANSIT` coverage part — any other `part_type` is `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH` — and names a `vehicle_id` that must resolve, else `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`. It carries an `origin_territory_code` and a `destination_territory_code`, each one of `T1_COASTAL`, `T2_URBAN`, `T3_INLAND` or `T4_REMOTE`; a `departure_at` and an `arrival_at` instant, the arrival strictly after the departure; and a `declared_value_cents` above zero. It is created with status `PLANNED` and null `departed_at` and `delivered_at`.

**The three competing limits.** `catastrophe_zone` is true when the `origin_territory_code` is `T1_COASTAL` **or** the `destination_territory_code` is `T1_COASTAL`; one coastal endpoint is enough, so an inland-to-coastal run is a catastrophe-zone run even though it starts inland. The conveyance limit is the part's `transit_owned_limit_cents` when the vehicle's `ownership` is `OWNED` and its `transit_hired_limit_cents` when the `ownership` is `HIRED`. The shipment's `applicable_limit_cents` is then the smallest of three quantities: that conveyance limit, the vehicle's own `per_conveyance_limit_cents`, and — only when `catastrophe_zone` is true — the part's `catastrophe_limit_cents`. When `catastrophe_zone` is false the third quantity does not participate and the minimum is taken over the first two alone. The tightest limit wins, so a hired conveyance is bounded by both the hired-equipment limit and the carrier's own per-conveyance limit. No rounding occurs; this is a pure minimum over integer cents. The shipment's `declared_value_cents` must not exceed the resulting `applicable_limit_cents`, else `ERR_TRANSIT_VALUE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`. The same derivation is re-evaluated at departure so that a limit endorsed in between is honoured.

**Double-booking a conveyance.** The occupation window of a shipment is half-open in the instant domain: it begins at `departure_at` inclusive and ends at `arrival_at` exclusive, and the conveyance is not free again until a turnaround buffer of four hours has elapsed after `arrival_at`. Two shipments on the same vehicle therefore conflict when the interval running from one shipment's `departure_at` inclusive to four hours after its `arrival_at` exclusive intersects the same interval computed for the other. The new shipment is tested against every shipment on the same vehicle whose status is `PLANNED` or `IN_TRANSIT`, and a conflict is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because both edges are exclusive at the trailing end, a shipment departing exactly four hours after the previous shipment's arrival is accepted, while one departing three hours and fifty-nine minutes after it is not. Delivered, lost and cancelled shipments release their windows.

**Daily capacity per conveyance.** Independently of the window test, a conveyance may not be loaded beyond its stated capacity on any one day. Sum `declared_value_cents` over the shipments on the same vehicle whose status is `PLANNED` or `IN_TRANSIT` and whose `departure_at` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the new shipment's `departure_at`, include the new shipment's own `declared_value_cents` in that sum, and compare the total against the vehicle's `per_conveyance_limit_cents`; a total that exceeds it is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This is a same-calendar-date test on the departure instant, not a rolling twenty-four-hour test, so two shipments departing twenty-three hours apart across midnight fall on different dates and do not aggregate, while two departing one minute apart within one date do.

**Pricing the transit part.** A `TRANSIT` part is priced from its own limits alone; individual shipments consume those limits but never change the premium. The owned component is `transit_owned_limit_cents` multiplied by 65 basis points and divided by ten thousand, rounded half-up; the hired component is `transit_hired_limit_cents` multiplied by 95 basis points and divided by ten thousand, rounded half-up. Each component is rounded on its own before the two are added, and their sum is the base transit premium. The catastrophe tier is then read off that summed, rounded, **pre-surcharge** base: the tier is `CAT_TIER_1` when the base is below 65,000 cents, `CAT_TIER_2` when the base is 65,000 cents or more but below 300,000 cents, and `CAT_TIER_3` when the base is 300,000 cents or more. The cutoffs are inclusive on the lower side, so a base of 64,999 cents is `CAT_TIER_1` and a base of exactly 65,000 cents is `CAT_TIER_2`. The catastrophe surcharge is the part's `catastrophe_limit_cents` multiplied by the tier's surcharge rate — 400 basis points for `CAT_TIER_1`, 900 for `CAT_TIER_2`, 1,400 for `CAT_TIER_3` — divided by ten thousand and rounded half-up. The part's `rated_premium_cents` is the base plus that surcharge. The self-reference is the trap to avoid: the surcharge depends on the tier, the tier depends on the base, and the base must never include the surcharge. Because hired limit is rated at 95 basis points against 65 for owned, moving limit from owned to hired can push the base across a tier cutoff and change the surcharge as well.

**Dispatch and delivery.** Departure requires the shipment to be `PLANNED`, else `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`; requires the owning policy to be `IN_FORCE`, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`; and requires the vehicle to be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_APPROVED`. For a `HIRED` conveyance the carrier approval must still be current, meaning the logical clock's date is strictly before `approval_expires_on`; a clock date on or after that date is a lapse, and the lapse has two effects in the same call — the vehicle is set to `SUSPENDED`, and the departure itself is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`. The vehicle's suspension persists after the rejection, so the operator must re-approve the conveyance with a fresh `approval_expires_on` before any shipment on it can depart. An `OWNED` conveyance is never subject to this test regardless of what `approval_expires_on` holds. On a successful departure the shipment moves `PLANNED` → `IN_TRANSIT`, `departed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the vehicle's `in_transit_value_cents` is recomputed to include the shipment's `declared_value_cents`.

Delivery requires the shipment to be `IN_TRANSIT`, else `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`. It moves the shipment `IN_TRANSIT` → `DELIVERED`, stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock, and recomputes `in_transit_value_cents`, freeing the declared value. The two remaining exits are system-driven: an `IN_TRANSIT` shipment that is the subject of a total-loss settlement becomes `LOST`, with `in_transit_value_cents` recomputed, and a `PLANNED` shipment on a cancelled policy becomes `CANCELLED`, releasing its window. `DELIVERED`, `LOST` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal.

### 3.8. Reporting Forms: Declared Values, Supersession and the Delinquency Cycle

**How a reporting form works.** A `REPORTING_FORM` part insures values the insured itself declares each period. Its `reporting_frequency` is `MONTHLY` or `QUARTERLY`, its `reporting_annual_rate_bp` is the annual rate applied to each period's declared values, and its `reporting_limit_cents` is both the limit of insurance on those values and the applicable claim limit for losses on the part. At quoting, the part's freshly computed `rated_premium_cents` is also copied into `deposit_premium_cents`, which stays null for every other `part_type` and for any reporting-form part that has never been quoted. Thereafter `earned_reported_premium_cents` accumulates the actual earned premium: it is at all times the running total of `period_earned_premium_cents` over the reports on the part whose status is `ACCEPTED` or `PENALIZED`. The field `latest_report_period_end_date` is the greatest `period_end_date` among the reports on the part whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `PENALIZED` or `DELINQUENT`, with ties broken by the greatest `created_at`, and is null when the part has no such report.

**Periods.** The reporting periods of a part tile the policy term forward from the policy's `effective_date`. The first period begins on `effective_date`; each subsequent period begins one calendar month later when `reporting_frequency` is `MONTHLY` and three calendar months later when it is `QUARTERLY`; and each period ends on the day before the next period would begin, so periods are closed intervals that abut without overlapping. When advancing by calendar months lands on a day that the target month does not have, the start date is clamped to the last day of that month. A period whose computed end would fall after the policy's `expiration_date` ends on the `expiration_date` instead, and no period begins on or after it.

**Filing a report.** A report is filed on a `REPORTING_FORM` part — any other `part_type` is `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH` — by either the `Underwriter` or the `Policyholder` that owns the policy, and carries `period_start_date`, `period_end_date` (strictly after the start), a `reported_value_cents` of zero or more, and the `expected_coverage_part_version` guard described in §2.7. Because a filing may legitimately restate a period already on file, it cannot be barred by a state precondition; the stale-version guard is what protects it, and a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_COVERAGE_PART_VERSION`. The period must sit inside the policy term, else `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM`. Its placement must then be one of exactly two shapes: either it restates a period already on the part, meaning both its `period_start_date` **and** its `period_end_date` are identical to those of an existing report on the part, or it starts strictly after the part's `latest_report_period_end_date`. Any other placement — a partial overlap, a period that starts on or before the latest reported period's end without matching it exactly, a gap-filling period behind the frontier — is `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_MISALIGNED`. When the part has no reports at all, `latest_report_period_end_date` is null and any in-term period is acceptable. Finally `reported_value_cents` must not exceed the part's `reporting_limit_cents`, else `ERR_REPORTED_VALUE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`.

A filed report has `period_days` equal to the whole days from `period_start_date` to `period_end_date` plus one — both endpoints count — and a `due_date` fifteen days after `period_end_date`, that being the reporting grace period. Its status is `SUBMITTED`, its `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `period_earned_premium_cents` and `late_penalty_cents` are both zero until adjudication. The filing increments the part's `version` by one.

**Supersession on filing.** Filing a report for a period already on the part supersedes the earlier one. Every prior report on the same part for the identical period whose status is not already `SUPERSEDED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, with its `superseded_by_report_id` set to the new report's `id`. Reports that were already superseded are left pointing where they were, so a restatement chain stays a chain rather than being re-pointed at the newest link. When a superseded predecessor was `ACCEPTED` or `PENALIZED` its money must be undone: its `period_earned_premium_cents` is subtracted from the part's `earned_reported_premium_cents` and a compensating `REPORTING_ADJUSTMENT` entry is appended to the premium ledger for the negative of that amount, after which `premium_balance_cents` is recomputed. A superseded report contributes nothing to `earned_reported_premium_cents` thereafter. If the predecessor being superseded was the last report in status `DELINQUENT` anywhere on the policy and the policy is `SUSPENDED`, the policy returns `SUSPENDED` → `IN_FORCE` in the same call: filing the missing report is itself the cure.

**Adjudication.** Adjudication requires the report to be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED` — which is also the error for adjudicating a report that is already `ACCEPTED`, `PENALIZED`, `DELINQUENT` or `SUPERSEDED`. The period premium is `reported_value_cents` multiplied by the part's `reporting_annual_rate_bp` and by `period_days`, divided by ten thousand for the basis points and by 365 for the annualisation, rounded half-up once; a month with more days therefore earns more premium on the same declared value. Lateness is then decided on the calendar date of `submitted_at` against `due_date`: a submission whose date falls on or before the `due_date` is timely, and only a submission strictly after it is late.

The timely branch moves the report `SUBMITTED` → `ACCEPTED`, sets `late_penalty_cents` to zero, and appends exactly one ledger entry, a `REPORTING_ADJUSTMENT` for the positive `period_earned_premium_cents`. The late branch moves the report `SUBMITTED` → `PENALIZED`, sets `late_penalty_cents` to 1,000 basis points — ten percent — of the **already-rounded** `period_earned_premium_cents`, divided by ten thousand and rounded half-up a second time, and appends **two** ledger entries: the same positive `REPORTING_ADJUSTMENT` for the period premium, plus a `LATE_REPORTING_PENALTY` for the positive `late_penalty_cents`. The two roundings occur in that pinned order, the penalty being computed from the rounded period premium and never from the exact rational. Both branches add `period_earned_premium_cents` — not the penalty — to the part's `earned_reported_premium_cents` and refresh the policy's `premium_balance_cents`. Both branches then run the same two follow-on hops: any other report on the same part with the identical `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `DELINQUENT` becomes `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_by_report_id` set to the adjudicated report's `id`, and if no report in status `DELINQUENT` remains on any coverage part of the policy while the policy is `SUSPENDED`, the policy returns `SUSPENDED` → `IN_FORCE`.

**The delinquency sweep.** The sweep is the underwriter's instrument for a silent insured. It runs on a `REPORTING_FORM` part — any other `part_type` is `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH` — and requires the owning policy to be `IN_FORCE`, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_FORCE`. For every reporting period of the part, derived as described above, whose `due_date` is strictly before the logical clock's current date and for which no report on the part in status `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `PENALIZED` exists for that exact period, the sweep mints a report with `reported_value_cents` of zero, `period_earned_premium_cents` and `late_penalty_cents` of zero, `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and status `DELINQUENT`. Note precisely which statuses satisfy the cover test: an existing `DELINQUENT` or `SUPERSEDED` report for a period does not count as covering it, so a period that is still uncured is minted again on a second sweep. A period whose `due_date` equals the clock date is not yet swept.

If the sweep minted at least one delinquent report and the policy is `IN_FORCE`, the policy moves `IN_FORCE` → `SUSPENDED`, and a suspended policy stops accepting new exposure on risk: item activation, floater activation and shipment departure all test for `IN_FORCE`, as does the sweep itself, which is why the sweep cannot be run twice in a row against an already-suspended policy. Endorsements and new children remain permitted, since `SUSPENDED` is a mutable status. Whether or not anything was minted, the sweep increments the part's `version` by one and sets `latest_report_period_end_date` to the end date of the last period it swept. The response reports `swept_period_count`, the `delinquent_report_ids` it created, the resulting `policy_status`, and the `part_id` it ran against.

**Reading the chain.** A report exposes the reports it superseded: the superseded-reports list of a given report returns every report whose `superseded_by_report_id` equals that report's `id`, which is how the self-referential restatement chain is walked. Delinquency also has a claims consequence: settlement of any claim on a part carrying a `DELINQUENT` report is barred, as §3.9 sets out.

### 3.9. Loss Adjustment: Two Axes, Two Bases and the Salvage Credit

**Reporting a loss.** A claim is opened on a policy by an `Underwriter` or by the owning `Policyholder`. It names the `coverage_part_id` answering the loss, a `loss_date`, a `loss_type` of `THEFT`, `COLLISION`, `FIRE`, `WINDSTORM` or `VANDALISM`, and exactly one loss subject: either an `equipment_item_id`, or a `transit_shipment_id`, or an `installation_floater_id`. When the governing part's `part_type` is `REPORTING_FORM` all three subject fields are null instead, because the loss is against declared inventory rather than an identified unit. Any other combination — two subjects, or none on a part that is not a reporting form — is a shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `coverage_part_id` or subject identifier that does not resolve to an existing row is `ERR_LOSS_SUBJECT_NOT_FOUND`. The `loss_date` must fall on or after the policy's `effective_date` and strictly before its `expiration_date`, and on a cancelled policy it must additionally fall on or before the `cancel_effective_date`; otherwise `ERR_LOSS_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM`. Finally the named coverage part must belong to the policy in the path and the named subject must belong to that same coverage part, else `ERR_LOSS_SUBJECT_NOT_ON_PART`.

A new claim has `adjustment_status` `OPEN` and `salvage_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Its inspection and settlement figures — `repair_estimate_cents`, `actual_value_at_loss_cents`, `valuation_at_loss_cents`, `constructive_total_loss_threshold_cents`, `repair_basis_settlement_cents`, `total_loss_basis_settlement_cents`, `adjustment_basis`, `settlement_amount_cents`, `settlement_paid_cents` and `salvage_proceeds_cents` — are all null until the calls that produce them, while `underreporting_factor_bp` starts at 10000 and `loss_of_use_days_claimed`, `loss_of_use_paid_cents` and `salvage_credit_cents` all start at zero.

**Two independent axes.** A claim carries two state fields that evolve separately. The adjustment axis, `adjustment_status`, runs `OPEN`, `INSPECTED`, then either `REPAIR_APPROVED` or `TOTAL_LOSS_DECLARED`, then `SETTLED`, then `CLOSED`. The salvage axis, `salvage_status`, sits at `NOT_APPLICABLE` until a total loss is declared, at which point it becomes `PENDING` and later `RECOVERED` or `WAIVED`. The two axes join at `CLOSED`: the claim reaches `CLOSED` only once the salvage axis is not `PENDING`. On the repair branch the salvage axis never leaves `NOT_APPLICABLE`, so the join happens inside the settlement call itself; on the total-loss branch the claim rests at `SETTLED` until the salvage disposition is recorded.

**Inspection.** Only a `ClaimsAdjuster` inspects, and only a claim whose `adjustment_status` is `OPEN`, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`. Inspection is permitted while the owning policy is `IN_FORCE`, `SUSPENDED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED`: a loss that occurred inside the term stays adjustable after the term has ended. The submission carries `repair_estimate_cents` and `actual_value_at_loss_cents`, both of which are stored, and the adjuster's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `inspected_by_user_id`. The claim then moves `OPEN` → `INSPECTED` and four derivations run in order.

First, the valuation. `valuation_at_loss_cents` is taken from the loss subject: an equipment item contributes its `insured_value_cents`, a transit shipment its `declared_value_cents`, an installation floater its `installed_value_limit_cents`. When the claim has no subject at all — the `REPORTING_FORM` case — `valuation_at_loss_cents` is the `actual_value_at_loss_cents` recorded by this inspection. Note that the figure carried into the claim is the insured value the unit was **rated** on, not its original cost, so an actual-cash-value item that was depreciated at underwriting is also settled on the depreciated figure. Then `constructive_total_loss_threshold_cents` is 7,500 basis points — seventy-five percent — of `valuation_at_loss_cents`, divided by ten thousand and rounded half-up once.

Second, the claim limit. `claim_limit_cents` is the governing part's `per_item_limit_cents` when `part_type` is `SCHEDULED_EQUIPMENT` or `RENTED_LEASED`, its `blanket_limit_cents` when `BLANKET_EQUIPMENT`, its `reporting_limit_cents` when `REPORTING_FORM`, the subject shipment's `applicable_limit_cents` when `TRANSIT`, and the subject floater's `installed_value_limit_cents` when `INSTALLATION_FLOATER`. When the governing limit field is not declared on the part, no limit binds and the gross loss passes through the cap step unchanged.

Third, the underreporting factor. For every `part_type` other than `REPORTING_FORM`, `underreporting_factor_bp` is 10000 and the penalty does not exist. On a reporting form, the governing report is the report on that part whose status is `ACCEPTED` or `PENALIZED` and whose closed period from `period_start_date` to `period_end_date` contains the claim's `loss_date`; when more than one qualifies they are ordered by `created_at` ascending and the last one wins. Reports in status `SUBMITTED`, `DELINQUENT` or `SUPERSEDED` are ignored entirely. If no report governs, `underreporting_factor_bp` is 10000, settlement being separately barred while a report is delinquent. Otherwise the reported ratio in basis points is the governing report's `reported_value_cents` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the claim's `actual_value_at_loss_cents`, rounded half-up once; the factor is 10000 when that ratio is at least 9,000 basis points, and is the ratio itself when it is below 9,000. The threshold is inclusive, so a ratio of exactly 9,000 basis points snaps to full reporting. The cliff is deliberate: declare ninety percent or more of the true value and the insurer pays in full, declare one basis point less and the claim is cut by the whole shortfall ratio rather than by the difference. When `actual_value_at_loss_cents` is zero the ratio is not computable and the factor is 10000.

Fourth, both settlement variants are computed and stored, so the worksheet can show them side by side. At inspection `loss_of_use_days_claimed` is still zero, so both variants are recomputed again at adjustment.

**The two settlement formulas.** `loss_of_use_paid_cents` is zero unless the governing part's `part_type` is `RENTED_LEASED` **and** the subject item's `ownership` is `RENTED` or `LEASED`; when both hold, take the smaller of `loss_of_use_days_claimed` and the part's `loss_of_use_max_days`, and multiply that day count by the part's `loss_of_use_daily_limit_cents`. No rounding is involved.

The `REPAIR` variant pays the estimate. Multiply `repair_estimate_cents` by `underreporting_factor_bp`, divide by ten thousand and round half-up **once**; take the smaller of that figure and `claim_limit_cents`; subtract the part's `deductible_cents`; if the result is below zero use zero; then add `loss_of_use_paid_cents`. That is `repair_basis_settlement_cents`.

The `TOTAL_LOSS` variant pays the valuation. Multiply `valuation_at_loss_cents` by `underreporting_factor_bp`, divide by ten thousand and round half-up once; take the smaller of that figure and `claim_limit_cents`; subtract `deductible_cents`; floor the result at zero. No loss-of-use is added. That is `total_loss_basis_settlement_cents`.

The order of those steps is pinned and every alternative ordering diverges: the factor is applied to the gross loss and rounded before the limit cap, the cap precedes the deductible, the zero floor follows the deductible, and loss-of-use is added last and only on the repair variant. Applying the factor after the cap, or subtracting the deductible before the cap, produces different money. Because the two variants differ in both the gross loss and the loss-of-use term, the difference between them is never a simple ratio of one another.

**Choosing the basis.** Only a `ClaimsAdjuster` adjusts, and only a claim that is `INSPECTED`, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`. The submission carries an `adjustment_basis` of `REPAIR` or `TOTAL_LOSS` and an optional `loss_of_use_days_claimed` that defaults to zero. A `loss_of_use_days_claimed` above zero is accepted only when the governing part's `part_type` is `RENTED_LEASED` and the subject item's `ownership` is `RENTED` or `LEASED`, else `ERR_LOSS_OF_USE_NOT_COVERED`. Choosing `REPAIR` additionally requires that either the claim has no `equipment_item_id` at all, or its `repair_estimate_cents` is strictly below `constructive_total_loss_threshold_cents`; an equipment repair estimate that has reached the threshold is refused with `ERR_CONSTRUCTIVE_TOTAL_LOSS`. The comparison is inclusive on the threshold: an estimate exactly equal to seventy-five percent of the valuation is already a constructive total loss. The `REPAIR` variant remains computable and is still returned on the worksheet in that situation; it is simply no longer selectable, which is what makes the two bases non-symmetric.

Either branch stores `adjustment_basis`, records the adjuster's `X-User-ID` in `declared_by_user_id`, and recomputes both variants. The `REPAIR` branch stores the supplied `loss_of_use_days_claimed`, recomputes `loss_of_use_paid_cents`, sets `settlement_amount_cents` to `repair_basis_settlement_cents`, moves the claim `INSPECTED` → `REPAIR_APPROVED`, and leaves `salvage_status` dormant at `NOT_APPLICABLE`; the damaged unit stays on risk and the part's premium is untouched. The `TOTAL_LOSS` branch is always selectable, sets `settlement_amount_cents` to `total_loss_basis_settlement_cents`, forces `loss_of_use_paid_cents` to zero even when days were claimed and accepted, moves the claim `INSPECTED` → `TOTAL_LOSS_DECLARED`, and arms the salvage axis `NOT_APPLICABLE` → `PENDING`.

**Authorising payment.** Only an `UnderwritingManager` settles, and only a claim whose `adjustment_status` is `REPAIR_APPROVED` or `TOTAL_LOSS_DECLARED`, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_IN_REQUIRED_STATE`. Three further gates apply in order: the settling manager's `X-User-ID` must differ from `declared_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; no report on the claim's coverage part may be in status `DELINQUENT`, else `ERR_REPORTING_DELINQUENT`; and `settlement_amount_cents` must not exceed the part's `aggregate_available_cents`, else `ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`. Settlement sets `settlement_paid_cents` to `settlement_amount_cents`, records `settled_by_user_id`, appends a `CLAIM_SETTLEMENT` entry to the **loss** ledger for the positive `settlement_paid_cents` carrying this claim's identifier, refreshes the policy's `incurred_loss_cents`, and moves the part's conserved capacity by adding the paid amount to `aggregate_paid_net_cents` and subtracting it from `aggregate_available_cents`.

On the repair branch that is the whole call: because `salvage_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, the adjustment axis advances straight through `SETTLED` to `CLOSED` in the same call, and the response shows `adjustment_status` `CLOSED`. On the total-loss branch the claim stops at `SETTLED` — the salvage axis is `PENDING` and holds the join open — and a four-hop cascade runs. Hop one is the settlement bookkeeping just described. Hop two takes the loss subject off risk: an equipment item becomes `TOTALED`, an `IN_TRANSIT` transit shipment becomes `LOST` with the vehicle's `in_transit_value_cents` recomputed. Hop three, for equipment subjects only, recomputes the parent part's `rated_premium_cents` over its remaining items in status `RATED` or `ACTIVE` and appends an `ENDORSEMENT_RETURN` entry for the removed item's prorated premium, computed exactly as an endorsement removal is. Hop four recomputes the policy's `total_insured_value_cents`, `written_premium_cents` and `premium_balance_cents`.

**Salvage.** Only a `ClaimsAdjuster` records the salvage outcome, and only on a claim whose `adjustment_status` is `SETTLED` and whose `salvage_status` is `PENDING`, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SETTLED` — the wording is exact, because only a declared total loss ever arms the salvage axis, so a settled repair claim can never take this call. The submission carries `salvage_proceeds_cents`, which is stored. Proceeds above zero move the salvage axis `PENDING` → `RECOVERED` and set `salvage_credit_cents` to the smaller of `salvage_proceeds_cents` and `settlement_paid_cents`, so a recovery worth more than the cheque credits only what was paid; a `SALVAGE_RECOVERY` entry is appended to the loss ledger for the negative of `salvage_credit_cents`, `incurred_loss_cents` is refreshed, and the conserved capacity is compensated by subtracting `salvage_credit_cents` from `aggregate_paid_net_cents` and adding it back to `aggregate_available_cents`. Proceeds of exactly zero move the axis `PENDING` → `WAIVED` with `salvage_credit_cents` zero, no ledger entry at all, and both conserved quantities untouched — the capacity consumed by the loss stays consumed. Either branch closes the join: `adjustment_status` advances `SETTLED` → `CLOSED`, `CLOSED` is terminal on the adjustment axis, and `RECOVERED` and `WAIVED` are terminal on the salvage axis. No further transition is possible on either.

**The worksheet.** The settlement worksheet is a computed read that derives everything on the fly and returns `valuation_at_loss_cents`, `constructive_total_loss_threshold_cents`, a `constructive_total_loss_reached` boolean stating whether `repair_estimate_cents` has reached the threshold, `underreporting_factor_bp`, `claim_limit_cents`, the part's `deductible_cents`, `loss_of_use_paid_cents`, both `repair_basis_settlement_cents` and `total_loss_basis_settlement_cents` side by side, a `repair_basis_selectable` boolean that is true exactly when an adjustment on the repair basis would be permitted, and the part's remaining `aggregate_available_cents`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is reached with the two authentication headers of §2.1, and every response — success or failure — carries the envelope of §2.2. Status codes are assigned uniformly: a call that creates a new resource returns HTTP 201, every other successful call returns HTTP 200, including the action endpoints that transition a resource and the computed reads. There is no 204 anywhere in this API; the equipment-item removal endpoint uses the HTTP DELETE method but returns HTTP 200 with the updated item in `data`, because the item is retained in status `REMOVED` rather than erased. Failures follow the precedence ladder of §2.8 and resolve to exactly one of the error identifiers catalogued in §5, with the HTTP status listed there: 400 for shape and pagination failures, 401 for authentication, 403 for authorisation and segregation of duties, 404 for unresolved identifiers, 409 for state and structural conflicts, 412 for stale-version guards, and 422 for business-rule refusals. List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3 and report `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`. All paths are mounted under the `/inland-marine` prefix except the administrative clock endpoint, which is deliberately outside it.
