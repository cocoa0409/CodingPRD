# Fiberglass Insulation Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A fiberglass insulation plant is one of the few manufacturing environments where the melt furnace, the environmental permit, the laboratory and the sales channel are all coupled to the same handful of decisions. Glass is melted and fiberized continuously, a binder is sprayed onto the blanket and cured in a multi-zone oven, and the finished blanket is compressed into bales. Every one of those steps leaves a trace somewhere else: the recycled cullet charged into the furnace moves the plant's year-to-date recycling ratio and can trip a permit-driven restriction on the melt shop; the binder chemistry sprayed on the line determines both the formaldehyde and VOC mass the plant must report to its air regulator and whether the product may carry a formaldehyde-free claim; the compression ratio chosen to cut freight cost degrades the thickness the product recovers when an installer opens the bag, which in turn changes the thermal resistance the plant is allowed to print on that bag.

This document specifies the API that runs that plant end to end. It governs thirteen peer resources — plants, certification programs, binder formulations, product lines (SKUs), production runs, changeover events, thermal tests, finished-goods inventory lots, allocation cycles, channel orders, allocation lines and cullet batches — and the rules that bind them. There is no URL nesting anywhere in this API: every resource is addressed through its own top-level collection, and relationships between resources are declared as identifiers inside request bodies and discovered through filtered list queries. Product managers should read this as a specification of plant behaviour; engineers should read the state names, field names and error identifiers in this document as literal, since the test contract is written against them exactly as spelled here.

Three concerns dominate the design and recur in almost every section below. The first is **segregation of duties**: production, quality measurement, certification sign-off, environmental filing and channel allocation are five separate hands, and no single operator identity may occupy two of them on the same SKU. The second is **exact conservation**: glass mass charged into a furnace, trim recovered off a changeover, and square footage promised to a distributor must all balance to the last unit after every compensating operation — a reversed cullet charge, an aborted run, a revoked allocation line. The third is **determinism under scarcity**: when construction season demand exceeds supply, the allocation waterfall must produce the same answer every time from the same inputs, without ever consulting a resource's identifier as a tie-break.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: identity, envelope shape, pagination, resource properties, numeric precision, concurrency control, cascade semantics and the order in which validations fire.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the liveness probe must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that operator acts. If either header is missing, empty, or made up only of whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; otherwise the value is read with its surrounding whitespace trimmed off. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below, or is a valid role that is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The seven roles are `CorporateOps`, `PlantOperator`, `QualityEngineer`, `CertificationOfficer`, `EnvironmentalCompliance`, `ChannelManager` and `ChannelBuyer`.

- `CorporateOps` owns master and reference data. It registers plants, certification programs and SKUs, re-tunes packaging economics, and drives the server's logical clock. It may read every collection but may not run production, measure quality, sign certifications, file emissions reports or allocate supply.
- `PlantOperator` is the forming-line hand, scoped to exactly one plant. It schedules and drives runs, opens and closes changeovers, repacks lots, and stages, reverses or rejects cullet.
- `QualityEngineer` is the laboratory hand. It drafts and revises binder formulations, submits SKUs for thermal certification, requests and records thermal tests, and quarantines, scraps or releases finished-goods lots.
- `CertificationOfficer` is the certification-program signatory. It records the binder emissions-test measurement and rules on both binder and SKU certification, including the derate decision. It never generates the measurements it rules on.
- `EnvironmentalCompliance` is the air-permit officer. It files permit-period emissions reports and reviews melt postures, and it holds the only authority to clear a plant whose melt shop has been frozen.
- `ChannelManager` is the distributor and retail allocator. It opens, proposes, commits and cancels allocation cycles, cuts and revokes manual expedite lines, and ships orders. It may place an order on a buyer's behalf but may never cancel one.
- `ChannelBuyer` is the distributor or retail buyer. It places its own orders and cancels only its own.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Plant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a plant compare that resource's `plant_id` to `X-User-ID`, directly for a plant addressed in the path and transitively through `plant_id` for a production run, a changeover event or a cullet batch. A `PlantOperator` acting on a row belonging to any other plant is rejected with `ERR_PLANT_NOT_OWNED`. The one `PlantOperator` write that reaches an inventory lot — the repack of §3.7 — deliberately carries **no** ownership comparison at all, so any plant's operator may repack any lot; `ERR_PLANT_NOT_OWNED` is therefore never returned by that endpoint. The comparison is a write-side gate only, as rung 4 of §2.8 states: the read endpoints a `PlantOperator` is authorized for — including `GET /plants/{plantId}` — serve any plant's row without an ownership comparison.

For the other six roles — `CorporateOps`, `QualityEngineer`, `CertificationOfficer`, `EnvironmentalCompliance`, `ChannelManager` and `ChannelBuyer` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail (as `placed_by_user_id` on a channel order, as `recorded_by_user_id` on a thermal test, and as `certified_by_user_id` on a product line) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Two of those recorded identifiers are subsequently used as equality subjects: a `ChannelBuyer` cancelling an order must present an `X-User-ID` equal to that order's `placed_by_user_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`; and a `CertificationOfficer` ruling on a SKU must present an `X-User-ID` that differs from the recorded quality identifiers described in §2.10.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request: the literal prefix `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and Cascade Discovery

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0 and has no upper bound. Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted; a value that is not a base-10 integer, or an integer outside the accepted range, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unless a section below says otherwise, list endpoints return rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with server insertion order breaking ties between rows that share a `created_at` to the millisecond. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when the returned page stops short of the end of that filtered set.

Filtered list queries are not a convenience in this API — they are the **only** discovery channel for rows the server creates as a side effect of some other operation. Packaging a run cascade-creates an `InventoryLot`, closing a changeover and scrapping a quarantined lot each cascade-create a `CulletBatch`, and proposing an allocation cycle cascade-creates a fanned-out set of `AllocationLine` rows. None of those rows appear in the response body of the operation that created them. A client finds them by filtering the corresponding collection: `GET /inventory-lots` accepts `plant_id`, `product_line_id`, `production_run_id`, `parent_lot_id`, `form` and `status`; `GET /cullet-batches` accepts `plant_id`, `source_run_id`, `charged_run_id`, `source_type` and `status`; `GET /allocation-lines` accepts `allocation_cycle_id`, `channel_order_id`, `inventory_lot_id` and `status`.

Filter matching is exact and never an error. A filter supplied as an empty string is ignored; otherwise a row matches only when its stored value renders exactly as the supplied string — for the two boolean filters (`formaldehyde_free` and `peak_season`) that means the literals `true` or `false`, and for the integer filter `line_index` a base-10 integer. A filter value that is unrecognised, malformed or simply matches nothing therefore yields an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than a rejection, and several filters supplied together are conjunctive.

The remaining collections expose filters over their own foreign keys and enumerations. `GET /certification-programs` accepts `conformance_basis`. `GET /plants` accepts `melt_posture` and `emissions_status`. `GET /binder-formulations` accepts `status` and `formaldehyde_free`. `GET /product-lines` accepts `plant_id`, `binder_formulation_id`, `certification_program_id`, `form`, `facing_type` and `certification_status`. `GET /production-runs` accepts `plant_id`, `product_line_id`, `line_index`, `run_status` and `qc_status`. `GET /changeover-events` accepts `plant_id`, `outgoing_run_id`, `incoming_run_id`, `line_index` and `status`. `GET /thermal-tests` accepts `production_run_id`, `product_line_id`, `certification_program_id`, `status` and `conformance_verdict`. `GET /allocation-cycles` accepts `plant_id`, `product_line_id`, `allocation_basis`, `peak_season` and `status`. `GET /channel-orders` accepts `product_line_id`, `channel_type`, `priority_tier` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, the Logical Clock and Status Codes

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Both `created_at` and `updated_at` are read from real wall-clock UTC time, never from the logical clock described below; the logical clock governs only the domain instants a workflow stamps or compares (`certified_at` on a formulation, `closed_at` on a run, and every scheduling window, purge window, laboratory window, reporting period and need-by date).

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row — including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a plant's cullet ledger or an allocation cycle's coverage totals. The single exception is `CertificationProgram`, which is immutable once registered: a certification program row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and there is no endpoint that modifies one.

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201. Every other successful operation — reads, action endpoints that drive a state machine, and the packaging re-tune — returns HTTP 200.

All temporal comparisons in this domain read the server's **logical clock**, never real wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it holds that value until an operator sets it explicitly by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability. The clock retains and reports that instant as the exact string the caller supplied rather than a normalised rendering of it, and the clock-setting capability answers with a `data` object holding just `current_utc`. Run scheduling windows, changeover purge windows, laboratory occupancy windows, emissions reporting periods and channel order need-by dates are all evaluated against the logical clock's current reading. The liveness probe reports the clock's current value as `current_utc` alongside a `status` of the literal `ok`, and it is the only endpoint in the API that does not require the two identity headers.

### 2.5. Decimal Discipline: Precision and Rounding

Every mass, area, ratio and cost figure in this system is computed with decimal arithmetic. Floating-point accumulation is not permitted anywhere, and intermediate ratios are carried at full decimal precision and quantized only at the point the specification names. The rounding mode is round-half-up everywhere except where a rule explicitly calls for truncation toward zero, and every such exception is named in §3. The declared scale governs the arithmetic and the stored value; each figure is then emitted in a response as a JSON number rather than a quoted string, so a value whose fixed scale ends in trailing zeros may serialize with those zeros dropped and must be compared numerically rather than textually.

The scale of each figure is fixed as follows. Masses in kilograms and areas in square feet are held to 2 decimal places: this covers the run charge ledger (`total_charge_kg`, `allowed_cullet_kg`, `cullet_charged_kg`, `virgin_batch_kg`), the changeover scrap figures (`provisional_scrap_kg`, `reconciled_scrap_kg`, `scrap_variance_kg`) together with the two weighed inputs they are reconciled from (`weighed_trim_kg` and `recovered_cullet_kg`), the cullet batch `mass_kg`, and every square-footage figure on lots, cycles, orders and lines. Recycling ratios are held to 4 decimal places (`cullet_fraction` on a run, `cullet_fraction_ytd` on a plant, and `shortage_ratio` on an allocation cycle). Emissions masses in kilograms are held to 4 decimal places (`formaldehyde_kg` and `voc_kg` on a run, `reported_formaldehyde_kg` and `reported_voc_kg` on a plant), while a plant's permitted `formaldehyde_allowance_kg` is a 3-decimal-place input and `voc_allowance_kg` preserves the precision supplied. Percentages are held to 2 decimal places (`density_delta_pct`, `recovered_thickness_pct`, `fill_rate_pct`), as are `dwell_s`, `cure_index`, `purge_minutes`, `mean_r_value` and `min_r_value`. Compressed bale volume (`compressed_bale_volume_ft3`) is held to 3 decimal places. The three per-square-foot cost figures — `freight_cost_per_sqft`, `recovery_penalty_per_sqft` and `net_landed_cost_per_sqft` — are held to 6 decimal places. `bales_per_truck` and `bags` are whole integers. A handful of figures are pure measured inputs that no rule quantizes, and each is stored exactly as the caller supplied it: a run's `target_pull_kg`, every element of its `zone_setpoints_f`, and the `avg_zone_temp_f`, `line_speed_fpm` and `binder_applied_kg` recorded at cure start.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Plant and the Allocation Cycle

Two write operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`. In both cases the guard exists because the operation is legitimately repeatable — staging a second cullet batch at the same plant, or cutting a second expedite line against the same cycle, are both normal business actions — so the only thing that distinguishes an intended second write from a client acting on a view it has not refreshed is whether its observed version is still current. These are stale-version checks, not timing checks; nothing about them depends on two requests overlapping.

Staging a cullet batch requires `expected_plant_version` as a mandatory field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Plant`. If that value does not equal the plant's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLANT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The plant row is what carries `cullet_on_hand_kg`, `cullet_fraction_ytd` and `melt_posture`, so the guard forces a staging writer to prove its view of the cullet ledger and the melt posture is the one it actually read. A successful stage increments the plant's `version` by one, which is why a second stage against a stale figure fails.

Cutting a manual allocation line requires `expected_allocation_cycle_version` as a mandatory field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AllocationCycle`. If that value does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ALLOCATION_CYCLE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Proposing, committing and cancelling a cycle each increment its `version`, as does every successful manual line, so any of those advances invalidates a client's earlier read.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.7. Parallel State Dimensions and Cascade Propagation

Two entities in this domain carry more than one state dimension on a single row, and the dimensions advance independently of one another. A `Plant` carries `melt_posture` (one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `FROZEN`) alongside `emissions_status` (one of `COMPLIANT`, `REPORTED`, `EXCEEDANCE`); a plant whose emissions come back inside allowance returns to `REPORTED` without its melt posture being cleared. A `ProductionRun` carries `run_status` (one of `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `MELTING`, `CURING`, `PACKAGED`, `CLOSED`, `ABORTED`) alongside `qc_status` (one of `UNSAMPLED`, `SAMPLED`, `CONFORMING`, `NONCONFORMING`, `RETEST_PENDING`); a run may reach `CLOSED` while its quality dimension still reads `RETEST_PENDING`, and may be judged `NONCONFORMING` while it is still physically `CURING`. Neither dimension is derivable from the other, and neither may be advanced as a side effect of the other except where §3 names the cascade explicitly.

Cascades in this system run to their full depth in the same request, and each hop is a real mutation: the affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented. The deepest chain in the system runs four hops from a single emissions report — the plant freezes, its non-formaldehyde-free certified binders suspend, the SKUs on those binders suspend, and those SKUs' scheduled runs go on hold and their open or proposed allocation cycles cancel, revoking the lines those cycles had cut. Two cascades are deliberately **not** symmetric on the way back: clearing a frozen plant does not release the runs the freeze put on hold, and a conforming retest verdict does not release the lots the earlier nonconforming verdict quarantined. Each of those requires its own explicit release operation, described in §3.4 and §3.7 respectively.

### 2.8. The Global Validation Priority Ladder

Every write endpoint evaluates the rules applicable to it in one strict global order and returns the **first** violation it encounters. A lower rung is checked earlier and therefore wins any conflict with a later rung. This ordering is normative: when a request violates two rules at once, only the earlier rung's error identifier may be returned.

1. Identity headers present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role recognized and authorized for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Every identifier appearing in the request **path** resolves to an existing row, checked left to right, the leftmost unresolved identifier deciding which not-found error is returned (§2.9).
4. Plant ownership for a `PlantOperator` write — `ERR_PLANT_NOT_OWNED`; order ownership for a `ChannelBuyer` cancellation — `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`.
5. Request shape: required fields present, enum values drawn from their declared sets, numeric fields inside their declared ranges, timestamps expressed as RFC3339 UTC instants, every interval's end strictly after its start, a required `zone_setpoints_f` array non-empty — an empty `specimen_r_values` or `child_bags` array is instead carried past this rung and left to the domain rung that owns its cardinality rule — and fields a chosen branch requires to be omitted genuinely absent — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unknown fields, and fields the server itself derives, are ignored rather than rejected. This rung also owns natural-key uniqueness collisions — a duplicate plant `site_code`, certification-program `code`, binder-formulation `code` or product-line `sku` — which are likewise reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A numeric field may be supplied either as a JSON number or as a decimal string, an integer field additionally as a JSON float with no fractional part, and a boolean field either as a JSON boolean or as the strings `true` or `false`; a JSON `null` counts as absent rather than as a value. A request body that is missing, empty or unparseable is read as an empty object, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request **body** resolves, checked in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint, and only after the request shape has been accepted (§2.9).
7. Optimistic concurrency: `expected_allocation_cycle_version` on a manual allocation line, then `expected_plant_version` on a cullet stage (§2.6).
8. The target row's current state permits the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
9. Melt-posture gates: `ERR_MELT_POSTURE_FROZEN`, then `ERR_MELT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (§3.1).
10. Certification and safety integrity, in this order: the formaldehyde-free claim check `ERR_FORMALDEHYDE_FREE_CLAIM_INVALID`, the certified-binder gate `ERR_BINDER_NOT_CERTIFIED`, the binder emissions limit `ERR_FORMALDEHYDE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, the quarantined-lot block `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`, the conforming-run gate on lot release `ERR_RUN_NOT_CONFORMING`, and segregation of duties `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
11. Temporal and resource-occupancy rules, in this order: `ERR_CHANGEOVER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_LINE_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`, `ERR_LINE_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED`, then `ERR_LAB_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — the retest-availability check sits here, ahead of the lab cap, because a test with no retest due is refused before the window it proposes is ever measured against the program's concurrency.
12. Domain constraints, in this order: `ERR_OVEN_ZONE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_WIDTH_CHANGE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_TRIM_MASS_INCONSISTENT`, `ERR_SPECIMEN_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_DERATE_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_LOT_HAS_ALLOCATIONS`, `ERR_BAG_MULTIPLE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_BINDER_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_CULLET_BATCH_NOT_STAGED`, `ERR_CULLET_REVERSAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_EMISSIONS_PERIOD_INVALID`, then `ERR_CURE_INDEX_BELOW_MINIMUM`.
13. Allocation feasibility, in this order: `ERR_NO_ALLOCATABLE_SUPPLY`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ORDERS`, then `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LOT_AVAILABLE`.

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis, because they are easy to get backwards. Because the state check at rung 8 precedes every domain constraint at rung 12, a request that both targets a wrongly-stated row and carries a bad quantity reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. And because the line-occupancy check at rung 11 precedes the line-index range check, a run scheduled onto a nonexistent line index whose window also collides with a live run reports `ERR_LINE_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT` rather than `ERR_LINE_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

Rung 8 is also the catch-all for state preconditions: any precondition a state transition declares that no more specific rung already covers reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` with HTTP 409. This explicitly includes attempting to start a melt before the run's scheduled window has opened, attempting to close a changeover before its purge window has ended, and attempting to certify or derate a SKU that has no completed thermal test on file. It equally covers every repeat of an already-applied trigger and every trigger aimed at a row already in a terminal state.

### 2.9. Reference Resolution: Path Identifiers Before Body Identifiers

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right before any body content is examined, and the leftmost unresolved identifier decides the error. The twelve not-found identifiers are `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND` (for `plantId` in a path or `plant_id` in a body), `ERR_CERTIFICATION_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (`programId`, `certification_program_id`), `ERR_BINDER_FORMULATION_NOT_FOUND` (`binderId`, `binder_formulation_id`), `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_FOUND` (`productLineId`, `product_line_id`), `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND` (`runId`, and `production_run_id`, `outgoing_run_id`, `incoming_run_id` or `source_run_id` in a body), `ERR_CHANGEOVER_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` (`changeoverId`), `ERR_THERMAL_TEST_NOT_FOUND` (`testId`), `ERR_INVENTORY_LOT_NOT_FOUND` (`lotId`, `inventory_lot_id`), `ERR_ALLOCATION_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` (`cycleId`, `allocation_cycle_id`), `ERR_CHANNEL_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` (`orderId`, `channel_order_id`), `ERR_ALLOCATION_LINE_NOT_FOUND` (`lineId`) and `ERR_CULLET_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` (`batchId`, and any element of a supplied `cullet_batch_ids` array). All twelve carry HTTP 404.

Body foreign keys are resolved only after the request shape has been accepted, and they are resolved in the order the endpoint declares them. Concretely: a SKU creation resolves `plant_id`, then `binder_formulation_id`, then `certification_program_id`; a run creation resolves `plant_id` then `product_line_id`; a changeover creation resolves `plant_id`, then `outgoing_run_id`, then `incoming_run_id`; a thermal test request resolves `production_run_id`, then `product_line_id`, then `certification_program_id`; an allocation cycle resolves `plant_id` then `product_line_id`; a channel order resolves `product_line_id`; a manual allocation line resolves `allocation_cycle_id`, then `channel_order_id`, then `inventory_lot_id`; a cullet stage resolves `plant_id` and then an optionally supplied `source_run_id`; and a furnace charge resolves each element of `cullet_batch_ids`. Within that sequence a required body identifier's own presence is checked immediately before it is resolved rather than in one up-front shape sweep, so a request whose earlier identifier fails to resolve reports that not-found error even when a later required identifier is missing from the payload altogether.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties Between Measurement and Sign-Off

Certification sign-off on a SKU is barred to any operator who touched that SKU's measurements. The `X-User-ID` issuing a product-line certification decision must differ from **every** `recorded_by_user_id` present on the thermal tests of that product line, and — on any second or later decision against the same SKU — must also differ from that SKU's current `certified_by_user_id`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 409. The certification decision that awards `CERTIFIED` or `DERATED` status carries a stricter, transition-level form of the same rule: the specific qualifying thermal test's `recorded_by_user_id` must not be the deciding officer.

The complementary half of the rule lives in the role matrix rather than in a runtime check: the operator who records a thermal specimen result acts as `QualityEngineer`, and the operator who signs the certification acts as `CertificationOfficer`, so the two hands are also separated by authorization. Recording the binder emissions measurement is likewise a `CertificationOfficer` action while drafting and revising the binder chemistry is a `QualityEngineer` action.

### 2.11. Whole-Bag Indivisibility and the Coverage Conservation Invariant

Insulation ships in compressed bags, and a bag cannot be split. Every SKU declares `bag_sqft`, the square feet of product in one bag, and this is the indivisible unit of allocation for that SKU. Any square-footage figure written onto an allocation line — whether supplied by hand or produced by the shortage waterfall — must be an exact positive integer multiple of the SKU's `bag_sqft`, and the line's `bags` is that multiple. A figure that is not such a multiple is rejected with `ERR_BAG_MULTIPLE_MISMATCH`. The same rule governs a lot split: each element of the supplied `child_bags` list must be a positive whole number and the elements must sum to exactly the parent lot's `bags`, so that the children's combined coverage reproduces the parent's `produced_sqft` to the square foot.

Coverage conservation is an invariant the server must be able to assert after every write that touches an allocation. For any single SKU, the sum of `available_sqft` across its live lots, plus the sum of `allocated_sqft` across the allocated lines drawn on those lots, equals the sum of `produced_sqft` across those live lots. A lot that has left the live set — because it was quarantined, repacked, consumed or scrapped — is excluded from all three sums. This invariant is what makes every compensating operation in §3.8 and §3.9 exact rather than approximate.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Plant, Its Recycling Policy and the Melt-Posture Dimension

A `CorporateOps` operator brings a plant online by registering its physical geometry and its permit envelope in one payload. The plant is identified by a human-readable `name` and a short `site_code` of three to eight uppercase letters and digits that must be unique across every plant on file. Oven geometry is captured by `oven_zone_count`, the number of independently controlled curing-oven zones, which must be at least one, and `oven_length_ft`, the oven's length in feet, which must be positive and which drives dwell time in every cure calculation. `production_line_count` records how many fiberization lines the plant runs and must be at least one; it defines the set of valid line indices for that plant. The permit envelope is captured by `permit_number`, the air-emissions permit identifier on file with the regulator, together with `formaldehyde_allowance_kg` and `voc_allowance_kg`, the permitted masses in kilograms for one reporting period, both of which must be positive.

Three policy dials are optional at registration and carry defaults. `max_cullet_fraction` is the hard permit cap on the recycled-glass share of any single melt charge; it must be greater than zero and no more than 0.5, is held to two decimal places, and defaults to 0.30 — thirty percent of the charge. `cullet_restrict_fraction` is the year-to-date recycling level at or above which the melt shop tightens; it must be greater than zero and no more than `max_cullet_fraction`, is held to two decimal places, and defaults to 0.28. That ceiling is measured against the `max_cullet_fraction` in force for the same request — the supplied value, or 0.30 when none was supplied — and it constrains only a value the caller actually supplies: the 0.28 default is written as-is and is never re-checked, so a plant registered with a `max_cullet_fraction` below 0.28 and no explicit restrict level legitimately carries a restrict level above its own cap. `max_width_change_in` is the largest absolute batt-width step, in inches, that the plant's mandrel and chopper set can absorb in a single changeover; it must be positive and defaults to 8.0 inches. A newly registered plant begins with `melt_posture` `OPEN`, `posture_reason` null, `override_reason` null, `emissions_status` `COMPLIANT`, `version` 1, every cullet and charge counter at zero, `reported_formaldehyde_kg` and `reported_voc_kg` at 0.0000, and `reported_period_start` and `reported_period_end` null.

The cullet ledger on a plant row is a running account of recycled glass, and it is maintained by the plant's own operations rather than written by the caller. `cullet_received_kg_ytd` is the total mass of every cullet batch ever staged at the plant and never decreases. `cullet_charged_kg_ytd` is the mass that has been charged into furnaces, `cullet_rejected_kg_ytd` the mass rejected as unsuitable, and `cullet_on_hand_kg` the mass still sitting in the yard — equal to the received total less the charged total less the rejected total, held to two decimal places, and identical to the summed mass of the plant's batches still in `STAGED` status. `total_charge_kg_ytd` accumulates the total charge of every run of the plant whose physical status is `MELTING`, `CURING`, `PACKAGED` or `CLOSED`. From those two accumulators the plant derives `cullet_fraction_ytd`: the charged cullet mass divided by the total charge mass, rounded half up to four decimal places, and reported as 0.0000 whenever the total charge mass is zero.

The melt-shop posture dimension has three values and four ways to move. `OPEN` becomes `RESTRICTED` automatically, evaluated by the server at the end of a furnace charge, whenever the freshly recomputed `cullet_fraction_ytd` stands **at or above** `cullet_restrict_fraction` — equality trips the restriction, it does not have to be exceeded. That transition sets `posture_reason` to `CULLET_FRACTION_AT_RESTRICT_LEVEL` and increments the plant's `version` by one. `RESTRICTED` returns to `OPEN` only through an `EnvironmentalCompliance` posture review carrying a `decision` of `RECOMPUTE`, which is permitted only while the plant is currently `RESTRICTED` and only if `cullet_fraction_ytd` has fallen **strictly below** `cullet_restrict_fraction` by review time; `override_reason` must be omitted on a `RECOMPUTE`. That path becomes reachable after a charge reversal lowers the charged-cullet accumulator. The review clears `posture_reason` to null and increments `version`, and it leaves any `override_reason` recorded by an earlier override exactly where it was.

`OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` becomes `FROZEN` when a permit-period emissions report comes back over allowance, as described in §3.9; in either case `posture_reason` becomes `EMISSIONS_EXCEEDANCE`, and when the plant was `RESTRICTED` the cullet restriction is simply superseded. A `FROZEN` plant returns to `OPEN` only through an `EnvironmentalCompliance` posture review carrying a `decision` of `OVERRIDE_TO_OPEN`, which requires a non-empty `override_reason`; the supplied text is stored on the plant in a field of that same name, `posture_reason` becomes `MANUAL_OVERRIDE`, `version` is incremented, and — critically — the `emissions_status` dimension is left exactly where it was and runs the freeze put on hold are **not** released automatically. Each held run needs its own release, described in §3.4.

Posture gates four operations. While a plant's `melt_posture` is `FROZEN`, scheduling a production run, charging a furnace, staging a cullet batch and committing an allocation cycle are all rejected with `ERR_MELT_POSTURE_FROZEN`. A `RESTRICTED` posture is narrower: it blocks only the staging of new cullet, rejected with `ERR_MELT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, because no further recycled glass may enter the yard until an environmental review has cleared the plant back to `OPEN`. A `RESTRICTED` plant may still schedule runs and commit allocations, and it may still charge a furnace — provided that charge names no cullet at all. A charge that names one or more batches while the plant is `RESTRICTED` has no legal transition and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Certification programs are the other piece of reference data `CorporateOps` owns, and they are immutable once written. A program declares a `code` unique across all programs, and a `conformance_basis` of either `PER_SPECIMEN` or `LOT_MEAN` that decides which of the two thermal verdict variants in §3.6 governs its tests. `r_value_tolerance` is the non-negative shortfall in R units a specimen or lot mean is allowed below the declared value before it is judged non-conforming, held to two decimal places. `min_specimen_count` is the minimum number of specimen R-values a recorded result must carry and must be at least one; `max_concurrent_lab_tests` is the ceiling on how many of the program's in-lab tests may hold overlapping laboratory windows and must be at least one; `retest_limit` is the highest attempt number a test of this program may ever reach and must be at least one. `derate_allowed` is the boolean policy switch that decides whether the program permits publishing a lowered declared R-value instead of rejecting a SKU outright.

### 3.2. Binder Chemistry: Emissions Certification and the Revision Loop

A `QualityEngineer` drafts a binder formulation by declaring its chemistry: a `code` unique across all formulations, the marketing and regulatory claim `formaldehyde_free`, the free-formaldehyde content of the wet binder in parts per million as `formaldehyde_ppm`, the VOC mass released per kilogram of binder applied in grams per kilogram as `voc_g_per_kg`, the minimum oven average temperature in Fahrenheit at which the chemistry cures as `min_cure_temp_f`, the minimum oven dwell in seconds at that temperature as `min_dwell_s`, and the target loss-on-ignition percentage of finished product mass as `loi_target_pct`. The last three must all be positive; `formaldehyde_ppm` and `voc_g_per_kg` must be non-negative. A new formulation lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0, and with `emissions_cert_id`, `lab_reference`, `revision_notes` and `certified_at` all null. The formulation's own certification state is carried in a field named `status` — matching the `status` filter of §2.3 — and a formulation row carries no `certification_status` field; `certification_status` belongs to a `ProductLine` (§3.3).

The formaldehyde-free claim must be backed by the chemistry, and the rule is symmetric in both directions: a formulation claiming `formaldehyde_free` true must declare `formaldehyde_ppm` of exactly 0, and a formulation claiming `formaldehyde_free` false must declare a `formaldehyde_ppm` strictly greater than 0. Either mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORMALDEHYDE_FREE_CLAIM_INVALID`, and the check guards both the initial drafting and any later revision of the chemistry.

The certification lifecycle is a cycle, not a line. Submitting a formulation moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, permitted only from `DRAFT` and only when either the formaldehyde-free claim is absent or the measured content is exactly zero; a violation of either precondition is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `CertificationOfficer` then records the certification lab's measurement, supplying a fresh `formaldehyde_ppm`, a fresh `voc_g_per_kg` and an external `lab_reference`; the two measured figures **overwrite** the drafted values on the row and the formulation advances `SUBMITTED → EMISSIONS_TESTING` with the reference stored and both `emissions_cert_id` and `certified_at` cleared to null. The officer's measurement is deliberately **not** re-checked against the `formaldehyde_free` claim — the symmetric claim check guards only the drafting and revision endpoints, so a formaldehyde-free formulation whose lab measurement comes back non-zero is judged only against the certification ceilings below. A measurement above the certification ceilings never reaches `EMISSIONS_TESTING` at all: free formaldehyde may not exceed 300 parts per million and VOC release may not exceed 6.0 grams per kilogram of binder, and a breach of either is rejected with `ERR_FORMALDEHYDE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The officer's verdict on a formulation under test forks four ways from one decision, and the four branches write genuinely different rows. A `decision` of `CERTIFY` requires the formulation to be in `EMISSIONS_TESTING` with free formaldehyde at or below 300 parts per million and VOC release at or below 6.0 grams per kilogram; it moves the formulation `EMISSIONS_TESTING → CERTIFIED`, stores the supplied `emissions_cert_id` as the certificate identifier — that field is optional, and `emissions_cert_id` simply stays null when it is omitted — and stamps `certified_at` with the logical clock's current reading, rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits rather than echoed in whatever form the clock was set with. A `decision` of `REQUEST_REVISION` requires the formulation to be in `EMISSIONS_TESTING` and a non-empty `revision_notes`; it moves the formulation `EMISSIONS_TESTING → REVISION_REQUIRED` and stores the note — no certificate is minted and `certified_at` stays null. A `decision` of `SUSPEND` moves a `CERTIFIED` formulation to `SUSPENDED`. A `decision` of `WITHDRAW` moves a `CERTIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVISION_REQUIRED` formulation to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`.

Both `SUSPEND` and `WITHDRAW` fire the same downstream cascade, and it reaches two levels below the binder. Every product line referencing the formulation whose own certification status is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, `CERTIFIED` or `DERATED` transitions to `SUSPENDED`; then, one hop further, every production run of each of those SKUs whose physical status is still `SCHEDULED` transitions to `HELD` with `hold_reason` set to `PRODUCT_LINE_SUSPENDED`. Each affected row's `updated_at` and `version` move with the cascade.

Reworking a formulation closes the revision loop. A `QualityEngineer` submits a `decision` of either `REVISE` or `WITHDRAW`. On `REVISE`, all six chemistry fields — `formaldehyde_free`, `formaldehyde_ppm`, `voc_g_per_kg`, `min_cure_temp_f`, `min_dwell_s` and `loi_target_pct` — are required, and the formulation moves from `REVISION_REQUIRED` or `SUSPENDED` back to `DRAFT`: `revision_count` increases by one, and `emissions_cert_id`, `lab_reference`, `certified_at` and `revision_notes` are all cleared to null. On `WITHDRAW`, the six chemistry fields must be omitted, and a `REVISION_REQUIRED` formulation moves straight to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, firing the same two-hop product-line suspension cascade described above. The loop is capped: `revision_count` must be strictly below two before a further revision is accepted, so a formulation may cycle back to `DRAFT` at most twice. A third attempt is rejected with `ERR_BINDER_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`.

A second cycle exists on the certified side. A formulation that was suspended can re-enter `EMISSIONS_TESTING` directly from `SUSPENDED` when a `CertificationOfficer` records a new lab measurement, provided no plant is still `FROZEN` — the server does not track which exceedance caused the suspension, so it tests every plant referenced by a SKU built on the formulation, and any one of them still `FROZEN` refuses the measurement with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; that transition stores the new `lab_reference` and clears both `emissions_cert_id` and `certified_at`, so the formulation must earn its certificate again. The full second cycle therefore reads `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED → EMISSIONS_TESTING → CERTIFIED`, and the revision cycle reads `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → EMISSIONS_TESTING → REVISION_REQUIRED → DRAFT`.

Binder certification is a hard gate on two downstream operations: a production run may not be scheduled against a SKU whose formulation is not in `CERTIFIED` status, and a SKU may not be submitted for thermal certification under the same condition. Both refusals carry `ERR_BINDER_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

### 3.3. Defining a SKU and Pricing the Compression Trade-Off

A `CorporateOps` operator defines a product line — a SKU — by binding a chemistry, a plant and a certification scheme to a physical description of the finished blanket. The identifiers `plant_id`, `binder_formulation_id` and `certification_program_id` name the licensed plant, the binder applied on the line and the thermal-performance scheme the SKU will be judged under. `sku` is the stock-keeping unit string and must be unique across all product lines. `form` is either `BATT` or `ROLL`. `density_pcf` is the nominal product density in pounds per cubic foot, positive and held to two decimal places. `facing_type` is the vapour-retarder facing applied to the blanket and is one of `UNFACED`, `KRAFT`, `FSK` or `FOIL`. `width_in` is the finished width in inches and must be exactly one of 15, 16, 23 or 24. `thickness_in` is the nominal recovered thickness in inches and must be positive. `declared_r_value` is the thermal resistance printed on the bag — the number every thermal test is judged against — positive and held to one decimal place. `bag_sqft` is the square feet of product in one compressed bag and is the indivisible allocation unit of §2.11. `bale_volume_ft3` is the uncompressed bale volume in cubic feet and `bale_mass_kg` the mass of one bale in kilograms, both positive. `mass_rate_kg_per_min` is the glass mass leaving the forming hood each minute at nominal line speed, positive, and it is what prices a changeover purge in §3.5. Finally `compression_ratio` — uncompressed volume divided by bagged volume — is optional, must lie between 1.0 and 8.0 inclusive, is held to one decimal place, and defaults to 6.0.

A new SKU lands in `certification_status` `DRAFT` with `nonconformance_count` 0, `effective_declared_r_value` null and `certified_by_user_id` null. The full certification enumeration for a SKU is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, `CERTIFIED`, `DERATED`, `SUSPENDED` and `WITHDRAWN`.

Packaging is where freight cost and installed performance pull against each other, and the API prices both sides on every SKU write. Compressing a bale harder puts more square footage on a truck but costs recovered thickness when the installer opens the bag. The trade-off is computed in five steps, all in decimal arithmetic, and it is recomputed in full whenever the packaging geometry changes.

First, **recovery at installation**. Compression up to a ratio of 4.0 is free of recovery loss; beyond that, each whole ratio point above 4.0 costs 5.0 percentage points of recovered thickness. The excess is the compression ratio less 4.0, or zero if the ratio is at or below 4.0. From a nominal 100 percent the system subtracts that excess multiplied by 5.0, rounded half up to two decimal places, and then subtracts a further flat 2.0 percentage points whenever the SKU's facing is anything other than `UNFACED`. The result is rounded half up to two decimal places, and `recovered_thickness_pct` is the greater of that result and a hard floor of 80.00 percent.

Second, **bale volume after compression**. `compressed_bale_volume_ft3` is the uncompressed `bale_volume_ft3` divided by the `compression_ratio`, rounded half up to three decimal places.

Third, **truckload capacity under two independent limits**. A trailer holds 3800 cubic feet and carries 19500 kilograms of payload. The volume-bound count is 3800 divided by `compressed_bale_volume_ft3`, truncated down to a whole number of bales; the payload-bound count is 19500 divided by `bale_mass_kg`, likewise truncated down. `bales_per_truck` is the smaller of those two counts — whichever limit binds first wins, and for a lightly compressed light-density SKU that is usually the cube rather than the scale.

Fourth, **freight per square foot**. A truckload moves for a flat 2450.00 US dollars. `freight_cost_per_sqft` is that rate divided by the square feet on one truckload — that is, by `bales_per_truck` multiplied by `bag_sqft` — rounded half up to six decimal places. When the geometry is bulky or heavy enough that `bales_per_truck` comes out 0, no square footage rides the truck and `freight_cost_per_sqft` is 0.000000 rather than an error.

Fifth, **the installed-recovery charge-back**. The declared recovery minimum is 95.0 percent. The shortfall is 95.0 less `recovered_thickness_pct`, or zero when recovery meets or beats the minimum, and only that shortfall is charged back. `recovery_penalty_per_sqft` is the shortfall in percentage points multiplied by 0.0012 US dollars per point, rounded half up to six decimal places. `net_landed_cost_per_sqft` is `freight_cost_per_sqft` plus `recovery_penalty_per_sqft`, rounded half up to six decimal places, and it is the single number a product manager compares across candidate packaging configurations.

Re-tuning packaging is a `CorporateOps` capability and is permitted while a SKU is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, `CERTIFIED` or `DERATED`. The caller may supply any subset of `compression_ratio`, `bale_volume_ft3`, `bale_mass_kg` and `bag_sqft`; the supplied fields replace their stored values and all six derived figures — `recovered_thickness_pct`, `compressed_bale_volume_ft3`, `bales_per_truck`, `freight_cost_per_sqft`, `recovery_penalty_per_sqft` and `net_landed_cost_per_sqft` — are recomputed from scratch by the five steps above. Supplying none of the four is accepted rather than rejected: the six derived figures are simply recomputed from the stored geometry, and the SKU's `updated_at` and `version` still move as on any other mutation.

### 3.4. Driving a Production Run: Occupancy, Charge, Cure and Close

Scheduling a run is a `PlantOperator` action and it reserves one forming line for one window. The payload names the `plant_id` and the `product_line_id` being produced, the `line_index` of the fiberization line to occupy, the inclusive `scheduled_start` and the exclusive `scheduled_end` of the occupancy, the `target_pull_kg` of fiberized glass to pull, and `zone_setpoints_f` — the planned curing-oven zone setpoints in Fahrenheit. `target_pull_kg` must be positive and `scheduled_end` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start`. Both instants are stored and echoed back as the exact strings the caller supplied — trimmed of surrounding whitespace but never re-rendered into a canonical form — while every comparison against them runs on the instants they denote. The new run lands with `run_status` `SCHEDULED`, `qc_status` `UNSAMPLED`, `hold_reason` null, `changeover_scrap_kg` 0.00, `cullet_charged_kg` 0.00, and every other mass, cure and emissions figure null.

Four constraints guard the schedule, and their relative order matters because a badly formed request can violate several at once. The plant must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MELT_POSTURE_FROZEN`. The SKU's binder formulation must be `CERTIFIED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BINDER_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The occupancy window is checked next: the half-open interval running from `scheduled_start` up to but not including `scheduled_end` must not overlap the interval of any other run on the same plant and the same `line_index` whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `MELTING` or `CURING`, and a violation returns `ERR_LINE_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`. Because the interval is half-open, two back-to-back runs that share an endpoint — one ending exactly where the next begins — do **not** conflict, and the comparison is made pairwise against each live run rather than against an aggregate. Only after occupancy does the system verify that `line_index` falls between 1 and the plant's `production_line_count` inclusive, returning `ERR_LINE_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE` otherwise — a `line_index` that is not an integer at all never reaches that rung, since being a whole number is a plain shape requirement reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 5; a request that both collides in time and names a nonexistent line therefore reports the occupancy conflict. Last, the array length of `zone_setpoints_f` must equal the plant's `oven_zone_count` exactly and every setpoint in it must be positive, or the request is refused with `ERR_OVEN_ZONE_MISMATCH`.

Charging the furnace advances the run `SCHEDULED → MELTING` and is the single most tightly coupled operation in the API. The payload carries only `cullet_batch_ids`, the list of staged cullet batches the operator wants to put into this melt; that field is **optional** — omitted, null or supplied as an empty array it simply means an all-virgin charge, which is always legal. The run must be `SCHEDULED`; the logical clock must have reached or passed `scheduled_start`, because a run may not be started before its scheduled window opens, and an early attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — but there is no upper temporal bound, so a charge is still accepted after `scheduled_end` has passed; the plant must not be `FROZEN`; and every named batch must be a cullet batch of the run's own plant currently in `STAGED` status, or the charge is refused with `ERR_CULLET_BATCH_NOT_STAGED`.

The charge itself resolves what looks like a circular cap — recycled glass is capped as a fraction of the total charge, and the total charge includes the recycled glass — by pinning the evaluation order. The total charge is derived from the pull target alone: `total_charge_kg` is `target_pull_kg` divided by a melt yield of 0.92, meaning ninety-two percent of what is charged survives as fiberized product, rounded half up to two decimal places. The cullet ceiling is then taken against that total and never against the virgin mass: `allowed_cullet_kg` is `total_charge_kg` multiplied by the plant's `max_cullet_fraction`, truncated **down** to two decimal places. Batches are then consumed **whole** and greedily in `created_at` ascending order: for each staged batch in turn, if adding its `mass_kg` to the mass charged so far would still leave the running total at or below `allowed_cullet_kg`, the batch is charged; otherwise it is skipped entirely — never partially charged — and left in `STAGED` for a later run, and smaller batches later in the ordering are still considered. The supplied array is consumed exactly as it was given and is never de-duplicated, so an identifier repeated in it is weighed once per occurrence. `cullet_charged_kg` is the resulting sum. Virgin glass is the residual, so the charge always balances exactly: `virgin_batch_kg` is `total_charge_kg` less `cullet_charged_kg`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `cullet_fraction` for this run alone is `cullet_charged_kg` divided by `total_charge_kg`, rounded half up to four decimal places.

Each batch the selection accepts transitions `STAGED → CHARGED` and has its `charged_run_id` set to the run. That field is never supplied by a caller: the charge selection writes it and only a reversal or a run abort clears it. In the same request the plant's ledger moves: `cullet_charged_kg_ytd` increases by the mass charged, `total_charge_kg_ytd` increases by the run's total charge, `cullet_fraction_ytd` is recomputed as the charged-cullet accumulator divided by the total-charge accumulator rounded half up to four decimal places, and `cullet_on_hand_kg` is recomputed as received less charged less rejected. Finally, if the plant was `OPEN` and the refreshed `cullet_fraction_ytd` now stands at or above `cullet_restrict_fraction`, the posture tightens to `RESTRICTED` as described in §3.1. The ledger refresh and that posture tightening are a single mutation of the plant row, so a successful charge advances the plant's `version` by exactly one whether or not the posture moved.

Starting the cure advances the run `MELTING → CURING` and requires three measurements from the line: `avg_zone_temp_f`, the measured average oven temperature in Fahrenheit; `line_speed_fpm`, the measured line speed in feet per minute; and `binder_applied_kg`, the mass of wet binder actually sprayed. All three must be positive. Dwell time follows from oven geometry: `dwell_s` is the plant's `oven_length_ft` divided by `line_speed_fpm`, multiplied by 60 to convert minutes to seconds, rounded half up to two decimal places.

From dwell and temperature the system derives `cure_index`, a single adequacy number for how well the binder actually cured. The temperature margin is measured against a 60-degree Fahrenheit span above the binder's minimum cure temperature: subtract the formulation's `min_cure_temp_f` from `avg_zone_temp_f`, divide by 60.0, and clamp the result into the closed range from 0 to 1 — an oven running below the binder's minimum contributes a margin of exactly 0, and one running 60 degrees or more above it contributes exactly 1. The dwell ratio is `dwell_s` divided by the formulation's `min_dwell_s`, capped at 1.25 so that no amount of extra dwell can compensate without limit. `cure_index` is then the dwell ratio multiplied by one plus the temperature margin, divided by two, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places — so a line running at exactly the binder's minimum dwell and exactly its minimum temperature scores 50.00, and a fully saturated margin at the dwell cap scores 125.00. Intermediate ratios are carried at full decimal precision and quantized only at the end. The gate is a floor of 100.00: a `cure_index` **below** 100.00 is refused with `ERR_CURE_INDEX_BELOW_MINIMUM`, and a value of exactly 100.00 passes.

The same request also books the run's environmental contribution from the binder actually applied. `formaldehyde_kg` is `binder_applied_kg` multiplied by the formulation's `formaldehyde_ppm` divided by one million, rounded half up to four decimal places — parts per million converted to a mass fraction. `voc_kg` is `binder_applied_kg` multiplied by the formulation's `voc_g_per_kg` divided by one thousand, rounded half up to four decimal places, converting grams per kilogram into kilograms. A formaldehyde-free chemistry therefore contributes exactly 0.0000 kilograms of formaldehyde while still contributing VOC mass.

Packaging advances the run `CURING → PACKAGED` and takes `bags_produced`, the whole count of bags baled off the line, which must be positive. The cure gate is re-checked here: `cure_index` must still stand at or above 100.00, and a failure of that re-check is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than as `ERR_CURE_INDEX_BELOW_MINIMUM`, because it is the row's condition and not the payload that is at fault. The product of `bags_produced` and the SKU's `bag_sqft` must be greater than zero, and the run must already carry a non-zero `binder_applied_kg`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success `produced_sqft` on the run is `bags_produced` multiplied by the SKU's `bag_sqft`, and the server cascade-creates exactly one finished-goods lot: status `AVAILABLE`, `parent_lot_id` null, `bags` equal to `bags_produced`, `produced_sqft` equal to the run's `produced_sqft`, `allocated_sqft` 0.00 and `available_sqft` equal to `produced_sqft`. That lot appears in no response body; it is found only by filtering the lot collection on the run, as described in §2.3.

Closing advances the run `PACKAGED → CLOSED` and stamps `closed_at` with the logical clock's current reading, rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits rather than echoed in whatever form the clock was set with. Closing requires the run to have been sampled at least once: its `qc_status` must be one of `SAMPLED`, `CONFORMING`, `NONCONFORMING` or `RETEST_PENDING`, and an `UNSAMPLED` run cannot be closed. Note that a run may close while its quality dimension is still unresolved — a `RETEST_PENDING` run is closable — because the two dimensions on the row are independent per §2.7.

Aborting is the compensating exit and is available from `SCHEDULED`, `MELTING` or `CURING`, each landing on the terminal `ABORTED`. The payload carries a mandatory non-empty `abort_reason`, which is retained internally for audit and is not surfaced as a field on the run row. Aborting from `SCHEDULED` has no ledger consequence. Aborting from `MELTING` or `CURING` unwinds the charge exactly: every cullet batch whose `charged_run_id` is this run and whose status is `CHARGED` transitions `CHARGED → STAGED` and has `charged_run_id` cleared to null; the run's `cullet_charged_kg` returns to 0.00, its `virgin_batch_kg` becomes equal to its `total_charge_kg` — both `total_charge_kg` and `allowed_cullet_kg` are left exactly as they were fixed at melt start — and its `cullet_fraction` returns to 0.0000. Nothing else on the row is rewound: a run aborted out of `CURING` keeps the `dwell_s`, `cure_index`, `formaldehyde_kg` and `voc_kg` figures booked at cure start, though the emissions roll-up of §3.9 excludes an `ABORTED` run from every period regardless. The plant's `cullet_on_hand_kg`, `cullet_charged_kg_ytd`, `total_charge_kg_ytd` and `cullet_fraction_ytd` are all recomputed, restoring the glass-mass ledger to precisely the state it held before the melt began.

Releasing a hold is the only way a `HELD` run returns to `SCHEDULED`, and the conditions that put it there must genuinely have cleared: the plant's `melt_posture` must read exactly `OPEN` — a merely `RESTRICTED` plant is not enough — and the SKU's `certification_status` must be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `WITHDRAWN`. On success `hold_reason` is cleared to null. `hold_reason` itself carries `PLANT_FROZEN` when a plant freeze caused the hold and `PRODUCT_LINE_SUSPENDED` when a SKU suspension caused it, and it is null whenever the run is not held.

### 3.5. Changeovers, Purge Scrap and Recycling Glass Back Into the Melt

Switching a forming line from one SKU to another wastes glass. Density, width and facing changes all take time to settle, and everything produced during that settling window is off-spec. A `PlantOperator` opens a changeover naming the `plant_id`, the `outgoing_run_id` of the run that has just finished, the `incoming_run_id` of the run scheduled to start next, the `line_index` being re-profiled, and the inclusive `window_start` and inclusive `window_end` of the purge window. The outgoing run must be `PACKAGED` or `CLOSED` and the incoming run must be `SCHEDULED` or `HELD`; both runs must belong to the plant named in the payload and both must sit on the payload's `line_index`. Those four conditions are state preconditions evaluated at rung 8 in exactly that order, and each of them is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Separately, `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`, and both instants are stored and echoed back as the exact strings the caller supplied — trimmed of surrounding whitespace but never re-rendered into a canonical form. As with a run schedule, `line_index` must lie between 1 and the plant's `production_line_count` inclusive, or the request is refused with `ERR_LINE_INDEX_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

Purge windows on the same line may not crowd each other. Each changeover's window is inflated by half an hour — 30 minutes — on **each** side and treated as a **closed** interval; the inflated interval of a new changeover must not intersect the identically inflated interval of any other changeover on the same plant and `line_index` whose status is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`. Because the intervals are closed, two inflated windows that merely touch at a single endpoint **do** conflict, and the request is refused with `ERR_CHANGEOVER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Separately, the width step the changeover demands must be physically absorbable: the absolute difference between the incoming SKU's `width_in` and the outgoing SKU's `width_in` must be no greater than the plant's `max_width_change_in`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WIDTH_CHANGE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

Opening the changeover snapshots the two SKUs so that later edits to either cannot rewrite history: `from_density_pcf` and `to_density_pcf` take the outgoing and incoming SKUs' `density_pcf`, `from_width_in` and `to_width_in` take their `width_in`, and `facing_changed` is true exactly when the two SKUs differ in `facing_type`. The event lands in status `OPEN` with `weighed_trim_kg`, `recovered_cullet_kg`, `reconciled_scrap_kg` and `scrap_variance_kg` all null until the close writes them; the only other status it ever holds is `CLOSED`. Nothing requires the two runs to name different SKUs — a changeover between two runs of the same product line is accepted and simply prices out at the bare base allowance.

Purge scrap is then priced **twice**, from two different sources, and the two figures answer two different questions. The **provisional** figure is computed at creation, before any trim has reached a scale, and it is a time-based estimate. `density_delta_pct` is the absolute difference between the two densities, divided by the outgoing density, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to four decimal places for storage and to two decimal places where it feeds the purge estimate. `purge_minutes` is then the sum of four terms: a base transition allowance of 12 minutes; a density term equal to `density_delta_pct` multiplied by 0.40 minutes per percentage point, rounded half up to two decimal places and then **capped at 20 minutes**; a further 8 minutes if and only if `from_width_in` and `to_width_in` differ; and a further 15 minutes if and only if `facing_changed` is true. `provisional_scrap_kg` is `purge_minutes` multiplied by the outgoing SKU's `mass_rate_kg_per_min`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The outgoing run's `changeover_scrap_kg` is set to that provisional figure immediately.

The **reconciled** figure is computed at close, once the trim has actually been weighed. Closing a changeover requires the event to be `OPEN` and the logical clock to have reached or passed `window_end`; an attempt to close early has no legal transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The payload carries `weighed_trim_kg`, the gross off-spec mass on the scale, and `recovered_cullet_kg`, the portion of that trim diverted back to the cullet pile; both are mandatory, and a missing one is a plain shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both must be non-negative and `recovered_cullet_kg` must not exceed `weighed_trim_kg`, or the close is refused with `ERR_TRIM_MASS_INCONSISTENT`. On success the event moves `OPEN → CLOSED`, both figures are stored quantized to two decimal places, and two derived numbers are written — each computed from the values exactly as supplied rather than from those stored quantizations. `reconciled_scrap_kg` — the mass genuinely lost — is `weighed_trim_kg` less `recovered_cullet_kg`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `scrap_variance_kg` — how far the time-based estimate missed the scale — is `weighed_trim_kg` less `provisional_scrap_kg`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it may legitimately be negative when the estimate ran high. The outgoing run's `changeover_scrap_kg` is then **overwritten** with the reconciled figure, replacing the provisional one.

Recovered trim does not vanish; it goes back in the furnace. When `recovered_cullet_kg` is greater than zero, closing the changeover cascade-creates one cullet batch in status `STAGED` with `source_type` `CHANGEOVER_SCRAP`, `source_run_id` set to the outgoing run, and `mass_kg` equal to the recovered mass. The plant's `cullet_received_kg_ytd` increases by that mass and `cullet_on_hand_kg` is recomputed. This cascade batch carries no version guard and is written whatever the plant's `melt_posture` reads — the `FROZEN` and `RESTRICTED` gates described below govern only the explicit staging endpoint, so trim recovered on a restricted or frozen plant still reaches the yard. The new batch appears in no response body and is found by filtering the cullet collection, per §2.3.

Cullet also arrives from outside the changeover flow. A `PlantOperator` stages a batch by naming the `plant_id` holding the pile, a `source_type` of `TRIM`, `CHANGEOVER_SCRAP`, `QUARANTINE_SCRAP` or `EXTERNAL`, a positive `mass_kg` held to two decimal places and immutable once written, an optional `source_run_id` identifying the run whose trim or scrap produced the glass (null for externally purchased cullet, and not required to name a run of the staging plant itself), and the mandatory `expected_plant_version` guard of §2.6. A successful stage creates the batch in `STAGED` with `charged_run_id` and `reject_reason` null, increases the plant's `cullet_received_kg_ytd` by `mass_kg`, recomputes `cullet_on_hand_kg`, and increments the plant's `version` by one. Staging is blocked entirely while the plant is `FROZEN` with `ERR_MELT_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and blocked while the plant is `RESTRICTED` with `ERR_MELT_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`.

Two exits leave the staging yard besides a furnace charge. Rejecting a batch — for contamination or wrong chemistry — requires the batch to be `STAGED` and a non-empty `reject_reason`; the batch moves `STAGED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, the reason is stored, and the plant's `cullet_on_hand_kg` decreases by `mass_kg` while `cullet_rejected_kg_ytd` increases by the same mass, so the glass leaves the on-hand pool without ever having been charged. Reversing a batch backs it out of a furnace that is still running: the batch must be `CHARGED` and the run named by its `charged_run_id` must still be in `MELTING`, since the reversal window shuts the moment curing starts — a later attempt is refused with `ERR_CULLET_REVERSAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. An optional `reversal_note` may accompany the request; when supplied it must be a non-empty string, and it is retained internally for audit rather than surfaced as a field on the batch row. The batch returns `CHARGED → STAGED` with `charged_run_id` cleared; the plant's `cullet_on_hand_kg` increases by `mass_kg`, its `cullet_charged_kg_ytd` decreases by the same mass and its `cullet_fraction_ytd` is recomputed; and on the run, `cullet_charged_kg` decreases by `mass_kg`, `virgin_batch_kg` increases by the same mass, `cullet_fraction` is recomputed, and `total_charge_kg` is left untouched — the glass-mass invariant holds exactly. A reversal does **not** clear a `RESTRICTED` posture on its own; lowering the ratio merely makes the environmental recompute review of §3.1 succeed.

### 3.6. Thermal Testing and the Certification Verdict on a SKU

A SKU enters the certification queue when a `QualityEngineer` submits it: the SKU must be `DRAFT` and its `BinderFormulation` must be `CERTIFIED`, and on success `certification_status` moves `DRAFT → PENDING_CERTIFICATION`. A suspended SKU does not re-enter the queue this way; it returns through the officer's `REINSTATE` decision described later in this subsection.

A thermal test is requested by a `QualityEngineer` against a specific run's output. The payload names the `production_run_id` whose product was sampled, the `product_line_id` under test, the `certification_program_id` whose scheme is applied, and the inclusive `window_start` and inclusive `window_end` of the laboratory occupancy window, with `window_end` strictly later than `window_start`. Both window instants — here and on the retest below — are stored and echoed back as the exact strings the caller supplied, trimmed of surrounding whitespace but never re-rendered into a canonical form, while every window comparison runs on the UTC instants those strings denote, so a window written with a non-UTC offset is normalised for comparison only. The `product_line_id` must equal the run's own `product_line_id` — a test cannot be filed against a run that made a different SKU — and the run must have reached `CURING`, `PACKAGED` or `CLOSED`, since there is no product to sample before the oven.

Two values are **pinned** onto the test row at that instant and never move afterwards: `conformance_basis` takes the program's `conformance_basis`, and `declared_r_value_at_test` takes the SKU's `declared_r_value`. Editing the program or the SKU later cannot rewrite the terms a test in flight is judged under. The test lands in status `REQUESTED` with `attempt_number` 1, `specimen_r_values` empty, `specimen_count` 0, and `mean_r_value`, `min_r_value`, `conformance_verdict` and `recorded_by_user_id` all null. The same request advances the run's quality dimension `UNSAMPLED → SAMPLED`. That advance fires only from `UNSAMPLED`: a further test requested against a run already `SAMPLED`, `CONFORMING`, `NONCONFORMING` or `RETEST_PENDING` is accepted but leaves the quality dimension — and with it the run's `updated_at` and `version` — exactly where it stood.

Starting lab work advances the test `REQUESTED → IN_LAB` and is the API's one **aggregate** occupancy check. Count the thermal tests whose status is `IN_LAB`, whose `certification_program_id` matches this test's, and whose **closed** window from `window_start` to `window_end` intersects this test's closed window; that count must be strictly less than the program's `max_concurrent_lab_tests`. Adjacency counts as an intersection, so a test whose window ends exactly where another begins occupies the lab alongside it. No single test is illegal on its own — only the collective breaches the cap — and a breach is refused with `ERR_LAB_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Recording a result submits `specimen_r_values`, one measured R-value per specimen. The test must be `IN_LAB`; the array must hold at least the program's `min_specimen_count` values and every value in it must be positive, or the request is refused with `ERR_SPECIMEN_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`. The verdict then follows from a conformance floor and the pinned basis. The floor is the pinned declared value less the program's tolerance: subtract `r_value_tolerance` from `declared_r_value_at_test` and quantize to two decimal places. `specimen_count` is the array length; `mean_r_value` is the sum of the specimens divided by `specimen_count`, quantized to two decimal places; `min_r_value` is the smallest specimen, quantized to two decimal places. The basis then chooses which of those two figures faces the floor. On a `PER_SPECIMEN` basis the verdict is `CONFORMING` when `min_r_value` is greater than or equal to the floor and `NONCONFORMING` otherwise — every single specimen must clear the floor. On a `LOT_MEAN` basis the verdict is `CONFORMING` when `mean_r_value` is greater than or equal to the floor and `NONCONFORMING` otherwise — a weak specimen may be carried by strong ones. Identical specimen data can therefore produce opposite verdicts on two programs, and the divergence is driven only by the pinned basis. A figure landing exactly on the floor conforms.

The recorded values are stored on the row and `recorded_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. Which status the test lands in depends on the verdict and on how many attempts the program allows. A `CONFORMING` verdict moves the test `IN_LAB → COMPLETED`. A `NONCONFORMING` verdict while `attempt_number` is still **strictly below** the program's `retest_limit` also moves the test to `COMPLETED`, leaving a retest available. A `NONCONFORMING` verdict when `attempt_number` has **reached** `retest_limit` instead moves the test to the terminal `EXHAUSTED`. Only the `EXHAUSTED` leg touches the SKU: it increments `nonconformance_count` by one, and once that counter reaches or exceeds a suspension threshold of 2 the SKU transitions to `SUSPENDED` from `CERTIFIED`, `DERATED` or `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, with the run-hold and cycle-cancellation consequences described below. Two SKUs with identical specimen data on identical programs can therefore end in different places purely because one had exhausted its attempts.

On the run, the quality dimension advances `SAMPLED → CONFORMING` or `SAMPLED → NONCONFORMING` (and `RETEST_PENDING → CONFORMING` or `RETEST_PENDING → NONCONFORMING` on a retest attempt) to match the verdict. A `NONCONFORMING` verdict fires the quality cascade in full depth: every finished-goods lot of that run whose status is `AVAILABLE` or `ALLOCATED` transitions to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` `RUN_NONCONFORMING` and `available_sqft` forced to 0.00; every allocation line on those lots whose status is `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` transitions to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `LOT_QUARANTINED`; every affected order's `allocated_sqft`, `fill_rate_pct` and `status` are recomputed and re-derived; and every affected cycle's `allocated_sqft` and `unallocated_sqft` are recomputed. Because that cascade is not self-reversing, a subsequent `CONFORMING` retest result does **not** auto-release the quarantined lots — each one must be released explicitly, as §3.7 describes. When a single result both fires this quality cascade and exhausts the SKU's attempts, the quality cascade runs **first** and the SKU suspension second, so a line that sits on a lot this cascade quarantines and inside a cycle the suspension then cancels records `revoke_reason` `LOT_QUARANTINED` rather than `CYCLE_CANCELLED`.

Requesting a retest reuses the same test row rather than creating a new one, closing the cycle `IN_LAB → COMPLETED → IN_LAB`. The test must be `COMPLETED` with a `conformance_verdict` of `NONCONFORMING` and an `attempt_number` strictly below the program's `retest_limit`; a conforming result or an already-maximal `attempt_number` leaves no retest due and the request is refused with `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED`. The payload supplies a fresh `window_start` and `window_end`, and the same aggregate lab-capacity check is re-run against that new window. On success `attempt_number` increments by one, the status returns to `IN_LAB`, the new window replaces the old one, and the previous result is erased on the row — `specimen_r_values` back to empty, `specimen_count` to 0, `mean_r_value`, `min_r_value`, `conformance_verdict` and `recorded_by_user_id` all back to null — while the run's quality dimension moves `NONCONFORMING → RETEST_PENDING`.

The `CertificationOfficer`'s verdict on the SKU is a single decision endpoint carrying a `decision` and an optional `decision_note` — the note is retained internally for audit and is not surfaced as a field on the SKU row — and its six branches diverge sharply in what they write. `CERTIFY` requires the SKU to be `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` and at least one thermal test on the SKU with status `COMPLETED` and `conformance_verdict` `CONFORMING`; it moves `PENDING_CERTIFICATION → CERTIFIED`, sets `effective_declared_r_value` to the SKU's `declared_r_value` unchanged, and stamps `certified_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`.

`DERATE` publishes a lower R-value instead of rejecting the line, and it is the officer's compromise branch. It requires the SKU to be `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, the program's `derate_allowed` to be true, and the SKU's most recently created thermal test — taken in `created_at` ascending order and using the **last** row — to have status `COMPLETED` or `EXHAUSTED` with a non-null `mean_r_value`. The derated figure is snapped down onto a half-point grid: the basis value is that test's `min_r_value` when the `conformance_basis` pinned on that test row is `PER_SPECIMEN` and its `mean_r_value` when the pinned basis is `LOT_MEAN` — the pinned basis governs the arithmetic while `derate_allowed` is read from the SKU's own certification program, so the two need not come from the same program row if the test was filed under a different one; divide the basis value by a derate step of 0.5 and take the floor to a whole number of steps; multiply the step count back by 0.5 and quantize to two decimal places. A basis value already sitting exactly on a step is not pushed down. The resulting `effective_declared_r_value` must be strictly greater than 0 **and** strictly less than `declared_r_value`, and any failure of the `derate_allowed` flag, the test-availability requirement or that strict band is refused with `ERR_DERATE_NOT_PERMITTED`. On success the SKU moves `PENDING_CERTIFICATION → DERATED`, stores the lowered figure and stamps `certified_by_user_id`. Unlike `CERTIFY` this branch rewrites the publishable R-value downward, and unlike `REJECT` it leaves `nonconformance_count` untouched.

`REJECT` is the revert leg: from `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` the SKU returns to `DRAFT`, `nonconformance_count` increments by one, and `effective_declared_r_value` is cleared to null. `SUSPEND` applies from `CERTIFIED` or from `DERATED`, and both origins carry identical consequences: `certification_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, every production run of the SKU whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED` transitions `SCHEDULED → HELD` with `hold_reason` `PRODUCT_LINE_SUSPENDED`, and every allocation cycle of the SKU whose status is `OPEN` or `PROPOSED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with its proposed lines revoked. The same suspension is reachable without an officer at all, as cascade hop 3 of a binder suspension or withdrawal; a `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` SKU is suspendable only along that system path, and that path holds scheduled runs without needing any cycles to cancel. A suspension leaves both `effective_declared_r_value` and `certified_by_user_id` exactly as they stood, which is why the officer who certified or derated a SKU remains barred by §2.10 from reinstating it. `REINSTATE` applies from `SUSPENDED` and requires the SKU's binder formulation to be `CERTIFIED` again — a formulation that is not is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rung 8, since the `ERR_BINDER_NOT_CERTIFIED` gate of §3.2 covers only run scheduling and certification submission; it returns the SKU to `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` and clears **both** `effective_declared_r_value` and `certified_by_user_id` to null, so the SKU must be re-adjudicated from scratch and the cycle `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED → PENDING_CERTIFICATION → CERTIFIED` closes. `WITHDRAW` applies from `SUSPENDED` and moves the SKU to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`.

### 3.7. Finished-Goods Lots: the Allocation Ledger, Quarantine, Repack and Scrap

A finished-goods lot is never created by a caller. Each one is cascade-created by a packaging step, carrying the `plant_id` that produced it, the `product_line_id` it holds, the `production_run_id` that packaged it, a `parent_lot_id` of null, a `form` copied from the SKU's `form` at creation, its whole-bag count in `bags`, and a `produced_sqft` equal to `bags` multiplied by the SKU's `bag_sqft`. `produced_sqft` is **never mutated after creation**; every subsequent movement is expressed in the ledger fields instead.

The ledger is recomputed from scratch after every write that could touch it — allocation commit, manual allocation, line revocation, cycle cancellation, order cancellation, and the lot quarantine, release and repack operations. `allocated_sqft` is the sum of `allocated_sqft` over the allocation lines whose `inventory_lot_id` is this lot and whose status is exactly `ALLOCATED`, quantized to two decimal places. Lines in `PROPOSED` are reservations only and are **excluded** from the sum, as are `REVOKED` lines — so a proposed-but-uncommitted cycle leaves inventory fully available. `available_sqft` is `produced_sqft` less `allocated_sqft` quantized to two decimal places for a live lot, but is forced to exactly 0.00 whenever the lot's status is `QUARANTINED`, `REPACKED`, `CONSUMED` or `SCRAPPED`.

For a live lot the status itself is **derived** from the arithmetic rather than set by hand: a lot in `AVAILABLE` or `ALLOCATED` becomes `ALLOCATED` when `available_sqft` reaches exactly 0.00 and `AVAILABLE` whenever it is greater than 0.00. Both directions are automatic — booking the last square foot flips a lot to `ALLOCATED`, and revoking any line flips it back to `AVAILABLE` — and the compensating direction must restore the ledger exactly. After every one of those writes the per-SKU conservation invariant of §2.11 holds: the sum of `available_sqft` over live lots plus the sum of `allocated_sqft` over the `ALLOCATED` lines on those lots equals the sum of `produced_sqft` over live lots. A quarantined lot leaves the live set entirely — its own available coverage goes to zero and its lines are revoked, so it contributes nothing to either side of the identity.

A `QualityEngineer` disposes of a lot through a single quarantine operation whose `disposition` chooses between two very different outcomes. A `disposition` of `HOLD` moves an `AVAILABLE` or an `ALLOCATED` lot to `QUARANTINED`, records `quarantine_reason` `MANUAL_HOLD`, forces `available_sqft` to 0.00, and revokes every allocation line on the lot whose status is `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` with `revoke_reason` `LOT_QUARANTINED`, recomputing the affected orders and cycles. Both origins unwind coverage that way, not just the `ALLOCATED` one: an `AVAILABLE` lot can still carry merely `PROPOSED` reservations, and the hold revokes those too. Every field the hold writes lands as a single mutation, so the lot's `version` advances by exactly one. The same `QUARANTINED` state is reachable without any request as cascade hop 2 of a nonconforming run, and the only difference is the reason recorded: `MANUAL_HOLD` for the explicit operation, `RUN_NONCONFORMING` for the cascade. An optional `quarantine_note` may accompany either disposition; it is retained internally for audit and is not surfaced as a field on the lot row.

A `disposition` of `SCRAP` applies only to a lot already in `QUARANTINED` and moves it to the terminal `SCRAPPED` with `available_sqft` 0.00. Scrapping recovers glass rather than discarding it: exactly one cullet batch is created in status `STAGED` with `source_type` `QUARANTINE_SCRAP`, `source_run_id` set to the lot's `production_run_id`, and a recovered mass computed from the lot. The gross mass has two sources and the scale wins whenever it is present: if the payload carries a `weighed_scrap_lb` reading, the gross mass is that reading multiplied by a pound-to-kilogram factor of 0.45359237, quantized to two decimal places; otherwise it is taken from the bale bill of materials as the lot's `bags` multiplied by the SKU's `bale_mass_kg`, quantized to two decimal places. The two paths disagree by design, and the scale reading is authoritative. `weighed_scrap_lb` must be zero or greater — a negative reading is a plain shape violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and a reading of exactly 0 still counts as present, driving the recovered mass to 0.00 rather than falling back to the bale bill of materials. Facing paper and foil are not glass and never reach the furnace, so an `UNFACED` SKU contributes its whole gross mass while any other `facing_type` contributes the gross mass multiplied by one minus a facing mass fraction of 0.08, quantized to two decimal places. The batch's `mass_kg` is finally that glass mass multiplied by a recovery yield of 0.85, quantized to two decimal places. The plant's `cullet_received_kg_ytd` increases by that mass and `cullet_on_hand_kg` is recomputed.

Releasing a quarantined lot returns it `QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE`, clears `quarantine_reason` to null and recomputes `available_sqft` from the ledger. The gate is a cross-entity one: the originating run's `qc_status` must read exactly `CONFORMING`, or the release is refused with `ERR_RUN_NOT_CONFORMING`. A run still `RETEST_PENDING`, still `NONCONFORMING` or never sampled cannot have its lots released, which is precisely why a conforming retest result frees the operator to release lots one at a time rather than releasing them itself. Note that release restores availability from the ledger, so a lot that was fully booked before the hold — and whose lines were revoked by the hold — comes back with its full coverage available.

Repacking splits one lot into several and is a `PlantOperator` capability — and, uniquely among that role's writes, one carrying no plant-ownership comparison, so an operator may repack a lot belonging to any plant (§2.1). The lot must be `AVAILABLE` with `allocated_sqft` exactly 0.00 and no allocation line on it in `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LOT_HAS_ALLOCATIONS` — the reservation-versus-booking distinction matters here, because a merely proposed line still blocks a repack even though it contributes nothing to `allocated_sqft`. The payload carries `child_bags`, an array of at least two elements, every element a positive whole number, and the elements summing to **exactly** the parent's `bags` so that the children's coverage reproduces the parent's `produced_sqft` to the square foot; a violation of any of those three conditions — including an array that is empty or holds a single element — is refused with `ERR_BAG_MULTIPLE_MISMATCH`. `child_bags` must be present and must be an array, and every element must arrive as a JSON number rather than as a decimal string, both being plain shape requirements refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the lot's own state is examined; the whole-number and summation conditions, by contrast, are evaluated after the state check and after the live-allocation check, so a lot that both carries a live line and a malformed `child_bags` reports `ERR_LOT_HAS_ALLOCATIONS`. On success the parent moves `AVAILABLE → REPACKED`, a terminal state, with `available_sqft` forced to 0.00, and one child lot is created per element **in the order the elements appear** with `parent_lot_id` pointing at the parent, status `AVAILABLE`, `bags` equal to that element, `produced_sqft` equal to the element multiplied by the SKU's `bag_sqft`, `available_sqft` equal to that same figure, `allocated_sqft` 0.00, `quarantine_reason` null, and `plant_id`, `product_line_id`, `production_run_id` and `form` all inherited from the parent's SKU and run. The response body is the parent lot. The children appear in no response body and are found only by filtering the lot collection on the parent, as described in §2.3.

The last exit is `CONSUMED`. When an order ships, every lot backing it moves `ALLOCATED → CONSUMED` with `available_sqft` forced to 0.00, but only for a lot on which every `ALLOCATED` allocation line belongs to the shipping order — a lot shared between two orders is not consumed by the first of them to ship.

### 3.8. Allocating Scarce Coverage: Cycles, the Shortage Waterfall, Expedites and Shipment

Demand arrives as channel orders. A `ChannelBuyer` or a `ChannelManager` places one by naming the `product_line_id` requested, a `channel_type` of `DISTRIBUTOR` or `RETAIL`, a `priority_tier` of `CONTRACT`, `PROGRAM` or `SPOT`, a positive `requested_sqft`, and a `required_by` date that is the latest acceptable ship date — stored and echoed back as the exact string the caller supplied, trimmed of surrounding whitespace but never re-rendered, while every comparison and ordering against it runs on the UTC instant it denotes. The SKU's `certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED` or `DERATED` — an uncertified, suspended or withdrawn SKU cannot be sold, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_CERTIFIED`; a derated SKU is fully sellable, merely at a lower published R-value. The order lands in status `SUBMITTED` with `allocated_sqft` 0.00, `fill_rate_pct` 0.00 and `placed_by_user_id` stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`.

A `ChannelManager` opens an allocation cycle over one plant, one SKU and one demand period, naming the `plant_id` whose lots supply it, the `product_line_id` it allocates, the inclusive `period_start`, the equally inclusive `period_end` (which must be strictly later than `period_start`), and the `allocation_basis` of `CONTRACT_FIRST` or `PRO_RATA`. Both period instants are stored and echoed back as the exact strings the caller supplied, trimmed of surrounding whitespace but never re-rendered into a canonical form, while every use of them — the demand-eligibility comparison against `required_by`, the `required_by` ordering of the demand sort, and the calendar month read off `period_start` for the peak-season flag — runs on the UTC instants those strings denote, so an instant written with a non-UTC offset is normalised to UTC before its month is read. The cycle lands in status `OPEN` with `peak_season`, `total_available_sqft`, `seasonal_reserve_sqft`, `allocatable_sqft`, `total_requested_sqft`, `shortage_ratio` and `unallocated_sqft` all null and `allocated_sqft` 0.00 until a proposal is run.

Proposing runs the waterfall and moves the cycle `OPEN → PROPOSED`. The SKU must be `CERTIFIED` or `DERATED`, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Supply is the set of lots whose `plant_id` and `product_line_id` match the cycle's and whose status is exactly `AVAILABLE`, taken in `created_at` ascending order — a quarantined or already fully-booked lot contributes nothing. Demand is the set of orders whose `product_line_id` matches the cycle's, whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `PARTIAL` or `BACKORDERED`, whose `required_by` falls at or before the cycle's `period_end`, and whose remaining need — `requested_sqft` less `allocated_sqft` — is strictly greater than zero. A partially filled order is therefore re-eligible in a later cycle for its unmet balance only.

The seasonal reserve comes first, because peak construction season must not be stripped bare by an early cycle. `total_available_sqft` is the sum of `available_sqft` over supply, quantized to two decimal places. `peak_season` is true when the calendar month of `period_start` is one of March, April, May, June, July, August or September, and false otherwise. `seasonal_reserve_sqft` is `total_available_sqft` multiplied by 0.15 in peak season and by 0.05 off peak, quantized to two decimal places, and `allocatable_sqft` is `total_available_sqft` less that reserve, quantized to two decimal places. Two feasibility gates then fire: `allocatable_sqft` must be strictly greater than 0.00 or the proposal is refused with `ERR_NO_ALLOCATABLE_SUPPLY`, and the demand set must be non-empty or the proposal is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ORDERS` — an empty demand set is rejected rather than producing an empty proposal.

`total_requested_sqft` is the sum of remaining need over the demand set, quantized to two decimal places. `shortage_ratio` measures how much of the ask can be met: it is `allocatable_sqft` divided by `total_requested_sqft`, quantized to four decimal places, and then **capped at 1.0000** so that an abundant cycle reports exactly 1.0000 rather than a number above one. Demand is then sorted deterministically by three keys in order: tier rank, with `CONTRACT` ranking 1, `PROGRAM` ranking 2 and `SPOT` ranking 3; then `required_by` ascending, so the most urgent need comes first within a tier; then `created_at` ascending. The row `id` is **never** consulted, so two orders identical on all three keys keep their creation order and the outcome is reproducible.

The basis then selects one of two profoundly different distributions of the same scarce pool. Under `CONTRACT_FIRST`, the pool starts at `allocatable_sqft` and each order in sorted position takes as much as it can: the wanted figure is the lesser of the order's remaining need and the pool that is left, the bag count is the floor of that figure divided by the SKU's `bag_sqft`, and the grant is that whole bag count multiplied by `bag_sqft`, quantized to two decimal places, with the pool reduced by the grant. Strict priority holds — an earlier tier is filled to its whole-bag maximum before the next tier sees anything at all — so a `SPOT` order behind a large contract can end with a grant of exactly 0.00.

Under `PRO_RATA` the same pool is shared proportionally in two passes. Pass one gives each order its proportional whole-bag share: multiply the order's remaining need by `shortage_ratio`, divide by `bag_sqft`, take the floor to whole bags, and multiply back to get the grant. Because every share is floored, pass one systematically under-distributes, so the leftover pool is `allocatable_sqft` less the sum of the pass-one grants. Pass two hands that remainder out **one bag at a time** in the same deterministic sorted order, sweeping the list repeatedly: an order receives one more bag only if the pool still holds at least one bag's worth of coverage and if that extra bag would not push its grant above its remaining need, and the loop stops when a full sweep hands out nothing or the pool can no longer fund a whole bag. The end state is a pool holding strictly less than one bag's coverage.

The two variants diverge sharply on identical inputs: `CONTRACT_FIRST` can leave a `SPOT` order at 0.00 and `BACKORDERED` while `PRO_RATA` funds it into `PARTIAL`, and the contract order's own grant differs between them. Only `allocation_basis` drives that difference.

After the variant runs, the cycle's `allocated_sqft` is the sum of all grants, quantized to two decimal places, and `unallocated_sqft` is `allocatable_sqft` less that sum, quantized to two decimal places. Lines are then cut against lots in `created_at` ascending order, draining each lot before moving to the next: for each granted order in sorted position, take the lesser of its outstanding grant and the current lot's `available_sqft`, emit one allocation line with status `PROPOSED`, `allocated_sqft` equal to that take and `bags` equal to the take divided by `bag_sqft` — always a whole number here — and continue to the next lot until the grant is exhausted. One order may therefore span several lots, and one lot may back several orders. The cycle's `version` increments by one, and the proposed lines appear in no response body: they are found only by filtering the allocation-line collection on the cycle, as described in §2.3.

Re-proposing is a self-loop on `PROPOSED` and it never mutates a line in place. Every existing line of the cycle in status `PROPOSED` first transitions to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `CYCLE_CANCELLED`, then the waterfall re-runs from scratch against current supply and demand and fresh `PROPOSED` lines are created. The same certification, allocatable-supply and eligible-demand preconditions apply on the self-loop as on the first proposal.

Committing moves the cycle `PROPOSED → COMMITTED`. The plant must not be `FROZEN`, and no lot referenced by a `PROPOSED` line of the cycle may be `QUARANTINED`, or the commit is refused with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. Every `PROPOSED` line of the cycle transitions to `ALLOCATED`; each referenced lot's `allocated_sqft`, `available_sqft` and status are recomputed by the ledger of §3.7 — which is the moment reservations become bookings and a fully drawn lot flips to `ALLOCATED`; each served order's `allocated_sqft` becomes its prior allocation plus its grant, quantized to two decimal places, its `fill_rate_pct` becomes `allocated_sqft` divided by `requested_sqft` multiplied by 100, quantized to two decimal places, and its status is **derived** from the comparison: `ALLOCATED` when `allocated_sqft` equals `requested_sqft` exactly, `PARTIAL` when it is greater than zero but short of the request, and `BACKORDERED` when it is exactly 0.00 — an order that was eligible but could not be funded a whole bag. Reaching that last case requires the commit to re-derive orders the proposal never cut a line for, so the set it recomputes is the demand set the **most recent** proposal recorded on the cycle, taken together with every order carrying a line of the cycle. An order already `CANCELLED` or `SHIPPED` when the commit runs is skipped rather than re-derived. The cycle's `version` increments by one.

Cancelling a cycle applies from `OPEN` or from `PROPOSED`, takes an optional `cancel_reason` — retained internally for audit and not surfaced as a field on the cycle row — and lands on the terminal `CANCELLED`. From `OPEN` there is nothing to unwind. From `PROPOSED` every line of the cycle in `PROPOSED` **or** `ALLOCATED` transitions to `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `CYCLE_CANCELLED` and the lot and order ledgers are recomputed. The cycle's `version` increments by one. The same cancellation is reachable without a request, as the cascade from a SKU suspension described in §3.6.

A manual expedite line bypasses the waterfall entirely and lands directly in `ALLOCATED`. The `ChannelManager` names the `allocation_cycle_id` hosting the line, the `channel_order_id` being served, the `inventory_lot_id` the coverage is drawn from, the `allocated_sqft` to draw, and the `expected_allocation_cycle_version` guard of §2.6. The cycle must be `OPEN` or `PROPOSED`; the order must be `SUBMITTED`, `PARTIAL` or `BACKORDERED` and its `product_line_id` must equal the cycle's; the lot must be `AVAILABLE` or `ALLOCATED` with its `product_line_id` and `plant_id` both matching the cycle's, and it must not be `QUARANTINED` or the request is refused with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED` — the quarantine leg is evaluated ahead of the residual liveness leg, so a `QUARANTINED` lot reports `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED` while a `REPACKED`, `CONSUMED` or `SCRAPPED` lot reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; `allocated_sqft` must be an exact positive multiple of the SKU's `bag_sqft` or the request is refused with `ERR_BAG_MULTIPLE_MISMATCH`; and finally `allocated_sqft` must not exceed the lot's `available_sqft` and must not push the order's `allocated_sqft` above its `requested_sqft`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LOT_AVAILABLE`. On success `bags` is `allocated_sqft` divided by `bag_sqft`, the lot ledger, the order roll-up and the cycle's `allocated_sqft` and `unallocated_sqft` are all recomputed, and the cycle's `version` increments by one. A cycle's `allocated_sqft` is always recomputed as the sum of `allocated_sqft` over its own lines whose status is `PROPOSED` **or** `ALLOCATED` — unlike the lot ledger of §3.7, a cycle counts reservations alongside bookings — and `unallocated_sqft` is `allocatable_sqft` less that sum. A cycle that has never been proposed therefore keeps `unallocated_sqft` null even once a manual line has moved its `allocated_sqft`, because its `allocatable_sqft` is still null and there is nothing to subtract from. Because a manual line is booked immediately rather than reserved, it can flip an order to `ALLOCATED` and a lot to `ALLOCATED` without any commit.

Revoking a line is the compensating leg and applies from `PROPOSED` or from `ALLOCATED`, landing on the terminal `REVOKED` with `revoke_reason` `MANUAL_REVOKE` and an optional `revoke_note` — the note being retained internally for audit and not surfaced as a field on the line row. The lot's ledger is recomputed and a fully drawn-down lot returns `ALLOCATED → AVAILABLE`; the order's `allocated_sqft`, `fill_rate_pct` and status are recomputed, moving `ALLOCATED → PARTIAL` when some coverage survives and `ALLOCATED → BACKORDERED` when the revocation removed the order's entire allocation in one step — a fully filled order can drop straight to `BACKORDERED` without passing through `PARTIAL` when its only line is revoked — while a `PARTIAL` order that loses its last allocated square foot moves `PARTIAL → BACKORDERED`; and the cycle's `allocated_sqft` and `unallocated_sqft` are recomputed. The `revoke_reason` field records which force revoked the line: `MANUAL_REVOKE` for this operation, `CYCLE_CANCELLED` for a cycle cancellation or re-proposal, `LOT_QUARANTINED` for a lot hold or a nonconforming-run cascade, and `ORDER_CANCELLED` for a buyer's cancellation. After every one of these writes the coverage conservation invariant of §2.11 must hold exactly. A ledger recomputation is only a mutation when it actually changes a figure: a recomputation that finds every derived value already correct writes nothing, leaving that row's `updated_at` and `version` untouched. Revoking a merely `PROPOSED` line is the clearest case — a reservation never entered either the lot ledger or the order roll-up, so the line and the cycle move while the lot and the order do not.

A `ChannelBuyer` cancels an order from `SUBMITTED`, `PARTIAL` or `BACKORDERED` with an optional `cancel_reason`, landing on the terminal `CANCELLED`. Only the buyer who placed it may do so — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `placed_by_user_id`, and any other buyer is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`. Cancelling from `PARTIAL` revokes every line of the order in `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` with `revoke_reason` `ORDER_CANCELLED` and recomputes the lot ledgers and the cycle totals; cancelling from `SUBMITTED` or `BACKORDERED` has no coverage to release. The `cancel_reason` is retained internally for audit and is not surfaced as a field on the order row, and the cancelled order keeps the `allocated_sqft` and `fill_rate_pct` it held at the moment of cancellation rather than having them recomputed to 0.00 — the released coverage reappears on the lots and the cycles, never on the dead order. A `CANCELLED` order is likewise inert to every later recomputation: no cascade reached through one of its revoked lines can move its figures or its status again.

Shipping is a `ChannelManager` action available from `ALLOCATED` **or** from `PARTIAL`, both landing on the terminal `SHIPPED` with identical consequences — a partial order ships what it holds and the unfilled balance is simply dropped. The order's `allocated_sqft` must be strictly greater than 0.00 and no lot referenced by an `ALLOCATED` line of the order may be `QUARANTINED`, or the shipment is refused with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. An optional `shipped_at` may be supplied; it is retained internally for audit and is not surfaced as a field on the order row. Every lot whose entire allocated coverage belongs to this order transitions `ALLOCATED → CONSUMED`, as described in §3.7 — and only a lot whose status is exactly `ALLOCATED` is consumed, so a lot that is still `AVAILABLE` because part of its coverage was never drawn survives the shipment untouched. Shipment consumes lots rather than unwinding bookings: the order's `ALLOCATED` lines stay `ALLOCATED`, and its own `allocated_sqft` and `fill_rate_pct` are left exactly as they stood. Like a cancelled order, a `SHIPPED` order is inert to every later recomputation.

### 3.9. Air-Permit Emissions Reporting and the Plant Freeze Cascade

Filing a permit-period emissions report is an `EnvironmentalCompliance` action and it is the only operation that moves the plant's `emissions_status` dimension. The payload carries `period_start` and `period_end`, with `period_end` strictly later than `period_start` — an inverted interval is a plain interval check reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` by the request-shape rung of the ladder. The period must also already be over: `period_end` must fall at or before the current logical clock reading, and a report filed against a period that has not yet closed is refused with `ERR_EMISSIONS_PERIOD_INVALID`. That rule owns only the future-period case, since the ordering leg has already been reported at a lower priority.

The roll-up draws on the runs whose masses were booked at cure start. The input set is the production runs of this plant whose `scheduled_start` falls inside the **closed** interval from `period_start` to `period_end` and whose `run_status` is `CURING`, `PACKAGED` or `CLOSED` — a run that never reached the oven has no emissions to report, and a `SCHEDULED`, `MELTING`, `HELD` or `ABORTED` run is excluded regardless of its dates. `reported_formaldehyde_kg` is the sum of `formaldehyde_kg` over that set, quantized to four decimal places, and `reported_voc_kg` is the sum of `voc_kg` over the same set, quantized to four decimal places. `reported_period_start` and `reported_period_end` record the period the figures describe; because each report **overwrites** all four fields, the plant row carries only the most recent period snapshot rather than a history.

The exceedance test compares each rolled-up mass against its allowance using a **strict** greater-than in both legs: the period exceeded when `reported_formaldehyde_kg` is greater than the plant's `formaldehyde_allowance_kg` **or** `reported_voc_kg` is greater than its `voc_allowance_kg`. A figure landing exactly on its allowance does **not** exceed it. When neither leg trips, `emissions_status` becomes `REPORTED`, reachable from `COMPLIANT`, as a self-loop from `REPORTED` for each later period, and — importantly — from `EXCEEDANCE` for a period back within allowance. When either leg trips, `emissions_status` becomes `EXCEEDANCE` from `COMPLIANT`, from `REPORTED`, or as a self-loop from `EXCEEDANCE` when a further period also comes back over allowance, and the two legs' shared side effects are identical. Filing a report is gated by no state precondition of its own: any `emissions_status` accepts a new report, and the freeze cascade below simply re-runs and finds most of its work already done. Either way the plant's `version` increments by one.

An exceedance drives the freeze cascade across four hops in full depth. The plant's `melt_posture` moves `OPEN → FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED → FROZEN` with `posture_reason` `EMISSIONS_EXCEEDANCE`. Every `CERTIFIED` binder formulation whose `formaldehyde_free` flag is false and which is used by a SKU of this plant transitions to `SUSPENDED` — formaldehyde-free chemistries are deliberately spared, since they cannot be the source of a formaldehyde exceedance. Every SKU built on those suspended formulations transitions to `SUSPENDED`. Every production run of the plant whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED` transitions `SCHEDULED → HELD` with `hold_reason` `PLANT_FROZEN`. The plant-wide hold is applied **before** the binder and SKU suspensions, and a hold is never re-stamped once a run has left `SCHEDULED`, so a run of this plant records `PLANT_FROZEN` even when its own SKU is suspended by the same cascade; only a `SCHEDULED` run at some other plant, reached because it sits on a SKU built on one of the suspended formulations, records `PRODUCT_LINE_SUSPENDED`. And every allocation cycle on those SKUs whose status is `OPEN` or `PROPOSED` is `CANCELLED`, with its proposed and allocated lines revoked and the lot and order ledgers recomputed.

Recovery is deliberately asymmetric, and the asymmetry proves the two plant dimensions are genuinely independent. A later report for a period back within allowance returns `emissions_status` from `EXCEEDANCE` to `REPORTED`, but it does **not** clear `melt_posture`: a `FROZEN` posture survives until an explicit `OVERRIDE_TO_OPEN` posture review, as described in §3.1. Clearing the posture in turn does not release the held runs — each one requires its own release, per §3.4 — and a suspended binder or SKU must be re-certified through its own lifecycle rather than being restored by the passage of a compliant period. Nothing the cascade suspended, held or cancelled comes back automatically.
