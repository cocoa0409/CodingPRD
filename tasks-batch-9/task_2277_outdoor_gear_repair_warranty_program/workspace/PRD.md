# Outdoor Gear Repair & Warranty Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Our brand has promised, for decades, that a jacket, a tent or a pack sold under our name will be repaired rather than replaced whenever repair is the honest answer. That promise now runs through five regional service centres, a bench of certified technicians, a stockroom that must keep serving models we stopped building years ago, and a finance team that needs to know, at any moment, how much warranty exposure the programme is carrying. Today the work is coordinated in spreadsheets: a technician's defect-versus-wear finding lives in one workbook, the repair-versus-replace decision in another, the parts substitution log in a third, and the turnaround promise given to the customer nowhere at all. Nobody can answer "why did this claim become a replacement instead of a repair?" without reconstructing it by hand.

This document specifies the API that replaces that patchwork. The service is mounted under `/gear-service` and owns the entire arc of a service event: intake of a warranty claim with proof of purchase, the bench assessment that separates a manufacturing defect from ordinary wear, the arithmetic that decides whether repairing a unit is still cheaper than shipping a new one, the scheduling of skilled bench time across fabric, zipper, frame and insulation lanes, the pricing and customer approval of non-warranty paid work, the reservation of parts (including substituted parts for discontinued models), the quality-control gate, and finally either a repaired unit handed back to its owner or a condemned unit accepted as a trade-in and pushed into the refurbished resale channel.

Two cross-cutting obligations shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **liability discipline**: every warranty outcome the brand accepts reserves money against the owning service centre, and once a centre's open exposure crosses defined fractions of its ceiling the system progressively restricts who may authorise further warranty generosity. The second is **repairability feedback**: the programme exists partly to tell the product teams which models keep coming back, so claim, denial and irreparable counters accumulate on the catalogue model and raise a design-review signal without any human remembering to file a report. Both obligations are computed, never typed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Roles and Identity Resolution

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or blank, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is present but is not one of the five recognised role names — `Customer`, `ServiceAgent`, `RepairTechnician`, `PartsCoordinator`, `ProgramAdmin` — the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401).

Two of the five roles are backed by persisted entities, and for those the value of `X-User-ID` must resolve. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Customer row; ownership checks on any resource that references a Customer compare the resource's owner reference to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `RepairTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Technician row; the server reads that technician's bench affiliation, roster status and certification matrix from the resolved row and, for bench assessments, stamps `assessor_technician_id` from the header rather than from the request body. In either case, if `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing row of the backing entity, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (HTTP 401).

The remaining three roles carry opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record the fact that an agent acted (for example, an adjudication stamps `decision_by_role` with the role name) but never compares the identifier to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `PartsCoordinator` and when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`: both are opaque operator identifiers, not rows, and no ownership comparison is ever performed against them.

Role capability is per endpoint. Each endpoint in §4 lists the roles permitted to invoke it; a syntactically valid role that is not in that list yields `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Beyond the endpoint list, a `Customer` may only reach their own gear. Whenever a `Customer` addresses an existing resource, the server walks from that resource to the gear unit behind it — directly for a unit, through the claim's referenced unit for a claim, through the order's referenced unit for a repair order, and through the estimate's referenced order and then that order's unit for an estimate — and compares the unit's `owner_customer_id` to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The role model is one of segregated duties, and the segregation is enforced in two places. A `RepairTechnician` who authored any bench assessment on a claim may never sign the quality-control inspection of that claim's repair order. And a `ProgramAdmin` is the only role that may push a warranty replacement through while the owning centre's exposure posture is restricted; the same admin role is deliberately absent from the list of roles that may author bench assessments.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of rows to skip, defaults to 0, and must not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

### 2.4. The Logical Service Clock

Nothing in this system reads the machine's wall clock. Every derivation that needs "now" — a unit's age, whether coverage has lapsed, the turnaround date promised to a customer, the expiry of a quote, whether a seasonal window covers today — reads a single server-held **logical clock**. At startup that clock is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until the clock is explicitly moved, every timestamp derivation reads that default value and never falls back to real time.

A `ProgramAdmin` may advance or rewind the clock through an admin-only test-driving capability that accepts a `current_utc` instant and returns it. Every subsequent derivation — `age_months`, `warranty_state`, `promised_return_at`, `valid_until` and every surge-window comparison — uses the new instant.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Audit Fields and the Immutable Bench Record

Every resource in this system is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits, recording the logical-clock instant at which the row was inserted.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of that row. This includes mutations that only touch derived fields: when a liability reservation opens and a centre's `open_liability_cents` therefore changes, the centre row is mutated, its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if an operator had edited it.

The one exception is the bench assessment. A ClaimAssessment is an immutable record of what a technician found on a given day: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, there is no endpoint that updates it, and a later finding never edits an earlier one — it supersedes it by setting the earlier row's `is_superseded` flag.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is expressed in whole integer cents; no monetary field ever carries a fractional part. Wherever a monetary amount is produced by multiplying by a rate or a percentage, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. Rounding happens only at the points named in the pricing and valuation rules, and never anywhere else — in particular, per-line labour charges are rounded individually before they are summed, and the sum itself is never rounded again.

Two percentage fields are numbers rather than integers. A centre's `liability_utilization_pct` and a claim's `repair_ratio_pct` are each rounded half up to two decimal places. A claim's `decision_threshold_pct` is a whole integer percentage. The multiplier on a seasonal window, `multiplier`, is a number between 1.0 and 3.0 inclusive with at most two decimal places, and a centre's `current_surge_multiplier` and an order's `surge_multiplier_applied` are numbers of the same shape.

One rounding trap is worth stating twice: `liability_utilization_pct` exists purely so that a dashboard can show a percentage. It is never a comparator. Every posture comparison is performed on integer cents by cross-multiplication, so no floating-point representation can move a boundary.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Claims and Orders

Two write operations in this system legitimately destroy information that a previous writer may have created, and both are therefore guarded by an explicit version token. Neither guard is optional, and no other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind.

The first guard protects the bench assessment record. A request that records a bench assessment against a warranty claim must carry `expected_claim_version`, holding the `version` of that claim as the client last observed it. If the value does not equal the claim's current `version` — because some other writer has advanced the claim since the client read it — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no assessment row is created. The reason this matters is that a second assessment supersedes every earlier assessment on the claim and rewrites `latest_determination` and `latest_repair_cost_cents`; a create issued from a stale view would silently overwrite a finding its author never saw.

The second guard protects the customer-facing quote. A request that issues a paid-repair estimate against a repair order must carry `expected_repair_order_version`, holding the `version` of that order as the client last observed it. A mismatch with the order's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REPAIR_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no estimate row is created — because issuing a new quote retires the order's live quote, and a stale-version create would retire a quote the writer never inspected.

Both operations, on success, increment the guarded parent's `version` themselves, so a client that wants to write twice must re-read in between.

### 2.8. Valuing a Gear Unit: Two Independent Bases

A single physical unit carries **two** replacement valuations at all times, and they answer different questions. Both are recomputed whenever the unit's computed fields are read or written, and both are consumed downstream by adjudication, by liability reservation and by trade-in pricing.

The shared inputs are the unit and its catalogue model, plus — only when the model is discontinued and names a successor — that successor model row. No claim or order state participates in either valuation. First, `age_months` is the number of whole months elapsed from the unit's `purchase_date` to the logical clock instant, floored, where a month is counted as elapsed once the same day-of-month is reached. Completed years are `age_months` divided by twelve and floored to a whole number.

`replacement_value_depreciated_cents` is the **depreciated-purchase** basis: what the owner actually paid, walked down over time. The depreciation fraction is ten percent for each completed year, capped so it can never exceed sixty percent — that is, the lesser of 0.60 and 0.10 multiplied by the completed years. A residual floor is computed as the model's own `msrp_cents` multiplied by twenty-five percent, rounded half up to whole cents. The depreciated value is then the greater of that residual floor and the unit's `purchase_price_cents` multiplied by one minus the depreciation fraction, rounded half up to whole cents. This basis ignores the successor catalogue entirely.

`replacement_value_successor_cents` is the **successor-MSRP** basis: what the brand would have to ship today. The base list price is the successor model's `msrp_cents` when the unit's model has both a non-null `discontinued_at` and a non-null `successor_model_id`; in every other case it is the unit's own model's `msrp_cents`. The successor valuation is that base list price multiplied by ninety percent, rounded half up to whole cents. This basis ignores what the customer paid and how old the unit is.

The split matters because the two bases feed different obligations. The depreciated basis caps a repair-type liability reservation and is the arithmetic basis of trade-in credit. The successor basis is the denominator of the repair-versus-replace ratio and is the full amount of a replacement-type liability reservation.

### 2.9. Warranty Liability Exposure and Centre Posture

Each service centre declares a `liability_cap_cents` ceiling at registration. Against that ceiling the system continuously rolls up the centre's open exposure: `open_liability_cents` is the sum of `reserved_amount_cents` over every liability entry whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose claim's `service_center_id` is this centre. Entries whose `status` is `RELEASED` are excluded from the sum but retain their `reserved_amount_cents` for audit. `liability_utilization_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `open_liability_cents` divided by `liability_cap_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and is display-only.

From the rolled-up cents the centre's `liability_posture` is re-derived, and it is one of `OPEN`, `CONSTRAINED` or `FROZEN`. If `open_liability_cents` is strictly greater than `liability_cap_cents`, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if `open_liability_cents` multiplied by one hundred is strictly greater than sixty multiplied by `liability_cap_cents`, the posture is `CONSTRAINED`. Otherwise it is `OPEN`. Two boundaries are pinned by that wording: open exposure exactly equal to the cap is `CONSTRAINED`, not `FROZEN`, and open exposure at exactly sixty percent of the cap is `OPEN`, not `CONSTRAINED`.

The posture is re-derived every time a liability entry is created or released and every time the queue-review checkpoint runs. Because releases can be large, the posture may skip a band in either direction: a single release that clears the whole band moves a centre from `FROZEN` straight to `OPEN`, and it may equally move `FROZEN` to `CONSTRAINED` or `CONSTRAINED` to `OPEN`. Tightening moves the other way, `OPEN` to `CONSTRAINED` and `CONSTRAINED` to `FROZEN`, on the same thresholds.

Posture is not decoration; it gates adjudication. Under `CONSTRAINED`, a warranty replacement outcome requires a `ProgramAdmin` actor and anyone else is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_REQUIRES_OVERRIDE`; warranty repair outcomes remain available to a `ServiceAgent`. Under `FROZEN`, both warranty outcomes — repair and replacement — are refused with `ERR_CENTER_LIABILITY_FROZEN`, while the two outcomes that cost the brand nothing, a paid-repair offer and a wear denial, remain available. Under `OPEN`, every outcome is available with no role escalation.

### 2.10. Parts Support and the Conserved Stockroom

Stock is held in **part lots**, each stocked for one catalogue model under a `part_code`. Three unit counters live on a lot and they are conserved together: `on_hand_units` is what is physically in the bin, `reserved_units` is the sum of `units` over reservations against the lot whose `status` is `RESERVED`, and `available_units` is `on_hand_units` minus `reserved_units`. `on_hand_units` is supplied once at lot creation and thereafter moves only by an explicit lot adjustment or by a reservation being consumed; it is never edited directly.

From `available_units` the lot's `status` is re-derived on every change. It is `DEPLETED` when `available_units` is zero; `LOW` when `available_units` is at least one and at most `low_watermark_units`; and `AVAILABLE` otherwise. This is a cycle, not a ladder — a lot moves `AVAILABLE` → `LOW` → `DEPLETED` as stock drains, and returns `DEPLETED` → `LOW` or `DEPLETED` → `AVAILABLE` when a restocking adjustment or a reservation release puts units back. It also moves `LOW` → `AVAILABLE` when a release lifts availability above the watermark, and it may jump `AVAILABLE` → `DEPLETED` directly when one draw takes every available unit and so crosses the whole `LOW` band in a single step.

Every change to a lot's `status` re-derives `parts_support_status` on the lot's model. That model-level field is `SUPPORTED` when some part lot for the model has a `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `LOW`. Failing that, it is `SUBSTITUTION_ONLY` when some part lot stocked for the model's `successor_model_id` has `substitution_allowed` set to true, has a non-null `substitute_for_part_code`, and has a `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `LOW`. Failing both, it is `NO_SUPPORT`. A `LOW` lot still counts as usable stock. Because `parts_support_status` feeds both adjudication and the turnaround promise, a stockroom movement can change the answer a claim gets.

### 2.11. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

Every request runs the same ordered gauntlet, and the **first** check that fails produces the response — no later check is evaluated, so a request that violates several rules always reports the earliest one. Text order in this document is not the ordering; the numbering below is. Checks are grouped into bands purely for readability.

**Authentication, then authorisation (checks 1–5).** (1) Both headers present and non-blank, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`. (2) `X-User-Role` is one of the five role names, else `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`. (3) For the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing row of the backing entity, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR`. (4) The role appears in the endpoint's permitted-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. (5) When acting as `Customer`, the addressed existing resource traces back to a unit whose `owner_customer_id` equals `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

**Existence (checks 6–8).** (6) Every identifier in the URL path resolves, validated left to right, so that the leftmost unresolvable segment is the one reported. The identifier-specific codes are `ERR_SERVICE_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GEAR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GEAR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESTIMATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PART_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_WORK_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. (7) Every foreign reference supplied in a request body resolves — `service_center_id`, `gear_item_id`, `gear_model_id`, `owner_customer_id`, `warranty_claim_id`, `repair_order_id` and `successor_model_id` — reported with the corresponding not-found code; this applies to unit registration, claim intake, repair-order creation, technician registration, part-lot stocking and the catalogue-model patch. (8) Second-level body references resolve: the `part_lot_id` on a reservation, an explicitly supplied `technician_id` on a work block, and every `work_block_id` inside a bench-complete `work_log`, reported as `ERR_PART_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WORK_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`.

**Request shape (checks 9–10).** (9) The body and query conform to the endpoint's input schema: required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, integers at or above their declared minimum, ISO-8601 timestamps parseable, and the `certifications`, `labor_lines`, `estimated_labor_lines` and `work_log` arrays well formed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). (10) On every list endpoint, `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` at least zero, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

**State preconditions (checks 11–19).** (11) A claim's `status` is a legal origin state for the requested claim transition, else `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE` (409); this guards claim submit, information request, resubmit, assessment recording, adjudication and repair-order creation. (12) An order's `work_status` is a legal origin state, else `ERR_INVALID_WORK_STATE` (409); this guards work-block scheduling, bench-complete, quality-control inspection, condemnation, invoicing, release-to-customer and parts reservation. (13) An order's `billing_status` is a legal origin state, else `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_STATE` (409): issuing an estimate requires `ESTIMATE_PENDING` or `ESTIMATE_SENT` and never `NOT_BILLABLE`; scheduling a work block requires `NOT_BILLABLE` or `ESTIMATE_APPROVED`; recording a payment requires `INVOICED`. The `ESTIMATE_APPROVED` case for estimate issuance is deliberately carved out of this check and answered later by check 40, so that a re-quote after approval reports its own cause; and this check does not apply to invoicing at all, whose billing gate is check 50. (14) An estimate's `status` is `SENT` for approve or decline, else `ERR_INVALID_ESTIMATE_STATE` (409). (15) A reservation's `status` is `RESERVED` for release or consume, else `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_RESERVED` (409). (16) A unit's `disposition_status` permits the requested resale-channel hop — `TRADED_IN` to `REFURB_LISTED` or `RECYCLED`, and `REFURB_LISTED` to `RESOLD` or `RECYCLED` — else `ERR_INVALID_DISPOSITION_STATE` (409). (17) The addressed resource is not already terminal in the dimension the call would move: a claim in `WEAR_DENIED`, `CLOSED_FULFILLED`, `CLOSED_REPLACED` or `CLOSED_DECLINED`, an order whose `work_status` is `RELEASED`, `IRREPARABLE` or `CLOSED_UNREPAIRED`, or a unit in `RETURNED`, `RESOLD` or `RECYCLED`, all yield `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409); this applies to every action endpoint that mutates a claim, a repair order or a gear unit. (18) `expected_claim_version` matches the claim's `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (412). (19) `expected_repair_order_version` matches the order's `version`, else `ERR_STALE_REPAIR_ORDER_VERSION` (412).

**Bench safety and compliance (checks 20–24).** (20) The acting technician, or the `technician_id` named in the body, has a roster `status` of `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ACTIVE` (422); this guards assessment recording, work-block scheduling, bench-complete and quality-control inspection. (21) The technician holds the required certification level, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (403); on assessment recording this is checked for every entry of the submitted `required_work_types`, and on work-block scheduling for the block's `work_type` when a `technician_id` is explicitly supplied. (22) The technician signing a quality-control inspection authored no assessment on that order's claim, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). (23) The centre's `liability_posture` is not `FROZEN` when the adjudication resolves to a warranty repair or a warranty replacement, else `ERR_CENTER_LIABILITY_FROZEN` (409); paid-repair and wear-denial outcomes are always permitted. (24) When the posture is `CONSTRAINED` and the adjudication resolves to a warranty replacement, `X-User-Role` must be `ProgramAdmin`, else `ERR_REPLACEMENT_REQUIRES_OVERRIDE` (403).

**Referential business rules (checks 25–29).** (25) The technician — acting or named — belongs to the same service centre as the claim or repair order, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CENTER_MISMATCH` (422); this guards assessment recording and work-block scheduling. (26) The part lot belongs to the order's model, or on a substitution draw to that model's `successor_model_id`, else `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH` (422). (27) A substitution draw requires the lot's `substitution_allowed` to be true, its `substitute_for_part_code` to equal the requested `required_part_code`, and its `part_kind` to match, else `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_NOT_PERMITTED` (422). (28) Every `work_block_id` in a bench-complete `work_log` belongs to the addressed order and has `status` `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_WORK_BLOCK_NOT_ON_ORDER` (422). (29) A `successor_model_id` must not point at the model itself and must not close a cycle through existing successor links, else `ERR_SUCCESSOR_CYCLE` (422); this guards both catalogue-model creation and the catalogue-model patch.

**Temporal rules (checks 30–35).** (30) A unit's `purchase_date` is not after the logical clock instant, else `ERR_PURCHASE_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422). (31) For an intake whose `intake_kind` is `WARRANTY_CLAIM`, the unit's `warranty_state` is not `EXPIRED`, else `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED` (422). (32) Interval endpoints are ordered — a work block's `start_at` is strictly before its `end_at`, and a surge window's `start_date` is at or before its `end_date` — else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). (33) A new active surge window must not overlap an existing active window of the same centre on closed-inclusive semantics, else `ERR_SURGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). (34) An explicitly named technician has no scheduled block overlapping the requested interval on half-open semantics, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). (35) The logical clock instant is at or before an estimate's `valid_until` when approving, else `ERR_ESTIMATE_EXPIRED` (409).

**Remaining business constraints (checks 36–49).** (36) For a `WARRANTY_CLAIM` intake, the unit carries proof of purchase, else `ERR_PROOF_OF_PURCHASE_MISSING` (422). (37) `info_request_count` is below the limit of two, else `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT` (409). (38) The newest non-superseded assessment's `determination` is not `INCONCLUSIVE`, else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INCONCLUSIVE` (409). (39) The claim has no repair order in a non-terminal `work_status` and its `decision` is one that authorises an order, else `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_OPEN` (409). (40) No estimate on the order has `status` `APPROVED`, else `ERR_ESTIMATE_ALREADY_APPROVED` (409). (41) The count of scheduled blocks at the centre on the new block's UTC calendar date is below `bench_capacity_blocks_per_day`, else `ERR_BENCH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). (42) A reservation's `units` is at least one and a lot adjustment's `delta_units` is not zero, else `ERR_INVALID_UNITS` (422). (43) A reservation's `units` does not exceed the lot's `available_units`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_UNITS` (409). (44) A negative adjustment does not push `on_hand_units` below `reserved_units`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_STOCK_ADJUSTMENT` (422). (45) Uniqueness holds: a model's `model_code` is unique across the whole catalogue (`ERR_MODEL_CODE_TAKEN`), a customer's `email` is globally unique (`ERR_EMAIL_TAKEN`), a technician's `employee_code` is globally unique (`ERR_EMPLOYEE_CODE_TAKEN`), a unit's `serial_number` is unique within its `gear_model_id` (`ERR_SERIAL_ALREADY_REGISTERED`), and a lot's `part_code` is unique within its `gear_model_id` (`ERR_PART_CODE_TAKEN`), all HTTP 409. (46) After applying the `work_log`, every entry of the order's `required_work_types` has at least one completed block, else `ERR_WORK_TYPES_INCOMPLETE` (409). (47) No reservation on the order is still `RESERVED` when quality control is signed, else `ERR_PARTS_NOT_SETTLED` (409). (48) When `technician_id` is omitted from a work block, the routing selector's candidate pool is non-empty, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` (422). (49) A unit's `disposition_status` is `IRREPARABLE_HOLD` for a trade-in, else `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IRREPARABLE` (409).

**Fiscal rules, evaluated last (checks 50–52).** (50) At invoicing, `billing_status` equals `ESTIMATE_APPROVED` — a paid order must carry an approved provisional quote, and a `NOT_BILLABLE` warranty order is never invoiceable — else `ERR_ESTIMATE_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 402). (51) A payment's `amount_cents` equals `final_invoice_total_cents` minus `paid_amount_cents` exactly, with neither partial payment nor overpayment accepted, else `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (402). (52) A paid-repair order's `billing_status` is `PAID` before release, else `ERR_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` (402).

### 2.12. Cascades, Derived Fields and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Business entities in this system are peers: every one of them is its own collection under `/gear-service`, and relationships are carried as references inside request and response bodies rather than as nesting in creation URLs. The consequence is that a change rarely stays where it was made. Consuming a reservation moves a lot's counters, which re-derives the lot's `status`, which re-derives the model's `parts_support_status`. Opening a liability entry moves a centre's `open_liability_cents`, which recomputes `liability_utilization_pct` and re-derives `liability_posture`. Every hop in such a chain is a real mutation of the affected row, refreshing its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version`, and every hop is applied within the same request.

A small number of read endpoints are nested under a parent purely as reverse-reachability channels — the estimates of an order, the liability entries of a claim, the surge windows of a centre. For any such path the server validates the identifiers left to right, reporting the first unresolvable segment, so a request for the liability entries of a non-existent claim answers `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and never looks for entries. Reverse reachability is otherwise provided by query filters on the flat collections: `service_center_id`, `gear_item_id`, `gear_model_id`, `owner_customer_id`, `warranty_claim_id`, `repair_order_id`, `part_lot_id`, `technician_id`, `assessor_technician_id` and `successor_model_id` are all available as list filters, alongside the status-shaped filters `status`, `work_status`, `billing_status`, `disposition_status`, `warranty_state`, `parts_support_status`, `repairability_flag`, `commitment_breached`, `decision`, `intake_kind`, `determination`, `is_superseded`, `variant`, `basis`, `category`, `part_kind` and `work_type`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Centre, a Catalogue and a Bench

A `ProgramAdmin` bootstraps the programme. A **service centre** is registered with a display `name` of one to one hundred and twenty non-blank characters, an uppercase `region_code` of two to eight characters, a `liability_cap_cents` of at least one cent, and optionally a `bench_capacity_blocks_per_day` between 1 and 200 that defaults to 24 when omitted. A freshly registered centre starts with `open_liability_cents` at 0, `liability_utilization_pct` at 0, `liability_posture` at `OPEN`, `active_queue_depth` at 0 and `current_surge_multiplier` at 1.0; its `breached_commitment_count` is 0 and its `last_queue_review_at` is null until the first checkpoint runs.

The **catalogue model** is where repair economics are configured. A model is created with a `model_code` of three to thirty-two characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and hyphens and unique across the catalogue; a marketing `name`; a `category` drawn from `SHELL_APPAREL`, `INSULATION`, `TENT_SHELTER`, `PACK_HARNESS` and `HARDGOODS_FRAME`; an `msrp_cents` of at least one cent; a `release_year` between 2000 and 2100; and a `warranty_years` between 0 and 30 stating limited coverage in whole years from purchase. A `lifetime_warranty` boolean defaults to false; when true the model carries coverage that never lapses and earns a bonus on its repair cap. A model may also be created with a `discontinued_at` instant and a `successor_model_id`, though both are more commonly set later. A new model begins with `parts_support_status` at `NO_SUPPORT`, because no lot has been stocked for it yet, with `repairability_flag` at `NONE`, and with its three feedback counters — `defect_claim_count`, `wear_denial_count` and `irreparable_count` — all at 0.

Models are retired rather than deleted. Patching a catalogue model may set `discontinued_at`, point `successor_model_id` at the model that replaces it, or reprice it by changing `msrp_cents`. The successor link is self-referential and cycle-checked: a model may not name itself as its own successor and may not close a loop through existing successor links, and either attempt is refused with `ERR_SUCCESSOR_CYCLE`. A successful patch re-derives the model's `parts_support_status` and forces every registered unit of that model to re-derive its `replacement_value_successor_cents`, since a repricing or a newly named successor changes what the brand would have to ship today.

A **technician** is registered against a centre with a `service_center_id`, a `full_name`, an `employee_code` of two to twenty-four characters unique across the whole bench, and a non-empty `certifications` array. Each certification entry names a `work_type` — one of `FABRIC`, `ZIPPER`, `FRAME` and `INSULATION` — and a skill `level` from 1 to 3, with at most one entry per `work_type`. A roster `status` may be supplied and otherwise defaults to `ACTIVE`; the other admissible values are `ON_LEAVE` and `INACTIVE`, and only an `ACTIVE` technician may take assessments or work blocks. New technicians start with `scheduled_minutes_open` and `completed_minutes` both at 0. Patching a technician may change the roster `status` or replace the `certifications` matrix; a technician moved to `ON_LEAVE` or `INACTIVE` drops out of the routing candidate pool immediately, while blocks already scheduled for them are left untouched.

Seasonal capacity is declared as **surge windows** on a centre. A window carries a `label` such as "Fall shoulder surge", a `start_date` that is the first covered instant and an `end_date` that is the last covered instant — the interval is closed on both ends — and a `multiplier` between 1.0 and 3.0 with at most two decimals. Its `status` begins as `ACTIVE` and becomes `RETIRED` once a queue-review observes that its `end_date` has fallen behind the logical clock. Because the interval is closed, two windows that merely share a single endpoint date already overlap, and a create that would do so against an existing `ACTIVE` window of the same centre is refused with `ERR_SURGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; a window whose `start_date` is after its `end_date` is refused earlier with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Every accepted window recomputes the centre's `current_surge_multiplier`.

### 3.2. Owners, Registered Units and What They Are Worth

A **customer** record is created by a `ServiceAgent` or a `ProgramAdmin` with a `full_name` and an `email` containing an at-sign and five to one hundred and twenty characters long, globally unique. A `loyalty_tier` may be supplied and defaults to `STANDARD`; the only other value is `PRO`, and the tier is what earns a discount on paid work. A new customer starts with `lifetime_claim_count` and `trade_in_credit_cents` at 0. Over time `lifetime_claim_count` accumulates one for each claim of theirs that has left `DRAFT`, and `trade_in_credit_cents` accumulates the running sum of credits granted on their traded-in gear.

A **gear unit** is the physical thing on the bench. Either its owner or a `ServiceAgent` registers it with a `gear_model_id` naming the catalogue model it instantiates, an `owner_customer_id` naming the registered owner who may thereafter file claims and approve quotes, a `serial_number` of three to forty characters that must be unique among units of the same model, a `purchase_date` that may not be later than the logical clock instant, and a `purchase_price_cents` of zero or more. Proof of purchase may be attached at registration: `proof_of_purchase_type` is one of `RECEIPT`, `REGISTRATION`, `RETAILER_LOOKUP` or `NONE` and defaults to `NONE`, and `proof_of_purchase_ref` carries the receipt or registration reference string and is null whenever the type is `NONE`. A cosmetic `condition_grade` of `A`, `B` or `C` may be supplied but is normally left null until the unit is actually graded.

On registration the server derives the unit's whole-month `age_months`, its `warranty_state`, and both valuations described in §2.8 — `replacement_value_depreciated_cents` and `replacement_value_successor_cents`. Its `disposition_status` starts at `WITH_CUSTOMER`. Coverage is a three-valued field: `warranty_state` is `LIFETIME` whenever the unit's model carries `lifetime_warranty`; otherwise it is `IN_WARRANTY` as long as the logical clock instant is at or before the `purchase_date` advanced by the model's `warranty_years` in whole calendar years; and otherwise it is `EXPIRED`. Lifetime coverage therefore never lapses, however old the unit is.

Three further money fields on a unit stay empty until the unit's afterlife begins. `trade_in_credit_cents` is 0 until the unit is accepted as a trade-in, `refurb_list_price_cents` is null until the same moment, and `resale_price_cents` is null until the unit is actually sold on the refurbished channel.

### 3.3. Claim Intake and the Documentation Cycle

A service event begins with a **warranty claim**, opened in `DRAFT` by the unit's owner or by a `ServiceAgent`. Intake carries a `service_center_id` naming the centre that will own the claim's liability and turnaround, a `gear_item_id` naming the unit whose defect is claimed, an `intake_kind`, a `reported_defect_type`, and an optional `customer_narrative` of one to two thousand characters describing the failure in the customer's words. The `reported_defect_type` is one of `SEAM_FAILURE`, `ZIPPER_FAILURE`, `DELAMINATION`, `FRAME_FRACTURE`, `INSULATION_LOSS` and `ABRASION_WEAR`. The `intake_kind` defaults to `WARRANTY_CLAIM` and may instead be `PAID_REPAIR_REQUEST`; the distinction is decisive at submit time, because a `WARRANTY_CLAIM` demands both proof of purchase and unexpired coverage while a `PAID_REPAIR_REQUEST` waives both. At creation the claim's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `info_request_count` and `assessment_count` are both 0, its `submitted_at`, `latest_determination`, `latest_repair_cost_cents`, `decision`, `decision_threshold_pct`, `repair_ratio_pct`, `decision_by_role` and `decided_at` are all null, and its `proof_verified` flag is derived from the unit: it is true exactly when the unit's `proof_of_purchase_type` is not `NONE` and its `proof_of_purchase_ref` is not null, and it is recomputed whenever those fields change.

Submitting moves the claim `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED`. The transition requires that the claim actually be in `DRAFT`; that either the `intake_kind` be `PAID_REPAIR_REQUEST` or `proof_verified` be true; and that either the `intake_kind` be `PAID_REPAIR_REQUEST` or the unit's `warranty_state` be `IN_WARRANTY` or `LIFETIME`. When the caller acts as `Customer`, their `X-User-ID` must equal the unit's `owner_customer_id`. A warranty intake with no verifiable proof is refused with `ERR_PROOF_OF_PURCHASE_MISSING`, and a warranty intake on a unit whose coverage has lapsed is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED`. On success the claim's `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, the unit moves `WITH_CUSTOMER` → `AT_CENTER` because the centre now has custody, and the owning customer's `lifetime_claim_count` increases by one.

Documentation is often incomplete, so the claim carries a bounded cycle. A `ServiceAgent` may issue an information request with a `reason`, moving the claim from either `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` into `INFO_REQUESTED` and increasing `info_request_count` by one. The cycle is bounded at two: an information request is only permitted while `info_request_count` is strictly below two, so a third attempt on the same claim is refused with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT`. When the request is issued from `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, the newest assessment on the claim remains non-superseded — asking for documents does not invalidate a finding.

Resubmitting closes the loop, moving `INFO_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED`, and is available to the owner or to a `ServiceAgent`; a `Customer` caller must again own the unit. The resubmission may carry `proof_of_purchase_type`, `proof_of_purchase_ref` and a revised `customer_narrative`. Any proof fields supplied are written through onto the gear unit itself — which mutates the unit, refreshing its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` — after which the claim's `proof_verified` is recomputed from the unit's stored proof fields. The claim's `submitted_at` is restamped with the current logical clock instant. The complete cycle is therefore `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `INFO_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED`, traversable at most twice before the information-request budget is exhausted.

### 3.4. The Bench Assessment: Defect Versus Wear

Only a `RepairTechnician` may record a **bench assessment**, and doing so is the transition `SUBMITTED` → `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, or the self-loop `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` → `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` when the bench re-assesses. Both legs carry the same preconditions apart from the origin state, and both are guarded by the claim version token described in §2.7: the request must carry `expected_claim_version` equal to the claim's current `version`, and a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION`. The acting technician's roster `status` must be `ACTIVE`, their `service_center_id` must equal the claim's `service_center_id`, and they must hold a certification for **every** entry of the submitted `required_work_types` at a level at or above the minimum for that work type. Those minimum levels differ by lane: fabric work needs level 1 or better, while zipper, frame and insulation work each need level 2 or better. A technician short of the bar is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`, one at the wrong centre with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CENTER_MISMATCH`, and one who is `ON_LEAVE` or `INACTIVE` with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The assessment body carries the finding and the bench-standard estimate behind it. Its `warranty_claim_id` is the claim being assessed. The `determination` is one of `MANUFACTURING_DEFECT`, `NORMAL_WEAR`, `USER_DAMAGE` and `INCONCLUSIVE` and is the defect-versus-wear judgement the whole programme turns on. Optional `defect_notes` of one to two thousand characters justify it. `required_work_types` is a non-empty array of distinct values drawn from `FABRIC`, `ZIPPER`, `FRAME` and `INSULATION` with no duplicates. `estimated_labor_lines` is an array of entries each naming a `work_type` and a whole number of `minutes` between 1 and 600, with exactly one line per work type and the set of work types equal to `required_work_types`. `estimated_parts_cents` is the estimated parts spend, zero or more. The `assessor_technician_id` is never accepted from the body: the server stamps it from `X-User-ID`.

From those inputs the server computes `estimated_repair_cost_cents` on the assessment row using the labour block of the paid-repair pricing rule described in §3.7 — that is, the per-line rounded labour charges summed into a labour subtotal, plus `estimated_parts_cents` used directly as the parts subtotal, plus the capped shop supply fee. It stops there: no loyalty discount, no expedite fee and no tax participate in an assessment estimate.

Recording an assessment cascades. Every earlier assessment on the same claim has its `is_superseded` flag set to true, so exactly one row is ever the live finding. The new row is created, the claim's `assessment_count` increases by one, and the claim's `latest_determination` and `latest_repair_cost_cents` are overwritten from the new row's `determination` and `estimated_repair_cost_cents`. The claim's `version` then increases by one — which is precisely why a second writer holding a stale `expected_claim_version` is turned away rather than allowed to bury a finding it never read.

### 3.5. Adjudication and Its Four Divergent Outcomes

Adjudication is a single operation, available to a `ServiceAgent` or a `ProgramAdmin`, that takes only an optional `decision_note` and derives its own outcome. **The caller never supplies the outcome.** The claim must be in `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`; the live finding must not be `INCONCLUSIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INCONCLUSIVE`; and the centre's `liability_posture` must not be `FROZEN` when the derived outcome is a warranty outcome. The inputs are the claim's newest non-superseded assessment (whose cost is mirrored on `latest_repair_cost_cents`), the gear unit, its catalogue model, and that model's `parts_support_status` as of the decision instant.

The derivation runs in three steps. **Step one** builds an age-decayed, category-specific, lifetime-boosted repair cap. The raw cap starts from the model's category allowance — sixty-five percent for `SHELL_APPAREL`, sixty percent for `INSULATION`, seventy percent for `TENT_SHELTER`, seventy-five percent for `PACK_HARNESS` and fifty-five percent for `HARDGOODS_FRAME`. Ten percentage points are added when the model carries `lifetime_warranty`, and nothing is added otherwise. Five percentage points are then subtracted for each completed year of the unit's age, where completed years are `age_months` divided by twelve and floored. The resulting raw cap is clamped into a band: take the lesser of ninety and the raw cap, then the greater of thirty and that result. The clamped value is the `decision_threshold_pct` stamped on the claim, and it is a whole integer percentage.

**Step two** computes the cost ratio. `repair_ratio_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the claim's `latest_repair_cost_cents`, divided by the unit's `replacement_value_successor_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The denominator is always the successor-MSRP valuation and never the depreciated one — the question being asked is "what would it cost the brand to ship a replacement today", not "what did this customer pay".

**Step three** branches, and the branches are evaluated top to bottom with the first match winning. If the live `determination` is `INCONCLUSIVE`, the request is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INCONCLUSIVE`. If it is `USER_DAMAGE`, the outcome is `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED` — the customer broke it, and the brand will happily fix it for money. If it is `NORMAL_WEAR`, the outcome depends on the stockroom: `WEAR_DENIED` when the model's `parts_support_status` is `NO_SUPPORT`, and `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED` otherwise. The remaining branches all assume a `MANUFACTURING_DEFECT`. If the unit's `warranty_state` is `EXPIRED`, the outcome is `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED`. If the model's `parts_support_status` is `NO_SUPPORT`, the outcome is `WARRANTY_REPLACE`, because there is nothing to repair the unit with. If `repair_ratio_pct` is greater than **or equal to** `decision_threshold_pct`, the outcome is `WARRANTY_REPLACE`; a ratio landing exactly on the threshold replaces rather than repairs. Otherwise the outcome is `WARRANTY_REPAIR`.

Whatever the outcome, the claim records `decision`, `decision_threshold_pct`, `repair_ratio_pct`, `decision_by_role` — set to `ServiceAgent` or `ProgramAdmin` according to the acting role — and `decided_at` from the logical clock. What happens next diverges sharply.

A `WARRANTY_REPAIR` outcome moves the claim `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` → `WARRANTY_APPROVED` and opens the brand's exposure in a three-hop cascade. First, a liability entry is created on the claim with `basis` `REPAIR`, `valuation_variant` `DEPRECIATED_PURCHASE`, `status` `OPEN`, and a `reserved_amount_cents` equal to the lesser of the claim's `latest_repair_cost_cents` and the unit's `replacement_value_depreciated_cents` — the brand never reserves more for a repair than the depreciated worth of the thing being repaired. Its `release_reason` and `released_at` are null while it is `OPEN`. Second, that new open entry increases the centre's `open_liability_cents` by the reserved amount and recomputes `liability_utilization_pct`. Third, the new exposure re-derives the centre's `liability_posture` on the bands of §2.9, so a single approval can push a centre from `OPEN` to `CONSTRAINED` or from `CONSTRAINED` to `FROZEN`. Separately, the model's `defect_claim_count` increases by one and its `repairability_flag` is then re-derived from the updated counters.

A `WARRANTY_REPLACE` outcome moves the claim `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` → `REPLACEMENT_AUTHORIZED` and is where posture bites hardest: while the centre is `CONSTRAINED`, only a `ProgramAdmin` may reach this outcome and any other role is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_REQUIRES_OVERRIDE`. On success a liability entry is created with `basis` `REPLACEMENT`, `valuation_variant` `SUCCESSOR_MSRP`, `status` `OPEN` and a `reserved_amount_cents` equal to the unit's full `replacement_value_successor_cents`; the centre's `open_liability_cents` rises by that amount and `liability_posture` is re-derived. The unit moves `AT_CENTER` → `IRREPARABLE_HOLD` and thereby skips the bench entirely: **no repair order may ever be opened on a replacement-authorised claim**, and the unit goes straight into the trade-in lane. As with a repair approval, the model's `defect_claim_count` increases by one and its `repairability_flag` is re-derived.

A `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED` outcome moves the claim `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` → `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED` and stamps `decision`, `decided_at` and `decision_by_role`. No liability entry is created and the centre's `open_liability_cents` is unchanged, because the brand carries no exposure on work the customer pays for. This outcome is available under every posture, `FROZEN` included.

A `WEAR_DENIED` outcome moves the claim `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` → `WEAR_DENIED`, which is terminal, and stamps `decision`, `decided_at` and `decision_by_role`. No liability entry is created and no repair order follows; the unit's `disposition_status` stays at `AT_CENTER`. The feedback signal is different from the warranty branches: the model's `wear_denial_count` increases by one and `defect_claim_count` is left untouched. This outcome, too, is available under every posture.

The repairability signal itself is recomputed on every counter change. A model's `repairability_flag` becomes `REDESIGN_REQUESTED` when its `defect_claim_count` is at least five **and** its `irreparable_count` is at least two; failing that, it becomes `WATCH` when its `defect_claim_count` is at least three; and otherwise it is `NONE`. The product teams read that flag; nobody sets it by hand.

### 3.6. Opening a Repair Order and Committing to a Turnaround

A `ServiceAgent` opens a **repair order** against an adjudicated claim by supplying its `warranty_claim_id` and, optionally, an `expedite_requested` flag that defaults to false. The claim must be in a state that authorises an order and its `decision` must be `WARRANTY_REPAIR` or `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED`; a claim whose `decision` is `WARRANTY_REPLACE` never gets an order, and neither does a claim that already has an order in a non-terminal `work_status` — both are refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_OPEN`. The order copies `service_center_id` and `gear_item_id` from the claim rather than accepting them from the body, and copies `required_work_types` from the claim's newest non-superseded assessment.

The order's `order_kind` follows the claim's `decision`: `WARRANTY_REPAIR` when the claim decided on warranty repair, and `PAID_REPAIR` when it offered paid repair. That same fork sets the money dimension's starting point, discussed in §3.7. The unit moves `AT_CENTER` → `IN_REPAIR`, and the order's bench dimension begins at `QUEUED` with `commitment_breached` false, `qc_fail_count` at 0, `paid_amount_cents` at 0, and `released_at`, `provisional_estimate_total_cents` and `final_invoice_total_cents` all null.

Creating the order also computes the **turnaround commitment**, and because the multiplier depends on how busy the centre is while this very order is joining the queue, the evaluation order is pinned.

First, the queue is snapshotted **before** the new order is inserted: `queue_depth_snapshot` is the count of repair orders at this centre whose `work_status` is `QUEUED`, `IN_WORK` or `BENCH_DONE`, excluding the row being created. Second, a congestion tier is looked up on that snapshot: a snapshot of at most 9 yields a tier multiplier of 1.00, at most 24 yields 1.25, at most 49 yields 1.60, and 50 or more yields 2.00. Third, the seasonal override is folded in but may never lower the tier: among the centre's surge windows whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `start_date` is at or before the logical clock instant and whose `end_date` is at or after it — the window is inclusive at both ends — the largest `multiplier` is taken, or 1.00 when no such window exists, and `surge_multiplier_applied` is the greater of the tier multiplier and that window multiplier. Fourth, a parts wait is read from the model's `parts_support_status` **as it stands before any reservation created by this request**: zero days when `SUPPORTED`, seven days when `SUBSTITUTION_ONLY`, and fourteen days when `NO_SUPPORT`, recorded as `parts_wait_days`. Fifth, base days are assembled from the model's `category` — ten days for `SHELL_APPAREL`, fourteen for `INSULATION`, twelve for `TENT_SHELTER`, nine for `PACK_HARNESS` and sixteen for `HARDGOODS_FRAME` — plus the largest work-type addition among the order's `required_work_types`, where fabric work adds two days, zipper work three, frame work five and insulation work six. Note that only the largest addition is taken, not the sum. Sixth, `promised_days` is the base-day total multiplied by `surge_multiplier_applied`, rounded **up** to a whole number of days, and only then increased by `parts_wait_days`; the rounding up happens before the parts wait is added and never after. `promised_return_at` is then the calendar date of the logical clock instant advanced by `promised_days` days, at 23:59:59.999Z on that date.

Only after the commitment is frozen onto the order is the order inserted, after which the centre's `active_queue_depth` and `current_surge_multiplier` are recomputed. The consequence is observable: immediately after a create, the centre's `active_queue_depth` equals the order's `queue_depth_snapshot` plus one. `active_queue_depth` itself is defined as the count of the centre's repair orders whose `work_status` is `QUEUED`, `IN_WORK` or `BENCH_DONE`.

### 3.7. Pricing Paid Work: Quote, Approval, Invoice, Payment

A repair order carries **two independent state dimensions** that advance on different operations and gate one another. The bench dimension is `work_status`; the money dimension is `billing_status`. A warranty order is minted with `billing_status` `NOT_BILLABLE`, which is already terminal in the money dimension — a warranty order is never quoted, never invoiced and never paid, and any attempt to issue an estimate on it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_STATE`. A paid order is minted with `billing_status` `ESTIMATE_PENDING`, and its bench dimension refuses to start until the money dimension has reached agreement.

Issuing a customer-facing quote is a `ServiceAgent` operation on a paid order, permitted while `billing_status` is `ESTIMATE_PENDING` (the first quote, `ESTIMATE_PENDING` → `ESTIMATE_SENT`) or `ESTIMATE_SENT` (a re-quote, the self-loop `ESTIMATE_SENT` → `ESTIMATE_SENT`). It requires `expected_repair_order_version` matching the order's `version`, refusing a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_REPAIR_ORDER_VERSION`. It also requires that no estimate on the order has `status` `APPROVED`: once a customer has said yes, re-quoting is refused with `ERR_ESTIMATE_ALREADY_APPROVED`. The body carries `labor_lines` — an array of entries each naming a `work_type` and whole `minutes` — and an optional `parts_lines` array whose entries each name a `part_code`, a count of `units` and a `unit_price_cents`. On success any prior estimate on the order whose `status` was `SENT` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, a new estimate row is created with `variant` `PROVISIONAL` and `status` `SENT` and a `repair_order_id` pointing at the order, the order's `billing_status` becomes `ESTIMATE_SENT`, and the order's `version` increases by one. The new row's `valid_until` is the logical clock instant advanced by seven days, and its `decided_at` is null until the customer answers.

The pricing arithmetic is shared by every estimate and is worth stating step by step, because the rounding order is load-bearing. **Step one** handles labour, and each line is rounded on its own before anything is summed. Taking the lines in ascending order of `work_type`, each line's charge is the hourly labour rate for its `work_type` multiplied by that line's `minutes`, divided by sixty, rounded half up to a whole cent. The hourly rates are 8500 cents for `FABRIC`, 9500 cents for `ZIPPER`, 11000 cents for `FRAME` and 9000 cents for `INSULATION`. `labor_subtotal_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-line charges; the sum itself is not rounded again, and it is emphatically not the rounding of one big rate-times-minutes total. **Step two** handles parts exactly: `parts_subtotal_cents` is the sum over the parts lines of `units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`, with no rounding because every term is already a whole number of cents. **Step three** applies a capped shop supply fee on labour alone: `shop_supply_fee_cents` is the lesser of 2500 cents and seven percent of `labor_subtotal_cents` rounded half up. **Step four** sums: `subtotal_cents` is `labor_subtotal_cents` plus `parts_subtotal_cents` plus `shop_supply_fee_cents`. **Step five** applies loyalty: `loyalty_discount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by the owner's tier rate, rounded half up, where a `STANDARD` owner's rate is zero percent and a `PRO` owner's rate is ten percent. **Step six** applies expedite: `expedite_fee_cents` is 3500 cents on a `FINAL` row when the order's `expedite_requested` is true, and zero in every other case — a `PROVISIONAL` row always carries zero here, even on an expedited order. **Step seven** taxes the discounted, expedited base: `tax_cents` is 8.25 percent of the quantity `subtotal_cents` minus `loyalty_discount_cents` plus `expedite_fee_cents`, rounded half up. **Step eight** totals: `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` minus `loyalty_discount_cents` plus `expedite_fee_cents` plus `tax_cents`. Steps three, five and seven each round exactly once.

The two pricing variants differ in their inputs, not their arithmetic, and identical nominal work can therefore produce two different totals. A `PROVISIONAL` row prices the bench-standard minutes an agent typed into `labor_lines` and the list prices they typed into `parts_lines`, and carries no expedite fee. A `FINAL` row is derived by the server and accepts no lines from the caller at all: its labour lines come from the order's work blocks whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, grouped by `work_type` with the group's minutes being the sum of `actual_minutes` for that work type and the groups taken in ascending `work_type` order; its parts lines come from the order's reservations whose `status` is `CONSUMED`, each priced at its lot's `unit_price_cents`, with `RESERVED` and `RELEASED` reservations excluded entirely. A substitution therefore reprices its line, since the substituted lot's own `unit_price_cents` is what is charged. And a `FINAL` row charges the expedite fee when the order asked for it.

The customer answers a quote themselves — approval and decline are the two operations reserved exclusively to the `Customer` role, and the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the unit's `owner_customer_id`. Approval requires the estimate's `status` to be `SENT` and the logical clock instant to be at or before its `valid_until`, refusing a late approval with `ERR_ESTIMATE_EXPIRED`. On approval the estimate moves `SENT` → `APPROVED`, its `decided_at` is stamped, the order's `provisional_estimate_total_cents` is set from the estimate's `total_cents`, and the order's `billing_status` moves `ESTIMATE_SENT` → `ESTIMATE_APPROVED` — which is the moment the bench dimension is unlocked and `QUEUED` → `IN_WORK` becomes reachable. Declining takes an optional `decline_reason` and moves the estimate `SENT` → `DECLINED`. Because a re-quote supersedes its predecessor, the estimate being declined is by construction the order's only `PROVISIONAL` row still in `SENT`, and declining it therefore unwinds the whole order: the order's `billing_status` becomes `ESTIMATE_DECLINED` and its `work_status` moves from `QUEUED` or `IN_WORK` to `CLOSED_UNREPAIRED`, every reservation on the order still in `RESERVED` is released with the full lot-and-model give-back chain described in §3.8, every work block on the order still in `SCHEDULED` is cancelled with its minutes given back to its technician, the unit moves `IN_REPAIR` → `RETURNED` and is shipped back unrepaired, and the claim closes `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED` → `CLOSED_DECLINED`.

Quotes also go stale on their own. The checkpoint described in §3.9 expires any `PROVISIONAL` estimate whose `status` is `SENT` and whose `valid_until` has fallen behind the logical clock, moving it `SENT` → `EXPIRED` and returning the order's `billing_status` `ESTIMATE_SENT` → `ESTIMATE_PENDING` so that a fresh quote can be issued. That is the money dimension's cycle: `ESTIMATE_PENDING` → `ESTIMATE_SENT` → `ESTIMATE_PENDING`.

Invoicing is a `ServiceAgent` operation taking no body, and it requires `billing_status` to be `ESTIMATE_APPROVED` and `work_status` to be `QC_PASSED`. A paid order with no approved quote, and any `NOT_BILLABLE` warranty order, are refused with `ERR_ESTIMATE_NOT_APPROVED`. On success a new estimate row is minted with `variant` `FINAL` directly in `status` `INVOICED` — a final row never passes through `SENT` or `APPROVED` — priced by the `FINAL` variant above, and the order's `final_invoice_total_cents` is set from that row's `total_cents` while its `billing_status` moves `ESTIMATE_APPROVED` → `INVOICED`.

Settlement is exact. A payment, available to the owner or to a `ServiceAgent`, carries an `amount_cents` that must equal `final_invoice_total_cents` minus `paid_amount_cents` precisely; anything less or more is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, and a payment attempted before invoicing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_STATE`. On success the amount is added to the order's `paid_amount_cents`, the order's `billing_status` moves `INVOICED` → `PAID`, and the `FINAL` estimate row moves `INVOICED` → `PAID`. Only then does release-to-customer become reachable in the bench dimension.

### 3.8. Bench Execution: Blocks, Routing, Parts and Quality Control

Bench time is booked as **work blocks**. A `ServiceAgent` or a `RepairTechnician` schedules one with a `repair_order_id`, a `work_type`, an inclusive `start_at` and an exclusive `end_at` — the interval is half-open, so a block ending exactly when another begins is adjacency and not overlap — and, optionally, a `technician_id`. The order's `work_status` must be `QUEUED` or `IN_WORK`, and its `billing_status` must be `NOT_BILLABLE` or `ESTIMATE_APPROVED`; scheduling bench time on a paid order whose quote is still `ESTIMATE_PENDING` or `ESTIMATE_SENT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_STATE`, which is exactly how the money dimension gates the bench dimension. A block whose `start_at` is at or after its `end_at` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The centre also has a daily bench ceiling: if the number of blocks at this centre already in `status` `SCHEDULED` whose `start_at` falls on the new block's UTC calendar date has reached `bench_capacity_blocks_per_day`, the request is refused with `ERR_BENCH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the block's `duration_minutes` is the whole number of minutes between `start_at` and `end_at`, its `status` is `SCHEDULED`, its `actual_minutes` is null, the assigned technician's `scheduled_minutes_open` increases by `duration_minutes`, and — if this is the first block on the order — the order's `work_status` moves `QUEUED` → `IN_WORK`. The centre's `active_queue_depth` is unaffected, since `QUEUED` and `IN_WORK` both count toward it.

When `technician_id` is supplied explicitly, that technician must have roster `status` `ACTIVE`, must belong to the order's centre, must hold a certification for the block's `work_type` at or above that lane's minimum level, and must have no block already in `status` `SCHEDULED` overlapping the requested half-open interval — a conflict is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Two blocks overlap when the existing block's `start_at` is strictly before the requested `end_at` **and** the requested `start_at` is strictly before the existing block's `end_at`.

When `technician_id` is omitted, the server **routes** the block itself. The candidate pool is drawn only from technicians of the order's own centre whose roster `status` is `ACTIVE`, who hold a certification whose `work_type` equals the requested `work_type` at a `level` at or above that lane's minimum — level 1 for fabric, level 2 for zipper, frame and insulation — and who have no block in `status` `SCHEDULED` overlapping the requested interval on the same half-open test. Blocks in `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` never block a window. The pool is then ranked, in order: first by the candidate's certification `level` for the requested `work_type`, highest first; then, among ties, by `scheduled_minutes_open`, lowest first; and finally, when both of those tie, by the technician's `created_at`, earliest first — never by `id`, and `created_at` is compared at millisecond resolution. The winner is stamped onto the block's `technician_id` and the block's `routed_by_selector` flag is set to true; a block whose technician was supplied by the caller keeps `routed_by_selector` false. If the candidate pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` and no block row is created at all.

A scheduled block may be cancelled by a `ServiceAgent` or a `RepairTechnician` with an optional `reason`, moving its `status` to `CANCELLED` and decreasing the technician's `scheduled_minutes_open` by the block's `duration_minutes`, which frees the window for future conflict checks. Technician minutes are a conserved quantity: every minute added to `scheduled_minutes_open` when a block is booked is given back exactly once, whether by cancellation or by completion.

**Parts** are drawn against the order. A `RepairTechnician` or a `PartsCoordinator` reserves stock by naming a `part_lot_id`, the `required_part_code` the repair actually needs, and a count of `units`, on an order whose `work_status` is `QUEUED`, `IN_WORK` or `BENCH_DONE`. The eligibility rule forks on whether the lot's own `part_code` equals the `required_part_code`. On an exact-code draw the lot must be stocked for the order's own model — a lot whose `gear_model_id` is anything else is refused with `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH` — and the reservation's `is_substitution` is false. On a substitution draw, which is the discontinued-model path, the lot's `gear_model_id` must be either the order's model or that model's `successor_model_id`, or the draw is refused with `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH`; the lot's `substitution_allowed` must be true and its `substitute_for_part_code` must equal the `required_part_code`, and the lot's `part_kind` must equal the `part_kind` of the lot registered under the required code on the order's model or its successor — any of those three failing is refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_NOT_PERMITTED` — and the reservation's `is_substitution` is set to true. The `part_kind` of a lot is one of `FABRIC_PANEL`, `ZIPPER_SLIDER`, `FRAME_SEGMENT`, `INSULATION_FILL` and `BUCKLE`, and a substitution must always match on it. Beyond eligibility, `units` below one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_UNITS` and `units` exceeding the lot's `available_units` is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PART_UNITS`; a draw exactly equal to `available_units` is allowed and depletes the lot. A successful reservation begins with `status` `RESERVED` and a null `settled_at`, increases the lot's `reserved_units` by `units`, recomputes the lot's `available_units`, re-derives the lot's `status`, and then re-derives `parts_support_status` on the lot's model.

A reservation settles one of two ways. A `RepairTechnician` **consumes** it — permitted while its `status` is `RESERVED` and the order's `work_status` is `QUEUED`, `IN_WORK` or `BENCH_DONE` — moving it `RESERVED` → `CONSUMED`, stamping `settled_at`, and decreasing **both** the lot's `on_hand_units` and its `reserved_units` by `units`, so `available_units` does not change; the lot's `status` is re-derived and then the model's `parts_support_status`. Consumption is also what makes the line billable on a final invoice. Alternatively a `PartsCoordinator` **releases** it with an optional `reason`, moving it `RESERVED` → `RELEASED`, stamping `settled_at`, and decreasing only the lot's `reserved_units` by `units`, so `available_units` rises by that many units while `on_hand_units` is untouched; again the lot's `status` and then the model's `parts_support_status` are re-derived. Release or consume attempted on a reservation that has already settled is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_RESERVED`. The same release, with the identical inventory give-back, is performed automatically by the system when an order is condemned or its quote is declined. Physical units are the second conserved quantity in this system: `on_hand_units` moves only through an explicit lot adjustment or a consumption, never through a reservation or a release.

A `PartsCoordinator` or a `ProgramAdmin` restocks or writes down a lot with a `delta_units` and a `reason`. A `delta_units` of zero is refused with `ERR_INVALID_UNITS`, and a negative adjustment that would push `on_hand_units` below `reserved_units` is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_STOCK_ADJUSTMENT`. Otherwise `on_hand_units` changes by `delta_units`, `available_units` is recomputed, the lot's `status` is re-derived — which is how a `DEPLETED` lot climbs back to `LOW` or `AVAILABLE` and closes the inventory cycle — and the model's `parts_support_status` is re-derived. Stocking a brand-new lot behaves similarly. A lot is created with a `gear_model_id`, a `part_code` of three to forty characters unique among the lots of that same model, a `part_kind`, an `on_hand_units` count of zero or more, and a `unit_price_cents` of zero or more that is what the line will be charged at on a final invoice. Optionally it carries a `low_watermark_units` of zero or more that defaults to 2, a `substitution_allowed` flag that defaults to false, and a `substitute_for_part_code` naming the discontinued part code this lot stands in for, which stays null unless `substitution_allowed` is true. The new lot begins with `reserved_units` at 0, `available_units` equal to `on_hand_units` and a derived `status`, and it re-derives `parts_support_status` both for its own model and for any model whose successor its model is.

**Bench completion** is a `RepairTechnician` operation on an order in `work_status` `IN_WORK`, moving it `IN_WORK` → `BENCH_DONE`. It carries a `work_log`, a non-empty array whose entries each name a `work_block_id` and the `actual_minutes` logged against it, with `actual_minutes` between 1 and 600. Every referenced block must belong to the addressed order and must be in `status` `SCHEDULED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WORK_BLOCK_NOT_ON_ORDER`. Furthermore, after the log is applied, every entry of the order's `required_work_types` must have at least one completed block on the order; a log that leaves a required lane unworked is refused with `ERR_WORK_TYPES_INCOMPLETE`. On success each listed block moves `SCHEDULED` → `COMPLETED` with its `actual_minutes` stamped, and for each block the technician's `scheduled_minutes_open` decreases by that block's `duration_minutes` while their `completed_minutes` increases by that block's `actual_minutes`.

**Quality control** is signed by a `RepairTechnician` on an order in `work_status` `BENCH_DONE`, supplying an `outcome` of either a pass or a fail plus optional `notes`. Two guards precede everything else. The signer must have authored no assessment on this order's claim, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the technician who judged defect versus wear may not also certify their own bench's repair. And no reservation on the order may still be in `status` `RESERVED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PARTS_NOT_SETTLED`; every drawn part must have been either consumed or released before the unit can pass. A passing inspection moves the order `BENCH_DONE` → `QC_PASSED`. A failing inspection branches on how many times the order has already failed. While `qc_fail_count` is below two before the increment, the count increases by one and the order moves `BENCH_DONE` → `IN_WORK`, reopening a rework loop that requires fresh work blocks; that is the bench cycle `IN_WORK` → `BENCH_DONE` → `IN_WORK`. When `qc_fail_count` is exactly two before the increment — that is, on the third failure — the count is set to three and the order moves `BENCH_DONE` → `IRREPARABLE` automatically, with no operator choice, and the full condemnation cascade of §3.9 runs identically to an explicit condemnation.

### 3.9. Endgame: Release, Condemnation, Trade-In, Resale and the Checkpoint

There are three ways an order can end, and each one reaches back through the whole graph.

**Release to customer** is a `ServiceAgent` operation on an order in `work_status` `QC_PASSED` whose `billing_status` is either `NOT_BILLABLE` or `PAID`; a paid order still carrying a balance is refused with `ERR_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING`. On success the order moves `QC_PASSED` → `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped from the logical clock, the unit moves `IN_REPAIR` → `RETURNED`, and the claim closes — `WARRANTY_APPROVED` → `CLOSED_FULFILLED` for a warranty order and `PAID_REPAIR_OFFERED` → `CLOSED_FULFILLED` for a paid one. For a warranty order the claim's open liability entry with `basis` `REPAIR` moves `OPEN` → `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `ORDER_RELEASED` and `released_at` stamped, the centre's `open_liability_cents` decreases by the released amount, and `liability_posture` is re-derived — which is how a centre unwinds from `FROZEN` back toward `OPEN`. For a paid order there is no liability movement at all, because a paid order never opened an entry. Finally the centre's `active_queue_depth` is recomputed, since this order has left the queue.

**Condemnation** is available to a `ServiceAgent` or a `RepairTechnician` with a `reason` on an order whose `work_status` is `QUEUED`, `IN_WORK` or `BENCH_DONE`, and it drives the order to `IRREPARABLE` from any of those three origins. Its cascade is the deepest in the system and runs identically whether it was requested explicitly or forced by a third quality-control failure. First, every reservation on the order still in `status` `RESERVED` moves to `RELEASED` with `settled_at` stamped. Second, each released reservation decreases its lot's `reserved_units` by that reservation's `units` and recomputes the lot's `available_units`. Third, each lot whose `available_units` changed re-derives its `status`, which is how a `DEPLETED` lot climbs back to `LOW` or `AVAILABLE`. Fourth, each lot status change re-derives `parts_support_status` on that lot's model. In parallel, every work block on the order still in `status` `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` and its technician's `scheduled_minutes_open` decreases by the block's `duration_minutes`. For a warranty order the liability is revalued rather than merely released: the claim's open entry with `basis` `REPAIR` moves `OPEN` → `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `REVALUED_TO_REPLACEMENT`, and a new entry is created with `basis` `REPLACEMENT`, `valuation_variant` `SUCCESSOR_MSRP` and `status` `OPEN`, so the centre's `open_liability_cents` is adjusted by subtracting the released repair amount and adding the new replacement amount before `liability_posture` is re-derived. The unit moves `IN_REPAIR` → `IRREPARABLE_HOLD`, which makes trade-in reachable. And the model's `irreparable_count` increases by one, after which its `repairability_flag` is re-derived.

**Quote decline** is the third ending, described in §3.7: it drives the order from `QUEUED` or `IN_WORK` to `CLOSED_UNREPAIRED` with `billing_status` `ESTIMATE_DECLINED`, releases reserved parts through the same four-hop lot-and-model chain, cancels scheduled blocks with the same minute give-back, returns the unit, and closes the claim as `CLOSED_DECLINED`.

A unit in `IRREPARABLE_HOLD` — whether it got there from a replacement authorisation or from a condemnation — is offered as a **trade-in** by its owner or by a `ServiceAgent`, who supplies a `condition_grade` of `A`, `B` or `C` in the body; the supplied grade overwrites whatever grade the unit was carrying. A trade-in attempted on a unit whose `disposition_status` is anything other than `IRREPARABLE_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IRREPARABLE`. The credit is a decay function on the depreciated basis: the base credit is the unit's `replacement_value_depreciated_cents` multiplied by the grade's rate — thirty-five percent for grade `A`, twenty-two percent for grade `B` and twelve percent for grade `C` — rounded half up. A goodwill bonus of 5000 cents is then added when the claim that condemned the unit had a `decision` of `WARRANTY_REPAIR` or `WARRANTY_REPLACE`, and nothing is added otherwise. The unit's `trade_in_credit_cents` is the lesser of 40000 cents and the base credit plus the goodwill bonus — the cap is applied **after** the bonus is added, never before. At the same moment the unit's `refurb_list_price_cents` is set to its `replacement_value_successor_cents` multiplied by fifty-five percent, rounded half up. The unit moves `IRREPARABLE_HOLD` → `TRADED_IN`, the owner's `trade_in_credit_cents` increases by the credit just granted, and the driving claim closes — `WARRANTY_APPROVED` → `CLOSED_REPLACED` or `REPLACEMENT_AUTHORIZED` → `CLOSED_REPLACED` — with every open liability entry on that claim moving to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `CLAIM_CLOSED` and `released_at` stamped, after which the centre's `open_liability_cents` decreases by the released amounts and `liability_posture` is re-derived.

A traded-in unit then moves through the refurbished channel under a `PartsCoordinator` or a `ProgramAdmin`, who names the target state in a `to` field. From `TRADED_IN` the unit may go to `REFURB_LISTED`, which lists it on the refurbished channel at its `refurb_list_price_cents`, or straight to `RECYCLED`. From `REFURB_LISTED` it may go to `RESOLD`, which requires a `sale_price_cents` in the body and stamps it onto the unit's `resale_price_cents`, or to `RECYCLED`, which strips it for parts and removes it from the resale channel. `RETURNED`, `RESOLD` and `RECYCLED` are all terminal. Any other hop is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPOSITION_STATE`.

Finally, the **queue-review checkpoint** is an ops-run capability restricted to a `ProgramAdmin` and executed against one centre. It performs four sweeps in one pass. It expires every `PROVISIONAL` estimate whose `status` is `SENT` and whose `valid_until` has fallen behind the logical clock, moving each to `EXPIRED` and its order's `billing_status` back to `ESTIMATE_PENDING`. It flags every repair order at the centre whose `work_status` is non-terminal and whose `promised_return_at` has fallen behind the logical clock by setting that order's `commitment_breached` to true. It retires every surge window whose `end_date` has fallen behind the logical clock, moving its `status` `ACTIVE` → `RETIRED`. And it recomputes the centre's derived surface: `breached_commitment_count` becomes the number of orders flagged as breached by this run, `active_queue_depth` and `current_surge_multiplier` are recomputed, `open_liability_cents` and `liability_utilization_pct` are rolled up afresh, and `liability_posture` is re-derived on the bands of §2.9 — so a checkpoint alone can move a centre's posture in either direction. `last_queue_review_at` is set to the logical clock instant. The centre's `current_surge_multiplier` is the multiplier implied by the `active_queue_depth` congestion tier together with any `ACTIVE` surge window covering the logical clock date, on the same tiers and the same take-the-greater rule that a repair order's `surge_multiplier_applied` uses.
