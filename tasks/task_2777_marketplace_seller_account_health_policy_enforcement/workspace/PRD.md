# Marketplace Seller Account Health & Policy Enforcement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An online marketplace lives or dies on buyer trust, and buyer trust is largely a function of how the marketplace treats sellers who ship late, cancel orders, misrepresent products, infringe trademarks, or list goods that are simply not allowed to be sold. Today those judgements are made in spreadsheets and inboxes: one team watches performance dashboards, another team fields rights-owner complaints, a third team decides whether to freeze a payout, and nobody can reconstruct afterwards why a particular account was suspended or on what evidence. Sellers experience this as arbitrary punishment, regulators experience it as an absence of due process, and the marketplace experiences it as an unbounded appeals backlog.

This document specifies the Seller Account Health & Policy Enforcement service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service measures each seller against four published performance metrics — order defect rate, late shipment rate, pre-fulfilment cancellation rate, and valid tracking rate — and converts breaches into an auditable health score and tier. It records policy violations against a published policy catalogue, converts substantiated violations into severity points, and lets a severity ladder choose the enforcement rung: a warning, a listing removal, or an account suspension. It gives the seller a documented remedy path — a plan of action with a root cause and corrective and preventive measures, a first-level appeal, and one escalation — and it holds the seller's proceeds in reserve while an investigation is open, with a full funds hold once an account is suspended or banned. It also watches for banned sellers who register again under a fresh legal name, by scoring registration signals against every account the marketplace has ever held.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is **separation of powers**: the investigator who issues an enforcement action may never review the plan of action that answers it, may never decide the appeal against it, and may never decide the escalation of that appeal; the officer who creates a funds hold may never release or forfeit it. The second is **transparency**: every consequential decision emits a notice to the seller carrying the policy code, the points assessed, the evidence reference, the rationale verbatim, and the deadline by which the seller must act. An enforcement decision that the seller cannot see and cannot answer is, for the purposes of this system, a defect.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Section 2 is the structural contract of the API. Everything in it holds for every endpoint unless a rule in §3 narrows it for a specific workflow.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller in two headers. `X-User-Role` names the acting role and `X-User-ID` names the acting user. If `X-User-Role` is absent or is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); the same rejection applies when `X-User-ID` is absent for any role other than `Anonymous`. Once the caller is authenticated, the server checks the acting role against the roles the target endpoint admits, and rejects a role that does not appear in that list with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `Anonymous`, `Seller`, `PolicyInvestigator`, `AppealReviewer`, and `ComplianceOfficer`.

- `Anonymous` is the unauthenticated registrant. Seller registration is the only operation this role may perform, and it is the only role that may perform it. When `X-User-Role` is `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is not required and, if supplied, is an opaque value not tied to any persisted entity: the server neither records it nor compares it to any row's `id`.
- `Seller` is the marketplace seller under enforcement. When `X-User-Role` is `Seller`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Seller` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Seller` compare the resource's owning seller reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Seller` caller may only reach a listing whose `seller_id` equals `X-User-ID`, an enforcement action whose `seller_id` equals `X-User-ID`, a plan of action or appeal whose enforcement action's `seller_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a seller-scoped path whose seller identifier equals `X-User-ID`. Any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Seller callers are never granted the operator-wide list endpoints, so no ownership filtering of query results is required.
- `PolicyInvestigator` files, investigates and adjudicates violations, issues enforcement actions, and opens reserve holds. When `X-User-Role` is `PolicyInvestigator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — as `issued_by_user_id` on an issued `EnforcementAction` and as `created_by_user_id` on an explicitly created `DisbursementHold` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `AppealReviewer` decides first-level appeals. When `X-User-Role` is `AppealReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `decided_by_user_id` on the decided `Appeal` but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `ComplianceOfficer` owns the policy catalogue, the metric feed, seller funds, plan-of-action review, escalation decisions, and related-account adjudication. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a reviewed `PlanOfAction`, as `escalation_decided_by_user_id` on an escalated `Appeal`, and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on an adjudicated `RelatedAccountLink`, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Because the four operator identifiers are opaque, the separation-of-powers rules in §2.7 are enforced on `X-User-ID` values and never on role names: two different `ComplianceOfficer` users are two different actors, and one `ComplianceOfficer` user acting twice on the same case is a single actor.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1, and may not be higher than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described in §2.2. Unless a specific list is called out below, rows are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Three lists deliberately invert that default because their readers want the newest evidence first: a single seller's performance snapshots are returned newest measured window first, ordered by `window_end` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; a single seller's transparency notices are returned newest first, ordered by `sent_at` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; and plans of action are returned newest revision first, ordered by `submitted_at` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

Beyond pagination, the operator list endpoints accept optional equality filters, all of them combinable with `limit` and `offset`. The seller list accepts `root_seller_id` (which resolves a whole registration family, since a seller minted from a matched registration carries the matched account's identifier there), `enforcement_state`, and `disbursement_state`. The health-evaluation list accepts `seller_id`, `auto_violation_id`, and `metric_tier`. The policy-rule list accepts `category`. A seller's own listing list accepts `state`, and a seller's own notification list accepts `template_code` and `state`. The violation list accepts `seller_id`, `policy_rule_id`, `listing_id`, and `state`. The enforcement-action list accepts `seller_id`, `violation_id`, and `state`. The plan-of-action list accepts `enforcement_action_id` and `state`. The appeal list accepts `enforcement_action_id`, `plan_of_action_id`, and `state`. The disbursement-hold list accepts `seller_id`, `enforcement_action_id`, and `state`. The related-account-link list accepts `candidate_seller_id`, `matched_seller_id`, and `state`, so a suspected re-registration can be found from either side of the pair.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored row is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix; a seller identifier and an appeal identifier are indistinguishable by shape, and callers must not attempt to infer a row's type from its `id`. Every row also carries a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation. Sellers, policy rules, listings, violations, enforcement actions, plans of action, appeals, disbursement holds, notifications, and related-account links all carry both fields. Two resources do not: a `MetricSnapshot` and a `HealthEvaluation` are immutable evidence records, and they carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resources entirely. Note that a server-driven recomputation counts as a mutation: when a cascade rewrites a seller's posture, severity balance, or funds fields without the client having asked for that specific row, the seller's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments exactly as if the client had written the row directly.

All temporal reasoning in this service reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. An administrative, test-driving capability lets a `ComplianceOfficer` set the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the call stores no row and changes no resource, but every subsequent clock reading returns the supplied instant. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host's wall-clock time. Appeal windows, escalation windows, plan-of-action deadlines, severity decay ages, health-evaluation lookback windows, and every `detected_at`, `issued_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `adjudicated_at`, `submitted_at`, `evaluated_at`, `decided_at`, `escalation_requested_at`, `released_at`, and `sent_at` value all read this clock.

### 2.5. The Two Posture Dimensions and the Recompute Order

A seller's account posture is not a single state. It is two independent dimensions that evolve separately and are stored separately. The **enforcement dimension** lives in `enforcement_state` and takes one of `ACTIVE`, `WATCHLIST`, `RESTRICTED`, `SUSPENDED`, or `BANNED`; it starts at `ACTIVE`. The **funds dimension** lives in `disbursement_state` and takes one of `NORMAL`, `RESERVE_HELD`, or `FULL_HOLD`; it starts at `NORMAL`. The two are coupled in exactly one direction: the enforcement dimension feeds the funds dimension, never the reverse. Releasing or forfeiting a hold never moves `enforcement_state`.

Neither dimension is ever set by a client. Both are recomputed from the seller's underlying facts at the end of any request that could have moved them, and the recomputation runs in a fixed three-step order that implementations must preserve: first `severity_points_balance` is recomputed from scratch, then `enforcement_state` is recomputed from the fresh balance, then `disbursement_state` is recomputed from the settled `enforcement_state`. Evaluating those steps in any other order produces a posture that lags one request behind the facts.

`BANNED` is absorbing. It is reached by setting `ban_reason` to either `RELATED_ACCOUNT_CONFIRMED` or `ESCALATION_EXHAUSTED`, and a non-null `ban_reason` pins `enforcement_state` to `BANNED` permanently: no later recomputation, appeal, decay, or vacated action lowers the posture again. While a seller is banned, every write operation against that seller or against a row that reaches that seller is refused with `ERR_SELLER_BANNED` (HTTP 409); the only exceptions are seller registration, crediting proceeds, forfeiting a hold, and setting the logical clock. Read operations are never blocked by a ban.

### 2.6. Rates, Points, Money and Rounding

Three numeric families coexist in this service and each has its own precision contract.

- **Rates** — the four performance metrics and the related-account confidence input — are fractions stored to exactly 4 decimal places, so a rate of `0.0125` means 1.25 percent. Every rate is rounded half-up to four decimals before it is stored and before it is compared against any threshold. The comparison is always made on the *rounded* value: twenty defect claims against two thousand and ten orders is 0.0099502 unrounded, rounds to `0.0100`, and therefore does breach a one-percent threshold.
- **Points and scores** — `severity_points_applied`, `severity_points_balance`, `health_score`, `base_severity_weight`, `confidence_score`, and `severity_points_disclosed` — are stored to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half-up. Where a formula in §3 pins the number of rounding steps, that pinning is normative: rounding more or fewer times than specified drifts the stored value by a cent of a point and is wrong.
- **Money** is integer cents throughout. `available_balance_cents`, `held_balance_cents`, `lifetime_credited_cents`, `lifetime_debited_cents`, `held_amount_cents`, `forfeited_amount_cents`, and the `amount_cents` disclosed on a funds notice are all integers and none of them may ever be negative. Where a monetary figure is derived from a percentage or a multiplier, the product is rounded half-up to a whole number of cents.

Every seller's funds satisfy one identity at all times, and every operation that touches money is specified so that the identity still holds when the operation returns: `available_balance_cents` plus `held_balance_cents` equals `lifetime_credited_cents` minus `lifetime_debited_cents`. Crediting proceeds raises the available balance and lifetime credits together. Creating a hold moves cents from the available balance into the held balance, leaving both lifetime figures alone. Releasing a hold moves them back. Forfeiting a hold is the only operation that ever raises `lifetime_debited_cents`, and it must return any un-forfeited residue to the available balance so the identity survives. `lifetime_credited_cents` never decreases and `lifetime_debited_cents` never decreases.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties

Four operator roles exist precisely so that no single human can investigate a seller, punish it, judge the remedy, and dismiss the complaint. The rule is enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against the user recorded for the conflicting earlier step of the same case, and every failure carries `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Four conflicts are checked:

- Taking an appeal under review, and deciding it at first level, require the acting user to differ from the `issued_by_user_id` of the enforcement action under appeal.
- Deciding an escalation requires the acting user to differ from **both** the `decided_by_user_id` of the first-level decision **and** the `issued_by_user_id` of the enforcement action. Either match is a rejection.
- Reviewing a plan of action requires the acting user to differ from the `issued_by_user_id` of the enforcement action the plan answers.
- Releasing or forfeiting a disbursement hold requires the acting user to differ from the `created_by_user_id` recorded on that hold. A hold minted by a posture cascade has no creating user, so this check cannot fail for such a row.

### 2.8. Order of Rejection

A single request frequently violates several rules at once, and readers of the error response must be able to predict which one is reported. Validation therefore proceeds in a strictly ordered sequence, and the **first** rule that fails decides the response; no later check is evaluated. The sequence is:

1. Authentication — the headers of §2.1, yielding `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation — the acting role against the endpoint's role list, yielding `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Path existence — every identifier templated into the URL, resolved left to right (§2.9), yielding the relevant not-found error.
4. Body foreign-key existence — every foreign key in the request body, resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields (§2.9).
5. Resource ownership — for any endpoint a `Seller` may call, the ownership comparison of §2.1, yielding `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
6. Segregation of duties — the four conflicts of §2.7, yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
7. Request shape — required fields present, types correct, enum values in range, string lengths and numeric ranges within their declared bounds, datetimes ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, and `limit` within 1 through 100; yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. Plan-of-action completeness — the content thresholds of §3.7, yielding `ERR_POA_INCOMPLETE`.
9. Ban check — the seller reached from the target row must not be banned, yielding `ERR_SELLER_BANNED`.
10. Transition legality — the target row must be in a state from which the requested transition exists, including the cross-entity gates, yielding `ERR_INVALID_STATE` or the more specific state error that applies.
11. Optimistic concurrency — the version expectations of §2.10, yielding `ERR_STALE_SELLER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ACTION_VERSION`.
12. Compliance safety gates — the overrides of operator discretion described in §3.8, §3.1, §3.2 and §3.6, yielding `ERR_REPEAT_OFFENSE_DENIAL`, `ERR_LINK_MATCH_NOT_BANNED`, `ERR_LISTING_RESTRICTED_NO_RELIST`, or `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_MORE_SEVERE`.
13. Clock windows and interval geometry — yielding `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_SNAPSHOT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_HOLD_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`, or `ERR_NO_SNAPSHOTS_IN_WINDOW`.
14. Uniqueness and at-most-one constraints — yielding `ERR_DUPLICATE_SELLER_EMAIL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE`, `ERR_ACTION_ALREADY_EXISTS`, `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS`, `ERR_POA_ALREADY_OPEN`, or `ERR_POA_REVISION_LIMIT`.
15. Cross-row coherence between rows that all exist — yielding `ERR_LISTING_SELLER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_LISTING_REQUIRED_FOR_SCOPE`, `ERR_REPORTER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_POA_ACTION_MISMATCH`, or `ERR_HOLD_ACTION_SELLER_MISMATCH`.
16. Money capacity — yielding `ERR_HOLD_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` or `ERR_FORFEIT_EXCEEDS_HOLD`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright. A banned seller's request fails with `ERR_SELLER_BANNED` in preference to any transition, concurrency, safety, temporal, uniqueness, coherence, or money error it also violates. And a stale version expectation is reported in preference to a safety gate, a closed window, a duplicate, or an unaffordable amount — but *after* the transition-legality check, so a request that is both stale and aimed at a row in the wrong state reports the state error.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Foreign-Key Existence

Identifiers templated into a URL are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not name a live row decides the rejection. A request that files a health evaluation against a non-existent seller therefore reports `ERR_SELLER_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the evaluation logic. The not-found codes are `ERR_SELLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_LINK_NOT_FOUND`, all with HTTP 404.

The same discipline governs foreign keys carried in a request body: they are resolved in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields, and the first unresolvable reference decides the rejection, drawing from the same catalogue plus `ERR_POLICY_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. Creating a listing, filing a violation, issuing an enforcement action, submitting a plan of action, filing an appeal, and creating a disbursement hold all carry body foreign keys and are all subject to this rule.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations require the client to declare which version of a parent row it believes it is acting on, and both reject a mismatch outright rather than proceeding against a row the client has not observed.

Creating a disbursement hold requires a non-optional `expected_seller_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Seller`. A new reserve hold is a legitimate operation whose computed `held_amount_cents` depends on that seller's `available_balance_cents`, snapshotted risk tier, and `disbursement_state` as they stood when the client read them. If the seller's `version` has advanced since — because another hold was created or released, because proceeds were credited, or because a posture cascade ran — the amount the client believes it is reserving is no longer the amount the server would compute, so the stale writer is refused with `ERR_STALE_SELLER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) instead of silently reserving a different figure.

Submitting a plan of action requires a non-optional `expected_enforcement_action_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `EnforcementAction`. A fresh submission legitimately supersedes the seller's prior returned plan, so the operation cannot be refused on state alone; but if the action's `version` has advanced — because it was acknowledged, because a plan against it was reviewed, or because an appeal outcome resolved it — the seller is answering a version of the action that no longer exists, and the submission is refused with `ERR_STALE_ACTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection are not subject to any version check, and supplying an unexpected version field to them is simply an unrecognised input.

### 2.11. Transparency Notices and Supersession

Every consequential decision emits a `Notification` addressed to the affected seller through `seller_id`. A notice names itself with `template_code`, one of `REGISTRATION_FLAGGED`, `HEALTH_BREACH`, `ACTION_ISSUED`, `POA_DECISION`, `APPEAL_DECISION`, `ESCALATION_DECISION`, `FUNDS_HELD`, `FUNDS_RELEASED`, or `ACCOUNT_BANNED`. It points at the row it is about with a deliberately untyped pair: `related_entity_type`, one of `SELLER`, `HEALTH_EVALUATION`, `ENFORCEMENT_ACTION`, `PLAN_OF_ACTION`, `APPEAL`, `DISBURSEMENT_HOLD`, or `RELATED_ACCOUNT_LINK`, together with `related_entity_id`, which the reader resolves through that type's own read endpoint. The instant of emission is recorded in `sent_at` from the logical clock.

The transparency payload a notice must carry depends on its template, and these requirements are not advisory. An `ACTION_ISSUED` notice must carry a non-null `policy_rule_code`, a non-null `severity_points_disclosed` copying the action's `severity_points_applied`, a non-null `appeal_deadline_at` copying the action's own deadline, and a non-null `evidence_reference` copying the cited violation's evidence bundle reference. A `POA_DECISION`, `APPEAL_DECISION`, or `ESCALATION_DECISION` notice must carry a non-null `decision_rationale`. An `APPEAL_DECISION` notice whose outcome was a denial must additionally carry a non-null `escalation_deadline_at`. A `FUNDS_HELD` or `FUNDS_RELEASED` notice must carry a non-null `amount_cents`. Fields not required by a template are null unless the source row happens to carry the corresponding value — a notice about a row that names a policy rule may disclose `policy_rule_code` even when its template does not demand it.

Notices are never destroyed. A notice is created in state `SENT` and remains there until a later notice with the identical combination of `seller_id`, `template_code`, `related_entity_type`, and `related_entity_id` is emitted, at which point the earlier row moves to `SUPERSEDED` and the newer row is `SENT`. Both rows remain readable in the seller's notice list, newest first, so the audit trail of what the seller was told and when is complete.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registration and the Hunt for Re-Registered Bans

A seller joins the marketplace by registering, which is the one operation an unauthenticated caller may perform. Registration supplies a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 characters that must still be non-empty after surrounding whitespace is trimmed, an `email` that must contain exactly one at-sign and that becomes the seller's login identity, and three opaque matching signals of 4 to 64 characters each: a `tax_id_hash` standing for the seller's tax identifier, a `payout_bank_token` standing for the bank account proceeds will be paid into, and an `address_fingerprint` standing for the normalised business address. A fourth signal, `device_fingerprint`, is optional; when supplied it must also be 4 to 64 characters, and when omitted it is stored as null. If another seller already holds the same `email` — compared without regard to letter case — registration is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SELLER_EMAIL`.

The server derives one field of its own at registration: `email_domain` is the portion of `email` following the at-sign, lowercased. That derived domain participates in matching only when it is not one of the four public mailbox providers the marketplace disregards — `gmail.com`, `yahoo.com`, `outlook.com`, and `hotmail.com` — because thousands of unrelated sellers share those.

The interesting work of registration is the search for a banned seller coming back under a new name. The candidate is scored against **every** pre-existing seller row, in every enforcement posture, banned accounts very much included, since finding those is the entire point of the exercise. For each incumbent, the server collects the signals that match exactly and adds up their weights: a matching `tax_id_hash` is worth 0.35, a matching `payout_bank_token` is worth 0.45, a matching `address_fingerprint` is worth 0.15, a matching non-public `email_domain` is worth 0.10, and a matching `device_fingerprint` is worth 0.20 — the device signal contributes only when the candidate actually supplied one, since two null fingerprints are not a match. The summed weight is capped at 1.00 and then rounded half-up to two decimals to become the pair's `confidence_score`; because the five weights total 1.25, the cap is genuinely reachable.

Every incumbent whose score reaches 0.35 or higher mints a `RelatedAccountLink`. The link records `candidate_seller_id` for the newly registered seller and `matched_seller_id` for the incumbent, lists the signals that matched in `matched_signals` using the fixed order `TAX_ID`, `BANK_TOKEN`, `ADDRESS`, `EMAIL_DOMAIN`, `DEVICE`, and freezes the incumbent's posture at that moment into `matched_seller_state_at_detection` so that a later change to the incumbent's account cannot rewrite the evidence. The link starts in state `DETECTED`, records `detected_at` from the logical clock, and leaves `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `adjudication_note` null until an officer looks at it. Where no incumbent reaches 0.35, no link is minted at all.

Among the links a single registration mints, exactly one is the **top link**: rank by `confidence_score` descending and break ties by the matched incumbent's `created_at` ascending — never by identifier. The top link's `matched_seller_id` is written into the new seller's `root_seller_id`, which is how a whole registration family becomes discoverable later; when no link was minted, `root_seller_id` stays null. If — and only if — that top link scores 0.60 or higher *and* its `matched_seller_state_at_detection` is `BANNED` or `SUSPENDED`, the new account's `registration_risk_flag` becomes true. A true risk flag drives the posture recomputation of §2.5 to `WATCHLIST` on the enforcement dimension even though the seller has no severity points at all, and a `WATCHLIST` posture in turn drives the funds dimension from `NORMAL` to `RESERVE_HELD`. A `REGISTRATION_FLAGGED` notice is emitted, pointing at the top link with `related_entity_type` `RELATED_ACCOUNT_LINK` and `related_entity_id` set to that link's identifier. A registration that mints links but whose top link scores below 0.60, or whose matched incumbent was merely `ACTIVE`, `WATCHLIST`, or `RESTRICTED`, leaves `registration_risk_flag` false and the new account fully `ACTIVE` and `NORMAL`.

Otherwise a fresh seller starts empty and clean: `enforcement_state` `ACTIVE`, `disbursement_state` `NORMAL`, `ban_reason` null, `severity_points_balance` 0.00, `health_score` and `metric_tier` both null because no evaluation has yet run, `available_balance_cents`, `held_balance_cents`, `lifetime_credited_cents`, `lifetime_debited_cents`, `open_defect_claim_units`, and `reinstated_count` all zero.

A detected link is evidence, not a verdict. A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates it by supplying a `decision` of either `CONFIRMED` or `DISMISSED` and an optional `adjudication_note`; the officer's identifier is stored in `adjudicated_by_user_id` and the note is stored on the link. Only a link in `DETECTED` may be adjudicated, and the adjudicating user must not be the candidate seller's own user identifier. Confirmation is deliberately harder than dismissal: a link may move `DETECTED → CONFIRMED` only when `matched_seller_state_at_detection` is exactly `BANNED`. A link to an incumbent who was merely suspended at detection cannot be confirmed at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_LINK_MATCH_NOT_BANNED` — suspension is a reversible sanction and a suspended seller's relatives are not, by that fact alone, evading a ban.

Confirming the link bans the candidate. `ban_reason` becomes `RELATED_ACCOUNT_CONFIRMED`, which pins `enforcement_state` to `BANNED`; every listing of the candidate in `ACTIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` moves to `REMOVED`; and because the account is now banned, the funds dimension moves to `FULL_HOLD` through the release-then-sweep sequence described in §3.9. Two notices go out, an `ACCOUNT_BANNED` notice pointing at the link and a `FUNDS_HELD` notice pointing at the newly minted hold, and the candidate's `version` increments. Dismissing the link instead clears the candidate's `registration_risk_flag` — but only when no other link pointing at that candidate remains in `DETECTED` or `CONFIRMED`, since one surviving suspicion is enough to keep the watchlist entry alive. When the flag does clear and the seller's severity balance is 0.00, the enforcement posture walks back from `WATCHLIST` to `ACTIVE`, which then relaxes the funds dimension. A dismissal also stores `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `adjudication_note` and increments the candidate's `version`.

### 3.2. The Policy Catalogue and the Seller's Listings

Enforcement is only legitimate against a published rule, so a `ComplianceOfficer` maintains the policy catalogue. A `PolicyRule` is published with a `code` of 2 to 32 characters that must be unique across the catalogue — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE` — a `category` drawn from `LISTING_ACCURACY`, `INTELLECTUAL_PROPERTY`, `RESTRICTED_PRODUCT`, `REVIEW_MANIPULATION`, and `SHIPPING_PERFORMANCE`, a `base_severity_weight` given to two decimals somewhere between 0.50 and 8.00 inclusive, and a `removal_scope` of either `LISTING` or `ACCOUNT` declaring whether a removal rung under this rule takes down only the cited item or the seller's whole catalogue. An optional `requires_poa` flag defaults to true and declares whether an appeal against an action citing this rule must be backed by an accepted plan of action. The server derives `decay_exempt` itself: it is true exactly when `category` is `INTELLECTUAL_PROPERTY` or `RESTRICTED_PRODUCT`, and false for the other three categories. Points earned under a decay-exempt rule never fade with time. The catalogue is readable by sellers as well as operators, filtered by `category`, because a seller cannot answer a charge it cannot look up.

A seller stocks the marketplace by creating listings. A listing is created with a `seller_id` that must equal the caller's own `X-User-ID` — any other value is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — a `sku` of 1 to 40 characters, a `title` of 1 to 200 characters shown to buyers, a `category_code` of 2 to 32 characters naming the marketplace category, and an optional `restricted_flag` that defaults to false and is the seller's own declaration that the item belongs to a restricted-product programme. A new listing begins in state `ACTIVE` with `removal_policy_category` null and `suppressed_by_suspension` false.

Nothing a seller does moves a listing's `state` afterwards; only enforcement cascades and one recovery operation do. An account suspension moves each of the seller's `ACTIVE` listings to `SUPPRESSED` and marks `suppressed_by_suspension` true on each, precisely so that a later reinstatement can distinguish a listing that was collateral damage from one that was individually removed. A listing removal, and a ban, move listings from `ACTIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` to `REMOVED`; the removal writes the enforcing action's snapshotted policy category into the listing's `removal_policy_category`, sets `suppressed_by_suspension` back to false, and counts the listing into the enforcing action's `removed_listing_count`. When the seller is reinstated and leaves `SUSPENDED`, every listing carrying `suppressed_by_suspension` true returns `SUPPRESSED → ACTIVE` and the flag clears; listings sitting in `REMOVED` are untouched by that conditional cascade, because a removal was a decision about the item and not about the account.

The one recovery a seller may drive itself is relisting, which moves a listing `REMOVED → ACTIVE` and clears `removal_policy_category`. It succeeds only for the listing's own owner and only under three conditions, and this operation is the clearest example in the service of a request whose outcome turns entirely on the parent seller's posture rather than on anything about the listing. If the owning seller is banned, the attempt fails with `ERR_SELLER_BANNED`. If the listing is not in `REMOVED`, the attempt fails with `ERR_LISTING_NOT_REMOVED`; and if the owning seller's `enforcement_state` is `RESTRICTED` or `SUSPENDED` — relisting is permitted only while the seller is `ACTIVE` or `WATCHLIST` — the attempt fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Finally, a listing whose `removal_policy_category` is `RESTRICTED_PRODUCT` may never come back at all, no matter how healthy the account becomes; that attempt is refused with `ERR_LISTING_RESTRICTED_NO_RELIST`. Relisting returns the updated listing with a 200 status because it modifies an existing resource. A seller's own listing collection, filterable by `state`, is the only place suppression and removal cascades can be observed.

### 3.3. Filing Measured Performance Windows

Performance measurement enters the service as immutable snapshots filed by a `ComplianceOfficer`, one per measured window per seller. A snapshot names its subject with `seller_id` and its interval with `window_start` and `window_end`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, where `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`. Windows are **half-open**: `window_start` is inclusive and `window_end` is exclusive, which is what makes back-to-back weekly windows tile a month without double-counting a single order.

The counters a snapshot carries are all non-negative integers. `order_volume` is the number of orders placed in the window and is the denominator of the late-shipment and cancellation rates. `delivered_or_refunded_volume` is the number of orders from the window that reached a delivered or refunded outcome, and is the denominator of the settled reading of the order defect rate. `defect_claims_pending` counts defect claims opened in the window that are still awaiting adjudication, while `defect_claims_substantiated` counts those already adjudicated against the seller. `late_shipments` counts shipments confirmed after the promised ship date, `pre_fulfillment_cancellations` counts seller-initiated cancellations before fulfilment, `shipments_total` counts shipments confirmed in the window, and `shipments_with_valid_tracking` counts those carrying carrier-validated tracking — this last counter may not exceed `shipments_total`. An optional `source` field records the provenance of the counters as either `PLATFORM_PIPELINE` or `MANUAL_CORRECTION`, and defaults to `PLATFORM_PIPELINE`.

Two windows of the same seller from the same `source` may not overlap; an attempt to file one that does is refused with `ERR_SNAPSHOT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the intervals are half-open, windows that merely touch do not overlap: a new window whose `window_start` equals an existing window's `window_end` is accepted. The exclusion is scoped to the pair of seller and provenance, so a `MANUAL_CORRECTION` window is free to cover exactly the same period as a `PLATFORM_PIPELINE` window of the same seller — that is precisely how a correction is filed.

Filing a snapshot has one derived consequence on the seller. `open_defect_claim_units` is refreshed to the `defect_claims_pending` of the seller's most recent snapshot, where "most recent" means the greatest `window_end` with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; where the seller has no snapshot at all this figure is zero. That single number is the exposure input to one of the two reserve bases in §3.9. Filing also increments the seller's `version` by one. The snapshot itself is evidence and is never modified after filing, which is why it carries no `updated_at` and no `version` of its own; the operation returns a 201 status. A seller's own snapshot collection is readable by the seller as well as by investigators and compliance officers.

### 3.4. Health Evaluation: Two Readings of Defect Rate, One Score, One Tier

Evaluating a seller's account health is a `PolicyInvestigator` or `ComplianceOfficer` capability that takes no payload whatsoever: the subject comes from the seller identified in the request path, and every input comes from snapshots already on file. The evaluation produces one immutable `HealthEvaluation` row and returns it with a 201 status.

**The input set.** The evaluation first assembles the snapshots it will read. A candidate snapshot must belong to the subject seller, must have a `window_end` no later than the current logical clock reading, and must have a `window_end` strictly later than ninety days before that reading — the trailing-quarter lookback. Candidates are ordered by `window_end` descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the ordered list is truncated to its first two rows. That truncated list is the evaluation's input set, and every metric in the evaluation reads that one set; no metric ever re-derives its own. Where the input set comes out empty — the seller has no snapshot inside the trailing ninety days — the whole request fails with `ERR_NO_SNAPSHOTS_IN_WINDOW` and no evaluation row is filed at all. Otherwise the row records `input_snapshot_count` as the number of snapshots in the set, which is therefore either 1 or 2; `input_window_start` as the `window_start` of the earliest snapshot in the set; and `input_window_end` as the `window_end` of the latest.

**Two readings of the same defect rate.** The order defect rate is deliberately computed twice, because "how many of this seller's orders went wrong" has two defensible answers and the seller is entitled to see both. The **provisional** reading, stored as `odr_provisional`, looks only at the latest snapshot in the input set — the one with the greatest `window_end`, ties broken by `created_at` ascending — and asks how many claims were opened against it: add that snapshot's `defect_claims_pending` to its `defect_claims_substantiated`, divide by that same snapshot's `order_volume`, and round half-up to four decimals. Where the latest snapshot's `order_volume` is zero, `odr_provisional` is exactly `0.0000`. The **settled** reading, stored as `odr_final`, looks across the whole input set and counts only claims already proven: sum `defect_claims_substantiated` over every snapshot in the set, divide by the sum of `delivered_or_refunded_volume` over the same snapshots, and round half-up to four decimals; where that summed denominator is zero, `odr_final` is exactly `0.0000`. The observable gap between the two readings is stored as `odr_variant_delta`, computed as `odr_provisional` minus `odr_final` and rounded half-up to four decimals.

The distinction matters because only one of the two readings has teeth. `odr_final` is the value that decides whether the order defect rate has breached, the value that feeds the health score, and therefore the value behind the tier. `odr_provisional` is disclosed for transparency and is what the seller sees in the breach notice trail; it is never a tier input. An implementation that lets the provisional reading drive the tier will produce a stricter verdict than the policy authorises.

**The three secondary rates.** All three read the same input set. `order_volume_final` is the sum of `order_volume` over the set. The `late_shipment_rate` is the sum of `late_shipments` over the set divided by `order_volume_final`, rounded half-up to four decimals; the `cancellation_rate` is the sum of `pre_fulfillment_cancellations` over the set divided by `order_volume_final`, rounded the same way. Where `order_volume_final` is zero, both of those rates are exactly `0.0000`. The `valid_tracking_rate` is the sum of `shipments_with_valid_tracking` over the set divided by the sum of `shipments_total` over the set, rounded half-up to four decimals — except that when the summed `shipments_total` is zero the rate is exactly `1.0000`, because a seller who shipped nothing has not failed to provide tracking.

**The health score.** The score starts from a perfect hundred and subtracts a penalty for each metric, in proportion to how far that metric *overshoots* its published threshold. Note the design intent: a metric sitting exactly on its threshold contributes no penalty at all yet still counts as breached, because the score measures overage while the tier measures crossing. Four thresholds are published: an order defect rate of one percent, expressed as `0.0100`; a late shipment rate of four percent, expressed as `0.0400`; a cancellation rate of two and a half percent, expressed as `0.0250`; and a valid tracking floor of ninety-five percent, expressed as `0.9500`.

For each of the three "lower is better" metrics the overshoot ratio is computed by subtracting the metric's threshold from the metric's rate, replacing a negative difference with zero, dividing the result by that same threshold, and capping the quotient at 2.0000. So the defect ratio uses `odr_final` against `0.0100`, the late ratio uses `late_shipment_rate` against `0.0400`, and the cancellation ratio uses `cancellation_rate` against `0.0250`. The tracking metric runs the other way: subtract `valid_tracking_rate` from the `0.9500` floor, replace a negative difference with zero, divide by `0.9500`, and cap at 2.0000.

Each ratio is then weighted, and **the order of the rounding steps is pinned**. Multiply the defect ratio by a weight of 42.50, the late ratio by 24.50, the cancellation ratio by 18.50, and the tracking ratio by 14.50, and round **each** of those four weighted penalties half-up to two decimals individually. Then add the four already-rounded penalties together and round that sum half-up to two decimals to obtain the penalty total. Finally, subtract the penalty total from 100.00, round half-up to two decimals, and take the greater of that figure and 0.00; the result is `health_score`. The competing reading — summing four unrounded penalties and rounding only the total once — differs by a cent on a great many inputs and is wrong. Where `order_volume_final` is zero all four ratios are zero and `health_score` is 100.00.

**Breached metrics and the tier.** The evaluation lists every breached metric in `breached_metrics`, always emitted in this fixed order: `ORDER_DEFECT_RATE`, `LATE_SHIPMENT_RATE`, `CANCELLATION_RATE`, `VALID_TRACKING_RATE`, and an empty array when nothing breached. The three "lower is better" metrics breach when their rate is greater than **or equal to** their threshold, so `odr_final` of exactly `0.0100` is a breach. Valid tracking breaches when its rate is **strictly below** the `0.9500` floor, so a rate of exactly `0.9500` is not a breach. That asymmetry is intentional and must be preserved.

The `metric_tier` is then chosen by evaluating four conditions top to bottom and taking the first that matches. If `order_volume_final` is zero the tier is `EXEMPT`, because a seller with no orders in the window cannot meaningfully be graded. Otherwise, if at least two metrics appear in `breached_metrics`, or `health_score` is strictly below 60.00, the tier is `BREACHING`. Otherwise, if at least one metric appears in `breached_metrics`, or `health_score` is strictly below 80.00, the tier is `AT_RISK`. Otherwise the tier is `HEALTHY`. All of these comparisons are made on the four-decimal rates and the two-decimal score exactly as stored.

The evaluation records `evaluated_at` from the logical clock and copies its own `health_score` and `metric_tier` onto the seller, incrementing the seller's `version` by one. Wherever the seller row exposes `health_score` and `metric_tier`, those are the values of the seller's most recent evaluation — most recent by `evaluated_at` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — and both are null until the first evaluation has run. Like a snapshot, an evaluation is immutable evidence and carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

**The automatic breach violation.** A `BREACHING` tier does not merely record a bad grade; it opens an investigation on its own. When the tier is `BREACHING`, the server looks for a rule to cite: the candidate pool is every `PolicyRule` whose `category` is `SHIPPING_PERFORMANCE` **and** whose `removal_scope` is `ACCOUNT`; candidates are ranked by `base_severity_weight` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the top candidate is selected. Against that rule the server mints a `Violation` with `auto_generated` true, opening directly in state `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` rather than in `REPORTED`, with `reporter_type` `PLATFORM_AUTOMATION`, `listing_id` null, `policy_rule_id` set to the selected rule, and `evidence_reference` formed by prefixing the evaluation's own identifier with the literal text `HEALTH-EVAL-`. The new violation's identifier is written into the evaluation's `auto_violation_id`. Because a violation of this seller is now under investigation, the funds dimension moves from `NORMAL` to `RESERVE_HELD`, and a `HEALTH_BREACH` notice is emitted with `related_entity_type` `HEALTH_EVALUATION`. Should the candidate pool be empty — no rule in the catalogue is both a shipping-performance rule and account-scoped — no violation is created, `auto_violation_id` stays null, and the evaluation is nonetheless filed and returned with a 201 status. A tier other than `BREACHING` never mints a violation and always leaves `auto_violation_id` null. The operator evaluation list can be filtered by `auto_violation_id`, which is how the evaluation that minted a given automatic violation is found.

### 3.5. Violation Reports, Investigation and Adjudication

A policy violation begins as an allegation, not a fact. Either a `PolicyInvestigator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may file one against a seller, naming the accused with `seller_id`, the rule allegedly broken with `policy_rule_id`, and the evidence bundle with an `evidence_reference` of 3 to 64 characters that will be disclosed to the seller verbatim. The filing also declares who complained through `reporter_type`, one of `RIGHTS_OWNER`, `BUYER`, `PLATFORM_AUTOMATION`, or `REGULATOR`. An optional `listing_id` cites the specific item at issue. A filed report opens in state `REPORTED` with `detected_at` taken from the logical clock, `auto_generated` false, and both `adjudicated_at` and `decision_note` null; it returns a 201 status.

Three coherence rules constrain what a report may say about rows that all exist. When the cited rule's `removal_scope` is `LISTING`, the report must cite a `listing_id`, since a listing-scoped removal has nothing to remove otherwise; omitting it is refused with `ERR_LISTING_REQUIRED_FOR_SCOPE`. When a `listing_id` is cited, that listing must belong to the accused seller; citing another seller's item is refused with `ERR_LISTING_SELLER_MISMATCH`. And an intellectual-property rule may be cited only by a rights owner: a report whose `policy_rule_id` names a rule in category `INTELLECTUAL_PROPERTY` while its `reporter_type` is anything other than `RIGHTS_OWNER` is refused with `ERR_REPORTER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, because the marketplace does not adjudicate infringement on the word of a buyer, a bot, or a regulator.

Opening an investigation is a `PolicyInvestigator`-only step that moves the report `REPORTED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION`; a report in any other state cannot be opened, and a report against a banned seller cannot be opened at all. Opening has a funds consequence that is easy to overlook: because one of the seller's violations is now under investigation, the funds dimension moves `NORMAL → RESERVE_HELD` per §3.9, which is what makes reserve holds available to the investigator in the first place. The seller's `version` increments by one and the updated violation is returned with a 200 status.

Closing the investigation is the same investigator role's decision, supplied as an `outcome` of either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `DISMISSED` together with an optional `decision_note` that is stored on the row and disclosed to the seller. Only a violation in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` may be adjudicated, and both outcomes are terminal for the violation. Both branches do exactly the same three things to the record: they set `adjudicated_at` from the logical clock, they store `decision_note`, and they recompute the seller's funds dimension — where no investigation of that seller remains open and the enforcement dimension is `ACTIVE`, the seller returns to `NORMAL`, which releases every one of that seller's holds still in `ACTIVE` with `release_reason` `INVESTIGATION_CLOSED`, moves each hold's cents from `held_balance_cents` back to `available_balance_cents`, and emits a `FUNDS_RELEASED` notice for each hold released. The branches differ in exactly one respect, and it is the consequential one: a `SUBSTANTIATED` violation is enforceable and a `DISMISSED` violation never is. No enforcement action may ever be issued for a dismissed violation, and an attempt to issue one against a violation in `REPORTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, or `DISMISSED` is refused with `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`. Adjudication returns a 200 status.

### 3.6. Severity Weighting and the Enforcement Ladder

Issuing enforcement is a `PolicyInvestigator` capability that cites one substantiated violation through `violation_id` and optionally proposes a harsher rung through `action_type_override`. It returns a new `EnforcementAction` with a 201 status. Three preconditions guard the issuance beyond the ordering of §2.8: the cited violation must be `SUBSTANTIATED`, no non-vacated action may already cite that violation — at most one action per violation may sit in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `UPHELD`, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_ACTION_ALREADY_EXISTS` — and where the cited rule's `requires_poa` is true, the seller must not currently have a plan of action in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, since piling a new charge on a seller mid-remedy is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

At issuance the action snapshots the facts it will be judged on, so that a later edit to the policy catalogue can never rewrite history. `seller_id` is copied from the cited violation. `policy_category` copies the rule's `category`, `policy_rule_code` copies the rule's `code`, and `decay_exempt` copies the rule's own derived exemption. `issued_by_user_id` is the acting investigator's `X-User-ID` and `issued_at` is the logical clock reading; both the severity decay clock and the appeal clock are anchored on `issued_at`. The action opens in state `ISSUED`, with `acknowledged_at` and `resolution_reason` null and `removed_listing_count` at zero.

**The points this action is worth.** `severity_points_applied` starts from the rule's `base_severity_weight` and escalates for repetition, for rights-owner infringement, and for a seller already failing its metrics. First count the seller's prior actions in the same policy category — every action of that seller whose `policy_category` equals this rule's category and whose state is `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `UPHELD`, which excludes vacated actions and excludes the action now being issued. Take the smaller of that count and 3, multiply it by 0.25, and add 1.00; that is the repeat factor, so a first offence multiplies by 1.00, a second by 1.25, a third by 1.50, a fourth by 1.75, and a fifth or later offence also by 1.75 because the count is capped at three. Multiply `base_severity_weight` by the repeat factor. Then, if the rule's category is `INTELLECTUAL_PROPERTY` and the cited violation's `reporter_type` is `RIGHTS_OWNER`, multiply the running figure by a further 1.50. Then, if the seller's current `metric_tier` is `BREACHING`, multiply the running figure by a further 1.25. The running figure is carried at full precision through every one of those multiplications and rounded **exactly once**, half-up to two decimals, at the end; rounding after each factor drifts the result by a hundredth of a point. Finally clamp the rounded figure to a floor of 0.50 and a ceiling of 12.00 — no action is ever worth less than half a point or more than twelve.

**The decayed balance.** `severity_points_balance` on the seller is never incremented in place; it is recomputed from scratch over the seller's actions in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `UPHELD`, and a vacated action simply leaves that set, which is what makes reinstatement genuinely compensating. For each action in the set, compute its age as the number of whole days from its `issued_at` to the current logical clock reading, discarding any part-day remainder. An action whose `decay_exempt` is true keeps a factor of 1.00 forever — intellectual-property and restricted-product points never fade. Otherwise, an action younger than 180 days takes a factor of 1.00, an action at least 180 days old but younger than 365 days takes a factor of 0.50, and an action 365 days old or older takes a factor of 0.00 and thereby contributes nothing. Multiply each action's `severity_points_applied` by its factor, accumulate the products at full precision, and round the accumulated sum half-up to two decimals exactly once at the end. The decay is a step function, not a curve: an action 179 days old still counts in full, and the very same action one day later counts half. The curve is anchored on `issued_at` and is never reset by an acknowledgement, an appeal denial, or a submitted plan.

**Choosing the rung.** The rung is chosen from the balance *including* this action's own points, and the evaluation order is pinned because the quantities look circular. First compute the prior balance: run the decay calculation above over the action set that exists **before** this issuance, decayed at the current clock reading. Second compute this action's `severity_points_applied` as described, whose repeat factor likewise counts only those pre-existing actions. Third add this action's points to the prior balance and round the sum half-up to two decimals — this action's own decay factor is 1.00 because its age is zero days — and call that the post-issuance balance. Fourth, select the rung from that post-issuance balance: a balance of 12.00 points or more selects `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION`; a balance of at least 4.00 but below 12.00 selects `LISTING_REMOVAL`; anything below 4.00 selects `WARNING`. Because the comparisons are inclusive, a post-issuance balance of exactly 12.00 suspends. One category floor then applies: a restricted-product action is never a bare warning, so if the rule's category is `RESTRICTED_PRODUCT` and the rung so far is `WARNING`, the rung is lifted to `LISTING_REMOVAL`. The result is stored as `ladder_rung_computed`. Fifth, the post-issuance balance becomes the seller's new `severity_points_balance`. Selecting the rung from the prior balance instead is the wrong reading and yields a strictly milder rung on the same facts.

**Overrides.** `action_type` equals `ladder_rung_computed` unless the issuing investigator supplied `action_type_override`. On the severity ordering where `WARNING` is milder than `LISTING_REMOVAL`, which is milder than `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION`, an override is honoured only when it is **strictly more severe** than the computed rung; an override equal to or milder than the computed rung is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_MORE_SEVERE`. When an override is honoured, `action_type` takes the override's value and `override_applied` becomes true; otherwise `override_applied` is false. Discretion in this system runs one way only: an operator may aggravate what the ladder computed, never mitigate it.

**Where the posture lands.** After the balance is written, `enforcement_state` is recomputed by evaluating six conditions top to bottom and taking the first that matches. If `ban_reason` is non-null the posture is `BANNED`. Otherwise, if any action of the seller has `action_type` `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION` and sits in `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `UPHELD`, the posture is `SUSPENDED` — a live suspension holds the posture regardless of how the balance has decayed. Otherwise, if `severity_points_balance` is 12.00 or more the posture is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if the balance is at least 4.00 the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if the balance is strictly greater than 0.00, or `registration_risk_flag` is true, the posture is `WATCHLIST`. Otherwise the posture is `ACTIVE`. Only once `enforcement_state` has settled is the funds dimension recomputed, per §2.5 and §3.9.

**The cascades.** A rung of `WARNING` changes no listing and moves no money; it contributes its points and nothing else. A rung of `LISTING_REMOVAL` moves listings to `REMOVED` according to the rule's `removal_scope`: under scope `LISTING` the single item cited by the violation's `listing_id` is removed, and under scope `ACCOUNT` every listing of the seller currently in `ACTIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` is removed. Each removed listing takes the action's `policy_category` into its `removal_policy_category` and has `suppressed_by_suspension` cleared, and `removed_listing_count` on the action records how many listings this action moved to `REMOVED`. A rung of `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION` runs the full suspension cascade: every listing of the seller in `ACTIVE` moves to `SUPPRESSED` with `suppressed_by_suspension` true; the funds dimension then moves to `FULL_HOLD`, which first releases every one of the seller's `ACTIVE` holds with `release_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_FULL_HOLD` and then mints a single sweeping hold as described in §3.9; and a `FUNDS_HELD` notice discloses the swept amount. Whichever rung applies, the seller's `version` increments by one and an `ACTION_ISSUED` notice goes out disclosing `policy_rule_code`, `severity_points_disclosed`, `appeal_deadline_at`, and the cited violation's `evidence_reference`; where the adjudication recorded a `decision_note`, that text is disclosed as the notice's `decision_rationale`.

**Acknowledgement.** `appeal_deadline_at` is set at issuance to `issued_at` plus thirty days, and an appeal is accepted while the clock reading is at or before that instant. The seller may acknowledge receipt of an action, moving it `ISSUED → ACKNOWLEDGED` and recording `acknowledged_at` from the clock; acknowledging returns a 200 status. Acknowledgement is a courtesy, not a remedy: it does not pause the appeal clock and it does not change the points. It does increment the action's `version` by one, which invalidates any `expected_enforcement_action_version` the seller was holding for a plan submission. An action already in `UPHELD` or `VACATED` cannot be acknowledged, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ACTION_NOT_APPEALABLE_STATE`.

### 3.7. Plans of Action: Submission, Revision and Review

A rule published with `requires_poa` true demands a remedy before it will entertain an appeal, and that remedy is a plan of action the seller writes itself. Submission is a `Seller` capability that names the charge it answers with `enforcement_action_id`, declares the client's last observed version of that action in `expected_enforcement_action_version` per §2.10, and supplies three substantive fields. `root_cause` is the seller's own explanation of why the failure happened; it must be at least 40 and at most 2000 characters once surrounding whitespace has been trimmed, a floor that exists specifically to reject plans that merely restate the symptom. `corrective_measures` is an array of the steps already taken to repair existing damage, and `preventive_measures` is an array of the steps that stop the failure recurring; each array must hold at least one and at most ten elements, and each element must itself be between 10 and 500 characters. Any breach of those content rules is refused with `ERR_POA_INCOMPLETE`. A submitted plan opens in state `SUBMITTED`, records `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and leaves `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_notes` null until a reviewer touches it; it returns a 201 status.

Beyond the ownership and version checks, submission is gated on the charge itself. The cited action must belong to the acting seller, must be in `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and must cite a rule whose snapshotted `requires_poa` is true — submitting a plan against an action whose rule never required one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. And only one plan per action may be open at a time: where a plan for that action is already in `SUBMITTED`, the new submission is refused with `ERR_POA_ALREADY_OPEN`. Every accepted submission also records `deadline_at`, computed as the cited action's `issued_at` plus seventeen days; the deadline is recorded for transparency and disclosed in notices. Submission increments the parent action's `version` by one, which is deliberate — it invalidates every other version expectation any client was holding against that action.

Review is a `ComplianceOfficer` capability, forbidden to the investigator who issued the action per §2.7, and it applies only to a plan in `SUBMITTED`. The officer supplies a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `RETURN`, or `REJECT` and optional `review_notes`; whichever branch runs, the officer's identifier is stored in `reviewed_by_user_id`, the notes are stored in `review_notes`, a `POA_DECISION` notice is emitted whose `decision_rationale` is those same notes, and the updated plan is returned with a 200 status.

- `ACCEPT` moves the plan `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`. Acceptance re-checks the content rules above — `root_cause` length after trimming, and the element counts and element lengths of `corrective_measures` and `preventive_measures` — and refuses with `ERR_POA_INCOMPLETE` if any of them fails. An accepted plan is terminal, and it is the precondition that unlocks an appeal against the same action whenever the rule requires one.
- `RETURN` moves the plan `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, which is the one branch that is not terminal: it hands the plan back for another attempt. A return is permitted only while the plan's `revision_count` is still below two. Once `revision_count` has reached two, a further return is refused with `ERR_POA_REVISION_LIMIT` — the officer must accept or reject instead.
- `REJECT` moves the plan `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal and fatal to the remedy path. A rejected plan can never satisfy the appeal precondition, and no further revision is accepted for that action.

The revision cycle is the reason `revision_count` exists. A plan's `revision_count` is 0 when it is the first plan filed for its action; when the seller answers a `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` plan with a fresh submission for the same action, the older row moves `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUPERSEDED` and the new row's `revision_count` is one higher than the superseded row's. The cycle from `SUBMITTED` to `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` and back to a new `SUBMITTED` may therefore run at most twice, so `revision_count` never exceeds 2, and `SUPERSEDED` — like `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` — is terminal for the row that carries it.

### 3.8. Appeals, Escalation, Reinstatement and the Final Ban

An appeal is the seller's formal challenge to one enforcement action, and only one appeal per action ever exists. Filing is a `Seller` capability naming the action through `enforcement_action_id`, optionally naming the backing plan through `plan_of_action_id`, and stating the case in `grounds` of between 20 and 2000 characters. The appealed action must belong to the acting seller and must be in `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`; an action already in `UPHELD` or `VACATED` is refused with `ERR_ACTION_NOT_APPEALABLE_STATE`. The clock reading must be at or before the action's `appeal_deadline_at`, thirty days from issuance; a strictly later reading is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Where the action's snapshotted rule had `requires_poa` true, a plan of action for that same action must already be in `ACCEPTED`, and the absence of one is refused with `ERR_POA_NOT_ACCEPTED`; a cited `plan_of_action_id` that belongs to some other action is refused with `ERR_POA_ACTION_MISMATCH`. A second appeal against the same action, in whatever state the first one now sits, is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS`. A filed appeal opens in state `SUBMITTED` with `reinstatement_granted` false and every decision field null, and returns a 201 status.

Review proceeds in two steps so that the reviewer's identity is on record before any decision is made. An `AppealReviewer` first takes the appeal under review, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; nothing but the state and `updated_at` changes, and only an appeal in `UNDER_REVIEW` may subsequently be decided. The reviewer must not be the user who issued the action under appeal.

The first-level decision supplies an `outcome` of `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, a `rationale` of between 20 and 2000 characters that is disclosed to the seller verbatim, and — on a denial — a `denial_reason` drawn from `REPEAT_OFFENSE`, `INSUFFICIENT_POA`, `EVIDENCE_UPHELD`, and `RESTRICTED_PRODUCT_RISK`. Deciding records `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, stores the `rationale`, and emits an `APPEAL_DECISION` notice; the decision returns a 200 status. Deciding an appeal that is not in `UNDER_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a decider who issued the action is refused per §2.7.

**The repeat-offence gate.** Reinstatement is not available to a seller with a pattern in the same policy family, and this gate overrides the reviewer's discretion entirely. Count the seller's other actions that are in the same category as the action under appeal: every action of the appealed action's seller whose identifier differs from the appealed action's, whose `policy_category` equals the appealed action's `policy_category`, whose state is `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `UPHELD`, and whose `issued_at` is no later than the appealed action's `issued_at`. Where that count reaches 2 or more, a `GRANTED` outcome is refused with `ERR_REPEAT_OFFENSE_DENIAL` and the reviewer must deny instead, recording `denial_reason` `REPEAT_OFFENSE`. Vacated actions are excluded from the count, so a successful earlier appeal genuinely restores the seller's eligibility rather than leaving a permanent black mark. The identical gate applies to a grant at escalation.

**A granted appeal.** `reinstatement_granted` becomes true exactly when the appealed action's `action_type` was `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION` — for a vacated warning or listing removal it stays false. The appealed action moves from `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` to `VACATED` with `resolution_reason` `APPEAL_GRANTED`, and because a vacated action leaves the severity input set, the whole compensating cascade runs in the order of §2.5: `severity_points_balance` is recomputed downward, `enforcement_state` walks back down the ladder — from `SUSPENDED` all the way to `ACTIVE` when the remaining balance is 0.00 and `registration_risk_flag` is false, or only as far as `RESTRICTED` or `WATCHLIST` when residual points from other actions survive — and `disbursement_state` is then recomputed from the settled posture. Every one of the seller's holds still in `ACTIVE` is released with `release_reason` `APPEAL_GRANTED`, returning their cents from `held_balance_cents` to `available_balance_cents`, and every listing carrying `suppressed_by_suspension` true returns to `ACTIVE` with the flag cleared while listings in `REMOVED` stay removed. Where the vacated action's `action_type` was `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION`, the seller's `reinstated_count` increments by one.

**A denied appeal.** The appeal moves `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, which is not terminal — it is the only state from which escalation is reachable. `escalation_deadline_at` is set to `decided_at` plus fourteen days. The appealed action moves from `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` to `UPHELD` with `resolution_reason` `APPEAL_DENIED`, and its `severity_points_applied` keeps counting toward the balance; no recomputation is needed because nothing left the input set. A seller who was `SUSPENDED` stays `SUSPENDED` and its sweeping hold stays in force. The `APPEAL_DECISION` notice carries the `decision_rationale` and, because the outcome was a denial, a non-null `escalation_deadline_at` so the seller knows exactly how long it has.

**Escalation.** A seller may escalate a denial once, supplying `escalation_grounds` of between 20 and 2000 characters. The appeal must be in `DENIED`, the acting seller must own the appealed action, and the clock reading must be at or before `escalation_deadline_at`; a strictly later reading is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Escalating moves the appeal `DENIED → ESCALATED_REVIEW`, records `escalation_requested_at` from the clock and stores `escalation_grounds`, and returns a 200 status. The appealed action stays in `UPHELD` throughout; escalation suspends nothing.

**The final decision.** An escalated appeal is decided by a `ComplianceOfficer` who must differ from **both** the `decided_by_user_id` of the first-level decision and the `issued_by_user_id` of the action; the officer's identifier is stored in `escalation_decided_by_user_id`. The decision supplies an `outcome` of `GRANTED_ON_ESCALATION` or `FINAL_DENIED`, a `rationale`, and a `denial_reason` on a denial, and it returns a 200 status alongside an `ESCALATION_DECISION` notice. Only an appeal in `ESCALATED_REVIEW` may be decided this way.

A `GRANTED_ON_ESCALATION` outcome is the only path in the entire service that reopens an action which already looked resolved: the action moves `UPHELD → VACATED` with `resolution_reason` `ESCALATION_GRANTED`, `reinstatement_granted` becomes true exactly when the vacated `action_type` was `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION`, and the identical reinstatement cascade described above runs in full — balance, then enforcement posture, then funds posture, then holds, then listings — with `reinstated_count` incrementing when the vacated action was a suspension. The grant is subject to the same repeat-offence gate and the same `ERR_REPEAT_OFFENSE_DENIAL` rejection.

A `FINAL_DENIED` outcome moves the appeal `ESCALATED_REVIEW → FINAL_DENIED` and sets `resolution_reason` `ESCALATION_DENIED` on the action, which stays in `UPHELD`. Where the exhausted action's `action_type` was `ACCOUNT_SUSPENSION`, the seller has now run out of process and is banned: `ban_reason` becomes `ESCALATION_EXHAUSTED`, which pins `enforcement_state` to `BANNED`; every listing of the seller in `ACTIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` moves to `REMOVED`; the funds dimension stays at `FULL_HOLD` and the existing sweeping hold is deliberately left in `ACTIVE` so that its cents remain available for forfeiture; and an `ACCOUNT_BANNED` notice is emitted alongside the escalation notice. Where the exhausted action was a warning or a listing removal, the final denial simply closes the matter and no ban follows.

Both `GRANTED` and `GRANTED_ON_ESCALATION` are terminal for the appeal, as is `FINAL_DENIED`. Once an action reaches `VACATED` it accepts nothing further: acknowledging it is refused with `ERR_ACTION_NOT_APPEALABLE_STATE` and a fresh appeal against it is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS`. An action in `UPHELD` refuses those same two operations for those same two reasons, and the escalation grant is the one and only move that can still shift it.

### 3.9. Seller Funds: Proceeds, Reserves, Release and Forfeiture

Money enters the system in exactly one way. A `ComplianceOfficer` credits settled marketplace proceeds to a seller by supplying an `amount_cents` figure and an optional `external_reference` identifying the settlement run it came from. The credit raises `available_balance_cents` by that amount and raises `lifetime_credited_cents` by the same amount, which is why the funds identity of §2.6 survives the operation untouched; the seller's `version` increments by one and the updated seller is returned with a 201 status. An `amount_cents` below 1 is a request-shape failure and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Crediting proceeds is one of the very few writes permitted against a banned seller, because settlements owed for orders already delivered do not evaporate when an account is closed.

**Where the funds posture lands.** After `enforcement_state` has settled, `disbursement_state` is recomputed by evaluating three conditions top to bottom and taking the first that matches. If `enforcement_state` is `SUSPENDED` or `BANNED`, the target is `FULL_HOLD`. Otherwise, if `enforcement_state` is `WATCHLIST` or `RESTRICTED`, **or** any violation of that seller sits in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, the target is `RESERVE_HELD`. Otherwise the target is `NORMAL`. The `RESERVE_HELD` posture moves no money by itself: it is a gating posture that suspends ordinary disbursement and authorises an investigator to open reserve holds, and the reserve rows themselves are created explicitly. Moving from `RESERVE_HELD` back to `NORMAL`, by contrast, does move money — every one of the seller's `ACTIVE` holds is released with `release_reason` `INVESTIGATION_CLOSED`, each hold's `held_amount_cents` returns from `held_balance_cents` to `available_balance_cents`, and a `FUNDS_RELEASED` notice is emitted per released hold.

**The risk tier a hold is priced at.** Whenever a hold is created, whether explicitly by an investigator or automatically by a cascade, a `risk_tier` is snapshotted onto the row from the seller as it stands at that instant, and both the reserve percentage and the exposure multiplier read that snapshot rather than the live seller. Three conditions are evaluated top to bottom, first match winning. The tier is `TIER_3` when `enforcement_state` is `SUSPENDED` or `BANNED`, or `severity_points_balance` is 12.00 or more, or `metric_tier` is `BREACHING`. Failing that, the tier is `TIER_2` when `severity_points_balance` is 4.00 or more, or `metric_tier` is `AT_RISK`, or `registration_risk_flag` is true. Failing both, the tier is `TIER_1`.

**Two defensible reserve policies.** A hold created explicitly must declare which reserve algorithm priced it through `reserve_basis`, and the two available bases answer the question "how much of this seller's money should we hold?" in genuinely different ways, producing different cents on identical data. Under `ROLLING`, the reserve is a share of the balance actually sitting on the account: multiply `available_balance_cents` by the tier's rolling percentage — 10 percent at `TIER_1`, 20 percent at `TIER_2`, and 30 percent at `TIER_3` — and round the product half-up to a whole number of cents. A rolling reserve scales with the balance and is therefore always affordable. Under `CLAIM_EXPOSURE`, the reserve is the money a wave of open defect claims could cost: multiply the seller's `open_defect_claim_units` by an assumed exposure of 9000 cents — ninety dollars — per claim, then by the tier's exposure multiplier of 1.00 at `TIER_1`, 1.25 at `TIER_2`, or 1.50 at `TIER_3`, and round the product half-up to a whole number of cents. An exposure reserve ignores the balance entirely, which means it can exceed it; there is deliberately **no** implicit clamp to the available balance, so a claim-exposure reserve larger than `available_balance_cents` is refused with `ERR_HOLD_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` rather than being quietly reduced. The third value of `reserve_basis`, `NONE`, appears only on cascade-minted sweeping rows and is never a legitimate input on an explicit creation.

**Creating a reserve hold.** An explicit hold is a `PolicyInvestigator` capability. It names its subject with `seller_id`, optionally names the investigation it freezes funds against with `enforcement_action_id`, declares `hold_type` and `reserve_basis`, bounds its coverage with `coverage_start` and `coverage_end`, and carries `expected_seller_version` per §2.10. The seller's `disbursement_state` must not be `NORMAL`; opening a hold against a seller in ordinary standing is refused with `ERR_SELLER_NOT_UNDER_HOLD_POSTURE`. A `hold_type` of `FULL_HOLD` is never a valid input here — sweeping rows are minted only by cascades — and supplying it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, so `RESERVE` is the only `hold_type` a client may ever send. A cited `enforcement_action_id` that belongs to a different seller than `seller_id` is refused with `ERR_HOLD_ACTION_SELLER_MISMATCH`.

Coverage intervals are **closed** at both ends, so `coverage_start` and `coverage_end` are both inclusive, and `coverage_end` must be strictly later than `coverage_start`. Unlike measurement windows, coverage intervals may not merely avoid overlapping: a new interval must be separated from the interval of every other `ACTIVE` hold of the same seller by strictly more than twenty-four hours, and a new interval that touches, overlaps, or sits within a full day of an existing active one is refused with `ERR_HOLD_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`. On success the row snapshots its `risk_tier`, computes `held_amount_cents` from the declared basis, records `created_by_user_id` from the acting investigator's `X-User-ID`, opens in state `ACTIVE` with `auto_created` false, `released_at` and `release_reason` null, and `forfeited_amount_cents` zero. The computed cents move from `available_balance_cents` into `held_balance_cents`, the seller's `version` increments by one, a `FUNDS_HELD` notice discloses the held `amount_cents`, and the row is returned with a 201 status.

**The sweeping cascade.** When the funds posture is driven to `FULL_HOLD` — by an account suspension or by a confirmed related-account ban — three steps run in this fixed order, and the order matters because the funds identity of §2.6 must hold after each one. Step one releases every `ACTIVE` hold of the seller with `release_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_FULL_HOLD`, returning their cents from `held_balance_cents` to `available_balance_cents`. Step two mints a single new hold with `hold_type` `FULL_HOLD`, `reserve_basis` `NONE`, `auto_created` true, a freshly snapshotted `risk_tier`, a null `created_by_user_id`, a null `enforcement_action_id` — a sweep is a consequence of the account's posture and not of one named investigation — `coverage_start` at the current clock reading and `coverage_end` ninety days later, and `held_amount_cents` equal to `available_balance_cents` as it stands **after** step one. Step three sets `available_balance_cents` to zero and `held_balance_cents` to that swept amount, leaving `lifetime_credited_cents` and `lifetime_debited_cents` untouched. A `FUNDS_HELD` notice discloses the swept `amount_cents`. Conversely, when a reinstatement takes the seller out of `SUSPENDED` and the funds posture leaves `FULL_HOLD` for `RESERVE_HELD` or `NORMAL`, every `ACTIVE` hold — the sweeping row included — is released with `release_reason` `APPEAL_GRANTED` and a `FUNDS_RELEASED` notice discloses the released total.

**Manual release.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may release a hold by hand, supplying a `release_reason` that a caller may only ever set to `MANUAL` or `INVESTIGATION_CLOSED`; the other two reasons, `APPEAL_GRANTED` and `SUPERSEDED_BY_FULL_HOLD`, are written by cascades alone. The hold must be in `ACTIVE`, the acting officer must not be the hold's `created_by_user_id` per §2.7, and the hold's `hold_type` must be `RESERVE` — a sweeping `FULL_HOLD` row may be released only by a reinstatement or posture cascade, and a manual attempt on one is refused with `ERR_HOLD_NOT_MANUALLY_RELEASABLE`. Release moves the hold `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, records `released_at` from the clock, returns `held_amount_cents` from `held_balance_cents` to `available_balance_cents`, increments the seller's `version` by one, emits a `FUNDS_RELEASED` notice, and returns a 200 status.

**Forfeiture.** Forfeiture is the only operation in the service that removes money from a seller permanently, and it is available only after the account is closed for good. A `ComplianceOfficer` — again, never the hold's creator — supplies a `forfeited_amount_cents` figure against a hold in `ACTIVE`. The seller's `enforcement_state` must be `BANNED`; forfeiting from a merely suspended account is refused with `ERR_SELLER_NOT_BANNED`. The figure may not exceed the hold's `held_amount_cents`, and a larger one is refused with `ERR_FORFEIT_EXCEEDS_HOLD`; partial forfeiture is entirely legitimate. The hold moves `ACTIVE → FORFEITED` and records the figure in `forfeited_amount_cents`. The seller's `held_balance_cents` falls by the hold's whole `held_amount_cents`, `lifetime_debited_cents` rises by `forfeited_amount_cents`, and the residue — the hold's `held_amount_cents` less the forfeited figure — returns to `available_balance_cents`, so that available plus held still equals lifetime credited less lifetime debited. The seller's `version` increments by one and the forfeited row is returned with a 200 status. A hold in `RELEASED` or `FORFEITED` is terminal and carries `forfeited_amount_cents` of zero unless forfeiture was what put it there.

Because cascade-minted holds are never announced to the investigator who triggered them, the hold list is the discovery channel for them, filterable by `seller_id`, `enforcement_action_id`, and `state`.
