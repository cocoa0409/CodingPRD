# Return-To-Work Modified Duty API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When an employee is hurt on the job, the expensive part of the claim is rarely the medical bill — it is the weeks of total disability that follow while the worker sits at home waiting for a full-duty release. Employers who run a structured modified-duty program shorten that gap by placing the worker into real, productive, medically-appropriate transitional work the moment a treating clinician publishes a set of physical restrictions. Running such a program by spreadsheet, however, fails at scale: restrictions arrive on paper and go stale, supervisors host restricted workers without current training, transitional assignments quietly run past statutory duration caps, wage-differential cheques are cut against the wrong pay period, and by the time anybody notices, the worker has been reinjured on a job that never fit the restrictions in the first place.

This document specifies the Return-To-Work & Modified Duty Program API, the system of record for an employer's occupational-health transitional work program. It is mounted under the `/rtw` path prefix. The service carries the whole chain of custody: intake of restrictions from a credentialed treating provider, verification by an occupational-health nurse, maintenance of a transitional job bank, scored matching of restrictions to jobs, funding authorization by the carrier's claim adjuster, supervisor training and compliance monitoring, periodic progression review to walk the worker back toward pre-injury capacity, reinjury reporting with risk escalation, a multi-disciplinary case-review panel with quorum voting, and a wage-differential ledger with reversible entries.

Two design forces shape almost every requirement that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the person who proposes a placement may not vote on the panel that judges it, and the person who voted may not be the one who closes the panel. The second is **safety-before-throughput**: whenever a restriction changes, a compliance finding is filed, or a reinjury is triaged, the program is expected to stop creating new transitional work and route the case to human review, even if that costs the employer productive hours. The service is deterministic and drives every date-sensitive rule from a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that program behaviour is reproducible and auditable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names enumerated below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This screening happens before anything else, including before the request body is parsed.

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `RTWCoordinator`, `OccHealthNurse`, `ClaimAdjuster`, `TreatingProvider`, `Supervisor`, and `Employee`. A `ProgramAdmin` is the program owner: they register workers, clinicians, supervisors and job-bank entries, own the ledger's compensating actions, and drive the logical clock. An `RTWCoordinator` owns placement creation and matching, the placement lifecycle, and the progression-review ladder. An `OccHealthNurse` is the clinical gatekeeper — restriction verification, reinjury triage, and stewardship of the case-review panel. A `ClaimAdjuster` represents the carrier and controls the money: the claim's spend ceiling and the funding decision on every individual placement. A `TreatingProvider` is the clinician who authors restriction sets. A `Supervisor` is the department manager who hosts transitional work and files monitoring observations. An `Employee` is the injured worker. When a caller's role is not listed among an endpoint's authorized roles in §4, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

Three of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is the `id` of a persisted row and ownership is enforced by comparing that value against the addressed resource's foreign key. When `X-User-Role` is `TreatingProvider`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TreatingProvider`; ownership checks compare it to the addressed restriction set's `treating_provider_id`, and on a filing request also to the `treating_provider_id` carried in the body. When `X-User-Role` is `Supervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supervisor`; ownership checks compare it to the addressed placement's `supervisor_id`, to the addressed training record's `supervisor_id`, or to the supervisor id named in the URL path. When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee`; ownership checks compare it to the `employee_id` of the addressed claim, to the `employee_id` of the claim owning the addressed placement, or to the employee id named in the URL path. Any mismatch on one of these three roles is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403.

The remaining four roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `RTWCoordinator`, `OccHealthNurse`, or `ClaimAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any row's `id`. The server does record these opaque identifiers where the workflow calls for provenance — a claim's `adjuster_user_id`, a placement's `created_by_user_id`, a directive's `decided_by_user_id`, a review's `reviewed_by_user_id`, an audit's `auditor_user_id`, a reinjury report's `reported_by_user_id`, a panel's `opened_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id`, and a ballot's `voter_user_id` and `voter_role` — but treats them as free-form strings rather than entity references. One opaque identifier is load-bearing: the claim's `adjuster_user_id` is the operator id of the adjuster who owns the claim, and only that same `X-User-ID` may decide the claim's placement funding; a directive decision arriving from any other operator is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403 at priority 6, while the claim-level authorization step that sets the spend ceiling carries no such check and accepts any `ClaimAdjuster` or `ProgramAdmin` caller.

Segregation of duties is enforced separately from ordinary role checks and carries its own code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403. Two situations trigger it. First, a panel ballot may not be cast by the principal whose `X-User-ID` equals the `created_by_user_id` of the placement under review — whoever proposed the assignment does not get a vote on whether it survives. Second, a panel may not be closed by a principal whose `X-User-ID` appears as the `voter_user_id` of any `COUNTED` ballot on that panel.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional pagination parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. Successful list responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, alongside the standard `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.

Rows are returned ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where two rows share an identical `created_at`, the tie is broken by the order in which they were inserted by the request that produced them; the `id` of a row is never used as an ordering key anywhere in this system. Every optional query parameter that is named after a field on the listed entity behaves as an exact-equality filter, and multiple such parameters combine conjunctively — a row must satisfy all of them to appear. Only the filters named for each listing in §3 are honoured; any other query parameter is ignored rather than refused. Filtering is applied before pagination, so `total_count` reports the size of the filtered set rather than the size of the table.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. Identifiers never carry a type prefix, and a client may not supply an `id` on creation — an `id` present in a creation payload is ignored rather than refused. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment the row is written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade originating elsewhere. Three record types are deliberate exceptions because they are append-only observations rather than mutable state: `ComplianceAudit`, `WageDifferentialEntry` and `CaseReviewVote` carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those three, `updated_at` and `version` are absent entirely, and the state that does move on them — a wage entry's `entry_status`, a ballot's `vote_status` — moves without a version counter. Consequently no optimistic-concurrency check may ever be expressed against one of these three record types.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this program reads wall-clock time. The server holds a single logical clock that starts, at process startup, at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to real time. Every date comparison in this specification — restriction expiry, training expiry, duration accrual, audit decay, review due dates — is evaluated against that clock. A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant — a required field holding any parseable ISO 8601 instant, or a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date which is read as midnight UTC, and which is honoured even when it precedes the clock's present value, a missing or unparseable value being a priority-3 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the clock endpoint answers with the new `current_utc` together with three arrays naming exactly what the advance changed: `expired_restriction_set_ids`, `expired_training_record_ids` and `warned_training_record_ids`, each ordered by the swept row's `created_at` ascending under the tie-break of §2.3. Advancing the clock is not a passive operation — it runs the expiry sweeps described in §3.2 and §3.3 and refreshes the duration ledger and claim postures described in §3.6.

### 2.6. Order of Rejection

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is normative: an implementation that reports a *different but also true* failure is wrong. Checks are evaluated in the following sequence, and the first failure ends the request.

Authentication runs first (priority 1, `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then endpoint role membership (priority 2, `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), then request-shape validation (priority 3, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). Request-shape validation covers every required field being present, every enum-typed value lying inside its declared set, every number lying inside its declared range, every date parsing as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, pagination staying within the bounds of §2.3, and every date range being non-inverted — `end_date` on or after `start_date`, `period_end` on or after `period_start`, and `valid_through` on or after `effective_from`.

Existence checks come next. Priority 4 resolves every id templated into the URL, validated strictly left to right in path order, so that a request naming a missing parent and a missing child reports the parent's absence; depending on which segment fails, the answer is one of `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESTRICTION_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPERVISOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIRECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRESSION_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REINJURY_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WAGE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, always with HTTP 404. Priority 5 then resolves every foreign-key id supplied in a request body or query string — this is what catches an unknown `treating_provider_id` when a clinician files restrictions, an unknown `restriction_set_id` or `transitional_job_id` when a placement is created either explicitly or by the selector, an unknown `supervisor_id` when a job-bank entry is added, and unknown filter ids on the placement, restriction-set and wage-ledger listings — answering with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESTRICTION_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPERVISOR_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WAGE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate.

Ownership and duty separation follow: priority 6 is the entity-backed ownership check of §2.1 (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), applied on the endpoints that address a row carrying the acting principal's own foreign key — the worker read and the worker's claim listing, the claim read and the claim's placement listing, restriction-set filing, reading and submission, the supervisor read, training-record filing, listing, reading and renewal, and the placement read together with hours logging and the monitoring, reinjury, progression-review and panel collections hanging off a placement — joined at the same priority by the adjuster-ownership check on a directive decision; the clinician read, a claim's restriction-set listing, the job read and job-bank listing, the program-wide placement listing, the individual progression-review, panel and ledger reads, the ledger listing, and ballot casting and listing run no ownership check at all; priority 7 is the segregation-of-duties check (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), applied on ballot casting and on panel closing.

State screening is next and is deliberately split in two. Priority 8 asks whether the addressed row already sits in a terminal state of its state machine; if so the answer is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` with HTTP 409. Priority 9 asks whether the row's current state is a declared origin state for the requested transition; if not, the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` with HTTP 409. The order matters: terminal-state lockdown is checked *before* the origin-state check, so a request aimed at a terminal row always answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` and never `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both apply to every transition endpoint.

After the generic state screen come the domain state screens, in this order: the referenced restriction set must be `ACTIVE` (priority 10, `ERR_RESTRICTION_SET_NOT_ACTIVE`); the owning claim must be `RTW_ELIGIBLE` (priority 11, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_RTW_ELIGIBLE`); the addressed directive must still be `PENDING` (priority 12, `ERR_DIRECTIVE_ALREADY_DECIDED`); the addressed panel must be in `VOTING` (priority 13, `ERR_CASE_REVIEW_NOT_VOTING`); and the addressed ledger row must still be `POSTED` (priority 14, `ERR_WAGE_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED`). Then the claim-posture gates: not `FROZEN` (priority 15, `ERR_CLAIM_POSTURE_FROZEN`) and, on the two endpoints that create brand-new transitional work, not `RESTRICTED` either (priority 16, `ERR_CLAIM_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`).

The remaining safety, temporal, capacity, fiscal and concurrency checks run in the numeric order given by their priorities in §3, which is: provider credentialing (17), restriction window validity at activation (18), hard restriction limits (19), match-score floor (20), baseline reinjury risk (21), logged hours against restrictions (22), supervisor oversight (23), supervisor training (24), placement window containment (25), worker-level window overlap (26), job-level changeover buffer (27), wage-period overlap (28), job seat capacity (29), job bookability (30), non-empty selector candidate set (31), progression-step ceiling (32), deferral ceiling (33), panel quorum (34), panel majority (35), duration hard cap (36), duration soft cap (37), directive presence (38), directive not denied (39), authorization ceilings (40), optimistic-concurrency version match (41), identifier uniqueness (42), and finally the case-review-in-progress bar on progression decisions (43). Each of these is stated in narrative form, together with the code it raises, in the workflow section that owns it.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parent Records

Three write operations in this program mutate a child collection whose meaning depends on a parent record that other actors are simultaneously entitled to change. Each of those three carries a mandatory version field naming the parent version the client last observed; the field is not optional, and omitting it is a priority-3 shape failure rather than a concurrency failure.

Filing a restriction set against a claim requires `expected_claim_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Claim`. If the value does not equal the claim's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A restriction intake legitimately supersedes the claim's prior clinical picture, so the server cannot distinguish an intentional re-intake from one authored against a stale view of the claim; the version check makes the difference explicit.

Creating a placement — whether against an explicitly named job or through the server-side selector — requires `expected_restriction_set_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `RestrictionSet`. If it does not equal that set's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESTRICTION_SET_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Every placement raises the set's `active_placement_count`, and every clarification round or activation cascade rewrites the set's published limits, so a creation carrying an out-of-date version was matched against limits the set no longer publishes.

Casting a ballot on a case-review panel requires `expected_case_review_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `CaseReview`. A mismatch with the panel's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_REVIEW_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A member's re-vote legitimately supersedes that member's earlier ballot, so a ballot written against a stale tally would silently displace a row the voter never saw.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for a version reason.

### 2.8. Cents, Rounding and Ledger Discipline

All money in this system is expressed in whole cents as integers — `pre_injury_weekly_wage_cents`, `hourly_rate_cents`, `max_authorized_weekly_cost_cents`, `requested_weekly_cost_cents`, `authorized_weekly_cost_cents`, `projected_weekly_cost_cents`, `employer_premium_share_cents`, `employee_premium_share_cents`, `gross_differential_cents`, `offset_cents`, `net_amount_cents` and `wage_differential_paid_cents` are never fractional. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fraction, the result is rounded half up to whole cents, and the specification in §3 always states exactly where in the sequence that rounding occurs, because a program that rounds at a different step produces a different cent.

Scores follow the same discipline with different scales. `severity_index` and `reinjury_risk_score` are integers rounded half up and clamped into the inclusive range from 0 to 100. `match_score` is rounded half up to two decimal places, with clamping applied before that single rounding. A panel's `decision_ratio` is rounded half up to four decimal places. Program metric rates are rounded half up to four decimal places and `average_duration_days` to two decimal places. Progression targets, `target_hours_per_day` and `target_lift_lbs`, are rounded half up to two decimal places.

The wage ledger is append-only in spirit: a posted row is never edited or deleted. When a posting must be undone, a compensating `REVERSAL` row is minted whose `net_amount_cents` is the negation of the original's, and the original flips to `REVERSED`. Because the claim's `wage_differential_paid_cents` sums `net_amount_cents` over every entry regardless of `entry_status`, an original and its reversal cancel exactly, returning the claim total to its pre-posting value.

### 2.9. Parallel Status Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

A transitional placement carries two independent status dimensions that evolve on separate axes and must never be conflated. `placement_status` is the lifecycle dimension, moving through `PROPOSED`, `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD`, `SUSPENDED`, and terminating in `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED_EARLY` or `DECLINED`. `compliance_status` is the monitoring dimension, cycling among `COMPLIANT`, `WATCH`, `NONCOMPLIANT` and `REMEDIATED` purely under the influence of monitoring observations and supervisor-suspension cascades. A placement can be `ACTIVE` and `NONCOMPLIANT` at the same time; a compliance movement never by itself changes `placement_status`, and a lifecycle movement never by itself changes `compliance_status`.

Several operations in this program mutate more than one record. When they do, every touched record's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed — the originating record and every record reached by the cascade alike. Derived fields are recomputed as part of the same request, never lazily: a claim's `rtw_posture`, `cumulative_transitional_days` and `wage_differential_paid_cents`, a job's `filled_seats`, a restriction set's `active_placement_count` and `severity_index`, a supervisor's `compliance_strike_points`, `effective_strike_points` and `training_status`, a placement's `match_score`, `reinjury_risk_score`, `cumulative_days`, premium shares and `projected_weekly_cost_cents`, and a panel's tally fields all reflect the post-cascade world in the very response that caused the cascade. The denormalized pointer fields — a claim's `active_restriction_set_id`, and a placement's `current_adjuster_directive_id`, `current_progression_review_id` and `open_case_review_id` — are likewise computed, never accepted as input.

Where a pointer must choose among several candidate rows, the tie-break is fixed and never uses `id`. A placement's `current_adjuster_directive_id` names the directive of that placement whose `directive_status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`, taking the latest `created_at`; at most one such row can exist. Its `current_progression_review_id` names the review of that placement whose `review_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_REVIEW` carrying the greatest `step_index`, and when two such rows share the greatest `step_index` the earlier `created_at` wins. Its `open_case_review_id` names the panel of that placement whose `review_status` is `OPEN` or `VOTING`, or null when there is none.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrolling Workers, Opening Claims, and the Carrier's Posture Gate

A program administrator opens the file on an injured worker by registering an `Employee` with their `full_name`, the employer-assigned payroll number `employee_number`, the `department_code` they belong to, the average weekly earnings before the injury as `pre_injury_weekly_wage_cents` in whole cents, the contracted `pre_injury_weekly_hours`, and the health-benefit tier `benefit_tier`, which is one of `TIER_A`, `TIER_B` or `TIER_C`. The optional `employment_status` may be supplied as `ACTIVE`, `ON_LEAVE` or `SEPARATED` and defaults to `ACTIVE` when omitted. The wage figure must be at least 1 cent and the weekly hours must be greater than zero and no more than 80. The payroll number must be between 1 and 32 characters long and must not already exist on another worker; the same uniqueness discipline applies to a claim's `claim_number` and a clinician's `npi`, and a collision on any of the three is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` and HTTP 409. A newly registered worker starts with `open_claim_count` at zero; thereafter that counter reports how many of the worker's claims sit in `OPEN`, `RTW_ELIGIBLE` or `SUSPENDED` — a `CLOSED` claim stops counting.

Claims are opened underneath a worker, either by a program administrator or by the carrier's adjuster, and carry the carrier's `claim_number`, the `adjuster_user_id` of the operator who will own the file, and the `injury_date` of the compensable injury as a calendar date. The claim's `employee_id` is taken from the worker in whose collection the claim was created rather than from the payload. A new claim starts with `claim_status` set to `OPEN`, `rtw_posture` at `OPEN`, and `max_authorized_weekly_cost_cents` at zero, and opening it raises the worker's `open_claim_count` by one.

A claim in `OPEN` is not yet clearable for transitional work. Only the adjuster's authorization step moves the eligibility flag: the caller supplies a `claim_status_directive` of `RTW_ELIGIBLE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED`, which is written straight onto `claim_status`, together with `max_authorized_weekly_cost_cents`, the ceiling — which must not be negative — that will bound every individual funding decision on this claim. That operation also refreshes the claim's `rtw_posture` and raises the claim's `version` by 1. The step is not itself a state machine: any of the three directives may be written over any current `claim_status`, so a `CLOSED` claim can be returned to `RTW_ELIGIBLE`, and neither a terminal-state nor an origin-state screen applies. No other operation writes `claim_status`. Because placement creation demands `RTW_ELIGIBLE` at priority 11, a claim left at `OPEN`, moved to `SUSPENDED`, or `CLOSED` will refuse new transitional work with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_RTW_ELIGIBLE`.

Alongside the carrier's own flag, every claim carries a derived program posture in `rtw_posture`, recomputed on every placement lifecycle or compliance movement, every panel status change, every restriction activation or expiry, every wage-ledger posting, and every clock advance. The posture lands on `FROZEN` when the claim's `claim_status` is `SUSPENDED`, or when any case-review panel attached to any of the claim's placements sits in `OPEN` or `VOTING`. Otherwise it lands on `RESTRICTED` when some placement of the claim is in `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED` while its `compliance_status` is `WATCH` or `NONCOMPLIANT`, or when the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` has reached 90 days or more. Failing both, the posture is `OPEN`. Placements whose compliance dimension reads `COMPLIANT` or `REMEDIATED` never raise the posture on their own. A `FROZEN` posture bars placement creation by either route, adjuster routing, activation, extension and resumption, each answering `ERR_CLAIM_POSTURE_FROZEN`; a `RESTRICTED` posture additionally bars only the two operations that mint brand-new transitional work — explicit placement creation and selector-driven matching — answering `ERR_CLAIM_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. Reading a claim recomputes `rtw_posture`, `cumulative_transitional_days` and `wage_differential_paid_cents` before the response is produced, and a worker's claim listing may be narrowed with a `claim_status` filter.

### 3.2. Restriction Intake from the Treating Provider

Clinicians are registered by a program administrator with their `full_name`, their `npi` National Provider Identifier — exactly ten characters, every one of them a digit, anything else being a priority-3 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — their clinical `specialty`, and optionally an initial `credential_status`, which defaults to `PROVISIONAL` and may otherwise be `VERIFIED` or `REVOKED`. Only the administrator may later move a clinician's `credential_status`, and only a clinician standing at `VERIFIED` may originate or submit restrictions; a filing, submission or verification touching a clinician who is `PROVISIONAL` or `REVOKED` is refused at priority 17 with `ERR_PROVIDER_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` and HTTP 422.

A restriction set is filed underneath a claim by the treating clinician (or, administratively, by a program administrator) and names the authoring clinician through `treating_provider_id` while inheriting `claim_id` from the claim in whose collection it was filed. The clinical content is a window and six limits: `effective_from` and `valid_through` bound the period during which the restrictions apply, with `valid_through` inclusive and never earlier than `effective_from`; `max_lift_lbs` is the greatest permitted lift in pounds and may not be negative; `max_continuous_standing_minutes` is the greatest permitted continuous standing time and may not be negative; `max_hours_per_day` is the daily work ceiling and must be greater than zero and no more than 12; `keyboard_minutes_per_hour` is the greatest permitted keyboarding time inside any hour, from 0 to 60 inclusive; and the two booleans `overhead_reach_allowed` and `driving_allowed` say whether overhead reaching and vehicle operation are permitted at all. The filing also carries `expected_claim_version` as described in §2.7. A newly filed set lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `clarification_note` null, `clarification_round_count` at zero, `supersedes_restriction_set_id` null, and `active_placement_count` at zero — that last counter reporting how many placements built on this set sit in `PROPOSED`, `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED`. Filing also counts as a mutation of the parent claim: the claim's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` rises by 1, so a second filing against the same claim must quote that raised number in its `expected_claim_version`.

Every restriction set publishes a `severity_index` between 0 and 100, where 0 means clinically unrestricted and 100 means maximally restricted. It is computed when the set is filed, again when the nurse verifies it, and again whenever any of the six limit fields is written. The index is derived from a capability contribution assembled from five weighted terms. The lift term takes the lesser of `max_lift_lbs` and the 50-pound reference, divides it by 50, scales the result to a percentage, and weights it at 0.40. The standing term takes the lesser of `max_continuous_standing_minutes` and the 120-minute reference, divides it by 120, scales it to a percentage, and weights it at 0.20. The hours term takes the lesser of `max_hours_per_day` and the eight-hour reference, divides it by 8, scales it to a percentage, and weights it at 0.25. The overhead term contributes a full 100 when `overhead_reach_allowed` is true and nothing when it is false, weighted at 0.10. The driving term contributes a full 100 when `driving_allowed` is true and nothing when it is false, weighted at 0.05. The five weighted terms are added together, that contribution is subtracted from 100, and the difference is rounded half up to a whole number and clamped into the range from 0 to 100. Nothing outside the restriction set's own six limit fields enters this computation.

Intake is a conversation, not a one-shot submission, and the set's `status` records where that conversation stands. A clinician submits a set that is in `DRAFT`, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`; the same submission action also re-submits a set that is in `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, moving it `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. Both submissions require that the acting clinician, when the caller's role is `TreatingProvider`, be the set's own `treating_provider_id`, and that that clinician's `credential_status` be `VERIFIED`; both raise the set's `version` by 1. A nurse — or a coordinator — may bounce a submitted set back to the author, moving it `SUBMITTED → CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED` while writing the supplied `note` into `clarification_note`, raising `clarification_round_count` by 1 and raising `version` by 1. The `SUBMITTED → CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` loop may run any number of rounds; each round leaves its mark in the round counter. A nurse verifies a submitted set, moving it `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`, which requires the authoring clinician still to be `VERIFIED`, recomputes `severity_index`, and raises `version` by 1.

Activation is where the intake becomes operationally binding, and it is the most consequential cascade in the program. A coordinator or nurse activates a set that is in `VERIFIED`, moving it `VERIFIED → ACTIVE`; the set's `valid_through` must be on or after the logical clock's calendar date, or the request is refused at priority 18 with `ERR_RESTRICTION_WINDOW_INVALID` and HTTP 422. The cascade then runs in five levels. At the first level the set becomes `ACTIVE` and the claim's `active_restriction_set_id` is pointed at it. At the second level, caused by the first, the claim's previously `ACTIVE` set moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` — clearing the claim's `active_restriction_set_id` before the new set writes its own id there — and the new set's `supersedes_restriction_set_id` is stamped with the displaced set's id. At the third level, caused by the second, every placement built on the displaced set whose `placement_status` is `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD` is re-checked against the new set's limits, and any placement whose transitional job now breaches a limit is moved to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `RESTRICTION_CONFLICT`, lowering that job's `filled_seats` by 1. At the fourth level, caused by the third, each placement suspended in this way has its progression review whose `review_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_REVIEW` moved to `ESCALATED`, and a case-review panel is created for it with `review_status` `OPEN`, `trigger_reason` `RESTRICTION_CONFLICT`, `quorum_required` 3, and `opened_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`. At the fifth level, caused by the fourth, the claim's `rtw_posture` is recomputed and necessarily lands on `FROZEN`, because an open panel now exists. Both the activated set and the superseded set have their `version` raised by 1.

Restriction sets also lapse on their own. When the logical clock advances past a set's `valid_through` while that set is `ACTIVE`, it moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, the owning claim's `active_restriction_set_id` is cleared, the claim's `rtw_posture` is recomputed, and the set's `version` rises by 1; the ids of every set expired by that advance come back in `expired_restriction_set_ids`. `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal: any transition request aimed at a set in either state answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Restriction sets can be listed under their claim or across the whole program, and the program-wide listing may be narrowed by `claim_id`, `treating_provider_id`, `supersedes_restriction_set_id` and `status`.

### 3.3. Supervisor Readiness and the Transitional Job Bank

Transitional work only exists where a trained supervisor is willing to host it, so supervisors are first-class records. A program administrator registers a `Supervisor` with a `full_name` and the `department_code` they oversee; the new record starts with `oversight_status` `ACTIVE` and `training_status` `NONE`. Two counters track accountability. `compliance_strike_points` totals the strike weight of every monitoring observation ever filed against a placement this supervisor hosts, where a finding of `NONE` or `MINOR` is worth no points, a `MAJOR` finding is worth 1 point, and a `CRITICAL` finding is worth 2 points. `strike_forgiveness_points` is a watermark, starting at zero, that is only ever set by the remediation path described below. `effective_strike_points` is the greater of zero and the difference between `compliance_strike_points` and `strike_forgiveness_points`, and it is the figure the suspension threshold actually reads. Reading a supervisor recomputes `training_status`, `compliance_strike_points` and `effective_strike_points` before responding.

Curriculum completion is recorded as a `TrainingRecord` underneath the supervisor, naming that supervisor through `supervisor_id`, the `curriculum_code` — one of `RTW_CORE`, `RESTRICTION_HANDLING` or `REINJURY_RESPONSE` — the `completed_on` date, and the inclusive `valid_through` date on which the certification lapses. A new record starts in `record_status` `VALID` with `renewal_count` at zero; neither date is screened against the other or against the clock, so a record whose `valid_through` already lies in the past is still admitted as `VALID` and only lapses when the next clock advance sweeps it. Filing a record has a displacement effect: any earlier record of the same supervisor with the same `curriculum_code` that is not already `SUPERSEDED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` — `VALID → SUPERSEDED`, `EXPIRING_SOON → SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED → SUPERSEDED` according to where it stood, the supervisor's `training_status` is recomputed, and the supervisor's `version` rises by 1. Training records can be listed under their supervisor and narrowed by `curriculum_code` and `record_status`, or read individually.

A supervisor's `training_status` is a projection over `RTW_CORE` records only — records certifying `RESTRICTION_HANDLING` or `REINJURY_RESPONSE` are recorded and expire like any other but never move the supervisor's readiness. The pool consists of every `RTW_CORE` record of the supervisor whose `record_status` is not `SUPERSEDED`. If that pool is empty, `training_status` is `NONE`. Otherwise the governing record is the one in the pool with the greatest `valid_through`, and when two share the greatest `valid_through` the one with the earlier `created_at` governs — never the one with a particular `id` — and the supervisor's `training_status` is simply that governing record's `record_status`, so it reads `VALID`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `EXPIRED`. This projection is refreshed whenever a record is filed or renewed, on every clock advance, and on every read of the supervisor.

Certifications age under the logical clock. A record standing at `VALID` moves `VALID → EXPIRING_SOON` when the clock lands inside the warning window, meaning the number of whole days from the clock's calendar date to `valid_through` is at least zero and no more than 30; the ids of records warned by that advance come back in `warned_training_record_ids`, and the supervisor's `training_status` is refreshed. A record whose `record_status` is `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON` moves to `EXPIRED` — `EXPIRING_SOON → EXPIRED`, and `VALID → EXPIRED` when the warning window was skipped over — when the clock's calendar date passes `valid_through`, refreshing the supervisor's `training_status` to `EXPIRED` and returning the record's id in `expired_training_record_ids`. Both movements raise the record's `version` by 1.

Renewal is the compensating action, and it is the only path out of `EXPIRED`. The caller supplies `new_valid_through`, which must be strictly later than the clock's calendar date — a bound enforced as request-shape validation at priority 3, so it answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the addressed record's own existence is resolved; the record's `record_status` returns to `VALID` from `VALID`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `EXPIRED` alike, so `EXPIRED → VALID` and `EXPIRING_SOON → VALID` are both legal renewals, `valid_through` is overwritten with the supplied date, `renewal_count` rises by 1, the supervisor's `training_status` is recomputed, and the record's `version` rises by 1. Renewal carries one further effect that only fires under a specific pairing: when the renewed record's `curriculum_code` is `RTW_CORE` **and** the supervisor's `oversight_status` is `SUSPENDED`, the supervisor's `strike_forgiveness_points` is set equal to their current `compliance_strike_points`, `oversight_status` flips back to `ACTIVE`, and every transitional job of that supervisor sitting at `PAUSED` flips back to `OPEN`. Renewing a `RESTRICTION_HANDLING` or `REINJURY_RESPONSE` record, or renewing anything while the supervisor is not suspended, does none of that. `SUPERSEDED` is the record's only terminal state.

The transitional job bank is maintained by administrators and coordinators. A `TransitionalJob` names its accountable `supervisor_id`, a `title`, the `department_code` it sits in, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1 giving the maximum number of concurrent occupying placements, and an `hourly_rate_cents` of at least 1 cent giving the transitional wage rate. Its physical profile mirrors the restriction vocabulary: `demand_lift_lbs` and `demand_standing_minutes` may not be negative, `demand_hours_per_day` must be greater than zero and no more than 12, `demand_keyboard_minutes_per_hour` runs from 0 to 60 inclusive, and the two optional booleans `requires_overhead_reach` and `requires_driving` each default to false. An optional `risk_class` records the ergonomic band as `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, defaulting to `LOW`. A new entry starts with `job_status` `OPEN` and `filled_seats` at zero; thereafter `filled_seats` counts the placements on that job whose `placement_status` is `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD`, and is recomputed before any read of the job. Availability and capacity can later be adjusted by writing `job_status` — `OPEN`, `PAUSED` or `RETIRED` — or `seat_capacity`, each raising the job's `version` by 1; both fields are optional on that update and a request supplying neither is accepted, still raising `version` by 1. Only an `OPEN` entry is bookable: naming a `PAUSED` or `RETIRED` job on an explicit placement creation is refused at priority 30 with `ERR_JOB_NOT_OPEN` and HTTP 409, and non-`OPEN` entries are silently invisible to the selector. The bank listing may be narrowed by `supervisor_id`, `department_code`, `job_status` and `risk_class`.

### 3.4. Matching a Worker to Transitional Work

A coordinator creates a transitional placement in one of two ways: by naming a job explicitly, or by asking the server's selector to choose one. Both routes take the same core inputs — `restriction_set_id` identifying the clinical picture being matched, `start_date` and `end_date` bounding the assignment inclusively, `scheduled_hours_per_day` giving the daily hours the worker will be rostered onto the job (greater than zero and no more than 12), and `expected_restriction_set_version` for the concurrency guard of §2.7. The explicit route additionally takes `transitional_job_id`; the selector route instead takes an optional `department_code` to confine the search and an optional `exclude_job_ids` list of jobs the coordinator has already ruled out. Whichever route is used, the new placement copies `claim_id` from the referenced restriction set and `supervisor_id` from the chosen job — that supervisor pointer is denormalized once at creation and is never re-pointed afterwards, even if the job's supervisor later changes — and stamps `created_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`. The row lands with `placement_status` `PROPOSED`, `compliance_status` `COMPLIANT`, `reinjury_risk_variant` `BASELINE`, `progression_step` at zero, `logged_hours` an empty array, and `activated_on`, `ended_on`, `suspension_reason` and `decline_reason` all null. Creation computes `match_score`, the `BASELINE` `reinjury_risk_score`, the premium split of §3.6 and `projected_weekly_cost_cents`, raises the referenced set's `active_placement_count`, and raises that set's `version` by 1.

**Fit is scored, not merely permitted.** The `match_score` measures how much clinical headroom a job leaves against a restriction set. For each of four dimensions the headroom is the limit minus the demand, divided by the limit, clamped into the range from 0 to 1 — and defined as zero outright whenever the limit is zero or negative, so a worker restricted to no lifting at all earns no lift headroom rather than an undefined one. The four pairings are `max_lift_lbs` against `demand_lift_lbs`, `max_continuous_standing_minutes` against `demand_standing_minutes`, `max_hours_per_day` against `demand_hours_per_day`, and `keyboard_minutes_per_hour` against `demand_keyboard_minutes_per_hour`. A fifth term reflects the job's ergonomic band: it is 1 minus the risk penalty for the job's `risk_class`, where the penalty is 0 for `LOW`, 0.35 for `MEDIUM` and 0.75 for `HIGH`. The five terms are combined with weights of 0.35 for lift, 0.20 for standing, 0.25 for hours, 0.10 for keyboarding and 0.10 for the risk term, and the weighted sum is scaled by 100 to give a raw score. That raw score is then damped by the clinical severity of the restriction set: the damping multiplier is the lesser of 1.0 and the quotient of the pivot 60 divided by the greater of the set's `severity_index` and 1, so a set at or below a severity index of 60 is undamped while a more severe set pulls every score down proportionally. The raw score multiplied by the damping multiplier is clamped into the range from 0 to 100 and then rounded half up to two decimal places — the clamp and the damping multiplication both precede that single rounding. Exactly one restriction set and one transitional job enter this computation, which is why the same job scores differently under two different restriction sets. The score is pinned at creation and recomputed only when a progression review advances the placement or when a panel closes on a modify outcome; both recomputations pair the transitional job with the restriction set pinned on the placement at creation rather than with the claim's currently `ACTIVE` set, and neither `scheduled_hours_per_day` nor `progression_step` enters the formula, so the recomputed figure moves only if the pinned set or the job itself changed.

**Risk is scored before anyone starts work.** A placement's `reinjury_risk_score` is an integer from 0 to 100. Its structural term begins from a base determined by the job's `risk_class` — 12 for `LOW`, 28 for `MEDIUM`, 46 for `HIGH` — adds the restriction set's `severity_index` multiplied by 0.45, and adds 22.0 multiplied by a demand-pressure factor. That demand-pressure factor is the largest of four ratios, each the job's demand divided by the corresponding restriction limit: `demand_lift_lbs` over `max_lift_lbs`, `demand_standing_minutes` over `max_continuous_standing_minutes`, `demand_hours_per_day` over `max_hours_per_day`, and `demand_keyboard_minutes_per_hour` over `keyboard_minutes_per_hour`; the largest of the four is then clamped into the range from 0 to 1.5. A ratio whose restriction limit is zero or negative is not divided at all: it reads 1.5 when the corresponding demand is positive and 0 when the demand is zero. At creation the placement is scored under the `BASELINE` variant, which consists of the structural term alone, evaluated against the restriction set named by the placement's own `restriction_set_id`; no monitoring observation and no reinjury report participates, because none exists yet. Whatever the variant, the result is clamped into the range from 0 to 100 and rounded half up to a whole number.

Creation is guarded by a long ladder of safety and calendar rules, and the order in which they fire is the order given in §2.6. Before them all, the referenced restriction set must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_RESTRICTION_SET_NOT_ACTIVE`), the owning claim must be `RTW_ELIGIBLE` (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_RTW_ELIGIBLE`), and the claim's posture must be neither `FROZEN` (`ERR_CLAIM_POSTURE_FROZEN`) nor `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_CLAIM_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`).

At priority 19 the hard restriction limits are enforced on explicit creation and again whenever an assignment is extended: `demand_lift_lbs` must not exceed `max_lift_lbs`, `demand_standing_minutes` must not exceed `max_continuous_standing_minutes`, `demand_hours_per_day` must not exceed `max_hours_per_day`, and `demand_keyboard_minutes_per_hour` must not exceed `keyboard_minutes_per_hour`; a job whose `requires_overhead_reach` is true is only acceptable when `overhead_reach_allowed` is true, and a job whose `requires_driving` is true is only acceptable when `driving_allowed` is true; and the requested `scheduled_hours_per_day` must not exceed `max_hours_per_day`. Any breach answers `ERR_RESTRICTION_VIOLATION` with HTTP 422. At priority 20, an explicit creation whose computed `match_score` falls below the eligibility floor of 40.0 is refused with `ERR_MATCH_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR` and HTTP 422 — a score exactly equal to 40.0 passes. At priority 21, an explicit creation whose `BASELINE` `reinjury_risk_score` reaches the escalation threshold of 70 or more is refused with `ERR_REINJURY_RISK_TOO_HIGH` and HTTP 422; only a score strictly below 70 is accepted.

Three calendar rules follow, and their interval conventions differ deliberately. At priority 25, the placement window must sit entirely inside the restriction window: `start_date` must be on or after the set's `effective_from` and `end_date` must be on or before the set's `valid_through`, or the request answers `ERR_PLACEMENT_OUTSIDE_RESTRICTION_WINDOW` with HTTP 422; this rule also guards extension. At priority 26, a worker may not be double-booked: treating each placement window as **half-open**, running from `start_date` up to but not including `end_date`, the proposed window must not intersect the window of any other placement of the same worker whose `placement_status` is `PROPOSED`, `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED`. Adjacency is explicitly legal here — a window beginning exactly on another's `end_date` does not collide, and a zero-day gap between two of a worker's assignments is fine. A collision answers `ERR_PLACEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` with HTTP 409, and the rule applies to both creation routes and to extension. At priority 27, the same job may not be handed from one occupant to the next without a changeover gap: treating each placement window on that job as the **closed** interval from `start_date` through `end_date` inclusive, a proposed window is rejected whenever the number of days from an existing occupant's `end_date` to the proposed `start_date` is at least zero and no more than the one-day changeover buffer, counting only occupants whose `placement_status` is `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD`. Unlike the worker rule, both endpoints are inclusive here and a zero-day gap is forbidden; the earliest legal successor start is two days after the outgoing occupant's `end_date`. A violation answers `ERR_JOB_CHANGEOVER_BUFFER` with HTTP 409, and this rule guards both creation routes.

Seat capacity is checked per day rather than in aggregate. At priority 29, for every calendar day inside the proposed closed window from `start_date` through `end_date`, the count of placements already on that job whose `placement_status` is `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD` and whose own window covers that day, plus one for the incoming placement, must not exceed the job's `seat_capacity`. Exceeding it on even a single day answers `ERR_JOB_SEAT_UNAVAILABLE` with HTTP 409. This rule guards both creation routes and also the resumption of a suspended assignment, which must re-take a seat it earlier released.

**The selector.** When a coordinator asks the server to choose the job, the candidate set is built by applying every one of the following predicates to the whole job bank: the job's `job_status` is `OPEN`; the job's supervisor has `oversight_status` `ACTIVE`; that supervisor's `training_status` is `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`; when a `department_code` was supplied, the job's `department_code` equals it; the job's id is not listed in `exclude_job_ids`; each of the job's four demand figures is no greater than the corresponding restriction limit; the restriction set permits overhead reaching or the job does not require it; the restriction set permits driving or the job does not require it; the requested window passes both the changeover-buffer rule of priority 27 and the seat-capacity rule of priority 29 against that job; the `match_score` of that pairing is at least the eligibility floor of 40.0; and the `BASELINE` `reinjury_risk_score` of that pairing is strictly below the escalation threshold of 70. That list is exhaustive: the selector route runs neither the restriction-window containment of priority 25 nor the `scheduled_hours_per_day` limb of priority 19, both of which guard only the explicit route. Surviving candidates are ranked by `match_score` from highest to lowest. Ties are broken first by ergonomic band, preferring `LOW` over `MEDIUM` over `HIGH`; then by `hourly_rate_cents` from highest to lowest, because a higher transitional wage yields a smaller wage differential; and finally by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. The single top-ranked job is taken and a placement is created against it with exactly the side effects of the explicit route. Should the candidate set be empty after filtering, no placement row is created at all and the request answers `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_JOB` with HTTP 422 at priority 31.

Placements can be read individually — a read recomputes `cumulative_days`, `benefit_continuation_status` and `projected_weekly_cost_cents` first — listed under their claim with an optional `placement_status` filter, or listed program-wide with optional `claim_id`, `restriction_set_id`, `transitional_job_id`, `supervisor_id`, `placement_status` and `compliance_status` filters.

### 3.5. Funding the Assignment: Adjuster Directives and Spend Ceilings

No transitional assignment starts on the employer's word alone; the carrier pays, so the carrier decides. A coordinator routes a proposed placement to the adjuster, advancing it `PROPOSED → ADJUSTER_PENDING`. Routing requires the placement to be in `PROPOSED`, its restriction set to still be `ACTIVE`, and the claim's posture not to be `FROZEN`. The routing mints an `AdjusterDirective` underneath the placement with `directive_status` `PENDING`, `authorized_weekly_cost_cents` at zero, `condition_note`, `denial_reason`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` all null, and `requested_weekly_cost_cents` copied from the placement's `projected_weekly_cost_cents` at that moment. The new directive's id is written into the placement's `current_adjuster_directive_id`, the chosen job's `filled_seats` rises by 1 — the seat is taken at routing time, not at activation — and the placement's `version` rises by 1. A placement's directives may be listed with an optional `directive_status` filter.

The figure the adjuster is asked to fund, `projected_weekly_cost_cents`, is recomputed on placement creation, at routing time when it is copied into the directive, when a progression review advances the placement, and when a panel closes on a modify outcome. It is the sum of three separately rounded addends. The first is the transitional wage: the job's `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by the placement's `scheduled_hours_per_day` multiplied by the five workdays in a week, rounded half up to whole cents. The second is the wage differential the employer expects to owe: take the worker's `pre_injury_weekly_wage_cents`, subtract that same weekly transitional wage figure — the job's `hourly_rate_cents` times `scheduled_hours_per_day` times five — floor the difference at zero so a transitional job that outpays the pre-injury wage never produces a negative differential, multiply by the statutory replacement rate of 0.6667, and round half up to whole cents. The third is the benefit share: the placement's `employer_premium_share_cents` divided by the four premium weeks in a month, rounded half up to whole cents. Each addend is rounded before they are added, and the three rounded addends are then summed exactly.

The adjuster who owns the claim — the operator whose `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `adjuster_user_id` — decides the directive by supplying a `decision`, which is `AUTHORIZED`, `AUTHORIZED_WITH_CONDITIONS` or `DENIED`. A caller holding the `ClaimAdjuster` role whose `X-User-ID` is some other operator is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403 at priority 6, ahead of every state screen. The `authorized_weekly_cost_cents` figure need only be an integer; no lower bound is imposed on it beyond the claim ceiling of priority 40. The directive must still be `PENDING`, or the request answers `ERR_DIRECTIVE_ALREADY_DECIDED` at priority 12. The three branches write disjoint effects and carry different input requirements. An `AUTHORIZED` decision moves the directive `PENDING → AUTHORIZED` and requires `authorized_weekly_cost_cents`, the weekly spend the carrier will fund. An `AUTHORIZED_WITH_CONDITIONS` decision moves it `PENDING → AUTHORIZED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, requires `authorized_weekly_cost_cents` in exactly the same way, and additionally requires a non-empty `condition_note` recording the strings attached; absent that note the request is a priority-3 shape failure answering `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `DENIED` decision moves it `PENDING → DENIED` and requires a non-empty `denial_reason`; it forces `authorized_weekly_cost_cents` to zero and writes the reason. All three branches stamp `decided_by_user_id` with the deciding operator's `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` with the logical clock instant, and raise the directive's `version` by 1. A denial deliberately leaves the placement sitting at `ADJUSTER_PENDING` rather than ending it, so the coordinator may re-route the case or terminate the placement outright.

Two ceilings bound the money, and both raise `ERR_ADJUSTER_AUTHORIZATION_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 422 at priority 40. On a decision, the `authorized_weekly_cost_cents` the adjuster grants may not exceed the claim's `max_authorized_weekly_cost_cents`; a grant exactly equal to the ceiling is allowed. On activation and on extension, the placement's `projected_weekly_cost_cents` may not exceed the directive's `authorized_weekly_cost_cents`; again, exact equality passes. This is the mechanism by which a progression advance that raises the weekly cost can render an already-authorized placement unfundable.

Activation reads the directive twice, and the two reads produce different codes. At priority 38 the placement must have a current directive at all and that directive must no longer be `PENDING`; a missing directive or a still-pending one answers `ERR_ADJUSTER_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING` with HTTP 409. A `DENIED` directive passes that first check and is caught by the second: at priority 39 the current directive's `directive_status` must be `AUTHORIZED` or `AUTHORIZED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, and a denial answers `ERR_ADJUSTER_DIRECTIVE_DENIED` with HTTP 409.

Directives do not linger past their placement. Whenever a placement reaches `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED_EARLY` or `DECLINED` by any route, every directive of that placement whose `directive_status` is `PENDING`, `AUTHORIZED` or `AUTHORIZED_WITH_CONDITIONS` moves to `SUPERSEDED` — `PENDING → SUPERSEDED`, `AUTHORIZED → SUPERSEDED` and `AUTHORIZED_WITH_CONDITIONS → SUPERSEDED` alike — each such move raising that directive's `version` by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is the directive's terminal state, and a decision aimed at one answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.6. Running the Assignment: Activation, Daily Hours, Interruption and Closure

Activation is the moment the worker actually reports to modified duty, and it is gated on the readiness of everyone involved. The placement must be sitting at `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, its restriction set must still be `ACTIVE`, its current directive must be `AUTHORIZED` or `AUTHORIZED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, the claim's posture must not be `FROZEN`, and the placement's `projected_weekly_cost_cents` must not exceed the directive's `authorized_weekly_cost_cents`. Two of the gates are about the host rather than the worker: the placement's supervisor must have `oversight_status` `ACTIVE`, or the request answers `ERR_SUPERVISOR_OVERSIGHT_SUSPENDED` with HTTP 409 at priority 23; and that supervisor's `training_status` must be `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, or the request answers `ERR_SUPERVISOR_TRAINING_EXPIRED` with HTTP 409 at priority 24. A supervisor whose training reads `EXPIRED` or `NONE` may not host a live assignment — but one whose certification is merely inside its 30-day warning window may, which is exactly why `EXPIRING_SOON` exists as a distinct state. When all gates pass, the placement advances `ADJUSTER_PENDING → ACTIVE`, `activated_on` is set to the placement's `start_date`, the benefit premium split and `benefit_continuation_status` are computed, the first progression review is created with `step_index` 1, `review_status` `SCHEDULED` and a `due_date` 30 days after `start_date`, that review's id is written into `current_progression_review_id`, the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` and `rtw_posture` are recomputed, and the placement's `version` rises by 1.

**Benefit continuation.** While a worker is on modified duty the employer continues their health coverage, and the split between employer and worker is recomputed on activation and on every subsequent placement status change. The evaluation order below is normative, and in particular the subsidy tier is keyed on the *provisional* employer share, never re-keyed on the final one. First, the monthly premium total is read from the worker's `benefit_tier`: 68000 cents for `TIER_A`, 52000 cents for `TIER_B`, 41000 cents for `TIER_C`. Second, a provisional employee contribution is that total multiplied by the base contribution rate of 0.28, rounded half up to whole cents. Third, the provisional employer share is the total minus that provisional employee contribution, computed as an exact integer subtraction. Fourth, the subsidy factor is chosen from the provisional employer share: 0.90 when that provisional employer share is at most 38000 cents, otherwise 0.70 when it is at most 55000 cents, otherwise 0.55. Fifth, `employee_premium_share_cents` is the provisional employee contribution multiplied by that subsidy factor, rounded half up to whole cents. Sixth, `employer_premium_share_cents` is the monthly premium total minus `employee_premium_share_cents`, again an exact integer subtraction. Only the second and fifth steps round. The accompanying `benefit_continuation_status` reads `LAPSED` when `placement_status` is `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED_EARLY` or `DECLINED`; `PENDING` when it is `PROPOSED` or `ADJUSTER_PENDING`; `EMPLOYER_SUBSIDIZED` when it is `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED` and the worker's `benefit_tier` is `TIER_A` or `TIER_B`; and `EMPLOYEE_PAID` when it is `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED` and the worker's `benefit_tier` is `TIER_C`.

**Daily hours.** The hosting supervisor — or a coordinator or administrator acting for them — logs what the worker actually worked, one calendar day at a time, supplying `work_date` and `hours_worked`. Each submission upserts a `{ work_date, hours_worked }` object into the placement's `logged_hours` array keyed on `work_date`, so a second submission for the same day overwrites the first rather than appending a duplicate, and the array is kept sorted by `work_date` ascending. Logging raises the placement's `version` by 1. At priority 22, the `hours_worked` figure may not exceed the `max_hours_per_day` published by the claim's *currently* `ACTIVE` restriction set — not the set pinned on the placement at creation — and an excess answers `ERR_HOURS_EXCEED_RESTRICTION` with HTTP 422; when the claim has no `ACTIVE` set at all that ceiling is simply not applied. `hours_worked` must be a number of at least zero, and logging is screened on neither the placement's lifecycle state nor its compliance state — even a terminal placement still accepts a log.

**Interruption.** A coordinator or nurse may pause a live assignment, moving it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and supplying a `suspension_reason` of `RESTRICTION_CONFLICT`, `MEDICAL_HOLD` or `OPERATIONAL`. Suspension lowers the job's `filled_seats` by 1, recomputes the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` and `rtw_posture`, and raises the placement's `version` by 1. The same `SUSPENDED` state is reached automatically, with `suspension_reason` `RESTRICTION_CONFLICT`, by the restriction-activation cascade of §3.2. A coordinator returns a paused assignment to work by moving it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, which requires the restriction set to still be `ACTIVE`, the supervisor to have `oversight_status` `ACTIVE` and `training_status` of `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, the claim posture not to be `FROZEN`, and the job to still have seat capacity across the placement's window under the per-day rule of priority 29. Resuming clears `suspension_reason`, raises the job's `filled_seats` by 1, and raises the placement's `version` by 1. The `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` cycle may run repeatedly.

**The duration ledger.** How long a worker has spent on modified duty is the program's central compliance number, and it is recomputed on every placement status transition, on every extension, and on every clock advance. For a single placement, the effective through-date is `ended_on` when `placement_status` is `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED_EARLY`; the logical clock's calendar date when it is `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED`; and undefined when it is `PROPOSED`, `ADJUSTER_PENDING` or `DECLINED`. The placement's `cumulative_days` is zero when `activated_on` is null or the effective through-date is undefined; otherwise it is the number of days from `activated_on` to the earlier of the effective through-date and the placement's `end_date`, counting both endpoints inclusively — that is, the day count plus one — and floored at zero. The claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` is the sum of `cumulative_days` over every placement of that claim whose `placement_status` is `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED_EARLY`; placements in `PROPOSED`, `ADJUSTER_PENDING` or `DECLINED` contribute nothing. The sum is order-independent, so no tie-break applies.

**Extension.** A coordinator may push a live assignment's finish line out by supplying a `new_end_date` and a `reason`. The projected cumulative duration is worked out as the claim's current `cumulative_transitional_days`, minus this placement's present `cumulative_days`, plus the inclusive day count from this placement's `start_date` through the proposed `new_end_date` floored at zero. Two caps read that projection. At priority 36, the projection must stay strictly below the hard cap of 180 days; reaching 180 or beyond answers `ERR_DURATION_HARD_CAP` with HTTP 422 and there is no override. At priority 37, the projection must stay strictly below the soft cap of 90 days **unless** the placement has a case-review panel sitting in `DECIDED_CONTINUE`; failing both answers `ERR_DURATION_SOFT_CAP_REQUIRES_CASE_REVIEW` with HTTP 409. In other words, running a worker past 90 cumulative transitional days is possible, but only after a panel has convened and voted to continue. Extension is also re-screened against the hard restriction limits of priority 19, the restriction-window containment of priority 25, the worker-level overlap rule of priority 26, the claim's posture at priority 15, and the directive ceiling of priority 40. Priority 19 and priority 25 read the restriction set pinned on the placement rather than the claim's currently `ACTIVE` one, and priority 40 is skipped outright when the placement has no current directive. Beyond the terminal lockdown of priority 8 no origin-state screen applies, so a `PROPOSED`, `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED` assignment may be extended exactly as a live one may, and `new_end_date` is required only to parse as a calendar date — it need not fall later than the present `end_date`, and neither the seat-capacity rule of priority 29 nor the changeover rule of priority 27 is re-run over the lengthened window. A successful extension writes the new `end_date`, recomputes `cumulative_days`, the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` and `rtw_posture`, and raises the placement's `version` by 1.

**Closure.** A coordinator closes a successful assignment, moving it `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`. Closure sets `ended_on` to the logical clock's calendar date, lowers the job's `filled_seats` by 1, moves every live directive of the placement to `SUPERSEDED`, recomputes `benefit_continuation_status` to `LAPSED`, recomputes the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` and `rtw_posture`, and raises the placement's `version` by 1.

Ending an assignment early is one operation with two destinations, and the branch is decided by the state the placement is in when the request arrives. The caller supplies a `reason`, which is required but is persisted only on the `DECLINED` branch; a placement ending at `TERMINATED_EARLY` records no reason anywhere. When the placement is `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD` or `SUSPENDED`, it lands on `TERMINATED_EARLY` — that is, `ACTIVE → TERMINATED_EARLY`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD → TERMINATED_EARLY` or `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED_EARLY`: `ended_on` is set to the clock's calendar date, the job's `filled_seats` is lowered by 1 when the placement was holding a seat — that is, when it was `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD` — every live directive moves to `SUPERSEDED`, `benefit_continuation_status` becomes `LAPSED`, the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` and `rtw_posture` are recomputed, and `version` rises by 1. When instead the placement is `PROPOSED` or `ADJUSTER_PENDING`, it lands on `DECLINED` — that is, `PROPOSED → DECLINED` or `ADJUSTER_PENDING → DECLINED`: the supplied `reason` is copied into `decline_reason`, the job's `filled_seats` is lowered by 1 only when the placement was `ADJUSTER_PENDING` and therefore already holding a seat, every live directive moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and `version` rises by 1. A `DECLINED` placement never accrues duration and never sets `ended_on`. All three of `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED_EARLY` and `DECLINED` are terminal: any further lifecycle request against such a placement answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.7. The Progression Ladder

Modified duty is meant to be a ramp back to full duty, not a parking space, so every live assignment carries a standing appointment to reassess it. A `ProgressionReview` belongs to a placement through `placement_id`, carries a 1-based `step_index` giving its ordinal on that placement's ladder, a `due_date`, a `review_status` and a `deferral_count` that starts at zero. The first review is created automatically at activation with `step_index` 1 and a `due_date` 30 days after the placement's `start_date`. Reviews start `SCHEDULED` with `reviewed_by_user_id`, `outcome_note`, `target_hours_per_day` and `target_lift_lbs` all null; the two target fields stay null until a decision is recorded. A placement's reviews may be listed with an optional `review_status` filter, or read individually.

A coordinator or nurse opens a scheduled review, moving it `SCHEDULED → IN_REVIEW`. Opening requires the review to be `SCHEDULED` and the owning placement to be in `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD`, a placement standing anywhere else answering `ERR_INVALID_STATE` with HTTP 409; it stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID` and raises the review's `version` by 1.

The outcome is then recorded with a `decision` of `ADVANCE`, `EXTEND`, `ESCALATE` or `DEFER`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `outcome_note`. All four branches require the review to be `IN_REVIEW`, and all four raise the review's `version` by 1, but they diverge sharply after that. Two of them share a common bar: at priority 43, an `ADVANCE` or an `EXTEND` is refused with `ERR_CASE_REVIEW_IN_PROGRESS` and HTTP 409 whenever any case-review panel of that placement sits in `OPEN` or `VOTING` — the ladder does not move while a panel is deliberating. `ESCALATE` and `DEFER` are not subject to that bar.

An **`ADVANCE`** moves the review `IN_REVIEW → ADVANCED` and steps the worker up. It additionally requires that the placement's `progression_step` be strictly below the ceiling of four steps, or the request answers `ERR_MAX_PROGRESSION_STEPS` with HTTP 422 at priority 32. The new targets are computed as follows. `target_hours_per_day` is the least of three quantities: the placement's current `scheduled_hours_per_day` plus the one-hour progression step; the `max_hours_per_day` published by the claim's currently `ACTIVE` restriction set; and the worker's `pre_injury_weekly_hours` divided by the five workdays in a week. `target_lift_lbs` is the lesser of two quantities: the job's `demand_lift_lbs` increased by 25 percent — that is, multiplied by 1.25 and rounded half up to two decimal places — and the same active set's `max_lift_lbs`. Both targets are expressed to two decimal places, rounded half up, and where the claim has no `ACTIVE` restriction set at the moment of the decision both fall back to the set pinned on the placement. The placement's `scheduled_hours_per_day` is then set to `target_hours_per_day`, its `progression_step` rises by 1, and its `match_score` and `projected_weekly_cost_cents` are recomputed against the new schedule. Finally the next rung is forked: a new review is created with `step_index` one greater than the review just decided, `review_status` `SCHEDULED`, and a `due_date` 30 days after the logical clock's calendar date.

An **`EXTEND`** moves the review `IN_REVIEW → EXTENDED` and means "hold the worker at the present level for another cycle". It changes neither `scheduled_hours_per_day` nor `progression_step` and computes no targets, but it does fork the next rung on exactly the same terms as an advance: a new review with `step_index` one greater than this one, `review_status` `SCHEDULED`, and a `due_date` 30 days after the clock's calendar date.

An **`ESCALATE`** moves the review `IN_REVIEW → ESCALATED` and hands the case to the panel. It moves the placement `ACTIVE → PROGRESSION_HOLD` — a move that fires only when the placement is standing at `ACTIVE`, leaving it exactly where it is from any other state — creates a case-review panel with `review_status` `OPEN`, `trigger_reason` `DURATION_CAP`, `quorum_required` 3 and `opened_by_user_id` set to the deciding caller's `X-User-ID` rather than to the literal `system`, minting that panel unconditionally even when one of the placement's panels is already `OPEN` or `VOTING`, and recomputes the claim's `rtw_posture`, which necessarily lands on `FROZEN` because an open panel now exists. No follow-up review is forked; the panel decides what happens next.

A **`DEFER`** is the one branch that is not terminal: it moves the review `IN_REVIEW → SCHEDULED`, raising `deferral_count` by 1, pushing `due_date` out by a further 14 days, and clearing `reviewed_by_user_id` so the next opener is recorded afresh. The `IN_REVIEW → SCHEDULED → IN_REVIEW` cycle is bounded: at priority 33 a deferral is refused with `ERR_MAX_DEFERRALS_REACHED` and HTTP 422 once the review's `deferral_count` has reached the ceiling of two. `ADVANCED`, `EXTENDED` and `ESCALATED` are terminal states of a review, and a further decision or open aimed at such a review answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

When a placement moves to `PROGRESSION_HOLD` — whether from an escalating review or from a triaged reinjury — a panel is opened for it, the escalating-review route minting one unconditionally and the reinjury-triage route only when none is already `OPEN` or `VOTING`; the panel's id is written into the placement's `open_case_review_id`, the claim's `rtw_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN`, and the placement's `version` rises by 1. A placement on hold is not dead: a panel closing on a continue outcome returns it `PROGRESSION_HOLD → ACTIVE`, clearing `open_case_review_id`, recomputing the claim's posture and raising the placement's `version` by 1; a panel closing on a terminate outcome carries it `PROGRESSION_HOLD → TERMINATED_EARLY` with the full closure side effects described in §3.6.

### 3.8. Workplace Monitoring, Supervisor Accountability, and Reinjury

Monitoring observations are filed against a placement by the hosting supervisor, a coordinator, a nurse or an administrator, and they are append-only: a `ComplianceAudit` carries only an `id` and a `created_at`, never an `updated_at` or a `version`. Each observation names the `observed_on` calendar date of the workplace visit, a `finding_severity` of `NONE`, `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, and a `finding_code` naming the dimension observed — `RESTRICTION_DEVIATION`, `HOURS_DEVIATION`, `SUPERVISION_GAP`, `DOCUMENTATION` or `NONE`. Two optional inputs complete it: `restriction_deviation_detected`, a boolean defaulting to false that records whether the observed work actually exceeded the active restriction limits, and a free-text `corrective_action_note` defaulting to null. The filer's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `auditor_user_id`. Whether or not the filing moves the compliance dimension, the call answers HTTP 201 carrying the new observation rather than the placement it may have moved. A placement's observations may be listed with an optional `finding_severity` filter.

Filing an observation moves the placement's `compliance_status` along its own cycle, entirely independently of `placement_status`. From `COMPLIANT`, a finding of `MINOR` or `MAJOR` moves the placement `COMPLIANT → WATCH`, while a finding of `CRITICAL` moves it straight `COMPLIANT → NONCOMPLIANT`. From `WATCH`, a finding of `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` moves it `WATCH → NONCOMPLIANT`. A finding of `NONE` is the way back: from `NONCOMPLIANT` it moves the placement `NONCOMPLIANT → REMEDIATED`, from `REMEDIATED` it moves it `REMEDIATED → COMPLIANT`, and from `WATCH` it moves it `WATCH → COMPLIANT` directly. Every one of these six movements raises the placement's `version` by 1. Any other pairing of current status and finding — a `NONE` filed against a `COMPLIANT` placement, a `MINOR` filed against one in `WATCH`, anything but `NONE` filed against one already in `NONCOMPLIANT` — is a silent no-op: the observation is still recorded and answered with HTTP 201, but `compliance_status` stays put and the placement's `version` does not move. Filing is screened on nothing about the placement's lifecycle state either, so even a terminal placement accepts observations. A move into `WATCH` recomputes the supervisor's `compliance_strike_points` and `effective_strike_points`, recomputes the placement's `reinjury_risk_score` under the `SURVEILLANCE` variant, and recomputes the claim's `rtw_posture`, which lands on `RESTRICTED`. The two recovery moves out of `NONCOMPLIANT` and `WATCH` recompute the claim's `rtw_posture`; the move `REMEDIATED → COMPLIANT` specifically returns the posture to `OPEN` when no other placement of that claim is `WATCH` or `NONCOMPLIANT`, no panel of that claim is `OPEN` or `VOTING`, and the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` is still below 90.

A move into `NONCOMPLIANT` — reached from either `WATCH` or `COMPLIANT` — runs an identical four-level cascade in both cases. The first level sets the placement's `compliance_status` to `NONCOMPLIANT`. The second, caused by the first, recomputes the hosting supervisor's `compliance_strike_points` and, when their `effective_strike_points` has reached the suspension threshold of 2 points or more, flips that supervisor's `oversight_status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`. The third, caused by the second, flips every transitional job of that supervisor whose `job_status` is `OPEN` to `PAUSED`, and flips every *other* placement of that supervisor whose `placement_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD` and whose `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` to `WATCH`. The fourth, caused by the third, recomputes `rtw_posture` for every claim owning a placement that was flipped to `WATCH` in the third level, each of which lands on `RESTRICTED`. A suspended supervisor is thereby removed from the bank and from new activations at once, and the only way back is the `RTW_CORE` renewal described in §3.3, which sets the forgiveness watermark, restores `oversight_status` to `ACTIVE` and returns their `PAUSED` jobs to `OPEN`.

Reinjuries and near-misses are reported against a placement by the supervisor, a nurse, a coordinator or the worker themselves. A `ReinjuryReport` names the `occurred_on` date, a `severity` of `NEAR_MISS`, `MINOR`, `SIGNIFICANT` or `SEVERE`, a coded `body_part_code` and a coded `mechanism_code`, and stamps the reporter's `X-User-ID` into `reported_by_user_id`. It lands with `report_status` `SUBMITTED`, `triage_note` null and `risk_points_applied` at zero — a submitted report contributes no risk until a clinician has looked at it. A placement's reports may be listed with an optional `report_status` filter.

Only a nurse may triage, supplying a `triage_note`. The report must still be `SUBMITTED`; `TRIAGED` and `ESCALATED` are not terminal states of a report, so a second triage answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` with HTTP 409 rather than `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Triage writes `risk_points_applied` with the severity contribution this report makes to the surveillance risk score, recomputes the placement's `reinjury_risk_score` under the `SURVEILLANCE` variant, and raises the report's `version` by 1. The destination depends on that recomputed score: an ordinary triage moves the report `SUBMITTED → TRIAGED`, but a recomputed surveillance score that reaches the escalation threshold of 70 or more moves it `SUBMITTED → ESCALATED` instead and additionally carries the placement `ACTIVE → PROGRESSION_HOLD`, opens a case-review panel for it and freezes the claim's posture. Because `report_status` and `risk_points_applied` are both written before that recomputation, this very report's severity points are already inside the score that decides the branch. The escalation panel carries `trigger_reason` `REINJURY`, `quorum_required` 3 and `opened_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`, and is minted only when no panel of the placement is already `OPEN` or `VOTING`; the lifecycle move fires only when the placement is standing at `ACTIVE`, and either branch raises the placement's `version` by 1 because its `reinjury_risk_score` moved.

**The surveillance risk variant.** Once a placement is live, its `reinjury_risk_score` is recomputed on every monitoring observation, on every triage, and whenever a panel closes on a modify outcome, and `reinjury_risk_variant` records which variant produced the current figure. Under `SURVEILLANCE` the score is the structural term of §3.4 *plus* an observed term, and — importantly — the structural term is re-evaluated against the claim's **currently** `ACTIVE` restriction set rather than the set pinned on the placement at creation, so an intervening restriction revision moves the score even when no new observation was filed. The observed term has two summands. The first walks every monitoring observation of the placement, ordered by `observed_on` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, and for each one takes its severity points — 0 for `NONE`, 4 for `MINOR`, 11 for `MAJOR`, 25 for `CRITICAL` — multiplied by a decay factor. That decay factor is 1 minus one half for each whole 30-day block that has elapsed between the observation's `observed_on` and the logical clock's calendar date, where the elapsed day count is floored at zero before the block count is taken and the block count is itself rounded down; the resulting factor is clamped into the range from 0 to 1, so an observation more than 60 days old contributes nothing. The second summand walks every reinjury report of the placement whose `report_status` is `TRIAGED` or `ESCALATED` and adds its severity points — 6 for `NEAR_MISS`, 14 for `MINOR`, 26 for `SIGNIFICANT`, 40 for `SEVERE`. Reinjury points never decay. The structural term and the observed term are added, and the total is clamped into the range from 0 to 100 and rounded half up to a whole number.

### 3.9. The Case-Review Panel

When a transitional assignment stops being routine, a multi-disciplinary panel decides its fate. A `CaseReview` belongs to a placement through `placement_id` and records why it was convened in `trigger_reason`, which is one of `REINJURY`, `RESTRICTION_CONFLICT`, `DURATION_CAP`, `COMPLIANCE_CRITICAL` or `ADJUSTER_DENIAL`. A nurse, coordinator or administrator may convene one directly — nothing about the placement's own lifecycle or compliance state gates that, and a panel may be convened even while another of the placement's panels is `OPEN` or `VOTING` — and the cascades of §3.2, §3.7 and §3.8 convene them automatically. The optional `quorum_required` gives the minimum number of counted ballots the panel needs before it may close; it defaults to 3 and must be between 1 and 7 inclusive. The panel opens with `review_status` `OPEN`, `closed_by_user_id` null, and `opened_by_user_id` holding either the convening operator's `X-User-ID` or, for a panel opened by a cascade rather than a person, the literal string `system`. Convening a panel recomputes the claim's `rtw_posture` to `FROZEN`. Panels can be read individually — the read recomputes the tally first — or listed under their placement with an optional `review_status` filter.

Balloting is opened separately from convening: a nurse or administrator moves the panel `OPEN → VOTING`, raising its `version` by 1. Ballots may only be cast, and the panel may only be closed, while it is in `VOTING`; either request against a panel in any other state answers `ERR_CASE_REVIEW_NOT_VOTING` with HTTP 409 at priority 13.

A ballot is cast by a coordinator, nurse, adjuster, supervisor or treating clinician. It carries the member's `ballot` — `CONTINUE`, `MODIFY` or `TERMINATE` — an optional free-text `rationale`, and `expected_case_review_version` for the concurrency guard of §2.7. The stored `CaseReviewVote` is append-only, carrying only `id` and `created_at` with no `updated_at` and no `version`; it points at the panel it belongs to through `case_review_id`, taken from the panel in whose collection it was cast rather than from the payload, and it stamps `voter_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and copies the caller's `X-User-Role` into `voter_role`, which is accordingly one of `RTWCoordinator`, `OccHealthNurse`, `ClaimAdjuster`, `Supervisor` or `TreatingProvider`. New ballots land with `vote_status` `COUNTED`, and the cast answers HTTP 201 carrying the newly stored ballot rather than the panel. A member may change their mind: casting a second ballot flips that same member's prior `COUNTED` row to `SUPERSEDED`, so each member contributes at most one counted ballot. Casting recomputes the panel tally and raises the panel's `version` by 1. The segregation-of-duties bar of priority 7 applies here: a principal whose `X-User-ID` equals the placement's `created_by_user_id` may not vote at all, and the attempt answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403. A panel's ballots may be listed with an optional `vote_status` filter.

**The tally.** Recomputed on every ballot and again at closing, the tally considers only rows whose `vote_status` is `COUNTED`; `SUPERSEDED` rows are excluded from every count. `vote_count` is how many counted ballots exist, and `continue_votes`, `modify_votes` and `terminate_votes` are the counted ballots reading `CONTINUE`, `MODIFY` and `TERMINATE` respectively. The `winning_ballot` is the option attaining the greatest of those three counts; when two or three of them tie at that greatest count, the more conservative option wins, with `TERMINATE` beating `MODIFY` and `MODIFY` beating `CONTINUE`. The `decision_ratio` is that greatest count divided by `vote_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places. A panel with no counted ballots at all has a `decision_ratio` of 0 and a null `winning_ballot`.

Closing is performed by a nurse or administrator and requires four things at once: the panel must be in `VOTING`; its `vote_count` must be at least its `quorum_required`, or the request answers `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` with HTTP 409 at priority 34; its `decision_ratio` must be at least the majority bar of 0.6600, or the request answers `ERR_MAJORITY_NOT_REACHED` with HTTP 409 at priority 35 — a ratio exactly equal to 0.6600 passes; and the closing principal's `X-User-ID` must not be the `voter_user_id` of any counted ballot on the panel, or the request answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Every close stamps `closed_by_user_id`, recomputes the claim's `rtw_posture`, and raises the panel's `version` by 1.

Beyond that shared spine the three outcomes fork into disjoint effects, and the fork is decided by `winning_ballot`. A `CONTINUE` win moves the panel `VOTING → DECIDED_CONTINUE` and, when the placement is sitting on hold, returns it `PROGRESSION_HOLD → ACTIVE`; nothing about the schedule changes. A `MODIFY` win moves the panel `VOTING → DECIDED_MODIFY` and leaves `placement_status` exactly where it is, but cuts the workload: the placement's `scheduled_hours_per_day` becomes the greater of 1 and the current `scheduled_hours_per_day` multiplied by one half and rounded down to a whole number, after which the placement's `match_score`, `projected_weekly_cost_cents` and `SURVEILLANCE` `reinjury_risk_score` are all recomputed, and a follow-up review is forked with `review_status` `SCHEDULED`, `step_index` one greater than the greatest `step_index` among that placement's existing reviews, and a `due_date` 14 days after the logical clock's calendar date. A `TERMINATE` win moves the panel `VOTING → DECIDED_TERMINATE` and ends the assignment: whatever lifecycle state the placement was standing in, it moves to `TERMINATED_EARLY` with `ended_on` set to the clock's calendar date, the job's `filled_seats` drops by 1, every live directive of the placement flips to `SUPERSEDED`, `benefit_continuation_status` becomes `LAPSED`, and the claim's `cumulative_transitional_days` and `rtw_posture` are recomputed. All three decided states — `DECIDED_CONTINUE`, `DECIDED_MODIFY` and `DECIDED_TERMINATE` — are terminal for the panel, and any further balloting or closing aimed at one answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.10. The Wage-Differential Ledger and Program Outcomes

A worker on modified duty usually earns less than they did before the injury, and the employer owes a statutory share of that gap. Each payment is a `WageDifferentialEntry` posted underneath a placement by an administrator or coordinator, naming the pay period through `period_start` and `period_end` — both inclusive — and the algorithm to apply through `variant`, which is either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`. The row inherits `placement_id` from the placement it was posted against and `claim_id` copied from that placement's own `claim_id`, lands with `entry_status` `POSTED` and `reverses_entry_id` null, and carries only an `id` and a `created_at` — no `updated_at`, no `version`. Posting recomputes the claim's `wage_differential_paid_cents` and raises the claim's `version` by 1. At priority 28, no other entry of the same placement **and the same variant** may already cover any day inside the requested period, treating both periods as closed intervals; an overlap answers `ERR_WAGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` with HTTP 409. A provisional accrual and a final reconciliation covering the very same days are therefore both permitted, which is the point of running two variants. Nothing else gates a posting: neither the placement's lifecycle state nor the claim's posture nor the claim's `claim_status` is screened.

Both variants share a common notion of the daily gap. The worker's pre-injury daily wage is `pre_injury_weekly_wage_cents` divided by the five workdays in a week, held as an exact rational value and never rounded at this step. For a given number of hours, the day's gap is that pre-injury daily wage minus the job's `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by those hours, floored at zero.

A **`PROVISIONAL`** entry is forward-looking and rounds exactly once, at the very end of the summation. Its `counted_day_count` is the number of calendar days in the period from `period_start` through `period_end` whose weekday falls Monday through Friday; the placement's `logged_hours` is ignored entirely. The pre-cap gross is that day count, multiplied by the statutory replacement rate of 0.6667, multiplied by the day gap evaluated at the placement's `scheduled_hours_per_day`, the whole product rounded half up to whole cents in that single operation. Its `tier_multiplier` is pinned at 1.00 because the cumulative duration is not yet known, and its `offset_cents` is zero.

A **`FINAL`** entry is a reconciliation against what was actually worked, and it rounds once per day *before* summing. Its `counted_day_count` is the number of entries in the placement's `logged_hours` whose `work_date` falls inside the period, walked in `work_date` ascending order. For each such entry, the statutory replacement rate of 0.6667 is multiplied by the day gap evaluated at that entry's `hours_worked` and the product is rounded half up to whole cents; those per-day amounts are then added together. The duration tier is then applied: the elapsed week count is the number of days from the placement's `start_date` to `period_start` divided by seven and rounded down, plus one. A placement in its first eight elapsed weeks takes the tier-one multiplier of 1.00; through the sixteenth elapsed week it takes the tier-two multiplier of 0.90; beyond that it takes the tier-three multiplier of 0.80. The summed per-day total multiplied by that `tier_multiplier` is rounded half up to whole cents to give the pre-cap gross. A final entry also withholds a benefit offset: when the placement's `benefit_continuation_status` is `EMPLOYER_SUBSIDIZED`, `offset_cents` is `gross_differential_cents` multiplied by the benefit offset rate of 0.05, rounded half up to whole cents; in every other benefit state the offset is zero.

Both variants then run the same tail, in this exact order. The period's week count is `counted_day_count` divided by the five workdays in a week and rounded **up** to a whole number. `gross_differential_cents` is the lesser of the pre-cap gross and the weekly cap of 90000 cents multiplied by that week count. `net_amount_cents` is `gross_differential_cents` minus `offset_cents`. Note that the offset is computed against the capped `gross_differential_cents`, not against the pre-cap figure.

Postings are undone by compensation rather than by deletion, and only an administrator may do it. Reversing an entry requires that entry still to be `POSTED`, or the request answers `ERR_WAGE_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED` at priority 14. The caller supplies a `reason`; the original row's `entry_status` flips to `REVERSED`, and a new row is minted with `entry_status` `REVERSAL`, `reverses_entry_id` pointing at the original, the same `placement_id` and `claim_id`, and `net_amount_cents` equal to the negation of the original's. That new row copies the original's `variant`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `counted_day_count`, `tier_multiplier`, `gross_differential_cents` and `offset_cents` unchanged — only `net_amount_cents` is negated — and the call answers HTTP 201 carrying that reversal row rather than the original. The claim's `wage_differential_paid_cents` — which sums `net_amount_cents` over every entry of the claim regardless of `entry_status` — is recomputed and returns to its pre-posting value, and the claim's `version` rises by 1. The ledger may be read one row at a time or listed with optional `placement_id`, `claim_id`, `reverses_entry_id`, `variant` and `entry_status` filters.

Finally, the program reports on itself. An administrator, coordinator or adjuster may pull outcome metrics over a cohort defined by activation date, optionally bounded by `window_start` and `window_end` and optionally confined to a single `department_code`. When omitted, `window_start` defaults to `1970-01-01` and `window_end` to `2999-12-31`. The cohort consists of every placement whose `placement_status` is `ACTIVE`, `PROGRESSION_HOLD`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED_EARLY`, whose `activated_on` is not null, whose `activated_on` falls inside the window with both endpoints inclusive, and — when a department was named — whose transitional job's `department_code` matches it. From that cohort, `total_placements` is the cohort size; `active_placements` counts those in `ACTIVE` or `PROGRESSION_HOLD`; `completed_count` counts those in `COMPLETED`; `reinjury_count` counts cohort placements having at least one reinjury report whose `report_status` is `TRIAGED` or `ESCALATED`; and a compliant count tallies those whose `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` or `REMEDIATED` — that tally is an intermediate of `compliance_rate` alone and is not itself a field of the response, which carries exactly `total_placements`, `active_placements`, `completed_count`, `successful_return_rate`, `average_duration_days`, `reinjury_count`, `reinjury_rate`, `compliance_rate` and `total_wage_differential_cents`. When the cohort is empty, `successful_return_rate`, `average_duration_days`, `reinjury_rate` and `compliance_rate` are all reported as 0.0. Otherwise `successful_return_rate` is `completed_count` divided by `total_placements`, `reinjury_rate` is `reinjury_count` divided by `total_placements`, and `compliance_rate` is the compliant count divided by `total_placements`, each rounded half up to four decimal places; `average_duration_days` is the sum of `cumulative_days` across the cohort divided by `total_placements`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Independently of the rate arithmetic, `total_wage_differential_cents` sums `net_amount_cents` over every wage-differential entry belonging to a cohort placement, regardless of `entry_status`, so reversed pairs cancel there too.

A request naming no route at all answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 404, and one naming a known path with an unsupported method answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405 — the two places where that identifier is not paired with HTTP 400.
