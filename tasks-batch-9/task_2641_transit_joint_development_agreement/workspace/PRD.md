# Transit Joint Development Agreement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A transit agency's real estate office sits on an awkward asset: land that was bought to run buses and trains, that is often worth far more as housing or retail than as surface parking, and that in many cases was bought with federal grant money the grantor has never stopped having an interest in. Turning such a parcel into a joint development project is not one decision but a chain of them — inventory the parcel, assess whether it is genuinely developable, put it to the market, score and rank what the market offers, take the winner off the market for an exclusive dealing period, appraise the land, negotiate a ground lease or an outright sale, get the board to approve it, get the federal grantor to concur in the disposition, replace whatever transit facility the project displaces, and then live alongside the developer for decades sharing plazas, bus bays and elevator banks. Today that chain lives in spreadsheets, email threads and a shared drive, and the office cannot answer basic questions such as how much of a site's federal interest is already pledged to a pending disposition request, or whether the number in the draft agreement clears the floor the appraiser certified.

This service is the system of record for that chain. It holds the agency-owned site inventory and its readiness scoring, the solicitations and the proposals they attract, the negotiated exclusive dealing periods, the fair-market-value determinations and the permissible below-market transfer floors that flow from them, the joint development agreements themselves, the federal disposition filings, the transit facility replacement commitments, and the ongoing shared-facility and construction-staging coordination that follows execution. Every API surface described here is mounted under the `/jda` path prefix, except the administrative logical-clock control described in the endpoint table.

Two properties matter more than any single feature. The first is **auditability through segregation of duties**: drafting, appraisal, board approval, federal compliance and transit operations are five separate duties held by five separate roles, and the system must be able to prove after the fact that the operator who put an agreement in front of the board was not the operator who recorded the board's approval. The second is **conservation of the federal interest**: the money the federal grantor has an interest in must never be pledged twice, so every filing that encumbers a slice of a site's federal interest and every withdrawal or rejection that releases it must move the same number of cents in opposite directions, leaving the ledger provably intact.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and the Duty Boundary

Every request carries two authentication headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the calling operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the duty the caller is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before anything else in the request pipeline.

The seven roles are `RealEstateOfficer`, `ValuationOfficer`, `BoardSecretary`, `GrantsComplianceOfficer`, `TransitOperationsManager`, `Developer` and `SystemAdmin`.

A `RealEstateOfficer` is agency real estate office staff: they own the site inventory, readiness assessments, solicitations, developer registration, proposal shortlisting, awards, exclusive negotiation periods, and the drafting, submission and execution of agreements. A `ValuationOfficer` is independent appraisal staff who record fair market value and certify the permissible transfer floor; they may not draft or approve the agreement that leans on their valuation. A `BoardSecretary` is the board clerk who records the board's revision requests and approvals. A `GrantsComplianceOfficer` files federal disposition requests and records the grantor's determination or withdrawal. A `TransitOperationsManager` owns transit facility replacement, shared facility registration, staging window decisions and maintenance cost allocation. A `Developer` is a registered development partner. A `SystemAdmin` operates the deterministic logical clock that every deadline and expiry rule reads. All seven roles may call every read endpoint.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** operator identity. When `X-User-Role` is `RealEstateOfficer`, `ValuationOfficer`, `BoardSecretary`, `GrantsComplianceOfficer`, `TransitOperationsManager` or `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on an agreement when a real estate officer submits that agreement for review — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

`Developer` is the one **entity-backed** role. When `X-User-Role` is `Developer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting registered `Developer` record, and ownership checks on any resource that references a developer compare that resource's developer reference to `X-User-ID`. Three operations enforce this. When a developer submits a proposal, the `developer_id` in the payload must equal `X-User-ID`. When a caller holding the `Developer` role reads a single proposal, that proposal's `developer_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. When a developer requests a construction staging window, the referenced agreement's `developer_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. A failure of any of the three is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Note that these ownership rules bind only callers acting under the `Developer` role; the five agency roles read every proposal freely.

Segregation of duties goes one step beyond role separation. Recording board approval of an agreement requires the `BoardSecretary` role, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` the agreement recorded when it was submitted for review. A single human being may legitimately hold both the real estate officer duty and the board secretary duty, so role separation alone is not enough; when the two identities coincide the approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1 and may not exceed 100; a value outside that inclusive range is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). `offset` defaults to 0 and counts records skipped from the head of the ordered result. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

Unless a specific list states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Five lists override that default in service of how the office actually reads them. The site inventory list is ordered by `site_code` ascending, and accepts optional `status`, `tier` and `acquisition_funding_source` filters. The readiness assessment list for a site is ordered by `created_at` descending with `id` ascending as the tie-break, so the newest assessment reads first, and accepts an optional `status` filter. The cross-solicitation proposal list is ordered by `rank` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and accepts optional `solicitation_id`, `developer_id` and `status` filters. The exclusive negotiation period list is ordered by `start_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and accepts optional `site_id`, `proposal_id` and `status` filters. The staging window list is ordered by `start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and accepts optional `jda_id`, `shared_facility_id` and `status` filters.

Other filtered lists follow the same pattern with their own parameters: the per-site solicitation list and the per-site valuation list each accept an optional `status`; the per-agreement facility replacement list accepts an optional `status`; the agreement list accepts optional `site_id`, `developer_id`, `proposal_id`, `valuation_id`, `negotiation_status` and `federal_approval_status` filters; the per-site shared-facility list, the per-agreement disposition-request list, the per-agreement maintenance-charge list and the per-facility maintenance-charge list accept only `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Immutable Event Records

Every persisted record carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional digits. Twelve of the thirteen record types additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. Whenever a rule in this document says a record's version is bumped, `updated_at` is refreshed to the same instant.

`MaintenanceCharge` is the single exception. A maintenance charge is an immutable event record: once posted it is never amended, there is no update operation for it, and it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and callers must not expect either field on it.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Deadlines, expiries and every `*_at` timestamp derive from a **logical clock** rather than the host wall clock, so that a test or a rehearsal can drive time forward deterministically. Before the clock is ever set, the logical current UTC instant is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `SystemAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new `current_utc` together with `expired_negotiation_period_ids`, the list of exclusive negotiation period identifiers that the move expired.

Moving the clock is not a passive act. Every exclusive negotiation period whose status is `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` and whose `end_date` falls strictly before the calendar date of the new instant becomes `EXPIRED`; the proposal each newly expired period was holding drops from `SELECTED` to `REJECTED`; and every site left with no `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` period and no agreement in `EXECUTED` returns from `UNDER_AGREEMENT` to `SOLICITATION_READY`, with its `version` bumped by 1. Comparisons against `end_date` are made on calendar dates, so a period ending on a given day survives every instant of that day and expires only once the clock's date is strictly greater.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and Pinned Evaluation Order

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in whole cents; field names ending in `_cents` are always integer cents, never decimal currency. Wherever a money figure is produced by multiplying by a rate or dividing by a term, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent, and the rounding is applied exactly once at the point the calculation names — never a second time downstream. Score-valued quantities that are not money — the readiness composite score, the proposal evaluation score, the affordable percentage — are rounded half up to **two decimal places**.

Three rounding orders in this domain are deliberately pinned, because the natural but wrong order produces a different answer from identical inputs, and the wrong answer is the one an implementation drifts into:

- In the readiness composite score, the weighted sum and both penalties are computed on unrounded operands and the subtraction is rounded exactly once at the end.
- In the annual revenue to the agency, the capital credit tier is keyed on the **pre-credit subtotal**, never on the post-credit total the calculation eventually produces.
- In maintenance cost allocation, each line item is rounded to whole cents **first** and the rounded per-line amounts are then summed. Summing the raw line costs and rounding once at the end is explicitly wrong here and can differ by several cents.

### 2.7. The Federal Interest Ledger

A parcel acquired with federal grant assistance carries a federal interest — a percentage of the parcel's value in which the grantor retains a claim — and the agency may not pledge the same cents of that claim to two different dispositions. Each site therefore keeps a three-field ledger. `federal_interest_basis_cents` is the total claim in cents, pinned from the most recent certified appraisal. `federal_interest_encumbered_cents` is the portion already pledged to federal disposition requests that are live, meaning requests whose `determination` is `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`; requests that were rejected or withdrawn contribute nothing. `federal_interest_unencumbered_cents` is the basis less the encumbered portion.

The invariant is that the encumbered portion plus the unencumbered portion always equals the basis, after every single write. Filing a disposition request moves cents from unencumbered to encumbered; rejecting or withdrawing one moves exactly the same cents back. A site with no certified valuation yet has a basis of 0, and a site created with a `federal_interest_pct` of 0 has a basis of 0 forever, which is why such an agreement never needs federal concurrence at all.

A second, structurally identical conservation rule governs construction staging. A shared facility's `available_bay_capacity` plus the sum of `bays_occupied` across that facility's approved staging windows always equals its `total_bay_capacity`. Approval consumes bays; closing or suspending an approved window returns them, exactly once.

### 2.8. Parallel State Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

Most records here advance along a single lifecycle. A joint development agreement is different: it carries **two independent state dimensions on the same record**. `negotiation_status` tracks the commercial negotiation and moves through `DRAFTING`, `AGENCY_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `BOARD_APPROVED` and `EXECUTED` under the real estate office and the board. `federal_approval_status` tracks the grantor's concurrence and moves through `NOT_REQUIRED`, `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `SUBMITTED`, `FTA_APPROVED` and `FTA_REJECTED` under grants compliance. The two dimensions advance under different roles through different operations, and recording a grantor approval leaves `negotiation_status` completely untouched. Only one linkage exists in the approval direction and one in the rejection direction, and both are spelled out in §3.8.

State changes cascade, and the cascades are multi-hop. The design rule throughout is that a cascade fires because of the state change immediately above it, not because the originating endpoint chose to fire it: awarding a solicitation selects a proposal, which creates an exclusive negotiation period, which moves the site under agreement, which cancels the site's other issued solicitations, which rejects those solicitations' live proposals. Each hop refreshes the `updated_at` of the record it touches and bumps that record's `version` by 1. Two cascades are **conditional** — the site returns to `SOLICITATION_READY` only if no other live negotiation period remains, and a suspended staging window returns its bays only if they have not already been returned — and a conditional cascade that does not fire changes nothing at all.

### 2.9. Nested Path Resolution

Several paths nest one resource inside another for readability, but ownership in this domain is a graph rather than a tree: a proposal joins a solicitation to a developer, an exclusive negotiation period joins a site to a proposal, an agreement joins four parents at once (a site, a developer, a proposal and a valuation), and a staging window joins an agreement to a shared facility that belongs to the site rather than to the agreement.

Path segments are resolved strictly **left to right**, in this order: site, then assessment, then solicitation, then proposal, then negotiation period, then valuation, then agreement, then disposition request, then facility replacement, then shared facility, then staging window. The first segment that names a record which does not exist decides the response, with its own not-found code: `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPOSITION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_REPLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHARED_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_STAGING_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, all at HTTP 404. Because resolution is left to right, an unknown site combined with an unknown assessment reports the site.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after every path segment has been resolved, and among themselves in body-field declaration order: `site_id`, then `developer_id`, then `proposal_id`, then `valuation_id`, then `jda_id`, then `shared_facility_id`. An unknown body reference reports `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVELOPER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SHARED_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` respectively. Six operations carry body references and therefore run this check: proposal submission, direct negotiation-period creation, solicitation award, agreement drafting, staging-window request, and maintenance-charge posting.

### 2.10. Stale-Read Protection on Sites and Shared Facilities

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent record's `version`, because in both cases the client must have re-read the parent's computed state before writing.

Recording a fair market value determination for a site (`POST /jda/sites/{id}/valuations`) requires `expected_site_version` as a mandatory field in the request body, carrying the `version` of the `AgencySite` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the site's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new valuation legitimately supersedes the site's previously certified valuation, so a create against a freshly re-read site would succeed on its own; the point of the check is to force the valuation officer to re-read the site — and therefore its current federal interest basis and readiness tier — before pricing the parcel again.

Requesting a construction staging window (`POST /jda/staging-windows`) requires `expected_facility_version` as a mandatory field in the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `SharedFacility` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the facility's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Another window's approval or closure will have changed the facility's remaining capacity, and a request placed against a freshly re-read facility would succeed on its own; the check forces the developer to re-read `available_bay_capacity` rather than book against a figure that no longer holds.

No other operation in this API takes a version field. In particular, revising an agreement, deciding a staging window, closing one, or posting a maintenance charge carry no expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When a request would fail more than one rule, the checks are evaluated in a strict, fixed order and the **first** failing check decides the response. Nothing later in the order can mask something earlier. The order is:

1. Authentication — headers present and role recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorisation for the endpoint (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`).
3. Request shape — required fields present, types correct, enum values inside their declared sets, numeric ranges honoured, structure-conditional money fields supplied, and `limit` inside 1 through 100 (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).
4. Existence of every path-segment record, left to right (§2.9).
5. Existence of every body foreign key, in declaration order (§2.9).
6. Developer resource ownership (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`).
7. Optimistic concurrency on the parent version (`ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION`).
8. Segregation of duties on board approval (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
9. Lifecycle-state checks on the records involved, in the order of the state rules listed in §3 — site state, then site readiness, then the agreement's site precondition, then assessment, solicitation, proposal, negotiation-period, agreement and staging-window states, then facility-replacement transition legality.
10. Business safety checks — developer debarment, then the affordable-housing minimum, then the joint-use commitment.
11. Referential checks — valuation certification status, then valuation/site alignment, then negotiation-period liveness, then facility-replacement completeness, then facility-replacement deficiency.
12. Temporal checks — interval well-formedness, then the solicitation response deadline, then the staging-window envelope.
13. Remaining business constraints — duplicate site code, negotiation-period overlap, extension ceiling, staging capacity, responsive-proposal minimum, rank deviation justification, and the non-empty line-items rule.
14. Fiscal checks last — sufficient unencumbered federal interest, then the affordable-commitment shortfall, then the permissible-floor test.

Three precedence pairs are worth calling out explicitly because both members can fail on the same request. On agreement drafting, a site that is not `UNDER_AGREEMENT` reports `ERR_SITE_NOT_UNDER_AGREEMENT` even when the named developer is also debarred. On submitting an agreement for review, an uncertified valuation reports `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_CERTIFIED` even when the consideration would also fall below the permissible floor. On requesting a staging window, an interval outside the permitted envelope reports `ERR_STAGING_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM` even when the same interval would also exceed the facility's bay capacity.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Parcel into Inventory and Proving It Developable

A `RealEstateOfficer` opens the story by adding an agency-owned parcel to the joint development inventory. The payload names the parcel with a `site_code` of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across the whole inventory — reusing an existing code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE` — plus a human-readable `name`, an `address_line`, and a `parcel_area_sqft` land area of at least 1 square foot. Two fields describe the parcel's funding history and drive everything federal that follows: `acquisition_funding_source` is one of `FEDERAL_GRANT`, `LOCAL_ONLY` or `MIXED`, and `federal_interest_pct` is the percentage of the parcel's value in which the grantor retains an interest, between 0 and 100 inclusive. A parcel bought entirely with local money cannot carry a federal claim, so when `acquisition_funding_source` is `LOCAL_ONLY` the `federal_interest_pct` must be exactly 0; any other value is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The optional `transit_facility_present` flag, false when omitted, marks a parcel on which an operating transit facility currently sits and which therefore must be replaced before the parcel can be conveyed.

A new site opens in `INVENTORIED` with `readiness_composite_score` and `readiness_tier` both null, all three federal-interest ledger fields at 0, and `version` at 1. The `status` of a site moves through `INVENTORIED`, `ASSESSED`, `SOLICITATION_READY`, `UNDER_AGREEMENT` and finally `CONVEYED`, and only the last of those is terminal.

Readiness is established by assessment. A `RealEstateOfficer` records a **development readiness assessment** against the site — the payload is taken as belonging to the parcel named in the path, so the assessment's `site_id` is never supplied in the body — carrying four sub-scores each between 0 and 100 inclusive: `environmental_score` for environmental clearance, `utility_score` for utility availability, `access_score` for street and pedestrian access, and `entitlement_score` for local land-use entitlement. An `encumbrance_count` of at least 0 records how many easements, licences and leases burden the parcel, and an optional `assessment_notes` free-text field defaults to the empty string. A newly recorded assessment lands in `DRAFT` with `certified_at` null — that instant stays null until certification stamps it — and creating it bumps the parent site's `version` by 1 even though nothing about the site itself changed.

The **composite score** of an assessment is computed twice from the same stored inputs, under two different variants, and the variant that produced the number currently on the record is reported in `variant_applied`. While the assessment is a `DRAFT`, `variant_applied` is `PRELIMINARY` and `composite_score` is simply the unweighted arithmetic mean of the four sub-scores — their sum divided by four, rounded half up to two decimal places. The preliminary variant is an intake screen: it ignores `encumbrance_count` and the site's `federal_interest_pct` entirely, and it leaves `tier` null.

Certification is where the real number appears. When a `RealEstateOfficer` certifies a draft assessment, the score is recomputed under the `CERTIFIED` variant, `variant_applied` is set to `CERTIFIED`, and the result can be materially lower than the preliminary figure the officer has been looking at. The certified variant weights the four sub-scores rather than averaging them: the environmental score counts for thirty-five hundredths of a point per point scored, the utility score and the access score each for twenty-five hundredths, and the entitlement score for fifteen hundredths. From that weighted sum two penalties are subtracted. The **encumbrance penalty** is three points for each recorded encumbrance, but is never allowed to exceed twelve points in total, so a parcel with four encumbrances and a parcel with forty are penalised identically. The **federal interest penalty** is one tenth of a point for each whole percent of `federal_interest_pct` on the parcel. The weighted sum and both penalties are computed on unrounded operands; the two subtractions happen first, the result is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once, and if that would be negative the score is recorded as 0.00 instead. To make the divergence concrete: sub-scores of 80, 75, 70 and 60 with one encumbrance on a parcel whose federal interest is two and a half percent yield a preliminary score of 71.25 and a certified score of 70.00 from identical stored inputs.

The certified score bands into a `tier`. A score of at least 70.00 is `READY`; a score of at least 50.00 but below 70.00 is `CONDITIONAL`; anything below 50.00 is `NOT_READY`. The `READY` boundary is inclusive on the lower side, so exactly 70.00 qualifies and 69.99 does not. While an assessment remains a `DRAFT`, `tier` stays null.

Certification is refused unless the assessment is in `DRAFT` — an already certified or superseded assessment reports `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_DRAFT` — and unless the parent site's status is `INVENTORIED` or `ASSESSED`, any other site status reporting `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`. On success, `certified_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, the assessment moves `DRAFT → CERTIFIED`, and four things happen elsewhere. Every **other** assessment of the same site that was `CERTIFIED` moves `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED`, each with its own `version` bumped by 1, so a site never has two certified assessments at once. The site's `readiness_composite_score` and `readiness_tier` are refreshed to the certifying assessment's `composite_score` and `tier` — those two site fields are always a copy of the most recently certified assessment, and are null whenever no certified assessment exists. A site sitting in `INVENTORIED` moves `INVENTORIED → ASSESSED`; a site already in `ASSESSED` stays there, so re-certifying is idempotent with respect to site status. And the site's `version` is bumped by 1.

Only an assessed and genuinely ready parcel goes to market. Releasing a site for solicitation advances it `ASSESSED → SOLICITATION_READY` and bumps its `version` by 1, and is refused in two ways: a site whose status is not `ASSESSED` reports `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`, and a site whose `readiness_tier` is anything other than `READY` reports `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY`.

The inventory cycle can be run backwards. Reopening inventory takes a site whose status is `ASSESSED` or `SOLICITATION_READY` back to `INVENTORIED`, and the request must carry a `reason`. It is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE` if the site is in any other status, and also refused with `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE` if any solicitation on the site is currently `ISSUED` — the market has already been told about this parcel and cannot be un-told by an inventory edit. On success the site's `readiness_composite_score` and `readiness_tier` are cleared back to null, every `CERTIFIED` assessment on the site moves `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED`, every `DRAFT` solicitation on the site moves `DRAFT → CANCELLED`, and the site's `version` is bumped by 1. Because the copied readiness fields are gone, leaving `INVENTORIED` a second time requires a fresh assessment to be recorded and certified — the cycle `INVENTORIED → ASSESSED → INVENTORIED` closes cleanly and no shortcut exists.

Reads of the inventory expose everything computed above: the per-site record reports its readiness score, tier and the three federal-interest ledger fields, and the assessment record reports its `composite_score`, `variant_applied`, `tier`, `status` and `certified_at`.

### 3.2. Appraisal, the Federal Interest Basis and the Permissible Transfer Floor

Valuation is an independent duty. A `ValuationOfficer` — never the drafting officer — records a **fair market value determination** against a site, naming the `method` of record as one of `INCOME_APPROACH`, `SALES_COMPARISON` or `COST_APPROACH`, the `appraised_value_cents` of at least 1 cent, and an `effective_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The determination's `site_id` comes from the path rather than the body. Two assumptions the appraisal made about the project are recorded alongside the value, each a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive: `transit_supportive_use_pct`, the share of project floor area devoted to transit-supportive uses, and `affordable_units_pct`, the affordable share of units the appraisal assumed. Finally, `transfer_basis` declares whether the transfer must recover full market value (`FMV_STRICT`) or may go below market because the use qualifies as transit-supportive (`TRANSIT_SUPPORTIVE_BELOW_MARKET`). The request must also carry `expected_site_version` as described in §2.10.

From those inputs the system derives the **permissible transfer floor**: the minimum consideration the agency is allowed to accept. It is built in four steps, and both `transfer_basis` variants run the first three identically.

First, a transit-supportive credit rate is looked up from the floor-area share. A `transit_supportive_use_pct` of at least 75 earns a rate of thirty hundredths; at least 50 but below 75 earns twenty hundredths; at least 25 but below 50 earns ten hundredths; anything below 25 earns nothing at all. Second, an affordable-housing bonus of five hundredths is added to that rate whenever `affordable_units_pct` is at least 20, and the resulting rate is then capped at thirty hundredths — so a project already in the top tier gains nothing from the affordable bonus. Third, `transit_supportive_credit_cents` is the appraised value multiplied by that final rate, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `federal_interest_floor_cents` is the appraised value multiplied by the site's `federal_interest_pct` and divided by 100, again rounded half up to a whole cent. Both of those fields are recorded on the determination under **both** variants; only the fourth step differs between them.

Fourth, the floor itself. Under `FMV_STRICT` the grantor has not concurred in any below-market transfer, so `permissible_floor_cents` is the full `appraised_value_cents` and the credit computed in step three is recorded but never applied. Under `TRANSIT_SUPPORTIVE_BELOW_MARKET` the floor is the appraised value less the transit-supportive credit, but never below the federal interest floor — whichever of those two figures is larger wins. Consider a parcel appraised at 48,500,000 cents with sixty percent transit-supportive floor area, twenty-five percent affordable units and a federal interest of two and a half percent: the credit rate is twenty hundredths plus the five-hundredths affordable bonus, the credit is 12,125,000 cents, and the floor is 36,375,000 cents under the below-market basis but the untouched 48,500,000 cents under the strict basis.

Recording a determination also refreshes the site's federal interest ledger. The site's `federal_interest_basis_cents` is set to the appraised value multiplied by `federal_interest_pct` and divided by 100, rounded half up, and `federal_interest_unencumbered_cents` is recomputed as that basis less whatever is already in `federal_interest_encumbered_cents`; the site's `version` is bumped by 1. A determination is created in `DRAFT` with `certified_at` null, and already carries its floor, which is what lets the office negotiate against a number before the appraisal is formally signed off.

Certification by a `ValuationOfficer` is the signature. It requires the determination to be in `DRAFT` — a determination already `CERTIFIED` or `SUPERSEDED` reports `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and requires the parent site's status to be `ASSESSED`, `SOLICITATION_READY` or `UNDER_AGREEMENT`, any other status reporting `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`. On success the determination moves `DRAFT → CERTIFIED` with `certified_at` stamped from the logical clock, the floor is re-evaluated and comes out unchanged, every other `CERTIFIED` determination on the same site moves `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED` with its `version` bumped by 1, and the site's `federal_interest_basis_cents` and `federal_interest_unencumbered_cents` are pinned from this appraisal with the site's `version` bumped by 1. Only a `CERTIFIED` determination may back an agreement that goes to review, which is why the basis figure the federal ledger works from is always the one the most recent certification pinned.

### 3.3. Registering Developers, Taking a Parcel to Market and Receiving Proposals

Before anyone can respond to a solicitation they must exist in the system. A `RealEstateOfficer` registers a development partner with a `legal_name` and a `contact_email`, plus two optional postures that are recorded once at registration and are not editable afterwards: `prequalified`, false when omitted, records whether the partner has passed agency prequalification, and `debarment_status`, `CLEAR` when omitted, is one of `CLEAR`, `SUSPENDED` or `DEBARRED`. A registered developer starts at `version` 1. The consequences of both fields are felt later — a partner that is not prequalified pays a scoring penalty rather than being blocked, while a partner that is `SUSPENDED` or `DEBARRED` is blocked outright from submitting a proposal, from being awarded, and from being named on an agreement, each refusal reported as `ERR_DEVELOPER_DEBARRED`.

A solicitation is drafted against a site and inherits its `site_id` from the path. The officer picks a `solicitation_type` of `RFP`, `RFQ` or `UNSOLICITED_RESPONSE`, sets a `response_deadline` instant after which proposals are refused, and supplies a `benchmark_value_cents` of at least 1 cent, which becomes the denominator against which every proposal's offered value is scored. The `affordable_housing_min_pct`, between 0 and 100 inclusive, is the minimum affordable share of total units a responsive proposal must commit. Two optional fields shape how the solicitation closes and what the winning project owes the agency: `min_proposals_required`, at least 1 and defaulting to 1, is the count of live proposals needed before the solicitation may close, and `joint_use_required`, false when omitted, marks a solicitation whose winning project must dedicate joint-use space to the agency. A drafted solicitation lands in `DRAFT` with `issued_at` null and `responsive_proposal_count` at 0. Its `status` runs through `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `CLOSED`, `AWARDED` and `CANCELLED`, with `AWARDED` and `CANCELLED` both terminal.

Issuance is the moment the market is told, and it is gated three ways. A solicitation that is not in `DRAFT` reports `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_ISSUED`. A parent site whose status is not `SOLICITATION_READY` reports `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE` — the readiness gate of §3.1 is enforced here, at issuance, not at drafting. And a `response_deadline` that is not strictly after the current logical clock instant reports `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`, because a solicitation cannot be issued already closed. On success the solicitation moves `DRAFT → ISSUED` and `issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

A caller holding the `Developer` role responds by submitting a proposal to an issued solicitation; the proposal's `solicitation_id` comes from the path. The payload names the responding `developer_id`, which must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a `proposed_structure` of either `GROUND_LEASE` or `FEE_DISPOSITION`. Both money fields default to 0 and neither may be negative, but the structure decides which one is mandatory: a `GROUND_LEASE` proposal must carry a `proposed_annual_rent_cents` of at least 1, and a `FEE_DISPOSITION` proposal must carry a `proposed_purchase_price_cents` of at least 1, the missing one being a malformed request reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The project itself is described by `affordable_units` (at least 0), `total_units` (at least 1), `transit_ridership_uplift` (the projected additional daily boardings, at least 0), a `design_compliance_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive rating the proposal against the agency's design guidelines, and the optional `joint_use_committed` flag, false when omitted.

Four business gates guard submission beyond the ownership check. A solicitation that is not `ISSUED` reports `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_ISSUED`; a solicitation whose `response_deadline` the logical clock has already reached reports `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`; a developer that is not `CLEAR` reports `ERR_DEVELOPER_DEBARRED`; the proposal's affordable share falling below the solicitation's `affordable_housing_min_pct` reports `ERR_AFFORDABLE_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`; and omitting or falsifying `joint_use_committed` while the solicitation's `joint_use_required` is true reports `ERR_JOINT_USE_NOT_COMMITTED`. The affordable share itself, `affordable_pct`, is one hundred multiplied by `affordable_units` divided by `total_units`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

A submitted proposal lands in `SUBMITTED` with `rank` null, and the parent solicitation's `responsive_proposal_count` is incremented by 1. The proposal `status` values are `SUBMITTED`, `SHORTLISTED`, `SELECTED` and `REJECTED`, the last two terminal.

The **evaluation score** is computed once, at submission, from four point components and one penalty, and is then frozen on the proposal row and never recomputed even if the developer's prequalification later changes. Its value basis branches on the offered structure: a `GROUND_LEASE` proposal is valued at its `proposed_annual_rent_cents` capitalised over twenty years, while a `FEE_DISPOSITION` proposal is valued at its `proposed_purchase_price_cents` outright. **Value points** are forty multiplied by that value basis and divided by the solicitation's `benchmark_value_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places and then capped at 40.00. **Affordable points** start at a base of fifteen and add half a point for each whole percentage point by which the proposal's `affordable_pct` exceeds the solicitation's `affordable_housing_min_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places and then capped at 25.00; a proposal exactly at the minimum therefore scores the bare 15.00. **Ridership points** are the `transit_ridership_uplift` divided by fifty boardings per point, rounded half up to two decimal places and then capped at 20.00. **Design points** are fifteen hundredths of a point for each point of `design_compliance_score`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and are not capped. Each of the four components is rounded to two decimal places *before* its cap is applied and before the four are added together. From that raw sum a flat penalty of ten points is subtracted when the developer's `prequalified` flag is false, and nothing is subtracted when it is true; the difference is rounded half up to two decimal places, and a negative result is recorded as 0.00.

A worked case: a `GROUND_LEASE` proposal offering 2,400,000 cents of annual rent, with 50 affordable units out of 200 total, 600 boardings of ridership uplift and a design compliance score of 88, responding to a solicitation with a benchmark of 48,500,000 cents and an affordable minimum of 15 percent, from a prequalified developer. Its `affordable_pct` is 25.00; value points are 39.59 (below the 40.00 cap); affordable points are 15.00 plus half a point for each of the ten percentage points of headroom, giving 20.00; ridership points are 12.00; design points are 13.20; the raw sum is 84.79, the penalty is 0.00, and the `evaluation_score` is 84.79.

Shortlisting is an optional intermediate act by a `RealEstateOfficer` that advances a proposal `SUBMITTED → SHORTLISTED`. It requires the proposal to be in `SUBMITTED`, any other status reporting `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and requires the parent solicitation to be `ISSUED` or `CLOSED`, any other status reporting `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_ISSUED` — so proposals may still be shortlisted after the response window shuts. Shortlisting refreshes the parent solicitation's `responsive_proposal_count`, which is unchanged in value because a `SHORTLISTED` proposal is still counted as responsive.

### 3.4. Closing, Ranking and the Award Cascade

Closing a solicitation stops the clock on responses and produces the ranking the award will be judged against. It requires the solicitation to be `ISSUED`, any other status reporting `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_ISSUED`. The system first recomputes `responsive_proposal_count` as the number of this solicitation's proposals whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED`, and if that count is strictly below `min_proposals_required` the close is refused with `ERR_NO_RESPONSIVE_PROPOSALS`. The empty case is the common one: zero responsive proposals against a `min_proposals_required` of 1 fails here.

On success the solicitation moves `ISSUED → CLOSED` and every responsive proposal is assigned a `rank`. The candidate set is exactly those proposals whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED`; proposals already `SELECTED` or `REJECTED` at close time are excluded and keep `rank` null. Candidates are ordered by `evaluation_score` descending; ties are broken by `affordable_pct` descending; remaining ties are broken by `created_at` ascending. The UUID `id` never participates in the ordering under any circumstance. Ranks are then assigned as contiguous integers starting at 1 with no gaps.

Awarding is the single most consequential act in the system, because it does five things at once and each of them causes the next. A `RealEstateOfficer` names the winning `proposal_id` and, in the same payload, opens the exclusive dealing period by supplying `negotiation_start_date` and `negotiation_end_date` as ISO 8601 calendar dates, plus an optional `deviation_justification`. Six preconditions are checked. The solicitation must be `CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_SOLICITATION_NOT_CLOSED`. The named proposal must belong to this solicitation and be `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED`, otherwise `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The named proposal must have `rank` equal to 1, or else a non-empty `deviation_justification` must be present, otherwise `ERR_RANK_DEVIATION_UNJUSTIFIED` — the agency may deviate from its own ranking, but only on the record. The proposal's developer must have a `debarment_status` of `CLEAR`, otherwise `ERR_DEVELOPER_DEBARRED`. The `negotiation_end_date` must be strictly later than the `negotiation_start_date`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. And the resulting interval must not collide, under the closed-closed rule of §3.5, with any other `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` exclusive negotiation period on the same site, otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.

The cascade then runs, hop by hop. The solicitation moves `CLOSED → AWARDED`. The awarded proposal moves `SUBMITTED → SELECTED` or `SHORTLISTED → SELECTED` (hop 1). Because that proposal became `SELECTED`, a new exclusive negotiation period is created over the supplied interval with `status` `ACTIVE`, `extension_count` 0, `start_date` from `negotiation_start_date` and `end_date` from `negotiation_end_date` (hop 2). Because that period became `ACTIVE`, the site moves `SOLICITATION_READY → UNDER_AGREEMENT` with its `version` bumped by 1 (hop 3) — a site already `UNDER_AGREEMENT` stays there, and an additional non-overlapping period does not change site status. Because the site became `UNDER_AGREEMENT`, every **other** solicitation on the same site that is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` moves to `CANCELLED`, each with its `version` bumped by 1 (hop 4); "other" excludes the solicitation being awarded, so an award never cancels itself. Because those solicitations became `CANCELLED`, each of their proposals whose status was `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED` moves to `REJECTED`, each with its `version` bumped by 1 and its parent's `responsive_proposal_count` refreshed (hop 5); a `DRAFT` solicitation can hold no proposals, so its cancellation runs no proposal cascade. Separately, every non-awarded proposal of *this* solicitation whose status was `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED` also moves to `REJECTED`.

### 3.5. Exclusive Dealing Periods: Direct Grants, Extensions, Termination and Expiry

Not every project arrives through a competition. For sole-source situations and unsolicited responses, a `RealEstateOfficer` may open an **exclusive negotiation period** directly against a site, naming the `proposal_id` that gets exclusivity and the `start_date` and `end_date` of the dealing window as ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, both days inclusive; the period's `site_id` comes from the path. The site must be `SOLICITATION_READY` or `UNDER_AGREEMENT`, any other status reporting `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`. The named proposal must be `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED`, otherwise `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The `end_date` must be strictly later than the `start_date`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.

**Overlap is judged closed-closed.** A candidate interval collides with an existing period on the same site whenever the candidate's `start_date` is on or before the other's `end_date` and the other's `start_date` is on or before the candidate's `end_date` — which means that sharing even a single boundary day is a collision, not an adjacency. Only periods whose status is `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` are considered; `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` and `CONVERTED` periods are invisible to the test. A collision is reported as `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The same rule is applied by the award path of §3.4 and by extension below.

A directly granted period is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `extension_count` 0 and `termination_reason` null. If the site was `SOLICITATION_READY` it moves `SOLICITATION_READY → UNDER_AGREEMENT` with its `version` bumped by 1, and the same downstream cancellation runs as on an award: every other solicitation on the site that is `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → CANCELLED` and their `SUBMITTED` or `SHORTLISTED` proposals move to `REJECTED`. Here "other" carries a subtlety worth stating plainly — the solicitation that owns the proposal being granted exclusivity is excluded, so a direct grant never cancels its own proposal's solicitation. The period `status` values are `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED`, `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` and `CONVERTED`, the last three terminal.

Negotiations run long, so a period may be extended, but only twice. An extension supplies a `new_end_date` and is refused four ways: a period whose status is not `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` reports `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_NOT_ACTIVE`; a period whose `extension_count` has already reached the ceiling of two reports `ERR_MAX_EXTENSIONS_REACHED`, so a third extension attempt on the same period always fails; a `new_end_date` that is not strictly later than the current `end_date` reports `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; and an extended interval that collides closed-closed with a sibling `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` period on the same site reports `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. On success `end_date` is replaced by `new_end_date` and `extension_count` is increased by 1. The first extension advances the period `ACTIVE → EXTENDED`; the second is a self-loop `EXTENDED → EXTENDED` that leaves the state unchanged while pushing `extension_count` to its ceiling of two.

Termination releases the parcel early. The request carries a `termination_reason`, which is recorded on the period. Termination requires the period to be `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED`, otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_NOT_ACTIVE`, and it is blocked when any agreement referencing the held proposal has already reached `EXECUTED` — an executed deal cannot be unwound by terminating the negotiation window that produced it — reported as `ERR_INVALID_SITE_STATE`. On success the period moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` or `EXTENDED → TERMINATED`, the held proposal drops `SELECTED → REJECTED` (hop 1), and **conditionally** — only when no `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` period remains on the site and no agreement on the site is `EXECUTED` — the site moves `UNDER_AGREEMENT → SOLICITATION_READY` with its `version` bumped by 1 (hop 2). If another live period remains, the site's status is untouched.

Expiry is the passive twin of termination and is driven entirely by the logical clock, as described in §2.5. When the clock's calendar date becomes strictly greater than a period's `end_date`, that period moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` or `EXTENDED → EXPIRED`, its held proposal drops `SELECTED → REJECTED`, and the same conditional site cascade back to `SOLICITATION_READY` runs. Any agreement referencing the expired period's proposal keeps whatever `negotiation_status` it holds — nothing about the agreement changes — but from that moment on it can no longer be advanced to review, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`.

The one happy ending for a period is conversion. When the agreement built on the period's proposal is executed, the period moves `ACTIVE → CONVERTED` or `EXTENDED → CONVERTED` with its `version` bumped by 1, recording that the exclusivity was consumed by a signed deal rather than lost to the calendar.

### 3.6. Drafting the Agreement and Computing What the Agency Earns

A joint development agreement is the document that joins four parents at once, and the drafting payload names all four: the `site_id` of the parcel, the `developer_id` of the counterparty, the `proposal_id` of the selected proposal being documented, and the `valuation_id` of the determination whose permissible floor will govern the consideration. The `structure` of record is `GROUND_LEASE` or `FEE_DISPOSITION`, and `term_years` of at least 1 is the lease term or, for a fee disposition, the amortisation horizon. As with proposals, both money fields default to 0 and neither may be negative, but the structure decides which is mandatory: a `GROUND_LEASE` agreement requires `base_rent_cents` of at least 1, a `FEE_DISPOSITION` agreement requires `purchase_price_cents` of at least 1, and the missing one is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Participation rent is described by `revenue_share_pct`, the agency's share of projected gross project revenue expressed as a decimal fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive, and `projected_gross_revenue_cents`, the projected annual gross revenue, at least 0. The affordable commitment is `affordable_units_committed` (at least 0) out of `total_units` (at least 1). `site_area_share_pct` is the share of the parcel this agreement disposes of, strictly greater than 0 and at most 100, and it is what scales the federal interest slice in §3.8. The optional `joint_use_committed` flag, false when omitted, records whether the agreement dedicates joint-use space to the agency.

Drafting is guarded by six checks in this order. The site must be `UNDER_AGREEMENT`, otherwise `ERR_SITE_NOT_UNDER_AGREEMENT` — and this check outranks every other, so a drafting attempt against a site still in `SOLICITATION_READY` reports the site problem even when the named developer is simultaneously debarred. The developer's `debarment_status` must be `CLEAR`, otherwise `ERR_DEVELOPER_DEBARRED`. The committed affordable share — one hundred multiplied by `affordable_units_committed` divided by `total_units` — must be at least the `affordable_housing_min_pct` of the solicitation that produced the referenced proposal, otherwise `ERR_AFFORDABLE_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. `joint_use_committed` must be true whenever that governing solicitation's `joint_use_required` is true, otherwise `ERR_JOINT_USE_NOT_COMMITTED`. The referenced valuation's site must be the same site the agreement names, and the referenced proposal's solicitation must belong to that same site, otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_SITE_MISMATCH`. And an exclusive negotiation period holding the referenced proposal must exist with status `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` and with the logical clock's date on or before that period's `end_date`, otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`.

A newly drafted agreement opens with `negotiation_status` `DRAFTING`. Its federal dimension is initialised from the parcel: `federal_approval_status` starts at `NOT_REQUIRED` when the site's `federal_interest_pct` is exactly 0, and at `NOT_SUBMITTED` otherwise. Nothing is recorded yet in `submitted_by_user_id`, `board_resolution_number`, `execution_date` or `revision_notes`, all of which begin null.

The money is computed at draft time, and it is computed in a **pinned order** that matters. First, the base component: for a `GROUND_LEASE` it is simply the `base_rent_cents`, while for a `FEE_DISPOSITION` it is the `purchase_price_cents` divided by `term_years`, rounded half up to a whole cent — the annualised equivalent of a one-time price. Second, the participation component is the `projected_gross_revenue_cents` multiplied by `revenue_share_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Third, those two are added to give a **pre-credit subtotal**. Fourth, a capital credit rate is looked up **from that pre-credit subtotal and from nothing else**: a subtotal of at least 5,000,000 cents earns twelve hundredths; at least 2,500,000 but below 5,000,000 earns eight hundredths; at least 1,000,000 but below 2,500,000 earns four hundredths; below 1,000,000 earns nothing. If the committed affordable share — `affordable_units_committed` divided by `total_units`, as a fraction — is at least twenty hundredths, three hundredths are added to the rate, and the rate is then capped at fifteen hundredths. Fifth, `capital_credit_cents` is the pre-credit subtotal multiplied by that final rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. Sixth, `annual_revenue_to_agency_cents` is the pre-credit subtotal less the capital credit.

The pin in step four is the whole point. The dependency looks circular — the total is the subtotal less the credit, while the credit depends on a tier — and it is resolved by keying the tier on the subtotal produced in step three, never on the post-credit figure produced in step six. Worked through: a `GROUND_LEASE` with 2,000,000 cents of base rent, 12,000,000 cents of projected gross revenue and a five-hundredths revenue share yields a participation of 600,000 and a subtotal of 2,600,000, which falls in the eight-hundredths tier; with 50 of 200 units committed as affordable the three-hundredths bonus applies, giving eleven hundredths; the capital credit is 286,000 and the annual revenue to the agency is 2,314,000. Keying the tier on the post-credit figure of 2,314,000 instead would drop it into the four-hundredths tier and produce 2,438,020 from the same inputs — a different and wrong answer.

`consideration_value_cents` follows immediately from the same inputs and is the figure the fiscal gate of §3.7 tests. For a `GROUND_LEASE` it is the `annual_revenue_to_agency_cents` just computed, capitalised over twenty years by simple integer multiplication; for a `FEE_DISPOSITION` it is the `purchase_price_cents` outright. No rounding is needed. Continuing the worked case, 2,314,000 capitalised over twenty years is 46,280,000 cents — which clears a below-market floor of 36,375,000 but falls short of a strict fair-market-value floor of 48,500,000.

Terms are renegotiated by amendment. A `RealEstateOfficer` may revise an agreement whose `negotiation_status` is `DRAFTING` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, any other status reporting `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFTING`. The amendable fields are `term_years`, `base_rent_cents`, `purchase_price_cents`, `revenue_share_pct`, `projected_gross_revenue_cents`, `affordable_units_committed` and `site_area_share_pct`, each optional; whichever are supplied are applied, and `capital_credit_cents`, `annual_revenue_to_agency_cents` and `consideration_value_cents` are then all recomputed from the resulting stored inputs. An amendment applied while the agreement is already `DRAFTING` amends the terms without changing either state dimension, while an amendment applied to a `REVISION_REQUESTED` agreement advances it `REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFTING`, closing the negotiation cycle `AGENCY_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFTING → AGENCY_REVIEW`. Any amendment clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, so the segregation-of-duties comparand is always the identity of the *most recent* submission. And if the federal dimension was sitting at `FTA_REJECTED`, the amendment resets it `FTA_REJECTED → NOT_SUBMITTED` with the agreement's `version` bumped by 1, which is what lets a corrected agreement file a fresh disposition request after a rejection; the two dimensions move on the same amendment but each through its own transition.

### 3.7. Agency Review, the Board, and Execution

Submitting an agreement for review is the gate where every commercial promise is tested against the appraisal, and it takes no payload at all — everything it needs is already on the record. Before anything is written, the three computed money fields `capital_credit_cents`, `annual_revenue_to_agency_cents` and `consideration_value_cents` are recomputed from the stored inputs so that the fiscal tests run against fresh figures; when nothing has changed since drafting the recomputation is simply idempotent.

Five checks then run, and their relative order is fixed. The agreement's `negotiation_status` must be `DRAFTING`, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFTING`. The exclusive negotiation period holding this agreement's proposal must still be `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` with the logical clock's date on or before its `end_date`, otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`. The referenced valuation determination must be `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and this referential check runs **before** either fiscal check, so an agreement backed by a draft appraisal reports the uncertified valuation even if its consideration would also fall below the floor. Then the two fiscal tests, in order: `affordable_units_committed` must be at least the `affordable_units` the selected proposal offered, otherwise `ERR_AFFORDABLE_COMMITMENT_SHORTFALL` — the agency will accept more affordable housing than was bid but never less — and `consideration_value_cents` must be at least the referenced valuation's `permissible_floor_cents`, otherwise `ERR_BELOW_PERMISSIBLE_FLOOR`.

On success the agreement advances `DRAFTING → AGENCY_REVIEW`, `submitted_by_user_id` is set to the submitting operator's `X-User-ID`, and `revision_notes` is cleared. That recorded identity is the sole comparand for the segregation-of-duties rule that follows.

The board has two possible answers. A `BoardSecretary` may send the agreement back, supplying `revision_notes` that are recorded verbatim on the record; that advances the agreement `AGENCY_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_REVIEW` if the agreement is not in `AGENCY_REVIEW`. Or a `BoardSecretary` may record approval, supplying the `resolution_number` that is stored as `board_resolution_number`; that advances the agreement `AGENCY_REVIEW → BOARD_APPROVED` and is likewise refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_REVIEW` outside `AGENCY_REVIEW`. Approval carries the extra duty test of §2.1: when the approving `X-User-ID` equals the stored `submitted_by_user_id`, the approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and that check runs before any lifecycle-state consideration of the records the approval touches.

Execution is the last act of the negotiation dimension, and it branches. A `RealEstateOfficer` supplies the `execution_date`, which is recorded on the agreement. Four preconditions apply, in order: the agreement's `negotiation_status` must be `BOARD_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_BOARD_APPROVED`; its `federal_approval_status` must be `NOT_REQUIRED` or `FTA_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_FEDERAL_APPROVAL_PENDING`; if the parcel's `transit_facility_present` is true, at least one transit facility replacement of this agreement must have reached `INTERIM_IN_PLACE` or `COMPLETE`, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_REPLACEMENT_INCOMPLETE` (when `transit_facility_present` is false this test is inert and passes trivially); and no replacement of this agreement may be sitting in `DEFICIENT`, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_REPLACEMENT_DEFICIENT`.

On success the agreement moves `BOARD_APPROVED → EXECUTED` and the exclusive negotiation period holding its proposal moves `ACTIVE → CONVERTED` or `EXTENDED → CONVERTED`. What happens to the parcel depends entirely on the `structure`. Under `FEE_DISPOSITION` the land actually changes hands, so the site moves `UNDER_AGREEMENT → CONVEYED`, its terminal state, and its `version` is bumped by 1. Under `GROUND_LEASE` the agency keeps the fee, so the site's status is deliberately left at `UNDER_AGREEMENT` for the duration of the lease — the two branches have genuinely different side-effect sets, and an implementation that conveys a leased parcel has lost the agency's asset.

### 3.8. Federal Disposition Approval and the Reversal Cascade

Where the grantor retains an interest, the agency cannot dispose of the parcel without filing. A `GrantsComplianceOfficer` files a **federal disposition request** against an agreement, supplying the `narrative` justification that goes to the grantor; the request's `jda_id` comes from the path. Three preconditions apply. The agreement's `federal_approval_status` must be `NOT_SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_FEDERAL_APPROVAL_PENDING` — which is precisely what stops a second filing while one is already `SUBMITTED`. The agreement's `negotiation_status` must be `AGENCY_REVIEW` or `BOARD_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`; filing runs in parallel with the board's own process, which is why either of those two negotiation states is acceptable. And the site must have enough federal interest left unpledged, as follows.

The slice this filing pledges is `federal_interest_amount_cents`: the site's `federal_interest_basis_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `site_area_share_pct` and divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent. Because the basis was pinned at the most recent valuation certification, the amount is stable regardless of when the filing happens. If the site's `federal_interest_unencumbered_cents` is strictly less than that amount, the filing is refused with `ERR_FEDERAL_INTEREST_INSUFFICIENT`. To make the arithmetic concrete: a parcel appraised at 48,500,000 cents with a federal interest of two and a half percent has a basis of 1,212,500 cents; an agreement disposing of sixty percent of the parcel pledges 727,500 cents and leaves 485,000 cents unencumbered.

On success the request is created with `determination` `PENDING` and `determined_at` null, the agreement moves `NOT_SUBMITTED → SUBMITTED` on its federal dimension, the site's `federal_interest_encumbered_cents` is increased by the pledged amount, its `federal_interest_unencumbered_cents` is decreased by exactly the same amount, and the site's `version` is bumped by 1. The request's `determination` values are `PENDING`, `APPROVED`, `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN`, and all four of the latter are terminal.

The grantor's answer arrives as a `determination` recorded by a `GrantsComplianceOfficer`, with optional `condition_notes`. The request must still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_DISPOSITION_ALREADY_DETERMINED`. A determination of `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` or of `REJECTED` requires non-empty `condition_notes` — the conditions or the reasons must be on the record — and omitting them is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a plain `APPROVED` needs no notes. In every branch `determined_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `condition_notes` is recorded when supplied.

A favourable answer is simple. Both `APPROVED` and `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` move the request `PENDING → APPROVED` or `PENDING → APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` respectively and move the agreement `SUBMITTED → FTA_APPROVED` on its federal dimension. The site's encumbrance stays exactly where it is — an approved disposition consumes the pledged federal interest rather than releasing it. And `negotiation_status` is not touched at all: the two dimensions of the agreement advance independently, and a board that has not yet voted is unaffected by the grantor's concurrence.

A rejection is the most complex cascade in the system, and it runs in five hops. Hop 1: the request moves `PENDING → REJECTED` with `determined_at` stamped. Hop 2: because the request was rejected, the agreement moves `SUBMITTED → FTA_REJECTED` on its federal dimension, and — if its `negotiation_status` was `BOARD_APPROVED` — that dimension moves `BOARD_APPROVED → REVISION_REQUESTED` as well. This is the one place where a federal outcome reaches into the negotiation dimension. The agreement's `revision_notes` is set to the literal prefix `federal disposition rejected: ` followed by the recorded `condition_notes`, and `board_resolution_number` is cleared back to null, because the resolution the board passed no longer describes a viable deal. Hop 3a: because `negotiation_status` became `REVISION_REQUESTED`, the agreement's referenced valuation determination moves `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED`, so the corrected agreement will need a fresh appraisal certified before it can go back to review. Hop 3b: also because `negotiation_status` became `REVISION_REQUESTED`, every staging window of this agreement whose status is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED`. Hop 4: because those windows left `APPROVED`, each one's shared facility has its `available_bay_capacity` increased by that window's `bays_occupied`, the window's `capacity_released` is set to true, and the facility's `version` is bumped by 1 — construction that is no longer authorised must not keep bus bays out of service. Hop 5: the site's `federal_interest_encumbered_cents` is decreased by this request's `federal_interest_amount_cents` and its `federal_interest_unencumbered_cents` is increased by exactly the same amount, with the site's `version` bumped by 1, restoring the ledger identity of §2.7.

Withdrawal is the compensating action for filing, and is deliberately exact. A `GrantsComplianceOfficer` withdraws a request that is still `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_DISPOSITION_ALREADY_DETERMINED` — supplying a `withdraw_reason` that is recorded in the request's `condition_notes`. The request moves `PENDING → WITHDRAWN` with `determined_at` stamped, the agreement moves `SUBMITTED → NOT_SUBMITTED` on its federal dimension so a corrected filing can be made, the site's `federal_interest_encumbered_cents` is decreased and its `federal_interest_unencumbered_cents` increased by this request's `federal_interest_amount_cents`, and the site's `version` is bumped by 1. Filing and withdrawing therefore conserve `federal_interest_basis_cents` exactly, leaving the pre-filing ledger untouched. Note the asymmetry with rejection: withdrawal touches only the ledger and the federal dimension, and never disturbs the valuation, the staging windows or `negotiation_status`.

### 3.9. Keeping the Buses Running: Facility Replacement, Shared Facilities, Staging and Maintenance

A joint development project frequently sits on top of something the agency is operating today, and that something has to come back. A `TransitOperationsManager` records a **transit facility replacement** commitment against an agreement; its `jda_id` comes from the path. The `facility_type` being displaced is one of `BUS_BAY`, `PARK_AND_RIDE`, `RAIL_PLATFORM` or `OPERATOR_FACILITY`. `original_capacity` is the displaced facility's capacity in its own native units — bays, stalls or berths — and must be at least 1. `replacement_capacity` is the permanent capacity promised and `interim_capacity` is what will be available during construction, each at least 0. `milestone_date` is the contractual ISO 8601 calendar date by which the permanent replacement must be in service. From these the `capacity_deficit` is derived as the amount by which `original_capacity` exceeds `replacement_capacity`, or 0 when the replacement is at least as large — never a negative number. A new commitment opens in `PLANNED`.

Delivery is reported in stages, and the report carries a `status` plus optional `interim_capacity` and `replacement_capacity` corrections; supplied corrections are applied and `capacity_deficit` is recomputed from them before the state decision is made. Only three transitions are legal: `PLANNED → INTERIM_IN_PLACE`, `INTERIM_IN_PLACE →` either completion outcome, and `DEFICIENT → COMPLETE`. Any other requested pair is a malformed request reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Reporting the interim stage additionally requires an effective `interim_capacity` — the body override when one is supplied, otherwise the stored value — of at least 1, since a zero-capacity interim arrangement is no arrangement at all; a shortfall there is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Completion branches on the arithmetic rather than on the caller's word. A report of `COMPLETE` from `INTERIM_IN_PLACE` lands on `INTERIM_IN_PLACE → COMPLETE` only when `capacity_deficit` is exactly 0, meaning `replacement_capacity` is at least `original_capacity`. When the deficit is greater than 0 the very same report lands on `INTERIM_IN_PLACE → DEFICIENT` instead — the status recorded is `DEFICIENT`, not the `COMPLETE` that was asked for — and from that moment execution of the agreement is blocked with `ERR_FACILITY_REPLACEMENT_DEFICIENT`. The deficit is cured by filing a corrected `replacement_capacity`, which recomputes `capacity_deficit`; once it reaches 0 a fresh report advances `DEFICIENT → COMPLETE` with the record's `version` bumped by 1. `COMPLETE` is the only terminal state of the four (`PLANNED`, `INTERIM_IN_PLACE`, `COMPLETE`, `DEFICIENT`).

Alongside replacement runs the long-term coordination problem: facilities the agency and the developer will share for decades. A `TransitOperationsManager` registers a **shared facility** on a site — its `site_id` comes from the path — with a `facility_type` of `BUS_BAY`, `PLAZA`, `PARKING_STRUCTURE` or `ELEVATOR_BANK`, a `total_bay_capacity` of at least 1 counting the simultaneously occupiable units of the facility, and a `maintenance_responsibility` of `AGENCY`, `DEVELOPER` or `SHARED`. The optional `cost_share_pct_developer`, a decimal fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive defaulting to 0, is the developer's share of maintenance cost and is consulted **only** when `maintenance_responsibility` is `SHARED`. A newly registered facility opens with `available_bay_capacity` equal to its `total_bay_capacity` and `version` at 1.

Construction needs room, and room comes out of those shared facilities. A caller holding the `Developer` role requests a **staging window**, naming the `jda_id` of the agreement whose construction needs the space and the `shared_facility_id` of the facility whose units it will occupy, together with `start_at` (the occupation start instant, inclusive), `end_at` (the occupation end instant, exclusive), a `bays_occupied` count of at least 0, and a free-text `purpose` describing the construction activity. The request also carries `expected_facility_version` as described in §2.10, and the developer must hold the named agreement or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A `bays_occupied` of 0 denotes a non-occupying window — surveying or monitoring — which never conflicts with anything and never changes any capacity figure.

Two temporal gates guard the request. The `end_at` must be strictly after the `start_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. And the interval must fall wholly inside the **staging envelope** derived from the exclusive negotiation period whose proposal matches the agreement's proposal: the envelope opens at midnight UTC on that period's `start_date` and closes at midnight UTC 180 days after its `end_date`, the closing instant itself being excluded. An interval that escapes the envelope is refused with `ERR_STAGING_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM`. The peak-occupancy test described below is then also applied at request time, so a window whose interval would already push the facility over its `total_bay_capacity` is refused at request with `ERR_STAGING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` rather than being parked in `REQUESTED` for the operations manager to discover; the envelope refusal takes precedence when both would fail. A successful request creates the window in `REQUESTED` with `capacity_released` false and `rejection_reason` null, and bumps the shared facility's `version` by 1 — which is exactly why the next requester must re-read the facility.

Capacity is judged on **half-open intervals**. Two windows overlap if and only if the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends; adjacency, where one window's `end_at` is exactly another's `start_at`, is never an overlap. The conflicting set for a window under consideration is every other staging window on the same shared facility whose status is `APPROVED` and whose interval overlaps it in that sense; windows in `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` are excluded entirely. Peak occupancy is then the candidate window's own `bays_occupied` plus the largest simultaneous total the conflicting windows reach: form the sorted set of boundary instants consisting of the candidate's `start_at` together with each conflicting window's `start_at`, and at each boundary instant sum the `bays_occupied` of every conflicting window whose own interval covers that instant — that is, whose `start_at` is at or before the boundary and whose `end_at` is strictly after it — then take the largest of those sums. The check passes when peak occupancy is at most the facility's `total_bay_capacity`, and fails with `ERR_STAGING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Boundary instants are evaluated in ascending timestamp order, and when two windows share a boundary the `created_at` ordering is presentational only and cannot change any sum; the UUID `id` is never consulted.

The facility's `available_bay_capacity` is a denormalised ledger of the same arithmetic, refreshed on every status write: it is the `total_bay_capacity` less the sum of `bays_occupied` across that facility's `APPROVED` windows, and, as §2.7 states, those two quantities always add back to the total.

A `TransitOperationsManager` decides a requested window by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, plus a `rejection_reason` that is mandatory and must be non-empty on the reject branch (its absence being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`) and is ignored on the approve branch. Both branches require the window to be in `REQUESTED`, an already-decided window reporting `ERR_STAGING_WINDOW_NOT_REQUESTED`. The approve branch carries three further gates: the parent agreement's `negotiation_status` must be `BOARD_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_BOARD_APPROVED`; if the site's `transit_facility_present` is true, a transit facility replacement of the same agreement must have reached `INTERIM_IN_PLACE` or `COMPLETE`, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_REPLACEMENT_INCOMPLETE` (and when `transit_facility_present` is false this conditional guard is inert); and the peak-occupancy test above must pass, otherwise `ERR_STAGING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

The two branches diverge sharply in what they write. `REQUESTED → APPROVED` decreases the shared facility's `available_bay_capacity` by the window's `bays_occupied`, bumps the facility's `version` by 1, and leaves `capacity_released` false so the bays are known to be still out. `REQUESTED → REJECTED` records the supplied `rejection_reason` and touches neither the facility's `available_bay_capacity` nor its `version` — a rejected window consumes nothing.

Closing a window is the compensating action for approval. It requires the window to be `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`, and a `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED` or already-`CLOSED` window reports `ERR_STAGING_WINDOW_NOT_CLOSABLE`. Closing an `APPROVED` window moves it `APPROVED → CLOSED` and, because its `capacity_released` was still false, increases the facility's `available_bay_capacity` by the window's `bays_occupied`, sets `capacity_released` to true and bumps the facility's `version` by 1. Closing a `SUSPENDED` window moves it `SUSPENDED → CLOSED` but, because the compliance-reversal cascade of §3.8 already set `capacity_released` to true when it suspended the window, the capacity is **not** returned a second time and the facility's version is not bumped again. That guard is what makes approval and closure conserve `total_bay_capacity` exactly, however the window got to its terminal state. `REJECTED` and `CLOSED` are the two terminal window states, out of `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED`.

Finally, the money for keeping the shared facility working. A `TransitOperationsManager` posts a **maintenance charge** against a shared facility — the charge's `shared_facility_id` comes from the path — naming the `jda_id` of the agreement being charged the developer share and the billed period as `period_start` and `period_end`, both ISO 8601 calendar dates and both days inclusive, with `period_end` required to be strictly later than `period_start`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. The costs themselves arrive as `line_items`, an ordered array of objects each carrying a `description` string and a `cost_cents` integer of at least 0. The array must contain at least one entry; an empty array is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_LINE_ITEMS`. Array order matters and is pinned as the allocation order.

Allocation resolves the share first. `effective_share_applied` is one whole when the facility's `maintenance_responsibility` is `DEVELOPER`, zero when it is `AGENCY`, and the facility's `cost_share_pct_developer` as it stands at request time when it is `SHARED`; prior maintenance charges never participate in the computation. The allocation itself is a waterfall over the array in index order: each line item's `cost_cents` is multiplied by `effective_share_applied` and **rounded half up to a whole cent immediately**, and those rounded per-line amounts are accumulated into `developer_share_cents`. `total_cost_cents` is the plain sum of every line item's `cost_cents`, and `agency_share_cents` is `total_cost_cents` less `developer_share_cents`, so the agency absorbs the rounding residue by construction and the two shares always add back to the total exactly.

The per-line rounding order is not a stylistic preference. With `maintenance_responsibility` `SHARED`, a `cost_share_pct_developer` of thirty-five hundredths and three line items of 41,250 cents each, every line rounds from 14,437.5 up to 14,438, giving a `developer_share_cents` of 43,314 against a `total_cost_cents` of 123,750 and an `agency_share_cents` of 80,436. Summing the raw costs and rounding once at the end would give 43,313 — one cent less — and with five identical lines the gap widens to two cents. The per-line figure is the correct one.
