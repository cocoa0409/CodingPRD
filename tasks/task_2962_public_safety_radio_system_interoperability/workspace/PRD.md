# Public Safety Radio Interoperability API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A regional radio system authority operates a shared trunked land-mobile radio network on behalf of dozens of member agencies — police departments, fire districts, ambulance services, public works fleets, and the authority's own staff. Every one of those agencies believes its traffic is the most important traffic on the system, and during a multi-alarm fire or a regional storm they are all correct at once. The authority's job is to keep that shared infrastructure fair, auditable, and encrypted, and today it does so with a filing cabinet of spreadsheets: one for subscriber-unit serial numbers, one for encryption key custody logs, one for talkgroup numbering, one for drive-test coverage worksheets, one for user-fee invoices, and one that nobody trusts for tracking which radios are actually programmed with which codeplug.

This API replaces those spreadsheets with a single governed system of record, mounted under `/radio-system`. It manages subscriber-unit inventory and programming template lineage, encryption keyset custody and over-the-air rekeying, talkgroup architecture and cross-agency interoperability patching, coverage engineering, site maintenance and backup-power outage escalation, channel-capacity allocation during major incidents, member-agency user fees and board governance standing, dispatch console configuration with statutory logging-recorder retention, and readiness reporting for the migration to next-generation air-interface standards.

Two design pressures shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the engineer who authors a programming change may never be the board member who approves it, and the custodian who creates an encryption keyset may never be the officer who activates it. The second is **cascade fidelity**: this is a safety-of-life network, so when a key is compromised, a site drops to its last hour of battery, or an agency loses its voting standing for non-payment, the consequences must propagate immediately and completely through every dependent record rather than being reconciled overnight. The specification therefore treats side effects as first-class product behaviour, not as implementation detail.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the system. Where a rule below and a workflow narrative in Section 3 describe the same behaviour, they are reinforcing the same requirement from two angles, not describing two behaviours.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. Both headers must be present and must be non-empty strings; a request missing either header, or supplying an empty string for either, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. The system recognises exactly eight roles, spelled exactly as follows: `SystemAdmin`, `RFEngineer`, `FleetEngineer`, `ChangeControlBoard`, `KeyCustodian`, `SecurityOfficer`, `AgencyRadioManager`, and `Dispatcher`. An `X-User-Role` value outside that set of eight is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. When the headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` instead.

The eight roles divide into two identity families, and the distinction determines what `X-User-ID` actually means.

Six roles are **operator roles** whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `RFEngineer`, `FleetEngineer`, `ChangeControlBoard`, `KeyCustodian`, or `SecurityOfficer`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier on a resource — for example as `created_by_user_id` on a `RadioTemplate` authored by a `FleetEngineer`, as `created_by_user_id` on a `Keyset` created by a `KeyCustodian`, as `approved_by_user_id` on a `Keyset` ruled on by a `SecurityOfficer`, or as `submitted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a `ProgrammingChangeRequest` — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`. `SystemAdmin` covers regional-authority operations and finance: the agency registry, site registration, fee assessment, incident declaration, and the logical clock. `RFEngineer` covers coverage and site engineering: drive-test submission, maintenance windows, and outage handling and escalation. `FleetEngineer` authors programming templates and change requests and drives fleet deployment, but never rules on a change request. `ChangeControlBoard` rules on submitted programming changes and does nothing else that mutates state. `KeyCustodian` creates keysets and schedules and runs over-the-air rekeying. `SecurityOfficer` approves or rejects keysets and declares compromises.

Two roles are **agency-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyRadioManager` or `Dispatcher`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `MemberAgency` itself; an `AgencyRadioManager` is that agency's radio administrator and a `Dispatcher` is that agency's dispatch console operator. On any endpoint whose authorised roles include either of these two, the server must first confirm that `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `MemberAgency` row; when it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, not with a not-found error. Ownership checks then compare `X-User-ID` against the owning-agency field of the resource being acted upon: `agency_id` on a `SubscriberUnit`, `owner_agency_id` on a `Talkgroup`, `requesting_agency_id` on an `InteropPatch`, the `id` of the `MemberAgency` addressed in the path, and — for a talkpath release — the `owner_agency_id` of the `Talkgroup` named by the request's `talkgroup_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Every read endpoint in the system is available to all eight roles. Write authority is narrow and is listed per endpoint in Section 4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter skips that many rows before the page begins and defaults to 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether further rows follow the returned page.

### 2.4. Path Resolution, Filters, and Rejection Precedence

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve decides the error. The system's path identifiers are `agency_id`, `site_id`, `template_id`, `unit_id`, `keyset_id`, `campaign_id`, `talkgroup_id`, `patch_id`, `event_id`, `change_request_id`, and `request_id`; they map respectively to `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KEYSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TALKGROUP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_TALKPATH_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign-key identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved after the path, in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields, and they raise the same family of not-found errors — an unresolvable `template_id` in a body raises `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `agency_id` or `target_agency_id` or `requesting_agency_id` raises `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `site_id` or `home_site_id` raises `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `keyset_id` raises `ERR_KEYSET_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `source_talkgroup_id`, `target_talkgroup_id` or `talkgroup_id` raises `ERR_TALKGROUP_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `unit_id` raises `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `site_event_id` raises `ERR_SITE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`. Body foreign-key resolution applies to unit creation, unit lifecycle actions, talkgroup creation, interoperability patch requests, OTAR campaign scheduling, site event creation, talkpath requests, and change request creation.

Because many operations are guarded by several rules at once, the order in which those rules are evaluated is itself part of the contract. Checks are applied in the following fixed precedence, and the first check that fails produces the response:

1. Both authentication headers are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the eight declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
4. For endpoints open to `AgencyRadioManager` or `Dispatcher`, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `MemberAgency` — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
5. Path identifiers resolve, left to right — otherwise the not-found error of the failing segment.
6. Body foreign keys resolve, in the endpoint's declared field order — otherwise the not-found error of the failing reference.
7. The body parses, required fields are present, enum values are members of their declared sets, integers lie inside their declared ranges, and datetime fields are ISO-8601 UTC instants ending in `Z` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. The optimistic-concurrency field required by the endpoint matches the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.
9. For `AgencyRadioManager` and `Dispatcher`, the resource's owning-agency field equals `X-User-ID` — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
10. The acting `X-User-ID` is not the author of the artefact being ruled on — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
11. The target resource is not already in a terminal state for the requested operation — otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.
12. The requested transition is an edge out of the resource's current state (and, for a site event, an edge belonging to its `event_class` arm) — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
13. Remaining domain rules, in the order given by their own sections below.

Within group 13 the relative order is likewise fixed: the retired-unit guard (`ERR_UNIT_RETIRED`), then the keyset-active guard (`ERR_KEYSET_NOT_ACTIVE`), then the template-published guard (`ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_PUBLISHED`), then the talkgroup-availability guard (`ERR_TALKGROUP_LOCKED`), then the site-on-air guard (`ERR_SITE_OFF_AIR`), then coverage sufficiency (`ERR_COVERAGE_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`), then recorder retention (`ERR_RECORDER_RETENTION_BELOW_MINIMUM`), then emergency and priority entitlement (`ERR_EMERGENCY_PREEMPTION_NOT_PERMITTED`), then the temporal window rules in the order `ERR_OTAR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_PATCH_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_MAINTENANCE_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, `ERR_MAINTENANCE_CONCURRENCY_CAP`, then the referential rules in the order `ERR_TEMPLATE_VERSION_STALE`, `ERR_ENCRYPTION_MODE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SELF_PATCH_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_INTEROP_PRIVILEGE_INSUFFICIENT`, then `ERR_PATCH_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_OTAR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CRYPTO_PERIOD`, then the uniqueness rules `ERR_DUPLICATE_RADIO_ID` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_TALKGROUP_ID`, then `ERR_TEMPLATE_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, then the fiscal rules in the order `ERR_AGENCY_VOTING_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_FEE_PERIOD_INVALID`, `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, and finally the capacity rule `ERR_NO_PREEMPTABLE_TALKPATH`.

Filter parameters on list endpoints are additive: supplying more than one narrows the result set, and supplying none returns every row. The registry list accepts `agency_type`, `billing_status`, and `interop_privilege`. The site list accepts `status`, `coverage_class`, and `capacity_mode`. The template list accepts `status`, `target_standard`, and `supersedes_template_id`, the last of which walks a template lineage forward. The unit list accepts `agency_id`, `template_id`, `active_keyset_id`, `provision_status`, and `key_status`. The keyset list accepts `status` and `algorithm`. The campaign list accepts `keyset_id`, `agency_id`, and `status`. The talkgroup list accepts `owner_agency_id`, `home_site_id`, `status`, and `kind`. The patch list accepts `source_talkgroup_id`, `target_talkgroup_id`, `requesting_agency_id`, and `status`. The site-event list accepts `site_id`, `event_class`, and `status`. The talkpath list accepts `site_id`, `talkgroup_id`, `unit_id`, `site_event_id`, `preempted_request_id`, `outcome`, and `request_kind`. The change-request list accepts `template_id`, `target_agency_id`, and `status`. Every one of these lists also accepts `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system — `MemberAgency`, `Site`, `RadioTemplate`, `SubscriberUnit`, `Keyset`, `OtarCampaign`, `Talkgroup`, `InteropPatch`, `SiteEvent`, `TalkpathRequest`, and `ProgrammingChangeRequest` — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the row was created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations caused by a cascade rather than by a direct request against that row.
- `version` (integer): a counter that increments by one on every mutation of the row. Recomputation of a derived field is a mutation for this purpose, so an agency whose `billable_subscriber_count` changes because one of its radios was assigned has both its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

No entity in this system is exempt from `updated_at` or `version`; all eleven resource types carry the full set of four.

Because deadlines, crypto periods, maintenance windows, backup-power decay, and escalation tiers all depend on the passage of time, the system reads time from a **logical clock** rather than from the host wall clock. At server start the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time — before any clock-setting call, that startup default is the current instant. A `SystemAdmin` moves the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, an ISO-8601 UTC instant; the response reports the new instant as `current_utc`. No entity is mutated by moving the clock, but every subsequent request observes the new instant. Wherever this document says "now", it means the logical clock's current value.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Rebasing Writes

Three writes in this system legitimately supersede an earlier write of the same kind, which means no state precondition can ever reject them: a later fee assessment supersedes an earlier one for the same period and rebases the agency's balance; a fresher drive test always wins over an older worksheet; and a second revision may legitimately branch off the same parent template. For exactly these three, the client must prove it was looking at current data by pinning the parent's `version`, and the pin is a required input field, not an optional one.

- Booking a fee assessment against a member agency requires `expected_agency_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `MemberAgency` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the agency's current `version`, the assessment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This is what keeps the fee ledger from being rebased off an out-of-date balance.
- Submitting drive-test results against a site requires `expected_site_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Site` as the client last observed it. A mismatch against the site's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, preventing an engineer from overwriting a newer survey with an older worksheet.
- Creating a revision of a programming template requires `expected_template_version`, carrying the `version` of the parent `RadioTemplate` as the client last observed it. A mismatch against that template's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, forcing the author to re-read the lineage before branching. Note that this field pins the row's mutation counter `version`, not the lineage number `template_version`.

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field, and none may be added. Endpoints outside this list must not require, and must not honour, any `expected_*_version` input.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline

The radio system is a graph, not a tree, and a single operator action routinely rewrites rows in four or five different collections. Three principles govern all of it.

**Cascades run to completion inside the originating request.** Consistency is immediate: by the time the response is emitted, every dependent row has already been rewritten. There is no deferred reconciliation and no eventual convergence. A caller that reads any affected row immediately after a cascading write observes the settled result.

**Every row a cascade touches is mutated in the ordinary sense.** Each such row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been the direct target of a request. This is what makes version pinning meaningful: an agency whose `version` advanced because a distant key compromise released one of its patches will correctly reject a fee assessment pinned to the older value.

**Derived counters are recomputed, never incrementally patched from stale inputs.** Fields such as `billable_subscriber_count`, `active_patch_count`, `active_patch_request_count`, `loaded_unit_count`, `deployed_unit_count`, `busy_talkpaths`, `granted_request_count`, `busied_request_count`, and `grade_of_service_pct` are defined by a query over current rows, and after any cascade they must agree with that query. The single exception is `peak_talkpaths`, which is a high-water mark: it is raised whenever `busy_talkpaths` exceeds it and is never lowered, not even when every talkpath on the site is released.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision, Rounding, and Interval Conventions

All money in this system is expressed in whole US cents as integers — `last_assessed_fee_cents`, `fee_balance_cents`, `lifetime_assessed_cents`, `lifetime_paid_cents`, and the payment input `amount_cents` are integers and never carry a fractional part. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fraction of a cent, the value is rounded **half up** to a whole cent, and the rounding is applied at each point the fee rules name it rather than once at the end; no intermediate value is truncated toward zero.

Percentages are real numbers with a fixed number of decimal places, each rounded half up. Raw drive-test coverage is computed to two decimal places; the derated `coverage_reliability_pct` is stored to one decimal place; `grade_of_service_pct` is stored to one decimal place; and `readiness_pct` is reported to one decimal place. `generator_fuel_hours` is supplied by the client with at most two decimal places. Counts of minutes, days, seconds, units, channels, and votes are integers throughout; where the underlying quantity is fractional, the rule that produces it says explicitly whether the fraction is discarded downward, rounded half up, or floored at zero.

Time intervals follow one of two conventions, and the specification always says which. A **half-open** interval includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, so a value falling exactly on the end instant lies outside the interval. Fee-assessment periods, OTAR rekey windows, interoperability patch windows, crypto periods, and the region-wide maintenance concurrency test all use half-open intervals. A **closed** interval includes both endpoints; the same-site maintenance buffer test is the one place that uses closed intervals. Where two datetime inputs bound a window — `window_start` and `window_end`, `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, `crypto_period_start` and `crypto_period_end`, `period_start` and `period_end` — the end instant must be strictly after the start instant, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (with the single exception of the fee period, whose own rule is described with the fee rules).

Every threshold comparison in this document that is written as "at least" or "at most" is inclusive of the stated boundary value, and every comparison written as "more than", "greater than", "fewer than", or "strictly after" excludes it. Datetime inputs are accepted with zero, three, or six fractional-second digits and must end in `Z`; a datetime carrying an offset other than `Z`, or no zone at all, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two artefacts in this system record their author, and in both cases the author is barred from ruling on their own work. A `ProgrammingChangeRequest` stores the `X-User-ID` of the `FleetEngineer` who most recently submitted it as `submitted_by_user_id`; when a `ChangeControlBoard` member rules on that request, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, and an identical value is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. A `Keyset` stores the `X-User-ID` of the `KeyCustodian` who created it as `created_by_user_id`; when a `SecurityOfficer` approves or rejects that keyset, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, and an identical value is likewise rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The bar applies to every review outcome symmetrically: approving, rejecting, and requesting changes are all rulings, so the check fires identically for all three change-request decisions and for both keyset decisions.

Note the asymmetry between authoring and acting. Nothing prevents the same `FleetEngineer` identifier from authoring a template, submitting the change request, and later deploying the approved change — deployment is execution, not review. Only the review step is segregated.

### 2.10. Dispatch Console Configuration and Statutory Recorder Retention

Each member agency configures how many dispatch console positions it operates and how long its logging recorders retain audio. `console_position_count` is an integer of at least 1 and at most 40, defaulting to 1 when the agency is registered. `recorder_retention_days` is an integer of at least 1 and at most 3650, defaulting to 90 days.

Retention is not merely a preference: a statutory floor applies, and it is selected by the agency's `agency_type`. A `LAW` agency must retain at least 180 days; a `FIRE` agency at least 90 days; an `EMS` agency at least 90 days; a `PUBLIC_WORKS` agency at least 30 days; and the `REGIONAL_AUTHORITY` itself at least 365 days. Separately, any agency that participates in interoperability patching — whether it is requesting a patch or activating one — must retain at least 180 days regardless of its discipline, because a patched conversation spans agencies and the longest applicable retention governs the recording. A configuration change that would place `recorder_retention_days` below the applicable floor is rejected with `ERR_RECORDER_RETENTION_BELOW_MINIMUM` at HTTP 422, and so is a patch request or patch activation by an agency whose retention is below 180 days. A successful console-configuration change increments the agency's `version` by one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Admitting a Member Agency to the Regional System

Everything in the radio system hangs off the member agency register, and only a `SystemAdmin` may add to it. Registration takes the agency's legal `name`, between 1 and 120 characters; its `agency_code`, a short radio-system code of between 4 and 6 characters drawn only from uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`; and its `agency_type`, which is one of `LAW`, `FIRE`, `EMS`, `PUBLIC_WORKS`, or `REGIONAL_AUTHORITY`. The code must be unique across the whole register, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TALKGROUP_ID` — the same conflict code the system uses for every registry-level identifier collision. Four further inputs are optional at registration and each has a defined default: `fee_basis`, which is either `SNAPSHOT` or `PRORATED` and defaults to `SNAPSHOT`; `interop_privilege`, which is `NONE`, `MONITOR`, or `PATCH_AUTHORITY` and defaults to `MONITOR`; `console_position_count`, which defaults to 1; and `recorder_retention_days`, which defaults to 90.

A freshly registered agency has an empty fleet and a clean ledger. Its `billing_status` opens at `CURRENT` — that field is never written by a client and moves only as a consequence of fee assessment and payment. Its `billable_subscriber_count`, `last_assessed_fee_cents`, `fee_balance_cents`, `lifetime_assessed_cents`, `lifetime_paid_cents`, and `active_patch_request_count` all open at 0. Its `last_assessment_period_start`, `last_assessment_period_end`, and `payment_due_at` are all null until the first assessment is booked. Its `voting_eligible` flag opens true and its `governance_vote_weight` is computed by the board-weighting rule, which for an empty fleet yields exactly 1.

`billable_subscriber_count` deserves an early word because so much else reads it. It is the number of that agency's subscriber units whose `provision_status` is either `ASSIGNED` or `SUSPENDED`; radios still in `INVENTORY` and radios already `RETIRED` are excluded. `active_patch_request_count`, similarly, is the number of interoperability patches whose `requesting_agency_id` is this agency and whose status is either `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE`. Both are recomputed whenever anything that could change them changes.

`interop_privilege` is the governance board's grant of interoperability standing. `NONE` means the agency has no interoperability role at all; `MONITOR` means it may listen but may not create or activate patches; `PATCH_AUTHORITY` means it may exercise patching. The privilege is granted at registration and can be taken away by the fiscal cascade described in Section 3.9, but no endpoint edits it directly.

### 3.2. Radio Sites and Coverage Engineering

A radio site is the physical infrastructure that carries traffic, and a `SystemAdmin` registers one with its `site_code` — 3 to 12 characters of uppercase letters, digits, and the hyphen character, unique across all sites, again with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TALKGROUP_ID` on collision — and a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters. The site's `channel_capacity` is the total number of RF channels installed, counting the control channel, and must be at least 2 and at most 30. Its `backup_power_type` is either `GENERATOR` or `BATTERY_ONLY`. Two ratings describe the backup plant: `battery_runtime_minutes`, the rated battery-only runtime, at least 0 and at most 2880 minutes and defaulting to 240; and `generator_fuel_hours`, the rated generator runtime on the installed fuel load, at least 0 and at most 96 hours with at most two decimal places, defaulting to 0. `generator_fuel_hours` is recorded even for a `BATTERY_ONLY` site but is ignored by every calculation that reads it there. Finally, `migration_standard` records which air-interface standard the site infrastructure currently supports — `P25_PHASE1`, `P25_PHASE2`, or `NG_LMR` — and defaults to `P25_PHASE1`.

A new site opens `IN_SERVICE` with its `capacity_mode` at `NORMAL`, its `coverage_class` at `UNRATED`, its `coverage_derate_tier` at `NONE`, its `coverage_reliability_pct` at 0, its `covered_test_points` and `total_test_points` at 0, its `busy_talkpaths`, `peak_talkpaths`, `granted_request_count`, and `busied_request_count` at 0, and its `grade_of_service_pct` at exactly 100.0. Neither `status` nor `capacity_mode` is ever written by a client; both move only through the site-event machinery of Section 3.7.

Coverage is established by drive testing, and only an `RFEngineer` may file the results. A submission reports `covered_test_points`, the number of drive-test points that met the delivered-audio-quality target, and `total_test_points`, the number of points in the survey; the covered count may not exceed the total, and it may not be negative. The submission also carries `expected_site_version` as described in Section 2.6. Filing results overwrites both point counts on the site and then recomputes three derived fields in one pinned, single pass.

When `total_test_points` is 0, the pass short-circuits: `coverage_reliability_pct` becomes exactly 0.0, `coverage_derate_tier` becomes `NONE`, and `coverage_class` becomes `UNRATED`. Otherwise the engine first computes the raw coverage percentage as one hundred multiplied by `covered_test_points` divided by `total_test_points`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

The raw percentage then selects a derate tier, and this is the subtle part: **the tier is selected once, from the raw percentage only, and is never re-selected from the derated result** — even when the derated result would have landed in a lower band. A raw percentage of at least 97.00 selects tier `A`, which costs half a percentage point. A raw percentage below 97.00 but at least 90.00 selects tier `B`, which costs one and a half percentage points. Anything below 90.00 selects tier `C`, which costs three percentage points. On top of the tier derate, a site whose `backup_power_type` is `BATTERY_ONLY` suffers a further fixed penalty of two percentage points; a `GENERATOR` site suffers none. `coverage_reliability_pct` is then the raw percentage minus the tier derate minus the backup penalty, rounded half up to one decimal place, and floored at 0.0 so it can never go negative.

Only after the derated figure exists is the service class assigned. A `coverage_reliability_pct` of at least 97.0 makes the site `MISSION_CRITICAL`; at least 90.0 but below 97.0 makes it `GENERAL`; anything below 90.0 makes it `MARGINAL`. The tier chosen in the previous step is stored in `coverage_derate_tier` as `A`, `B`, or `C`. Nothing outside the two submitted point counts and the site's own `backup_power_type` participates in this computation — no other site, and no site event, has any influence on it.

Coverage class is not a report card; it is a gate. `MISSION_CRITICAL` is the only class that permits encrypted interoperability patching, and `MARGINAL` or `UNRATED` sites bar the deployment of an encryption-required programming template to agencies homed on them. Both gates are described where they bite, in Sections 3.6 and 3.3 respectively. A successful coverage submission increments the site's `version` by one.

### 3.3. Programming Templates, Lineage, and Change Control

A **programming template** is the codeplug definition a subscriber unit is programmed with, and only a `FleetEngineer` authors one. Creating a root template takes a `template_name` of 1 to 80 characters, a `target_standard` of `P25_PHASE1`, `P25_PHASE2`, or `NG_LMR`, and a `talkgroup_slot_count` — the number of talkgroup slots the codeplug exposes — of at least 1 and at most 512. The optional `encryption_required` flag defaults to false; when true, radios programmed with this template are expected to hold a loaded keyset. A root template opens with `template_version` equal to 1, `supersedes_template_id` null, `status` `DRAFT`, `deployed_unit_count` 0, and `created_by_user_id` set to the authoring engineer's `X-User-ID`.

Templates evolve as a lineage rather than by editing. Revising a template creates a *new* template row that supersedes the old one, and the revision request carries the same four content inputs — `template_name`, `target_standard`, `talkgroup_slot_count`, and the optional `encryption_required` — plus `expected_template_version` as described in Section 2.6. The new row's `template_version` is the parent's `template_version` plus one, its `supersedes_template_id` is the parent's `id`, its `status` is `DRAFT`, and its `created_by_user_id` is the acting engineer. The parent is not left alone: its `status` becomes `RETIRED` and its `version` is incremented by one. That version bump has a deliberate downstream consequence — any already-approved change request pinned to the parent will now fail at deployment time with `ERR_TEMPLATE_VERSION_STALE`, which is precisely the serialization point between revising a template and deploying a change built on it. Publication moves a template's `status` from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` and increments its `version` by one; a template may be assigned to radios and approved for deployment only once it is `PUBLISHED`, and any other lifecycle position is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

Deploying a template to a whole fleet goes through change control. A `FleetEngineer` opens a **programming change request** by naming the `template_id` to be deployed, the `target_agency_id` whose fleet receives it, and a `title` of 1 to 120 characters. The request opens in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0, `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `review_comment`, `pinned_template_version`, `pinned_template_record_version`, `deployment_started_at`, and `deployed_at` all null, and `target_unit_count`, `deployed_unit_count`, and `failed_unit_count` all 0. Its lifecycle runs `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` → `DEPLOYING` → `DEPLOYED`, with `REJECTED` and `DEPLOYED` as the two terminal positions and a genuine loop through `CHANGES_REQUESTED` in the middle.

Submission is the engineer's act. From `DRAFT`, submitting moves the request to `SUBMITTED` and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`. From `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, submitting again closes the revision loop: it moves back to `SUBMITTED`, increases `revision_count` by one, and rewrites `submitted_by_user_id` to the acting engineer. That loop is bounded — a request that has already been revised twice may not be re-submitted a third time, so a re-submission from `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is permitted only while `revision_count` is less than 2, and an attempt beyond that is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Note that the increment fires only on the `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED` edge, never on the `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` edge. Every successful submission increments the request's `version` by one.

Review is the board's act, and only a `ChangeControlBoard` member may perform it. The ruling names a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_CHANGES`, or `REJECT` and may carry an optional `review_comment`; when the comment is omitted, `review_comment` is stored as null. All three rulings require the request to be in `SUBMITTED` and all three enforce the segregation rule of Section 2.9 against `submitted_by_user_id`. All three record the ruling member's `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id` and increment the request's `version` by one.

- `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the request `SUBMITTED` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and stores the supplied `review_comment`, or null when none was supplied. Nothing is pinned.
- `REJECT` moves the request `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED`, which is terminal, and likewise stores the supplied `review_comment` or null.
- `APPROVE` moves the request `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED`, but only when the named template is `PUBLISHED` — otherwise the ruling is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. On approval three snapshots are taken and frozen: `pinned_template_version` captures the template's lineage number `template_version`; `pinned_template_record_version` captures the template row's mutation counter `version`; and `target_unit_count` captures the number of that target agency's subscriber units whose `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` or `SUSPENDED` at that instant.

Deployment is the engineer's act again, and it runs the whole fan-out inside one request. An `APPROVED` request may be deployed only after three gates pass, evaluated in this order. First, when the template's `encryption_required` flag is true, every site that serves as the `home_site_id` of a talkgroup owned by the target agency whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED` must have a `coverage_class` of `MISSION_CRITICAL` or `GENERAL`; if any such home site is `MARGINAL` or `UNRATED`, deployment is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`. Second, the live template's `version` must still equal the `pinned_template_record_version` captured at approval; if the template has been published, revised, or otherwise mutated since, deployment is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_VERSION_STALE`. Third, the target agency's `billing_status` must not be `SUSPENDED_FROM_VOTING`; a fiscally suspended agency cannot receive a fleet deployment, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_VOTING_SUSPENDED`.

Once the gates pass, the request moves `APPROVED` → `DEPLOYING`, `deployment_started_at` is stamped with now, and the fan-out walks the target agency's fleet. Every subscriber unit of that agency whose `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` has its `template_id` rewritten to the change request's template and adds one to `deployed_unit_count`. Every unit whose `provision_status` is `SUSPENDED` is skipped — a suspended radio cannot be reprogrammed — its `template_id` is left exactly as it was, and it adds one to `failed_unit_count` instead. The template's own `deployed_unit_count` is then recomputed as the number of subscriber units currently carrying that `template_id`. Because the fan-out covers exactly the set that was pinned at approval, the sum of `deployed_unit_count` and `failed_unit_count` always ends up equal to `target_unit_count`, which is the condition for the automatic `DEPLOYING` → `DEPLOYED` transition evaluated at the end of the same request; when it holds, `deployed_at` is stamped with now. In practice this means a caller never observes a request sitting in `DEPLOYING`.

Attempting to submit, review, or deploy a request that has already reached `DEPLOYED` or `REJECTED` is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; attempting an action that is not an edge out of the request's current position — deploying something still in `SUBMITTED`, for instance, or reviewing something in `DRAFT` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.4. Subscriber Units and Their Two Independent Dimensions

Radios are agency property, so an `AgencyRadioManager` — and nobody else — adds them to inventory. A new subscriber unit names its owning `agency_id`, its manufacturer `serial_number` of 6 to 24 characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits, and its `radio_id`, the P25 subscriber decimal identifier, an integer of at least 1 and at most 16777215. Both the serial and the decimal identifier must be unique across every subscriber unit in the system, and a collision on either is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RADIO_ID`. The `model` declares the form factor as `PORTABLE`, `MOBILE`, or `CONTROL_STATION`. Two optional inputs shape how the radio behaves when channels are scarce: `priority_class`, an integer from 1 to 10 where 1 is the highest priority, defaulting to 5; and `emergency_button_enabled`, defaulting to false, which determines whether the radio may originate emergency traffic at all. A new unit opens with `provision_status` `INVENTORY`, `key_status` `KEYLESS`, `template_id` null, `active_keyset_id` null, `billable` false, `days_in_service_last_period` 0, and `assigned_at`, `retired_at`, and `last_otar_at` all null.

The critical structural fact about a subscriber unit is that it carries **two state machines on one row, and they never touch each other**. The provisioning dimension, `provision_status`, walks `INVENTORY`, `ASSIGNED`, `SUSPENDED`, and `RETIRED`, with `RETIRED` terminal. The encryption dimension, `key_status`, walks `KEYLESS`, `KEYED`, `KEY_STALE`, and `ZEROIZED`, with `ZEROIZED` terminal. Assigning a radio never alters its `key_status`; loading a key over the air never alters its `provision_status`. Every rule below states explicitly which dimension it moves and confirms that the other is left exactly as it was.

The provisioning dimension is driven entirely by the unit lifecycle operation, which takes an `action` of `ASSIGN`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE`, `RETIRE`, or `ZEROIZE`, plus an optional `template_id` used by one of them. In every case the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the unit's `agency_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, and every successful lifecycle action increments the unit's `version` by one.

**`ASSIGN`** takes a radio from `INVENTORY` to `ASSIGNED` and is the only action that consumes `template_id`; the field must be present and must resolve to an existing programming template. That template's `status` must be `PUBLISHED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. A capacity rule then applies, and it is easy to misread: what is checked is not how many radios the template has been loaded onto but how many live talkgroups the radio's own agency operates. Specifically, the number of talkgroups whose `owner_agency_id` is the unit's `agency_id` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED` must not exceed the template's `talkgroup_slot_count`; if the agency runs more live talkgroups than the codeplug has slots, the assignment is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the unit's `template_id` is set to the supplied value, `assigned_at` is stamped with now, `billable` becomes true, and `provision_status` becomes `ASSIGNED` while `key_status` is left exactly as it was. Three derived figures are then refreshed: the owning agency's `billable_subscriber_count`, the owning agency's `governance_vote_weight` by the board-weighting rule of Section 3.9, and the template's `deployed_unit_count`.

**`SUSPEND`** takes a radio from `ASSIGNED` to `SUSPENDED`. A suspended radio keeps its loaded key, so `key_status` is untouched, and it remains billable — `billable` stays true, because suspension does not stop the meter. Suspension does, however, evict the radio from the air: every talkpath request originated by this unit whose `outcome` is `GRANTED` and whose `released_at` is still null becomes `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped at now, and because those channels came free, `busy_talkpaths` is recomputed on each site that was affected.

**`REINSTATE`** takes a radio from `SUSPENDED` back to `ASSIGNED`, closing a cycle in the provisioning dimension, and leaves `key_status` untouched. One detail matters for billing: `assigned_at` is **not** rewritten. The original assignment instant survives reinstatement, so a radio that was assigned, suspended, and reinstated inside one billing period still prorates from its first assignment.

**`RETIRE`** takes a radio from `INVENTORY`, `ASSIGNED`, or `SUSPENDED` to `RETIRED`, which is terminal in the provisioning dimension; retiring a radio that is already `RETIRED` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_RETIRED`. On success `retired_at` is stamped with now and `billable` becomes false, while `key_status` is left exactly as it was — a retired radio may still hold a key. The owning agency's `billable_subscriber_count` and `governance_vote_weight` are then recomputed.

**`ZEROIZE`** is the one lifecycle action that touches the encryption dimension instead. It takes `key_status` from `KEYLESS`, `KEYED`, or `KEY_STALE` to `ZEROIZED`, which is terminal; a radio already `ZEROIZED` cannot be zeroized again. Zeroizing clears `active_keyset_id` to null and leaves `provision_status` exactly as it was, and the `loaded_unit_count` of the keyset that was previously loaded is recomputed to reflect the departure.

Two computed fields on the unit round out the picture. `billable` is true exactly when `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` or `SUSPENDED`, and false for `INVENTORY` and `RETIRED`. `days_in_service_last_period` records the whole UTC days the radio was billable during its owning agency's most recently assessed period and is rewritten by the fee-assessment rule of Section 3.9. A retired radio is also barred from originating traffic: a talkpath request naming a `RETIRED` unit is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_RETIRED`.

### 3.5. Key Custody, Over-the-Air Rekeying, and the Compromise Cascade

Encryption on a public-safety system is a custody problem before it is a cryptography problem, and the system splits the duty in two. A `KeyCustodian` creates a keyset by supplying its `keyset_name`, a common key reference name of 3 to 40 characters that must be unique across all keysets — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TALKGROUP_ID` — and its `algorithm`, which is either `AES256` or `DES_OFB`. The keyset's validity is bounded by `crypto_period_start`, which is inclusive, and `crypto_period_end`, which is exclusive and must be strictly after the start. A new keyset opens in `PENDING_APPROVAL` with `created_by_user_id` set to the custodian's `X-User-ID`, `approved_by_user_id` null, `compromised_at` null, and `loaded_unit_count` 0.

Approval belongs to a `SecurityOfficer`, who supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. Either ruling requires the keyset to be in `PENDING_APPROVAL` and enforces the segregation rule of Section 2.9 against `created_by_user_id`, and either increments the keyset's `version` by one. `REJECT` moves the keyset `PENDING_APPROVAL` → `REJECTED`, which is terminal, and leaves `approved_by_user_id` null. `APPROVE` moves it `PENDING_APPROVAL` → `ACTIVE` and records the officer's `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`; it also performs a quiet housekeeping step, retiring the keys the new one displaces. Every other keyset that shares the same `algorithm`, is currently `ACTIVE`, and whose `crypto_period_end` falls at or before the new keyset's `crypto_period_start` becomes `SUPERSEDED`. Keysets whose crypto periods still overlap the new one are left `ACTIVE`; only wholly-elapsed ones are superseded. `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, and `COMPROMISED` are all terminal.

Getting a key into radios is a scheduled campaign, again run by a `KeyCustodian`. Scheduling names the `keyset_id` to push, the `agency_id` whose fleet is targeted, and the rekey window bounded by an inclusive `window_start` and an exclusive `window_end` that must be strictly after it. The keyset must be `ACTIVE` at scheduling time, or the request is rejected with `ERR_KEYSET_NOT_ACTIVE`. Two window rules then apply. First, the campaign window must not overlap another campaign for the same `keyset_id` and the same `agency_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`; overlap is judged on half-open intervals, so a new window beginning exactly at an existing window's end is accepted, and a genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_OTAR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Second, the window must lie wholly inside the keyset's crypto period — `window_start` at or after `crypto_period_start` and `window_end` at or before `crypto_period_end` — and a window that pokes out either side is rejected with `ERR_OTAR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CRYPTO_PERIOD`. A scheduled campaign opens with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `target_unit_count`, `rekeyed_unit_count`, and `failed_unit_count` all 0, and `executed_at` null.

Executing a campaign must happen inside its own window: the logical clock must lie at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, and an execution attempted outside that half-open interval is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The keyset must still be `ACTIVE`, or the execution is rejected with `ERR_KEYSET_NOT_ACTIVE`; a campaign already `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Execution selects its targets by a precise rule. Every subscriber unit whose `agency_id` is the campaign's target agency, whose `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` or `SUSPENDED`, and whose `key_status` is anything other than `ZEROIZED` enters the target set; units in `INVENTORY` or `RETIRED`, and units already zeroized, are outside the campaign entirely and are not counted anywhere. `target_unit_count` is the size of that set. The targets are then processed in ascending `created_at` order — never in identifier order — so that the counters are reproducible. A target whose `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` is reachable over the air: its `key_status` moves to `KEYED` (whether it was `KEYLESS` or `KEY_STALE` before), its `active_keyset_id` is set to the campaign's `keyset_id`, its `last_otar_at` is stamped with now, its `provision_status` is left exactly as it was, and `rekeyed_unit_count` rises by one. A target whose `provision_status` is `SUSPENDED` cannot be reached over the air, so it is left untouched and `failed_unit_count` rises by one instead. When the walk finishes, `executed_at` is stamped with now and the campaign's `status` becomes `COMPLETED` — `COMPLETED` even when every single target failed, because the campaign did run. The sum of `rekeyed_unit_count` and `failed_unit_count` always equals `target_unit_count` afterwards. Finally the keyset's `loaded_unit_count` is recomputed as the number of subscriber units whose `active_keyset_id` is that keyset and whose `key_status` is `KEYED`.

Declaring a compromise is the most consequential single action in the system, and it belongs to a `SecurityOfficer`. It takes no request body. The keyset must be `ACTIVE`, and any other custody position is rejected with `ERR_KEYSET_NOT_ACTIVE`. The keyset moves `ACTIVE` → `COMPROMISED`, `compromised_at` is stamped with now, and `loaded_unit_count` is forced to 0. From there, five linked consequences run to completion inside the same request, each caused by the one before it:

1. Because the key can no longer be trusted, every subscriber unit whose `active_keyset_id` is the compromised keyset and whose `key_status` is `KEYED` moves to `key_status` `KEY_STALE`. Its `provision_status` is left exactly as it was, and its `active_keyset_id` is **not** cleared — the radio still holds the bad key, which is why the state is called stale rather than keyless.
2. Because those radios went stale, encrypted talkgroups may have lost their last usable radio. Every talkgroup whose `encryption_mode` is `ENCRYPTED`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `owner_agency_id` now owns zero subscriber units with `key_status` `KEYED` moves to `status` `LOCKED` with `lock_reason` set to `KEY_COMPROMISE`. A talkgroup whose owning agency still has at least one keyed radio stays `ACTIVE`, and talkgroups whose `encryption_mode` is `CLEAR` or `SELECTABLE` are never locked by this cascade.
3. Because those talkgroups locked, the patches riding on them must drop. Every interoperability patch whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` and which names a newly locked talkgroup as either its `source_talkgroup_id` or its `target_talkgroup_id` moves to `status` `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped at now and `release_reason` set to `KEY_COMPROMISE`; each such patch has its `duration_minutes` recomputed by the patch-duration rule of Section 3.6.
4. Because those patches were released, `active_patch_count` is recomputed on every talkgroup touched and `active_patch_request_count` is recomputed on every member agency touched.
5. Independently of the talkgroup chain, every OTAR campaign whose `keyset_id` is the compromised keyset and whose `status` is still `SCHEDULED` moves to `ABORTED`. Campaigns already `COMPLETED` are historical records and are left alone.

### 3.6. Talkgroup Architecture and Interoperability Patching

A talkgroup is the logical channel a conversation lives on, and an `AgencyRadioManager` creates one for its own agency. The creation payload names the `owner_agency_id` that will own and administer it, the `home_site_id` of the site whose coverage class gates encrypted patching of this talkgroup, an `alias` of 1 to 16 characters as shown on radios and consoles, and the `tg_decimal_id`, a P25 talkgroup decimal identifier of at least 1 and at most 65535 that must be unique across all talkgroups — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TALKGROUP_ID`. Its architectural role is declared by `kind`, one of `DISPATCH`, `TACTICAL`, `INTEROP`, or `ANNOUNCEMENT`, and its encryption posture by `encryption_mode`, one of `CLEAR`, `ENCRYPTED`, or `SELECTABLE`. An optional `priority_level`, an integer from 1 to 10 where 1 is highest, defaults to 5. A new talkgroup opens `ACTIVE` with `lock_reason` `NONE` and `active_patch_count` 0, where `active_patch_count` is the number of interoperability patches naming this talkgroup as either source or target whose status is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE`.

Availability is managed by the owning agency through a single operation taking an `action` of `LOCK`, `UNLOCK`, or `DECOMMISSION`; in all three cases the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the talkgroup's `owner_agency_id`, and each successful action increments the talkgroup's `version` by one. `LOCK` moves an `ACTIVE` talkgroup to `LOCKED` and sets `lock_reason` to `MANUAL`; every patch naming this talkgroup whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped at now and `release_reason` set to `OPERATOR`, after which `active_patch_count` is recomputed. `UNLOCK` moves a `LOCKED` talkgroup back to `ACTIVE` and resets `lock_reason` to `NONE`, closing the lock cycle — but with one guard: when the `lock_reason` is `KEY_COMPROMISE`, the owning agency must have at least one subscriber unit whose `key_status` is `KEYED` before the talkgroup may go live again, and an unlock attempted while the agency still has no keyed radio is rejected with `ERR_KEYSET_NOT_ACTIVE`. A talkgroup locked with `lock_reason` `MANUAL` carries no such requirement. `DECOMMISSION` moves a talkgroup from either `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED` to `DECOMMISSIONED`, which is terminal; decommissioning something already `DECOMMISSIONED` is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Decommissioning also releases every patch naming this talkgroup whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE`, with `release_reason` set to `OPERATOR`.

Cross-agency interoperability is the point of the whole regional system, and it is expressed as a **patch** between two talkgroups for a reserved window. A `Dispatcher` requests one by naming the `source_talkgroup_id` that initiates the patch, the `target_talkgroup_id` to be patched to it, the `requesting_agency_id` exercising patch authority, and the window bounded by an inclusive `scheduled_start` and an exclusive `scheduled_end` that must be strictly after it. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal `requesting_agency_id`. Requesting a patch is the most heavily guarded write in the system, and its checks are evaluated in the following order:

- Both named talkgroups must be `ACTIVE`; a `LOCKED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` participant is rejected with `ERR_TALKGROUP_LOCKED`.
- Neither talkgroup's home site may be `OFF_AIR`; an off-air site is rejected with `ERR_SITE_OFF_AIR`.
- When either talkgroup's `encryption_mode` is `ENCRYPTED`, the home site of **both** talkgroups must have a `coverage_class` of exactly `MISSION_CRITICAL`; anything less is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`. A patch between two `CLEAR` or `SELECTABLE` talkgroups is not subject to this gate.
- The requesting agency's `recorder_retention_days` must be at least 180 days, or the request is rejected with `ERR_RECORDER_RETENTION_BELOW_MINIMUM`.
- The requested window must not overlap any patch whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` that names either the source or the target talkgroup. Overlap is judged on half-open intervals, so two back-to-back windows sharing an endpoint are both accepted; a genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_PATCH_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
- The two encryption postures must be compatible: if either talkgroup is `ENCRYPTED`, the other must be `ENCRYPTED` or `SELECTABLE`. A `CLEAR` talkgroup may never be patched to an `ENCRYPTED` one, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ENCRYPTION_MODE_MISMATCH`.
- The two talkgroups must be distinct — a `source_talkgroup_id` equal to the `target_talkgroup_id` is rejected with `ERR_SELF_PATCH_NOT_PERMITTED`.
- The requesting agency must own at least one of the two talkgroups; an agency trying to patch two talkgroups that both belong to others is rejected with `ERR_INTEROP_PRIVILEGE_INSUFFICIENT`.
- The requesting agency's `interop_privilege` must be `PATCH_AUTHORITY`; an agency standing at `NONE` or `MONITOR` is rejected with `ERR_PATCH_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED`.
- The requesting agency's `billing_status` must not be `SUSPENDED_FROM_VOTING`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_VOTING_SUSPENDED`.

A patch that survives all of that opens in `REQUESTED` with `activated_at`, `released_at`, `duration_minutes`, and `recorder_retention_days_at_activation` all null and `release_reason` `NONE`; `active_patch_count` is then recomputed on both talkgroups and `active_patch_request_count` on the requesting agency.

The requesting dispatcher later rules on its own request with a `decision` of `ACTIVATE` or `DENY`, and either ruling increments the patch's `version` by one. `DENY` moves the patch `REQUESTED` → `DENIED`, which is terminal, and recomputes `active_patch_count` on both talkgroups and `active_patch_request_count` on the agency. `ACTIVATE` moves the patch `REQUESTED` → `ACTIVE`, and it re-checks at activation time what was checked at request time, because the world may have moved: the requesting agency must still hold `PATCH_AUTHORITY` (otherwise `ERR_PATCH_AUTHORITY_REQUIRED`), must still retain at least 180 days of recordings (otherwise `ERR_RECORDER_RETENTION_BELOW_MINIMUM`), and both talkgroups must still be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_TALKGROUP_LOCKED`). Activation must also happen inside the reserved window: the logical clock must be at or after `scheduled_start` and strictly before `scheduled_end`, and an activation outside that half-open interval is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. On success `activated_at` is stamped with now and `recorder_retention_days_at_activation` snapshots the requesting agency's `recorder_retention_days` as it stands at that instant — a later console reconfiguration does not rewrite the snapshot, which is what makes the retention obligation on the recorded audio auditable after the fact.

Releasing an `ACTIVE` patch is the ordinary end of its life. The requesting dispatcher stamps `released_at` with now, sets `release_reason` to `OPERATOR`, computes `duration_minutes`, and triggers a recomputation of `active_patch_count` on both talkgroups and `active_patch_request_count` on the agency; the patch's `version` increments by one. `RELEASED` and `DENIED` are the two terminal positions.

`duration_minutes` is the logged airtime of the patch and it follows one rule wherever a patch is released, whether by an operator or by a cascade. When either `activated_at` or `released_at` is null the value stays null — a patch released straight out of `REQUESTED` without ever having been activated therefore never acquires a duration. Otherwise it is the number of whole minutes from `activated_at` to `released_at`, with any partial minute discarded downward, so a patch released in the same logical minute it was activated logs 0 rather than null.

Patches also drop for reasons that have nothing to do with the dispatcher. A patch in `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` is released by the system when the keyset behind one of its talkgroups is compromised, when one of its talkgroups is locked or decommissioned, when the requesting agency loses its patch authority to a fiscal sanction, or when the patch's home site escalates to its most severe outage tier. In each case `released_at` is stamped with now, `duration_minutes` is recomputed, `active_patch_count` and `active_patch_request_count` are recomputed on every row touched, and `release_reason` records *why*: `KEY_COMPROMISE`, `OPERATOR`, `PRIVILEGE_REVOKED`, or `SITE_ESCALATION` respectively.

### 3.7. Site Events: Maintenance Windows, Outages, and Incident Surge

Anything that happens to a radio site is recorded as a **site event**, opened by either a `SystemAdmin` or an `RFEngineer`. The event names its `site_id`, its `event_class`, and its `cause`. The `event_class` is one of `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE`, `UNPLANNED_OUTAGE`, or `MAJOR_INCIDENT` and behaves as a configuration flag: it selects which arm of the event lifecycle applies and is fixed for the life of the event. The `cause` is one of `COMMERCIAL_POWER_LOSS`, `LINK_FAILURE`, `RF_FAULT`, `SCHEDULED_WORK`, or `INCIDENT_SURGE`. Two optional datetime inputs, `window_start` and `window_end`, bound a planned maintenance window; both are **required** when `event_class` is `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE` and both must be **omitted** otherwise, and a violation in either direction — a planned event with no window, or an outage or incident that supplies one — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two region-wide scheduling rules apply only to `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE` events, and both look at other events whose `status` is `OPEN` or `IN_PROGRESS`. The first protects a single site from back-to-back crews: a new planned window must clear every other planned window **on the same site** by more than 45 minutes on either side. The intervals here are closed, so both endpoints count, and the buffer is applied on both sides — the request is rejected when the new window's start falls at or before the existing window's end plus 45 minutes *and* the existing window's start falls at or before the new window's end plus 45 minutes. A gap of exactly 45 minutes is therefore rejected while a gap of 46 minutes is accepted, and the rejection is `ERR_MAINTENANCE_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. The second rule protects the region from losing too much infrastructure at once: at most 2 distinct sites may hold planned maintenance windows covering the same instant, so a new window is rejected when 2 or more *other* sites already have a planned maintenance event whose window intersects the new one. That intersection test uses half-open intervals, meaning a new window that begins exactly when another site's window ends does not intersect it. The rejection is `ERR_MAINTENANCE_CONCURRENCY_CAP`.

A newly opened event sits in `OPEN` with `detected_at` stamped at the current logical clock instant, `escalation_tier` 0, `backup_minutes_remaining` null, `restored_at` null, and `preempted_talkpath_count` 0. Crucially, opening an event changes nothing about the parent site: its `status` and `capacity_mode` stay exactly as they were until the event is advanced. `detected_at` is the origin from which backup-power decay is measured, which is why it is stamped at open time rather than at the moment the crew engages the generator.

Events are driven forward by a single operation taking an `action` of `START`, `BACKUP_ENGAGED`, `DECLARE_SURGE`, `ESCALATE`, `RESTORE`, `CLOSE`, or `CANCEL`, and each successful advance increments the event's `version` by one. From `OPEN` the lifecycle forks three ways on `event_class`, and each arm carries a different set of consequences for the site. An action that does not belong to the event's own arm — asking a `MAJOR_INCIDENT` to `START`, or an `UNPLANNED_OUTAGE` to `DECLARE_SURGE` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, as is any action that is not an edge out of the event's current position. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and advancing an event that has reached either is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

**The planned-maintenance arm.** `START` moves a `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE` event from `OPEN` to `IN_PROGRESS`, but only once the logical clock has reached `window_start` — starting early is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Its single consequence is that the parent site's `status` becomes `MAINTENANCE`; `capacity_mode` is untouched, so channel access continues under normal rules.

**The unplanned-outage arm.** `BACKUP_ENGAGED` moves an `UNPLANNED_OUTAGE` event from `OPEN` to `ON_BACKUP_POWER`. The parent site's `status` becomes `DEGRADED`, and `backup_minutes_remaining` is computed for the first time. That figure is the site's total backup capacity less the time already elapsed. Capacity is the site's `battery_runtime_minutes` plus, for a site whose `backup_power_type` is `GENERATOR`, its `generator_fuel_hours` converted to minutes by multiplying by 60 and rounding half up to a whole minute; for a `BATTERY_ONLY` site the generator contribution is dropped entirely even when `generator_fuel_hours` is non-zero. Elapsed time is the number of whole minutes from `detected_at` to now, with any partial minute discarded downward. `backup_minutes_remaining` is the capacity less the elapsed time, floored at 0 so it never goes negative. For any event whose `event_class` is not `UNPLANNED_OUTAGE`, `backup_minutes_remaining` stays null and the escalation machinery below does not apply.

**Escalation.** `ESCALATE` is available from `ON_BACKUP_POWER` and again from `ESCALATED`, and it recomputes `backup_minutes_remaining` before deciding anything. The remaining-minutes figure maps to an escalation tier on inclusive low-side boundaries: at most 30 minutes remaining is tier 3, more than 30 but at most 60 minutes is tier 2, more than 60 but at most 120 minutes is tier 1, and more than 120 minutes remaining is tier 0. Escalation is monotonic — the tier computed from the fresh remaining-minutes figure must be strictly greater than the event's current `escalation_tier`, and an escalation that would hold the tier steady or lower it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. When the check passes, the event's `status` becomes `ESCALATED` (a self-loop when it was already `ESCALATED`) and `escalation_tier` is set to the newly computed tier. Tiers 1 and 2 have no further effect beyond recording the tier.

Tier 3 is where the escalation bites, and it bites in four linked steps. First, the parent site's `capacity_mode` becomes `EMERGENCY_ONLY`, and its `status` becomes `OFF_AIR` when `backup_minutes_remaining` has reached exactly 0 or `DEGRADED` when some backup time is still left. Second, because the site went `EMERGENCY_ONLY`, every talkpath request on that site whose `outcome` is `GRANTED`, whose `released_at` is still null, and whose `request_kind` is `NORMAL` becomes `outcome` `PREEMPTED` with `released_at` stamped at now; those victims are processed in descending `priority_class_at_grant` order — least important first, since a higher class number means lower priority — with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and `preempted_talkpath_count` on the event is set to how many were preempted. `PRIORITY` and `EMERGENCY` grants survive. Third, because those talkpaths went away, the site's `busy_talkpaths` and `grade_of_service_pct` are recomputed. Fourth, every interoperability patch whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` and whose source or target talkgroup has this site as its `home_site_id` becomes `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped at now, `release_reason` `SITE_ESCALATION`, and `duration_minutes` recomputed, after which `active_patch_count` is recomputed on every talkgroup touched and `active_patch_request_count` on every agency touched.

**The major-incident arm.** `DECLARE_SURGE` is reserved to a `SystemAdmin` and moves a `MAJOR_INCIDENT` event from `OPEN` to `SURGE_ACTIVE`. The site must not already be `OFF_AIR`, or the declaration is rejected with `ERR_SITE_OFF_AIR`. Its only consequence is that the site's `capacity_mode` becomes `PRIORITY_ONLY`; the site's `status` is deliberately left unchanged, because a surge is a demand problem rather than an infrastructure fault.

**Rejoining.** `RESTORE` is available from `ON_BACKUP_POWER` or `ESCALATED` and moves the event to `RESTORED`: `restored_at` is stamped with now, `backup_minutes_remaining` is cleared back to null, and the site returns to `status` `IN_SERVICE` with `capacity_mode` `NORMAL`. Note that restoring does not lower `escalation_tier` or clear `preempted_talkpath_count` — the escalation history stays on the record. `CLOSE` is available from `IN_PROGRESS`, `SURGE_ACTIVE`, or `RESTORED` and moves the event to `CLOSED`, likewise returning the site to `IN_SERVICE` and `NORMAL`; this is how all three arms rejoin. `CANCEL` is available only from `OPEN` and moves the event to `CANCELLED` with no effect at all on the site, because the event never engaged.

### 3.8. Channel Capacity: Talkpath Allocation, Preemption, and Grade of Service

Every push-to-talk on a trunked system is a request for a channel, and this API records each one as a **talkpath request** — including the ones it refuses. A `Dispatcher` submits a request naming the `site_id` whose channel pool is being asked for, the `talkgroup_id` the transmission is addressed to, the `unit_id` of the originating radio, and the `request_kind`, which is `NORMAL`, `PRIORITY`, or `EMERGENCY`. An optional `site_event_id` attributes the traffic to a site event; it is left null for routine traffic. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `owner_agency_id` of the named talkgroup. The originating unit must not be `RETIRED` (otherwise `ERR_UNIT_RETIRED`), the talkgroup must be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_TALKGROUP_LOCKED`), and the site must not be `OFF_AIR` (otherwise `ERR_SITE_OFF_AIR`).

Allocation is a waterfall over integer channel counts, and it begins by establishing three numbers. The site's **usable** channel count is its `channel_capacity` less the one channel permanently reserved as the control channel. **Available** headroom is that usable count less the site's current `busy_talkpaths`. The **surge reserve** is 0 whenever the site's `capacity_mode` is `NORMAL`; otherwise it is a quarter of the usable count with any fraction discarded downward, capped at 4 channels, whichever is smaller. Both of the latter two figures are snapshotted onto the request itself as `available_talkpaths_at_decision` and `reserve_talkpaths_at_decision`, and they are recorded whatever the outcome, so a refused request still tells an auditor exactly how tight the site was.

A `NORMAL` request is the ordinary case and is the only class that respects the surge reserve. When the site's `capacity_mode` is `EMERGENCY_ONLY`, a `NORMAL` request is barred outright and its `outcome` is `BUSIED` without any headroom arithmetic. Otherwise the request is `GRANTED` when the available headroom less the surge reserve is greater than zero, and `BUSIED` in every other case. The comparison is strictly greater than zero: a site with exactly zero channels of headroom above its reserve busies the call.

A `PRIORITY` request may eat into the surge reserve but may never displace anybody. It is only available to sufficiently important radios: when the originating unit's `priority_class` is greater than 3, the request is refused outright with `ERR_EMERGENCY_PREEMPTION_NOT_PERMITTED`. A `PRIORITY` request on a site in `EMERGENCY_ONLY` is `BUSIED`. Otherwise it is `GRANTED` when the available headroom is greater than zero — the reserve is not subtracted for this class — and `BUSIED` otherwise.

An `EMERGENCY` request is the emergency button, and it is entitled to take a channel away from somebody else. Only a radio whose `emergency_button_enabled` flag is true may originate one; a radio without that provisioning is refused with `ERR_EMERGENCY_PREEMPTION_NOT_PERMITTED`. When the available headroom is greater than zero, the request is simply `GRANTED` and its `preempted_request_id` stays null. When there is no headroom, the system looks for a victim, and the search is narrow and explicit. The candidate set is every talkpath request on the *same site* whose `outcome` is `GRANTED`, whose `released_at` is still null, and whose `request_kind` is `NORMAL`; `PRIORITY` and `EMERGENCY` grants are never victims and already-released rows are excluded. Candidates are ranked least-important-first, by descending `priority_class_at_grant`, with ties broken by ascending `created_at`; the row's identifier is never consulted. The first row in that order is the victim. If the candidate set is empty, the emergency request is refused with `ERR_NO_PREEMPTABLE_TALKPATH` at HTTP 409. Otherwise the victim's `outcome` becomes `PREEMPTED` with `released_at` stamped at now and its `hold_seconds` recomputed, the new request is `GRANTED`, and its `preempted_request_id` records the displaced request's identifier — which is also how the talkpath list can be filtered to find what a given preemption cost.

A granted request stamps `granted_at` with now and snapshots the originating unit's `priority_class` into `priority_class_at_grant`, which is the ranking key any future preemption will consult. The site's `busy_talkpaths` rises by one, `peak_talkpaths` is raised to the new busy figure whenever that figure exceeds it, `granted_request_count` rises by one, and `grade_of_service_pct` is recomputed. When the grant required a preemption, one channel was freed as another was taken, so `busy_talkpaths` nets back to the value it held before the request. A busied request leaves `granted_at` null, adds one to `busied_request_count`, and triggers the same grade-of-service recomputation; the row is still persisted and still answered with 201, because refused calls are exactly what a grade-of-service statistic is made of.

Releasing a held talkpath returns the channel to the pool. The request must have `outcome` `GRANTED` with `released_at` still null, and anything else — an already-released row, a preempted row, a busied row — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `owner_agency_id` of the talkgroup the request was addressed to. On release, `released_at` is stamped with now, `hold_seconds` is computed as the number of whole seconds from `granted_at` to `released_at` with any partial second discarded downward, the site's `busy_talkpaths` falls by one, `grade_of_service_pct` is recomputed, and `peak_talkpaths` is deliberately left where it is. `hold_seconds` stays null while a talkpath is still held and for any request that was `BUSIED`. `BUSIED`, `PREEMPTED`, and `RELEASED` are all terminal outcomes.

**Grade of service** is the site's headline capacity statistic and is recomputed at the end of every talkpath request and of every cascade that preempts talkpaths. Four counters feed it, all defined as queries over the site's entire request history. `busy_talkpaths` is the number of requests for the site whose `outcome` is `GRANTED` and whose `released_at` is null. `peak_talkpaths` is the greater of its previous value and the freshly computed busy figure — monotonic, and never lowered. `granted_request_count` is the number of requests for the site whose `outcome` is `GRANTED`, `PREEMPTED`, or `RELEASED`; preempted and released rows still count as grants because the channel really was delivered. `busied_request_count` is the number whose `outcome` is `BUSIED`. `grade_of_service_pct` is then one hundred multiplied by `granted_request_count` divided by the sum of `granted_request_count` and `busied_request_count`, rounded half up to one decimal place — and exactly 100.0 when that sum is zero, which is why a brand-new site reports perfect service. Every request the site has ever seen participates, regardless of which `site_event_id` it was attributed to or whether it was attributed to one at all.

### 3.9. User Fees, Board Governance, and Migration Readiness

The authority funds itself by billing member agencies for the radios they operate, and only a `SystemAdmin` books an assessment. An assessment covers one billing period, given as `period_start` and `period_end`, and carries `expected_agency_version` as described in Section 2.6. The period itself is validated before anything is computed: `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, the period may not be longer than 366 days, and `period_end` may not lie after the current logical clock instant — an authority cannot bill for the future. Any of those three failures is rejected with `ERR_FEE_PERIOD_INVALID`. The period is half-open, so the length used in the arithmetic below, referred to as the **period length**, is the exact count of whole UTC days from `period_start` up to but not including `period_end` — never a nominal 30.

The assessment then runs in a fixed five-step order that must not be rearranged: select the basis variant, compute the tier subtotal, add the patch-authority surcharge, add the late fee computed from the balance *as it stood before this assessment*, and finally book the result.

**Step one** reads the agency's `fee_basis`, which selects one of two genuinely different pricing variants. Under both variants the rate schedule is the same three-tier ladder: the first 50 billable positions are charged at 4,200 cents each, positions 51 through 200 at 3,600 cents each, and every position beyond 200 at 3,100 cents each.

**Step two under the `SNAPSHOT` variant** takes a headcount at assessment time and tiers it once. The headcount is the number of that agency's subscriber units whose current `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` or `SUSPENDED`; units in `INVENTORY` or `RETIRED` are excluded outright. Three tier amounts are then formed: the first is the smaller of the headcount and 50, multiplied by 4,200 cents; the second is the smaller of the headcount and 200, less 50, floored at zero, multiplied by 3,600 cents; the third is the headcount less 200, floored at zero, multiplied by 3,100 cents. Each of those three amounts is rounded half up to whole cents **individually**, and the tier subtotal is their sum. Under this variant every billable radio pays for a full period regardless of when it entered service, and the rounding happens per tier.

**Step two under the `PRORATED` variant** weights each radio by how long it was actually in service. The candidate list is wider here: it is the agency's subscriber units whose current `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED`, `SUSPENDED`, or `RETIRED`. Those candidates are ordered by ascending `created_at` — never by identifier — and that order assigns each one a one-based rank, which in turn selects its rate: rank 1 through 50 is charged at 4,200 cents, rank 51 through 200 at 3,600 cents, and rank 201 onward at 3,100 cents. Each candidate's days in service is the number of whole UTC days inside the half-open period for which the radio was billable: a day counts when the unit's `assigned_at` is not null, `assigned_at` falls at or before midnight UTC beginning that day, and either `retired_at` is null or `retired_at` falls strictly after that same midnight. A unit that was never assigned contributes zero days, and a suspension interval still counts — suspension does not stop billing. A unit assigned exactly at midnight UTC on a day does count that day. Each candidate's fee is its rate multiplied by its days in service, divided by the period length, **rounded half up to whole cents for that unit**, and the tier subtotal is the sum of those per-unit amounts. The rounding order is pinned: round per unit first, then add. Summing the fractional per-unit amounts and rounding once at the end is wrong and produces a different total. For the same fleet, this variant yields a strictly smaller — and differently rounded — total than `SNAPSHOT` whenever any unit was in service for fewer days than the full period.

The same day-counting rule is what writes `days_in_service_last_period` onto each subscriber unit. Every assessment recomputes that field for each of the agency's candidate units — those whose `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED`, `SUSPENDED`, or `RETIRED` — against the period just assessed, under either fee basis.

**Step three** applies the interoperability surcharge. An agency whose `interop_privilege` is `PATCH_AUTHORITY` pays a surcharge of 800 basis points — eight percent — of the tier subtotal, rounded half up to whole cents. An agency at `NONE` or `MONITOR` pays no surcharge at all.

**Step four** applies the late fee, and it reads the agency's `fee_balance_cents` **as it stands before this assessment is booked**. When that prior balance is greater than zero, the late fee is 150 basis points — one and a half percent — of the prior balance, rounded half up to whole cents; when the prior balance is zero or negative, the late fee is zero. The total assessed amount is the tier subtotal plus the surcharge plus the late fee.

**Step five** books it. `last_assessed_fee_cents` is set to the total assessed amount; `lifetime_assessed_cents` has that amount added to it; `fee_balance_cents` is then recomputed as `lifetime_assessed_cents` less `lifetime_paid_cents`; `last_assessment_period_start` and `last_assessment_period_end` record the period just assessed; and `payment_due_at` is set to `period_end` plus a 30-day payment grace. Re-assessing the same period is legitimate by design — the later assessment supersedes the earlier one and rebases the balance — which is exactly why the version pin of Section 2.6 is the only thing standing between the ledger and an out-of-date rebase. The assessment then finishes by re-evaluating the agency's `billing_status`, and the agency's `version` increments by one.

Payments run the other direction and may be recorded by the agency's own `AgencyRadioManager` or by a `SystemAdmin`. A payment names `amount_cents`, which must be at least 1 and must not exceed the agency's current `fee_balance_cents`; a payment below one cent or larger than the outstanding balance is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. On success `amount_cents` is added to `lifetime_paid_cents`, `fee_balance_cents` is recomputed as `lifetime_assessed_cents` less `lifetime_paid_cents`, `billing_status` is re-evaluated, and the agency's `version` increments by one. A payment that clears the balance entirely returns `billing_status` to `CURRENT` and restores the agency's `governance_vote_weight`.

**Billing status** is re-evaluated at the end of both a fee assessment and a payment, and it inspects only the agency's own row against the logical clock. When `fee_balance_cents` is zero or negative, the target status is `CURRENT`. Otherwise, when `payment_due_at` is not null and the current instant is at or after `payment_due_at` plus a further 60-day voting-suspension grace, the target status is `SUSPENDED_FROM_VOTING`. Otherwise, when `payment_due_at` is not null and the current instant is at or after `payment_due_at`, the target status is `DELINQUENT`. In every remaining case — including an outstanding balance with no due date yet recorded — the target status is `CURRENT`. The order of those tests matters: an agency 90 days past due is `SUSPENDED_FROM_VOTING`, not `DELINQUENT`.

Landing in `SUSPENDED_FROM_VOTING` triggers a four-link sanction cascade inside the same request. Because the agency lost its standing, its `governance_vote_weight` is forced to 0 and its `voting_eligible` flag to false. Because it is no longer eligible to vote, an agency holding `interop_privilege` `PATCH_AUTHORITY` is downgraded to `MONITOR` — a fiscally suspended agency does not get to patch other agencies' talkgroups. Because the privilege was withdrawn, every interoperability patch whose `requesting_agency_id` is this agency and whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped at now, `release_reason` `PRIVILEGE_REVOKED`, and `duration_minutes` recomputed. Because those patches were released, `active_patch_count` is recomputed on every talkgroup touched and the agency's `active_patch_request_count` is recomputed.

**Board voting weight** follows from fleet size, and it is recomputed whenever an agency's `billable_subscriber_count` or `billing_status` changes. A `SUSPENDED_FROM_VOTING` agency has a `governance_vote_weight` of 0 and a `voting_eligible` of false. Every other agency is `voting_eligible` and its weight is one vote plus one additional vote for each complete block of 250 billable subscribers — the block count is the billable subscriber count divided by 250 with any fraction discarded downward — with the whole figure capped at 5 votes. The board-level standing read reports the requested agency's `agency_id`, `billing_status`, `billable_subscriber_count`, `governance_vote_weight`, and `voting_eligible`, and alongside them two aggregates computed across the entire register: `total_eligible_weight`, the sum of `governance_vote_weight` over every agency whose `voting_eligible` is true (ineligible agencies contribute nothing and are excluded from the sum entirely), and `quorum_weight_required`, which is three fifths of `total_eligible_weight` rounded **up** to the next whole vote.

**Migration readiness** is a read-only projection that writes nothing, and it answers the authority's planning question: how much of the fleet could move to a next-generation air interface today. The next-generation standards are `P25_PHASE2` and `NG_LMR`; `P25_PHASE1` is the legacy standard. The evaluation scope is every subscriber unit whose `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` or `SUSPENDED`, optionally narrowed to a single agency by an `agency_id` filter; units in `INVENTORY` or `RETIRED` are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator. The size of that scope is reported as `evaluated_unit_count`.

A `basis` query parameter selects between two readiness definitions, and it defaults to `STRICT` when omitted. Under the `STRICT` basis a unit counts as ready only when its own `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED`, its `key_status` is `KEYED`, its `template_id` is not null, and the programming template it names has a `target_standard` of `P25_PHASE2` or `NG_LMR` — that is, the radio already carries a live key and a next-generation codeplug. Under the `PLANNED` basis a unit counts as ready when it satisfies the strict definition, or, additionally, when its `provision_status` is `ASSIGNED` and its owning agency has at least one programming change request whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `DEPLOYING` and whose template's `target_standard` is `P25_PHASE2` or `NG_LMR` — such radios count even though they are still programmed to `P25_PHASE1` and may still be `KEYLESS`. For the same fleet the `PLANNED` basis therefore yields a strictly higher percentage whenever such a change request exists. Note that under neither basis can a `SUSPENDED` unit ever be ready, even though suspended units sit in the denominator. The count of ready units is reported as `ready_unit_count`, and `readiness_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `ready_unit_count` divided by `evaluated_unit_count`, rounded half up to one decimal place — and exactly 0.0 when the scope is empty. The response also echoes the `basis` in force and the `agency_id` the projection was scoped to.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that register a genuinely new resource in the system — a member agency, a site, a template or a template revision, a change request, a subscriber unit, a keyset, an OTAR campaign, a talkgroup, an interoperability patch request, a site event, or a talkpath decision — answer with `201 Created`. Every operation that modifies an existing resource, including all action sub-paths, both configuration updates, the fee and payment writes, and the logical-clock control, answers with `200 OK`. Read endpoints answer with `200 OK`. All error responses use the status code listed for the error in Section 5. Endpoints marked "Any" below are readable by all eight roles. Request and response payloads are not repeated here; they are specified in the workflow narratives of Section 3.

| POST | `/radio-system/agencies` | Register a member agency | SystemAdmin |
