# Cottage-Food Pre-Order Bakery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A cottage-food operator bakes at home, under a permit that trades away the right to sell wholesale in exchange for the right to sell directly to neighbours. Everything about the business follows from that trade: production happens in one oven on one day, so the storefront cannot promise inventory it has not planned; the statute caps annual gross sales, so the eight-hundred-and-fifty-first dollar of the year is a compliance event and not just revenue; and every parcel that leaves the porch carries a label whose allergen statement must match the recipe that was actually baked. The operators we build for today run this on a spreadsheet, a messaging thread, and a shoebox of supplier lot codes. They lose money to over-selling, they lose evenings to re-planning batches by hand, and they lose sleep over the one thing they cannot reconstruct after the fact: which customer received product made from a flour lot that was later recalled.

This document specifies the API for a platform that carries all of it. The system is mounted under `/bakery` and is organised around a **pre-order window** — a bounded interval during which neighbours place orders against a single future bake day. A window meters three physically distinct kinds of capacity at once (orderable units, batch slots in the oven, and oven hours), and it moves through its own lifecycle from draft to open to locked at cutoff, then into production, handoff, and closure. Orders hold capacity for a short while before they are confirmed, which means available capacity in this system is *not monotonically decreasing*: an abandoned cart returns its units to the pool without anybody cancelling anything. A subscription club (the bread-and-cookie club) pre-claims a bounded share of each window ahead of walk-up traffic. Money is authorised, captured, tipped, partially refunded through a fixed waterfall, and settled into fortnightly payouts. Reviews roll up into a stored rating that can move backwards when a moderator hides a review.

Two cross-cutting obligations shape most of the hard rules below, and implementers should read the whole specification with them in mind. The first is **statutory compliance**: the permit posture of the operator is a global gate over order-affecting writes, only shelf-stable product classes may be sold direct, delivery may not leave the permitted radius, and the annual gross-sales ceiling is enforced order by order. The second is **traceability**: every recipe resolves to a recursive tree of components, sub-recipes, and physical ingredient lots, and when a lot is recalled the system must walk that tree forward through recipes, batches, order lines, orders, labels, and money — six hops deep — voiding exactly what must be voided and deliberately leaving alone what has already been handed to a customer.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Buyers, Regulators: Authentication and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the four role names below, the rejection is likewise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` — an unrecognised role is treated as a failure to authenticate rather than as a permission problem. When the role is recognised but is not among the roles the called endpoint admits, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The four roles are `Baker`, `Customer`, `PlatformAdmin`, and `HealthInspector`, spelled exactly so.

The `Baker` is the cottage-food operator. Recipes, ingredient lots, menu items, offer windows, batches, labels, and delivery routes all belong to a baker, and the baker is also the party who advances production, issues labels, captures payments, refunds them, and answers reviews. When `X-User-Role` is `Baker`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Baker`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Baker` compare the resource's owning-baker field to `X-User-ID`, and for an order the comparison is made against the baker that owns the order's offer window and customer. The `Customer` is the buyer: opening orders, holding capacity, confirming, paying and tipping, joining the club, skipping or confirming cycles, and reviewing fulfilled orders. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's customer field to `X-User-ID`. A role acting on a resource owned by a different instance of its own entity is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) — this applies to every `Customer`-callable endpoint that operates on an existing `Order`, `Subscription`, `SubscriptionCycle`, `Payment`, or `Review`, and to every `Baker`-callable endpoint that operates on an existing `Recipe`, `IngredientLot`, `MenuItem`, `OfferWindow`, `Batch`, `Label`, `DeliveryRoute`, or `Order`.

The remaining two roles are not entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and a platform administrator is therefore never subject to `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The platform administrator registers operators, moves permit posture, issues recalls, moderates reviews, and drives the two test-driving capabilities (the logical clock and the hold sweep). When `X-User-Role` is `HealthInspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque identifier not tied to any persisted entity and never compared to one. The health inspector is strictly read-only: it appears in the authorised-role list of exactly eighteen read endpoints (operators and their compliance summary, recipes and recipe components, allergen profiles, ingredient lots, menu items, offer windows, batches, and labels) and of no write endpoint anywhere in the system.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint narrative says otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

Filter query parameters are additive: supplying several narrows the result to records matching all of them. The filters each list endpoint accepts are named in §4's description column and in the workflow narratives — for example operators may be listed by `permit_status` and `jurisdiction_code`, customers by `baker_id` and `email`, recipes by `baker_id` and `recipe_status`, recipe components by `recipe_id`, `sub_recipe_id` and `ingredient_lot_id`, ingredient lots by `baker_id`, `lot_status` and `lot_code`, menu items by `baker_id`, `recipe_id`, `listing_status` and `product_class`, offer windows by `baker_id`, `window_status`, `plan_status` and `capacity_posture`, orders by `customer_id`, `offer_window_id`, `subscription_cycle_id`, `delivery_route_id`, `order_status`, `payment_status` and `handoff_status`, order lines by `order_id`, `menu_item_id`, `batch_id` and `line_status`, batches by `offer_window_id`, `menu_item_id`, `recipe_id` and `batch_status`, labels by `order_line_id`, `recipe_id` and `label_status`, subscriptions by `customer_id`, `menu_item_id`, `sub_status` and `billing_mode`, cycles by `subscription_id`, `offer_window_id` and `cycle_status`, payments by `order_id` and `payment_state`, money movements by `customer_id`, `order_id`, `payment_id`, `subscription_id`, `movement_kind`, `reason` and `ledger_scope`, delivery routes by `baker_id`, `offer_window_id` and `route_status`, and reviews by `order_id`, `menu_item_id`, `customer_id` and `review_status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning, and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at` (same format, refreshed on every mutation that touches the resource) and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that resource. The one exception is `MoneyMovement`: a money movement is an immutable ledger entry, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and nothing in the system ever mutates one after it is written. Where a workflow below says a cascade "bumps the version" of a parent, it means both the integer increment and the `updated_at` refresh.

Version increments are the currency of this system's concurrency control, so the rule is stated once here and relied on throughout: a request that mutates two entities increments both. Adding an order line, for instance, increments the line's owning `Order` **and** the `OfferWindow` whose capacity it consumed, because the window's capacity picture changed. Cascades are no different — when a recall voids nine orders, all nine order versions move, and so do the versions of every line, label, batch, and recipe the cascade touched.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrator-only test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with `current_utc` and `previous_utc`; setting the clock mutates no entity. Every temporal comparison in this document — cutoffs, hold expiry, pickup grace, skip deadlines, review windows, payout periods, credit expiry — is evaluated against this logical clock, referred to below as "the current instant" or "logical now". Instant comparisons are made at second granularity.

Path parameters are validated left to right. A request to a nested path resolves each templated segment in the order it appears in the URL, and the first unresolvable segment produces its own 404 before any later segment is examined: an `{offer_window_id}` that does not resolve gives `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, an `{order_id}` or `{order_line_id}` that does not resolve gives `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, and every other templated segment — `{baker_id}`, `{customer_id}`, `{recipe_id}`, `{recipe_component_id}`, `{ingredient_lot_id}`, `{menu_item_id}`, `{batch_id}`, `{label_id}`, `{subscription_id}`, `{subscription_cycle_id}`, `{payment_id}`, `{money_movement_id}`, `{delivery_route_id}`, `{review_id}` — gives `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Identifiers supplied in a request *body* follow the same idea with their own codes: a body `offer_window_id` that does not resolve gives `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, a body `menu_item_id` that does not resolve gives `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, and every other body foreign key gives `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, those remaining keys being examined in alphabetical order of their field names so that the reported failure is deterministic when two of them are bad.

### 2.5. Money, Units, and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a request or a response. Where a monetary computation produces a fraction — a percentage tax, a percentage tip, a proportional shortfall credit, a percentage club discount — the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Where a computation is a pure sum or difference of amounts already in cents, no rounding is involved and none should be applied.

Two non-monetary quantities also carry declared precision. Oven hours are rounded half up to **2 decimal places**, both per menu item and where a window's consumed oven hours are summed. A menu item's stored rating average is rounded half up to **2 decimal places**. Distances are held in kilometres as decimal numbers; where a route's total distance is summed it is likewise expressed to 2 decimal places, rounded half up. Unit counts, batch slots, and stop numbers are integers.

One rounding **order** is pinned and is a frequent source of off-by-a-cent errors: sales tax is rounded per line and only then summed across the lines of an order. Summing the taxable line totals first and rounding the aggregate once is the rejected reading and can differ by whole cents from the pinned one. As a worked illustration, two taxable lines of 4600 and 5400 cents produce 334 and 392 cents of tax respectively, for an order tax total of 726 cents; rounding a single 10000-cent aggregate at the same rate would have produced 725.

### 2.6. The Rejection Ladder: A Total, Deterministic Evaluation Order

Requests in this domain routinely violate several rules at once — a suspended operator adding a twelfth line to a locked window past its cutoff with a stale version number is four failures in one call — so the order in which failures are reported is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. The system evaluates its checks as a single **ladder of 55 rungs**, numbered 1 through 55. For any request that violates more than one rung, **the lowest-numbered violated rung wins and no later rung is evaluated at all**. The ladder is total: every rejection in this specification is one of these rungs, and no two rungs can both be reported for one request.

Two rungs are **ordered global write gates** that pre-empt most of what follows them, and each carries an explicit carve-out list so that the operations which must remain reachable during a compliance problem stay reachable. Rung 15 is the permit gate; rung 16 is the locked-window gate. Their exact scopes are given in place below.

**Rungs 1–4 — the request itself.** Rung 1: both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Rung 2: `X-User-Role` names one of the four declared roles, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Rung 3: the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Rung 4: where a body is expected it parses as a JSON object, and on list endpoints `limit` is between 1 and 100 inclusive with `offset` zero or greater, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). All four rungs apply to every endpoint in the system.

**Rungs 5–10 — existence, path before body.** Rung 5: an `offer_window_id` appearing in the path resolves to an offer window, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND` (404); this applies to every endpoint whose path contains that segment. Rung 6: an `order_id`, and then an `order_line_id`, appearing in the path resolves, evaluated left to right, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` (404); this applies to every endpoint whose path contains either segment, including the standalone order-line read. Rung 7: every other templated path segment resolves to its entity, evaluated left to right, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 8: a body `offer_window_id` resolves, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND` (404); this applies to order creation, club-cycle generation, and delivery-route creation. Rung 9: a body `menu_item_id` resolves, otherwise `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404); this applies to line addition, subscription creation, and review submission. Rung 10: every other body foreign key resolves to its entity, examined in the alphabetical order of the field names, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404); this applies to every endpoint that accepts a foreign key in its body.

**Rungs 11–12 — ownership.** Rung 11: when the acting role is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` equals the `customer_id` that owns the target order, subscription, club cycle, payment, or review, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403); this applies to every `Customer`-callable endpoint operating on an existing resource. Rung 12: when the acting role is `Baker`, `X-User-ID` equals the `baker_id` that owns the target recipe, ingredient lot, menu item, offer window, batch, label, or delivery route — or the operator behind the target order — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403); this applies to every `Baker`-callable endpoint operating on an existing resource. Note that both ownership rungs sit *after* existence: a request naming a resource that does not exist gets a 404, never a 403.

**Rungs 13–14 — body shape and enumerations.** Rung 13, on every creating or modifying endpoint: every required body field is present with its declared type, integers are zero or greater wherever the field is a quantity or an amount, and a review's `rating` is between 1 and 5 inclusive; violations are `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Rung 14, on the same endpoints: every enum-valued body field carries a member of that field's declared value set, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). This enum routing is **exhaustive and closed**: `channel`, `handoff_mode`, `disposition`, `pricing_basis`, `product_class`, `oven_profile`, `cadence`, `billing_mode`, `tip_mode`, `outcome`, `to_status`, `mode`, `movement_kind`, and the `severity` member of an allergen-avoidance entry all route here, and **no other field routes here** — a bad value in any field not on that list is a rung-13 `ERR_VALIDATION` instead.

**Rung 15 — the permit gate.** The owning operator's `permit_status` is `ACTIVE`; a `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED`, or `CAP_REACHED` permit blocks the call with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). This gate applies to exactly twelve operations: menu-item creation, menu-item listing-status changes, offer-window creation, offer-window lifecycle advancement, order creation, line addition, line quantity or disposition patching, order confirmation, subscription creation, club-cycle generation, club-cycle confirmation, and payment authorisation. It carves out — and therefore **never** applies to — permit-status changes themselves, ingredient-lot recalls, order cancellation, refunds, review submission, and the two administrative capabilities (clock setting and hold sweeping). Reinstatement, recalls, cancellations, refunds, and reviews consequently stay reachable while a permit is suspended. Operations not named in either list, such as recipe or ingredient-lot writes, batch advancement, yield confirmation, label issuance, handoff scheduling and outcome recording, delivery-route work, capture, and review responses, are not subject to this rung at all.

**Rung 16 — the locked-window gate.** The offer window the call touches does not have `window_status` `LOCKED`; if it does, the call is rejected with `ERR_WINDOW_LOCKED` (409). This gate applies to exactly nine operations: order creation, line addition, line patching, order confirmation, order cancellation, handoff scheduling, club-cycle generation, club-cycle confirmation, and club-cycle skipping. It carves out — and therefore never applies to — window locking, cutoff extension, production planning, window advancement, batch advancement, yield confirmation, label issuance, handoff-outcome recording, and delivery-route status changes, all of which are legal only once the window is locked or later.

**Rungs 17–19 — window timing and state.** Rung 17: the current instant is strictly before the window's `order_cutoff_at`; an instant exactly equal to the cutoff is already past it, and the rejection is `ERR_CUTOFF_PASSED` (409). This rung applies to four operations: line addition, line patching, order confirmation, and club-cycle confirmation. Rung 18: the window's `window_status` is `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (409); this applies to six operations — order creation, line addition, line patching, order confirmation, club-cycle generation, and club-cycle confirmation. Note that rung 17 precedes rung 18, so a call against a window that is both past cutoff and not open reports the cutoff. Rung 19: the target entity's current state on the relevant axis is a declared source state of the requested transition, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Rung 19 applies to every state-transition endpoint with two carve-outs: it does **not** apply to yield confirmation, whose batch-state precondition is owned exclusively by rung 36 so that `ERR_BATCH_NOT_BAKED` remains reachable, and it does **not** apply to refund creation, whose payment-state precondition is owned exclusively by rung 49 so that `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CAPTURED` remains reachable in the same way. A refund therefore takes no source-state check of its own on either the payment's `payment_state` or the order's `payment_status` axis; rung 49 is the whole of it.

**Rungs 20–23 — stale-version rejection.** Rung 20: `expected_offer_window_version` equals the offer window's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_OFFER_WINDOW_VERSION` (412), on line addition and line patching. Rung 21: `expected_order_version` equals the order's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (412), on refund creation. Rung 22: `expected_recipe_version` equals the recipe's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_RECIPE_VERSION` (412), on label issuance. Rung 23: `expected_subscription_version` equals the subscription's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION` (412), on club-cycle confirmation. §2.7 gives the reasoning behind each.

**Rungs 24–28 — statutory and traceability safety.** Rung 24: the order's `channel` is `DTC`, because wholesale is not permitted to a cottage-food operation, otherwise `ERR_WHOLESALE_NOT_PERMITTED` (422); this applies to order creation and order confirmation. Rung 25: every referenced menu item's `product_class` is `SHELF_STABLE`, since a `REFRIGERATION_REQUIRED` item may not be sold direct, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_CLASS_NOT_DTC_ELIGIBLE` (422); this applies to line addition, subscription creation, and menu-item listing-status changes. Rung 26: the recipe backing every referenced menu item has `recipe_status` `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_RECIPE_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409); this applies to line addition, menu-item listing-status changes, and label issuance. Rung 27: no recipe component in the referenced recipe's transitive closure has `recalled_component` set, and no referenced ingredient lot has `lot_status` `RECALLED`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_RECALLED` (409); this applies to recipe publication, component attachment, line addition, batch advancement, and label issuance. Rung 28: the recipe's component and sub-recipe closure is acyclic and its `tree_depth` is at most 5, otherwise `ERR_RECIPE_CYCLE_DETECTED` (422); this applies to component attachment and recipe publication.

Rungs 25 and 26 are declared against menu-item listing-status changes without naming a target posture. Both readings should be regarded as open: the narrow reading is that they guard only transitions **into** `LISTED` (so a refrigerated or unpublished-recipe item can still be paused or delisted), and the broad reading is that they guard **every** listing-status call on that endpoint (so an item whose backing recipe later left `PUBLISHED` could not even be delisted). Nothing in this specification settles which applies.

**Rungs 29–33 — the consumer-safety block.** Rung 29: no allergen in the order's allergen union matches an avoidance the acting customer has recorded at `ANAPHYLACTIC` severity, otherwise `ERR_ALLERGEN_HARD_BLOCK` (422) on order confirmation. Rung 30: where an allergen matches an avoidance recorded at `INTOLERANCE` severity, the order's `allergen_ack` is true, otherwise `ERR_ALLERGEN_ACK_REQUIRED` (422) on order confirmation. Rung 31: when the order's `handoff_mode` is `PORCH_SELF_SERVE`, its `unattended_consent` is true, otherwise `ERR_UNATTENDED_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (422) on handoff scheduling. Rung 32: when the order's `handoff_mode` is `PORCH_SELF_SERVE` and any pending line's menu item has `temp_sensitive` true, the order's `cooler_provided` is true, otherwise `ERR_COOLER_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (422) on handoff scheduling. Rung 33: when the order's `handoff_mode` is `DELIVERY`, the customer's `delivery_distance_km` is at most the operator's `permitted_radius_km`, otherwise `ERR_OUT_OF_PERMITTED_ZONE` (422); this rung applies to **both** order confirmation and handoff scheduling.

**Rungs 34–37 — order shape, labels, batches, duplicates.** Rung 34: the order has at most 12 lines that are not `RELEASED` and not `RECALL_VOID`, and the requested `quantity` is between 1 and 120 inclusive; a violation of either half is `ERR_MAX_LINES_EXCEEDED` (422) on line addition and line patching. Rung 35: on a handed-off outcome, every order line that is not `RELEASED` and not `RECALL_VOID` has a label whose `label_status` is `ISSUED`; a line whose label is `SUPERSEDED`, `VOID`, `CONSUMED`, or altogether missing fails with `ERR_LABEL_SUPERSEDED` (409). Rung 36: the batch's `batch_status` is `BAKED`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_BAKED` (409) on yield confirmation. Rung 37: no review already exists for this `order_id`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW` (409) on review submission.

**Rungs 38–42 — temporal deadlines.** Rung 38: the current instant is strictly before the order's `hold_expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED` (409); this applies to order confirmation and line patching, but deliberately not to line addition, since adding a line to an order whose hold lapsed is how a buyer re-opens it. Rung 39: when the order's `handoff_mode` is `PICKUP_COUNTER` or `PORCH_SELF_SERVE`, the current instant is strictly before the order's `pickup_forfeit_at`, otherwise `ERR_PICKUP_GRACE_EXPIRED` (409) on a handed-off outcome; this rung does **not** apply to the delivery pipeline, which has no grace check at all. Rung 40: the current instant is strictly before the cycle's `skip_deadline_at`, otherwise `ERR_SKIP_DEADLINE_PASSED` (409) on a cycle skip. Rung 41: the current instant is strictly before the order's `review_window_closes_at`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409) on review submission. Rung 42: the current instant is strictly before the review's `created_at` plus the baker response window of 14 days, otherwise `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409) on a baker response.

**Rungs 43–45 — counted caps and window shape.** Rung 43: the subscription's `pause_count` is below the pause cap of 2, otherwise `ERR_PAUSE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409) on a status change to `PAUSED`. Rung 44: a new offer window's instants are ordered so that `opens_at` is strictly before `order_cutoff_at`, `order_cutoff_at` is at or before `production_date`, `production_date` is at or before `handoff_date`, and `handoff_date` is strictly before `handoff_window_end_at`; a violation is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) on window creation. Rung 45: the order's `rebook_count` is below the rebook maximum of 1 and its `forfeit_latched` is false, otherwise `ERR_REBOOK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409) on handoff scheduling when the order's `handoff_status` is `MISSED`.

**Rungs 46–51 — fiscal limits.** Rung 46: for a percentage tip, `tip_pct` is between 0 and 0.30 inclusive; for a fixed tip, `tip_fixed_cents` is between 0 and 5000 inclusive; either violation is `ERR_TIP_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422) on payment authorisation. Rung 47: when the order's `handoff_mode` is `DELIVERY`, its `subtotal_cents` is at least the delivery minimum of 3500 cents, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_MINIMUM_NOT_MET` (422) on handoff scheduling. Rung 48: the requested `apply_credit_cents` is at most the customer's `credit_balance_cents`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT` (422) on payment authorisation. Rung 49: the payment's `payment_state` is `CAPTURED`, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CAPTURED` (409) on refund creation. Rung 50: the requested `amount_cents` is at most the payment's `captured_cents` less its `refunded_cents`, otherwise `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_CAPTURED` (422) on refund creation. Rung 51: the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` plus this order's counted gross does not **exceed** its `annual_cap_cents` — landing exactly on the cap is admitted — otherwise `ERR_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422); this rung applies to order confirmation and club-cycle confirmation.

**Rungs 52–55 — capacity.** Rung 52: the window's `reserved_units` plus the subscription's `units_per_cycle` does not exceed the club's share cap, which is the window's `capacity_units` multiplied by 0.30 and rounded **down** to a whole unit, otherwise `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_RESERVE_SHARE_EXCEEDED` (422) on club-cycle generation. Rung 53: the window's remaining orderable units are at least the unit delta the request asks for, otherwise `ERR_UNITS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422); this applies to line addition, line patching, and club-cycle generation. Rung 54: the window's remaining batch slots are at least the additional slots the request would require, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422) on line addition and line patching. Rung 55: the window's remaining oven hours are at least the additional oven hours the request would require, otherwise `ERR_OVEN_HOURS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422) on line addition and line patching. The three capacity rungs are evaluated in that order, so a line that overruns two axes at once reports the units failure.

A number of state transitions carry preconditions that are neither a source-state check nor any of the 55 rungs above — the window lifecycle's four temporal gates (opening at or after `opens_at`, locking at or after `order_cutoff_at`, entering production at or after `production_date`, opening handoff at or after `handoff_date`), the aggregate gates (handoff may open only when every batch on the window has reached `ALLOCATED`, `CLOSED`, or `SCRAPPED_RECALL`; a window may close only when no order on it is still `READY`; an open window may be cancelled only when no order on it is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, or `READY`; a cutoff may be extended only while the window's `plan_status` is `UNPLANNED` and its `cutoff_extension_count` is below the extension maximum of 1; a plan may be torn down only while every batch on the window is still `PLANNED`; a recipe may be retired only when no menu item backed by it is `LISTED`; a route may be sequenced only when at least one order is assigned to it and completed only when no assigned order is still `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`; a lot may be released only while the current instant is before its `expires_at`, and marked expired only at or after it), and the club gates (a membership may be activated only while its club menu item is `LISTED`, marked past due only when some placed cycle's generated order is still `UNPAID`, returned to active only when no such arrears remain, and cancelled from `PAST_DUE` only once 72 hours have elapsed since `past_due_since`). All of these are genuine, enforced preconditions: a request that fails one is rejected and nothing is mutated. The specification does **not** settle which error identifier such a rejection carries, and two readings are equally defensible — that an inadmissible transition is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) because the requested move is not available from the entity's current situation, or that it is reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) because the request asks for something the current facts do not support. Implementers should treat this as an open point rather than assume the reading that happens to be convenient.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Five Write Paths

Five write paths in this system depend on a picture of a parent entity that a client has already read, and each of them therefore demands that the client echo back the `version` it observed. The field is a **required, non-optional** input on those five endpoints; if the echoed value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected and nothing is mutated. Endpoints that are not on this list take no version field at all.

1. `POST /bakery/orders/{order_id}/lines` requires `expected_offer_window_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `OfferWindow`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OFFER_WINDOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Adding a line legitimately consumes window capacity, so a writer whose view of the window predates another line addition, a hold release, or a club reservation is working from a stale capacity picture and must re-read before trying again.
2. `PATCH /bakery/orders/{order_id}/lines/{order_line_id}` requires the same `expected_offer_window_version` against the same parent, with the same `ERR_STALE_OFFER_WINDOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rejection. A quantity change is a re-pricing and a re-capacity operation whose admissibility depends on the window's current remaining units, batch slots, and oven hours, so it must carry the version the client priced against.
3. `POST /bakery/labels` requires `expected_recipe_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Recipe` the label derives from. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECIPE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A republish supersedes every previously issued label, so a label minted from a stale recipe view would carry an allergen statement that no longer matches the published recipe.
4. `POST /bakery/payments/{payment_id}/refunds` requires `expected_order_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Order` behind the payment. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A shortfall allocation or an earlier partial refund advances the refundable remainder and the waterfall's leg positions, so a refund computed from a stale order view would draw from legs that are already consumed.
5. `POST /bakery/subscription-cycles/{subscription_cycle_id}/confirm` requires `expected_subscription_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the owning `Subscription`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBSCRIPTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Confirming a cycle debits prepaid balance and may follow a pause, a resume, or another cycle's confirmation, all of which change the balance the confirmation prices against.

Because the version check sits at rungs 20 through 23 of the ladder, it is evaluated *after* authentication, existence, ownership, body shape, the two write gates, the cutoff and window-state rungs, and the source-state rung — and *before* every statutory, safety, fiscal, and capacity rung. A request that is both stale and over capacity reports staleness.

### 2.8. The Cottage-Food Statutory Envelope

Four statutory facts constrain the entire product and are worth stating once, up front, because they surface again in a dozen places. **Wholesale is not permitted.** An order's `channel` may be recorded as `WHOLESALE` — the value is a member of the field's declared set — but such an order can never be created or confirmed; the attempt is rejected as described at rung 24. **Only shelf-stable product may be sold direct.** A menu item's `product_class` is either `SHELF_STABLE` or `REFRIGERATION_REQUIRED`, and only the former is eligible for direct-to-consumer sale. **Delivery may not leave the permitted radius**, which is recorded per operator as `permitted_radius_km` and defaults to 40.0 kilometres. **Annual gross sales are capped**, per operator and per calendar year, by `annual_cap_cents`, which defaults to 8,500,000 cents — that is, eighty-five thousand dollars — and is measured over the year recorded in `cap_year`, which defaults to 2026.

What counts toward that ceiling is precisely defined, and the exclusion is as important as the inclusion. Counted gross is the sum, over the operator's orders whose `order_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `READY`, or `FULFILLED` and whose `placed_at` falls in the calendar year named by `cap_year`, of `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_total_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents`; from that sum is subtracted, for refund movements belonging to those same counted orders, the sum of `leg_subtotal_cents` plus `leg_delivery_fee_cents`. Orders in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_HOLD`, `EXPIRED_HOLD`, `CANCELLED`, `FORFEITED`, and `VOIDED_RECALL` are excluded — and `FORFEITED` is excluded even though the customer was charged and kept nothing back. **Tips never count**: the flag exposed to regulators as `tip_counts_toward_cap` is false, which has the consequence that a refund which only consumes the tip leg of the waterfall leaves the counted figure completely unchanged. The remaining headroom, `cap_remaining_cents`, is the operator's `annual_cap_cents` less its `annual_gross_sales_cents`.

The permit itself is the enforcement mechanism. An operator's `permit_status` is one of `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, and `CAP_REACHED`, and only `ACTIVE` admits the twelve order-affecting writes named at rung 15. Two statutory strings are fixed text that must be reproduced exactly wherever they appear. The cottage-food statement is `Made in a home kitchen that is not subject to routine state inspection.` The cross-contact advisory is `Made in a home kitchen that also handles wheat, egg, milk, soy, peanut and tree nuts.`

### 2.9. Three Axes of Capacity and the Derived Posture

An offer window meters three physically different resources simultaneously, and a request must clear all three. `capacity_units` is the count of orderable units the window will sell and defaults to 240. `capacity_batch_slots` is the number of slots the oven schedule has room for and defaults to 12. `capacity_oven_hours` is the total oven time available and defaults to 18.0 hours. Against these sit three consumption figures, all recomputed on every capacity-affecting write and all exposed on the window itself: `confirmed_units`, `held_units`, and `reserved_units`.

Remaining orderable units are the window's `capacity_units` less confirmed units, less held units, less reserved units. For this computation, **confirmed units** are the summed `quantity` of every order line that is neither `RELEASED` nor `RECALL_VOID` whose owning order's `order_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `READY`, or `FULFILLED`; **held units** are the summed `quantity` of every line that is `PENDING` whose owning order is `PENDING_HOLD` and whose `hold_expires_at` is strictly in the future — a hold whose expiry instant equals the current instant no longer counts; and **reserved units** are the summed `reserved_units` of every club cycle on the window whose `cycle_status` is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`. The stored `confirmed_units` field is separately documented on the window as counting lines whose `line_status` is `ALLOCATED`, `FULFILLED`, `SHORTED`, or `CREDITED`, plus `PENDING` lines of `CONFIRMED` orders. These two descriptions of the same figure do not coincide in every situation — a `PENDING` line on an order that has advanced to `IN_PRODUCTION` is counted by the first and not the second, and an `ALLOCATED` line on a `FORFEITED` order is counted by the second and not the first — and this specification deliberately leaves open which of the two definitions governs the stored field.

Batch-slot demand is computed per menu item and never on the aggregate. For each menu item present on the window, its demand in units is the summed `quantity` of exactly the lines counted above; `slot_count` is that demand divided by the 24 units a slot holds and **rounded up** to a whole slot; and `planned_yield_units` is `slot_count` multiplied by that item's `yield_units_per_slot`. The window's `slots_used` is the sum of the per-item slot counts and `batch_slots_remaining` is `capacity_batch_slots` less `slots_used`. Rounding up per item rather than on the total matters: 25 units of one item and 25 of another need two slots plus two slots, four in all, where rounding up a combined fifty units would have suggested three.

Oven hours follow the slot counts and are the system's clearest dual-variant computation. When a menu item's `oven_profile` is `STANDARD`, its oven hours are its `slot_count` multiplied by the fixed 1.5 hours a slot costs, rounded half up to 2 decimals — the item's own `oven_hours_per_batch` value is ignored entirely in this variant. When the profile is `LONG_BAKE`, the item's own `oven_hours_per_batch` replaces that fixed figure, so the same slot count can consume a quite different share of the window's oven capacity. The window's `oven_hours_used` is the sum over menu items, and `oven_hours_remaining` is `capacity_oven_hours` less `oven_hours_used`.

From those three axes the window derives `capacity_posture`, one of `OPEN_CAPACITY`, `CONSTRAINED`, and `EXHAUSTED`, recomputed after every capacity-affecting write and on every capacity read. The posture is `EXHAUSTED` when remaining units are zero, or remaining batch slots are zero, or remaining oven hours are zero or negative. Otherwise, taking unit utilisation as confirmed plus held plus reserved units over `capacity_units`, slot utilisation as `slots_used` over `capacity_batch_slots`, and oven utilisation as `oven_hours_used` over `capacity_oven_hours`, the posture is `CONSTRAINED` when the greatest of those three utilisations is at or above 0.80 — the comparison is inclusive — and `OPEN_CAPACITY` otherwise. Utilisations are compared as exact rational arithmetic rather than as rounded percentages. Because the three axes interact through that maximum, a single line can leave unit utilisation at a fifth of capacity and still drive the posture straight to `EXHAUSTED` by consuming the last batch slot.

Posture is explicitly **not monotone**. When a hold expires or a club reservation is released, remaining capacity rises with nobody having cancelled anything, and the posture moves back down through `CONSTRAINED` toward `OPEN_CAPACITY`. Every posture change increments the window's `version`.

A window's `version` nevertheless moves **at most once per request**, and merely refreshing the four derived figures — `confirmed_units`, `held_units`, `reserved_units`, and `capacity_posture` — is not by itself an increment. The version is incremented when the request writes the window's own recorded state (a status axis, one of its five instants, its `cutoff_extension_count`), and on the four capacity-affecting writes whose narratives declare the window's version bumped without touching that recorded state — line addition, line quantity patching, the administrative hold sweep, and club-cycle reservation — and otherwise **only** when the recomputation leaves `capacity_posture` on a different value than it found. Order confirmation, order cancellation, a cycle skip, a cycle confirmation, a membership pause or cancellation that releases reserved cycles, and the recall cascade therefore move the window's `version` exactly when they move its posture and not otherwise. A request that both writes the window and moves its posture still increments the `version` by exactly 1.

### 2.10. Six Conservation Invariants

Six quantities in this system are conserved, and the invariants below must hold after the writes named with each. They are stated as invariants rather than as procedures deliberately: an implementation that satisfies them cannot silently lose units or cents.

**Yield conservation.** For a batch, the summed `allocated_units` of its order lines whose `line_status` is `ALLOCATED`, `SHORTED`, `CREDITED`, or `FULFILLED`, plus the batch's `surplus_units`, equals the batch's `actual_yield_units`. This must hold after yield confirmation on **both** forks — the fully-supplied fork and the under-yield fork. A violation is a rejected request carrying `ERR_VALIDATION`.

**Window unit conservation.** A window's `capacity_units` equals its remaining units plus `confirmed_units` plus `held_units` plus `reserved_units`, where remaining units are computed as in §2.9. This must hold after every capacity-affecting write: line addition, line quantity patching, order confirmation, order cancellation, hold expiry, club-cycle reservation, club-cycle release, and window locking.

**Order money conservation.** For every order that *has* a payment — club orders settled entirely from a prepaid balance have none and are covered by credit-ledger conservation instead — the payment's `captured_cents` less its `refunded_cents` equals the order's `grand_total_cents` less the summed `amount_cents` of the order's refund movements. Additionally, for every refund movement, its `amount_cents` equals the sum of its four legs `leg_tip_cents`, `leg_delivery_fee_cents`, `leg_subtotal_cents`, and `leg_credit_cents`. This must hold after payment authorisation, capture, refund creation, and every cascade that issues a refund.

**Credit ledger conservation.** A customer's `credit_balance_cents` equals the summed `amount_cents` of that customer's money movements whose `ledger_scope` is `CUSTOMER_CREDIT`, whose `movement_kind` is `SHORTFALL_CREDIT`, `PREPAY_DEPOSIT`, or `CREDIT_REDEEM`, and whose `expires_at` is either absent or strictly in the future. Separately, a subscription's `prepaid_balance_cents` equals the summed `amount_cents` of the movements whose `subscription_id` is that subscription and whose `ledger_scope` is `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAID`. Both must hold after every write that grants, restores, or redeems credit.

**Gross sales conservation.** An operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` equals the counted-gross figure defined in §2.8 — order money over the four counted statuses, less the subtotal and delivery-fee legs of refunds, with tips excluded. This must hold after order confirmation, order cancellation, every refund, and both the recall and shortfall cascades.

**Rating rollup conservation.** A menu item's `rating_count` equals the number of reviews on that item whose `counts_toward_rollup` is true, and its `rating_avg` is absent exactly when `rating_count` is below 3. This must hold after review submission, a baker response, and a moderation change.

### 2.11. Cascades, Propagation, and Nested Reads

Three cascades in this system reach beyond the entity a request names, and their depth is part of the contract. The deepest is the **recall cascade**, which runs six hops from an ingredient lot through components, recipes, batches, order lines, orders, and finally labels and money; its maximum declared depth is 6 and §3.10 walks every hop, including the two places where it deliberately stops. The **label supersession cascade** runs three hops from a recipe republication through labels and the handoff gate to menu-item allergen summaries. The **shortfall cascade** runs four hops from a batch's yield confirmation through line allocation, shortfall crediting, and the order's money axis. In all three, every entity a hop mutates has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, and a hop that does not fire mutates nothing.

Parent and child states propagate downward, never upward by accident. When a window advances, the orders on it move with it; when an order is handed off, its lines and their labels move with it; when a recipe is republished, the labels pinned to its previous published version move with it. The converse is not true: nothing a child does changes its parent's own status axis, with the sole exceptions explicitly named in §3 — a confirm that crosses the sales ceiling latching the operator's permit posture, a capacity-affecting line change moving the window's `capacity_posture`, and the plan axis reaching `RECONCILED` when the window closes.

Read endpoints that expose computed figures recompute them in the request. Reading a customer refreshes `credit_balance_cents`; reading an operator's compliance summary refreshes `annual_gross_sales_cents` and `payout_net_cents`; reading a window's capacity refreshes `confirmed_units`, `held_units`, `reserved_units`, and `capacity_posture`. Plain resource reads and list endpoints have no side effects.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding an Operator, the Permit Posture, and the Compliance Summary

A platform administrator registers a cottage-food operator by supplying the trade name as `display_name`, the permitting jurisdiction as `jurisdiction_code` (a code of the form CA-ALAMEDA), and the first instant of the current payout period as `payout_period_start`. Three statutory parameters may be supplied and otherwise take their defaults: `annual_cap_cents` defaults to 8,500,000, `cap_year` defaults to 2026, and `permitted_radius_km` defaults to 40.0. A fourth optional flag, `shared_kitchen`, defaults to false and records whether the home kitchen also produces for other operators; it feeds the cross-contact advisory described in §3.2. A newly registered operator begins in `permit_status` `PENDING` with `annual_gross_sales_cents` at 0 and `payout_net_cents` at 0.

Permit posture is administrator-driven and cyclic. Moving from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` — the request carries the target posture as `to_status` — sets `annual_gross_sales_cents` to 0 and bumps the operator's `version`; this is the moment the operator becomes able to perform the twelve order-affecting writes of rung 15. `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` is the compliance stop: from that instant, every one of those twelve writes for this operator fails at rung 15 with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`, while recalls, cancellations, refunds, reviews, and reinstatement itself remain available. `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE` is a plain reinstatement loop — no order state anywhere is rewound. Each of these moves increments the operator's `version` by one.

The fourth posture is not requested by anyone. `CAP_REACHED` is latched by the system at the *end* of an order confirmation, when the confirm's recomputation of counted gross leaves `annual_gross_sales_cents` at or above `annual_cap_cents`. The sequencing here is deliberate and easy to get wrong: **the confirm that crosses the ceiling succeeds**, and the crossing latches the gate for the *next* confirm, which then fails at rung 15 with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`. An order whose own counted gross would push the figure strictly *past* the ceiling never gets that far — it is refused earlier, at rung 51, with `ERR_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and an order landing exactly on the ceiling is admitted. Escaping `CAP_REACHED` requires a new statutory year: an administrator moves the posture back to `ACTIVE` while supplying a `cap_year` strictly greater than the operator's current one, which sets `cap_year` to the requested value, resets `annual_gross_sales_cents` to 0, and bumps the `version`.

Regulators and operators alike read a computed compliance summary, which recomputes and then reports `annual_gross_sales_cents`, `cap_remaining_cents`, `annual_cap_cents`, `cap_year`, `permit_status`, `payout_net_cents`, and the constant `tip_counts_toward_cap`, which is always false.

Customers are registered against exactly one operator. The payload carries the `baker_id` of that operator, the buyer's `display_name`, an `email` that must be unique within the operator (the same address may appear under two different operators), and `delivery_distance_km`, the road distance in kilometres from the home kitchen to this buyer's address. Three optional fields shape later handoffs: `allergen_avoidances`, an array of objects each pairing an `allergen` string with a `severity` of `ANAPHYLACTIC` or `INTOLERANCE`, defaulting to empty; `unattended_consent`, a standing consent to unattended porch handoff, defaulting to false; and `cooler_provided`, recording that the buyer will leave a cooler out for temperature-sensitive porch handoffs, also defaulting to false. A new customer starts with `credit_balance_cents` at 0. The order-level consent flags described in §3.8 carry the same two names, and this specification does not settle whether a customer's standing flags flow onto an order that omits them — one reading is that the customer-level values act as defaults inherited by each new order, the other is that they are informational only and the order's own flags are the sole gate the safety rungs consult.

### 3.2. Recipes, Sub-Recipe Trees, Ingredient Lots, and the Allergen Profile

A recipe is created with the owning `baker_id` and a `name`, and begins in `recipe_status` `DRAFT` with `published_version` at 0. Composition happens by attaching components: each carries a `component_name`, its `quantity_grams` per batch, and optionally `declared_allergens` (an array of allergen strings declared directly on that component, defaulting to empty), a `sub_recipe_id` pointing at another recipe, and an `ingredient_lot_id` pointing at the physical lot that supplies it. A component may reference a sub-recipe, a lot, both, or neither. Each component also carries `recalled_component`, a system-maintained flag that starts false and is latched true when the lot it references is recalled.

Because a component may point at another recipe, a recipe is really the root of a tree, and the same recipe can be reachable through several component paths. The **allergen union** of a recipe is computed by walking that tree from the root at depth 1: at each recipe, its components are visited in ascending order of their `created_at`, and for each component the union accumulates the component's own `declared_allergens`, then — where the component names a lot — that lot's `allergens`, then — where the component names a sub-recipe — the full union of that sub-recipe computed one level deeper. The result stored in `allergen_union` is the accumulated set, deduplicated and sorted ascending as lowercase strings; traversal order affects nothing but the walk itself. Alongside it, `tree_depth` is 1 plus the greatest depth of any sub-recipe subtree beneath the root, or simply 1 when the root has no sub-recipe children. Depth three and beyond is ordinary here — a cookie recipe references a cookie-dough sub-recipe which references a spice-blend sub-recipe — but the tree may not exceed a depth of **5**, and a recipe reachable from itself through any chain of sub-recipe links is a cycle. Either condition is refused with `ERR_RECIPE_CYCLE_DETECTED`, at rung 28, on both component attachment and publication. Attaching a component recomputes the owning recipe's `tree_depth` and `allergen_union` and bumps its `version`.

The **cross-contact advisory** is derived separately and this separation is load-bearing. The advisory applies when the operator's `shared_kitchen` is true, or when any ingredient lot anywhere in the recipe's transitive closure carries a non-empty `cross_contact_allergens` list. When it applies, `cross_contact_advisory` holds the exact advisory text quoted in §2.8; otherwise it is absent. An allergen that appears *only* in some lot's `cross_contact_allergens` therefore never enters `allergen_union`, and consequently can never trigger the anaphylactic hard block of rung 29 — it only ever produces advisory text. Anyone may read a recipe's computed allergen profile, which reports `allergen_union`, `tree_depth`, `cross_contact_advisory`, a `component_count`, and the current `published_version`.

Publication is the operator's act of freezing a recipe for sale. A `DRAFT` recipe becomes `PUBLISHED` provided its tree is acyclic and no deeper than 5; the publish increments `published_version` by one, recomputes `allergen_union`, `tree_depth`, and `cross_contact_advisory`, recomputes `allergen_summary` on every menu item whose `recipe_id` is this recipe, and bumps the recipe's `version`. A published recipe may be moved to `REVISING` at any time (a plain posture change that bumps the `version`), and **republishing from `REVISING` back to `PUBLISHED` triggers the three-hop label supersession cascade**: hop one, every label whose `recipe_id` is this recipe and whose `label_status` is `ISSUED`, and whose pinned recipe version is now behind the freshly incremented `published_version`, moves from `ISSUED` to `SUPERSEDED`; hop two, each superseded label's order line thereby loses the handoff write gate, so rung 35 now blocks a handed-off outcome for the owning order with `ERR_LABEL_SUPERSEDED` until a fresh label is issued; hop three, `allergen_summary` is recomputed on every menu item backed by this recipe. The republish also increments `published_version` and recomputes the three allergen-derived fields exactly as the first publication did, and bumps `version`. Retirement is available from either `PUBLISHED` or `REVISING`, and in both cases only when no menu item backed by this recipe is still `LISTED`; `RETIRED` is terminal. Both retirement paths bump the `version` and have no other effect — they are specified with the same force and the same single side effect.

Physical ingredient lots are received, released, consumed, recalled, or expired. Receipt records the owning `baker_id`, the supplier's `lot_code`, the `received_at` and `expires_at` instants, and optionally `allergens` (intrinsic to the lot) and `cross_contact_allergens` (what the supplier flags as possible cross contact); the lot starts in `RECEIVED`. Releasing a received lot for production requires the current instant to be strictly before its `expires_at` and moves it to `RELEASED`, bumping its `version`. The same release capability carries a second, distinct behaviour: with `force_expiry_check` set true and the clock at or after `expires_at`, an already-`RELEASED` lot moves to `EXPIRED`, likewise bumping the `version`. A `RELEASED` lot moves to `CONSUMED` by the system when a batch whose recipe tree references it is baked. Both `RECALLED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. Only a `RELEASED` lot may back a component used in production, and rung 27 refuses any publication, component attachment, line addition, batch advancement, or label issuance that would touch a `RECALLED` lot or a component already flagged `recalled_component`, with `ERR_LOT_RECALLED`.

### 3.3. The Storefront: Menu Items, Pricing Bases, and Listing Posture

A menu item is the sellable face of a recipe. Creating one names the owning `baker_id`, the backing `recipe_id`, a `name`, the statutory `product_class`, and the `pricing_basis` that selects how lines of this item are priced, together with both price points: `unit_price_cents`, the price of a single unit, and `dozen_price_cents`, the price of one baker's dozen. Four optional production characteristics complete it: `temp_sensitive` (false by default) marks an item whose porch handoff requires a cooler; `oven_profile` defaults to `STANDARD`; `oven_hours_per_batch` defaults to 1.5 and is consulted only under the `LONG_BAKE` profile; and `yield_units_per_slot` defaults to 24, the same figure as a slot's nominal unit capacity, and states how many units one batch slot of this item actually produces. A new item starts in `listing_status` `DRAFT` with `rating_avg` absent and `rating_count` at 0, and carries `allergen_summary`, a snapshot of the backing recipe's allergen union taken at the item's last recompute.

Two price points are held because the two pricing bases need different ones, and the distinction between an ordinary dozen and a baker's dozen is the point of the whole design. A **dozen** in this domain is 12 units; a **baker's dozen** is 13. Under the `PER_UNIT` basis a line's total is simply its `quantity` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`, and the total rises strictly with quantity. Under `PER_DOZEN_BAKERS`, the quantity is divided into whole blocks of 13: each full block is charged at `dozen_price_cents`, and whatever units remain after the last full block are charged individually at `unit_price_cents`. The line total under this basis is **not** monotone in quantity, which the storefront treats as a feature rather than a defect: with a unit price of 450 and a dozen price of 4600, twelve units cost 5400 while thirteen units cost 4600, and twenty-six units cost 9200 while twenty-five cost 10,000. A buyer who *reduces* a quantity across a block boundary can therefore see the line total go **up**.

Listing posture is a small cyclic axis the operator drives by naming a `to_status`. A `DRAFT` item becomes `LISTED` only when its backing recipe's `recipe_status` is `PUBLISHED`; entering `LISTED` recomputes `allergen_summary` from that recipe's current allergen union and bumps the item's `version`. A `LISTED` item may be moved to `PAUSED` (version bump, nothing else) and a `PAUSED` item back to `LISTED`, which again requires the backing recipe to be `PUBLISHED` and again recomputes `allergen_summary`. Either `LISTED` or `PAUSED` may be moved to `DELISTED`, which is terminal; both delisting paths do nothing beyond bumping the `version`. Since a recipe cannot be retired while any item backed by it is `LISTED`, delisting or pausing is the operator's route to retiring an old recipe.

### 3.4. The Pre-Order Window: Opening, the Cutoff, Locking, and the One Extension

Everything the storefront sells is sold into a window. Creating one names the owning `baker_id`, a human `label` for the window, and five instants that lay out the whole bake: `opens_at`, the first moment orders may be placed; `order_cutoff_at`, after which they may not; `production_date`, when baking begins; `handoff_date`, when collection opens; and `handoff_window_end_at`, the last scheduled moment of collection, from which the pickup grace period is measured. Those five must be ordered — opening strictly before cutoff, cutoff at or before production, production at or before handoff, and handoff strictly before the end of the handoff window — and a window whose instants are not so ordered is refused at rung 44 with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The three capacity ceilings may be supplied and otherwise default: `capacity_units` to 240, `capacity_batch_slots` to 12, and `capacity_oven_hours` to 18.0. A new window begins with `window_status` `DRAFT`, `plan_status` `UNPLANNED`, `capacity_posture` `OPEN_CAPACITY`, and `cutoff_extension_count` at 0.

Opening the window — the operator advancing it from `DRAFT` to `OPEN` by naming `to_status` — requires the operator's permit to be `ACTIVE` and the current instant to have reached `opens_at`. The advance recomputes `capacity_posture` and bumps the window's `version`. From `OPEN`, the operator locks the window at cutoff, which is permitted only once the current instant has reached `order_cutoff_at`. **Locking is the system's largest single sweep.** Every order on the window sitting in `PENDING_HOLD` moves to `EXPIRED_HOLD` and each of its `PENDING` lines moves to `RELEASED`; every club cycle on the window sitting in `RESERVED` moves to `RELEASED` with its `reserved_units` dropping to 0; `held_units`, `reserved_units`, and `capacity_posture` are all recomputed; and the window's `version` is bumped. From that moment the window is a global write gate — rung 16 rejects every order-mutating call on it with `ERR_WINDOW_LOCKED`.

A locked window may be re-opened exactly once. The cutoff extension is available only while `plan_status` is still `UNPLANNED` and `cutoff_extension_count` is below 1; it returns `window_status` to `OPEN`, sets `order_cutoff_at` to the current instant plus **120 minutes** — two hours from *now*, not two hours from the old cutoff — increments `cutoff_extension_count`, recomputes `skip_deadline_at` on every club cycle attached to the window, and bumps the `version`. The re-entry is deliberately lossy: the holds and club reservations that the lock released are **not** restored, so capacity is strictly higher after the re-open than it was before the lock. A second extension attempt is refused because the extension count has reached its maximum.

Production begins when the operator advances a `LOCKED` window to `IN_PRODUCTION`, which requires `plan_status` to be `PLANNED` and the current instant to have reached `production_date`; every order on the window in `CONFIRMED` moves to `IN_PRODUCTION` alongside it. Handoff opens when an `IN_PRODUCTION` window is advanced to `HANDOFF_OPEN`, which requires the current instant to have reached `handoff_date` and **every** batch on the window to have reached `ALLOCATED`, `CLOSED`, or `SCRAPPED_RECALL`. That advance does four things at once: every order in `IN_PRODUCTION` moves to `READY`; every counter-pickup and porch order on the window has its `pickup_forfeit_at` set to the window's `handoff_window_end_at` plus **90 minutes** of grace; every such order already sitting in `SCHEDULED` moves on to `PICKUP_READY`; and every batch in `ALLOCATED` moves to `CLOSED`. Finally, a `HANDOFF_OPEN` window is advanced to `CLOSED` once no order on it is still `READY`, and that closure also sets `plan_status` to `RECONCILED`. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are the window's terminal states.

Cancellation is available from two places. A `DRAFT` window is cancelled outright, with no effect beyond the version bump. An `OPEN` window may be cancelled only when no order on it is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, or `READY`; when it is, every order on the window in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_HOLD`, or `EXPIRED_HOLD` moves to `CANCELLED` and its lines move to `RELEASED`.

The planning axis runs independently of all this. With the window `LOCKED` and `plan_status` `UNPLANNED`, the operator builds the plan by requesting the `PLAN` mode, which is described in §3.7. The same capability accepts the `REPLAN` mode, which is admissible only while the window is `LOCKED`, `plan_status` is `PLANNED`, and **every** batch on the window is still `PLANNED`; it deletes every batch on the window, clears `batch_id` on every order line that pointed at one, returns `plan_status` to `UNPLANNED`, and bumps the window's `version`. Once any batch has been mixed, the plan can no longer be torn down. The third move on this axis is not requested by anyone: `PLANNED` becomes `RECONCILED` as part of the same request in which the window's own status moves from `HANDOFF_OPEN` to `CLOSED`.

Anyone with an interest in the window may read its computed capacity, which recomputes and reports `units_remaining`, `batch_slots_remaining`, `oven_hours_remaining`, `capacity_posture`, `confirmed_units`, `held_units`, `reserved_units`, `slots_used`, and `oven_hours_used`, all as defined in §2.9.

### 3.5. Placing an Order: The Hold, the Lines, Re-Pricing, and Confirmation

A buyer opens an order against an `OPEN` window by naming the `customer_id` and the `offer_window_id` together with the `handoff_mode` that selects the fulfilment pipeline — `PICKUP_COUNTER`, `PORCH_SELF_SERVE`, or `DELIVERY`. The optional `channel` defaults to `DTC`, and `allergen_ack`, `unattended_consent`, and `cooler_provided` may all be supplied at this point, each defaulting to false. A new order is created with `order_status` `DRAFT`, `payment_status` `UNPAID`, `handoff_status` `NOT_SCHEDULED`, `source` `WALKUP`, and `prepaid` false; `source` becomes `SUBSCRIPTION_CLUB` and `prepaid` is set from the membership's billing mode only for the club-generated orders of §3.6. An order in `DRAFT` holds nothing and has a null `placed_at`.

**Adding the first line is what opens the hold.** A line names the `menu_item_id` and the `quantity`, echoes `expected_offer_window_version`, and may carry a `disposition` which defaults to `WHOLE_UNHEATED`. Adding the first line to a `DRAFT` order moves the order to `PENDING_HOLD`, sets `placed_at` to the current instant, sets `hold_expires_at` to `placed_at` plus **45 minutes**, adds the line's units to the window's `held_units`, recomputes `capacity_posture`, and bumps both the order's and the window's `version`. Each line is priced as it is added: `pricing_basis_applied` is copied from the menu item's `pricing_basis` at pricing time, `line_total_cents` follows the per-unit or baker's-dozen arithmetic of §3.3, `allergen_union` is pinned as the union of the backing recipe tree at that moment, and `hold_expires_at` is mirrored onto the line for as long as the line is `PENDING` and its order is `PENDING_HOLD`. A line also carries `allocated_units` and `shorted_units`, both 0 until production allocates it, and `batch_id`, which is null until the window is planned.

Tax is a per-line matter and turns entirely on the disposition. A line is `taxable` when its `disposition` is anything other than `WHOLE_UNHEATED`: an unheated whole bakery item sold as-is is exempt, and its `line_tax_cents` is 0 no matter how large `line_total_cents` is, whereas supplying utensils (`WITH_UTENSILS`), slicing it (`BY_THE_SLICE`), or heating it (`HEATED`) flips the identical units to taxable. For a taxable line, `line_tax_cents` is the line total multiplied by the tax rate of **725 basis points** — seven and a quarter percent — rounded half up to a whole cent. The order's `tax_total_cents` is then the sum of those per-line figures over the lines that are `PENDING`, in that pinned order of operations (§2.5), while the order's `subtotal_cents` is the sum of `line_total_cents` over the lines that are neither `RELEASED` nor `RECALL_VOID`.

A buyer may change a `PENDING` line: the patch carries a new `quantity`, echoes `expected_offer_window_version`, and may carry a new `disposition`. The line is re-priced from scratch, which under the baker's-dozen basis can **raise** `line_total_cents` even though the quantity fell; the window's `held_units` is adjusted by the quantity delta, `capacity_posture` is recomputed, and both the line's and the window's `version` are bumped. Patching also demands a live hold — at rung 38 the current instant must be strictly before `hold_expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`. An order may carry at most 12 lines that are neither `RELEASED` nor `RECALL_VOID`, and each line's quantity must be between 1 and 120 inclusive; both are rung 34 with `ERR_MAX_LINES_EXCEEDED`. Every line addition and every patch is then checked against all three capacity axes in the fixed order units, then batch slots, then oven hours, producing `ERR_UNITS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_BATCH_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, or `ERR_OVEN_HOURS_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` respectively.

Holds lapse on their own. An administrative sweep releases every order whose hold is due — that is, whose `hold_expires_at` is at or before the current instant — and it may be narrowed to a single window by supplying an `offer_window_id`. For each due hold, the order moves from `PENDING_HOLD` to `EXPIRED_HOLD`, each of its `PENDING` lines moves to `RELEASED`, the released units are subtracted from the window's `held_units`, `hold_expires_at` is cleared to null, `capacity_posture` is recomputed — which may move it back toward `OPEN_CAPACITY` — and the order's and window's versions are bumped. The sweep answers with `expired_order_count`, `released_line_count`, `released_units`, and the resulting `capacity_posture`. An expired order is not dead: adding another line to an `EXPIRED_HOLD` order, while the window is still `OPEN` and the cutoff still ahead, returns it to `PENDING_HOLD` with a fresh `placed_at` and a fresh 45-minute `hold_expires_at`. The lines released earlier stay `RELEASED` forever and never re-enter pricing.

Confirmation converts a hold into a commitment. It is available to the buyer from `PENDING_HOLD` only, requires the window to still be `OPEN` with the cutoff still ahead and the hold still live, and requires at least one `PENDING` line. Once past the ladder, confirmation prices every `PENDING` line and its tax, sets `subtotal_cents`, `tax_total_cents`, and `delivery_fee_cents`, copies `delivery_distance_km` from the customer onto the order when the handoff mode is `DELIVERY` (it stays null for the two pickup modes), moves the order's units out of the window's `held_units` and into its `confirmed_units`, clears `hold_expires_at`, recomputes the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents`, possibly latches the permit to `CAP_REACHED` as described in §3.1, recomputes `capacity_posture`, and bumps the order's `version`. The delivery fee follows the zone tiers of §3.8; for the two pickup modes it is 0 and no distance is examined at all.

The **allergen gate** runs at confirmation and nowhere else. The order's allergens are the union of `allergen_union` over its `PENDING` lines. Any allergen in that union which the buyer has recorded as an avoidance at `ANAPHYLACTIC` severity is a hard block: the confirm is refused with `ERR_ALLERGEN_HARD_BLOCK` and no acknowledgement flag can clear it. Any allergen matching an avoidance recorded at `INTOLERANCE` severity is instead an acknowledgement requirement: the confirm succeeds provided the order's `allergen_ack` is true, and the acknowledgement is recorded on the order rather than blocking it; otherwise the refusal is `ERR_ALLERGEN_ACK_REQUIRED`. The confirm request may itself carry `allergen_ack` to satisfy that second branch. Because the hard block sits one rung above the acknowledgement requirement, an order matching one avoidance of each severity reports the hard block.

Cancellation releases whatever the order was holding. From `PENDING_HOLD`, every `PENDING` line moves to `RELEASED` and `held_units` drops accordingly. From `EXPIRED_HOLD`, the lines were already released, so cancellation only moves the order. From `CONFIRMED` — permitted only while the window is still `OPEN` and the cutoff still ahead — the `PENDING` lines move to `RELEASED`, the order's units are subtracted from `confirmed_units`, `capacity_posture` is recomputed, the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` is recomputed *downward*, and the money is unwound: an `AUTHORIZED` payment is voided while a `CAPTURED` one is fully refunded through the waterfall of §3.9, in both cases with a movement `reason` of `CUSTOMER_CANCEL`. An optional `cancel_reason` may accompany the request. Every cancellation bumps the order's `version`, and `CANCELLED` is terminal.

A read-only money breakdown is available on any order and reports, in the pinned evaluation order of §3.9, its `subtotal_cents`, `tax_total_cents`, `delivery_fee_cents`, `tip_cents`, `credit_applied_cents`, and `grand_total_cents`, together with a `taxable_line_count` and an `exempt_line_count`. The order also carries `shortfall_flag`, which becomes true the moment any of its lines is shorted or credited in production.

### 3.6. The Bread-and-Cookie Club: Memberships, Cycles, and the Reserved Share

A membership binds one buyer to one menu item on a repeating rhythm. Creating it names the `customer_id`, the club `menu_item_id`, the `cadence` (`WEEKLY`, `BIWEEKLY`, or `MONTHLY`), the `units_per_cycle` claimed each time round, the `billing_mode` that selects how the cycle is paid for, and the `handoff_mode` that every generated order will inherit. A new membership sits in `sub_status` `PENDING` with `pause_count` at 0 and `prepaid_balance_cents` at 0.

Activation is admissible only while the club's menu item is `LISTED`. For a `PAY_PER_CYCLE` membership, activation does nothing but move the status and bump the `version`. For a `PREPAID` membership it also takes a deposit: a money movement is written with `movement_kind` `PREPAY_DEPOSIT`, `reason` `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAY`, and `ledger_scope` `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAID`, whose `amount_cents` is **four cycles** at the membership's cycle price, and `prepaid_balance_cents` is set from it. That money lives on the membership and **not** on the buyer: a subscription's prepaid balance is the sum of the `amount_cents` of the movements whose `subscription_id` is this membership and whose `ledger_scope` is `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAID`, with deposits positive and redemptions negative, and the buyer's own `credit_balance_cents` is not affected in either direction. A `PAY_PER_CYCLE` membership takes no deposit and its prepaid balance stays at 0.

The **cycle price** is computed from the membership alone and does not depend on which window a cycle lands in. Its base is the line-pricing arithmetic of §3.3 applied to a quantity of `units_per_cycle` at the club menu item's own price points. Under `PAY_PER_CYCLE` the price is that base unchanged, and the generated order must be paid for like any other, so a missed payment is what drives the membership into arrears. Under `PREPAID` the price is the base less a **10 percent** club discount, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the cycle is settled out of the prepaid balance at confirmation rather than through a payment, which is why a prepaid club order carries applied credit while still sitting at `payment_status` `UNPAID`. A worked case, using the same 450-per-unit, 4600-per-baker's-dozen cookie as §3.3: a prepaid club of 24 units per cycle prices at 9550 cents before the discount and `cycle_price_cents` 8595 after it, so activation deposits 34,380 cents and the first confirmed cycle leaves 25,785.

Pausing is capped. An `ACTIVE` membership may be paused only while `pause_count` is below **2**; the pause increments `pause_count`, moves every cycle of this membership that is `RESERVED` to `RELEASED` — dropping the corresponding units out of the window's `reserved_units` — and bumps the `version`. Resuming a `PAUSED` membership returns it to `ACTIVE` and, like the cutoff extension, does not restore the cycles the pause released. A third pause attempt is refused at rung 43 with `ERR_PAUSE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Arrears are an operator-driven loop and apply only to `PAY_PER_CYCLE` memberships. The operator moves an `ACTIVE` membership to `PAST_DUE` when at least one of its cycles sitting in `ORDER_PLACED` has a generated order still at `payment_status` `UNPAID`; this sets `past_due_since` to the current instant. The operator returns it to `ACTIVE` once no such unpaid placed cycle remains, which clears `past_due_since` to null. From `PAST_DUE` the operator may cancel the membership, but only once **72 hours** have elapsed since `past_due_since`; that cancellation moves every cycle in `GENERATED` or `RESERVED` to `RELEASED`. A buyer may also cancel from `ACTIVE` — which likewise releases `GENERATED` and `RESERVED` cycles and, for a `PREPAID` membership, moves the remaining prepaid balance out with a negative `CREDIT_REDEEM` movement scoped `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAID` mirrored by an equal positive `PREPAY_DEPOSIT` movement scoped `CUSTOMER_CREDIT` whose `reason` is `CUSTOMER_CANCEL`, so nothing is created or destroyed and the money simply becomes ordinary buyer credit — or from `PAUSED`, which has no effect beyond the version bump. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

Cycles are generated by the operator, one at a time, against an `OPEN` window. The request names the `offer_window_id` and the `sequence_number`, a 1-based index within the membership. The cycle is created in `GENERATED` and, in the same request, immediately reserves capacity and moves to `RESERVED`. Reservation is bounded by a **club share cap** of 30 percent of the window's `capacity_units`, rounded **down** to a whole unit: with the default capacity of 240 units the cap is 72, so a third weekly cycle of 24 units lands exactly on 72 and is admitted, while a fourth is refused at rung 52 with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_RESERVE_SHARE_EXCEEDED` — the comparison is strictly greater, so landing exactly on the cap is legal. The reservation sets the cycle's `reserved_units` to the full `units_per_cycle`, its `cycle_price_cents`, and its `skip_deadline_at`, which is the window's `order_cutoff_at` less **24 hours**; it adds the reserved units to the window's `reserved_units`, recomputes `capacity_posture`, and bumps the window's `version`.

A buyer may skip a `RESERVED` cycle while the current instant is strictly before its `skip_deadline_at`; the skip releases `reserved_units` back to the window, recomputes the window's `reserved_units` and `capacity_posture`, and bumps the cycle's `version`. For a `PREPAID` membership a skip makes no charge and leaves `prepaid_balance_cents` untouched. `SKIPPED` is terminal. Note that the skip deadline is the one derived deadline in this system that is permitted to **re-open**: it is recomputed from the window's stored cutoff rather than latched, so extending the cutoff moves it forward by the same amount and a cycle whose skip deadline had already passed can become skippable again. The re-opening is always the consequence of an explicit operator action, never of the clock alone.

Confirming a `RESERVED` cycle is the buyer's commitment, and it requires the membership to be `ACTIVE`, the window to be `OPEN`, the cutoff still ahead, and the echoed `expected_subscription_version` to match. The confirmation moves the cycle to `CONFIRMED` and then, at the end of the same request, on to `ORDER_PLACED`, creating along the way a club order: `source` `SUBSCRIPTION_CLUB`, `prepaid` set from whether the membership's billing mode is `PREPAID`, `handoff_mode` inherited from the membership, `order_status` `CONFIRMED`, and exactly one order line of `units_per_cycle` units in `PENDING`. The reserved units move out of the window's `reserved_units` and into its `confirmed_units`; the generated line is priced by the ordinary line-pricing and line-tax rules, so the order's money fields follow from its line; the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` is recomputed; and both the membership's and the cycle's `version` are bumped. For a `PREPAID` membership the confirmation additionally debits `prepaid_balance_cents` by `cycle_price_cents` through a negative movement with `movement_kind` `CREDIT_REDEEM`, `reason` `ORDER_APPLY`, and `ledger_scope` `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAID`, sets the generated order's `credit_applied_cents` to that same cycle price, and creates **no** payment at all — the generated order remains at `payment_status` `UNPAID` indefinitely. `ORDER_PLACED` and `RELEASED` are, with `SKIPPED`, the cycle's three terminal states, and a cycle reaches `RELEASED` either from `RESERVED` (at window lock, on a pause, or on a cancellation) or from `GENERATED` (on a cancellation from `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE`), in the former case zeroing `reserved_units` and subtracting them from the window before recomputing `capacity_posture`.

Two aspects of the club-generated order are deliberately left unsettled here. Its `placed_at` may reasonably be read either as being set to the current instant when the order is created, in which case the order falls inside the counted year and contributes to the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` like any walk-up order, or as remaining null because no line was ever *added* to a draft, in which case the calendar-year filter of §2.8 excludes it from the counted set. Likewise, for a `PREPAID` cycle whose order is settled from the prepaid balance and never sees a payment, `grand_total_cents` may reasonably be read either as staying at its default of 0 because the grand-total computation is triggered by payment authorisation, or as being computed at cycle confirmation from the six pinned steps of §3.9 with a tip of 0 and the cycle price as applied credit. Implementers should not treat either question as settled by this document.

### 3.7. Production: Planning the Bake, Confirming Yield, Allocating a Shortfall, and Issuing Labels

Planning turns a locked window's confirmed demand into an oven schedule. Requesting the `PLAN` mode on a `LOCKED`, `UNPLANNED` window creates **one batch per distinct menu item** appearing across the `CONFIRMED` orders on that window. Each batch records the `offer_window_id` it belongs to, the `menu_item_id` it produces, and the `recipe_id` in force at planning time; its `slot_count` and `planned_yield_units` follow the per-item arithmetic of §2.9 (demand divided by the 24 units a slot holds and rounded up, then multiplied by that item's `yield_units_per_slot`), and its `oven_hours` follow the profile-dependent arithmetic of the same section. The plan also captures `recipe_version_pinned` from the backing recipe's current `published_version`, and sets `batch_id` on every line of every `CONFIRMED` order to the batch that will produce it. The window's `plan_status` becomes `PLANNED` and its `version` is bumped. Batches begin in `batch_status` `PLANNED` with `actual_yield_units` still null and `surplus_units` at 0.

Baking proceeds in two operator-driven steps once the window itself has reached `IN_PRODUCTION`. A `PLANNED` batch advances to `MIXED` only while the window is `IN_PRODUCTION` and no recipe component anywhere in that batch's recipe tree has `recalled_component` set; the attempt is otherwise refused at rung 27 with `ERR_LOT_RECALLED`. A `MIXED` batch advances to `BAKED`, and that advance is where physical stock is spent: every ingredient lot referenced anywhere in the batch's recipe tree whose `lot_status` is `RELEASED` moves to `CONSUMED`. Both advances bump the batch's `version`.

**Yield confirmation is the fork on which the whole allocation story turns.** The operator supplies `actual_yield_units` for a batch that must be in `BAKED` — any other status is rung 36's `ERR_BATCH_NOT_BAKED`, and this rung, not the generic source-state rung, owns the check. The batch's ordered demand is the summed `quantity` of the `PENDING` lines pointing at it. When the actual yield is greater than or equal to that demand, the batch moves to `YIELD_CONFIRMED`: every `PENDING` line pointing at it moves to `ALLOCATED` with `allocated_units` equal to its `quantity` and `shorted_units` at 0, and `surplus_units` is set to the actual yield less the total allocated. When the actual yield falls short of demand, the batch moves to `UNDER_YIELD` instead, `surplus_units` is set to 0, and a four-hop cascade runs. Either way, at the end of the same request the batch auto-advances to `ALLOCATED` provided the yield conservation invariant of §2.10 holds, and it is later moved to `CLOSED` when the window opens handoff. `CLOSED` and `SCRAPPED_RECALL` are the batch's terminal states.

The under-yield cascade allocates scarcity by a fixed priority, and the priority is the club's whole commercial promise. Candidate lines are exactly the `PENDING` lines pointing at this batch — `RELEASED` and `RECALL_VOID` lines are excluded — sorted ascending by a four-part key. The **first** key is club membership: a line whose owning order has `source` `SUBSCRIPTION_CLUB` sorts ahead of one whose order is a walk-up. The **second** key is prepayment: among lines equal on the first key, one whose owning order has `prepaid` true sorts ahead of one that does not. The **third** key is the owning order's `created_at`, earliest first. The **fourth** key, which breaks any remaining tie and makes the sort total, is the line's own `created_at`, earliest first. Allocation then walks that sorted list greedily: each line in turn is granted the lesser of its own `quantity` and the units still remaining from `actual_yield_units`; its `allocated_units` is that grant and its `shorted_units` is its quantity less the grant; and the remaining pool is reduced by the grant. Whatever remains at the end becomes the batch's `surplus_units`. Because each earlier-priority line is filled to its **full** quantity before the next line receives anything, one line can be partially shorted while every line behind it receives nothing at all. A line granted its full quantity moves to `ALLOCATED`; a line granted less moves to `SHORTED` and sets its owning order's `shortfall_flag` to true.

Every `SHORTED` line then moves on to `CREDITED` in the third hop, carrying a proportional credit: the line's `line_total_cents` plus its `line_tax_cents`, multiplied by its `shorted_units` over its `quantity`, multiplied by a shortfall multiplier of **1.00** — the buyer is made whole, with no haircut and no penalty — and rounded half up to a whole cent. Where that credit goes depends on how far the order's money had progressed, and the two branches differ in kind, not just in amount. When the order's payment is `CAPTURED`, money leaves the operator: a movement is written with `movement_kind` `REFUND`, `reason` `SHORTFALL`, and `ledger_scope` `SETTLEMENT`, its four legs filled by the waterfall of §3.9, and the order's `payment_status` moves from `CAPTURED` to `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. When the order's payment is `INITIATED` or `AUTHORIZED`, or the order has no payment at all, no money leaves the operator: a movement is written with `movement_kind` `SHORTFALL_CREDIT`, `reason` `SHORTFALL`, and `ledger_scope` `CUSTOMER_CREDIT` against the buyer's credit balance, and the order's `payment_status` is left exactly as it was. The fourth hop is the arithmetic consequence: the order's money axis settles into `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` where a refund was issued, and both the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` and `payout_net_cents` are recomputed. The two branches as written cover a payment that is `CAPTURED`, `INITIATED`, or `AUTHORIZED`, and an order with no payment; a payment sitting in `DECLINED` or `VOIDED` matches neither branch, and this document leaves open whether such an order takes the credit branch (on the ground that no money ever left the operator) or whether no movement is written for it at all.

Labels are the last production artefact and the gate on handoff. Issuing one names the `order_line_id` it will be affixed to, the `recipe_id` the allergen statement is derived from, the declared `net_weight_grams`, and the echoed `expected_recipe_version`. The label is created in `label_status` `ISSUED` with `recipe_version_pinned` taken from the recipe's current `published_version`, with `allergen_union` pinned by the recursive walk of §3.2, with `cross_contact_advisory` set to the advisory text when the advisory applies at issuance and absent otherwise, and with `statement_text` formed by concatenating the cottage-food statement quoted in §2.8 with the allergen statement. Issuance is refused at rung 26 when the recipe is not `PUBLISHED` and at rung 27 when anything in its closure is recalled. A label's other three statuses are reached only by the system: `SUPERSEDED` by a republication of its recipe (§3.2), `VOID` by a recall (§3.10), and `CONSUMED` when its line is handed off (§3.8); `VOID` and `CONSUMED` are terminal. One point about issuance is genuinely ambiguous and is left open here: the operation is specified as incrementing the **recipe's** `version` by one, which — given that the same recipe version is what `expected_recipe_version` must match — means a client issuing labels for several lines of one order must re-read the recipe between each; the competing reading is that the increment belongs to the newly created label alone and that issuing a label leaves the recipe's `version` untouched, since a label does not change the recipe.

### 3.8. Handoff: Counter Pickup, the Porch, and Delivery Routes

The handoff axis runs independently of both the fulfilment axis and the money axis, and it carries **two structurally different pipelines**. Scheduling is the common entry point: from `NOT_SCHEDULED`, with the order in `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, or `READY`, the buyer or the operator moves `handoff_status` to `SCHEDULED`. `READY` belongs in that source set: once a window has advanced to `HANDOFF_OPEN` every order on it is already `READY`, so excluding it would strand this section's own "window already `HANDOFF_OPEN` at the moment of scheduling" branch and would leave an order that reached `READY` unscheduled unable ever to be handed off. An order whose `order_status` lies outside those three is refused at rung 19 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The request may carry `unattended_consent` and `cooler_provided` to satisfy the porch rungs, and a `delivery_route_id` naming the route the order should join; for a delivery order the scheduling sets `delivery_route_id` on the order. Which safety rungs apply depends entirely on the pipeline: a `PORCH_SELF_SERVE` order requires `unattended_consent` true (rung 31, `ERR_UNATTENDED_CONSENT_REQUIRED`) and, if any of its pending lines names a menu item with `temp_sensitive` true, also `cooler_provided` true (rung 32, `ERR_COOLER_CONSENT_REQUIRED`); a `DELIVERY` order instead requires the buyer's `delivery_distance_km` to be within the operator's `permitted_radius_km` (rung 33, `ERR_OUT_OF_PERMITTED_ZONE`) and the order's `subtotal_cents` to be at least the **3500-cent delivery minimum** (rung 47, `ERR_DELIVERY_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`); a `PICKUP_COUNTER` order requires none of the four.

The **pickup pipeline** proceeds through `PICKUP_READY`. An order in `SCHEDULED` whose handoff mode is `PICKUP_COUNTER` or `PORCH_SELF_SERVE` is moved to `PICKUP_READY` by the system, either as part of the window's advance to `HANDOFF_OPEN` or, when the window is *already* `HANDOFF_OPEN` at the moment of scheduling, within the scheduling request itself. Its collection deadline is `pickup_forfeit_at`, set when the window opened handoff to the window's `handoff_window_end_at` plus **90 minutes** of grace, and null for delivery orders. Recording a handed-off outcome from `PICKUP_READY` requires the current instant to be strictly before that deadline — the grace expires inclusively, so an instant exactly equal to `pickup_forfeit_at` is already expired and rung 39 refuses with `ERR_PICKUP_GRACE_EXPIRED`.

The **delivery pipeline** proceeds through `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` instead, and it has no grace check at any point. An order in `SCHEDULED` whose handoff mode is `DELIVERY` and whose `order_status` is `READY` is moved to `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` by the system when its route is dispatched. Recording a handed-off outcome from `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` is unconditional on time.

An outcome is recorded by the operator naming it as `outcome`, and it may be `HANDED_OFF`, `MISSED`, or `FORFEITED`. A **handed-off** outcome requires the order to be `READY` with `handoff_status` in `PICKUP_READY` or `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`, and — the write gate that makes labelling non-optional — requires **every** order line that is neither `RELEASED` nor `RECALL_VOID` to have a label whose `label_status` is `ISSUED`; a superseded, voided, already-consumed, or missing label fails at rung 35 with `ERR_LABEL_SUPERSEDED`. When it succeeds, the order's `handoff_status` becomes `HANDED_OFF` and its `order_status` moves from `READY` to `FULFILLED`; `fulfilled_at` is set to the current instant and `review_window_closes_at` to that instant plus **21 days**; every line in `ALLOCATED` or `CREDITED` moves to `FULFILLED` and the label on each of those lines moves from `ISSUED` to `CONSUMED`; a payment sitting in `CAPTURED` moves to `SETTLED` and the operator's `payout_net_cents` is recomputed. A **missed** outcome is available from either `PICKUP_READY` or `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`, moves `handoff_status` to `MISSED`, and moves no money whatsoever. A **forfeited** outcome is available from `MISSED` once the current instant has reached `pickup_forfeit_at`; it moves `handoff_status` to `FORFEITED`, moves `order_status` from `READY` to `FORFEITED`, sets `forfeit_latched` to true **permanently** — the latch is never cleared, which is what stops the derived grace status from silently re-entering its pre-state if the clock is moved backward — and refunds **zero percent** of the captured amount, which is to say it refunds nothing at all. Both `HANDED_OFF` and `FORFEITED` are terminal on this axis. The order-axis move to `FORFEITED` is specified as requiring the handoff mode to be `PICKUP_COUNTER` or `PORCH_SELF_SERVE`, while the handoff-axis move from `MISSED` to `FORFEITED` names no mode restriction and compares against a deadline that is null for delivery orders; whether a missed **delivery** order can be forfeited at all is therefore left open by this document.

A missed order can be rebooked exactly once. Rebooking is a fresh scheduling request against an order whose `handoff_status` is `MISSED` and whose `order_status` is still `READY` (the widened source set of the first paragraph governs the `NOT_SCHEDULED` branch only; a rebook admits `READY` alone), admissible only while `rebook_count` is below **1** and `forfeit_latched` is false — otherwise rung 45 refuses with `ERR_REBOOK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. It returns the order to `SCHEDULED` and increments `rebook_count`. There is a second step inside the same request that matters: when the window is `HANDOFF_OPEN` and the order's handoff mode is `PICKUP_COUNTER` or `PORCH_SELF_SERVE`, `handoff_status` advances immediately on from `SCHEDULED` to `PICKUP_READY`, so the rebooked order is collectable again without any further call — and that is precisely what makes a *second* miss, and therefore the rebook ceiling itself, reachable.

The **delivery fee** is fixed at confirmation from three zone tiers, measured on the order's `delivery_distance_km`, and the tier upper bounds are **inclusive**. Up to and including 8.0 kilometres the fee is 500 cents; beyond that and up to and including 20.0 kilometres it is 900 cents; beyond that and up to and including 40.0 kilometres it is 1400 cents; beyond 40.0 kilometres the order is refused with `ERR_OUT_OF_PERMITTED_ZONE`. Exactly 8.0 kilometres is therefore a tier-one order at 500 cents while 8.01 kilometres is a tier-two order at 900. For `PICKUP_COUNTER` and `PORCH_SELF_SERVE` orders the fee is 0 and the zone rule is not applied at all — no distance is examined for a pickup.

Delivery routes gather a window's delivery orders. Creating one names the owning `baker_id` and the `offer_window_id` whose delivery orders it will serve; it starts in `route_status` `DRAFT` with `stop_count` at 0. Sequencing it — the operator naming `to_status` `SEQUENCED` — requires at least one order to have been assigned to the route, and assigns `stop_sequence` as a 1-based index over exactly those assigned orders whose `handoff_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `order_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, or `READY`; orders that are `MISSED`, `HANDED_OFF`, `FORFEITED`, `CANCELLED`, or `VOIDED_RECALL` are excluded from the sequence. The ordering is by `delivery_distance_km` ascending, with the owning order's `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — never the identifier. Sequencing also sets the route's `stop_count` to the number of sequenced orders and its `total_distance_km` to the sum of their distances. Returning a `SEQUENCED` route to `DRAFT` is the resequencing loop: it clears `stop_sequence` to null on every assigned order and resets `stop_count` to 0, so that a newly assigned order can be inserted in distance order on the next sequencing. Dispatching a `SEQUENCED` route requires the window to be `HANDOFF_OPEN`; it sets `dispatched_at` to the current instant and moves every assigned order that is `SCHEDULED` and `READY` to `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`. Completing a `DISPATCHED` route requires no assigned order to remain `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`; `COMPLETED` is terminal. Every one of these moves bumps the route's `version`.

### 3.9. Money: Authorising, Tipping, Applying Credit, Capturing, Refunding, and Paying Out

Payment is a buyer's act against a confirmed order. The request names the `order_id`, chooses a `tip_mode` of `PERCENT` or `FIXED`, and supplies whichever of `tip_pct` and `tip_fixed_cents` that mode needs; it may also carry `apply_credit_cents` to spend the buyer's stored credit and an `outcome`, which defaults to `SUCCEEDED` and exists so that a declined processor response can be simulated. Authorisation is admissible only while the order's `payment_status` is `UNPAID` and its `order_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, or `READY`. A payment is born in `payment_state` `INITIATED` and moves in the same request to `AUTHORIZED` on a `SUCCEEDED` outcome or to `DECLINED` on a `DECLINED` one. A decline is not fatal: it leaves the order's `payment_status` at `UNPAID` so that a further payment may be attempted. On success the order's money axis moves from `UNPAID` to `AUTHORIZED` and the payment's `authorized_cents` is set to the order's `grand_total_cents`; `captured_cents` stays at 0 until capture and `refunded_cents` accumulates across every refund movement.

**The tip is computed on the subtotal alone.** Under `PERCENT`, the tip is the order's `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `tip_pct` and rounded half up once, at the end; tax, the delivery fee, and applied credit are all excluded from the base. Under `FIXED`, the tip is the literal `tip_fixed_cents` with no rounding at all. Tipping on the subtotal-plus-tax-plus-fee figure is the rejected reading and must not be implemented. A `tip_pct` above **0.30** or below zero, and a `tip_fixed_cents` above **5000** cents, are refused at rung 46 with `ERR_TIP_OUT_OF_RANGE`. A *negative* `tip_fixed_cents` never reaches rung 46: `tip_fixed_cents` is an integer amount, so rung 13's "integers are zero or greater" requirement is violated first and, the lowest-numbered violated rung winning, the rejection is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Rung 46's below-zero half is consequently reachable through `tip_pct` — a fractional number, which rung 13 does not bound — and not through `tip_fixed_cents`.

**Credit never offsets a tip**, even when the buyer's balance would comfortably cover the whole bill. The credit-eligible portion of an order is its `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_total_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents`, and `credit_applied_cents` is the lesser of the buyer's available `credit_balance_cents` and that eligible portion. A request whose `apply_credit_cents` exceeds the buyer's balance is refused at rung 48 with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT`. Two readings of the interaction between the request field and the computation are equally supportable and neither is settled here: on one reading `apply_credit_cents` is merely validated against the balance and the amount actually applied is the full lesser-of computation just given, so a buyer asking to spend 100 cents of a 5000-cent balance would still see the whole eligible amount consumed; on the other, `apply_credit_cents` is the amount to apply and the figure that lands on the order is the lesser of the requested amount and the eligible portion. Where credit is applied at all, a movement is written with `movement_kind` `CREDIT_REDEEM`, `reason` `ORDER_APPLY`, and `ledger_scope` `CUSTOMER_CREDIT`, carrying a **negative** `amount_cents`.

The order's money fields are computed in a **pinned six-step evaluation order**, and the steps are not interchangeable because step five deliberately depends on a total that excludes the tip computed at step four while step six includes it. Step one: for each `PENDING` line, its `line_total_cents` and then its `line_tax_cents`. Step two: the order's `subtotal_cents` and `tax_total_cents` as the aggregations of §3.5. Step three: `delivery_fee_cents` from the zone tiers of §3.8. Step four: `tip_cents`, on the subtotal only. Step five: `credit_applied_cents`, whose cap excludes `tip_cents`. Step six: `grand_total_cents`, being `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_total_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents` plus `tip_cents`, less `credit_applied_cents`. The dependency graph looks circular at a glance — the tip depends on the subtotal, the credit cap on a total that excludes the tip, the grand total on both — and the pinned order is what makes the value well defined.

Capture is the operator's act. An `AUTHORIZED` payment moves to `CAPTURED`, its `captured_cents` set equal to its `authorized_cents`, only while the order's `order_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, or `READY`; the order's money axis moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `CAPTURED` and the operator's `payout_net_cents` is recomputed. A `CAPTURED` payment later moves to `SETTLED` by the system when the order is handed off, which recomputes the payout figure again. `SETTLED`, `DECLINED`, and `VOIDED` are the payment's terminal states. An `AUTHORIZED` payment is instead moved to `VOIDED` by the system when its order is cancelled, and that void carries a compensating ledger entry: where the order had applied credit, a movement with `movement_kind` `PREPAY_DEPOSIT`, `reason` `CUSTOMER_CANCEL`, and `ledger_scope` `CUSTOMER_CREDIT` is written whose `amount_cents` equals the order's `credit_applied_cents`, restoring the buyer's balance.

**Refunds draw through a fixed four-leg waterfall, and the leg order is load-bearing.** A refund request carries the `amount_cents` to refund, a `refund_reason`, and the echoed `expected_order_version`; it is admissible only against a payment whose `payment_state` is `CAPTURED` (rung 49, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_CAPTURED`) and for an amount no greater than `captured_cents` less `refunded_cents` (rung 50, `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_CAPTURED`). The legs are drawn in this order and no other. First the **tip leg**, whose availability is the order's `tip_cents` less whatever prior refunds already took from the tip, filling `leg_tip_cents`. Second the **delivery-fee leg**, availability being `delivery_fee_cents` less what prior refunds took from it, filling `leg_delivery_fee_cents`. Third the **taxable-subtotal leg**, availability being `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_total_cents` less what prior refunds took from it — tax travels with the subtotal proportionally rather than as a leg of its own — filling `leg_subtotal_cents`. Fourth and last the **credit leg**, availability being `credit_applied_cents` less what prior refunds took from it, filling `leg_credit_cents`. Each leg takes the lesser of what remains to be refunded and what that leg still has available, and the remainder passes to the next leg. If any amount still remains unallocated after the fourth leg, the request is refused with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_CAPTURED`. The movement's `amount_cents` is the requested amount, and the four legs always sum exactly to it. The movement written by this operator-initiated refund carries `movement_kind` `REFUND`, `ledger_scope` `SETTLEMENT`, and a `reason` that is **absent (null)**: every one of the five declared `reason` members names a different, system-driven pathway, so none of them may be attributed to a refund the operator asked for by hand. The request's free-text `refund_reason` is required and validated as a string but is not stored on the movement or anywhere else — the field is a required input the system deliberately discards. Every *other* movement in the system carries one of the five members, assigned verbatim by the narrative that writes it. The per-leg "already refunded" figures are the per-leg sums over every prior refund movement on the same order, which is why a second partial refund resumes at whichever leg the first one left partly consumed rather than starting again at the tip.

Why the order matters is best seen in cents: a 600-cent partial refund against an order with a 450-cent tip, a 500-cent delivery fee, a 4600-cent taxable subtotal, and no applied credit draws 450 from the tip leg and 150 from the delivery-fee leg, leaves the taxable subtotal untouched, and therefore reduces the operator's counted `annual_gross_sales_cents` by only 150 — because the counted figure subtracts the subtotal and delivery-fee legs and never the tip leg.

Each refund increases the payment's `refunded_cents` and moves the order's money axis. A refund for less than the remaining refundable amount moves `CAPTURED` to `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, and each further partial refund keeps it at `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` — a self-loop, with the waterfall resuming from the first unexhausted leg. A refund for exactly the remaining refundable amount moves `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` to `REFUNDED`, and a refund for exactly the whole `captured_cents` moves `CAPTURED` straight to `REFUNDED`, in both cases setting `refunded_cents` equal to `captured_cents`. `REFUNDED` and `VOIDED` are terminal on the money axis. Every refund also recomputes the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` and `payout_net_cents`.

Every movement of money or credit is recorded as an immutable ledger entry naming the `customer_id` whose money moved, and, where applicable, the `order_id` it belongs to, the `payment_id` it refunds, and the `subscription_id` behind a club deposit or redemption. Its `ledger_scope` says which balance it belongs to — `CUSTOMER_CREDIT`, `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAID`, or `SETTLEMENT` — and this scoping is what keeps club prepaid money out of the buyer's general credit. Its `movement_kind` and `reason` record what it is and why it happened. Its `amount_cents` is signed: positive increases a balance, negative consumes it. Credit-bearing movements carry an `expires_at` of their `created_at` plus **365 days**; refund movements carry none.

A buyer's `credit_balance_cents` is accordingly the sum of `amount_cents` over that buyer's movements whose `ledger_scope` is `CUSTOMER_CREDIT`, whose `movement_kind` is `SHORTFALL_CREDIT`, `PREPAY_DEPOSIT`, or `CREDIT_REDEEM`, and whose `expires_at` is either absent or strictly in the future. Shortfall credits and prepay deposits carry positive amounts; credit redemptions carry negative ones. Refund movements, being scoped `SETTLEMENT`, never enter this sum — refunded money leaves the system rather than becoming credit — and movements scoped `SUBSCRIPTION_PREPAID` never enter it either, because club prepaid money lives on the membership.

Settlement to the operator runs on a fortnightly rhythm. The payout period is the half-open interval beginning at the operator's `payout_period_start` and running **14 days**, including its first instant and excluding its last. Within that period, captured money is the sum of `captured_cents` over the payments on this operator's orders whose own `created_at` falls in the period and whose `payment_state` is `CAPTURED` or `SETTLED`; refunded money is the sum of `amount_cents` over the refund movements whose `created_at` falls in the period. The operator's `payout_net_cents` is the former less the latter, and it is recomputed on capture, on every refund, when a payment settles, and whenever the compliance summary is read.

### 3.10. Withdrawal from Sale: The Six-Hop Recall Cascade, and Reviews

A recall is the platform administrator's instrument and the deepest write in the system. It names a `recall_reason` and moves an ingredient lot to `RECALLED` from any of three states — `RECEIVED`, `RELEASED`, or `CONSUMED` — and in every case bumps the lot's `version`. Recalling a **never-released** lot has no downstream product, so the cascade terminates early: there are no batches, no lines, no orders, and no labels to touch. Recalling a `RELEASED` lot and recalling a `CONSUMED` lot run the **identical** six hops. That `CONSUMED` is recallable is essential rather than incidental: a lot is most often recalled *after* it has been baked into product, and without that path the deep cascade would be unreachable in every scenario where any batch has already been baked. The maximum declared depth of the cascade is **6**.

**Hop 1.** Every recipe component whose `ingredient_lot_id` is the recalled lot has its `recalled_component` set to true. The flag latches and is never cleared, which is what makes rung 27 refuse any later publication, component attachment, line addition, batch advancement, or label issuance touching that closure with `ERR_LOT_RECALLED`.

**Hop 2.** Each affected component's owning recipe moves from `PUBLISHED` to `REVISING` on its `recipe_status` axis, and the recipe's `version` increments. A recipe that was not `PUBLISHED` is not moved.

**Hop 3.** Every batch whose `recipe_id` is one of those recipes and whose `batch_status` is `PLANNED`, `MIXED`, or `BAKED` moves to `SCRAPPED_RECALL`. **This is the cascade's first deliberate stopping point:** a batch already sitting in `YIELD_CONFIRMED`, `ALLOCATED`, or `CLOSED` is *not* scrapped, hop 3 simply does not fire for it, and no later hop reaches anything behind it.

**Hop 4.** Every order line whose `batch_id` points at a scrapped batch moves to `RECALL_VOID` and has its `allocated_units` reset to 0. In practice the only lines in that position are `PENDING` ones, and that follows from hop 3's own guard rather than from a separate rule: a line becomes `ALLOCATED` or `SHORTED` only at yield confirmation, which moves its batch out of `BAKED`, and hop 3 scraps only batches in `PLANNED`, `MIXED`, or `BAKED`. There is deliberately no transition from `ALLOCATED` to `RECALL_VOID`, because it would be unreachable.

**Hop 5.** Every order owning at least one line that just moved to `RECALL_VOID`, and whose `order_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, or `READY`, moves to `VOIDED_RECALL`; the operator's `annual_gross_sales_cents` is recomputed downward as those orders leave the counted set. **This is the cascade's second deliberate stopping point:** an order whose `order_status` is `FULFILLED`, `CANCELLED`, `FORFEITED`, or already `VOIDED_RECALL` is left completely untouched. Product already in a customer's hands is not un-sold by a recall.

**Hop 6.** For each order that actually moved to `VOIDED_RECALL`, every label on its lines whose `label_status` is `ISSUED` moves to `VOID`, as does any label already sitting in `SUPERSEDED`; and where that order's payment is `CAPTURED`, a full refund runs through the four-leg waterfall of §3.9, creating a movement with `movement_kind` `REFUND` and `reason` `RECALL_VOID`. Because hop 5 skips fulfilled orders, hop 6 **does not** void the labels of a fulfilled order and issues it no refund — the guard at hop 5 is the only thing standing between a recall and a wave of unwarranted refunds. `VOIDED_RECALL` is terminal on the fulfilment axis.

Reviews close the loop with the buyer. A review may be submitted only by the buyer, only on an order that has been fulfilled, and only while the current instant is strictly before that order's `review_window_closes_at`, which is its `fulfilled_at` plus **21 days**; a late submission is rung 41's `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and a second review for the same `order_id` is rung 37's `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW`. The submission names the `order_id`, the `menu_item_id` whose rating it rolls up to, a `rating` between **1 and 5** inclusive, and a free-text `body`. The review is created in `review_status` `SUBMITTED` with `counts_toward_rollup` true and with `shortfall_affected` derived from the reviewed order — true when that order's `shortfall_flag` is true or any refund movement exists against it — and the submission recomputes the menu item's rating rollup and bumps the item's `version`. Alongside `review_window_closes_at` the order carries `review_window_closed_latch`, described as a monotone latch set the first time the review window is observed closed and never cleared thereafter; this specification does not settle whether any request writes it — one reading is that the first observation of a closed window (a late submission attempt, for instance) sets the latch and the rung then rejects on the strength of the latch alone, the other is that the field is a passive record nothing writes and the rung is evaluated purely against the clock.

The operator may answer a `SUBMITTED` review while the current instant is strictly before the review's own `created_at` plus **14 days**; a later attempt is rung 42's `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The response records `baker_response` and `responded_at`, moves the review to `RESPONDED`, recomputes the rollup, and bumps the review's `version`. Moderation belongs to the platform administrator, who names a `to_status`. Hiding a review — available from either `SUBMITTED` or `RESPONDED`, and specified identically for both — sets `counts_toward_rollup` to false and recomputes the rollup, which may drop the average away entirely when the visible count falls below the minimum. Unhiding returns a `HIDDEN` review to `SUBMITTED`, sets `counts_toward_rollup` back to true, and recomputes the rollup again; a `baker_response` recorded earlier is preserved even though the status returns to `SUBMITTED`. No review status is terminal — this axis is a cycle.

The rating rollup is **stored, not derived on read**, and is recomputed in the same request as every review submission, response, hide, and unhide. Its inputs are the reviews on the menu item whose `counts_toward_rollup` is true, which is to say those in `SUBMITTED` or `RESPONDED`, with `HIDDEN` reviews excluded; there is no time window and the result does not depend on ordering. `rating_count` is the number of such reviews. When that count is below **3**, `rating_avg` is null; otherwise `rating_avg` is the sum of their ratings divided by the count, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Hiding the third review of a menu item therefore takes its published average back to null, and unhiding it restores the average — the rollup is expected to move in both directions.
