# Employer Tuition Benefit Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employers who fund education for their workforce sit between two systems that were never designed to talk to each other: a payroll and HR record that knows who is eligible and for how much, and a patchwork of colleges, bootcamps and certificate providers that each invoice differently. The Employer Tuition Benefit Administration platform closes that gap. It curates an eligible program catalog per employer, negotiates and stores per-school discounts, decides who qualifies on the basis of tenure, scheduled hours and job family, and then moves money to schools either directly (the employer is billed up front) or through reimbursement after the learner passes.

Because education dollars are also tax-regulated dollars, the platform is simultaneously a compliance record. Every disbursement is split between the amount excluded from the employee's taxable income under the annual educational-assistance exclusion limit and the amount that must be imputed as taxable wages. Every clawback — a failed term, a withdrawal after the drop window, a resignation inside the service-commitment period — reverses that split on an append-only ledger and opens a repayment obligation the learner can pay down or the employer can forgive. A per-employee annual benefit cap behaves as a finite pool: enrollments reserve against it, disbursements consume it, clawbacks hold it hostage until the obligation resolves, and compensating actions release it. That pool is conserved at all times, and the invariant is the single most important correctness property in the system.

The third concern the API serves is advisory. Coaches log advising sessions, the platform scores each enrollment's persistence risk from credential type, academic history, coaching recency and observed risk flags, and a high-risk open enrollment places a hold that blocks the learner from taking on more coursework until coaching brings the score down. Benefit administrators read all of this back as a utilization report covering participation, completion, retention, skill attainment and cost per completion for the current plan year. This document specifies the complete contract: the resources, the two-axis enrollment lifecycle, the money and tax arithmetic, the validation ordering and the error vocabulary.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts that hold for every endpoint. Where a rule here and a workflow narrative in §3 describe the same behaviour, they are reinforcing statements of one rule, not alternatives.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the acting role is not listed among the endpoint's authorized roles in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five recognized roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `BenefitAdministrator`, `Employee`, `SchoolPartner`, and `Coach`. No role is a superset of another; each owns a distinct slice of the workflow. A `PlatformAdmin` is the Guild-side operator who onboards employer clients and schools, drafts partner agreements, disburses approved invoices, drives the logical clock and runs the term-close batch. A `BenefitAdministrator` is the employer-side benefits owner who authors policy versions, activates and terminates partner agreements, curates the catalog, registers employees, decides non-catalog approval requests and reimbursement claims, approves invoices for payment and waives repayment obligations. An `Employee` is the learner, who requests non-catalog approvals, enrolls, withdraws, files reimbursement claims and repays obligations. A `SchoolPartner` is the school-side registrar, who publishes programs, confirms registrations and posts academic outcomes. A `Coach` is a Guild advising coach who logs coaching sessions and monitors persistence risk.

Three of these roles are entity-backed, which ties `X-User-ID` directly to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `BenefitAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employer`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employer` compare the resource's employer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. For resources one hop away — an invoice, a claim, an obligation, an approval request — the comparison walks to the owning employee's `employer_id` and compares that to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Employee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee`; ownership checks compare the target resource's `employee_id` (directly, or via the enrollment the resource hangs off) to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`; ownership checks compare the `school_id` of the program behind the target resource to `X-User-ID`.

The remaining two roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Coach`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `coach_user_id` on each `CoachingSession` the coach logs, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. An ownership-scope violation by an entity-backed role is reported as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), the same code as a role that is not permitted on the endpoint at all.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records; it defaults to 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered collection before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond it.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Identifiers and the Logical Clock

Most resources in this system carry four system-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped once at creation. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative during a cascade. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, cascade-driven mutations included. `Employer`, `BenefitPolicy`, `Employee`, `School`, `PartnerAgreement`, `Program`, `CatalogListing`, `ProgramApprovalRequest`, `Enrollment`, `TuitionInvoice`, `ReimbursementClaim` and `RepaymentObligation` all carry the full set.

Three record types are append-only history and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all: `AcademicOutcome`, `TaxLedgerEntry` and `CoachingSession`. Once written, such a row is never edited; a correction takes the form of another row.

All date and time reasoning in this system is against a **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Tenure, eligibility, plan-year bucketing, drop deadlines, coaching staleness, policy and agreement windows, and every stamped date such as `posted_on`, `assessed_on`, `terminated_on`, `paid_on` and `posted_at` are all evaluated against the logical clock as of the request being served. Date-only fields are ISO 8601 calendar dates with no time component; `posted_at` on a ledger entry is a full ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits.

### 2.5. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field or a response body. Wherever a computation would produce a fraction, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent, and the specification always pins *where* in the computation that rounding happens, because moving the rounding step changes the answer.

Two consequences deserve emphasis up front. First, the negotiated per-credit tuition rate is rounded to whole cents once, at the moment a catalog listing is created, and it is that already-rounded rate that gets multiplied by a term's credit count later; the alternative order — multiplying the exact discounted product by credits and rounding the total — is not what this system does and diverges whenever the exact per-credit product carries a half-cent remainder. Second, the cap ledger and the tax ledger are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding at all; only the rate derivation, the reimbursement percentage applications, the platform fee and the prorated repayment principal round.

Percentage and ratio inputs are decimal numbers, not cents: `negotiated_discount_pct` accepts at most four decimal places and must be at least 0 and at most 0.60; `reimbursement_pct` accepts at most two decimal places and must be greater than 0 and at most 1; `min_weekly_hours` and `weekly_hours` accept at most two decimal places; `min_passing_grade_points` and `grade_points` accept at most two decimal places within the range 0.00 to 4.00. The utilization report's three rates are numbers rounded half up to four decimal places, while its cent amounts are integers rounded half up.

### 2.6. The Annual Benefit Cap as a Conserved Pool

Each employee has one benefit cap per **plan year**, and the plan year is not the calendar year. Given a date, the plan year is that date's calendar year when the date's month is greater than or equal to the employer's `fiscal_year_start_month`, and the previous calendar year otherwise. An employee's `cap_plan_year` is the plan year of the current logical clock date; an enrollment's `plan_year` is the plan year of its own `term_start_date`, so a term that starts before the fiscal boundary consumes the prior year's pool even if it is created afterwards.

The cap is tracked as four buckets on the `Employee`, all scoped to the enrollments of that employee whose `plan_year` equals the employee's `cap_plan_year`. `cap_reserved_cents` is the sum of `employer_reserved_cents` over those enrollments whose `funding_status` is `RESERVED` or `BILLED`. `cap_disbursed_cents` is the sum of `employer_disbursed_cents` over those whose `funding_status` is `SETTLED`. `cap_clawback_hold_cents` is the sum of `employer_disbursed_cents` over those whose `funding_status` is `CLAWED_BACK` and whose repayment obligation has a `status` of `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `WAIVED`. `cap_available_cents` is the governing policy's `annual_cap_cents` minus the reserved bucket minus the disbursed bucket minus the clawback-hold bucket. Enrollments whose `funding_status` is `RELEASED`, and clawed-back enrollments whose obligation has reached `SETTLED`, contribute zero to every bucket.

The invariant the system preserves after every forward action and every compensating action is that the policy's `annual_cap_cents` equals `cap_available_cents` plus `cap_reserved_cents` plus `cap_disbursed_cents` plus `cap_clawback_hold_cents`. When the employer has no current policy at all, the four buckets are still computed exactly as described and `cap_available_cents` is reported as 0. The ledger is recomputed inside the request that changed anything relevant to it — creating an enrollment, withdrawing, paying an invoice, deciding a claim, posting an outcome, recording or waiving a repayment, patching an employee, activating a policy, or running term-close — so the caller reads the settled numbers in that same response or in the very next read.

### 2.7. Version Pinning and Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations require the client to echo the `version` of a parent resource it has already read, and both reject a mismatch rather than proceeding on a stale view.

Creating an enrollment requires `expected_employee_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Employee`. If it does not equal that employee's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The employee row is bumped by anything that changes the picture the client reasoned about — another enrollment consuming cap headroom, a settlement, a clawback, an employment patch, or a policy activation that recomputes the ledger — so the version check is what stops a client from committing an enrollment against cap headroom it never actually saw. A second enrollment is perfectly legitimate business behaviour on its own, which is precisely why the guard is a version precondition and not a state check.

Curating a catalog listing requires `expected_agreement_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `PartnerAgreement`. If it does not equal that agreement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The negotiated discount that the listing's per-credit rate is frozen from lives on the agreement, so a stale view would freeze an outdated rate into the catalog permanently. Curating a second listing under the same agreement is legitimate, so again the guard is a version precondition rather than a state check. Note that a successful curation itself increments the agreement's `version` by 1, so a client curating several programs under one agreement must re-read the agreement between creates.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection perform no optimistic-concurrency check.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Hierarchical Consistency

Several operations in this system mutate more than the resource named in the path. Every such cascade completes inside the triggering request — there is no deferred worker and no eventual convergence — and every resource the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if the client had modified it directly. Where a cascade iterates a collection, the iteration order is pinned: `CatalogListing` rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, `Enrollment` rows in `created_at` ascending order, and `Employee` rows in `created_at` ascending order. Order matters because the cap ledger and the tax exclusion are both order-sensitive.

Cascades in this system chain rather than fan out from a single point. Terminating a partner agreement suspends its listings, which withdraws the registered enrollments sitting on those listings, which voids those enrollments' open invoices and recomputes the affected employees' cap ledgers. Posting a failing outcome on an enrollment whose money has already moved assesses a repayment obligation, which drives the funding axis to `CLAWED_BACK`, which appends a reversing tax ledger entry, which in turn refreshes the employee's tax and cap aggregates and the employer's roll-ups. An implementation that performs only the first hop of a chain is incorrect even though the first hop looks complete.

The employer-level roll-ups are derived, never client-supplied. `total_disbursed_cents` on an `Employer` is the sum of `employer_disbursed_cents` over enrollments belonging to that employer's employees whose `funding_status` is `SETTLED`; clawed-back and released enrollments contribute zero. `total_admin_fee_cents` is the sum of `platform_admin_fee_cents` over that employer's claims whose `status` is `APPROVED`. `total_imputed_income_cents` is the sum of `imputed_income_cents` across all tax ledger entries — `BENEFIT`, `REVERSAL` and `TRUE_UP` alike — belonging to that employer's employees whose `tax_year` equals the calendar year of the logical clock. All three initialise to 0 when the employer is onboarded.

### 2.9. Validation Ordering and Path Resolution

When a single request violates several rules at once, the response reports exactly one error: the one belonging to the lowest-numbered rule in the ordering below. The ordering is absolute and applies across every endpoint. 2. Role authorization — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-roles list; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Schema — required fields present, types and enum members valid, ISO dates parseable, `credits` between 1 and 30 inclusive, amount fields at least 1, `effective_to` strictly after `effective_from`, `term_end_date` strictly after `term_start_date`, `grade_points` supplied exactly when `completion_status` is `PASSED` or `FAILED`, `termination_date` supplied exactly when `employment_status` is being set to `TERMINATED`, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, `offset` at least 0; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. The `Employer` named in the path exists; otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `GET /employers/{id}`, `GET /employers/{id}/utilization-report`, `POST /employers/{id}/benefit-policies`, `GET /employers/{id}/benefit-policies`, `POST /employers/{id}/employees`, `GET /employers/{id}/employees`.

42. The employer's current policy has `non_catalog_approval_required` set to true; otherwise `ERR_NON_CATALOG_APPROVAL_DISABLED` (422). Applies to `POST /program-approval-requests`.
43. The effective funding model — the supplied `funding_model` if present, otherwise the policy's — is a member of the listing's `allowed_funding_models` and matches the policy's `funding_model` whenever that is not `HYBRID`; otherwise `ERR_FUNDING_MODEL_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Applies to `POST /enrollments`.
44. The employee's `cap_available_cents` is greater than 0; otherwise `ERR_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). A plan year with zero remaining headroom admits no further enrollment, but headroom that is positive yet insufficient is allowed and simply produces partial coverage. Applies to `POST /enrollments`.

### 2.10. The Educational-Assistance Tax Ledger

Every movement of benefit money leaves a row on an append-only tax ledger, and those rows are the only record of how much of an employee's benefit escaped taxation. Under the statutory educational-assistance exclusion, at most 525,000 cents — five thousand two hundred fifty dollars — of employer-provided education benefit may be excluded from an employee's income in a calendar year; anything beyond that is imputed income added to taxable wages. Each entry records the `employee_id`, the `enrollment_id` whose money moved, the `entry_type`, the `tax_year`, and the three-way split of `benefit_cents` into `excluded_cents` and `imputed_income_cents`, stamped with `posted_at`.

The `entry_type` vocabulary is `BENEFIT` when money is disbursed, `REVERSAL` when a disbursement is clawed back, and `TRUE_UP` for the annual aggregate reconciliation. Reversal rows carry negative amounts. True-up rows are annual in scope rather than enrollment-scoped, so their `enrollment_id` is null and their `benefit_cents` is always 0. On every row without exception, `benefit_cents` equals `excluded_cents` plus `imputed_income_cents`. Across all of an employee's rows for one `tax_year`, the sum of `benefit_cents` equals the sum of `excluded_cents` plus the sum of `imputed_income_cents`, the employee's `taxable_excluded_cents_ytd` equals the sum of `excluded_cents`, and the employee's `imputed_income_cents_ytd` equals the sum of `imputed_income_cents`. Both employee aggregates are scoped to the entries whose `tax_year` equals the calendar year of the logical clock, counted across all three entry types.

Attribution order is pinned: entries are considered in `posted_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the only tie-break — never by `id`. This matters because the exclusion headroom is consumed in arrival order, so which disbursement absorbs the imputed income depends entirely on sequence.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Employer Onboarding, Policy Versions and the Eligibility Verdict

A `PlatformAdmin` opens the relationship by onboarding the employer client, supplying the `legal_name` (a non-blank string of 1 to 160 characters), the `benefit_admin_contact_email` mailbox the benefits owner is reachable at (which must contain an at-sign), and the `fiscal_year_start_month` as a calendar month number from 1 through 12 — the value that later decides which plan-year bucket an enrollment consumes. An optional `status` may be supplied; it accepts `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`. The three reporting roll-ups on the employer record — `total_disbursed_cents`, `total_admin_fee_cents` and `total_imputed_income_cents` — are all initialised to 0 at onboarding and are thereafter derived rather than written.

Benefit rules live in versioned policies rather than on the employer itself, and a `BenefitAdministrator` drafts each version. A draft carries the half-open window it governs — `effective_from` is the inclusive first date and `effective_to` the exclusive last date, and `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from` — along with the eligibility gates and money rules: `min_tenure_months` as a count of whole months of at least 0, `min_weekly_hours` as a non-negative number of scheduled hours, `eligible_job_families` as a non-empty subset of `ENGINEERING`, `OPERATIONS`, `CLINICAL`, `RETAIL_FRONTLINE` and `CORPORATE`, `annual_cap_cents` of at least 1, a `funding_model` of `DIRECT_BILL`, `REIMBURSEMENT` or `HYBRID`, a `reimbursement_pct` greater than 0 and at most 1 naming the share of negotiated tuition the employer covers under reimbursement, `max_concurrent_credits` of at least 1, `drop_window_days` of at least 0, and `service_commitment_months` of at least 1 describing how long continued employment is owed after a funded term ends. Two further fields are optional: `min_passing_grade_points`, which defaults to 2.00 and sets the grade-point floor for a term to count as academic progress, and `non_catalog_approval_required`, which defaults to true and, when false, closes the non-catalog exception path entirely. A freshly drafted policy always has `status` `DRAFT` — the field is system-managed and cannot be supplied — and drafting recomputes nothing; no employee's eligibility changes while a policy sits in draft. The policy's owning employer is recorded as `employer_id`, and the collection of an employer's versions can be filtered by `status`.

Activation moves a policy `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is the moment the rules take hold. A `BenefitAdministrator` may activate only a policy that is still `DRAFT` — activating one that is already `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT` — only for its own employer, and only when the policy's half-open window does not intersect the window of any other `ACTIVE` policy of the same employer; overlap is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, while two windows that merely touch at a boundary are explicitly not an overlap. On success the policy becomes `ACTIVE`, and every `Employee` of that employer is reprocessed in `created_at` ascending order: each one recomputes `eligibility_status`, `cap_plan_year`, `cap_reserved_cents`, `cap_disbursed_cents`, `cap_clawback_hold_cents` and `cap_available_cents` against the employer's now-current policy, and each recomputed employee has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Enrollments that already exist are deliberately left alone: each keeps the `policy_id` it pinned at creation and every money field it has already computed. Activation never repoints an enrollment at the newer policy.

The other end of a policy's life is automatic. When the operator batch closes a term, any policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` is on or before the logical clock date moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, and every `Employee` of that employer then recomputes `eligibility_status` in `created_at` ascending order. An employer left with no current policy yields `INELIGIBLE_NO_POLICY` for all of its people. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

Employees are registered by the `BenefitAdministrator` under the employer, with a `full_name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `work_email` containing an at-sign, a `hire_date`, `weekly_hours` between 0 and 80 inclusive, and a `job_family` drawn from the same five-value vocabulary the policy filters on. An optional `employment_status` accepts `ACTIVE`, `LEAVE` or `TERMINATED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; `termination_date` defaults to null and is required exactly when `employment_status` is being set to `TERMINATED`, and must be null otherwise. Each employee also carries the `employer_id` of the employing organisation. Tenure is derived, never supplied: `tenure_months` counts the whole months from `hire_date` to the logical clock date, where a month completes once the day-of-month is reached or passed.

The eligibility verdict itself is a computed enum, and its evaluation order is fixed because several conditions can hold at once. The verdict is measured against the employer's current policy — the unique policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_from` is on or before the logical clock date while its `effective_to` is strictly after it — and resolves as follows, stopping at the first match: no current policy yields `INELIGIBLE_NO_POLICY`; an `employment_status` other than `ACTIVE` yields `INELIGIBLE_EMPLOYMENT`; `tenure_months` below the policy's `min_tenure_months` yields `INELIGIBLE_TENURE`; `weekly_hours` below the policy's `min_weekly_hours` yields `INELIGIBLE_HOURS`; a `job_family` that is not a member of the policy's `eligible_job_families` yields `INELIGIBLE_JOB_FAMILY`; and anything that survives all five yields `ELIGIBLE`. Reading an employee returns the live verdict together with the cap ledger, the year-to-date tax aggregates `taxable_excluded_cents_ytd` and `imputed_income_cents_ytd`, the `open_repayment_balance_cents` figure — the sum of `balance_cents` across that employee's obligations whose `status` is `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` — and the `coaching_hold` flag. The employer's roster can be filtered by `eligibility_status` and by `job_family`.

Employment facts change, and a `BenefitAdministrator` may patch `weekly_hours`, `job_family`, `employment_status` and `termination_date` on an employee. Any such patch recomputes `eligibility_status`; a patch to `TERMINATED` yields `INELIGIBLE_EMPLOYMENT` and additionally runs the early-departure cascade described in §3.7.

### 3.2. Schools, Programs and Partner Agreements

Schools are directory records maintained by a `PlatformAdmin`, described by a `legal_name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the main campus's US state, a `contact_email` containing an at-sign, and an `accreditation_status` that accepts `ACCREDITED`, `PROBATION` or `UNACCREDITED` and defaults to `ACCREDITED`. Accreditation is not decorative: it gates both agreement activation and non-catalog approval.

Programs are published under a school by either a `SchoolPartner` acting for that school or a `PlatformAdmin`, and each records the `school_id` of the offering institution together with a `name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `credential_type` of `CERTIFICATE`, `ASSOCIATE`, `BACHELOR` or `MASTER`, a `skill_track` of `DATA_ANALYTICS`, `NURSING`, `SUPPLY_CHAIN`, `CYBERSECURITY` or `BUSINESS_ADMIN`, a `list_tuition_per_credit_cents` of at least 1 giving published tuition before any discount, a `total_credits` of at least 1 for the whole credential, and a nominal `term_length_weeks` of at least 1. The credential type drives the persistence base risk described in §3.8, and the skill track is what gets reported as skill attainment when a learner completes. A school's program list can be filtered by `credential_type` and by `skill_track`.

Commercial terms between one school and one employer live on a partner agreement, which a `PlatformAdmin` drafts. The draft names the `school_id` and `employer_id` parties, a `negotiated_discount_pct` off list tuition that must be at least 0 and at most 0.60, a `direct_bill_enabled` flag that defaults to false and decides whether listings under this agreement may be enrolled on a direct-bill basis, and the coverage window `effective_from` and `effective_to`, both inclusive, with `effective_to` strictly later than `effective_from`; an enrollment's `term_start_date` must fall inside that closed window. A new agreement's `status` is `PENDING` and its `terminated_on` is null; both are system-managed. Agreements can be listed and filtered by `school_id`, by `employer_id` and by `status`, and reading a single agreement is how a client obtains the `version` it must echo when curating a listing.

Activation is the employer's act. A `BenefitAdministrator` activating its own agreement moves it `PENDING → ACTIVE`, provided the agreement is still `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE` — and provided the school's `accreditation_status` is not `UNACCREDITED`; an unaccredited school is rejected with `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_ACCREDITED`. A school on `PROBATION` may be activated. Activation increments the agreement's `version` by 1.

Termination is the same actor's undoing of the relationship, and it is the widest cascade in the system. The agreement must currently be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. On success the agreement moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, `terminated_on` is stamped with the logical clock date, and `version` is incremented by 1. Then, in order: every `CatalogListing` under this agreement whose `status` is `CURATED` moves to `SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Because those listings are now suspended, every `Enrollment` sitting on them whose `academic_status` is `REGISTERED` moves its academic axis to `WITHDRAWN` and its funding axis from `RESERVED` or `BILLED` to `RELEASED` — enrollments already `IN_PROGRESS` are deliberately left running, on the reasoning that a learner mid-term should not be stranded. Because those enrollments are now released, each of their `TuitionInvoice` rows whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` moves to `VOIDED`, and every affected employee's cap ledger is recomputed. Separately, every `ProgramApprovalRequest` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and whose program belongs to the terminated agreement's school moves to `REJECTED` with `decision_note` set to the literal text `school partnership terminated`. `TERMINATED` is terminal for an agreement.

### 3.3. Curating the Eligible Catalog and the Non-Catalog Exception Path

A program only becomes spendable for an employer's people once it is curated into that employer's catalog. A `BenefitAdministrator` curates by naming the `employer_id`, the `program_id`, the `agreement_id` under which the program is covered, and the `expected_agreement_version` described in §2.7. The referenced agreement must have `status` `ACTIVE`, must actually join the supplied employer to the school that offers the supplied program, and must have a non-empty coverage window; a failure on any of those three counts is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Curation is also idempotence-guarded: if a `CURATED` listing, or an approval request in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, already exists for the same employer-and-program pair, the attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING`.

A curated listing records `approval_source` `CATALOG`, `status` `CURATED`, and a null `source_request_id`, and it freezes two computed values. The `negotiated_tuition_per_credit_cents` is the program's `list_tuition_per_credit_cents` multiplied by one minus the agreement's `negotiated_discount_pct`, with the result rounded half up to a whole cent at that point and only that point. The `allowed_funding_models` array is the pair `DIRECT_BILL` and `REIMBURSEMENT` when the listing's source is `CATALOG` and the agreement's `direct_bill_enabled` is true, and the single-element list containing only `REIMBURSEMENT` in every other case. Neither value is ever re-derived: an agreement terminated a year later suspends the listing but never rewrites its rate. Curating also increments the agreement's `version` by 1. Listings can be filtered by `employer_id`, `program_id`, `agreement_id`, `source_request_id`, `status` and `approval_source`.

Withdrawing a program from the catalog is a suspension, not a deletion. A `BenefitAdministrator` may move a listing `CURATED → SUSPENDED`; a listing that is already `SUSPENDED` cannot be suspended again and the attempt returns `ERR_LISTING_NOT_CURATED`. Suspension is intentionally narrow — enrollments already sitting on the listing continue completely unchanged — but a suspended listing can no longer be enrolled against and can no longer have a registration confirmed on it, and both of those refusals also carry `ERR_LISTING_NOT_CURATED`.

Learners sometimes want a program nobody curated, and the exception path exists for exactly that. An `Employee` opens the request by naming its own `employee_id`, the `program_id` being requested, a `justification` of 1 to 2,000 characters, and a `requested_annual_cost_cents` estimate of at least 0 supplied purely for the reviewer's context. The request is only admissible when the employer has a current policy (otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`), when that policy has `non_catalog_approval_required` set to true (otherwise `ERR_NON_CATALOG_APPROVAL_DISABLED`), when the requester's `eligibility_status` is not one of `INELIGIBLE_EMPLOYMENT`, `INELIGIBLE_HOURS` or `INELIGIBLE_JOB_FAMILY` (otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`), when `tenure_months` reaches the policy's `min_tenure_months` (otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYEE_TENURE_NOT_MET`), and when no curated listing or open request already covers the same employer-and-program pair (otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING`). A new request starts in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_count` 0 and a null `decision_note`. The collection can be filtered by `employee_id`, `program_id` and `status`.

Review is a two-step affair. A `BenefitAdministrator` for the requester's employer first takes the request `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; calling for review on a request in any other state is `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`. The reviewer then decides, supplying a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`, and the three decisions produce sharply different outcomes. A `decision` of `APPROVE` moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, records the supplied note in `decision_note`, and materialises a brand-new catalog listing for the requesting employee's employer: `program_id` copied from the request, `agreement_id` null because no agreement exists, `source_request_id` set to the request's own `id`, `approval_source` `APPROVED_EXCEPTION`, `status` `CURATED`, `negotiated_tuition_per_credit_cents` equal to the program's `list_tuition_per_credit_cents` with no discount applied at all, and `allowed_funding_models` containing only `REIMBURSEMENT`. Approval carries two extra preconditions the other branches do not: the offering school's `accreditation_status` must be exactly `ACCREDITED`, so a school on `PROBATION` is refused here with `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_ACCREDITED`, and the requesting employee's `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE`. A `decision` of `REJECT` moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, records `decision_note`, and creates no listing whatsoever. A `decision` of `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, records `decision_note`, creates no listing, and opens the revision cycle. Both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

The revision cycle is bounded. From `CHANGES_REQUESTED` the requesting `Employee` may resubmit, moving the request `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and incrementing `revision_count` by 1; the resubmission may optionally replace `justification` and `requested_annual_cost_cents`, and each supplied value overwrites the previous one. Resubmission requires `revision_count` to be strictly less than 2, so the loop from `CHANGES_REQUESTED` back through `SUBMITTED` and `UNDER_REVIEW` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` can run at most twice; a third resubmission attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Resubmitting a request that is not in `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is `ERR_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.4. Enrolling: Reserving Against the Annual Cap

Enrollment is where eligibility, catalog and money meet. An `Employee` enrolls by supplying its own `employee_id`, the `catalog_listing_id` that authorizes the benefit, a `term_code` label of 1 to 16 characters such as `2026-FA`, the `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` bounding a term whose interval is treated as closed at both ends, a `credits` count between 1 and 30 inclusive, and the `expected_employee_version` that §2.7 requires. An optional `funding_model` completes the payload; its handling is described below.

Several gates are evaluated before anything is written, and their relative order is the one fixed in §2.9. The listing must be `CURATED` or the attempt fails with `ERR_LISTING_NOT_CURATED`. The employer must have exactly one current policy, or `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`. The employee's `eligibility_status` must not be `INELIGIBLE_EMPLOYMENT`, `INELIGIBLE_HOURS` or `INELIGIBLE_JOB_FAMILY`, or `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; note that a tenure shortfall is reported separately and more specifically as `ERR_EMPLOYEE_TENURE_NOT_MET` when `tenure_months` falls below `min_tenure_months`. A `coaching_hold` of true blocks the enrollment with `ERR_COACHING_HOLD_ACTIVE`. The concurrent-credit ceiling is then checked: the system sums `credits` across the employee's enrollments whose `academic_status` is `REGISTERED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose closed term interval shares at least one calendar day with the new term's closed interval — a single shared day is an overlap — adds the new enrollment's own `credits`, and refuses with `ERR_CONCURRENT_CREDIT_LIMIT` when that total exceeds the policy's `max_concurrent_credits`. A repeat enrollment for the same employee, listing and `term_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` unless the earlier one is `WITHDRAWN` or `FAILED`, which are the two outcomes that release the slot. The effective funding model must be a member of the listing's `allowed_funding_models` and must agree with the policy's own `funding_model` whenever that is not `HYBRID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_MODEL_NOT_ALLOWED`. Finally, `cap_available_cents` must be strictly greater than 0, or `ERR_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED` is returned; positive-but-insufficient headroom is explicitly allowed and simply yields partial coverage.

The funding model resolves as follows. When the pinned policy's `funding_model` is `HYBRID`, the enrollment's `funding_model` must be supplied and may be either `DIRECT_BILL` or `REIMBURSEMENT` — the learner picks per enrollment. When the policy names a single model, the enrollment's `funding_model` defaults to the policy's value if omitted, and if it is supplied it must match that value exactly.

On success the enrollment pins its context and computes its money. `policy_id` is pinned to the employer's current policy and never repointed afterwards. `plan_year` is the plan year of the enrollment's own `term_start_date`. `drop_deadline_date` is the `term_start_date` advanced by the policy's `drop_window_days`; a withdrawal on or before that date releases funding with no repayment obligation. `gross_tuition_cents` is the listing's already-rounded `negotiated_tuition_per_credit_cents` multiplied by the enrollment's `credits`. The provisional employer entitlement is then computed: under `DIRECT_BILL` the uncapped amount is the whole `gross_tuition_cents`, because the employer fronts the full negotiated tuition, whereas under `REIMBURSEMENT` the uncapped amount is `gross_tuition_cents` multiplied by the policy's `reimbursement_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. `employer_reserved_cents` is then the smaller of that uncapped amount and the employee's `cap_available_cents`, and `employee_share_cents` is `gross_tuition_cents` minus `employer_reserved_cents`. No academic input participates at this stage — there is no outcome yet.

The new enrollment starts with `academic_status` `REGISTERED` on the academic axis and `funding_status` `RESERVED` on the money axis, with `credits_earned` 0, `employer_settled_cents` 0, `employer_disbursed_cents` 0 and `last_session_date` null. Its `persistence_risk_score` and `persistence_tier` are computed immediately, the owning employee's `coaching_hold` is recomputed, the employee's cap ledger is recomputed, and the employee's `version` is incremented by 1 — which is precisely why a client enrolling twice in a row must re-read the employee between the two calls.

The `employee_share_cents` field is not frozen at creation; it tracks the money axis. While `funding_status` is `RESERVED` or `BILLED` it is `gross_tuition_cents` minus `employer_reserved_cents`. Once `funding_status` is `SETTLED` or `CLAWED_BACK` it is `gross_tuition_cents` minus `employer_settled_cents`. When `funding_status` is `RELEASED` the employer contributes nothing and the field is the full `gross_tuition_cents`; the `employer_reserved_cents` figure is retained on the record for audit but excluded from every cap bucket. Enrollments can be listed and filtered by `employee_id`, `catalog_listing_id`, `policy_id`, `term_code`, `academic_status` and `funding_status`, and a single read returns both status axes together with every money field.

### 3.5. Registration, Direct Billing and the Up-Front Money Path

Registration confirmation is the school's acknowledgement that the learner actually showed up, and it is the single trigger from which the two funding models produce entirely disjoint outcomes. A `SchoolPartner` acting for the school that offers the program behind the listing may confirm only when the enrollment's `academic_status` is `REGISTERED`, its `funding_status` is `RESERVED`, and the underlying listing's `status` is still `CURATED`. A confirmation moves the academic axis `REGISTERED → IN_PROGRESS` in every case, recomputes the enrollment's `persistence_risk_score` and `persistence_tier`, and refreshes the owning employee's `coaching_hold`.

The fork follows. When the enrollment's `funding_model` is `DIRECT_BILL`, a `TuitionInvoice` is raised against the enrollment: its `enrollment_id` points back at the enrollment, its `amount_cents` is the enrollment's `employer_reserved_cents` as of that moment, its `due_date` is the `term_start_date` advanced by 30 days, its `status` is `ISSUED` and its `paid_on` is null — and the enrollment's money axis moves `RESERVED → BILLED`. When the `funding_model` is `REIMBURSEMENT`, no invoice is created at all and the money axis stays `RESERVED` until a claim is approved. The invoices raised for an enrollment can be listed and filtered by `status`.

Paying an invoice is deliberately split between two parties. The employer's `BenefitAdministrator` approves, moving an invoice `ISSUED → APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`; this is permitted only while the invoice is `ISSUED` — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_INVALID_STATE` — and only while the underlying enrollment's `academic_status` is `REGISTERED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. The `PlatformAdmin` then disburses, moving the invoice `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT → PAID` and stamping `paid_on` with the logical clock date. Disbursement requires the invoice to be `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`, the enrollment's `academic_status` not to be `WITHDRAWN`, and the enrollment's `funding_status` to be `BILLED`. Both `PAID` and `VOIDED` are terminal invoice states.

Disbursement is where the up-front variant of the award computation lands, and its defining property is that money leaves before any academic outcome exists — so no earned-credit count and no grade point participates. The enrollment's money axis moves `BILLED → SETTLED`, and both `employer_settled_cents` and `employer_disbursed_cents` are set to the invoice's `amount_cents`, which is itself the reservation the employer committed at confirmation time. Academic performance is not a condition here at all; it is recovered afterwards, if at all, through a repayment obligation. The same request appends a `BENEFIT` tax ledger entry, recomputes the employee's cap ledger and both year-to-date tax aggregates, and recomputes the employer's `total_disbursed_cents` and `total_imputed_income_cents`.

The tax split on a disbursement works forward through the year's exclusion headroom. The system first sums `excluded_cents` across all of that employee's ledger entries whose `tax_year` matches the year in question, then takes the headroom as the exclusion limit of 525,000 cents minus that prior total, floored at 0 so an already-exhausted year yields no headroom rather than a negative one. The `excluded_cents` on the new entry is the smaller of the amount just disbursed and that headroom, and `imputed_income_cents` is the amount disbursed minus `excluded_cents`. The entry is appended with `entry_type` `BENEFIT`, `benefit_cents` equal to the full amount disbursed, the `employee_id` and `enrollment_id` of the money that moved, the `tax_year` taken from the calendar year of the logical clock, and `posted_at` stamped at the logical clock instant. Because headroom is consumed in arrival order, the first disbursements of a year are fully excluded and later ones absorb the imputed income. The ledger can be read whole and filtered by `employee_id`, `enrollment_id`, `tax_year` and `entry_type`, or one entry at a time.

Invoices do not always get paid. Any invoice whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` moves to `VOIDED` when the system releases the enrollment behind it — a withdrawal inside the drop window, an employment-termination cascade, or an agreement-termination cascade. Correspondingly, the money axis may move `BILLED → RELEASED`, but only while no invoice of that enrollment has reached `PAID`; once cash has left, release is no longer the right compensating action and clawback is. A release retains `employer_reserved_cents` on the record for audit while excluding it from every cap bucket, and it recomputes the employee's `cap_reserved_cents` and `cap_available_cents`.

### 3.6. Academic Outcomes and the Reimbursement Money Path

Outcomes are reported by the school and are append-only. A `SchoolPartner` posts one against an enrollment whose `academic_status` is `IN_PROGRESS` — any other academic state is `ERR_ENROLLMENT_INVALID_ACADEMIC_STATE` — supplying a `term_code` that must equal the enrollment's own `term_code`, a `completion_status` of `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `INCOMPLETE`, a `credits_earned` count that must be at least 0 and at most the enrollment's `credits`, and `grade_points` between 0.00 and 4.00 which are required exactly when `completion_status` is `PASSED` or `FAILED` and must be null otherwise. The row records the `enrollment_id` it closes, a `posted_on` stamped with the logical clock date, and a `posted_by_role` of `SCHOOL_PARTNER` when a school posted it or `SYSTEM` when the platform synthesized it. A `PASSED` outcome must additionally clear the pinned policy's `min_passing_grade_points`: `grade_points` below that floor is refused with `ERR_ACADEMIC_PROGRESS_NOT_MET`. The enrollment's own `credits_earned` mirrors the `credits_earned` of its latest outcome, and is 0 while no outcome exists. The outcome log for an enrollment is readable as a paginated collection.

A passing outcome moves the academic axis `IN_PROGRESS → PASSED`, copies `credits_earned` onto the enrollment, and recomputes `persistence_risk_score`, `persistence_tier` and the employee's `coaching_hold`. What it pointedly does **not** do is touch the money axis: a reimbursement enrollment stays `RESERVED` until a claim is approved, and a direct-bill enrollment stays exactly where its invoice left it. A failing outcome moves the academic axis `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED` and an incomplete one moves it `IN_PROGRESS → INCOMPLETE`; both copy `credits_earned`, both recompute the persistence figures and the coaching hold, and both run the clawback chain described in §3.7 when the money has already settled. All four of `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `INCOMPLETE` are terminal on the academic axis.

Reimbursement claims are the learner's route to money on a reimbursement enrollment. An `Employee` files a claim carrying an `amount_requested_cents` of at least 1 and a `receipt_reference` of 1 to 64 characters identifying the school receipt that supports it. Filing requires the enrollment's `academic_status` to be `PASSED`, its `funding_status` to be `RESERVED`, and its `funding_model` to be `REIMBURSEMENT` — filing against a direct-bill enrollment is refused with `ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_NOT_ALLOWED` — and the enrollment's latest outcome must have `completion_status` `PASSED` with `grade_points` at least the policy's `min_passing_grade_points`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_ACADEMIC_PROGRESS_NOT_MET`. A filed claim records its `enrollment_id`, starts with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `approved_amount_cents` 0, `platform_admin_fee_cents` 0 and a null `decision_note`. An enrollment's claims can be filtered by `status`.

Deciding a claim is the employer's act, carried out with a `decision` and an optional `decision_note`, and the two branches diverge completely. Deciding a claim that is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`; both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

On a `decision` of `APPROVE`, the claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and six computation steps run in exactly this pinned order. First, the outcome-conditioned final entitlement is derived. The earned-credit base is `gross_tuition_cents` multiplied by the ratio of the latest outcome's `credits_earned` to the enrollment's `credits`, rounded half up to a whole cent at that point. The effective share is the policy's `reimbursement_pct` plus a grade bonus of 0.10 when the latest outcome's `grade_points` are at least 3.50 and plus nothing otherwise, with the sum capped at 1.00 so the employer never covers more than the whole tuition. The uncapped final entitlement is the earned-credit base multiplied by that effective share, rounded half up to a whole cent — note that the base is rounded *before* the share is applied, not after. The enrollment's `employer_settled_cents` is then the smaller of that uncapped final entitlement and the sum of `employer_reserved_cents` and the employee's `cap_available_cents`; because the headroom is added back to the original reservation, this final figure can legitimately come out *above* the provisional reserve as well as below it. Second, `approved_amount_cents` is the smallest of three quantities: the claim's `amount_requested_cents`, that final entitlement, and the sum of the enrollment's `employer_reserved_cents` and the employee's `cap_available_cents`; this three-way comparison is exact integer arithmetic with no rounding. Third, the fee basis is snapshotted as the employer's `total_disbursed_cents` as it stood *before* this request mutated anything, which means it excludes the amount being approved right now. Fourth, the fee percentage is chosen from that basis: 5 percent while the basis is below 5,000,000 cents (fifty thousand dollars of lifetime employer spend), 4 percent while the basis is at least 5,000,000 cents and below 20,000,000 cents, and 3 percent once the basis reaches 20,000,000 cents. Fifth, `platform_admin_fee_cents` is `approved_amount_cents` multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to a whole cent. Sixth, the employer's `total_disbursed_cents` grows by `approved_amount_cents` and its `total_admin_fee_cents` grows by `platform_admin_fee_cents`, so the *next* settlement may well land in a cheaper tier. The same request moves the enrollment's money axis `RESERVED → SETTLED`, sets both `employer_settled_cents` and `employer_disbursed_cents` to `approved_amount_cents`, appends a `BENEFIT` tax ledger entry under the same headroom arithmetic described in §3.5, and recomputes the employee's cap ledger and tax aggregates.

On a `decision` of `REJECT`, the claim moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, the supplied note is recorded in `decision_note`, `approved_amount_cents` stays 0 and `platform_admin_fee_cents` stays 0. The enrollment's money axis moves `RESERVED → RELEASED`, and the employee's `cap_reserved_cents` and `cap_available_cents` are recomputed so the headroom returns to the pool. No tax ledger entry is appended on this branch, and no employer roll-up changes.

### 3.7. Withdrawal, Clawback and the Repayment Obligation

Withdrawal is the learner's own exit, permitted while the enrollment's `academic_status` is either `REGISTERED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; from both states the academic axis moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and in both cases an `AcademicOutcome` is appended with `completion_status` `WITHDRAWN`, `grade_points` null, `credits_earned` 0 and `posted_by_role` `SYSTEM`. What differs is the money, and the discriminator is the `drop_deadline_date`. When the logical clock date is on or before that deadline, the money axis moves from `RESERVED` or `BILLED` to `RELEASED`, every `TuitionInvoice` of the enrollment whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` moves to `VOIDED`, and no repayment obligation is created at all. When the logical clock date is past the deadline and the money axis has already reached `SETTLED`, a repayment obligation is assessed with `trigger_reason` `WITHDRAWAL_AFTER_DROP_WINDOW` and a `principal_cents` equal to the full `employer_disbursed_cents`, the money axis moves `SETTLED → CLAWED_BACK`, and a `REVERSAL` tax ledger entry is appended. Either way, the employee's cap ledger, tax aggregates, `open_repayment_balance_cents` and `coaching_hold` are recomputed, and the employer's roll-ups follow.

The same clawback chain fires on a `FAILED` or `INCOMPLETE` outcome, with one difference in the reason recorded. Because the academic axis turned `FAILED` (or `INCOMPLETE`) while the money axis stood at `SETTLED`, an obligation is created with `trigger_reason` `ACADEMIC_FAILURE` and `principal_cents` equal to the full `employer_disbursed_cents`, and the money axis moves `SETTLED → CLAWED_BACK`. Because the money axis became `CLAWED_BACK`, a `REVERSAL` tax entry mirroring the assessed principal is appended. Because that reversal landed, the employee's `taxable_excluded_cents_ytd`, `imputed_income_cents_ytd`, `cap_disbursed_cents`, `cap_clawback_hold_cents`, `cap_available_cents` and `open_repayment_balance_cents` are recomputed, and so are the employer's `total_disbursed_cents` and `total_imputed_income_cents`. The `employer_disbursed_cents` figure itself is never zeroed by a reversal — the money genuinely left, and the record of it is retained while the enrollment sits in `CLAWED_BACK`. If instead the money axis stood at `RESERVED` or `BILLED` when the failing outcome arrived, there is nothing to recover: no obligation and no reversal are produced, the money axis moves to `RELEASED`, and any open invoice moves to `VOIDED`.

Employment termination produces the third clawback reason. When a `BenefitAdministrator` patches an employee's `employment_status` to `TERMINATED` — which requires `termination_date` in the same request — every enrollment of that employee whose `academic_status` is `REGISTERED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with the money axis going from `RESERVED` or `BILLED` to `RELEASED` and open invoices moving to `VOIDED`. Separately, every enrollment whose `funding_status` is `SETTLED` and whose `term_end_date` falls within the policy's `service_commitment_months` before the `termination_date` moves `SETTLED → CLAWED_BACK` with a prorated obligation whose `trigger_reason` is `EARLY_DEPARTURE`, plus the mirroring `REVERSAL` entry. The employee's cap ledger, tax aggregates, `open_repayment_balance_cents` and `coaching_hold` are recomputed, as are the employer's aggregates.

Assessment arithmetic depends on the reason. For `ACADEMIC_FAILURE` and for `WITHDRAWAL_AFTER_DROP_WINDOW`, `principal_cents` is the full `employer_disbursed_cents` — the employer recovers everything it paid. For `EARLY_DEPARTURE`, the recovery is prorated by the part of the service commitment the employee did not serve: the system counts the whole months elapsed from the enrollment's `term_end_date` to the employee's `termination_date`, where a month completes once the day-of-month is reached or passed and a negative span clamps to zero months; the unserved remainder is the policy's `service_commitment_months` minus that elapsed count, floored at zero; and `principal_cents` is `employer_disbursed_cents` multiplied by the unserved remainder divided by `service_commitment_months`, rounded half up to a whole cent. When the unserved remainder comes out at zero months the principal would be zero, and in that case **no obligation row is created at all** — the commitment was fully served and nothing is owed. Only an enrollment whose `funding_status` is `SETTLED` with a positive `employer_disbursed_cents` is ever assessed; enrollments in `RESERVED`, `BILLED`, `RELEASED` or already `CLAWED_BACK` are never assessed.

The reversing tax entry mirrors the original disbursement rather than recomputing headroom from scratch. The system locates the `BENEFIT` entry for the same `enrollment_id` and `tax_year`, takes the reversed exclusion as the smaller of the obligation's `principal_cents` and that origin entry's `excluded_cents`, and appends a `REVERSAL` entry whose `benefit_cents` is the negative of `principal_cents`, whose `excluded_cents` is the negative of that reversed exclusion, and whose `imputed_income_cents` is the negative of the principal minus the reversed exclusion. The per-row identity that `benefit_cents` equals `excluded_cents` plus `imputed_income_cents` therefore holds on reversals as it does everywhere else.

An obligation records the `enrollment_id` whose benefit is being recovered, the `trigger_reason`, the assessed `principal_cents`, a `paid_cents` starting at 0, a `waived_cents` starting at 0, a `balance_cents` equal to `principal_cents` minus `paid_cents` minus `waived_cents`, a `status` of `ASSESSED`, and an `assessed_on` stamped with the logical clock date. The identity that `principal_cents` equals `paid_cents` plus `waived_cents` plus `balance_cents` holds after every operation on the row. An enrollment's obligations can be filtered by `status`.

Repayment is the learner's compensating action, submitted as an `amount_cents`. A payment strictly smaller than the current `balance_cents` increases `paid_cents` by that amount, recomputes `balance_cents`, and moves the obligation `ASSESSED → PARTIALLY_PAID`; a further such payment from `PARTIALLY_PAID` keeps it in `PARTIALLY_PAID`, so repeated instalments loop in that state until the balance reaches zero. A payment exactly equal to the current `balance_cents` — permitted from either `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` — raises `paid_cents` to `principal_cents` minus `waived_cents`, drives `balance_cents` to 0 and moves the obligation to `SETTLED`. Any `amount_cents` greater than `balance_cents` is refused with `ERR_REPAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, and paying an obligation that is already `SETTLED` or `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_INVALID_STATE`. While a balance remains, the clawback hold on the cap is emphatically **not** released; only on reaching `SETTLED` does the enrollment's amount leave `cap_clawback_hold_cents`, raising `cap_available_cents` by that amount, because the employer has been made whole. Every payment recomputes `open_repayment_balance_cents`.

Waiving is the employer's compensating action and its cap treatment is the mirror image. A `BenefitAdministrator` may waive an obligation whose `status` is `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; `waived_cents` is set to the `balance_cents` as it stands at waive time, `balance_cents` becomes 0, and the obligation moves to `WAIVED`. Because the employer absorbed the cost rather than recovering it, the amount **stays** in `cap_clawback_hold_cents` and `cap_available_cents` does not rise — a waiver forgives the learner without refilling that plan year's pool. `open_repayment_balance_cents` is recomputed. Both `SETTLED` and `WAIVED` are terminal for an obligation.

### 3.8. Coaching, Persistence Risk and the Coaching Hold

Coaches log advising contacts against a specific enrollment. A `Coach` supplies a `session_date`, which must not be later than the logical clock date, a `modality` of `PHONE`, `VIDEO`, `IN_PERSON` or `ASYNC_MESSAGE`, an optional `risk_flags` array that defaults to the empty array and must be a duplicate-free subset of `FINANCIAL_STRESS`, `SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `ACADEMIC_STRUGGLE`, `LOW_ENGAGEMENT` and `CARE_RESPONSIBILITY`, and optional free-text `notes` of up to 2,000 characters that default to null. The session records the `enrollment_id` it advises and a `coach_user_id` copied verbatim from the acting coach's `X-User-ID` header — an operational identifier, deliberately not a foreign key to anything. After the score is recomputed at the end of the request, the resulting value is stamped onto the session as `persistence_risk_score_after`, giving a per-session audit trail of the coaching's effect. Each session also updates the enrollment's `last_session_date`, which is the `session_date` of the most recent session ordered by `session_date` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and is null while no session exists. An enrollment's coaching log is readable as a paginated collection.

The `persistence_risk_score` itself is a whole number of points assembled from five contributions and then clamped into the range 0 through 100. The base is set by the program's `credential_type`: 10 points for a `CERTIFICATE`, 20 for an `ASSOCIATE`, 30 for a `BACHELOR` and 25 for a `MASTER`. An outcome penalty of 30 points is added when the enrollment's latest outcome has a `completion_status` of `FAILED` or `INCOMPLETE`, and nothing is added otherwise. A staleness penalty of 20 points is added when either no coaching session exists for the enrollment at all, or the logical clock date is more than six weeks and three days — 45 days — after the `last_session_date`; otherwise nothing is added. An engagement credit is *subtracted*: the system counts the enrollment's sessions whose `session_date` is within those same 45 days of the logical clock date, awards 5 points of credit per such session, and caps the total credit at 20 points, so a fifth recent session adds nothing further. A flag penalty is added at 5 points per observed risk flag, counting the total number of `risk_flags` entries across the three most recent sessions of that enrollment ordered by `session_date` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and capping the counted flags at 4 — so the flag penalty never exceeds 20 points. The score is the base plus the outcome penalty plus the staleness penalty plus the flag penalty minus the engagement credit, clamped so that it is never below 0 and never above 100. Only sessions belonging to the enrollment being scored count; sessions on a learner's other enrollments never leak across.

The `persistence_tier` is a plain banding of that score: `LOW` while the score is below 30, `MEDIUM` while it is at least 30 and below 60, and `HIGH` once it reaches 60. The tier is what gates further study. An employee's `coaching_hold` is true whenever at least one of that employee's enrollments has an `academic_status` of `REGISTERED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `INCOMPLETE` together with a `persistence_tier` of `HIGH`, and it is false otherwise. While the hold is up, a new enrollment is refused with `ERR_COACHING_HOLD_ACTIVE` — the remedy is coaching, since recent sessions both earn engagement credit and clear the staleness penalty, which is usually enough to drop the tier below `HIGH`. Score, tier and hold are recomputed on enrollment creation, on every coaching session, on registration confirmation, on every posted outcome, on withdrawal, and during the term-close batch.

### 3.9. Term Close, the Annual True-Up and Employer Reporting

A `PlatformAdmin` closes out a term with an operator batch that names the `term_code` being closed and optionally a `run_annual_true_up` flag and a `tax_year`. The batch runs five steps in order and reports what it did as `superseded_policy_count`, `closed_enrollment_count`, `obligation_assessed_count`, `true_up_entry_count` and `coaching_hold_employee_count`.

Step one supersedes elapsed policy versions: every policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` is on or before the logical clock date moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, and the affected employers' employees then recompute `eligibility_status` in `created_at` ascending order. Step two closes unreported coursework: every enrollment carrying the given `term_code` whose `academic_status` is `IN_PROGRESS` and which has no outcome on file at all moves `IN_PROGRESS → INCOMPLETE`, and an outcome row is synthesized for it with `completion_status` `INCOMPLETE`, `grade_points` null, `credits_earned` 0 and `posted_by_role` `SYSTEM`; these enrollments are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Step three applies the clawback chain from §3.7 to each newly incomplete enrollment whose `funding_status` is `SETTLED`, assessing an `ACADEMIC_FAILURE` obligation at the full `employer_disbursed_cents`, moving the money axis `SETTLED → CLAWED_BACK`, appending the `REVERSAL` entry and refreshing every affected ledger. Step four refreshes `persistence_risk_score` and `persistence_tier` for every enrollment of the closing term and re-evaluates each owning employee's `coaching_hold`.

Step five runs only when `run_annual_true_up` is true, and it applies a second, aggregate reading of the same exclusion limit. For every employee holding at least one ledger entry in the target `tax_year` — defaulting to the calendar year of the logical clock when the field is omitted — processed in `created_at` ascending order, the system sums `benefit_cents` across that employee's entries for the year to obtain the net benefit, takes the target exclusion as the smaller of that net benefit and the 525,000-cent limit, and takes the target imputed income as the net benefit minus that target exclusion. It then compares those targets against what the per-event attribution actually booked: the excluded delta is the target exclusion minus the sum of `excluded_cents` already on file, and the imputed delta is the target imputed income minus the sum of `imputed_income_cents` already on file. When either delta is non-zero, a single `TRUE_UP` entry is appended with a null `enrollment_id`, `benefit_cents` of 0, `excluded_cents` equal to the excluded delta and `imputed_income_cents` equal to the imputed delta; when both deltas are zero, nothing is appended for that employee. Employees with no entries in the year are skipped entirely. The two readings agree until a reversal occurs; afterwards they diverge, because chronological attribution leaves imputed income stranded on a later disbursement whose earlier neighbour has since been reversed, and the aggregate reading therefore yields a strictly smaller `imputed_income_cents_ytd`. The per-event chronological attribution remains authoritative for the values stored on each individual entry; the true-up books only the difference.

Employers read their programme's health from a computed utilization report covering the current plan year — the plan year of the logical clock date — for one employer. The report names that `plan_year` and then reports ten figures. `eligible_employee_count` is the number of that employer's employees whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`. `participating_employee_count` is the number of distinct employees among the employer's enrollments whose `plan_year` matches the report's plan year; that set of enrollments is the scope for everything that follows. `participation_rate` is the participating count divided by the eligible count, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is reported as 0.0 when the eligible count is zero. Closed enrollments are those in scope whose `academic_status` is `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `INCOMPLETE`, and completions are the subset of those that are `PASSED`; `completion_rate` is the number of completions divided by the number of closed enrollments, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0.0 when nothing has closed. `retention_rate` is the number of distinct participating employees whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` divided by `participating_employee_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0.0 when nobody participated. `skill_attainment_count` counts the distinct pairings of employee and program `skill_track` across the completions, so a learner who completes two programs on different tracks counts twice while two terms of the same track count once. `total_employer_disbursed_cents` is the sum of `employer_disbursed_cents` over in-scope enrollments whose `funding_status` is `SETTLED`. `total_imputed_income_cents` is the sum of `imputed_income_cents` across every ledger entry of that employer's employees whose `tax_year` equals the calendar year of the logical clock. `cost_per_completion_cents` is `total_employer_disbursed_cents` divided by the number of completions, rounded half up to a whole cent, and is reported as 0 when there are no completions. Empty collections always short-circuit to 0.0 or 0 rather than producing a division error.
