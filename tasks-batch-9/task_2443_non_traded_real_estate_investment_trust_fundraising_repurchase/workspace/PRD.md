# Non-Traded REIT Fundraising & Repurchase API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A non-traded real estate investment trust raises capital the slow way. There is no exchange, no closing bell and no market maker: the sponsor keeps a registration statement continuously effective, sells shares at an administratively determined price, and promises stockholders a repurchase plan instead of a ticker. Every one of those promises is a compliance obligation with a number attached to it. Money tendered before the minimum offering amount is reached must sit in escrow and be returned if the offering never breaks escrow. The price at which shares are sold and repurchased must trace to a monthly net asset value that an independent valuation advisor has confirmed. Distributions must be disclosed as covered or uncovered, on two different accounting bases, before a dollar is paid. Repurchases must be throttled by monthly and quarterly caps that the sponsor may not quietly relax.

This document specifies the backend service that carries those obligations. It is a single API surface used by seven distinct constituencies — the sponsor's fundraising desk, the compliance department, an external valuation firm, the transfer agent, the board's independent-directors committee, the registered representatives of selling broker-dealers, and the stockholders themselves — and its central design premise is that these constituencies must not be able to do each other's jobs. The operator who prepares a valuation may never confirm it. The desk that declares a distribution may never pay it. The officer who suspends the repurchase plan is not the officer who sells shares. Where a rule exists to protect an investor, the service enforces it as a hard failure rather than a warning.

The service is also, deliberately, arithmetically fussy. Money is carried in integer cents and share quantities in integer ten-thousandths of a share, because a fee schedule quoted in basis points and applied to a per-lot balance produces different totals depending on where the rounding happens — and the regulator's answer, not the convenient answer, is the required one. Readers implementing this specification should treat every rounding instruction, every ordering instruction and every inclusive-or-exclusive boundary in the sections that follow as normative.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the caller and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which the caller is acting. The service recognises exactly seven roles, spelled precisely as follows: `SponsorOperator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `IndependentValuationAdvisor`, `TransferAgent`, `BoardCommittee`, `BrokerDealerRep`, and `InvestorUser`.

If either header is absent, blank, or if `X-User-Role` names a value outside those seven, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check runs before any resource is looked up, so an unauthenticated request addressed to an identifier that does not exist still returns 401 rather than a not-found error. When the headers are well formed but the acting role is not authorised for the endpoint — or is authorised for the endpoint yet not permitted to drive the specific `action` or `to_status` being requested — the service answers `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` with HTTP 403.

The five operator roles carry opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `SponsorOperator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `IndependentValuationAdvisor`, `TransferAgent`, or `BoardCommittee`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any stored row's `id`, and no ownership rule constrains what those roles may reach.

Two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `BrokerDealerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BrokerDealer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a broker-dealer compare that resource's `broker_dealer_id` to `X-User-ID`, so a representative may place business only under a selling agreement belonging to its own firm. When `X-User-Role` is `InvestorUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Investor`; ownership checks compare a resource's `investor_id` to `X-User-ID`, so a stockholder may tender, withdraw, vote and read only on its own account. An entity-backed role that is otherwise authorised for an endpoint but reaches for a row belonging to a different firm or a different stockholder receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403. That ownership rule guards exactly six operations: subscription creation (`POST /subscriptions`), recording suitability documentation (`POST /investors/{investor_id}/suitability-documentation`), reading an investor statement (`GET /investors/{investor_id}/statement`), creating a repurchase request (`POST /repurchase-requests`), withdrawing a repurchase request (`POST /repurchase-requests/{repurchase_request_id}/status`), and casting a ballot (`POST /stockholder-votes`).

The division of labour among the operator roles is not advisory; it is enforced. `SponsorOperator` runs the offering's commercial machinery — creating offerings and share classes, closing a class to new money, taking subscriptions, preparing and publishing valuations, proposing liquidity events, and opening and closing stockholder votes — but may never confirm a valuation it prepared, never pay a distribution, and never approve or execute a liquidity event. `ComplianceOfficer` owns registration, escrow, the repurchase plan's suspension and termination authority, broker-dealer registration and supervision, selling agreements and supervision findings, and is the only role that may clear a stockholder whose suitability has been flagged. `IndependentValuationAdvisor` is the only role that may confirm a valuation or push one back for correction, and may never prepare or publish one. `TransferAgent` escrows, refunds and rescinds subscriptions, pays declarations, opens and processes repurchase periods, and issues investor statements. `BoardCommittee` approves, executes and abandons liquidity events, cancels declarations, and may itself author a distribution declaration. `BrokerDealerRep` opens investor accounts, records suitability documentation for a stockholder whose file is still incomplete, and places subscriptions under its own firm's agreements. `InvestorUser` tenders shares for repurchase, withdraws its own queued tender, and casts ballots.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection listing accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and it may not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a non-integer value for either parameter, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400 rather than being silently clamped.

Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order unless a subsection of §3 states otherwise for a particular computation. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the total number of rows matching the filters, not the number returned on this page — and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning: an offering identifier and a share-lot identifier are indistinguishable by shape, and no rule anywhere in this specification may branch on, sort by, or otherwise interpret an `id` value.

Resources also carry timestamps. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp recording the most recent mutation of the row, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Both fields move together: any change to a row — including a change to a purely derived figure such as an offering's `aggregate_nav_cents` or an agreement's `open_major_finding_count`, and including a change applied as a cascade from an operation aimed at some other entity — refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`.

One resource is exempt. A `StockholderVote` is immutable from the instant it is written: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and no operation in this specification modifies a ballot once cast.

### 2.5. Money, Share Units and Rounding Discipline

Monetary amounts are integer counts of cents throughout; every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer and is never expressed as a fraction or a string. Share quantities are integer counts of ten-thousandths of a share; every field whose name ends in `_e4` is an integer, and one whole share is 10,000 of these units, giving four decimal places of a share. Fee and ratio fields whose names end in `_bps` are integer basis points, where 10,000 basis points is one hundred percent.

Two rounding operations appear in this specification and they are not interchangeable. Rounding **half up** takes a rational value to the nearest integer, resolving an exact tie away from zero. Taking the **floor** of a value truncates it downward, toward negative infinity. Where a rule says a figure is floored, a fractional remainder is discarded even when it is large; where a rule says a figure is rounded half up, it is not.

The discipline that matters most is *where* rounding happens. Wherever a total is described as the sum of independently rounded components — the two upfront load lines of a subscription, each share class's contribution to an aggregate valuation, each lot's monthly trail fee, each lot's early-repurchase deduction — each component is reduced to a whole number of cents on its own and only then added. Combining the components' inputs first and rounding once at the end produces a different answer for some inputs, and it is not the required behaviour.

Where an ordering is needed and the natural business key does not settle it, the only sanctioned tie-break is ascending `created_at`. No calculation consults an `id`.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — escrow deadlines, valuation periods, rescission windows, repurchase request windows, payment dates, holding periods, effective dates — is measured against a logical clock that the service maintains, never against the host's wall clock. At boot the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

Any of the seven roles may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability; the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced. Setting the clock changes no business row whatsoever: it changes only the instant that subsequent requests observe. Where a rule speaks of the "current logical date", it means the calendar date in UTC of the logical clock's current instant.

Calendar-date fields are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings interpreted in UTC; `period_month` is a `YYYY-MM` string; instant fields such as `opens_at`, `request_cutoff_at`, `issued_date` and `accepted_at` are ISO 8601 instants compared to the second. A malformed month, date or instant, or an inverted interval such as a `period_end_date` earlier than its `period_start_date` or a `request_cutoff_at` at or before its `opens_at`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.7. Resource Addressing and Referential Resolution

The API is flat. Every entity is a top-level collection reached by its own path segment, and no resource is nested beneath another in a URL; relationships travel as foreign keys in request bodies and as filters in query strings. Consequently a path never carries more than one identifier, and the path parameters used across the surface are `offering_id`, `share_class_id`, `investor_id`, `broker_dealer_id`, `selling_agreement_id`, `subscription_id`, `share_lot_id`, `nav_record_id`, `distribution_declaration_id`, `repurchase_period_id`, `repurchase_request_id`, `liquidity_event_id`, and `stockholder_vote_id`.

Reference resolution is nevertheless strictly ordered, and the order is observable because every unresolvable reference reports the same code. The path parameter is resolved first. Body foreign keys are then resolved in this sequence: `offering_id`, `share_class_id`, `investor_id`, `selling_agreement_id`, `subscription_id`, `repurchase_period_id`, `liquidity_event_id`, `nav_record_id`. The first reference that does not resolve to a stored row determines the response, which is always `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404 regardless of which entity was missing. A subscription request that names both an unknown offering and an unknown investor therefore reports the offering, because the offering is resolved first.

Listing endpoints expose the same relationships as optional filters rather than as nested paths. Share classes may be filtered by `offering_id`, `class_code` and `status`; investors by `suitability_status`, `account_status` and `state_code`; broker-dealers by `supervision_status` and `crd_number`; selling agreements by `offering_id`, `broker_dealer_id` and `status`; subscriptions by `offering_id`, `share_class_id`, `investor_id`, `selling_agreement_id` and `status`; share lots by `subscription_id`, `investor_id`, `share_class_id`, `converted_from_share_class_id` and `status`; valuation records by `share_class_id`, `status` and `period_end_date`; declarations by `offering_id`, `status` and `record_date`; repurchase periods by `offering_id`, `status`, `quarter_key` and `period_month`; repurchase requests by `repurchase_period_id`, `investor_id`, `share_class_id`, `status` and `hardship_type`; liquidity events by `offering_id`, `status` and `event_type`; ballots by `liquidity_event_id`, `investor_id` and `choice`; and offerings by `registration_status`, `escrow_status` and `repurchase_plan_status`.

### 2.8. Version Echoes on Valuation and Declaration Writes

Two creation operations are protected by optimistic concurrency control, because both of them capture a snapshot of a parent row at the moment they are authored and both of them advance that parent's revision counter as a side effect.

A `POST /distribution-declarations` request must carry `expected_offering_version` as a mandatory field, holding the `version` of the target `Offering` that the client last read. If that value does not equal the offering's currently stored `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OFFERING_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing at all is mutated. A successful create increments the offering's `version` by 1, so a second author still holding the value it read earlier is rejected until it re-reads the offering. This is not incidental: a board may legitimately supersede a still-unpaid declaration by authoring a new one for the same `record_date`, and the echo forces that second declaration to have been written against an offering state that already reflects the first.

A `POST /nav-records` request must carry `expected_share_class_version` as a mandatory field, holding the `version` of the target `ShareClass` that the client last read. If that value does not equal the share class's currently stored `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SHARE_CLASS_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no valuation row is created. A successful create increments the share class's `version` by 1. Because a valuation record snapshots the class's outstanding share count at creation, the echo forces the preparer to have observed the class at its current outstanding total rather than at a superseded one.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Operations that are not listed above take no `expected_offering_version`, no `expected_share_class_version` and no equivalent, and are never rejected with 412.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder

Every write request is evaluated against a single global priority ladder, from the top down. The **first** rule that is violated determines the response; later violations are never reported, never merged into the message, and never change the status code. Within one priority level, path parameters are examined before body fields, and body fields are examined in the order in which the endpoint lists them.

1. **Authentication (401).** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` naming one of the seven roles, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation (403).** The acting role is listed for the endpoint and is permitted to drive the requested `action` or `to_status`, otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Schema (400).** The body parses, every required input is present, and every value matches its declared type, enum, range and format, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This level catches zero or negative money and share amounts, unknown enum members, malformed `YYYY-MM`, `YYYY-MM-DD` and ISO 8601 values, a `period_end_date` earlier than its `period_start_date`, a `request_cutoff_at` at or before its `opens_at`, a `maximum_offering_amount_cents` below its `minimum_offering_amount_cents`, and a pagination `limit` outside 1 through 100.
4. **Existence (404).** Every referenced identifier resolves, in the order given in §2.7, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Ownership (403).** An entity-backed role acts only on its own rows, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This is distinct from level 2: the role is allowed to call the endpoint, just not against this row.
6. **Concurrency (412).** `expected_offering_version` equals the stored offering `version`; `expected_share_class_version` equals the stored share class `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_OFFERING_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SHARE_CLASS_VERSION`. Because this level sits above every state and business rule, a stale echo is reported even when the request would also have failed a later check.
7. **Own-state transition (409).** The requested transition is defined in the target row's own state machine for the state that row is currently in, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. This covers terminal-state lockdown and repeated triggers — paying an already paid declaration, processing an already processed period, breaking an already broken escrow.
8. **Dependency state (409).** Every *other* entity the operation depends on is in an admissible state. Dependencies are examined in a fixed order: the offering's registration, then the share class, then the selling agreement, then the broker-dealer, then the repurchase plan, then the published valuation. The applicable codes are `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE`, `ERR_SHARE_CLASS_CLOSED`, `ERR_SELLING_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_BROKER_DEALER_BARRED`, `ERR_REPURCHASE_PLAN_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_NAV` and `ERR_VOTE_NOT_OPEN`. A dependency's state outranks every amount-based and window-based rule below it.
9. **Temporal windows (409).** The operation falls inside its permitted window as measured by the logical clock, otherwise one of `ERR_REPURCHASE_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `ERR_REPURCHASE_PERIOD_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_NAV_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_NAV_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_ESCROW_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_READY_FOR_PROCESSING`, `ERR_PAYMENT_DATE_NOT_REACHED` or `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. Window membership is decided before any quantity is examined, so a tender filed after a period's cutoff reports `ERR_REPURCHASE_PERIOD_CLOSED` even if the stockholder also lacks the shares.
10. **Investor-protection gates (422).** Suitability documentation, the concentration limit, the minimum investment, the uncovered-distribution acknowledgement and the advisor opinion for a large valuation move, reported as `ERR_SUITABILITY_NOT_DOCUMENTED`, `ERR_CONCENTRATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_INVESTMENT`, `ERR_UNCOVERED_DISTRIBUTION_UNACKNOWLEDGED` and `ERR_ADVISOR_OPINION_REQUIRED`. Within this level, suitability is checked before the concentration limit, and the concentration limit before the minimum investment. These gates outrank every capacity, commission and fiscal rule below them: an undocumented stockholder's oversized subscription reports `ERR_SUITABILITY_NOT_DOCUMENTED`.
11. **Structural and quantity constraints (422).** Share availability, duplicate business keys and commission ceilings, reported as `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOCKHOLDER_VOTE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SHARE_CLASS_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPURCHASE_PERIOD`, `ERR_COMMISSION_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_INVESTOR_EMAIL` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_CRD_NUMBER`.
12. **Referential integrity between individually valid rows (409).** A lot may not be rescinded once it has been partially repurchased (`ERR_LOT_PARTIALLY_REPURCHASED`) and a paid declaration may not be superseded (`ERR_DECLARATION_ALREADY_PAID`). These sit below level 7 because the blocking row is a *different* entity that is itself in a perfectly legal state.
13. **Offering-level money ceilings and floors (422).** `ERR_OFFERING_MAXIMUM_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_MINIMUM_OFFERING_NOT_MET`. Being last means a subscription aimed at a suspended offering reports `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE` rather than `ERR_OFFERING_MAXIMUM_EXCEEDED` even when it would also breach the registered maximum.

The ladder is global. When several rules from the same level could fire on one endpoint, the ordering notes given in the level above (and repeated in the relevant §3 subsection) decide which one is reported.

### 2.10. Cascades, Derived Figures and Parallel Status Axes

An offering does not have a single status. It carries three independent axes, each with its own state machine and its own transition vocabulary: `registration_status` (`DRAFT`, `EFFECTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal), `escrow_status` (`COLLECTING`, `BROKEN`, `FAILED`, with both `BROKEN` and `FAILED` terminal), and `repurchase_plan_status` (`ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `TERMINATED`, with `TERMINATED` terminal). A change on one axis never implies a change on another unless a rule in §3 says so explicitly. Every other entity in the system has exactly one status axis, except the stockholder record, which carries both a `suitability_status` and an `account_status`.

Derived figures are never stale. An offering's `escrow_balance_cents`, `gross_proceeds_cents` and `aggregate_nav_cents`, a share class's `shares_outstanding_e4`, a stockholder's `concentration_limit_cents`, `cumulative_invested_cents`, `total_shares_e4`, `cumulative_distributions_received_cents` and `cumulative_early_repurchase_deduction_cents`, a firm's `suspended_agreement_count`, an agreement's `open_minor_finding_count` and `open_major_finding_count`, and a lot's `remaining_shares_e4` and `cumulative_distribution_fee_cents` are all recomputed synchronously within the request that disturbs them, and are visible on the very next read.

Cascades are equally synchronous. When an operation aimed at one row changes another row's state or derived figures, the second row's mutation is part of the same request: it refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` exactly as a direct write would. There is no deferred settlement, no background sweep and no eventual consistency anywhere in this specification. Where a cascade touches many rows — accepting a queue of escrowed subscriptions, consuming a stockholder's lots, holding a period's tenders — the order in which those rows are processed is specified in §3 and is part of the contract, because the arithmetic depends on it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Offering and Its Share Class Structure

Everything in this system hangs off an **Offering**, the registered continuous offering that a `SponsorOperator` creates. The registration is described by a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `minimum_offering_amount_cents` of at least 1 cent representing the proceeds that must accumulate in escrow before escrow may be broken, a `maximum_offering_amount_cents` that may not be smaller than that minimum and caps aggregate proceeds for the life of the offering, an `escrow_deadline_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form giving the last calendar date on which escrow may be broken, and an `initial_offering_price_cents` strictly greater than zero that prices shares in any class for which no valuation has yet been published. The sponsor may also supply `rescission_window_days`, the number of days after a lot's issuance during which a stockholder may unwind the purchase; the field must not be negative and defaults to 5 when omitted. A newly created offering opens with `registration_status` at `DRAFT`, `escrow_status` at `COLLECTING`, `repurchase_plan_status` at `ACTIVE`, a null `repurchase_plan_suspension_reason`, and `escrow_balance_cents`, `gross_proceeds_cents` and `aggregate_nav_cents` all at zero.

Share classes give the offering its fee architecture. A `SponsorOperator` adds a **ShareClass** to an offering by naming the parent `offering_id` and a `class_code`, which must be one of `T`, `S`, `D` or `I` and must be unique among that offering's classes — a second attempt at a code the offering already uses is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SHARE_CLASS_CODE`. The class's economics are three basis-point figures and a cap. `upfront_selling_commission_bps` is the selling commission taken off the top of each subscription and must lie between 0 and 1000 inclusive. `dealer_manager_fee_bps` is the dealer manager's cut, between 0 and 300 inclusive. `annual_distribution_fee_bps` is the annual trail fee charged against a lot's value, between 0 and 1500 inclusive, and it accrues in monthly instalments each time a declaration is paid. `distribution_fee_cap_bps`, between 0 and 2000 inclusive, is the cumulative ceiling on trail fees expressed in basis points of the gross amount that bought the lot; a class configured with a cap of zero basis points never charges a distribution fee at all. Finally, `converts_to_class_code` optionally names the class code a lot moves into once its trail-fee cap is exhausted, and is null for classes that never convert. A new class opens `ACTIVE` with `shares_outstanding_e4` at zero.

Registration is `ComplianceOfficer` territory, driven by an `action` and an optional `reason`. `DECLARE_EFFECTIVE` moves `registration_status` from `DRAFT` to `EFFECTIVE`, and it has two preconditions: the offering must already have at least one share class, and the current logical date must be on or before the `escrow_deadline_date`. Declaring an offering effective after the escrow deadline has passed is refused with `ERR_ESCROW_DEADLINE_PASSED`; declaring one effective with no share class attached is refused as an invalid transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `SUSPEND` moves `EFFECTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` unconditionally, and while the offering sits in `SUSPENDED` no new subscription may be written for it — such attempts are rejected with `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. `REINSTATE` returns `SUSPENDED` to `EFFECTIVE`, but only while `escrow_status` is not `FAILED`; a reinstatement attempt on an offering whose escrow has failed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, since a failed escrow is terminal and there is nothing left to sell into. `CLOSE` moves either `EFFECTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to the terminal `CLOSED`, and requires that no subscription of the offering still sits in `PENDING` or `ESCROWED`; an attempt to close over unfinished subscriptions is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Every one of these four actions increments the offering's `version` by 1, and closing additionally cascades: every share class of the offering still in `ACTIVE` is moved to the terminal `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, each such class having its own `version` incremented.

A class may also be retired on its own. A `SponsorOperator` closes one class to new money by requesting `to_status` of `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, which is permitted only while the parent offering's `registration_status` is `EFFECTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. This is how a sponsor withdraws one fee structure without ending the offering: the closed class's `version` increments, subsequent subscriptions into it fail with `ERR_SHARE_CLASS_CLOSED`, and sibling classes of the same offering remain open for business.

### 3.2. Stockholder Onboarding, Suitability and the Concentration Basis

An **Investor** account may be opened by a `SponsorOperator`, a `TransferAgent` or a `BrokerDealerRep`. The account is described by a `legal_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `email` that must be unique across all investors — a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_INVESTOR_EMAIL` — a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, and two attested figures that may each be zero or positive but never negative: `net_worth_cents` and `annual_income_cents`. New accounts start with `suitability_status` at `INCOMPLETE`, `account_status` at `ACTIVE`, a null `suitability_documentation_reference`, and the running totals `cumulative_invested_cents`, `total_shares_e4`, `cumulative_distributions_received_cents` and `cumulative_early_repurchase_deduction_cents` all at zero.

The state of residence matters because it determines how much of the offering a single stockholder may own. Five states supervise non-traded offerings more strictly than the rest: `OH`, `MA`, `NM`, `IA` and `AL`. For a stockholder resident in one of those five, the concentration basis is the **lesser** of the attested net worth and the attested annual income; for a stockholder resident anywhere else, the basis is the attested net worth alone, and annual income does not enter the arithmetic. `concentration_limit_cents` is then ten percent of that basis — the basis multiplied by 1000 basis points, divided by 10,000, and floored to a whole cent. The limit is computed when the account is opened and recomputed whenever `net_worth_cents` or `annual_income_cents` changes.

Suitability documentation is recorded against an account by supplying a `documentation_reference`; the field is mandatory and a request omitting it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `BrokerDealerRep` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may take an account from `INCOMPLETE` to `DOCUMENTED`, which stores the supplied string in `suitability_documentation_reference` and increments the account's `version`. Clearing a `FLAGGED` account is different: only a `ComplianceOfficer` may move `FLAGGED` back to `DOCUMENTED`, replacing `suitability_documentation_reference` with the new package's reference and incrementing `version`. A `BrokerDealerRep` attempting to clear a flagged stockholder is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` — the representative may paper an incomplete file but may not adjudicate one that compliance has flagged. Both variants of this operation are also subject to the ownership rule of §2.1, so an entity-backed caller reaching an account that is not its own receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Flagging is never a manual act. An account moves from `DOCUMENTED` to `FLAGGED` as a consequence of an acceptance — either a single subscription accepted directly or the batch acceptance that follows an escrow break — whenever `cumulative_invested_cents` exceeds `concentration_limit_cents` after that acceptance has been booked. The account's `version` increments, and from that point any further subscription for the stockholder placed under an agreement that requires documentation fails with `ERR_SUITABILITY_NOT_DOCUMENTED` until compliance re-documents the file. The reverse movement happens too: when a rescission pulls investment back out, `suitability_status` is re-evaluated and a `FLAGGED` account returns to `DOCUMENTED` once the limit is respected again.

The second axis on an account tracks whether the stockholder still owns anything. `account_status` starts `ACTIVE` and becomes `CLOSED_NO_POSITION` when `total_shares_e4` falls to zero — either because a repurchase settlement closed the last open lot or because a rescission voided it. It returns to `ACTIVE` the moment a new lot is minted for that stockholder. Both movements increment the account's `version`.

### 3.3. Selling Agreements, Broker-Dealer Supervision and the Commission Ceiling

Distribution runs through registered firms. A `ComplianceOfficer` registers a **BrokerDealer** with a `firm_name` and a `crd_number`, the latter unique across all firms — a duplicate reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CRD_NUMBER`. Firms open at `IN_GOOD_STANDING` with `suspended_agreement_count` at zero. The supervision axis is driven by a `to_status` and an optional `reason`, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may drive it. `IN_GOOD_STANDING` may be escalated to `HEIGHTENED_SUPERVISION` at any time. `HEIGHTENED_SUPERVISION` may be de-escalated back to `IN_GOOD_STANDING`, but only while `suspended_agreement_count` is zero — a firm with a suspended agreement still on its books cannot be returned to good standing, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `HEIGHTENED_SUPERVISION` may also be escalated to the terminal `BARRED`, which is the most destructive single act in the system: every selling agreement of that firm still in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` is driven to the terminal `TERMINATED`, and every subscription still in `PENDING` under any of those agreements is driven to `REJECTED` carrying the `rejection_reason` string `BROKER_DEALER_BARRED`. The firm's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every agreement and subscription the cascade touches.

A **SellingAgreement** binds one firm to one offering. A `ComplianceOfficer` creates it with the `offering_id`, the `broker_dealer_id`, a `max_commission_bps` between 0 and 1300 inclusive that ceilings the total upfront load the firm may place, and an optional `suitability_documentation_required` flag that defaults to true and determines whether stockholders placed under the agreement must carry documented suitability. An agreement opens `PENDING` with `open_minor_finding_count` and `open_major_finding_count` both at zero.

Agreement status may be driven explicitly with a `to_status` and an optional `reason`, always by a `ComplianceOfficer`. `PENDING` becomes `ACTIVE` only when the firm behind it is not `BARRED` — otherwise `ERR_BROKER_DEALER_BARRED` — and the covered offering's `registration_status` is `EFFECTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. `ACTIVE` may be driven to `SUSPENDED`, which increments the firm's `suspended_agreement_count` and, when the firm was `IN_GOOD_STANDING`, escalates it to `HEIGHTENED_SUPERVISION`; while an agreement is suspended, subscriptions under it fail with `ERR_SELLING_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Termination is reachable from all three earlier states and behaves differently in each: from `PENDING` it merely increments the agreement's `version`; from `ACTIVE` it additionally drives every `PENDING` subscription placed under the agreement to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`; from `SUSPENDED` it additionally decrements the firm's `suspended_agreement_count`.

Supervision findings offer the same transitions by a different route. A `ComplianceOfficer` records or resolves a finding against an agreement by supplying an `action` of `RECORD` or `RESOLVE`, a `severity` of `MINOR` or `MAJOR`, and an optional `finding_reference`. `RECORD` increments `open_minor_finding_count` or `open_major_finding_count` according to the severity given; `RESOLVE` decrements the matching counter. The agreement's status is then re-derived from the resulting counters rather than being set by hand: an `ACTIVE` agreement is automatically suspended as soon as it carries three or more open minor findings or one or more open major findings, and a `SUSPENDED` agreement automatically returns to `ACTIVE` once, after the resolution, its open minor count is below three **and** its open major count is zero — provided the firm behind it is not `BARRED`. Auto-suspension carries the same secondary effects as an explicit suspension, incrementing the firm's `suspended_agreement_count` and escalating an `IN_GOOD_STANDING` firm to `HEIGHTENED_SUPERVISION`; auto-reactivation decrements that counter. Every finding operation increments the agreement's `version`.

### 3.4. Subscription Intake: Pricing, the Two Load Lines and Share Issuance

A **Subscription** is authored by a `SponsorOperator` or by a `BrokerDealerRep` acting for its own firm, and its payload is short: the `offering_id`, the `share_class_id`, the `investor_id`, the `selling_agreement_id` under which the sale was placed, and the `gross_investment_cents` tendered, which must be at least 1 cent.

The dependency gates fire in a fixed order. The offering's `registration_status` must be `EFFECTIVE` (`ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE`); the target class must not be `CLOSED_TO_NEW` (`ERR_SHARE_CLASS_CLOSED`); the agreement must be `ACTIVE`, so a `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` agreement reports `ERR_SELLING_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the firm behind that agreement must not be `BARRED` (`ERR_BROKER_DEALER_BARRED`). Only once every dependency is admissible do the investor-protection gates run, and they too run in a fixed order. First suitability: where the agreement's `suitability_documentation_required` is true and the stockholder's `suitability_status` is `INCOMPLETE` or `FLAGGED`, the subscription is refused with `ERR_SUITABILITY_NOT_DOCUMENTED`. Then concentration: if this one subscription would by itself carry `cumulative_invested_cents` past `concentration_limit_cents`, it is refused with `ERR_CONCENTRATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Then the investment floor: a stockholder's **first** subscription in an offering must tender at least 250,000 cents — twenty-five hundred dollars — while every subsequent subscription in that offering must tender at least 50,000 cents, or five hundred dollars; a shortfall reports `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_INVESTMENT`.

Two more checks sit below the protection gates. The class's `upfront_selling_commission_bps` and `dealer_manager_fee_bps` added together must not exceed the agreement's `max_commission_bps`, or the subscription is refused with `ERR_COMMISSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` — a firm that negotiated a lower load ceiling cannot be routed into an expensive class. Last of all, the offering's `gross_proceeds_cents` plus this subscription's `gross_investment_cents` must not exceed `maximum_offering_amount_cents`, or the subscription reports `ERR_OFFERING_MAXIMUM_EXCEEDED`.

Pricing is pinned at authoring time into `transaction_price_cents`, and the rule for choosing it is used in several other places in this specification, so it is worth stating once and precisely. Consider the valuation records for the share class that are currently `PUBLISHED`. If there are none, the price is the offering's `initial_offering_price_cents`. If there are some, the winner is the one whose `period_end_date` is the greatest, and where two published records share that end date the one created earliest wins; the price is that record's `nav_per_share_cents`.

The upfront load is then computed as **two separate rounded lines**. `upfront_selling_commission_cents` is the gross investment multiplied by the class's `upfront_selling_commission_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent. `dealer_manager_fee_cents` is the gross investment multiplied by the class's `dealer_manager_fee_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent. Each line is rounded on its own and only then are the two added together to form the total load; combining the two basis-point rates into one rate and rounding a single time is a different computation that disagrees with this one for some gross amounts, and it is not what this service does. `net_invested_cents` is the gross investment less the sum of those two rounded lines.

Share issuance follows from the net amount. `shares_issued_e4` is the net invested amount multiplied by 10,000 and divided by the pinned transaction price, floored to a whole share unit — a stockholder is never issued a fraction of a ten-thousandth of a share. Because of that floor a sliver of money is usually left over: `unapplied_cash_cents` is the net invested amount less the value of the issued shares, that value being the issued share units multiplied by the transaction price, divided by 10,000, and floored. The unapplied cash is returned to the stockholder at acceptance. A freshly authored subscription sits in `PENDING` with a null `rejection_reason` and a null `accepted_at`.

### 3.5. Escrow Collection, the Break, Refunds and the Rescission Window

Money tendered before the offering has raised its minimum sits in escrow. A `TransferAgent` moves a subscription from `PENDING` to `ESCROWED`, which is permitted only while the offering's `escrow_status` is `COLLECTING` and its `registration_status` is `EFFECTIVE` — an offering that is not `EFFECTIVE` refuses the move with `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE`; the subscription's `version` increments and its `gross_investment_cents` is added to the offering's `escrow_balance_cents`.

The escrow axis itself is driven by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying an `action`. `BREAK` takes `escrow_status` from `COLLECTING` to the terminal `BROKEN` and has three preconditions: the offering's `registration_status` must be `EFFECTIVE`, the accumulated `escrow_balance_cents` must be at least the `minimum_offering_amount_cents` — a shortfall reports `ERR_MINIMUM_OFFERING_NOT_MET` — and the current logical date must be on or before the `escrow_deadline_date`, a late break reporting `ERR_ESCROW_DEADLINE_PASSED`. Breaking escrow is a batch event. The offering's `version` increments; every subscription of the offering in `ESCROWED` is moved to `ACCEPTED` in ascending `created_at` order; one share lot is minted per accepted subscription with `issued_date` set to the current logical clock instant; `shares_outstanding_e4` on every touched class and `total_shares_e4` on every touched stockholder are recomputed; each affected stockholder's `cumulative_invested_cents` is recomputed and their `suitability_status` re-evaluated against `concentration_limit_cents`, so a stockholder pushed over the limit by the batch lands in `FLAGGED`; and the whole of `escrow_balance_cents` is moved into `gross_proceeds_cents`.

`FAIL` is the other exit, taking `COLLECTING` to the terminal `FAILED`. It is available when the current logical date is past the `escrow_deadline_date`, or when the offering's `registration_status` is `SUSPENDED` — either condition alone suffices. Failure increments the offering's `version`, drives every `ESCROWED` subscription of the offering to `REFUNDED` with `rejection_reason` set to `ESCROW_FAILED`, zeroes `escrow_balance_cents`, and moves `repurchase_plan_status` to the terminal `TERMINATED`, because an offering that never raised its minimum has no plan to run.

Once escrow has broken, subscriptions are accepted one at a time by a `SponsorOperator` moving `PENDING` to `ACCEPTED`. Four preconditions apply: `escrow_status` must be `BROKEN`, `registration_status` must be `EFFECTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE` — a `PUBLISHED` valuation record must exist for the target share class — without one the acceptance is refused with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_NAV` — and the offering's `gross_proceeds_cents` plus the subscription's `gross_investment_cents` must not exceed `maximum_offering_amount_cents`, or the acceptance reports `ERR_OFFERING_MAXIMUM_EXCEEDED`. Note the asymmetry this creates with §3.4: the fallback to `initial_offering_price_cents` is reachable only while escrow is still `COLLECTING`, because after the break no subscription can be accepted into a class that has never published a valuation.

Acceptance is where a **ShareLot** comes into existence, and the lot is the unit against which every later fee and repurchase is measured. The subscription's `version` increments and `accepted_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant. The minted lot records the `subscription_id` that produced it, the `investor_id` of the holder of record, the `share_class_id` it currently sits in, a null `converted_from_share_class_id`, the `issued_date` at which it was minted, `original_shares_e4` equal to the subscription's `shares_issued_e4`, `remaining_shares_e4` equal to that same figure, `gross_offering_price_cents` equal to the gross investment attributable to the lot, `cumulative_distribution_fee_cents` at zero, and a status of `OPEN`. Its `distribution_fee_cap_cents` is the lot's `gross_offering_price_cents` multiplied by the `distribution_fee_cap_bps` of the class the lot sat in **at minting**, divided by 10,000 and floored; that cap is fixed for the life of the lot. Acceptance also adds `gross_investment_cents` to the offering's `gross_proceeds_cents` and to the stockholder's `cumulative_invested_cents`; recomputes the class's `shares_outstanding_e4`, the stockholder's `total_shares_e4` and the offering's `aggregate_nav_cents`; sets `suitability_status` to `FLAGGED` when `cumulative_invested_cents` now exceeds `concentration_limit_cents`; and returns `account_status` to `ACTIVE` if the stockholder had been sitting at `CLOSED_NO_POSITION`.

Three terminal exits complete the subscription machine. A `SponsorOperator` may drive `PENDING` to `REJECTED` at any time, supplying a `rejection_reason`. A `TransferAgent` may drive `ESCROWED` to `REFUNDED` while `escrow_status` is `COLLECTING` or `FAILED`, which subtracts the `gross_investment_cents` from `escrow_balance_cents`; this is also the path the escrow-failure cascade takes. And a `TransferAgent` may drive `ACCEPTED` to `RESCINDED`, again with a `rejection_reason`, but only inside the rescission window and only against an untouched lot. The window test is that the current logical clock is at or before the lot's `issued_date` advanced by the offering's `rescission_window_days` days; past that instant the request is refused with `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The untouched test is that the lot's `remaining_shares_e4` still equals its `original_shares_e4`; if a repurchase has already consumed part of it, the rescission is refused with `ERR_LOT_PARTIALLY_REPURCHASED`. A successful rescission increments the subscription's `version`, sets the minted lot to the terminal `RESCINDED` and zeroes its `remaining_shares_e4`, subtracts the `gross_investment_cents` from both the offering's `gross_proceeds_cents` and the stockholder's `cumulative_invested_cents`, recomputes the class's `shares_outstanding_e4`, the stockholder's `total_shares_e4` and the offering's `aggregate_nav_cents`, re-evaluates `suitability_status` so that a `FLAGGED` stockholder returns to `DOCUMENTED` once the concentration limit is respected again, and moves `account_status` to `CLOSED_NO_POSITION` when the stockholder is left holding no `OPEN` lot at all.

Lots themselves are never written directly. They are read, filterable by the subscription that minted them, the holder, the class they now sit in, the class they converted out of, or their status, and each is individually addressable so that a transfer agent can audit `remaining_shares_e4` and `cumulative_distribution_fee_cents` lot by lot.

### 3.6. Monthly Valuation: Preparation, Advisor Oversight and Publication

The price at which a non-traded REIT transacts is not discovered by a market; it is published monthly by the sponsor and blessed by an outside firm. A **NavRecord** is prepared by a `SponsorOperator` for one class, identified by `share_class_id`, over a valuation period bounded by `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` — both `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates in UTC, both inclusive, with the end date never earlier than the start date. The appraisal itself arrives as `gross_asset_value_cents`, the advisor-appraised gross asset value allocable to the class, and `other_liabilities_cents`, the class-allocable liabilities *excluding* declared-but-unpaid distributions; neither may be negative. The request must also echo `expected_share_class_version` as described in §2.8.

Valuation periods are closed intervals on both ends, which makes a single shared calendar date an overlap. Against sibling records of the same class whose status is `ADVISOR_REVIEW`, `ADVISOR_CONFIRMED` or `PUBLISHED`, the proposed period conflicts whenever its `period_start_date` is on or before the sibling's `period_end_date` **and** the sibling's `period_start_date` is on or before the proposed `period_end_date`; such a conflict is rejected with `ERR_NAV_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Back-to-back months are therefore legal — one period ending on the last day of a month and the next beginning on the first day of the following month share no date. A sibling still sitting in `DRAFT` does not block the write at all: it is instead driven to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, with its `version` incremented, and the new record takes its place.

Two per-share figures come out of one dataset. The record snapshots `shares_outstanding_e4_snapshot` from the class's `shares_outstanding_e4` at the instant of creation, and computes `accrued_distribution_payable_cents` as the sum of `total_declared_cents` across every declaration of the class's offering that is currently `DECLARED` and whose `record_date` falls on or before this record's `period_end_date`. Net assets before the distribution accrual are the gross asset value less the other liabilities; net assets after it are that same figure less the accrued distribution payable. When the snapshotted share count is zero, both per-share figures are set to the offering's `initial_offering_price_cents`. Otherwise `nav_per_share_pre_accrual_cents` is the pre-accrual net assets multiplied by 10,000, divided by the snapshotted share count and rounded half up to a whole cent, while `nav_per_share_cents` is the post-accrual net assets multiplied by 10,000, divided by the same snapshotted share count and rounded half up. The first figure is the disclosure variant — the number published as "NAV before distribution accrual" — and it ignores declared-but-unpaid distributions entirely. The second is the official variant, and once the record is published it becomes the class's transaction price. Both are always computed and both are always stored.

The record also measures how far the valuation has moved. The prior figure is taken from the `PUBLISHED` record of the same class with the greatest `period_end_date`, with the earliest-created record winning a tie on that date. When no prior published record exists, `variance_bps` is zero. Otherwise `variance_bps` is the absolute difference between this record's `nav_per_share_cents` and the prior record's `nav_per_share_cents`, multiplied by 10,000, divided by the prior figure, and floored to whole basis points. An independent advisor opinion becomes mandatory only when `variance_bps` is **strictly greater** than 500 basis points, that is, strictly more than a five percent month-over-month move; a move landing on exactly 500 basis points does not require one. New records open in `DRAFT` with a null `advisor_opinion_reference` and a null `rejection_reason`.

The review cycle is where segregation of duties bites hardest, and it is deliberately a cycle rather than a straight line. A `SponsorOperator` submits `DRAFT` to `ADVISOR_REVIEW`, which is permitted only once the valuation period has fully elapsed — the current logical date must be strictly after the `period_end_date`, and an early submission is refused with `ERR_NAV_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. Submission increments the record's `version` and clears any `rejection_reason` left over from an earlier push-back. From `ADVISOR_REVIEW` the record can go two ways, and only an `IndependentValuationAdvisor` may send it either way. A push-back returns the record to `DRAFT` and requires a `rejection_reason`, which is stored on the record; without one the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A corrected record may be resubmitted, which is why `DRAFT` and `ADVISOR_REVIEW` form a loop. A confirmation moves the record to `ADVISOR_CONFIRMED` and records the supplied `advisor_opinion_reference`; where `variance_bps` exceeds 500 basis points that reference is mandatory and a confirmation without one is refused with `ERR_ADVISOR_OPINION_REQUIRED`. A `SponsorOperator` attempting either of those two movements is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and so is an `IndependentValuationAdvisor` attempting to submit or publish.

Publication returns to the sponsor. A `SponsorOperator` moves `ADVISOR_CONFIRMED` to `PUBLISHED`, which is permitted only when no other `PUBLISHED` record for the same class covers an overlapping period. Publication increments the record's `version`, drives the class's previously `PUBLISHED` record to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` with its own `version` incremented, and recomputes the offering's `aggregate_nav_cents` from the new transaction price.

That aggregate is the denominator of both repurchase caps, so its construction matters. `aggregate_nav_cents` is the sum, taken over every share class of the offering, of each class's transaction price multiplied by that class's `shares_outstanding_e4`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — each class's contribution rounded to a whole cent on its own before the contributions are added. It is recomputed after any change to a class's `shares_outstanding_e4` and after any publication.

### 3.7. Declaring Distributions, Disclosing Coverage and Accruing Trail Fees

A **DistributionDeclaration** is authored by a `SponsorOperator` or by the `BoardCommittee` itself. Its payload names the `offering_id`, a `record_date` that decides which lots participate, a `payment_date` that may not precede the record date, a `declared_per_share_cents` of at least 1 cent, and the two accounting figures the coverage disclosure rests on: `gaap_net_income_cents` and `adjusted_cash_flow_cents`, the latter being adjusted cash flow from operations for the declaration period. Either figure may be negative, zero or positive. An optional `board_uncovered_acknowledgement` flag defaults to false. The request must also echo `expected_offering_version` as described in §2.8.

The declared total is built class by class: `total_declared_cents` is the sum, over every share class of the offering, of the `declared_per_share_cents` multiplied by that class's `shares_outstanding_e4`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent — again each class's contribution rounded independently before summation. The same declared total is then measured against both accounting bases. `coverage_ratio_gaap_bps` is `gaap_net_income_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by `total_declared_cents` and floored to whole basis points. `coverage_ratio_cash_bps` is `adjusted_cash_flow_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by the same total and floored. Full coverage is 10,000 basis points, so the disclosed return of capital on each basis is what the coverage falls short of full: `return_of_capital_bps` is the greater of zero and 10,000 less `coverage_ratio_cash_bps`, while `gaap_return_of_capital_bps` is the greater of zero and 10,000 less `coverage_ratio_gaap_bps`. The GAAP basis typically shows the larger return-of-capital portion because depreciation is expensed there, and both figures must be reported — neither substitutes for the other.

Only the cash-flow basis gates the declaration. When `coverage_ratio_cash_bps` is below 10,000 basis points the distribution is not fully covered by adjusted cash flow, and the board must have said so: if `board_uncovered_acknowledgement` is false the declaration is refused with `ERR_UNCOVERED_DISTRIBUTION_UNACKNOWLEDGED`. A coverage ratio landing on exactly 10,000 basis points is fully covered, produces a `return_of_capital_bps` of zero, and needs no acknowledgement. A weak GAAP ratio, on its own, never blocks a declaration.

Authoring a declaration can legitimately supersede an earlier one. Any declaration of the same offering with the same `record_date` that is still `DECLARED` is driven to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, with its own `version` incremented and the offering's `version` incremented as well. What may not be superseded is a declaration for that record date that has already been paid: that attempt is refused with `ERR_DECLARATION_ALREADY_PAID`. A new declaration opens `DECLARED` with `total_distribution_fee_accrued_cents` at zero.

Payment belongs to the `TransferAgent`, driven by an optional `action` that defaults to `PAY`. `DECLARED` becomes the terminal `PAID` only when the current logical date is on or after the declaration's `payment_date` — an early payment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_DATE_NOT_REACHED` — and only when the offering's `escrow_status` is `BROKEN`. Payment increments the declaration's `version` and does three things to the stockholder ledgers. First, every lot that was `OPEN` as of the `record_date` produces a credit to its holder's `cumulative_distributions_received_cents`, that credit being the `declared_per_share_cents` multiplied by the lot's `remaining_shares_e4`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent. Second, the monthly trail fee accrues against each open lot, capped, as described below. Third, the declaration ceases to be an accrual: it is removed from the `accrued_distribution_payable_cents` of every valuation record prepared thereafter, because it is no longer declared-but-unpaid.

The trail-fee accrual is strictly ordered — accrue first under the cap, and only then convert. Open lots are visited in ascending `issued_date`, with ascending `created_at` breaking a tie. For each lot, the price is the current transaction price of the class the lot sits in, and the lot's value is that price multiplied by the lot's `remaining_shares_e4`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. The full monthly fee is that lot value multiplied by the class's `annual_distribution_fee_bps`, divided by 10,000 and then divided again by the twelve months of the year, the whole rounded half up to a whole cent. The lot's remaining headroom is the greater of zero and its `distribution_fee_cap_cents` less its `cumulative_distribution_fee_cents`. The fee actually applied is the lesser of the full monthly fee and that headroom, and it is added to the lot's `cumulative_distribution_fee_cents`. A lot that reaches its cap during a month still bears that month's partial fee — the cap truncates the instalment, it does not waive it. The declaration's `total_distribution_fee_accrued_cents` is the sum of the fees applied across all lots, each lot's fee having been rounded to a whole cent on its own before the summation.

Conversion happens only after the capped accrual has been booked. If a lot's `cumulative_distribution_fee_cents` has reached or exceeded its `distribution_fee_cap_cents` and the class it sits in names a `converts_to_class_code`, the lot moves classes. The destination is the share class of the same offering whose `class_code` equals that code. The lot's new share count is its `remaining_shares_e4` multiplied by the price of the class it is leaving, divided by the destination class's price, and floored to a whole share unit — value is preserved, share count is not. The lot's `converted_from_share_class_id` is set to the class it is leaving, its `share_class_id` becomes the destination class, and both `original_shares_e4` and `remaining_shares_e4` are set to the newly computed count. Two figures are deliberately *never* re-based by a conversion: `gross_offering_price_cents` and `distribution_fee_cap_cents` stay exactly as they were set at minting, so a converted lot cannot be charged a second cap's worth of trail fees.

The board retains a veto. A `BoardCommittee` may cancel a declaration that is still `DECLARED`, moving it to the terminal `CANCELLED`, incrementing its `version` and removing it from the `accrued_distribution_payable_cents` of every valuation record prepared thereafter. A cancelled declaration pays nothing and accrues no fee.

### 3.8. The Share Repurchase Plan: Periods, Caps, the Waterfall and Settlement

The repurchase plan is the stockholder's only routine exit, and the `ComplianceOfficer` holds its emergency brake. Driving the plan axis takes an `action` and an optional `reason`. `SUSPEND` moves `repurchase_plan_status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, increments the offering's `version`, records the supplied text in `repurchase_plan_suspension_reason`, drives every `OPEN` repurchase period of the offering to `SUSPENDED`, and drives every `QUEUED` tender inside those periods to `HELD`, where it is excluded from any allocation. `RESUME` reverses all of that — it returns `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`, increments `version`, clears `repurchase_plan_suspension_reason`, returns every `SUSPENDED` period of the offering to `OPEN` and every `HELD` tender in those periods to `QUEUED` — but it is permitted only while the offering's `escrow_status` is `BROKEN`. `TERMINATE` is reachable from both `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, moves the axis to the terminal `TERMINATED`, increments `version`, drives every `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED` period of the offering to the terminal `CANCELLED`, and drives every `QUEUED` or `HELD` tender in those periods to the terminal `REJECTED`. While the plan is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, opening a period, filing a tender and processing a period all fail with `ERR_REPURCHASE_PLAN_SUSPENDED`.

A **RepurchasePeriod** is opened by a `TransferAgent` or a `SponsorOperator`, naming the `offering_id`, the `period_month` as a `YYYY-MM` string, the instant `opens_at` at which the request window opens, the instant `request_cutoff_at` at which it closes, which must be strictly later than `opens_at`, and a `settlement_date` calendar date that may not fall before the calendar date of the cutoff. The offering's `registration_status` must be `EFFECTIVE` and its plan must be `ACTIVE`. The offering may hold only one non-cancelled period per calendar month: a second attempt at a `period_month` the offering already covers with a period in any status other than `CANCELLED` reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPURCHASE_PERIOD`. The service derives `quarter_key` from the month as the four-digit year, a hyphen, the letter `Q`, and the quarter number obtained by taking the month number less one, dividing by three, flooring, and adding one — so January through March fall in the first quarter, April through June in the second, and so on.

The request window is the half-open interval running from `opens_at` inclusive to `request_cutoff_at` exclusive, and windows of the same offering may abut but never overlap. Against every sibling period of the same offering whose status is anything other than `CANCELLED`, two tests run. A true overlap exists when this period's `opens_at` falls before the sibling's `request_cutoff_at` and the sibling's `opens_at` falls before this period's `request_cutoff_at`; that is rejected with `ERR_REPURCHASE_PERIOD_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Separately, where this period's `opens_at` is at or after a sibling's `request_cutoff_at`, the calendar date of this period's `opens_at` must fall on or after that sibling's `settlement_date` advanced by five days; opening inside that five-day settlement blackout is rejected with the same code. Adjacency is legal: an `opens_at` exactly equal to a sibling's `request_cutoff_at` is not an overlap, provided the settlement blackout is also satisfied. A new period opens `OPEN` with `monthly_cap_cents`, `quarterly_remaining_cents`, `capacity_cents` and `settled_amount_cents` all at zero and `prorated` false.

Tendering is the stockholder's own act. An `InvestorUser` files a **RepurchaseRequest** naming the `repurchase_period_id`, the `share_class_id` whose shares are being tendered, the `shares_requested_e4` of at least one share unit, and optionally a `hardship_type` of `NONE`, `DEATH` or `DISABILITY`, defaulting to `NONE`. The `investor_id` is never supplied in the payload; it is taken from the acting stockholder's `X-User-ID`. The plan must be `ACTIVE`; the current logical clock must fall inside the half-open window, so a tender filed before `opens_at` or at or after `request_cutoff_at` is refused with `ERR_REPURCHASE_PERIOD_CLOSED`; and the shares must actually be there — `shares_requested_e4` may not exceed what the stockholder holds across `OPEN` lots in that class, net of shares already tendered in that stockholder's other `QUEUED` or `HELD` requests in the same period, or the tender is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES`. A filed tender sits in `QUEUED` with `requested_value_cents`, `approved_value_cents`, `approved_shares_e4`, `early_repurchase_deduction_cents` and `net_proceeds_cents` all at zero until the period is processed. The stockholder may also withdraw its own tender by requesting `to_status` of `WITHDRAWN`, which is permitted only while the current logical clock is strictly before `request_cutoff_at` — otherwise `ERR_REPURCHASE_PERIOD_CLOSED` — and only against a tender it owns, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Withdrawal is terminal and increments the request's `version`.

Processing a closed period is the exclusive act of a `TransferAgent`, and it takes the period from `OPEN` to the terminal `PROCESSED`. It requires that the current logical clock has reached `request_cutoff_at`, an early attempt being refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_READY_FOR_PROCESSING`; that the plan is `ACTIVE`; and that a `PUBLISHED` valuation record exists for every class holding a `QUEUED` tender in the period, without which the run is refused with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_NAV`.

Capacity is captured at that moment and not before. The offering's `aggregate_nav_cents` is read fresh. `monthly_cap_cents` is two percent of it — the aggregate multiplied by 200 basis points, divided by 10,000, floored. The quarterly leg is measured against a five percent ceiling: the quarter's usage is the sum of `settled_amount_cents` across the offering's *other* periods that share this period's `quarter_key` and have already reached `PROCESSED`, and `quarterly_remaining_cents` is the greater of zero and the aggregate multiplied by 500 basis points, divided by 10,000, floored, less that usage. `capacity_cents` is the lesser of the monthly cap and the quarterly remainder. Note that the quarterly ceiling is recomputed from the *current* aggregate valuation every month rather than being fixed as a dollar budget at the start of the quarter, so a falling valuation can make the quarterly leg bind in the third month of a quarter even where the monthly leg alone would not have.

Each `QUEUED` tender is then valued: `requested_value_cents` is the `shares_requested_e4` multiplied by the transaction price of the tender's class, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. Tenders in `HELD` are excluded from the run entirely. Allocation is a two-tier waterfall. Tier one is every queued tender whose `hardship_type` is `DEATH` or `DISABILITY`; visited in ascending `created_at` order, each such tender is granted the lesser of its own requested value and whatever capacity remains, so hardship tenders are satisfied in full until the capacity is dry and any hardship tender reached after that point is granted nothing. Tier two is every queued tender whose `hardship_type` is `NONE`, and it shares only what tier one left. If the tier-two tenders' requested values sum to no more than the remainder, each of them is granted its full requested value. Otherwise each tier-two tender is granted the remainder multiplied by its own requested value, divided by the tier-two total, and floored; the flooring leaves a shortfall of a few cents against the remainder, and that leftover is distributed one cent at a time to tier-two tenders in ascending `created_at` order until it is exhausted.

Outcomes follow mechanically from the grant. `approved_shares_e4` is the `approved_value_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by the class's transaction price and floored. A tender whose approved value equals its requested value becomes `FULFILLED`; one whose approved value is zero becomes `REJECTED`, consuming no lot and paying nothing; anything in between becomes `PARTIALLY_FULFILLED`. The unapproved remainder of a partially fulfilled tender is **not** carried into the next period — a stockholder who wants the rest must tender again. The period's `settled_amount_cents` is the sum of every `approved_value_cents` in the run, and that gross figure is what is charged against both caps; `prorated` is set true when at least one non-hardship tender was cut back below its requested value.

Settlement then consumes lots, and this is where the early-repurchase deduction is assessed, per lot rather than per request, because the holding period is a property of the lot. For each tender, the price is the transaction price of the tender's class, and the stockholder's `OPEN` lots in that class are visited in ascending `issued_date` order with ascending `created_at` breaking a tie — oldest first. From each lot the run takes the lesser of that lot's `remaining_shares_e4` and the share units still to be consumed. The lot's gross proceeds are the price multiplied by the units taken, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. The lot's holding period is the whole number of days from the calendar date of its `issued_date` to the period's `settlement_date`; where that count is fewer than 365 days the lot bears a deduction of two percent of its own gross proceeds — the lot gross multiplied by 200 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — and where it is 365 days or more the lot bears no deduction at all. The boundary is inclusive at the top: a lot held for exactly 365 days is not charged, a lot held for 364 days is. Each lot's gross and each lot's deduction are rounded to a whole cent on their own before being accumulated, which is why the request's totals can differ from a single deduction taken on the aggregate. The lot's `remaining_shares_e4` is reduced by the units taken and, if it falls to zero, the lot moves to the terminal `CLOSED` with its `version` incremented and drops out of both `shares_outstanding_e4` and `total_shares_e4`. If share units remain to be consumed after every eligible lot has been exhausted, the settlement fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHARES`.

The request records `early_repurchase_deduction_cents` as the sum of its per-lot deductions and `net_proceeds_cents` as the sum of its per-lot gross amounts each less that lot's deduction, and the deduction total is added to the stockholder's `cumulative_early_repurchase_deduction_cents`. Once the whole run is complete the period reports `settled_amount_cents` and `prorated` and reaches `PROCESSED`; every touched class's `shares_outstanding_e4`, every touched stockholder's `total_shares_e4` and the offering's `aggregate_nav_cents` are recomputed; and any stockholder left holding no `OPEN` lot has `account_status` moved to `CLOSED_NO_POSITION`.

Investor reporting closes the loop. A `TransferAgent`, a `SponsorOperator`, or a stockholder reading its own account may pull a reconciling statement, which is computed rather than stored. It reports the `investor_id`, the current `total_shares_e4`, an `open_lot_count` giving the number of lots for that stockholder whose status is `OPEN`, the `cumulative_invested_cents`, `cumulative_distributions_received_cents` and `cumulative_early_repurchase_deduction_cents` copied from the account row, and an `estimated_value_cents` computed as the sum over those open lots of each lot's `remaining_shares_e4` multiplied by the transaction price of the class the lot currently sits in, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — once again each lot's contribution rounded on its own before summation. A stockholder reaching for someone else's statement receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 3.9. Liquidity Event Planning and Stockholder Approval

Every non-traded offering must eventually promise its stockholders a way out, and that promise is a **LiquidityEvent**. A `SponsorOperator` proposes one against an `offering_id` with an `event_type` of `LISTING`, `MERGER` or `ASSET_SALE` and a `proposed_effective_date`. It opens in `PROPOSED` with `record_date_shares_e4`, `votes_for_e4`, `votes_against_e4` and `votes_abstain_e4` all at zero and `quorum_met` false. Movement along the axis is driven by an `action` with an optional `reason`, and the authority for each action is split deliberately between the board and the sponsor.

The `BoardCommittee` approves the plan first: `BOARD_APPROVE` moves `PROPOSED` to `BOARD_APPROVED` and is permitted only while the offering's `escrow_status` is `BROKEN`, since an offering that never funded has nothing to liquidate. The `SponsorOperator` then opens the solicitation: `OPEN_VOTE` moves `BOARD_APPROVED` to `VOTE_OPEN`, is permitted only while the offering's `registration_status` is anything other than `DRAFT`, snapshots `record_date_shares_e4` as the sum of `shares_outstanding_e4` across every share class of the offering, and is the moment ballots become acceptable.

A **StockholderVote** is cast by an `InvestorUser` naming the `liquidity_event_id` and a `choice` of `FOR`, `AGAINST` or `ABSTAIN`. As with a tender, the `investor_id` comes from the acting stockholder's `X-User-ID` rather than the payload, and `voting_shares_e4` is snapshotted from that stockholder's `total_shares_e4` at the instant the ballot is written. The ballot is immutable thereafter: it carries no revision counter and no operation revises it, so a stockholder whose holdings later change does not gain or lose voting power. A ballot cast against an event that is not `VOTE_OPEN` is refused with `ERR_VOTE_NOT_OPEN`, and a stockholder who has already voted on the event is refused a second ballot with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOCKHOLDER_VOTE`.

Closing the vote resolves the event, and the arithmetic is deliberately demanding. `CLOSE_VOTE`, driven by the `SponsorOperator`, tallies `votes_for_e4`, `votes_against_e4` and `votes_abstain_e4` as the sums of `voting_shares_e4` over the ballots choosing `FOR`, `AGAINST` and `ABSTAIN` respectively. The shares cast are the three tallies added together, and `quorum_met` is true when the shares cast reach or exceed half of `record_date_shares_e4`, that half being the record-date share count divided by two and floored. Approval requires both that quorum and a majority of **all** shares outstanding on the record date rather than merely a majority of those voting: the event is approved when quorum is met and twice `votes_for_e4` is strictly greater than `record_date_shares_e4`. Where both conditions hold, `VOTE_OPEN` becomes `APPROVED`; where either fails, `VOTE_OPEN` becomes `REJECTED`. Either way the tallies and `quorum_met` are written and the event's `version` increments. An abstention therefore helps a vote reach quorum while never helping it pass — an event that draws exactly half its record-date shares in favour and the rest in abstentions meets quorum and is still rejected, because twice half is not strictly greater than the whole.

Approval has a consequence for the repurchase plan. When `CLOSE_VOTE` resolves to `APPROVED`, the offering's `repurchase_plan_status` is moved to `SUSPENDED` with `repurchase_plan_suspension_reason` recorded as `LIQUIDITY_EVENT_APPROVED`, which in turn holds the plan's open periods and queued tenders exactly as an ordinary compliance suspension would.

Execution returns to the board. `EXECUTE` moves `APPROVED` to the terminal `EXECUTED` and is permitted only once the current logical date has reached the `proposed_effective_date`; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. Execution winds the offering down in one transaction: the event's `version` increments, the offering's `registration_status` is set to `CLOSED` and its `repurchase_plan_status` to `TERMINATED`, and every share class of the offering still `ACTIVE` is moved to `CLOSED_TO_NEW`.

Finally, the board may abandon a liquidity event, moving it to the terminal `ABANDONED` from `PROPOSED`, from `BOARD_APPROVED`, from `VOTE_OPEN` or from `REJECTED`, in each case incrementing the event's `version`. Abandoning an event that was mid-solicitation closes the ballot box: any further ballot against it fails with `ERR_VOTE_NOT_OPEN`.
