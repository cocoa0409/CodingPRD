# News Paywall & Digital Subscription Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Consumer revenue has become the load-bearing wall of the modern newsroom, and the machinery that collects it is no longer a simple "subscribe" button. A single reader may arrive anonymously on a device we have never seen, burn through a metered allowance that our propensity model deliberately sized for their conversion likelihood, hit a registration wall, subscribe on an introductory price, be stepped up twice, pause for a holiday, suffer an expired card, be rescued by a network card-updater, be pitched exactly one regulator-compliant save offer on their way out, and still end up grandfathered against a price increase they were notified about thirty days in advance. Every one of those moments is a revenue event, a compliance obligation, or both.

This document specifies the API that runs that machinery. It covers four tightly coupled surfaces: the **paywall decision engine** (meter design, dynamic rules by content and reader propensity, registration and hard walls), the **offer and pricing architecture** (introductory pricing, step-up ladders, print-digital and institutional bundles, scheduled increases with grandfathering), the **subscription lifecycle** (activation, invoicing, dunning and card recovery, pauses, vacation holds and delivery interruptions, regulated cancellation with save offers), and **institutional access** (group accounts, domain authentication, seat concurrency).

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **everything is immediate**: there is no eventual consistency and no background worker. Every derived value — a reader's engagement score, a plan's active subscription count, a group's seat occupancy, a subscription's next-cycle price, its pending credit balance — is recomputed inside the request that invalidated it. The one batch surface, the nightly billing run, is an explicit operator-triggered epoch advance whose effects are likewise immediate and fully observable in its response. Second, **duties are segregated**: the commercial team that authors a price increase is structurally incapable of notifying subscribers about it or applying it, and the compliance officer who dispatches notices is structurally incapable of being the one who applies the increase.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The whole API is mounted under the `/paywall` prefix. This section defines the rules that hold everywhere; §3 then walks the business workflows that consume them.

### 2.1. Reader Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. Five roles exist and their spelling is exact: `Reader`, `GroupAdmin`, `SubscriptionOpsAgent`, `RevenueManager`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries any string that is not one of those five, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not permitted to perform the requested operation, or a role bound to a specific entity acts on a resource it does not own, the answer is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the five roles are entity-backed, meaning their `X-User-ID` is a real row identifier and ownership is checked against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Reader`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reader`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Reader` compare the resource's reader-referencing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Reader` may only act on a subscription whose `reader_id` equals `X-User-ID`, on reader events whose `reader_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and on a seat whose `reader_id` equals `X-User-ID`; the same rule extends transitively to invoices, holds, cancellation requests and save offers hanging beneath that reader's own subscription.
- When `X-User-Role` is `GroupAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `GroupAccount`; ownership checks compare the `GroupAccount` identifier in the path (or the `group_account_id` of the targeted seat) to `X-User-ID`. A group administrator is an institutional credential, not a person with a reader row.

The remaining three roles are opaque operator identities:

- When `X-User-Role` is `SubscriptionOpsAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operations identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server never compares it to an entity's `id`. This role runs consumer-revenue operations: it creates readers, opens subscriptions, creates group accounts, records processor results, presents save offers, releases holds, and triggers the nightly batch. It is also the only role permitted to backfill a reader event at a past instant.
- When `X-User-Role` is `RevenueManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque commercial-team identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It owns the commercial surface: authoring and activating offer plans, scheduling price increases, configuring dynamic paywall rules, and publishing articles with their access classes. It may never dispatch a notice or apply an increase.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque compliance-desk identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does record it — dispatching subscriber notices for a scheduled price increase stores the acting `X-User-ID` on the plan as `notice_sent_by_user_id` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is retained solely as the comparand for the segregation-of-duties check described in §3.2, whose violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A compliance officer may never author a plan or a paywall rule.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filters

List endpoints are paginated by two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list endpoints return their rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. Four listings override that default because their domain ordering is more useful: paywall-rule listings are ordered by `specificity_score` descending, then `priority` ascending, then `created_at` ascending; reader-event listings by `occurred_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; a subscription's invoice listing by `cycle_index` ascending; a subscription's hold listing and the seat listing by `start_date` (respectively `starts_on`) ascending then `created_at` ascending; an invoice's payment-attempt listing by `attempt_no` ascending; and a cancellation request's save-offer listing by `presented_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending.

Filters are supplied as additional query parameters and are all optional. Offer-plan listings accept `status` and `bundle_type`. Paywall-rule listings accept `status`, `content_type_match` and `propensity_band_match`. Reader-event listings accept `reader_id`, `article_id`, `applied_rule_id` and `event_type`. Subscription listings accept `reader_id`, `offer_plan_id`, `group_account_id`, `lifecycle_status` and `dunning_status`. A subscription's invoice listing accepts `status`; its hold listing accepts `hold_type` and `status`; its cancellation-request listing accepts `status`. An invoice's payment-attempt listing accepts `outcome`. Save-offer listings accept `status`. Group-account listings accept `offer_plan_id` and `status`. Seat listings accept `group_account_id`, `reader_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. All date and time arithmetic is performed against a server-held **logical clock**, which before any call reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and is available to all five roles; it mutates no entity and returns the new clock value. The clock is strictly monotonic: a request whose `current_utc` is earlier than the clock's present value is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). A request whose `current_utc` equals the present value is accepted.

Wherever this document says "the clock date", it means the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC. Wherever it says "the clock instant", it means the full timestamp. Date-typed fields are ISO calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; datetime-typed fields are RFC 3339 instants with a trailing `Z`. All day counts are whole days on the logical clock date, never fractional.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Mutation Bookkeeping

Most resources carry a standard system-managed property set:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID with no type prefix.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation, with three fractional digits.
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent modification, refreshed on every mutation.
- `version` (integer): an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of that resource, including mutations that only touch derived fields.

Offer plans, paywall rules, articles, readers, subscriptions, invoices, cancellation requests, save offers, holds, group accounts and seats all carry the full set. Two resources are append-only records of something that already happened and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `PaymentAttempt` and from a `ReaderEvent`.

Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation. When a seat is reclaimed and the owning group's `seats_assigned_count` and `seats_available_count` change, the group's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments. When a price change is applied and a subscription's `locked_price_cents` and `next_cycle_price_cents` change, that subscription's `updated_at` is refreshed and its own `version` increments. When a new subscription is opened against a plan and the plan's `active_subscription_count` is recomputed, the plan's `version` increments. This is why the stale-view guards in §2.7 bite: derived-field maintenance moves versions just as decisively as an operator action does.

### 2.6. Money, Counts and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. There are no fractional currency fields and no currency strings; a price of eleven dollars and ninety-nine cents is the integer 1199.

Wherever a division or a percentage produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** (a value of exactly one half rounds away from zero). Three rounding **orders** are pinned and are deliberately different from one another, because getting them wrong is the most common source of off-by-one-cent divergence:

- **Per-line, then sum, never re-rounded.** Institutional seat charges are rounded once per seat line and the resulting lines are added; the sum is never rounded again. Three seats on a plan whose `per_seat_price_cents` is 400, each occupying 8 active days of a 30-day cycle, each produce a line of 107 cents (400 multiplied by 8, divided by 30, is 106 and two thirds, which rounds half up to 107) for a `seat_line_cents` of 321 — not the 320 that summing first and rounding once would give.
- **Rate first, then multiply.** The daily print rate is rounded once to whole cents and then multiplied by the number of credited delivery days; the product is never rounded.
- **Sum, then round once.** A reader's engagement score is accumulated at full precision across all in-window events and rounded to two decimal places exactly once, at the end. Engagement is therefore the deliberate opposite of the seat-line order.

Only two fields in the system are non-integer decimals: `engagement_score` and `projected_engagement_score` are numbers carried to two decimal places, and the discount fractions `discount_pct` and `retention_discount_pct` are fractions between 0 and 0.40 inclusive. Integer counters — allowances, meter counts, cycle indices, day counts, seat counts, `specificity_score` — are exact integers with no rounding.

### 2.7. Stale-View Guards on the Price Ladder and on Seat Capacity

Two write operations act on a picture of a parent resource that the client must have read first, and both are protected by an optimistic-concurrency guard. The guard is a mandatory, non-optional field in the request body.

**Opening a subscription** requires `expected_offer_plan_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `OfferPlan`. If that value does not equal the plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OFFER_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A plan's `version` advances whenever a price change is scheduled, whenever notices are dispatched, whenever the change is applied, and whenever another subscription is opened against it — because `active_subscription_count` is a derived field on the plan. A stale value therefore means the client is subscribing against a price ladder it can no longer see.

**Scheduling a price increase** on a plan likewise requires `expected_offer_plan_version` to equal the plan's current `version`, and is rejected with the same `ERR_STALE_OFFER_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412) when it does not.

**Inviting a reader to a seat** requires `expected_group_account_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `GroupAccount`. If that value does not equal the group's current `version`, the invitation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GROUP_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because `seats_assigned_count` and `seats_available_count` are derived fields on the group, any prior seat assignment, reclaim or invite expiry has already advanced the version and invalidated the capacity picture the administrator based this assignment on.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Activating a plan, dispatching notices, applying an increase, recording a payment attempt, creating a hold, releasing a hold, presenting or deciding a save offer, confirming a cancellation, activating or reclaiming a seat, and triggering a billing run all proceed without an expected-version input.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule could reject a request, the rejection reported is always the one from the earliest rule in the following ladder. The ordering is normative, not incidental; a later check must never mask an earlier one.

1. **Header presence and role validity** — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`.
2. **Role authorization and ownership** — the acting role must be listed for the endpoint, and an entity-backed role must own the target resource — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Segregation of duties** on the price-increase apply step — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
4. **Request-body shape** on every write endpoint: all required fields present, enum-valued fields inside their declared value sets, integer fields non-negative, date fields in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, datetime fields RFC 3339 with a trailing `Z`, discount fractions between 0 and 1 inclusive, and the billing-run `scope` inside its enum — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Path existence**, checked left to right (§2.9): the offer plan (`ERR_OFFER_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`), then the paywall rule (`ERR_PAYWALL_RULE_NOT_FOUND`), the article (`ERR_ARTICLE_NOT_FOUND`), the reader (`ERR_READER_NOT_FOUND`), the subscription (`ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`), the invoice (`ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`), the cancellation request (`ERR_CANCELLATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`), the save offer (`ERR_SAVE_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`), the hold (`ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`), the group account (`ERR_GROUP_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`) and the seat (`ERR_SEAT_NOT_FOUND`), each in that relative order.
6. **Body referential integrity**: a `reader_id` in the body must reference an existing reader (`ERR_READER_NOT_FOUND`); an `offer_plan_id` in the body must reference an existing plan (`ERR_OFFER_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`); an `article_id` must reference an existing article and a supplied `group_account_id` must reference an existing group account (`ERR_ARTICLE_NOT_FOUND`).
7. **Stale-view guards** (§2.7): first `ERR_STALE_OFFER_PLAN_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_GROUP_ACCOUNT_VERSION`.
8. **State admissibility**, in this relative order: plan not active (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`), plan already active (`ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_ACTIVE`), price-change pipeline state wrong for the requested step (`ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_STATE_INVALID`), subscription terminal (`ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERMINAL`), subscription not active (`ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`), dunning episode exhausted (`ERR_DUNNING_EXHAUSTED`), invoice not payable (`ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`), retry attempted too early (`ERR_RETRY_TOO_EARLY`), cancellation request closed (`ERR_CANCELLATION_REQUEST_CLOSED`), save offer already decided (`ERR_SAVE_OFFER_ALREADY_DECIDED`), hold not releasable (`ERR_HOLD_NOT_RELEASABLE`), seat not invited (`ERR_SEAT_NOT_INVITED`), seat invite expired (`ERR_SEAT_INVITE_EXPIRED`).
9. **Domain and safety rules**, in this relative order: notice window too short (`ERR_PRICE_NOTICE_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT`), notices not sent (`ERR_PRICE_NOTICE_NOT_SENT`), step-up ladder invalid (`ERR_STEP_UP_SCHEDULE_INVALID`), price change not yet effective (`ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_NOT_EFFECTIVE_YET`), save-offer limit exceeded (`ERR_SAVE_OFFER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), save-offer parameters out of range (`ERR_SAVE_OFFER_DISCOUNT_CAP`), seat domain mismatch (`ERR_SEAT_DOMAIN_MISMATCH`), group funding subscription not active (`ERR_GROUP_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`), intervention not warranted (`ERR_INTERVENTION_NOT_WARRANTED`), system-only event type (`ERR_EVENT_TYPE_SYSTEM_ONLY`), reader not registered (`ERR_READER_NOT_REGISTERED`), article unpublished (`ERR_ARTICLE_UNPUBLISHED`), plan not a print bundle (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_PRINT_BUNDLE`), plan not institutional (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_INSTITUTIONAL`), hold start not future (`ERR_HOLD_START_NOT_FUTURE`), hold start in the past (`ERR_HOLD_START_IN_PAST`), hold interval invalid (`ERR_HOLD_INTERVAL_INVALID`), hold buffer violation (`ERR_HOLD_BUFFER_VIOLATION`), vacation day cap exceeded (`ERR_VACATION_DAY_CAP_EXCEEDED`), pause limit exceeded (`ERR_PAUSE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), seat interval invalid (`ERR_SEAT_INTERVAL_INVALID`), duplicate seat for the same reader (`ERR_SEAT_READER_DUPLICATE`), seat capacity exceeded (`ERR_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), payment amount mismatch (`ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`), and finally the clock monotonicity rule (`ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`).

Two precedence choices inside that ladder are deliberate and are worth calling out because a naive implementation gets them backwards. First, the dunning-episode check precedes the invoice-status check: a further payment attempt against the written-off invoice of a non-pay-cancelled subscription answers `ERR_DUNNING_EXHAUSTED`, not `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`. This is also why recording a payment attempt is **not** covered by the terminal-subscription rule at all — an attempt against a subscription whose lifecycle has already ended is answered by the dunning rule. Second, the pipeline-state check on the apply step admits a plan whose price-change pipeline is either `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICE_SENT`; a plan still merely `SCHEDULED` therefore passes that check and is refused by the notices-not-sent rule instead, which is the only way `ERR_PRICE_NOTICE_NOT_SENT` is ever reachable.

### 2.9. Nested Path Resolution

When a request path names more than one resource identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the request fails on the **first** one that matches nothing, with that resource's own 404 code. A request for the holds of a subscription that does not exist answers `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND` without ever looking for a hold. The same left-to-right discipline governs body references: a path identifier is always resolved before a body identifier, and within the body, references are resolved in the order given in §2.8 step 6.

### 2.10. Parallel State Dimensions and Cascading Side Effects

A subscription does not have "a status". It has **three independent status dimensions**, each with its own value set, its own transitions and its own cycles, and any combination that the transition rules permit is legal.

- `lifecycle_status` is the primary dimension. Its values are `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `CANCEL_SCHEDULED`, `CANCELLED` and `CANCELLED_NONPAY`; it begins at `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, and `CANCELLED` and `CANCELLED_NONPAY` are terminal.
- `dunning_status` is the payment-recovery dimension, entirely independent of the lifecycle. Its values are `CURRENT`, `RETRYING`, `RECOVERED` and `FAILED_FINAL`; it begins at `CURRENT`, and `FAILED_FINAL` is terminal.
- `print_fulfillment_status` is the print-delivery dimension. Its values are `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `INTERRUPTED`, `STOPPED_AT_CYCLE_END` and `STOPPED`; it begins at `NOT_APPLICABLE` and stays there for the whole life of a plan that is not a print bundle. `STOPPED` is terminal.

An offer plan likewise carries two parallel dimensions: a `status` that runs `DRAFT` then `ACTIVE` and never reopens, and a `price_change_state` compliance pipeline that runs `NONE`, `SCHEDULED`, `NOTICE_SENT`, `APPLIED`, with `APPLIED` terminal.

Because dimensions are parallel, one operator action routinely propagates through several entities in a fixed chain of hops. Where §3 describes such a chain, every hop is part of the same request: there is no partial application and no deferred step. The chains that matter most are the confirm-a-cancellation chain (four hops, §3.8), the final-decline chain (five hops, §3.6) and the apply-a-price-increase chain (three hops, §3.2). Each entity touched in a chain has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented.

### 2.11. Occupation Geometry: Closed Hold Intervals versus Half-Open Seat Intervals

Two parts of the system reason about intervals, and their geometries are deliberately different.

A **hold** occupies a `[start_date, end_date]` **closed** interval on a subscription: both endpoints are inside the occupation, and a hold running from the 5th to the 14th occupies ten days. Holds are additionally separated by a two-day buffer, so back-to-back and near-adjacent holds are both refused (§3.7).

A **seat** occupies a `[starts_on, ends_on)` **half-open** interval on a group account: the start day is inside the occupancy and the end day is outside it. Adjacency is therefore legal — a seat ending on 2026-09-09 and a candidate starting on 2026-09-09 do not overlap — and a seat whose `ends_on` equals its `starts_on` is a zero-length occupancy and is rejected outright. A null `ends_on` means open-ended, which for capacity purposes is treated as extending indefinitely and for proration purposes is treated as the end of the cycle being billed.

### 2.12. The Nightly Billing Run

The billing run is the system's only batch surface and is triggered explicitly by a `SubscriptionOpsAgent` against the current logical clock. It takes one input, `scope`, whose value is `FULL`, `HOLDS_ONLY`, `EXPIRY_ONLY`, `BILLING_ONLY`, `DUNNING_ONLY` or `ENGAGEMENT_ONLY`. Five passes exist and they always execute in the pinned order **HOLDS, then EXPIRY, then BILLING, then DUNNING, then ENGAGEMENT**; `scope` restricts which of them run, and `FULL` runs all five in that order. Ordering is observable: a hold that completes in the HOLDS pass has its credit available to the BILLING pass of the same run.

The run's response is not an entity. It is a record of what the epoch advance did, carrying `run_id`, the echoed `scope`, and ten arrays of identifiers: `holds_activated`, `holds_completed`, `subscriptions_paused`, `subscriptions_resumed`, `cancellations_completed`, `seats_expired`, `invoices_created`, `subscriptions_cancelled_nonpay`, `cards_updated` and `readers_rescored`. A pass that is out of scope contributes an empty array rather than being absent.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Authoring the Commercial Surface: Offer Plans and the Price Ladder

A `RevenueManager` opens the commercial conversation by authoring an **offer plan**, the object that owns a price ladder and a bundle configuration. Authoring supplies a `name` (between 1 and 120 characters, and unique across all plans) and a `bundle_type`, which is one of `DIGITAL_ONLY`, `PRINT_DIGITAL` or `INSTITUTIONAL_SEAT`. A newly authored plan is born in `status` `DRAFT`, with `active_subscription_count` at 0 and its `price_change_state` at `NONE`. Draft plans are fully authored but not sellable; only a plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE` may take a new subscription, fund a group account, or receive a scheduled price change, and an attempt to do any of those against a `DRAFT` plan answers `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The ladder itself is four inputs plus one optional array. `intro_price_cents` (zero or more) is the all-inclusive price charged for the leading cycles, and `intro_cycles` (zero or more) says how many leading cycles get it. `standard_price_cents` (zero or more) is the all-inclusive price charged from `standard_from_cycle` onward. Between those two rungs sits `step_up_schedule`, an ordered array whose entries are objects with a `from_cycle` and a `price_cents`. The ladder is checked for monotonicity both when the plan is authored and again when it is activated, and a ladder that fails answers `ERR_STEP_UP_SCHEDULE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). To pass, the `from_cycle` values must be strictly increasing across the array, every `from_cycle` must be strictly greater than `intro_cycles` and strictly less than `standard_from_cycle`, the `price_cents` values must be strictly increasing across the array, the first step's `price_cents` must be greater than or equal to `intro_price_cents`, and the last step's `price_cents` must be less than or equal to `standard_price_cents`. Independently, `standard_from_cycle` must be strictly greater than `intro_cycles` and strictly greater than every `from_cycle` in the schedule.

Cycles have a fixed length: `cycle_days` defaults to 30 and must be between 1 and 92 inclusive, and there is no calendar-month drift anywhere in the system — a cycle is exactly `cycle_days` days long, always.

Print economics live on the plan and are only meaningful for a `PRINT_DIGITAL` bundle. `print_component_cents` defaults to 0, is the portion of the cycle price attributable to print delivery, must not exceed `standard_price_cents`, and must be 0 unless `bundle_type` is `PRINT_DIGITAL`. `print_delivery_days` defaults to an empty array, is a subset of the weekday codes `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU`, `FRI`, `SAT`, `SUN`, and must be empty unless the bundle is `PRINT_DIGITAL`. `print_deliveries_per_cycle` defaults to 0, must be at least 1 when the bundle is `PRINT_DIGITAL` and exactly 0 otherwise; it is the contractual divisor from which the daily print rate is derived. Institutional economics are a single field: `per_seat_price_cents` defaults to 0, must be zero or more, and must be strictly greater than 0 when `bundle_type` is `INSTITUTIONAL_SEAT`.

Activation is the one-way door: a `RevenueManager` moves the plan `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, which re-runs the ladder monotonicity check and increments the plan's `version`. `ACTIVE` is absorbing on that dimension — plans are never closed and never reopened — so activating a plan whose `status` is already `ACTIVE` answers `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_ACTIVE`.

**How a cycle's base price is resolved.** Given a subscription, its plan and a cycle index, the base price for that cycle is resolved by walking rungs in this exact order. First, if the subscription carries a non-null `locked_price_cents` and the cycle index is less than or equal to `grandfathered_until_cycle`, the answer is `locked_price_cents` and the resolution is labelled `GRANDFATHERED`. Grandfathering is checked before every other rung, without exception. Otherwise, if the cycle index is less than or equal to `intro_cycles`, the answer is `intro_price_cents`, labelled `INTRO`. Otherwise, if the cycle index is greater than or equal to `standard_from_cycle`, the answer is `standard_price_cents`, labelled `STANDARD`. Otherwise, among the `step_up_schedule` entries whose `from_cycle` is less than or equal to the cycle index, the entry with the greatest `from_cycle` supplies the answer through its `price_cents`, labelled `STEP_UP`. If no step-up entry qualifies, the answer falls back to `standard_price_cents`, labelled `STANDARD`. Only the plan's own step-up entries ever participate.

That resolution runs in two **variants that deliberately disagree about the same subscription**, because they answer different questions.

The **forward-looking variant** is written to `next_cycle_price_cents` and answers "what will this reader be charged next time if nothing changes". It evaluates the rungs for the cycle **after** the one currently being served — one greater than `cycle_index`. When the resolved rung is `STANDARD` and the plan's `price_change_state` is either `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICE_SENT` and the plan's `pending_effective_on` falls on or before the next cycle's start date (that is, `cycle_start_on` advanced by `cycle_days`), it substitutes `pending_price_cents`: a not-yet-applied increase is assumed to land. It then applies `retention_discount_pct` when `retention_discount_cycles_remaining` is at least 1, rounding the discounted line half up to whole cents, and it **ignores all hold credits**, because unrealised credits are not money yet. This value is recomputed on every subscription mutation and on every price-change pipeline transition of the plan.

The **billed variant** is written to an invoice's `base_line_cents` and answers "what did this cycle actually cost". It evaluates the rungs for the cycle actually being billed, and it uses only prices that have already been applied — a `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICE_SENT` change is invisible to it. It has one extra behaviour: when the plan's `price_change_state` is `APPLIED` and `pending_effective_on` falls strictly inside the cycle window (strictly after `cycle_start_on` and strictly before `cycle_start_on` advanced by `cycle_days`), the cycle is prorated into two segments. The first segment is the old price multiplied by the number of days before the effective date, divided by `cycle_days`, rounded half up to whole cents. The second segment is the new price multiplied by the number of days from the effective date onward, divided by `cycle_days`, rounded half up to whole cents. `base_line_cents` is the sum of the two rounded segments — per-segment rounding then addition, never addition then rounding — and `price_source` records `PRORATED_SEGMENTS`. Otherwise `price_source` records whichever of `INTRO`, `STEP_UP`, `STANDARD` or `GRANDFATHERED` the rung walk produced.

### 3.2. The Regulated Price-Increase Pipeline and Grandfathering

Raising a standard price is a three-step regulated pipeline across two different desks, and the plan's `price_change_state` is the pipeline's memory. Each step requires the pipeline to be in the state that step expects — `NONE` to schedule, `SCHEDULED` to dispatch notices, and either `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICE_SENT` to apply — and a step attempted out of turn answers `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_STATE_INVALID`.

**Step one: scheduling.** A `RevenueManager` moves the pipeline `NONE → SCHEDULED` by supplying `pending_price_cents`, `pending_effective_on` and `grandfather_policy`, together with `expected_offer_plan_version` (§2.7). The plan must be `ACTIVE`. The new price must be strictly greater than the plan's current `standard_price_cents` — this pipeline exists for increases, not reductions. `pending_effective_on` must be at least one day after the clock date, so a same-day increase is impossible. The `grandfather_policy` is one of `NONE`, `TENURE_12_CYCLES` or `ALL_EXISTING`, and defaults to `NONE`. Scheduling persists all three inputs, sets `price_change_state` to `SCHEDULED`, recomputes `next_cycle_price_cents` on every subscription of the plan whose `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` — which is how a merely scheduled increase already shows up in a reader's forward-looking quote once `pending_effective_on` falls on or before their next cycle start — and increments the plan's `version`.

**Step two: notices.** Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may dispatch the regulated advance notices, moving the pipeline `SCHEDULED → NOTICE_SENT`. The notice window is the hard compliance gate: the number of days from the clock date to `pending_effective_on` must be at least thirty (a full four weeks plus two days of margin), and a shorter window is refused with `ERR_PRICE_NOTICE_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). Dispatch sets `notice_sent_on` to the clock date, records the acting `X-User-ID` in `notice_sent_by_user_id`, and creates one reader event of type `PRICE_INCREASE_NOTICE` — carrying a `weight` of 0, so notices never move an engagement score — for each reader holding a subscription on this plan whose `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `CANCEL_SCHEDULED`. The plan's `version` increments.

**Step three: applying.** A `ComplianceOfficer` moves the pipeline `NOTICE_SENT → APPLIED`, and three guards stand in the way. Notices must actually have been dispatched — a non-null `notice_sent_on` — otherwise `ERR_PRICE_NOTICE_NOT_SENT`. The clock date must be on or after `pending_effective_on`, otherwise `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_NOT_EFFECTIVE_YET`. And the officer applying the increase must not be the officer who noticed it: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `notice_sent_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `RevenueManager` who scheduled the change can do neither of these steps at all, because neither is open to that role.

Applying runs a three-hop chain. In the first hop, `standard_price_cents` is overwritten with `pending_price_cents` and `price_change_state` becomes `APPLIED`. In the second hop, grandfathering is resolved for every subscription on the plan whose `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `CANCEL_SCHEDULED`, according to the plan's `grandfather_policy`: `ALL_EXISTING` grandfathers every such subscription created on or before `notice_sent_on`; `TENURE_12_CYCLES` grandfathers those whose `tenure_cycles` — the number of paid invoices on the subscription — is at least twelve; `NONE` grandfathers none. Each grandfathered subscription receives `locked_price_cents` equal to the **pre-change** standard price and `grandfathered_until_cycle` equal to its current `cycle_index` plus twelve, so the old price is honoured for a further twelve cycles. In the third hop, each subscription mutated in the second hop recomputes `next_cycle_price_cents` from its new locked-or-standard basis and increments its own `version`. The plan's own `version` increments as well.

### 3.3. Dynamic Paywall Rules, Articles, and the Access Decision

Editorial inventory enters the system as **articles**. A `RevenueManager` publishes one with a `headline` of 1 to 240 characters, a `content_type` drawn from `NEWS`, `OPINION`, `INVESTIGATION` and `LIVE_BLOG`, and a lowercase `section` slug of 1 to 40 characters. Each article also carries an `access_class` — `OPEN`, `METERED` or `PREMIUM`, defaulting to `METERED` — which is an article-level override evaluated ahead of any rule, and a `published_at` instant that defaults to the logical clock at creation. An access decision requested for an article whose `published_at` is later than the clock instant is refused with `ERR_ARTICLE_UNPUBLISHED` (HTTP 422): an embargoed article yields no verdict.

Alongside articles the `RevenueManager` configures **paywall rules**, each a matcher plus a gate. A rule carries an operator-facing `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` defaulting to `ACTIVE`, and three matchers that all default to the literal `ANY`: `content_type_match` (one of `NEWS`, `OPINION`, `INVESTIGATION`, `LIVE_BLOG`, `ANY`), `section_match` (a lowercase slug or `ANY`) and `propensity_band_match` (one of `HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW`, `ANY`). The gate itself is `action`, one of `ALLOW_FREE`, `METER`, `REGISTRATION_WALL` or `HARD_PAYWALL`. An optional `meter_allowance_override` (zero or more when supplied) sets this rule's base free-article allowance; when it is absent, the site default of four free articles applies. Finally, `priority` is an operator-assigned tie-break weight between 1 and 999, unique across the combination of the three matchers.

Each rule also carries a computed `specificity_score`. It is the sum of three contributions: one hundred if `content_type_match` is anything other than `ANY`, ten if `section_match` is anything other than `ANY`, and one if `propensity_band_match` is anything other than `ANY`. A rule matching all three axes therefore scores 111; a fully generic rule scores 0.

**Selecting a rule.** The candidate pool is the set of rules whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose three matchers each either equal the corresponding value on the article or reader, or are `ANY`. An `INACTIVE` rule is excluded from the pool even when it matches perfectly. Candidates are ranked by `specificity_score` descending, then by `priority` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending — and never by `id`. The first candidate under that ordering is selected: its `action` becomes the gate, its `meter_allowance_override` (or the site default of four when the override is null) becomes the allowance base, and its identifier is recorded as `applied_rule_id`. When the candidate pool is empty, that is **not** an error: the gate falls back to `METER`, the allowance base falls back to the site default of four, and `applied_rule_id` is left null.

**The access decision.** A `Reader` or a `SubscriptionOpsAgent` submits a `reader_id` and an `article_id` and receives back the reader event carrying the verdict. The decision runs the following steps in exactly this order.

Before anything else, the meter period is reset lazily: if the number of whole days from `meter_period_started_on` to the clock date is thirty or more, `metered_article_ids` is emptied, `metered_distinct_article_count` is set to 0, and `meter_period_started_on` is set to the clock date. Then the three overrides fire in order. An article whose `access_class` is `OPEN` yields `GRANTED_FREE` and leaves the meter untouched. Otherwise, a reader whose `entitlement_source` is anything other than `NONE` yields `GRANTED_ENTITLED` and leaves the meter untouched. Otherwise, an article whose `access_class` is `PREMIUM` yields `BLOCKED_PAYWALL`.

Only a `METERED` article reaching an unentitled reader gets as far as rule selection. If the selected gate is `ALLOW_FREE`, the verdict is `GRANTED_FREE`; if it is `HARD_PAYWALL`, the verdict is `BLOCKED_PAYWALL`; if it is `REGISTRATION_WALL` and the reader's `is_registered` is false, the verdict is `BLOCKED_REGISTRATION`.

Surviving that, the **effective allowance** is computed from three interacting inputs. Take the allowance base from rule selection and adjust it by the reader's propensity band: a `HIGH`-propensity reader loses two articles from the allowance, a `MEDIUM`-propensity reader is unchanged, and a `LOW`-propensity reader gains two. The result is then floored at one — the effective allowance is the greater of one and the adjusted base — so no combination of a zero override and a `HIGH` band can ever produce a zero or negative allowance. The value actually used is recorded on the event as `effective_allowance`.

Next, a registration wall is checked **before** the meter, and it applies to unregistered readers only: an unregistered reader whose `metered_distinct_article_count` has reached two or more yields `BLOCKED_REGISTRATION`. Then re-reads are honoured for free, because the meter counts **distinct** articles: if the article's identifier is already inside `metered_article_ids`, the verdict is `GRANTED_METERED` with `meter_index` set to that article's existing one-based position in the list, and the counter is not incremented. Finally the allowance boundary is applied: if `metered_distinct_article_count` is greater than or equal to the effective allowance, the verdict is `BLOCKED_METER`; otherwise the article's identifier is appended to `metered_article_ids`, the counter is updated to the new length, and the verdict is `GRANTED_METERED` with `meter_index` set to that new length. The comparison is inclusive on the counter side, which means an allowance of four grants exactly four distinct articles and blocks the fifth.

Every access decision appends exactly one reader event. A granted verdict produces an event of type `ARTICLE_VIEW_GRANTED`; a blocked verdict produces one of type `ARTICLE_VIEW_BLOCKED`. Either way the event carries `decision` — one of `GRANTED_FREE`, `GRANTED_ENTITLED`, `GRANTED_METERED`, `BLOCKED_REGISTRATION`, `BLOCKED_METER` or `BLOCKED_PAYWALL` — along with `meter_index`, `effective_allowance` and `applied_rule_id`. `meter_index` and `effective_allowance` are both null when the meter was never consulted, and `applied_rule_id` is null when the pool was empty or an article-level override decided the outcome. Granted events recompute the reader's `engagement_score` and re-derive their `churn_risk_band`; blocked events carry a `weight` of 0 and leave the score exactly where it was.

**What counts as an entitlement.** A reader's `entitlement_source` is `SUBSCRIPTION`, `SEAT` or `NONE`, and `SUBSCRIPTION` outranks `SEAT` when both apply. A subscription entitles its reader when its `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` or `CANCEL_SCHEDULED`, its `dunning_status` is anything other than `FAILED_FINAL`, and its `access_until` is either null or on or after the clock date. A seat entitles its reader when its `status` is `ACTIVE` and the clock date falls inside its half-open occupancy — on or after `starts_on` and strictly before `ends_on`, with a null `ends_on` treated as never ending. Subscriptions in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` and `PAUSED` do **not** entitle: a paused reader is a metered reader again.

### 3.4. Readers, Engagement Signals, Churn Bands, and Interventions

A `SubscriptionOpsAgent` creates the reader record, which may be an anonymous device row or a registered reader. `email` is optional in general but must be present, and unique among readers that have one, whenever `is_registered` is true; `is_registered` itself defaults to false. `propensity_band` is seeded from the offline conversion model as `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` or `LOW`, defaulting to `MEDIUM`. A card may be attached at creation through `card_last4` (exactly four digits when present), `card_exp_month` (1 through 12) and `card_exp_year` (2020 through 2099) — the two expiry fields are required whenever `card_last4` is present — plus `card_updater_registered`, a boolean defaulting to false that records enrolment with the network card-updater service. On creation, `meter_period_started_on` is set to the clock date, `engagement_score` is 0.00, `churn_risk_band` is `SEVERE`, `entitlement_source` is `NONE`, `metered_article_ids` is empty, `metered_distinct_article_count` is 0, and `card_updater_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

`card_status` is never supplied; it is derived against the logical clock. It is `MISSING` whenever `card_last4` is null. It is `EXPIRED` when the stored expiry — the pair of `card_exp_year` and `card_exp_month` — precedes the clock's year-and-month pair, comparing year first and month second. Otherwise it is `CURRENT`. A card that expires in the current month is still `CURRENT`. Either the `Reader` themselves or a `SubscriptionOpsAgent` may replace the stored card by supplying a fresh `card_last4`, `card_exp_month`, `card_exp_year` and optionally `card_updater_registered`; doing so recomputes `card_status` against the clock, resets `card_updater_status` to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and increments the reader's `version`. Resetting the updater status is what re-arms the recovery pipeline after a manual card fix.

**Engagement scoring.** A reader's `engagement_score` is a decayed thirty-day signal, carried to two decimal places. It is accumulated over all of that reader's events whose `occurred_at` lies inside a half-open thirty-day window ending at the clock instant: an event is in the window when the whole number of days between its `occurred_at` and the clock instant is at least zero and strictly less than thirty. Future-dated events and events thirty days old or older contribute nothing. Each in-window event contributes the weight of its class multiplied by a decay factor of one half raised to the power of the event's age in whole days divided by ten, rounded down to a whole number — so events aged 0 through 9 days contribute at full weight, those aged 10 through 19 days at half weight, and those aged 20 through 29 days at a quarter weight. The class weights are: a granted article view counts 3, a newsletter open counts 2, an app open counts 1, and a posted comment counts 5, while a blocked article view, an editorial intervention, a product intervention and a price-increase notice all count 0 — blocked paywall hits and outbound messages never move the score. The accumulated total is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once, after the summation is complete, and the result is stored on the reader.

That standard, **rolling** reading of the score is what is stored on `engagement_score` and what drives both churn banding and the propensity refresh. A second, **cohort-normalized** reading exists for one purpose only: it is frozen onto a cancellation request as `projected_engagement_score`. When the reader's `tenure_days` is less than thirty, the cohort-normalized reading takes the accumulated total, multiplies it by thirty, divides by the greater of one and `tenure_days`, and rounds half up to two decimals; when `tenure_days` is thirty or more it is identical to the rolling reading. `tenure_days` itself is the whole number of days between the reader's `created_at` and the clock instant, rounded down. The two readings deliberately diverge for young accounts: a reader with an accumulated total of 5.25 and a `tenure_days` of 7 has a rolling score of 5.25 but a cohort-normalized projection of 22.50, and is therefore simultaneously a high churn risk and eligible for the richest save-offer tier.

**Churn banding.** `churn_risk_band` is derived, never supplied, and it has one override that wins outright: if **any** subscription of the reader — considering subscriptions whose `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `CANCEL_SCHEDULED` or `CANCELLED_NONPAY` — has a `dunning_status` of `RETRYING` or `FAILED_FINAL`, the band is `SEVERE` regardless of engagement. Absent that override, the band is read off the two-decimal `engagement_score` with strictly-less-than comparisons: below 5 is `SEVERE`, from 5 up to but not including 15 is `HIGH`, from 15 up to but not including 30 is `MEDIUM`, and 30 or above is `LOW`. A score of exactly 15.00 is `MEDIUM`; 14.99 is `HIGH`. A brand-new reader with no events scores 0.00 and is therefore `SEVERE` — the empty-set edge is a real band, not an error. The band is re-derived every time `engagement_score` is written and on every dunning-status transition of any of the reader's subscriptions.

**Logging signals and interventions.** A `Reader` or a `SubscriptionOpsAgent` records an engagement signal by supplying an `event_type`, optionally an `occurred_at` instant (defaulting to the logical clock) and optionally an `article_id`. The full event-type vocabulary is `ARTICLE_VIEW_GRANTED`, `ARTICLE_VIEW_BLOCKED`, `NEWSLETTER_OPEN`, `APP_OPEN`, `COMMENT_POST`, `INTERVENTION_EDITORIAL`, `INTERVENTION_PRODUCT` and `PRICE_INCREASE_NOTICE`, but three of those are system-generated and cannot be posted directly: an attempt to log `ARTICLE_VIEW_GRANTED`, `ARTICLE_VIEW_BLOCKED` or `PRICE_INCREASE_NOTICE` answers `ERR_EVENT_TYPE_SYSTEM_ONLY` (HTTP 422). Backdated instants are accepted, but only from a `SubscriptionOpsAgent`. Each logged event stores its `reader_id`, its optional `article_id`, its `weight` taken from the class table above, and its `occurred_at`, then triggers a recomputation of the reader's `engagement_score`, a re-derivation of `churn_risk_band`, and a recount of `intervention_count_in_period`.

Interventions are the editorial and product levers for a reader who looks likely to leave, and they are rationed. An `INTERVENTION_EDITORIAL` or `INTERVENTION_PRODUCT` event may only be logged for a reader whose `churn_risk_band` is `HIGH` or `SEVERE`, and only while `intervention_count_in_period` is less than two; a reader whose band is `LOW` or `MEDIUM`, or whose count has already reached two, answers `ERR_INTERVENTION_NOT_WARRANTED` (HTTP 422). `intervention_count_in_period` is the number of the reader's `INTERVENTION_EDITORIAL` and `INTERVENTION_PRODUCT` events whose `occurred_at` falls on or after `meter_period_started_on`, so the intervention budget refills whenever the meter period rolls over.

**The propensity refresh.** `propensity_band` is an input at reader creation and is overwritten by exactly one thing: the ENGAGEMENT pass of a billing run. That pass walks every reader in `created_at` ascending order, rescores `engagement_score` against the current clock, re-derives `churn_risk_band`, resets any meter period that has aged past thirty days, and then re-bands propensity from the freshly written score: 30 or above becomes `HIGH`, from 10 up to but not including 30 becomes `MEDIUM`, and below 10 becomes `LOW`. Because the refresh lives only in that pass, a scoped run over billing or dunning alone never disturbs a seeded band. Readers touched by the pass are reported in `readers_rescored`.

### 3.5. Opening a Subscription and Billing Its Cycles

A `SubscriptionOpsAgent` opens a subscription by supplying the `reader_id` of the paying reader, the `offer_plan_id` of the governing plan, the mandatory `expected_offer_plan_version` guard, and — only for the institutional subscription that funds a group's seats — a `group_account_id`. Everything else is derived. The reader must be a real subscriber first: a reader whose `is_registered` is false or whose `email` is null answers `ERR_READER_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 422). The plan must be `ACTIVE`, on pain of `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`.

On creation the subscription's `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, its `dunning_status` is `CURRENT`, its `print_fulfillment_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE` — print fulfilment does not begin until the first payment succeeds, even on a print bundle — its `cycle_index` is 1, and `cycle_start_on` is the clock date. Its `paid_through_on` is not yet set, its `access_until` and `cancel_effective_on` are null, its `retention_discount_pct` is 0 with `retention_discount_cycles_remaining` at 0, its `pending_credit_cents` and `lifetime_credit_cents` are 0, its `locked_price_cents` and `grandfathered_until_cycle` are null, its `dunning_first_failed_on` and `next_retry_on` are null, its `payment_attempt_count` is 0, its `tenure_cycles` is 0 and its `vacation_days_used_365` is 0. The same request generates the cycle-1 invoice, computes `next_cycle_price_cents` through the forward-looking price variant, recomputes the plan's `active_subscription_count` — the count of subscriptions on that plan whose `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `CANCEL_SCHEDULED` — and increments the plan's `version`.

**Assembling an invoice.** Every invoice, whether the cycle-1 invoice created at subscription time or a later invoice created by the BILLING pass, is assembled by the same waterfall, and the evaluation order is pinned because the discount tier is keyed on the pre-credit subtotal — credits reduce what the reader pays but can never change the discount percentage.

1. `base_line_cents` is the billed variant of the cycle base price resolution described in §3.1, and `price_source` records which rung produced it.
2. `seat_line_cents` is the assembled per-seat charge described in §3.9, and is 0 whenever `group_account_id` is null.
3. The subtotal is `base_line_cents` plus `seat_line_cents`.
4. The discount fraction starts as the subscription's `retention_discount_pct` when `retention_discount_cycles_remaining` is at least 1, and 0 otherwise. When that fraction is greater than zero **and** the subtotal is at least 1500 cents — fifteen dollars, the high-value threshold — the fraction is upgraded by ten percentage points, then capped at the forty-percent save-offer ceiling: the fraction becomes the lesser of the uplifted fraction and 0.40.
5. `discount_line_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that fraction, rounded half up to whole cents.
6. `credit_line_cents` is the sum of `credit_cents` over the subscription's holds whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `credit_applied` is false, taken in `end_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending order. Holds that are `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE` or `RELEASED` never contribute.
7. `total_cents` is the subtotal minus `discount_line_cents` minus `credit_line_cents`, floored at zero.
8. If `total_cents` came out as zero the invoice is created directly in `status` `VOID`; otherwise it is created `OPEN`. Every hold swept in step 6 has its `credit_applied` set to true and its `credit_applied_cycle_index` set to the cycle this invoice bills, which is also what moves that money out of `pending_credit_cents` and into `lifetime_credit_cents`. When the discount fraction was greater than zero, `retention_discount_cycles_remaining` is decremented by one.

An invoice records `subscription_id`, the `cycle_index` it bills — unique per subscription and cycle — its `due_on`, which is the date it was generated, and `paid_on`, which stays null until it reaches `PAID`. Its `status` moves through `OPEN`, `PAID`, `PAST_DUE`, `WRITTEN_OFF` and `VOID`, of which `PAID`, `WRITTEN_OFF` and `VOID` are terminal.

A `VOID` invoice is a real transition, `OPEN → VOID`, taken at generation time when the assembled `total_cents` is zero. It is the one path by which service advances without any money changing hands: `paid_through_on` is advanced by the plan's `cycle_days` at creation, with no payment attempt ever recorded, and the credits swept onto it are still marked applied.

**Activation.** The subscription's first successful payment is what makes it live. Recording a `SUCCEEDED` outcome against the cycle-1 invoice of a `PENDING_ACTIVATION` subscription — the invoice must be `OPEN`, must have `cycle_index` 1, and must belong to that subscription — drives `PENDING_ACTIVATION → ACTIVE`. It sets the invoice to `PAID` with `paid_on` at the clock date, sets `paid_through_on` to `cycle_start_on` advanced by the plan's `cycle_days`, recomputes `tenure_cycles` as the count of paid invoices, recomputes the reader's `entitlement_source` to `SUBSCRIPTION`, and recomputes `next_cycle_price_cents`. On a `PRINT_DIGITAL` plan the same success also moves `print_fulfillment_status` from `NOT_APPLICABLE` to `SCHEDULED`; on any other bundle that dimension stays `NOT_APPLICABLE` for life.

**Subsequent cycles.** The BILLING pass of a run picks up every subscription whose `paid_through_on` has fallen on or before the clock date and generates that subscription's next invoice through the waterfall above, sweeping any unapplied completed hold credits and decrementing `retention_discount_cycles_remaining` where a discount applied. The subscription's `cycle_index` advances to the cycle the new invoice bills and `cycle_start_on` advances by the plan's `cycle_days`. Identifiers of the invoices created are reported in `invoices_created`.

### 3.6. Payment Failure Recovery: The Dunning Ladder and the Card Updater

Processor results arrive as **payment attempts** recorded by a `SubscriptionOpsAgent` against a specific invoice. Each attempt supplies an `outcome` of `SUCCEEDED`, `SOFT_DECLINE` or `HARD_DECLINE`, an `amount_cents` tendered, and optionally a `decline_code` of 1 to 40 characters, which is null on success. Partial tenders do not exist: `amount_cents` must equal the invoice's `total_cents` exactly, and any other figure answers `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The recorded attempt also carries the `invoice_id` it charged, its `attempt_no`, its `attempted_on` clock date, its computed `is_final` flag, and `card_last4_snapshot` — the reader's `card_last4` at the moment of the attempt, null when no card was on file. Payment attempts are append-only records with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

`attempt_no` is the one-based ordinal of this attempt on the invoice: the count of existing attempts on that invoice, counted chronologically by `created_at` ascending, plus one. That count is also mirrored on the subscription as `payment_attempt_count` for the invoice of the open dunning episode. The ceiling is `max_payment_attempts`, which is four normally and five when the reader's `card_updater_status` is `UPDATED` — successfully refreshing a card through the network buys exactly one bonus attempt. `is_final` is true when the `outcome` is `HARD_DECLINE`, and also when the `outcome` is `SOFT_DECLINE` and `attempt_no` is greater than or equal to `max_payment_attempts`. Before any attempt is admitted at all, the episode must still be open — the `dunning_status` must not be `FAILED_FINAL` and `attempt_no` must not exceed `max_payment_attempts`, otherwise `ERR_DUNNING_EXHAUSTED` — and only then is the invoice checked for payability: it must be `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE`, since a `PAID` or `VOID` invoice takes no further attempt, otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAYABLE`.

**The retry ladder.** A non-final `SOFT_DECLINE` against an `OPEN` or `PAST_DUE` invoice drives the dunning dimension `CURRENT → RETRYING` and moves the invoice `OPEN → PAST_DUE`. On the first failure of an episode, `dunning_first_failed_on` is set to the clock date. `next_retry_on` is then computed as `dunning_first_failed_on` advanced by an offset drawn from the ladder of three, five and seven days: the first attempt schedules three days out, the second five days out, and the third and every later one seven days out. Offsets are **cumulative from the first failure, never measured from the previous attempt** — failures beginning on day 0 schedule retries on day 3, day 5 and day 7, not day 3, day 8 and day 15. The whole ladder is designed to sit inside a fourteen-day dunning grace period, which is why the last offset is seven days rather than something longer. The same failure arms the card-updater pipeline: the reader's `card_updater_status` moves from `NOT_APPLICABLE` to `PENDING` when `card_updater_registered` is true and `card_status` is `EXPIRED`, and to `UNAVAILABLE` otherwise. It also re-derives the reader's `churn_risk_band`, which the dunning override forces to `SEVERE`.

Further non-final soft declines keep the subscription in `RETRYING` — a `RETRYING → RETRYING` self-loop — recomputing `next_retry_on` from the same ladder and recomputing `payment_attempt_count`. A retry is only admissible once the clock has caught up: when `next_retry_on` is set, the clock date must be on or after it, and an earlier attempt answers `ERR_RETRY_TOO_EARLY`.

**The card-updater sweep.** The DUNNING pass of a run handles cards for subscriptions whose `dunning_status` is `RETRYING` and whose reader's `card_updater_status` is `PENDING`. When the reader's `card_updater_registered` is true, the sweep succeeds: `card_exp_year` is advanced by three years, `card_status` becomes `CURRENT` and `card_updater_status` becomes `UPDATED` — which is precisely what raises `max_payment_attempts` to five. When the reader is not enrolled, `card_updater_status` becomes `UNAVAILABLE`. Readers whose cards were refreshed appear in `cards_updated`.

**Recovery.** A `SUCCEEDED` outcome against a `PAST_DUE` invoice drives `RETRYING → RECOVERED` and moves the invoice `PAST_DUE → PAID`. It sets `paid_on` to the clock date, clears `next_retry_on` to null, advances `paid_through_on` by the plan's `cycle_days`, recomputes `tenure_cycles`, and re-derives the reader's `churn_risk_band` from `engagement_score` — the dunning override is lifted, so a well-engaged reader immediately climbs back out of `SEVERE`. The DUNNING pass then closes the episode: a subscription whose `dunning_status` is `RECOVERED` and none of whose invoices is `PAST_DUE` moves `RECOVERED → CURRENT` with `dunning_first_failed_on` cleared to null. That closes the full recovery cycle `CURRENT → RETRYING → RECOVERED → CURRENT`, and a subscription may walk it any number of times.

**The final decline.** An attempt whose `is_final` is true — a `HARD_DECLINE` at any point, or a `SOFT_DECLINE` whose `attempt_no` has reached `max_payment_attempts` — ends the relationship in a single five-hop chain. Hop one persists the attempt with `is_final` true. Hop two moves the invoice to `WRITTEN_OFF`. Hop three closes the dunning episode unsuccessfully: `dunning_status` becomes `FAILED_FINAL`, `next_retry_on` is cleared to null, and `lifecycle_status` moves `ACTIVE → CANCELLED_NONPAY` with `access_until` set to the clock date, so entitlement lapses today rather than at the end of a paid period. Hop four releases the terminal subscription's occupations and seats: every hold on it whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `RELEASED`, and when `group_account_id` is set, the funding relationship collapses — the group account's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and every seat under it whose `status` is `INVITED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `RECLAIMED` with `ends_on` set to the clock date. Hop five recomputes the counters the previous hop invalidated: the released holds leave the pending-credit input set so `pending_credit_cents` falls to 0, the reclaimed seats leave the occupancy measure so the group's `seats_assigned_count` and `seats_available_count` are recomputed, the reader's `entitlement_source` becomes `NONE`, and the reader's `churn_risk_band` is recomputed to `SEVERE` under the dunning override. Subscriptions ended this way are reported in `subscriptions_cancelled_nonpay` when the cascade is reached during a run.

### 3.7. Service Holds: Billing Pauses, Vacation Holds, and Delivery Interruptions

A **hold** is an occupation of a subscription's service window, and its `hold_type` selects entirely different semantics. `PAUSE_BILLING` suspends billing and access. `VACATION_DELIVERY` is a reader-requested suspension of print delivery. `DELIVERY_INTERRUPTION` is a publisher-caused failure of print delivery. Either a `Reader` acting on their own subscription or a `SubscriptionOpsAgent` creates one by supplying `hold_type`, `start_date` and `end_date`; every other field is derived. A hold begins in `status` `SCHEDULED`, and its states are `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED` and `RELEASED`, of which the last two are terminal.

Holds may only be created against a live subscription. A subscription whose `lifecycle_status` is `CANCELLED` or `CANCELLED_NONPAY` answers `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERMINAL`; one that exists but is not `ACTIVE` — `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `PAUSED` or `CANCEL_SCHEDULED` — answers `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Both print hold types additionally require the economics to exist: a `VACATION_DELIVERY` or `DELIVERY_INTERRUPTION` hold on a plan whose `bundle_type` is not `PRINT_DIGITAL`, or whose `print_deliveries_per_cycle` is below 1, answers `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PRINT_BUNDLE` (HTTP 422).

Timing rules differ by type, and this is deliberate. Reader-initiated holds must be genuinely future-dated: a `VACATION_DELIVERY` or `PAUSE_BILLING` hold whose `start_date` is on or before the clock date answers `ERR_HOLD_START_NOT_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). A publisher-caused `DELIVERY_INTERRUPTION` may start today, because the presses have already failed by the time anyone files it, but never earlier: a `start_date` before the clock date answers `ERR_HOLD_START_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). Regardless of type, `end_date` must be on or after `start_date` and the closed interval must span no more than ninety days, otherwise `ERR_HOLD_INTERVAL_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

Occupation conflicts use the closed-plus-buffer geometry of §2.11. For a `VACATION_DELIVERY` or `PAUSE_BILLING` hold, the request is refused with `ERR_HOLD_BUFFER_VIOLATION` when any existing hold on the same subscription whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` satisfies both of these: the new hold's `start_date` is on or before that hold's `end_date` advanced by two days, and that hold's `start_date` is on or before the new hold's `end_date` advanced by two days. Back-to-back holds and holds separated by only a day or two are therefore both refused. A `DELIVERY_INTERRUPTION` is exempt from the buffer — the publisher's own failure should not be blocked by a reader's calendar — but it must not begin inside the closed interval of an existing `VACATION_DELIVERY` hold, and one that does is refused with the same code. Holds whose `status` is `RELEASED` never block anything.

Two aggregate quotas guard the reader-initiated types. Vacation days are capped at twenty-eight per rolling year — four weeks: `vacation_days_used_365`, which is the sum of the inclusive day counts of `VACATION_DELIVERY` holds whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` and whose `start_date` falls on or after the clock date less 365 days, plus the requested hold's own inclusive day count, must not exceed 28, otherwise `ERR_VACATION_DAY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Pauses are capped by count rather than by length: at most two `PAUSE_BILLING` holds whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` may exist on a subscription with a `start_date` inside the trailing 365 days, and a third answers `ERR_PAUSE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

**Credit is frozen at creation and never re-evaluated.** For a `PAUSE_BILLING` hold, `delivery_days_credited` is 0 and `credit_cents` is 0, because paused days are not billed at all and there is nothing to give back. For the two print types, the daily print rate is the plan's `print_component_cents` divided by its `print_deliveries_per_cycle`, rounded half up to whole cents **once**; `delivery_days_credited` is the number of dates in the closed interval from `start_date` through `end_date` inclusive whose weekday code appears in the plan's `print_delivery_days`; and `credit_cents` is that rounded daily rate multiplied by `delivery_days_credited`, with no further rounding. Worked through: a plan whose `print_component_cents` is 700 across 4 deliveries per cycle yields a daily print rate of 175 cents, and a `VACATION_DELIVERY` hold running 2026-09-05 through 2026-09-14 on a plan delivering only on `SUN` covers 2026-09-06 and 2026-09-13, giving `delivery_days_credited` of 2 and `credit_cents` of 350.

Creating a hold recomputes the subscription's `pending_credit_cents` upward — that field is the sum of `credit_cents` over holds on the subscription whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` and whose `credit_applied` is false — and recomputes `vacation_days_used_365` for a vacation hold. A `DELIVERY_INTERRUPTION` whose `start_date` is the clock date does not wait: the hold is created directly in `status` `ACTIVE` and the print dimension moves `SCHEDULED → INTERRUPTED` in the same request.

**Holds coming into and out of force.** The HOLDS pass of a run activates every hold whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `start_date` has arrived — on or before the clock date — provided the subscription's `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, and reports them in `holds_activated`. What activation does depends on the type. A `PAUSE_BILLING` hold reaching its start date moves the hold to `ACTIVE` and drives the lifecycle dimension `ACTIVE → PAUSED`, then recomputes the reader's `entitlement_source` — and because `PAUSED` is not an entitling status, a reader with no seat drops to `NONE` and is metered again. A `VACATION_DELIVERY` hold reaching its start date moves the hold to `ACTIVE` and drives the print dimension `SCHEDULED → HELD`.

The same pass retires holds that have run their course: an `ACTIVE` hold whose `end_date` is strictly before the clock date becomes `COMPLETED`, is reported in `holds_completed`, and its credit stays inside `pending_credit_cents` until a BILLING pass sweeps it onto an invoice. Completing the pause that caused a pause drives `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, provided that hold is the only `ACTIVE` `PAUSE_BILLING` hold on the subscription; the subscription is reported in `subscriptions_resumed`, `cycle_start_on` is shifted forward by the number of paused days so paused days are never billed, and `entitlement_source` is recomputed back to `SUBSCRIPTION`. Completing a print hold drives the print dimension `HELD → SCHEDULED` or `INTERRUPTED → SCHEDULED`, provided no other hold with `hold_type` `VACATION_DELIVERY` or `DELIVERY_INTERRUPTION` is still `ACTIVE` on that subscription.

**Releasing a hold early is a compensating action.** A `SubscriptionOpsAgent` may release a hold whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` and whose `credit_applied` is still false; a hold that is already `COMPLETED` or `RELEASED`, or whose credit has already been swept onto an invoice, answers `ERR_HOLD_NOT_RELEASABLE`. Release sets `status` to `RELEASED` and recomputes `pending_credit_cents` downward by exactly this hold's `credit_cents`, leaving `lifetime_credit_cents` — which is the sum of `credit_cents` over holds whose `credit_applied` is true, and which never decreases — completely untouched. It also recomputes `vacation_days_used_365`. When the released hold was the one in force, release unwinds the paired dimension too: the print dimension moves from `HELD` or `INTERRUPTED` back to `SCHEDULED`, and the lifecycle dimension moves `PAUSED → ACTIVE`. The distinction between the two exits is the money: a hold that runs to `COMPLETED` keeps its credit, while a hold that exits to `RELEASED` has its credit reversed.

### 3.8. The Regulated Cancellation Flow and Save Offers

Cancellation is a regulated flow with a fixed shape, and the shape matters as much as the outcome. A `Reader` on their own subscription, or a `SubscriptionOpsAgent` on their behalf, starts it by supplying a `reason_code` — one of `PRICE`, `CONTENT`, `TECHNICAL`, `LIFE_CHANGE` or `OTHER`. The subscription must be `ACTIVE`: a terminal one answers `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERMINAL` and a merely non-active one answers `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The request is created with `status` `INITIATED`, `save_offer_count` at 0 and `retained` false, and its `confirmed_on` and `effective_on` null. It also freezes, once and for all, the cohort-normalized reading of the reader's engagement as `projected_engagement_score`, from which `eligible_save_offer_tier` is derived: `RICH` when the projection is 20 or above, `STANDARD` when it is at least 8 but below 20, and `MINIMAL` when it is below 8. The request states run `INITIATED`, `SAVE_OFFER_PRESENTED`, `SAVE_OFFER_ACCEPTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `COMPLETED` and `ABANDONED`, of which `COMPLETED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal. A request that is `CONFIRMED`, `COMPLETED` or `ABANDONED` — or `SAVE_OFFER_ACCEPTED`, since retention closes the flow — accepts nothing further and answers `ERR_CANCELLATION_REQUEST_CLOSED`.

**Exactly one save offer, ever.** A `SubscriptionOpsAgent` may present a single retention instrument inside an open flow, moving the request `INITIATED → SAVE_OFFER_PRESENTED`. The offer supplies an `offer_type` of `RETENTION_DISCOUNT`, `PAUSE` or `DOWNGRADE`, plus the parameters that type needs: `discount_pct` (a fraction defaulting to 0, required and strictly greater than 0 for a `RETENTION_DISCOUNT`), `duration_cycles` (defaulting to 0, the number of cycles an accepted discount runs for) and `pause_days` (defaulting to 0, required for a `PAUSE`). Ranges are enforced with `ERR_SAVE_OFFER_DISCOUNT_CAP` (HTTP 422): `discount_pct` must not exceed 0.40 — the forty-percent ceiling that also caps the high-value uplift in §3.5 — `duration_cycles` must be between 1 and 12 inclusive for a `RETENTION_DISCOUNT` or `DOWNGRADE`, and `pause_days` must be between 7 and 90 inclusive for a `PAUSE`. The presented offer is created with `status` `PRESENTED` and `presented_on` at the clock date, its `decided_on` null, carrying the `cancellation_request_id` of the flow it belongs to; its states are `PRESENTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` and `EXPIRED`. The request's `save_offer_count` is then recomputed. Because the regulatory cap is one offer per flow, a second presentation answers `ERR_SAVE_OFFER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` — and a **declined** offer still counts, so a reader who says no can never be re-pitched inside the same flow.

**Deciding the offer** is the `Reader`'s exclusive prerogative, expressed as a `decision` of `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED`. The offer must still be `PRESENTED`, otherwise `ERR_SAVE_OFFER_ALREADY_DECIDED`, and the subscription must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Accepting takes the retention branch. The offer becomes `ACCEPTED` with `decided_on` at the clock date, the request moves `SAVE_OFFER_PRESENTED → SAVE_OFFER_ACCEPTED`, and `retained` becomes true. On a `RETENTION_DISCOUNT` or `DOWNGRADE`, the subscription's `retention_discount_pct` is set to the offer's `discount_pct` and its `retention_discount_cycles_remaining` to the offer's `duration_cycles`, then `next_cycle_price_cents` is recomputed. On a `PAUSE`, a `PAUSE_BILLING` hold is created instead, in `status` `SCHEDULED`, with `start_date` at the subscription's `paid_through_on` and `end_date` at `paid_through_on` advanced by `pause_days` less one day — a closed interval exactly `pause_days` long — and with `credit_cents` of 0, after which `next_cycle_price_cents` is recomputed. On either shape, `lifecycle_status` stays `ACTIVE`: nothing is cancelled on this branch.

Declining takes the decline branch. The offer becomes `DECLINED` with `decided_on` at the clock date and the request returns `SAVE_OFFER_PRESENTED → INITIATED`, so the reader is back on the cancellation path with one keystroke. `save_offer_count` stays at 1, which is what makes the single-offer rule bite on the way back.

**Confirming the cancellation** is the regulated one-step exit, and it is reachable both directly from `INITIATED` and from `SAVE_OFFER_PRESENTED` without deciding the offer at all — that is the point of the regulation. Either the `Reader` or a `ComplianceOfficer` acting on their behalf may confirm, and the subscription must be `ACTIVE`. Confirmation runs a four-hop chain. Hop one sets the request's `status` to `CONFIRMED`, its `confirmed_on` to the clock date and its `effective_on` to the subscription's `paid_through_on`, and expires any save offer on that request still in `PRESENTED` to `EXPIRED`. Hop two moves the subscription `ACTIVE → CANCEL_SCHEDULED`, sets both `access_until` and `cancel_effective_on` to `paid_through_on` — the reader keeps what they paid for — and moves the print dimension from `SCHEDULED`, `HELD` or `INTERRUPTED` to `STOPPED_AT_CYCLE_END`. Hop three follows from the print stop: every hold on the subscription whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `start_date` falls after `access_until` becomes `RELEASED`, because a hold can no longer be served once service has ended. Hop four recomputes what hop three invalidated: each hold released in hop three drops out of the pending-credit input set, so `pending_credit_cents` is recomputed downward by exactly the sum of those holds' `credit_cents`, while `lifetime_credit_cents` is unchanged.

**Two endings, both driven by the EXPIRY pass.** When the clock date passes `cancel_effective_on` — strictly after it — the pass drives `CANCEL_SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, moves the print dimension `STOPPED_AT_CYCLE_END → STOPPED`, moves the linked `CONFIRMED` cancellation request `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED` (reported in `cancellations_completed`), and recomputes the reader's `entitlement_source` to `NONE` unless an `ACTIVE` seat still entitles them. Separately, a flow the reader simply walked away from is cleaned up: a request whose `status` is `INITIATED` or `SAVE_OFFER_PRESENTED` and whose `created_at` is thirty or more days behind the clock date becomes `ABANDONED`, any offer still `PRESENTED` on it becomes `EXPIRED`, and the subscription itself is left completely untouched.

### 3.9. Institutional Accounts, Domain Authentication, and Seat Concurrency

Institutions buy access in bulk. A `SubscriptionOpsAgent` creates a **group account** with a `name` of 1 to 160 characters, an `admin_email` for the institutional administrator — an opaque credential, not a reader row — an `allowed_email_domain` (a lowercase host of 3 to 80 characters, for example `univ.edu`), a `seat_capacity` between 1 and 10000, and the `offer_plan_id` of the funding plan. That plan must be sellable and must be the right shape: a plan whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` answers `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and a plan whose `bundle_type` is not `INSTITUTIONAL_SEAT` or whose `per_seat_price_cents` is 0 answers `ERR_PLAN_NOT_INSTITUTIONAL` (HTTP 422). The group is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `seats_assigned_count` at 0 and `seats_available_count` equal to `seat_capacity`. A group's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and it only becomes `SUSPENDED` when its funding subscription dies of non-payment (§3.6).

Seats are the junction between an institution and its readers, and only the `GroupAdmin` of that group may create them. An invitation supplies the `reader_id` of the invitee, a `starts_on` date, the mandatory `expected_group_account_version` guard, and optionally an `ends_on` date; the seat is created with `status` `INVITED`, its `group_account_id` set from the path, `invited_on` at the clock date, `invite_expires_on` at `invited_on` advanced by fourteen days (two weeks to claim), and `claimed_on` null. Seat states are `INVITED`, `ACTIVE`, `RECLAIMED` and `EXPIRED`, with the last two terminal.

Four rules guard an invitation. The funding relationship must be alive: the group must have a funding subscription — the subscription whose `group_account_id` is this group — with `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE` and `dunning_status` other than `FAILED_FINAL`, otherwise `ERR_GROUP_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). The interval must be sane: `starts_on` must be on or after the clock date and, when `ends_on` is supplied, it must be strictly after `starts_on`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_INTERVAL_INVALID` (HTTP 422) — a zero-length half-open occupancy is not a seat. The same reader may not hold two overlapping seats on the same group: if any existing seat on the group has the same `reader_id`, a `status` of `INVITED` or `ACTIVE`, and a half-open interval that intersects the candidate's, the answer is `ERR_SEAT_READER_DUPLICATE`. And capacity is admitted by **aggregate concurrency, not by pairwise overlap**.

**How seat capacity is measured.** Take the half-open intervals of every seat on the group whose `status` is `INVITED` or `ACTIVE`, treating a null `ends_on` as extending indefinitely, and add the candidate's interval. Collect every start and end date as a sorted set of boundaries, and for each consecutive pair of boundaries count how many intervals cover that sub-window; the peak of those counts is the maximum concurrent occupancy. The invitation is admitted only when that peak is less than or equal to `seat_capacity`, and refused with `ERR_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` otherwise. Because the geometry is half-open, adjacency is free: an existing seat ending 2026-09-09 and a candidate starting 2026-09-09 never contend. The group's derived counters use the **same** peak-occupancy measure, computed over the `INVITED` and `ACTIVE` seats only with the candidate excluded: `seats_assigned_count` is that peak, and `seats_available_count` is `seat_capacity` less `seats_assigned_count`, floored at zero. This is why three staggered seat rows can coexist on a capacity-two group — the raw row count is 3 but the peak concurrency is 2 — and why the conservation invariant `seat_capacity` equals `seats_assigned_count` plus `seats_available_count` holds after every seat transition. `RECLAIMED` and `EXPIRED` seats free their capacity immediately. Each invitation also increments the group's `version`.

**Claiming a seat** is the `Reader`'s exclusive act and is where institutional authentication happens. The seat must still be `INVITED`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_NOT_INVITED`; the clock date must be on or before `invite_expires_on`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_INVITE_EXPIRED`; the reader's `email` must end with an at-sign followed by the group's `allowed_email_domain`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_DOMAIN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); and the group's funding subscription must still be `ACTIVE` with a `dunning_status` other than `FAILED_FINAL`, otherwise `ERR_GROUP_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Claiming drives `INVITED → ACTIVE`, sets `claimed_on` to the clock date, and recomputes the reader's `entitlement_source` to `SEAT` when no subscription already entitles them — a reader with their own live subscription keeps `SUBSCRIPTION`, since it outranks `SEAT`.

**Reclaiming a seat** is the `GroupAdmin`'s exclusive act and works from either `INVITED` — revoking a pending invitation before it is claimed — or `ACTIVE`. Either way the seat becomes `RECLAIMED` with `ends_on` set to the clock date, the group's `seats_assigned_count` and `seats_available_count` are recomputed so the conservation invariant still holds, the group's `version` increments, and the reader's `entitlement_source` is recomputed to `NONE` unless a subscription entitles them. Invitations nobody claims lapse on their own: the EXPIRY pass of a run moves a seat whose `status` is `INVITED` and whose `invite_expires_on` is strictly behind the clock date to `EXPIRED`, recomputes the group's two counters, and reports the seat in `seats_expired`.

**Billing the seats.** When the BILLING pass invoices a subscription whose `group_account_id` is not null, the seat charge is assembled per seat and then summed. The cycle window is half-open: it begins at `cycle_start_on` and ends, exclusively, at `cycle_start_on` advanced by the plan's `cycle_days`. The seats that participate are those on the subscription's group account whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RECLAIMED`, whose `starts_on` is strictly before the cycle end, and whose `ends_on` is either null or strictly after the cycle start; `INVITED` and `EXPIRED` seats are never billed. They are processed in `starts_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending order. For each, the billable occupancy runs from the later of `starts_on` and the cycle start to the earlier of `ends_on` (or the cycle end when `ends_on` is null) and the cycle end; the number of active days is the whole-day span of that half-open window, which is 0 when the two endpoints coincide. That seat's line is the plan's `per_seat_price_cents` multiplied by the active days, divided by the plan's `cycle_days`, rounded half up to whole cents. `seat_line_cents` is the sum of those per-line figures, and the sum is never re-rounded — the pinned order described in §2.6.

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — an offer plan, a paywall rule, an article, a reader, a reader event, an access decision, a subscription, a hold, a cancellation request, a save offer, a payment attempt, a group account, a seat — return `201 Created`.
