# Property Improvement Plan Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a hotel changes hands, fails a brand inspection, or comes up for relicensing, the brand issues the owner a Property Improvement Plan: a scoped, priced, deadline-bearing list of work the property must complete to keep its flag. Today that process lives in spreadsheets and email threads. Nobody can say with confidence which of a franchisee's forty hotels are quietly drifting toward default, whether the capital the owner authorised actually covers the escalated cost of the scope, or how much revenue a proposed renovation phase will displace before the keys come out of service. The disputes that follow — over whether a scoped item was reasonable at all, and over whose cost estimate is right — are settled from memory rather than from a record.

This document specifies the Property Improvement Plan Compliance API, the system of record for that entire arc. It carries a plan from brand-side scoping through issuance, owner acknowledgement, capital budgeting, FF&E reserve draws, phased room closures with contractor crews, item-by-item execution, deadline extension requests, scope disputes, the completion inspection and its punch list, and — where the work is not done — notices of default, liquidated damages and franchise termination. Every date comparison in the system reads a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall time, so the passage of a cure period or the arrival of a deadline is an observable, driveable event.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, the brand's figure and the owner's figure are kept separately and never allowed to contaminate each other: the plan carries a brand-scoped cost total and an owner-claimed cost total side by side, and only the brand variant funds anything. Second, rounding order is pinned per calculation rather than left to the implementer — the funding requirement rounds each item before summing, while liquidated damages sum exact per-item products and round once at the end. These are not stylistic preferences; the two orders produce different integers, and the API contract is the specific one stated below. All resources are mounted under the `/pip` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, resource identity, the logical clock, rejection ordering, concurrency control, rounding, cascade semantics and the audit ledger.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If `X-User-Role` is absent the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_ROLE`. If it is present but carries a value outside the three recognised roles the rejection is `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`. If `X-User-ID` is absent, empty, or longer than 64 characters the rejection is `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID`. All three are 401-class failures and are evaluated before anything else about the request is inspected.

The system recognises exactly three roles, spelled precisely as follows: `FranchiseeOwner`, `BrandManager`, and `BrandInspector`. A `FranchiseeOwner` is the hotel owner holding the franchise agreement; it executes plans — acknowledging issuance, authorising and locking capital, requesting and disbursing reserve draws, scheduling and activating phased room closures, booking contractor crews, starting and completing item work, remediating defects, and filing scope disputes. A `BrandManager` is the brand's compliance and franchising operator; it registers franchisees, properties and contractors, scopes and issues plans, revises scope while a plan is still in draft, approves capital budgets, consents to reserve draws, decides extension requests, resolves scope disputes, runs compliance sweeps, and issues, cures or escalates notices of default. A `BrandInspector` is the brand's field inspector; it performs scheduled visits, raises punch-list defects, verifies, reopens or waives them, and certifies completion. An inspector can neither scope nor issue a plan, nor decide extensions, budgets, draws, disputes or notices. When a caller presents a role that is not listed for the endpoint it is invoking, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).

Identity semantics differ by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `FranchiseeOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FranchiseeAccount`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `FranchiseeAccount` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`: concretely, every owner-side action resolves the governing `Property` and requires that its `franchisee_id` equal the presented `X-User-ID`, failing with `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_FRANCHISEE` (403) otherwise. One owner can therefore never act on another owner's property. The system also stamps the presented id onto records the owner authors, such as `filed_by_user_id` on a scope dispute.
- When `X-User-Role` is `BrandManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — as `issued_by_user_id` on a plan and on a default notice, as `approved_by_user_id` on a capital budget, as `consented_by_user_id` on a reserve draw, and as `decided_by_user_id` on an extension request — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. One recorded value does carry weight: the operator id that issued a plan is barred from later certifying that plan's completion, which is the segregation-of-duties rule described in §3.8.
- When `X-User-Role` is `BrandInspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is not compared to any entity's `id`, but it is compared to the `inspector_user_id` string that a `BrandManager` assigned when scheduling the visit; only that exact id may perform the visit, sign it off, or verify defects raised by it, and any other id is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR` (403). The server records the signing id as `signed_off_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. Filter parameters on list endpoints behave differently from path identifiers: a filter value that resolves to nothing — a `plan_id` that does not exist, a `status` no row currently holds — yields an empty page with `total_count` of 0, never a 404.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every resource in the system carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, generated at creation and never reassigned. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row. Because `version` is what optimistic concurrency reads (§2.7), the specification is careful throughout §3 to say which entity's counter a given transition advances — a cascade that touches a plan, an item and a property advances three separate counters.

The one exception is the compliance ledger. A `ComplianceLedgerEntry` is append-only: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation entirely, because a ledger row is never modified after it is appended.

### 2.5. The Logical Compliance Clock

Deadlines, overdue flags, cure windows, inspection scheduling and every interval comparison in this domain read a server-controlled logical clock rather than the host's wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time.

Any role may move the clock through the administrative endpoint `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `current_utc`; the response echoes `{ "current_utc": ... }`. Moving the clock changes how subsequent requests evaluate date comparisons, but it does not itself recompute anything already stored. A deadline that has passed becomes recorded overdue state only when an endpoint that recomputes it is called — which is why the brand-side compliance sweep described in §3.9 exists.

Where the specification says "the clock date", it means the calendar date in UTC of the current logical clock instant. Date arithmetic — deadline offsets, cure periods, changeover buffers, extension days — is in whole calendar days on UTC dates.

### 2.6. Rejection Ordering: How the Server Chooses Which Error to Return

Many requests in this domain break more than one rule at once. The response must be deterministic, so checks are evaluated in a fixed order and the first failure is what the caller sees. The bands, in order:

1. **Authentication and authorisation.** `X-User-Role` present (`ERR_MISSING_ROLE`), then recognised (`ERR_INVALID_ROLE`), then `X-User-ID` present and between 1 and 64 characters (`ERR_MISSING_USER_ID`), then the presented role listed for the endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
2. **Existence.** Resource paths in this API are flat: every resource lives at its own collection under `/pip` and is addressed by a single `{id}`, with children discovered through filtered list endpoints rather than nested routes. That `{id}` is resolved before any request-body field is read, and a miss yields the collection's own not-found code — `ERR_FRANCHISEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXTENSION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRAW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PHASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUNCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, each 404. Only then are foreign keys carried in the body resolved, in body-field declaration order — `franchisee_id`, `property_id`, `plan_id`, then the item references (`item_id`, `pip_item_id`, `purpose_item_id`), then `predecessor_item_id`, `capital_budget_id`, `contractor_id`, `phase_id`, `inspection_id` — each yielding its own parent's not-found code.
3. **Request shape.** Required fields present and non-null, enum-typed inputs drawn from their declared sets, numeric bounds respected, pagination parameters in range — all `ERR_VALIDATION` (422) — followed by the shape rules that carry their own codes: `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, `ERR_ROOM_IMPACT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_CONTINGENCY_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_PHASE_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_REVISION_COST_REQUIRED` and `ERR_REVISION_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_RESOLUTION`.
4. **State preconditions.** Whether the target row is in a state from which the requested action is declared at all: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ISSUABLE`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGEABLE`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_STARTABLE`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVABLE`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_LOCKABLE`, `ERR_DRAW_NOT_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_DRAW_NOT_DISBURSABLE`, `ERR_DRAW_NOT_REVERSIBLE`, `ERR_PHASE_NOT_ACTIVATABLE`, `ERR_PHASE_NOT_CLOSEABLE`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PERFORMABLE`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SIGNABLE`, `ERR_PUNCH_NOT_REMEDIABLE`, `ERR_PUNCH_NOT_VERIFIABLE`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESOLVABLE`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Then the governing plan's window (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_EXECUTION_WINDOW`), the draft-only restriction on scope edits (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`), the terminated-franchise bar (`ERR_FRANCHISE_TERMINATED`), the open-dispute rules (`ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN`, then `ERR_DISPUTE_OPEN_BLOCKS_SIGNOFF` on resolution), `ERR_ITEM_NOT_REMOVABLE`, and `ERR_INSPECTION_TYPE_NOT_SIGNABLE`.
5. **Optimistic concurrency.** The stale-version checks of §2.7: `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION`, both 412.
6. **Safety and brand-standard compliance.** `ERR_LIFE_SAFETY_PRIORITY_VIOLATION`, `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_PHASE_FOR_ROOM_WORK`, `ERR_OOO_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CRITICAL_DEFECT_NOT_WAIVABLE`, `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_OPEN`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_TOO_LONG_FOR_LIFE_SAFETY`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_BRAND_APPROVED`, `ERR_ROOM_WORK_STILL_IN_PROGRESS`, in that order.
7. **Temporal windows and interval conflicts.** `ERR_INSPECTION_TOO_EARLY`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_PHASE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, `ERR_BOOKING_OUTSIDE_PHASE_WINDOW`, `ERR_PHASE_OUTSIDE_PLAN_HORIZON`, `ERR_NO_BREACH_TO_NOTICE`, `ERR_EXTENSION_AFTER_DEADLINE`, in that order.
8. **Referential coherence and duty separation.** `ERR_PREDECESSOR_PLAN_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_SATISFIED`, `ERR_PREDECESSOR_CYCLE`, `ERR_ITEM_PLAN_MISMATCH`, `ERR_DRAW_ITEM_PLAN_MISMATCH`, `ERR_DRAW_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`, `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_FRANCHISEE`, `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR`, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the sign-off dispute bar `ERR_DISPUTE_OPEN_BLOCKS_SIGNOFF`, in that order. Note the consequence: an owner-side action aimed at a row in the wrong state reports the state failure, not the ownership failure — a start attempted by the wrong franchisee on an item that is not scoped returns `ERR_ITEM_NOT_STARTABLE`, because band 4 precedes band 8.
9. **Aggregate and quota constraints.** `ERR_EXTENSION_ALREADY_PENDING`, `ERR_EXTENSION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_EXTENSION_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_PLAN_HAS_NO_ITEMS`.
10. **Fiscal capacity, checked last.** `ERR_BUDGET_UNDERFUNDED`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_LOCKED`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FUNDABLE`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESERVE`, `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`, `ERR_BUDGET_CLOSED`, in that order.

Two collisions are worth naming explicitly because they are easy to get backwards. First, locking a capital budget that is still in draft breaks both the state rule in band 4 and the funding-gap rule in band 10; the response is `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_LOCKABLE` (409), never `ERR_BUDGET_UNDERFUNDED`. Second, starting a guest-facing item whose sequencing predecessor is still in progress on a plan that also has an overdue life-safety item breaks the safety rule in band 6 and the predecessor rule in band 8; the response is `ERR_LIFE_SAFETY_PRIORITY_VIOLATION` (409), never `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_SATISFIED`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Plan and Property Writes

Three write operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control rather than by a state precondition, because in each case a second write is a perfectly legitimate business act and the only thing that distinguishes a legitimate one from a mistaken one is whether the writer had seen the current state of the parent.

Creating a capital budget — `POST /pip/capital-budgets` — requires a non-optional `expected_plan_version` field in its body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PipPlan`. A new budget legitimately supersedes the plan's prior budget, so no state check blocks a second create; if `expected_plan_version` does not equal the plan's current `version` the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), which is what stops a writer whose view of the plan's scope, cost totals or existing budget is out of date from silently replacing a budget it never saw.

Revising a scoped item — `PATCH /pip/items/{id}` — likewise requires a non-optional `expected_plan_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the item's `PipPlan`. Repeated scope revisions on a draft plan are all legitimate and each one rewrites the plan's cost aggregates, so a mismatch against the plan's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the only thing that prevents a revision computed against superseded aggregates from landing.

Creating a reserve draw — `POST /pip/reserve-draws` — requires a non-optional `expected_property_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Property`. Two draws that each fit inside the available reserve headroom are both legitimate, so no state check rejects the second; a mismatch against the property's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), stopping a writer whose view of the reserve balance, retention floor or outstanding encumbrances is out of date.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none of the three fields is optional on the endpoint that declares it. Clients obtain the values they need from the single-resource reads of the plan and the property, both of which expose `version`.

### 2.8. Money, Ratios and Pinned Rounding Order

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents and are represented in JSON as integers, never as decimal strings or floats. Ratios and percentages are decimal numbers with an explicitly stated scale. Wherever rounding is required the method is **round half up**, and — critically — the *order* of rounding relative to summation is pinned per calculation rather than left to the implementer, because the two orders produce different integers.

The funding requirement rounds **per item first, then sums**: each scoped item's escalated, contingency-loaded figure is rounded half up to whole cents and stored on the item, and the plan's requirement is the sum of those already-rounded item figures. The sum-then-round order is explicitly not used here. Liquidated damages pin the opposite order: the exact per-item products are summed as exact rational values and the **total is rounded once**, at the end. Displacement revenue applies two rounding stages in a fixed sequence: the gross figure is rounded to whole cents first, and only then is the compression-recapture factor applied and the result rounded again.

The scales elsewhere: cost variance as a percentage is carried to 2 decimal places; a phase's out-of-service ratio recorded at activation and an extension request's schedule-pressure ratio are both carried to 4 decimal places; a budget's contingency percentage is accepted with at most 4 decimal places; a property's baseline occupancy is supplied with exactly 2 decimal places; the compliance risk score is an integer clamped to the range 0 through 100; priority ranks are contiguous integers beginning at 1; and key counts and day counts are whole numbers.

Boundary inclusivity is never left to inference. Thresholds stated with "at least" or "no more than" in §3 are inclusive on the stated edge, and the few strict comparisons — an item is overdue only when the clock date is strictly later than its deadline date, a notice may be escalated only when the clock date is strictly later than its cure deadline, a booking interval collides only on strict overlap — are called out at the point of use.

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation and the Two Independent Plan Dimensions

A single request in this domain routinely mutates several rows. The specification describes these as ordered cascade links, and every link is mandatory: an implementation that performs the primary mutation but skips a downstream one is incorrect even though the request appears to succeed. Where a cascade advances a row, it refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments that row's `version`; where the cascade recomputes a derived field, the field must reflect the state as of the end of the request, not the state at its start. Roll-up fields — a franchisee's `portfolio_property_count` and `standing`, a property's `open_plan_count`, `rooms_out_of_service_current`, `compliance_risk_score`, `compliance_posture` and `franchise_agreement_status`, a plan's `scope_item_count`, cost totals and `overdue_item_count`, a budget's `committed_cents`, an inspection's `punch_open_count` — are always recomputed from their sources rather than incrementally patched, so that a cascade arriving from an unexpected direction cannot leave them drifting.

A plan is unusual in carrying **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. Its `status` is the lifecycle dimension, moving through `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `IN_EXECUTION`, `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION` and then one of the terminal values `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Its `dispute_state` is the scope-dispute dimension, taking `NONE`, `DISPUTE_OPEN`, `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_REVISED` or `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_REMOVED`. The two dimensions evolve independently: filing or resolving a dispute never moves `status`, and issuing, acknowledging or completing a plan never moves `dispute_state`. `dispute_state` reads `DISPUTE_OPEN` while any dispute of the plan is still filed, and otherwise records the terminal outcome of the most recently resolved dispute. The one place the dimensions meet is completion sign-off, which is refused while the dispute dimension reads `DISPUTE_OPEN`.

One further structural note: the transition from `IN_EXECUTION` to `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION` is *compound* — the parent's state is a function of the aggregate state of its children rather than of an explicit client request, and it is re-evaluated in both directions. The plan rises to `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION` the moment its surviving items are all finished, and falls back to `IN_EXECUTION` the moment that aggregate condition stops holding.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities: Reserve Cash and Key Inventory

Two physical quantities in this domain are conserved, and every compensating action exists to keep them so.

The first is the property's FF&E reserve cash corpus. The corpus paid in is the property's `initial_reserve_balance_cents` — the reserve balance exactly as supplied at property creation, pinned once and never changed again — plus `reserve_contributed_total_cents`, the cumulative total of every reserve contribution credited since. At every instant, the property's current `reserve_balance_cents` plus the sum of `amount_cents` over all of that property's disbursed draws must equal that corpus. A disbursement moves money from the balance into the outstanding-disbursed set; the compensating reversal moves it back and drops the draw out of that set, so the pair leaves the conserved total exactly unchanged and no cent drifts.

The second is the property's physical key inventory. At every instant, `rooms_out_of_service_current` must equal the sum of `rooms_out_of_service` over the renovation phases of that property's plans that are currently active, and `rooms_out_of_service_current` plus the derived count of sellable keys must equal `room_count`. Activating a phase debits sellable keys and credits keys out of service; closing the phase out and cancelling it are the two compensating actions, and both restore the split exactly — including when the cancellation arrives as part of a termination cascade.

### 2.11. The Append-Only Compliance Ledger

Every economically or contractually significant event appends a `ComplianceLedgerEntry`. The ledger has no create, update or delete surface of its own: entries are minted only as side effects of the transitions that cause them, and they are read back through a single-entry read and a filtered list. Each entry carries the `plan_id` it belongs to, an `item_id` naming the scoped item it concerns (null for plan-level events), an `entry_type` drawn from `SCOPE_ISSUED`, `COST_REVISED`, `ITEM_REMOVED`, `EXTENSION_GRANTED`, `DRAW_DISBURSED`, `DRAW_REVERSED`, `PUNCH_WAIVED`, `DEFAULT_ISSUED`, `DEFAULT_CURED`, `PLAN_COMPLETED` and `PLAN_TERMINATED`, a signed `delta_cents` carrying the cash or cost movement the event represents (0 for events with no monetary effect), a human-readable `memo` minted by the triggering transition, and `recorded_at_clock`, the logical-clock instant at which the entry was appended. Because cascade-appended entries are never returned in the body of the request that caused them, the filtered list is the only channel through which they can be observed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding the Portfolio: Franchisees, Properties and the Reserve Position

Everything begins with a franchisee. A `BrandManager` registers one by supplying its registered `legal_name` — between 1 and 200 characters — and a `contact_email` that must contain an `@` and likewise run between 1 and 200 characters; this address is the notice address for plan issuance and for notices of default. The account is created with `portfolio_property_count` at 0 and `standing` at `GOOD`. Both of those are computed, never supplied: `portfolio_property_count` is the count of properties whose `franchisee_id` names this account, and `standing` is a portfolio-wide roll-up described at the end of §3.9. The `id` the system mints for this account is the value the hotel owner will present in `X-User-ID`.

A property is then registered against that account, again by a `BrandManager`, with `franchisee_id` naming the owning account, a `brand_code` of 1 to 20 characters identifying the brand flag it operates under, a trade `name` of 1 to 200 characters, a `room_count` of at least 1 key, an `adr_baseline_cents` of at least 0 giving the baseline average daily rate in cents, an `occupancy_baseline_pct` between 0 and 1 inclusive expressed as a decimal fraction with exactly 2 decimal places, a `reserve_balance_cents` of at least 0 giving the current FF&E reserve cash position, and a `minimum_retention_cents` of at least 0 giving the contractual floor the reserve may never be drawn below. Any numeric bound breached here yields `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).

Creation pins several system values at once. `initial_reserve_balance_cents` is set to the supplied `reserve_balance_cents` and becomes the immutable anchor of the reserve-corpus invariant; `reserve_contributed_total_cents` starts at 0; `rooms_out_of_service_current` starts at 0; `franchise_agreement_status` starts at `ACTIVE`; `compliance_risk_score` starts at 100 and `compliance_posture` at `COMPLIANT`; `open_plan_count` starts at 0. The owning franchisee's `portfolio_property_count` increments by one.

Two ongoing operations touch a registered property. The brand may correct a property's commercial baselines and retention floor, supplying any of `adr_baseline_cents`, `occupancy_baseline_pct`, `minimum_retention_cents` or `room_count`; the correction increments the property's `version` and does **not** retroactively rewrite displacement figures already computed on phases that are active or closed out, though phases activated afterwards read the new baselines and therefore produce a different figure than their create-time projection. Separately, the owner credits a monthly reserve contribution by supplying `amount_cents`; the contribution adds that amount to both `reserve_balance_cents` and `reserve_contributed_total_cents`, so the reserve corpus of §2.10 still balances, and increments the property's `version`.

Contractors are portfolio-level too, and are registered by a `BrandManager` with a `name` of 1 to 200 characters, a `trade` drawn from `FIRE_PROTECTION`, `FF_AND_E`, `MEP` and `GENERAL`, an optional `brand_approved` flag recording whether the brand has vetted the firm (default `false`), and an optional `max_concurrent_bookings` of at least 1 stating how many booked engagements the firm may have occupying any single instant (default 1).

### 3.2. Scoping and Issuing a Plan

A `BrandManager` scopes a plan by naming the `property_id` it is issued against, a `trigger_type` drawn from `POST_INSPECTION`, `TRANSACTION_CHANGE_OF_OWNERSHIP` and `RELICENSING`, and a `standards_edition` of 1 to 40 characters pinning the brand standards manual edition against which the plan's clause references are read. The plan is born with `status` at `DRAFT` and `dispute_state` at `NONE`, with `issued_by_user_id` and `issued_at` null, with all three category deadline dates null, and with every cost aggregate at 0. A plan may not be scoped against a property whose `franchise_agreement_status` is `TERMINATED`; that attempt, like item creation, budget creation, phase creation and draw creation on such a property, is refused with `ERR_FRANCHISE_TERMINATED`.

Scope is then filled in item by item, and only while the plan is `DRAFT` — an attempt to add or revise an item after issuance is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`. Each scoped item names its `plan_id`, a `category` drawn from `LIFE_SAFETY`, `GUEST_FACING` and `BACK_OF_HOUSE`, a `standards_reference` of 1 to 40 characters naming the clause in the plan's pinned edition, a `brand_estimated_cost_cents` of at least 0, and a `room_impacting` boolean stating whether the work requires taking keys out of service. Two optional fields complete it: `rooms_affected_count`, the number of keys the work takes out of service while it runs, and `predecessor_item_id`, a sequencing predecessor. The room fields must agree — `rooms_affected_count` must be 0 when `room_impacting` is false and at least 1 when `room_impacting` is true, and a mismatch is `ERR_ROOM_IMPACT_MISMATCH` (422). A supplied `predecessor_item_id` must reference an item of the *same* plan (`ERR_PREDECESSOR_PLAN_MISMATCH`, 422), and must neither point at the item itself nor close a cycle anywhere in the sequencing chain (`ERR_PREDECESSOR_CYCLE`, 422). A new item is created with `status` `SCOPED`, with `approved_cost_cents` equal to its `brand_estimated_cost_cents`, with `owner_claimed_cost_cents` null, with `extension_days_granted` at 0, with `item_funding_cents` at 0, and with `deadline_date`, `priority_rank` and `is_overdue` left null, null and false respectively for as long as the plan remains `DRAFT`. Creating an item recomputes the plan's `scope_item_count` and both cost totals and increments the plan's `version`.

Revising a draft item is the one operation guarded by `expected_plan_version` on the item side (§2.7). Alongside that mandatory field the revision may carry any of `brand_estimated_cost_cents`, `standards_reference`, `category`, `room_impacting`, `rooms_affected_count` and `predecessor_item_id`. A revision rewrites the supplied fields, re-derives `approved_cost_cents` from the new `brand_estimated_cost_cents`, recomputes `scope_item_count`, both cost totals and the plan's funding requirement, and increments both the item's `version` and the plan's `version`. The same room-impact agreement rule, the same-plan predecessor rule and the cycle rule are all re-checked on revision.

**The two cost totals.** A plan carries the brand's figure and the owner's figure side by side, and they are computed over the same item set but from different columns. The included set is every item of the plan whose `status` is neither `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` nor `VOIDED`. The **brand-scoped variant**, published as `brand_scoped_total_cents`, always reads `brand_estimated_cost_cents` and ignores every owner claim; it is the sole input to the funding maths. The **owner-claimed variant**, published as `owner_claimed_total_cents`, substitutes `owner_claimed_cost_cents` for any item on which a dispute has been filed and falls back to `brand_estimated_cost_cents` for the rest; it exists for dispute reporting only and never feeds the funding maths. The two variants are identical until the first dispute exists and diverge the instant one does. From them, `variance_cents` is the owner-claimed total minus the brand-scoped total, and the variance as a percentage is that variance multiplied by 100 and divided by the brand-scoped total, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — or exactly `0.00` when the brand-scoped total is 0. An empty included set yields 0 for both totals, 0 variance and `0.00` for the variance percentage.

**Issuance.** Issuing the plan is the brand's act of freezing the scope and starting the clock, and it is refused unless the plan is `DRAFT` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_ISSUABLE`), unless at least one item of the plan is in `SCOPED` (`ERR_PLAN_HAS_NO_ITEMS`), and unless the property's franchise agreement is not terminated. Issuance advances `DRAFT → ISSUED` and performs the following in one transaction. It stamps `issued_by_user_id` with the acting operator's `X-User-ID` — the id thereby barred from certifying this plan's completion — and `issued_at` with the logical clock. It stamps the three category deadlines from the date of `issued_at`: `life_safety_deadline_date` 90 calendar days out, `guest_facing_deadline_date` 270 calendar days out, and `back_of_house_deadline_date` 450 calendar days out. It then sets `deadline_date` on every item of the plan to the deadline of that item's own category — life-safety items to the 90-day date, guest-facing items to the 270-day date, back-of-house items to the 450-day date — plus that item's `extension_days_granted`, which is 0 at this point. It runs the priority ranking described next and writes `priority_rank` on every item that is not removed or voided. It recomputes `brand_scoped_total_cents`, `owner_claimed_total_cents`, `scope_item_count`, `funding_requirement_cents` and `funding_gap_cents`, the last of which is 0 while no active capital budget exists. It appends a ledger entry of type `SCOPE_ISSUED` with a null item reference and a `delta_cents` equal to the plan's brand-scoped total. Finally it recomputes the property's `open_plan_count`, `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture`, then the franchisee's `standing`, and increments the plan's `version` by one.

**Priority ranking.** The ranking is recomputed from scratch — never patched incrementally — over the candidate set of items whose `status` is neither `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` nor `VOIDED`. Candidates are ordered by three keys in strict succession: first by category, with life safety ordering before guest facing and guest facing before back of house; then, within a category, by `approved_cost_cents` descending, so the most expensive work in a band ranks first; then, for items still tied, by `created_at` ascending. Never by `id`. The ordered candidates receive `priority_rank` 1, 2, 3 and so on — contiguous integers with no gaps. Items outside the candidate set keep whatever rank they last held and are ignored by everything downstream. The ranking re-runs whenever an item's `approved_cost_cents` changes and whenever an item leaves the candidate set.

**Overdue.** An item is overdue when three things hold together: its `deadline_date` is not null, the clock date is strictly later than that `deadline_date`, and its `status` is none of `WORK_COMPLETE`, `ACCEPTED`, `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` or `VOIDED`. The comparison is strict, so an item is not overdue on its deadline date itself — it becomes overdue on the following day. Items of a draft plan have a null `deadline_date` and are never overdue. The plan's `overdue_item_count` is the count of its items whose overdue flag is true.

**Withdrawal.** The brand may withdraw a plan while its `status` is either `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`; from any other state the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`. Both source states behave identically: `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN` and `ISSUED → WITHDRAWN` each void every item of the plan whose `status` is `SCOPED` or `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION`, recompute the property's `open_plan_count`, `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` and then the franchisee's `standing`, and increment the plan's `version` by one.

**Acknowledgement.** The owner's contractual receipt moves the plan `ISSUED → OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED` and opens the execution window. It is refused from any other state with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGEABLE`, and refused to any `X-User-ID` other than the governing property's `franchisee_id` with `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_FRANCHISEE`. Acknowledgement recomputes nothing — no cost figure and no deadline moves — and simply increments the plan's `version` by one.

### 3.3. Capital Authorisation and the Funding Requirement

Before any work may start, the owner must authorise capital and the brand must consent to it. A `FranchiseeOwner` creates a capital budget naming the `plan_id` it funds, a `total_authorized_cents` of at least 1, a `contingency_pct` between 0 and 0.50 inclusive carrying at most 4 decimal places, a `financing_source` drawn from `RESERVE_DRAW`, `TERM_LOAN`, `OWNER_EQUITY` and `MIXED`, and the mandatory `expected_plan_version` of §2.7. A contingency outside that range, or one carrying more than four decimal places, is refused with `ERR_CONTINGENCY_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). The budget is created in `DRAFT` with `committed_cents` at 0 and `approved_by_user_id` null.

Because a plan may be re-budgeted, creation supersedes. Any existing budget of the same plan whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `APPROVED`, `LOCKED` or `UNDERFUNDED` becomes `SUPERSEDED` and stops being the plan's active budget; at most one active budget therefore exists per plan at any instant. All four source states behave alike in becoming `SUPERSEDED`, but the practical consequences differ and are worth stating separately: superseding a `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` budget simply discards an authorisation that was never in force; superseding a `LOCKED` budget removes the gate every item start reads, so no further item of that plan may start until the replacement budget is itself locked; and superseding an `UNDERFUNDED` budget is the intended remedy for a funding gap, the replacement carrying the higher authorisation that closes it. Creation then recomputes the plan's funding requirement using the new `contingency_pct`, recomputes `funding_gap_cents`, and increments the plan's `version` by one.

**The funding requirement.** The requirement is computed from the brand's figures alone, item by item, and the rounding order is pinned. First the applicable contingency is chosen: if the plan has an active budget — one whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `APPROVED`, `LOCKED` or `UNDERFUNDED` — the contingency is that budget's `contingency_pct`; if the plan has no active budget, the contingency is a default of ten percent, that is, 0.10. Then, for each included item (again, every item whose `status` is neither `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` nor `VOIDED`), the item's `approved_cost_cents` is multiplied by one plus the contingency and then multiplied by that item's category escalation factor — 1.00 for `LIFE_SAFETY`, 1.075 for `GUEST_FACING`, and 1.035 for `BACK_OF_HOUSE`. That product is rounded half up to whole cents and stored on the item as `item_funding_cents`. Only after every item has been individually rounded are those rounded figures summed, and the sum is the plan's `funding_requirement_cents`. Rounding each item before summing is the specified order; summing the exact products and rounding the total once is not, and produces a different integer.

The gap follows immediately. When the plan has no active budget, `funding_gap_cents` is 0. Otherwise it is the funding requirement minus the active budget's `total_authorized_cents`, floored at 0 — a requirement below the authorisation leaves no gap rather than a negative one. The gap as a percentage, reported by the plan's cost read, expresses `funding_gap_cents` against `funding_requirement_cents` as a percentage rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and is `0.00` when the requirement is 0.

**Approval and locking.** The brand consents to the owner's authorisation, moving the budget `DRAFT → APPROVED` and stamping `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`; the consent is refused unless the budget is in `DRAFT` (`ERR_BUDGET_NOT_APPROVABLE`) and unless the governing plan's `status` is `ISSUED`, `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED` or `IN_EXECUTION` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_EXECUTION_WINDOW`), and increments the budget's `version` by one. The owner then locks the approved budget, moving it `APPROVED → LOCKED`; the lock is refused unless the budget is in `APPROVED` (`ERR_BUDGET_NOT_LOCKABLE`), unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id` (`ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_FRANCHISEE`), and unless the plan's `funding_gap_cents` is exactly 0 (`ERR_BUDGET_UNDERFUNDED`); it increments the budget's `version` by one. Locking is what opens item execution, since a locked active budget is the gate every item start reads. Remember the ordering rule of §2.6 here: a lock attempted on a budget still in `DRAFT` reports `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_LOCKABLE`, not `ERR_BUDGET_UNDERFUNDED`, even when the plan also carries a gap.

Two further transitions arrive without a client request. A locked budget falls `LOCKED → UNDERFUNDED` whenever a cost revision pushes the plan's recomputed `funding_requirement_cents` above the budget's `total_authorized_cents` — that is, whenever the recomputed `funding_gap_cents` becomes strictly positive; the budget's `version` increments, and because a positive gap now exists the property's `compliance_risk_score` is recomputed with the tiered funding-gap penalty and `compliance_posture` is re-derived. And a budget in `APPROVED`, `LOCKED` or `UNDERFUNDED` becomes `CLOSED` when its plan reaches `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED`; all three source states behave identically, and in each case `committed_cents` is frozen at whatever value it then holds. `SUPERSEDED` and `CLOSED` are the two terminal budget states.

### 3.4. Drawing on the FF&E Reserve

Where the financing source is the reserve fund, cash moves out of the property's FF&E reserve one draw at a time, each earmarked to a specific scoped item. The owner creates a draw naming the `property_id` whose reserve is tapped, the `capital_budget_id` the draw is committed to, the `purpose_item_id` naming the scoped item it funds, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, and the mandatory `expected_property_version` of §2.7. Two referential rules bind the three references together: `purpose_item_id` must belong to the same plan as the budget named by `capital_budget_id` (`ERR_DRAW_ITEM_PLAN_MISMATCH`, 422), and `property_id` must be the property of the plan that budget funds (`ERR_DRAW_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`, 422). A draw is created in `REQUESTED`, with `consented_by_user_id` and `disbursed_at` null, and increments the property's `version` by one.

**Two capacity gates at creation.** A requested or consented draw *encumbers* its amount: it is earmarked but not yet paid. The reserve gate therefore works from an availability figure. Encumbered cash is the sum of `amount_cents` over that property's draws whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `BRAND_CONSENTED`. The amount available for a new draw is the property's `reserve_balance_cents` less its `minimum_retention_cents` less that encumbered total, floored at 0. The new `amount_cents` must be no greater than that available figure; a draw that consumes the availability exactly is admitted, and one that exceeds it is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESERVE` (409). This is how two individually affordable draws that would collectively breach the retention floor are caught. Rejected and reversed draws release their encumbrance and raise the availability again; disbursed draws have already left the balance and are not counted as encumbrances.

The second gate is the budget's own headroom. Headroom is the referenced budget's `total_authorized_cents` less its `committed_cents` less the sum of `amount_cents` over the *other* draws against that same budget whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `BRAND_CONSENTED`. The new `amount_cents` must be no greater than that headroom — again with the boundary inclusive — or the create is refused with `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_BUDGET` (409).

**Consent, disbursement and reversal.** The brand records a `decision` on a requested draw. A decision of `CONSENT` moves it `REQUESTED → BRAND_CONSENTED` and stamps `consented_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`; the amount stays encumbered against the reserve. A decision of `REJECT` moves it `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, stamps `consented_by_user_id` in the same way, and releases the encumbrance, raising the availability for the next draw. Either decision is refused unless the draw is in `REQUESTED` (`ERR_DRAW_NOT_DECIDABLE`); consent additionally requires the draw's budget to be in `APPROVED`, `LOCKED` or `UNDERFUNDED` (`ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FUNDABLE`).

The owner then disburses a consented draw, moving it `BRAND_CONSENTED → DISBURSED`. The disbursement is refused unless the draw is in `BRAND_CONSENTED` (`ERR_DRAW_NOT_DISBURSABLE`), unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id` (`ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_FRANCHISEE`), and unless the property's `reserve_balance_cents` less the draw's `amount_cents` is at least the property's `minimum_retention_cents` — the retention floor is re-checked at disbursement time, not merely at creation, and a shortfall is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESERVE`. On success the draw's `amount_cents` is subtracted from `reserve_balance_cents` and the property's `version` increments; `disbursed_at` is stamped with the logical clock; the funding budget's `committed_cents` increases by the same amount; and a ledger entry of type `DRAW_DISBURSED` is appended, carrying the draw's `purpose_item_id` as its item reference and the draw amount as a positive `delta_cents`.

Reversal is the compensating action for a disbursement booked in error, moving the draw `DISBURSED → REVERSED`. It is refused unless the draw is in `DISBURSED` (`ERR_DRAW_NOT_REVERSIBLE`), unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id`, and unless the funding budget is not `CLOSED` (`ERR_BUDGET_CLOSED`) — once plan completion or termination has closed the budget, the disbursement can no longer be unwound. Reversal adds the `amount_cents` back to `reserve_balance_cents` and increments the property's `version`, subtracts the same amount from the budget's `committed_cents`, and appends a `DRAW_REVERSED` ledger entry carrying the `purpose_item_id` and the draw amount negated as `delta_cents`. After the disbursement-and-reversal pair, the reserve corpus of §2.10 balances exactly as it did before. `REJECTED` and `REVERSED` are the terminal draw states.

### 3.5. Phased Room Closures and Contractor Crews

Room-impacting work happens inside scheduled closure windows. The owner schedules a phase naming the `plan_id` whose closures it covers, a `phase_number` of at least 1 that sequences the phases within the plan, a `start_date` and `end_date` as ISO dates bounding the closure inclusively at both ends, and a `rooms_out_of_service` count of at least 1 giving the keys held out of service for the whole closed interval. The phase is created in `PLANNED` with `ooo_pct_at_activation` null.

Four checks guard creation. The dates must parse and `end_date` must be no earlier than `start_date`, or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (422). The `phase_number` must be unique among the phases of the same plan whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` — a cancelled phase releases its number — and a collision is `ERR_PHASE_NUMBER_DUPLICATE` (409). For a plan that has already been issued, the new phase's `end_date` may not fall after the plan's `back_of_house_deadline_date`, or the request fails with `ERR_PHASE_OUTSIDE_PLAN_HORIZON` (422); for a plan still in `DRAFT`, which has no deadlines yet, this check is skipped entirely.

**The closure calendar.** The plan's closure calendar has a capacity of exactly one phase, and each phase's closed date interval is expanded by a mandatory two-day changeover buffer on its **end** side. Two phases of the same plan conflict when the new phase's `start_date` is no later than the other phase's `end_date` advanced by two days *and* the other phase's `start_date` is no later than the new phase's `end_date` advanced by two days; both conditions must hold for a conflict. The conflict is evaluated against every other phase of the same plan whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE`; phases that are `CLOSED_OUT` or `CANCELLED` free their window. A conflict is refused with `ERR_PHASE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Concretely, a phase ending `2026-09-10` admits a successor starting `2026-09-13` but rejects one starting `2026-09-12` — adjacency is forbidden here, unlike the contractor rule below.

**The out-of-service cap.** No more than a quarter of a property's keys may be out of service at once. At phase creation, the property's current `rooms_out_of_service_current` plus the new phase's `rooms_out_of_service`, divided by the property's `room_count`, must be no greater than 0.25; the comparison is inclusive, so a ratio of exactly 0.25 is admitted and only a ratio strictly greater than 0.25 is refused with `ERR_OOO_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). The identical ratio is re-evaluated at activation against the property's *then-current* `rooms_out_of_service_current`, not against the position that held at creation — a phase that fitted when it was scheduled can therefore be refused activation because another phase went active in the meantime.

**Displacement revenue.** Every phase carries a projection of the revenue its closure displaces, computed at creation against the property's baselines as they then stand and recomputed at activation against the baselines as they stand at that moment. The number of closed nights is the count of days from `start_date` to `end_date` inclusive — the difference between the two dates plus one. Displaced room nights are the phase's `rooms_out_of_service` multiplied by that night count. The phase's own out-of-service ratio, for this purpose, is its `rooms_out_of_service` divided by the property's `room_count`, taken as an exact ratio. A compression-recapture allowance recognises that a partially closed hotel sells some of the displaced demand into its remaining keys: the recapture is 30 percent, that is 0.30, when the ratio is below 0.10; 15 percent, that is 0.15, when the ratio is at least 0.10 and below 0.25; and zero when the ratio is 0.25 or above. Note that tier selection is inclusive on the lower edge of each band, so a ratio of exactly 0.25 lands in the zero-recapture band even though the cap rule above still admits it. The gross displacement is the displaced room nights multiplied by the property's `adr_baseline_cents` and by its `occupancy_baseline_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — that is the first rounding. The phase's `displacement_revenue_impact_cents` is that rounded gross figure multiplied by one minus the recapture fraction, rounded half up to whole cents again — the second rounding. The order is pinned: round the gross first, then apply the recapture and round again.

**Activation and close-out.** Activating a phase moves it `PLANNED → ACTIVE` and is refused unless the phase is in `PLANNED` (`ERR_PHASE_NOT_ACTIVATABLE`), unless the governing plan's `status` is `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED` or `IN_EXECUTION` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_EXECUTION_WINDOW`), unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id` (`ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_FRANCHISEE`), and unless the re-evaluated out-of-service ratio above still holds (`ERR_OOO_CAP_EXCEEDED`). Activation adds the phase's `rooms_out_of_service` to the property's `rooms_out_of_service_current` and increments the property's `version`, recomputes `displacement_revenue_impact_cents` against the property's current `adr_baseline_cents` and `occupancy_baseline_pct`, and writes `ooo_pct_at_activation` as the phase's own out-of-service ratio rounded half up to 4 decimal places — a field only the activation writes.

Closing a phase out moves it `ACTIVE → CLOSED_OUT` and is refused unless the phase is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_PHASE_NOT_CLOSEABLE`), unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id`, and unless no room-impacting item of the phase's plan is still `IN_PROGRESS` (`ERR_ROOM_WORK_STILL_IN_PROGRESS`) — keys may not return to inventory while work that needs them out of service is running. Close-out subtracts the phase's `rooms_out_of_service` from the property's `rooms_out_of_service_current`, increments the property's `version`, restores the key-inventory invariant of §2.10, and marks every booking of the phase that is still `BOOKED` as `COMPLETED`, freeing that contractor's capacity. Phases also reach `CANCELLED` — from either `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` — when their plan is terminated; §3.9 details that cascade, and in both cases every still-booked engagement of the phase becomes `CANCELLED`, with the difference that a phase cancelled out of `ACTIVE` additionally returns its `rooms_out_of_service` to the property. `CLOSED_OUT` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal phase states.

**Crew bookings.** The owner books a contractor crew onto a phase by naming the `contractor_id`, the `phase_id`, a `start_datetime` and `end_datetime` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, and a `crew_size` of at least 1 worker. The interval is **half-open**: the start instant is occupied and the end instant is not. The dates must parse and `end_datetime` must be strictly later than `start_datetime`, so a zero-length booking is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (422). The interval must sit inside its phase's closure window: `start_datetime` must be at or after the phase's `start_date` at `00:00:00Z`, and `end_datetime` must be at or before the day after the phase's `end_date` at `00:00:00Z`; otherwise the booking fails with `ERR_BOOKING_OUTSIDE_PHASE_WINDOW` (422).

Contractor capacity is enforced by counting occupants rather than forbidding all overlap. The occupant count is the number of that contractor's bookings still in `BOOKED` whose interval strictly overlaps the requested one — strictly, meaning the new start is earlier than the existing end *and* the existing start is earlier than the new end. No buffer is applied. If that count is already at or above the contractor's `max_concurrent_bookings`, the booking is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409). Two consequences follow directly: a booking starting exactly at another's end instant does not overlap and is always admitted, and a contractor whose `max_concurrent_bookings` is 2 admits a second genuinely overlapping booking but refuses the third. Bookings that are `CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` free their capacity.

One brand-standard rule guards bookings. A contractor whose `trade` is `FIRE_PROTECTION` may be booked only when its `brand_approved` flag is true, and the same vetting requirement extends to *any* contractor booked onto a phase of a plan that still carries a life-safety item in `SCOPED`, `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION` or `IN_PROGRESS`. A booking that breaches either arm is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_BRAND_APPROVED` (409). A booking is created in `BOOKED`; `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` are reached only through the phase cascades above, which means the filtered booking list is the only channel through which those cascade-driven states can be observed.

### 3.6. Executing Scoped Work and Moving Deadlines

Starting work on a scoped item is the most heavily guarded action in the domain, and its guards run in the band order of §2.6. The item must be in `SCOPED`, or the request fails with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_STARTABLE`. The governing plan's `status` must be `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED` or `IN_EXECUTION`, or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_IN_EXECUTION_WINDOW`. The plan's active capital budget must exist and be `LOCKED`, or `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_LOCKED` — a budget never locked, one superseded by a replacement, or one knocked to `UNDERFUNDED` by a cost revision all fail this gate identically. If the item is not itself a life-safety item, no life-safety item of the same plan may be overdue, or the start is refused with `ERR_LIFE_SAFETY_PRIORITY_VIOLATION`: the brand will not let cosmetic work proceed while a fire-safety deficiency is past its date. If the item carries a `predecessor_item_id`, that predecessor's `status` must be `WORK_COMPLETE`, `ACCEPTED` or `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE`, or the start is refused with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_SATISFIED`; an item with a null predecessor clears this check trivially. If the item's `room_impacting` flag is true, some renovation phase of the same plan must be `ACTIVE`, must cover the clock date inside its closed interval — start date at or before the clock date, clock date at or before end date — and must hold `rooms_out_of_service` at least equal to the item's `rooms_affected_count`, or the start is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_PHASE_FOR_ROOM_WORK`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must be the property's `franchisee_id`, or `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_FRANCHISEE`.

A successful start cascades in three links. The item moves `SCOPED → IN_PROGRESS` and its `version` increments. Because the item left `SCOPED`, if the plan's `status` was `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED` the plan advances `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED → IN_EXECUTION` and the plan's `version` increments — this is a system transition with no client request of its own, fired by the first item of the plan to leave `SCOPED`. And because the plan's status changed, the property's `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed, and then the franchisee's `standing`.

Reporting the work finished moves the item `IN_PROGRESS → WORK_COMPLETE`; it is refused unless the item is `IN_PROGRESS` (`ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`) and unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id`. The item's `version` increments and its overdue flag is recomputed to false, since a completed item is by definition no longer overdue. The plan then re-evaluates the compound readiness condition described below, and the property's `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed, followed by the franchisee's `standing`.

**Compound plan readiness.** A plan rises `IN_EXECUTION → READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION` when two things hold: its `scope_item_count` is at least 1, and every item of the plan whose `status` is neither `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` nor `VOIDED` is in `WORK_COMPLETE` or `ACCEPTED`. No client request drives it; the condition is re-evaluated after an item's work is reported complete and after a punch-list defect is verified, and the plan's `version` increments when the transition fires. The reverse edge exists for the same reason: when a major or critical defect knocks an item back out of `WORK_COMPLETE`, the aggregate condition stops holding and the plan falls `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION → IN_EXECUTION`, incrementing its `version` again.

**Requesting more time.** The owner may ask the brand to move an item's deadline, supplying the `item_id`, a `requested_days` count of at least 1 additional calendar day, and a `business_justification`. The justification must be at least 40 characters after trimming, or the request is refused with `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Timing matters: an extension may be requested only while the clock date is at or before the item's `deadline_date`; once the item is already overdue its deadline can no longer be moved, and the request is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_AFTER_DEADLINE` (409). Only one request may be in flight per item — if the item already has a request in `SUBMITTED` or `NEEDS_MORE_JUSTIFICATION`, the new one is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_ALREADY_PENDING` (409). A new request is created in `SUBMITTED` with `round_number` 1, `granted_days` 0, `pressure_ratio_at_decision` null, `decided_by_user_id` null and `decision_note` null. Creating it also moves the charged item `SCOPED → DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION` and increments the item's `version`; crucially, the item's `deadline_date` does **not** change while the request is pending.

**The brand's decision.** A `BrandManager` records a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `REQUEST_MORE_JUSTIFICATION`, optionally with a `granted_days` override and a `decision_note` recording the rationale; the decision is refused unless the request is in `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_DECIDABLE`). A decision of `REQUEST_MORE_JUSTIFICATION` moves the request `SUBMITTED → NEEDS_MORE_JUSTIFICATION`, stores `decision_note` and `decided_by_user_id`, and leaves the charged item in `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION`; it is available only while `round_number` is at most 3. The owner may then strengthen the request, moving it `NEEDS_MORE_JUSTIFICATION → SUBMITTED` with a replacement `requested_days` and `business_justification`; the resubmission is refused unless the request is in `NEEDS_MORE_JUSTIFICATION` (`ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE`), unless `round_number` is still strictly below 3 (`ERR_EXTENSION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, 409), unless the replacement `business_justification` is at least 40 characters (`ERR_JUSTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`), and unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id`. A successful resubmission increments `round_number` by one and replaces both the justification and the day count. This is the bounded revision loop: `SUBMITTED → NEEDS_MORE_JUSTIFICATION → SUBMITTED`, capped at three rounds.

**How many days are actually granted.** On `APPROVE` the grant is clamped by a six-step calculation whose evaluation order is pinned, and it is the *server* — not the brand — that decides between `APPROVED` and `APPROVED_PARTIAL`: the resulting status simply records whether the clamp bit. The order matters because granting days moves deadline dates, and deadline dates are what the schedule-pressure measurement reads; step 1 deliberately reads the deadlines as they stand *before* this grant.

The effective request is the body's `granted_days` when the brand supplied one, and otherwise the request's own `requested_days`. Step 1 measures schedule pressure across the item's plan. The numerator is the count of the plan's items whose `status` is `SCOPED`, `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose `deadline_date` falls at or before the clock date advanced by 60 days. The denominator is the count of the plan's items whose `status` is none of `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE`, `VOIDED` or `ACCEPTED`. The pressure ratio is the numerator divided by the denominator rounded half up to 4 decimal places, or exactly `0.0000` when the denominator is 0. The requesting item itself is counted in both figures while it sits in `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION`. Step 2 derives a multiplier: 0.5 when the pressure ratio is at least 0.60, and 1.0 otherwise. Step 3 takes the item's category maximum — 30 days for `LIFE_SAFETY`, 120 days for `GUEST_FACING`, 180 days for `BACK_OF_HOUSE` — multiplies it by that multiplier, and rounds the product **down** to a whole number of days, giving the raw cap. Step 4 computes the days remaining in the item's category budget as that same category maximum less the item's `extension_days_granted` accumulated so far. Step 5 takes the smallest of three figures: the effective request, the raw cap, and the remaining days. Step 6 branches on that result: if it is zero or negative the approval is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (409) — a request whose category budget is already consumed, or whose pressure clamp leaves no room, is refused rather than granted zero days; if it equals the effective request the request becomes `APPROVED`; otherwise the request becomes `APPROVED_PARTIAL`. Step 7 then commits: `pressure_ratio_at_decision` is stored as the ratio measured in step 1, the granted days are added to the item's `extension_days_granted`, and the item's `deadline_date` is rewritten as its base category deadline plus the new accumulated `extension_days_granted`. Only now do deadlines move, so the next request's step 1 sees the new value.

Both `APPROVED` and `APPROVED_PARTIAL` do exactly the same work, differing only in the clamped day count they carry: each stores `granted_days`, `pressure_ratio_at_decision`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note`; each adds the granted days to the item's `extension_days_granted` and moves its `deadline_date` out by the same number of days; each returns the item `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION → SCOPED`; each appends a ledger entry of type `EXTENSION_GRANTED` carrying the item's id and a `delta_cents` of 0; and each recomputes the item's overdue flag, the plan's `overdue_item_count`, the property's `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture`, and then the franchisee's `standing`. A `DENY` moves the request `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, sets `granted_days` to 0, stores `pressure_ratio_at_decision`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note`, and returns the item `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION → SCOPED` with `extension_days_granted` and `deadline_date` unchanged. `APPROVED`, `APPROVED_PARTIAL` and `DENIED` are the terminal request states; a decision of `REQUEST_MORE_JUSTIFICATION` alone leaves the item deferred.

### 3.7. Contesting Scope and Cost

An owner who believes a scoped item is unreasonable, misapplied, or mispriced files a dispute naming the `plan_id`, the contested `item_id`, a `dispute_basis` drawn from `SCOPE_REASONABLENESS`, `COST_ESTIMATE` and `STANDARDS_APPLICABILITY`, and a `claimed_cost_cents` of at least 0 giving the owner's own figure for the item. The contested `item_id` must belong to the supplied `plan_id`, or the filing fails with `ERR_ITEM_PLAN_MISMATCH` (422). Only one dispute may be live per item: if another dispute on the same `item_id` is still in `FILED`, the filing is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_OPEN` (409). The dispute is created in `FILED` with `filed_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and with `revised_cost_cents` and `panel_decision_note` null.

Filing has three effects beyond the dispute row itself. The plan's `dispute_state` moves to `DISPUTE_OPEN` while its `status` is left completely untouched. The contested item's `owner_claimed_cost_cents` is set to the dispute's `claimed_cost_cents`, and the plan's `owner_claimed_total_cents` is recomputed, which is the moment the owner-claimed variant diverges from the brand-scoped one. And the plan's `version` increments by one. Filing works identically from every prior value of the dispute dimension: a plan whose `dispute_state` was `NONE`, `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_REVISED` or `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_REMOVED` all return to `DISPUTE_OPEN` for the newly filed dispute, each leaving `status` unchanged and each incrementing the plan's `version` by one.

**Resolution.** A `BrandManager` resolves a filed dispute with a `resolution` of `UPHOLD`, `REVISE` or `REMOVE`, optionally recording a `panel_decision_note`. Resolution is refused unless the dispute is in `FILED` (`ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_RESOLVABLE`), and refused while the plan has any *other* dispute still in `FILED` (`ERR_DISPUTE_OPEN_BLOCKS_SIGNOFF`, 409) — the plan's dispute dimension cannot record a single terminal outcome while a second contest is live. Whichever branch is taken, the plan's `version` increments by one. The `revised_cost_cents` field is branch-bound: it must be present and at least 0 when the resolution is `REVISE`, failing with `ERR_REVISION_COST_REQUIRED` (422) otherwise, and it must be absent when the resolution is `UPHOLD` or `REMOVE`, failing with `ERR_REVISION_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_RESOLUTION` (422) if supplied.

A dispute's three resolved values — `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `RESOLVED_REVISED` and `RESOLVED_REMOVED` — are all terminal; a resolved dispute is never reopened, and a fresh contest requires a new filing.

`UPHOLD` moves the dispute from `FILED` to `RESOLVED_UPHELD` and sets the plan's `dispute_state` to `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_UPHELD`, leaving `status` untouched. No cost moves: the item's `approved_cost_cents` stays at its `brand_estimated_cost_cents`.

`REVISE` moves the dispute from `FILED` to `RESOLVED_REVISED` and drives the deepest cascade in the domain, in five ordered links. Link one stores `revised_cost_cents` on the dispute and sets the contested item's `approved_cost_cents` to that value. Link two follows from the item's cost having changed: the plan's `funding_requirement_cents` is recomputed by the per-item-then-sum rule of §3.3, the priority ranking re-runs and rewrites `priority_rank` on every item of the plan, and the plan's `dispute_state` becomes `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_REVISED` with `status` untouched. Link three follows from the requirement having changed: `funding_gap_cents` is recomputed, and if the gap has become strictly positive the plan's active capital budget moves `LOCKED → UNDERFUNDED`, which silently closes the item-start gate until a replacement budget is authorised and locked. Link four follows from the budget having gone `UNDERFUNDED`: the property's `compliance_risk_score` is recomputed with the tiered funding-gap penalty and its `compliance_posture` is re-derived. Link five follows from the posture having changed: the franchisee's `standing` is recomputed from the postures of all of that franchisee's properties. A ledger entry of type `COST_REVISED` is appended, carrying the disputed `item_id` and a `delta_cents` equal to the new revised cost less the item's previous `approved_cost_cents` — negative when the brand revises downward.

`REMOVE` moves the dispute from `FILED` to `RESOLVED_REMOVED` and takes the item out of scope entirely. It is available only while the contested item's `status` is `SCOPED`, `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION` or `IN_PROGRESS`; from any other state the resolution is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_REMOVABLE` (409). All three admissible source states behave identically: the item becomes `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` and thereby leaves every plan aggregate — `scope_item_count`, `brand_scoped_total_cents`, `owner_claimed_total_cents`, `funding_requirement_cents` and `funding_gap_cents` are all recomputed over the surviving items, and `priority_rank` is re-assigned across those survivors. The plan's `dispute_state` becomes `DISPUTE_RESOLVED_REMOVED` with `status` untouched, and a ledger entry of type `ITEM_REMOVED` is appended carrying the item's id and a `delta_cents` equal to the item's `approved_cost_cents` negated. `ACCEPTED`, `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` and `VOIDED` are the terminal item states.

The item's `approved_cost_cents` is thus always derivable: it is the `revised_cost_cents` of the most recent dispute on that item resolved as `REVISE`, and otherwise the item's `brand_estimated_cost_cents`. Its `owner_claimed_cost_cents` is the `claimed_cost_cents` of the most recently filed dispute on that item, and null while no dispute has ever been filed.

### 3.8. Completion Inspection, Punch Lists and Sign-Off

A `BrandManager` schedules a brand inspection against a plan, naming the `plan_id`, an `inspection_type` drawn from `SCOPING`, `PROGRESS`, `COMPLETION` and `RE_INSPECTION`, an `inspector_user_id` of 1 to 64 characters naming the field inspector assigned to the visit, and a `scheduled_date` as an ISO date. The inspection is created in `SCHEDULED` with `punch_open_count` 0 and `performed_at`, `signed_off_at` and `signed_off_by_user_id` all null. The assigned `inspector_user_id` is the *only* `X-User-ID` that may act on this inspection.

The assigned inspector records the visit as performed, moving it `SCHEDULED → PERFORMED`. The action is refused unless the inspection is in `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PERFORMABLE`), unless the acting `X-User-ID` equals the inspection's `inspector_user_id` (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR`), and unless the clock date is at or after the inspection's `scheduled_date` (`ERR_INSPECTION_TOO_EARLY`) — a visit may be recorded on its scheduled date or later, never before. Performing stamps `performed_at` with the logical clock and recomputes `punch_open_count`, which is 0 until defects are filed.

**Punch-list defects.** The inspector raises a defect naming the `inspection_id` that found it, the `pip_item_id` it is charged against, a `defect_note` of 1 to 500 characters describing it, and a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`. The charged `pip_item_id` must belong to the plan of the named inspection, or the filing fails with `ERR_ITEM_PLAN_MISMATCH` (422). The defect is created in `OPEN` with `verification_note` null and with `cure_deadline_date` set to the clock date advanced by 14 days. The raising inspection's `punch_open_count` increases by one, and the property's `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed.

Severity decides whether the defect reopens work. A `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` defect knocks the charged item `WORK_COMPLETE → PUNCH_FAIL`, increments that item's `version`, and — because the plan's aggregate readiness condition no longer holds — reverts the plan `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION → IN_EXECUTION`, incrementing the plan's `version`. The property's `compliance_risk_score` is recomputed with the open-defect penalties. A `MINOR` defect does neither: it is recorded, it counts toward `punch_open_count`, and the charged item stays in `WORK_COMPLETE`.

The owner reports a defect fixed, moving it `OPEN → REMEDIATED`; this is refused unless the defect is `OPEN` (`ERR_PUNCH_NOT_REMEDIABLE`) and unless the acting `X-User-ID` is the property's `franchisee_id`. Remediation is a claim, not a closure: the defect still counts toward `punch_open_count` and still carries its risk-score penalty until the inspector acts.

The assigned inspector then records an `outcome` of `CLOSE`, `REOPEN` or `WAIVE`, optionally with a `verification_note`. Any of the three is refused unless the acting `X-User-ID` equals the `inspector_user_id` of the defect's inspection (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR`). `CLOSE` and `REOPEN` additionally require the defect to be in `REMEDIATED` (`ERR_PUNCH_NOT_VERIFIABLE`). `CLOSE` moves the defect `REMEDIATED → VERIFIED_CLOSED` and decreases the inspection's `punch_open_count` by one; if every defect charged to the same item is now `VERIFIED_CLOSED` or `WAIVED`, that item returns `PUNCH_FAIL → WORK_COMPLETE` with its `version` incremented and the plan re-evaluates its compound readiness condition; the property's `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed. `REOPEN` moves the defect `REMEDIATED → OPEN` and leaves `punch_open_count` unchanged; this loop may run any number of times. `WAIVE` is admissible from either `OPEN` or `REMEDIATED` — both source states behave identically — but never for a `CRITICAL` defect, which may only be remediated and verified closed; a waiver attempted on a critical defect is refused with `ERR_CRITICAL_DEFECT_NOT_WAIVABLE` (409). A waiver moves the defect to `WAIVED`, decreases `punch_open_count` by one, appends a ledger entry of type `PUNCH_WAIVED` carrying the defect's `pip_item_id` and a `delta_cents` of 0, and — like a closure — returns the charged item `PUNCH_FAIL → WORK_COMPLETE` if every defect charged to it is now closed or waived. `VERIFIED_CLOSED` and `WAIVED` are the terminal defect states.

**Sign-off.** Certifying completion is the single act that finishes a plan, and it carries seven preconditions. The inspection must be in `PERFORMED`, or `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SIGNABLE`. Its `inspection_type` must be `COMPLETION`, or `ERR_INSPECTION_TYPE_NOT_SIGNABLE` — `SCOPING`, `PROGRESS` and `RE_INSPECTION` visits stop permanently at `PERFORMED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the inspection's `inspector_user_id`, or `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the plan's `issued_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403): the operator who issued a plan can never certify its completion. The plan's `status` must be `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION`, or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_READY_FOR_SIGNOFF`. The plan's `dispute_state` must not be `DISPUTE_OPEN`, or `ERR_DISPUTE_OPEN_BLOCKS_SIGNOFF`. And the inspection's `punch_open_count` must be exactly 0 — no defect of the inspection still `OPEN` or `REMEDIATED` — or `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_OPEN`.

A successful sign-off moves the inspection `PERFORMED → SIGNED_OFF`, stamping `signed_off_at` with the logical clock and `signed_off_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and drives the plan's completion cascade in full. The plan moves `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION → COMPLETED` and its `version` increments. Every item of the plan still in `WORK_COMPLETE` becomes `ACCEPTED`, each with its `version` incremented. Every renovation phase of the plan still `ACTIVE` becomes `CLOSED_OUT`, which returns its `rooms_out_of_service` to the property's `rooms_out_of_service_current` and marks its still-`BOOKED` engagements `COMPLETED`. The plan's active capital budget — whichever of `APPROVED`, `LOCKED` or `UNDERFUNDED` it holds — becomes `CLOSED`, freezing `committed_cents`. A ledger entry of type `PLAN_COMPLETED` is appended with a null item reference and a `delta_cents` of 0. Finally the property's `open_plan_count`, `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed, followed by the franchisee's `standing`. `SIGNED_OFF` is the terminal inspection state.

### 3.9. Risk Scoring, Default and Termination

**The compliance risk score.** Every property carries an integer `compliance_risk_score` between 0 and 100 that summarises how far its open improvement work has slipped. Only plans in the four open lifecycle states contribute: `ISSUED`, `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `IN_EXECUTION` and `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION`. Plans that are `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded entirely, along with all of their items and defects. Within the open plans, the scored items are those whose `status` is neither `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` nor `VOIDED`, and the open defects are the punch-list rows charged to those scored items whose `status` is `OPEN` or `REMEDIATED`.

The score starts at 100 and subtracts five penalty terms. Overdue life-safety items cost 25 points each, but that term is capped at 50 points in total, so a third overdue life-safety item changes nothing. Overdue guest-facing items cost 8 points each and overdue back-of-house items 3 points each, both uncapped. Open `MAJOR` defects cost 5 points each and open `CRITICAL` defects 12 points each; `MINOR` defects carry no penalty at all. The fifth term is the funding-gap penalty, computed across the open plans: the total gap is the sum of `funding_gap_cents` over those plans and the total requirement is the sum of `funding_requirement_cents`; the gap ratio is the total gap divided by the total requirement, or 0 when the total requirement is 0. The penalty is 0 points when the total gap is 0, 10 points when the gap ratio is greater than 0 and no more than 0.10, and 25 points when the gap ratio is strictly greater than 0.10. The five penalties are subtracted from 100 and the result is clamped into the range 0 through 100.

Two variants of that score exist, differing only in what counts as overdue. The **snapshot** variant treats an item as overdue exactly when its own overdue flag is true — that is, strictly past its `deadline_date`. This is the variant written to the property's `compliance_risk_score` by every triggering transition and by the compliance sweep, and it is the only variant that drives `compliance_posture`. The **projected** variant treats an item as overdue when its overdue flag is true *or* when its `status` is `SCOPED` or `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION` and its `deadline_date` falls at or before the clock date advanced by 30 days: unstarted work due inside that horizon is treated as already missed. The projected variant is never persisted; it is returned only by the property's risk read, and because it counts a superset of items it yields a strictly lower figure than the snapshot variant whenever such an item exists.

The property's risk read exposes both variants side by side along with the inputs that produced them: `property_id`, `snapshot_score` and `snapshot_posture`, `projected_score` and `projected_posture`, the `projection_horizon_days` used (30), the snapshot overdue counts per category as `overdue_life_safety_count`, `overdue_guest_facing_count` and `overdue_back_of_house_count`, the projected counts per category as `projected_life_safety_count`, `projected_guest_facing_count` and `projected_back_of_house_count`, then `funding_gap_cents` and the `funding_gap_penalty` derived from it, the open-defect counts `open_major_defect_count` and `open_critical_defect_count`, and the property's `open_plan_count`. Recomputing the score is also what the plan's cost read does for its own figures: `plan_id`, `included_item_count`, `brand_scoped_total_cents`, `owner_claimed_total_cents`, `variance_cents`, `variance_pct`, the `contingency_pct_applied` (the active budget's `contingency_pct`, or the ten-percent default when no active budget exists), `funding_requirement_cents`, the `authorized_cents` taken from the active budget's `total_authorized_cents` (0 when no active budget exists), `funding_gap_cents` and `funding_gap_pct`.

**Posture and standing.** Immediately after every recomputation of the score, the property's `compliance_posture` is re-derived by a rule in which the contractual overrides beat the score bands however high the score is. The posture is `TERMINATED` when the property's `franchise_agreement_status` is `TERMINATED`. Failing that, it is `IN_DEFAULT` when any default notice on any plan of the property is still `ISSUED`. Failing that, it is `AT_RISK` when the `compliance_risk_score` is below 80. Otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. The band boundary is inclusive on the compliant side: a score of exactly 80 is `COMPLIANT` and a score of 79 is `AT_RISK`. The franchisee's `standing` then rolls up across every property it owns: `TERMINATED_PORTFOLIO` when any of its properties is `TERMINATED`, else `DEFAULT` when any is `IN_DEFAULT`, else `WATCH` when any is `AT_RISK`, else `GOOD`.

Because moving the logical clock does not by itself rewrite stored rows, the brand has an explicit sweep for a plan: it re-evaluates the overdue flag on every item of the plan against the current clock, recomputes the plan's `overdue_item_count`, recomputes the property's snapshot `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` and then the franchisee's `standing`, and increments the plan's `version` by one. This is the operator action that turns the passage of logical time into recorded overdue state.

**Notice of default.** When work has slipped, a `BrandManager` issues a notice over the plan, supplying the `plan_id` and a `cure_period_days` count. That count must be between 1 and 60 days inclusive, or the request fails with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). At least one item of the plan must be overdue at the instant of issuance, or the request fails with `ERR_NO_BREACH_TO_NOTICE` (409). And where any of the plan's overdue items is a life-safety item, the cure period is tightened: `cure_period_days` may then not exceed 10 days, and a longer period is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_TOO_LONG_FOR_LIFE_SAFETY` (422).

Issuance creates the notice in `ISSUED` with `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` and `cure_deadline_date` set to the clock date advanced by `cure_period_days`. It snapshots `breached_item_ids` as the ids of every item of the plan whose overdue flag is true at that instant, ordered by `priority_rank` ascending — the snapshot is fixed at issuance and does not follow later scope changes. It computes `liquidated_damages_cents` over exactly those breached items: each item's `approved_cost_cents` is multiplied by its category's damages rate — 0.15 for `LIFE_SAFETY`, 0.08 for `GUEST_FACING`, 0.03 for `BACK_OF_HOUSE` — the exact per-item products are summed, and the total is rounded half up to whole cents **once, at the end**. This is deliberately the opposite order from the funding requirement of §3.3, which rounds per item before summing; the two orders produce different integers and each calculation uses only its own. Items already `WORK_COMPLETE`, `ACCEPTED`, `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` or `VOIDED` are never overdue and so never enter the sum. Issuance also moves the property's `franchise_agreement_status` to `DEFAULT_NOTICED`, its `compliance_posture` to `IN_DEFAULT` and the franchisee's `standing` to `DEFAULT`, and appends a ledger entry of type `DEFAULT_ISSUED`.

**Curing.** The brand records the breach cured, moving the notice `ISSUED → CURED`. The action is refused unless the notice is in `ISSUED` (`ERR_NOTICE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`) and unless *every* item id in `breached_item_ids` now holds a `status` of `WORK_COMPLETE`, `ACCEPTED` or `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE` — a single laggard yields `ERR_BREACH_NOT_CURED` (409). On success the property's `franchise_agreement_status` returns to `ACTIVE`, but only once no other notice of that property remains `ISSUED`; the property's `compliance_risk_score` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed and then the franchisee's `standing`; and a ledger entry of type `DEFAULT_CURED` is appended.

**Escalation to termination.** Where the cure period runs out with work still undone, the brand escalates, moving the notice `ISSUED → ESCALATED_TO_TERMINATION`. Escalation is refused unless the notice is `ISSUED` (`ERR_NOTICE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`), unless the clock date is *strictly* later than the notice's `cure_deadline_date` — on the deadline date itself escalation is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (409) — and unless at least one item id in `breached_item_ids` still holds a `status` outside `WORK_COMPLETE`, `ACCEPTED` and `REMOVED_BY_DISPUTE`, since a fully cured breach cannot be escalated and yields `ERR_BREACH_ALREADY_CURED` (409). `CURED` and `ESCALATED_TO_TERMINATION` are the terminal notice states.

Escalation then drives the termination cascade in five ordered links, and it does so identically from every lifecycle state the plan may hold at that moment — `ISSUED`, `OWNER_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `IN_EXECUTION` and `READY_FOR_COMPLETION_INSPECTION` all terminate the same way. Link one moves the plan's `status` to `TERMINATED` and increments the plan's `version` by one. Link two, caused by link one, voids and cancels the plan's children: every item of the plan whose `status` is `SCOPED`, `DEFERRED_PENDING_EXTENSION`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `WORK_COMPLETE` or `PUNCH_FAIL` becomes `VOIDED` and thereby leaves every plan aggregate; every renovation phase whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `CANCELLED`; and the plan's active capital budget becomes `CLOSED`. Link three, caused by link two, follows each cancelled phase: its still-`BOOKED` engagements become `CANCELLED`, and for any phase that had been `ACTIVE`, its `rooms_out_of_service` is subtracted from the property's `rooms_out_of_service_current`, restoring the key-inventory invariant. Link four, caused by link one, sets the property's `franchise_agreement_status` to `TERMINATED`. Link five, caused by link four, sets the property's `compliance_posture` to `TERMINATED` and the franchisee's `standing` to `TERMINATED_PORTFOLIO`. A ledger entry of type `PLAN_TERMINATED` is appended with a null item reference and a `delta_cents` of 0. Once the property's franchise agreement is terminated, no new plan, item, budget, phase or draw may be created against it.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create a new stand-alone resource return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource — including every state transition, every computed read and the clock control — return `200 OK`. All resource paths are mounted under `/pip`; the clock control sits outside that prefix. Where the Authorized Role(s) column reads "Any", all three of `FranchiseeOwner`, `BrandManager` and `BrandInspector` may call the endpoint.
