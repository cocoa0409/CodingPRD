# Court Interpreter Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state court system cannot lawfully proceed against a limited-English-proficient party without a qualified interpreter in the room, on the video cart, or on the telephone line. Today that obligation is met by a patchwork of spreadsheets: one workbook tracks who has passed which examination, another tracks who is available in which county, a third reconciles mileage claims against a fiscal-year budget, and nobody can answer the question the state's language-access plan actually asks — *what fraction of hearings that needed an interpreter got one, and which languages are failing?* This document specifies the Court Interpreter Program API, a single service that owns the whole chain: credentialing, roster maintenance, calendar-driven assignment, remote-interpreting technology constraints, interpreter compensation, and the complaint-and-discipline process that keeps the roster honest.

The service is deliberately opinionated about two things. First, **credentials are cyclical, not permanent**: a written examination and (where an instrument exists) an oral performance examination earn a tier, mandatory ethics training activates it, and continuing education renews it on a fixed cycle whose lapse silently strips an interpreter off the assignable roster and cancels the work already booked in their name. Second, **money is encumbered before it is spent**: the moment an interpreter accepts an assignment the program reserves a provisional amount against the hosting courthouse's pay authority, and that reservation is either released or converted into an obligation by exactly one downstream event. Both properties mean that a great many of the service's rules are cascades — a single write ripples through three or four entities — and the specification below is explicit about every hop.

Two further concerns shape the design. Rare languages have no certification examination at all, so the program must be able to qualify interpreters provisionally and to fall back on telephonic language-service vendors, while still counting those hearings honestly in its compliance reporting. And because a complaint against an interpreter can end a career, the workflow enforces segregation of duties: the person who files a complaint may not be the person who reviews it, the person who adjudicates it may not be the person who decides its appeal, and no one who touches the schedule may approve the resulting payment.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` names the role that user is acting under. If either header is absent, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the six roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the requested endpoint (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `CredentialingOfficer`, `SchedulingCoordinator`, `FiscalOfficer`, `QualityReviewer`, and `Interpreter`.

- A `ProgramAdmin` is the state program administrator. This role alone owns program configuration (languages, courthouses, rate schedules), credential revocation, complaint adjudication, appeal decisions, the operations sweep, and the logical-clock control endpoint. It also shares roster enrolment and ethics recording with `CredentialingOfficer`, calendar and assignment work with `SchedulingCoordinator`, complaint intake with `QualityReviewer` and `SchedulingCoordinator`, compliance reporting with `QualityReviewer`, and it may perform every read in the system.
- A `CredentialingOfficer` runs the credentialing pipeline: opening credential applications, recording examination attempts, advancing a credential through its stages, and renewing a cycle. This role may not revoke a credential and may not touch the calendar.
- A `SchedulingCoordinator` works the case calendar: creating hearings, continuing them, pulling the ranked eligible-interpreter pool, proposing assignments, cancelling them, and recording no-shows. It may also confirm or complete an assignment on an interpreter's behalf. It may neither credential nor approve money.
- A `FiscalOfficer` approves and pays interpreter claims and does nothing else that mutates state. It cannot create or finalize a claim, cannot schedule, and cannot credential.
- A `QualityReviewer` reviews complaints, files new ones, and pulls language-access compliance reports. It may not adjudicate a complaint and may not decide an appeal; that separation is the core segregation of duties in the program.
- An `Interpreter` acts only for themselves: accepting, declining, checking in to and completing their own assignments, submitting their own pay claims, and appealing a substantiated complaint against them.

Identity binding differs across the six roles, and the difference matters for every ownership check. When `X-User-Role` is `Interpreter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Interpreter` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Interpreter` compare that resource's interpreter foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `Interpreter` caller acting on an assignment must be that assignment's `interpreter_id`; acting on a pay claim, the claim's assignment's `interpreter_id`; acting on a complaint, the complaint's `interpreter_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `CredentialingOfficer`, `SchedulingCoordinator`, `FiscalOfficer`, or `QualityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `filed_by_user_id` when a complaint is filed, as `reviewer_user_id` when a complaint is reviewed, and as `adjudicator_user_id` when a complaint is adjudicated — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers are, however, compared to *each other*: the segregation-of-duties rules in §2.10 are expressed entirely in terms of them.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request addressed to a path the service does not route, or carrying a method that path does not accept, is answered in this same error envelope with `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 404 or HTTP 405 respectively. A trailing slash is insignificant in routing, so `/languages/` resolves exactly as `/languages` does.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Unless a specific list endpoint declares a different ordering in §4, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. The endpoints that declare their own ordering are: languages by `code` ascending; courthouses by `name` ascending; rate schedules by `effective_from` ascending; interpreters by `full_name` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; hearings by `scheduled_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; examination attempts by `taken_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; disciplinary actions by `effective_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; unmet-need reports by `period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; and the ranked eligible-interpreter pool, whose ordering is described in §3.6. Every declared ordering, the default `created_at` one included, is completed by one final implicit tie-break: rows equal on all of the declared sort keys are returned in insertion order, the order in which the service created them. That tie-break is load-bearing rather than theoretical, because the logical clock stamps a whole batch of rows created without an intervening clock move with an identical `created_at`. A list filter whose value falls outside its declared enumeration is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), whereas an id-valued filter that resolves to no row is not an error at all and simply matches nothing.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the service creates is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits recording when the row was inserted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 each time the row is mutated. `Language`, `Courthouse`, `RateSchedule`, `Interpreter`, `Credential`, `Hearing`, `Assignment`, `PayClaim`, `Complaint`, and `DisciplinaryAction` all behave this way.

Two resource types are immutable ledger entries and therefore behave differently: an examination attempt and an unmet-need report each carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those two, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response entirely — not null, absent — because neither row is ever updated after insertion and neither exposes an update endpoint.

Whenever a cascade recomputes a derived field on a row (a courthouse's ledger totals, an interpreter's quality scores, a language's credentialed count), that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented by 1 in the same way an explicit write would do it. The specification below is deliberate about naming each such hop, because a client holding a version number needs to know which apparently unrelated operations invalidate it.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in the program runs against a server-held **logical clock**, never against wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`; the server never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant, and any attempt to move the clock backwards is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Setting the clock mutates no entity: clock-driven consequences — credential lapse, sanction expiry, hearings passing uncovered — are applied only when the daily operations sweep runs.

Where a rule speaks of a *date* rather than an instant (a credential's `expires_on`, a sanction's `effective_from`, a report window boundary), the date is the UTC calendar date of the logical clock instant, and all date arithmetic — "plus 730 days", "plus 90 days" — is whole-day arithmetic on that calendar date.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. An unresolvable interpreter segment yields `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_FOUND`; a credential segment, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`; a hearing segment, `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`; an assignment segment, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a pay-claim segment, `ERR_PAY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; a complaint segment, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`; a disciplinary-action segment, `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`; and a courthouse segment, `ERR_CONFIG_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All eight are HTTP 404.

The same catalogue governs foreign keys arriving in a request body, and the mapping is by *referent type*, not by field name. A `language_id`, `courthouse_id`, or `rate_schedule_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_CONFIG_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; an `interpreter_id`, `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_FOUND`; a `hearing_id`, `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`; an `assignment_id`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `complaint_id`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key resolution is checked on exactly five write endpoints: `POST /credentials`, `POST /hearings`, `POST /assignments`, `POST /complaints`, and `POST /unmet-need-reports`.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Calendar and Roster Writes

Two create operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a create legitimately *destroys* a sibling row and a client working from an outdated view would destroy something it never saw.

`POST /assignments` requires a non-optional `expected_hearing_version` field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Hearing`. If that value does not equal the hearing's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HEARING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because a create landing on a hearing that already carries a `PROPOSED` assignment pushes that assignment to `SUPERSEDED`; without the version pin, a client holding a stale hearing view would silently replace an assignment it never observed. Note that the hearing's `version` is incremented by the create itself, by the supersede it triggers, by a continuance, and by every coverage transition, so a client must re-read the hearing after any of those before attempting another create.

`POST /credentials` requires a non-optional `expected_interpreter_version` field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Interpreter`. If that value does not equal the interpreter's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INTERPRETER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The same reasoning applies: a higher-tier credential create for a language in which the interpreter already has a pending lower-tier application legitimately withdraws that application and bumps the interpreter's `version`, so a stale-version create would withdraw an application the client never observed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Confirming, cancelling, completing, advancing, renewing, revoking, reviewing, adjudicating, appealing, submitting, approving and paying are all unguarded by version pinning, and supplying a version field to them is not part of their contract.

### 2.8. Money, Hours and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in the program are integers denominated in cents; the API exposes no floating-point currency. Hourly rates, budgets, ledger totals and claim components are therefore whole-cent integers, and the ledger arithmetic in §2.9 performs no rounding at all because it only ever adds and subtracts integers.

Rounding enters in exactly one place: converting hours and rates into a claim's components. Billable time is expressed as a number with two decimal places and is always a multiple of 0.25. Each monetary component of a claim — the service component, the rare-language premium, and the travel reimbursement — is rounded to whole cents using **round half up** (a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero) **before** the three components are summed. The summation order is pinned: rounding each component and then adding is the rule, and adding the three unrounded components and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule, because for many inputs the two differ by a cent.

One further rounded quantity exists outside the pay ledger: a language-access coverage rate is a percentage carried to two decimal places, also rounded half up.

### 2.9. The Courthouse Encumbrance Ledger

Each courthouse holds a fiscal-year interpreter pay authority in `pay_budget_cents`, against which the program tracks two derived totals and one remainder. `encumbered_pay_cents` is the sum of `provisional_total_cents` over every pay claim whose `status` is `ENCUMBERED` and whose assignment's hearing sits at this courthouse. `obligated_pay_cents` is the sum of `final_total_cents` over every pay claim whose `status` is one of `FINALIZED`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, or `PAID`, reached through the same assignment-to-hearing-to-courthouse path. `available_pay_cents` is `pay_budget_cents` less `encumbered_pay_cents` less `obligated_pay_cents`. A claim in `VOIDED` status appears in neither sum, and a vendor-served assignment produces no claim at all and therefore never touches either sum.

All three derived values are recomputed on every pay-claim status change and on every assignment confirmation, cancellation, completion and no-show. The ledger is a conserved quantity: confirming an assignment and then cancelling it early returns `encumbered_pay_cents` to exactly its pre-confirmation value, because the claim leaves the `ENCUMBERED` set and no other field absorbs the amount.

Budget checks are inclusive at the boundary. A confirmation whose provisional total is exactly equal to the courthouse's `available_pay_cents` is accepted; only an amount strictly greater than the remainder is refused, with `ERR_COURTHOUSE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three independence rules are enforced across the complaint and payment workflows, and two of them compare recorded operator identifiers.

Complaint-review independence: the `QualityReviewer` whose `X-User-ID` records a review must differ from the complaint's `filed_by_user_id`. Two distinct `QualityReviewer` identifiers are therefore required whenever a complaint was filed by a `QualityReviewer`; a complaint generated automatically by a no-show carries the acting `SchedulingCoordinator`'s identifier as its filer, which leaves any `QualityReviewer` free to review it. A violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

Appeal-decision independence: the `ProgramAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` decides an appeal must differ from the `adjudicator_user_id` that the same complaint's adjudication recorded. A violation carries the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

Payment independence: only a `FiscalOfficer` may approve or pay a claim. The `Interpreter` who submits a claim and the `SchedulingCoordinator` who created the underlying assignment can never move it past `SUBMITTED`; an attempt is refused by the ordinary role check with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Many program operations mutate more than one row. Where they do, the specification names every hop, the order hops are processed in, and the fields each hop touches; an implementation that performs only the primary mutation is incomplete.

Four cascade families recur. **Credential-to-roster propagation**: whenever a credential enters or leaves `ACTIVE`, the owning language's `active_credentialed_count` is recomputed, then the holder's `credential_tier` and `credentialed_language_ids` are recomputed, and only then may the holder's `roster_status` move. **Roster-to-assignment propagation**: whenever an interpreter's `roster_status` leaves `ACTIVE`, that interpreter's live assignments are cancelled, each cancellation carrying a `cancel_reason_code` that names the cause. **Assignment-to-ledger propagation**: whenever an assignment's `service_status` reaches a terminal value, the assignment's pay claim moves to `VOIDED` or `FINALIZED` and the hosting courthouse's three ledger totals are recomputed. **Assignment-to-hearing propagation**: whenever the covering assignment gains or loses coverage, the hearing's `status` follows.

Whenever a cascade cancels a set of assignments, they are processed in `created_at` ascending order, so that the resulting sequence of ledger recomputations is deterministic.

### 2.12. Order of Evaluation

When a single request could fail several checks at once, exactly one error is returned: the first failure in the ordered ladder below. The ladder is global — a check at a lower number always masks a check at a higher number — and it is the reason a request that is both unauthorized and malformed reports only the authorization failure.

1. Authentication: both headers present, `X-User-ID` non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Authorization: the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Request shape: unknown value types, malformed dates or datetimes, enum values outside the declared set, negative money or mileage, non-positive counts, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, a `scheduled_end` at or before `scheduled_start`, a `period_end` at or before `period_start`, and any missing required input field — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. Path existence: every `id` path segment resolves, left to right, mapped to the not-found codes listed in §2.6. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
5. Body referential integrity: every foreign key in the request body resolves, mapped as in §2.6. Applies to `POST /credentials`, `POST /hearings`, `POST /assignments`, `POST /complaints`, and `POST /unmet-need-reports`.
6. Interpreter-role ownership: an `Interpreter` caller's `X-User-ID` matches the target resource's interpreter — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to `POST /assignments/{id}/confirm`, `POST /assignments/{id}/decline`, `POST /assignments/{id}/check-in`, `POST /assignments/{id}/complete`, `POST /pay-claims/{id}/submit`, and `POST /complaints/{id}/appeal`.
7. Segregation of duties, per §2.10 — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Applies to `POST /complaints/{id}/review` and `POST /complaints/{id}/appeal-decision`.
8. Optimistic concurrency: `expected_hearing_version` equals the hearing's `version` on `POST /assignments`, and `expected_interpreter_version` equals the interpreter's `version` on `POST /credentials` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_HEARING_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_INTERPRETER_VERSION` (both 412).
9. Credential state admits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /credentials/{id}/exam-attempts`, `POST /credentials/{id}/advance`, `POST /credentials/{id}/renew`, and `POST /credentials/{id}/revoke`.
10. Assignment `service_status` admits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments/{id}/confirm`, `POST /assignments/{id}/decline`, `POST /assignments/{id}/check-in`, `POST /assignments/{id}/complete`, `POST /assignments/{id}/cancel`, and `POST /assignments/{id}/no-show`.
11. Pay-claim `status` admits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAY_CLAIM_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /pay-claims/{id}/submit`, `POST /pay-claims/{id}/approve`, and `POST /pay-claims/{id}/pay`.
12. Complaint `status` admits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /complaints/{id}/review`, `POST /complaints/{id}/adjudicate`, `POST /complaints/{id}/appeal`, and `POST /complaints/{id}/appeal-decision`.
13. Hearing status admits scheduling work — otherwise `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULABLE` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments` and `POST /hearings/{id}/continue`.
14. The named interpreter is on the active roster — otherwise `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_ON_ACTIVE_ROSTER` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
15. The named interpreter holds an active credential in the hearing's language — otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
16. That credential's tier meets the hearing's tier floor — otherwise `ERR_TIER_BELOW_HEARING_REQUIREMENT` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
17. Ethics training is complete — otherwise `ERR_ETHICS_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to both `POST /credentials/{id}/advance` and `POST /assignments`.
18. The interpreter's rolling quality score clears the assignment floor for a top-priority hearing — otherwise `ERR_QUALITY_SCORE_BELOW_ASSIGNMENT_FLOOR` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
19. The examination attempt limit has not been reached — otherwise `ERR_EXAM_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to `POST /credentials/{id}/exam-attempts`.
20. The retake waiting period has elapsed — otherwise `ERR_EXAM_RETAKE_TOO_SOON` (409). Applies to `POST /credentials/{id}/exam-attempts`.
21. A provisional target tier is permitted for the language — otherwise `ERR_PROVISIONAL_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED` (422). Applies to `POST /credentials`.
22. An oral attempt is only recorded where an oral instrument exists — otherwise `ERR_ORAL_EXAM_NOT_OFFERED` (422). Applies to `POST /credentials/{id}/exam-attempts`.
23. No other live credential exists for the same interpreter-and-language pair — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_CREDENTIAL` (409). Applies to `POST /credentials`.
24. Continuing-education hours meet the renewal minimum — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT_HOURS` (422). Applies to `POST /credentials/{id}/renew`.
25. A lapsed credential is still inside its renewal grace window — otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_GRACE_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /credentials/{id}/renew`.
26. The hearing is not already covered by an accepted assignment — otherwise `ERR_HEARING_ALREADY_COVERED` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
27. Video remote interpreting is technically possible at the courthouse — otherwise `ERR_REMOTE_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
28. Telephonic delivery, and vendor service in particular, is permitted for the language — otherwise `ERR_TELEPHONIC_NOT_PERMITTED` (422). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
29. Concurrent video-remote occupancy at the courthouse stays within capacity — otherwise `ERR_VRI_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
30. The hearing window does not overlap another live assignment of the same interpreter — otherwise `ERR_INTERPRETER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
31. The travel buffer between two in-person assignments at different courthouses is respected — otherwise `ERR_TRAVEL_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`.
32. The hearing's timing window is open — otherwise `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments`, `POST /assignments/{id}/confirm`, `POST /assignments/{id}/complete`, `POST /assignments/{id}/cancel`, and `POST /hearings/{id}/continue`. On `POST /assignments/{id}/confirm` this test runs immediately after the `service_status` test of check 10 and therefore ahead of that endpoint's roster, credential, facility, rate-schedule and budget tests, as §3.7 sets out; on `POST /hearings/{id}/continue` it runs after the schedulability test of check 13.
33. An appeal is inside its filing window — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /complaints/{id}/appeal`.
34. A rate schedule is in effect for the hearing's date — otherwise `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments/{id}/confirm` and `POST /assignments/{id}/complete`.
35. No quality review is open against the assignment — otherwise `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /pay-claims/{id}/approve`.
36. The courthouse has budget headroom — otherwise `ERR_COURTHOUSE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /assignments/{id}/confirm` and `POST /assignments/{id}/complete`.
37. An unmet-need report period is closed and of legal length — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_INVALID` (422). Applies to `POST /unmet-need-reports`.

Uniqueness conflicts (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LANGUAGE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_INTERPRETER_EMAIL`, `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`) are evaluated after request-shape validation on the create endpoints that own them, and each is HTTP 409.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Configuration: Languages, Courthouses and Rate Schedules

Nothing in the program can happen until a `ProgramAdmin` has described the world the program operates in, and all three configuration resources are administrator-owned.

Registering a language records its ISO-639-3 `code` — three lowercase letters, unique across the whole program, with a second registration of the same code refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_LANGUAGE_CODE` — together with a human-readable `name`, its coverage class in `tier_class`, and whether the program administers an oral performance examination in it via `oral_exam_available`. The coverage class is one of `DESIGNATED` (the program's high-volume languages, fully examinable), `NON_DESIGNATED` (examinable but lower volume), and `RARE` (no certification instrument exists at all). An optional `telephonic_vendor_available` flag, false when omitted, records whether a telephonic language-service vendor covers the language; only a language with that flag set may be served telephonically. Registration also initialises the derived `active_credentialed_count` to 0; thereafter it always equals the number of credentials in this language whose status is `ACTIVE`, and it is recomputed by every credential activation, lapse and revocation.

Registering a courthouse records its `name`, a three-uppercase-letter `county_code` used for geographic-availability ranking, a `courtroom_count` of at least 1, and the fiscal-year interpreter pay authority `pay_budget_cents`, which may not be negative. Two fields describe the facility's remote-interpreting technology: `remote_equipment_tier` is one of `NONE`, `BASIC`, or `CERTIFIED_VRI`, and `vri_concurrent_capacity` is the number of video-equipped courtrooms that can run a video remote interpreting session at the same time, which may be zero. The facility's `status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED` and defaults to `OPEN`. Creation initialises `encumbered_pay_cents` and `obligated_pay_cents` to 0, which leaves `available_pay_cents` equal to `pay_budget_cents`.

A courthouse's equipment tier, video capacity, facility status and pay budget can all be revised later by a `ProgramAdmin`, and two of those revisions have teeth. Downgrading `remote_equipment_tier` to `NONE`, or setting `status` to `CLOSED`, withdraws the facility's ability to host video sessions, and every video assignment at that courthouse whose `service_status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` is therefore cancelled with `cancel_reason_code` set to `EQUIPMENT_WITHDRAWN`, processed in assignment `created_at` ascending order; each such cancellation runs the ordinary lead-time-dependent consequences of §3.7 rather than a special case of its own — the encumbered pay claim is voided and a `COVERED` hearing returns to `SCHEDULED` when the lead time to `scheduled_start` is at or above the governing rate schedule's `late_cancellation_hours`, while a withdrawal landing inside that threshold instead finalizes the claim on the late-cancellation terms and moves the hearing to `UNMET` — after which the courthouse's `encumbered_pay_cents`, `obligated_pay_cents` and `available_pay_cents` are recomputed. Reducing `pay_budget_cents` to a figure below the sum of `encumbered_pay_cents` and `obligated_pay_cents` is refused outright with `ERR_COURTHOUSE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, because the program will not un-commit money it has already promised. Every accepted revision increments the courthouse's `version` by 1, and a revision that supplies none of the four mutable fields is accepted as a no-op that still increments `version` by 1 and triggers no cascade.

A rate schedule is the price list for interpreter service over a date range. `effective_from` is the first date the schedule governs and `effective_through` the last, both inclusive and both plain `YYYY-MM-DD` dates matched against the date part of a hearing's `scheduled_start`; an omitted or null `effective_through` means the schedule is open-ended. The service imposes no further ordering on the two boundaries: a window whose `effective_through` falls before its `effective_from` is accepted exactly as submitted and simply governs no date at all. Because exactly one schedule must govern any given hearing date, a submission whose window overlaps an existing schedule's window is refused with `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`. The schedule's price terms are four tier hourly rates — `master_hourly_cents`, `certified_hourly_cents`, `registered_hourly_cents` and `provisional_hourly_cents` — plus `minimum_call_hours`, the floor applied to billable time however short the actual appearance was; `travel_mileage_rate_cents`, the reimbursement per reimbursable mile, which may carry one decimal place; `travel_free_radius_miles`, the one-way distance at or below which no travel money is owed at all; `remote_rate_multiplier`, applied to the service component whenever delivery is not in person; `late_cancellation_hours`, the lead-time threshold in hours below which a cancellation is still compensated; and `late_cancellation_pay_hours`, the number of hours paid when it is. Three further fields price rare-language work: `rare_premium_low_pct` and `rare_premium_high_pct` are the two premium rates, and `rare_premium_bracket_cents` is the rounded-service-component breakpoint that selects between them. Rate schedules are readable by list, optionally restricted to the single schedule in effect on a given date supplied as `effective_on`, and by direct fetch.

### 3.2. The Roster: Enrolment, Ethics Training and Standing

An interpreter joins the program as an applicant. Enrolment, performed by a `CredentialingOfficer` or a `ProgramAdmin`, records `full_name`, a contact `email` that must be unique across the roster — a second enrolment with the same address is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INTERPRETER_EMAIL` — an optional `phone` that is null when omitted, and a `home_county_code` of three uppercase letters that the program later compares against a courthouse's `county_code` when it ranks candidates by geographic availability. Two willingness flags, `accepts_in_person` and `accepts_remote`, both default to true when omitted; the first governs eligibility for in-person work and the second governs both video and telephonic work. Enrolment sets `roster_status` to `APPLICANT` and `ethics_training_status` to `NOT_STARTED`, and it initialises `complaint_points` to 0 with `rolling_12_month_score` and `lifetime_score` both at 100.

Roster standing moves through six values and is never set directly by a client — every transition is a consequence of credentialing or discipline. `APPLICANT` is where enrolment leaves the interpreter. The first credential application opened in their name promotes `APPLICANT → CANDIDATE`, and that promotion increments the interpreter's `version` by 1. The first credential of theirs to reach an activated state promotes `CANDIDATE → ACTIVE`, recomputing `credential_tier` and `credentialed_language_ids` on the way through. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and `ACTIVE → REVOKED` are consequences of discipline, described in §3.8. `ACTIVE → INACTIVE` happens the moment the interpreter's last activated credential stops being activated, which requires that the count of their credentials in `ACTIVE` status has fallen to zero; that transition also sets `credential_tier` to `NONE` and empties `credentialed_language_ids` to an empty list. The reverse leg `INACTIVE → ACTIVE` fires when a lapsed credential is renewed back into service, and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` fires when the last suspension against the interpreter stops being in effect. `REVOKED` is terminal: an interpreter reaches it from `ACTIVE`, from `SUSPENDED`, or from `INACTIVE`, and never leaves it.

Both derived credential fields follow the same convention. `credential_tier` is the highest tier among the interpreter's credentials whose status is `ACTIVE`, ranked `MASTER` above `CERTIFIED` above `REGISTERED` above `PROVISIONAL`, and reads `NONE` when no credential of theirs is active. `credentialed_language_ids` lists the language of each of those active credentials, ordered by the credential's own `created_at` ascending.

Ethics training is a single mandatory course and a single recorded fact. A `CredentialingOfficer` or `ProgramAdmin` records completion by supplying the `completed_on` date and the `provider` that delivered the course; the service sets `ethics_training_status` to `COMPLETED`, copies the supplied date into `ethics_completed_on`, and increments the interpreter's `version` by 1. The supplied `completed_on` is client-supplied history and is never compared against the logical clock, the `provider` is required but is not persisted on the interpreter, and recording completion again for an interpreter whose `ethics_training_status` already reads `COMPLETED` is accepted rather than refused: it overwrites `ethics_completed_on` with the newly supplied date and increments `version` once more. Recording completion does **not** by itself activate anything: a credential waiting on ethics still requires an explicit advancement call from a `CredentialingOfficer`, and until `ethics_training_status` reads `COMPLETED` both that advancement and any attempt to assign the interpreter to a hearing are refused with `ERR_ETHICS_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE`.

An interpreter's standing in the program is summarised by two quality scores, and the pair is the program's most-consulted derived figure. Both are computed from the interpreter's disciplinary actions whose status is either `IN_EFFECT` or `EXPIRED`; a `RESCINDED` action is invisible to both. Each action contributes `points`, and points accrue by sanction type: a letter of instruction is worth 5 points, probation 10 points, a suspension 12 points, and a revocation 20 points. Take the current logical-clock date and subtract 365 days to obtain a cutoff; an action whose `effective_from` falls on or after that cutoff is *recent*, and an action whose `effective_from` falls strictly before it is *older* — the boundary is inclusive on the recent side, so an action dated exactly on the cutoff counts as recent. Sum the points of the recent actions to obtain the recent-points figure and the points of the older actions to obtain the older-points figure. Then `rolling_12_month_score` is 100 less four times the recent-points figure, floored at 0 so it never reports a negative number. And `lifetime_score` is 100 less four times the recent-points figure less twice the older-points figure, also floored at 0. Because the lifetime variant adds the older term while reading exactly the same rows, an interpreter carrying old sanctions always scores strictly lower on `lifetime_score` than on `rolling_12_month_score`, while an interpreter whose entire history is inside the last year scores identically on both. The separate field `complaint_points` is the plain, unweighted sum of `points` over the same `IN_EFFECT` and `EXPIRED` rows, with no decay and no floor.

Both scores are recomputed, and the owning interpreter's `version` incremented, every time a disciplinary action is created, expires, or is rescinded. A dedicated computed read exposes the two side by side for one interpreter, returning `interpreter_id`, `rolling_12_month_score`, `lifetime_score`, `complaint_points`, and the two intermediate figures `recent_points` and `older_points` that the formula above derived. The roster search itself accepts filters on `roster_status`, `credential_tier`, `language_id` (matching against the interpreter's credentialed languages) and `home_county_code`, where `credential_tier` additionally accepts the value `NONE` to select exactly those interpreters holding no active credential.

Two money fields on the interpreter mirror the courthouse ledger from the payee's side. `pending_pay_cents` is the sum of `final_total_cents` over this interpreter's pay claims whose status is `FINALIZED`, `SUBMITTED`, or `APPROVED`, and `paid_pay_cents` is the same sum restricted to claims in `PAID`. The field `suspension_through` holds the date through which an in-effect suspension keeps `roster_status` at `SUSPENDED`, and is null whenever the interpreter is not suspended.

### 3.3. The Credential Pipeline: Applications, Examinations, Activation

A credential is an application that becomes a licence. A `CredentialingOfficer` opens one by naming the holder in `interpreter_id`, the language in `language_id`, and the tier being pursued in `target_tier` — one of `MASTER`, `CERTIFIED`, `REGISTERED`, or `PROVISIONAL` — together with the `expected_interpreter_version` pin described in §2.7. The two body foreign keys are resolved in that order, interpreter first and language second, so a create naming two unresolvable ids reports `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_FOUND`. The target tier is fixed at creation and never changes; the separate field `tier` stays null until the credential is activated and then mirrors `target_tier` for as long as the credential remains active, reverting to null if it lapses or is revoked. A new credential's `status` is `PENDING_WRITTEN`, its `written_best_score` and `oral_best_score` are null, its `written_attempt_count` and `oral_attempt_count` are 0, its `cycle_credit_hours` is 0, its `renewal_count` is 0, and its three cycle dates — `issued_on`, `expires_on`, and `grace_through` — are all null until the credential is first activated.

Two rules constrain what may be opened. A `PROVISIONAL` target is the program's rare-language and no-instrument escape hatch, and it is permitted only when the language's `tier_class` is `RARE` or the language's `oral_exam_available` is false; requesting `PROVISIONAL` for a fully examinable designated language is refused with `ERR_PROVISIONAL_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). And a live credential is unique per interpreter-and-language pair: if the interpreter already holds a credential in the same language whose status is `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED`, `PENDING_WRITTEN`, `PENDING_ORAL`, or `PENDING_ETHICS`, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_CREDENTIAL`.

That uniqueness rule has one deliberate exception, and it is the reason the create is version-pinned. When the pending credential the interpreter already holds is being *upgraded* — the new application names a strictly higher tier rank, where `MASTER` ranks 3, `CERTIFIED` ranks 2, `REGISTERED` ranks 1 and `PROVISIONAL` ranks 0 — the older application is not a blocker but a casualty: whichever of `PENDING_WRITTEN`, `PENDING_ORAL`, or `PENDING_ETHICS` it currently occupies, it moves to `WITHDRAWN`, its own `version` is incremented by 1, and the interpreter's `version` is incremented by 1 in that same create call. All three source states reach `WITHDRAWN` by exactly this trigger and by no other. Opening a first credential for an interpreter still in `APPLICANT` also promotes them to `CANDIDATE` in the same call.

Rare-language provisional qualification skips the examination machinery entirely. When the target tier is `PROVISIONAL` and the language's `tier_class` is `RARE`, the create call itself performs `PENDING_WRITTEN → PENDING_ETHICS` before returning, so the resource the client receives is already sitting in `PENDING_ETHICS` with its `version` incremented for that auto-transition. There is nothing to examine, because no certification instrument exists in the language.

Everyone else examines. A `CredentialingOfficer` records an attempt against a credential by supplying `exam_type` (`WRITTEN` or `ORAL`), a raw `score` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and `taken_on`, the date the examination was sat. `taken_on` is client-supplied history and is never compared against the logical clock. Recording an attempt writes `credential_id` from the credential the attempt is filed under, derives `attempt_ordinal` as the 1-based index of this attempt among the credential's attempts of the *same* `exam_type` ordered by `taken_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending — an ordinal is computed once, at the moment the attempt is recorded, and the ordinals already stored on that credential's earlier attempts of the same type are never recomputed, so a back-dated attempt takes an ordinal that an earlier-recorded attempt already carries — and derives `result` as `PASS` when the score reaches the passing floor for the credential's target tier and this exam type and `FAIL` otherwise. The written floor is 90 for a `MASTER` target and 80 for every other target. The oral floor is 85 for a `MASTER` target and 70 for every other target, `REGISTERED` and `PROVISIONAL` included, which matters because an oral attempt may be recorded against a credential whose target tier never reaches an oral stage. Each recorded attempt refreshes the credential's best score for that exam type — `written_best_score` and `oral_best_score` each hold the highest `score` seen across that type's attempts, remaining null while no attempt of that type exists — increments the matching `written_attempt_count` or `oral_attempt_count`, and increments the credential's `version` by 1. An attempt never moves the credential's `status` forward; advancement is always an explicit, separate call.

Three guards surround examination recording. Attempts may only be recorded while the credential's status is `PENDING_WRITTEN` or `PENDING_ORAL`, and otherwise the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE`. At most three attempts of each exam type are ever allowed against one credential, so a fourth attempt of the same type is refused with `ERR_EXAM_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_REACHED`. And a candidate who has failed must wait: the supplied `taken_on` must be at least 90 days after the `taken_on` of the most recent failed attempt of the same exam type on this credential, with a gap of exactly 90 days accepted and anything shorter refused as `ERR_EXAM_RETAKE_TOO_SOON`. An `ORAL` attempt is additionally only recordable in a language whose `oral_exam_available` is true; otherwise the program refuses it with `ERR_ORAL_EXAM_NOT_OFFERED` (HTTP 422).

Exhausting the attempts on a stage ends the application. When a written attempt brings `written_attempt_count` to 3 while the credential sits in `PENDING_WRITTEN` and `written_best_score` is still below the written floor for the target tier, the recording call itself drives `PENDING_WRITTEN → DENIED` and increments the credential's `version`. Symmetrically, when an oral attempt brings `oral_attempt_count` to 3 while the credential sits in `PENDING_ORAL` and `oral_best_score` is still below the oral floor for the target tier, the same call drives `PENDING_ORAL → DENIED` with its own `version` increment. `DENIED` is terminal, as are `WITHDRAWN` and `REVOKED`.

Advancement walks the credential up its stages one call at a time, and each branch has its own entry condition. From `PENDING_WRITTEN` the credential moves to `PENDING_ORAL` when the target tier is `MASTER` or `CERTIFIED`, the language's `oral_exam_available` is true, and `written_best_score` has reached the written floor for that target tier — 90 for `MASTER`, 80 for `CERTIFIED`. From `PENDING_WRITTEN` the credential instead moves straight to `PENDING_ETHICS` when `written_best_score` has reached 80 and either the target tier is `REGISTERED`, for which the program administers no oral performance examination at all, or the language offers no oral instrument, so there is no oral stage to enter. From `PENDING_ORAL` the credential moves to `PENDING_ETHICS` once `oral_best_score` has reached the oral floor for the target tier — 85 for `MASTER`, 70 for `CERTIFIED`. Each of these three advancements increments the credential's `version` by 1 and changes nothing else. An advancement request whose current stage requirement is unmet, or which matches none of the branches above, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE`.

The final advancement, `PENDING_ETHICS → ACTIVE`, is the one that issues the licence, and it is gated on the *holder* rather than the credential: the interpreter's `ethics_training_status` must read `COMPLETED`, or the call fails with `ERR_ETHICS_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE`. On success the credential's `status` becomes `ACTIVE` and `tier` is set to `target_tier`; `issued_on` records the logical-clock date of this first activation; `expires_on` is set 730 days — two full years — after that date for a `MASTER`, `CERTIFIED`, or `REGISTERED` credential, and 365 days after it for a `PROVISIONAL` one, whose shorter cycle reflects that it rests on no examination; and `grace_through` is set 90 days after `expires_on`. The activation then cascades: the language's `active_credentialed_count` is recomputed, the holder's `credential_tier` and `credentialed_language_ids` are recomputed, and the holder's `roster_status` bubbles up from `CANDIDATE` to `ACTIVE` if this is their first active credential or from `INACTIVE` to `ACTIVE` if they had fallen off the roster. The credential's `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.4. Credential Cycles: Renewal, Lapse and Revocation

Credentials expire, and the program treats expiry as a routine, recoverable event rather than a failure. A credential whose `expires_on` date has been passed by the logical clock is swept into `LAPSED` by the daily operations run described in §3.9; the sweep sets `tier` back to null, recomputes the language's `active_credentialed_count`, then recomputes the holder's `credential_tier` and `credentialed_language_ids`, and — when that recomputation leaves `credential_tier` reading `NONE` — drives the holder from `ACTIVE` to `INACTIVE`, which in turn cancels every one of that interpreter's assignments still in `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` with `cancel_reason_code` set to `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, processed in assignment `created_at` ascending order. That is a four-hop cascade from one clock tick, and every hop increments the `version` of the row it touches.

Renewal is how a credential earns another cycle, and a `CredentialingOfficer` performs it by submitting `cycle_credit_hours`, the continuing-education hours completed for the cycle just ending. At least 16 hours are required; exactly 16 is accepted and 15.5 is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT_HOURS` (HTTP 422). A successful renewal sets `expires_on` to 730 days after the later of the credential's current `expires_on` and the current logical-clock date — 365 days for a `PROVISIONAL` credential — recomputes `grace_through` as 90 days after the new `expires_on`, overwrites `cycle_credit_hours` with the submitted figure, adds 1 to `renewal_count`, and increments the credential's `version` by 1.

That single rule produces two visibly different behaviours, and the difference is worth stating twice. Renewing a credential that is still `ACTIVE` is a self-loop, `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`: because the clock has not yet passed `expires_on`, the extension is measured from the existing `expires_on`, so an early renewer neither gains nor loses days and their cycle boundary simply slides forward by one full cycle. Renewing a credential that has already gone `LAPSED` is the reinstatement leg, `LAPSED → ACTIVE`: the clock is by then past `expires_on`, so the new cycle is measured from the clock date, and the days spent lapsed are lost rather than credited. Reinstatement also restores service — `status` returns to `ACTIVE` and `tier` returns to `target_tier`, the language's `active_credentialed_count` is recomputed, and the holder's `roster_status` bubbles `INACTIVE → ACTIVE` provided no suspension is in effect against them.

Reinstatement is time-boxed. A `LAPSED` credential may be renewed only while the logical-clock date is on or before `grace_through`, which is to say within the 90 days following `expires_on`; the `grace_through` date itself is inside the window and the day after it is not, and a late attempt is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_GRACE_EXPIRED`. A renewal is also refused outright with `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE` when the holder's `roster_status` is `REVOKED`, and that refusal is evaluated ahead of both the continuing-education minimum and the grace window, so a revoked holder submitting too few hours still reports the state error. Requesting renewal from any status other than `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE`.

Revocation is the terminal exit, available only to a `ProgramAdmin` and accompanied by a written `reason`. It may be applied to a credential in any of `PENDING_WRITTEN`, `PENDING_ORAL`, `PENDING_ETHICS`, `ACTIVE`, or `LAPSED`, and it sets `status` to `REVOKED` and clears `tier` to null. Its cascade is the widest in the credentialing area: every assignment in `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` that was pinned to this credential is cancelled with `cancel_reason_code` set to `CREDENTIAL_REVOKED`, processed in assignment `created_at` ascending order, each such cancellation running the same lead-time branch as §3.7 — voiding the encumbered pay claim and returning a `COVERED` hearing to `SCHEDULED` when lead time is at or above the governing rate schedule's `late_cancellation_hours`, and finalizing that claim on the late-cancellation terms and moving the hearing to `UNMET` when lead time is below it; the language's `active_credentialed_count` is decremented when the credential had been `ACTIVE`; the holder's `credential_tier` is recomputed and, if the holder now has no active credential at all, their `roster_status` drops to `INACTIVE` with its own downstream cancellations, which sweep every one of that interpreter's remaining `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` assignments in any language and carry the same `CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` reason code; and the credential's `version` is incremented by 1. Revoking a credential that had not been `ACTIVE` — one still pending, or already lapsed — cancels the assignments pinned to it but leaves the language's `active_credentialed_count`, the holder's `roster_status` and the holder's `version` untouched, because nothing active was withdrawn. Credentials are readable individually and by list, the list accepting filters on `interpreter_id`, `language_id`, `status`, and `target_tier`; an individual credential's examination history is readable as a nested list under it, filterable by `exam_type`.

### 3.5. The Case Calendar: Placing, Scoring and Continuing Hearings

A hearing is the demand signal the whole program answers. A `SchedulingCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` places one on a courthouse calendar by supplying `courthouse_id`, the `language_id` the hearing needs, the court's `case_number`, the `hearing_type`, an `in_custody` flag that is true when the limited-English party is held in custody, the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` instants as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and the `requested_delivery_mode` the calendar is asking for — `IN_PERSON`, `VRI`, or `TELEPHONIC`. A `scheduled_end` at or before `scheduled_start` is malformed and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every one of those fields is required, `in_custody` included. Placement consults neither the courthouse's `status` nor its equipment tier, so a hearing may be calendared at a `CLOSED` facility and in a delivery mode that facility could not host, and it does not require `scheduled_start` to lie in the future. Creation sets `status` to `SCHEDULED` and `continuance_count` to 0, and leaves `continuance_reason_code` null. The seven recognised calendar event types are `ARRAIGNMENT`, `DETENTION_REVIEW`, `TRIAL`, `PLEA`, `SENTENCING`, `MOTION`, and `CIVIL_MOTION`.

Placement is also the only moment at which the program judges how urgently the hearing needs an interpreter, and that judgement is frozen onto the row so that priority never drifts as the calendar approaches. Five inputs feed it, and no other hearing participates in the arithmetic. First, a **type weight** taken from the event type: an arraignment scores 50, a detention review 45, a trial 40, a plea 30, a sentencing 30, a motion 20, and a civil motion 10. Second, a **custody bonus** of 25 points added when `in_custody` is true and nothing added when it is false. Third, a **rarity bonus** taken from the needed language's `tier_class`: 15 points for a `RARE` language, 5 points for a `NON_DESIGNATED` one, and 0 for a `DESIGNATED` one. Fourth, a **lead-time figure** stored on the row as `lead_time_days`: take the difference between `scheduled_start` and the logical clock at the moment of creation, express it in whole days by discarding any remaining fraction of a day, and then treat any negative result as 0. Fifth, an **urgency term**: start from 20 points, subtract 2 points for each whole day of `lead_time_days`, and treat any negative result as 0 — so a hearing 10 or more days out contributes no urgency at all while one starting the same day contributes the full 20.

Add the type weight, the custody bonus, the rarity bonus and the urgency term, then cap the sum at 100; the result is `priority_score`. From it the program derives `priority_class`: `A` when `priority_score` is 80 or more, `B` when it is 55 or more but below 80, and `C` otherwise. Both boundaries are inclusive at the floor, so a score of exactly 80 is class `A` and a score of exactly 55 is class `B`. `lead_time_days`, `priority_score` and `priority_class` are all computed once and never recomputed; a hearing created a month out stays class `C` even on the morning it is heard.

Coverage is tracked separately in the hearing's `status`, which is never written by a client after creation. `SCHEDULED → COVERED` fires when an assignment for the hearing reaches `CONFIRMED`; note that a merely proposed assignment leaves the hearing `SCHEDULED`, because coverage means acceptance, not intent. `COVERED → SERVED` fires when the covering assignment reaches `COMPLETED`. `COVERED → SCHEDULED` returns the hearing to the pool when the covering assignment leaves `CONFIRMED` — by cancellation or by a suspension, lapse or equipment cascade — with at least the governing rate schedule's `late_cancellation_hours` of lead time still remaining before `scheduled_start`. `COVERED → UNMET` is the same event with less than that much lead time remaining, and it is also where a no-show lands the hearing. `SCHEDULED → UNMET` is the clock-driven leg: a hearing still uncovered when its start time arrives is swept to unmet by the daily operations run. `SERVED`, `UNMET` and `CONTINUED` are all terminal. Every one of these transitions increments the hearing's `version` by 1, which is exactly why a client caching a hearing view must re-read it before pinning `expected_hearing_version` on a fresh assignment.

Continuing a hearing is the coordinator's escape from a date that will not work, and it is deliberately one-way. A `SchedulingCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` continues a hearing that is `SCHEDULED` or `COVERED`, supplying a `reason_code` of `COURT_CONGESTION`, `PARTY_REQUEST`, or `NO_INTERPRETER_AVAILABLE`; the logical clock must still be strictly before `scheduled_start`, and a continuance requested at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_CLOSED`, a test evaluated after the schedulability test named at the end of this paragraph. The hearing moves to `CONTINUED`, `continuance_count` is increased by 1, `continuance_reason_code` records the supplied code, and `version` is incremented by 1 — which invalidates any `expected_hearing_version` a client was holding. As the second hop, every assignment of this hearing whose `service_status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` is cancelled with `cancel_reason_code` set to `COURT_CONTINUANCE`, processed in assignment `created_at` ascending order, and each of those cancellations then runs the ordinary pay consequences of §3.7: a cancelled confirmed assignment inside `late_cancellation_hours` of `scheduled_start` finalizes its claim on the late-cancellation terms instead of voiding it, moving the amount out of the courthouse's `encumbered_pay_cents` and into its `obligated_pay_cents` and into the interpreter's `pending_pay_cents`. A continued hearing is terminal — the coordinator files a fresh hearing for the new date rather than reopening this one — and it is excluded from every counter in language-access reporting, so a continuance never depresses a courthouse's coverage rate. Requesting a continuance for a hearing already `SERVED`, `UNMET`, or `CONTINUED` is refused with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. Hearings are readable individually and by list, the list filterable by `courthouse_id`, `language_id`, `status`, and `priority_class`.

### 3.6. Matching Interpreters to Hearings

The tier a hearing demands is derived, not stored, and it depends on the hearing's frozen priority class and its language's coverage class only. If the needed language's `tier_class` is `RARE`, the required minimum tier is `PROVISIONAL`, because no certification examination exists in that language and demanding a certified interpreter would guarantee an unmet hearing. Otherwise, a class `A` hearing requires `CERTIFIED`, a class `B` hearing requires `REGISTERED`, and a class `C` hearing requires `PROVISIONAL`. The comparison is on tier rank — `MASTER` 3, `CERTIFIED` 2, `REGISTERED` 1, `PROVISIONAL` 0 — and the interpreter's best active credential in the hearing's language must rank at or above the required tier's rank. A credential ranking below it is refused with `ERR_TIER_BELOW_HEARING_REQUIREMENT`.

Before proposing anything, a `SchedulingCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` can pull the ranked pool of interpreters eligible to serve a given hearing. Every clause of the eligibility filter is explicit. The interpreter's `roster_status` must be `ACTIVE`, so `APPLICANT`, `CANDIDATE`, `SUSPENDED`, `INACTIVE` and `REVOKED` interpreters never appear at all. They must hold a credential in the hearing's `language_id` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose tier rank reaches the required minimum tier derived above. Their `ethics_training_status` must be `COMPLETED`. If the hearing's `priority_class` is `A`, their `rolling_12_month_score` must be at least 60; for class `B` and class `C` hearings no quality threshold applies. Their willingness must match the calendar's request: when the hearing's `requested_delivery_mode` is `IN_PERSON`, `accepts_in_person` must be true, and for `VRI` or `TELEPHONIC` requests, `accepts_remote` must be true. Finally, none of their existing assignments may collide with the hearing window under the exclusivity rules below, which this read applies with the prospective appearance treated as being delivered in the hearing's own `requested_delivery_mode`; either collision merely drops the interpreter from the pool rather than raising an error, and because a live assignment held against this very hearing overlaps its own window, an interpreter already proposed or confirmed for it does not appear in its own pool. The read itself is gated on nothing but the hearing's existence, so it answers for a hearing in any `status`, terminal ones included.

Survivors are ordered by a four-part key applied in sequence: highest tier rank of their best active credential in the hearing's language first; then interpreters whose `home_county_code` equals the hosting courthouse's `county_code` ahead of those whose does not; then higher `rolling_12_month_score` ahead of lower; and finally, as the last tie-break, earlier interpreter `created_at` ahead of later. The interpreter's `id` is never consulted, so the order is a deterministic total order. When no interpreter qualifies the read succeeds with HTTP 200 and an empty array rather than an error — the rare-language unmet-need case the program exists to measure — and the hearing simply stays `SCHEDULED` until the daily run marks it unmet.

Two exclusivity rules protect the interpreter as an occupied resource, and both consider only that interpreter's own assignments whose `service_status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `CHECKED_IN`; rows in `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `NO_SHOW`, or `COMPLETED` are ignored entirely. Those live assignments are examined in order of their hearing's `scheduled_start` ascending and then their own `created_at` ascending, and the first colliding pair decides which of the two errors is returned.

The first rule is plain double-booking. Treat each hearing window as half-open — it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant — and reject the proposal with `ERR_INTERPRETER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` when an existing window starts before the new window ends *and* the new window starts before that existing window ends. Because the intervals are half-open, two hearings that merely touch do not collide: an interpreter finishing at 11:00 may begin another hearing at 11:00.

The second rule is the travel buffer, and it applies far more narrowly. It is considered only when the existing assignment's `delivery_mode` is `IN_PERSON`, the proposed `delivery_mode` is `IN_PERSON`, and the two hearings sit at *different* courthouses; a remote leg on either side, or two in-person legs at the same courthouse, require no gap whatsoever. Where it does apply, take the later of the two window start instants, subtract the earlier of the two window end instants, and express the difference in minutes: at least 60 minutes must separate the two appearances, with exactly 60 minutes accepted and anything less refused as `ERR_TRAVEL_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. Both rules compare the stored instants at their full millisecond precision and round nothing to whole minutes or whole seconds, so a gap one millisecond short of an hour is a violation.

Creating the assignment itself is a coordinator or administrator action carrying `hearing_id`, the `expected_hearing_version` pin from §2.7, the `delivery_mode` that will actually be used, an optional `interpreter_id`, an optional `vendor_served` flag defaulting to false, and an optional `estimated_one_way_miles` defaulting to 0 that will drive the provisional travel reimbursement. The two body foreign keys are resolved hearing first and interpreter second, so a create naming two unresolvable ids reports `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`. The hearing must be in `SCHEDULED` status: a hearing that is `SERVED`, `UNMET`, or `CONTINUED` is not schedulable at all and is refused with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, while a hearing that is merely `COVERED` — which is to say one that already carries a `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `COMPLETED` assignment — is refused with the dedicated `ERR_HEARING_ALREADY_COVERED`. An existing `PROPOSED` assignment is not a blocker: it moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the hearing's `version` is incremented by 1 for that supersede in the same call, on top of the increment the create itself performs. `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `NO_SHOW` rows free the hearing entirely. The `scheduled_start` must also still lie strictly in the future on the logical clock, or the create is refused with `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Where a rostered interpreter is named, the roster gates of §2.12 apply in order — active roster standing, an active credential in the language, sufficient tier rank, completed ethics training, and the quality floor for a class `A` hearing — and on success the program pins `credential_id` and `credential_tier_snapshot` from the interpreter's best active credential in the hearing's language. That snapshot is the pay tier for the life of the assignment even if the credential later lapses or is revoked, which is what makes late-cancellation compensation computable after the credential has gone. Creation sets `service_status` to `PROPOSED` and `compliance_status` to `CLEAR`, leaves `actual_one_way_miles`, `check_in_utc`, `actual_end_utc` and `cancel_reason_code` null, and deliberately leaves the hearing in `SCHEDULED` — coverage is recorded only on confirmation.

Delivery mode has its own constraints, applied at creation. A `VRI` assignment requires the courthouse's `remote_equipment_tier` to be `BASIC` or `CERTIFIED_VRI` and its `status` to be `OPEN`; a facility at tier `NONE` cannot host video remote interpreting at all and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REMOTE_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE`. An `IN_PERSON` assignment carries no facility constraint at creation at all — neither `status` nor `remote_equipment_tier` is consulted — because the closed-facility test for in-person work is applied only at confirmation. A `TELEPHONIC` assignment requires the hearing's language to have `telephonic_vendor_available` set to true, and when `vendor_served` is true it additionally requires the language's `tier_class` to be `RARE` and `interpreter_id` to be absent from the request; any of those failing is refused with `ERR_TELEPHONIC_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). A vendor-served assignment is the program's last resort for a rare language with nobody on the roster, and it carries no credential pin, no roster checks and, as §3.7 explains, no pay claim. The pairing of `interpreter_id` and `vendor_served` is policed by that `TELEPHONIC` clause and by nothing else, so the two fields are otherwise stored exactly as submitted: a `vendor_served` flag accompanying `IN_PERSON` or `VRI` delivery draws no complaint, and an omitted `interpreter_id` on a create that does not set `vendor_served` is likewise accepted, leaving `interpreter_id`, `credential_id` and `credential_tier_snapshot` all null on the resulting row.

Video capacity is an aggregate constraint on the courthouse rather than on the interpreter, and its interval semantics are deliberately the opposite of double-booking's. Count the assignments at the same courthouse whose `delivery_mode` is `VRI` and whose `service_status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `CHECKED_IN`, keeping only those whose hearing window intersects the new hearing's window treating both windows as **closed** — an existing window intersects when its start is at or before the new window's end and the new window's start is at or before its end. Add one for the assignment being created; if the result exceeds the courthouse's `vri_concurrent_capacity`, the create is refused with `ERR_VRI_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Touching endpoints *do* collide here, because the video cart needs setting up and tearing down: a session ending at 10:00 still occupies the courtroom for a session starting at 10:00. In-person and telephonic assignments never occupy video capacity, and terminal-service rows never occupy it. Assignments are readable individually and by list, the list filterable by `hearing_id`, `interpreter_id`, `credential_id`, `service_status`, and `compliance_status`.

### 3.7. Serving the Assignment

An assignment carries two independent status dimensions, and understanding that they do not order each other is essential. `service_status` tracks the work — `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED`, `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `NO_SHOW`, with the last five terminal on that dimension. `compliance_status` tracks quality — `CLEAR`, `UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW`, `SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT`, with no terminal value at all. Complaint events move the compliance dimension regardless of where the service dimension sits, and service events move the service dimension regardless of where the compliance dimension sits. An assignment can perfectly well be `COMPLETED` and `UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW` at the same time; indeed that combination is the normal one after a served hearing draws an accuracy complaint.

Acceptance is `PROPOSED → CONFIRMED`, and either the named `Interpreter` or a `SchedulingCoordinator` may perform it. Seven conditions guard it. The `service_status` must be `PROPOSED`; the logical clock must still be strictly before the hearing's `scheduled_start`; the interpreter's `roster_status` must be `ACTIVE` and the pinned credential's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, both of which checks are skipped entirely for a vendor-served assignment; the courthouse's `status` must be `OPEN` when `delivery_mode` is `IN_PERSON`; the courthouse's `remote_equipment_tier` must be exactly `CERTIFIED_VRI` when `delivery_mode` is `VRI` — a stricter bar than the `BASIC`-or-better test applied at creation, because a proposal may be drafted against a partially equipped facility but the program will not commit money against one; and, last, the courthouse's `available_pay_cents` must be at least the provisional amount about to be encumbered. Those conditions are evaluated in exactly the order just listed rather than in the global numbering of §2.12, so a confirmation aimed at a hearing that has already started reports `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_CLOSED` even when the interpreter has meanwhile left the roster or the credential has lapsed. The roster failure is `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_ON_ACTIVE_ROSTER` and the credential failure `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_ACTIVE` — the latter covering a pinned credential that has been deleted or was never pinned as well as one no longer `ACTIVE` — while both facility failures, a courthouse not `OPEN` for in-person work and one below `CERTIFIED_VRI` for video, report the same `ERR_REMOTE_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE`.

Acceptance is also where money first moves. Exactly one rate schedule must be in effect for the date part of the hearing's `scheduled_start` — its `effective_from` at or before that date, and either a null `effective_through` or an `effective_through` at or on that date — and if none is, confirmation is refused with `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. That schedule's `id` is pinned onto a freshly created pay claim as `rate_schedule_id`, and a schedule published later never changes an existing claim. The claim is created in `ENCUMBERED` status with `variant` set to `PROVISIONAL` and its `provisional_total_cents` computed by the provisional arithmetic of §3.8; the courthouse's `encumbered_pay_cents` and `available_pay_cents` are recomputed; the hearing bubbles `SCHEDULED → COVERED`; and the assignment's `version` is incremented by 1. If the provisional total exceeds `available_pay_cents` the confirmation is refused with `ERR_COURTHOUSE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` and nothing is created. A vendor-served assignment skips the whole money leg: the rate-schedule requirement and the budget check are not applied to it at all, so a vendor row confirms even on a date no schedule governs, and no claim, no encumbrance and no ledger recomputation follow — the vendor bills the court outside the program's ledger.

Declining is the interpreter's own refusal, `PROPOSED → DECLINED`, and only the assignment's own interpreter may do it. It carries a required `reason_code` drawn from the same seven-value set a cancellation uses, and that value is validated and then discarded: `cancel_reason_code` is recorded as `INTERPRETER_DECLINED` whatever was supplied. Because a proposed assignment never moved the hearing out of `SCHEDULED`, the hearing is left completely untouched — its `status` stays `SCHEDULED` and its `version` does not change — and because no claim exists yet, no ledger movement occurs. The coordinator is simply free to propose someone else.

Checking in is the interpreter's own act and nobody else's, `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN`. It requires that the caller is the assignment's interpreter and that the interpreter's `roster_status` is still `ACTIVE` at that moment, a failure of the latter reported as `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_ON_ACTIVE_ROSTER`. There is no separate vendor test here: a vendor-served assignment carries a null `interpreter_id`, which can never equal the calling interpreter's `X-User-ID`, so the ownership check of §2.12 already refuses it with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. It records `check_in_utc` as the logical-clock instant, which becomes the start of the billable window for final pay, and increments the assignment's `version` by 1.

Completion closes out served interpretation and may be performed by the interpreter or by a `SchedulingCoordinator`. The caller supplies `actual_end_utc`, the instant the appearance actually ended, and an optional `one_way_miles` figure defaulting to 0 which is stored on the assignment as `actual_one_way_miles`. For a rostered interpreter the path is `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED` and `actual_end_utc` must be strictly after `check_in_utc`; for a vendor-served assignment the path is `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED` — there is no check-in for a vendor — and `actual_end_utc` must be strictly after the hearing's `scheduled_start`. In both cases `actual_end_utc` must be at or after the hearing's `scheduled_start` and no more than 6 hours past its `scheduled_end`; a value outside that envelope is refused with `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Completion moves the hearing `COVERED → SERVED` and increments the hearing's `version` by 1.

For a rostered interpreter, completion also settles the money: the pay claim moves `ENCUMBERED → FINALIZED` with `variant` set to `FINAL` and `final_total_cents` computed by the final arithmetic of §3.8, the courthouse's `encumbered_pay_cents`, `obligated_pay_cents` and `available_pay_cents` are all recomputed, and the interpreter's `pending_pay_cents` is recomputed to include the newly finalized amount. Completion re-checks that a rate schedule is in effect for the hearing's date, refusing with `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` if none is — that re-check asks only whether some schedule now governs the hearing's date, while the final figures are still computed from the schedule pinned on the claim — and re-checks the courthouse's budget headroom against the amount now being obligated. The headroom for that test is the courthouse's current `available_pay_cents` *plus* this claim's own `provisional_total_cents`, because the claim's existing encumbrance is released before the final figure is measured against what remains; only a final total strictly greater than that sum is refused, with `ERR_COURTHOUSE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. For a vendor-served assignment every one of those pay-ledger hops is skipped: no claim is created and none is finalized. This is the program's one conditional cascade, and the condition is exactly whether `vendor_served` is true. The assignment's `version` is incremented by 1 either way.

Cancellation is available to a `SchedulingCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` on an assignment in `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED`, with the logical clock still strictly before the hearing's `scheduled_start` — a later attempt is refused with `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_CLOSED`, tested after the `service_status` gate — and it is also the shape every system cascade takes — the continuance in §3.5, the suspension and lapse cascades in §3.4 and §3.8, and the equipment withdrawal in §3.1 all cancel through this same machinery. An explicit cancellation carries a `reason_code`, which is written to `cancel_reason_code`; a cascaded one writes the reason the cascade dictates. The recognised reasons are `INTERPRETER_DECLINED`, `COURT_CONTINUANCE`, `INTERPRETER_SUSPENDED`, `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, `CREDENTIAL_REVOKED`, `EQUIPMENT_WITHDRAWN`, and `COORDINATOR_CANCELLED`.

What happens next depends entirely on lead time, measured as the difference between the hearing's `scheduled_start` and the logical clock and compared against the governing rate schedule's `late_cancellation_hours`. The governing schedule is the one pinned on the assignment's own pay claim, falling back to whichever schedule is in effect on the hearing's date when the assignment carries no claim at all; when neither can be found the cancellation is treated as early. An **early** cancellation — lead time at or above that threshold — voids the encumbered claim: it moves `ENCUMBERED → VOIDED`, `provisional_total_cents` is preserved on the row for audit while `final_total_cents` stays null, and exactly `provisional_total_cents` is released from the courthouse's `encumbered_pay_cents`, restoring the ledger to its pre-confirmation value. The interpreter's own row is not touched at all on an early cancellation, because a claim voided straight out of `ENCUMBERED` never entered their `pending_pay_cents`. A **late** cancellation — lead time strictly below the threshold — compensates the interpreter instead: the claim moves `ENCUMBERED → FINALIZED` with `variant` set to `FINAL`, `late_cancellation` set to true, and `final_total_cents` computed by the late-cancellation arithmetic of §3.8, after which the interpreter's `pending_pay_cents` is recomputed from the newly finalized claim. The hearing follows suit only when the assignment being cancelled was itself `CONFIRMED` and the hearing reads `COVERED` at that moment: it returns to `SCHEDULED` on an early cancellation and moves to `UNMET` on a late one, incrementing its `version` by 1. Every other combination leaves the hearing exactly where it is — a hearing still in `SCHEDULED` because the assignment was only `PROPOSED`, a hearing already in `CONTINUED`, and equally a hearing left `COVERED` by an accepted assignment while a merely proposed sibling of it is cancelled. Cancelling an assignment that was only `PROPOSED` moves no money at all, since no claim was ever created.

A no-show is recorded by a `SchedulingCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` against a `CONFIRMED` assignment once the logical clock has reached or passed the hearing's `scheduled_start` — an attempt made before that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE` rather than `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_CLOSED` — and it is the one operation that touches both status dimensions in a single call. On the service dimension it drives `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW`; on the compliance dimension it drives `CLEAR → UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW`, and an assignment whose `compliance_status` already reads `UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW` or `SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT` is left on that value untouched. The encumbered claim moves `ENCUMBERED → VOIDED`, releasing its provisional amount from the courthouse's `encumbered_pay_cents` — the interpreter who did not appear is paid nothing, unlike the interpreter whose assignment was cancelled late. A complaint is filed automatically, in `FILED` status with `category` set to `NO_SHOW`, `severity` set to `MAJOR`, `assignment_id` pointing at this assignment, `filed_by_user_id` set to the acting caller's `X-User-ID`, and a server-supplied `narrative` reading exactly `The confirmed interpreter failed to appear for the hearing.`; the complaint's own intake cascade is not re-run, so the compliance move above is the only one the call makes. The hearing moves `COVERED → UNMET` with its `version` incremented by 1: a no-show records the hearing as uncovered for the language-access record and does not reopen it for scheduling. The assignment's `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.8. Interpreter Compensation: One Formula, Three Variants

A pay claim is created by the program, never by a client. It records the `assignment_id` it compensates and the `rate_schedule_id` pinned at creation, and it exposes a `variant` field naming which of the program's three calculation variants produced its currently authoritative figures — `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`. Its `status` walks `ENCUMBERED`, then either `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED`, then `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, and `PAID`, with `PAID` and `VOIDED` terminal. Four component fields always describe the authoritative variant: `billable_hours`, `service_cents`, `rare_premium_cents`, and `travel_cents`. Two total fields describe the two moments: `provisional_total_cents` is frozen at claim creation and is never overwritten, while `final_total_cents` stays null until the claim reaches `FINALIZED`. The boolean `late_cancellation` is false unless the final figures were produced by the late-cancellation path, and `payment_reference` stays null until the warrant is issued.

Every variant reads exactly one assignment, that assignment's hearing, that hearing's language, and the rate schedule pinned on the claim — nothing else, and never a rate schedule published afterwards. The arithmetic runs in six pinned steps.

**Step one — the raw window and the mileage figure.** The `PROVISIONAL` variant measures the *scheduled* window, from the hearing's `scheduled_start` up to but not including its `scheduled_end`, and takes its mileage from the assignment's `estimated_one_way_miles`. The `FINAL` variant measures the *served* window, from the assignment's `check_in_utc` up to but not including its `actual_end_utc`, and takes its mileage from `actual_one_way_miles`. The `LATE_CANCELLATION` variant measures nothing at all: no window is taken, its mileage is treated as zero because no travel was undertaken, and it skips straight past step two.

**Step two — rounding up to the quarter hour, then the minimum-call floor.** Express the window's duration in hours by dividing its length in seconds by 3600. Multiply that figure by four, round the product *up* to the next whole number, and divide by four again — the effect is that any part-used quarter hour is billed as a whole quarter hour. Then take the larger of that rounded figure and the rate schedule's `minimum_call_hours`; the result is `billable_hours`, always a multiple of 0.25 and always carried to two decimal places. For the `LATE_CANCELLATION` variant, `billable_hours` is set directly to the schedule's `late_cancellation_pay_hours` and neither the quarter-hour round-up nor the minimum-call floor is applied.

**Step three — the service component.** Select the hourly rate by the assignment's `credential_tier_snapshot`: `master_hourly_cents` for a `MASTER` snapshot, `certified_hourly_cents` for `CERTIFIED`, `registered_hourly_cents` for `REGISTERED`, and `provisional_hourly_cents` for `PROVISIONAL`. Select the delivery multiplier by the assignment's `delivery_mode`: exactly 1 for `IN_PERSON`, and the schedule's `remote_rate_multiplier` for both `VRI` and `TELEPHONIC`. Multiply `billable_hours` by the selected hourly rate and then by the multiplier, and round the product to whole cents half up; that is `service_cents`.

**Step four — the rare-language premium.** When the hearing's language has `tier_class` `RARE`, compare the already-rounded `service_cents` against the schedule's `rare_premium_bracket_cents`: a service component at or above the breakpoint takes `rare_premium_high_pct` and one below it takes `rare_premium_low_pct`. Multiply `service_cents` by the selected rate and round to whole cents half up; that is `rare_premium_cents`. For any language that is not `RARE`, `rare_premium_cents` is 0. The bracket test is keyed on the rounded service component alone and never on a running total — adding travel before the bracket test would select the higher rate on some claims and is explicitly not the rule.

**Step five — the travel reimbursement.** Travel is owed only for in-person work beyond a free radius. If the assignment's `delivery_mode` is anything other than `IN_PERSON`, or if the variant's one-way mileage is at or below the schedule's `travel_free_radius_miles`, the reimbursable mileage is 0. Otherwise the reimbursable mileage is twice the one-way figure, because the interpreter drives there and back once past the radius. Multiply the reimbursable mileage by the schedule's `travel_mileage_rate_cents` and round to whole cents half up; that is `travel_cents`. Note that the free radius is a cliff rather than a deduction: an interpreter one mile past the radius is reimbursed for the whole round trip, not for the excess.

**Step six — summation, in that order.** Add the three already-rounded components — `service_cents`, `rare_premium_cents`, and `travel_cents` — to obtain the total. The `PROVISIONAL` variant writes that total to `provisional_total_cents`; the `FINAL` and `LATE_CANCELLATION` variants write it to `final_total_cents` and leave `provisional_total_cents` frozen at whatever the provisional run produced. As §2.8 insists, each component is rounded before the addition; summing three unrounded components and rounding once at the end is a different number and is not the rule.

The three variants therefore differ in exactly two inputs and one shortcut. `PROVISIONAL` is the encumbrance amount, computed at confirmation from the scheduled window and the estimated mileage, and added to the courthouse's `encumbered_pay_cents`. `FINAL` is computed at completion from the check-in-to-actual-end window and the reported mileage; it writes `final_total_cents`, releases the encumbrance and adds to `obligated_pay_cents`, and it diverges from the provisional figure whenever the served window or the reported mileage differs from what was scheduled or estimated. `LATE_CANCELLATION` is computed when a confirmed assignment is cancelled inside the schedule's `late_cancellation_hours`; it pays `late_cancellation_pay_hours` at the snapshot tier's rate with the delivery multiplier applied, adds any rare-language premium on that service component, contributes no travel at all, and sets `late_cancellation` to true.

Once a claim is `FINALIZED` the payee drives it. The `Interpreter` who served the assignment submits their own claim, `FINALIZED → SUBMITTED`. A `FiscalOfficer` then approves it, `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, but only while the underlying assignment's `compliance_status` reads exactly `CLEAR`: an assignment `UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW` or carrying a `SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT` holds the money, and approval is refused with `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_OPEN`. Finally the same `FiscalOfficer` role issues the warrant, `APPROVED → PAID`, supplying a `payment_reference` that is recorded on the claim; that transition moves `final_total_cents` out of the interpreter's `pending_pay_cents` and into their `paid_pay_cents`. Each of the three transitions increments the claim's `version` by 1, and each also refreshes the hosting courthouse's ledger row and increments *its* `version` by 1 — even though none of the three actually moves a total, because a claim reading `FINALIZED`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `PAID` sits in `obligated_pay_cents` alike. The interpreter's own row is touched by the payment alone, that being the only one of the three that changes `pending_pay_cents` or `paid_pay_cents`; submission and approval leave the interpreter's `version` where it was. A `VOIDED` claim accepts none of them, and any of the three requested from the wrong status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAY_CLAIM_STATE`. Claims are readable individually and by list, the list filterable by `assignment_id`, `rate_schedule_id`, and `status`; neither read is narrowed to the caller's own rows, so an `Interpreter` sees every claim in the program and not merely the claims compensating their own assignments.

### 3.9. Quality Review, Discipline and Appeals

A complaint is the program's disciplinary instrument, and its workflow is built around the principle that no single person may carry it from intake to sanction. Intake is open to a `QualityReviewer`, a `SchedulingCoordinator`, or a `ProgramAdmin`, who names the `interpreter_id` complained about, optionally an `assignment_id` when the complaint concerns a specific appearance rather than the interpreter's conduct generally, a `category` of `ACCURACY`, `ETHICS`, `PROFESSIONALISM`, or `NO_SHOW`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, or `SEVERE`, and a `narrative` describing what happened. Filing sets `status` to `FILED`, records `filed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and leaves `reviewer_user_id`, `reviewer_recommendation`, `adjudicator_user_id`, `disposition`, `adjudicated_at` and `appeal_filed_at` all null with `remand_count` at 0. Intake changes neither the interpreter's roster standing nor their `complaint_points` — nothing is proven yet. The two foreign keys are resolved interpreter first and assignment second, and they are never cross-checked against each other: a complaint may name an assignment that belongs to some other interpreter entirely and is accepted exactly as submitted. When an `assignment_id` is present, however, the named assignment's `compliance_status` moves `CLEAR → UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW`, which immediately freezes fiscal approval of that assignment's pay claim; an assignment already reading `UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW` or `SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT` is left on that value with its `version` unchanged.

Review is a `QualityReviewer`'s exclusive act, `FILED → REVIEWED`. The reviewer supplies a `recommendation` of `SUBSTANTIATE` or `DISMISS` and written `findings`; the service records `reviewer_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and copies the recommendation into `reviewer_recommendation`. `findings` is a required non-empty string but it is not persisted anywhere — the complaint resource exposes no findings field at all. The reviewer must not be the filer: a caller whose `X-User-ID` equals `filed_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A complaint the program itself filed after a no-show carries the acting `SchedulingCoordinator`'s identifier as its filer, so any quality reviewer may take it up.

Adjudication belongs to a `ProgramAdmin` and to nobody else — not to the reviewer whose recommendation it weighs, and that recommendation is advisory only: the administrator may substantiate a complaint the reviewer recommended dismissing and dismiss one the reviewer recommended substantiating, because `reviewer_recommendation` is never compared against `disposition`. The administrator supplies a `disposition` of `SUBSTANTIATED` or `DISMISSED`, and on a substantiation also a `sanction_type` and, for a suspension, the `suspension_days` the suspension is to run; `suspension_days` is shape-checked as an integer of at least 1 wherever it appears but is otherwise ignored on a `LETTER`, a `PROBATION` or a dismissal, and a `sanction_type` accompanying a `DISMISSED` disposition is likewise accepted and ignored. Both branches record `adjudicator_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and `adjudicated_at` from the logical clock, which starts the appeal window; both set `disposition` to match; and both increment the complaint's `version` by 1.

Which sanctions are available is constrained by the intake severity, and the mapping is a sliding window rather than a cumulative one: a `MINOR` complaint admits `LETTER` or `PROBATION`; a `MAJOR` complaint admits `PROBATION` or `SUSPENSION`; a `SEVERE` complaint admits `SUSPENSION` or `REVOCATION`. A `sanction_type` outside the window its complaint's `severity` permits is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That comparison needs the complaint row and is therefore made *after* the `REVIEWED` state gate rather than with the request-shape checks of §2.12: a complaint in the wrong state reports `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` even when the sanction requested is also outside its window. The two shape defects that do sit at their ordinary place in the ladder are a substantiation naming no `sanction_type` at all and a `SUSPENSION` naming no `suspension_days`; both are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and both are raised ahead of even the complaint's own path resolution.

A dismissal is the quiet branch: `REVIEWED → DISMISSED`, no disciplinary action is created, and the interpreter's `complaint_points` is unchanged. If the complaint named an assignment, that assignment's `compliance_status` returns `UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW → CLEAR` — but only when no *other* complaint against that same assignment is still in `FILED`, `REVIEWED`, or `APPEALED`, because one dismissal does not clear a second open file.

A substantiation is the loud branch, `REVIEWED → SUBSTANTIATED`, and it cascades in four hops. **Hop one** creates a single disciplinary action in `IN_EFFECT` status, recording `complaint_id` and copying `interpreter_id` from the complaint, with `action_type` set to the requested sanction and `points` taken from the sanction scale — 5 for a `LETTER`, 10 for `PROBATION`, 12 for a `SUSPENSION`, 20 for a `REVOCATION`. Its `effective_from` is the logical-clock date, and its `effective_through` is 365 days after `effective_from` for a `LETTER`, 180 days after for `PROBATION`, the requested `suspension_days` after for a `SUSPENSION`, and null for a `REVOCATION`, which is permanent. **Hop two** is triggered by that action becoming in-effect: the interpreter's `complaint_points` is recomputed to include the new points, both quality scores are recomputed, and the interpreter's `roster_status` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` for a suspension, with `suspension_through` set to the action's `effective_through` whatever that field previously held — the roster move itself fires only from `ACTIVE`, so a holder already `SUSPENDED` or sitting in `INACTIVE` keeps that standing while still taking the new `suspension_through` and a `version` increment — or `ACTIVE → REVOKED` for a revocation, which also clears `suspension_through` back to null. A `LETTER` or `PROBATION` moves neither field: it recomputes the interpreter's derived figures and increments their `version` by 1, and nothing else. **Hop three** applies to a suspension: every assignment of that interpreter whose `service_status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose hearing's `scheduled_start` date falls on or before `suspension_through` is cancelled with `cancel_reason_code` set to `INTERPRETER_SUSPENDED`, processed in assignment `created_at` ascending order, each cancellation running the ordinary lead-time branch of §3.7 rather than a special case of its own — voiding an encumbered claim and returning a `COVERED` hearing to `SCHEDULED` when the lead time to `scheduled_start` is at or above the governing rate schedule's `late_cancellation_hours`, and finalizing that claim on the late-cancellation terms and moving the hearing to `UNMET` when it is below, which is the common outcome precisely because a suspension bites on the days immediately ahead. A revocation is broader still: it cancels every `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` assignment of the interpreter regardless of date, again with `cancel_reason_code` set to `INTERPRETER_SUSPENDED` rather than a code of its own, and it drives every credential of theirs in `PENDING_WRITTEN`, `PENDING_ORAL`, `PENDING_ETHICS`, `ACTIVE`, or `LAPSED` to `REVOKED`, processed in credential `created_at` ascending order. An interpreter reaches `REVOKED` from `ACTIVE`, from `SUSPENDED`, or from `INACTIVE` by this same trigger, and never leaves it. **Hop four** applies when the complaint named an assignment: that assignment's `compliance_status` moves `UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW → SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT`, which keeps its pay claim frozen at `SUBMITTED`. Every row a hop touches has its `version` incremented by 1 for that hop, and a row that more than one hop touches advances once per touching hop: a revocation increments its interpreter's `version` once for the roster move itself and once more for each of their credentials that was `ACTIVE` when the revocation reached it.

Appeal is the disciplined interpreter's own remedy and no one else's. The interpreter supplies written `grounds`, and the complaint moves `SUBSTANTIATED → APPEALED` with `appeal_filed_at` recording the logical clock. `grounds` is required and must be a non-empty string, but like a review's `findings` it is validated and then discarded rather than stored on the complaint. The filing must fall within 30 days of `adjudicated_at`, measured on the logical clock, with day 30 exactly still inside the window and anything later refused as `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The disciplinary action stays `IN_EFFECT` while the appeal is pending, so an appealing interpreter remains suspended.

Deciding the appeal is again a `ProgramAdmin` act, and the decider must not be the administrator whose `X-User-ID` is recorded in `adjudicator_user_id`; a self-review of one's own adjudication is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The decision carries an `outcome` of `UPHELD`, `VACATED`, or `REMANDED`, and the three branches are specified with equal force.

`APPEALED → UPHELD` confirms the sanction: the disciplinary action stays `IN_EFFECT` and the interpreter's `complaint_points` is unchanged. `UPHELD` is terminal.

`APPEALED → VACATED` overturns it. The complaint's `disposition` is cleared to null. The disciplinary action created by this complaint's adjudication moves `IN_EFFECT → RESCINDED`, at which point its points drop out of both quality scores and out of `complaint_points`, all three of which are recomputed. The action in question is the most recently created action of this complaint that still reads `IN_EFFECT`; an action the daily run has already moved to `EXPIRED` is left exactly as it stands, and the appeal decision then completes without any rescission at all. The interpreter's `roster_status` moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` when no other suspension action of theirs remains `IN_EFFECT`, and `suspension_through` returns to null. If the complaint named an assignment, that assignment's `compliance_status` moves `SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT → CLEAR`, releasing the fiscal hold on its claim — and, exactly as on a dismissal, only when no *other* complaint against that same assignment is still `FILED`, `REVIEWED`, or `APPEALED`. What a vacatur does *not* do is resurrect the assignments the sanction cancelled: those rows stay `CANCELLED` with their reason codes intact and their hearings stay wherever the cascade left them. Nor does it undo a revocation's wider damage: the credentials a revocation drove to `REVOKED` stay `REVOKED`, and because only a `SUSPENDED` holder is returned to `ACTIVE`, an interpreter whose `roster_status` reads `REVOKED` stays there — vacating a revocation restores neither the licences nor the roster standing, and the rescission of the action is the whole of its effect on the interpreter. `VACATED` is terminal.

`APPEALED → FILED` is the remand, and it is the program's one cycle on the complaint dimension: the complaint returns to the beginning of its own workflow, `remand_count` is increased by 1, and `reviewer_user_id`, `reviewer_recommendation`, `adjudicator_user_id`, `disposition` and `adjudicated_at` are all cleared to null. `appeal_filed_at` is deliberately *not* cleared, so a remanded complaint still records the instant its appeal was filed even while it sits back in `FILED`. Its compensating effects match a vacatur's on the discipline side: the disciplinary action produced by the now-erased adjudication moves `IN_EFFECT → RESCINDED`, `complaint_points` is recomputed without it, and the interpreter returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` when no suspension action of theirs remains `IN_EFFECT`. On the compliance side it does nothing at all: a named assignment stays at `SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT` and its pay claim stays frozen, because a remand reopens the complaint rather than resolving it. Because the remand clears `reviewer_user_id`, the same quality reviewer is free to review the complaint a second time — but the original filer still may not, since `filed_by_user_id` is never cleared. The full cycle is therefore `FILED → REVIEWED → SUBSTANTIATED → APPEALED → FILED`, and the compliance dimension has a cycle of its own: `CLEAR → UNDER_QUALITY_REVIEW → SUBSTANTIATED_DEFECT → CLEAR`.

Disciplinary actions have a short lifecycle of their own. `IN_EFFECT` is where adjudication leaves them. `IN_EFFECT → EXPIRED` is clock-driven: once `effective_through` is not null and the logical-clock date has moved strictly past it, the daily operations run expires the action — and an expired action's `points` still count toward `complaint_points` and toward both quality scores, because the program remembers a served sanction. `IN_EFFECT → RESCINDED` is the appeal-driven leg described above, and a rescinded action's points drop out of every figure. Both are terminal and both increment the action's `version` by 1. Complaints are readable individually and by list, the list filterable by `interpreter_id`, `assignment_id`, `status`, and `severity`; disciplinary actions are likewise readable individually and by list, filterable by `complaint_id`, `interpreter_id`, `status`, and `action_type`. None of those four reads is narrowed to the caller's own rows: an `Interpreter` reading either collection sees every complaint and every disciplinary action in the program, and the ownership rule of §2.12 constrains only the appeal they file.

### 3.10. Language-Access Compliance and the Daily Operations Run

The state's language-access plan judges a courthouse by a single number, and the program computes it as a frozen snapshot rather than a live query so that the figure a court reported last quarter cannot change underneath it. A `ProgramAdmin` or `QualityReviewer` produces a report by naming the `courthouse_id` and the reporting window's `period_start` and `period_end`, both plain `YYYY-MM-DD` dates. The window is half-open on the hearing's `scheduled_start`: it begins at midnight UTC on `period_start` inclusive and ends at midnight UTC on `period_end` exclusive. `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start` — otherwise the request is malformed and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the window must be entirely in the past, meaning midnight UTC on `period_end` is at or before the current logical clock, and it may span at most 400 days, the span measured as the plain whole-day difference between the two dates so that a `period_end` exactly 400 days after `period_start` is accepted and 401 days is refused; a window that is still open or too long is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

The report's pool is every hearing at the named courthouse whose `scheduled_start` falls inside that window and whose `status` is either `SERVED` or `UNMET`. Hearings still `SCHEDULED` or `COVERED` are not yet resolved and are excluded; hearings that were `CONTINUED` are excluded too, which is what keeps a postponement from depressing a court's coverage rate. From that pool, `resolved_hearing_count` is the size of the pool, `served_hearing_count` counts the pool members whose `status` is `SERVED`, `unmet_hearing_count` counts those whose `status` is `UNMET`, and `rare_language_unmet_count` counts the unmet subset restricted to hearings whose language has `tier_class` `RARE`.

`coverage_rate_pct` is 100 multiplied by `served_hearing_count`, divided by `resolved_hearing_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. When `resolved_hearing_count` is 0 the division is undefined, and the program pins the convention: `coverage_rate_pct` is exactly 100.00 for an empty pool. The verdict in `compliance_status` then follows three ordered tests. A `coverage_rate_pct` below 85.00 is `NON_COMPLIANT`. Otherwise, a `coverage_rate_pct` below 95.00, or a `rare_language_unmet_count` greater than 0, is `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` — a court that hits its overall target while leaving a single rare-language hearing uninterpreted still owes the state a corrective plan. Otherwise the verdict is `COMPLIANT`. Both thresholds are inclusive at the floor, so exactly 95.00 with no rare-language unmet hearing is `COMPLIANT` and exactly 85.00 is `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`, never `NON_COMPLIANT`. Finally `by_language` breaks the same window down per language: it is an object keyed by the language's `code`, ordered by `code` ascending, whose value for each language appearing in the pool is an object carrying that language's `resolved`, `served`, and `unmet` counts. A report is immutable once created — it carries no `version` and there is no way to amend it — and producing one leaves the courthouse row itself untouched, its own `version` unchanged. A courthouse's reports are readable as a nested list under the facility, ordered by `period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

The daily operations run is a `ProgramAdmin`-only capability that advances every clock-driven consequence in the program to the current logical time, and its three steps run in a fixed order because the later steps depend on what the earlier ones did.

**Step one, credential expiry.** Every credential in `ACTIVE` status whose `expires_on` date lies strictly before the current logical-clock date, taken in credential `created_at` ascending order, moves `ACTIVE → LAPSED` with its `tier` cleared, the owning language's `active_credentialed_count` is decremented, the holder's `credential_tier` and `credentialed_language_ids` are recomputed, and a holder left with no active credential at all drops `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`, which cancels each of their `PROPOSED` and `CONFIRMED` assignments in assignment `created_at` ascending order with `cancel_reason_code` set to `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`. Each of those cancellations runs the ordinary lead-time branch of §3.7 rather than a special case of its own: an assignment cancelled with at least the governing rate schedule's `late_cancellation_hours` of lead time left has its encumbered claim voided and returns a `COVERED` hearing to `SCHEDULED`, while one cancelled inside that threshold has its claim finalized on the late-cancellation terms and moves its hearing to `UNMET`. Each lapsing credential adds a single `version` increment to its holder's row, covering that credential's recomputation and any roster drop together, plus one further increment for each claim the late branch finalizes — so a holder losing two credentials in one run advances twice on that account.

**Step two, sanction expiry and reinstatement.** Every disciplinary action in `IN_EFFECT` status whose `effective_through` is not null and lies strictly before the current logical-clock date, taken in action `created_at` ascending order, moves `IN_EFFECT → EXPIRED`. A `SUSPENDED` interpreter with no remaining `IN_EFFECT` suspension against them is then reinstated — the roster is walked in interpreter `created_at` ascending order, and only rows actually reading `SUSPENDED` are considered: `suspension_through` is cleared to null, `complaint_points` and both quality scores are recomputed, and their `roster_status` returns to `ACTIVE` when they still hold a credential in `ACTIVE` status, or to `INACTIVE` when they do not.

**Step three, uncovered hearings.** Every hearing still in `SCHEDULED` status whose `scheduled_start` has been reached or passed by the logical clock, and which has no assignment in `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `COMPLETED`, moves `SCHEDULED → UNMET`, the calendar being walked in `scheduled_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending order; each of that hearing's assignments still in `PROPOSED` is then cancelled, in assignment `created_at` ascending order, with `cancel_reason_code` set to `COORDINATOR_CANCELLED`, and because a merely proposed assignment never carried a claim none of those cancellations moves money or disturbs the hearing the step has just marked `UNMET`. Because this step runs after step one, a hearing that step one has just returned to `SCHEDULED` by cancelling its covering assignment is swept to `UNMET` in the very same call.

The run reports what it did rather than mutating silently. Its response carries `swept_at`, the logical-clock instant the run executed, together with seven identifier arrays: `lapsed_credential_ids`, `downgraded_interpreter_ids`, `expired_action_ids`, `reinstated_interpreter_ids`, `unmet_hearing_ids`, `cancelled_assignment_ids`, and `voided_pay_claim_ids`. Each array lists its rows in the order the run touched them and never repeats an identifier, and `voided_pay_claim_ids` names only the claims the run actually voided — a claim the lead-time branch finalized on late-cancellation terms appears in none of the seven, though its assignment still appears in `cancelled_assignment_ids`. The run is idempotent with respect to the logical clock: invoked a second time without the clock having moved, it returns all-empty arrays and mutates nothing.

The logical clock itself is set by an administrator-only test-driving capability that takes the target instant as `to_iso8601` and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. It mutates no entity, and a target earlier than the clock's present reading is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
