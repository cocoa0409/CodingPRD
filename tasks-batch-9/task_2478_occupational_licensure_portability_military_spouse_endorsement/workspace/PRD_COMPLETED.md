# Licensure Portability API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every year a state licensing agency receives thousands of applications from nurses, therapists, electricians and technicians who already hold a credential somewhere else. A registered nurse who moves across a state line does not become less competent in transit, yet the agency is statutorily obliged to satisfy itself that the out-of-state scope of practice substantially matches what this state licenses, that the applicant is in good standing everywhere they have ever practiced, and that the fee schedule the legislature enacted has been applied correctly. Military families feel the friction most sharply: a spouse who relocates on orders cannot wait ninety days for a full credential review before earning a living, and a service member's documented military training frequently duplicates classroom hours the applicant is now being asked to repeat.

The Licensure Portability API is the agency's system of record for that whole problem. It models three statutory portability pathways — endorsement of an existing out-of-state licence, a universal-recognition pathway gated on in-state residency and good standing, and an expedited pathway reserved for military spouses — and it drives each application through a reviewable lifecycle that produces an auditable substantial-equivalency score, an examination-waiver decision, a military training and experience credit tally, a temporary practice permit that bridges the review period, and finally a minted licence. Fees are never mutated in place: every assessment, statutory waiver, payment, reversal and prorated conversion credit lands as an immutable ledger movement, so an auditor can reconstruct the balance of any application from its own history.

Two properties of the domain shape the entire specification. First, the agency publishes processing-time standards and is measured against them, so the system computes elapsed processing days and a standard-met verdict on every read of an application, not merely at decision time. Second, the agency must protect against the reviewer who evaluates a file also being the officer who approves it, and against national-database findings arriving mid-review; both concerns turn into hard preconditions and automatic cascades rather than manual clean-up procedures. All business resources are mounted under the `/licensure` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that every endpoint in the system inherits: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and audit fields, arithmetic precision, stale-view rejection, resolution order, the global order in which rejections are evaluated, cascade discipline, ledger discipline, and the segregation-of-duties rule.

### 2.1. Agency Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Exactly five roles are recognised, spelled precisely as follows: `Applicant`, `LicensingAnalyst`, `Evaluator`, `BoardDeputy`, and `AgencyAdmin`. If either header is absent — and a header present but carrying the empty string counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` carries any string that is not one of those five, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the role is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Where §4 lists a row as authorised for "Any role", all five roles are permitted.

Two of the five roles are entity-backed, meaning `X-User-ID` names a persisted row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` compare the resource's `applicant_id` to `X-User-ID`. An `Applicant` may only file credentials and training records against their own applicant record, may only open, submit, cure and pay for their own applications, and on any read may only be shown rows that belong to them. Concretely, reading an applicant, an applicant's credential, training-record or disciplinary-record list, an application, an evaluation, a credit award, or an application's fee-ledger or temporary-permit list under a foreign `applicant_id` is refused outright, whereas the three unscoped collection reads — `GET /licensure/applications`, `GET /licensure/credential-evaluations` and `GET /licensure/credit-awards` — instead drop foreign rows silently from both the window and `total_count`; professions, reciprocal agreements, evaluators, review assignments and licences carry no applicant scoping at all and are readable in full by every role. A violation of any of these is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Evaluator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Evaluator` row; the disciplinary finding on an evaluation may only be recorded by the evaluator whose `id` equals the `evaluator_id` of a review assignment booked against that evaluation's application.

The remaining three roles are opaque operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `LicensingAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (as `reviewed_by_user_id` on the evaluation and as `scope_reviewed_by_user_id` on the application when the scope review is recorded) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardDeputy`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; the server records it as `decided_by_user_id` on the application at decision time and compares it only against `scope_reviewed_by_user_id` for the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.11. When `X-User-Role` is `AgencyAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not persisted on any entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`.

Role reach, in narrative terms: an `AgencyAdmin` configures professions and compacts, performs applicant intake, posts national-database findings and resolves them, records exam results, registers evaluators, runs the permit expiry sweep and drives the logical clock. A `LicensingAnalyst` opens and works the substantial-equivalency evaluation, records scope review, finalises evaluations, records deficiencies, awards and revokes military credit, issues temporary permits and books evaluators. An `Evaluator` records the national-database disciplinary finding on evaluations they are assigned to. A `BoardDeputy` issues the final decision and may revoke a temporary permit. An `Applicant` opens, submits, cures and pays for their own applications and files their own credentials and training records. All five roles may perform every read operation, subject to the `Applicant` ownership restriction above.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three transport-level failures reuse the same envelope while falling outside the §5 catalogue: a request whose path matches no declared route answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, a declared path invoked with an undeclared method answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405, and an unexpected server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two query-string parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the window begins; when omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range from 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped. A `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-ten integer is rejected the same way, while either parameter supplied as the empty string counts as absent and takes its default. The per-endpoint filters obey the same discipline: a filter whose value is not a declared member of the enumeration it names is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, a `resolved`-style boolean filter accepts only `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`, a filter supplied as the empty string counts as absent, and an identifier-valued filter that matches nothing simply yields an empty window rather than a 404.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock stands still unless a client moves it, many rows share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were persisted, so a list is always in true creation order. The fee-ledger read of an application is explicitly held to that same ordering, because the ledger is an audit artefact and its read order is part of the contract.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the pagination window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching those filters remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the system carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix of any kind — a random version-4 UUID in the canonical hyphenated 36-character form. Clients never supply an `id`; where an `id` appears as a path segment it is always the identifier of an already-persisted row.

Most resources also carry a standard audit triplet. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first persisted, read from the logical clock described below rather than from wall-clock time. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format recording the most recent mutation of the row, likewise read from the logical clock; it is refreshed on every mutation, including mutations of purely derived fields such as an application's `fee_balance_cents` or an evaluator's `open_assignment_count`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row. `Profession`, `Applicant`, `OutOfStateCredential`, `LicenseApplication`, `CredentialEvaluation`, `MilitaryTrainingRecord`, `CreditAward`, `TemporaryPermit`, `License`, `DisciplinaryRecord`, `Evaluator`, `ReviewAssignment` and `ReciprocalAgreement` all carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

`FeeLedgerEntry` is the single exception. Ledger entries are append-only and are never modified after they are written, so a `FeeLedgerEntry` carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field, and those keys are absent from its representation entirely.

All time-dependent behaviour in the system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and before any client has moved it the clock reads that instant — it never falls back to real time. An `AgencyAdmin` may reposition the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`, and no entity is mutated by the repositioning itself. Every deadline computation, disciplinary decay factor, permit window, expiry sweep, review-window lapse check and elapsed-processing metric described in this document reads that logical clock.

One single rendering serves every instant the API emits. Whenever a response body carries a timestamp — the audit fields, the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` (which is the one instant in the whole system taken from real wall-clock time rather than the logical clock, and which therefore never appears on a resource), the clock endpoint's `current_utc`, and equally every domain instant on a resource (for example `submitted_at`, `review_deadline_at`, `decided_at`, `finalized_at`, `effective_at`, `expires_at`, `converted_at`, `revoked_at`, `closed_at`, `window_start_at`, `window_end_at`, `issued_at`, `expires_on`, `action_effective_at`, `reported_at` and `resolved_at`) — it is written as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, so an instant on a whole second is emitted as `...T00:00:00.000Z` and never as `...T00:00:00Z`. Client-supplied instants are normalised to UTC on the way in, so the rendering is the server's and does not echo whatever spelling, precision or offset the client used; an inbound instant may end in `Z`, carry a numeric offset, or carry no zone marker at all, and a value supplied as a bare calendar date is read as midnight UTC on that date. An instant that is null is emitted as JSON `null`.

### 2.5. Numeric Precision, Cents and Rounding Discipline

Money is integer cents throughout. There is no cent fraction anywhere in the system: fee components, assessments, waiver credits, payments, reversals and prorated conversion credits are all whole numbers of cents, and every intermediate product that produces cents is rounded half up to a whole cent at the point the specification says to round it. Where a formula in §3 pins the rounding order — rounding each component before summing, rather than summing and rounding once — that order is normative, because the two procedures produce different answers.

Scores and ratios are decimal. The substantial-equivalency score and both disciplinary risk indexes are carried to exactly two decimal places, rounded half up, and are constrained to the closed range from 0.00 through 100.00. Requirement hours — the `requirement_hours_total` a profession demands, the `requirement_hours_satisfied` a candidate has earned and the `requirement_hours_remaining` still outstanding — are carried to exactly one decimal place, rounded half up. Waiver and surcharge fractions (`statutory_waiver_pct`, `fee_waiver_pct`, `expedite_surcharge_pct`) are decimals in the closed range from 0.00 through 1.00 carried to two decimal places. A client-supplied decimal is quantised half up to its field's declared precision on the way in — `requirement_hours_total` to one decimal place, and `exam_waiver_min_score`, `fee_waiver_pct` and `expedite_surcharge_pct` to two — and every decimal leaves the system as a JSON number, never as a quoted string. Day counts derived from two instants, and whole-year blocks alike, are always computed by taking the mathematical floor of the elapsed fraction — that is, taking whole elapsed days and discarding any partial day — so a reference instant that actually precedes the earlier instant yields a negative count rather than zero.

### 2.6. Stale-View Rejection on Capacity-Bearing Writes

Two write operations in this system reason about a parent row's current shape before committing, and both therefore demand that the caller declare which version of that parent it actually observed.

Issuing a temporary practice permit requires a non-optional `expected_application_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `LicenseApplication`. If that value does not equal the application's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rationale is substantive rather than mechanical: a permit grants practice authority over a window, and a permit issued against a view of the application that has since moved on — an application that has since been suspended, denied, or already fitted with a permit — would silently stack a second authority on top of an authority the writer never saw. The API refuses instead.

Creating a review assignment requires a non-optional `expected_evaluator_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Evaluator`. If that value does not equal the evaluator's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The evaluator's remaining capacity and calendar geometry are exactly what the analyst was reasoning about when they chose that evaluator, so a booking built on a superseded reading of `open_assignment_count` is refused rather than admitted and reconciled afterwards.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two never carry `expected_application_version` or `expected_evaluator_version`, and no version comparison is performed on them.

### 2.7. Path and Foreign-Key Resolution Order

Where a URL contains a resource identifier, existence is validated left to right, and the first segment that does not resolve decides the response with HTTP 404. A read of the compacts recorded for a profession that does not exist therefore fails with `ERR_PROFESSION_NOT_FOUND` before anything else about the request is considered. The per-type codes are `ERR_PROFESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.

Once every path segment has resolved, the foreign keys carried in the request body are resolved with the same discipline and the same family of 404 codes. The body references that must resolve are `applicant_id`, `profession_id`, `primary_credential_id`, `credential_id`, `application_id`, `training_record_id` and `evaluator_id`; an unresolvable `credential_id` or `primary_credential_id` yields `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND` and an unresolvable `training_record_id` yields `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. This body-reference pass applies to opening an application, opening a credential evaluation, awarding military credit, issuing a temporary permit, creating a review assignment, and posting a disciplinary record; within each of those requests the references are themselves resolved in a fixed order, so that the first unresolvable one decides — `applicant_id`, then `profession_id`, then `primary_credential_id` when opening an application; `application_id` then `credential_id` when opening an evaluation; `application_id` then `training_record_id` when awarding credit; `evaluator_id` then `application_id` when creating a review assignment.

### 2.8. The Order of Rejection

A single request can violate many rules at once, and the agency's appeals process depends on the applicant being told the *same* reason every time. The system therefore evaluates checks in one fixed global order and the **first** violated check decides the response; no later check can mask an earlier one, and no earlier success excuses a later failure. The order is:

1. Authentication — both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. Authorisation — the caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Request shape — every required body field present; every enum value a declared member; every datetime a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instant; every cents field and hour field at or above its declared floor; every array within its size bounds; a profession's `critical_scope_elements` a subset of its `required_scope_elements`; a review assignment's `window_end_at` strictly after its `window_start_at`; a payment's `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero; `profession_code` unique across professions; the pair of `profession_id` and `partner_state` unique across reciprocal agreements; `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Applies to every endpoint.
4. Path existence, left to right, as described in §2.7. The matching 404 code.
5. Body foreign-key existence, as described in §2.7. The matching 404 code.
6. Ownership — an entity-backed caller may only act on its own rows. `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`, 403. This applies to filing an out-of-state credential, filing a military training record, opening an application, submitting, curing, paying, recording a disciplinary finding, and every read whose target belongs to a different applicant when the caller's role is `Applicant`. For the `Evaluator` role the clause reads: `X-User-ID` must equal the `evaluator_id` of a review assignment backing the operation — and this clause is evaluated **only** when at least one `ASSIGNED` review assignment exists for the target application. When no such assignment exists at all, the case is not an ownership violation; it falls to check 18 below.
7. Stale-view rejection — `expected_application_version` equals the application's `version`, and `expected_evaluator_version` equals the evaluator's `version`, as described in §2.6. `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, 412. Applies to permit issuance and review-assignment creation.
8. Application from-state — the application is in a state this operation permits. Submitting requires `DRAFT`; curing requires `DEFICIENT`; recording a deficiency and issuing a board decision require `UNDER_REVIEW`; opening the credential evaluation requires `SUBMITTED`; recording the scope review and finalising the evaluation require the parent application to be in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`; permit issuance, exam-result recording, credit-award creation and credit-award revocation require the application to be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`; review-assignment creation likewise requires `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`. Recording the disciplinary finding and recording a payment impose no from-state condition on the application at all, and neither does opening a fresh application. `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, 409.
9. Permit state — the targeted temporary permit is `ACTIVE`. `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409. Applies to permit revocation.
10. Evaluation write state — the evaluation has no `finalized_at` yet and the dimension being written is still `PENDING`; finalisation additionally requires both dimensions to be non-`PENDING`. `ERR_EVALUATION_ALREADY_FINALIZED`, 409. Applies to scope review, disciplinary finding and finalisation.
11. Evaluation uniqueness — at most one credential evaluation per `application_id`. `ERR_EVALUATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`, 409. Applies to opening a credential evaluation.
12. Compensating-action state — the targeted credit award is still `AWARDED`, and the targeted disciplinary record still has `resolved` false. `ERR_CREDIT_AWARD_ALREADY_REVOKED` and `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_RECORD_ALREADY_RESOLVED` respectively, both 409.
13. Disciplinary bar — reject when the applicant's `good_standing_flag` is false, or when the application's `pathway_risk_index` is at or above the pathway bar of 12.00 under the pathway-selected variant. `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_BAR`, 422. Applies to submitting an application, issuing a temporary permit, and a board decision of `APPROVE`.
14. Segregation of duties — `X-User-ID` must differ from the application's `scope_reviewed_by_user_id`. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. Applies to the board decision, for both `APPROVE` and `DENY`.
15. Credential ownership match — the referenced out-of-state credential's `applicant_id` equals the applicant of the application. `ERR_CREDENTIAL_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to opening an application and to opening a credential evaluation.
16. Evaluator profession match — the evaluator's `profession_code` equals the `profession_code` of the application's profession. `ERR_EVALUATOR_PROFESSION_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to review-assignment creation.
17. Evaluator standing — the evaluator's `status` is `ACTIVE`. `ERR_EVALUATOR_INACTIVE`, 422. Applies to review-assignment creation.
18. Open assignment — the calling evaluator holds a review assignment for this evaluation's application with `status` `ASSIGNED`. `ERR_NO_OPEN_REVIEW_ASSIGNMENT`, 422. Applies to recording the disciplinary finding.
19. Review window still open — the logical clock is at or before that assignment's `window_end_at`. `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_LAPSED`, 422. Applies to recording the disciplinary finding.
20. Evaluation-before-permit — for the `ENDORSEMENT` and `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` pathways a credential evaluation must already exist for the application; the `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED` pathway is exempt, because its whole purpose is to authorise practice during full review. `ERR_EVALUATION_REQUIRED_FOR_PERMIT`, 422. Applies to permit issuance.
21. Evaluation completeness — the application's credential evaluation exists and its `overall_status` is `COMPLETE`. `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_COMPLETE`, 422. Applies to a board decision of `APPROVE`.
22. Military-spouse eligibility — the `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED` pathway requires the applicant's `military_status` to be `MILITARY_SPOUSE`. `ERR_MILITARY_STATUS_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to submitting an application.
23. Residency — the `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` pathway requires the applicant's `residency_months` to be at least the profession's `universal_recognition_min_residency_months`. `ERR_RESIDENCY_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET`, 422. Applies to submitting an application.
24. Qualifying credential — the qualifying set is non-empty and the nominated `primary_credential_id` belongs to it. `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_CREDENTIAL`, 422. Applies to submitting an application.
25. One live application per profession — no other application for the same applicant and profession pair is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `DEFICIENT`, `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`. `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION`, 409. Applies to opening an application.
26. Training-record ownership match — the training record's `applicant_id` equals the application's `applicant_id`. `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to awarding credit.
27. Training-record verification — the training record's `verified` flag is true. `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_UNVERIFIED`, 422. Applies to awarding credit.
28. Credit uniqueness — no credit award with `status` `AWARDED` already exists for this pair of `application_id` and `training_record_id`. `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_ALREADY_CREDITED`, 409. Applies to awarding credit.
29. Permit start not in the past — the requested `effective_at` is at or after the logical clock. `ERR_PERMIT_EFFECTIVE_IN_PAST`, 422. Applies to permit issuance.
30. Permit window collision — as specified in §3.8. `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409. Applies to permit issuance.
31. Evaluator calendar collision — as specified in §3.6. `ERR_EVALUATOR_SLOT_CONFLICT`, 409. Applies to review-assignment creation.
32. Evaluator capacity — the evaluator's `open_assignment_count` plus one must be at most their `max_concurrent_reviews`. `ERR_EVALUATOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409. Applies to review-assignment creation.
33. Requirement hours — the application's `requirement_hours_remaining` is exactly 0.0. `ERR_REQUIREMENT_HOURS_UNSATISFIED`, 422. Applies to a board decision of `APPROVE`.
34. National examination — either the evaluation's `exam_waiver_granted` is true or the application's `national_exam_passed` is true. `ERR_NATIONAL_EXAM_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to a board decision of `APPROVE`.
35. Jurisprudence examination — either the profession's `jurisprudence_exam_required` is false, or the application's `jurisprudence_exam_passed` is true, or an unexpired reciprocal agreement for this profession and the applicant's state has `jurisprudence_waiver_allowed` true. `ERR_JURISPRUDENCE_EXAM_REQUIRED`, 422. Applies to a board decision of `APPROVE`.
36. Fee balance settled — the application's `fee_balance_cents` is at most zero. `ERR_FEE_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING`, 402. Applies to permit issuance and to a board decision of `APPROVE`.
37. No overpayment — a payment's `amount_cents` is at most the application's current `fee_balance_cents`. `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, 422. Applies to recording a payment.

A board decision of `DENY` is deliberately lighter than `APPROVE`: it is subject only to checks 1 through 8 and check 14, and skips every substantive check from 21 onward. An application that cannot lawfully be approved must still be deniable.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline When an Application Leaves the Reviewable Set

An application is *reviewable* while its `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`. Two events push an application out of that set involuntarily — a disciplinary record that destroys the applicant's good standing, and a denial — and both carry the same two-part cascade, run in this order: every temporary permit of that application whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `REVOKED`, and then every review assignment of that application whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` moves to `RELEASED`, each set walked in `created_at` ascending order. Each of those child transitions is itself a mutation: the child's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, the revoked permit appends its fee reversal and forces a recomputation of the application's `fee_balance_cents`, and the released assignment forces a recomputation of its evaluator's `open_assignment_count` with the evaluator's own `version` incrementing.

Recovery is deliberately asymmetric. When good standing is restored the application returns to the reviewable set, but permits revoked during the suspension are **not** restored — the applicant must ask for a new permit — and review assignments released during the suspension are not reinstated either. Moving to `DEFICIENT`, by contrast, is *not* leaving the reviewable set: an `ACTIVE` permit stays `ACTIVE` across a deficiency cycle, and assignments stay `ASSIGNED`.

Whenever the server changes a row on its own initiative to preserve one of these invariants, the change is a full mutation of that row — `updated_at` refreshed, `version` incremented — exactly as though a client had requested it. This includes purely derived fields: recomputing `fee_balance_cents`, `requirement_hours_satisfied`, `requirement_hours_remaining`, `open_assignment_count`, `disciplinary_risk_index_reported`, `disciplinary_risk_index_effective` or `good_standing_flag` all count as mutations of their owning row. An application's `pathway_risk_index` is the sole exception: it is never stored but recomputed from the applicant's current index on every read, so a change to the applicant's index moves the figure the application reports without mutating the application, touching its `updated_at` or incrementing its `version`.

### 2.10. The Append-Only Fee Ledger

Money moves only by appending a `FeeLedgerEntry`. Each entry names the `application_id` whose balance it moves, an `entry_type` drawn from `ASSESSMENT`, `WAIVER_CREDIT`, `PAYMENT`, `EXAM_FEE_REVERSAL`, `PERMIT_FEE`, `PERMIT_FEE_REVERSAL` and `CONVERSION_CREDIT`, a signed `amount_cents`, and a fixed machine `memo` string determined by the entry type, namely `submit assessment` for an `ASSESSMENT`, `statutory waiver credit` for a `WAIVER_CREDIT`, `applicant payment` for a `PAYMENT`, `exam fee reversal` for an `EXAM_FEE_REVERSAL`, `permit fee` for a `PERMIT_FEE`, `permit fee reversal` for a `PERMIT_FEE_REVERSAL` and `permit conversion credit` for a `CONVERSION_CREDIT`. `ASSESSMENT` and `PERMIT_FEE` entries carry positive amounts; `WAIVER_CREDIT`, `PAYMENT`, `EXAM_FEE_REVERSAL`, `PERMIT_FEE_REVERSAL` and `CONVERSION_CREDIT` entries all carry negative amounts.

An application's `fee_balance_cents` is, by construction, the arithmetic sum of the `amount_cents` of every ledger entry whose `application_id` is that application. There is no status filter on the sum and no notion of a voided entry, because entries are immutable; the read order of `created_at` ascending pins the audit trail but does not change the total. Every append recomputes the balance immediately.

The ledger is engineered so that cents are conserved across compensating pairs. A `PERMIT_FEE` of some amount followed by a `PERMIT_FEE_REVERSAL` of exactly the negative of that amount returns `fee_balance_cents` to precisely its pre-issuance value, for any amount — the reversal is full, never prorated. Prorating appears in exactly one place, the conversion credit described in §3.9.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties in Licensure Decisions

The officer who forms the scope-of-practice opinion may not be the officer who signs the licence. When a `LicensingAnalyst` records the scope review of an evaluation, the acting `X-User-ID` is stamped onto both the evaluation's `reviewed_by_user_id` and the parent application's `scope_reviewed_by_user_id`. When a `BoardDeputy` later issues the decision, their `X-User-ID` is compared against that stamp, and if the two are equal the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The comparison is on user identifier alone, not on role, so an operator who acted as an analyst on a file is barred from deciding it even when they legitimately hold board authority. The rule guards both branches of the decision — a self-reviewing officer may neither approve nor deny.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Regulatory Frame: Professions, Compacts and Evaluators

Before anyone can apply, an `AgencyAdmin` registers the regulated profession itself. Registration supplies the agency's short `profession_code` — from two to eight uppercase letters `A` through `Z`, with no digits or punctuation admitted, unique across all professions, and reused later as the prefix of every licence number minted for that profession — together with a human-readable `title`. The substantive content is the scope requirement: `required_scope_elements` is an ordered list of between 1 and 12 unique scope-of-practice element codes, each drawn from uppercase letters, digits and underscores only, and its order is fixed at registration and is the canonical order in which matched and missing elements are later reported. A subset of those may be nominated as `critical_scope_elements`, defaulting to the empty list; every member of that subset must also appear in `required_scope_elements`, and a submission that names an outsider is a malformed request. Registration also fixes `required_practice_hours`, the documented supervised practice hours that count as a complete hours record and which must be at least 1, and `requirement_hours_total`, the overall licensure requirement hours a candidate must satisfy, which must be strictly greater than zero and is carried to one decimal place.

Three more registration values govern review policy. `exam_waiver_min_score` is the substantial-equivalency score at or above which the national examination is waived; it lies between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive with two decimal places, and the comparison against it is inclusive of the threshold itself. `jurisprudence_exam_required` states whether this state's own law examination must be passed before licensure and defaults to true. `universal_recognition_min_residency_months` is the number of completed in-state residency months the universal-recognition pathway demands and may be zero. `standard_processing_days`, at least 1, is the published processing-time standard used to compute the review deadline for ordinary reviews. Every registration value named in this subsection is mandatory except `critical_scope_elements`, which defaults to the empty list, and `jurisprudence_exam_required`, which defaults to true; omitting any other — including `universal_recognition_min_residency_months` and each of the five fee-schedule amounts — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The remainder of registration is the fee schedule, all in integer cents and none of them negative: `base_fee_cents`, charged on every pathway; `endorsement_fee_cents`, charged only on the endorsement and universal-recognition pathways; `exam_fee_cents`, the national examination seat fee, which is reversed when the examination turns out to be waived; `jurisprudence_fee_cents`, charged when the profession requires the jurisprudence examination; and `permit_fee_cents`, the list price of one temporary practice permit before any statutory waiver. Finally, `expedite_surcharge_pct` — a fraction between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive with two decimal places — is the proportion of the pre-surcharge subtotal added when an applicant buys expedited processing.

Interstate compacts are recorded against a profession as reciprocal agreements. Each names a `partner_state` as exactly two uppercase letters, a `fee_waiver_pct` between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive at two decimal places, a mandatory `expires_on` instant, and two permissions: `exam_waiver_allowed`, which lets the national examination be waived irrespective of the equivalency score, and `jurisprudence_waiver_allowed`, which waives this state's jurisprudence examination; both default to false. A compact applies while the logical clock is at or before `expires_on` — the expiry instant itself is still inside the compact's life. Only one compact may exist for a given profession and partner state, so the compact matching an applicant's `state_code` for a profession is always unique and never needs a tie-break. Compacts are readable per profession as a list.

Contract reviewers are registered separately as evaluators. Each carries a `full_name`, the `profession_code` they are credentialed to review — recorded as a free-form non-empty string that is neither pattern-checked nor required to name an already-registered profession, and later matched against a profession's own `profession_code` by exact string equality — a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` defaulting to `ACTIVE`, and a `max_concurrent_reviews` cap of at least 1 that defaults to 2. A newly registered evaluator has an `open_assignment_count` of 0.

### 3.2. Intake: Applicants, Out-of-State Credentials and Service Records

Applicant intake is performed by an `AgencyAdmin`, and the `id` of the row created is exactly the value that person will later present as `X-User-ID` when acting in the `Applicant` role. Intake records the applicant's `full_name`, their current `state_code` as exactly two uppercase letters — the value later matched against a compact's `partner_state` — an optional `residency_months` count of completed in-state residency months defaulting to 0 and never negative, and an optional `military_status` drawn from `NONE`, `SERVICE_MEMBER`, `MILITARY_SPOUSE` and `VETERAN`, defaulting to `NONE`. At intake the applicant's `good_standing_flag` is true, both `disciplinary_risk_index_reported` and `disciplinary_risk_index_effective` are 0.00, and `fee_waiver_eligible` is derived immediately: it is true exactly when `military_status` is `SERVICE_MEMBER`, `MILITARY_SPOUSE` or `VETERAN`, and false when it is `NONE`.

An applicant — or an `AgencyAdmin` acting on their behalf — files each out-of-state credential against the applicant record. A filing carries the `issuing_state` as two uppercase letters, the `license_number` exactly as printed by that state, the `scope_elements` the issuing state grants under the credential as between 0 and 12 unique uppercase codes, the `practice_hours` documented against it as a non-negative integer, the `issued_at` instant the credential was granted, an optional `status` drawn from `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED`, `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED` defaulting to `ACTIVE`, and an optional `verified_good_standing` flag defaulting to false which is set true once the issuing state's verification letter has arrived. Only credentials that are both `ACTIVE` and `verified_good_standing` can carry an application; that filter is applied once, at submission, and is examined in §3.4.

Military training and field experience are filed the same way, as training records against the applicant. Each record names a `category` of `MOS_TRAINING`, `FIELD_EXPERIENCE` or `JOINT_SERVICE_TRANSCRIPT`, the `documented_hours` shown on the service transcript as an integer of at least 1, the `source_document` identifier of the transcript or evaluation letter backing those hours, and a `verified` flag defaulting to false which agency staff set once they have inspected the transcript. Unverified records may be filed freely; they simply cannot be credited.

Both collections, and the applicant's disciplinary history, are readable per applicant as lists. The credential list accepts an optional `status` filter, and the disciplinary list accepts an optional `resolved` filter.

### 3.3. National-Database Discipline: Two Risk Indexes and the Good-Standing Gate

Sister-state discipline reaches the agency through national database sweeps, and an `AgencyAdmin` posts each finding as a disciplinary record. A posting names the `applicant_id` it concerns, the `source_state` that took the action as two uppercase letters, the `source_database` it surfaced from — one of `NPDB`, `NURSYS` or `STATE_BOARD_DIRECT` — the `severity` of the action, drawn from `ADVISORY`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENSION` and `REVOCATION`, and two distinct instants: `action_effective_at`, when the sister state's action took effect, and `reported_at`, when the national database actually surfaced it. A freshly posted record has `resolved` false and `resolved_at` null.

The gap between those two instants is the heart of this subsection. The agency scores disciplinary exposure two ways at once, because a stale action that only just appeared in a database is a different kind of risk from one the applicant disclosed years ago. `disciplinary_risk_index_reported` ages every action from the date the national database surfaced it, so recently-surfaced old actions have barely decayed and score high. `disciplinary_risk_index_effective` ages every action from the sister state's own effective date, so those same rows have decayed further — and because the two variants sort the applicant's history by different keys, the order in which the sweep processes rows can differ between them as well. Both are recomputed from scratch on every posting and on every resolution, and the applicant's `version` increments on each such recomputation.

Each sweep runs as follows. Gather every disciplinary record belonging to the applicant whose `resolved` flag is false; resolved rows leave both sweeps entirely, and records are never filtered by `source_database`. Order the gathered rows by that variant's timestamp key ascending — `reported_at` for the reported variant, `action_effective_at` for the effective variant — breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, then, where `created_at` is identical too, by the order in which the records were persisted, and never by identifier. Begin a running total at 0.00 and walk the rows in that order. For each row, measure its age as the number of whole 365-day blocks that have elapsed from the variant's timestamp to the logical clock, discarding any partial block. Derive a decay factor by subtracting, from 1.0, the product of 0.20 and that whole-year age, but never let the factor fall below a floor of 0.20 no matter how old the action is; no ceiling is imposed on the other side, so a variant timestamp lying in the future of the logical clock yields a negative whole-year age and therefore a factor above 1.0. Multiply the row's severity point value — 5 for `ADVISORY`, 15 for `PROBATION`, 30 for `SUSPENSION`, 60 for `REVOCATION` — by that factor, and round the product half up to two decimal places to obtain the row's contribution. Add the contribution to the running total, and then, if the running total now exceeds the ceiling of 100.00, clamp it back to exactly 100.00 before moving to the next row. Because the clamp is applied after every row rather than only once at the end, the result is sensitive to the processing order established above. The stored index is the final running total, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Good standing is a conjunction of two independent conditions, and both must hold for `good_standing_flag` to be true: the applicant must have no unresolved disciplinary record whose `severity` is `SUSPENSION` or `REVOCATION`, and `disciplinary_risk_index_reported` must be strictly below the good-standing ceiling of 25.00. Note the asymmetry — the good-standing gate always reads the reported variant, never the effective one.

The variant that a given application is judged against is different again, and depends on the pathway. An application's `pathway_risk_index` mirrors `disciplinary_risk_index_reported` when its `pathway` is `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION`, and mirrors `disciplinary_risk_index_effective` for the `ENDORSEMENT` and `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED` pathways. The pathway bar is 12.00: an index strictly below 12.00 clears the bar, and an index at or above 12.00 fails it with `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_BAR`.

When a posting flips `good_standing_flag` from true to false, the system does not wait for a human. Every application of that applicant sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT` is moved to `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` — three separate transitions, `SUBMITTED → SUSPENDED_REVIEW`, `UNDER_REVIEW → SUSPENDED_REVIEW` and `DEFICIENT → SUSPENDED_REVIEW`, each incrementing the application's `version` — and each suspended application then sheds its dependents exactly as §2.9 prescribes: its `ACTIVE` temporary permits become `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason_code` set to the literal `DISCIPLINARY_ACTION`, each revocation appending its `PERMIT_FEE_REVERSAL` entry and forcing a recomputation of `fee_balance_cents`, and its `ASSIGNED` review assignments become `RELEASED` with the affected evaluators' `open_assignment_count` refreshed. A posting that leaves `good_standing_flag` unchanged — because it was already false, or because the new row is not severe enough to change the conjunction — cascades nothing.

Resolution is the mirror image and is also an `AgencyAdmin` action. Resolving a record sets its `resolved` flag to true and its `resolved_at` to the logical clock, then recomputes both risk-index variants and `good_standing_flag` and increments the applicant's `version`. Resolving a record that is already resolved is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_RECORD_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. When the recomputation flips `good_standing_flag` from false to true, every application of that applicant in `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` re-enters the reviewable set, and where it lands depends on how far it had got: an application that already has a credential evaluation returns along `SUSPENDED_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW`, while an application with no credential evaluation at all returns along `SUSPENDED_REVIEW → SUBMITTED`. Both restorations increment the application's `version`. Permits revoked during the suspension stay revoked and assignments released during the suspension stay released; the applicant must request a new permit and the analyst must book a new reviewer.

### 3.4. Opening an Application and Crossing the Submission Gate

An `Applicant` opens their own application by nominating the `applicant_id` — which must equal their `X-User-ID` — the `profession_id` of the licence sought, the `primary_credential_id` of the out-of-state credential offered as the equivalency basis, and the `pathway`, which is one of `ENDORSEMENT`, `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` or `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED`. An optional `expedited` flag, defaulting to false, purchases expedited processing; it both adds a surcharge and shortens the review deadline. The credential nominated must belong to the same applicant as the application, or the request is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`. An applicant may not hold two live applications for the same profession: if another application for the same applicant and profession is already in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `DEFICIENT`, `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`, the new one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION`.

A newly opened application is in `DRAFT`. Its `submitted_at`, `review_deadline_at` and `decided_at` are all null, its `processing_standard_met` is null, its `processing_elapsed_days` is 0, its `deficiency_cycle_count` is 0, its `statutory_waiver_pct` is 0.00, its `gross_total_cents` and `fee_balance_cents` are 0, its `national_exam_passed` and `jurisprudence_exam_passed` are both false, its `scope_reviewed_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` are both null, its `requirement_hours_satisfied` is 0.0 and its `requirement_hours_remaining` starts equal to the profession's full `requirement_hours_total`. Its `pathway_risk_index` is not stored on the row at all but reported afresh on every read as whichever of the applicant's two risk-index variants the chosen pathway selects, so it is correct from the moment the application is opened and tracks the applicant's current index for the whole life of the application.

Submission is the gate, and it is the applicant's own action along `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Eight conditions must hold. The application must still be in `DRAFT`. The caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the application's `applicant_id`. The applicant's `good_standing_flag` must be true and the application's `pathway_risk_index` must be strictly below 12.00 under the pathway-selected variant, both of which fail as `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_BAR`. Choosing `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED` requires the applicant's `military_status` to be exactly `MILITARY_SPOUSE`, failing as `ERR_MILITARY_STATUS_REQUIRED`. Choosing `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` requires the applicant's `residency_months` to be at least the profession's `universal_recognition_min_residency_months`, failing as `ERR_RESIDENCY_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET`. And finally the qualifying credential test: the applicant must hold at least one out-of-state credential whose `status` is `ACTIVE` **and** whose `verified_good_standing` is true, and the row named by `primary_credential_id` must itself be a member of that qualifying set. Either failure — an empty qualifying set, or a primary credential that is outside it — is `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_CREDENTIAL`.

Crossing the gate freezes the money and starts the clock. The application's `submitted_at` is set to the logical clock and its `status` becomes `SUBMITTED`. Its `review_deadline_at` is derived by choosing a processing allowance and adding it to `submitted_at` as whole days: the allowance is the expedite allowance of 30 days whenever `expedited` is true **or** the pathway is `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED`, and otherwise it is the profession's published `standard_processing_days`. Note that the military-spouse pathway earns the expedited allowance without paying the expedite surcharge, which is keyed on the `expedited` flag alone.

The fee assessment is computed in a pinned order, and the order matters because a later step's input is an earlier step's output.

1. **Components, pre-waiver.** The base component is the profession's `base_fee_cents`. The endorsement component is the profession's `endorsement_fee_cents` when the pathway is `ENDORSEMENT` or `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION`, and zero on the `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED` pathway. The examination component is always the profession's `exam_fee_cents`. The jurisprudence component is the profession's `jurisprudence_fee_cents` when the profession's `jurisprudence_exam_required` is true, and zero otherwise. The gross subtotal is the sum of those four components.
2. **Expedite surcharge.** When `expedited` is true, the surcharge is the gross subtotal multiplied by the profession's `expedite_surcharge_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent; when `expedited` is false the surcharge is zero. The surcharge is keyed on the step-1 subtotal and never on any net figure.
3. **Gross total.** `gross_total_cents` is the gross subtotal plus the expedite surcharge, and it is frozen on the application at this moment.
4. **Waiver tier.** The tier is selected from `gross_total_cents` as computed in step 3, never from the net total that step 6 produces; reading the tier off the net would be circular and is forbidden. If the pathway is `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED`, or the applicant's `military_status` is `SERVICE_MEMBER`, `MILITARY_SPOUSE` or `VETERAN`, then `statutory_waiver_pct` is 1.00 when `gross_total_cents` is at most the military full-waiver cap of 40000 cents, and 0.50 when it is above that cap. Otherwise, if an unexpired reciprocal agreement exists for this profession whose `partner_state` equals the applicant's `state_code` — unexpired meaning the logical clock is at or before its `expires_on` — then `statutory_waiver_pct` takes that compact's `fee_waiver_pct`. Otherwise `statutory_waiver_pct` is 0.00. Military eligibility short-circuits the compact branch entirely: a veteran residing in a partner state gets the military tier, not the compact's fraction.
5. **Waiver credit.** Each of the five money figures — base component, endorsement component, examination component, jurisprudence component and expedite surcharge — is multiplied by `statutory_waiver_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent **individually**, and the five rounded results are then added together. Summing first and rounding once is a different answer and is not permitted.
6. **Net.** The net fee due is `gross_total_cents` minus the waiver credit.

Two ledger entries are then appended, in a pinned order: first an `ASSESSMENT` entry whose `amount_cents` is the positive `gross_total_cents` and whose `memo` is `submit assessment`, then a `WAIVER_CREDIT` entry whose `amount_cents` is the negative of the waiver credit and whose `memo` is `statutory waiver credit`. The `WAIVER_CREDIT` entry is appended even when the waiver credit is zero, so a submitted application's ledger always carries exactly two entries and never one. `fee_balance_cents` is then recomputed as the sum of the ledger, which after submission equals the net fee due. `requirement_hours_remaining` is recomputed as the profession's `requirement_hours_total` less `requirement_hours_satisfied`, and the application's `version` increments by one.

An applicant settles the balance by recording payments, each of which supplies an `amount_cents` that must be strictly positive and, per check 37, at most the application's current `fee_balance_cents` — overpayment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, so the ledger can never be driven credit-positive by a payment. Each payment appends a `PAYMENT` entry whose `amount_cents` is the negative of the amount tendered, recomputes `fee_balance_cents`, and increments the application's `version`. The payment operation returns the ledger entry it created rather than the application.

Every read of a single application refreshes its processing-transparency figures from the logical clock, without incrementing `version` or touching `updated_at`. The reference instant is the application's `decided_at` when a decision has been issued, and the current logical clock while the application is still undecided. `processing_elapsed_days` is 0 whenever `submitted_at` is null, and is otherwise the number of whole days from `submitted_at` to that reference instant, discarding any partial day. `processing_standard_met` is null while `decided_at` is null, and once a decision exists it is true exactly when `decided_at` is at or before `review_deadline_at`.

### 3.5. Substantial Equivalency: Two Parallel Reviews and One Score

A `LicensingAnalyst` opens the substantial-equivalency evaluation for a submitted application by naming the `application_id` and the `credential_id` to be compared against the profession's requirement. The application must be in `SUBMITTED`, failing as `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_REVIEWABLE`; no evaluation may already exist for it, failing as `ERR_EVALUATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`; and the nominated credential must belong to the application's applicant, failing as `ERR_CREDENTIAL_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`. Those three are evaluated strictly in the global order of §2.8 — the from-state check 8 first, then the uniqueness check 11, then the credential-ownership check 15 — and the first violated check decides, with no special-casing for this endpoint. Because opening an evaluation itself drives the application out of `SUBMITTED`, a second opening attempt against the same application is judged on its from-state before its uniqueness and is therefore refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_REVIEWABLE` (409) whenever the application has left `SUBMITTED` in the meantime; `ERR_EVALUATION_ALREADY_EXISTS` is reserved for an attempt that reaches the uniqueness check with the application still in `SUBMITTED`. Opening the evaluation drives the application along `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and increments its `version`.

The new evaluation is created with `scope_review_status` `PENDING`, `discipline_review_status` `PENDING`, `overall_status` `IN_PROGRESS`, `equivalency_score` null, `exam_waiver_granted` false, `finalized_at` null and `reviewed_by_user_id` null. Its `variant` is pinned at creation from the application's pathway and never changes afterwards: `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` when the pathway is `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION`, and `STRICT_SCOPE` for both `ENDORSEMENT` and `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED`. The scope comparison itself is computed immediately at creation, not deferred to finalisation: `matched_scope_elements` lists those of the profession's `required_scope_elements` that also appear in the credential's `scope_elements`, and `missing_scope_elements` lists those that do not, both reported in the profession's own registration order.

An evaluation advances along two dimensions that move independently of one another on the same row. The first is the analyst's scope-of-practice opinion. Recording it supplies a `decision` of either `EQUIVALENT` or `DEFICIENT_SCOPE` and an optional `note` — validated as a non-empty string when present, but not persisted on the evaluation and not echoed back — and requires that `scope_review_status` still be `PENDING`, that `finalized_at` still be null, and that the parent application be in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`. An `EQUIVALENT` opinion carries one extra condition: `missing_scope_elements` must contain no member of the profession's `critical_scope_elements`. Where a critical element is genuinely absent the analyst must record `DEFICIENT_SCOPE`; an attempt to record `EQUIVALENT` in that situation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), evaluated after the evaluation write-state check of §2.8. Recording `DEFICIENT_SCOPE` has no extra condition at all — a deficient scope does not block finalisation, it only feeds the score through the missing elements. Either outcome, `PENDING → EQUIVALENT` or `PENDING → DEFICIENT_SCOPE`, stamps the acting `X-User-ID` onto the evaluation's `reviewed_by_user_id` and onto the parent application's `scope_reviewed_by_user_id`, increments both the evaluation's `version` and the application's `version`, and recomputes `overall_status` — which stays `IN_PROGRESS`, because finalisation has not happened.

The second dimension is the contract reviewer's national-database finding, recorded by the assigned `Evaluator` with a `finding` of either `CLEAR` or `ADVERSE` and a `databases_checked` value recording which national databases were searched; `databases_checked` is mandatory but otherwise unconstrained — any non-null JSON value satisfies it — and it is not persisted on the evaluation. The write requires that `discipline_review_status` still be `PENDING`, that `finalized_at` still be null, that a review assignment exist for this evaluation's application whose `evaluator_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID` and whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` — otherwise `ERR_NO_OPEN_REVIEW_ASSIGNMENT` — and that the logical clock be at or before that assignment's `window_end_at`, where "that assignment" is the earliest-created qualifying assignment should the caller hold more than one, a lapse being `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_LAPSED`. `PENDING → CLEAR` and `PENDING → ADVERSE` are treated identically in every mechanical respect: each sets `discipline_review_status`, increments the evaluation's `version`, and recomputes `overall_status`. An `ADVERSE` finding does **not** by itself suspend the application; only a posted disciplinary record that flips `good_standing_flag` does that.

`overall_status` is a pure function of three things on the row: it is `COMPLETE` exactly when `scope_review_status` is not `PENDING`, `discipline_review_status` is not `PENDING`, and `finalized_at` is not null, and it is `IN_PROGRESS` in every other case. No child entity participates in the compound. Because `finalized_at` is one of the three conjuncts, resolving both dimensions is necessary but not sufficient — the analyst must still finalise.

Finalisation is the analyst's closing act along `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETE`, and it requires that `overall_status` be `IN_PROGRESS` with `finalized_at` null, that both parallel dimensions be resolved to something other than `PENDING`, and that the parent application be in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`. Finalising while a dimension is still `PENDING`, or finalising twice, is `ERR_EVALUATION_ALREADY_FINALIZED`.

Finalisation computes the `equivalency_score` under the row's pinned variant, using exactly one credential — the row named by `credential_id` — regardless of how many other credentials the applicant has filed, and without re-applying the `ACTIVE`-and-verified filter, which was already enforced at submission. The computation proceeds as follows. The scope ratio is the count of `matched_scope_elements` divided by the count of the profession's `required_scope_elements`. The hours ratio is the credential's `practice_hours` divided by the profession's `required_practice_hours`, capped at 1.0 so that surplus hours never earn extra credit; under the `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` variant only, if the applicant's `residency_months` is at least the profession's `universal_recognition_min_residency_months` then the hours ratio is lifted to exactly 1.0 outright, because residency substitutes for documented hours on that pathway. The standing term is 1.0 when the credential's `verified_good_standing` is true and 0.0 when it is false. The raw figure is the scope ratio weighted at 0.60, plus the hours ratio weighted at 0.25, plus the standing term weighted at 0.15. Count how many members of `missing_scope_elements` also appear in the profession's `critical_scope_elements`; under the `STRICT_SCOPE` variant the penalty is 10.0 points for each such critical miss, and under the `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` variant the penalty is zero and critical misses are ignored entirely. Finally, multiply the raw figure by 100, subtract the penalty, round the result half up to two decimal places, and clamp it into the closed range from 0.00 through 100.00 — the clamp is applied after the penalty, so a heavily penalised score floors at 0.00 rather than going negative. The consequence of the two variants is that identical credential data scores strictly higher on the universal-recognition pathway than under strict scope.

Finalisation then decides the examination waiver. `exam_waiver_granted` is true when the computed `equivalency_score` is at or above the profession's `exam_waiver_min_score` — the threshold itself grants the waiver — **or** when an unexpired reciprocal agreement exists for this profession whose `partner_state` equals the applicant's `state_code` and whose `exam_waiver_allowed` is true, in which case the compact grants the waiver irrespective of the score. `finalized_at` is set to the logical clock, `overall_status` becomes `COMPLETE`, and the evaluation's `version` increments.

When and only when the waiver is granted, the seat fee the applicant was assessed at submission must come back. The system appends an `EXAM_FEE_REVERSAL` entry with the memo `exam fee reversal` whose `amount_cents` is the negative of the applicant's own share of the examination component — that is, the profession's `exam_fee_cents` less the amount already waived, where the amount already waived is `exam_fee_cents` multiplied by the application's frozen `statutory_waiver_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent. A fully waived applicant therefore receives a reversal of zero cents, which is correct: they never paid for the seat. The application's `fee_balance_cents` is recomputed and the application's `version` increments.

Examination results themselves are recorded by an `AgencyAdmin` against the application while it is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`, supplying an `exam_kind` of either `NATIONAL` or `JURISPRUDENCE`, a `passed` boolean, and an optional `score` that is validated as a decimal at two places but is not persisted on the application and not echoed back. An `exam_kind` of `NATIONAL` writes `passed` into the application's `national_exam_passed`; an `exam_kind` of `JURISPRUDENCE` writes it into `jurisprudence_exam_passed`. Either way the application's `version` increments. Recording a result is a plain assignment, so a later recording overwrites an earlier one.

### 3.6. Booking Contract Reviewers Onto the Calendar

A `LicensingAnalyst` books a reviewer by creating a review assignment naming the `evaluator_id`, the `application_id` under review, and the closed occupation interval bounded by `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, where the end must be strictly after the start. The request also carries the `expected_evaluator_version` demanded by §2.6. The application must be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`. The evaluator's `profession_code` must equal the `profession_code` of the application's profession, failing as `ERR_EVALUATOR_PROFESSION_MISMATCH`, and the evaluator's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, failing as `ERR_EVALUATOR_INACTIVE`.

Reviewer calendars are protected by a **closed** interval test inflated by a mandatory rest buffer of a full 24 hours on each side. A booking is refused when any review assignment of the same evaluator whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` satisfies both of the following: the new window's `window_start_at` reduced by 24 hours is at or before the existing assignment's `window_end_at`, and the existing assignment's `window_start_at` is at or before the new window's `window_end_at` increased by 24 hours. Because both comparisons are inclusive and the buffer is applied on both sides, windows that merely touch, and even windows separated by exactly 24 hours, do collide; the rejection is `ERR_EVALUATOR_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Only `ASSIGNED` assignments occupy the calendar — `COMPLETED` and `RELEASED` rows are invisible to the test.

Capacity is the last gate. The evaluator's `open_assignment_count` plus the one new booking must not exceed their `max_concurrent_reviews`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EVALUATOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. That count is itself derived rather than stored by hand: it is the number of review assignments of that evaluator whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, with `COMPLETED` and `RELEASED` rows excluded. A successful booking creates the assignment with `status` `ASSIGNED` and `closed_at` null, recomputes the evaluator's `open_assignment_count`, and increments the evaluator's `version`.

Assignments leave `ASSIGNED` only by cascade, never by direct request, and the two exits are mechanically symmetric. `ASSIGNED → COMPLETED` fires when the application the assignment serves is approved; `ASSIGNED → RELEASED` fires when that application enters `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` or `DENIED`. In both cases `closed_at` is set to the logical clock, the assignment's `version` increments, the evaluator's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed, and the evaluator's `version` increments. Assignments are readable individually and as a filtered list.

### 3.7. Crediting Military Training and Experience

Military service frequently satisfies part of what the classroom would otherwise have to. A `LicensingAnalyst` converts a verified training record into a credit award against an application by naming the `application_id` and the `training_record_id`. The application must be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`; the training record must belong to the same applicant as the application, failing as `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`; the record's `verified` flag must be true, failing as `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_UNVERIFIED`; and no credit award with `status` `AWARDED` may already exist for that pair of application and training record, a duplicate being `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_ALREADY_CREDITED`. Because the unverified check bites at award time, an unverified transcript can never enter the hours arithmetic at all.

The conversion is category-dependent. Each award's `awarded_hours` is the training record's `documented_hours` multiplied by the factor for its `category` — 0.75 for `MOS_TRAINING`, 0.40 for `FIELD_EXPERIENCE`, and 0.90 for `JOINT_SERVICE_TRANSCRIPT` — with the product rounded half up to one decimal place, and that already-rounded value is what the award stores. The application's `requirement_hours_satisfied` is then recomputed by adding together the already-rounded `awarded_hours` of every credit award of this application whose `status` is `AWARDED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, rounding that sum half up to one decimal place, and capping the result at the profession's `requirement_hours_total` so credit can never overshoot the requirement. Summing the raw unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different answer and is not permitted. `requirement_hours_remaining` is then the profession's `requirement_hours_total` less `requirement_hours_satisfied`, and the pair is a conserved quantity: satisfied plus remaining always equals the profession's total exactly, and remaining is never negative. The application's `version` increments on each recomputation.

Revocation is the compensating action, available to a `LicensingAnalyst` while the award is still `AWARDED` and the application is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`. It drives `AWARDED → REVOKED`, sets `revoked_at` to the logical clock, and recomputes `requirement_hours_satisfied` and `requirement_hours_remaining` over the remaining `AWARDED` rows only — `REVOKED` rows leave the sum entirely — incrementing both the award's `version` and the application's `version`. Revoking an already-revoked award is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_AWARD_ALREADY_REVOKED`. Awards are readable individually and as a filtered list.

### 3.8. Temporary Practice Permits: Bridging the Review Period

A temporary practice permit is what lets an applicant earn a living while their file is being worked. A `LicensingAnalyst` issues one by naming the `application_id`, the requested `effective_at` start of the practice window, and the `expected_application_version` of §2.6. The application must be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DEFICIENT`. The applicant must be in good standing and clear the pathway bar, or the issuance fails with `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_BAR`. The requested `effective_at` must be at or after the logical clock — a permit may start now or in the future but never in the past — failing as `ERR_PERMIT_EFFECTIVE_IN_PAST`. And the application's `fee_balance_cents` must be at most zero, so an applicant cannot obtain practice authority while owing the agency money; that failure is `ERR_FEE_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` with HTTP 402.

Pathway matters for eligibility as well as duration. On the `ENDORSEMENT` and `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION` pathways a credential evaluation must already exist for the application before a permit may issue, because the agency wants a scope comparison on file first; the rejection is `ERR_EVALUATION_REQUIRED_FOR_PERMIT`. The `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED` pathway is exempt from that requirement entirely — permits on that pathway issue during full review, which is the statutory point of the pathway.

The window geometry is a nested cap over three inputs. The `permit_max_days` frozen onto the permit at issuance is selected by the application's `pathway`: 90 days for `ENDORSEMENT`, 120 days for `UNIVERSAL_RECOGNITION`, and 180 days for `MILITARY_SPOUSE_EXPEDITED`. One candidate end is the requested `effective_at` advanced by that many whole days. The other candidate end is the application's `review_deadline_at` advanced by a grace allowance of 30 whole days. The permit's `expires_at` is whichever of those two candidates is earlier, so a permit can never outlive the review it bridges by more than the grace allowance, and can never exceed its pathway's statutory maximum either.

The permit's own `permit_fee_cents` is frozen at issuance as the profession's list `permit_fee_cents` less the waived portion, where the waived portion is that list price multiplied by the application's frozen `statutory_waiver_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent. A `PERMIT_FEE` entry is appended for the positive frozen amount, `fee_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the application's `version` increments. The permit itself starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `converted_at` null, `revoked_at` null and `revocation_reason_code` null.

Practice authority must not double up, and the collision test here is **half-open** — the interval runs from `effective_at` inclusive to `expires_at` exclusive. Issuance is refused when any temporary permit whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `CONVERTED`, and whose own application shares both the same applicant and the same profession as this application, satisfies both of the following: the new permit's `effective_at` is strictly before that permit's `expires_at`, and that permit's `effective_at` is strictly before the new permit's `expires_at`. Because both comparisons are strict, two windows that merely touch — a new permit starting at precisely the instant an existing permit expires — do **not** collide, and no buffer of any kind is applied. The rejection is `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Note the deliberate contrast with reviewer calendars in §3.6, which are closed intervals with a 24-hour buffer on both sides: touching permits are fine, touching review windows are not. `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` permits are invisible to the collision test.

One `ACTIVE` node forks to three terminal outcomes with quite different consequences. A `BoardDeputy` may revoke an `ACTIVE` permit directly, supplying a `reason_code` that is copied verbatim into the permit's `revocation_reason_code`; the same `ACTIVE → REVOKED` transition also fires automatically when the application leaves the reviewable set, in which case the reason is the literal `DISCIPLINARY_ACTION` for a good-standing cascade and the literal `APPLICATION_DENIED` for a denial cascade. However it is triggered, revocation sets `status` to `REVOKED`, sets `revoked_at` to the logical clock, truncates `expires_at` down to `revoked_at`, appends a `PERMIT_FEE_REVERSAL` entry whose `amount_cents` is the exact negative of the permit's frozen `permit_fee_cents`, recomputes the application's `fee_balance_cents` and increments both the permit's `version` and the application's `version`. The reversal is full and never prorated: it is the compensating action of the `PERMIT_FEE` entry and returns the balance to precisely its pre-issuance value. Revoking a permit that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Expiry is the second fork and is driven by an ops sweep that an `AgencyAdmin` runs. The sweep walks every permit whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, in `created_at` ascending order, and drives each along `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` with the permit's `version` incrementing. There is deliberately **no** ledger movement on expiry: a permit that ran its full window consumed what it was sold, so the fee stands. The sweep reports how many rows it touched as `expired_count` together with their identifiers as `expired_permit_ids`.

Conversion is the third fork and is described with the approval that causes it, in §3.9. Permits issued against one application are readable as a list, filterable by `status`.

### 3.9. Deficiency Cycles, the Board Decision and Minting the Licence

Review does not always run straight. A `LicensingAnalyst` who finds the file wanting records a deficiency with a `reason_code` — a mandatory non-empty string that is validated but not persisted on the application — and the application must be in `UNDER_REVIEW` for that to be possible. The recording always increments `deficiency_cycle_count` by one, and then branches on the resulting count against a cap of two cycles. When the incremented count is at most 2, the application takes `UNDER_REVIEW → DEFICIENT` and waits for the applicant; its `version` increments. When the incremented count is greater than 2 — that is, on the third deficiency — the application takes `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` instead of looping: `decided_at` is set to the logical clock, `decided_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `processing_elapsed_days` and `processing_standard_met` are computed as in §3.4, every `ACTIVE` temporary permit of the application is revoked with `revocation_reason_code` `APPLICATION_DENIED` and its full `PERMIT_FEE_REVERSAL`, every `ASSIGNED` review assignment is released, and the application's `version` increments. Both branches belong to the same operation; the count decides which one fires.

The applicant closes a revision cycle themselves, driving `DEFICIENT → UNDER_REVIEW` on their own application with the caller's `X-User-ID` matching `applicant_id`. Curing increments the application's `version` and pointedly does **not** decrement `deficiency_cycle_count`: the cap of two cycles is cumulative across the life of the application, so an applicant who has already been through two deficiencies faces denial on the third finding rather than a third revision.

The terminal act belongs to a `BoardDeputy`, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `memo` that is validated as a non-empty string when present but is not persisted on the application. Both branches require the application to be in `UNDER_REVIEW` and both are subject to the segregation-of-duties comparison of §2.11. From there the branches diverge sharply.

`DENY` requires nothing further. It drives `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, sets `decided_at` to the logical clock and `decided_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, computes `processing_elapsed_days` and `processing_standard_met`, revokes every `ACTIVE` temporary permit of the application with `revocation_reason_code` `APPLICATION_DENIED` and its full fee reversal, releases every `ASSIGNED` review assignment, and increments the application's `version`. The asymmetry is intentional — a file that cannot lawfully be approved must always be closable.

`APPROVE` carries six substantive gates beyond state and segregation of duties, and they are evaluated in the order §2.8 fixes. The applicant must still be in good standing with a `pathway_risk_index` strictly below 12.00 (`ERR_DISCIPLINARY_BAR`). The application's credential evaluation must exist with `overall_status` `COMPLETE` (`ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_COMPLETE`). `requirement_hours_remaining` must be exactly 0.0 (`ERR_REQUIREMENT_HOURS_UNSATISFIED`). Either the evaluation's `exam_waiver_granted` must be true or the application's `national_exam_passed` must be true (`ERR_NATIONAL_EXAM_REQUIRED`). And the jurisprudence requirement must be satisfied one of three ways — the profession's `jurisprudence_exam_required` is false, or the application's `jurisprudence_exam_passed` is true, or an unexpired reciprocal agreement for this profession whose `partner_state` equals the applicant's `state_code` has `jurisprudence_waiver_allowed` true (`ERR_JURISPRUDENCE_EXAM_REQUIRED`). Finally `fee_balance_cents` must be at most zero (`ERR_FEE_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING`, HTTP 402).

An approval that clears all of that drives `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, sets `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id`, computes `processing_elapsed_days` and `processing_standard_met`, and then sets off a chain of three linked effects, each caused by the one before it.

The first link mints the licence. A new `License` row is created carrying the `application_id` it came from and the `profession_id` copied from that application, with `status` `ACTIVE` — licences are minted active and are not deactivated in this release — an `issued_at` equal to the logical clock, and an `expires_at` of `issued_at` advanced by a licence term of 730 days, which is two 365-day years. Its `license_number` is minted as the profession's `profession_code`, then a hyphen, then a zero-padded six-digit mint sequence; the sequence advances by one for each licence row in `created_at` ascending order and begins at `000001`. That sequence is one single global counter shared by every profession, not a per-profession counter, so the six digits after the hyphen count every licence the agency has ever minted rather than only those of this profession. Alongside link one, and caused by it, every review assignment of this application whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` moves `ASSIGNED → COMPLETED` with the evaluator bookkeeping of §3.6.

The second link exists because a licence now exists for this application, which makes any temporary bridge redundant. Every temporary permit of the application whose `status` is `ACTIVE` takes `ACTIVE → CONVERTED`, walked in `created_at` ascending order and each earning its own third link, though the half-open collision test of §3.8 normally admits only one such permit at a time: its `converted_at` is set to the logical clock, its `expires_at` is truncated down to `converted_at`, and the newly minted licence records the first such permit's identifier in `converted_from_permit_id`. When the applicant held no `ACTIVE` permit, `converted_from_permit_id` stays null and the second and third links simply do not fire.

The third link exists because the permit's window was truncated: the applicant paid for days they will now never use, so a prorated `CONVERSION_CREDIT` entry with the memo `permit conversion credit` is appended. The credit is computed from the permit alone, using its **pre-truncation** `expires_at`. Take the total days of the original window as the whole days from `effective_at` to that pre-truncation end, discarding any partial day. Take the elapsed days as the whole days from `effective_at` to the logical clock, again discarding any partial day. The unused days are the total less the elapsed, floored at zero so an over-run window never produces a negative refund. When the total days is zero — a permit converted at the very instant it began — the credit is exactly zero cents and no drift is introduced; otherwise the credit is the permit's frozen `permit_fee_cents` multiplied by the unused days, divided by the total days, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The ledger entry's `amount_cents` is the negative of that credit. The application's `fee_balance_cents` is then recomputed, and the application's `version` increments once for the approval as a whole.

Minted licences are readable individually and as a filtered list, and the applicant's own view of their journey — pathway, fee balance, requirement hours, elapsed processing days and whether the published standard was met — is available on every read of the application, which is what the agency's processing-time transparency obligation ultimately rests on.
