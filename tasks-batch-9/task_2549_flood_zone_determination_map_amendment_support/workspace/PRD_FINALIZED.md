# Flood Zone Determination API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A flood zone determination provider sits between three parties who rarely agree: the federal flood-mapping agency that publishes and revises Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRM) panels, the mortgage lenders who must prove that every loan secured by a building in a Special Flood Hazard Area (SFHA) carries flood insurance for the life of the loan, and the borrowers who bear the cost of that insurance and who occasionally have very good evidence that the map is wrong about their house. The provider's job is to answer one deceptively small question — *is this specific structure inside the SFHA, and if so at what base flood elevation* — and then to keep answering it, correctly, every time the map changes, every time the geocode is corrected, and every time a borrower challenges the answer through the statutory dispute channel.

This document specifies the Flood Zone Determination API, a REST service mounted under `/flood-zone` that carries that workload end to end. It models the map itself as a versioned timeline of panels walking a preliminary → appeal → letter-of-final-determination → effective path; it models each determination as an immutable, superseding record tied to exactly one structure and exactly one panel; and it models the lender's compliance obligation as a pair of independent status dimensions on a loan folio — whether the loan is still being monitored, and whether coverage is in place — so that a community suspension and a force-placed premium never overwrite each other. Around that core sit the money mechanics: mandatory-purchase notices with response deadlines, force-placed premiums drawn from escrow and refunded pro rata when the borrower finally produces a policy, and a graduated fee schedule for Letter of Map Amendment and Letter of Map Revision Based on Fill packages.

Two properties make this system harder than a CRUD catalogue and both are deliberate. First, almost every write ripples: making a revision panel effective supersedes its predecessor, flags every determination that cited the predecessor, re-postures every affected loan folio, and closes the compliance notices those folios were carrying — five hops from a single agency action. Second, the arithmetic is order-sensitive. Datum conversions round before differencing, amendment fee tiers key on the pre-surcharge subtotal, and the same geometric inputs deliberately produce a different flood zone at origination than they do during life-of-loan monitoring, because a conservative first answer protects the lender while a lenient re-read protects the borrower from having an obligation flipped by cartographic noise. Implementations must honour the pinned evaluation orders exactly; they are not stylistic preferences.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identity and time, the order in which validations fire, numeric precision, concurrency protection, and the domain-wide gates and invariants that individual workflows in Section 3 assume without restating.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which that user is acting. `X-User-Role` must be present, non-empty, and exactly one of the five recognised role names — `ProviderAnalyst`, `ProviderReviewer`, `AgencyOfficer`, `Lender`, or `Borrower`. `X-User-ID` must be present and non-empty on every request that is not a `GET`; read-only requests may omit it. A request that fails either of these checks is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), whether the failure is a missing header, an empty header, or a role string outside the recognised five. Once the caller is authenticated, the role is matched against the set of roles authorised for the target operation as listed in Section 4; a caller whose role is not in that set is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The provider side of the business is deliberately split in two. A `ProviderAnalyst` is determination-provider staff: it registers communities and lenders, geocodes properties, adds structures, issues determinations, prepares map amendment packages and submits them to the agency, and runs the life-of-loan monitoring sweep. A `ProviderReviewer` is the independent second pair of eyes: it validates or rejects elevation certificates and forms the provider's recommendation on a borrower dispute, and it never issues determinations. An `AgencyOfficer` acts for the federal flood-mapping agency: it publishes and revises FIRM panels, opens and resolves appeal periods, makes panels effective, concurs with or overrides provider dispute recommendations, and decides map amendment requests. A `Lender` is the institution servicing the loan: it opens monitoring folios, reports borrower-obtained coverage, and force places insurance. A `Borrower` is the property owner: it disputes determinations, files elevation certificates, and may acknowledge notices.

Identity semantics differ by role and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Lender`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Lender` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Lender` compare the resource's lender foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a `Lender` caller may act on a loan folio only when `X-User-ID` equals the `Lender` named by that folio's `lender_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Borrower`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque identifier not tied to any persisted entity: it is compared against the plain string field `borrower_user_id` recorded on the loan folio, never against any entity's `id`, and a borrower acting on a folio that does not name them is rejected with `ERR_BORROWER_NOT_ON_LOAN` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderAnalyst`, `ProviderReviewer`, or `AgencyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `issued_by_user_id` on a determination, `prepared_by_user_id` on an amendment request, `submitted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on an elevation certificate, and `provider_reviewed_by_user_id` and `agency_reviewed_by_user_id` on a dispute — but it never compares those recorded values to any entity's `id`. Those recorded operator identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties rule in Section 2.11 compares against each other.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position; it defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Collections are always returned sorted by `created_at` ascending, and the `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Collection reads are also the only way to reach entities that the system creates on its own behalf. Cascades mint determinations and send notices without the caller ever supplying an id for them, so each list endpoint accepts its entity's foreign keys as optional equality filters and those filters are the reverse-reachability channel: a determination minted by a sustained dispute is discoverable by filtering determinations on `supersedes_determination_id`, and a notice closed by a cascade is discoverable by filtering notices on `loan_folio_id`. Filters combine conjunctively, and a filter value that matches nothing yields an empty `data` array with `total_count` of 0 rather than an error. The specific filter names available on each collection are the foreign keys and status enumerations of the underlying entity, namely `state_code` and `participation_status` on communities; `community_id`, `supersedes_panel_id`, `map_panel_number` and `revision_status` on map panels; `community_id`, `map_panel_number` and `geocode_status` on properties; `property_id`, `elevation_source_certificate_id` and `occupancy_type` on structures; `lender_id`, `property_id`, `current_determination_id`, `compliance_posture` and `insurance_status` on loan folios; `loan_folio_id`, `structure_id`, `map_panel_id`, `supersedes_determination_id`, `status` and `determination_type` on determinations; `determination_id`, `revised_determination_id`, `status` and `basis` on disputes; `structure_id`, `dispute_id`, `reference_panel_id` and `status` on elevation certificates; `determination_id`, `elevation_certificate_id`, `status` and `request_type` on map amendment requests; and `loan_folio_id`, `determination_id`, `notice_type` and `status` on compliance notices.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Identity and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID with no type prefix, a `created_at` timestamp, an `updated_at` timestamp, and an integer `version`. Timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row; `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant on every mutation. All eleven entity types in this system — communities, lenders, map panels, properties, structures, loan folios, determinations, disputes, elevation certificates, map amendment requests and compliance notices — carry the full set, so no entity in this API is missing `updated_at` or `version`.

Time is not read from the host. The server maintains a logical clock which, before any client has set it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the implementation must never fall back to wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability sets that clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes the new `current_utc` back; it is available to all five roles and writes no entity. Advancing the clock is inert by itself: it does not expire notices, does not make panels effective, and does not re-determine anything. Those effects are produced only when a caller drives the monitoring sweep, the force-placement action, or the panel-effective action, each of which reads the clock at the moment it runs. Every field this document describes as being set "at the current instant" — `sent_at`, `issued_at`, `filed_at`, `resolved_at`, `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `force_placed_at`, `superseded_at`, `closed_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `lfd_issued_at`, `last_participation_change_at` — takes its value from the logical clock, as do the `created_at` and `updated_at` of every row.

### 2.5. The Validation Ladder

Requests are checked against a single ordered ladder, and the ladder is evaluated strictly from the lowest priority number upward. When one request violates two rules, the violation with the smaller priority number supplies both the `error_id` and the HTTP status of the response, and the lower-priority violation is never reported. The domain ordering behind the ladder is that regulatory and safety failures are reported ahead of referential failures, referential ahead of temporal, temporal ahead of structural constraints, and structural constraints ahead of money — telling a lender that coverage is unavailable in this community is more actionable than telling it that the escrow is short.

The ladder, in evaluation order, is as follows. First (1) the authentication headers must be present and non-empty as described in Section 2.1, then (2) `X-User-Role` must be one of the five declared roles — both failures report `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Third (3) the caller's role must appear in the target endpoint's authorised role list, reporting `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Fourth (4) the request shape must be valid: no unknown body or query fields, all required fields present, every enumeration value within its declared range, integers non-negative where declared non-negative, decimals within their declared scale, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` zero or greater — reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Fifth (5) every identifier in the URL path must resolve, validated left to right so that the leftmost unresolvable segment decides which not-found error is returned. Sixth (6) every foreign key supplied in the request body must resolve, validated in the order the fields are declared for that operation; both existence checks report the not-found error of the entity that failed to resolve, at HTTP 404. Seventh (7) a `Lender` caller's ownership of the target folio is checked, reporting `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_OWNED`; and eighth (8) a `Borrower` caller's presence on the target folio is checked, reporting `ERR_BORROWER_NOT_ON_LOAN`.

The ladder then continues into business territory. Ninth (9) comes segregation of duties, reporting `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Tenth (10) comes the stale-folio concurrency check of Section 2.6, reporting `ERR_STALE_LOAN_FOLIO_VERSION`. Eleventh (11), the target entity must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, reporting the invalid-state error for that entity type at HTTP 409. Twelfth (12) the community's coverage availability must permit the operation, reporting `ERR_COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. Thirteenth (13) force placement requires a lapsed mandatory-purchase notice and no pending re-determination, reporting `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_EXPIRED`; fourteenth (14) the lender must be authorised to force place, reporting `ERR_FORCE_PLACEMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; fifteenth (15) a mandatory-purchase notice requires a determination that actually triggers the mandatory purchase requirement, reporting `ERR_MANDATORY_PURCHASE_NOT_TRIGGERED`. Sixteenth (16) a referenced elevation certificate must be validated, reporting `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_VALIDATED`; seventeenth (17) supplied identifiers must be mutually coherent, reporting `ERR_STRUCTURE_FOLIO_MISMATCH`; eighteenth (18) the amendment type must match the structure's fill history, reporting `ERR_AMENDMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH`; nineteenth (19) the property must be geocoded, reporting `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_GEOCODED`; twentieth (20) an effective panel must exist for the property, reporting `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_PANEL`.

The tail of the ladder holds the temporal, constraint and fiscal rules. Twenty-first (21) the referenced certificate's survey must still be current, reporting `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED`; twenty-second (22) the letter-of-final-determination waiting period must have elapsed before a panel goes effective, reporting `ERR_LFD_WAITING_PERIOD`; twenty-third (23) panel effective-date interval hygiene must hold, reporting `ERR_PANEL_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OVERLAP`; twenty-fourth (24) the notice cooldown window must be clear, reporting `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_ACTIVE`; twenty-fifth (25) panel sequencing and uniqueness must hold, reporting `ERR_PANEL_SEQUENCE_INVALID`; twenty-sixth (26) the amendment resubmission ceiling must not be exceeded, reporting `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT`; twenty-seventh (27) the structure must sit above the base flood elevation for an amendment to be submitted, reporting `ERR_STRUCTURE_NOT_ABOVE_BFE`; twenty-eighth (28) a dispute's asserted grounds must be admissible, reporting `ERR_DISPUTE_BASIS_UNSUPPORTED`; twenty-ninth (29) a folio may carry only one origination determination, reporting `ERR_ORIGINATION_ALREADY_COMPLETE`; thirtieth (30) reported coverage must be sufficient and sane, reporting `ERR_COVERAGE_BELOW_REQUIRED`; thirty-first (31) escrow must be able to fund the force-placed premium, reporting `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT`; and thirty-second (32) an appeal decision may only be recorded after the posted appeal period has closed, reporting `ERR_APPEAL_PERIOD_OPEN`. Section 3 states the substance of each of these rules in the workflow that it guards; the ladder above fixes only which one wins when several are violated at once.

### 2.6. Stale-Folio Protection When Issuing Determinations

Issuing a determination is the one write in this system that must be protected against a client acting on a stale read, because a life-of-loan review determination legitimately supersedes whatever determination the folio currently points at. A writer holding an out-of-date view of the folio would otherwise silently overwrite a determination it never saw — including one minted moments earlier by a monitoring sweep or by a sustained dispute. The determination-issuing operation therefore requires `expected_loan_folio_version` as a mandatory, non-optional field of its request body, carrying the `version` of the target loan folio as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the folio's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOAN_FOLIO_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and nothing is written. Because a successful issuance increments the folio's `version` by 1, replaying the same request body a second time necessarily fails this check. No other operation in this API takes a version field; the folio's other write paths do not require one.

### 2.7. Measurement, Datum and Monetary Precision

Three different precision regimes coexist and must not be blended. Elevations, boundary offsets and freeboard figures are measured in feet: raw surveyed elevations and panel base flood elevations are carried to 2 decimal places, while every converted elevation and every freeboard figure is rounded to 1 decimal place using round-half-up. Geographic coordinates carry 6 decimal places and geocoder confidence carries 2. Money is carried exclusively as integer cents — there are no fractional cents anywhere in this system — and every division that produces money (a prorated premium, an expedite surcharge, an earned-premium recognition) is rounded to a whole number of cents using round-half-up. Counts and day-counts are plain integers.

Geometric comparisons against the SFHA boundary are exact decimal comparisons with strict inequalities as described in Section 3.5; a value that lands exactly on a comparison boundary does not satisfy a strict comparison. Where a rounding step is described as happening before a subtraction, that order is mandatory: rounding after the subtraction instead produces a different answer at the tenth-of-a-foot scale, and because amendment eligibility turns on whether a freeboard figure is strictly above zero, the difference between the two orders is the difference between a grant and a rejection rather than a cosmetic one.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Recomputation Semantics

Many actions in this system touch more than one row, and Section 3 describes those ripples hop by hop. Three global rules govern all of them. First, every row a cascade writes is a real mutation of that row: its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant and its `version` increases by 1, exactly as if a client had written it directly. Second, every computed field is recomputed by full re-evaluation from its inputs, never by applying an incremental delta — `open_notice_count` on a folio, `pending_redetermination_count` on a folio, `structure_count` on a property, `active_folio_count` on a community and `open_force_placement_count` at a lender are all obtained by re-counting the underlying rows. `pending_redetermination_count` in particular is the number of determinations belonging to that folio whose `status` is `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW`, with no other filter applied. Third, cascades run to completion within the request that triggered them and their effects are visible to the very next read; there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this API.

Path parameters are resolved left to right before any body validation, so a request against a nested action whose parent identifier does not resolve reports the parent's not-found error and never inspects the rest of the request. Where an action endpoint hangs off an owner resource, the owner is the leftmost segment and therefore the first thing checked: `community_id` on a participation change, `map_panel_id` on an appeal-period, appeal-decision, effective or impact-analysis action, `property_id` on a geocode correction, `loan_folio_id` on an insurance report or force placement, `dispute_id` on a provider or agency review, `elevation_certificate_id` on a certificate review, `map_amendment_request_id` on an amendment submission or agency decision, and `compliance_notice_id` on a notice acknowledgement. The same identifiers, plus `lender_id`, `structure_id` and `determination_id`, address the single-resource reads of their respective collections.

Each entity type has its own not-found error, returned at HTTP 404 whenever an identifier — whether it arrived in the path or as a foreign key in the request body — names no such row. An unresolvable community identifier reports `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND`; a lender identifier reports `ERR_LENDER_NOT_FOUND`; a map panel identifier, including a `supersedes_panel_id` that names nothing, reports `ERR_MAP_PANEL_NOT_FOUND`; a property identifier reports `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`; a structure identifier reports `ERR_STRUCTURE_NOT_FOUND`; a loan folio identifier reports `ERR_LOAN_FOLIO_NOT_FOUND`; a determination identifier reports `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`; a dispute identifier reports `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`; an elevation certificate identifier reports `ERR_ELEVATION_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`; a map amendment request identifier reports `ERR_MAP_AMENDMENT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; and a compliance notice identifier reports `ERR_COMPLIANCE_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`. Body foreign keys are checked in the order the operation declares its fields, so the first unresolvable one decides the response.

### 2.9. Community Participation as a System-Wide Coverage Gate

Whether federal flood insurance can be written at all is a property of the community, not of the loan, and it gates operations across the entire API. Each community carries a `participation_status` describing its standing in the national program and a `cbrs_flag` recording whether it sits inside a Coastal Barrier Resources System unit, and from those two inputs the system derives `coverage_availability` by evaluating the following clauses from top to bottom and taking the first that matches: if `cbrs_flag` is true the availability is `UNAVAILABLE`; otherwise if `participation_status` is `SUSPENDED`, `NON_PARTICIPATING` or `WITHDRAWN` the availability is `UNAVAILABLE`; otherwise if `participation_status` is `EMERGENCY` the availability is `EMERGENCY_LIMITS_ONLY`; otherwise if `participation_status` is `PROBATION` the availability is `FULL_WITH_SURCHARGE`; otherwise — that is, when `participation_status` is `REGULAR` — the availability is `FULL`. The clause order matters: a community inside a Coastal Barrier Resources System unit whose `participation_status` is `REGULAR` is still `UNAVAILABLE`, because federal flood insurance is unavailable in such a unit regardless of participation standing. The derivation reads the community row alone; no child entity participates in it.

`coverage_availability` is recomputed when a community is registered and on every participation change, and it is read whenever the mandatory purchase flag, the required coverage amount or a folio's compliance posture is evaluated. An `UNAVAILABLE` community blocks force placement and blocks the issuance of mandatory-purchase notices, both with `ERR_COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE`; an `EMERGENCY_LIMITS_ONLY` community sharply lowers the coverage caps described in Section 3.6; and a `PROBATION` community carries the annual `probation_surcharge_cents` amount recorded on the community row, which is meaningful only while `participation_status` is `PROBATION`.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Two quantities in this system are conserved and implementations are expected to hold them exactly, with no drift.

The first is escrow cash plus recognised premium. When a folio is opened, its `escrow_balance_cents` is snapshotted into `initial_escrow_balance_cents`. Thereafter, at every point where the folio is at rest, the sum of `escrow_balance_cents`, `cumulative_earned_premium_cents` and the placement premium currently outstanding in `force_placed_premium_cents` must equal `initial_escrow_balance_cents` — across the forward action of force placement, which draws a premium out of escrow and parks it in `force_placed_premium_cents`, and across its compensating action of coverage replacement, which recognises the earned portion, refunds the rest and resets `force_placed_premium_cents` to 0. The two-term form of the identity — `escrow_balance_cents` plus `cumulative_earned_premium_cents` equal to `initial_escrow_balance_cents` — therefore holds exactly at those points where no placement is outstanding, that is before any force placement and again after each settlement; immediately after a placement it is short by exactly the drawn premium, since force placement deliberately leaves `cumulative_earned_premium_cents` untouched (nothing has been earned yet). Section 3.6 gives the arithmetic; the invariant is the acceptance test.

The second is obligation cardinality. `open_notice_count` on a folio is defined as the number of compliance notices on that folio whose `status` is `SENT`, counted across every notice type and with no time window applied, and it is recomputed by full re-count after every notice creation and after every notice transition out of `SENT`. Consequently, sending a notice and then acknowledging that same notice must return `open_notice_count` to exactly the value it held before the notice was sent, for every folio, regardless of which cascades fired in between.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

The provider role split exists so that no single operator can both produce a record and bless it. Three review actions therefore compare the acting `X-User-ID` against an identifier the system recorded earlier, and reject the request with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) when they are equal. Reviewing an elevation certificate requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the certificate's `submitted_by_user_id`. Producing the provider recommendation on a dispute requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from `issued_by_user_id` on the disputed determination. Recording the agency's position on a dispute requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `provider_reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on the dispute for the current round. Because a `ProviderReviewer` never issues determinations and an `AgencyOfficer` never produces provider recommendations, these checks are satisfiable in normal operation; they exist to catch an operator wearing two hats. Determinations minted by a cascade rather than by a person record the literal string `system` as their `issued_by_user_id`, which no human caller can present.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering the Parties: Communities and Lenders

Everything in this system hangs off a community, because the community is what participates (or fails to participate) in the national flood insurance program and what determines which vertical datum offset applies to survey data. A `ProviderAnalyst` registers one by supplying the community's six-character NFIP community identification number as `community_id_code`, which must be unique across all communities; the community `name` as printed on the FIRM; and a two-letter USPS `state_code`, which must be one of `CA`, `FL`, `TX` or `NC`. Three further inputs are optional at registration and each has a default: `participation_status` defaults to `REGULAR` and must be one of `REGULAR`, `EMERGENCY`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED`, `NON_PARTICIPATING` or `WITHDRAWN`; `cbrs_flag` defaults to false and records whether the community sits inside a Coastal Barrier Resources System unit; and `probation_surcharge_cents` defaults to 0, must be zero or greater, and carries the annual probation surcharge. On creation the system derives `coverage_availability` by the clause ladder of Section 2.9, sets `last_participation_change_at` to the current logical instant — which at creation is the same instant as `created_at` — and initialises `active_folio_count` to 0.

`active_folio_count` is a cross-entity count: it is the number of loan folios whose property belongs to this community and whose `monitoring_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. Because both values of that dimension are counted, opening a folio raises the number and a community suspension does not lower it. The count is re-derived whenever a folio is opened.

A community's standing is not static. When a community is suspended, placed on probation, reinstated or withdrawn, a `ProviderAnalyst` records the change by supplying the new `participation_status` and, when relevant, a new `probation_surcharge_cents`; the operation returns the updated community and is a modification rather than a creation. The change recomputes `coverage_availability`, stamps `last_participation_change_at` with the current logical instant, and then reaches into every loan folio sitting on a property in that community. Each such folio has its monitoring dimension re-evaluated: a folio whose `monitoring_status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` when the community's new `coverage_availability` is `UNAVAILABLE`, and a folio whose `monitoring_status` is `SUSPENDED` moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` when the new availability is anything other than `UNAVAILABLE` — that is, when the community has been restored to `REGULAR`, `EMERGENCY` or `PROBATION` standing. Neither direction of that transition touches the folio's `insurance_status`; the two dimensions are independent, and a force-placed folio in a suspended community keeps its coverage dimension untouched while its monitoring dimension goes dormant. Those folios then recompute `required_coverage_cents` and `uninsured_exposure_cents` — a community dropping to `EMERGENCY` standing lowers the caps and can therefore lower the required amount without anything about the loan or the structure changing — and finally recompute `compliance_posture`, which becomes `COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE` on the suspending direction and is otherwise re-derived from the unchanged coverage dimension and notice set.

Lenders are registered just as simply. A `ProviderAnalyst` supplies the institution `name` and a `contact_email` used as the delivery address for compliance notices, and may override two flags that both default to true: `force_placement_authorized_flag` is false when the lender's servicing agreement forbids force placement, and `escrow_servicing_flag` is false when the lender does not escrow and therefore may not have force-placed premiums debited. A newly registered lender has `open_force_placement_count` initialised to 0; thereafter that field is a re-count of the loan folios naming this lender whose `insurance_status` is `FORCE_PLACED`, and it is re-derived whenever a force placement is executed or settled.

### 3.2. The Map Timeline: Publishing and Revising FIRM Panels

Only an `AgencyOfficer` may put a FIRM panel into the system. A panel is identified within its community by `map_panel_number`, and the pair of `community_id` and `map_panel_number` is the map timeline key: all panels sharing that pair are successive versions of the same sheet of the map. Publication requires `community_id`, `map_panel_number`, `revision_sequence`, `zone_code`, `base_flood_elevation_ft` and `effective_date`, and optionally accepts `supersedes_panel_id`, `sfha_boundary_shift_ft`, `horizontal_uncertainty_ft` and `study_case_number`. `revision_sequence` must be at least 1. `zone_code` on a panel is the flood zone the panel assigns to structures inside its Special Flood Hazard Area and must be one of `A`, `AE`, `AO`, `AH` or `VE`. `base_flood_elevation_ft` is the base flood elevation in NAVD88 feet carried to 2 decimal places, and is null for an unnumbered zone `A` panel where no elevation has been determined. `effective_date` is the printed effective date, and it means something precise: a panel governs the half-open interval that starts at its `effective_date` and ends at its successor's `effective_date`. `study_case_number` is the federal agency's study case number tracking the revision project, and defaults to null.

Two geometry inputs deserve emphasis because Section 3.5 consumes them. `sfha_boundary_shift_ft` defaults to 0 and is the signed number of feet by which this panel moves the SFHA boundary relative to the original digitisation on the community's first-sequence FIRM, where a positive value means the hazard area expanded outward. `horizontal_uncertainty_ft` also defaults to 0, must be zero or greater, and is the cartographic uncertainty of this particular panel, which is added to the geocoder's positional buffer when a structure is classified against the boundary.

Whether a newly published panel is immediately live depends entirely on `revision_sequence`. An initial FIRM is published, not revised: when `revision_sequence` is 1 the panel's `revision_status` is set directly to `EFFECTIVE` at creation rather than to `PRELIMINARY`, its `effective_date` is stored from the request body, `supersedes_panel_id` must be null, and no other panel with the same community and `map_panel_number` may already be `EFFECTIVE`. Every panel with `revision_sequence` of 2 or more instead enters `PRELIMINARY` and must walk the full appeal and letter-of-final-determination path before it can govern anything, and its `supersedes_panel_id` must name the currently `EFFECTIVE` panel with the same community and `map_panel_number` whose `revision_sequence` is exactly one less than the new panel's. Either way `revision_round` starts at 0. The sequencing and uniqueness rules are enforced together and all report `ERR_PANEL_SEQUENCE_INVALID`: `revision_sequence` must equal the superseded panel's `revision_sequence` plus one; a `revision_sequence` above 1 may not be published with `supersedes_panel_id` left null; a `revision_sequence` of 1 may not be published with `supersedes_panel_id` set; the triple of community, `map_panel_number` and `revision_sequence` must be unique; and at most one panel per community and `map_panel_number` may be `EFFECTIVE` at a time. A panel's `revision_status` is always one of `PRELIMINARY`, `APPEAL_OPEN`, `LFD_ISSUED`, `EFFECTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

Opening the statutory appeal period is an agency action on a panel that is still `PRELIMINARY` and whose `revision_sequence` is 2 or more, and it moves the panel `PRELIMINARY → APPEAL_OPEN`. The caller may supply `appeal_period_end_date` explicitly; when omitted, it defaults to 90 days after the current logical instant, which is the statutory appeal window. Attempting to open an appeal period on a panel in any other `revision_status` — an already `EFFECTIVE` panel, for instance — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PANEL_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Resolving the appeal period is the panel's branch point, and both branches are available only once the posted window has closed, meaning the current logical instant is at or after `appeal_period_end_date`; recording a decision while `appeal_period_end_date` is still in the future is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_PERIOD_OPEN`, and recording one against a panel that is not `APPEAL_OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PANEL_STATE`. The `AgencyOfficer` supplies an `outcome`. When `outcome` is `REVISED_PRELIMINARY`, the appeals forced a re-issue of the preliminary map: the panel returns `APPEAL_OPEN → PRELIMINARY`, its `revision_round` is incremented by 1, its `appeal_period_end_date` is cleared to null so that a fresh appeal period can be opened for a second round, and `lfd_issued_at` remains null. When `outcome` is `LFD_ISSUED`, the letter of final determination is issued instead: the panel moves `APPEAL_OPEN → LFD_ISSUED` and `lfd_issued_at` is set to the current logical instant, while `revision_round` and `appeal_period_end_date` are left exactly as they were. The two outcomes write disjoint field sets and neither writes the other's fields.

Making a panel govern the map is the heaviest single action in this API. The panel must be in `LFD_ISSUED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PANEL_STATE`. At least 180 days must have elapsed since `lfd_issued_at` — the letter-of-final-determination waiting period — and the panel's appeal period must have closed, meaning `appeal_period_end_date` is at or before the current logical instant; a premature attempt is rejected with `ERR_LFD_WAITING_PERIOD`. The panel's own `effective_date` must be at or before the current logical instant, so a future-dated panel cannot go live, and it must be strictly later than the `effective_date` of the panel named by `supersedes_panel_id`, so that the half-open governing intervals of the two panels cannot overlap; either failure is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OVERLAP`.

When those gates pass, five hops fire in order and every one of them is a real mutation with its own `updated_at` refresh and `version` increment. In the first hop the panel itself moves `LFD_ISSUED → EFFECTIVE`. In the second hop, because this panel is now effective, the predecessor panel it names in `supersedes_panel_id` moves `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_at` set to the current logical instant. In the third hop, because that predecessor is now superseded, every determination whose `map_panel_id` is the predecessor and whose `status` is `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW` with `flagged_reason` set to `MAP_REVISION`. In the fourth hop, because those determinations are now flagged, each loan folio referenced by them re-counts `pending_redetermination_count` and recomputes `compliance_posture`, which the clause ladder of Section 3.6 resolves to `REVIEW_PENDING`. In the fifth hop, because those folios are now review-pending, every compliance notice on them whose `status` is `SENT` and whose `notice_type` is either `MANDATORY_PURCHASE` or `FORCE_PLACEMENT_WARNING` moves `SENT → SUPERSEDED` with `closed_at` set to the current logical instant, and each of those folios re-counts `open_notice_count`. A determination that was already `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW`, `UNDER_DISPUTE` or `SUPERSEDED` when the panel went effective is not touched by the third hop, and a notice whose `notice_type` is `COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE` or `RE_DETERMINATION` is not touched by the fifth.

Panels never leave `SUPERSEDED`. The `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED` transition is reachable only as that second hop: it requires the panel to be `EFFECTIVE` and requires the successor panel that is going effective to name it in `supersedes_panel_id`, and it always carries the third hop against the determinations citing it.

### 3.3. Prospective Analysis of a Preliminary Map

Long before a revision panel governs anything, lenders and the agency want to know what it will do. A read-only projection capability, available to `ProviderAnalyst`, `ProviderReviewer` and `AgencyOfficer` callers only, evaluates a panel that has not yet gone live and reports the damage. The panel under study must have a `revision_status` of `PRELIMINARY`, `APPEAL_OPEN` or `LFD_ISSUED`; asking for a projection against an `EFFECTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` panel is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_NOT_PRELIMINARY` (HTTP 409), since a live panel is not a hypothesis. The projection creates no determination, mutates no structure and sends no notice.

The source set is pinned exactly. The projection considers every property in the panel's community whose `map_panel_number` equals the panel's own and whose `geocode_status` is `GEOCODED`, and then every structure on those properties walked in `created_at` ascending order. For each structure it locates the loan folio on the same property whose `current_determination_id` names a determination for that structure; structures with no such folio are skipped entirely and do not count toward any figure. The determination so located is the structure's current one, in `status` `ISSUED`, `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW` or `UNDER_DISPUTE`, and against it the projection computes a prospective classification of the structure using the panel under study in place of the effective panel, applying the conservative resolution rule described in Section 3.5.

The response reports the panel's own identifier as `map_panel_id` and its `revision_status`, then six figures. `structures_evaluated` is the number of structures that had a folio. `newly_in_sfha_count` is the number of those structures whose current determination has a false `sfha_flag` while the prospective classification yields a true one. `newly_out_of_sfha_count` is the mirror image: current `sfha_flag` true, prospective `sfha_flag` false. `bfe_increase_count` is the number of structures whose current determination carries a non-null `base_flood_elevation_ft`, where the panel under study also carries a non-null `base_flood_elevation_ft`, and where the panel's figure is greater than the determination's. `folios_requiring_new_notice_count` is the number of distinct folios behind the newly-in-SFHA structures whose `insurance_status` is `NONE` or `NOT_REQUIRED` — the folios that would need a fresh mandatory-purchase notice. `aggregate_new_required_coverage_cents` is the sum, over the newly-in-SFHA structures only, of the required coverage each would carry under the prospective classification, computed by the coverage rule of Section 3.6 using the prospective determination, the structure, its folio and its community. The aggregate is order-insensitive; the `created_at` ascending walk is pinned only so that a per-structure evaluation log is reproducible.

### 3.4. Geocoding Properties and Cataloguing Structures

A determination is only as good as the coordinates behind it, so property registration is where the provider's positional risk is recorded honestly. A `ProviderAnalyst` registers a parcel by supplying the owning `community_id`, the situs `street_address`, `city` and `postal_code`, the `map_panel_number` of the FIRM sheet the parcel falls on — which is what later joins the parcel to a map panel within its community — and the geocoder's output: `latitude` and `longitude` each carried to 6 decimal places, a `geocode_method` naming the positional method the geocoder actually resolved, and a `geocode_confidence_score` carried to 2 decimal places which must be between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive. `geocode_method` must be one of `ROOFTOP`, `PARCEL_CENTROID`, `ZIP_CENTROID` or `FAILED`.

From those inputs the system derives two fields and initialises a third. `geocode_status` is `GEOCODED` when, and only when, `geocode_method` is anything other than `FAILED` **and** `geocode_confidence_score` is at or above the confidence floor of 0.60; in every other case it is `UNRESOLVED`. `geocode_buffer_ft` is the positional buffer implied by the method: 0 feet for a `ROOFTOP` hit, 25 feet for a `PARCEL_CENTROID` hit, 250 feet for a `ZIP_CENTROID` hit, and 0 feet for a `FAILED` geocode. `structure_count` starts at 0 and is thereafter a re-count of the structures on this property. An unresolved property is unusable for the two operations that need to know where the building is: issuing a determination against it and filing an elevation certificate for one of its structures are both rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_GEOCODED` (HTTP 422).

Geocodes get corrected — a rooftop hit replaces a ZIP centroid, or a transposed coordinate is fixed — and correcting one is a two-hop cascade owned by a `ProviderAnalyst`. The correction supplies a fresh `latitude`, `longitude`, `geocode_method` and `geocode_confidence_score`, all four required. In the first hop those four values replace the stored ones, `geocode_status` and `geocode_buffer_ft` are recomputed from them, and `geocode_corrected_count` is incremented by 1 to record how many accepted corrections this property has had. In the second hop, because the parcel has effectively moved, every determination in `status` `ISSUED` that was issued against a structure of this property moves `ISSUED → FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW` with `flagged_reason` set to `GEOCODE_CORRECTION`, and each affected folio re-counts `pending_redetermination_count` and recomputes `compliance_posture`. `flagged_reason` is therefore always `MAP_REVISION`, `GEOCODE_CORRECTION` or null, and it is the only signal distinguishing a map-driven re-determination from a geometry-driven one.

Structures are catalogued against a parcel one at a time, again by a `ProviderAnalyst`, using the parcel's `property_id`. Each structure carries a `structure_label` distinguishing it from its siblings on the same parcel (`PRINCIPAL_RESIDENCE` is the typical value), an `occupancy_type` that must be one of `SINGLE_FAMILY`, `TWO_TO_FOUR_FAMILY`, `OTHER_RESIDENTIAL` or `NON_RESIDENTIAL` and which selects the building coverage cap, a `foundation_type` that must be one of `SLAB`, `CRAWLSPACE`, `BASEMENT` or `PIER_PILE` as recorded on the elevation certificate, a `replacement_cost_cents` figure that must be strictly greater than zero, and `sfha_offset_ft` — the signed horizontal distance in feet from the SFHA boundary as digitised on the community's first-sequence FIRM, where a negative value places the building inside the hazard area, a positive value places it outside, and zero puts it exactly on the boundary. Two flags are optional: `post_firm_flag` defaults to true and records that construction started on or after the community's initial FIRM date, and `fill_placed_flag` defaults to false and records that engineered fill raised the structure, which is what later distinguishes a fill-based map revision from a plain amendment. Adding a structure re-counts the parent property's `structure_count`.

A new structure has no surveyed elevation. `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` and `lowest_adjacent_grade_ft` both stay null, and `elevation_source_certificate_id` stays null, until an elevation certificate for that structure is validated; only then are those three fields populated, as described in Section 3.8.

### 3.5. Loan Folios and the Determination Itself

A `Lender` opens a life-of-loan monitoring folio for each loan it services, naming its own `lender_id`, the `property_id` securing the loan, its internal `loan_number` — which must be unique among that lender's folios — and the `borrower_user_id`, the opaque identifier the borrower will present when acting on this folio. It also supplies `principal_balance_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero, and `policy_anniversary_date`, the annual policy renewal date that anchors premium proration. `escrow_balance_cents` is optional, defaults to 0, and must be zero or greater. At creation the folio's `monitoring_status` is `ACTIVE` and its `insurance_status` is `NONE`; `initial_escrow_balance_cents` is snapshotted from `escrow_balance_cents` to anchor the conservation invariant of Section 2.10; `current_determination_id`, `required_coverage_cents`, `reported_coverage_cents`, `uninsured_exposure_cents`, `force_placed_premium_cents`, `force_placed_coverage_days`, `cumulative_earned_premium_cents`, `open_notice_count` and `pending_redetermination_count` all start empty or at zero, with `force_placed_at` null; `compliance_posture` is `COMPLIANT`, because with no determination yet the required coverage is zero; and the community's `active_folio_count` is re-counted.

Issuing a determination is a `ProviderAnalyst` action and takes exactly four body fields: `loan_folio_id`, `structure_id`, `determination_type` and the concurrency guard `expected_loan_folio_version` from Section 2.6. `determination_type` must be `ORIGINATION` or `LIFE_OF_LOAN_REVIEW` when supplied by a client; the third value, `DISPUTE_REVISION`, is minted only by the sustained-dispute cascade and a client may not request it. The named structure must sit on the property the folio names, or the request is rejected with `ERR_STRUCTURE_FOLIO_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A second `ORIGINATION` determination on a folio that already has a `current_determination_id` is rejected with `ERR_ORIGINATION_ALREADY_COMPLETE` (HTTP 409) — origination happens once per folio.

**Panel selection.** Before any geometry is evaluated, the system must decide which panel governs. It considers only map panels whose `revision_status` is `EFFECTIVE`, whose community matches the property's community, whose `map_panel_number` matches the property's, and whose `effective_date` is at or before the current logical instant; panels in `PRELIMINARY`, `APPEAL_OPEN`, `LFD_ISSUED` or `SUPERSEDED` never qualify. Among the qualifying panels the one with the latest `effective_date` wins, and if two share that date the one created earlier wins — the tie is broken on `created_at` ascending and never on the identifier. If no panel qualifies, the operation fails with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_PANEL` (HTTP 422). Because at most one panel per community and `map_panel_number` may be `EFFECTIVE`, the qualifying set is normally of size zero or one; the ranking and tie-break are pinned anyway so that the selection stays deterministic. The winning panel is recorded on the determination as `map_panel_id`, and the same selection rule runs when an elevation certificate is filed and on each re-determination inside a monitoring sweep.

**Classifying the structure.** The geometry then resolves in a fixed order. The applicable buffer in feet is the sum of the property's method-derived positional buffer — 0 for `ROOFTOP`, 25 for `PARCEL_CENTROID`, 250 for `ZIP_CENTROID`, 0 for `FAILED` — and the governing panel's `horizontal_uncertainty_ft`. The structure's effective offset in feet is its `sfha_offset_ft` minus the panel's `sfha_boundary_shift_ft`, which is how a panel that pushed the hazard boundary outward drags buildings into the SFHA without anyone re-surveying them. The structure's `position_class` is then `INSIDE` when the effective offset plus the buffer is strictly less than zero; otherwise `OUTSIDE` when the effective offset minus the buffer is strictly greater than zero; and otherwise `INDETERMINATE`, which is the case where the buffer straddles the boundary. Both comparisons are exact decimal comparisons against zero and both are strict, so an effective offset whose buffered value lands exactly on zero yields `INDETERMINATE` rather than a definite side.

An `INSIDE` classification takes the panel's `zone_code` as the determination's `zone_code` and sets `sfha_flag` true. An `OUTSIDE` classification sets `zone_code` to `X` and `sfha_flag` false — `X` is a determination-only zone value that no panel ever carries, so the determination's `zone_code` ranges over `A`, `AE`, `AO`, `AH`, `VE` and `X`. An `INDETERMINATE` classification is where the two evaluation variants diverge, and the divergence is deliberate. Under the strict variant — used for every `ORIGINATION` determination, for every `DISPUTE_REVISION` determination, and for every prospective evaluation performed by the preliminary-map projection of Section 3.3 — an `INDETERMINATE` result resolves conservatively to the panel's `zone_code` with `sfha_flag` true, so that whenever the buffer straddles the boundary the lender is told insurance is mandatory. Under the lenient variant — used for every `LIFE_OF_LOAN_REVIEW` determination, whether issued manually or minted by a monitoring sweep — an `INDETERMINATE` result carries forward the superseded determination's `zone_code` and `sfha_flag` unchanged, so an ambiguous re-read never flips a borrower's obligation; if there is no prior determination to carry forward, the lenient variant falls back to the strict rule. The consequence is that identical structure, property and panel inputs can produce `sfha_flag` true under the strict variant and `sfha_flag` false under the lenient one, whenever the prior determination said the building was outside.

Two derived elevation fields close out the classification. The determination's `base_flood_elevation_ft` is a snapshot of the governing panel's `base_flood_elevation_ft` when `sfha_flag` is true, and null when `sfha_flag` is false. The determination's `freeboard_ft` is the structure's `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` minus that snapshotted base flood elevation, rounded to 1 decimal place half-up; when either input is null — an unsurveyed structure, or a building outside the SFHA — `freeboard_ft` is null. Finally `mandatory_purchase_flag` is true when `sfha_flag` is true **and** the property's community has a `coverage_availability` other than `UNAVAILABLE`; a building inside the hazard area of a community where federal coverage cannot be written carries no mandatory purchase obligation.

**Recording and superseding.** A new determination is stored in `status` `ISSUED` with `issued_at` set to the current logical instant and `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`. Any prior determination for the same folio and structure that was in `ISSUED` or `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_at` set to the current logical instant, and the new row names it in `supersedes_determination_id`; on the flagged path the retired row keeps the `flagged_reason` it was carrying and the folio's `pending_redetermination_count` is decremented, while on the plain issued path there is no flagged reason to carry. Either way the folio's `current_determination_id` is repointed at the new determination, `required_coverage_cents` and `uninsured_exposure_cents` are recomputed, `compliance_posture` is recomputed, and the folio's `version` is incremented by 1 — which is exactly why replaying the same `expected_loan_folio_version` a second time yields the stale-version rejection. `status` on a determination is always one of `ISSUED`, `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW`, `UNDER_DISPUTE` or `SUPERSEDED`, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

The coverage dimension of the folio reacts to the new determination on its own. A folio whose `insurance_status` is `NONE` moves `NONE → NOT_REQUIRED` when the incoming current determination has `mandatory_purchase_flag` false; that transition sets `required_coverage_cents` to 0, sets `uninsured_exposure_cents` to 0, and recomputes `compliance_posture` to `COMPLIANT`. Symmetrically, a folio whose `insurance_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` moves `NOT_REQUIRED → NONE` when a re-determination brings the structure back into the hazard area with `mandatory_purchase_flag` true; that transition recomputes `required_coverage_cents` and recomputes `compliance_posture`, which lands on `NOTICE_DUE` because an obligation now exists with no notice out. Both directions are system-driven consequences of the determination write, not separate client actions.

### 3.6. The Mandatory Purchase Obligation: Coverage, Notices, Force Placement and Escrow

**How much coverage is required.** The obligation amount follows directly from the folio's current determination. When `mandatory_purchase_flag` on that determination is false, `required_coverage_cents` is 0 and there is nothing to buy. When it is true, the system first picks a statutory cap from the structure's `occupancy_type`: in a community whose `coverage_availability` is `EMERGENCY_LIMITS_ONLY` the cap is 3,500,000 cents (thirty-five thousand dollars) for a `SINGLE_FAMILY` or `TWO_TO_FOUR_FAMILY` building and 10,000,000 cents (one hundred thousand dollars) for an `OTHER_RESIDENTIAL` or `NON_RESIDENTIAL` building, while in a community at any other availability the cap is 25,000,000 cents (two hundred and fifty thousand dollars) for a `SINGLE_FAMILY` or `TWO_TO_FOUR_FAMILY` building and 50,000,000 cents (five hundred thousand dollars) for an `OTHER_RESIDENTIAL` or `NON_RESIDENTIAL` building. `required_coverage_cents` is then the smallest of three integer amounts: the folio's `principal_balance_cents`, the structure's `replacement_cost_cents`, and that cap. No rounding is involved; all three are integer cents. Four inputs therefore interact non-linearly, and a change to community standing alone can drop the required amount from 25,000,000 cents to 3,500,000 cents without anything about the loan or the building changing.

`uninsured_exposure_cents` is 0 whenever the folio's `insurance_status` is `FORCE_PLACED`, because a force-placed folio has no gap by construction; otherwise it is `required_coverage_cents` minus `reported_coverage_cents`, floored at 0 so that over-reported coverage never produces a negative exposure. Both figures are recomputed on every write that repoints `current_determination_id`, on every community participation change, and whenever a compliance notice is created so that the notice can snapshot them. The inputs are the folio's current determination in `status` `ISSUED`, `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW` or `UNDER_DISPUTE`, that determination's structure, the property's community, and the folio itself.

**The compliance posture ladder.** `compliance_posture` is the single derived gate that governs whether a notice may be sent and whether force placement is permitted, and it is computed by evaluating seven clauses top to bottom and taking the first that matches. First, if the community's `coverage_availability` is `UNAVAILABLE` the posture is `COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. Second, if the folio's `insurance_status` is `FORCE_PLACED` the posture is `FORCE_PLACED`. Third, if `pending_redetermination_count` is greater than zero the posture is `REVIEW_PENDING`. Fourth, if `required_coverage_cents` is 0, or `reported_coverage_cents` is at or above `required_coverage_cents`, the posture is `COMPLIANT`. Fifth, if a compliance notice exists on this folio whose `notice_type` is `MANDATORY_PURCHASE`, whose `status` is `SENT` or `EXPIRED`, and whose `response_deadline_at` is strictly earlier than the current logical instant, the posture is `FORCE_PLACE_ELIGIBLE`. Sixth, if a compliance notice exists on this folio whose `notice_type` is `MANDATORY_PURCHASE`, whose `status` is `SENT`, and whose `response_deadline_at` is at or after the current logical instant, the posture is `NOTICE_PENDING`. Seventh and otherwise, the posture is `NOTICE_DUE`. Only mandatory-purchase notices participate in the fifth and sixth clauses, and notices that have reached `SUPERSEDED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` are excluded from both. The clause order is load-bearing: a folio that has both a lapsed notice and a pending re-determination reports `REVIEW_PENDING`, not `FORCE_PLACE_ELIGIBLE`, so an in-flight map revision blocks force placement. The posture is recomputed on every write that touches the folio, its current determination, its notice set, or its property's community. It is a stored field on the folio rather than a read-time derivation: reading a folio — singly or in a collection, and when filtering a collection by `compliance_posture` — reports the value the most recent recomputation left there, and each recomputation reads the logical clock at the moment it runs. Because advancing the clock is inert by itself (Section 2.4), a mandatory-purchase deadline that lapses between two recomputations does not by itself move a stored `NOTICE_PENDING` to `FORCE_PLACE_ELIGIBLE`; the ladder has to be re-evaluated by an action on the folio before the newer posture is observable.

**Sending a notice.** A `Lender` or a `ProviderAnalyst` sends a compliance notice by naming the `loan_folio_id`, the `determination_id` the notice cites, a `notice_type`, and an `annual_premium_rate_cents` figure — the annual force-placement premium quoted by the lender's carrier, which must be zero or greater and is supplied as 0 when not applicable. `notice_type` must be one of `MANDATORY_PURCHASE`, `FORCE_PLACEMENT_WARNING`, `COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE` or `RE_DETERMINATION`; the first three are the ones a caller may issue, while `RE_DETERMINATION` notices are minted only by system cascades. The cited determination must be the folio's `current_determination_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_STRUCTURE_FOLIO_MISMATCH`; a determination already `SUPERSEDED` when a notice names it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE`. A notice may not be sent into a community whose `coverage_availability` is `UNAVAILABLE`, which is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE`, and a `MANDATORY_PURCHASE` notice specifically requires that the folio's current determination carry `mandatory_purchase_flag` true, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_MANDATORY_PURCHASE_NOT_TRIGGERED`. A cooldown prevents notice spam: a new notice of the same `notice_type` on the same folio is refused while an existing `SENT` notice of that type has a closed window — running from its `sent_at` through 5 days past its `response_deadline_at`, both ends inclusive — that contains the current logical instant, and the refusal is `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_ACTIVE`.

A notice that survives all of that is created in `status` `SENT` with `sent_at` at the current logical instant, and its `response_deadline_at` is computed as `sent_at` plus a response window that depends on the type: 45 days for `MANDATORY_PURCHASE`, 30 days for `FORCE_PLACEMENT_WARNING`, 30 days for `RE_DETERMINATION` and 15 days for `COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. That instant is the right-hand end of a closed response window, so an action taken exactly at the deadline is still inside it. The notice snapshots the folio's obligation as `required_coverage_snapshot_cents`, taken at the instant the notice was sent, and it stores a forward-looking premium estimate in `estimated_force_place_premium_cents`. `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by_role` and `closed_at` all start null. The folio then re-counts `open_notice_count` and recomputes `compliance_posture`: when this notice is the folio's only mandatory-purchase obligation and its deadline is still in the future the posture becomes `NOTICE_PENDING`, but an older `MANDATORY_PURCHASE` notice whose deadline has already lapsed still wins the ladder and holds the posture at `FORCE_PLACE_ELIGIBLE`.

**Premium arithmetic, in two variants.** Both premium figures start from the same anniversary roll. Take the folio's `policy_anniversary_date`; while that date is at or before the variant's own anchor instant, advance it by exactly one calendar year, repeating until it is strictly after the anchor. The rolled anniversary is the end of the coverage the premium buys.

The forward-looking estimate is computed once, at notice send time, and stored on the notice as `estimated_force_place_premium_cents`. Its anchor is the notice's own `response_deadline_at`. It counts whole calendar months between anchor and rolled anniversary: take twelve times the difference in years, add the difference in months, then subtract one if the anniversary's day-of-month is earlier than the anchor's day-of-month, and floor the result at zero. The estimate is `annual_premium_rate_cents` multiplied by that whole-month count, divided by 12, and rounded half-up to a whole cent. The day-level remainder is discarded entirely by design.

The actual premium is computed at force placement and stored on the folio as `force_placed_premium_cents`. Its anchor is the current logical instant at placement. `force_placed_coverage_days` is the exact number of days from that anchor up to but not including the rolled anniversary, and `force_placed_premium_cents` is `annual_premium_rate_cents` multiplied by `force_placed_coverage_days`, divided by 365, and rounded half-up to a whole cent — so the actual figure charges precisely the day-level remainder the estimate threw away, and the two numbers, living on two different entities, are both observable and normally differ. The `annual_premium_rate_cents` used at placement comes from the notice that made the folio eligible: among the folio's notices with `notice_type` `MANDATORY_PURCHASE` and `status` `SENT` or `EXPIRED`, the one with the greatest `sent_at`, breaking a tie on `created_at` ascending.

**Force placing.** Force placement is a `Lender` action on its own folio and takes no body fields at all; everything it needs it derives. Before evaluating eligibility it first sweeps the folio's notices: any notice still in `SENT` whose `response_deadline_at` has passed is moved `SENT → EXPIRED` with `closed_at` set to the current logical instant and the folio's `open_notice_count` re-counted, which is what allows the posture ladder to read `FORCE_PLACE_ELIGIBLE` rather than `NOTICE_PENDING`. That sweep is unconditional and is not undone when the placement is then refused: because it runs ahead of every one of the checks below, a request that ends in `ERR_INVALID_FOLIO_STATE`, `ERR_COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_EXPIRED`, `ERR_FORCE_PLACEMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED` or `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT` still leaves the notices it swept in `EXPIRED` with their `closed_at` set at that instant and the folio's re-counted `open_notice_count` and recomputed `compliance_posture` persisted. What such a refusal spares is the money and the coverage move, not the sweep. Force placement then requires the folio's `insurance_status` to be `NONE` — attempting it on a folio already `FORCE_PLACED` or `BORROWER_PROVIDED`, or on a folio whose `monitoring_status` is `SUSPENDED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FOLIO_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and requires `compliance_posture` to be exactly `FORCE_PLACE_ELIGIBLE`, failing which the rejection is `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_EXPIRED`: either no mandatory-purchase notice was ever sent, or its deadline has not passed, or a pending re-determination is holding the folio in `REVIEW_PENDING`. A community whose `coverage_availability` is `UNAVAILABLE` blocks placement with `ERR_COVERAGE_UNAVAILABLE`. The owning lender must have `force_placement_authorized_flag` true, or the request is rejected with `ERR_FORCE_PLACEMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and must have `escrow_servicing_flag` true, since a non-escrowing lender may not have premiums debited. Finally the folio's `escrow_balance_cents` must be at or above the computed `force_placed_premium_cents`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ESCROW_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402) and nothing is drawn.

On success the folio's coverage dimension moves `NONE → FORCE_PLACED`. `force_placed_premium_cents` and `force_placed_coverage_days` are computed and stored, `force_placed_at` is set to the current logical instant, `escrow_balance_cents` is reduced by `force_placed_premium_cents` while `cumulative_earned_premium_cents` is deliberately left untouched (nothing has been earned yet), `uninsured_exposure_cents` is set to 0, `compliance_posture` becomes `FORCE_PLACED`, the citing notice is left in `EXPIRED` with its `closed_at` recorded, `open_notice_count` is re-counted, the owning lender's `open_force_placement_count` is re-counted, and the folio's `version` increments by 1.

**Reporting borrower coverage, and unwinding a placement.** A `Lender` reports borrower-obtained coverage on its own folio by supplying `reported_coverage_cents`. The figure must be at or above the folio's `required_coverage_cents` and must not exceed the structure's `replacement_cost_cents`; either violation — under-insurance or an uninsurable over-report — is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_BELOW_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Reporting coverage while the folio's `insurance_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FOLIO_STATE`, since there is no obligation to satisfy. From `NONE` the folio moves `NONE → BORROWER_PROVIDED`: `reported_coverage_cents` is stored, `uninsured_exposure_cents` recomputes to 0, `compliance_posture` recomputes to `COMPLIANT`, and every notice on the folio in `status` `SENT` whose `notice_type` is `MANDATORY_PURCHASE` or `FORCE_PLACEMENT_WARNING` moves `SENT → SUPERSEDED` with `closed_at` set, after which `open_notice_count` is re-counted. The folio's `version` increments by 1.

From `FORCE_PLACED` the same report moves the folio `FORCE_PLACED → BORROWER_PROVIDED` and additionally runs the escrow settlement, which is the compensating action for the placement. Settlement counts `days_in_force` as the number of days from `force_placed_at` up to but not including the current logical instant. The earned premium is `force_placed_premium_cents` multiplied by `days_in_force`, divided by `force_placed_coverage_days`, rounded half-up to a whole cent. The refund is `force_placed_premium_cents` minus that earned amount. The refund is added to `escrow_balance_cents`, the earned amount is added to `cumulative_earned_premium_cents`, `force_placed_premium_cents` is reset to 0, `force_placed_coverage_days` is reset to 0, and `force_placed_at` is cleared to null. Because the refund absorbs the rounding remainder, the conservation invariant of Section 2.10 holds exactly afterwards: `escrow_balance_cents` plus `cumulative_earned_premium_cents` equals `initial_escrow_balance_cents`. If `days_in_force` has reached or exceeded `force_placed_coverage_days` the whole premium is earned and the refund is 0; `days_in_force` is never negative because `force_placed_at` is always at or before the current instant. The lender's `open_force_placement_count` is re-counted and `compliance_posture` recomputes to `COMPLIANT`.

There is one further way out of a placement, and it produces exactly the same settlement arithmetic. When a sustained dispute yields a replacement determination whose `mandatory_purchase_flag` is false, a folio in `FORCE_PLACED` moves `FORCE_PLACED → NOT_REQUIRED`: the settlement runs identically to the borrower-provided path, `required_coverage_cents` is set to 0, and `compliance_posture` recomputes to `COMPLIANT`. Section 3.9 describes the cascade that drives it.

**Acknowledging and expiring notices.** Either a `Lender` or a `Borrower` may acknowledge a notice, and doing so closes the obligation. The notice must be in `status` `SENT` — acknowledging one already `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE` — and the current logical instant must be at or before `response_deadline_at`, since the response window is closed at its right end. The acting identity must own the folio: a `Lender` caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the folio's `lender_id`, and a `Borrower` caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the folio's `borrower_user_id`. Acknowledgement moves the notice `SENT → ACKNOWLEDGED`, sets `acknowledged_at` and `closed_at` to the current logical instant, and records `acknowledged_by_role` as the acting role — the value is `Lender` or `Borrower` and is otherwise null. The folio then re-counts `open_notice_count`, which by the cardinality invariant returns to the value it held before this notice was sent, and recomputes `compliance_posture`. A notice's `status` is always one of `SENT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and the last three are terminal. Beyond acknowledgement, a `SENT` notice leaves that state in only two ways: it expires, when a monitoring sweep or a force placement observes that the current logical instant is strictly past its `response_deadline_at`, and it is superseded, when the determination it cites leaves the governing position or the folio's coverage obligation is satisfied — the fifth hop of the panel-effective cascade, the fifth hop of the sustained-dispute cascade, or the folio's move into `BORROWER_PROVIDED`. Both of those set `closed_at` to the current logical instant and both re-count `open_notice_count`.

### 3.7. The Life-of-Loan Monitoring Sweep

Life-of-loan monitoring is what the lender is actually paying for, and it is driven by a `ProviderAnalyst` running a sweep. The sweep accepts two optional filters, `community_id` and `map_panel_id`, which narrow the work to one community or one panel; with neither supplied it processes everything outstanding. It runs in two phases and returns a set of counters rather than an entity.

Phase one takes as its scope every determination whose `status` is `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW`, restricted by whichever filters were supplied, and processes them in `created_at` ascending order; that ordering is total for a single-writer request sequence and the identifier is never consulted as a tie-break. For each flagged determination the sweep re-selects the governing panel by the effective-panel rule of Section 3.5 and re-classifies the structure under the lenient variant, so an `INDETERMINATE` re-read carries the flagged determination's own `zone_code` and `sfha_flag` forward rather than flipping the borrower into the hazard area. The outcome then branches on whether the answer changed.

When the re-classification reproduces both the flagged determination's `zone_code` and its `sfha_flag`, nothing of substance has changed and the determination returns `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW → ISSUED`: `flagged_reason` is cleared to null, `map_panel_id` is repointed at the panel the re-selection chose, the folio's `pending_redetermination_count` is decremented and its `compliance_posture` recomputed. No new determination row is created and no notice is sent. The sweep's `redeterminations_cleared` counter is incremented by one.

When the re-classification yields a different `zone_code` or a different `sfha_flag`, the flagged determination instead moves `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW → SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` at the current logical instant, and a fresh determination is minted with `determination_type` `LIFE_OF_LOAN_REVIEW`, `supersedes_determination_id` naming the retired row, and `issued_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`. The folio's `current_determination_id` is repointed at the new row, `required_coverage_cents` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed and `pending_redetermination_count` is decremented. If the new determination carries `mandatory_purchase_flag` true and the folio's `insurance_status` is `NONE` or `NOT_REQUIRED` — that is, the borrower has just acquired an obligation and has no coverage — a system notice with `notice_type` `RE_DETERMINATION` is sent citing the new determination, and the folio re-counts `open_notice_count`; the sweep's `notices_sent` counter is incremented for each such notice. The `redeterminations_issued` counter is incremented for each superseded-and-reissued determination.

Phase two always runs, even when phase one's scope was empty. Every compliance notice whose `status` is `SENT` and whose `response_deadline_at` is strictly earlier than the current logical instant moves `SENT → EXPIRED` with `closed_at` set and the owning folio's `open_notice_count` re-counted, and `notices_expired` is incremented once per notice so closed. The response reports `determinations_scanned` (the size of phase one's scope), `redeterminations_cleared`, `redeterminations_issued`, `notices_sent`, `notices_expired`, and `run_at`, the logical instant at which the sweep ran. An empty scope is a success, not an error: every counter reads 0 and no row is written.

### 3.8. Elevation Evidence and Map Amendment Packages

**Filing a certificate.** A surveyed elevation certificate is the only evidence that can move a building's recorded elevation, and either a `Borrower` or a `ProviderAnalyst` may file one. The filing names the `structure_id`, the `surveyor_license_number` of the licensed surveyor who signed the FEMA form, the `survey_date`, the vertical `datum` of the raw figures — which must be `NAVD88` or `NGVD29` — and the two surveyed elevations, `raw_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` and `raw_lowest_adjacent_grade_ft`, each carried to 2 decimal places in the stated datum. Two fields are optional: `machinery_elevation_ft`, the elevation of machinery and equipment servicing the building in the stated datum, which defaults to null; and `dispute_id`, set when the certificate is being filed as evidence in an open dispute, which also defaults to null. A `Borrower` filer must be the borrower named on the relevant folio or the filing is rejected with `ERR_BORROWER_NOT_ON_LOAN`. When a `dispute_id` is supplied, the certificate's structure must be the same structure as the disputed determination's, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_STRUCTURE_FOLIO_MISMATCH`. The structure's property must be geocoded, failing which the rejection is `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_GEOCODED`, and an effective panel must exist for that property, failing which the rejection is `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_PANEL`; the panel chosen by the same effective-panel rule used for determinations is recorded on the certificate as `reference_panel_id` and supplies the base flood elevation the freeboard figures are measured against.

**The datum conversion, in a pinned order.** Vertical datums differ by region, and the shift from NGVD29 to NAVD88 is selected by the community's `state_code`: −2.44 feet in `CA`, −1.15 feet in `FL`, −0.87 feet in `TX`, and −0.55 feet in `NC`. When the certificate's `datum` is `NGVD29` that state-specific offset applies; when it is already `NAVD88` the offset is zero. The conversion then proceeds in four steps and the order is mandatory. Step one shifts each raw survey value into NAVD88 by adding the offset. Step two rounds each shifted value to 1 decimal place half-up, producing `converted_lowest_floor_ft` from `raw_lowest_floor_elevation_ft` and `converted_lag_ft` from `raw_lowest_adjacent_grade_ft` — the rounding happens here, before any subtraction. Step three subtracts the reference panel's `base_flood_elevation_ft`, which carries 2 decimal places, from each already-rounded converted value. Step four rounds each difference to 1 decimal place half-up, producing `freeboard_ft` from the lowest floor and `lag_freeboard_ft` from the lowest adjacent grade. Rounding the difference once, instead of rounding the converted value first, produces a different answer at the tenth-of-a-foot scale, and because amendment eligibility requires a freeboard figure strictly above zero, the two orders differ by grant versus rejection rather than by a cosmetic tenth. A newly filed certificate is stored in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` — the anchor of the certificate-review segregation-of-duties check — and with `rejection_reason` and `reviewed_by_user_id` null.

**Reviewing a certificate.** Only a `ProviderReviewer` may review, the certificate must be in `SUBMITTED` (reviewing one already `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATE_STATE`), and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id` or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The reviewer supplies a `decision` of `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED` and, on the rejecting branch, a `rejection_reason` which must be one of `DATUM_MISMATCH`, `SURVEY_STALE` or `MISSING_MACHINERY_ELEVATION`. Validation additionally requires that the survey still be current: the current logical instant must be at or before 365 days after `survey_date`, and a stale survey is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422).

The two branches are deliberately asymmetric in what they write, and both write `reviewed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. On `VALIDATED` the certificate moves `SUBMITTED → VALIDATED` and the surveyed elevations are published onto the structure: `lowest_floor_elevation_ft` takes the certificate's `converted_lowest_floor_ft`, `lowest_adjacent_grade_ft` takes its `converted_lag_ft`, and `elevation_source_certificate_id` is set to this certificate's identifier, recording which certificate is the source of truth for those numbers. The folio's current determination then recomputes its own `freeboard_ft` from the newly populated `lowest_floor_elevation_ft`; its `zone_code`, `sfha_flag` and `position_class` are explicitly **not** recomputed, because a survey tells you how high the building is, not where it sits relative to the boundary. On `REJECTED` the certificate moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, `rejection_reason` is stored, and no field of the structure is written at all — in particular `elevation_source_certificate_id` remains whatever it was. `VALIDATED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal.

**Preparing an amendment package.** A `ProviderAnalyst` prepares a Letter of Map Amendment or a Letter of Map Revision Based on Fill by naming the `determination_id` the amendment seeks to overturn, the `elevation_certificate_id` supporting it, and a `request_type` of `LOMA` for naturally high ground or `LOMR_F` where engineered fill raised the structure; `expedite_flag` is optional, defaults to false, and is set when the requester pays the expedite surcharge. The referenced certificate must be in `status` `VALIDATED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_VALIDATED` (HTTP 409). The certificate's structure must be the determination's structure, or the request is rejected with `ERR_STRUCTURE_FOLIO_MISMATCH`. The `request_type` must match the structure's fill history — `LOMA` requires `fill_placed_flag` false and `LOMR_F` requires `fill_placed_flag` true — and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_AMENDMENT_TYPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A package is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `resubmission_count` 0, `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `agency_case_number`, `additional_data_note`, `submitted_at` and `decided_at` all null.

**The fee schedule.** The fee is quoted on the draft and re-quoted on every submission, and it has a dependency that only looks circular. `base_review_fee_cents` is the base fee for the `request_type` — nothing at all for a `LOMA`, and 50,000 cents (five hundred dollars) for a `LOMR_F` — plus 12,500 cents (one hundred and twenty-five dollars) for each resubmission already recorded, that is, 12,500 multiplied by `resubmission_count`. `fee_tier` is then keyed on that pre-surcharge subtotal and never on the total: a `base_review_fee_cents` of zero or less is `TIER_1`, a value above zero but at or below 50,000 cents is `TIER_2`, and anything above 50,000 cents is `TIER_3`. `expedite_surcharge_cents` is 0 when `expedite_flag` is false; when it is true, the surcharge is the tier's percentage of `base_review_fee_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole cent, where `TIER_1` carries no surcharge at all, `TIER_2` carries thirty percent, and `TIER_3` carries forty-five percent. `total_fee_cents` is `base_review_fee_cents` plus `expedite_surcharge_cents`. Keying the tier on the total instead of the base would push an expedited `LOMR_F` into the higher tier and inflate its surcharge; the tier is keyed on the base.

**Submitting, resubmitting and deciding.** A `ProviderAnalyst` submits a package; the same capability serves both the first submission and every resubmission. From `DRAFT` the package moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY`; from `ADDITIONAL_DATA_REQUESTED` it moves `ADDITIONAL_DATA_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY` with `resubmission_count` incremented by 1 first, which is what makes the fee grow and can push the package across a tier boundary. Submission from any other state — a package already `SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY`, `GRANTED` or `DENIED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Submission requires that the referenced certificate still be `VALIDATED` (otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_VALIDATED`), that its `survey_date` still be within 365 days of the current logical instant (otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED`), that the referenced determination be in `status` `ISSUED` or `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW`, that a resubmission not exceed the ceiling of two resubmissions, and that the structure actually sit above the base flood elevation: a `LOMA` submission requires the certificate's `freeboard_ft` to be strictly greater than 0.0 and a `LOMR_F` submission requires its `lag_freeboard_ft` to be strictly greater than 0.0, failing which the submission is rejected with `ERR_STRUCTURE_NOT_ABOVE_BFE` (HTTP 422). A successful submission sets `submitted_at` to the current logical instant and re-runs the fee schedule.

An `AgencyOfficer` decides a package that is in `SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY`, supplying a `decision` and optionally an `agency_case_number` and an `additional_data_note`; deciding a package in any other state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE`. On a `decision` of `GRANTED` the package moves `SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY → GRANTED` with `agency_case_number` stored and `decided_at` set to the current logical instant — and, importantly, the structure's `sfha_offset_ft` is **not** modified: a granted amendment is evidence for a fresh determination, not a determination in itself, and its effect on future determinations is out of scope for this transition. On a `decision` of `DENIED` the package moves `SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY → DENIED`, likewise storing `agency_case_number` and setting `decided_at`. On a `decision` of `ADDITIONAL_DATA_REQUESTED` the package moves `SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY → ADDITIONAL_DATA_REQUESTED`, the agency's `additional_data_note` is stored, and `decided_at` is deliberately left null because the request has not terminated; that branch is available only while `resubmission_count` is below the ceiling of 2, and asking for additional data on a package already resubmitted twice is rejected with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409), forcing the agency to grant or deny. `resubmission_count` therefore never exceeds 2, `GRANTED` and `DENIED` are terminal, and the `ADDITIONAL_DATA_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED_TO_AGENCY` cycle can run at most twice.

### 3.9. Borrower Disputes and Joint Agency Review

Consider a borrower who has just been told their house is in the hazard area and who has a survey, a rooftop geocode, or a newer preliminary panel that says otherwise. The dispute channel gives them a structured way to say so, and it puts the provider and the federal agency in the same room: the provider forms a recommendation, the agency concurs with it or overrides it, and if the agency is unconvinced the packet goes back for a second look.

Only a `Borrower` may file, and only against the folio they are named on; a filer whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the folio's `borrower_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_BORROWER_NOT_ON_LOAN`. The filing names the `determination_id` under challenge, a `basis`, a `borrower_statement` giving the narrative, and an `asserted_sfha_offset_ft` — the signed boundary offset in feet the borrower claims is the correct one, which is the number that will be written onto the structure if the dispute succeeds. The challenged determination must be in `status` `ISSUED`; a determination already `FLAGGED_FOR_REVIEW`, `UNDER_DISPUTE` or `SUPERSEDED` may not be disputed, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINATION_STATE`.

Each `basis` carries its own admissibility precondition, and all four failures report `ERR_DISPUTE_BASIS_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422). A `basis` of `ELEVATION_ABOVE_BFE` requires that the structure already have a `VALIDATED` elevation certificate on file. A `basis` of `MAP_PANEL_STALE` requires that a later-sequence panel that is not yet `EFFECTIVE` actually exist for that map timeline — you cannot claim the map is out of date when no revision is in flight. A `basis` of `GEOCODE_ERROR` requires that the property's `geocode_method` be something other than `ROOFTOP`, since a rooftop hit is the best positional evidence the geocoder can produce. A `basis` of `STRUCTURE_LOCATION` carries no additional evidentiary precondition of its own. Separately, a second-round filing must assert a different `asserted_sfha_offset_ft` from the one the first round asserted; repeating the same number is not a new argument and is refused under the same error.

An admissible filing is created in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `round_number` 1, `filed_at` at the current logical instant, and `decision_due_at` computed as 30 days after `filed_at`; `provider_recommendation`, `provider_reviewed_by_user_id`, `agency_concurrence`, `agency_reviewed_by_user_id`, `resolution`, `revised_determination_id` and `resolved_at` all start null. The challenged determination simultaneously moves `ISSUED → UNDER_DISPUTE`, which locks it for the duration of the joint review, and the folio's `compliance_posture` is recomputed — force placement is blocked while the review is open. `round_number` never leaves the range 1 through 2.

A `ProviderReviewer` then forms the provider's position on a dispute that is in `SUBMITTED`, supplying a `provider_recommendation` of `UPHOLD` (the determination stands) or `REVISE` (the borrower is right). The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `issued_by_user_id` recorded on the disputed determination, or the review is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Reviewing a dispute that is not `SUBMITTED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE` (HTTP 409). The recommendation is stored, `provider_reviewed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the dispute moves `SUBMITTED → JOINT_AGENCY_REVIEW`.

An `AgencyOfficer` closes the joint review on a dispute that is in `JOINT_AGENCY_REVIEW` and whose `provider_recommendation` is not null, supplying an `agency_concurrence` of `CONCUR` or `NON_CONCUR`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the dispute's current `provider_reviewed_by_user_id`. Calling it on a dispute that is not in `JOINT_AGENCY_REVIEW` — including a second call against a dispute already `SUSTAINED` or `DENIED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`. The `agency_concurrence` and `agency_reviewed_by_user_id` are recorded, and the outcome is then read off the combination of concurrence, recommendation and round number.

There are three outcomes. The dispute is **sustained** — `JOINT_AGENCY_REVIEW → SUSTAINED` — either when the agency concurs with a `REVISE` recommendation, in which case `resolution` is `REVISED_BY_CONCURRENCE`, or when the agency does not concur with an `UPHOLD` recommendation in the final round, in which case the agency has overridden the provider and `resolution` is `REVISED_BY_AGENCY_OVERRIDE`. The dispute is **denied** — `JOINT_AGENCY_REVIEW → DENIED` — either when the agency concurs with an `UPHOLD` recommendation, giving `resolution` `UPHELD_BY_CONCURRENCE`, or when the agency does not concur with a `REVISE` recommendation in the final round, giving `resolution` `UPHELD_BY_AGENCY_OVERRIDE`. Both terminal outcomes set `resolved_at` to the current logical instant. The third outcome is a **remand**: when the agency returns `NON_CONCUR` while `round_number` is still below the maximum of 2, the dispute cycles `JOINT_AGENCY_REVIEW → SUBMITTED` for a second provider review. On a remand `round_number` is incremented by 1, `provider_recommendation` and `provider_reviewed_by_user_id` are both cleared to null so a fresh provider review is required, `agency_concurrence` is recorded as `NON_CONCUR` with `agency_reviewed_by_user_id` set, `filed_at` is reset to the current logical instant and `decision_due_at` is recomputed as 30 days after the new `filed_at`, and the disputed determination stays `UNDER_DISPUTE` throughout. Because the maximum is 2, a `NON_CONCUR` in round 2 can only sustain or deny; it can never remand again.

Denial is the quiet branch. The disputed determination returns `UNDER_DISPUTE → ISSUED` and nothing else about it changes — no field other than its `status`, `updated_at` and `version` is written, so the same row resumes the governing position. No new determination is created and the structure's `sfha_offset_ft` is left untouched. The folio's `compliance_posture` is recomputed, which typically restores whatever posture the open dispute had been masking.

Sustaining a dispute is the second five-hop cascade in this system, and it runs in order. In the first hop the dispute itself moves to `SUSTAINED` with `resolved_at` set. In the second hop, because the borrower's assertion has been accepted, the disputed structure's `sfha_offset_ft` is overwritten with the dispute's `asserted_sfha_offset_ft` — this is the only path in the system that rewrites a structure's geometry. In the third hop, because the geometry changed, the disputed determination moves `UNDER_DISPUTE → SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` at the current logical instant and a replacement determination is minted with `determination_type` `DISPUTE_REVISION`, `supersedes_determination_id` naming the disputed row, `issued_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`, and its geometry recomputed from the corrected offset under the strict variant of Section 3.5; the dispute's `revised_determination_id` names that new determination. In the fourth hop, because the folio's governing determination changed, the folio's `current_determination_id` is repointed and `required_coverage_cents`, `uninsured_exposure_cents` and `insurance_status` are recomputed; when the revision yields `sfha_flag` false and the folio's `insurance_status` was `FORCE_PLACED`, the coverage dimension moves `FORCE_PLACED → NOT_REQUIRED` and the escrow settlement of Section 3.6 refunds the unearned premium into `escrow_balance_cents`. In the fifth hop, because the obligation has been cleared, every compliance notice on the folio whose `status` is `SENT` moves `SENT → SUPERSEDED` with `closed_at` set, `open_notice_count` is re-counted, `compliance_posture` is recomputed, and a system notice with `notice_type` `RE_DETERMINATION` citing the new determination is sent to the lender so the servicing file records why the obligation went away.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is mounted under the `/flood-zone` prefix. Operations that create a new stand-alone resource — a community, a lender, a map panel, a property, a structure, a loan folio, a determination, a dispute, an elevation certificate, a map amendment request, a compliance notice — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including all state-transition actions, the geocode correction, the monitoring sweep and the clock capability, return `200 OK`. Read operations return `200 OK`. Request bodies, response payloads and side effects are not repeated here; Section 3 is the normative description of each operation's inputs and consequences.
