# Dubbing & Localization Media API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A media localization vendor sits between a content owner's master files and the dozens of platform-specific dubbed deliverables that content eventually becomes. Every property arrives as a catalogue entry with one or more editorial cuts; each cut may be localized into many languages, and each of those localizations must be scripted, cast, recorded, mixed, reviewed by a native speaker, and finally delivered against a platform's technical specification. The work is expensive, the booth calendar is the scarcest resource in the building, and the money owed to voice talent depends on territory rights that differ per performer. Today that coordination happens in spreadsheets, and the recurring failures are always the same three: a session booked into a room that was already occupied, a dub approved by the very person who wrote it, and a talent invoice paid twice because a quality failure sent the loops back into the booth after the payment had already been accrued.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service is a workflow orchestration engine: almost every meaningful state change on a localization order is a *consequence* of an action taken on one of its children rather than a direct command, and several of those consequences cascade three and four links deep. The service is also a ledger. Booth minutes and money are both conserved quantities — every minute a booking holds must be returned when it is cancelled, and every cent accrued against an order must be traceable either to the committed pool or to the released pool, never lost and never duplicated. Two independent optimistic-concurrency checks protect the two places where a stale client view would otherwise silently destroy another writer's work.

Finally, the service is deliberately not a single-operator tool. Seven distinct roles act on it, and the permission model is segregation of duties rather than a hierarchy: the operational back office cannot cast talent, the casting director cannot mix, and the person who signed off a script is barred from also signing off the quality review that judges its recording. Product teams reading this document should treat the role boundaries and the validation precedence ladder in Section 2 as hard contract, and the workflow narratives in Section 3 as the authoritative description of what each action actually does to the data.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this API is mounted at the root: there is no shared prefix, and no resource is nested inside another resource's URL. Relationships are carried by identifier fields in request payloads, never by URL nesting.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the role they are acting in. If either header is absent or empty — a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty — the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` refusal, also at HTTP 401, applies when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven declared roles. Only once both headers survive that gate does the service ask whether the named role is permitted on the requested operation; when it is not, the answer is `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ProductionCoordinator`, `Adaptor`, `NativeReviewer`, `CastingDirector`, `VoiceTalentUser`, `MixEngineer` and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `ProductionCoordinator` owns the catalogue, the calendar and the order lifecycle — properties, cuts, characters, platform specifications, studios, orders, booth bookings and delivery. An `Adaptor` translates and lip-sync adapts scripts and may never review their own work. A `NativeReviewer` signs off scripts, runs quality-control rounds, and raises or waives retakes. A `CastingDirector` owns the talent pool and the casting deals. A `VoiceTalentUser` is a performer acting on their own holds and nothing else. A `MixEngineer` owns mixing and music-and-effects stem handling. A `PlatformAdmin` is the operational back office responsible for talent money and for the test-driving logical clock; it is deliberately **not** a superset of the other roles and cannot cast, book, mix or review. Every role may perform every read operation in the system.

Role identity binds to `X-User-ID` as follows. When `X-User-Role` is `VoiceTalentUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `VoiceTalent`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `VoiceTalent` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — concretely, responding to a hold compares the targeted assignment's `talent_id` against `X-User-ID` and refuses a mismatch with `ERR_TALENT_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ProductionCoordinator`, `Adaptor`, `NativeReviewer`, `CastingDirector`, `MixEngineer` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `adapted_by_user_id` or `reviewed_by_user_id` on a `ScriptAdaptation`, or as `reviewer_user_id` on a `QualityReview`, where a transition writes it — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`. Those recorded operator identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties and reviewer-continuity rules described in §2.11 compare against.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0; there is no upper bound on `offset`. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 to 100, a non-integer `limit`, a negative `offset` or a non-integer `offset` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a subsection below states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with rows created in the same clock tick falling back to their insertion order. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective starting index), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Each list endpoint also accepts a set of optional equality filters described in the workflow narratives of Section 3 and summarised implicitly by the endpoint table of Section 4. Filters combine conjunctively: a request naming two filters returns only the rows that satisfy both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource in this system carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the row is first written and never changed afterwards. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row. The four instant-valued business fields — `start_utc`, `end_utc`, `delivered_at` and `settled_at` — are rendered differently: they carry no fractional digits at all when the instant falls on a whole second (for example `2026-08-05T10:00:00Z`), and only fall back to the three-digit form when a sub-second part is actually present. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. All fourteen resource types — properties, cuts, characters, platform specifications, voice talents, studios, localization orders, script adaptations, casting assignments, recording sessions, mix jobs, quality reviews, retake items and talent payments — carry all four of these properties; none of them is an identity-only record.

The `version` counter is load-bearing well beyond bookkeeping. Cascades routinely mutate rows the client never named in its request: a single recording-session completion bumps the session's own `version`, the parent order's `version`, the casting assignment's `version` and, indirectly through counter recomputation, the studio and talent rows. Every one of those bumps is an increment of exactly 1 on the row that changed, and every one of them refreshes that row's `updated_at`. Where a workflow narrative in Section 3 says a cascade "advances" or "regresses" an entity, that entity's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes even though the client addressed a different resource.

### 2.5. Path and Payload Resolution Order

Although no resource is nested in another's URL, several actions carry an identifier in the path: `titleId`, `cutId`, `characterId`, `platformSpecId`, `talentId`, `studioId`, `orderId`, `adaptationId`, `assignmentId`, `sessionId`, `mixJobId`, `reviewId`, `retakeItemId` and `paymentId`. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that names no stored row terminates the request with that entity's own not-found code at HTTP 404 — `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHARACTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLATFORM_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TALENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADAPTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MIX_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAKE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` respectively.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, and among themselves they are resolved in the order the endpoint's input list declares them. The same not-found codes apply: a body naming an unknown `title_id`, `cut_id`, `character_id`, `platform_spec_id`, `talent_id`, `studio_id`, `order_id`, `casting_assignment_id`, `quality_review_id` or `mix_job_id` is refused at HTTP 404 with the code belonging to the entity that identifier points at. A foreign-key field that is absent altogether, null, or carries a non-string value is treated exactly like one that names no stored row: it earns that entity's own not-found code at HTTP 404, not `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, because resolution runs ahead of the field checks.

### 2.6. The Validation Precedence Ladder

Because a single request frequently violates more than one rule at once, the order in which the service evaluates its checks is itself part of the contract. Checks are evaluated strictly in ascending priority order, and **the first failing check determines the entire response — no later check runs and no later error code can surface**. An implementation that evaluates in a different order will return a defensible-looking but wrong error code. The full ladder, in evaluation order, is:

1. Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty on every endpoint without exception; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles, on every endpoint; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. The acting role appears in the set of roles authorised for the requested endpoint, on every endpoint; otherwise `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (403).
4. On `POST /casting-assignments/{assignmentId}/respond-hold` only: when the acting role is `VoiceTalentUser`, `X-User-ID` equals the targeted assignment's `talent_id`; otherwise `ERR_TALENT_NOT_OWNER` (403). Because the comparison needs the row, the `assignmentId` in the path is resolved before it: an `assignmentId` naming no stored row still reports `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404) rather than this code.
5. On `POST /quality-reviews/{reviewId}/decide` only: the acting `X-User-ID` equals the `reviewer_user_id` stamped on that review when the round was started; otherwise `ERR_REVIEWER_MISMATCH` (403). As with position 4, the `reviewId` in the path is resolved first, so an unknown `reviewId` reports `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` (404); and a review that has never been started carries a null `reviewer_user_id`, which no acting identifier matches, so deciding an unstarted round reports this code rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
6. On every endpoint carrying a path parameter: each path identifier resolves to a stored row, checked left to right; otherwise the corresponding not-found code at 404 as listed in §2.5.
7. On every endpoint accepting a foreign key in its body: each such identifier resolves to a stored row, checked in the order the endpoint declares its inputs; otherwise the corresponding not-found code at 404.
8. On every write endpoint: every required body field is present, every value matches its declared type, every enum value is a declared member of its enumeration, and every declared numeric range holds — the "at least 1" fields, the "at least 0" fields and the fields confined to the closed interval 0.0 through 1.0, every such bound being inclusive; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). A request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as a JSON object is treated as an empty object, so it fails on the first required field it is missing. Every string-valued input is additionally required to be non-empty once whitespace is trimmed — the sole exceptions are the optional free-text `notes` and `reason` inputs, which may be empty. Every declared uniqueness constraint — `title_code` across properties, `cut_code` within a property, `character_code` within a property and `platform_code` across specifications — is also evaluated at this position, immediately after the field checks of the same request, and a collision is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).
9. On every list endpoint: `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422).
10. On order-scoped mutations — creating a script adaptation and every script-adaptation action, creating a casting assignment and every casting-assignment action, creating a recording session and every recording-session action, creating a mix job, and creating a talent payment — the owning `LocalizationOrder` is not in `DELIVERED`, `RECALLED` or `CANCELLED`; otherwise `ERR_ORDER_LOCKED` (409).
11. On every action endpoint: the target row currently sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
12. On `POST /casting-assignments`: `expected_order_version` equals the order's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (412).
13. On `POST /cuts/{cutId}/derive`: `expected_parent_cut_version` equals the parent cut's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_CUT_VERSION` (412).
14. On `POST /script-adaptations/{adaptationId}/review` and `POST /quality-reviews/{reviewId}/start`: the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the adaptation's `adapted_by_user_id` when reviewing a script, and differs from the adaptation's `reviewed_by_user_id` when starting a quality review on the same order; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
15. On `POST /casting-assignments` and `POST /talent-payments`: the talent's `licensed_territories` contains the order's `territory`, and for a payment the supplied `territory_scope` is a subset of `licensed_territories` and contains the order's `territory`; otherwise `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_LICENSED` (422).
16. On `POST /casting-assignments`: the talent's `status` is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_TALENT_SUSPENDED` (409).
17. On `POST /casting-assignments`: the talent's `language_code` equals the order's `target_language`; otherwise `ERR_TALENT_LANGUAGE_MISMATCH` (422).
18. On `POST /localization-orders`: the order's `target_language` differs from the `source_language` of the `Title` reached through the order's cut; otherwise `ERR_SAME_LANGUAGE` (422).
19. On `POST /casting-assignments`: the character's `title_id` equals the `title_id` of the cut behind the order; otherwise `ERR_CHARACTER_TITLE_MISMATCH` (422).
20. On `POST /recording-sessions` and `POST /quality-reviews`: the session's `casting_assignment_id` belongs to the same order as the `order_id` in the request, and the review's `mix_job_id` belongs to the same order; otherwise `ERR_CROSS_ORDER_REFERENCE` (422).
21. On `POST /mix-jobs/{mixJobId}/stem-check`: the job's `stem_layout` equals the platform specification's `stem_layout` and `me_stem_present` is true; otherwise `ERR_STEM_LAYOUT_MISMATCH` (422).
22. On `POST /localization-orders`: the named cut's `status` is `LOCKED`; otherwise `ERR_CUT_NOT_LOCKED` (409).
23. On `POST /cuts/{cutId}/derive`: the parent cut's `lineage_depth` is less than 3; otherwise `ERR_LINEAGE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` (422).
24. On `POST /casting-assignments`: `agreed_rate_cents_per_hour` is greater than or equal to the talent's `base_rate_cents_per_hour`; otherwise `ERR_RATE_BELOW_TALENT_FLOOR` (422).
25. On `POST /casting-assignments`: no other non-superseded assignment of this order in `PROPOSED`, `HOLD_REQUESTED`, `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `RECORDED` already names the same `talent_id` for a different character; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASTING` (409).
26. On `POST /recording-sessions/{sessionId}/confirm`: the order's script adaptation exists and its `status` is `APPROVED`; otherwise `ERR_ADAPTATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
27. On `POST /mix-jobs`: the order has at least one non-superseded assignment whose `casting_status` is `RECORDED`; otherwise `ERR_NO_RECORDED_CAST` (409).
28. On `POST /mix-jobs/{mixJobId}/mix`: every non-superseded assignment of the order sitting in `PROPOSED`, `HOLD_REQUESTED`, `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `RECORDED` has `casting_status` equal to `RECORDED`; otherwise `ERR_CAST_NOT_FULLY_RECORDED` (409).
29. On `POST /script-adaptations/{adaptationId}/review` with an approving decision: `final_fidelity_score` is greater than or equal to the platform specification's `min_fidelity_score`, the comparison being inclusive; otherwise `ERR_FIDELITY_BELOW_SPEC` (409).
30. On `POST /mix-jobs/{mixJobId}/conform`: `conformance_index` is greater than or equal to 0.80, the comparison being inclusive; otherwise `ERR_CONFORMANCE_BELOW_MIN` (409).
31. On `POST /quality-reviews/{reviewId}/start`: the supplied `sampled_loop_count` is at least 1; otherwise `ERR_EMPTY_QC_SAMPLE` (422).
32. On `POST /quality-reviews/{reviewId}/decide`: when the decision is to pass, the computed `pass_rate` is greater than or equal to the platform specification's `qc_pass_threshold`, the comparison being inclusive; and when the decision is to fail, the `defects` list is non-empty; otherwise `ERR_QC_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (409).
33. On `POST /retake-items/{retakeItemId}/waive`: the item's `defect_category` is not `LIPSYNC`; otherwise `ERR_RETAKE_NOT_WAIVABLE` (409).
34. On `POST /quality-reviews/{reviewId}/start` when the review is re-entering review after a failure: every retake item of that review has `status` of either `RECORDED` or `WAIVED`; otherwise `ERR_OPEN_RETAKES` (409).
35. On `POST /quality-reviews/{reviewId}/decide` with a failing decision: the order's `retake_cycles` is strictly less than 2; otherwise `ERR_RETAKE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
36. On `POST /localization-orders/{orderId}/deliver`: every key listed in the platform specification's `metadata_fields_required` is present and non-empty in the supplied `metadata_locale_fields`; otherwise `ERR_METADATA_INCOMPLETE` (422).
37. On `POST /recording-sessions` and `POST /recording-sessions/{sessionId}/confirm`: the studio's `status` is `ACTIVE`; otherwise `ERR_STUDIO_UNAVAILABLE` (409).
38. On `POST /recording-sessions`: `start_utc` is strictly earlier than `end_utc`, both instants fall exactly on a whole minute, and `start_utc` is at or after the current logical clock; otherwise `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_INVALID` (422).
39. On `POST /recording-sessions` when `on_conflict` is `REJECT`: the buffered studio interval does not intersect the same-buffered interval of any session on that room in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`; otherwise `ERR_STUDIO_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409).
40. On `POST /recording-sessions` when `on_conflict` is `REJECT`: the room's already-booked minutes for the requested UTC calendar day plus the new booking's `duration_minutes` is at most `daily_capacity_minutes`; otherwise `ERR_STUDIO_DAILY_CAPACITY` (409).
41. On `POST /recording-sessions`, whatever the value of `on_conflict`: the closed talent interval does not intersect the closed interval of any other session of the same talent in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`; otherwise `ERR_TALENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409).
42. On `POST /recording-sessions`, whatever the value of `on_conflict`: the talent's recorded plus held minutes on the requested UTC calendar day plus the new booking's `duration_minutes` is at most `max_daily_recorded_minutes`; otherwise `ERR_TALENT_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
43. On `POST /recording-sessions/{sessionId}/complete`: the order's `committed_cents` plus the restamped `session_cost_cents` is at most `budget_cap_cents`, with equality allowed; otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (402).
44. On `POST /talent-payments/{paymentId}/settle`: the payment's `status` is `ACCRUED` and it has not already been settled; otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_SETTLED` (409).
45. On `POST /talent-payments`: the assignment carries no payment currently in `ACCRUED`; otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_ACCRUED` (409).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. The stale-version checks at positions 12 and 13 sit *below* the state check at position 11, so a request that is both stale and aimed at a wrongly-stated row reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not the stale-version code. And the booth-conflict family at positions 39 through 42 sits *below* the window validity check at position 38, so a malformed window never surfaces as a conflict.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Two write operations mutate a parent row that other writers may also be advancing, and both therefore require the client to prove it has observed the parent's latest state.

A `POST /casting-assignments` request must carry `expected_order_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `LocalizationOrder`. If that value does not equal the order's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and nothing is written. The check exists because re-casting a character legitimately supersedes the character's previous assignment; a writer holding an outdated view of the order would otherwise release a row it had never seen.

A `POST /cuts/{cutId}/derive` request must carry `expected_parent_cut_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Cut` named in the path. If that value does not equal the parent cut's current `version`, the derive is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CUT_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and no child cut is created. Because multiple derived cuts may legitimately hang off one parent, this check is the only barrier stopping a client from branching off conform data it has not observed.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind. In particular, locking a cut, confirming an assignment, completing a session, printing a mix and delivering an order all take the row as they find it.

### 2.8. The Logical Clock

Time in this service is entirely logical. Before any client touches it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant — any parseable ISO 8601 instant is accepted, backwards as readily as forwards — and any role may read it. Both clock endpoints answer at HTTP 200 with a `data` object carrying the single field `current_utc`, holding the instant the clock now reads; neither returns a persisted resource. Moving the clock mutates no entity and fires no scheduled action on its own — nothing becomes overdue, nothing auto-starts, nothing auto-cancels. The clock is read at exactly three points: when a booking window is validated against "not in the past", when a session may be opened or marked as a no-show ("at or after its start"), and when an instant is stamped into `delivered_at` or `settled_at`.

### 2.9. Money: Rounding Discipline and the Conservation Invariant

All monetary amounts in this system are whole-cent integers. Wherever a formula produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up — a value ending in exactly one half cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Score-valued calculations round half up to four decimal places instead; the relevant subsection of Section 3 states which precision applies to which figure.

One rounding rule causes more implementation divergence than any other and is stated here as a global principle: **where a formula is described as a sum of separately-named line items, each line item is rounded to a whole cent independently, from its own unrounded raw value, and only then are the rounded line items summed.** Accumulating the raw fractional values and rounding once at the end is a different computation and produces a different figure whenever two or more lines carry a fractional part. The session cost described in §3.7 is the canonical case.

Money on a `LocalizationOrder` lives in three integer counters. `committed_cents` is the money currently standing against the order: the sum of the costs of its completed sessions, less every amount a compensating action has withdrawn. `released_cents` is the money a compensating action — a retake void or a recall void — has withdrawn from the committed pool; it only ever grows. `gross_accrued_cents` is the conserved quantity: every accrual ever booked against the order, regardless of what happened to it afterwards. After every single request, the invariant `gross_accrued_cents` equals `committed_cents` plus `released_cents` must hold.

The two directions of movement are exactly these. A forward action — a recording session moving from `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED` — adds that session's cost to both `committed_cents` and `gross_accrued_cents` and leaves `released_cents` untouched. A compensating action — the fourth link of the quality-failure cascade acting on one casting assignment — computes the amount to void as the sum of the session costs over that assignment's completed non-retake sessions, but only when that assignment's `payment_status` is currently `ACCRUED`; when the payment dimension sits anywhere else, the amount to void is zero and no money moves at all, which is precisely what keeps the compensation idempotent across repeated retake rounds. Where the amount is non-zero it is subtracted from `committed_cents` and added to `released_cents`, and `gross_accrued_cents` is left exactly where it was. The two other money movements — order recall and order cancellation — move the whole of `committed_cents` into `released_cents` and likewise leave `gross_accrued_cents` untouched. A cascade that runs end to end must therefore leave `gross_accrued_cents` at exactly the value it held before the compensating action began.

### 2.10. Booth Time: The Second Conserved Quantity

Booth minutes are conserved in the same disciplined way money is, but they are a different quantity with different counters, and confusing the two is a common failure. A `Studio` carries `booked_minutes`, defined at all times as the sum of `duration_minutes` over that room's sessions in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`. A `VoiceTalent` carries `held_minutes`, the sum of `duration_minutes` over that performer's sessions in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and separately `recorded_minutes`, the sum of `duration_minutes` over that performer's sessions in `COMPLETED`. Sessions in `WAITLISTED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` contribute zero to all three counters.

Booking a slot that lands on the calendar adds its `duration_minutes` to the room's `booked_minutes` and to the performer's `held_minutes`. Cancelling that slot from either `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` subtracts the same `duration_minutes` from both counters. A create followed by its cancel therefore restores both counters to their exact pre-create values — and it does so even when the cancel additionally promotes a waitlisted row, because the promotion adds the *promoted* row's own `duration_minutes` and never the cancelled row's. Completion is the one movement that is not symmetric: it transfers `duration_minutes` out of `held_minutes` and into `recorded_minutes`, while leaving `booked_minutes` unchanged, because a completed session still occupies the room on the calendar.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Reviewer Continuity

Three separate identity rules keep the sign-off chain honest, and they are distinct from one another. First, the `X-User-ID` that most recently adapted a script — recorded as `adapted_by_user_id` — may not be the `X-User-ID` that reviews that adaptation. Second, the `X-User-ID` recorded as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an order's script adaptation may not be the `X-User-ID` that starts a quality review on that same order. Both of those refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. Third — and this is a continuity rule rather than a separation rule — the `X-User-ID` that decides a quality-review round must be the same `X-User-ID` that started it, recorded as `reviewer_user_id`; a mismatch is `ERR_REVIEWER_MISMATCH` at HTTP 403, and the same recorded reviewer is the only principal permitted to waive that review's retake items.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Cataloguing a Property and Its Cuts

Work begins when a `ProductionCoordinator` registers a property in the vendor catalogue. Registration supplies a `title_code` — the vendor-side catalogue code, which must be non-empty and unique across all properties — together with `source_language`, the BCP-47 tag of the original audio, and `runtime_seconds`, the reference runtime of the original master, which must be at least 1. A `content_rating` may be supplied and defaults to `GENERAL` when it is not; the permitted band values are `GENERAL`, `TEEN` and `MATURE`, and the band is what will later drive reviewer eligibility. The property is stored with a `cut_count` of 0.

A **cut** is one editorial version of a property — theatrical, streaming, airline, and so on. A root cut is registered directly against a property by supplying its `title_id`, a `cut_code` such as THEATRICAL or STREAMING which must be non-empty and unique among that property's cuts, a `loop_count` of at least 1 giving the number of dialogue loops in the cut, and a `runtime_seconds` of at least 1. A root cut is stored with `derived_from_cut_id` of null, `lineage_depth` of 0, `conform_delta_seconds` of 0 and `derived_cut_count` of 0, and its creation increments the owning property's `cut_count` by 1. The `loop_count` of a cut is the denominator of every coverage ratio computed later in the pipeline, which is why it can never be zero.

Cuts branch. Deriving a new cut from an existing one supplies the child's own `cut_code`, `loop_count` and `runtime_seconds`, plus the `expected_parent_cut_version` described in §2.7. The child's `cut_code` must be unique among the cuts of the same property as the parent, a collision being the `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` of position 8 and therefore reported ahead of both the stale-version and the lineage-depth gates. The child is stored with `derived_from_cut_id` pointing at the cut it was branched from, a `lineage_depth` of one more than the parent's, a `derived_cut_count` of its own at 0, and a `conform_delta_seconds` equal to the child's `runtime_seconds` minus the parent's `runtime_seconds` — a signed figure that may be negative when the branch is shorter than its ancestor. Deriving also increments the parent's `derived_cut_count` by 1, increments the parent cut's `version` by 1, and increments the property's `cut_count` by 1. Lineage may not run arbitrarily deep: a derive whose parent already sits at a `lineage_depth` of 3 or more is refused with `ERR_LINEAGE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, so the deepest cut the catalogue will ever hold has a `lineage_depth` of 3.

Each cut is `OPEN` while its conform data may still change and `LOCKED` once it has been frozen. Locking is a `ProductionCoordinator` action that advances `OPEN → LOCKED` and increments the cut's `version` by 1. Two preconditions guard it: the cut must currently be `OPEN`, and — when the cut has a `derived_from_cut_id` — the cut it was derived from must itself already be `LOCKED`. Attempting to lock a cut that is already `LOCKED`, or one whose lineage parent is still `OPEN`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `LOCKED` is terminal: no path returns a cut to `OPEN`. Locking matters because a localization order may only be raised against a `LOCKED` cut, which makes locking the gate that freezes conform data for every downstream order. Cuts may be listed and filtered by `title_id`, by `derived_from_cut_id` — the discovery channel for a cut's immediate children — and by `status`.

### 3.2. Characters, Delivery Specifications and the Talent Bench

Characters belong to properties, not to cuts. A `ProductionCoordinator` registers each one against a `title_id` with a `character_code` that must be non-empty and unique within that property, a `loop_count` of at least 0 giving the dialogue loops attributed to the character, and a `screen_time_seconds` of at least 0. The optional `is_principal` flag defaults to false and marks principal cast, whose deals attract the union-eligible rate scrutiny described in §3.7. Characters may be listed filtered by `title_id`.

A **platform specification** captures everything one delivery destination demands. It is registered with a `platform_code` that is non-empty and unique across all specifications; a `loudness_target_lufs` in the closed interval -40.0 through 0.0 giving the integrated loudness the final mix must hit; a `stem_layout` of either `STEREO_ME` or `FIVE_ONE_ME` naming the required music-and-effects stem layout; a `max_lipsync_deviation_ms` of at least 1 giving the per-loop lip-sync tolerance; a `min_fidelity_score` between 0.0 and 1.0 giving the lowest measured fidelity a script adaptation may carry and still be approvable; a `qc_pass_threshold` between 0.0 and 1.0 giving the lowest quality-review pass rate that still permits a pass decision, compared inclusively; and `metadata_fields_required`, an array of non-empty strings — possibly empty — naming the metadata keys that must be present and non-empty at delivery.

Voice talent is registered by a `CastingDirector` with a `full_name` that is non-empty, a `language_code` giving the BCP-47 tag of the language the performer records in, a `base_rate_cents_per_hour` of at least 1 which acts as the floor for any deal struck with that performer, and `licensed_territories`, a non-empty array drawn from the nine territory codes `US`, `CA`, `UK`, `DE`, `FR`, `JP`, `BR`, `AU` and `ROW`. Two optional fields carry defaults: `union_status` defaults to `NON_UNION` and may instead be `UNION`, in which case every session that performer records attracts the union uplift line described in §3.7; and `max_daily_recorded_minutes` defaults to 300 and must be at least 1 when supplied, being the aggregate per-UTC-day minute cap across all of that performer's sessions. A newly registered performer starts `ACTIVE` with `held_minutes` and `recorded_minutes` both at 0.

A performer's availability is toggled by a `CastingDirector` supplying a `to_status` of either `SUSPENDED` or `ACTIVE`. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` requires the performer to be `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` requires them to be `SUSPENDED`; either transition increments the performer's `version` by 1, and any other combination is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Suspension is deliberately narrow in effect: it does not disturb a single existing booking, and it blocks only the creation of new casting assignments.

Studios are recording rooms, registered by a `ProductionCoordinator` with a non-empty `name`, a `territory` drawn from the same nine territory codes, and an `hourly_rate_cents` of at least 1 charged in whole thirty-minute blocks. A `turnaround_buffer_minutes` of at least 0 may be supplied and defaults to 30; it is the mandatory clean-down gap appended after every booking on that room. A `daily_capacity_minutes` of at least 1 may be supplied and defaults to 600; it is the room's aggregate per-UTC-day booked-minute ceiling. A new room starts `ACTIVE` with `booked_minutes` at 0. A `ProductionCoordinator` moves a room between `ACTIVE` and `MAINTENANCE` by supplying a `to_status`; `ACTIVE → MAINTENANCE` requires that the room currently be `ACTIVE` **and** that no session on that room is currently `IN_PROGRESS`, while `MAINTENANCE → ACTIVE` requires only that the room currently be `MAINTENANCE`. Both directions increment the room's `version` by 1, and a room in `MAINTENANCE` accepts no bookings.

### 3.3. Raising a Localization Order

A **localization order** is the unit of work: one cut, into one language, for one territory, against one platform specification. A `ProductionCoordinator` raises it by naming the `cut_id` of the cut being localized, the `platform_spec_id` of the specification the deliverable must satisfy, a non-empty `target_language`, a `territory` drawn from the nine territory codes, and a `budget_cap_cents` of at least 0 which is a hard ceiling on the order's committed money. A `priority_tier` may be supplied and defaults to `STANDARD`; the alternatives are `EXPEDITED` and `RUSH`, and the tier chosen here reaches into three separate calculations later — the fidelity tier weight, the rush surcharge on the invoice, and the ranking of the order's bookings in the studio waitlist.

Two rejections guard the raise. The cut named by `cut_id` must be `LOCKED`; a cut still `OPEN` earns `ERR_CUT_NOT_LOCKED`. And the `target_language` must differ from the `source_language` of the property reached through that cut — the service will not accept an order to dub a property into the language it was shot in, and refuses with `ERR_SAME_LANGUAGE`.

A new order lands in `DRAFT` with `committed_cents`, `released_cents`, `gross_accrued_cents` and `retake_cycles` all at 0, `metadata_locale_fields` an empty object, and `delivered_at` null. From there the order's `status` is advanced almost entirely by cascades from its children rather than by direct commands. The full set of stages is `DRAFT`, `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS`, `CASTING`, `RECORDING`, `MIXING`, `QC`, `DELIVERED`, `RECALLED` and `CANCELLED`, and each of the cascade edges is described where the child action that fires it is described: `DRAFT → SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS` in §3.4, `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS → CASTING` in §3.4, `CASTING → RECORDING` in §3.6, `RECORDING → MIXING` and `RECALLED → MIXING` and `MIXING → QC` in §3.8, `QC → RECORDING` in §3.9, and `QC → DELIVERED`, `DELIVERED → RECALLED` and the cancel edges in §3.11. Every one of those transitions increments the order's `version` by 1. Orders may be listed filtered by `cut_id`, by `platform_spec_id` and by `status`.

Once an order has reached `DELIVERED`, `RECALLED` or `CANCELLED`, it is closed to child mutation. Opening a script workstream on it, acting on its adaptation, creating or acting on a casting assignment, creating or acting on a recording session, opening a mix job, or accruing a talent payment against it are all refused with `ERR_ORDER_LOCKED`. What stays available on a closed order is everything position 10 does not name: every read, the recall of a `DELIVERED` order, the invoice computation, every action on an existing mix job — including the mix print that pulls a `RECALLED` order back to `MIXING` — the opening, starting and deciding of a quality review, the waiving of a retake item, and the settlement of an already-accrued talent payment, which is precisely how a delivered order's performers get paid.

### 3.4. The Script Workstream: Translation, Adaptation and Native Review

An `Adaptor` opens the script workstream by creating a **script adaptation** against an `order_id`. Two preconditions apply: the order must be in `DRAFT`, and it must not already carry a script adaptation — one order has exactly one adaptation row for its whole life, and revision rounds happen inside that single row rather than by creating new ones. Both refusals are the `ERR_INVALID_STATE` of position 11. The adaptation is stored in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 1, `adapted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` null, `declared_loop_count`, `measured_sync_deviation_ms_total` and `lines_over_deviation` all at 0, and both `provisional_fidelity_score` and `final_fidelity_score` null. Creating it cascades the owning order `DRAFT → SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS` and increments the order's `version` by 1.

The adaptation then walks `DRAFT → TRANSLATED → ADAPTED → IN_REVIEW`, and from `IN_REVIEW` either terminates at `APPROVED` or falls back to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and re-enters `ADAPTED`. Recording the raw translation pass advances `DRAFT → TRANSLATED` and takes no payload. Both that step and the adaptation step additionally require the owning order to be in one of `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS`, `CASTING` or `RECORDING`; an order that has already moved on to `MIXING` or beyond refuses them with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The lip-sync adaptation pass is where the first score is stamped. The acting `Adaptor` supplies `declared_loop_count`, the number of loops they claim to have written, which must be at least 0. The pass is accepted from either `TRANSLATED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and in both cases it stores the supplied `declared_loop_count`, stamps `adapted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, recomputes `provisional_fidelity_score`, sets the adaptation to `ADAPTED` and increments the adaptation's `version` by 1. The two entry paths differ in exactly one respect: when the pass arrives from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, closing the `ADAPTED → IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → ADAPTED` cycle, `revision_round` is first incremented by 1; when it arrives from `TRANSLATED`, `revision_round` stays where it is.

**The provisional fidelity score** is the declared-count variant of the lip-sync fidelity score, and it is stamped only at the adaptation pass, before any sync measurement exists. It is computed from three ingredients. Coverage is the declared loop count divided by the `loop_count` of the order's cut, capped at 1.0 so that over-writing cannot earn extra coverage credit. The density penalty is the amount by which the declared loop count exceeds the cut's `loop_count`, again divided by the cut's `loop_count`, and floored at 0.0 so that under-writing incurs no density penalty; that penalty is itself capped at 1.0 before being subtracted from 1. The weighted sum is six tenths of coverage plus four tenths of one-minus-the-capped-density-penalty. That sum is then multiplied by the tier weight of the order's `priority_tier` — 1.00 for a `STANDARD` order, 0.97 for an `EXPEDITED` order and 0.94 for a `RUSH` order — and the product is rounded half up to four decimal places. Because it reads only what the adaptor declared, a perfectly-covered but badly-synced adaptation scores high here; it never reads `measured_sync_deviation_ms_total` or `lines_over_deviation`.

Handing the adaptation to a native reviewer advances `ADAPTED → IN_REVIEW` and carries the measured sync data: `measured_sync_deviation_ms_total`, the sum of the absolute per-loop sync deviations the sync pass measured, at least 0; and `lines_over_deviation`, the count of loops whose measured deviation exceeded the platform tolerance, also at least 0. The submission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` unless the adaptation is currently `ADAPTED` and its stored `declared_loop_count` is at least 1, both tests belonging to position 11; unlike the translate and adapt passes, this step imposes no condition on the owning order's own stage. Both supplied values are stored, `final_fidelity_score` is recomputed and stamped, and the adaptation's `version` increments by 1. On the *first* submission only, the owning order cascades `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS → CASTING` with its own `version` incremented by 1; submissions in later revision rounds find the order already past `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS` and leave it alone.

**The final fidelity score** is the measured-deviation variant, and it is the only variant the approval gate ever compares against `min_fidelity_score`. The average deviation is `measured_sync_deviation_ms_total` divided by the larger of 1 and `declared_loop_count`. The deviation ratio is that average divided by the platform specification's `max_lipsync_deviation_ms`, capped at 1.0. The over-tolerance ratio is `lines_over_deviation` divided by the larger of 1 and `declared_loop_count`, likewise capped at 1.0. The weighted sum is fifty-five hundredths of one-minus-the-deviation-ratio plus forty-five hundredths of one-minus-the-over-tolerance-ratio, that sum is multiplied by the same tier weight the provisional variant uses — 1.00, 0.97 or 0.94 for `STANDARD`, `EXPEDITED` and `RUSH` respectively — and the product is rounded half up to four decimal places. This variant never reads the cut's `loop_count` coverage at all, which is why the same adaptation row legitimately carries two different numbers at once, and why the two must be stamped by two different steps rather than recomputed together.

Native review closes the loop. A `NativeReviewer` supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION` and may attach free-text `notes`; the notes are validated as an optional string and then discarded — no field of the adaptation records them. Either way the adaptation must currently be `IN_REVIEW`, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `adapted_by_user_id` — a self-review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the acting `X-User-ID` is stamped into `reviewed_by_user_id`, and the adaptation's `version` increments by 1. `REQUEST_REVISION` moves `IN_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and imposes no further condition. `APPROVE` moves `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED` and imposes exactly one further condition: the stamped `final_fidelity_score` must be greater than or equal to the `min_fidelity_score` of the order's platform specification, the comparison being inclusive so that an exact match approves. A score strictly below the specification is refused with `ERR_FIDELITY_BELOW_SPEC`. `APPROVED` is terminal for the adaptation, and it is the precondition that unlocks booth confirmation in §3.6.

### 3.5. Casting: One Deal, Two Independent State Dimensions

A **casting assignment** is a three-way junction between an order, a character and a performer. A `CastingDirector` creates one by naming the `order_id`, the `character_id` of the role being voiced, the `talent_id` of the performer voicing it, a `usage_variant` of either `BUYOUT` or `RESIDUAL` selecting which usage-fee arithmetic the eventual payment will use, an `agreed_rate_cents_per_hour` of at least 1, and the `expected_order_version` required by §2.7.

Five business rules guard the create, and their relative order is the ladder given in §2.6: the territory licence check first, then talent suspension, then language, then character ownership, then the rate floor, then the duplicate-casting check. Concretely, the performer's `licensed_territories` must contain the order's `territory` or the create is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_LICENSED`. The performer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, since a `SUSPENDED` performer earns `ERR_TALENT_SUSPENDED`. The performer's `language_code` must equal the order's `target_language`, otherwise `ERR_TALENT_LANGUAGE_MISMATCH`. The character's `title_id` must match the `title_id` of the cut behind the order, otherwise `ERR_CHARACTER_TITLE_MISMATCH`. The `agreed_rate_cents_per_hour` may equal but never fall below the performer's `base_rate_cents_per_hour`, and a shortfall is `ERR_RATE_BELOW_TALENT_FLOOR`. Finally, one performer may not hold two live roles on the same order: if any other non-superseded assignment of this order in `PROPOSED`, `HOLD_REQUESTED`, `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `RECORDED` already names the same `talent_id` for a different character, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASTING`.

A surviving create stores the assignment with `casting_status` of `PROPOSED`, `payment_status` of `UNBILLED`, `superseded_by_id` null, and `recorded_minutes` and `retake_minutes` both at 0, and it increments the owning order's `version` by 1. Re-casting is handled here rather than by a separate action: if a non-superseded assignment already exists for the *same* order and the *same* character **and** its `casting_status` is one of the five live values `PROPOSED`, `HOLD_REQUESTED`, `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `RECORDED`, that older row has its `superseded_by_id` set to the new assignment's `id` and its `casting_status` moved to `RELEASED`, with its own `version` incremented by 1. A prior row already sitting in `DECLINED` or `RELEASED` is left entirely alone — it is neither re-released nor given a supersede pointer. This supersede path is exactly why the create carries a version check — a director working from a stale view of the order would otherwise release a row they had never seen.

The booking dimension of an assignment is `casting_status`, and it runs `PROPOSED → HOLD_REQUESTED → HELD → CONFIRMED → RECORDED` with two exits. A `CastingDirector` asks the performer to hold dates, advancing `PROPOSED → HOLD_REQUESTED`, provided the assignment is `PROPOSED` and the owning order is in `CASTING` or `RECORDING`. The performer themselves — acting as `VoiceTalentUser`, with `X-User-ID` equal to the assignment's `talent_id` — answers with a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, moving `HOLD_REQUESTED → HELD` or `HOLD_REQUESTED → DECLINED` respectively; either answer requires the assignment to be in `HOLD_REQUESTED`, and `DECLINED` is terminal. Confirming the deal advances `HELD → CONFIRMED` and additionally requires that the owning order be in `CASTING` or `RECORDING` and that the assignment's `superseded_by_id` still be null — a superseded row can never be confirmed; both extra refusals are `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Releasing a deal moves it to `RELEASED` from any of `PROPOSED`, `HOLD_REQUESTED`, `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` — the edges `PROPOSED → RELEASED`, `HOLD_REQUESTED → RELEASED`, `HELD → RELEASED` and `CONFIRMED → RELEASED` are all declared — and is permitted only when the assignment holds no session in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, a live booking earning `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; release imposes no condition on the owning order's stage. `RELEASED` is terminal, as is `DECLINED`. Every one of these edges increments the assignment's `version` by 1, and any attempt from an undeclared origin state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The last two edges of the booking dimension are driven by the system rather than by a person, and they form a cycle. When one of the assignment's sessions whose `is_retake` is false reaches `COMPLETED`, the assignment advances `CONFIRMED → RECORDED`, its `recorded_minutes` is recomputed, and its `version` increments by 1. When a quality failure later regresses the order and this assignment is named by an open retake item of the failing review, the assignment travels back `RECORDED → CONFIRMED`, closing the `RECORDED → CONFIRMED → RECORDED` cycle; that regression and its money consequences are detailed in §3.9.

The payment dimension is `payment_status`, and it is genuinely independent of the booking dimension: a `CONFIRMED` assignment can be `UNBILLED`, and a `RECORDED` assignment can be `UNBILLED`, `ACCRUED`, `SETTLED` or `VOIDED`. It starts at `UNBILLED`. Accruing a payment against the assignment moves it `UNBILLED → ACCRUED`, and the same edge exists as `VOIDED → ACCRUED` for a replacement payment raised after a retake round; both require the assignment's `casting_status` to be `RECORDED` at the moment of accrual. Settling that payment moves the dimension `ACCRUED → SETTLED`, which is terminal on this dimension. The retake cascade moves it `ACCRUED → VOIDED` and simultaneously voids the assignment's newest payment row. Each of these four edges increments the assignment's `version` by 1. The two dimensions never share a trigger except that single retake cascade, which moves each dimension along its own edge independently. Assignments may be listed filtered by `order_id`, `character_id`, `talent_id`, `superseded_by_id`, `casting_status` and `payment_status`.

### 3.6. Booking the Booth: Buffers, Caps, Conflicts and the Waitlist

A **recording session** is the second three-way junction in the model: it binds an order, a room and a casting assignment to an interval on the calendar. A `ProductionCoordinator` books one by naming the `order_id`, the `studio_id` of the room, the `casting_assignment_id` of the talent-and-character pair recording in the slot, and the interval endpoints `start_utc` and `end_utc`. Two optional inputs shape the booking: `is_retake`, which defaults to false and is set true when the slot re-records loops flagged by a quality review; and `on_conflict`, which defaults to `REJECT` and may instead be `WAITLIST`. The session's `duration_minutes` is derived as the whole number of minutes between `start_utc` and `end_utc`.

Referential integrity comes first among the domain checks: the assignment named by `casting_assignment_id` must belong to the very order named by `order_id`, and a mismatch is `ERR_CROSS_ORDER_REFERENCE`. The room must then be `ACTIVE`; booking onto a room in `MAINTENANCE` earns `ERR_STUDIO_UNAVAILABLE`. The interval itself must be well-formed: `start_utc` strictly earlier than `end_utc`, both endpoints falling exactly on a whole minute, and `start_utc` at or after the current logical clock — any of those three failing gives `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Two different interval geometries then apply, and mixing them up is the single most common booking bug. **The studio geometry is half-open with a trailing buffer.** The interval a booking occupies on a room runs from its `start_utc` up to but not including its `end_utc` plus that room's `turnaround_buffer_minutes`. A new booking collides when its buffered interval intersects the identically-buffered interval of any session already on that room in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`. Because the geometry is half-open, a booking whose `start_utc` falls exactly on the instant a previous booking's buffered interval ends does **not** collide. **The talent geometry is closed and unbuffered.** A booking collides on the performer when the closed interval from its `start_utc` through its `end_utc` intersects the closed interval of any other session of the same performer in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` — and because both intervals are closed, two sessions that merely touch endpoints *do* collide. A talent collision is always `ERR_TALENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, and it is raised whatever the value of `on_conflict`.

Two daily ceilings apply on top of the geometries, each counted over the requested booking's own UTC calendar day only. The room's already-booked minutes for that day plus the new `duration_minutes` must be at most that room's `daily_capacity_minutes`, or the booking is short of room capacity. The performer's recorded plus held minutes for that day plus the new `duration_minutes` must be at most that performer's `max_daily_recorded_minutes`; exceeding the performer's cap is `ERR_TALENT_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` and is likewise raised whatever the value of `on_conflict`.

What happens when the room is unavailable depends entirely on `on_conflict`. Under the default `REJECT`, a buffered studio collision is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_SLOT_CONFLICT`, a room-capacity shortfall is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_DAILY_CAPACITY`, and no row is created in either case. Under `WAITLIST`, no error is raised for either condition: the session is created in `WAITLISTED` with `waitlisted_reason` set to `STUDIO_SLOT_CONFLICT` when the buffered interval collided and to `STUDIO_DAILY_CAPACITY` when the day's capacity was short. When the buffered interval is clear and the day still has capacity, the session is created in `SCHEDULED` with `waitlisted_reason` null, regardless of which `on_conflict` value was supplied. The performer-side checks are not diverted by `on_conflict` at all — a double-booked or over-capped performer always earns an error and never a waitlist entry.

The two landing states have sharply different side effects, and they must be given equal attention. A session landing in `SCHEDULED` adds its `duration_minutes` to the room's `booked_minutes` and to the performer's `held_minutes`, has its provisional `session_cost_cents` stamped, and — when the owning order is still in `CASTING` — cascades that order `CASTING → RECORDING` with the order's `version` incremented by 1. A session landing in `WAITLISTED` adds nothing to either counter, because it holds no booth time at all; it still has its provisional `session_cost_cents` stamped, and it fires no order cascade whatsoever. An order therefore stays in `CASTING` until one of its sessions actually reaches the calendar, whether by direct booking or by later promotion off the waitlist.

**Waitlist promotion** runs whenever booth time is returned to a room — that is, on a session cancellation from `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED`, and on a no-show. It selects at most one row per freeing event, and it selects it deterministically. The candidate set is every session on the freeing room that is currently `WAITLISTED`, whose owning order is in `CASTING` or `RECORDING`, whose buffered studio interval — from its `start_utc` up to but not including its `end_utc` plus the room's `turnaround_buffer_minutes` — no longer intersects the interval of any session on that room in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, whose closed talent interval from `start_utc` through `end_utc` no longer intersects the closed interval of any session of the same performer in those same four states, and whose `duration_minutes` added to the room's `booked_minutes` for that candidate's own UTC calendar day remains at most `daily_capacity_minutes`. Rows on other rooms are never considered, nor are rows whose order sits in `DRAFT`, `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS`, `MIXING`, `QC`, `DELIVERED`, `RECALLED` or `CANCELLED`.

Surviving candidates are ranked on two keys in order. The first key is the priority rank of the candidate's own order — a `RUSH` order ranks ahead of an `EXPEDITED` order, which ranks ahead of a `STANDARD` order. The second key is `start_utc` ascending, so the earlier slot wins among equals. Remaining ties are broken by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. Exactly one row, the first after ranking, is promoted. If the candidate set is empty, no promotion happens, the room simply keeps its freed minutes, and no error is raised.

Promotion itself is a `WAITLISTED → SCHEDULED` transition with four consequences: `waitlisted_reason` is cleared to null, the promoted row's `duration_minutes` is added to the room's `booked_minutes`, the same `duration_minutes` is added to the performer's `held_minutes`, and — when the promoted session's order is still in `CASTING` — that order cascades `CASTING → RECORDING`. The promoted session's `version` increments by 1. Sessions may be listed filtered by `order_id`, `studio_id`, `casting_assignment_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Running the Session: Confirm, Start, Complete, No-Show and Cancel

Once the script is signed off and the deal is confirmed, a `ProductionCoordinator` locks the slot by advancing it `SCHEDULED → CONFIRMED`. Four preconditions apply in exactly this order: the session must be `SCHEDULED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the owning order's script adaptation must exist and be `APPROVED`, since a missing or unapproved adaptation is `ERR_ADAPTATION_NOT_APPROVED`; the session's casting assignment must be in `CONFIRMED` or `RECORDED`, a further `ERR_INVALID_STATE` which — uniquely on this endpoint — is tested *after* the adaptation gate rather than with the session's own state at position 11; and the room must be `ACTIVE`, since a room moved to `MAINTENANCE` in the meantime gives `ERR_STUDIO_UNAVAILABLE`. Confirmation increments the session's `version` by 1 and moves no minutes and no money — the booking already held its minutes from the moment it landed in `SCHEDULED`.

Opening the booth advances `CONFIRMED → IN_PROGRESS` and is permitted only once the logical clock has reached or passed the session's `start_utc`; an attempt before that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. From `CONFIRMED` the session forks two ways, and the two branches differ in exactly three respects, so both are spelled out in full.

The **no-show branch** advances `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW`, is likewise permitted only once the logical clock has reached or passed `start_utc`, and does five things: it subtracts the session's `duration_minutes` from the room's `booked_minutes`, subtracts the same `duration_minutes` from the performer's `held_minutes`, books no money at all so that the order's `committed_cents` and `gross_accrued_cents` are both untouched, leaves the casting assignment's `casting_status` exactly where it was, and runs the waitlist promotion selector on that room, promoting at most one `WAITLISTED` row. The session's `version` increments by 1 and `NO_SHOW` is terminal.

The **completion branch** advances `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and does the opposite on all three counts: minutes are converted rather than returned, money is accrued, and the assignment advances. Completion requires the session to be `IN_PROGRESS` and the owning order to be in `RECORDING`. It then restamps `session_cost_cents` from live data and checks the budget: the order's `committed_cents` plus that restamped cost must be at most `budget_cap_cents`, with equality allowed, and a completion that would push past the ceiling is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 402 with nothing written. A surviving completion moves the session's `duration_minutes` out of the performer's `held_minutes` and into their `recorded_minutes` while leaving the room's `booked_minutes` unchanged, adds the restamped `session_cost_cents` to both the order's `committed_cents` and its `gross_accrued_cents`, and increments the session's `version` by 1. Every completion, retake or not, recomputes the assignment's `recorded_minutes` and `retake_minutes` from its `COMPLETED` sessions and increments that assignment's `version` by 1 — the bump happens even when the booking dimension is already `RECORDED` and therefore does not move. When `is_retake` is false, the casting assignment advances `CONFIRMED → RECORDED`. When `is_retake` is true, every retake item still `OPEN` whose `casting_assignment_id` matches this session's assignment is first moved to `RECORDED` — items raised by any review, not only the newest — and then the casting assignment advances `CONFIRMED → RECORDED` as well, the advance being skipped when it already sits there. `COMPLETED` is terminal, and no promotion selector runs on completion, because a completed session continues to occupy its room.

Cancelling a session accepts an optional free-text `reason` — validated as a string if present, then discarded, since no field of the session records it — and moves the row to `CANCELLED` from `WAITLISTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED`; the declared edges are `WAITLISTED → CANCELLED`, `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`. The three origins are not equivalent. A cancel from `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` returns the session's `duration_minutes` to both the room's `booked_minutes` and the performer's `held_minutes`, and then runs the waitlist promotion selector on that room. A cancel from `WAITLISTED` returns nothing, because a waitlisted row never held any minutes, and it therefore does not run the promotion selector at all. Every cancel increments the session's `version` by 1, and `CANCELLED` is terminal.

**The cost of a session** is stamped provisionally when the row is created and restamped from live data at completion; the restamped figure is the one the budget check and the invoice use. It is built from four separately-rounded line items and one derived quantity. The billable block count is the session's `duration_minutes` divided by thirty and rounded *up* to a whole number, and the billable minutes are that block count multiplied by thirty — a room is therefore always billed in whole half-hours, so a twenty-five-minute session is billed as thirty minutes of room time. The studio line is the room's `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by the billable minutes and divided by sixty. The talent line is the assignment's `agreed_rate_cents_per_hour` multiplied by the session's actual `duration_minutes` — not the billable minutes — and divided by sixty. The union uplift line is one hundred and twenty-five thousandths of the assignment's `agreed_rate_cents_per_hour` multiplied by the actual `duration_minutes` and divided by sixty, and it is zero unless the performer's `union_status` is `UNION`. The retake credit line is four tenths of the assignment's `agreed_rate_cents_per_hour` multiplied by the actual `duration_minutes` and divided by sixty, and it is zero unless the session's `is_retake` is true.

As §2.9 requires, each of those four line items is rounded half up to a whole cent independently, from its own unrounded raw value, *before* any addition happens. The session's `session_cost_cents` is then the rounded studio line plus the rounded talent line plus the rounded union uplift, minus the rounded retake credit. Summing the raw values and rounding once at the end is a different computation and is not the declared behaviour. Only the one session's own row, joined to its room, its casting assignment and that assignment's performer, participates in this figure; no other session contributes anything to it.

### 3.8. Mixing, the M&E Stem and Conformance

A `MixEngineer` opens a **mix job** against an `order_id`, declaring the `stem_layout` of the music-and-effects stem being delivered — `STEREO_ME` or `FIVE_ONE_ME` — and `me_stem_present`, a boolean defaulting to false that records whether a separated music-and-effects stem was supplied at all. The job may only be opened on an order that already has at least one non-superseded casting assignment sitting in `casting_status` of `RECORDED`; an order with none earns `ERR_NO_RECORDED_CAST`. A new job is stored in `QUEUED` with `mix_round` at 1, `loudness_lufs` null and `conformance_index` null. Mix jobs may be listed filtered by `order_id` and by `status`.

The stem check advances `QUEUED → STEM_CHECKED` and is a pure gate: the job must be `QUEUED`, `me_stem_present` must be true, and the job's `stem_layout` must equal the `stem_layout` of the order's platform specification. A missing stem or a layout that disagrees with the specification is refused with `ERR_STEM_LAYOUT_MISMATCH`. The check increments the job's `version` by 1 and takes no payload.

Printing the mix advances `STEM_CHECKED → MIXED` and carries `loudness_lufs`, the measured integrated loudness of the mix. Before the print is accepted, every non-superseded casting assignment of the order that sits in `PROPOSED`, `HOLD_REQUESTED`, `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `RECORDED` must have a `casting_status` of `RECORDED`; a single live assignment short of that earns `ERR_CAST_NOT_FULLY_RECORDED`. The supplied `loudness_lufs` is stored, `conformance_index` is recomputed and stamped, the job's `version` increments by 1, and the owning order cascades — `RECORDING → MIXING`, whether this is the order's first mix round or a re-print of a job a retake regression has sent back to `STEM_CHECKED`, or `RECALLED → MIXING` when the order is being re-mixed after a recall — with the order's `version` incremented by 1. An order sitting in neither `RECORDING` nor `RECALLED` when the print lands is not advanced and its `version` is not touched; the print itself still succeeds.

**The conformance index** is a weighted score over three components, each valued between 0.0 and 1.0. The loudness component starts from the absolute difference between the mix's measured `loudness_lufs` and the platform specification's `loudness_target_lufs`; that difference is divided by a tolerance of two loudness units, the quotient is subtracted from 1, and the result is floored at 0.0 so that a wildly off-target mix scores zero rather than negative. The stem component is 1.0 when `me_stem_present` is true *and* the job's `stem_layout` equals the specification's `stem_layout`, and 0.0 otherwise. The cast-coverage component counts the characters registered against the property behind the order's cut and counts the order's non-superseded casting assignments whose `casting_status` is `RECORDED`; when the property has no characters at all the component is 0.0, and otherwise it is the recorded count divided by the character count, capped at 1.0. The index is four tenths of the loudness component plus thirty-five hundredths of the stem component plus twenty-five hundredths of the cast-coverage component, rounded half up to four decimal places.

Conforming the mix advances `MIXED → CONFORMED` and is permitted only when the stamped `conformance_index` is greater than or equal to 0.80, the comparison being inclusive so that an index of exactly 0.80 conforms. A strictly lower index is refused with `ERR_CONFORMANCE_BELOW_MIN`. A successful conform increments the job's `version` by 1 and cascades the owning order `MIXING → QC`, incrementing the order's `version` by 1 — this is the handoff that puts the deliverable in front of a native reviewer. As with the print, an order sitting in any stage other than `MIXING` when the conform lands is simply not advanced and its `version` is not touched; the conform itself still succeeds.

Two edges leave `CONFORMED`, both driven by the system. A quality failure regresses the job `CONFORMED → STEM_CHECKED`, incrementing `mix_round` by 1 and clearing `conformance_index` back to null so the next print must earn its score afresh; this closes the `STEM_CHECKED → MIXED → CONFORMED → STEM_CHECKED` cycle and is described as link 2 of the cascade in §3.9. An order recall instead moves the job `CONFORMED → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal. Both edges increment the job's `version` by 1.

### 3.9. Quality Review, Retake Items and the Four-Link Regression Cascade

A `NativeReviewer` opens a **quality review** by naming the `order_id` and the `mix_job_id` of the conformed mix under inspection; the mix job must belong to that same order or the create is refused with `ERR_CROSS_ORDER_REFERENCE`. Beyond that same-order test the create gates nothing: the named mix job's own `status` is never inspected, so a review may be opened over a job that is still `QUEUED` or has already been superseded, and the owning order's stage is not tested either. The review is stored in `PENDING` with `round_number` at 1, `reviewer_user_id` null, `sampled_loop_count` and `defect_count` at 0, and `pass_rate` and `decision` null. Reviews may be listed filtered by `order_id`, by `mix_job_id` and by `status`.

Starting a round advances `PENDING → IN_REVIEW` and carries `sampled_loop_count`, the number of loops sampled in this round, which must be at least 1 — a start with a sample of 0 is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_QC_SAMPLE`. The two ways of falling short are not equivalent: a value below 0 fails the position-8 field range and is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while exactly 0 clears position 8 and reaches the position-31 `ERR_EMPTY_QC_SAMPLE`. Two further conditions apply: the owning order must be in `QC` — a start attempted while the order sits in any other stage is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, that stage test forming part of the same position-11 state check as the review's own state test and therefore being evaluated ahead of the segregation, sample-size and open-retake conditions — and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on that order's script adaptation, since the person who signed off the script may not also run QC on its recording — that refusal is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The start stores `sampled_loop_count`, stamps `reviewer_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and increments the review's `version` by 1.

The same start action also serves as the re-entry after a failure, advancing `FAILED_RETAKE → IN_REVIEW` and closing the `IN_REVIEW → FAILED_RETAKE → IN_REVIEW` cycle. That path carries one extra precondition and one extra effect: every retake item belonging to the review must have reached either `RECORDED` or `WAIVED`, and a review with even one item still `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_OPEN_RETAKES`; and on re-entry `round_number` is incremented by 1 before the new `sampled_loop_count` is stored. Everything else — the order being in `QC`, the segregation check, the fresh stamping of `reviewer_user_id`, the `version` increment — is identical on both paths.

Deciding a round carries a `decision` of either `PASS` or `FAIL`, a `defect_count` of at least 0 giving the defective loops found in the round, and — when the decision is `FAIL` — a `defects` list describing the loops that must be re-recorded; the list is itself optional and defaults to empty, and it is ignored entirely on a `PASS`. The `casting_assignment_id` inside each defect entry is a body foreign key like any other and is resolved at position 7, before the position-8 field checks: an entry naming no stored assignment refuses the whole decision with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Whichever way the decision goes, the review must be `IN_REVIEW` and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `reviewer_user_id` stamped when the round was started, since a reviewer may not decide someone else's round; that refusal is `ERR_REVIEWER_MISMATCH`. **The pass rate** is computed and stamped on both outcomes: it is 1 minus the quotient of `defect_count` divided by the round's stored `sampled_loop_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places. Because a round can only be started with a sample of at least 1, that denominator is never zero.

A `PASS` decision is permitted only when the computed `pass_rate` is greater than or equal to the platform specification's `qc_pass_threshold`, the comparison being inclusive so that a rate exactly equal to the threshold passes. A rate strictly below it is refused with `ERR_QC_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. A surviving pass stores `defect_count`, stamps `pass_rate`, advances the review `IN_REVIEW → PASSED`, sets `decision` to `PASS`, and increments the review's `version` by 1; `PASSED` is terminal. Note carefully what a pass does *not* do: it does not move the order out of `QC`. Delivery remains a separate, deliberate act by a `ProductionCoordinator`, described in §3.11.

A `FAIL` decision is permitted only when the request carries at least one defect entry — an empty `defects` list on a fail is likewise refused with `ERR_QC_BELOW_THRESHOLD` — and only when the owning order's `retake_cycles` is strictly less than 2. An order that has already been round-tripped twice cannot be failed a third time and earns `ERR_RETAKE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; at that point the reviewer must either pass the mix or the order must be cancelled. A surviving fail stores `defect_count`, stamps `pass_rate`, sets `decision` to `FAIL` and the review to `FAILED_RETAKE`, increments the review's `version` by 1, and then fires a four-link regression cascade that must run in order and in full.

**Link 1** creates one **retake item** per entry in the supplied `defects` list, each in `OPEN`. Each item records the `quality_review_id` of the failing review, the `casting_assignment_id` of the performance that must be re-recorded, a non-empty `loop_ref` identifying the loop on the dubbing sheet, a `defect_category` of `LIPSYNC`, `PERFORMANCE`, `AUDIO` or `TRANSLATION` classifying which downstream step must repeat, and an `estimated_minutes` of at least 0 giving the booth time the reviewer estimates the retake needs, which is optional per entry and defaults to 0. Retake items are created only by this cascade — no direct create exists — and listing them filtered by `quality_review_id`, by `casting_assignment_id` and by `status` is the only discovery channel for them.

**Link 2** regresses the order's newest mix job `CONFORMED → STEM_CHECKED`, incrementing its `mix_round` by 1 and clearing its `conformance_index` to null. The link is conditional on finding that job in `CONFORMED`: when the order's newest job sits anywhere else, or there is none, link 2 does nothing and the cascade carries on to link 3.

**Link 3** regresses the owning order `QC → RECORDING`, increments the order's `retake_cycles` by 1, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

**Link 4** acts on every casting assignment named by an `OPEN` retake item of the failing review. Each such assignment regresses `RECORDED → CONFIRMED` on its booking dimension. On its payment dimension, the behaviour is conditional and this condition is what makes the cascade safe to run twice: when the assignment's `payment_status` is currently `ACCRUED`, its newest talent payment is moved `ACCRUED → VOIDED`, the assignment's payment dimension follows `ACCRUED → VOIDED`, and the amount to void — the sum of `session_cost_cents` over that assignment's `COMPLETED` sessions whose `is_retake` is false — is subtracted from the order's `committed_cents` and added to its `released_cents`, with `gross_accrued_cents` left exactly as it was. When the assignment's `payment_status` is anything other than `ACCRUED`, the amount to void is zero, no money moves, and no payment row is touched. An assignment named by several open items of the same review is acted on exactly once. Each affected assignment's `version` increments by 1 — one bump for the row however many of its two dimensions moved, and no bump at all when neither did — as does the `version` of any payment row that was voided.

Retake items themselves have a short life. An item advances `OPEN → RECORDED` automatically when a session whose `is_retake` is true, booked against that item's `casting_assignment_id`, reaches `COMPLETED`, as described in §3.7; the item's `version` increments by 1. Alternatively the reviewer may waive it, advancing `OPEN → WAIVED`, but only under two conditions, tested in this order after the item's own `OPEN` state has been confirmed at position 11: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `reviewer_user_id` of the item's parent review, a mismatch being `ERR_REVIEWER_MISMATCH` (403); and the item's `defect_category` must not be `LIPSYNC`. Lip-sync defects can never be waived and must be re-recorded; an attempt earns `ERR_RETAKE_NOT_WAIVABLE`. Both `RECORDED` and `WAIVED` are terminal, and the item's `version` increments on either edge. An assignment's `retake_minutes` — the sum of `duration_minutes` over its `COMPLETED` sessions whose `is_retake` is true — accumulates across these rounds and is reported alongside its `recorded_minutes`, which counts only the non-retake completions.

### 3.10. Talent Money: Usage Variants, Territory Bands and Settlement

Talent is paid per casting deal, not per session. A `PlatformAdmin` accrues a **talent payment** by naming the `casting_assignment_id` of the deal being paid and a `territory_scope`, a non-empty array drawn from the nine territory codes naming the territories this payment licenses. The scope must be a subset of the performer's `licensed_territories` and must itself contain the owning order's `territory`; a scope that escapes the performer's rights or omits the order's own territory is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_LICENSED`. The assignment must be in `casting_status` of `RECORDED`, and its own payment dimension must be one of `UNBILLED`, `VOIDED` or `ACCRUED` — a deal already `SETTLED` can never be re-accrued. Both of those are the `ERR_INVALID_STATE` of position 11 and are therefore reported ahead of the territory and duplicate-accrual tests. Beyond them the assignment must not already carry a payment in `ACCRUED` — a second concurrent accrual on the same deal is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_ACCRUED`. A deal whose previous payment was voided by a retake round is explicitly re-accruable, which is how a re-recorded performance gets paid again.

The payment is stored in `ACCRUED` with `settled_at` null, its `talent_id` denormalized from the assignment for territory checking, and its `variant` copied from the assignment's `usage_variant` at accrual time so that a later change of deal terms cannot rewrite history. Creating it advances the assignment's payment dimension `UNBILLED → ACCRUED`, or `VOIDED → ACCRUED` when this is a replacement after a retake round. Payments may be listed filtered by `casting_assignment_id`, by `talent_id` and by `status`.

**The base fee** is computed identically under both variants. For each of the assignment's own sessions that is `COMPLETED` and whose `is_retake` is false, the service multiplies the assignment's `agreed_rate_cents_per_hour` by that session's `duration_minutes`, divides by sixty, and rounds half up to a whole cent. Those per-session rounded figures are then summed to give `base_fee_cents`. Rounding happens once per session, before the sum — not once at the end. Sessions in any other status contribute nothing, and retake sessions never enter the base at all, which is precisely why a retake is a cost to the vendor rather than a second payday for the performer.

**The usage fee** is where the two variants diverge sharply on identical session data. Under `BUYOUT`, a single flat multiplier buys every territory at once: `usage_fee_cents` is `base_fee_cents` multiplied by one and seventy-five hundredths, rounded half up to a whole cent. The `territory_scope` is ignored entirely — the figure is the same whether the scope names one territory or all nine. Under `RESIDUAL`, the fee is banded per territory. The supplied `territory_scope` is first sorted ascending by each territory's position in the canonical order `US`, `CA`, `UK`, `DE`, `FR`, `JP`, `BR`, `AU`, `ROW` — the ordering is by that canonical position only, never by the order the client listed them and never by row identifier. Walking the sorted list from its first position, the first territory contributes forty-five hundredths, the second contributes thirty hundredths, the third contributes eighteen hundredths, and every territory from the fourth position onward contributes seven hundredths each. Those contributions are summed into a single band total, that total is capped at one and twenty hundredths — a seven-territory scope would otherwise reach one and twenty-one hundredths and is therefore clipped — and `usage_fee_cents` is `base_fee_cents` multiplied by the capped total, rounded half up to a whole cent exactly once. A three-territory residual scope therefore pays ninety-three hundredths of the base while a buyout on the same data pays one and seventy-five hundredths of it.

`total_fee_cents` is simply `base_fee_cents` plus `usage_fee_cents`, both already whole cents.

Settlement is the terminal act. A `PlatformAdmin` settles a payment that is currently `ACCRUED`; a payment already `SETTLED` or `VOIDED` earns `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Two further conditions guard it: the parent casting assignment must be in `casting_status` of `RECORDED`, and the owning order's newest quality review must be in `PASSED` — the vendor does not pay out on a performance the reviewer has not signed off, an order with no review at all failing this test too. Both are position-11 state tests and are therefore evaluated *ahead* of the already-settled test at position 44, so a settled payment on an assignment that is no longer `RECORDED` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Settlement is not blocked by the owning order having closed; a `DELIVERED` order still settles. A surviving settlement moves the payment `ACCRUED → SETTLED`, stamps `settled_at` with the current logical-clock instant, advances the assignment's payment dimension `ACCRUED → SETTLED`, and increments the payment's `version` by 1. The other exit from `ACCRUED` is `VOIDED`, driven only by link 4 of the retake cascade described in §3.9; both `SETTLED` and `VOIDED` are terminal for the payment row.

### 3.11. Delivery, Recall, Cancellation and the Invoice

Delivery is the one late-stage transition a human commands directly. A `ProductionCoordinator` supplies `metadata_locale_fields`, the localized metadata map for the deliverable — a required object on every delivery, even when the platform specification lists no required keys — and the order advances `QC → DELIVERED`. Three preconditions apply: the order must be in `QC`; the order's newest quality review must be in `PASSED`, and an order whose newest review has not passed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and every key listed in the platform specification's `metadata_fields_required` must be present in the supplied map with a non-empty value, a shortfall being refused with `ERR_METADATA_INCOMPLETE`. Delivery stores the supplied map in `metadata_locale_fields`, stamps `delivered_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and increments the order's `version` by 1. It moves no money and no minutes.

Recall reverses a delivery without erasing its history. A `ProductionCoordinator` may supply an optional free-text `reason`, which is validated if present and then discarded, no field recording it; the order moves `DELIVERED → RECALLED` and three things happen alongside. The whole of the order's `committed_cents` is moved into its `released_cents`, leaving `gross_accrued_cents` untouched so that the conservation invariant of §2.9 still holds. The order's newest mix job moves `CONFORMED → SUPERSEDED`, but only when that newest job is actually in `CONFORMED`; a newest job in any other state, or no job at all, is left untouched and the recall still succeeds. And the order's `version` increments by 1. A recalled order is not finished: opening a fresh mix job is blocked while it stays `RECALLED`, but printing a mix on an existing job cascades `RECALLED → MIXING`, which puts the order back on the `MIXING → QC` road and lets a new review round run to a fresh delivery. The `delivered_at` stamp from the previous delivery survives until a later delivery overwrites it, since it records the most recent delivery rather than the first.

Cancellation is the escape hatch, and it is available from six stages: `DRAFT`, `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS`, `CASTING`, `RECORDING`, `MIXING` and `QC`. A `ProductionCoordinator` may attach an optional `reason`, again validated and then discarded. The order moves to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal — `DRAFT → CANCELLED`, `SCRIPT_IN_PROGRESS → CANCELLED`, `CASTING → CANCELLED`, `RECORDING → CANCELLED`, `MIXING → CANCELLED` and `QC → CANCELLED` are all declared edges — and the cancel releases everything the order was holding: every one of its recording sessions in `WAITLISTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` is moved to `CANCELLED` with that session's `version` incremented by 1, and each row that was actually holding booth time — that is, each one cancelled from `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` — returns its `duration_minutes` to the relevant room's `booked_minutes` and to the relevant performer's `held_minutes`, while a `WAITLISTED` row returns nothing because it held nothing; unlike a single-session cancel, this bulk release runs no waitlist promotion selector on any room; the whole of the order's `committed_cents` is moved into its `released_cents` with `gross_accrued_cents` untouched; and the order's `version` increments by 1. Attempting to cancel an order that is already `DELIVERED`, `RECALLED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_LOCKED`.

**The invoice** is a pure computed read available to a `ProductionCoordinator` or a `PlatformAdmin`; it writes nothing and it may be taken at any stage of an order's life. Its five steps must be evaluated in the pinned order below, because the dependency between the surcharge and the discount tier only resolves one way round.

Step one computes `recorded_subtotal_cents` as the sum of `session_cost_cents` over the order's sessions whose status is `COMPLETED`. Sessions in `WAITLISTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` are excluded entirely, while retake sessions *are* included at their already-credited cost.

Step two computes `rush_surcharge_cents` as the bare subtotal from step one multiplied by the surcharge percentage of the order's `priority_tier` — zero for a `STANDARD` order, one tenth for an `EXPEDITED` order and twenty-two hundredths for a `RUSH` order — rounded half up to a whole cent. The surcharge is keyed on the bare subtotal, not on any later figure.

Step three computes `pre_discount_total_cents` as `recorded_subtotal_cents` plus `rush_surcharge_cents`.

Step four selects `volume_discount_pct` from that post-surcharge, pre-discount total: eight hundredths when the total is at least five hundred thousand cents, otherwise five hundredths when the total is at least two hundred thousand cents, otherwise zero. The tier is keyed on the post-surcharge total from step three, never on the bare subtotal from step one — an order whose bare subtotal falls short of a tier but whose surcharge carries it over the line does earn that tier's discount.

Step five computes `volume_discount_cents` as `pre_discount_total_cents` multiplied by `volume_discount_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `invoice_total_cents` as `pre_discount_total_cents` minus `volume_discount_cents`. Rounding happens at step two and step five only; steps one, three and four introduce no rounding of their own. The computed read reports the `order_id` alongside all six figures.
