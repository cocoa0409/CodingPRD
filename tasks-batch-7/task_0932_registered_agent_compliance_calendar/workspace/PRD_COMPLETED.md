# Registered-Agent & Compliance-Calendar API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A registered-agent firm sells one promise to its clients: nothing statutory will ever be missed. The firm holds an address of record in every jurisdiction a client's companies occupy, accepts service of process on their behalf, and keeps a rolling calendar of annual reports, franchise taxes, beneficial-ownership reports and final reports across every one of those jurisdictions. What makes the promise expensive to keep is that no two jurisdictions agree on anything. One state anchors the annual report on the anniversary of formation; the next fixes a single calendar date for every company on its rolls; a third counts a number of days forward from whatever fiscal year end the company happens to use. Grace periods are sometimes calendar days and sometimes business days. Late fees are sometimes a flat charge, sometimes an unbounded monthly accrual, sometimes a capped percentage. Franchise tax may be a single number, a step function on authorized shares, a continuous function of assets and share ratios, or a bracket table on gross receipts. Every one of those numbers changes over time, and a change enacted in 2028 must not silently re-price a period that was determined in 2026.

This document specifies the API for the platform that carries that promise. It manages the full portfolio of a client: the legal entities, the registrations those entities hold in each jurisdiction, the agent appointments that make a registration lawful, the compliance calendar and its reminder ladder, the filings lodged against that calendar, the money that funds those filings, the service-of-process documents that arrive at the agent's address and must be forwarded within statutory business hours, the beneficial-ownership reports that run on a parallel federal clock, and the administrative-dissolution and reinstatement machinery that engages when a client falls behind. Two properties dominate the design. First, **effective-dated jurisdiction rules are never retroactive**: every calendar row pins the rule version that governed it at the moment it was created, and no later publication moves it. Second, **money is conserved**: every cent captured from a client is, at every instant, either allocated to a filing, sitting unallocated, or refunded to account credit, and the platform must be able to prove that identity after every operation.

The platform is operated by four internal role families and read by the client itself, with a deliberate segregation of duties between the operator who prepares a filing and the operator who lodges it with the state. Because the system reasons entirely in terms of statutory dates, it does not read the host machine's clock: an administrator drives a logical clock forward, and an ops-run checkpoint sweeps the estate to advance calendar statuses, send reminders, retire resignation notices, dissolve delinquent registrations, expire lapsed reinstatement windows and recompute every portfolio rollup.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Request Identity

Every request to every endpoint, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or present but blank — blank meaning empty or entirely whitespace — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. Both header values have their surrounding whitespace trimmed before anything else looks at them, so the role is matched and the identifier is recorded and compared in trimmed form. The same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401 is returned when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the five declared roles.

There are exactly five roles, spelled exactly as follows:

- **`PlatformAdmin`** operates the platform. It alone drives the logical clock and the ops-run checkpoint, registers clients, publishes jurisdiction rule versions, captures and voids money, records administrative dissolutions, and waives obligations. It also has read access across the estate.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** prepares the compliance surface: legal entities and their beneficial owners, name clearances, home registrations and foreign qualifications, good-standing certificate records, agent appointments, the compliance calendar and its rebuilds, BOI report drafts, filing drafts, refiles and abandonments, and reinstatement cases. It may never lodge with a jurisdiction a filing it prepared.
- **`FilingAgent`** acts against the jurisdictions: it records name-search results, accepts and rejects proposed agent appointments, activates, withdraws and terminates registrations, submits filings and BOI reports, records the outcomes those bodies return, and grants or denies reinstatement.
- **`SopHandler`** runs the service-of-process mailroom: it records receipt of served papers, scans them, forwards them to the client, and raises escalations when the client does not acknowledge.
- **`ClientContact`** is the portfolio owner's own user. It authorizes and reads its own payments, reads its own portfolio, and acknowledges service of process forwarded to it.

If the acting role is well formed but is not listed among the roles permitted for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403. Section 4 lists the permitted roles for each endpoint.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ClientContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`; ownership checks on any addressed resource walk the chain from that resource up through its `Registration` and `LegalEntity` to the owning `Client` and compare that client's `id` to `X-User-ID`. When a `ClientContact` addresses a resource that resolves to a different client, the request is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_CLIENT_ACCESS` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `FilingAgent` or `SopHandler`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is, however, recorded: the operator that drafts a filing has its `X-User-ID` stored as that filing's `prepared_by_user_id`, and the operator that lodges the filing has its `X-User-ID` stored as `submitted_by_user_id`. Those two recorded identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties rule compares.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; the literal prefix `req_` is followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Like every other server-written instant it is read from the **logical clock** of Section 2.5, not from the host machine's wall clock, so it does not move between two requests made at the same logical instant.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and for list endpoints only: the `meta` of a single-object 2xx response carries `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` and nothing else.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted the server uses 10. The smallest accepted `limit` is 1 and the largest is 100. When `offset` is omitted the server uses 0, and the smallest accepted `offset` is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400, as is a `limit` or `offset` whose query-string value is not a base-ten integer at all.

List responses populate four fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` reports how many rows matched the filters before the page window was applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows returned in this page is less than `total_count`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list endpoints return rows in ascending `created_at` order, and rows sharing the same `created_at` value are returned in the order they were created — insertion order — so that a page boundary is always deterministic. One endpoint states otherwise: the jurisdiction rule listing returns rule versions in descending `effective_from` order, newest first, and where two rule versions for the same jurisdiction share an `effective_from` the more recently created row is returned first — and that descending order is taken across the whole catalogue, not per jurisdiction. The insertion-order tie-break is carried by a monotone creation counter, and because row identifiers are minted from that same counter, ordering by insertion and ordering by `id` coincide.

Filter parameters on a list endpoint narrow the matched set before ordering and paging. When a filter parameter is omitted, no narrowing on that dimension takes place. Filters are exact-match on the named field: the raw query-string value is compared for string equality against the stored field value, so a filter value that is not a declared member of the field's enumeration, or that names a row that does not exist, simply narrows to the empty set and returns an empty page with `total_count` 0 — never `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, never `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and never any other 4xx. Beyond the filters named for each endpoint in Section 3, the jurisdiction rule listing also accepts an optional `state_code` filter. Unrecognised query parameters are ignored.

Three list endpoints address no single resource and yet admit `ClientContact`: `GET /registrations`, `GET /compliance-obligations` and `GET /filings`. On those three the cross-client rung of Section 2.9 has nothing to test, so instead of raising, the server **narrows** the matched set to the rows the acting client owns before any filter is applied; a `ClientContact` therefore sees its own portfolio and never another client's rows.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and Audit Fields

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a request body is ignored, as is any other body field the endpoint does not name — unrecognised body keys are never an error. A body field supplied with an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: an optional field falls back to its declared default and a required field fails the presence check.

Most resources also carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. `created_at` is stamped once when the row is inserted and never changes. `updated_at` is stamped with the same value at insertion and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations the row undergoes as a side effect of an operation addressed at some other resource. "Mutation" here means a **declared state change or field write**, and it is counted once per affected row per request even when the request writes several of that row's fields: a `PATCH` that changes five columns and moves the beneficial-ownership axis is one mutation and moves `version` by exactly 1. The **recomputation of a derived rollup is not a mutation for this purpose**: the rank, posture, portfolio-count, portfolio-money, escalation-count, risk-flag and credit-balance fields of Section 2.11 are refreshed in place without refreshing `updated_at` and without moving `version`, so a client row whose `portfolio_open_obligation_count` changed because an obligation was created elsewhere keeps the `version` and `updated_at` it already had. Only the transitions and field writes that this document names explicitly move a version. Conversely, an accepted write always moves the addressed row's `version` even when it changed nothing observable — a `PATCH` carrying an empty body, or carrying only values the row already held, still counts as one mutation.

Three resource types are deliberately leaner. `GoodStandingCertificate`, `PaymentAllocation` and `SopEscalationEvent` carry only `id` and `created_at`: they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, because nothing in the system ever mutates a row of those three types once written. Every other resource type — `Client`, `StateRule`, `LegalEntity`, `BeneficialOwner`, `NameClearance`, `Registration`, `AgentAppointment`, `ComplianceObligation`, `ReminderNotice`, `Filing`, `Payment`, `SopDocument`, `BoiReport` and `ReinstatementCase` — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Every instant this server writes — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `api_request_timestamp` and every `*_at` field of a service-of-process document or escalation event — is rendered by one single renderer in exactly the form `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`: ISO 8601 UTC, always exactly three fractional digits even when they are zero, always the literal `Z` suffix and never a numeric offset. Every date this server writes — every `*_on`, `*_date` and `*_deadline` field, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `issued_on`, `appointed_on`, `scheduled_for`, `due_date_snapshot` and the rest — is rendered as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date with no time part at all. A caller-supplied instant is **normalised through that same renderer rather than echoed back verbatim**, so whatever spelling the caller used (a trailing `z`, a `+00:00` offset, a naive instant with no zone, more or fewer than three fractional digits) the response carries the server's canonical rendering. A null instant or null date is rendered as JSON `null`, never as an empty string.

All computed fields described anywhere in this document are response-visible attributes of the resource that owns them: they are serialized in the `data` object exactly like input fields, and a reader of a resource sees the current value of every rollup, every derived date and every money component. The only values that are internal intermediates, never serialized, are the per-step working values inside a calculation — for example the intermediate assumed par capital inside the franchise-tax computation, or the running remainder inside the payment waterfall — together with three pieces of bookkeeping the entity model declares no field for and no response therefore carries: which rule version priced a `Filing`, which `ReinstatementCase` a `Filing` names, and which obligations a `ReinstatementCase` counted at its last assessment. Wherever a calculation produces a value that this document names as a field of an entity, that field is part of the response, and no resource emits a field this document does not name for it.

Several declared fields are, conversely, **recorded for the reader and consulted by no code path in the system**: no gate reads them, no derivation reads them and no rollup reads them, so no request ever succeeds or fails because of their value. They are: a beneficial owner's `date_of_birth` and `is_company_applicant`; a name clearance's `conflicting_name`; a registration's `qualified_on`, `assumed_name`, `admin_dissolved_on` and `sop_priority_hold`; an obligation's `rule_determination_date` and `satisfied_on`; a filing's `expedite_fee_cents`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_on` and `rejection_reason`; a payment allocation's `sequence_index`; a payment's `unallocated_amount_cents`; a service-of-process document's `plaintiff_name`, `court_case_number`, `deferred`, `deferral_count`, `forwarded_late`, `scanned_at` and `acknowledged_at`; a BOI report's `deadline_date`, `triggering_change_date`, `owner_snapshot_count` and `accepted_on`; a reinstatement case's `obligations_cured_count` and `denial_reason`; and a client's `portfolio_risk_flag`. Each is written exactly as its own narrative describes, and each remains available as a list filter where its endpoint declares one; the claim being narrowed here is only that no gate, no date or money derivation and no rollup reads any of them back. Two of them are read by their own bookkeeping and by nothing else: `sop_priority_hold` is compared against the value about to be written so that an unchanged value suppresses the write, and `deferral_count` is read in order to increment itself.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, the Business Calendar and the Business-Hour Cursor

The platform does not read the host machine's wall clock for any business decision. It keeps a **logical clock**, and before an administrator has ever moved it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. It never falls back to wall-clock time. Every "current instant" and "current date" this document refers to — including the `created_at` and `updated_at` stamps of Section 2.4, every `*_at` instant such as `received_at`, `scanned_at`, `forwarded_at` and `acknowledged_at`, and every `*_on` date such as `qualified_on`, `closed_on`, `submitted_on`, `satisfied_on`, `sent_on`, `admin_dissolved_on`, `outcome_recorded_on`, `accepted_on` and `assessment_date` — is read from the logical clock. Where a rule speaks of the "current clock date", it means the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC.

An administrator moves the clock by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`. It must be a string that parses as ISO 8601; a trailing `Z` or `z`, an explicit numeric offset (which is converted to UTC), a bare date such as `2026-06-01` (read as midnight UTC on that date) and an instant with no zone at all (read as UTC) are all accepted, and anything that does not parse is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. The response is a bare two-field object carrying `current_utc`, the instant the clock now reads, and `previous_utc`, the instant it read before the call, and nothing else — no `id`, no audit fields, no envelope pagination. Both are rendered by the single instant renderer of Section 2.4, so a target supplied with sub-millisecond precision reads back truncated to three fractional digits even though the clock itself retains what it was given. The clock may be set forward or left where it is, but it may never be moved backwards: a target instant strictly earlier than the current logical clock instant is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` and HTTP 409. Setting the clock mutates no entity — it moves the clock and nothing else. It is the ops-run checkpoint, described in Section 3.13, that propagates the consequences of elapsed time into the estate.

**Weekends and observed holidays.** Saturday and Sunday are non-business days. In addition, the platform observes twenty-four holiday dates across four years: `2026-01-01`, `2026-05-25`, `2026-07-03`, `2026-09-07`, `2026-11-26` and `2026-12-25`; `2027-01-01`, `2027-05-31`, `2027-07-05`, `2027-09-06`, `2027-11-25` and `2027-12-24`; `2028-01-03`, `2028-05-29`, `2028-07-04`, `2028-09-04`, `2028-11-23` and `2028-12-25`; and `2029-01-01`, `2029-05-28`, `2029-07-04`, `2029-09-03`, `2029-11-22` and `2029-12-25`. A **business day** is any date that is neither a Saturday, nor a Sunday, nor one of those twenty-four dates.

**The forward roll.** Several date derivations end with a forward roll. Rolling a date forward means: while the date falls on a Saturday, on a Sunday, or on an observed holiday, advance it by one calendar day and test again; return the first date that is a business day. The roll only ever moves a date later, never earlier, and a date that is already a business day is returned unchanged.

**The business-hour cursor.** Service-of-process deadlines are counted in business hours, not calendar hours. The business day runs from 09:00 UTC inclusive to 17:00 UTC exclusive, giving 8 business hours per business day. Advancing an instant by a whole number of business hours works as follows. First the cursor is normalized: if the starting instant does not fall on a business day, or falls at or after 17:00 UTC, the cursor moves to 09:00 UTC of the next business day; if it falls on a business day but strictly before 09:00 UTC, the cursor moves to 09:00 UTC of that same day; otherwise the cursor stays where it is. Then, while hours remain to be consumed, the server measures how many hours are left between the cursor and 17:00 UTC of the cursor's own day: if the remaining requirement fits within that span, the result is the cursor advanced by that requirement and the advance is finished; otherwise the whole span is consumed, the requirement is reduced by it, and the cursor moves to 09:00 UTC of the next business day. A deadline produced this way therefore always lands inside the 09:00-to-17:00 UTC window of a business day.

### 2.6. Effective-Dated Jurisdiction Rules

Every per-jurisdiction number in the system lives on a `StateRule` row. A rule row is scoped to one jurisdiction by its `state_code`, which must be exactly two uppercase letters, and is effective-dated by `effective_from` and `effective_to`. `effective_from` is an inclusive ISO date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form supplied by the publisher. `effective_to` is an exclusive ISO date written by the server; it is null while the row is the open row for its `state_code`.

**Publication.** A `PlatformAdmin` publishes a new rule version for a jurisdiction. The new row is created with `effective_to` null, and the row that was previously open for that same `state_code` has its `effective_to` stamped with the new row's `effective_from`, closing it. A new row's `effective_from` must be strictly later than the `effective_from` of the currently open row for the same `state_code`; a value equal to or earlier than the incumbent's is rejected with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409, and where the jurisdiction has no open row the check is vacuous and the publication proceeds. Closing the incumbent is a mutation of that row, so the incumbent's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1 while the new row is born at `version` 1. Publishing a rule row never rewrites `governing_rule_id` on any existing `ComplianceObligation`, and never re-prices anything.

**Selection.** Whenever a jurisdiction-specific number is needed — creating a home registration, creating a foreign qualification, creating a calendar obligation, drafting a standalone filing, or assessing reinstatement fees — the server selects the governing rule row for a target `state_code` at a **determination date**. The candidate set is every rule row for that `state_code` whose `effective_from` is on or before the determination date and whose `effective_to` is either null or strictly after the determination date. A determination date exactly equal to a row's `effective_to` therefore falls to the successor row, not to the closing one. Candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, so the latest applicable version wins; when two candidate rows share the same `effective_from`, the more recently created row wins. If the candidate set is empty, the operation is rejected with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE` and HTTP 422.

**Non-retroactivity.** For a compliance obligation, the determination date is the logical clock date at the moment the obligation row is created; that date is persisted as `rule_determination_date`, and the selected row's `id` is persisted as `governing_rule_id`. Neither is ever re-resolved. A rule version published in a later year does not move an obligation that was already determined, and a calendar rebuild explicitly re-derives dates under the obligation's own pinned rule rather than under today's.

**The rule catalogue.** A rule row carries the following publisher-supplied fields. Four are required on publication: `state_code`, `effective_from`, `annual_report_basis` and `franchise_tax_basis`. Every other field is optional and takes the stated server-side default when the publisher omits it. A rule read returns every one of those fields, defaults filled in, alongside `effective_to` and the four audit fields; the two array fields are returned normalised to their two declared keys, so an item carrying extra keys reads back without them.

`annual_report_basis` is a discriminator selecting the annual-report due-date formula and is one of `ANNIVERSARY_MONTH_DAY`, `FIXED_CALENDAR_DATE` or `FISCAL_YEAR_END_OFFSET`. It is supported by `fixed_due_month` (default 1, accepted range 1 through 12), `fixed_due_day` (default 1, accepted range 1 through 31) and `fiscal_offset_days` (default 0, accepted range 0 through 365).

`franchise_tax_basis` is a discriminator selecting the franchise-tax formula and is one of `FLAT`, `AUTHORIZED_SHARES_TIERS`, `ASSUMED_PAR_VALUE` or `GROSS_RECEIPTS_BRACKETS`. It is supported by `flat_franchise_tax_cents` (default 0, never negative); `authorized_shares_tiers`, an ordered array defaulting to the empty array whose items are objects each carrying an `up_to_shares` integer or null together with a `tax_cents` integer; `assumed_par_value_rate_bps` (default 0, never negative), `min_franchise_tax_cents` (default 0, never negative) and `max_franchise_tax_cents` (default 100000000, and never below `min_franchise_tax_cents`); and `gross_receipts_brackets`, an ordered array defaulting to the empty array whose items are objects each carrying an `up_to_cents` integer or null together with a `tax_cents` integer.

The post-due window is described by `grace_days` (default 0, accepted range 0 through 120) and `grace_basis`, which defaults to `CALENDAR_DAYS` and is one of `CALENDAR_DAYS` or `BUSINESS_DAYS`. Lateness is priced by `late_fee_style`, which defaults to `FLAT` and is one of `FLAT`, `PER_MONTH` or `PERCENT_CAPPED`, supported by `late_fee_flat_cents`, `late_fee_per_month_cents`, `late_fee_percent_bps` and `late_fee_cap_cents`, each defaulting to 0 and none of which may be negative.

The decay path is described by `delinquency_to_dissolution_days` (default 60, accepted range 1 through 1095), the number of calendar days after the grace window closes at which the state may administratively dissolve, and `reinstatement_window_days` (default 365, accepted range 1 through 3650), the number of calendar days after administrative dissolution during which reinstatement remains available.

Money charged by the jurisdiction is described by `annual_report_fee_cents`, `franchise_tax_filing_fee_cents`, `foreign_qualification_fee_cents`, `withdrawal_fee_cents`, `dissolution_fee_cents`, `reinstatement_fee_cents` and `expedite_surcharge_cents`, each defaulting to 0 and none of which may be negative. Finally, `good_standing_freshness_days` (default 30, accepted range 1 through 365) fixes how young a certificate of good standing must be to be accepted in this jurisdiction, and `requires_tax_clearance` (default false) says whether dissolution and withdrawal filings in this jurisdiction require a tax-clearance reference.

### 2.7. Money, Precision and the Conservation of Client Funds

All money in this system is expressed as an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a request, a response or an intermediate. Wherever a computation produces a non-integer, it is rounded half up to a whole cent — a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude. Each component of a total is rounded to whole cents by its own calculation **before** the components are summed; a total is never produced by summing unrounded parts and rounding at the end.

Four conservation invariants must hold after every operation that touches money or the calendar, and the platform is expected to be able to demonstrate each of them at any time.

1. **Payment identity.** For every `Payment`, `captured_amount_cents` equals the sum of `allocated_amount_cents`, `unallocated_amount_cents` and `refunded_amount_cents`, as exact integers with no tolerance.
2. **Client credit identity.** For every `Client`, `credit_balance_cents` equals the sum of `refunded_amount_cents` over all of that client's payments, again as an exact integer equality.
3. **Allocation split identity.** On every `PaymentAllocation` row, `state_portion_cents` and `service_portion_cents` sum to exactly `amount_cents`. This is what forces the pinned rounding order described in Section 3.8: the state portion is rounded and the service portion takes the remainder, rather than both being rounded independently.
4. **Rollup identity.** For every `Client`, `portfolio_open_obligation_count` equals the number of that client's obligations in an open status and `portfolio_outstanding_cents` equals the sum of `total_due_cents` over exactly those same rows. The rollup must equal the sum of its parts after every mutation, not merely after an ops-run.

The system also distinguishes two kinds of money permanently. The **state portion** of a filing is money paid over to a jurisdiction and is never refundable under any circumstance. The **service portion** is the platform's own fee and is the only money that can be carried to a successor filing or returned to the client as account credit.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Three write endpoints create a child row whose correctness depends on the caller having observed the current state of its parent. Each of those three requires a non-optional version field in its request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of that parent. If the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no row is created.

- A request to `POST /compliance-obligations/{id}/filings` must carry `expected_compliance_obligation_version` matching the addressed `ComplianceObligation`'s current `version`. A newly drafted filing legitimately supersedes any earlier draft on the same obligation, and an ops-run may have re-priced the obligation's `late_fee_cents` since the caller last read it, so a caller holding a stale view must re-read before drafting.
- A request to `POST /registrations/{id}/agent-appointments` must carry `expected_registration_version` matching the addressed `Registration`'s current `version`. Accepting an appointment supersedes the incumbent agent, so a caller working from a stale registration view could transfer away an agent it never saw.
- A request to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/boi-reports` must carry `expected_legal_entity_version` matching the addressed `LegalEntity`'s current `version`. Any beneficial-ownership reportable change re-arms `boi_update_deadline` and bumps the entity's version, so a report drafted against a stale view would be pinned to a superseded deadline.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and none may be added. In particular, creating a compliance obligation on a registration carries no version field and can never return `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder and Gate Precedence

Validation is a strictly ordered ladder of forty-two rungs. The rungs are evaluated in ascending order, and **the first rung that fails determines the entire response**: the error identifier and HTTP status of that rung are returned and no later rung is consulted. When two conditions on a request would each fail a rung, the one occupying the lower ordinal position wins. Rungs that do not apply to the endpoint being called are skipped without effect.

The ladder, in order:

1. **Rung 1** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-blank. Applies to every endpoint. Failure returns `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401).
2. **Rung 2** — `X-User-Role` carries one of the five declared roles: `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `FilingAgent`, `SopHandler` or `ClientContact`. Applies to every endpoint. Failure returns `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401).
3. **Rung 3** — the acting role appears in the set of roles the called endpoint permits. Applies to every endpoint. Failure returns `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).
4. **Rung 4** — the `{id}` segment under the clients path resolves to an existing `Client`. Applies to every path of the form `/clients/{id}...`. Failure returns `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
5. **Rung 5** — the `{id}` segment under the legal-entities path resolves to an existing `LegalEntity`. Applies to every path of the form `/legal-entities/{id}...`. Failure returns `ERR_LEGAL_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
6. **Rung 6** — the `{id}` segment under the registrations path resolves to an existing `Registration`. Applies to every path of the form `/registrations/{id}...`. Failure returns `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
7. **Rung 7** — the `{id}` segment under the compliance-obligations path resolves to an existing `ComplianceObligation`. Applies to every path of the form `/compliance-obligations/{id}...`. Failure returns `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
8. **Rung 8** — the `{id}` segment under the filings path resolves to an existing `Filing`. Applies to every path of the form `/filings/{id}...`. Failure returns `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
9. **Rung 9** — the `{id}` segment under the payments path resolves to an existing `Payment`. Applies to every path of the form `/payments/{id}...`. Failure returns `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
10. **Rung 10** — the `{id}` segment under the sop-documents path resolves to an existing `SopDocument`. Applies to every path of the form `/sop-documents/{id}...`. Failure returns `ERR_SOP_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
11. **Rung 11** — the `{id}` segment under the beneficial-owners, agent-appointments, boi-reports, name-clearances or reinstatement-cases paths resolves to a row of the matching type, and likewise a body-supplied `name_clearance_id`, `good_standing_certificate_id`, `reinstatement_case_id` or `target_filing_id` resolves to an existing row. Applies to every path of the form `/beneficial-owners/{id}...`, `/agent-appointments/{id}...`, `/boi-reports/{id}...`, `/name-clearances/{id}...` or `/reinstatement-cases/{id}...`, plus any endpoint whose body carries one of those four identifiers. The body half of this rung is applied uniformly and **before the handler decides whether it cares**: any request body carrying one of the four keys with a non-null value has that value resolved, on every endpoint, even one whose narrative never mentions the field, and a value that is not a string — a number, a boolean, an object — resolves to nothing and fails this rung exactly like an unknown identifier. Failure returns `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).
12. **Rung 12** — when the acting role is `ClientContact`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the `Client` that owns the addressed resource, resolved through the legal-entity and registration chain. Applies to every endpoint whose permitted-role set includes `ClientContact` **and whose path addresses a resource by `{id}`**, and the resource it tests is the one named by that `{id}` segment — the client on `/clients/{id}...`, the entity on `/legal-entities/{id}...`, and so on. The three estate-wide list endpoints that admit `ClientContact` but address nothing (`GET /registrations`, `GET /compliance-obligations`, `GET /filings`) cannot fail this rung; they narrow instead, as Section 2.3 describes. Failure returns `ERR_CROSS_CLIENT_ACCESS` (HTTP 403).
13. **Rung 13** — all required body fields are present, non-null and of their declared type; numeric fields honour their declared ranges; `limit` lies between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is zero or greater. Applies to every endpoint. A request body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or that parses to something other than a JSON object, fails this rung too — but only after rungs 1 through 12 have passed, so a malformed body sent to an unknown resource or by an unauthorised role still reports the not-found or authorisation failure first. String fields declared non-empty reject a value that is blank or whitespace-only; integer fields reject booleans and reject numbers carrying a fractional part; boolean fields reject the strings `"true"` and `"false"`; date fields require exactly `YYYY-MM-DD`. Failure returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
14. **Rung 14** — every enum-valued body field carries a declared member. This rung routes exactly these fields and no others: `entity_type` and `boi_exempt_class` on a legal entity, `owner_status` on a beneficial owner, `annual_report_basis`, `franchise_tax_basis`, `grace_basis` and `late_fee_style` on a jurisdiction rule, `obligation_type` on a compliance obligation, `filing_kind` and `expedite_tier` on a filing, `sop_class` on a service-of-process document, `report_kind` on a BOI report, `result` on a name clearance, and the `outcome` field on the two outcome-recording endpoints. No other field routes to this rung; an out-of-range or unrecognised value in any field not on that list is a rung-13 failure instead, and no query-string filter value ever routes here at all. Applies to the POST and PATCH endpoints carrying one of the listed fields. The declared member set for the name-clearance `result` field is `PENDING`, `AVAILABLE` and `CONFLICT` — the same three values the field itself takes — so recording a result of `PENDING` passes this rung and is accepted by the endpoint, leaving `resolved_name` null. Membership is tested only after **every** rung-13 check on the same body has passed, so a request that both carries a bad enum member and omits a required field, or carries an out-of-range number, reports the rung-13 failure. A field whose enum value is supplied as a non-string is likewise a rung-13 failure, not a rung-14 one. Failure returns `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 422).
15. **Rung 15** — the supplied `expected_compliance_obligation_version`, `expected_registration_version` or `expected_legal_entity_version` equals the parent entity's current `version`. Applies to `POST /compliance-obligations/{id}/filings`, `POST /registrations/{id}/agent-appointments` and `POST /legal-entities/{id}/boi-reports`. Failure returns `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
16. **Rung 16** — the target row's current state is a declared origin state for the requested transition, and every precondition of that transition that no later rung covers more specifically holds. Concretely, this rung additionally carries four checks: a `WITHDRAWAL` filing in status `ACCEPTED` must exist on the registration before a foreign registration is withdrawn; a `DISSOLUTION` filing in status `ACCEPTED` must exist on the registration before the home registration is terminated; a BOI report with `report_kind` `UPDATED` requires the parent entity's `boi_status` to be `UPDATE_DUE`; and a `HOME` registration may not be withdrawn while a `FOREIGN` registration may not be terminated. Applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine. Failure returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).
17. **Rung 17** — the clock target instant is greater than or equal to the current logical clock instant. Applies to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. Failure returns `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 409).
18. **Rung 18** — a jurisdiction rule row is effective for the target `state_code` on the determination date. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/registrations`, `POST /legal-entities/{id}/foreign-qualifications`, `POST /registrations/{id}/compliance-obligations`, `POST /registrations/{id}/filings` and `POST /reinstatement-cases/{id}/assess-fees`. Failure returns `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE` (HTTP 422).
19. **Rung 19** — a new rule row's `effective_from` is strictly later than the `effective_from` of the currently open row for the same `state_code`. Applies to `POST /state-rules`. Failure returns `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).
20. **Rung 20** — no other registration for the same combination of legal entity and `state_code` currently occupies the interval. A row in status `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `DELINQUENT` or `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` blocks outright; a closed row blocks when the new registration's start date is strictly before that row's `closed_on`. Because a registration is created `PENDING` with `qualified_on` still null, **the new registration's start date for this test is the current clock date**, which is why a jurisdiction closed on or before today can be re-entered and one closed with a future `closed_on` cannot. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/registrations` and `POST /legal-entities/{id}/foreign-qualifications`. Failure returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION` (HTTP 409).
21. **Rung 21** — no compliance obligation with the same combination of registration, `obligation_type` and `period_year` and a status other than `VOIDED` already exists. Applies to `POST /registrations/{id}/compliance-obligations`. Failure returns `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBLIGATION` (HTTP 409).
22. **Rung 22** — obligations of type `BOI_INITIAL` and `BOI_UPDATE` may only be created on a registration whose `registration_kind` is `HOME`. Applies to `POST /registrations/{id}/compliance-obligations`. Failure returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
23. **Rung 23** — the home registration of the legal entity is not in status `DELINQUENT`, `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`. The gate looks for the entity's first `HOME` registration; an entity that holds **no** `HOME` registration at all passes this rung rather than failing it. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/foreign-qualifications`. Failure returns `ERR_HOME_STATE_DELINQUENT` (HTTP 409).
24. **Rung 24** — the referenced good-standing certificate is strictly fresh enough against the target jurisdiction's rule. Only the certificate's `issued_on` is consulted: the server does **not** require the certificate to have been drawn from a registration of this entity, nor its `state_code` to match either the home or the target jurisdiction. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/foreign-qualifications`. Failure returns `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STALE` (HTTP 422).
25. **Rung 25** — the referenced name clearance has `result` `AVAILABLE`, or has `result` `CONFLICT` together with a non-null `fallback_assumed_name`. Only the clearance's `result` and `fallback_assumed_name` are consulted: the server does **not** require the clearance to belong to this entity, nor its `state_code` to equal the target `state_code`. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/foreign-qualifications`. Failure returns `ERR_NAME_CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 409).
26. **Rung 26** — when the governing jurisdiction rule has `requires_tax_clearance` true, the request carries a non-blank `tax_clearance_reference`. The governing rule here is the one effective for the registration's `state_code` on the current clock date; when **no** rule row is effective there today the gate is skipped entirely, because there is no published requirement to enforce and no declared error covers that case on these two endpoints. `tax_clearance_reference` itself is an ordinary optional string that may legitimately be blank as far as rung 13 is concerned — it is this rung alone that demands a non-blank value, and it demands it only under a rule that requires clearance. Applies to `POST /registrations/{id}/withdraw` and `POST /registrations/{id}/terminate`. Failure returns `ERR_TAX_CLEARANCE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409).
27. **Rung 27** — every `FOREIGN` registration of the same legal entity is in status `WITHDRAWN` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`. Applies to `POST /registrations/{id}/terminate`. Failure returns `ERR_FOREIGN_QUALIFICATIONS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409).
28. **Rung 28** — every registration of the legal entity is in status `WITHDRAWN`, `TERMINATED` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`; registered-agent service cannot end while any registration is still occupied. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/dissolve`. Failure returns `ERR_ACTIVE_REGISTRATIONS_EXIST` (HTTP 409).
29. **Rung 29** — when `final_report_required` is true, every `FINAL_REPORT` obligation of the entity is in status `SATISFIED` or `WAIVED`. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/dissolve`. Failure returns `ERR_FINAL_REPORT_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409).
30. **Rung 30** — an agent appointment in status `APPOINTED` exists on the registration. The status must be exactly `APPOINTED`: an appointment that has already moved on to `RESIGNATION_NOTICED`, even one still of record inside its notice window, does not satisfy this rung. Applies to `POST /registrations/{id}/activate`. Failure returns `ERR_AGENT_OF_RECORD_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409).
31. **Rung 31** — no agent appointment on the registration is in status `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` with the current clock date strictly earlier than its `resignation_effective_on`. Applies to `POST /registrations/{id}/withdraw` and `POST /registrations/{id}/terminate`. Failure returns `ERR_RESIGNATION_NOTICE_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
32. **Rung 32** — `X-User-ID` differs from the filing's `prepared_by_user_id`; the preparer may not also submit. Applies to `POST /filings/{id}/submit`. Failure returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
33. **Rung 33** — the current clock date is on or before the filing's `refile_window_end`. Applies to `POST /filings/{id}/refile`. Failure returns `ERR_REFILE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).
34. **Rung 34** — the filing's `attempt_number` is strictly less than 3, the maximum number of refile attempts. Applies to `POST /filings/{id}/refile`. Failure returns `ERR_REFILE_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).
35. **Rung 35** — `capture_amount_cents` is less than or equal to the payment's `authorized_amount_cents`. Applies to `POST /payments/{id}/capture`. Failure returns `ERR_CAPTURE_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZATION` (HTTP 402).
36. **Rung 36** — when `target_filing_id` is supplied, that filing belongs to the paying client, is in status `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED`, and has `paid_amount_cents` strictly below its `total_fee_cents`. Applies to `POST /payments/{id}/capture`. Failure returns `ERR_ALLOCATION_TARGET_INVALID` (HTTP 409).
37. **Rung 37** — the filing's `paid_amount_cents` is greater than or equal to its `total_fee_cents`; an underfunded filing cannot be lodged with a jurisdiction. Applies to `POST /filings/{id}/submit`. Failure returns `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CAPTURED_FUNDS` (HTTP 402).
38. **Rung 38** — the current logical clock instant is greater than or equal to the document's `ack_deadline_at`. Applies to `POST /sop-documents/{id}/escalate`. Failure returns `ERR_SOP_ACK_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409).
39. **Rung 39** — the document's `escalation_level` is strictly less than 3, the maximum escalation level. Applies to `POST /sop-documents/{id}/escalate`. Failure returns `ERR_SOP_ESCALATION_CAP_REACHED` (HTTP 409).
40. **Rung 40** — the parent legal entity has `boi_exempt_class` `NONE`; an exempt class suppresses the beneficial-ownership obligation entirely. Applies to `POST /legal-entities/{id}/boi-reports` and `POST /boi-reports/{id}/submit`. On the draft endpoint this rung sits where its ordinal puts it, **after** rung 16: an `UPDATED` report drafted on an exempt entity whose axis is not `UPDATE_DUE` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_BOI_EXEMPT_ENTITY`. Failure returns `ERR_BOI_EXEMPT_ENTITY` (HTTP 409).
41. **Rung 41** — when the parent legal entity has `company_applicant_required` true, `includes_company_applicant` is true. Applies to `POST /boi-reports/{id}/submit`. Failure returns `ERR_COMPANY_APPLICANT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).
42. **Rung 42** — the current clock date is on or before the reinstatement window's closing date. Applies to `POST /registrations/{id}/reinstatement-cases`, `POST /reinstatement-cases/{id}/assess-fees` and `POST /reinstatement-cases/{id}/grant`. The closing date is the registration's `reinstatement_deadline` when a case is opened and the case's own frozen `window_close_date` on the other two; a null closing date fails the rung. On all three endpoints the rung keeps its ordinal position, so rung 16 is evaluated first (the registration must be `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` to open a case; the case must be `OPEN` or `DENIED` to be assessed; the case must be `FILED` and its registration still `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` to be granted), and on the assessment endpoint **rung 18 is evaluated before rung 42** — an assessment made after the window closed in a jurisdiction with no effective rule today reports `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE`. Failure returns `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).

#### 2.9.1. Declared Gate Precedence

For nine operations the relative order of the write gates is fixed explicitly below rather than left to be inferred from rung numbering. In each case the gates are evaluated in the order written, and the first failure is what the caller sees. Two of these orderings are deliberately counter-intuitive and are called out as such.

- **Creating a foreign qualification** (`POST /legal-entities/{id}/foreign-qualifications`) evaluates, in this order: rung 18 `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE`, because a rule row must exist before anything can be priced; then rung 20 `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`, the interval-occupancy check; then rung 23 `ERR_HOME_STATE_DELINQUENT`, the sequencing gate that forbids qualifying outward out of a delinquent home state; then rung 24 `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STALE`, the freshness of the good-standing certificate; and only last rung 25 `ERR_NAME_CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED`, name availability. **Home-state delinquency is checked before certificate freshness**, which inverts the order a reader might assume from the request body, where the certificate is named first. A request that would trip both rung 23 and rung 24 returns `ERR_HOME_STATE_DELINQUENT`.
- **Terminating a home registration** (`POST /registrations/{id}/terminate`) evaluates, in this order: rung 16 `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — which here covers both that the registration must be `ACTIVE` and that an `ACCEPTED` `DISSOLUTION` filing must already exist on it; then rung 26 `ERR_TAX_CLEARANCE_REQUIRED`; then rung 27 `ERR_FOREIGN_QUALIFICATIONS_OUTSTANDING`; then rung 31 `ERR_RESIGNATION_NOTICE_ACTIVE`. **Tax clearance is checked before the foreign-qualification sweep.**
- **Dissolving a legal entity** (`POST /legal-entities/{id}/dissolve`) evaluates, in this order: rung 16 `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since the entity must be `WINDING_DOWN`; then rung 28 `ERR_ACTIVE_REGISTRATIONS_EXIST`; then rung 29 `ERR_FINAL_REPORT_OUTSTANDING`. **The referential sweep over registrations precedes the final-report safety gate here**, deliberately inverting the safety-first ordering used elsewhere: an entity that both still holds an open registration and still owes a final report reports the open registration, not the outstanding report.
- **Capturing a payment** (`POST /payments/{id}/capture`) evaluates, in this order: rung 16 `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since the payment must be `AUTHORIZED`; then rung 35 `ERR_CAPTURE_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZATION`; then rung 36 `ERR_ALLOCATION_TARGET_INVALID`. A request that both over-captures and names an ineligible target returns `ERR_CAPTURE_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZATION`.
- **Submitting a filing** (`POST /filings/{id}/submit`) evaluates, in this order: rung 16 `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since the filing must be `DRAFT`; then rung 32 `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; then rung 37 `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CAPTURED_FUNDS`. Segregation of duties is checked before funding, so a preparer trying to submit an underfunded filing it prepared sees `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
- **Refiling a rejected filing** (`POST /filings/{id}/refile`) evaluates, in this order: rung 16 `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since the filing must be `REJECTED`; then rung 33 `ERR_REFILE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; then rung 34 `ERR_REFILE_LIMIT_REACHED`.
- **Escalating a service-of-process document** (`POST /sop-documents/{id}/escalate`) evaluates, in this order: rung 16 `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since the document must be `FORWARDED` or `ESCALATED`; then rung 38 `ERR_SOP_ACK_NOT_DUE`; then rung 39 `ERR_SOP_ESCALATION_CAP_REACHED`.
- **Submitting a BOI report** (`POST /boi-reports/{id}/submit`) evaluates, in this order: rung 16 `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since the report must be `DRAFT`; then rung 40 `ERR_BOI_EXEMPT_ENTITY`; then rung 41 `ERR_COMPANY_APPLICANT_REQUIRED`.
- **Creating a compliance obligation** (`POST /registrations/{id}/compliance-obligations`) evaluates, in this order: rung 18 `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE`; then rung 21 `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBLIGATION`; then rung 22 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` for a beneficial-ownership obligation type requested on a non-home registration. Rung 15 does not apply to this operation at all, because the operation takes no version field.

### 2.10. Nested Path Resolution

Paths are resolved strictly left to right. When a path names a parent and then a child, the parent segment is checked first and its own not-found error is returned before the child segment is examined at all. A request naming a non-existent client and a non-existent legal entity under it returns `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, never `ERR_LEGAL_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`. This ordering is already encoded in the ladder: rung 4 precedes rung 5, which precedes rung 6, which precedes rung 7, and rung 11 — which covers the remaining resource types and the body-supplied references — comes after all of them, so a body reference to a missing row is never reported before a missing path segment.

Existence is also scoped in principle. A child addressed under a parent must actually belong to that parent; a child row that exists but hangs off a different parent would be treated as not found under the addressed path and would produce the parent-appropriate not-found error for the child's own type. In practice this rule never fires on the declared surface, because **no path in Section 4 carries two identifiers**: every path holds at most one `{id}` segment, and a nested path such as `/registrations/{id}/compliance-obligations` names the parent only, with the child either created or listed rather than addressed. The left-to-right ordering above is therefore a statement about the single addressed segment and the body references that follow it.

### 2.11. Delinquency Ranking and Portfolio Rollups

Compliance health is expressed as an integer rank so that it can be rolled up by taking a maximum. The five ranks are: current is 0, due-soon is 1, in-grace is 2, delinquent is 3, and administratively-dissolved is 4. Mapping back the other way, rank 0 reads `CURRENT`, rank 1 reads `DUE_SOON`, rank 2 reads `IN_GRACE`, rank 3 reads `DELINQUENT` and rank 4 reads `ADMIN_DISSOLVED`.

**Per registration.** A registration's `delinquency_rank` is recomputed after every compliance-obligation status change and on every ops-run. If the registration's own `status` is `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`, the rank is 4 regardless of what its obligations say — and because `CLOSED_PERMANENT` is terminal, a permanently closed registration reads rank 4 for ever, which keeps its entity's `compliance_posture` and its client's `portfolio_delinquency_status` at `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` for ever too. The rank is derived from the obligations alone on every other status, so it is **not** a proxy for the registration's own status in either direction: a `PENDING` registration carrying a delinquent obligation reads rank 3 while its `status` is still `PENDING`, because only an `ACTIVE` registration is driven to `DELINQUENT`, and a `WITHDRAWN` or `TERMINATED` registration reads rank 0 because closure voided everything it owed. Otherwise the server gathers the registration's obligations whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `DUE_SOON`, `IN_GRACE` or `DELINQUENT` — the four **open** statuses — and ignores everything in `SATISFIED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED`. If no open obligation remains, the rank is 0. Otherwise each open obligation contributes a rank of 0 for `SCHEDULED`, 1 for `DUE_SOON`, 2 for `IN_GRACE` and 3 for `DELINQUENT`, and the registration's rank is the largest of those contributions.

**Per legal entity.** An entity's `compliance_posture` is the rank-to-status reading of the largest `delinquency_rank` across all of that entity's registrations, taken over registrations in any status. An entity with no registrations at all reads `CURRENT`.

**Per client.** A client's `portfolio_delinquency_status` is the rank-to-status reading of the largest `delinquency_rank` across all registrations of all of that client's legal entities, again over registrations in any status, and a client with no registrations reads `CURRENT`. Alongside it, `portfolio_open_obligation_count` is the number of obligations in one of the four open statuses across every registration of every legal entity of the client; `portfolio_outstanding_cents` is the sum of `total_due_cents` over exactly those same rows; and `portfolio_entity_count` is the number of the client's legal entities whose `lifecycle_status` is anything other than `DISSOLVED`.

Two further client-level rollups track service of process. `sop_open_escalation_count` is the number of escalation events whose service-of-process document is still in status `FORWARDED` or `ESCALATED`, counted across every registration of the client. `portfolio_risk_flag` is true exactly when `sop_open_escalation_count` has reached 2 or more, and false below that. And `credit_balance_cents` is the client's account credit, equal at all times to the sum of `refunded_amount_cents` over the client's payments; it starts at 0 and can never go negative.

`sop_open_escalation_count` is derived rather than incremented: it is recomputed from scratch on every rollup pass as the number of `SopEscalationEvent` rows whose document is currently in `FORWARDED` or `ESCALATED`, so a document that leaves that pair — by acknowledgment, by rejection as not our entity, or by any other route — takes every event it ever raised out of the count at once, and a document re-forwarded out of `ESCALATED` brings them all back in.

Every one of these rollups is recomputed at the end of every mutation that touches an obligation, a registration or a legal entity — not lazily on read, and not only on the ops-run. When an obligation changes status, the chain runs upward: the registration's rank, then the entity's posture, then the client's rollup. Recomputation writes the rollup fields in place and, as Section 2.4 states, moves no `version` and refreshes no `updated_at` on the rows it rewrites.

### 2.12. Cascades, Side Effects and Version Propagation

Many operations in this system mutate more than the resource they address. Wherever this document says an operation drives a second resource to a new state, that second resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1, exactly as if it had been written directly. A caller that holds a version of a parent must therefore expect that version to move as a consequence of writes aimed at children, which is precisely why the three optimistic-concurrency endpoints of Section 2.8 exist.

The deepest chain in the system runs five hops, and it begins when a jurisdiction's acceptance of a filing is recorded. Hop one moves the `Filing` from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`. Hop two, taken only when that filing carries a `compliance_obligation_id`, drives the referenced obligation to `SATISFIED`. Hop three suppresses every still-pending reminder notice of that obligation. Hop four, taken only when the obligation's registration is in status `DELINQUENT` and no obligation on that registration remains in status `DELINQUENT` after hop two, returns the registration from `DELINQUENT` to `ACTIVE`. Hop five recomputes the owning legal entity's `compliance_posture` and then the owning client's portfolio rollup. Each hop's condition is checked independently: a filing with no obligation stops after hop one; an obligation whose registration was never delinquent stops after hop three; a registration that still has another delinquent obligation stops after hop three as well, with the registration left in `DELINQUENT`. A hop that does not fire leaves the corresponding resource entirely untouched — no state change, no `updated_at` refresh and no version bump. Three details of the chain are pinned. Hop two carries **no precondition on the obligation's own current status**: the referenced obligation is driven to `SATISFIED` from wherever it stood, terminal statuses included, so an acceptance recorded against a filing whose obligation was meanwhile waived or voided re-terminates that row as `SATISFIED` and re-stamps its `satisfied_on`. Hop three is the same suppression Section 3.6 describes and therefore reaches notices in `SENT` as well as notices in `PENDING`, each moving to `SUPPRESSED` with its own version bumped by 1. Hop five writes only rollups, so it moves no version at all; every other hop bumps the version of each row it moves exactly once, no matter how many fields that hop writes on it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Portfolios, Legal Entities and the Two Independent State Axes

A portfolio begins with a client. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying a `legal_name` — non-empty and at most 200 characters — and a `billing_email`, which must be non-empty but is otherwise unconstrained: no address syntax is checked and no length ceiling applies. Nothing else is accepted, and anything else supplied is ignored. The new client is created with every rollup field at its declared starting value: `credit_balance_cents` 0, `portfolio_entity_count` 0, `portfolio_open_obligation_count` 0, `portfolio_outstanding_cents` 0, `portfolio_delinquency_status` `CURRENT`, `sop_open_escalation_count` 0 and `portfolio_risk_flag` false.

Reading a client returns those rollups directly. A separate portfolio summary view is also available, and it is a computed multi-state projection rather than a stored row: it reports `client_id`, `entity_count` (the count of the client's legal entities that are not `DISSOLVED`, the same figure as `portfolio_entity_count`), `registration_count` (the number of registration rows the client holds across all of its entities, counted in every status), `open_obligation_count` and `outstanding_cents` (the same two figures as `portfolio_open_obligation_count` and `portfolio_outstanding_cents`), `delinquency_status` (the same reading as `portfolio_delinquency_status`), `worst_state_code` (the `state_code` of the registration carrying the highest `delinquency_rank` in the portfolio, or null when the client holds no registrations at all), and `credit_balance_cents`. The per-state figures behind this view must sum to the client-level totals — that is the point of the endpoint. Two readings are pinned here. `registration_count` is taken over registrations **in every status**, terminal rows included, exactly as written, and it is the same population `worst_state_code` and `delinquency_status` are drawn from. Where two or more registrations tie at the highest `delinquency_rank`, `worst_state_code` is taken from the **first of them by the document's default list ordering** — ascending `created_at`, then insertion order — so the tie resolves to the earliest-created registration at that rank. The summary is a bare projection of those eight keys: it carries no `id`, no audit fields and no `version` of its own, and `delinquency_status` is recomputed from the registrations at read time rather than copied from the stored `portfolio_delinquency_status`, though the two always agree.

A `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` then creates legal entities under the client; the client's `id` is carried by the path, and the created entity records it as `client_id`. Creation requires a `legal_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), an `entity_type` drawn from `LLC`, `CORP`, `LP`, `LLP` and `NONPROFIT`, a `home_state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the formation jurisdiction, and a `formation_date` as an ISO date. Seven further fields are optional and take concrete server-side defaults when omitted: `fiscal_year_end_month` defaults to 12 and must lie between 1 and 12; `fiscal_year_end_day` defaults to 31, must lie between 1 and 31, and is clamped downward to the length of whatever month it is applied to; `authorized_shares` defaults to 0 and may not be negative; `issued_shares` defaults to 0, may not be negative, and may not exceed `authorized_shares`; `total_gross_assets_cents` defaults to 0 and may not be negative; `annual_gross_receipts_cents` defaults to 0 and may not be negative; and `boi_exempt_class` defaults to `NONE` and is drawn from `NONE`, `LARGE_OPERATING`, `PUBLICLY_TRADED`, `BANK` and `TAX_EXEMPT`. The `issued_shares` ceiling is enforced against the effective `authorized_shares` of the same request and is a rung-13 failure, so a body that both breaches it and carries an undeclared `entity_type` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Creating an entity recomputes the owning client's `portfolio_entity_count` — a rollup write, so the client's own `version` and `updated_at` do not move.

An entity carries **two state axes that evolve entirely independently of one another**. The first is `lifecycle_status`, which begins at `DRAFT` and runs `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `WINDING_DOWN` and `DISSOLVED`, with `DISSOLVED` terminal. The second is `boi_status`, the federal beneficial-ownership axis, which begins at `PENDING_INITIAL` and takes values `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING_INITIAL`, `FILED`, `UPDATE_DUE` and `DELINQUENT`; it is described in Section 3.10. A change on one axis never moves the other except at dissolution, where the lifecycle axis reaching `DISSOLVED` forces the BOI axis to `NOT_REQUIRED`.

At creation the server also derives `company_applicant_required`, which is true exactly when `formation_date` falls on or after the company-applicant cutover date of `2024-01-01` and false otherwise, and derives the entity's initial beneficial-ownership deadline. `compliance_posture` starts at `CURRENT`, `final_report_required` starts at false and `boi_update_deadline` starts null. The BOI axis starts at `PENDING_INITIAL` **whatever `boi_exempt_class` the creation request carried**: an entity created already exempt is born `PENDING_INITIAL` with a null `boi_initial_deadline`, not `NOT_REQUIRED`, because the edge into `NOT_REQUIRED` described in Section 3.10 is a transition driven by a later update rather than a creation-time reading.

**Activation.** A `ComplianceOfficer` moves an entity from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`. Two conditions must hold: the entity's `lifecycle_status` must currently be `DRAFT`, and at least one registration of `registration_kind` `HOME` must already exist for the entity. If either fails, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and the entity is left exactly as it was. On success the entity's `lifecycle_status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `compliance_posture` is recomputed from the registration rank rollup, the entity's `version` increases by 1, and the owning client's `portfolio_entity_count` and `portfolio_delinquency_status` are recomputed.

**Mutable attributes.** A `PlatformAdmin` or `ComplianceOfficer` may update `legal_name`, `fiscal_year_end_month`, `fiscal_year_end_day`, `authorized_shares`, `issued_shares`, `total_gross_assets_cents`, `annual_gross_receipts_cents` and `boi_exempt_class`. Every one of them is optional on the update; fields not supplied are left untouched. Two of them have consequences beyond their own column. Changing `legal_name` is a beneficial-ownership reportable change and arms the update clock described in Section 3.10 — but only when the supplied name actually **differs** from the one on file; re-sending the same name arms nothing. Changing `boi_exempt_class` moves the BOI axis, and likewise only when the supplied class differs from the one on file. When one request does both, the exempt-class change is applied first and the name-triggered arming second, so a request that both exempts the entity and renames it ends with the deadlines the exemption cleared, not with a re-armed update clock. Any accepted update increases the entity's `version` by exactly 1 — one request is one mutation, so axis movement inside it adds no second bump, and a request that supplies nothing at all, or supplies only values the row already held, still bumps that one. Note that `home_state_code`, `formation_date`, `entity_type` and `client_id` are not updatable; the fiscal-year-end and share fields are, and a later calendar rebuild will read the current values of `fiscal_year_end_month` and `fiscal_year_end_day` rather than the values that were in place when an obligation was first scheduled.

Activation is otherwise inert: it writes no date onto the entity, does not touch the registrations it required, and leaves `boi_status`, `final_report_required` and both beneficial-ownership deadlines exactly as they were. Listing a client's legal entities accepts an optional `lifecycle_status` filter alongside `limit` and `offset`. Reading a single legal entity returns both state axes together — `lifecycle_status` and `boi_status` — alongside `boi_initial_deadline`, `boi_update_deadline`, `company_applicant_required`, `compliance_posture` and `final_report_required`. Reads and lists take no request body; their only failure modes are the header, role, path-existence and cross-client rungs of Section 2.9.

### 3.2. Occupying a Jurisdiction

An entity is only lawfully present in a jurisdiction while it holds a registration there. Registrations come in two kinds, distinguished by `registration_kind`: exactly one `HOME` registration in the formation state, and any number of `FOREIGN` registrations elsewhere. A registration is created in status `PENDING` and moves through `ACTIVE`, and from there to `DELINQUENT` and back, or onward to `ADMIN_DISSOLVED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `TERMINATED` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`. The last three are terminal. Each registration records the `legal_entity_id` it belongs to and the `state_code` it occupies, and it exposes `qualified_on` (the ISO date it became `ACTIVE`, null while `PENDING`), `closed_on` (the ISO date it left the occupancy interval by reaching `WITHDRAWN`, `TERMINATED` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`, and null otherwise), `agent_of_record_id`, `admin_dissolved_on`, `reinstatement_deadline`, `delinquency_rank`, `sop_priority_hold` and `assumed_name`. Three of those are recorded for the reader and consulted by no check anywhere in the system: `qualified_on`, `assumed_name` and `sop_priority_hold` are written and re-evaluated as described below, but no gate, no derivation and no rollup ever reads them, so no operation succeeds or fails because of their value. The occupancy interval a registration holds is half-open: it starts on `qualified_on` and runs up to but not including `closed_on`.

**The home registration.** A `ComplianceOfficer` creates the home registration directly from the entity; no body fields are accepted at all. The new row takes `registration_kind` `HOME` and `state_code` equal to the entity's `home_state_code`, is created in `PENDING` with `name_clearance_id` null and `assumed_name` null, and the governing jurisdiction rule is resolved for the current clock date — if no rule row is effective for that state on that date, the creation fails with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE`. That resolved rule is used for nothing but the existence test; the home registration stores no reference to it. The interval-occupancy check of rung 20 applies here too, and in the same order as on a foreign qualification — rule availability first, occupancy second: an entity may not open a second registration in a state it already occupies, and may not backdate one into a closed interval. The entity's `lifecycle_status` is not consulted, so a home registration can be created against an entity in any lifecycle state, and no limit forbids a second `HOME` registration in a different state than `home_state_code` — the state is always taken from `home_state_code`, so the occupancy check alone bounds it.

**Name clearance.** Before qualifying into a new state, the officer opens a name-availability search there. The search records the target `state_code`, the `requested_name` submitted for search, and optionally a `fallback_assumed_name` — a doing-business-as name to fall back on if the requested name collides. When `fallback_assumed_name` is omitted it is stored as null. The clearance is created with `result` `PENDING`, `conflicting_name` null and `resolved_name` null. A `FilingAgent` later records the jurisdiction's answer by supplying a `result` of `AVAILABLE` or `CONFLICT`, optionally accompanied by the `conflicting_name` already on file in that jurisdiction, which is meaningful only alongside a `CONFLICT` result. Recording the result also resolves the usable name: `resolved_name` becomes the `requested_name` when the result is `AVAILABLE`; it becomes the `fallback_assumed_name` when the result is `CONFLICT` and a fallback was supplied; and it is set to null in every other case, which in practice means a `CONFLICT` with no fallback and the pass-through case of a result re-recorded as `PENDING` — that third member is accepted by the endpoint, since the `result` field's declared enumeration is the same three values the field itself takes. `conflicting_name` is stored verbatim whenever the request supplies it, whatever the accompanying result, and is left exactly as it was when the request omits it: it is never cleared, and nothing anywhere reads it. Re-recording is permitted from any current `result`, and each recording overwrites `resolved_name` from scratch. Recording a result increases the clearance's `version` by 1. Clearances can be listed for an entity with optional `state_code` and `result` filters.

**Certificates of good standing.** A certificate is a record of a document the jurisdiction issued, drawn from a registration the entity already holds. A `ComplianceOfficer` records one against a registration by supplying the `issued_on` date printed on the certificate and its `certificate_number`, which must be non-empty. The server copies the parent registration's `state_code` onto the certificate as `state_code` and stores `registration_id`. Neither the registration's status nor its `state_code` is otherwise checked, and `issued_on` may be any parseable ISO date, past or future — a future `issued_on` yields a negative age and is therefore always fresh. A certificate row is never mutated: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

**Foreign qualification.** Qualifying into a new state is where the interesting gating lives. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies the target `state_code`, a `name_clearance_id` and a `good_standing_certificate_id`; all three are required. The gates run in the declared order of Section 2.9.1 — rule availability, then interval occupancy, then home-state standing, then certificate freshness, then name resolution.

Home-state standing means this: the entity's `HOME` registration must not be in status `DELINQUENT`, `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`. A firm that has fallen behind where it was formed may not expand into a new jurisdiction, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_HOME_STATE_DELINQUENT`.

Certificate freshness is measured **strictly** and against the **target** jurisdiction's rule, not the home jurisdiction's. The server takes the rule effective for the target `state_code` on the current clock date, computes the certificate's age in days as the current clock date minus its `issued_on`, and accepts the certificate only when that age is strictly less than the target rule's `good_standing_freshness_days`. An age exactly equal to `good_standing_freshness_days` is stale and is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STALE`; with the default freshness of 30 days, an age of 29 days is the oldest certificate accepted. "Within N days" in this system always means strictly fewer than N.

Name resolution means the referenced clearance either carries `result` `AVAILABLE`, or carries `result` `CONFLICT` together with a non-null `fallback_assumed_name`. A clearance still `PENDING`, or a `CONFLICT` with no fallback, is rejected with `ERR_NAME_CONFLICT_UNRESOLVED`.

On success the server creates a registration in `PENDING` with `registration_kind` `FOREIGN`, `state_code` set to the requested target, and `name_clearance_id` set to the referenced clearance. When the referenced clearance's result was `CONFLICT`, the clearance's `resolved_name` is copied onto the registration as `assumed_name`; when the result was `AVAILABLE`, `assumed_name` stays null. Nothing later reads `assumed_name`. The registration list endpoint can be filtered by `legal_entity_id`, `name_clearance_id`, `state_code` and `status`, and reading a single registration returns its `agent_of_record_id` and `delinquency_rank` alongside every other field named above. The certificates drawn from one registration can likewise be listed, and because a certificate has no `updated_at`, that list is ordered by `created_at` ascending like any other.

**Activation.** A `FilingAgent` activates a `PENDING` registration. The registration must be in `PENDING`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and an agent appointment in status `APPOINTED` must already exist on it, or the request is rejected with `ERR_AGENT_OF_RECORD_REQUIRED`. On success the status becomes `ACTIVE`, `qualified_on` is stamped with the current clock date, the agent-of-record selection is re-run so that `agent_of_record_id` and every sibling appointment's `of_record` are refreshed, `delinquency_rank` is recomputed, the registration's `version` increases by 1, and the entity's `compliance_posture` and then the client's `portfolio_delinquency_status` are recomputed.

### 3.3. The Agent of Record and the Bounded Overlap Window

A registration is only lawful while some provider holds the registered-agent appointment for it. Appointments are proposed, accepted, sometimes rejected, sometimes transferred out when a successor takes over, and sometimes wound down through a statutory resignation notice. An appointment carries the `registration_id` it belongs to, the `provider_name` of record under it (which must be non-empty), the `appointed_on` ISO date on which it takes effect, its `status`, and three derived or system-written fields: `superseded_by_appointment_id`, `resignation_noticed_on` and `resignation_effective_on`, plus the boolean `of_record`.

**Proposing.** A `ComplianceOfficer` proposes an appointment against a registration, supplying `provider_name`, `appointed_on` and the mandatory `expected_registration_version`. The new appointment is created in status `PROPOSED` with `superseded_by_appointment_id` null, `resignation_noticed_on` null, `resignation_effective_on` null and `of_record` false, and the parent registration's `version` increases by 1. A stale `expected_registration_version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before anything is created. Proposing is otherwise ungated: the registration's own status is not consulted, `appointed_on` may be any parseable ISO date whether past or future and is never compared to the clock at proposal time, and any number of proposals may sit open on one registration at once. The agent-of-record selection is not re-run by a proposal, because a `PROPOSED` row is never a candidate.

**Accepting.** A `FilingAgent` accepts a proposal, moving it `PROPOSED → APPOINTED`. Two conditions must hold: the appointment must currently be `PROPOSED`, and the parent registration must be in status `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`. If either fails the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and nothing is written. On success, any sibling appointment on the same registration that was in status `APPOINTED` is moved to `TRANSFERRED_OUT` and has its `superseded_by_appointment_id` set to the accepted appointment's `id` and its `of_record` set to false; that sibling's own `version` increases by 1 as well. The agent-of-record selection is then re-run across the registration, refreshing `agent_of_record_id` on the registration and `of_record` on every appointment. The accepted appointment's `version` increases by 1 and the registration's `version` increases by 1. Where several siblings were somehow in `APPOINTED` at once, every one of them is transferred out by the single acceptance. Because `superseded_by_appointment_id` points from the outgoing appointment to its successor, the appointment list endpoint's `superseded_by_appointment_id` filter walks the transfer chain; it also accepts `registration_id` and `status` filters. That list is estate-wide and is not narrowed by client, since `ClientContact` is not among its permitted roles. Reading a single appointment returns `of_record` and `resignation_effective_on` alongside its other fields.

**Rejecting.** A `FilingAgent` may instead reject a proposal, moving it `PROPOSED → REJECTED`. The only condition is that the appointment is currently `PROPOSED`; from any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Rejection touches nothing else: no sibling changes, no registration change, no selector re-run. The appointment's `version` increases by 1.

**Resignation notice.** A `ComplianceOfficer` gives statutory notice on an appointment, moving it `APPOINTED → RESIGNATION_NOTICED`. The appointment must currently be `APPOINTED` **and** must currently be the one of record — `of_record` true — or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success, `resignation_noticed_on` is stamped with the current clock date and `resignation_effective_on` is computed as that date plus 30 calendar days, the statutory notice period. The appointment stays of record for the whole notice window. The appointment's `version` and the registration's `version` each increase by 1.

**Which appointment is of record.** The selection is recomputed on every appointment transition, on registration activation, and on every ops-run. The candidate set for a registration is every appointment on it whose `status` is `APPOINTED` or `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` and whose notice window has not yet closed — that is, whose `resignation_effective_on` is either null or strictly later than the current clock date. Appointments in `PROPOSED`, `RESIGNED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT` or `REJECTED` are never candidates. Candidates are ranked first by a priority in which a `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` appointment outranks an `APPOINTED` one, then by `appointed_on` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and finally by insertion order where even that ties. The first candidate under that ranking is the winner: the registration's `agent_of_record_id` is set to the winner's `id` and `of_record` is set true on the winner and false on every other appointment of that registration — including appointments in `PROPOSED`, `RESIGNED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT` and `REJECTED`, which the selection re-writes to false without bumping their versions. If the candidate set is empty, `agent_of_record_id` becomes null and no appointment is of record; the selection itself raises no error in that case. Re-running the selection is a rollup-style write: on its own it moves no `version` and refreshes no `updated_at`, on either the appointments or the registration. Versions move only where the transition that provoked the re-run says they do — which is why the ops-run's closing re-derivation of the agent of record across the whole estate can flip `of_record` and `agent_of_record_id` silently, with no version bump and with no counter reporting it.

That priority ordering is what creates the **bounded overlap window**. A provider who has given notice stays of record for the entire 30-day window even after a successor with a later `appointed_on` has already been accepted, because the resigning appointment outranks the successor. The comparison against `resignation_effective_on` is strict: on `resignation_effective_on` itself the resigning appointment is no longer a candidate, so the successor becomes of record on exactly that day. For every registration in status `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`, exactly one appointment is of record before a provider transfer and exactly one is of record after it — that count is a conserved quantity of the transfer.

**Retirement of a notice.** The ops-run checkpoint retires appointments whose notice period has elapsed. An appointment in `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` whose `resignation_effective_on` is on or before the current clock date moves `RESIGNATION_NOTICED → RESIGNED` with `of_record` set false; the registration's `agent_of_record_id` is then re-derived by re-running the selection, and both the appointment's `version` and the registration's `version` increase by 1. An appointment whose `resignation_effective_on` has not yet arrived is left untouched by the sweep — no status change, no version bump.

**Closure of the registration.** When a registration leaves `ACTIVE` for `WITHDRAWN` or `TERMINATED`, every appointment on it whose status is `PROPOSED`, `APPOINTED` or `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` is moved to `RESIGNED` with `of_record` set false, the registration's `agent_of_record_id` is set to null, and each affected appointment's `version` increases by 1. Appointments already in `RESIGNED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT` or `REJECTED` are not touched. Note that a registration cannot in fact be withdrawn or terminated while a notice window is still open, because rung 31 rejects that with `ERR_RESIGNATION_NOTICE_ACTIVE`; the sweep's `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` branch therefore only bites on a notice whose window has closed but which the ops-run has not yet retired.

### 3.4. The Compliance Calendar: Deriving Dates

Each statutory duty a registration owes for a reporting period is one calendar row. A `ComplianceOfficer` creates one against a registration by supplying just two things: an `obligation_type` drawn from `ANNUAL_REPORT`, `FRANCHISE_TAX`, `BOI_INITIAL`, `BOI_UPDATE` and `FINAL_REPORT`, and a `period_year`, an integer between 2020 and 2039 inclusive naming the reporting period the duty covers. The operation takes no version field and therefore never returns `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

Duplicate protection is on the triple of registration, `obligation_type` and `period_year`: if a row with that same triple already exists in any status other than `VOIDED`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBLIGATION`. A voided row does not block, so a calendar entry that was voided when a registration closed can be re-created if the registration later reopens. Beneficial-ownership obligations are further restricted: `BOI_INITIAL` and `BOI_UPDATE` may only be created on a registration whose `registration_kind` is `HOME`, and an attempt on a `FOREIGN` registration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The first thing creation does is **pin the rule**. The determination date is the current logical clock date; it is stored on the row as `rule_determination_date`, and the rule version selected for the registration's `state_code` at that date has its `id` stored as `governing_rule_id`. Every date and every money figure on the row is then derived under that pinned version, and every later re-derivation of this row uses the same pinned version — never the version that happens to be open at the time of the re-derivation. If no rule row is effective for that state on that date, the creation fails with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE` and nothing is written. `rule_determination_date` is written once for the reader's benefit and is consulted by nothing thereafter — every re-derivation reaches the pinned version through `governing_rule_id`. Obligations can be listed with `registration_id`, `governing_rule_id`, `obligation_type` and `status` filters, so an operator can see exactly which rule version each row was pinned to, and reading a single obligation returns its full money breakdown — `state_fee_cents`, `franchise_tax_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `service_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents` — alongside `due_date`, `grace_end_date`, `delinquency_date`, `rule_determination_date`, `status` and `satisfied_on`.

**The due date.** The same derivation produces `due_date` for every obligation type; the type does not branch the formula, only the pinned rule's `annual_report_basis` does. This holds for `BOI_INITIAL`, `BOI_UPDATE` and `FINAL_REPORT` exactly as it does for `ANNUAL_REPORT` and `FRANCHISE_TAX`: **a beneficial-ownership obligation's `due_date` comes from the jurisdiction rule's formula, not from the entity's federal `boi_initial_deadline` or `boi_update_deadline`**, which belong to the separate axis of Section 3.10 and are never consulted here. A raw date is computed first and is then rolled forward to the next business day as described in Section 2.5.

- When the pinned rule's `annual_report_basis` is `ANNIVERSARY_MONTH_DAY`, the month is the month of the entity's `formation_date` and the day is the day of the entity's `formation_date`, reduced to the last day of that month in the obligation's `period_year` if the formation day exceeds it. The raw date is that month and day in `period_year`. A company formed on 31 January therefore lands on 28 or 29 February in a jurisdiction whose anniversary month is February, never on a non-existent 31 February.
- When the basis is `FIXED_CALENDAR_DATE`, the entity's `formation_date` is ignored entirely. The month is the rule's `fixed_due_month` and the day is the rule's `fixed_due_day`, again reduced to the last day of that month in `period_year` if it exceeds it. Every company on that jurisdiction's rolls therefore shares one due date.
- When the basis is `FISCAL_YEAR_END_OFFSET`, the month is the entity's `fiscal_year_end_month` and the day is the entity's `fiscal_year_end_day`, reduced to the last day of that month in `period_year` if it exceeds it; the rule's `fiscal_offset_days` calendar days are then added to that date. Two companies in the *same* jurisdiction get *different* due dates under this basis, because each carries its own fiscal year end.

Whichever branch produced the raw date, the final `due_date` is that raw date rolled forward past weekends and observed holidays.

**The grace window.** `grace_end_date` is derived from `due_date` under the pinned rule's `grace_basis`, and the two branches behave differently on purpose.

- Under `CALENDAR_DAYS`, `grace_end_date` is simply `due_date` plus the rule's `grace_days` calendar days. The result is **not** rolled: it may legitimately fall on a Saturday, a Sunday or an observed holiday.
- Under `BUSINESS_DAYS`, the server starts at `due_date` and repeats the following step exactly `grace_days` times: advance the working date by one calendar day, then roll it forward past weekends and observed holidays. The final working date is `grace_end_date`, and it is therefore always a business day. Whenever the window spans a weekend, this branch yields a strictly later date than the calendar branch would for the same `grace_days`.

A `grace_days` of 0 leaves `grace_end_date` equal to `due_date` under either basis. The boundary matters: a filing recorded **on** `grace_end_date` is still inside grace and carries no lateness at all. The first late day is the day after `grace_end_date`.

**The dissolution horizon.** `delinquency_date` is `grace_end_date` plus the pinned rule's `delinquency_to_dissolution_days` calendar days, with no weekend or holiday roll. It is the earliest date on which the jurisdiction may administratively dissolve the registration over this obligation.

**The status ladder.** An obligation is created in `SCHEDULED` and advances through `DUE_SOON`, `IN_GRACE` and `DELINQUENT`, or leaves the ladder for one of the three terminal statuses `SATISFIED`, `WAIVED` and `VOIDED`. Advancement happens on the ops-run and is **monotone**: the server computes a target rung from the clock and then takes whichever of the current rung and the target rung is further along the ladder, so an obligation can never be pulled back to an earlier rung. The target is `DELINQUENT` when the current clock date is strictly after `grace_end_date`; otherwise `IN_GRACE` when the clock date is strictly after `due_date`; otherwise `DUE_SOON` when the clock date is on or after `due_date` minus 30 calendar days; otherwise `SCHEDULED`. Concretely: on `due_date` minus exactly 30 days the row is `DUE_SOON` because that comparison is inclusive; on `due_date` itself the row is still `DUE_SOON` because the step to `IN_GRACE` is strict; on `grace_end_date` itself the row is `IN_GRACE` because the step to `DELINQUENT` is strict; and on the day after `grace_end_date` the row is `DELINQUENT`. Because the ladder is monotone, a clock jump that crosses several rungs in one step lands the row on the furthest rung reached and the intermediate rungs are skipped and never revisited — a `SCHEDULED` row whose `grace_end_date` is already behind the new clock date goes straight to `DELINQUENT`. Terminal rows are skipped by the advance entirely.

Every advance recomputes the row's `late_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents`, increases the row's `version` by 1, and then recomputes the registration's `delinquency_rank`, the entity's `compliance_posture` and the client's rollup. A row that does not move rungs on a given ops-run is left untouched: no version bump, no re-pricing — so a row that is already `DELINQUENT` is **never re-priced by a later ops-run**, however much further past its grace end the clock has travelled, and its `late_fee_cents` stays frozen at whatever the advance into `DELINQUENT` computed until a filing is drafted against it or a reinstatement assessment recomputes a figure of its own. Reaching `DELINQUENT` additionally drives the parent registration from `ACTIVE` to `DELINQUENT`, as described in Section 3.11; the advance never drives a registration in any other status, and the ladder itself never runs the cure hop in the other direction.

### 3.5. The Compliance Calendar: Pricing a Period

Every calendar row carries four money components — `state_fee_cents`, `franchise_tax_cents`, `late_fee_cents` and `service_fee_cents` — and a `total_due_cents` that is exactly their sum. Each component is rounded to whole cents by its own rule before the sum is taken; the total is never produced by adding unrounded parts.

**The state filing fee.** `state_fee_cents` is drawn from the pinned rule and depends only on `obligation_type`: for `ANNUAL_REPORT` and for `FINAL_REPORT` it is the rule's `annual_report_fee_cents`; for `FRANCHISE_TAX` it is the rule's `franchise_tax_filing_fee_cents`; and for `BOI_INITIAL` and `BOI_UPDATE` it is 0, because the beneficial-ownership registry charges no state filing fee.

**The franchise tax.** `franchise_tax_cents` is 0 for every obligation whose `obligation_type` is anything other than `FRANCHISE_TAX`. For a franchise-tax obligation, the pinned rule's `franchise_tax_basis` selects one of four computations.

- Under `FLAT`, the tax is the rule's `flat_franchise_tax_cents`, one number for every company on the rolls regardless of size.
- Under `AUTHORIZED_SHARES_TIERS`, the rule's `authorized_shares_tiers` are considered in ascending order of `up_to_shares`, with any tier whose `up_to_shares` is null placed last. The server walks that ordered list and returns the `tax_cents` of the first tier whose `up_to_shares` is null or is greater than or equal to the entity's `authorized_shares`. A company holding exactly the tier's ceiling stays inside that tier. If the list is empty, or no tier matches, the tax is 0. The entity's `issued_shares` and asset figures are ignored entirely on this basis.
- Under `ASSUMED_PAR_VALUE`, the server first computes an assumed par capital: if the entity's `authorized_shares` is 0 the assumed par capital is 0, otherwise it is the entity's `total_gross_assets_cents` multiplied by its `issued_shares` and divided by its `authorized_shares`, **rounded half up to a whole cent at that point**. The raw tax is then the assumed par capital multiplied by the rule's `assumed_par_value_rate_bps` and divided by 10000, again rounded half up to a whole cent. Finally the raw tax is clamped from below by the rule's `min_franchise_tax_cents` and from above by its `max_franchise_tax_cents`: a raw tax below the minimum is raised to the minimum, and one above the maximum is reduced to the maximum. The rounding order is pinned — the assumed par capital is rounded first, then the tax is rounded, then the clamp is applied — and the clamp bites on this basis alone.
- Under `GROSS_RECEIPTS_BRACKETS`, the rule's `gross_receipts_brackets` are considered in ascending order of `up_to_cents`, with any bracket whose `up_to_cents` is null placed last, and the server returns the `tax_cents` of the first bracket whose `up_to_cents` is null or is greater than or equal to the entity's `annual_gross_receipts_cents`. If the list is empty, or no bracket matches, the tax is 0. Share counts are ignored entirely on this basis.

Under `ASSUMED_PAR_VALUE`, `GROSS_RECEIPTS_BRACKETS` and `AUTHORIZED_SHARES_TIERS` alike the entity figures read are the entity's **current** ones at the moment of the computation, not values snapshotted when the obligation was created; only the *rule* is pinned. That matters at reinstatement assessment, where the franchise tax of a long-standing period is recomputed from today's share and asset figures under that period's own rule version.

**The late fee.** `late_fee_cents` is recomputed on every ops-run and again whenever a filing is created against the obligation. The number of days late is the assessment date — the logical clock date at the moment of the recomputation — minus `grace_end_date`, floored at zero, so that a recomputation on or before `grace_end_date` yields zero days late. When the days late is zero, the late fee is 0 and no branch is evaluated. Otherwise the pinned rule's `late_fee_style` selects one of three computations.

- Under `FLAT`, the fee is the rule's `late_fee_flat_cents`, and day 1 costs exactly what day 400 costs.
- Under `PER_MONTH`, the days late are divided by 30 and rounded **up** to the next whole number of monthly blocks, and the fee is that number of blocks multiplied by the rule's `late_fee_per_month_cents`. One day late already buys a full block; thirty days late is still one block; thirty-one days late is two. This accrual is unbounded and grows without limit.
- Under `PERCENT_CAPPED`, the base is the obligation's own `state_fee_cents` plus its `franchise_tax_cents` — the late fee's own value is not part of its base. The raw fee is that base multiplied by the rule's `late_fee_percent_bps` and divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the fee is the lesser of that raw figure and the rule's `late_fee_cap_cents`. The cap is inclusive: a raw figure exactly equal to `late_fee_cap_cents` is not reduced. Because nothing in this branch depends on how late the row is, the fee stops growing the moment lateness begins.

**The service fee.** `service_fee_cents` is the platform's own charge and is **evaluated exactly once, at creation, and never recomputed** by any later ops-run, filing or rebuild. The base charge is 4900 cents. A volume discount of 1500 basis points applies when the owning client already carries a large open book: the server counts the client's compliance obligations in one of the four open statuses — `SCHEDULED`, `DUE_SOON`, `IN_GRACE` or `DELINQUENT` — across every registration of every one of the client's legal entities, **measured before the new row is inserted**, and applies the discount when that count is 5 or more. Obligations in `SATISFIED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED` are excluded from the count. The discount itself is 4900 multiplied by 1500 and divided by 10000, rounded half up, which is exactly 735 cents, so the discounted service fee is exactly 4165 cents and the undiscounted fee is 4900 cents. The comparison is inclusive on the threshold: a prior open count of 4 gets no discount and pays 4900, while a prior open count of 5 gets the discount and pays 4165. Measuring the count before the insert is the pinned resolution of an apparent circularity — the fee feeds `total_due_cents`, which feeds the client rollup, which feeds the very count the fee is keyed on.

**The total.** `total_due_cents` is `state_fee_cents` plus `franchise_tax_cents` plus `late_fee_cents` plus `service_fee_cents`. It is recomputed at creation, on every ops-run recomputation, and on every waiver.

**Waiving.** A `PlatformAdmin` may waive an outstanding obligation, supplying a mandatory `waiver_reason`. The field must be present and must be a string, but it is **not required to be non-blank, and it is not persisted anywhere**: no field of the obligation records it and no response returns it, so its only observable effect is that omitting it fails rung 13. The obligation must be in `DUE_SOON`, `IN_GRACE` or `DELINQUENT`; a waiver attempted against a `SCHEDULED` row, or against a row already in a terminal status, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and nothing changes — and because rung 13 precedes rung 16, a waiver that both omits the reason and addresses a `SCHEDULED` row reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the obligation moves to `WAIVED`, `satisfied_on` is stamped with the current clock date, `total_due_cents` is set to 0 **while the four components that fed it are left standing at their last computed values**, every reminder notice of the obligation in `PENDING` or in `SENT` is moved to `SUPPRESSED` with each such notice's `version` increased by 1, the obligation's `version` increases by 1, the cure hop of Section 3.11 is offered the parent registration exactly as an acceptance would offer it, and the registration's `delinquency_rank`, the entity's `compliance_posture` and the client's rollup are all recomputed.

### 3.6. The Reminder Ladder and Calendar Rebuilds

Creating a calendar row also mints its reminder ladder: exactly five notices, all in status `PENDING`, each recording the `compliance_obligation_id` it belongs to, an `offset_label` naming its rung, a `scheduled_for` date, a `due_date_snapshot` capturing the obligation's `due_date` at the moment the notice row was created, `sent_on` null, and its own `version`. The five rungs are `T_MINUS_90`, scheduled for 90 calendar days before `due_date`; `T_MINUS_60`, scheduled for 60 days before; `T_MINUS_30`, scheduled for 30 days before; `T_MINUS_7`, scheduled for 7 days before; and `OVERDUE`, scheduled for one calendar day after `grace_end_date`. None of those five dates is rolled to a business day — a rung may legitimately fall on a weekend or an observed holiday. The five rows are minted in exactly that order — 90, 60, 30, 7, then `OVERDUE` — which, since they all share one `created_at`, is the order the ladder listing returns them in. The ladder of an obligation can be listed, filtered by `status`.

On each ops-run, a notice fires when three conditions hold together: the current clock date is on or after its `scheduled_for`, its `due_date_snapshot` still equals its obligation's current `due_date`, and the parent obligation is in one of the four open statuses. A firing notice moves `PENDING → SENT`, has `sent_on` stamped with the current clock date, and has its `version` increased by 1. A notice failing any of the three conditions is left in `PENDING` with nothing written. The behaviour is idempotent by construction: the identity of a notice is the combination of its obligation, its `offset_label` and its `due_date_snapshot`, so re-running the ops-run on the same clock date creates no additional row and re-sends nothing — the already-`SENT` rows simply no longer qualify.

Notices are suppressed when their obligation leaves the ladder. When an obligation reaches `SATISFIED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED`, every notice of that obligation in `PENDING` moves to `SUPPRESSED` and every notice in `SENT` also moves to `SUPPRESSED`; each such move increases that notice's `version` by 1. `SUPPRESSED` is terminal.

**Rebuilding a calendar.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may re-derive the calendar of one registration after the entity's underlying facts have changed — most usefully after its fiscal year end was corrected. The request carries `rebuild_types`, a list of obligation-type values naming which types are eligible for re-derivation; a `rebuild_types` value that is absent, not a list, or containing a member outside the declared obligation types is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, since `rebuild_types` is not one of the fields that route to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

A row is eligible when its `obligation_type` appears in `rebuild_types` **and** its `status` is still `SCHEDULED`. Every other row on the registration is preserved verbatim. Eligible rows have `due_date`, `grace_end_date` and `delinquency_date` re-derived — under **their own pinned** rule version, using the entity's **current** `formation_date` and fiscal-year-end fields. `governing_rule_id` is never written by a rebuild; that is the operational proof that effective-dated rules are not retroactive, since a rule published after the row was determined cannot move it. Neither is any money field: a rebuild re-derives dates and nothing else, so `state_fee_cents`, `franchise_tax_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `service_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents` all survive it unchanged even though `grace_end_date` — the anchor the late fee is measured from — has moved. A rebuild is accepted whatever status the registration itself is in, and an empty `rebuild_types` list is well formed and simply makes every row ineligible.

The response reports `registration_id`, `rebuilt_count`, `preserved_count` and `reminders_rearmed`. A row is counted in `rebuilt_count` only when its `due_date` actually moved; a row that was eligible but whose re-derivation produced the same `due_date` lands in `preserved_count`, as does every ineligible row. The response is that bare four-key object and carries no resource of its own — no `id`, no audit fields. Where a `due_date` moved, every reminder notice of that obligation whose `due_date_snapshot` no longer matches the new `due_date` is moved to `REARMED` — this applies to notices in `PENDING` and to notices already in `SENT` alike, and to those two statuses only, so a notice already `SUPPRESSED` or already `REARMED` by an earlier rebuild is left where it is — and a fresh ladder of five `PENDING` notices is minted against the new `due_date`, one for each of the four countdown rungs plus the overdue rung, each carrying the new `due_date_snapshot` and starting at `version` 1 with `sent_on` null. Each re-armed notice has its `version` increased by 1, and `reminders_rearmed` reports how many notices were moved to `REARMED`. The obligation itself has its `version` increased by 1 exactly when its `due_date` moved, and not at all otherwise.

### 3.7. Filings, Fees and the Capped Refile Cycle

A filing is a document lodged with a jurisdiction. It records the `registration_id` of the jurisdiction it is lodged in, a `filing_kind` drawn from `ANNUAL_REPORT`, `FRANCHISE_TAX`, `FOREIGN_QUALIFICATION`, `WITHDRAWAL`, `DISSOLUTION`, `REINSTATEMENT` and `FINAL_REPORT`, an `expedite_tier` drawn from `NONE`, `STANDARD`, `RUSH` and `SAME_DAY` and defaulting to `NONE` when omitted, and a `status` that begins at `DRAFT` and runs through `SUBMITTED` to either `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, with `REJECTED` leading onward to `ABANDONED`. `ACCEPTED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.

There are two ways to draft one, and they differ in what the filing is attached to.

**Drafting against a calendar obligation.** A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts a filing against a compliance obligation, supplying `filing_kind`, an optional `expedite_tier` and the mandatory `expected_compliance_obligation_version`. The created filing records the obligation as its `compliance_obligation_id` and the obligation's registration as its `registration_id`, with `good_standing_certificate_id` null — null unconditionally, so a `good_standing_certificate_id` sent to this endpoint is still resolved by rung 11 and can still produce `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, but is otherwise discarded. The `filing_kind` supplied here is **unconstrained by the parent obligation's `obligation_type`**: no rung, no state precondition and no error identifier covers a mismatch, so all seven kinds are draftable against any obligation. Before the fee breakdown is computed, the obligation's `late_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents` are recomputed at the current clock date and the obligation's `version` increases by 1 — unconditionally, whether or not the recomputation changed a number, and whatever rung the obligation currently occupies, terminal statuses included — which is exactly why the caller had to prove it was holding a current view. That re-pricing also recomputes the registration rank and the rollups above it.

**Drafting a standalone filing.** A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts a filing directly against a registration when the document is not tied to a calendar row at all: a foreign qualification, a withdrawal, a dissolution or a reinstatement. The request carries `filing_kind`, an optional `expedite_tier`, an optional `good_standing_certificate_id` (meant for a foreign-qualification filing, though the server records whatever the request supplies whatever the kind, and leaves it null when the request omits it), and an optional `reinstatement_case_id` naming the reinstatement case a `REINSTATEMENT` filing answers. That case identifier is retained internally to fix which case prices the draft and which case the later cascades prefer; it is not a field of the `Filing` resource and never appears in a filing response. A standalone filing has `compliance_obligation_id` null. No version field is taken, and the jurisdiction rule effective on the current clock date must exist or the draft fails with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE`. Neither the registration's status nor its `registration_kind` is gated here, so any of the seven kinds may be drafted standalone against any registration.

Either way the new filing is created in `DRAFT` with `attempt_number` 1, `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_on`, `outcome_recorded_on`, `rejection_reason` and `refile_window_end` all null. `paid_amount_cents` starts at 0.

**The fee breakdown.** Six money fields are derived at draft time: `state_fee_cents`, `service_fee_cents`, `expedite_fee_cents`, `state_portion_cents`, `service_portion_cents` and `total_fee_cents`. The expedite component splits in two. Its service half is 0 cents at tier `NONE`, 5000 cents at `STANDARD`, 12500 cents at `RUSH` and 30000 cents at `SAME_DAY`. Its state half is 0 at tier `NONE`, and at any other tier is the jurisdiction rule's `expedite_surcharge_cents`. Which rule version supplies that surcharge is pinned by how the filing was drafted: **an obligation-backed filing takes it from the obligation's own pinned `governing_rule_id`**, never from whatever version is open today — which is why that endpoint carries no rung-18 gate and can never fail with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE` — while a standalone filing takes it from the rule effective on the draft date, the very rule its own rung 18 just required. `expedite_fee_cents` is the sum of those two halves, and is carried for the reader only: the two portions are formed from the halves directly, so nothing downstream reads `expedite_fee_cents` itself.

For a filing attached to an obligation, `state_fee_cents` is the obligation's `state_fee_cents` plus its `franchise_tax_cents` plus its `late_fee_cents` — the money owed to the jurisdiction for that period, lateness included — and `service_fee_cents` is the obligation's own `service_fee_cents`. For a standalone filing, `state_fee_cents` comes from the jurisdiction rule by kind: `foreign_qualification_fee_cents` for a `FOREIGN_QUALIFICATION`, `withdrawal_fee_cents` for a `WITHDRAWAL`, `dissolution_fee_cents` for a `DISSOLUTION`, and for a `REINSTATEMENT` the `back_fee_total_cents` already assessed on the reinstatement case; and `service_fee_cents` is the base service charge of 4900 cents, undiscounted — the volume discount of Section 3.5 keys on the calendar and never touches a filing. A standalone filing drafted under any of the three remaining kinds — `ANNUAL_REPORT`, `FRANCHISE_TAX` or `FINAL_REPORT`, none of which the rule catalogue prices outside a calendar row — takes `state_fee_cents` 0. The `REINSTATEMENT` case whose `back_fee_total_cents` is drawn on is the one the request named; when the request named none, it is **the most recently created case on that registration that has already been assessed** (has a non-null `assessment_date`), and when the registration has no assessed case at all the state fee is 0. A successor filing produced by a refile is the one exception to the service charge: its `service_fee_cents` is 0, because the predecessor's service portion is carried into it rather than charged again.

The two portions are then formed: `state_portion_cents` is `state_fee_cents` plus the state half of the expedite fee, and `service_portion_cents` is `service_fee_cents` plus the service half. `total_fee_cents` is the sum of the two portions. The distinction is permanent and consequential — the state portion is money that leaves for the jurisdiction and is never refundable, while the service portion is the only money that can be carried or refunded.

**Submission.** A `FilingAgent` lodges a draft. Three gates fire in the declared order: the filing must be in `DRAFT` and its parent registration must be in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `DELINQUENT` or `ADMIN_DISSOLVED`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; then the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the filing's `prepared_by_user_id`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; then `paid_amount_cents` must have reached `total_fee_cents`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CAPTURED_FUNDS`. On success the filing moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, `submitted_on` is stamped with the current clock date, `submitted_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` — a value written for the audit trail that no later check reads — and the filing's `version` increases by 1. If the filing is a `REINSTATEMENT` answering a reinstatement case that is in status `PAID`, that case moves `PAID → FILED` and its `version` increases by 1; a case in any other status is not moved. "Answering" is resolved the same way on both of the reinstatement cascades: when the draft named a `reinstatement_case_id`, that case alone is considered and the hop fires only if it is in the required status; when it named none, the candidate is the most recently created case on the filing's own registration that is in the required status, so the cascade stays live either way. At most one case is moved by one filing.

**Recording the outcome.** A `FilingAgent` records what the jurisdiction decided, supplying an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` and, with a rejection, an optional `rejection_reason` string. The filing must be in `SUBMITTED`; from any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

An `ACCEPTED` outcome moves the filing `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `outcome_recorded_on` with the current clock date, increases the filing's `version` by 1, and starts the five-hop cascade of Section 2.12: when — and only when — the filing carries a `compliance_obligation_id`, the referenced obligation moves to `SATISFIED` with `satisfied_on` stamped at the current clock date and its `version` increased by 1, its pending reminder notices are suppressed, and if that leaves no obligation on the registration in `DELINQUENT` while the registration itself is `DELINQUENT`, the registration returns to `ACTIVE`, after which the entity's posture and the client's rollup are recomputed. A standalone filing's acceptance stops at the filing itself; it does not move any obligation.

An `ACCEPTED` outcome ignores any `rejection_reason` the request happens to carry; the field is written only on the rejection leg, where it is recorded verbatim, may be blank, and stays null when omitted. A `REJECTED` outcome moves the filing `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stamps `outcome_recorded_on` with the current clock date, records the supplied `rejection_reason`, and sets `refile_window_end` to `outcome_recorded_on` plus 15 calendar days, with no weekend or holiday roll. The whole `state_portion_cents` is retained by the jurisdiction and is forfeited from this moment; the `service_portion_cents` is held pending the client's next move. The filing's `version` increases by 1. No obligation is touched by a rejection: the calendar row stays exactly where it was on the ladder.

**Refiling.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may replace a rejected filing with a fresh attempt. The gates fire in the declared order: the filing must be in `REJECTED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the current clock date must be on or before `refile_window_end`, else `ERR_REFILE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and `attempt_number` must be strictly below 3, else `ERR_REFILE_LIMIT_REACHED`. Because those two gates reject every out-of-window and over-cap attempt outright, no refund is ever produced by a refile — the refund path is reached only by abandoning.

On success the rejected filing moves `REJECTED → ABANDONED` and its `version` increases by 1, and a **successor** filing is created in `DRAFT` carrying the same `registration_id`, the same `compliance_obligation_id`, the same `good_standing_certificate_id` and the same `filing_kind` as its predecessor, with `attempt_number` one greater than the predecessor's, `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `paid_amount_cents` 0 and `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_on`, `outcome_recorded_on`, `rejection_reason` and `refile_window_end` all null. The successor's `expedite_tier` is whatever the refile request supplies, defaulting to `NONE`; that tier is the only field of the request. The successor's `state_fee_cents` is **the predecessor's stored `state_fee_cents`, reused verbatim**: an obligation-backed refile does **not** re-price the obligation's late fee at the refile date and does not touch the parent obligation at all — no version bump, no re-pricing, no cascade — and only the two expedite halves are re-derived, from the successor's own tier under the same rule version that priced the predecessor. Its `service_fee_cents` is 0 — the predecessor's allocated service portion is **carried**, consumed by the successor rather than returned — and its `service_portion_cents` is therefore just the service half of its own expedite tier. The client's `credit_balance_cents` does not move at all on a refile, no reversal allocation is created, and the predecessor's own allocation rows are left exactly as they are, so the abandoned predecessor keeps whatever `paid_amount_cents` it had. It is the successor filing that is returned in the response, with HTTP 201.

**Abandoning.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may instead abandon a rejected filing outright. The filing must be in `REJECTED`, else the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the filing moves `REJECTED → ABANDONED` and its `version` increases by 1, and the service money it consumed is compensated back to the client. The server sums the `service_portion_cents` of the forward allocation rows that funded this filing — reversal rows are excluded from that sum, as they are from the parallel sum of `state_portion_cents` — and creates a reversal `PaymentAllocation` against the funding payment with `is_reversal` true, `amount_cents` equal to the negated allocated service sum, `state_portion_cents` 0, `service_portion_cents` equal to the same negated figure, and a `sequence_index` continuing the count of allocation rows already on this filing. "The funding payment" is singular and is resolved as **the payment behind the earliest forward allocation row on the filing**; where several payments funded one filing, the whole reversal is booked against that first one, so the credit lands there in full. That amount is added to the funding `Payment`'s `refunded_amount_cents` and to the owning `Client`'s `credit_balance_cents`, the payment's `allocated_amount_cents` falls by the same figure while its `unallocated_amount_cents` stays where it was — which is what keeps the payment identity exact — and the payment moves to `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, or straight to `REFUNDED` when the reversal brings `refunded_amount_cents` up to equal `captured_amount_cents`. Where the abandoned filing was never funded, or its forward service sum is zero or less, **no reversal row is written at all**: the filing simply moves to `ABANDONED`, no payment is touched, no payment version moves and no credit is issued. The abandonment itself is otherwise unconditional — the state of the parent obligation, registration and entity is neither checked nor changed. The allocated state sum is forfeited on this path exactly as it is on the refile path — the state portion is never returned under either.

Filings can be listed with `registration_id`, `compliance_obligation_id`, `good_standing_certificate_id`, `status` and `filing_kind` filters, and a single filing read exposes its full refundability split alongside `paid_amount_cents`, which is at all times the net sum of the `amount_cents` of every allocation row pointing at it, reversal rows included as negatives.

### 3.8. Client Money: Authorization, Capture, Allocation and Refund

Money enters the system as a card authorization against a client and only becomes real at capture. A `PlatformAdmin` or the client's own `ClientContact` authorizes one by supplying `authorized_amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero; the client's `id` comes from the path and is recorded as `client_id`. The payment is created in status `AUTHORIZED` with `captured_amount_cents`, `allocated_amount_cents`, `unallocated_amount_cents` and `refunded_amount_cents` all 0. A payment's `status` takes one of five values — `AUTHORIZED`, `CAPTURED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, `REFUNDED` and `VOIDED` — of which `REFUNDED` and `VOIDED` are terminal. Payments of a client can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

**Voiding.** A `PlatformAdmin` may void an authorization that was never captured. The payment must be in `AUTHORIZED`; from any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the status becomes `VOIDED` and the `version` increases by 1. Nothing else moves — no allocation is created, no filing is touched, no credit is issued. `VOIDED` is terminal.

**Capturing.** A `PlatformAdmin` captures, supplying a mandatory `capture_amount_cents` — an integer of zero or more, so a capture of exactly 0 is well formed, moves the payment to `CAPTURED` with every money field at 0 and writes no allocation row — and an optional `target_filing_id`. The gates fire in the declared order of Section 2.9.1: the payment must be `AUTHORIZED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; `capture_amount_cents` must not exceed `authorized_amount_cents`, else `ERR_CAPTURE_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZATION` with HTTP 402; and when `target_filing_id` is supplied it must name a filing belonging to the paying client, in status `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED`, whose `paid_amount_cents` is strictly below its `total_fee_cents`, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_TARGET_INVALID`. On success the payment moves `AUTHORIZED → CAPTURED`, `captured_amount_cents` is set to `capture_amount_cents`, the allocation waterfall runs, and the payment's `version` increases by 1.

**The waterfall.** The candidate set depends on whether a target was named. When `target_filing_id` is supplied, the candidate set is that one filing alone. When it is omitted, the candidates are every filing of the paying client — reached through the client's legal entities and their registrations — whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` and whose `paid_amount_cents` is strictly below its `total_fee_cents`. Filings in `ACCEPTED` or `ABANDONED` are never candidates, and neither is a filing that is already fully funded.

Candidates are ordered by earliest statutory deadline first. Each candidate's sort key is the `due_date` of the obligation it is attached to; a standalone filing, having no obligation, sorts as though its deadline were `9999-12-31`, which places every standalone filing after every obligation-backed one. Where two candidates share the same key, the one created earlier comes first, and where even `created_at` ties, insertion order decides. When a `target_filing_id` was named the ordering is moot — the candidate set is that filing alone, and its eligibility was already settled by rung 36, so the waterfall never re-tests it.

The server then walks that ordered list with a running remainder that starts at `capture_amount_cents` and a zero-based counter that starts at 0. For each candidate it computes the shortfall as `total_fee_cents` minus `paid_amount_cents`, and the amount to place as the lesser of the remaining funds and that shortfall. If the amount to place is zero or less, the candidate is skipped entirely — no allocation row is written for it and the counter does not advance. Otherwise a `PaymentAllocation` row is created recording `payment_id`, `filing_id`, `amount_cents` equal to the amount placed, a `sequence_index` equal to the current counter value, and `is_reversal` false. The row's `state_portion_cents` is the amount placed multiplied by the filing's `state_portion_cents` and divided by the filing's `total_fee_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent; its `service_portion_cents` is then the amount placed **minus** that state portion. That rounding order is pinned: rounding both halves independently would let them drift a cent away from `amount_cents`, whereas taking the service half as the remainder makes the pair sum exactly, which is what the allocation split identity of Section 2.7 requires. The filing's `paid_amount_cents` is re-derived as the net sum of every allocation row now pointing at it, the remainder shrinks by the amount placed, and the counter advances by one. `sequence_index` is therefore zero-based **per capture**, not per filing or per payment: a second capture on a second payment starts its own numbering at 0 again.

When the walk finishes, the payment's `allocated_amount_cents` is `capture_amount_cents` less whatever remainder is left, and `unallocated_amount_cents` is that remainder. If the candidate set was empty, no allocation rows are created at all and the entire captured amount lands in `unallocated_amount_cents`.

Capture also has one cascade of its own: if the waterfall brings a `REINSTATEMENT` filing to the point where its `paid_amount_cents` has reached its `total_fee_cents`, and that filing answers a reinstatement case in status `FEES_ASSESSED`, the case moves `FEES_ASSESSED → PAID` and its `version` increases by 1. A case in any other status is left alone. Only filings this capture actually placed money on are examined, so a `REINSTATEMENT` filing that was already fully funded before this capture never re-fires the hop; the case is resolved by the same rule as on submission — the named case if the draft named one, otherwise the most recently created case on the registration that is in `FEES_ASSESSED`.

**Reading the money.** A payment read exposes all four identity fields, and the allocation rows drawn on a payment can be listed, reversals included, as can the allocation rows that funded a particular filing. Neither allocation listing takes any filter beyond `limit` and `offset`, and both return rows in the default ascending-`created_at`, then-insertion order. Allocation rows carry `id` and `created_at` only — they have no `updated_at` and no `version`, because an allocation is never edited; a compensation is expressed by writing a new row with `is_reversal` true, never by amending an old one.

### 3.9. Service of Process

The registered agent's most time-critical duty is forwarding served papers. A `SopHandler` records receipt of a document against a registration, supplying a `sop_class` of `ORDINARY` or `LITIGATION` and, on litigation papers, the `plaintiff_name` named on them and the `court_case_number`; both of those default to null when omitted. Neither is ever required — a `LITIGATION` document may be recorded with both absent — neither is required to be non-blank, both are accepted on an `ORDINARY` document just as readily, and nothing anywhere reads either of them. The registration's own status is not gated: papers can be recorded against a registration in any status. The document is created in status `RECEIVED` with `received_at` stamped from the logical clock, `scanned_at`, `forwarded_at` and `acknowledged_at` all null, `escalation_level` 0, `deferred` false, `deferral_count` 0, `forwarded_late` false and `ack_deadline_at` null.

**The forward deadline.** `forward_deadline_at` is computed at receipt by advancing `received_at` along the business-hour cursor of Section 2.5. A `LITIGATION` document gets 4 business hours — half a business day, so a Friday-morning receipt lands Friday afternoon and a Friday-afternoon receipt lands Monday morning. An `ORDINARY` document gets 16 business hours, which is two full business days, so a Friday receipt lands on Tuesday. The litigation deadline is never later than the ordinary deadline for the same receipt instant.

**Litigation pre-emption.** Recording a `LITIGATION` document also disrupts the ordinary queue on the same registration. Every `ORDINARY` document on that registration whose `status` is `RECEIVED` or `SCANNED` — that is, every ordinary document not yet forwarded — has its `forward_deadline_at` advanced by a further 8 business hours along the same cursor **starting from the deadline it currently carries**, not from its own `received_at` and not from the litigating document's, so a second litigation arrival defers it again from the already-deferred figure; has `deferred` set true; has `deferral_count` increased by one; and has its own `version` increased by 1. Documents already in `FORWARDED`, `ESCALATED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `REJECTED_NOT_OUR_ENTITY` are untouched by the pre-emption. The registration's `sop_priority_hold` becomes true. That flag is true exactly while at least one `LITIGATION` document on the registration is in status `RECEIVED`, `SCANNED`, `FORWARDED` or `ESCALATED`, and false otherwise; it is re-evaluated on litigation receipt and on every scan and every acknowledgment, and the acknowledgment leg re-evaluates it **whether the document was acknowledged out of `FORWARDED` or out of `ESCALATED`**, so the invariant holds at every instant. The re-evaluation writes the flag and bumps the registration's `version` by 1 **only when the value actually changes**, and leaves the row entirely untouched when it does not. Nothing anywhere gates on `sop_priority_hold`: no deadline, no rung and no rollup consults it, and neither withdrawal nor termination nor permanent closure clears it, so a closed registration can carry a stale `true` for ever.

**Scanning.** A `SopHandler` scans the papers, optionally supplying `not_our_entity`, which defaults to false. The document must be in `RECEIVED`; from any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. With `not_our_entity` false or omitted, the document moves `RECEIVED → SCANNED` and `scanned_at` is stamped from the logical clock. With `not_our_entity` true, the document moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED_NOT_OUR_ENTITY` instead, `scanned_at` is still stamped, and the registration's `sop_priority_hold` is cleared if no other litigation document on that registration remains open. The flag is in fact re-evaluated on both legs of the scan — on the ordinary leg it necessarily finds no change, since a document moving `RECEIVED → SCANNED` stays inside the open set. Either way the document's `version` increases by 1, and `REJECTED_NOT_OUR_ENTITY` is terminal.

**Forwarding.** A `SopHandler` forwards a scanned document to the client. This same action is also how a document is re-forwarded after an escalation, so it is accepted from `SCANNED` and from `ESCALATED`; from any other status it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the document moves to `FORWARDED` and `forwarded_at` is stamped from the logical clock. `forwarded_late` is then set to true when `forwarded_at` is **strictly** later than `forward_deadline_at`, and false otherwise — forwarding exactly at the deadline instant is on time. `ack_deadline_at` is set to `forwarded_at` advanced by 8 business hours. On a re-forward out of `ESCALATED`, `escalation_level` is deliberately left unchanged: the escalations already raised are not forgiven by a fresh forward. The document's `version` increases by 1 on every forward.

**Escalating.** When the client does not acknowledge in time, a `SopHandler` raises the next rung. The gates fire in the declared order: the document must be in `FORWARDED` or `ESCALATED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the current logical clock instant must have reached `ack_deadline_at`, else `ERR_SOP_ACK_NOT_DUE` — the comparison is inclusive, so escalating exactly at `ack_deadline_at` is permitted and only strictly before it is refused; and `escalation_level` must be strictly below 3, else `ERR_SOP_ESCALATION_CAP_REACHED`. On success the document's status becomes `ESCALATED` — from `FORWARDED` it is a transition, and from `ESCALATED` it is a self-loop — and `escalation_level` increases by one. A `SopEscalationEvent` is created recording the `sop_document_id`, the new `level`, `raised_at` stamped from the logical clock, and a `channel` fixed by that level: `EMAIL` at level 1, `PHONE` at level 2 and `ACCOUNT_MANAGER` at level 3. The owning client's `sop_open_escalation_count` increases and `portfolio_risk_flag` is re-evaluated, becoming true once that count has reached 2 — both as the derived recomputation of Section 2.11, so the client row moves no `version` and refreshes no `updated_at`. `ack_deadline_at` is then re-armed to the current instant advanced by 4 business hours, measured from the instant of the escalation rather than from the deadline it replaces, and the document's `version` increases by 1. Escalation events can be listed for a document; each carries `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`, and the listing takes no filter.

**Acknowledging.** The client's own `ClientContact` acknowledges receipt. The document must be in `FORWARDED` or `ESCALATED`; from any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the document moves to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `acknowledged_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the owning client's `sop_open_escalation_count` is reduced by this document's `escalation_level` — every escalation this document raised leaves the open set at once — after which `portfolio_risk_flag` is re-evaluated against the threshold of 2 and may fall back to false. Because acknowledgment takes the document out of the open litigation set, the registration's `sop_priority_hold` is re-evaluated and clears when no other litigation document on the registration remains in `RECEIVED`, `SCANNED`, `FORWARDED` or `ESCALATED`. The document's `version` increases by 1. `ACKNOWLEDGED` is terminal, and the document's `forward_deadline_at`, `forwarded_late` and `escalation_level` are all preserved as the permanent record of how the duty was performed. Documents of a registration can be listed with `status` and `sop_class` filters, and reading a single document returns `forward_deadline_at`, `ack_deadline_at` and `forwarded_late` alongside `received_at`, `scanned_at`, `forwarded_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `escalation_level`, `deferred`, `deferral_count`, `plaintiff_name` and `court_case_number`.

### 3.10. Beneficial Ownership and the Federal Axis

Beneficial-ownership reporting runs on a federal clock that is entirely independent of any jurisdiction's calendar, and on the entity's second state axis, `boi_status`.

**Owners.** A `ComplianceOfficer` adds a beneficial owner to an entity, supplying a `full_name` and a `residential_address` and an `id_document_number`, each of which must be non-empty; a `date_of_birth` as an ISO date; an `ownership_pct`, a number that must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than 100; and optionally `is_company_applicant`, which defaults to false. The owner is created with `owner_status` `ACTIVE`; `owner_status` may later be moved to `REMOVED` once the person is no longer a reportable owner. `ownership_pct` may be any number, integer or fractional, in that range, and no constraint of any kind is imposed on the sum of the percentages across an entity's owners. Owners can be listed for an entity with an optional `owner_status` filter, and an owner can be updated field by field, every field being optional on the update — every field, that is, except `is_company_applicant`, which is settable only at creation and is silently ignored on an update. That flag is in any case consulted by nothing: the company-applicant gate of Section 2.9 reads the *entity*'s `company_applicant_required` and the *report*'s `includes_company_applicant`, never any owner's flag, so `is_company_applicant` is recorded and returned but affects no check. `date_of_birth` is likewise recorded, updatable and read by nothing. An update bumps the owner's `version` by 1 whether or not any supplied value differed from the value on file, and whether or not the body supplied anything at all.

**What counts as a reportable change.** Exactly six things arm the federal update clock: a change to an owner's `full_name`, `residential_address`, `id_document_number`, `ownership_pct` or `owner_status`, and a change to the entity's own `legal_name`. Adding a new beneficial owner is also a reportable change. Two fields are deliberately **not** triggers: changing an owner's `date_of_birth` alone arms nothing, and neither does changing `is_company_applicant` alone. An update that touches only non-triggering fields still increases the owner's `version` by 1, but leaves the entity's BOI axis and `boi_update_deadline` exactly as they were — and so does an update that re-sends a triggering field with the value it already had, since a change is only reportable when the supplied value genuinely **differs** from the stored one. Adding an owner, by contrast, is unconditionally a reportable change. Where a change is reportable and the axis is in a state that admits arming, it is the entity's `version` that moves by 1, on top of the owner's own.

**The initial deadline.** When an entity is created — and again whenever its `boi_exempt_class` returns to `NONE` — the server derives `boi_initial_deadline` from `formation_date` alone, with no weekend or holiday roll, because this is a federal deadline rather than a state filing date. An entity whose `boi_exempt_class` is anything other than `NONE` gets a null deadline. Otherwise there are three bands, and both band comparisons are strict: an entity formed strictly before `2024-01-01` gets the fixed deadline `2025-01-01`, shared by every legacy entity regardless of when it was formed; an entity formed on or after `2024-01-01` but strictly before `2025-01-01` gets `formation_date` plus 90 calendar days; and an entity formed on or after `2025-01-01` gets `formation_date` plus 30 calendar days. An entity formed exactly on `2024-01-01` therefore sits in the 90-day band, an entity formed exactly on `2025-01-01` sits in the 30-day band, and one day of formation date can shorten the runway by sixty days.

**The update deadline.** A reportable change sets `boi_update_deadline` to the date of the change plus 30 calendar days and moves the axis to `UPDATE_DUE` — but only when the axis is currently `FILED`, `UPDATE_DUE` or `DELINQUENT`. While the axis is still `PENDING_INITIAL` a reportable change arms no update clock at all, because the initial report has not yet been made. An entity whose `boi_exempt_class` is not `NONE` arms nothing either. Re-arming **discards** the previous deadline entirely rather than extending it: a second reportable change on day 20 of a running 30-day clock resets the deadline to that second change date plus 30 days, it does not add another 30 days to what was left. Where a report row records the change that armed its clock, it does so as `triggering_change_date`.

**The axis itself.** `boi_status` starts at `PENDING_INITIAL` and moves as follows.

- Setting `boi_exempt_class` to a value other than `NONE` while the axis is `PENDING_INITIAL` moves it to `NOT_REQUIRED` and sets both `boi_initial_deadline` and `boi_update_deadline` to null. Claiming an exemption from any *other* axis state — `FILED`, `UPDATE_DUE` or `DELINQUENT` — leaves the axis exactly where it stood, since `NOT_REQUIRED` has only the one declared origin, but **still nulls both deadlines**, because the class suppresses the deadlines it governs wherever the axis happens to be. Moving between two non-`NONE` classes changes only the class.
- Setting `boi_exempt_class` back to `NONE` while the axis is `NOT_REQUIRED` moves it back to `PENDING_INITIAL` and recomputes `boi_initial_deadline` from the formation bands. This return edge requires that the entity's `lifecycle_status` is not `DISSOLVED`; an exemption claimed on a dissolved entity is not withdrawable. Where the edge is blocked — because the axis is not `NOT_REQUIRED`, or because the entity is `DISSOLVED` — the `boi_exempt_class` column itself is still written to `NONE`; it is only the axis and the deadlines that stay put.
- Recording an `ACCEPTED` outcome on an `INITIAL` report while the axis is `PENDING_INITIAL` moves it to `FILED` and clears `boi_update_deadline` to null. The rule is in fact general and neither the report's `report_kind` nor the axis's exact position narrows it: **an `ACCEPTED` outcome on any submitted report drives the axis to `FILED` and clears `boi_update_deadline`**, from `PENDING_INITIAL`, `UPDATE_DUE` or `DELINQUENT` alike. Two readings only are inert and bump no entity version: an axis already at `NOT_REQUIRED` is never disturbed by an acceptance, and an axis already `FILED` with a null `boi_update_deadline` has nothing left to move.
- A reportable change while the axis is `FILED` moves it to `UPDATE_DUE` and arms `boi_update_deadline`.
- A further reportable change while the axis is already `UPDATE_DUE` is a self-loop: the axis stays `UPDATE_DUE` and `boi_update_deadline` is re-armed from the new change date.
- Recording an `ACCEPTED` outcome on an `UPDATED` report while the axis is `UPDATE_DUE` moves it back to `FILED` and clears `boi_update_deadline`.
- The ops-run moves the axis to `DELINQUENT` from `UPDATE_DUE` when the current clock date is strictly past `boi_update_deadline`, and from `PENDING_INITIAL` when the current clock date is strictly past `boi_initial_deadline`. On the deadline date itself the axis does not move, and an axis whose governing deadline is null — a `PENDING_INITIAL` entity created already exempt, say — never moves either, however far the clock travels.
- Recording an `ACCEPTED` outcome on any submitted report while the axis is `DELINQUENT` returns it to `FILED` and clears `boi_update_deadline`.

Every one of those moves increases the entity's `version` by 1 — except where the move is folded into a `PATCH` that is already counted as one mutation of the entity, in which case that single bump covers it. `NOT_REQUIRED` is the terminal reading of the axis, and dissolving the entity forces the axis there from wherever it stood; that forcing is unconditional and writes nothing else on the axis, so both deadlines survive dissolution exactly as they stood.

**Reports.** A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts a report against an entity, supplying a `report_kind` of `INITIAL` or `UPDATED`, an optional `includes_company_applicant` defaulting to false, and the mandatory `expected_legal_entity_version`. A stale version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; an `UPDATED` report drafted while the entity's `boi_status` is anything other than `UPDATE_DUE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and an entity whose `boi_exempt_class` is not `NONE` is rejected with `ERR_BOI_EXEMPT_ENTITY`. Those three fire in ladder order — rung 15, then rung 16, then rung 40 — so an `UPDATED` draft against an exempt entity that is not `UPDATE_DUE` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An `INITIAL` report carries no state precondition at all and may be drafted from any axis position other than the exempt one. On success the report is created in `DRAFT` with `submitted_on` and `accepted_on` null, its `deadline_date` pinned from the entity's `boi_initial_deadline` for an `INITIAL` report or from `boi_update_deadline` for an `UPDATED` one — copied as it stands, null included — its `triggering_change_date` set to the date of the reportable change that armed the current update clock on an `UPDATED` report and null on an `INITIAL` one, and its `owner_snapshot_count` set to the number of the entity's beneficial owners whose `owner_status` is `ACTIVE` at that instant. All three of those are recorded for the reader and read back by nothing: no later gate, deadline or outcome consults `deadline_date`, `triggering_change_date` or `owner_snapshot_count`. The entity's `version` increases by 1 — always, as the price of the draft, even though nothing on the entity changed, which is what makes a second draft against the same entity need a freshly read version. Reports of an entity can be listed with `status` and `report_kind` filters.

**Submission and outcome.** A `FilingAgent` submits a drafted report. The gates fire in the declared order: the report must be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the entity's `boi_exempt_class` must be `NONE`, else `ERR_BOI_EXEMPT_ENTITY`; and when the entity's `company_applicant_required` is true, `includes_company_applicant` must be true, else `ERR_COMPANY_APPLICANT_REQUIRED`. On success the report moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, `submitted_on` is stamped with the current clock date and the report's `version` increases by 1.

A `FilingAgent` then records the registry's decision by supplying an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`; the report must be in `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `ACCEPTED` moves the report to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_on` with the current clock date, and drives the entity's BOI axis to `FILED` with `boi_update_deadline` cleared to null. `REJECTED` moves the report to `REJECTED` and **deliberately leaves the entity's BOI axis exactly where it stood**, including its `boi_update_deadline`, so that a fresh report row can be drafted against the same unexpired deadline. Either outcome increases the report's `version` by 1. `ACCEPTED` is the terminal status of a report; a `REJECTED` report is never re-submitted in place — the cycle runs through a new report row.

### 3.11. Delinquency, Administrative Dissolution and Reinstatement

The registration lifecycle is deliberately cyclic. A registration that falls behind can be cured and return to good standing; one that is administratively dissolved can be reinstated and return to `ACTIVE`; and only when the reinstatement window lapses does the cycle finally close.

**Falling behind.** When any compliance obligation of an `ACTIVE` registration reaches `DELINQUENT` on an ops-run, the registration follows: its `status` becomes `DELINQUENT`, its `delinquency_rank` is set to 3, its `version` increases by 1, and the entity's `compliance_posture` and then the client's `portfolio_delinquency_status` are recomputed. A registration that is not `ACTIVE` is not moved by this rule.

**Curing.** When the last obligation of a `DELINQUENT` registration in status `DELINQUENT` leaves that status by reaching `SATISFIED` or `WAIVED`, the registration returns to `ACTIVE`. The condition is checked as a whole: the registration must be `DELINQUENT` and no obligation of it may remain in `DELINQUENT`. The registration's `delinquency_rank` is then recomputed from whatever obligations remain open — it does not merely reset to 0 — the `version` increases by 1, and the entity's posture and the client's rollup follow. If another delinquent obligation is still outstanding, nothing at all happens to the registration: it stays `DELINQUENT`, its version is not bumped, and the cure hop is simply skipped.

**Administrative dissolution.** A `PlatformAdmin` may record the jurisdiction's administrative dissolution of a delinquent registration, and the ops-run does the same sweep automatically. Two conditions must hold: the registration must be in `DELINQUENT`, and at least one of its obligations must be in `DELINQUENT` with the current clock date strictly past that obligation's `delinquency_date`. A registration that is delinquent but has not yet passed any obligation's dissolution horizon is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` when the operation is invoked directly, and is simply skipped by the sweep. On success the `status` becomes `ADMIN_DISSOLVED`, `admin_dissolved_on` is stamped with the current clock date, `delinquency_rank` is set to 4, the registration's `version` increases by 1, and the entity's posture and the client's rollup are recomputed. `reinstatement_deadline` is set at the same moment: it is `admin_dissolved_on` plus the `reinstatement_window_days` of the rule effective for the registration's `state_code` **on `admin_dissolved_on`**, with no weekend or holiday roll. No declared error covers a jurisdiction with no rule effective on that date, and this operation therefore never fails for that reason: where the selection finds nothing, the window falls back to the `reinstatement_window_days` of the most recent rule version ever published for that jurisdiction, and where the jurisdiction has no rule row at all, to the catalogue default of 365 days.

**Permanent closure.** When the ops-run finds an `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` registration whose `reinstatement_deadline` is strictly behind the current clock date, the registration moves to `CLOSED_PERMANENT` and `closed_on` is stamped with the current clock date. A null `reinstatement_deadline` never closes a registration. Every reinstatement case still open on that registration — every case in `OPEN`, `FEES_ASSESSED`, `PAID`, `FILED` or `DENIED` — moves to `EXPIRED` with its `version` bumped, and **those cases are reported in the sweep's `cases_expired` counter alongside the ones the window-expiry pass finds**; the obligations the closure voids are reported in no counter at all. Every obligation of the registration in one of the four open statuses is voided — set to `VOIDED` with `satisfied_on` stamped at the current clock date and `total_due_cents` zeroed, with its pending reminder notices suppressed. The registration's `version` increases by 1. `CLOSED_PERMANENT` is terminal, and a registration in it has `delinquency_rank` 4.

**Opening a reinstatement case.** A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a case against an administratively dissolved registration. The registration must be in `ADMIN_DISSOLVED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — that gate is rung 16 and is evaluated **first**, so a request against a registration that was never dissolved reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than a closed window. Then the current clock date must be on or before the registration's `reinstatement_deadline`; a request after that date is rejected with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and a request on that date exactly is accepted. Nothing forbids opening a second case on a registration that already carries one. The case is created in status `OPEN` with `window_close_date` copied once from the registration's `reinstatement_deadline` and never moved thereafter, `assessment_date` null, `missed_period_count` 0, `back_fee_total_cents` 0, `obligations_cured_count` 0 and `denial_reason` null. Cases on a registration can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

**Assessing the back fees.** A `ComplianceOfficer` assesses. The case must be in `OPEN` or in `DENIED` — the second is the retry path after a denial — and the current clock date must still be on or before `window_close_date`, else `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. On success the case moves to `FEES_ASSESSED`, `assessment_date` is stamped with the current clock date, and the money is computed.

The missed periods are the registration's obligations whose `status` is `IN_GRACE` or `DELINQUENT` at assessment time; obligations in `SCHEDULED`, `SATISFIED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED` are excluded. `missed_period_count` is how many there are. They are considered in order of `period_year` ascending, then `due_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Each missed period contributes its own `state_fee_cents` — the figure stored on the row, not a re-read of the rule — plus its franchise tax recomputed under **its own pinned rule version** from the entity's *current* share, asset and receipts figures, plus its late fee recomputed under that same pinned version at the assessment date, taking the freshly recomputed franchise tax as its base where the pinned style is `PERCENT_CAPPED`. This is the pinned interpretation and it matters: a fee increase enacted after a period was determined does not apply retroactively to that period. Pricing every missed period under today's open rule row instead would produce a different figure whenever any rule version changed between the earliest missed period and the assessment date. Only one component uses today's rule — the reinstatement filing fee itself. `back_fee_total_cents` is the sum of the per-period components plus the `reinstatement_fee_cents` of the rule effective for the registration's `state_code` on the assessment date, and each component is rounded to whole cents by its own calculation before the sum. If no rule row is effective on the assessment date, the assessment fails with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STATE_RULE`, and that check is made **before** the window check, so an out-of-window assessment in a jurisdiction with no effective rule reports the missing rule. The assessment writes only the case: **not one of the missed obligations is touched by it** — no status, no money field and no version moves on any of them, so the figures it summed live on the case alone. The set of obligations it counted is remembered on the case so that a later grant can cure exactly those rows. Re-assessing after a denial re-runs the whole computation at the new `assessment_date` and replaces both the figure and the remembered set.

**Funding and lodging.** A `REINSTATEMENT` filing drafted against the registration carries `back_fee_total_cents` as its state fee. When a capture brings that filing's `paid_amount_cents` up to its `total_fee_cents`, the case moves `FEES_ASSESSED → PAID`; when the filing is then submitted, the case moves `PAID → FILED`. Both moves increase the case's `version` by 1 and neither is invocable directly.

**Granting.** A `FilingAgent` grants reinstatement. Three conditions must hold: the case must be in `FILED`, the current clock date must be on or before `window_close_date` — else `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` — and the parent registration must still be in `ADMIN_DISSOLVED`. On success the case moves to `GRANTED` and `obligations_cured_count` is set equal to `missed_period_count` — set to that stored count outright, whatever the cure actually managed to move. The registration moves `ADMIN_DISSOLVED → ACTIVE` with `admin_dissolved_on` cleared to null while `reinstatement_deadline` is deliberately **left standing**, and every obligation counted in the case's `missed_period_count` is flipped to `SATISFIED` with `satisfied_on` stamped at the current clock date, which in turn suppresses each of their reminder notices in `PENDING` or `SENT`. The rows cured are exactly the ones the **most recent assessment** recorded, identified individually and not re-derived at grant time: an obligation that has since left the open statuses is skipped, and one that fell into `IN_GRACE` or `DELINQUENT` after the assessment is not cured. Each cured row's `version` increases by 1; a case granted with an empty remembered set cures nothing and moves only the case and the registration. The registration's `delinquency_rank` is recomputed from what remains, its `version` increases by 1, and the entity's posture and the client's rollup are recomputed. `GRANTED` is terminal.

**Denying.** A `FilingAgent` may deny instead, supplying a mandatory `denial_reason` — a string that must be present but that may legitimately be blank, and which, unlike the waiver's reason, is persisted on the case and returned by every read of it. The case must be in `FILED`, else the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; because rung 13 precedes rung 16, a denial that omits the reason on a case that is not `FILED` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The case moves to `DENIED`, records the reason, and its `version` increases by 1. The registration is not moved and its obligations are untouched. A denied case is not dead: it can be assessed again while the window is open, which returns it to `FEES_ASSESSED`.

**Expiry.** The ops-run expires any case whose `window_close_date` is strictly behind the current clock date and whose status is `OPEN`, `FEES_ASSESSED`, `PAID`, `FILED` or `DENIED`. Such a case moves to `EXPIRED` and its `version` increases by 1. Cases already in `GRANTED` or `EXPIRED` are skipped. `EXPIRED` is terminal.

### 3.12. Winding Down, Closing Jurisdictions and Dissolution

Leaving jurisdictions is the mirror image of entering them, and the order is enforced: foreign qualifications close first, the home registration second, the entity itself last.

**Beginning the wind-down.** A `ComplianceOfficer` starts the process on an entity. The entity's `lifecycle_status` must be `ACTIVE`; from any other value the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success `lifecycle_status` becomes `WINDING_DOWN`, `final_report_required` is set true, and the entity's `version` increases by 1.

**Aborting the wind-down.** The same officer may reverse the decision, returning the entity from `WINDING_DOWN` to `ACTIVE`. Two conditions must hold: the entity must currently be `WINDING_DOWN`, and no filing of kind `DISSOLUTION` in status `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` may exist on any registration of the entity — once a dissolution has actually been lodged with a jurisdiction, the decision can no longer be taken back, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success `lifecycle_status` returns to `ACTIVE` and the entity's `version` increases by 1. `final_report_required` is **deliberately not cleared**: it stays true, so the final-report obligation survives the abort and will still have to be settled if the entity is wound down again later.

**Withdrawing a foreign qualification.** A `FilingAgent` withdraws a foreign registration, optionally supplying `tax_clearance_reference`. The gates are: the registration must be `ACTIVE` and its `registration_kind` must be `FOREIGN` and a filing of kind `WITHDRAWAL` on this registration must already be in status `ACCEPTED`, failing any of which the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; then, when the governing jurisdiction rule has `requires_tax_clearance` true, the request must carry a non-blank `tax_clearance_reference`, else `ERR_TAX_CLEARANCE_REQUIRED`; then no appointment on the registration may be in `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` with the current clock date strictly before its `resignation_effective_on`, else `ERR_RESIGNATION_NOTICE_ACTIVE`. A `HOME` registration can never be withdrawn.

On success the registration moves `ACTIVE → WITHDRAWN` and `closed_on` is stamped with the current clock date. Every obligation of the registration in one of the four open statuses is voided — status `VOIDED`, `satisfied_on` stamped at the current clock date, `total_due_cents` set to 0, pending reminders suppressed — with **no exemption for any obligation type**. Every appointment on the registration in `PROPOSED`, `APPOINTED` or `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` is set to `RESIGNED` with `of_record` false and its own `version` increased by 1, and `agent_of_record_id` becomes null. The `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` leg of that sweep is live rather than vestigial: rung 31 keeps an *open* notice window out of this path, so what the leg actually bites on is a notice whose window has already closed but which no ops-run has yet retired, and without it such a notice would sit in `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` on a terminal registration for ever. The registration's `version` increases by 1, and the entity's posture and the client's rollup are recomputed. `sop_priority_hold` is not cleared by closure.

**Terminating the home registration.** A `FilingAgent` terminates the home registration, optionally supplying `tax_clearance_reference`. The gates fire in the declared order given in Section 2.9.1: the registration must be `ACTIVE`, its `registration_kind` must be `HOME`, and a filing of kind `DISSOLUTION` on this registration must already be in status `ACCEPTED`, failing any of which the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; then the tax-clearance gate; then every `FOREIGN` registration of the same entity must be in `WITHDRAWN` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT`, else `ERR_FOREIGN_QUALIFICATIONS_OUTSTANDING`; then the resignation-notice gate. A `FOREIGN` registration can never be terminated.

On success the registration moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and `closed_on` is stamped with the current clock date. Obligations of the registration in one of the four open statuses are voided in the same way as on withdrawal, **except that obligations whose `obligation_type` is `FINAL_REPORT` are left untouched**: the final-report duty deliberately survives termination of the registration it belongs to, because it is the last thing the jurisdiction expects to receive. Appointments are resigned exactly as on withdrawal, `agent_of_record_id` becomes null, and the registration's `version` increases by 1.

**Dissolving the entity.** A `ComplianceOfficer` dissolves the entity. The gates fire in the declared order of Section 2.9.1: the entity must be `WINDING_DOWN`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; then every registration of the entity must be in `WITHDRAWN`, `TERMINATED` or `CLOSED_PERMANENT` — registered-agent service cannot end while any registration is still occupied — else `ERR_ACTIVE_REGISTRATIONS_EXIST`; then, when `final_report_required` is true, every `FINAL_REPORT` obligation of the entity must be in `SATISFIED` or `WAIVED`, else `ERR_FINAL_REPORT_OUTSTANDING`. The referential sweep genuinely precedes the safety gate here: an entity that still holds an open registration **and** still owes a final report reports the open registration.

The final-report sweep looks only at obligations of kind `FINAL_REPORT` hanging off the entity's own registrations, in every status but the two it accepts, and it is skipped entirely when `final_report_required` is false. On success `lifecycle_status` becomes `DISSOLVED`, the parallel `boi_status` axis is forced to `NOT_REQUIRED` from wherever it stood while both beneficial-ownership deadlines are left exactly as they were, `final_report_required` is left standing at whatever it held, the entity's `version` increases by 1 — once for the whole operation, both axes included — and the owning client's `portfolio_entity_count` is recomputed as a rollup write that moves no client version. The entity drops out of that count. `DISSOLVED` is terminal on the lifecycle axis.

### 3.13. The Ops-Run Checkpoint

Time only has consequences when an administrator asks for them. Setting the logical clock forward changes no entity; it is the ops-run checkpoint, invoked by a `PlatformAdmin` with no request body at all, that sweeps the estate at the current clock instant and propagates everything that has become due. Its work, in the order the sweep performs it, is:

1. Advance every open compliance obligation along the monotone status ladder, re-pricing `late_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents` on each row that actually moves.
2. Run the reminder send pass, flipping each qualifying `PENDING` notice to `SENT`.
3. Retire every appointment in `RESIGNATION_NOTICED` whose `resignation_effective_on` has arrived, moving it to `RESIGNED` and re-deriving the agent of record on its registration.
4. Drive registrations from `DELINQUENT` to `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` where an obligation's dissolution horizon has passed, and from `ADMIN_DISSOLVED` to `CLOSED_PERMANENT` where the reinstatement window has lapsed.
5. Expire every reinstatement case whose `window_close_date` is behind the clock.
6. Drive the beneficial-ownership axis of every entity past its deadline to `DELINQUENT`.
7. Re-derive the agent of record on every registration, then recompute every registration's `delinquency_rank` and every entity and client rollup.

Those seven steps each run exactly once, in the order written, and a later step reads whatever an earlier one wrote: **the sweep is multi-hop within a single run**. An obligation that reaches `DELINQUENT` in step 1 drives its `ACTIVE` registration to `DELINQUENT` inside that same step and is therefore already eligible for step 4's dissolution sweep on the same invocation, and a registration dissolved in step 4 whose window is already behind the clock is closed permanently by the very next pass of that same step. No step ever re-enters itself, so the depth is bounded by the seven steps themselves. What the sweep does *not* do is run the cure hop: a registration in `DELINQUENT` is never returned to `ACTIVE` by an ops-run, only by the satisfaction or waiver of its last delinquent obligation.

The response is a bare object of exactly seven counters reporting what the sweep did: `obligations_advanced`, `reminders_sent`, `appointments_resigned`, `registrations_dissolved`, `registrations_closed_permanent`, `cases_expired` and `boi_axes_delinquent`. Each counter reports the number of rows that actually changed state on this run, not the number of rows examined; `cases_expired` counts both the cases step 5 expires on their own window and the cases step 4 expires by closing their registration permanently, while the obligations that same closure voids and the agent-of-record flips of step 7 are reported by no counter at all. A sweep that finds nothing to do returns every counter at 0 and mutates nothing at all — no version bumps, no `updated_at` refreshes — and running the sweep twice at the same clock instant is therefore harmless: the second run finds no qualifying row and reports all zeros.
