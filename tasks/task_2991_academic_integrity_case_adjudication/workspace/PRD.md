# Academic Integrity Case Adjudication API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university academic integrity office sits between two constituencies that rarely agree. Faculty want a fast, low-friction way to report suspected misconduct and to resolve minor incidents themselves; students want documented notice, an advisor, a hearing before a panel that has no stake in the outcome, and a route to appeal. The office in the middle must run both of those tracks at once, on the same case record, while keeping a defensible audit trail of who decided what, on what evidence, and in what order. This document specifies the service that carries out that work.

The system models the full life of an allegation: intake of a faculty report with hashed evidence attachments and a text-similarity reading, office triage, formal notice to the accused student and the opening of a response window, an informal instructor resolution track that is deliberately blind to the student's disciplinary history, a formal hearing track with rooms, panels, quorum and recorded votes, a sanction ladder that runs from a written warning through grade penalties and probation to a recommendation of expulsion, appeal on the three recognised grounds, and — running on its own clock, driven by the registrar rather than the office — the posting and eventual expungement of a transcript notation. Two derived oversight metrics sit on top: per-department faculty training currency, and a reporting rate normalised per one thousand enrolled students, which together flag departments that appear to be under-reporting or over-reporting relative to their peers.

Three design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **segregation of duties**: the reporting faculty member, the hearing panel, the integrity office, the appeals dean and the registrar are five separate hands, and no single acting identity may occupy two of them on the same case. Second, **two independent state dimensions on one case record**: the procedural dimension and the transcript-notation dimension advance on different triggers, by different roles, and each contains a legitimate cycle (a case may be re-heard; an expungement may be re-requested after a denial). Third, **an assessment that is deliberately computed twice**: policy bars instructors from reading the repeat-offense database, so the same case yields one severity reading for the office and a different, lower one for the instructor, and both are persisted and both are readable. Implementers should expect these two numbers to disagree; that disagreement is the product, not a bug. All business paths are mounted under `/integrity`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Acting Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, which names the acting individual, and `X-User-Role`, which names the capacity in which that individual is acting. There are exactly six roles, spelled exactly as follows: `IntegrityOfficer`, `FacultyReporter`, `PanelChair`, `Student`, `AppealsDean` and `RegistrarOfficer`.

Authentication is checked before anything else. If `X-User-Role` is absent, or is present but carries an empty string or a value consisting only of spaces, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same rejection applies when `X-User-ID` is absent, empty or whitespace-only, with one narrow tolerance: `IntegrityOfficer`, `AppealsDean` and `RegistrarOfficer` may omit `X-User-ID` on read-only endpoints, because those three roles are not tied to any stored record. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` is also the answer when `X-User-Role` names a value outside the six-role set, and when a role that is backed by a stored entity carries an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no such row.

Three of the six roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `FacultyReporter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FacultyMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a faculty member compare that resource's faculty foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PanelChair`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `FacultyMember`. When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student` row, and student-side writes compare the target case's student reference to `X-User-ID`.

The other three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `IntegrityOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque office-staff identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `AppealsDean`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque dean identifier, treated the same way. When `X-User-Role` is `RegistrarOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque registrar identifier, also never resolved against stored rows.

Once the acting identity is established, the role must appear in the authorized-role set of the endpoint being called; §4 lists that set for all 74 endpoints. A role that is authenticated but not authorized receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). A role that is authorized for the endpoint but is not the specific owner the operation demands receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership is endpoint-specific: reporter actions require the acting identity to equal the originating report's `reporter_id`; student actions require it to equal the case's `student_id`; hearing-session actions require it to equal the session's `chair_id`; recording a `HELD` or `NO_SHOW` meeting outcome requires it to equal the meeting's `instructor_id`; and casting a panel vote requires the acting identity to equal the session's `chair_id`, because the chair records each panelist's vote onto the record rather than each panelist voting for themselves.

The role boundaries are strict in both directions, and the following prohibitions are as much part of the contract as the permissions. An `IntegrityOfficer` may not create a report, attach evidence, propose an informal resolution, convene, adjourn or decide a hearing, cast a panel vote, file, screen or decide an appeal, post a transcript notation, or decide an expungement request. A `FacultyReporter` may not open a case, triage a report, propose a formal office sanction, schedule a hearing, or touch any panel or appeal operation. A `PanelChair` may not create a report, propose a sanction, or decide an appeal. A `Student` may not perform any office, panel, dean or registrar write, and is additionally barred from the unscoped case list and the department list. An `AppealsDean` may not create, impose or complete a sanction — which is why a reduced sanction can only ever come into existence as a side effect of an appeal outcome — and may not decide a hearing. A `RegistrarOfficer` may not perform any adjudication write at all, dismissal included; the registrar owns the notation dimension exclusively, and posting a notation can never advance or close a case. Each of these attempts returns `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, a `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts every record matching the request's filters rather than only those on the current page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every temporal rule — notice periods, response deadlines, appeal windows, training validity, pattern-detection windows, expungement waiting periods — is evaluated against a single server-held **logical clock**, and every timestamp the server mints (`created_at`, `updated_at`, `submitted_at`, `sent_at`, `cast_at`, `imposed_at`, `decided_at`, `filed_at`, `triaged_at`, `returned_at`, `acknowledged_at`) is that clock's current value. Before any client moves it, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A test-driving capability, available to all six roles, advances or rewinds the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes back an object carrying `current_utc`. A missing or malformed `current_utc` is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a request with no usable identity headers is an `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Moving the clock has no direct write effect on stored rows, but it changes the answers that derived fields give: each `FacultyMember`'s `training_status`, and each `Department`'s `training_completion_pct` and `monitoring_flag`, are re-derived against the new clock the next time they are read.

All instants exchanged with the API are ISO 8601 UTC. Server-minted audit timestamps carry three fractional digits, as in `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Every date-range window described in this document is **inclusive at both ends** unless the text explicitly says otherwise; where a boundary is exclusive, the text says "strictly".

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Thirteen of the fifteen resource types carry the full set of system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID string. It carries no type prefix; a case's `id` and a sanction's `id` are indistinguishable by shape.
- `created_at` — the ISO 8601 UTC instant, with three fractional digits, at which the row was created, taken from the logical clock.
- `updated_at` — the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent modification, refreshed on every mutation including mutations that only rewrite derived fields.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row.

Two resource types are immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: an **EvidenceItem**, which is an attached exhibit that can never be edited or withdrawn, and a **PanelVote**, which is a recorded ballot. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field; there is no update endpoint for either, and responses for them must not invent those two keys.

Because several derived fields on the `Case` record are recomputed by operations that target other rows entirely, an implementation should treat a derived-field rewrite as a real mutation of the case: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` exactly as a status change does. That coupling is what gives the optimistic-concurrency mechanism of §2.8 its teeth.

### 2.6. Path Resolution for Sub-Resource Reads

Single-resource and sub-resource paths take exactly one path variable, always spelled `{id}`. Where a path names a parent and then a collection beneath it — a report's evidence, a case's notices, a case's advisor engagements, a case's appeals, a faculty member's training completions, a department's reporting metrics, a case's severity assessment, or either of the case's notation operations — the parent identifier is resolved first, left to right. A request whose parent identifier matches no stored row is rejected immediately with that parent's not-found error, before the server looks at anything nested, at any request body, or at any business rule. Asking for the evidence of a report that does not exist yields `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`; asking for the notices of a case that does not exist yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`.

The same left-to-right discipline governs foreign keys carried in a request body. Body references are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and the first unresolvable reference determines the error. The full catalogue of not-found errors is one per resource type: `ERR_DEPARTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACULTY_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADVISOR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEETING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANEL_VOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SANCTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, each returning HTTP 404. A faculty reference is a faculty reference wherever it appears: an unknown `instructor_id`, `reporter_id`, `chair_id`, `panelist_id` or element of `panelist_ids` all produce `ERR_FACULTY_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`. A `replaces_sanction_id` filter that names no stored sanction produces `ERR_SANCTION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Rounding

This is a money-free domain, but it is a heavily arithmetic one, and the arithmetic must be reproducible to the cent. All percentages and index values — `evidence_strength_score`, `provisional_severity_index`, `severity_index`, `informal_severity_index`, `baseline_course_grade_pct`, `adjusted_course_grade_pct`, `grade_penalty_delta_pct`, `training_completion_pct`, `reporting_rate_per_1k_submitted` and every derived percentage returned by a computed read — carry exactly 2 decimal places and are produced with decimal arithmetic, never binary floating point. Every rounding step named anywhere in this document uses **round half up**. Ladder ranks, point counts, page counts, minute counts and vote counts are integers and are never rounded to decimals.

Numbers cross the API boundary as JSON numbers, not strings. An input decimal carrying more than two fractional digits is a shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Where a formula in §3 prescribes an order of rounding — rounding each component before weighting rather than after, or selecting an escalation band from a pre-escalation value rather than a post-escalation one — that order is part of the specification and not an implementation detail. Departing from it produces answers that differ in the second decimal place or in whole ladder ranks, and the difference is observable.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Case Record

Two write operations propose a sanction against a case, and both of them are written against a snapshot of that case that the client read earlier. Because the case's severity assessment can be rewritten underneath a client by activity on a completely different case belonging to the same student, both operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the `Case` row.

`POST /integrity/sanctions` requires a non-optional `expected_case_version` field in its request body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on the target case. `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution` requires the same non-optional `expected_case_version` field, carrying the `version` the instructor last observed. In both cases the server compares the supplied value with the target case's current `version`; if they differ, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created. The authoritative value to send is the `version` field of the case as returned by `GET /integrity/cases/{id}`.

The office-side guard exists because an accepted formal proposal supersedes any sanction already sitting in `PROPOSED` on that case; a stale proposal must never be allowed to displace a newer one the client has not seen. The instructor-side guard exists because the office may have re-assessed the case's severity — through the sibling-case recomputation described in §2.10 — between the instructor's read and the instructor's write, which would have moved the permitted informal band under the instructor's feet. Every accepted write on a case bumps its `version`, so a client that has not re-read the case after any earlier accepted write on the same case is rejected. No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a concurrency check.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must carry the error of the **earliest-ranked** rule that matches. The ranking below is exhaustive and strictly ordered; it is the single most consequential contract in this document, because several endpoints are guarded by a dozen rules at once. Every rule is evaluated before any side effect is applied, and no partial write is ever observable: a rejected request leaves the store byte-identical to its pre-request state, including every derived counter and ledger.

Ranks 1 and 2 — **authentication**, on every endpoint including the clock-setting endpoint: missing, blank or whitespace-only headers, an unknown role name, or an entity-backed role whose `X-User-ID` resolves to no row → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).

Rank 3 — **role authorization**, on every endpoint: the resolved role is absent from the endpoint's authorized set → `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

Rank 4 — **ownership**, on `POST /integrity/reports/{id}/submit`, `POST /integrity/evidence-items`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-response`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notation/expungement-request`, `POST /integrity/appeals`, `POST /integrity/notifications/{id}/acknowledge`, `POST /integrity/case-meetings/{id}/outcome`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/convene`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/decide`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/adjourn`, `POST /integrity/panel-votes` and `POST /integrity/advisor-engagements`: the acting identity is not the owner the endpoint demands → `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).

Ranks 5 and 6 — **request shape**, on every write endpoint. Rank 5 covers a required body field that is missing or null, a field of the wrong JSON type, an enum value outside its declared set, a string violating its length or pattern rule, an integer outside its declared range, a decimal with more than two fractional digits, and any unknown field. Rank 6 covers conditional presence on `POST /integrity/evidence-items`, `POST /integrity/sanctions`, `POST /integrity/panel-votes`, `POST /integrity/appeals/{id}/decide`, `POST /integrity/advisor-engagements/{id}/review` and `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notation/expungement-decision`: `similarity_pct` must be present exactly when `kind` is `SIMILARITY_REPORT`; `decision_reason` is required on every deny decision; `reduced_ladder_rank` is required exactly when `outcome` is `SANCTION_REDUCED`; `adjourn_reason` is required when adjourning. Both ranks return `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).

Rank 7 — **pagination**, on every list endpoint → `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).

Rank 8 — **path existence**: an `{id}` in the path that matches no stored row → the resource's not-found error (404).

Rank 9 — **body foreign-key existence**, on every write endpoint carrying a foreign key, checked in the endpoint's declared input order → the referenced resource's not-found error (404).

Rank 10 — **uniqueness**, on `POST /integrity/departments`, `POST /integrity/faculty-members`, `POST /integrity/students`, `POST /integrity/course-offerings` and `POST /integrity/hearing-rooms`: a duplicate department `code`, hearing-room `code`, faculty `email`, student `email`, or a duplicate pairing of `course_code` with `term_code` → `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409).

Ranks 11 through 22 — **state**, all returning HTTP 409, in this exact order:

- 11: report status does not admit the operation, on `POST /integrity/reports/{id}/submit`, `POST /integrity/reports/{id}/triage`, `POST /integrity/evidence-items`, `POST /integrity/cases` and `POST /integrity/cases/legacy-import` → `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`.
- 12: a case already exists for the supplied `report_id`, on `POST /integrity/cases` and `POST /integrity/cases/legacy-import` → `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_OPENED`.
- 13: the case's `adjudication_status` does not admit the operation, on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/pathway`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notifications`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/dismiss`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-response`, `POST /integrity/case-meetings`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions`, `POST /integrity/sanctions`, `POST /integrity/sanctions/{id}/impose`, `POST /integrity/sanctions/{id}/complete`, `POST /integrity/appeals` and `POST /integrity/appeals/{id}/screen` → `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`.
- 14: the case's `pathway` is already `INFORMAL` or `FORMAL`, on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/pathway` → `ERR_CASE_PATHWAY_ALREADY_SET`.
- 15: the case's `pathway` is `UNASSIGNED` or is set to the pathway the operation does not belong to, on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions` and `POST /integrity/appeals/{id}/decide` with a remand outcome → `ERR_CASE_PATHWAY_NOT_SET`.
- 16: the case's `notation_status` does not admit the operation, on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notation/post`, `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notation/expungement-request` and `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notation/expungement-decision` → `ERR_NOTATION_STATE_INVALID`.
- 17: the session status does not admit the operation, on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/convene`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/adjourn`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/decide`, `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/cancel` and `POST /integrity/panel-votes` → `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`.
- 18: the meeting is no longer `SCHEDULED`, on `POST /integrity/case-meetings/{id}/outcome` → `ERR_MEETING_STATE_INVALID`.
- 19: the sanction status does not admit the operation, on `POST /integrity/sanctions/{id}/impose` and `POST /integrity/sanctions/{id}/complete` → `ERR_SANCTION_STATE_INVALID`.
- 20: the appeal status does not admit the operation, on `POST /integrity/appeals/{id}/screen` and `POST /integrity/appeals/{id}/decide` → `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`.
- 21: the engagement is not `REQUESTED`, on `POST /integrity/advisor-engagements/{id}/review` → `ERR_ADVISOR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.
- 22: the notification's `acknowledged_at` is already set, on `POST /integrity/notifications/{id}/acknowledge` → `ERR_NOTIFICATION_ALREADY_ACKNOWLEDGED`.

Rank 23 — **optimistic concurrency**, on `POST /integrity/sanctions` and `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution` → `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (412). This runs after every state check, so a request that is both stale and state-invalid reports the state error.

Ranks 24 through 31 — **due-process and competence guards**, all HTTP 409 except where noted:

- 24: the submitting reporter's `training_status` is `LAPSED`, on `POST /integrity/reports/{id}/submit` → `ERR_REPORTER_TRAINING_LAPSED`.
- 25: the nominated chair's `training_status` is `LAPSED`, on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions` → `ERR_CHAIR_TRAINING_LAPSED`.
- 26: on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions` and `POST /integrity/panel-votes`, `chair_id` or any element of `panelist_ids` equals the reporter of the case's originating report or the `instructor_id` of the case's offering, or a vote is cast by a panelist who is the reporter → `ERR_PANEL_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`.
- 27: `advisor_kind` is `ATTORNEY` while the case's `recommended_ladder_rank` is below 6, on `POST /integrity/advisor-engagements` → `ERR_ATTORNEY_ADVISOR_NOT_PERMITTED`.
- 28: `advisor_kind` is `ATTORNEY` with `advisor_bar_licensed` false, on `POST /integrity/advisor-engagements` → `ERR_ATTORNEY_NOT_LICENSED`.
- 29: the case's `recommended_ladder_rank` is greater than 3, on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/pathway` when the requested pathway is `INFORMAL` and on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution` → `ERR_INFORMAL_PATHWAY_NOT_PERMITTED`.
- 30: the case is on the `INFORMAL` pathway in `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED` and the appeal's `grounds` is not `PROCEDURE`, on `POST /integrity/appeals/{id}/screen` → `ERR_INFORMAL_RESOLUTION_NOT_APPEALABLE`.
- 31: on `POST /integrity/reports/{id}/submit` and on `POST /integrity/reports/{id}/triage` with an accept decision → `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`.

Ranks 32 through 37 — **cross-entity membership guards**:

- 32: `grade_component_code` names no code in the case's offering gradebook, on `POST /integrity/sanctions` and `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution` → `ERR_GRADE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
- 33: the resolved sanction kind is `GRADE_PENALTY_ASSIGNMENT` and `grade_component_code` is absent, or the resolved kind is anything else and `grade_component_code` is present, on the same two endpoints → `ERR_GRADE_COMPONENT_REQUIRED` (409).
- 34: the report's `component_scores` array does not contain exactly one entry per code in the offering's `grade_components`, or contains a code the gradebook does not declare, on `POST /integrity/reports` and `POST /integrity/sanctions/{id}/impose` → `ERR_COMPONENT_SCORES_INCOMPLETE` (409).
- 35: `panelist_id` is not an element of the session's `panelist_ids`, on `POST /integrity/panel-votes` → `ERR_NOT_A_PANELIST` (409).
- 36: `verdict` is `RESPONSIBLE` and `severity_recommendation` is absent, on `POST /integrity/panel-votes` → `ERR_SEVERITY_RECOMMENDATION_REQUIRED` (409).
- 37: `verdict` is `NOT_RESPONSIBLE` or `ABSTAIN` and `severity_recommendation` is present, on `POST /integrity/panel-votes` → `ERR_SEVERITY_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_ALLOWED` (409).

Ranks 38 through 45 — **notice, overlap and waiting-period guards**, all HTTP 409:

- 38: insufficient hearing notice, on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions` → `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`.
- 39: buffered room-interval collision, on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions` → `ERR_ROOM_BUFFER_CONFLICT`.
- 40: instructor calendar collision, on `POST /integrity/case-meetings` → `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CALENDAR_CONFLICT`.
- 41: a malformed or out-of-range interval, or an outcome recorded too early, on `POST /integrity/case-meetings`, `POST /integrity/case-meetings/{id}/outcome` and `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions` → `ERR_MEETING_WINDOW_INVALID`.
- 42: the student's response window is still open, on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/convene` → `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_OPEN`.
- 43: the appeal filing window has closed, on `POST /integrity/appeals` → `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
- 44: `effective_from` is earlier than the case's `decided_at`, on `POST /integrity/sanctions` and `POST /integrity/sanctions/{id}/impose` → `ERR_SANCTION_EFFECTIVE_BEFORE_DECISION`.
- 45: the expungement waiting period has not elapsed, on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notation/expungement-request` → `ERR_EXPUNGEMENT_TOO_EARLY`.

Ranks 46 through 61 — **capacity, band and count guards**, all HTTP 409: 46 `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_OUT_OF_RANGE` on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notifications`; 47 `ERR_ADVISOR_LIMIT_REACHED` on `POST /integrity/advisor-engagements`; 48 `ERR_ADVISOR_REQUEST_PENDING` on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/convene`; 49 `ERR_PANEL_SIZE_INVALID` on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions`; 50 `ERR_ROOM_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions`; 51 `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE` on `POST /integrity/panel-votes`; 52 `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` and 53 `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_VOTES`, in that order, on `POST /integrity/hearing-sessions/{id}/decide`; 54 `ERR_SANCTION_ABOVE_LADDER_BAND` on `POST /integrity/sanctions` and `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution`; 55 `ERR_SANCTION_BELOW_LADDER_BAND` on `POST /integrity/sanctions`; 56 `ERR_SANCTION_KIND_RANK_MISMATCH` on `POST /integrity/sanctions` and `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/informal-resolution`; 57 `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED` on `POST /integrity/appeals`; 58 `ERR_APPEAL_GROUNDS_MISMATCH`, 59 `ERR_REDUCED_RANK_INVALID` and 60 `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_REACHED`, in that order, on `POST /integrity/appeals/{id}/decide`; and 61 `ERR_NOTATION_NOT_EXPUNGEABLE` on `POST /integrity/cases/{id}/notation/expungement-request`.

Three collisions are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. On hearing scheduling, the state check at rank 13 outranks the notice-period check at rank 38: a case that is already `HEARING_SCHEDULED` and is handed a second session whose `start_at` is only two days after the governing notice reports `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, not `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`. On advisor requests, the attorney-eligibility check at rank 27 outranks the advisor-count check at rank 47: a case at `recommended_ladder_rank` 3 that already holds two approved engagements and receives a third request naming an attorney reports `ERR_ATTORNEY_ADVISOR_NOT_PERMITTED`, not `ERR_ADVISOR_LIMIT_REACHED`. On formal sanction creation, the gradebook-lookup check at rank 32 outranks the effective-date check at rank 44: a decided case handed an assignment-grade-penalty proposal whose `grade_component_code` is absent from the offering and whose `effective_from` predates the finding reports `ERR_GRADE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, not `ERR_SANCTION_EFFECTIVE_BEFORE_DECISION`.

### 2.10. The Two Independent Case Dimensions

A `Case` row carries two state fields that advance on separate tracks and must never be conflated.

`adjudication_status` tracks the progress of the allegation itself through the twelve values `INTAKE_REVIEW`, `NOTIFIED`, `INFORMAL_PROPOSED`, `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED`, `FORMAL_REFERRED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, `HEARING_IN_PROGRESS`, `DECIDED`, `APPEAL_UNDER_REVIEW`, `SANCTION_IMPOSED`, `CLOSED` and `DISMISSED`. It begins at `INTAKE_REVIEW`; `CLOSED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal. It is driven by the integrity office, the reporting instructor, the student, the panel chair and the appeals dean.

`notation_status` tracks whether a permanent transcript notation exists, through the six values `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `PENDING_POSTING`, `POSTED`, `EXPUNGEMENT_REQUESTED`, `EXPUNGED` and `EXPUNGEMENT_DENIED`. It begins at `NOT_APPLICABLE`; `EXPUNGED` is terminal. It is driven by the registrar and the student, never by the integrity office.

Neither dimension constrains the other except where a rule in §3 explicitly names the other field. A case may sit in `adjudication_status` `CLOSED` while its `notation_status` is still `POSTED`, and it may sit in `EXPUNGEMENT_REQUESTED` while an appeal keeps the adjudication dimension in `APPEAL_UNDER_REVIEW`. Both dimensions may also cycle: the adjudication dimension returns from `HEARING_IN_PROGRESS` to `FORMAL_REFERRED` on an adjournment and from `APPEAL_UNDER_REVIEW` to `FORMAL_REFERRED` on a remand, in both cases making a further hearing possible; the notation dimension cycles between `EXPUNGEMENT_REQUESTED` and `EXPUNGEMENT_DENIED` without bound. Where an operation touches both dimensions in one request, the `version` still advances exactly once.

Three operations propagate beyond the row they name, and each must run to completion before the response is written. Imposing a sanction runs a five-part cascade that touches the sanction, the case's two dimensions, the student's derived roll-up, every other open case belonging to the same student, and any stale proposals sitting on those other cases. Deciding an appeal as overturned runs a four-part cascade of the same shape in reverse. Deciding an appeal as remanded unwinds the finding, vacates every live sanction, resets the notation dimension when nothing was posted, and re-derives the case's severity. All three are specified hop by hop in §3; the discipline that matters here is that a re-assessment of a sibling case is ordered by that case's `created_at` ascending, that every derived counter named in §2.11 is left consistent, and that no cascade is ever partially applied.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

Three derived counters are ledgers rather than free-standing fields. Each must equal its defining sum after every request, including every rejected one.

`HearingRoom.booked_minutes_total` always equals, summed over the room's hearing sessions whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `CONVENED`, `DECIDED` or `ADJOURNED`, the whole minutes between the session's `start_at` and `end_at` plus twice the room's `buffer_minutes`. Scheduling a session adds exactly that amount; cancelling a scheduled session subtracts exactly the amount that scheduling added, returning the ledger to its previous value. Adjourning releases nothing, because the room was genuinely occupied.

`Student.active_sanction_points` always equals the sum of `ladder_rank` over that student's sanctions whose status is exactly `IMPOSED`. Sanctions in `PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `VACATED` or `COMPLETED` contribute nothing. Imposing then completing therefore returns the counter to its prior value, and imposing then vacating on appeal returns it to the same prior value by a different route.

`Department.open_case_count` always equals the number of cases whose offering belongs to that department and whose `adjudication_status` is neither `CLOSED` nor `DISMISSED`. Opening a case increments it; dismissing the case, completing its imposed sanction, or overturning it on appeal each decrement it exactly once. A legacy import never increments it.

### 2.12. The Sanction Ladder

Sanction severity is expressed as an integer `ladder_rank` from 1 to 8, and each rank has exactly one corresponding `kind`: rank 1 is `WARNING`, rank 2 is `GRADE_PENALTY_ASSIGNMENT`, rank 3 is `GRADE_PENALTY_COURSE`, rank 4 is `TRANSCRIPT_NOTATION_ONLY`, rank 5 is `DISCIPLINARY_PROBATION`, rank 6 is `SUSPENSION_ONE_TERM`, rank 7 is `SUSPENSION_ONE_YEAR` and rank 8 is `EXPULSION_RECOMMENDATION`. Any request that pairs a rank with a different kind is rejected with `ERR_SANCTION_KIND_RANK_MISMATCH`.

Four thresholds are expressed against this scale and are referenced throughout §3. A transcript notation becomes applicable only at rank 4 or above. An informal instructor resolution may never exceed rank 3. An attorney advisor may be engaged only when the case's `recommended_ladder_rank` is 6 or above. A five-member hearing panel is required at rank 6 or above, and a three-member panel below it.

Rank 4 occupies an unusual position: the severity tier table in §3 never yields it, because the tiers step from 3 straight to 5. `TRANSCRIPT_NOTATION_ONLY` can therefore enter the system only by a hearing panel recommending rank 4 as a floor, or by an appeal reducing a higher sanction to rank 4. An implementation that derives the kind solely from the tier table will never emit it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Departments, Faculty Competence and Reporting Oversight

Registering a department captures its `code` (unique, two to eight characters of uppercase letters and digits), its `name` (one to 120 characters), its `enrolled_student_count` (at least 1) and its `faculty_headcount` (at least 1). The two census counts are supplied figures, not derived from the rows actually created, and they serve as the denominators of the two oversight metrics. A department also exposes four derived fields: `open_case_count`, `training_completion_pct`, `reporting_rate_per_1k_submitted` and `monitoring_flag`, which start at 0, 0.00, 0.00 and `NORMAL` respectively. Because a brand-new department has a reporting rate of exactly 0.00, its `monitoring_flag` is computed as `UNDER_REPORTING` the moment it is created, not `NORMAL`. Departments may be listed with a `code` filter and a `monitoring_flag` filter.

Faculty members are registered against a department, with `department_id`, `full_name` and a `email` that is unique across faculty and must contain exactly one `@` character. Each member carries four derived fields: `training_status`, `training_completed_at`, `training_module_code` and `reports_submitted_count`. A newly created member has no training on record, so `training_status` is `LAPSED`, `training_completed_at` and `training_module_code` are null, and `reports_submitted_count` is 0. Creating a member recomputes the employing department's `training_completion_pct` and `monitoring_flag`. Faculty may be listed with `department_id`, `training_status` and `email` filters.

Training is recorded by attaching a completion to a faculty member, supplying a `module_code` and a `completed_at` instant. `completed_at` may lie in the past but never after the current logical clock. A member's `training_completed_at` and `training_module_code` are then re-derived from the newest completion on record — newest meaning the greatest `completed_at`, breaking ties by the most recently created row — and `training_status` is re-derived from it. A member is `CURRENT` when the most recent completion satisfies `completed_at` plus 730 days at or after the current logical clock; a completion exactly 730 days old still counts as current, while one 731 days old does not. A member with no completion at all is `LAPSED`. Recording a completion also recomputes the department's `training_completion_pct` and `monitoring_flag`. Note that `training_status` is a function of the clock, so moving the clock forward can turn a current member lapsed with no write against the member at all.

`training_completion_pct` is the count of that department's faculty members whose `training_status` is `CURRENT`, multiplied by 100, divided by the department's `faculty_headcount`, rounded to two decimals. Because the denominator is the census headcount, the percentage can sit below 100.00 even when every created member is current: five headcount with four current members gives 80.00, four headcount with two current members gives 50.00, and three headcount with one current member gives 33.33 (from 33.3333 recurring).

The reporting rate exists in two variants over the same 365-day window. The `SUBMITTED` variant counts the department's integrity reports — those whose offering belongs to the department — whose `status` is anything other than `DRAFT` and whose `submitted_at` falls inside the inclusive window running from 365 days before the evaluation instant up to and including that instant. A report that was returned for more information and resubmitted is counted once, by its original `submitted_at`. The `ADJUDICATED` variant instead counts the department's cases whose `responsibility_finding` is `RESPONSIBLE`, whose `overturned` is false, and whose `decided_at` falls inside the same inclusive window; cases dismissed or still undecided are excluded. Both variants divide the counted numerator by the department's `enrolled_student_count`, multiply by 1000, and round to two decimals. A report submitted exactly 365 days before the evaluation instant is inside the window; one submitted 366 days before is outside. A department of 2000 enrolled students with three submitted reports of which one reached an upheld finding therefore reports 1.50 on the submitted basis and 0.50 on the adjudicated basis; a department of 1000 with no submitted reports reports 0.00 on both; a department of 500 with two submitted reports and no decisions reports 4.00 and 0.00.

`monitoring_flag` is chosen by evaluating four branches in a pinned order, first match winning; a department can satisfy several branches at once, and evaluating them in another order yields a different flag. First, the peer median rate is computed as the median of `reporting_rate_per_1k_submitted` across all departments including this one, sorted ascending; for an even number of departments the median is the two middle values added together, divided by two, rounded to two decimals. Then: branch one selects `UNDER_REPORTING` when this department's rate equals exactly 0.00; branch two selects `ELEVATED` when the rate is strictly greater than twice the peer median rounded to two decimals; branch three selects `TRAINING_DEFICIT` when `training_completion_pct` is strictly less than 80.00; branch four selects `NORMAL`. With three departments at rates 0.00, 1.50 and 4.00 the peer median is 1.50 and the elevated threshold is 3.00: the department at 0.00 is `UNDER_REPORTING` even though its 50.00 training percentage would also satisfy branch three; the department at 1.50 is `NORMAL`, because 1.50 is not greater than 3.00 and 80.00 is not less than 80.00; the department at 4.00 is `ELEVATED`, branch two winning over its 33.33 training deficit. With only two departments at 0.00 and 1.50 the median is 0.75 and the threshold is 1.50, so the department at 1.50 is `NORMAL` — the comparison is strict at the exact boundary. A department at 79.99 training completion with a non-zero, non-elevated rate is `TRAINING_DEFICIT`.

The department reporting-metrics read exposes all of this in one response. It accepts an optional `basis` (`SUBMITTED` or `ADJUDICATED`, defaulting to `SUBMITTED`) and an optional `as_of` instant (defaulting to the current logical clock; an `as_of` after the clock is permitted). It returns `department_id`, the `basis` used, the `as_of` used, `window_days` (365), `numerator` (the counted rows), `enrolled_student_count`, `reporting_rate_per_1k`, `peer_median_rate`, `elevated_threshold_rate`, `training_completion_pct`, `faculty_current_count`, `faculty_headcount` and `monitoring_flag`. The read has a side effect that depends on the basis: with `basis` omitted or `SUBMITTED` it recomputes and persists the department's `reporting_rate_per_1k_submitted`, `training_completion_pct` and `monitoring_flag`; with `basis` set to `ADJUDICATED` it persists nothing at all, so an adjudicated-basis read can never disturb the peer median that other departments' flags depend on. `training_completion_pct` is always measured from the current logical clock and is never shifted by `as_of`, which moves only the reporting window.

### 3.2. Students, Course Offerings and the Gradebook

A student is registered with `full_name`, a unique `email` containing exactly one `@`, a `program_code` of two to sixteen characters, and a `class_year` from 1 to 8. Four fields are derived and never supplied. `prior_upheld_count` is the number of that student's cases whose `responsibility_finding` is `RESPONSIBLE`, whose `overturned` is false, and whose `adjudication_status` is `SANCTION_IMPOSED` or `CLOSED`. `active_sanction_points` is the sum of `ladder_rank` over the student's sanctions currently in `IMPOSED`. `standing` is `SUSPENDED` when any imposed sanction has `ladder_rank` 6 or greater, otherwise `PROBATION` when any imposed sanction has `ladder_rank` exactly 5, otherwise `GOOD_STANDING` — note that a rank-4 sanction leaves the student in good standing. `transcript_notation` is `POSTED` when any of the student's cases has `notation_status` `POSTED`, otherwise `EXPUNGED` when any case has `notation_status` `EXPUNGED`, otherwise `NONE`. Students may be listed with `email`, `standing` and `transcript_notation` filters.

A course offering ties a `department_id` to an `instructor_id` (a faculty member) for a `course_code` matching two to six uppercase letters, a hyphen and three digits, in a `term_code` of four digits followed by `SP`, `SU` or `FA`. The pairing of `course_code` with `term_code` must be unique across offerings. Offerings may be listed with `department_id`, `instructor_id`, `term_code` and `course_code` filters.

Each offering carries a `grade_components` array of one to eight entries defining the weighted gradebook. Every entry has a `code` of two to sixteen characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and underscore, unique within the array; a `max_points` integer of at least 1; and a `weight_pct` integer from 1 to 100. The `weight_pct` values must sum to exactly 100 — any other sum is rejected as an invalid request. The array's order is significant: it is the evaluation order for the course-grade computation in §3.8.

### 3.3. Report Intake, Evidence and Office Triage

An instructor opens an allegation by creating a report against an `offering_id` and a `student_id`, with an `alleged_violation_type` drawn from `PLAGIARISM`, `UNAUTHORIZED_COLLABORATION`, `EXAM_CHEATING`, `FABRICATION_OF_DATA`, `CONTRACT_CHEATING` and `FACILITATION`, a `narrative` of 20 to 4000 characters, an `incident_at` instant no later than the current logical clock, and a `component_scores` array snapshotting the student's gradebook. The report's `reporter_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID` and any `reporter_id` in the body is ignored. `component_scores` entries each carry a `code` and an `earned_points` integer that is at least 0 and no greater than the matching component's `max_points`; the array must contain exactly one entry per code declared in the offering's `grade_components`, and a missing, duplicated or unknown code is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_SCORES_INCOMPLETE`. A newly created report has `status` `DRAFT`, `submitted_at` null, `returned_at` null, `triaged_at` null, `triage_note` null, `resubmission_count` 0, `evidence_strength_score` 0.00 and `evidence_complete` false. Reports may be listed with `offering_id`, `student_id`, `reporter_id` and `status` filters.

Evidence items are attached to a report that is in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, and only by the report's own reporter. Each item carries a `report_id`, a `kind` of `SIMILARITY_REPORT`, `PROCTOR_LOG`, `WITNESS_STATEMENT` or `DOCUMENT`, a `sha256` digest of exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, a `page_count` from 0 to 500, and a `similarity_pct` from 0.00 to 100.00 with at most two decimals. `similarity_pct` is required exactly when `kind` is `SIMILARITY_REPORT` and must be absent for every other kind; both the missing and the surplus case are rejected as an invalid request. Evidence items are immutable: there is no update operation, and they carry only `id` and `created_at`. Attaching an item recomputes the report's `evidence_strength_score` and `evidence_complete` immediately. Items may be listed with `report_id` and `kind` filters.

`evidence_complete` is true when the report has at least one evidence item and, when `alleged_violation_type` is `PLAGIARISM`, at least one of those items has `kind` `SIMILARITY_REPORT`. For every other violation type a single item of any kind is sufficient.

`evidence_strength_score` is computed over all of the report's evidence items in a strictly dependency-ordered sequence. First, the adjusted similarity percentage is the greatest `similarity_pct` among the report's `SIMILARITY_REPORT` items, less an excluded-quote allowance of 5.00, floored at 0.00; a report with no similarity report has an adjusted similarity of 0.00. Second, the band multiplier is chosen from that adjusted value, not from the raw one: 1.20 when the adjusted value is 40.00 or greater, otherwise 1.00 when it is 20.00 or greater, otherwise 0.70. Third, the similarity points are the adjusted value multiplied by the band multiplier, rounded to two decimals. Fourth, the page points are the sum of `page_count` across all of the report's items, capped at 20, multiplied by 0.50, rounded to two decimals. Fifth, the kind-diversity points add one weight per distinct kind present — 1.00 for `SIMILARITY_REPORT`, 0.60 for `PROCTOR_LOG`, 0.40 for `WITNESS_STATEMENT` and 0.20 for `DOCUMENT` — multiply the sum by 10, and round to two decimals. Sixth, a corroboration bonus of 15.00 applies when the report has two or more distinct kinds and at least one of those kinds is not `DOCUMENT`, and is 0.00 otherwise. Finally the four parts are added, rounded to two decimals, and capped at 100.00.

Choosing the band from the raw similarity percentage rather than the allowance-adjusted one is observable at the 45.00 boundary. A single similarity report at 45.00 with 4 pages adjusts to 40.00, takes the 1.20 band for 48.00 similarity points, adds 2.00 page points and 10.00 diversity points with no corroboration, and scores 60.00. The same item at 44.99 adjusts to 39.99, falls to the 1.00 band for 39.99 similarity points, and scores 51.99. Adding a one-page proctor log to the 45.00 case gives 48.00 similarity points, 2.50 page points, 16.00 diversity points and a 15.00 corroboration bonus, for 81.50; the same pair at 44.99 scores 73.49. A single 6-page document with no similarity report adjusts to 0.00, takes the 0.70 band for 0.00 similarity points, adds 3.00 page points and 2.00 diversity points with no corroboration, and scores 5.00.

Submitting a report is available to its own reporter, from `DRAFT` or from `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`. From `DRAFT` the report must have at least one evidence item, and at least one `SIMILARITY_REPORT` item when the violation type is `PLAGIARISM`, otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`; the reporter's `training_status` must be `CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_REPORTER_TRAINING_LAPSED`. On success `status` becomes `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` is set to the current logical clock, `evidence_strength_score` is recomputed over the evidence set, and the reporter's `reports_submitted_count` increases by one. From `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` the requirement is different: at least one evidence item must have been added since the report's `returned_at`, otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`; the training requirement is unchanged. On a resubmission `status` becomes `SUBMITTED` again, `submitted_at` is left untouched so that it always records the first submission, `resubmission_count` increases by one, `evidence_strength_score` is recomputed over the enlarged evidence set, and `reports_submitted_count` is not incremented a second time. Either submission recomputes the reporting department's rate and monitoring flag. The cycle from `SUBMITTED` to `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` and back may repeat without bound.

Office triage acts on a `SUBMITTED` report with a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `RETURN_FOR_INFO` or `REJECT_INSUFFICIENT` and a `triage_note`. Accepting additionally requires `evidence_strength_score` of at least 20.00, otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`; on success `status` becomes `ACCEPTED`, `triaged_at` is set to the clock and `triage_note` is recorded. Returning for information sets `status` to `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, sets `returned_at` to the clock and records the note, leaving `triaged_at` untouched. Rejecting sets `status` to `REJECTED_INSUFFICIENT`, sets `triaged_at` to the clock and records the note; no case may afterwards be opened from that report, because case opening requires `ACCEPTED`. `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED_INSUFFICIENT` are terminal.

### 3.4. Opening a Case and the Two-Algorithm Severity Assessment

The office opens a case by naming a `report_id`. The report must exist and its `status` must be `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`; if a case already exists for that report the request is rejected with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_OPENED`. The new case copies `student_id` and `offering_id` from the report, sets `origin` to `OFFICE_INTAKE`, `pathway` to `UNASSIGNED`, `adjudication_status` to `INTAKE_REVIEW`, `notation_status` to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `responsibility_finding` to `UNDETERMINED`, `finding_basis` to `NONE`, `hearing_round` to 0, `overturned` to false, `decided_at` to null, `panel_severity_recommendation` to null, `pre_appeal_adjudication_status` to null, `dismiss_reason` to null, `expungement_eligible_at` to null and `version` to 1. It is assigned a `case_number` of the form `AIC-` followed by a zero-padded six-digit sequence, allocated in case `created_at` ascending order beginning at `AIC-000001`. The case's `evidence_strength_score` is copied from the report and frozen — later evidence activity on the report never changes it. `baseline_course_grade_pct` is computed from the offering's `grade_components` joined to the report's `component_scores` by the rules in §3.8, and an incomplete join is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_SCORES_INCOMPLETE`. The full assessment then runs, writing `cross_course_pattern_points`, `prior_upheld_count_at_assessment`, `prior_escalation_points`, `provisional_severity_index`, `severity_index` and `recommended_ladder_rank` under the formal algorithm and `informal_severity_index` and `informal_recommended_ladder_rank` under the informal algorithm. Finally the offering's department has `open_case_count` increased by one.

The legacy import exists so that the repeat-offense database and the cross-course pattern detector have prior history to read, and it is the only way to create a case whose `decided_at` precedes the current logical clock. It takes a `report_id`, a `decided_at` no later than the current clock, a `responsibility_finding`, an `imposed_ladder_rank` and a `completed_at` at or after `decided_at`. The imported case is created directly in `adjudication_status` `CLOSED` with `pathway` `FORMAL`, `origin` `LEGACY_IMPORT`, `overturned` false, `notation_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `finding_basis` `LEGACY`, `evidence_strength_score` 0.00 and the supplied `decided_at`. A sanction is created alongside it directly in `COMPLETED`, with `ladder_rank` equal to `imposed_ladder_rank`, the `kind` that rank maps to, `imposed_at` equal to `decided_at`, and the supplied `completed_at`. The student's `prior_upheld_count` is recomputed, so a `RESPONSIBLE` import raises it; `active_sanction_points` is unchanged, because the sanction is already completed and completed sanctions contribute nothing. The department's `open_case_count` is not incremented — an imported case is closed on arrival. The report named by a legacy import must be in `DRAFT`, because a historical record is backfilled directly and is never submitted or triaged; any other `status` is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. A report that already has a case is rejected with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_OPENED`.

Cases may be listed with `report_id`, `student_id`, `offering_id`, `adjudication_status`, `notation_status` and `pathway` filters. The single-case read exposes both state dimensions, every severity field and the `version` that the concurrency-guarded writes require.

**The cross-course pattern detector.** `cross_course_pattern_points` looks at cases other than this one belonging to the same student, with `responsibility_finding` `RESPONSIBLE`, `overturned` false, a non-null `decided_at`, and that `decided_at` inside the inclusive window running from 730 days before the current logical clock up to the clock itself. Cases whose `adjudication_status` is `DISMISSED` are excluded even if a finding was once recorded on them. Over that set, count the distinct `course_code` values of the cases' offerings, excluding this case's own `course_code`. That count yields 0 points when it is 0, 4 points when it is exactly 1, and 10 points when it is 2 or more. The metric measures breadth, not volume: two prior cases in the same other course count as one distinct code and therefore yield 4, not 10. A prior decided 731 days before the clock falls outside the window and contributes nothing.

**The severity index.** The order of operations is pinned. First, the evidence contribution is the case's `evidence_strength_score` multiplied by 0.40, rounded to two decimals; the score used is the case's own frozen snapshot, never re-read from the report. Second, `provisional_severity_index` is the base points for the violation type, plus the evidence contribution, plus `cross_course_pattern_points`, rounded to two decimals. The base points are 20 for `PLAGIARISM`, 18 for `UNAUTHORIZED_COLLABORATION`, 28 for `EXAM_CHEATING`, 32 for `FABRICATION_OF_DATA`, 40 for `CONTRACT_CHEATING` and 24 for `FACILITATION`. Third, `prior_upheld_count_at_assessment` is the student's `prior_upheld_count` at assessment time, excluding this case itself. Fourth, `prior_escalation_points` is selected from the **provisional** index, not the final one: 0 when there are no priors; 8 with exactly one prior and a provisional index below 50.00; 14 with exactly one prior and a provisional index of 50.00 or above; 18 with two or more priors and a provisional index below 50.00; 26 with two or more priors and a provisional index of 50.00 or above. Fifth, `severity_index` is the provisional index plus the escalation points, rounded to two decimals, capped at 120.00.

Selecting the escalation band from the final index instead of the provisional one is a self-referential shortcut that silently inflates the answer whenever the provisional index sits from 42.00 up to but not including 50.00 with exactly one prior, or from 32.00 up to but not including 50.00 with two or more priors. A plagiarism case with an evidence score of 60.00, no cross-course points and one prior contributes 24.00, reaches a provisional index of 44.00, takes 8 escalation points because 44.00 is below the pivot, and lands at 52.00; keying the band on the final value would give 14 points and 58.00. The same case with 4 cross-course points reaches a provisional 48.00, still takes 8, and lands at 56.00. With no priors it stays at 44.00. An exam-cheating case with an evidence score of 81.50 and 10 cross-course points and two priors contributes 32.60, reaches a provisional 70.60, takes 26, and lands at 96.60. A contract-cheating case with the same evidence and pattern points reaches a provisional 82.60, takes 26, and lands at 108.60, still under the cap. An unauthorized-collaboration case with a 60.00 evidence score, no pattern points and two priors reaches a provisional 42.00, takes 18 because 42.00 is below the pivot, and lands at 60.00; keying on the final value would give 26 and 68.00.

**Ladder placement, formal variant.** The tier rank is read from `severity_index`: below 25.00 gives 1; below 40.00 gives 2; below 55.00 gives 3; below 70.00 gives 5; below 85.00 gives 6; below 100.00 gives 7; 100.00 or above gives 8. `recommended_ladder_rank` is the tier rank, except that when `panel_severity_recommendation` is not null it is the greater of the tier rank and the panel recommendation — the panel's number acts as a floor and can never lower the tier. The permitted office band is the closed interval from the recommended rank minus 1 to the recommended rank plus 1, clamped to the range 1 through 8. The required panel size is 5 when the recommended rank is 6 or above and 3 otherwise. This variant is used whenever the case's `pathway` is `FORMAL`, for every office-side sanction proposal, and for every severity-assessment read whose `pathway_basis` is omitted or `FORMAL`. Its input set is the full picture: the evidence score, the violation type, the cross-course pattern points and the prior escalation points.

**Ladder placement, informal variant.** Policy bars instructors from reading the repeat-offense database, so this variant sees only the evidence score and the violation type. `informal_severity_index` is the base points for the violation type plus the evidence score multiplied by 0.40 and rounded to two decimals, with the sum rounded to two decimals — `cross_course_pattern_points` and `prior_escalation_points` are excluded entirely, not merely zeroed after the fact. `informal_recommended_ladder_rank` is the tier rank of that informal index, capped at 3. The permitted informal band is the closed interval from 1 to `informal_recommended_ladder_rank`: an instructor may always propose a rank lower than the recommendation but never a higher one. This variant is used for the instructor's informal proposal and for a severity-assessment read whose `pathway_basis` is `INFORMAL`.

The two variants routinely disagree, and the disagreement is the point. A case whose formal index is 52.00 has a tier rank of 3, a recommended rank of 3 and an office band from 2 to 4, while its informal index of 44.00 also gives rank 3 but an informal band from 1 to 3 — the ranks agree yet the office may propose rank 4 where the instructor may not. Add 4 cross-course points and the formal index becomes 56.00 for a recommended rank of 5 and a band from 4 to 6, against an unchanged informal band from 1 to 3: the two bands are disjoint, so no single proposed rank is legal under both. An exam-cheating case with 10 pattern points and two priors reaches a formal rank of 7 with a band from 6 to 8, against an informal rank of 3 — and here the informal pathway is barred outright, because the recommended rank exceeds 3. Finally, a case whose tier rank is 3 but whose panel recommended 4 ends with a recommended rank of 4 and a band from 3 to 5; this is the only route by which the rank-4 sanction kind can be reached.

**The severity-assessment read.** This read takes an optional `pathway_basis` (`FORMAL` or `INFORMAL`, defaulting to `FORMAL`) and exposes every intermediate: `case_id`, the `pathway_basis` used, `evidence_strength_score`, `base_points`, `evidence_contribution`, `cross_course_pattern_points`, `distinct_other_course_codes`, `prior_upheld_count_at_assessment`, `provisional_severity_index`, `prior_escalation_points`, `severity_index`, `tier_rank`, `panel_severity_recommendation`, `recommended_ladder_rank`, `recommended_kind`, `permitted_band_min`, `permitted_band_max`, `required_panel_size`, `attorney_advisor_permitted` and `informal_pathway_permitted`. With `pathway_basis` set to `INFORMAL`, `cross_course_pattern_points` and `prior_escalation_points` are returned as 0 because the informal variant may not read them, and `severity_index` carries the informal index. The read re-runs the assessment, so a case whose sibling history has changed reports fresh numbers. `attorney_advisor_permitted` is true when the recommended rank is 6 or above; `informal_pathway_permitted` is true when the formal recommended rank is 3 or below.

**Choosing the pathway.** The office sets `pathway` to `INFORMAL` or `FORMAL` on a case in `INTAKE_REVIEW` or `NOTIFIED`. The field can be set exactly once by this operation; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_CASE_PATHWAY_ALREADY_SET`. Choosing `INFORMAL` for a case whose `recommended_ladder_rank` exceeds 3 is rejected with `ERR_INFORMAL_PATHWAY_NOT_PERMITTED`. The only other way `pathway` ever changes is the student declining an informal proposal, which flips it to `FORMAL`.

**Dismissal.** The office may dismiss a case whose `adjudication_status` is `INTAKE_REVIEW`, `NOTIFIED` or `FORMAL_REFERRED`, supplying a `dismiss_reason`; any other status is rejected with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. Dismissal records the reason, moves every sanction of the case in `PROPOSED` or `ACCEPTED` to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` `CASE_DISMISSED`, decreases the department's `open_case_count` by one, sets `adjudication_status` to `DISMISSED`, and advances `version`. `DISMISSED` is terminal.

### 3.5. Notice, Response Windows, Advisor Rights and Instructor Meetings

**Official notices.** The office sends a notice on a case by supplying a `kind` and an optional `response_window_days`, which defaults to 7. Only `ALLEGATION_NOTICE` may be created this way; `MEETING_NOTICE`, `HEARING_NOTICE`, `REHEARING_NOTICE`, `SANCTION_NOTICE`, `SANCTION_RECOMPUTE_NOTICE` and `APPEAL_OUTCOME_NOTICE` are all raised as side effects of other operations. Every notice records `sent_at` from the current logical clock, computes `response_deadline_at` as `sent_at` plus `response_window_days` days, and starts with `acknowledged_at` null. `response_window_days` must be an integer of at least 1 to satisfy the request shape, but the policy range of 5 to 30 inclusive is a business rule, not a shape rule: a value of 4 or of 31 is rejected with `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_OUT_OF_RANGE` and HTTP 409, whereas a value of 0, a negative value or a non-integer is rejected as an invalid request with HTTP 400.

Sending an `ALLEGATION_NOTICE` requires the case to be in `INTAKE_REVIEW` and requires that no `ALLEGATION_NOTICE` already exists for the case; either failure is `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. On success the notice is created, the case's notified instant is set to the notice's `sent_at`, `adjudication_status` becomes `NOTIFIED`, and the case's `version` advances. Whenever a `REHEARING_NOTICE` is created for a case — which happens on adjournment and on remand — every advisor engagement of that case that is `APPROVED` returns to `REQUESTED`.

Notices may be listed for a single case, or listed globally with `case_id`, `kind` and `acknowledged` filters. The student named by the case's `student_id` may acknowledge a notice, which sets `acknowledged_at` to the current logical clock; a second acknowledgement is rejected with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_ALREADY_ACKNOWLEDGED`. Acknowledgement closes the response window early. The window counts as closed when `acknowledged_at` is set or when the current logical clock has passed `response_deadline_at`; it is still open when `acknowledged_at` is null and the clock is at or before the deadline, and convening a hearing in that condition is rejected with `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_OPEN`.

**Advisor engagements.** The student names an advisor by supplying the `case_id`, an `advisor_full_name` of one to 120 characters, an `advisor_kind` of `FACULTY_ADVISOR`, `STUDENT_PEER` or `ATTORNEY`, and an optional `advisor_bar_licensed` flag defaulting to false. Only the case's own student may make the request. The new engagement has `status` `REQUESTED`, `speaking_rights` false, `reconfirmation_required` false and `decision_reason` null. Three guards apply in this order: an `ATTORNEY` request is rejected with `ERR_ATTORNEY_ADVISOR_NOT_PERMITTED` when the case's `recommended_ladder_rank` is below 6; an `ATTORNEY` request with `advisor_bar_licensed` false is rejected with `ERR_ATTORNEY_NOT_LICENSED`; and a request is rejected with `ERR_ADVISOR_LIMIT_REACHED` when the case already holds two engagements whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. Because the attorney-eligibility check comes first, a low-rank case that is also at its limit reports the attorney error. Denied engagements stop counting against the limit. Engagements may be listed for a single case or globally with `case_id`, `status` and `advisor_kind` filters.

The office reviews a `REQUESTED` engagement with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`; a `DENY` requires `decision_reason`, and its absence is an invalid request. Approval sets `status` to `APPROVED`, clears `reconfirmation_required`, and grants `speaking_rights` only when `advisor_kind` is `ATTORNEY` — a faculty advisor or student peer may attend but never speak. Denial sets `status` to `DENIED`, records `decision_reason`, and leaves `speaking_rights` false; `DENIED` is terminal. Reviewing an engagement that is not `REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_ADVISOR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. `speaking_rights` as a derived field is true exactly when `status` is `APPROVED`, `advisor_kind` is `ATTORNEY`, and the case's `pathway` is `FORMAL`; in every other combination it is false. When a rehearing notice pushes an approved engagement back to `REQUESTED`, `reconfirmation_required` becomes true and `speaking_rights` returns to false, and the case cannot be convened again until the engagement is reviewed afresh — a pending engagement blocks convening with `ERR_ADVISOR_REQUEST_PENDING`. An advisor therefore has to be re-confirmed for every new hearing round.

**Instructor meetings.** A meeting is scheduled by the reporting instructor or by the office, supplying `case_id`, `instructor_id`, `start_at`, `end_at` and an optional `advisor_present` flag defaulting to false. `instructor_id` must resolve to a faculty member; the meeting's own `instructor_id` is the reporter of the case's originating report, and it is that member's calendar which is the occupied resource. The case's `adjudication_status` must be exactly `NOTIFIED`, `INFORMAL_PROPOSED` or `FORMAL_REFERRED` — a meeting cannot be booked before the student has been notified, nor after the case has been decided — and any other status is `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. `duration_minutes` is derived as the whole minutes between `start_at` and `end_at`, and `status` starts at `SCHEDULED`.

The interval rules are exact. A meeting occupies the half-open interval that includes `start_at` and excludes `end_at`. `ERR_MEETING_WINDOW_INVALID` is raised when `start_at` is at or after `end_at`, when the interval is not a whole number of minutes, or when the duration is outside the range 15 to 240 minutes inclusive. `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CALENDAR_CONFLICT` is raised when the requested half-open interval intersects the half-open interval of any other meeting for the same instructor whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `HELD` or `NO_SHOW`; because the intervals are half-open, two back-to-back meetings that share an endpoint do not conflict. The window check outranks the conflict check. Meetings may be listed with `case_id`, `instructor_id` and `status` filters.

The outcome is recorded once, with an `outcome` of `HELD`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`, and only from `SCHEDULED`; anything else is `ERR_MEETING_STATE_INVALID`. Recording `HELD` or `NO_SHOW` is reserved to the meeting's own instructor, while `CANCELLED` is an office action. `HELD` may not be recorded before `start_at` and `NO_SHOW` may not be recorded before `end_at`; an early attempt is `ERR_MEETING_WINDOW_INVALID`. On `HELD`, `advisor_present` is set to true when the case has an engagement in `APPROVED` at that moment and false otherwise, and the calendar interval remains occupied. On `NO_SHOW`, `advisor_present` is set to false and the interval remains occupied. On `CANCELLED`, `advisor_present` is set to false and the instructor's interval is released, so a later meeting may occupy exactly the same half-open interval. All three are terminal.

### 3.6. The Informal Instructor Pathway

The informal pathway lets the reporting instructor settle a low-severity case directly with the student, and it deliberately runs on less information than the office has.

The instructor proposes a resolution on a case whose `adjudication_status` is `NOTIFIED` and whose `pathway` is `INFORMAL`, supplying `expected_case_version`, a `proposed_ladder_rank`, a `kind`, an `effective_from` and, when the kind demands it, a `grade_component_code`. The acting identity must be the reporter of the case's originating report. The guards run in the order set out in §2.9: the case state, then the pathway, then the version check, then the informal-eligibility check that rejects a case whose formal `recommended_ladder_rank` exceeds 3 with `ERR_INFORMAL_PATHWAY_NOT_PERMITTED`, then the gradebook checks, then the band and kind checks. `grade_component_code`, when present, must name a code in the case's offering `grade_components`, otherwise `ERR_GRADE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`; it is required when the resolved kind is `GRADE_PENALTY_ASSIGNMENT` and forbidden for every other kind, otherwise `ERR_GRADE_COMPONENT_REQUIRED`. `proposed_ladder_rank` must lie inside the informal band, which runs from 1 up to `informal_recommended_ladder_rank`; a rank above that upper bound is `ERR_SANCTION_ABOVE_LADDER_BAND`, and the informal band has no lower bound so the below-band error can never arise here. `kind` must be the kind that `proposed_ladder_rank` maps to, otherwise `ERR_SANCTION_KIND_RANK_MISMATCH`.

On success, any sanction of the case still in `PROPOSED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `vacated_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_PROPOSAL`, and a new sanction is created in `PROPOSED` with `origin` `INFORMAL_PROPOSAL`, `ladder_rank` equal to `proposed_ladder_rank` and `replaces_sanction_id` pointing at the superseded row. The case moves to `INFORMAL_PROPOSED` and its `version` advances. The response carries the created sanction.

The student then responds with a `response` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and only the case's own student may respond. Accepting is an admission: the proposed sanction moves to `ACCEPTED`, `responsibility_finding` becomes `RESPONSIBLE`, `finding_basis` becomes `ADMISSION`, `decided_at` is set to the current logical clock, `adjudication_status` becomes `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED`, and `version` advances. Declining is the branch that changes the arithmetic: the proposed sanction moves to `DECLINED`, `pathway` flips to `FORMAL`, and `cross_course_pattern_points`, `prior_escalation_points`, `provisional_severity_index`, `severity_index` and `recommended_ladder_rank` are all recomputed under the formal algorithm, because the informal cap no longer applies. `responsibility_finding` stays `UNDETERMINED` and `decided_at` stays null. The case moves to `FORMAL_REFERRED` and `version` advances. A case that was informally capped at rank 3 can therefore emerge from a decline with a recommended rank of 5 or higher, which is exactly what makes the subsequent formal proposal band differ from the one the instructor saw.

### 3.7. Formal Hearings: Rooms, Panels, Quorum and Votes

**Hearing rooms.** A room is registered with a `code` unique across rooms and matching two to twelve characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens; a `seat_capacity` from 4 to 200; an optional `buffer_minutes` from 0 to 120 defaulting to 30; and an optional `sessions_per_day_cap` from 1 to 8 defaulting to 2. `booked_minutes_total` starts at 0. The buffer is a turnover allowance applied on **both** sides of every session, so a room's occupied interval for a session is the closed interval from `start_at` minus `buffer_minutes` to `end_at` plus `buffer_minutes`. Rooms may be listed with a `code` filter.

**Scheduling a session.** The office schedules a hearing with `case_id`, `room_id`, `chair_id`, `panelist_ids`, `start_at` and `end_at`. `end_at` must be after `start_at`. The case's `adjudication_status` must be `NOTIFIED` or `FORMAL_REFERRED`, and its `pathway` must be `FORMAL`, otherwise `ERR_CASE_PATHWAY_NOT_SET`. The guards then run in the §2.9 order: chair competence, conflict of interest, notice period, room buffer conflict, interval validity, panel size, and daily capacity.

The chair's `training_status` must be `CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_CHAIR_TRAINING_LAPSED`. Segregation of duties is absolute: neither `chair_id` nor any element of `panelist_ids` may equal the `reporter_id` of the case's originating report or the `instructor_id` of the case's offering, otherwise `ERR_PANEL_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. The notice period is measured from the governing notice, which is the case's most recent notification whose `kind` is `ALLEGATION_NOTICE` or `REHEARING_NOTICE`: `start_at` must be at or after that notice's `sent_at` plus 10 days, otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`. For a first hearing that governing notice is the allegation notice; for a re-hearing after adjournment or remand it is the rehearing notice, so the ten days run afresh from the rehearing notice.

The room conflict test uses the **closed** buffered interval: the requested interval from `start_at` minus the room's `buffer_minutes` to `end_at` plus `buffer_minutes` must not intersect the same buffered interval of any session in that room whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONVENED`, and because the interval is closed, two sessions whose buffered intervals merely touch at an endpoint **do** conflict. This is the opposite convention from the half-open instructor calendar in §3.5, and the difference is deliberate. A violation is `ERR_ROOM_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. The interval itself must be well formed by the same rule that governs meetings: `start_at` before `end_at`, a whole number of minutes, and a duration from 15 to 240 minutes inclusive, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_WINDOW_INVALID`.

`required_panel_size` is derived from the case's `recommended_ladder_rank` at scheduling time: 3 when the rank is 5 or below, 5 when it is 6 or above. The supplied `panelist_ids` must have exactly that length, must contain no duplicates, and must not contain `chair_id`; any violation is `ERR_PANEL_SIZE_INVALID`. Finally, the room may not already hold `sessions_per_day_cap` sessions with `status` `SCHEDULED` or `CONVENED` whose `start_at` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the requested `start_at`, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

On success the session is created in `SCHEDULED` with `round_number` set to the case's `hearing_round` plus one, `quorum_required` set to the panel size multiplied by two, divided by three and rounded **up** — 2 for a three-member panel and 4 for a five-member panel — `effective_vote_count` 0, `panel_finding` null, `panel_severity_recommendation` null and `adjourn_reason` null. The case's `hearing_round` increases by one, the room's `booked_minutes_total` increases by the session's whole minutes plus twice the room's `buffer_minutes`, a `HEARING_NOTICE` notification is created for the case, `adjudication_status` becomes `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, and `version` advances. Sessions may be listed with `case_id`, `room_id`, `chair_id` and `status` filters.

**Cancelling.** The office may cancel a session that is still `SCHEDULED`; any other status is `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`. Cancellation takes a `cancel_reason`, sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, releases exactly the minutes that scheduling reserved from the room's `booked_minutes_total`, and returns the case to `FORMAL_REFERRED` with `version` advanced. `hearing_round` is **not** decremented, so the round counter records rounds ever opened rather than rounds surviving.

**Convening.** The session's own chair convenes a `SCHEDULED` session. The current logical clock must have reached `start_at`, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_WINDOW_INVALID`. No advisor engagement of the case may still be `REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_ADVISOR_REQUEST_PENDING`. The student's response window must be closed — the case's allegation notice must have a non-null `acknowledged_at`, or the clock must have passed its `response_deadline_at` — otherwise `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_OPEN`. On success `status` becomes `CONVENED`, the case moves to `HEARING_IN_PROGRESS`, and `version` advances.

**Recording votes.** Votes are recorded only while the session is `CONVENED`, and only by that session's chair, who enters each panelist's vote onto the record. A vote carries `session_id`, `panelist_id`, a `verdict` of `RESPONSIBLE`, `NOT_RESPONSIBLE` or `ABSTAIN`, and a `severity_recommendation` from 1 to 8 that is required exactly when the verdict is `RESPONSIBLE` and must be omitted for the other two verdicts. `panelist_id` must be an element of the session's `panelist_ids`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_A_PANELIST`; a panelist who is the case's reporter can never vote, which is `ERR_PANEL_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`; a missing recommendation on a responsible verdict is `ERR_SEVERITY_RECOMMENDATION_REQUIRED` and a surplus one on the other verdicts is `ERR_SEVERITY_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_ALLOWED`; and a second vote for the same session-and-panelist pair is `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`. A vote records `cast_at` from the current logical clock and is immutable: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and can never be changed or withdrawn. Votes may be listed with `session_id`, `panelist_id` and `verdict` filters.

**Deciding.** The chair closes a `CONVENED` session. The number of votes cast for the session must be at least `quorum_required`, otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; abstentions **do** count toward quorum. Then at least one vote must be something other than `ABSTAIN`, otherwise `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_VOTES` — an all-abstain panel that satisfies quorum still cannot decide. The quorum check runs strictly before the effective-vote check, so a panel that is both all-abstain and short of quorum reports the quorum error.

The tally then runs. `effective_vote_count` is the number of votes whose `verdict` is not `ABSTAIN`. `panel_finding` is `RESPONSIBLE` when the number of responsible votes multiplied by two is strictly greater than `effective_vote_count`, and `NOT_RESPONSIBLE` otherwise — a dead-even split is `NOT_RESPONSIBLE`. `panel_severity_recommendation` is null when the finding is `NOT_RESPONSIBLE`; otherwise it is the median of the `severity_recommendation` values of the responsible votes, sorted ascending, and for an even count it is the **lower** of the two middle values, never their mean.

Three worked cases fix the rules. A three-member panel voting responsible at 6, responsible at 4, and not responsible has quorum 2, three cast, three effective, two responsible; four is greater than three so the finding is `RESPONSIBLE`, and the two recommendations 4 and 6 give the lower middle, 4. A three-member panel voting responsible at 7, abstain, and not responsible has two effective votes and one responsible; two is not greater than two, so the finding is `NOT_RESPONSIBLE` and the recommendation is null. A five-member panel with four votes cast — responsible at 8, responsible at 5, responsible at 6, and one abstention — meets its quorum of 4, has three effective votes and three responsible; six is greater than three so the finding is `RESPONSIBLE`, and the sorted recommendations 5, 6, 8 give the median 6. The same five-member panel with only three votes cast is rejected for want of quorum.

On success the session's derived fields are written, `status` becomes `DECIDED`, and no further vote may be cast. The case then takes the finding: `responsibility_finding` becomes the panel's finding, `finding_basis` becomes `PANEL`, `panel_severity_recommendation` is copied onto the case (null when the finding is `NOT_RESPONSIBLE`), and `severity_index`, `prior_escalation_points` and `recommended_ladder_rank` are recomputed under the formal algorithm with the panel recommendation acting as a floor. `decided_at` is set to the current logical clock. `adjudication_status` becomes `DECIDED` when the finding is `RESPONSIBLE`; when it is `NOT_RESPONSIBLE` the case goes straight to `DISMISSED` with `dismiss_reason` `NOT_RESPONSIBLE_PANEL_FINDING`. `version` advances. Deciding also recomputes the department's reporting rate on the adjudicated basis.

**Adjourning.** The chair may adjourn a `CONVENED` session instead of deciding it, supplying an `adjourn_reason`, which is required. `status` becomes `ADJOURNED` and the reason is recorded. The room's `booked_minutes_total` is **not** released, because the room was genuinely occupied. Votes already cast stay attached to the adjourned session and are never counted again in a later round. A `REHEARING_NOTICE` is created for the case, every advisor engagement in `APPROVED` returns to `REQUESTED` with `reconfirmation_required` true and `speaking_rights` false, `adjudication_status` returns to `FORMAL_REFERRED`, and `version` advances. `hearing_round` is not changed by the adjournment itself; it rises only when the next session is actually scheduled. `DECIDED`, `ADJOURNED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal for the session.

### 3.8. Sanctions: Proposal, Grade Arithmetic, the Imposition Cascade and Completion

**Proposing a formal sanction.** The office proposes a sanction on a decided case, supplying `case_id`, `expected_case_version`, a `kind`, a `ladder_rank`, an `effective_from` and, for an assignment grade penalty, a `grade_component_code`. The case's `adjudication_status` must be `DECIDED`, otherwise `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`; proposing against a case that is merely `NOTIFIED` or still `HEARING_IN_PROGRESS` is rejected on that rule. `expected_case_version` must match the case's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` with HTTP 412. `grade_component_code`, when present, must name a code in the case's offering `grade_components`, otherwise `ERR_GRADE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404; it is required when the kind is `GRADE_PENALTY_ASSIGNMENT` and forbidden for every other kind, otherwise `ERR_GRADE_COMPONENT_REQUIRED`. `effective_from` may not precede the case's `decided_at`, otherwise `ERR_SANCTION_EFFECTIVE_BEFORE_DECISION`. `ladder_rank` must lie inside the office band, which runs from the case's `recommended_ladder_rank` minus 1 clamped up to 1, through that rank plus 1 clamped down to 8; a rank above the upper bound is `ERR_SANCTION_ABOVE_LADDER_BAND` and a rank below the lower bound is `ERR_SANCTION_BELOW_LADDER_BAND`. `kind` must be the kind the rank maps to, otherwise `ERR_SANCTION_KIND_RANK_MISMATCH`.

On success the sanction is created in `PROPOSED` with `origin` `OFFICE_PROPOSAL`, `imposed_at` null, `completed_at` null, `vacated_reason` null, and `baseline_course_grade_pct`, `adjusted_course_grade_pct` and `grade_penalty_delta_pct` at 0.00. Any sanction of the case already in `PROPOSED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `vacated_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_PROPOSAL`, and the new row's `replaces_sanction_id` points at it. The case's `version` advances, which is what makes a second proposal written against the same snapshot fail. Superseding never touches `active_sanction_points`, because a proposed sanction never contributed to it. Sanctions may be listed with `case_id`, `status`, `kind` and `replaces_sanction_id` filters; a `replaces_sanction_id` filter naming no stored sanction is rejected with `ERR_SANCTION_NOT_FOUND`.

**The course-grade arithmetic.** The rounding order is pinned, and getting it wrong is off by one cent. Step one: for each component of the offering's `grade_components`, in array order, the component percentage is that component's `earned_points` from the report's `component_scores`, multiplied by 100, divided by its `max_points`, rounded to two decimals. Step two: the weighted total is the sum over components of the **rounded** component percentage multiplied by its `weight_pct` and divided by 100; this intermediate is not rounded. Step three: the course grade percentage is the weighted total rounded once, to two decimals. Weighting the unrounded percentages and rounding only at the end gives a different answer and is wrong.

`baseline_course_grade_pct` is that figure computed from the untouched `component_scores`, and it is written onto the case at opening and snapshotted onto a grade-penalty sanction. `adjusted_course_grade_pct` depends on the kind: for `GRADE_PENALTY_ASSIGNMENT` the three steps are re-run with the `earned_points` of the named `grade_component_code` forced to 0; for `GRADE_PENALTY_COURSE` it is 0.00, the whole course grade being forfeited; for every other kind there is no grade effect. `grade_penalty_delta_pct` is `baseline_course_grade_pct` minus `adjusted_course_grade_pct`, rounded to two decimals.

A gradebook of four components — one worth 200 points at 40 percent, one worth 70 points at 30 percent, one worth 90 points at 20 percent and one worth 180 points at 10 percent — against earned scores of 156, 68, 56 and 113 gives component percentages of 78.00, 97.14 (from 97.142857 recurring), 62.22 (from 62.222222 recurring) and 62.78 (from 62.777777 recurring). Weighting those rounded values gives 31.2000 plus 29.1420 plus 12.4440 plus 6.2780, which is 79.0640 and rounds to a baseline of 79.06; rounding last instead would give 79.07. Zeroing the first component gives 0.0000 plus 29.1420 plus 12.4440 plus 6.2780, which is 47.8640 and rounds to 47.86, for a delta of 31.20. Zeroing the second component instead gives 31.2000 plus 0.0000 plus 12.4440 plus 6.2780, which is 49.9220 and rounds to 49.92, for a delta of 29.14. A whole-course penalty gives 0.00 with a delta of 79.06.

**The five-hop imposition cascade.** The office imposes a sanction that is in `PROPOSED` on a case in `DECIDED`, or in `ACCEPTED` on a case in `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED`; any other pairing is `ERR_SANCTION_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. For the formal route the case's `responsibility_finding` must be `RESPONSIBLE`, otherwise `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. `effective_from` must not precede `decided_at`, otherwise `ERR_SANCTION_EFFECTIVE_BEFORE_DECISION`. When the kind is a grade penalty the report's `component_scores` must still join exactly to the offering's `grade_components`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_SCORES_INCOMPLETE`. Once every guard passes, five hops run in order, and all of them must complete before the response is returned.

Hop one, on the sanction itself: `status` becomes `IMPOSED` and `imposed_at` is set to the current logical clock. When the kind is `GRADE_PENALTY_ASSIGNMENT` or `GRADE_PENALTY_COURSE`, `baseline_course_grade_pct`, `adjusted_course_grade_pct` and `grade_penalty_delta_pct` are written by the arithmetic above.

Hop two, on the case: `adjudication_status` becomes `SANCTION_IMPOSED` — from `DECIDED` on the formal route, from `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED` on the informal one — and `notation_status` becomes `PENDING_POSTING` when the sanction's `ladder_rank` is 4 or greater, leaving `expungement_eligible_at` null until the sanction is completed. `version` advances once.

Hop three, on the student: `prior_upheld_count` is recomputed and now includes this case, `active_sanction_points` increases by the sanction's `ladder_rank`, and `standing` is re-derived from the new total.

Hop four, on the student's other cases: every other case of the same student whose `adjudication_status` is neither `CLOSED` nor `DISMISSED` is re-assessed, in that case's `created_at` ascending order. For each, `cross_course_pattern_points`, `prior_upheld_count_at_assessment`, `prior_escalation_points`, `provisional_severity_index`, `severity_index` and `recommended_ladder_rank` are recomputed from the student's new `prior_upheld_count`.

Hop five, on those siblings' proposals: for each sibling case re-assessed in hop four, any sanction still in `PROPOSED` whose `ladder_rank` now falls outside that case's recomputed office band moves to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` `LADDER_RECOMPUTE`, and a notification of kind `SANCTION_RECOMPUTE_NOTICE` is created on that sibling case. A proposal that still fits its recomputed band is left alone. This is the mechanism that makes the optimistic-concurrency guard in §2.8 necessary: a client holding a version of a sibling case read before the cascade is now stale.

**Completion.** The office completes a sanction that is in `IMPOSED`; any other status is `ERR_SANCTION_STATE_INVALID`. No appeal of the case may be in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. On success `status` becomes `COMPLETED` and `completed_at` is set to the current logical clock; the student's `active_sanction_points` decreases by the sanction's `ladder_rank`, exactly reversing hop three, and `standing` is re-derived; the case's `expungement_eligible_at` is computed when its `notation_status` is `POSTED` and left unchanged otherwise; the department's `open_case_count` decreases by one; `adjudication_status` becomes `CLOSED`; and `version` advances. `DECLINED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `VACATED` and `COMPLETED` are all terminal for a sanction.

### 3.9. Appeals

**Filing.** The case's own student files an appeal, supplying `case_id`, a single `grounds` of `PROCEDURE`, `EVIDENCE` or `SANCTION_SEVERITY`, and a `statement` of 20 to 4000 characters. The case must have reached a finding — `adjudication_status` `DECIDED`, `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED` or `SANCTION_IMPOSED` — otherwise `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. The filing window is ten days: the current logical clock must not have passed the case's `decided_at` plus 10 days, otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing exactly at `decided_at` plus ten days is inside the window; one second later is outside. An appeal is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED` when the case already has an appeal in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, or when the case already has two appeals of any status — only one appeal may be open at a time and no more than two may ever be filed. On success the appeal is created in `SUBMITTED` with `filed_at` set to the current logical clock, `hearing_round_at_filing` set to the case's `hearing_round`, and `reduced_ladder_rank`, `decision_reason` and `decided_at` all null. Appeals may be listed for a single case or globally with `case_id`, `status` and `grounds` filters.

**Screening.** The appeals dean screens a `SUBMITTED` appeal with a `decision` of `ACCEPT_FOR_REVIEW` or `DENY_NO_GROUNDS`; a denial requires `decision_reason`, whose absence is an invalid request. Screening an appeal in any other status is `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`, and the case must still be in `DECIDED`, `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED` or `SANCTION_IMPOSED`. One extra rule applies to a case whose `pathway` is `INFORMAL` and whose `adjudication_status` is `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED`: its `grounds` must be `PROCEDURE`, otherwise `ERR_INFORMAL_RESOLUTION_NOT_APPEALABLE`. A student who accepted an informal resolution waived the evidence and severity grounds but never the procedural one.

Accepting for review sets the appeal's `status` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, copies the case's current `adjudication_status` into `pre_appeal_adjudication_status` — the value will be `DECIDED`, `INFORMAL_ACCEPTED` or `SANCTION_IMPOSED` — sets `adjudication_status` to `APPEAL_UNDER_REVIEW`, and advances `version`. Denying for want of grounds sets `status` to `DENIED_NO_GROUNDS`, records `decision_reason`, sets `decided_at` to the current logical clock, creates a notification of kind `APPEAL_OUTCOME_NOTICE`, and leaves the case's `adjudication_status` untouched, because the appeal never reached review.

**Deciding.** The dean decides an `UNDER_REVIEW` appeal with an `outcome` of `UPHELD_ORIGINAL`, `SANCTION_REDUCED`, `REMANDED` or `OVERTURNED`, always with a `decision_reason`, plus a `reduced_ladder_rank` that is required exactly when the outcome is `SANCTION_REDUCED` and forbidden otherwise. Deciding an appeal in any other status is `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`. The grounds must match the outcome: `SANCTION_REDUCED` requires `grounds` `SANCTION_SEVERITY`, while `REMANDED` and `OVERTURNED` require `grounds` `PROCEDURE` or `EVIDENCE`; a mismatch is `ERR_APPEAL_GROUNDS_MISMATCH`. Every decision sets `decided_at` to the current logical clock and creates a notification of kind `APPEAL_OUTCOME_NOTICE` on the case. The governing sanction, wherever the rules below refer to it, is the case's sanction in `IMPOSED` or `COMPLETED`, and when there is more than one, the most recently imposed.

*Upholding the original.* `status` becomes `UPHELD_ORIGINAL`. No sanction changes status and the case's severity fields are untouched. `adjudication_status` is restored verbatim from `pre_appeal_adjudication_status`, which is then set back to null, and `version` advances.

*Reducing the sanction.* `reduced_ladder_rank` must be at least 1 and strictly less than the governing sanction's `ladder_rank`, otherwise `ERR_REDUCED_RANK_INVALID`. On success `status` becomes `SANCTION_REDUCED`; the governing sanction moves to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` `APPEAL_REDUCED`; and a replacement sanction is created with `origin` `APPEAL_REDUCTION`, `replaces_sanction_id` pointing at the vacated row, `ladder_rank` equal to `reduced_ladder_rank`, the `kind` that rank maps to, and a `status` of `IMPOSED` when the vacated sanction was `IMPOSED` and `PROPOSED` otherwise. The student's `active_sanction_points` is recomputed, so it falls by the difference between the old and new ranks when the sanction was imposed, and `standing` is re-derived. The case's `notation_status` becomes `NOT_APPLICABLE` if it was `PENDING_POSTING`, or `EXPUNGED` if it was `POSTED`, but only when `reduced_ladder_rank` is below 4; at rank 4 or above the notation stands. `adjudication_status` is restored from `pre_appeal_adjudication_status`, which returns to null, and `version` advances. This is the only route by which a sanction can come into existence without an office proposal, which is why the appeals dean has no sanction-creation permission of their own.

*Remanding.* The case's `pathway` must be `FORMAL`, otherwise `ERR_CASE_PATHWAY_NOT_SET`, and its `hearing_round` must be no greater than 1, otherwise `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_REACHED` — a case may be remanded once, never twice. On success `status` becomes `REMANDED`; every sanction of the case in `PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED` or `IMPOSED` moves to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` `APPEAL_REMANDED`; `responsibility_finding` returns to `UNDETERMINED`, `finding_basis` to `NONE`, `panel_severity_recommendation` to null and `decided_at` to null; `recommended_ladder_rank` and the office band are recomputed under the formal algorithm with a null panel recommendation, so the rank falls back to the plain tier rank; `notation_status` becomes `NOT_APPLICABLE` if it was `PENDING_POSTING` and stays `POSTED` if it was already posted; a notification of kind `REHEARING_NOTICE` is created, which returns every approved advisor engagement to `REQUESTED`; the student's `active_sanction_points` is recomputed, falling by the rank of any sanction that had been imposed; `pre_appeal_adjudication_status` returns to null; `adjudication_status` becomes `FORMAL_REFERRED`; and `version` advances. The case is now schedulable again, and the ten-day notice clock runs from the new rehearing notice.

*Overturning.* This is a four-hop cascade and every hop must complete before the response is written. Hop one: the appeal's `status` becomes `OVERTURNED` and `decided_at` is set to the clock. Hop two, on the case: `overturned` becomes true, `responsibility_finding` becomes `NOT_RESPONSIBLE`, `finding_basis` becomes `APPEAL`, `adjudication_status` becomes `CLOSED`, `pre_appeal_adjudication_status` returns to null, `notation_status` becomes `EXPUNGED` if it was `POSTED` and `NOT_APPLICABLE` if it was `PENDING_POSTING`, and every sanction of the case in `PROPOSED`, `ACCEPTED` or `IMPOSED` moves to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` `APPEAL_OVERTURNED`. Hop three, on the student: `prior_upheld_count` is recomputed and this case no longer counts, `active_sanction_points` falls by the `ladder_rank` of every sanction that had been imposed, `standing` and `transcript_notation` are re-derived, and the department's `open_case_count` falls by one. Hop four, on the student's other cases: every other case whose `adjudication_status` is neither `CLOSED` nor `DISMISSED` is re-assessed in `created_at` ascending order, recomputing `cross_course_pattern_points`, `prior_upheld_count_at_assessment`, `prior_escalation_points`, `provisional_severity_index`, `severity_index` and `recommended_ladder_rank`, and any sibling sanction in `PROPOSED` that now falls outside its recomputed office band moves to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` `LADDER_RECOMPUTE`. `version` advances once. `DENIED_NO_GROUNDS`, `UPHELD_ORIGINAL`, `SANCTION_REDUCED`, `REMANDED` and `OVERTURNED` are all terminal for an appeal.

### 3.10. Transcript Notation and Expungement

The notation dimension belongs to the registrar and the student. No office or dean operation posts a notation, and posting one can never advance or close a case.

A case's `notation_status` starts at `NOT_APPLICABLE` and is moved to `PENDING_POSTING` only as a side effect of imposing a sanction whose `ladder_rank` is 4 or greater; a rank-1 through rank-3 sanction never produces a notation at all. `expungement_eligible_at` stays null while the notation is pending.

**Posting.** The registrar posts a notation for a case whose `notation_status` is exactly `PENDING_POSTING`; any other value is `ERR_NOTATION_STATE_INVALID`. `notation_status` becomes `POSTED`, the student's `transcript_notation` becomes `POSTED`, `expungement_eligible_at` is computed — or left null when the governing sanction is not yet `COMPLETED` — and `version` advances.

**Eligibility.** The governing sanction is the case's sanction in `IMPOSED` or `COMPLETED`; when the case has more than one, it is the most recently imposed, breaking ties by the most recently created. `expungement_eligible_at` is null when there is no governing sanction, when the governing sanction is not `COMPLETED`, or when the governing sanction's `ladder_rank` is 8, because a notation arising from an expulsion recommendation is permanent. Otherwise it is the **later** of two instants: the governing sanction's `completed_at` plus the ladder waiting period, and the case's `decided_at` plus 365 days. The ladder waiting periods are 365 days at rank 4, 365 days at rank 5, 730 days at rank 6 and 1095 days at rank 7. Taking the later of the two is what makes the rule forward-looking: a sanction completed very quickly is still gated by the 365-day floor measured from the finding, while a slowly completed high-rank sanction is gated by its ladder period instead.

A rank-5 sanction on a case decided on 20 August 2026 and completed on 1 September 2026 yields 1 September 2027, because the ladder period ends then and the decision floor ends on 20 August 2027. A rank-4 sanction on the same case completed on 21 August 2026 yields 21 August 2027, again the later of the two. A rank-7 sanction completed on 1 September 2026 yields 31 August 2029, the ladder period of 1095 days dominating the floor. A completed rank-8 sanction yields null, and so does a rank-6 sanction that is still merely `IMPOSED`.

**Requesting expungement.** The case's own student requests expungement, supplying a `request_statement`. The `notation_status` must be `POSTED` or `EXPUNGEMENT_DENIED`, otherwise `ERR_NOTATION_STATE_INVALID`. Two temporal guards then run in order: the request is rejected with `ERR_EXPUNGEMENT_TOO_EARLY` when `expungement_eligible_at` is null because the governing sanction is not yet completed, or when the current logical clock has not reached `expungement_eligible_at`; and it is rejected with `ERR_NOTATION_NOT_EXPUNGEABLE` when the governing sanction's `ladder_rank` is 8 and therefore carries no waiting period at all. Because the too-early check runs first, a rank-8 notation whose governing sanction is not yet completed reports `ERR_EXPUNGEMENT_TOO_EARLY` rather than the not-expungeable error. On success `notation_status` becomes `EXPUNGEMENT_REQUESTED` and `version` advances.

**Deciding expungement.** The registrar decides with a `decision` of `GRANT` or `DENY` and a `decision_reason` that is required on a denial. The `notation_status` must be `EXPUNGEMENT_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_NOTATION_STATE_INVALID`. Granting sets `notation_status` to `EXPUNGED` and re-derives the student's `transcript_notation`: it is `POSTED` when any other case of that student still has a posted notation, and otherwise `EXPUNGED` — never `NONE`, because this case is now expunged. Denying sets `notation_status` to `EXPUNGEMENT_DENIED`, records the reason, and leaves `transcript_notation` unchanged; the student may request again, and the request-and-deny cycle may repeat without bound. `EXPUNGED` is terminal.

Two system routes also move this dimension without any registrar action, both driven by an appeal. An overturned finding sets `notation_status` from `POSTED` straight to `EXPUNGED` with no waiting period at all, an overturned notation being expunged immediately, and recomputes the student's `transcript_notation`. An overturned or remanded appeal sets `notation_status` from `PENDING_POSTING` back to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, since nothing was ever posted and there is nothing to expunge.

| GET | `/integrity/cases/{id}/appeals` | Sub-resource listing of a case's appeals | IntegrityOfficer, AppealsDean, Student |
| POST | `/integrity/appeals/{id}/screen` | Dean screens the appeal: accept it for review or deny it for want of grounds | AppealsDean |
| POST | `/integrity/appeals/{id}/decide` | Dean decides the appeal: UPHELD_ORIGINAL, SANCTION_REDUCED, REMANDED or OVERTURNED. OVERTURNED is the head of the four-hop cascade | AppealsDean |
