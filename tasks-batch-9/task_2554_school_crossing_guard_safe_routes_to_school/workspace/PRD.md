# School Crossing Safe Routes API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every school morning, a municipal Safe Routes to School program has to answer the same question at hundreds of street corners simultaneously: does this crossing need an adult guard, is that guard actually standing there today, and if not, who can be there within the hour? Today those answers live in spreadsheets, radio calls and the institutional memory of a single supervisor. A warrant study sits in a field notebook until someone types it up; a guard calls in sick at 6:40 a.m. and the substitute search is a sequence of phone calls with no record of who was asked or who declined; a grant cycle closes with money unspent because nobody could rank the candidate projects defensibly. The cost of these gaps is not administrative — it is a child crossing four lanes of unmitigated arterial alone.

This document specifies the backend service that consolidates the entire program into one auditable system of record, mounted under `/srts`. It covers intersection warrant analysis driven by field pedestrian counts, traffic-gap adequacy and three-year crash history; the guard workforce from applicant intake through training, equipment issuance, certification and reliability tracking; post activation and dated staffing assignments; absence intake with an automated substitute-offer ladder and a priced cost of coverage; recommended walking routes with per-segment hazard mapping; public complaint intake with engineering triage and referral; competitive scoring and award of grant-funded infrastructure projects against a conserved funding pool; and term-by-term program evaluation of walk and bike mode share.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **the program is a set of interlocking invariants, not a set of independent records** — a warrant determination that turns negative must strip the posts it justified, vacate their assignments, release their guards, degrade the school's coverage posture and pull the school's published walking routes back to advisory status, all inside the request that recorded the determination. Second, **time is an explicit, server-controlled input**. The service reads a logical UTC clock that only an administrator advances; no rule anywhere consults real wall-clock time. Reliability windows, offer expiry, crash lookbacks and assignment activation are all evaluated against that logical clock, which makes an entire school year reproducible in a single test run.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Roles and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming that user's role. If either header is absent, empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the six roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before the request body is inspected.

The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `TrafficEngineer`, `CrossingSupervisor`, `CrossingGuard`, `SchoolLiaison` and `PublicReporter`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the municipal program administrator: they own the school and intersection registry, guard-post activation, the grant award decision, and the logical clock. A `TrafficEngineer` owns warrant analysis, crash data, complaint triage, engineering referrals and project scoring. A `CrossingSupervisor` owns the guard roster, certification, equipment issuance, post staffing and substitute dispatch. A `CrossingGuard` is an individual guard who may report their own absences, answer substitute offers and read their own roster. A `SchoolLiaison` is the school-side program contact who drafts and publishes walking routes and files the term travel-tally survey. A `PublicReporter` is a member of the public whose write access is limited to filing a complaint.

Role identity binds `X-User-ID` to persisted data differently depending on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `CrossingGuard`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Guard`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Guard` compare the resource's guard reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `CrossingGuard` filing an absence must be the guard named on the target assignment — `X-User-ID` must equal that assignment's `guard_id`, and a mismatch is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) — and a `CrossingGuard` answering a substitute offer must be the current recipient, with `X-User-ID` compared against `current_offer_guard_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `School` compare the resource's `school_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `TrafficEngineer`, `CrossingSupervisor` or `PublicReporter`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `authored_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on a warrant study, and as `triaged_by_user_id` on a complaint, where the corresponding transitions write it — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`.

One rule turns an opaque identifier into an enforced separation of duties. The `TrafficEngineer` who opens a warrant study has their `X-User-ID` recorded as that study's `authored_by_user_id`, and the engineer who later adopts the study's determination onto the intersection must present a *different* `X-User-ID`. An attempt to finalize one's own study is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Two distinct traffic-engineer identities are therefore structurally required before any warrant determination can reach an intersection. Scoring a grant project and awarding money to it are split the same way, but across roles rather than identities: a `TrafficEngineer` scores, a `ProgramAdmin` funds.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, must be an integer, and must fall between a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100 inclusive. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0, must be an integer, and must be 0 or greater. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 range, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Results are always returned in ascending `created_at` order unless a specific endpoint says otherwise. List responses carry `response_type` of `"array"`, and their `meta` object includes the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` — where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window. Several list endpoints additionally accept filter parameters; those are described alongside the workflows they serve in §3 and summarized in §4.

### 2.4. The Logical Program Clock

The service never reads real wall-clock time. Instead it maintains a single **logical UTC clock**, and every timestamp the system mints — `created_at`, `updated_at`, and the domain-specific stamps `recorded_at`, `offered_at`, `offer_expires_at`, `published_at`, `activated_at` and `funded_at` — is derived from that clock's current value. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A `ProgramAdmin` moves the clock with an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a single `to_iso8601` instant. The clock may only move forward or stay where it is: a `to_iso8601` value earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MOVED_BACKWARD` (HTTP 422). A successful call returns a small object rather than a domain entity, carrying `current_utc` (the clock's new value), `activated_assignment_count` and `ended_assignment_count`.

Advancing the clock also runs a **checkpoint sweep**, which is the only mechanism by which staffing assignments change state on their own. The sweep walks assignments in ascending order of `start_date` and then `created_at`, so its outcome is deterministic. Every assignment in `SCHEDULED` whose `start_date` is on or before the new clock date moves `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE`; every assignment in `ACTIVE` whose `end_date` is strictly earlier than the new clock date moves `ACTIVE → ENDED`, and each such ending releases the guard's `duty_status` from `ASSIGNED` to `UNASSIGNED` when the guard is left holding no assignment in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`, then recomputes the affected post's `coverage_status` and the owning school's coverage counters and `coverage_posture`. The counts of assignments moved into `ACTIVE` and into `ENDED` are what `activated_assignment_count` and `ended_assignment_count` report. Beyond the sweep, advancing the clock recomputes every guard's `reliability_score_rolling_90d`, because a clock advance can age absences out of the 90-day rolling window and lift a suspended guard back above the reliability floor, and it recomputes every intersection's `pedestrian_crash_count_window` and `crash_severity_points` against the newly positioned lookback window.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Append-Only Ledgers

Every persisted resource in this system — schools, intersections, warrant studies, guards, guard posts, post assignments, absence reports, walking routes, complaints, grant programs and infrastructure projects — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by a client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at the moment the resource was created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation of the resource, including mutations that only touch derived fields.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the resource. No entity in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`.

A derived field is a mutation like any other. When a school's `coverage_rate_pct` is recomputed because a post two levels below it lost its guard, the school's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments — which is precisely what makes the stale-version guards of §2.9 bite.

Several resources carry **append-only arrays** that record history rather than current state. A school's `mode_share_surveys`, an intersection's `crash_history`, a warrant study's `count_sessions`, a guard's `equipment_issued` and an absence report's `dispatch_offers` all grow by append and are never rewritten wholesale, never reordered and never pruned. Two narrow exceptions exist, and both are rewrites of a status marker on an existing entry rather than deletions: reopening a warrant study for a recount flips the `voided` marker to true on every count session already recorded, and answering or lapsing a substitute offer rewrites that offer's `offer_status`. Entries are never removed.

### 2.6. Resolving Paths and Foreign Keys

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve decides the error. A school path segment that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`; an intersection segment yields `ERR_INTERSECTION_NOT_FOUND`; a warrant-study segment yields `ERR_WARRANT_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`; a guard segment yields `ERR_GUARD_NOT_FOUND`; a guard-post segment yields `ERR_GUARD_POST_NOT_FOUND`; a post-assignment segment yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an absence-report segment yields `ERR_ABSENCE_NOT_FOUND`; a walking-route segment yields `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`; a complaint segment yields `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`; a grant-program segment yields `ERR_GRANT_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; and an infrastructure-project segment yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`. All of these are HTTP 404.

Foreign keys carried in the request body are resolved only after every path identifier has resolved, and among themselves they are checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. A `school_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`; `intersection_id` yields `ERR_INTERSECTION_NOT_FOUND`; `guard_id` yields `ERR_GUARD_NOT_FOUND`; `post_assignment_id` yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `grant_program_id` yields `ERR_GRANT_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; and `source_complaint_id` yields `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`. Walking-route segments are a special case: every segment carrying a non-null `intersection_id` is resolved at draft time, with the segments examined in ascending `segment_order` and the first unresolvable one reported as `ERR_INTERSECTION_NOT_FOUND`. Body foreign-key resolution applies when registering an intersection, opening a warrant study, proposing a guard post, creating a staffing assignment, filing an absence report, drafting a walking route, and proposing an infrastructure project.

### 2.7. Coverage Propagation: Post to School to Route

Coverage is a four-level derived quantity, and every level is recomputed inside the request that disturbs the level below it. There is no deferred or eventual recomputation anywhere in this system.

At the lowest level, a `PostAssignment` carries `open_absence_count` — the number of absence reports on that assignment sitting in `DISPATCH_PENDING`, `OFFER_OUT` or `UNCOVERED_GAP` — and derives `coverage_state`, which is `GAP` when `open_absence_count` is 1 or more and `STAFFED` otherwise. One level up, a `GuardPost` carries `open_gap_count`, the number of absence reports in those same three statuses across all assignments belonging to that post, and derives `coverage_status`: `UNSTAFFED` when no assignment on the post is in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`; `UNCOVERED` when at least one such assignment exists and `open_gap_count` is 1 or more; and `COVERED` when at least one such assignment exists and `open_gap_count` is exactly 0.

At the school level, only guard posts whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose intersection belongs to the school participate. `active_post_count` is the number of such posts. `uncovered_post_count` is the subset of those whose `coverage_status` is either `UNCOVERED` or `UNSTAFFED`, and `covered_post_count` is `active_post_count` less `uncovered_post_count`; the partition invariant that `active_post_count` equals `covered_post_count` plus `uncovered_post_count` holds at every observable instant. `coverage_rate_pct` is 100.00 when `active_post_count` is 0; otherwise it is 100 multiplied by `covered_post_count`, divided by `active_post_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `coverage_posture` is then `OPEN` when `uncovered_post_count` is exactly 0; otherwise `SUSPENDED` when `coverage_rate_pct` is strictly below 50.00; otherwise `RESTRICTED`. A rate of exactly 50.00 is `RESTRICTED`, not `SUSPENDED`. A school with no active posts is `OPEN` with a rate of 100.00. Note the consequence: activating a post that has not yet been staffed makes it `UNSTAFFED`, which counts as uncovered, so activation alone can move a school from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` with no absence involved at all.

The topmost level of propagation reaches the school's published walking routes. When `coverage_posture` enters `SUSPENDED` — whether from `OPEN` or from `RESTRICTED` — every walking route of that school whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` moves to `ADVISORY_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `SCHOOL_COVERAGE_SUSPENDED`. When the posture later leaves `SUSPENDED` for either `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN`, every route of that school in `ADVISORY_HOLD` returns to `PUBLISHED` and its `hold_reason` is cleared to null. The transitions `OPEN → RESTRICTED` and `RESTRICTED → OPEN` carry no route cascade, because neither crosses the suspension boundary. This recomputation is triggered by any change to a guard post's `coverage_status` or `status` under the school, and by the creation, coverage or rescission of any absence report under the school.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Every rounding operation in this specification uses **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. All decimal arithmetic is performed in exact decimal, never accumulated in binary floating point.

Percentages and scores are stored to two decimal places: `coverage_rate_pct`, `walk_bike_mode_share_pct`, `walk_bike_mode_share_weighted_pct`, `mode_share_trend_pct`, `warrant_score`, `hazard_score`, `benefit_cost_score`, `reliability_score_rolling_90d` and `reliability_score_season_to_date`. Two intermediate quantities are carried at four decimal places before they are consumed, so that weight rounding cannot drift the result: the gap-deficit ratio inside the warrant score, and `benefit_points` inside the project benefit-cost score.

Money is never a decimal. `total_cents`, `committed_cents`, `remaining_cents`, `next_cycle_carryover_cents`, `requested_cost_cents`, `awarded_cents` and `substitute_cost_cents` are all integer cents. In the substitute-cost computation, every multiplication is rounded half up to a whole cent at the step in which it appears and never deferred to the end; in grant allocation no rounding occurs at all, because only integer cents move.

Comparisons against thresholds run against the already-rounded stored value, never against an unrounded intermediate. The coverage-posture test reads the rounded `coverage_rate_pct`; the warrant determination reads the rounded two-decimal `warrant_score` without re-rounding; the substitute selector compares `reliability_score_season_to_date` at its stored two-decimal precision.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Guards on Warrant, Roster and Grant Writes

Three write operations reach across an aggregate boundary in a way that would let a client silently overwrite a picture it has never observed. Each of those three requires the client to declare, as a non-optional body field, the `version` it last observed on the relevant parent resource. If the declared value does not equal the parent's current stored `version`, the write is refused. No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and where a version field is required it may not be omitted.

Opening a warrant study requires `expected_intersection_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Intersection`. A new study legitimately supersedes the intersection's standing warrant picture — on finalization it rewrites `warrant_status` and `latest_warrant_score` — so a writer holding a stale view would be choosing a count window and a crash lookback against an outdated warrant history. When `expected_intersection_version` does not match the intersection's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INTERSECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Creating a staffing assignment requires `expected_guard_post_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `GuardPost`. Each new assignment changes the post's staffing picture — its `coverage_status`, its `open_gap_count` and the overlap surface every later assignment is checked against — so a writer whose declared version is behind the stored one is reasoning about a roster that no longer exists. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GUARD_POST_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Both awarding grant funds to a project and withdrawing an award require `expected_grant_program_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `GrantProgram`. Every award and every withdrawal moves `committed_cents` and `remaining_cents` and increments the program's `version`, so a writer holding a stale version is deciding against a balance and a ranking pool that have already moved. A mismatch on either operation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRANT_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

When one request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fixed rather than incidental. Checks are grouped into bands, and the earliest band that finds a violation decides the response:

1. **Header presence and role validity** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Endpoint authorization** — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every required body field must be present; every enum-typed field must name a declared member; integers must be integral and within their declared bounds; dates must parse as ISO 8601 dates and datetimes as ISO 8601 UTC instants; `limit` must be an integer in the range 1 to 100 and `offset` an integer of 0 or more; `session_end_date` must be on or after `session_start_date`; `count_window_end` must be strictly later than `count_window_start`; `end_date` must be on or after `start_date`; `certification_expires_on` must be strictly later than `certified_on`; a walking route's `segments` array must be non-empty with `segment_order` values forming exactly 1 through N with no gaps and no duplicates and each `length_ft` at least 1; a mode-share survey whose five tally buckets sum to zero, or whose `enrollment_at_survey` is below 1, is rejected here; `requested_cost_cents` must be at least 1; `total_cents` must be at least 1; and training `hours` must be greater than 0.
4. **Path resource existence** — the 404 family of §2.6, resolved left to right.
5. **Body foreign-key existence** — the same 404 family, resolved in declared field order.
6. **State precondition** — the target resource must be in a state from which the requested transition exists (HTTP 409 for most of this band).
7. **Stale parent version** — the three guards of §2.9 (HTTP 412).
8. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` on warrant finalization (HTTP 403).
9. **Safety and compliance** — warrant coverage for posts and routes, guard certification, equipment and leave status, route hazard ceiling, guard reliability floor and training hours.
10. **Temporal conflict** — assignment overlap, count-window overlap with its cool-off buffer, session-window containment, session-date containment and absence-date containment.
11. **Business constraint** — count coverage sufficiency, absence duplication, offer recipient identity, offer expiry, absence-filing ownership for a `CrossingGuard`, funding rank, warrant precondition for scoring, and clock monotonicity.
12. **Referential consistency** — complaint referability and walking-route segment ownership.
13. **Fiscal capacity** — `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, evaluated last of all.

Two consequences of this ordering are load-bearing and are repeated where they apply in §3. A project that is not in `SCORED` and also could not be afforded reports `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_SCORED` from band 6, never `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_EXHAUSTED` from band 13. And a route whose unmitigated crossing sits at an unwarranted intersection reports `ERR_ROUTE_CROSSING_NOT_WARRANTED` from band 9, never the segment-ownership error from band 12, even when both faults are present.

### 2.11. Conservation of Grant Funds

A grant program's `total_cents` is fixed at creation and never changes. At every observable instant, `total_cents` equals `committed_cents` plus `remaining_cents` — there is no third bucket and no rounding slack, because only whole cents ever move. `committed_cents` is by definition the sum of `awarded_cents` across the program's projects whose `status` is `FUNDED`, and `remaining_cents` is `total_cents` less `committed_cents`. Awarding funds and withdrawing an award are exact inverses on the balance, so a fund followed by a withdraw of the same project returns the program to a byte-identical balance split, with only the program's `version` and the project's retained `funded_at` history bearing witness that anything happened.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Schools, Crossing Windows and the Seasonal Calendar

Everything in the program hangs off a **School**, and only a `ProgramAdmin` may register one. Registration captures the school's `name`, its municipal `district_code`, and its `enrollment_count` — which must be at least 1, because it later serves as the denominator of one of the two mode-share variants. The instructional season is bounded by `session_start_date` and `session_end_date`, both ISO 8601 calendar dates in UTC, with the end date required to fall on or after the start date. Those two dates are not decoration: they are the outer bounds every staffing assignment window must fit inside, and the closed window over which one of the two guard-reliability variants is computed.

Daily crossing coverage is described by four clock times, each given as an `"HH:MM"` string interpreted as a UTC clock time on whatever calendar date is under consideration: `am_window_start_utc` and `am_window_end_utc` bound the morning arrival crossing, and `pm_window_start_utc` and `pm_window_end_utc` bound the afternoon dismissal crossing. Two calendar exception lists refine that pattern. `no_school_dates` is an array of ISO 8601 dates on which no crossing coverage is required at all; it defaults to an empty array, and an absence report filed for a date that appears in it is refused with `ERR_ABSENCE_ON_NON_SCHOOL_DAY`. `early_dismissal_dates`, also defaulting to an empty array, lists the dates on which children leave early; on each of those dates the afternoon shift's start is displaced by `early_dismissal_pm_offset_minutes`, a signed minute offset that defaults to negative 120 — that is, two hours earlier — so that the pinned notice horizon of a PM absence on an early-dismissal day reflects the real, earlier shift.

The registration payload closes with `baseline_walk_bike_mode_share_pct`, the program's starting walk-plus-bike share expressed as a percentage between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive; it is the figure every later survey is measured against. A freshly registered school begins with `coverage_posture` at `OPEN`, `coverage_rate_pct` at 100.00, `active_post_count`, `covered_post_count` and `uncovered_post_count` all at 0, an empty `mode_share_surveys` array, and `walk_bike_mode_share_pct`, `walk_bike_mode_share_weighted_pct`, `mode_share_trend_pct` and `latest_survey_term_code` all null. Reading a school back returns that derived coverage posture and those mode-share figures together, and a school's intersections and walking routes are each reachable as their own filtered collection.

### 3.2. Crossing Locations, Crash History and Opening a Study

An **Intersection** is a physical crossing location registered under a school by a `ProgramAdmin`. It is created with a payload naming the parent `school_id`, a human-readable `cross_streets` label such as "Maple Ave & 3rd St", the traffic control actually present as `control_type` — one of `UNCONTROLLED`, `STOP_SIGN` or `SIGNALIZED` — the posted `speed_limit_mph`, which must be between 5 and 65 inclusive, and `lanes_crossed`, the number of travel lanes a child must cross, which must be at least 1. An optional `posted_school_zone` boolean records whether a school-zone speed reduction is posted; it defaults to false. Both `school_id` and, on the study side, `intersection_id` are immutable once set. Registering an intersection increments the parent school's `version` by 1. A new intersection starts with `warrant_status` at `NOT_STUDIED`, `latest_warrant_score` null, an empty `crash_history` and `open_complaint_count` at 0.

Crash data arrives one record at a time, appended by a `TrafficEngineer` or a `ProgramAdmin`, and each record carries a `crash_date`, a `severity` drawn from `PROPERTY_DAMAGE_ONLY`, `INJURY`, `SEVERE_INJURY` and `FATAL`, and two booleans, `involved_pedestrian` and `involved_school_age`. The appended entry is stamped with `recorded_at` from the logical clock. Appending refreshes two rolling aggregates on the intersection over a **1095-day window — three years — ending at the logical clock's date, with both ends inclusive**. `pedestrian_crash_count_window` counts the entries in that window whose `involved_pedestrian` is true. `crash_severity_points` weights those same pedestrian-involved entries: 3 points for each `PROPERTY_DAMAGE_ONLY`, 8 for each `INJURY`, 15 for each `SEVERE_INJURY` and 25 for each `FATAL`, summed. The append increments the intersection's `version` by 1. Because the window is anchored on the clock rather than on the record, both aggregates are also refreshed on every clock advance.

A **WarrantStudy** is opened by a `TrafficEngineer` against one intersection, and its creation payload is short: the `intersection_id`, the observation window bounds `count_window_start` and `count_window_end`, and the stale-version declaration `expected_intersection_version` described in §2.9. The window's lower bound is inclusive, and `count_window_end` must be strictly later than `count_window_start`. That end date does double duty: it is also the inclusive anchor of the study's own three-year crash lookback.

Windows on the same intersection may not crowd each other. A new study is refused with `ERR_COUNT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) when the intersection already carries a study — **in any status, including finalized ones** — such that the new window's start falls strictly earlier than the existing window's end extended forward by a 30-day cool-off buffer, and the new window's end falls strictly later than the existing window's start. Half-open comparison at the leading edge and the buffer at the trailing edge together mean that a new window starting exactly 30 days after a prior window's end is accepted, while one starting 29 days after is rejected. This check sits in the temporal band, after the stale-intersection-version guard has cleared.

A study is born in `DRAFT`, with an empty `count_sessions` array, `recount_round` at 0, `authored_by_user_id` set to the creating engineer's `X-User-ID`, and `finalized_by_user_id`, `scoring_variant`, `warrant_score`, `determination`, `reopen_reason`, `included_child_count`, `included_adequate_gaps`, `included_vehicle_count`, `pedestrian_crash_count_3yr` and `severe_pedestrian_crash_count_3yr` all null. Creation immediately pushes the parent intersection's `warrant_status` to `UNDER_STUDY` and increments the intersection's `version` by 1. Standing determinations are deliberately not sticky: a study opened at an intersection currently sitting in `WARRANTED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `NOT_WARRANTED` moves it to `UNDER_STUDY` all the same, and additionally clears `latest_warrant_score` back to null — which is exactly why a client reasoning from a stale intersection view must be refused.

### 3.3. Counting, Scoring and Adopting a Determination

Field observation is recorded session by session, each appended to the study's `count_sessions` array by a `TrafficEngineer`. A session carries a `session_date`, a `period` naming which part of the day was observed — `AM_ARRIVAL`, `MIDDAY` or `PM_DISMISSAL` — the `observed_minutes` spent counting, which must be at least 1, and four tallies that must each be 0 or greater: `child_count`, `adult_count`, `vehicle_count` and `adequate_gaps`, the last being the number of gaps in traffic judged long enough for a child to cross. The appended entry is stamped `recorded_at` from the logical clock and marked with `voided` set to false. Recording is permitted only while the study's `status` is `DRAFT` or `COUNTING`; anywhere else the request is refused with `ERR_STUDY_NOT_IN_COUNTING` (HTTP 409). The `session_date` must fall inside the study's closed observation window, from `count_window_start` through `count_window_end` inclusive; outside it, the rejection is `ERR_SESSION_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). The first session recorded carries the study `DRAFT → COUNTING`; every subsequent session is a self-loop that leaves the study in `COUNTING`. Any number of sessions may be recorded inside the window, and every append increments the study's `version` by 1.

Analysis is a separate, deliberate act. It requires the study to be in `COUNTING` — otherwise `ERR_STUDY_NOT_IN_COUNTING` — and it requires the observation to actually cover both ends of the school day: there must be at least two non-voided sessions whose `period` is `AM_ARRIVAL` or `PM_DISMISSAL`, **and at least one of each of those two periods**. A study holding only `MIDDAY` sessions is refused here with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COUNT_COVERAGE` (HTTP 422) rather than being scored at zero, and so is a study holding two morning sessions and no afternoon session.

Analysis first fixes the study's `scoring_variant`, snapshotting it so that a later change to the intersection's `control_type` cannot retroactively rewrite a finalized score. The variant is `GAP_ADEQUACY` when the parent intersection's `control_type` is `UNCONTROLLED`, and `EXPOSURE_INDEX` when it is `STOP_SIGN` or `SIGNALIZED`.

Analysis then aggregates. The **included set** consists of exactly those `count_sessions` entries whose `voided` is false and whose `period` is `AM_ARRIVAL` or `PM_DISMISSAL`; `MIDDAY` sessions are stored and returned but excluded from every term, and voided sessions are excluded whatever their period. Over that included set, `included_child_count` is the sum of `child_count`, `included_adequate_gaps` is the sum of `adequate_gaps`, and `included_vehicle_count` is the sum of `vehicle_count`. Separately, over the parent intersection's `crash_history`, `pedestrian_crash_count_3yr` counts the entries whose `involved_pedestrian` is true and whose `crash_date` falls in the closed 1095-day window ending at the study's `count_window_end` — both ends inclusive, so a crash falling exactly 1095 days before the window end is counted while one 1096 days before is not — and `severe_pedestrian_crash_count_3yr` is the subset of those whose `severity` is `SEVERE_INJURY` or `FATAL`.

The score itself is assembled from four ingredients. A **speed multiplier** is 1.30 when the intersection's `speed_limit_mph` is 35 or more, 1.15 when it is 30 or more but below 35, and 1.00 otherwise. A **gap-deficiency ratio** is one minus the ratio of `included_adequate_gaps` to the greater of 1 and `included_child_count`, floored at zero so it never goes negative, rounded half up to four decimal places. A **crash term** is 5.0 points for each pedestrian-involved crash in the window plus a further 10.0 points for each of those crashes that was severe or fatal — severe crashes therefore contribute to both counts, so a single fatal pedestrian crash adds 15.0 — with the whole term capped at 25.0. An **exposure term** multiplies `included_child_count` by `included_vehicle_count` and divides the product by a variant-specific divisor.

Under `GAP_ADEQUACY`, gap deficiency dominates: the exposure term divides the child-times-vehicle product by 10000 and is capped at 40.0, the gap-deficiency ratio is multiplied by 45.0 with no independent ceiling of its own, and the raw score is the sum of that weighted gap term, the exposure term and the crash term, the whole sum then multiplied by the speed multiplier. Under `EXPOSURE_INDEX`, volume dominates: the exposure term divides the same product by 8000 — a term 25 percent larger per unit of exposure — and is capped at 55.0, while the gap contribution is the gap-deficiency ratio multiplied by 20.0 and capped at 20.0; again the three terms are summed and the sum is multiplied by the speed multiplier. In both variants the raw result is then capped at 100.0 and rounded half up to two decimal places to give `warrant_score`. Identical count sessions and identical crash history therefore yield materially different scores at an uncontrolled crossing and at a signalized one; the divergence is the intended behaviour, not an artifact. Recording the score also stores the five aggregates named above alongside it, and moves the study `COUNTING → ANALYZED`.

Immediately after the score is computed, a `determination` is pinned onto the study. A `warrant_score` of 60.00 or more yields `WARRANTED` — exactly 60.00 qualifies. Failing that, a score of 40.00 or more accompanied by `pedestrian_crash_count_3yr` of at least 1 yields `CONDITIONAL`. Anything else yields `NOT_WARRANTED`; so 59.99 with one pedestrian crash is `CONDITIONAL`, while 59.99 with no pedestrian crashes is `NOT_WARRANTED`. The comparison runs against the already-rounded two-decimal score with no re-rounding.

Adoption of the determination onto the intersection is a distinct step, and it must be performed by a different traffic engineer than the one who opened the study — an `X-User-ID` equal to the study's `authored_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Adoption requires the study to be in `ANALYZED`; from any other status the refusal is `ERR_STUDY_NOT_ANALYZED` (HTTP 409). An optional `engineer_note` may accompany the request. On success the study's `status` moves to whichever of `WARRANTED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `NOT_WARRANTED` matches its pinned `determination`, `finalized_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, the study's `version` increments, and the determination is projected onto the parent intersection: `warrant_status` becomes the determination and `latest_warrant_score` becomes the study's `warrant_score`, with the intersection's `version` incrementing by 1. Because `latest_warrant_score` always reflects the most recently created finalized study on the intersection, ordered by `created_at` descending among studies in `WARRANTED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `NOT_WARRANTED`, re-finalizing an older study never displaces a newer determination. `WARRANTED` is the study lifecycle's one terminal state.

What follows adoption differs sharply across the three branches, and the differences are the whole point. On the `ANALYZED → WARRANTED` branch, nothing else happens: the intersection is fully warranted, every existing guard post remains as it was, and no suspension cascade fires. On the `ANALYZED → CONDITIONAL` branch, a conditional warrant permits morning staffing only, so a four-link cascade runs. First the intersection's `warrant_status` becomes `CONDITIONAL` and `latest_warrant_score` is rewritten. Second, every guard post at that intersection whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `required_shift` is `PM` or `BOTH` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `WARRANT_LAPSED`; posts whose `required_shift` is `AM` are untouched. Third, every assignment on each newly suspended post whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` set to `POST_SUSPENDED`. Fourth, each guard whose assignment was vacated and who is now left holding no assignment in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` has `duty_status` moved `ASSIGNED → UNASSIGNED`. Every affected post's `coverage_status` is then recomputed, along with the school's `active_post_count`, `covered_post_count`, `uncovered_post_count`, `coverage_rate_pct` and `coverage_posture` — and if the posture enters `SUSPENDED`, the school's `PUBLISHED` walking routes go to `ADVISORY_HOLD` per §2.7.

The `ANALYZED → NOT_WARRANTED` branch runs the identical four-link cascade with one difference in its second link: because the intersection is no longer warranted for any part of the day, **every** `ACTIVE` guard post at the intersection moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `WARRANT_LAPSED`, regardless of `required_shift`. Links three and four are unchanged — assignments in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` become `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` set to `POST_SUSPENDED`, and guards left with nothing return to `UNASSIGNED` — and the same coverage recomputation follows, with each post falling to `UNSTAFFED` once all of its assignments are terminal.

Counting can be reopened, and this is what makes the study lifecycle cyclic from two distinct points. A `TrafficEngineer` may reopen a study whose `status` is `ANALYZED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `NOT_WARRANTED`, supplying a mandatory free-text `reopen_reason`; a `WARRANTED` study is terminal and any other status is refused with `ERR_STUDY_NOT_REOPENABLE` (HTTP 409). Reopening from `ANALYZED` is a recount; reopening from `NOT_WARRANTED` is an appeal of a negative determination; reopening from `CONDITIONAL` is a request to reconsider a partial one. All three carry the study back to `COUNTING` and all three run an identical effect set: `voided` is flipped to true on every existing entry of `count_sessions` with no entry deleted; `warrant_score`, `scoring_variant`, `determination`, `included_child_count`, `included_adequate_gaps`, `included_vehicle_count`, `pedestrian_crash_count_3yr` and `severe_pedestrian_crash_count_3yr` are all cleared to null; `recount_round` increments by 1 and the supplied `reopen_reason` is stored; the parent intersection returns to `UNDER_STUDY` with `latest_warrant_score` cleared to null and its `version` incremented by 1; and the study's own `version` increments. A study sitting in `COUNTING` with nothing but voided sessions cannot be analyzed until at least one fresh `AM_ARRIVAL` and one fresh `PM_DISMISSAL` session have been recorded.

### 3.4. Building the Guard Workforce: Training, Equipment, Certification and Duty

A **Guard** enters the system as an applicant, enrolled by a `CrossingSupervisor` or a `ProgramAdmin` with a payload naming the guard's legal `full_name`, the `home_zone_code` of the zone they live in, and their `hire_date`. An optional `substitute_pool_optin` boolean records whether the guard is willing to be called for other people's shifts; it defaults to true. A new guard begins with `certification_status` at `APPLICANT`, `duty_status` at `UNASSIGNED`, `training_hours_completed` and `hours_since_last_certification` at 0, an empty `equipment_issued` array, `equipment_complete` false, `certified_on`, `certification_expires_on` and `suspension_reason` all null, and both reliability figures — `reliability_score_rolling_90d` and `reliability_score_season_to_date` — at 100.00.

A guard carries **two independent state dimensions on the same record**, and neither one drives the other. `certification_status` moves among `APPLICANT`, `IN_TRAINING`, `CERTIFIED` and `SUSPENDED`. `duty_status` moves among `UNASSIGNED`, `ASSIGNED` and `ON_LEAVE`. A guard may be `SUSPENDED` while `ON_LEAVE`, or `CERTIFIED` while `UNASSIGNED`; the only place the certification dimension reaches into the duty dimension is the reliability-breach cascade described at the end of this subsection.

Training is recorded one entry at a time by a `CrossingSupervisor` or `ProgramAdmin`, supplying the `training_date`, the number of `hours` completed — which must be greater than 0 — and the `curriculum_code` of the module taken. Each record adds those hours both to the lifetime `training_hours_completed` and to `hours_since_last_certification`, the refresher counter. The first training record carries the guard `APPLICANT → IN_TRAINING`; further records are a self-loop that leaves `IN_TRAINING` in place, and a guard who is already `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED` may also log training without their certification dimension moving. Every training record recomputes both reliability variants, because the training bonus that feeds them can change, and increments the guard's `version` by 1.

Equipment is issued the same way, one item at a time, each issuance naming an `item_type` — one of `HIGH_VIS_VEST`, `STOP_PADDLE` or `WHISTLE` — and the `issued_on` date. The issuance is appended to `equipment_issued` and stamped with `recorded_at` from the logical clock. Once the array holds at least one entry for each of the three item types, the derived `equipment_complete` flag becomes true. Nothing about a guard's certification or staffing is possible while that flag is false.

Certification, recertification and reinstatement are all the same act performed by a `CrossingSupervisor`, distinguished only by the guard's current `certification_status`, and all three supply the same two dates: `certified_on` and `certification_expires_on`, the latter required to be strictly later than the former. A guard still in `APPLICANT` has no training on record and is refused with `ERR_GUARD_NOT_IN_TRAINING` (HTTP 409); the operation is available only from `IN_TRAINING`, `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`. From `IN_TRAINING` this is **initial certification**, which requires `training_hours_completed` of at least 12.0. From `CERTIFIED` it is **recertification**, a self-loop that requires `hours_since_last_certification` of at least 4.0 — refresher hours accumulated since the last certification, which is why that counter exists separately from the lifetime total. Either shortfall is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). From `SUSPENDED` it is **reinstatement**, which requires `reliability_score_rolling_90d` of at least 75.00 evaluated against the current logical clock; below that floor the refusal is `ERR_RELIABILITY_TOO_LOW` (HTTP 422), and because the rolling window is anchored on the clock, reinstatement becomes reachable only once the clock has advanced far enough for the offending absences to age out. All three paths additionally require `equipment_complete` to be true, refusing otherwise with `ERR_GUARD_EQUIPMENT_INCOMPLETE`; the reliability floor is checked before the training-hours gate. On success, `certification_status` becomes `CERTIFIED`, `certified_on` and `certification_expires_on` are written (overwriting any prior values), `hours_since_last_certification` resets to 0, `suspension_reason` is cleared to null, and the guard's `version` increments by 1.

The duty dimension is moved by a `CrossingSupervisor` supplying a single `duty_action`, either `ON_LEAVE` or `RETURN`. `ON_LEAVE` is accepted from `UNASSIGNED` or `ASSIGNED` and puts the guard in `ON_LEAVE`, which removes them from every future substitute candidate set while leaving all of their existing assignments completely untouched — the two dimensions really are independent. `RETURN` is accepted only from `ON_LEAVE`, and its destination is a fork decided by the guard's assignment set rather than by anything in the request body: a guard still holding at least one assignment in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` returns to `ASSIGNED`, while a guard holding none returns to `UNASSIGNED`. Asking for `ON_LEAVE` on a guard already `ON_LEAVE`, or `RETURN` on a guard who is not `ON_LEAVE`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DUTY_ACTION` (HTTP 422). Guards also move between `UNASSIGNED` and `ASSIGNED` without any explicit request: taking on a new staffing assignment moves `UNASSIGNED → ASSIGNED`, and losing the last assignment in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` — whether it ended, was vacated or was superseded — moves `ASSIGNED → UNASSIGNED`. Every duty-status change increments the guard's `version` by 1.

Reliability is measured twice, on the same underlying absence history, by two variants that deliberately rank the same guard differently. Both begin from a base of 100.0 and both add the same **training bonus**: the whole number of complete four-hour blocks in `training_hours_completed`, taken as one point per block, capped at 10.0 — so 4.0 hours earn 1 point, 11.9 hours earn 2 points, and 40 or more hours earn the maximum 10. Both variants exclude absence reports whose `status` is `RESCINDED` entirely, since a withdrawn report never counts against a guard, while absences that reached `COVERED` **do** still count, because the guard was still absent and a substitute merely filled in. In each variant the penalty is a coefficient times the number of qualifying absences plus a second coefficient times the number of those absences whose `reason` is `UNEXCUSED`; an unexcused absence is therefore charged under both coefficients. The score is the base, less the penalty, plus the training bonus, clamped into the range 0.00 to 100.00 and rounded half up to two decimal places.

`reliability_score_rolling_90d` counts absences whose `created_at` falls in the closed 90-day window ending at the current logical clock — anchored on when the report was filed, not on the day being missed, so a report filed for a future school day counts immediately and ages out 90 days after filing. Its coefficients are 4.0 per absence and a further 6.0 per unexcused absence, so a single unexcused absence costs 10.0 points and three unexcused absences alone cost 30.0, dropping an untrained guard to 70.00. `reliability_score_season_to_date` instead counts absences whose `absence_date` falls in the closed window from `session_start_date` through `session_end_date` of the school the absence belongs to — resolved from the absence through its assignment, that assignment's post, that post's intersection, and finally the school — with no reference to the logical clock at all, so nothing ever ages out mid-season. Its coefficients are 2.5 per absence and a further 7.5 per unexcused absence, forgiving excused absences more and punishing unexcused ones harder. Neither variant is ever substituted for the other: the certification gate reads the rolling figure, the substitute selector ranks on the season-to-date figure, and both are always maintained on the guard. The rolling figure is re-evaluated on every read of the guard, on absence creation and rescission, on absence coverage, on every training record, and on every clock advance.

The rolling figure carries one hard consequence. When a newly created absence report drops `reliability_score_rolling_90d` **strictly below 75.00** for a guard whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`, that guard moves `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `RELIABILITY_BREACH`, and a cascade follows: every assignment of that guard whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` set to `GUARD_SUSPENDED`; the guard's `duty_status` then moves `ASSIGNED → UNASSIGNED` once no non-terminal assignment remains; and each post that lost an assignment recomputes its `coverage_status` while each affected school recomputes its post counters and `coverage_posture`, with the walking-route consequences of §2.7 following from there.

### 3.5. Activating Posts and Staffing Them

A **GuardPost** is a specific station at an intersection for a specific part of the school day, proposed by a `CrossingSupervisor` or a `ProgramAdmin` with a payload naming the parent `intersection_id`, an operational `post_label` such as "Maple NW corner", the dispatch `zone_code` of the post, and the `required_shift` it must be staffed for — `AM`, `PM` or `BOTH`. Proposing a post increments the parent intersection's `version` by 1. The post starts in `PROPOSED` with `coverage_status` at `UNSTAFFED`, `open_gap_count` at 0, and `activated_at` and `suspension_reason` null.

Activation is reserved to a `ProgramAdmin` and is gated by the warrant. The post must currently be in `PROPOSED` or `SUSPENDED`, since activating an already-`ACTIVE` post is refused with `ERR_POST_NOT_ACTIVATABLE` (HTTP 409); activation from `SUSPENDED` is the reactivation loop that follows a re-established warrant. Next, the parent intersection's `warrant_status` must be `WARRANTED` or `CONDITIONAL`; anything else — `NOT_STUDIED`, `UNDER_STUDY` or `NOT_WARRANTED` — is refused with `ERR_POST_NOT_WARRANTED` (HTTP 409). A `CONDITIONAL` warrant is not disqualifying by itself, but it permits morning coverage only, so a separate check runs immediately after the first one clears: at a `CONDITIONAL` intersection the post's `required_shift` must be `AM`, and a post declaring `PM` or `BOTH` is refused with `ERR_POST_SHIFT_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). On success the post's `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `suspension_reason` is cleared to null, and the post's `version` increments by 1 — this last is the anchor the stale-post-version guard of §2.9 compares against. The owning school then recomputes `active_post_count`, `covered_post_count`, `uncovered_post_count`, `coverage_rate_pct` and `coverage_posture`; because a freshly activated post has no assignment yet and is therefore `UNSTAFFED`, it counts as uncovered, and activating a post can degrade a school's posture on its own.

Staffing is the exclusive province of a `CrossingSupervisor`. A **PostAssignment** is created against an active post with a payload naming the `guard_id` to install, the inclusive first day of duty `start_date`, the inclusive last day `end_date` — which must fall on or after the start — and the `expected_guard_post_version` declaration of §2.9. The post itself is identified by the path, and both `guard_post_id` and `guard_id` are immutable on the resulting assignment. The post must be in `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_POST_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409); the stale-version guard is evaluated immediately after that state check.

Four safety gates on the guard then run **in this order**, and each carries HTTP 422. The named guard's `certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED`, or the refusal is `ERR_GUARD_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The guard's `certification_expires_on` must be on or after the assignment's `end_date`, so that a certification lapsing mid-window is caught up front rather than discovered in February; a shortfall is `ERR_GUARD_CERT_EXPIRED`. The guard's `equipment_complete` must be true — all three of `HIGH_VIS_VEST`, `STOP_PADDLE` and `WHISTLE` issued — or the refusal is `ERR_GUARD_EQUIPMENT_INCOMPLETE`. And the guard's `duty_status` must not be `ON_LEAVE`, or the refusal is `ERR_GUARD_ON_LEAVE`.

Two temporal gates follow, in this order. First, **overlap on the guard**: the request is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) when the same guard already holds an assignment in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` whose date range overlaps the new one on closed intervals — that is, the new `start_date` falls on or before the existing `end_date` and the new `end_date` falls on or after the existing `start_date` — **and** the two shifts conflict. Shift conflict is asymmetric and worth stating exactly: `BOTH` conflicts with `AM`, with `PM` and with `BOTH`; `AM` conflicts only with `AM` and with `BOTH`; `PM` conflicts only with `PM` and with `BOTH`. Back-to-back windows are legal — when the new `start_date` is the day immediately after the existing `end_date` there is no overlap. Second, **containment in the season**: the whole window must lie inside the school's instructional session, meaning `start_date` on or after `session_start_date` and `end_date` on or before `session_end_date`, with the school resolved from the post through its intersection; a window poking outside the session is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OUTSIDE_SESSION` (HTTP 422).

On success the assignment is created in `SCHEDULED`, with `shift` copied from the parent post's `required_shift`, `open_absence_count` at 0 and `coverage_state` at `STAFFED`. Note that `shift` is a copy, not a reference: it is what all later shift-conflict arithmetic reads. The guard moves `UNASSIGNED → ASSIGNED` if they were unassigned, the post's `coverage_status` moves from `UNSTAFFED` to `COVERED`, the owning school's coverage counters and `coverage_posture` are recomputed — releasing any `ADVISORY_HOLD` routes if the school thereby leaves `SUSPENDED` — and the post's `version` increments by 1.

Overlap is checked against the *guard*, but supersession is resolved against the *post*, and this is where the assignment lifecycle forks. When a new assignment lands on a post that already carries another assignment in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` whose shift conflicts and whose closed date range overlaps the new one, the incumbent's fate is decided purely by start-date ordering. If the new assignment's `start_date` is on or before the incumbent's `start_date`, the incumbent is terminated: its `status` moves to `SUPERSEDED` — from either `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` — and its guard moves `ASSIGNED → UNASSIGNED` if no other non-terminal assignment remains. If instead the new assignment's `start_date` is strictly after the incumbent's, the incumbent is **truncated rather than terminated**: its `end_date` is rewritten to the day immediately before the new assignment's `start_date`, its `status` is left exactly as it was, and its `version` increments by 1.

Beyond supersession and truncation, an assignment leaves the live roster in two other ways, both described elsewhere: the checkpoint sweep of §2.4 carries it `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE` when the clock reaches its window and `ACTIVE → ENDED` once the clock passes its `end_date`, and a suspension cascade — from a post losing its warrant, or from a guard breaching the reliability floor — carries it from `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` to `VACATED` with `vacated_reason` set to `POST_SUSPENDED` or `GUARD_SUSPENDED` respectively. `ENDED`, `VACATED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal, and every one of them releases the guard to `UNASSIGNED` when it was their last non-terminal assignment. Assignments can be read individually, or listed and filtered by `guard_post_id`, by `guard_id` and by `status`.

### 3.6. Absences, Substitute Dispatch and the Cost of Coverage

An **AbsenceReport** announces that the guard installed on an assignment will miss one specific duty day. Either the affected `CrossingGuard` or a `CrossingSupervisor` may file it, with a payload naming the `post_assignment_id` of the duty being missed, the `absence_date`, a declared `reason` drawn from `ILLNESS`, `FAMILY`, `WEATHER`, `TRAINING` and `UNEXCUSED`, and an optional free-text `note`. The report's `guard_id` and `shift` are copied from the parent assignment by the server and are never supplied by the client.

Filing is gated in this order. The parent assignment's `status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`; against a terminal assignment the refusal is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The `absence_date` must fall inside the assignment's closed window from `start_date` through `end_date` inclusive, or the refusal is `ERR_ABSENCE_OUTSIDE_ASSIGNMENT` (HTTP 422); and it must not appear in the owning school's `no_school_dates`, since there is nothing to cover on a day with no school, or the refusal is `ERR_ABSENCE_ON_NON_SCHOOL_DAY` (HTTP 422). At most one **non-terminal** report — one whose `status` is neither `COVERED` nor `RESCINDED` — may exist for any given pairing of `post_assignment_id` and `absence_date`; a second live report for the same duty day is refused with `ERR_ABSENCE_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). Finally, when the acting role is `CrossingGuard`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the assignment's `guard_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); a `CrossingSupervisor` may file on behalf of any guard.

Three derived fields are **pinned at creation and never recomputed afterward**, and they are what make the price of a substitute a function of how much warning the program received rather than of when the substitute happened to accept. `shift_start_utc` is the `absence_date` combined with the school's `am_window_start_utc` when the report's `shift` is `AM` or `BOTH`, or with the school's `pm_window_start_utc` when the `shift` is `PM`; and when the `shift` is `PM` and the `absence_date` appears in the school's `early_dismissal_dates`, the school's `early_dismissal_pm_offset_minutes` is added to that instant, moving the shift earlier by the configured amount. `notice_hours` is the interval from the report's `created_at` to `shift_start_utc`, expressed in hours, and it may be negative when a report is filed after the shift has already begun. `urgency_tier` follows from it: `SAME_DAY` when `notice_hours` is strictly below 12, `SHORT` when it is 12 or more but strictly below 48, and `STANDARD` when it is 48 or more. Exactly 12.0 hours of notice is therefore `SHORT`, not `SAME_DAY`; exactly 48.0 hours is `STANDARD`, not `SHORT`; and negative notice is `SAME_DAY`. Advancing the clock between the report and the eventual acceptance does not re-price anything.

A new report is created in `DISPATCH_PENDING` with `dispatch_attempt_count` at 0, empty `dispatch_offers` and `excluded_guard_ids` arrays, and `current_offer_guard_id`, `current_offer_expires_at`, `substitute_guard_id`, `substitute_cost_cents` and `rescind_reason` all null. Creation opens a coverage gap and runs the propagation of §2.7 forward through four links: the parent assignment's `open_absence_count` increments by 1 and its `coverage_state` becomes `GAP`; the post's `open_gap_count` increments by 1 and its `coverage_status` becomes `UNCOVERED`; the school's `covered_post_count`, `uncovered_post_count`, `coverage_rate_pct` and `coverage_posture` are recomputed; and if the school thereby enters `SUSPENDED`, every `PUBLISHED` walking route of that school moves to `ADVISORY_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `SCHOOL_COVERAGE_SUSPENDED`. Creation also recomputes both reliability variants of the absent guard and, when the rolling figure falls strictly below 75.00 for a `CERTIFIED` guard, triggers the reliability-breach suspension and its assignment-vacating cascade described in §3.4.

Dispatch is initiated by a `CrossingSupervisor` and is permitted when the report is in `DISPATCH_PENDING`, when it is in `UNCOVERED_GAP` — a retry after an earlier empty pool — or when it is in `OFFER_OUT` with the logical clock strictly past `current_offer_expires_at`. A report in `OFFER_OUT` whose offer is still live, or one already terminal, is refused with `ERR_ABSENCE_NOT_DISPATCHABLE` (HTTP 409).

Dispatch assembles a candidate pool from all guards, and a guard qualifies only when **every one** of the following holds: `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`; `certification_expires_on` is on or after the `absence_date`, so a certification expiring exactly on the absence date is still valid; `duty_status` is `UNASSIGNED` or `ASSIGNED`, which excludes anyone `ON_LEAVE`; `equipment_complete` is true; `substitute_pool_optin` is true; the guard is not the absent guard themselves; the guard's identifier does not appear in the report's `excluded_guard_ids`; and the guard holds no assignment in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` whose closed date range contains the `absence_date` and whose post's `required_shift` conflicts with the absent post's `required_shift`, using the same asymmetric conflict rule stated in §3.5.

The eligible pool is then ranked by three keys applied in order: `reliability_score_season_to_date` descending, compared at its stored two-decimal precision without re-rounding; then a zone-match preference descending, where a guard whose `home_zone_code` equals the post's `zone_code` sorts ahead of one whose does not; then the guard's `created_at` ascending as the terminal tie-break. The guard's identifier is never consulted for ordering. The single top-ranked guard receives the offer: an object is appended to `dispatch_offers` carrying `attempt_number` equal to the current `dispatch_attempt_count` plus one, `offered_guard_id` naming the selected guard, `offered_at` from the logical clock, `offer_expires_at` set to the logical clock plus **45 minutes**, and `offer_status` set to `OPEN`. `dispatch_attempt_count` increments by 1, `current_offer_guard_id` and `current_offer_expires_at` are set, and the report moves to `OFFER_OUT` — from `DISPATCH_PENDING`, from `UNCOVERED_GAP`, or as a self-loop from `OFFER_OUT`. When the dispatch is a re-offer over a lapsed offer, the lapsed entry's `offer_status` is first rewritten from `OPEN` to `EXPIRED` and its recipient is appended to `excluded_guard_ids` before the next candidate is chosen; no state field changes on that self-loop, but the offer ledger and the exclusion set both advance, which is what makes the round observable.

An **empty eligible pool is not an error**. When nobody qualifies, the report moves to `UNCOVERED_GAP` — from `DISPATCH_PENDING` or as a no-op retry from `UNCOVERED_GAP` — no offer object is appended, `dispatch_attempt_count` is left untouched, and the response is 200 OK carrying the report. The post's `open_gap_count` is likewise unchanged, because an `UNCOVERED_GAP` report still counts as an open gap, so the post's `coverage_status` stays `UNCOVERED`. Every dispatch increments the report's `version` by 1.

Only the current recipient may answer an offer, and only a `CrossingGuard` may do so, supplying a single `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`. The report must be in `OFFER_OUT`, or the refusal is `ERR_NO_OPEN_OFFER` (HTTP 409). The acting `X-User-ID` must equal `current_offer_guard_id`, or the refusal is `ERR_NOT_OFFER_RECIPIENT` (HTTP 403). Only then is expiry checked: the logical clock must be at or before `current_offer_expires_at`, or the refusal is `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Checking recipient identity before expiry is deliberate — a stranger never learns whether an offer is still live.

`DECLINE` is the decline loop. The open offer's `offer_status` is rewritten to `DECLINED`, the declining guard is appended to `excluded_guard_ids`, `current_offer_guard_id` and `current_offer_expires_at` are cleared to null, and the report returns `OFFER_OUT → DISPATCH_PENDING`. Crucially, `dispatch_attempt_count` is **not** decremented, so the repeat-dispatch penalty in the cost formula grows with every round of declines and lapses.

`ACCEPT` closes the gap. The open offer's `offer_status` is rewritten to `ACCEPTED`, `substitute_guard_id` is set to the accepting guard, the report moves `OFFER_OUT → COVERED`, `substitute_cost_cents` is computed and pinned, and `current_offer_guard_id` and `current_offer_expires_at` are cleared to null. The four-link coverage propagation of §2.7 then runs in reverse: the parent assignment's `open_absence_count` drops by 1 and its `coverage_state` returns to `STAFFED` once it reaches 0; the post's `open_gap_count` drops by 1 and its `coverage_status` returns to `COVERED` once it reaches 0; the school's `covered_post_count`, `uncovered_post_count`, `coverage_rate_pct` and `coverage_posture` are recomputed; and only when the posture actually leaves `SUSPENDED` does every walking route of that school in `ADVISORY_HOLD` return to `PUBLISHED` with `hold_reason` cleared to null.

The price of that coverage is assembled in a **pinned sequence of steps, and the order matters**. First, shift duration: a `shift` of `AM` or `PM` is 1.50 hours, while `BOTH` is 3.00 hours. Second, the base: the guard hourly rate of 1850 cents — $18.50 an hour — multiplied by those hours and rounded half up to a whole cent, giving 2775 cents for a single shift and 5550 cents for a double. Third, the notice horizon is read straight off the report's pinned `urgency_tier`, never recomputed. Fourth and fifth, the surge: 35 percent of the base for `SAME_DAY`, 15 percent for `SHORT`, and nothing at all for `STANDARD`, with the surge amount rounded half up to a whole cent. Sixth, the subtotal is the base plus the surge — **and this subtotal, not the eventual total, is the key the administrative fee tier reads**. Seventh, the administrative fee is 8 percent of that subtotal when the subtotal is 4000 cents or more and 5 percent otherwise, rounded half up to a whole cent; a final total that happens to cross 4000 cents does not retroactively promote the tier. Eighth, the repeat-dispatch penalty is 250 cents for every dispatch attempt after the first — the count of attempts less one, floored at zero — so declines and lapsed offers are both charged. Ninth, `substitute_cost_cents` is the subtotal plus the administrative fee plus the repeat-dispatch penalty. Only the total is persisted; the intermediate components are not stored. Reports that never reach `COVERED` — those left in `UNCOVERED_GAP` or withdrawn as `RESCINDED` — leave `substitute_cost_cents` null.

Withdrawal is the compensating action, available to a `CrossingGuard` or a `CrossingSupervisor` with a mandatory free-text `rescind_reason`. It is accepted from `DISPATCH_PENDING`, `OFFER_OUT` or `UNCOVERED_GAP`; a report already `COVERED` or `RESCINDED` is refused with `ERR_ABSENCE_NOT_RESCINDABLE` (HTTP 409). The report moves to `RESCINDED`, the `rescind_reason` is stored, and any offer still `OPEN` has its `offer_status` rewritten to `EXPIRED` with `current_offer_guard_id` and `current_offer_expires_at` cleared. The same four compensation links then run: the assignment's `open_absence_count` drops by 1 with `coverage_state` returning to `STAFFED` at 0; the post's `open_gap_count` drops by 1 with `coverage_status` returning to `COVERED` at 0; the school's counters, `coverage_rate_pct` and `coverage_posture` are restored to their pre-report values; and every `ADVISORY_HOLD` route of that school returns to `PUBLISHED` once the posture leaves `SUSPENDED`. Withdrawal also recomputes both reliability variants of the absent guard, which no longer count this report at all. `COVERED` and `RESCINDED` are the two terminal states of an absence report. Reports can be read individually — the response includes the complete dispatch offer ledger — or listed and filtered by `post_assignment_id`, by `guard_id` and by `status`.

### 3.7. Recommended Walking Routes and Hazard Mapping

A **WalkingRoute** is the program's published advice about how to walk to a particular school along a particular corridor. A `SchoolLiaison` or a `ProgramAdmin` drafts one with a payload naming the parent `school_id`, a stable `route_code` identifying the corridor, a human-readable `route_name`, and an ordered `segments` array. Each segment object carries a `segment_order`, a `hazard_type`, a `length_ft`, a `mitigated` boolean recording whether the hazard has already been treated, and an `intersection_id` that may be null. The `segment_order` values must form exactly the run 1 through N with no gaps and no duplicates, each `length_ft` must be at least 1, and the array must hold at least one segment; violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `hazard_type` is one of `NO_SIDEWALK`, `UNCONTROLLED_CROSSING`, `HIGH_SPEED_ARTERIAL`, `RAIL_CROSSING`, `DRIVEWAY_CONFLICT` or `POOR_LIGHTING`. Every non-null `intersection_id` must reference an intersection that exists — checked at draft time in ascending `segment_order`, reporting `ERR_INTERSECTION_NOT_FOUND` for the first one that does not — but at draft time it need **not** belong to this route's own school. Drafting increments the parent school's `version` by 1.

Drafting computes `segment_count`, simply the number of entries in `segments`, and `hazard_score`. Because segments are immutable once submitted, `hazard_score` is computed exactly once, at creation, and is never recomputed thereafter. Each hazard type carries a weight per hundred feet: `NO_SIDEWALK` 1.25, `UNCONTROLLED_CROSSING` 3.50, `HIGH_SPEED_ARTERIAL` 2.75, `RAIL_CROSSING` 4.00, `DRIVEWAY_CONFLICT` 0.85 and `POOR_LIGHTING` 0.60. **The evaluation order is pinned and is not interchangeable with the arithmetically simpler alternative.** Segments are traversed in ascending `segment_order`. A segment whose `mitigated` is true contributes exactly 0.00 and is still traversed, so it does not shift the ordering of the others. Any other segment contributes its hazard weight multiplied by its `length_ft`, divided by 100, **rounded half up to two decimal places on that segment alone**. Those already-rounded per-segment values are then summed, and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places to give `hazard_score`. Rounding twice is deliberate: three unmitigated `NO_SIDEWALK` segments of 133 feet each contribute 1.6625 raw, which rounds to 1.66 per segment and sums to 4.98, whereas summing raw values and rounding once would give 4.99. The per-segment-first order is normative and 4.98 is the correct answer. Individual per-segment values are not persisted; only the total is.

A route begins in `DRAFT` with `published_at` and `hold_reason` null. Publication, again by a `SchoolLiaison` or a `ProgramAdmin`, requires the route to be in `DRAFT`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). Then three gates run **in this order**. First, the warrant gate: every segment whose `hazard_type` is `UNCONTROLLED_CROSSING` and whose `mitigated` is false must carry a non-null `intersection_id` pointing at an intersection whose `warrant_status` is `WARRANTED` or `CONDITIONAL` — the program will not advise children across an untreated, unwarranted crossing. Segments are examined in ascending `segment_order` and the first offender decides, with the refusal `ERR_ROUTE_CROSSING_NOT_WARRANTED` (HTTP 422). Second, the hazard ceiling: `hazard_score` must be at most 25.00, and equality publishes; above it the refusal is `ERR_ROUTE_HAZARD_SCORE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Third and last, the ownership consistency check that was deliberately skipped at draft time: every non-null `intersection_id` in `segments` must reference an intersection whose `school_id` equals the route's `school_id`, or the refusal is `ERR_ROUTE_SEGMENT_SCHOOL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Because this third gate sits after the first, a route with both faults reports the warrant failure, not the ownership failure.

On successful publication the route moves `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Publication also supersedes: any **other** route of the same school carrying the same `route_code` whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `ADVISORY_HOLD` moves to `RETIRED`, with `hold_reason` cleared to null when the retired route was on hold. `RETIRED` is terminal — one code, one live route per school. There is one immediate qualification: when the owning school's `coverage_posture` is already `SUSPENDED` at the moment of publication, the freshly published route is placed on `ADVISORY_HOLD` right away. Publication increments the route's `version` by 1.

Between publication and retirement a route may cycle through `ADVISORY_HOLD` any number of times, and it never does so by client request. The transition `PUBLISHED → ADVISORY_HOLD` fires as a system cascade when the owning school's `coverage_posture` enters `SUSPENDED`, setting `hold_reason` to `SCHOOL_COVERAGE_SUSPENDED`; the reverse transition `ADVISORY_HOLD → PUBLISHED` fires when the posture leaves `SUSPENDED` for `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, clearing `hold_reason` back to null. A route on hold is still advice of record — it is simply flagged as unreliable while the school cannot staff its crossings. Routes can be read individually, including their per-segment hazard mapping and the aggregate score, or listed per school with an optional `status` filter; a `PublicReporter` may read both.

### 3.8. Complaint Intake, Engineering Referral and Grant-Funded Projects

The public's channel into the program is a **Complaint** filed against a specific intersection, and it is the one write a `PublicReporter` is permitted; a `SchoolLiaison` or a `ProgramAdmin` may also file one. The intersection comes from the path and is immutable on the resulting record. The payload names the `complaint_type` — one of `SPEEDING`, `NEAR_MISS`, `FAILURE_TO_YIELD`, `SIGHT_OBSTRUCTION` or `SIGNAL_TIMING` — the `reporter_name`, the `occurred_at` UTC instant at which the event was observed, a free-text `narrative`, and the reporter's own `severity_claim` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`. An optional `reporter_contact` string may be supplied. The `severity_claim` is recorded faithfully and is deliberately **never used in any calculation** — engineering judgement, not the reporter's impression, drives prioritization. A new complaint is created in `RECEIVED` with `disposition`, `triaged_by_user_id`, `triage_note` and `referred_project_id` all null, and it increments the intersection's `open_complaint_count` by 1 and the intersection's `version` by 1. An intersection's `open_complaint_count` is by definition the number of its complaints whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `TRIAGED` or `REFERRED`.

Triage belongs to a `TrafficEngineer`, who supplies a `disposition` and an optional `triage_note`. The complaint must be in `RECEIVED`, or the refusal is `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_TRIAGEABLE` (HTTP 409). A `disposition` of `REFER_TO_ENGINEERING` moves the complaint `RECEIVED → TRIAGED` and makes it eligible to be cited by an infrastructure project; the intersection's `open_complaint_count` is deliberately **unchanged**, because a referred complaint is still an open concern. A `disposition` of `CLOSE_NO_ACTION` moves the complaint `RECEIVED → CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, which is terminal, and decrements the intersection's `open_complaint_count` by 1 while incrementing the intersection's `version` by 1. Either way, `disposition` is stored, `triaged_by_user_id` records the acting engineer's `X-User-ID`, `triage_note` is stored when supplied, and the complaint's `version` increments by 1.

A **GrantProgram** is a funding cycle opened by a `ProgramAdmin` with a `program_name`, a `fiscal_year`, the pool size `total_cents` — an integer of at least 1, immutable thereafter — and an `application_deadline` date that is recorded for reporting only and gates nothing. A new program begins with `committed_cents` at 0, `remaining_cents` equal to `total_cents`, and `funded_project_count`, `deferred_project_count` and `next_cycle_carryover_cents` all at 0. `deferred_project_count` is the number of the program's projects whose `status` is `DEFERRED`, and `next_cycle_carryover_cents` is the sum of `requested_cost_cents` across exactly that set — the money the program will need to carry into the next cycle to fund what it could not fund this time.

An **InfrastructureProject** is proposed by a `TrafficEngineer` and joins one program's competition. Its payload names the `grant_program_id` of the pool it competes in, the `intersection_id` it would treat, the `project_type` countermeasure — one of `RRFB`, `CURB_EXTENSION`, `SIDEWALK_INFILL`, `SIGNAL_RETIMING` or `RAISED_CROSSWALK` — a free-text `scope_summary`, the `requested_cost_cents` sought (at least 1), and `students_benefited` (0 or more). All three identifiers are immutable once set. An optional `source_complaint_id` cites the referred complaint that motivated the project; when supplied, that complaint must be in `TRIAGED` with `disposition` of `REFER_TO_ENGINEERING` **and** must sit on the same intersection as the project, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_REFERABLE` (HTTP 422). On success the cited complaint moves `TRIAGED → REFERRED`, its `referred_project_id` back-pointer is set to the new project's identifier, and its `version` increments by 1; that back-pointer is a convenience rather than an independent reference, and the reverse direction is discovered by filtering the project collection on `source_complaint_id`. The project itself is created in `PROPOSED` with `benefit_points`, `cost_factor`, `benefit_cost_score` and `referred_complaint_count_at_scoring` null, `awarded_cents` at 0, and `funded_at` and `withdrawal_reason` null; the program's `version` increments by 1.

Scoring is performed by a `TrafficEngineer` and requires the project to be in `PROPOSED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_SCORED` (HTTP 409). It also requires the project's intersection to carry a non-null `latest_warrant_score`: a project cannot be ranked before a warrant determination has been finalized at its location, and the refusal is `ERR_INTERSECTION_NOT_STUDIED` (HTTP 422). Scoring reads three inputs beyond the project's own figures. The intersection's `latest_warrant_score` is the first. The second is a crash-severity total over the intersection's `crash_history`, restricted to entries whose `involved_pedestrian` is true and whose `crash_date` falls in the closed 1095-day window ending at the date part of the logical clock at scoring time, weighted 3 points per `PROPERTY_DAMAGE_ONLY`, 8 per `INJURY`, 15 per `SEVERE_INJURY` and 25 per `FATAL`. The third is the count of complaints on the same intersection whose `status` is `TRIAGED`, `REFERRED` or `RESOLVED`; complaints still in `RECEIVED` and those in `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` are excluded. That count is frozen onto the project as `referred_complaint_count_at_scoring`, so a later triage cannot silently re-rank an already-scored project until it is scored again.

`benefit_points` is then the weighted sum of four contributions: 0.40 times the intersection's `latest_warrant_score`; plus 0.25 times the crash-severity total capped at 60.0; plus 0.20 times `students_benefited` divided by 10, that quotient capped at 50.0; plus 0.15 times five points per referred complaint, that product capped at 30.0 — the whole sum rounded half up to four decimal places so that weight rounding cannot drift before the division. `cost_factor` is `requested_cost_cents` divided by 5,000,000, floored at 1.00, and is stored to four decimal places; the floor means a project costing under $50,000 is never rewarded for being cheap, while above that threshold the score falls linearly with cost. `benefit_cost_score` is `benefit_points` divided by `cost_factor`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Scoring moves the project `PROPOSED → SCORED`, which enters it into the program's ranking pool, and increments the project's `version` by 1.

Awarding money is a `ProgramAdmin` act, and it is the point at which competition is resolved. The request carries `expected_grant_program_version` and an optional `award_note`. The project must be in `SCORED` — a `PROPOSED`, `DEFERRED` or already-`FUNDED` project is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_SCORED` (HTTP 409) — and the stale-program-version guard runs immediately after that state check. Only then is the ranking evaluated. The candidate pool is every project of the same program whose `status` is `SCORED`; projects in `PROPOSED`, `FUNDED` or `DEFERRED` are excluded, and the pool is never empty for a `SCORED` target because the target is itself a member. The pool is ranked by `benefit_cost_score` descending, then by `requested_cost_cents` ascending so that the cheaper project wins an exact score tie, then by `created_at` ascending as the terminal tie-break; the project identifier is never used for ordering. A target that is not the single top-ranked candidate is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_TOP_RANKED` (HTTP 409). Last of all, affordability: `requested_cost_cents` must be at most the program's `remaining_cents`, and exact equality is allowed and drives the balance to precisely 0; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 402). Because affordability is checked last, a `DEFERRED` project that also could not be afforded reports the state error, never the funds error.

The award moves the project `SCORED → FUNDED`, sets `awarded_cents` equal to `requested_cost_cents`, and stamps `funded_at` from the logical clock. Three consequences then chain on the program: `committed_cents` increases by the awarded amount while `remaining_cents` decreases by the same amount, `funded_project_count` increments by 1, and the program's `version` increments by 1. When and only when `remaining_cents` reaches exactly 0, every **other** project of the program still in `SCORED` moves `SCORED → DEFERRED`, leaving the ranking pool, and the program's `deferred_project_count` and `next_cycle_carryover_cents` are recomputed from that newly deferred set. Independently of the balance chain, when the project carries a non-null `source_complaint_id`, the cited complaint moves `REFERRED → RESOLVED`, which drops the intersection's `open_complaint_count` by 1. The project's own `version` increments by 1.

Withdrawal is the exact inverse, also a `ProgramAdmin` act, carrying a mandatory `withdrawal_reason` and the same `expected_grant_program_version` declaration. The project must be in `FUNDED`, or the refusal is `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FUNDED` (HTTP 409). Withdrawal moves the project `FUNDED → SCORED`, resets `awarded_cents` to 0 and stores the `withdrawal_reason`, while `funded_at` is retained as history so the fact that money once moved remains visible. On the program, `committed_cents` decreases and `remaining_cents` increases by the released amount, `funded_project_count` decrements by 1, and `version` increments by 1. When the released money leaves `remaining_cents` greater than 0, every project of the program in `DEFERRED` returns `DEFERRED → SCORED`, re-entering the ranking pool with the `benefit_cost_score` it was previously given rather than being re-scored, and `deferred_project_count` and `next_cycle_carryover_cents` are recomputed. And when the withdrawn project carries a `source_complaint_id`, that complaint moves `RESOLVED → REFERRED` and the intersection's `open_complaint_count` increments by 1, restoring the pre-funding value exactly. A single project can therefore be funded, deferred, revived and funded again as the pool balance moves, and a single complaint can be resolved and reopened as many times as funding is granted and withdrawn. Projects can be read individually or listed with filters on `grant_program_id`, `intersection_id`, `source_complaint_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Program Evaluation: Travel Tallies and the Mode-Share Trend

The program justifies itself to its funder with mode share — the fraction of students who walk or bike — measured once per term by a classroom hand tally. A `SchoolLiaison` or a `ProgramAdmin` appends a survey to a school with a payload naming the `term_code` the tally belongs to, the `survey_date` it was taken, `classrooms_reporting` for participation context, `enrollment_at_survey` recording the school's enrolled-student figure as of that tally, and the five travel-mode buckets `walk_count`, `bike_count`, `bus_count`, `car_count` and `other_count`. The survey object is appended to the school's `mode_share_surveys` array with `recorded_at` stamped from the logical clock, and the school's `version` increments by 1. A survey whose five buckets sum to zero, or whose `enrollment_at_survey` is below 1, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — which is why the denominators below are always positive. The school's own `enrollment_count` is the roster figure liaisons copy into `enrollment_at_survey`, and it is likewise constrained to be at least 1.

Every append recomputes the school's mode-share picture from **exactly one survey — the latest — never from an average**. The latest survey is selected by `survey_date` descending, then by `recorded_at` descending as the tie-break, so when two surveys share a `survey_date` the later-recorded one wins. Earlier surveys are retained in the array as history and never averaged in.

Two variants are computed from that one survey, and **both are always stored**, because the gap between them is itself the reportable quantity. `walk_bike_mode_share_pct` is the raw-tally variant: 100 multiplied by the sum of `walk_count` and `bike_count`, divided by the sum of all five buckets, rounded half up to two decimal places. Non-responding students are invisible to it — they neither help nor hurt — so a low-participation survey of enthusiastic walkers reports a high share. `walk_bike_mode_share_weighted_pct` is the enrollment-weighted variant: 100 multiplied by the same walk-plus-bike numerator, but divided by `enrollment_at_survey`, again rounded half up to two decimal places. Because enrollment normally exceeds the number of responses, identical tallies yield a strictly lower share under the weighted variant, and the gap between the two stored figures is exactly the non-response inflation. The weighted figure is what program evaluation reports to the grant funder, precisely because it cannot be inflated by low participation.

Two further fields close the picture. `latest_survey_term_code` records the `term_code` of whichever survey the selection rule chose. `mode_share_trend_pct` is the raw-tally share less the school's `baseline_walk_bike_mode_share_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places — and it is built from the raw-tally variant only, deliberately **not** from the weighted one, because the raw tally is comparable across terms with differing participation. All four fields are null until the first survey is recorded.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/srts` prefix. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other successful call answers HTTP 200 with the affected resource, or with the list envelope described in §2.3 for collection reads. Operations that append to a ledger or drive a state machine answer 200 with the full updated parent resource rather than the appended fragment, so a client never has to re-read to see recomputed derived fields. The Authorized Roles column is exhaustive: any other declared role receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and a request with no usable identity headers receives `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before authorization is considered.
