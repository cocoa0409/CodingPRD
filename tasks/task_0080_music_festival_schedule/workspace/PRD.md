# Festival Program Schedule API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An online music festival is a multi-day choreography of artists, stages, equipment, sound-check teams, ticket buyers, and sponsors, all bound to a rigid wall-clock window. The Festival Program Schedule API is the backbone service that lets festival producers and their stage, artist, ticketing, and sponsorship counterparts publish and continually revise the program while four external systems — the ticketing platform, the audience-facing instant-messaging push channel, the broadcasting partner, and the sponsor-management system — observe each change through an outbound event stream and acknowledge it back. The service is also the system of record for audit log entries that regulators routinely inspect after a festival closes.

The core tension this service mediates is that any single program change ripples in four directions at once: a cancelled set has to refund its single-day ticket holders pro-rata, it has to free the sound-engineer team that was warming the stage for it, it has to void any sponsor exposure unit that was tied to its stage time, and it has to fan out an announcement to the four downstream subscribers. The service therefore models the change itself as a first-class object — a ScheduleChange in PROPOSED state that the producer either applies (firing the cascade) or rejects, and that may be reverted while the external acknowledgements are still in flight. Around that pivot sit a draft-and-publish workflow on a versioned program list, an interval-arithmetic engine that detects conflicts on stages and equipment kits and engineer teams in three different geometric flavours, a waterfall allocator that hands a finite catalog of sponsor exposure units down a fixed tier order, and a clock-advance test endpoint that the harness uses to drive time forward and trigger the automatic state transitions that depend on it.

This document is the complete specification of the API contract. Every payload key, every error code, every state-transition guard, and every numeric formula stated in this document is a requirement that the implementation must honour exactly. Numeric examples in §3 are illustrative of the formula but are not themselves the data the implementation hard-codes.

## 2. Global Specifications and System-Wide Principles

The domain root path is `/festivals`. Every resource other than the global Artist catalog and the four operator-only test endpoints lives beneath a specific festival in the URL: a stage is at `/festivals/{festivalId}/stages/{stageId}`, a ticket type at `/festivals/{festivalId}/ticket-types/{ticketTypeId}`, and so on. Artists are festival-independent — an artist registered through `/artists` may appear on the program of any festival. The four operator-only endpoints live under `/_clock`, `/_events`, and `/_sweep` and are used by the harness to drive time forward, inject external-system acknowledgements, and run the post-event tally sweep.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers, exactly named `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. The header `X-User-ID` is the opaque identifier of the actor making the call. The header `X-User-Role` names the actor's role and must be one of the role names declared below; an unrecognised role name fails fast with the error `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` at HTTP 403, distinct from the permission-denied case `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS` at HTTP 403 which fires when the role is recognised but is not on the endpoint's allowlist. If either header is missing or an empty string, the request is rejected at HTTP 400 with `ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER`. The header check is the first validation that runs on every endpoint — including the developer endpoints under `/_clock`, `/_events`, and `/_sweep`.

The eight role names are `FestivalProducer`, `ArtistLiaison`, `StageManager`, `TicketingAdmin`, `SponsorshipAdmin`, `AuditViewer`, `PlatformAdmin`, and a synthetic catch-all label `Any` that the endpoint table uses for read-only list and detail endpoints open to every authenticated role. The label `Any` is not itself a value the client puts in `X-User-Role`; every real request carries one of the seven concrete role names. All eight roles are opaque operator identities in the sense that `X-User-ID` is not the primary key of any persisted entity in the database — it is recorded as the `actor_user_id` field of the audit-log entries written by the operations the actor performs, and that is the extent of its semantic linkage. For system-driven entries (the clock-advance cascade, the festival-close cascade, the post-event sweep, and the inverse fan-out of a revert), the literal string `"system"` is used for both `actor_user_id` and the recorded `actor_role` on the audit entry.

The role-to-endpoint matrix is the source of truth for which role may call which path. The headline shape: `FestivalProducer` owns the festival lifecycle and the program-version draft-and-publish workflow and the `ScheduleChange` apply/reject/revert sequence and the exposure-catalog seeding. `ArtistLiaison` handles the global artist catalog through `/artists` (registration and patching of `external_commitments`, `minimum_travel_buffer_hours`, and `tier`), the program-item lifecycle from creation through the announce step, the return-for-revision edge of the program-version cycle, and the rider list and rider fulfilment-flag flips. `StageManager` owns the stage catalog (creating stages with their `stage_name`, `zone_capacity`, `changeover_buffer_seconds`, `equipment_kits` wiring, and `is_emergency_substitute` flag), the equipment kit catalog (creating kits with their `kit_code`, `total_units`, and `description`), the sound-engineer-team roster (with `team_name`, `head_engineer_name`, and `warmup_minutes`), the sound-check slots and their team assignment, the post-window completion call, the cancel call on a slot, the rehearsal create and cancel, and the waive on a program-item's sound-check axis. `TicketingAdmin` owns the ticket-type catalog (with `ticket_class`, `tied_program_item_ids`, `zone_name`, `unit_price_cents`) and the issue, suspend, and resume operations. `SponsorshipAdmin` owns the sponsor-tier registry (with `tier_code`, `tier_name`, `priority_rank`, `sponsor_external_id`, `sponsor_display_name`, `contracted_units`, `contract_kinds_allowed`), the per-allocation deliver and void-delivery, and shares the waterfall sweep and the post-event report read with `FestivalProducer`. `AuditViewer` is read-only on the audit log, the fulfilment report, the clock view, and every other GET. `PlatformAdmin` is the operator role that owns the operational endpoints — the clock-advance operator capability, the external-system sync-acknowledgement ingress capability, the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability, the post-event sweep operator capability, and the festival-level hard cancel the festival-level hard-cancel capability, and also the recompute-priority knob on a program item.

Audit-log access is gated more strictly than the standard role-permission check. `GET /festivals/{festivalId}/audit-entries` is open only to `AuditViewer`, `FestivalProducer`, and `PlatformAdmin`. Every other role receives `ERR_AUDIT_LOG_FORBIDDEN` at HTTP 403, even when the request's headers are otherwise valid. This is a separate error from the generic `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS` so the client can distinguish "you have no read access at all" from "you're not on the audit-log allowlist".

Because the role identities are opaque, there is no "the acting `Artist`'s `id` must equal the resource's `artist_id`" ownership check anywhere in the API — the system trusts the role's permission set, the audit log records `X-User-ID` as a string, and an in-house authorisation layer outside the scope of this service is presumed to issue the headers truthfully. The only "ownership" relations the system enforces are entity-to-entity (a program item's `festival_id` must match the festival in the URL, a rider's `program_item_id` must match the program item in the URL, a sound-check's `program_item_id` must match its program item, an exposure-allocation's `sponsor_tier_id` must match its tier and its `festival_id` must match the path) — and any cross-parent mismatch surfaces as `ERR_RESOURCE_MISMATCHED_PARENT` at HTTP 404. The URL-path existence check is run left-to-right: a request to `/festivals/{festivalId}/program-items/{itemId}` first verifies the festival id resolves (returning `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_FOUND` if not), then verifies the program-item id resolves under it (returning `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` if not), then the parent-match guard fires (returning `ERR_RESOURCE_MISMATCHED_PARENT` if the resolved program item belongs to a different festival). The full not-found family is one error per resource type: `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_KIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOUND_ENGINEER_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REHEARSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPONSOR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPOSURE_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND`. All of these are HTTP 404.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters, `limit` and `offset`, both integers. The defaults are `limit` 10 and `offset` 0. The accepted range for `limit` is from 1 to 100 inclusive on both ends; the accepted range for `offset` is any non-negative integer. A value out of these ranges — `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer — is rejected at HTTP 400 with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. The list result is always ordered by the resource's `created_at` ascending, so a fresh page-2 read sees the items the page-1 read just paginated past. The `meta` block on a list response contains the four pagination fields named in §2.2: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the total number of rows that match the filter, independent of the limit window), and `has_more` (true when `offset + limit < total_count`).

The list endpoints that accept filters in addition to pagination are noted on the endpoint table in §4. Filters are query-string parameters whose names mirror the field names of the entity: `status`, `change_kind`, `ticket_class`, `zone_name`, `event_category`, `subject_entity_type`, `created_at_after`, `tier_code`, `kind`, `sponsor_tier_id`, `artist_id`, `stage_id`, `program_item_id`, `program_version_id`, `sound_engineer_team_id`, `rehearsal_type`, `is_blocking`, `category`, `tier`, and `target_program_item_id`. Filters are case-sensitive on enum-valued fields and are interpreted as exact-match equality.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Resources with mutation history carry four common properties on top of their domain-specific fields. The first is `id`, a server-minted UUID string with no entity-type prefix and no client-supplied component — the client supplies no `id` on the create body and the server returns the minted value in the response. The second is `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped at the moment the row was inserted and never changed thereafter. The third is `updated_at`, the same shape as `created_at`, stamped at row insertion to the same value as `created_at` and refreshed on every mutation that changes any other field. The fourth is `version`, a non-negative integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by 1 on every mutation, used for the optimistic-concurrency contract described in §2.5.

Two entity types are append-only event-log rows and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`, without `updated_at` and without `version`: `RiderRequirement` (the only mutable field on it is the `fulfilled_at` timestamp flag, whose flip from null to a server-stamped UTC string is itself audited but does not bump a `version` on the rider row), `FulfilmentLog` (a pure append-only entry under an ExposureAllocation; the row has its `id`, its parent `exposure_allocation_id`, its optional `program_item_id`, its `event_type` enum, its `units` integer, its free-text `note`, and `created_at` — and that is the entire schema), and `AuditEntry` (immutable on creation, no transition states, the lifecycle is the single act of being appended). These three are the entities the system never PATCHes — except for the single rider-fulfilment flag flip on a `RiderRequirement` row, which sets `fulfilled_at` from null to the server clock and is itself an idempotent operation: a second PATCH on an already-fulfilled rider returns the row unchanged with no further audit entry.

Resource creation (the POST body of a create endpoint) returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in the `data` envelope. Resource modification (a PATCH or a state-changing POST that already targets an existing resource id, such as `/program-items/{itemId}/confirm`) returns HTTP 200. The Festival creation endpoint stamps `status` to the initial state `PLANNED`, `clock_offset_seconds` to 0, and computes `festival_day_count` as the ceiling of `(end_time − start_time) / 86400` — the number of UTC calendar days the festival window spans, with any partial day rounding up to a full one. The Festival window is required to satisfy `end_time > start_time` and both times must be positive Unix-seconds integers; otherwise the create rejects at HTTP 400 with `ERR_INVALID_FESTIVAL_WINDOW`, which also fires when the computed `festival_day_count` is zero. The same constraint applies to a PATCH on the festival's time fields. The festival's bookable window is the half-open interval whose start is the inclusive `start_time` and whose end is the exclusive `end_time`. The festival also carries a computed `outstanding_tickets_total` derived field equal to the sum across all of its ticket types of `issued_count` minus `refunded_count`, and a `current_program_version_id` pointer that begins as null and is pinned to the published program-version's id the first time a version transitions to `PUBLISHED`.

A windowed child entity — meaning a `ProgramItem`, a `Rehearsal` of either `rehearsal_type` flavour, or the `scheduled_window_start`/`scheduled_window_end` interval of a `SoundCheckSlot` — must have its half-open `[start, end)` interval contained entirely inside the parent festival's `[start_time, end_time)` window. A request that violates this containment is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_CHILD_WINDOW_OUT_OF_FESTIVAL_RANGE`. Within the body, `end_time` strictly greater than `start_time` is required for every windowed entity, surfaced as `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400 if violated.

For every entity that carries a Unix-seconds integer time field — `start_time` and `end_time` on `Festival`, on `ProgramItem`, on `Rehearsal`, and the equivalents `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` on `SoundCheckSlot` — the field is interpreted in UTC. The `ProgramItem`'s `duration_seconds` is a read-only computed field equal to `end_time - start_time`. The system's notion of "now" for time-gated transitions is `real_now + Festival.clock_offset_seconds`, where `clock_offset_seconds` is a per-festival counter advanced exclusively by the clock-advance operator capability and persisted on the festival row.

The body sanity validation that runs on every POST and PATCH covers four conditions in one umbrella: required fields are present, types match the declared types in this specification, enum-typed fields have one of the declared enum values, and integer timestamps are positive Unix seconds with `end_time > start_time` on every windowed entity. A wholesale parse failure on the JSON body is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_BODY` at HTTP 400; a specific field's value-violation is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400 with the offending field's name carried in the message string.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency Control and Idempotency

The optimistic-concurrency contract is keyed on the `version` integer field of every mutable resource. When a mutating request — a PATCH, or a state-changing POST such as confirm, announce, cancel, publish, suspend, resume, apply, revert, reject, deliver, void-delivery, or the sound-check schedule, complete, and cancel — carries an `If-Match` header whose value parses as an integer, the server compares the value to the target row's current `version` and rejects with `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` at HTTP 409 on any mismatch. An audit entry of category `OCC_CONFLICT` is appended on rejection. A request without an `If-Match` header skips the version check entirely and proceeds against whatever the row's current state is — the client is responsible for accepting the lost-update risk that comes with omitting the header. The endpoints that honour `If-Match` are the program-version publish, the program-item confirm, the program-item announce, the program-item cancel, the program-item PATCH, the stage PATCH, the equipment-kit PATCH, the artist PATCH, the sound-check schedule PATCH, the sound-check complete and cancel, the ticket-type issue and suspend and resume, the schedule-change apply and reject and revert, and the exposure-allocation deliver and void-delivery. The `version` field is also the basis of the festival-level publish race: two simultaneous program-version publishes on two different DRAFT versions of the same festival resolve via a compare-and-swap on `Festival.current_program_version_id` keyed on `Festival.version`, the loser returning `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT`.

The idempotency contract is keyed on an optional `idempotency_key` body field. Four endpoints honour the field: the schedule-changes create, the schedule-changes apply, the program-item cancel, and the ticket-type issue. All other POST endpoints ignore an `idempotency_key` field if the client sends one. The window during which the key is considered active is `IDEMPOTENCY_WINDOW_SECONDS = 86400` (twenty-four hours), measured on the same `real_now + clock_offset_seconds` clock that the time-gated transitions read. Within the window, a second POST with the same key and the same SHA-256 hash of the request body is the idempotent-replay case: the server returns the stored response of the first call, the success envelope's `meta` carries an extra `is_idempotent_replay` boolean set to true, no side effects are re-applied, and no new audit entry is written. A second POST with the same key but a different body hash is the conflict case: it is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`. Past the 86 400-second window the key is expired and the same key value may be reused. The simultaneous-arrival case where two cancels of the same program item land at the same time with no idempotency keys (or with mismatched ones) serialises via a row lock on the program item: the winner flips the status to `CANCELLED` and the loser observes the terminal state and returns `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_ALREADY_CANCELLED` at HTTP 409.

There is one other concurrency error that the sponsor-allocation pathway introduces: the sponsor-allocation-sweep capability takes an advisory lock keyed on the festival id and the literal string `sponsor-allocation-sweep` for its duration, and an `ExposureAllocation` POST that arrives during the sweep window is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_CONCURRENT_SWEEP_IN_PROGRESS`. A related-but-distinct case is the `ERR_TIER_POOL_EXHAUSTED` error at HTTP 409, which fires when an allocation POST loses the festival-level pool-counter lock for its `(kind, tier_code)` pair against an in-progress sweep; the two error ids are reserved for the two outcomes of the same two-sponsors-race-for-the-last-unit scenario, the zero-units-success row being the happy-path outcome when the lock is released cleanly with the pool already drained, and the `ERR_TIER_POOL_RACE` id sitting in the catalog as a forward-compatibility placeholder for a sponsor-management-system retry contract that is otherwise unused. The race between a the external-system sync-acknowledgement ingress capability and a the schedule-change revert capability on the same schedule change serialises via a row lock on the change row, with the post-revert envelope status `CANCELLED` causing a subsequent sync-ack to see a non-QUEUED envelope and return `ERR_SYNC_ENVELOPE_NOT_QUEUED` at HTTP 409 — the mirror order has the ack landing first and the revert seeing a non-zero acknowledgement count and being rejected with `ERR_CHANGE_ALREADY_ACKED` at HTTP 409. The race between an apply and a reject of the same schedule change reduces to a status-field CAS on the schedule change, with the loser observing that the status is no longer `PROPOSED` and being rejected with `ERR_CHANGE_NOT_PROPOSED` at HTTP 409, also appending an audit entry of category `OCC_CONFLICT`. The race between a ticket-issue and a cascade-induced cancellation of the issue's tied program item serialises on the ticket-type row: when the cascade wins, the issue sees a now-trimmed `tied_program_item_ids` array on the type and is rejected with `ERR_TICKET_TYPE_TIE_INVALID` at HTTP 409; when the issue wins, the cascade observes one extra issued ticket and includes it in the provisional refund bucket described in §3.

The race between two sound-check schedule PATCHes that attempt to assign the same engineer team to two different slots in the same `REQUESTED → SCHEDULED` transition serialises on the team row: the implementation acquires a row lock on the `SoundEngineerTeam.id` and tests that the team's `status` equals `AVAILABLE` and that its `current_booking_id` is null; the winner flips status to `BUSY` and the loser sees the post-flip state and returns the team-double-book error `ERR_ENGINEER_TEAM_DOUBLE_BOOKED` at HTTP 409.

### 2.6. Validation Priority Ordering

When more than one of the validation rules below would reject the same request, the order in which they are evaluated determines which error_id the client sees. The evaluation order is fixed in the following category sequence — authentication first, then the role permission check, then existence checks for the URL path segments, then state and cross-entity-state preconditions, then business-safety rules (equipment-wiring, team double-booking, headliner blocking-rider minimum and fulfilment), then business-temporal rules (the stage changeover-buffer overlap, the artist multi-festival overlap, the artist travel-buffer gap, the artist self-overlap, the sound-check window position relative to the parent program item's start, the rehearsal-room same-name overlap, the festival-window containment guard on windowed children), then business-referential rules (the sponsor-tier `contract_kinds_allowed` membership for an exposure-kind, the cross-festival refund guard, the cross-festival exposure-program-item reference), then business-fiscal rules (the exposure catalog floor, the sponsor-tier contract-units cap, the ticket-zone capacity ceiling, the refund-magnitude check), and finally the business-constraint rules (idempotency-key reuse, version conflict, pagination-parameter sanity, body schema sanity). The audit-log-access gate `ERR_AUDIT_LOG_FORBIDDEN` is a category-after-permissions specialisation on the GET `/festivals/{festivalId}/audit-entries` endpoint.

Two specific priority conflicts that the test harness pins explicitly:

First, when a `POST /festivals/{festivalId}/program-items/{itemId}/confirm` request would fail both the cross-entity state check (because the item's `program_version_id` is not the festival's current published `current_program_version_id` — the program-version-superseded gate) and the business-temporal artist-multi-festival-overlap check, the cross-entity state check wins because its category sits earlier in the evaluation order. The state-category error is `ERR_PROGRAM_VERSION_SUPERSEDED` at HTTP 409; the business-temporal error is `ERR_ARTIST_MULTI_FESTIVAL_CONFLICT` at HTTP 409. The client sees the state error.

Second, when an `ExposureAllocation` POST under a sponsor tier would fail both the referential check that the `kind` field must lie in the parent `SponsorTier`'s `contract_kinds_allowed` array (with the artist-mention specialisation `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_TIER` at HTTP 422 firing when the kind is the literal string `artist_mention` and the parent's `contract_kinds_allowed` does not contain it, otherwise the generic `ERR_EXPOSURE_KIND_NOT_CONTRACTED` at HTTP 422) and the fiscal contract-units cap check (the sum of the requested units plus the tier's existing allocations of the same kind exceeds the `contracted_units` field of the parent tier, surfacing as `ERR_TIER_CONTRACT_UNITS_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 422), the referential category wins because it is numerically earlier in the priority order. The artist-mention specialisation takes the place of the generic kind-not-contracted error when the offending kind happens to be `artist_mention`.

The full priority chain in numerical order, where 1 is highest priority and is evaluated first, is: 1) header presence (`ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER`); 2) role name recognised (`ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`); 3) role on the endpoint allowlist (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS`, with the audit-log-specific `ERR_AUDIT_LOG_FORBIDDEN` for the audit-entries endpoint); 4) URL path id existence, left-to-right (the `ERR_*_NOT_FOUND` family and `ERR_RESOURCE_MISMATCHED_PARENT`); 5) target resource is in a state that permits the operation (the family `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_PROPOSED`, `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_CONFIRMED`, `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_MUTABLE`, `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_ALREADY_CANCELLED`, `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_TERMINAL_FOR_PERFORM`, `ERR_TICKET_TYPE_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_TICKET_TYPE_TIE_INVALID`, `ERR_REHEARSAL_NOT_BOOKED`, `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_NOT_TERMINAL`, `ERR_CHANGE_NOT_PROPOSED`, `ERR_CHANGE_NOT_APPLIED`, `ERR_CHANGE_ALREADY_ACKED`, `ERR_SYNC_ENVELOPE_NOT_QUEUED`, `ERR_FESTIVAL_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_TEAM_HAS_ACTIVE_SLOT`, `ERR_EMPTY_PROGRAM_VERSION`, `ERR_MISSING_REVISION_REASON`, `ERR_MISSING_REJECTION_REASON`, `ERR_MISSING_VOID_REASON`, `ERR_EXPOSURE_ALLOCATION_TERMINAL`); 6) cross-entity-state preconditions (`ERR_PROGRAM_VERSION_SUPERSEDED`, `ERR_PARENT_ITEM_NOT_READY`, `ERR_TEAM_NOT_AVAILABLE`, `ERR_TIED_ITEM_NOT_PERFORMED` aka `ERR_EXPOSURE_ALLOCATION_NOT_DELIVERABLE` on the deliver path, `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_SCHEDULING_OR_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_PUBLISHED`); 7) business-safety equipment rules (`ERR_STAGE_LACKS_EQUIPMENT_KIT`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_KIT_OVERSUBSCRIBED`); 8) business-safety team double-booking (`ERR_ENGINEER_TEAM_DOUBLE_BOOKED`); 9) business-safety headliner-rider checks (`ERR_HEADLINER_MISSING_BLOCKING_RIDER`, `ERR_BLOCKING_RIDERS_UNFULFILLED`); 10) the stage changeover overlap (`ERR_STAGE_CHANGEOVER_OVERLAP`); 11) the artist multi-festival conflict (`ERR_ARTIST_MULTI_FESTIVAL_CONFLICT`); 12) the artist travel-buffer gap (`ERR_ARTIST_TRAVEL_BUFFER_VIOLATION`); 13) the artist self-overlap (`ERR_ARTIST_SELF_OVERLAP`); 14) the sound-check window precedence (`ERR_SOUND_CHECK_WINDOW_INVALID`); 15) the rehearsal-room overlap (`ERR_REHEARSAL_ROOM_OVERLAP`); 16) the exposure-kind-contract referential check (`ERR_EXPOSURE_KIND_NOT_CONTRACTED`, `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_TIER`, and the cross-festival exposure-program-item guard `ERR_EXPOSURE_PROGRAM_ITEM_CROSS_FESTIVAL`); 17) the cross-festival refund guard (`ERR_CROSS_FESTIVAL_REFUND`); 18) the exposure-catalog disbursed-floor (`ERR_EXPOSURE_CATALOG_BELOW_DISBURSED`, which is also the error reused by the equipment-kit `total_units` PATCH for the analogous floor); 19) the tier-contract-units cap (`ERR_TIER_CONTRACT_UNITS_EXCEEDED`); 20) the ticket-zone capacity ceiling (`ERR_TICKET_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, with the zero-capacity edge `ERR_TICKET_ZONE_NO_STAGE` when the zone group has no tied stage to derive a capacity from); 21) the refund-magnitude check (`ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PROVISIONAL`); 22) the idempotency-key reuse check (`ERR_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED`); 23) the version-conflict check (`ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT`); 24) the pagination-parameter sanity check (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`); 25) the body-schema sanity check (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_BODY`, `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`); 26) the audit-log access gate (`ERR_AUDIT_LOG_FORBIDDEN`); 27) the sound-check stage-mismatch guard `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_STAGE_MISMATCH` at HTTP 409 (the slot's persisted `stage_id` differs from the parent program item's `stage_id` because a `STAGE_SWAP` schedule-change elapsed in between); 28) the empty-pool advisory rule that produces a zero-`actual_units` row on a fresh allocation when the pool is currently drained (no error, the success-with-zero-units outcome); 29) the windowed-child containment in the festival window (`ERR_CHILD_WINDOW_OUT_OF_FESTIVAL_RANGE`); 30) the festival's own window validity (`ERR_INVALID_FESTIVAL_WINDOW`). The two priority-conflict cases pinned above are at the 6-vs-11 boundary (the cross-entity state ahead of the artist-temporal conflict) and the 16-vs-19 boundary (the referential check ahead of the fiscal-tier-cap check).

The clock-advance endpoint has two of its own input-validation errors that sit outside the cross-cutting validation order, because the body is bespoke: `ERR_CLOCK_ADVANCE_BACKWARD` at HTTP 400 fires on a negative `delta_seconds` or a `to_unix_seconds` that would move the clock backward, and `ERR_CLOCK_INVALID_ARGS` at HTTP 400 fires when neither `delta_seconds` nor `to_unix_seconds` is supplied, or when both are. The sweep endpoint's festival-not-found error is `ERR_SWEEP_FESTIVAL_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404. The sync-ack endpoint's bad-target error is `ERR_SYNC_TARGET_UNKNOWN` at HTTP 400 when the body's `target` field is not one of the four downstream-system names `ticketing`, `im`, `broadcasting`, or `sponsor`.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding, and Money

Every monetary value in the system is stored as an integer number of cents — never as a fractional currency value. A ticket's price is `unit_price_cents`, a refund is reported in cents, the sponsor payout is computed in cents from an underlying integer-mills rate (thousandths of a cent), and the audit log carries cents-denominated payload summaries. There is no floating-point currency arithmetic anywhere in the contract.

Two divisions in the system can produce non-integer intermediates and therefore have their rounding behaviour explicitly pinned. The first is the per-ticket cents division in the forward-looking provisional refund formula of §3.6, where the per-ticket figure is `unit_price_cents` times the integer `cancelled_days = 1` divided by the festival's `festival_day_count`, with the division rounded half up to the nearest cent at that step (the standard half-up rule, not banker's rounding) and the rest of the across-tickets and across-types arithmetic remaining in exact integer cents. The second is the mills-to-cents conversion at the end of the sponsor payout formula of §3.7, where the cross-line sum of `delivered_units * pinned_rate_cents_per_unit` mills is divided by 1000 once at the very end and the single quotient is rounded half up to integer cents — the alternative ordering of rounding each line's mills-to-cents conversion separately and then summing the rounded cents is explicitly rejected because the two orderings can diverge by exactly one cent on the canonical test input of three lines each delivering one unit at the default rate of 25 mills per unit, where the pinned single-final-rounding answer is 8 cents (the unrounded `0.075` cents intermediate rounds half-up to `0.08` cents and then the line is the only line so the integer cents result is the rounded value across lines as a whole, that being 8 cents on the three-times-one-unit input vector) and the per-line-rounded sum would be 9 cents (`0.025` cents per line rounds half-up to `0.03` cents each, summing to `0.09` cents = 9 cents).

Percentages — the sponsor-tier `projected_fulfilment_pct` and the post-event `actual_fulfilment_pct` — are stored with two decimal places, rounded half up. The rounding step is applied exactly once, after the underlying unit count has been divided by the contracting denominator `contracted_units` and multiplied by 100. The fractional intermediates of the sum across the tier's exposure allocations are kept in exact Decimal arithmetic — the projected formula sums each allocation's projected contribution (an integer for program-item-tied allocations whose tied item is in a "will or did happen" state, zero for cancelled-tie allocations, and a window-fraction-weighted contribution for festival-wide allocations) before the single final division-by-`contracted_units`-times-100-and-round-half-up-to-two-decimals step. The two-decimals rounding constant is fixed as `PERCENT_PRECISION = 2`.

The general "round half up to N decimal places" rule is the IEEE 754 round-half-away-from-zero rule for non-negative values, applied uniformly. There is no asymmetric rounding for negative numbers because every quantity that gets rounded is a non-negative count, percentage, or cents value.

The window-fraction-remaining term that scales a festival-wide exposure allocation's projected contribution is a number between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive, equal to the fraction of the festival window that has not yet elapsed at projection time. Concretely it is the difference between `Festival.end_time` and the maximum of (`real_now + Festival.clock_offset_seconds`) and `Festival.start_time`, divided by the festival's window length (`end_time − start_time`, or 1 if that would be zero, to avoid a division-by-zero on a degenerate festival window), clamped from below at 0.0 and from above at 1.0. This fraction is multiplied into the allocation's `actual_units` to produce a Decimal intermediate, the intermediates of all the tier's allocations are summed, the sum is divided by the tier's `contracted_units`, the quotient is multiplied by 100, and the final number is rounded half up to two decimal places to land in the `projected_fulfilment_pct` field.

The `actual_fulfilment_pct` field, by contrast, is computed only by the post-event sweep operator capability while the parent festival is in status `CLOSED`. It is null before then. The sweep walks the `FulfilmentLog` event stream for every `ExposureAllocation` under the tier, computes each allocation's `delivered_units` as the sum of the `units` of the `DELIVERED` log rows minus the sum of the `units` of the `VOIDED` and `PROGRAM_VOIDED` log rows on the same allocation (the `REPORTED` row carries `units = 0` and contributes nothing to the count), sums those `delivered_units` figures across the tier, divides by the tier's `contracted_units`, multiplies by 100, and rounds half up to two decimal places. The two percentages — projected and actual — can legitimately diverge: a confirmed program item that gets day-of cancelled contributes its allocation's full `actual_units` to the projection but contributes zero `delivered_units` to the post-event tally, and the gap shows up as a non-zero difference between the two columns on the same `SponsorTier` row.

The festival's `festival_day_count` is the integer `ceil((end_time - start_time) / 86400)`, computed once on creation from the times in the request body. The integer-arithmetic form is the standard ceiling: a window of exactly two days' worth of seconds gives 2, a window with one extra second over the two-day boundary gives 3.

### 2.8. The Outbound Event Stream and the Four Downstream Targets

Every program-relevant change emits a record in the `sync_envelopes` array of a `ScheduleChange` that the client created or that the system auto-created on a state transition. The array is an inline list on the `ScheduleChange` row and is read through `GET /festivals/{festivalId}/schedule-changes/{changeId}`. Each element of the array has four fields — `target`, `status`, `queued_at`, `acked_at`, and a `payload_hash`. The `target` is a string drawn from a fixed four-element set: the literal strings `ticketing`, `im` (the instant-messaging push channel for the audience-facing announcement), `broadcasting`, and `sponsor`. The four elements are the connectors to the four external systems the scenario names — the ticketing platform, the audience IM channel, the broadcasting partner, and the sponsor-management system — and the order in which they appear in the array is the canonical order `ticketing`, `im`, `broadcasting`, `sponsor`. The `status` of each element is one of the strings `QUEUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `FAILED`, or — for the revert path — `CANCELLED`. A fresh apply seeds all four elements with `QUEUED` and stamps `queued_at` on each. The harness then drives the external acknowledgements in by calling the external-system sync-acknowledgement ingress capability for the non-ticketing targets and the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability for the ticketing target on a refund-bearing change kind (the latter endpoint is functionally the ticketing-leg sync-ack with the additional `refund_breakdown` payload that carries the externally-final per-type cents-total figures). The body of the external-system sync-acknowledgement ingress capability is `festival_id`, `schedule_change_id`, `target`, the boolean `succeeded`, and the `payload_hash`. A successful ack flips the matching element to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamps `acked_at`; a failed ack (with `succeeded` false) flips the element to `FAILED`. The `payload_hash` field on the request body is the SHA-256 of the payload the external system observed, echoed back so the test harness can assert it round-trips into the envelope element's `payload_hash` field. When the last `QUEUED` element of the four flips to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, the change itself advances from `APPLIED` to the terminal state `SYNCED` and an audit entry of category `SYNC_ACK` is appended. A target outside the four-element set returns `ERR_SYNC_TARGET_UNKNOWN` at HTTP 400. A sync-ack against an envelope element that is not currently `QUEUED` (because the revert path or a prior ack already flipped it) returns `ERR_SYNC_ENVELOPE_NOT_QUEUED` at HTTP 409.

For the ticketing-leg-with-refund variant, the the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability body's `refund_breakdown` is an array of `{ticket_type_id, refunded_count_delta, final_cents_total_for_type}` rows. Each row's `ticket_type_id` must point to a ticket type whose `festival_id` matches the `schedule_change_id`'s `festival_id`, otherwise the request is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_CROSS_FESTIVAL_REFUND`. The endpoint runs the chronological-variant final-refund computation described in §3.6, incrementing the ticket type's `refunded_count` by the row's `refunded_count_delta`, decrementing the ticket type's `provisional_refund_cents_outstanding` by the matching provisional cents that the original apply had pencilled in, and stamping the schedule change's `final_refund_cents_total` to the sum across rows of the `final_cents_total_for_type` figures. The cross-row magnitude bound is hard: the `final_cents_total_for_type` for any row may not exceed the matching provisional figure attributed to that type by the originating apply by any positive amount (the absolute tolerance is `ABS_TOLERANCE_CENTS = 0`), otherwise the request is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PROVISIONAL`. Equality is the standard outcome before any admin-fee deduction; strictly less than is the standard outcome after the external system's admin-fee deduction is applied. The ticketing envelope element flips to `ACKNOWLEDGED` on the same call, and when the other three targets have already acknowledged through their respective sync-acks, the change advances `APPLIED → SYNCED`. The festival's `outstanding_tickets_total` is recomputed as a side effect. A late the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability against a ticket type whose festival has already reached the `CLOSED` state is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_FESTIVAL_ALREADY_CLOSED`.

The clock-advance test endpoint the clock-advance operator capability carries a body of `{festival_id?, delta_seconds?, to_unix_seconds?}` with exactly one of `delta_seconds` and `to_unix_seconds` supplied. A `festival_id` omitted means the advance is applied to every festival in the database in a single transaction. The semantics is monotonic non-decreasing: a negative `delta_seconds` or a `to_unix_seconds` earlier than the festival's current logical wall clock returns `ERR_CLOCK_ADVANCE_BACKWARD` at HTTP 400, and supplying neither or both of the two time arguments returns `ERR_CLOCK_INVALID_ARGS` at HTTP 400. When the advance crosses one or more of the time-gated thresholds described in §3, the corresponding transitions fire in deterministic order — the festival's own `start_time` crossing fires the festival's `PUBLISHED → LIVE` transition, the program-item's `start_time` crossing fires that item's `ANNOUNCED → ON_STAGE` while the festival is `LIVE`, the rehearsal's `start_time` crossing fires the rehearsal's `BOOKED → IN_PROGRESS`, the program-item's `end_time` crossing fires `ON_STAGE → PERFORMED`, the rehearsal's `end_time + REHEARSAL_GRACE_SECONDS` crossing fires the rehearsal's `IN_PROGRESS → ARCHIVED`, and the festival's `end_time + FESTIVAL_CLOSE_GRACE_SECONDS` crossing fires the festival's `LIVE → CLOSED` and the festival-close cascade. The two grace constants are `REHEARSAL_GRACE_SECONDS = 600` (ten minutes) and `FESTIVAL_CLOSE_GRACE_SECONDS = 3600` (one hour). The ordering rule within a single tick that crosses multiple thresholds is wall-clock order of the thresholds, with the entity-type ordering listed above as the tiebreak at exactly equal threshold times. Each fired transition appends an audit entry of category `CLOCK_TICK`. The companion the clock-state read capability endpoint, restricted to `PlatformAdmin` and `AuditViewer`, returns the per-festival `clock_offset_seconds` and the implied logical-wall-clock-of-record for each festival, optionally filtered to a specific `festival_id`.

The post-event sweep the post-event sweep operator capability carries a body of `{festival_id, compute_payout?}`. While the festival is still open (`status` not yet `CLOSED`), the sweep is a no-op on the actual percentage field — it may refresh the projected percentages but it does not flip any allocation's status to `REPORTED` nor stamp the actual percentage. After the festival has closed, the sweep is the path that flips every `DELIVERED` allocation to `REPORTED` with a closing `REPORTED` `FulfilmentLog` row appended (its `units` field is zero), recomputes each tier's `actual_fulfilment_pct`, and — when the body's optional `compute_payout` flag is true — computes the sponsor payout total in cents per the formula in §3.7 and surfaces it on the fulfilment report. The sweep is idempotent: a second invocation after close returns the same numeric snapshot as the first. A nonexistent `festival_id` on the sweep body returns `ERR_SWEEP_FESTIVAL_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404. The audit entries the sweep writes are of category `EXPOSURE`. The matching read endpoint is the per-tier fulfilment-report read, which is open to `SponsorshipAdmin`, `FestivalProducer`, and `AuditViewer` and returns a synthetic `FulfilmentReportView` whose payload exposes the projected and actual percentages, the per-allocation `delivered_units` vector, the tier's `tier_remaining_quota`, and the `sponsor_payout_total_cents` figure when the sweep has computed it.

The exposure-catalog dictionary is the festival-level table the producer seeds to declare the total units of each of the four exposure kinds the festival is selling. It is read and written through `GET`, `POST`, and `PATCH` on `/festivals/{festivalId}/exposure-catalog` with a body shape of `{catalog: {kind: total_units}}` where the four allowed keys of the inner dictionary are the four kind strings `stage_name`, `screen_time`, `artist_mention`, and `branded_zone` (the four catalog-resource kinds the scenario names: the festival's flagship stage carrying a sponsor name, sponsor screen-time during interludes, an artist's on-stage mention of a sponsor, and a sponsor's branded zone on the festival grounds). Each value is a non-negative integer total. The synthetic return shape is named `ExposureCatalogView` and is not a stored entity in its own right — the dictionary lives in a single row keyed by the festival. A PATCH that would lower any kind's `total_units` below the currently-disbursed sum across all the festival's allocations of that kind is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_EXPOSURE_CATALOG_BELOW_DISBURSED`. The same error id is reused by the equipment-kit `total_units` PATCH endpoint when the requested new total would be lower than the festival-wide peak concurrent demand for that kit at any instant in the festival window.

The audit-log entries themselves are the append-only event-log rows for every state transition, every concurrency loser, every sync acknowledgement, every clock tick that fires a transition, every schedule-change apply and revert, every exposure allocation deliver and void, and the catch-all generic state-transition class for the routine transitions. The `event_category` enum on the row has six values: `SCHEDULE_CHANGE` (the category for schedule-change apply/reject/revert and the auto-emitted `VERSION_PUBLISH` and `TICKET_NOTIFY` and `ITEM_CANCEL` and `ITEM_RESCHEDULE` and `STAGE_SWAP` changes), `EXPOSURE` (for sponsor-tier sweeps, the catalog write, the per-allocation deliver and void, and the post-event sweep's per-tier roll-up), `SYNC_ACK` (for every flip of an envelope element to `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `FAILED`, and the unified ticketing-leg refund-confirmed path), `CLOCK_TICK` (for every transition that the clock-advance triggers, including the rehearsal-archive cascade on festival close and the program-item start and end transitions and the festival start and end transitions), `STATE_TRANSITION` (the generic category for the manual state-flip endpoints such as confirm, announce, cancel, suspend, resume, publish, submit-for-review, return-for-revision, withdraw, the team withdraw, the rehearsal cancel, and the explicit-only transitions that the clock does not drive), and `OCC_CONFLICT` (for the version-conflict loser and the apply-vs-reject race loser). The audit entry's `from_state` is the prior status enum value and `to_state` is the posterior status enum value, both null for non-state-transition categories. The `subject_entity_type` is the string name of the entity the event targets — `Festival`, `ProgramVersion`, `Stage`, `EquipmentKit`, `Artist`, `ProgramItem`, `RiderRequirement`, `SoundEngineerTeam`, `SoundCheckSlot`, `Rehearsal`, `TicketType`, `SponsorTier`, `ExposureAllocation`, `FulfilmentLog`, `ScheduleChange`, or `AuditEntry`. The `subject_entity_id` is the targeted row's `id`. The `payload_summary` is a short, human-readable, free-form English string the implementation writes — the scenario's per-cascade summaries that include figures such as "Item X cancelled, 3 tickets queued for refund, 2 exposure allocations voided" are the canonical examples. The audit-entries list endpoint accepts filters on `event_category`, on `subject_entity_type`, and on a `created_at_after` timestamp string in ISO 8601 UTC form, and the order is the per-festival `created_at` ascending. The entire audit-log surface is gated behind the audit-log allowlist of three roles described in §2.1.

## 3. Business Logic and Workflows

The system's business logic is most naturally read as a chronological pipeline from the moment a festival is conceived to the moment its post-event sweep prints the sponsor fulfilment report. The following subsections walk that pipeline, weaving the cross-cutting safety and consistency rules into the stages they constrain rather than pulling them into a single rules block.

### 3.1. Festival Setup: The Producer's Opening Moves

A festival begins life when a `FestivalProducer` (or, for the rare case of a platform-administrative seeding, a `PlatformAdmin`) creates the festival record with a `name`, a `venue_name`, an integer `start_time` and an integer `end_time` in Unix seconds UTC. The system stamps the new row's `id` to a fresh UUID, sets `status` to the initial state `PLANNED`, sets `clock_offset_seconds` to zero, computes `festival_day_count` from the two times per §2.7, leaves `current_program_version_id` null until the first program version publishes, leaves the computed `outstanding_tickets_total` at zero (because no ticket type has been created yet, so the cross-type sum of `issued_count` minus `refunded_count` is the empty sum), and writes an audit entry of category `STATE_TRANSITION` for the implicit `null → PLANNED` jump on the `Festival` subject. A `Festival` whose window fails the validity check — either `end_time` is not strictly greater than `start_time`, or either time is non-positive, or `festival_day_count` would be zero — is rejected at HTTP 400 with `ERR_INVALID_FESTIVAL_WINDOW`. A PATCH on the time fields runs the same check.

From `PLANNED` the festival moves to the working state `SCHEDULING` through the open-scheduling capability the producer exercises (no body required), appending an audit entry. The lifecycle states of a `Festival` are exactly `PLANNED`, `SCHEDULING`, `PUBLISHED`, `LIVE`, `CLOSED`, and `CANCELLED`, with `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` the two terminal absorbing states. From `SCHEDULING` the festival advances to `PUBLISHED` automatically the first time a `ProgramVersion` under it transitions to the `PUBLISHED` state (the publish call is on the program-version, and the festival's own status is a side effect of that). Subsequent publishes of newer program versions leave the festival's status at `PUBLISHED` without ringing a new transition on the festival itself. The advance from `PUBLISHED` to `LIVE` is system-driven: it fires when the clock-advance operator capability carries the logical wall clock past the festival's `start_time`, where the logical wall clock is `real_now + clock_offset_seconds` per the convention in §2.7. The advance from `LIVE` to `CLOSED` is similarly clock-driven: it fires when the logical wall clock crosses `end_time + FESTIVAL_CLOSE_GRACE_SECONDS` (the festival's end plus the one-hour grace constant of 3600 seconds, the trailing buffer that lets in-flight sound-check completions land before the close). Each of the clock-driven transitions writes an audit entry of category `CLOCK_TICK`.

A festival-level hard cancel is the privileged path the producer rarely needs and the platform admin handles when a festival has to be scrubbed in flight. The festival-cancel capability, restricted to the `PlatformAdmin` role and shared with the `FestivalProducer` role per the role matrix and named in the §4 table, takes a `reason` string in the body, flips the festival to `CANCELLED` from any non-terminal state, and fires a broad fan-out: every non-terminal `ProgramItem` flips to its own `CANCELLED` state with a per-item `ScheduleChange` of kind `ITEM_CANCEL` batched under the festival-level cancel record so the cascade does not recurse infinitely; every non-terminal `Rehearsal` flips to `CANCELLED`; every `TicketType` flips to `WITHDRAWN`; every `PLANNED` or `DELIVERED` `ExposureAllocation` flips to `VOIDED` with a `FulfilmentLog` row of `event_type` equal to `PROGRAM_VOIDED`; and a flurry of audit entries of category `STATE_TRANSITION` records the per-child status flips. Because this fan-out is a single-trigger-to-many-targets broadcast rather than a chain where each link's event causes the next link's event, it does not count toward the depth-3 transitive cascades described in §3.5.

The festival window enforcement is the cross-cutting child-containment rule. Every windowed child entity that the producer or her counterparts create under a festival — a `ProgramItem` with its `start_time` and `end_time`, a `Rehearsal` of either flavour with its `start_time` and `end_time`, a `SoundCheckSlot`'s `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` — must have its half-open interval `[start, end)` contained inside the parent festival's half-open `[start_time, end_time)` window. A child that pokes out of either end is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_CHILD_WINDOW_OUT_OF_FESTIVAL_RANGE`. The only thing that legitimately walks past the festival's `end_time` is the test clock-advance mechanic, which sets `clock_offset_seconds` to a value that puts `real_now + clock_offset_seconds` past the end-plus-grace boundary, triggering the auto-close.

### 3.2. The Stage, Equipment, and Engineer-Team Catalogues

A festival's stages are the durable resources on which program items perform. A `Stage` is registered through a producer- or stage-manager-issued create call carrying `stage_name`, an integer `zone_capacity` (the audience-zone seat or standing-area cap that the ticket-zone capacity check in §3.4 walks back from), an optional `changeover_buffer_seconds` integer that overrides the global default of 1800 seconds (the thirty-minute teardown-and-setup interval between two consecutive acts on the same stage), an `equipment_kits` array of kit-code strings that names which entries of the festival-wide equipment-kit catalogue this stage is physically wired for, and an optional boolean `is_emergency_substitute` flag (defaulting false) that marks the stage as an emergency-replacement venue eligible to absorb an item whose primary stage failed. The stage's `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may move to `DECOMMISSIONED` through the PATCH endpoint, but only when no non-terminal program item is scheduled on the stage. The stage's PATCH otherwise updates the mutable fields `zone_capacity`, `changeover_buffer_seconds`, `equipment_kits`, `is_emergency_substitute`, and `status`, with an `If-Match` header honoured per §2.5.

The festival-wide equipment kit catalogue is the `EquipmentKit` table, a per-festival list of named hardware kits — the lighting rig, the subwoofer stack, the drum-fill monitor — that program items demand. Each kit row carries a `kit_code` short-string identifier that is unique within a festival (a `kit_code` is the literal string that appears in the `equipment_kits` array on a stage and in the `equipment_kit_codes` array on a program item), a `total_units` non-negative integer counting how many physical copies of that kit the festival owns, and an optional `description` free-text annotation. A kit's `total_units` is the global ceiling that the across-stages aggregate-capacity rule of §3.3 enforces. The PATCH endpoint on a kit updates `total_units` and `description`. Lowering `total_units` below the current peak concurrent demand at any instant in the festival window is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_EXPOSURE_CATALOG_BELOW_DISBURSED` — the same error id the exposure-catalog floor reuses, scoped here by the request path to the equipment-kit floor.

The sound-engineer-team roster is the `SoundEngineerTeam` table, one row per crew. Each team carries a `team_name` display string, a `head_engineer_name`, an optional integer `warmup_minutes` that overrides the global ten-minute default `TEAM_WARMUP_MINUTES` (the post-slot warmup gap between two consecutive sound-checks the same team runs), a `status` enum of `AVAILABLE`, `BUSY`, or the terminal staffing-removed `WITHDRAWN`, and a `current_booking_id` field that points to the `SoundCheckSlot` the team is presently committed to or is null when the team is idle. The team's status cycles `AVAILABLE → BUSY` when a `SoundCheckSlot` referencing it transitions `REQUESTED → SCHEDULED`, and back `BUSY → AVAILABLE` when that slot reaches `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. The terminal `WITHDRAWN` is reached through an explicit withdraw call by a `StageManager`, which is rejected with `ERR_TEAM_HAS_ACTIVE_SLOT` at HTTP 409 if the team is the `current_booking_id` of any `SCHEDULED` slot (the orchestrator must cancel the slot first). An audit entry is appended on every team-status change. The "the slot referenced by `current_booking_id` finished" rule is the third link of the depth-three cascade described in §3.5 when the chain originates from a program-item cancel.

A festival's `Artist` catalogue is global rather than per-festival — the artist-registration capability sits at the API root rather than under any festival path precisely so the same artist row can appear on the programs of different festivals and so the cross-festival commitment table travels with the artist. An artist row carries a `stage_name` (the artist's performance name), a `tier` enum drawn from the four marketing tiers `HEADLINER`, `MAIN`, `OPENER`, and `SURPRISE`, a `home_city` free-text label that the deterministic tie-break in the priority scoring uses as a fixed seed, an `external_commitments` array of `{festival_external_id, start_time, end_time, city}` objects describing the artist's bookings on other festivals during the same calendar window, and an optional integer `minimum_travel_buffer_hours` that overrides the global four-hour default `TRAVEL_HOURS_MIN` for the travel-buffer rule of §3.3.

### 3.3. The Program-Version Workflow and the Program-Item Lifecycle

The program of a festival is the ordered list of `ProgramItem` rows the audience reads on the festival's homepage. Because the producer refines the program over weeks of negotiation, the system models the program as a sequence of versioned snapshots — a `ProgramVersion` is the parent grouping that the items hang under, and the festival's `current_program_version_id` points at whichever version is currently authoritative. A version is born in the initial state `DRAFT` through a producer-issued create call that carries a human-readable `version_label`. From `DRAFT` the producer transitions the version to `READY_FOR_REVIEW` through the submit-for-review capability, which is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_PROGRAM_VERSION` at HTTP 409 when the version has zero attached program items (the implementation's `ALLOW_EMPTY_PROGRAM` flag is false in benchmark mode and is documented as such because the rule is testable through the absence of any program-item with a matching `program_version_id`). From `READY_FOR_REVIEW` the artist-liaison may bounce the version back to `DRAFT` through the return-for-revision capability, which requires a `reason` string in the body (missing reason returns `ERR_MISSING_REVISION_REASON` at HTTP 400) — this is the cyclic `DRAFT ↔ READY_FOR_REVIEW` revision loop the scenario calls out, and the audit entry records the reason in its `payload_summary`. From either `DRAFT` or `READY_FOR_REVIEW` the producer transitions the version forward to `PUBLISHED` through the publish capability, which sets the version's `published_at` timestamp to the server's logical wall clock, snapshots the current set of `ProgramItem.id` values whose `program_version_id` matches this version into the version's immutable `program_item_ids` array (this is the version-pinning-on-approval contract: items added later under a different version are not visible through the published version's snapshot), and atomically does a compare-and-swap on the parent `Festival.current_program_version_id` keyed on the festival's `version` integer (the OCC field on the festival row, see §2.5), with the loser of two simultaneous publishes receiving `ERR_VERSION_CONFLICT` at HTTP 409 and an audit entry of category `OCC_CONFLICT`. A prior `PUBLISHED` version under the same festival, if any, is automatically flipped to the absorbing state `SUPERSEDED` and its `superseded_by_version_id` back-pointer is stamped with the new pinned version's id, forming a chain that traces the festival's program history. The version's preconditions before it can publish are that the parent `Festival.status` is in the set `SCHEDULING` or `PUBLISHED` (the festival has at least passed the open-scheduling step, and is not yet `LIVE` or `CLOSED`) — otherwise the cross-state error `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_SCHEDULING_OR_PUBLISHED` at HTTP 409 fires — and that no `ProgramItem` under the version has an unfulfilled blocking rider (otherwise `ERR_BLOCKING_RIDERS_UNFULFILLED` at HTTP 422 fires, the same error id the announce path uses for the same condition on a single item, scoped here by the publish endpoint to the whole version). The publish side effects include the auto-emission of a `ScheduleChange` of kind `VERSION_PUBLISH` whose `sync_envelopes` array is seeded with the four `QUEUED` records for `ticketing`, `im`, `broadcasting`, `sponsor`, fanning out the publish announcement to all four downstream systems through the standard event-driven-synchronisation surface of §2.8. The terminal-on-the-discard-axis state of an unpublished version is `WITHDRAWN`, reached via the withdraw capability from either `DRAFT` or `READY_FOR_REVIEW` and gated on the version's having never published. A withdrawn version's program items are not auto-cancelled (operators are expected to migrate them to the new draft first), so the items merely become unreachable through any "current version" query against the festival.

A `ProgramItem` is the individual booking of an artist on a stage at a time. Items are created by `ArtistLiaison` (and shared with `FestivalProducer`) through the program-item create endpoint, which carries the `program_version_id` the item attaches to, the global `artist_id`, the `stage_id` the item performs on, a free-text `genre` label, the integer Unix-seconds `start_time` and `end_time` of the slot, and an optional `equipment_kit_codes` array listing which kit_code strings the act demands (the system requires every code on the list to appear in the parent stage's `equipment_kits` array, otherwise the item's create or PATCH is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_STAGE_LACKS_EQUIPMENT_KIT`). The item's `duration_seconds` is the read-only difference `end_time - start_time`. The lifecycle states are `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, `ANNOUNCED`, `ON_STAGE`, `PERFORMED`, and the absorbing branch state `CANCELLED`; the initial state on creation is `PROPOSED`. The `slot_priority_score` is a computed float on the row, the weighted-scoring formula of §3.5 used to break ties when two `PROPOSED` items compete for similar windows.

Alongside the lifecycle axis, a `ProgramItem` carries an independent parallel-dimensions axis named `sound_check_status` with enum values `NOT_REQUIRED` (the default at creation), `REQUESTED` (set as a side effect of the first sound-check-slot creation on the item), `SCHEDULED` (set when a slot under the item transitions to its own `SCHEDULED` state), `COMPLETED` (set when a slot reaches its own `COMPLETED` state), and the explicit-skip absorbing state `WAIVED`. The two axes interact at exactly one synchronisation point, described under the announce transition below. The third axis on the row, also independent, is `announcement_sync_state` with enum values `PENDING` (the create-time default), `QUEUED` (set when the announce side-effect-emits a `TICKET_NOTIFY` `ScheduleChange`), `ACKED` (set when the `im` envelope element of that change reaches `ACKNOWLEDGED`), and `FAILED` (set when the `im` envelope's ack carries `succeeded` false).

The lifecycle transitions of a `ProgramItem` are pinned as follows. The advance from `PROPOSED` to `CONFIRMED` is the `confirm` capability the artist-liaison (or, for hot-path overrides, the producer) exercises through the confirm endpoint with an optional `If-Match` header. Its preconditions are the most numerous in the whole machine: the parent festival's status must be in the set `SCHEDULING` or `PUBLISHED` (otherwise the cross-state `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_SCHEDULING_OR_PUBLISHED` at HTTP 409 fires); the item's `program_version_id` must equal the parent festival's `current_program_version_id` once any version has been published, otherwise the cross-entity state error `ERR_PROGRAM_VERSION_SUPERSEDED` at HTTP 409 fires (this is the cross-entity state gate against the festival's pinned version, and before any version has yet published the gate is relaxed to "the `program_version_id` references a non-`WITHDRAWN`-non-`SUPERSEDED` version under the festival"); the per-stage changeover-buffer overlap check of §3.5 must pass against the item's `[start_time, end_time)` interval on the chosen stage_id (otherwise `ERR_STAGE_CHANGEOVER_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409); the festival-wide aggregate-capacity rule on each kit_code in the item's `equipment_kit_codes` must pass (otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_KIT_OVERSUBSCRIBED` at HTTP 409, with the offending `kit_code` and the peak observed count in the message); the artist's travel-buffer gap and multi-festival overlap checks of §3.5 must both pass (with their respective errors `ERR_ARTIST_TRAVEL_BUFFER_VIOLATION` and `ERR_ARTIST_MULTI_FESTIVAL_CONFLICT`, both at HTTP 409); the artist self-overlap rule must pass — no other `ProgramItem` of the same `artist_id` in any festival in the set of statuses `CONFIRMED`, `ANNOUNCED`, or `ON_STAGE` has an intersecting half-open window with this item's window, otherwise `ERR_ARTIST_SELF_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409; the festival-window containment from §2.4 must hold; and the optimistic-concurrency `If-Match` check if a header was supplied must succeed. The side effect of a successful confirm is the recomputation of `slot_priority_score` and an audit entry of category `STATE_TRANSITION`. The priority-conflict pinning between the cross-state version-superseded check and the artist-multi-festival-overlap check is the one stated under §2.6: the cross-state error wins because it sits in the earlier category in the evaluation order, and the test fixture exercises the precise case where both conditions are simultaneously violated and asserts that the client sees `ERR_PROGRAM_VERSION_SUPERSEDED` rather than `ERR_ARTIST_MULTI_FESTIVAL_CONFLICT`.

The advance from `CONFIRMED` to `ANNOUNCED` is the `announce` capability the artist-liaison exercises. Its preconditions are: every blocking `RiderRequirement` on the item — a rider whose `is_blocking` flag is true — has a non-null `fulfilled_at` timestamp (the `ERR_BLOCKING_RIDERS_UNFULFILLED` at HTTP 422 fires otherwise), and a headliner-specific cardinality minimum (the `blocking_rider_minimum_check`): when the artist's `tier` is `HEADLINER`, the total count of blocking riders on the item must be at least the constant `HEADLINER_MIN_BLOCKING_RIDERS = 1`, otherwise `ERR_HEADLINER_MISSING_BLOCKING_RIDER` at HTTP 422 fires, with the boundary precisely at zero versus one — a HEADLINER announcing with zero blocking riders is rejected and a HEADLINER announcing with exactly one fulfilled blocking rider on file is accepted. Non-headliner tiers (`MAIN`, `OPENER`, `SURPRISE`) have a required-blocking minimum of 0 and so trivially pass the cardinality step, but still must satisfy the universal "every blocking rider that does exist must be fulfilled" condition. The parent festival's status must be exactly `PUBLISHED` for an announcement to be legal (`ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_PUBLISHED` at HTTP 409 otherwise — the announcement has no public surface to publish to before then and the festival's window-close at `CLOSED` rules out any post-close announcement). The cross-entity gate between the lifecycle axis and the sound-check axis is the rule that says a non-headliner cannot announce while its `sound_check_status` is in the set `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` (the sound-check is in flight and the audience-facing announcement would be premature); a headliner is granted a carve-out from that gate because the headliner's announcement is what drives the bulk of late ticket sales, and so a headliner may announce even with its sound-check still in `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED`. The carve-out is symmetric across both directions: the announce is allowed when the headliner's sound_check_status is in `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` or `NOT_REQUIRED`, and the carve-out adds the `REQUESTED` and `SCHEDULED` states to the allowed set when and only when the artist's tier is `HEADLINER`. When the gate rejects, the error id is the cross-entity-state error `ERR_PARENT_ITEM_NOT_READY` at HTTP 409 — the same error id the sound-check schedule path uses for its mirror cross-entity check. The side effects of a successful announce are the auto-emission of a `ScheduleChange` of kind `TICKET_NOTIFY` with four `QUEUED` sync envelopes (the fanout-only no-state-change change kind whose only purpose is the four-target sync surface), the flip of the item's `announcement_sync_state` from `PENDING` to `QUEUED`, the recomputation to zero of the `provisional_refund_cents_outstanding` of every ticket type whose `tied_program_item_ids` includes this item (since the item moving forward in lifecycle clears any pending refund estimate that had been hanging on the item's provisional cancellation possibility), and an audit entry of category `STATE_TRANSITION`.

The advance from `ANNOUNCED` to `ON_STAGE` is system-driven: the clock-advance endpoint's logical wall clock crossing the item's `start_time` while the parent festival is `LIVE` flips the item's status, and an audit entry of category `CLOCK_TICK` is written. The advance from `ON_STAGE` to the terminal state `PERFORMED` is also clock-driven: the wall clock crossing the item's `end_time` flips the status, with an audit entry, and as a side effect any `PLANNED` `ExposureAllocation` whose `program_item_id` equals this item's id becomes eligible for delivery (the cross-entity-state gate on the deliver endpoint of §3.6 is exactly "the tied program-item's status is `PERFORMED` for item-tied allocations, or the festival is `LIVE` or `CLOSED` for festival-wide allocations whose `program_item_id` is null"). The explicit-internal-consistency error `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_TERMINAL_FOR_PERFORM` at HTTP 409 is reserved for the case where an implementation accidentally tries to mark `PERFORMED` outside the standard clock-tick path.

The branch from any of `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `ANNOUNCED` to the absorbing state `CANCELLED` is the `cancel` capability the artist-liaison (or producer) exercises directly through the cancel endpoint, with a required `reason` string, an optional `idempotency_key` honoured per the rules in §2.5, and an optional `If-Match`. The same final state is reached when a `ScheduleChange` of kind `ITEM_CANCEL` whose `target_program_item_id` is this item is applied (the apply endpoint runs the cascade described under §3.5 in full). The state-gate error when the item is already `CANCELLED` or has reached `ON_STAGE` or `PERFORMED` is `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_ALREADY_CANCELLED` at HTTP 409 for the already-cancelled case, and the implicit rule that an `ON_STAGE` item cannot be cancelled mid-set is enforced by the state-precondition family. The cancellation fires two parallel cascades and a refund-and-sync chain, all described in §3.5.

The recompute-priority capability, exposed at the recompute-priority idempotent capability for `FestivalProducer` and `PlatformAdmin`, recomputes the `slot_priority_score` field without driving any state transition and is the harness's hook for forcing the formula to re-evaluate. The capability is idempotent — a recompute that yields the same numeric value is a no-op except for the score-unchanged audit entry. The sound-check-waive capability, at the sound-check-waive capability on a program item, flips the parallel `sound_check_status` axis to `WAIVED` from whichever of `NOT_REQUIRED` or `REQUESTED` it currently sits in (the waive is rejected for items past `ANNOUNCED` on the lifecycle axis or whose sound-check axis is already at `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, those cases requiring the orchestrator to cancel the slot first). The waive requires a `reason` string in the body and is shared between `StageManager` and `FestivalProducer`.

The PATCH on a program item is the path the orchestrator uses to swap a stage (the emergency-substitution path that the `is_emergency_substitute` flag on the substitute stage marks as eligible), to shift the times, to change the `genre` label, or to update the `equipment_kit_codes`. The PATCH is rejected on an item already past `ANNOUNCED` on the lifecycle axis with `ERR_PROGRAM_ITEM_NOT_MUTABLE` at HTTP 409 — the orchestrator must instead create a `ScheduleChange` of kind `ITEM_RESCHEDULE` (for a time-only change) or kind `STAGE_SWAP` (for a stage swap) and apply it, so the four-target sync envelope and the audit trail are written through the canonical schedule-change pathway. Each PATCH re-runs the overlap-and-buffer-and-travel-and-equipment-wiring check cascade against the new tuple as if it were a fresh confirm.

The `RiderRequirement` rows under a program item are the append-only list of technical, hospitality, security, catering, or accessibility line items the artist's contract specifies — the `category` enum's values are exactly `TECHNICAL`, `HOSPITALITY`, `SECURITY`, `CATERING`, and `ACCESSIBILITY`. Each row carries a free-text `description`, a boolean `is_blocking` flag defaulting to false, and a nullable `fulfilled_at` timestamp that starts null and is flipped to the server clock by the rider-fulfilment PATCH endpoint at the rider-fulfilment PATCH capability whose body's `fulfilled: true` discriminator is the marker that the rider is now satisfied (the field name `fulfilled` on the request body distinguishes the call from a no-op PATCH). The rider PATCH is idempotent on an already-fulfilled rider (the second call returns the row unchanged without a fresh audit entry). The flip from null to the timestamp is the only mutation the rider entity supports, which is why the rider row carries only `id` and `created_at` from the common-fields set and not `updated_at` or `version` — the rider audit trail flows through `AuditEntry` rather than through a `version` integer on the rider itself. The rider list is also filterable by `category` and by the `is_blocking` flag on the list endpoint.

### 3.4. Ticketing and the Refund Pathway

A `TicketType` is the saleable unit of admission. It carries a `ticket_class` enum from the three classes `SINGLE_DAY`, `FULL_FESTIVAL`, and `VIP`; an array `tied_program_item_ids` of the program items the ticket grants access to (a single-day ticket ties to items happening on a single UTC calendar-day bucket of the festival window, a full-festival ticket ties to every item, and a VIP ticket ties to a curated subset that the producer configures explicitly); a `zone_name` string naming the audience-zone label whose capacity ceiling the issuance check walks back from; an integer `unit_price_cents` per-ticket cents price; two computed counters `issued_count` and `refunded_count`; and one computed cents-bucket `provisional_refund_cents_outstanding` that holds the sum of provisional refund estimates that have been pencilled in by every `ScheduleChange.apply` of kind `ITEM_CANCEL` whose target item is in this ticket's tie array and whose external ticketing-system acknowledgement has not yet arrived. The ticket type's status enum is `ACTIVE`, the cyclic-paired-with-`ACTIVE` operational pause state `SUSPENDED`, the festival-close terminal `CLOSED`, and the terminal "staffing-removed while suspended" state `WITHDRAWN`. The default on creation is `ACTIVE` with all the counters at zero. The cycle is operated by the `TicketingAdmin` through the suspend and resume capabilities: an active type may be paused (suspend, optionally carrying a `reason`) to halt fresh issuance — a a ticket-issuance call against a suspended type is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_TICKET_TYPE_NOT_ACTIVE` — and a paused type may be unpaused (resume) back to `ACTIVE` provided the parent festival is in one of the running states `SCHEDULING`, `PUBLISHED`, or `LIVE`. At festival close, the cascade visits every ticket type: a type that was `ACTIVE` at the close moment flips to `CLOSED`, a type that was `SUSPENDED` at the close moment flips to `WITHDRAWN`, and the `issued_count` and `refunded_count` figures are frozen as of the close — any subsequent the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability ack against a closed type is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_FESTIVAL_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The ticket-type machine's status enum is therefore `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED`, `WITHDRAWN`, with `CLOSED` and `WITHDRAWN` both absorbing.

The issuance endpoint the ticket-issuance capability accepts a positive integer `count` body field, an optional `idempotency_key`, and an optional `If-Match`. It enforces the zone-capacity ceiling: the cumulative net-issued count across every ticket type that shares the same `zone_name` within the festival (the "zone group") must remain less than or equal to the zone capacity after the increment of `count`. The zone capacity is the maximum across the zone group's stages — meaning the stages that host any program item appearing in any `tied_program_item_ids` of any type in the zone group — of those stages' `zone_capacity` integers. The boundary is strict-greater-than on the post-increment cumulative count: pre-increment cumulative equal to `zone_capacity − count` (the very-last-ticket case at the boundary) is accepted, and the next increment that would push the cumulative count above `zone_capacity` is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_TICKET_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The zero-capacity edge — when the zone group has no tied stage at all from which to derive a `zone_capacity`, so the implicit cap is zero — is rejected at the same priority with `ERR_TICKET_ZONE_NO_STAGE` at HTTP 422. The issuance's optimistic-concurrency check serialises the counter increment so that two simultaneous issues at the boundary cannot both succeed and overshoot. On success the response is HTTP 200, the type's `issued_count` is incremented by `count`, the festival's computed `outstanding_tickets_total` is recomputed, and an audit entry is appended.

The refund pathway is the dual-variant arithmetic that the dual columns `provisional_refund_cents_total` and `final_refund_cents_total` on a `ScheduleChange` row capture. The forward-looking variant fires when a `ScheduleChange` of kind `ITEM_CANCEL` is applied: for each `TicketType` `t` whose `tied_program_item_ids` includes the cancelled program item, the system computes the live outstanding count `outstanding_t = t.issued_count - t.refunded_count`, the per-ticket cents figure as the round-half-up of the type's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the implicit constant `cancelled_days` (which equals 1 per `ITEM_CANCEL`, because the cancellation lops off one day's worth of the ticket's coverage in the per-day taxonomy of the scenario) and divided by the parent festival's `festival_day_count`, and the per-type refund cents as the product of the integer outstanding count and that rounded-per-ticket figure. The half-up rounding step is applied exactly once per type, at the per-ticket-cents division; the across-tickets multiplication is exact-integer because `outstanding_t` is an integer, and the across-types sum is exact-integer for the same reason. The per-type figure is added to that type's `provisional_refund_cents_outstanding` bucket — a "pending refund" cents counter that holds the not-yet-externally-acknowledged refund estimate. The across-types sum is stamped to the schedule change's `provisional_refund_cents_total` field, and a per-type audit summary line is appended to the audit log. The half-up convention is the standard round-half-away-from-zero rule, not banker's rounding. The illustrative numeric calibration is the canonical case of a single ticket type whose `unit_price_cents` is 12000, with an outstanding count of three and the parent festival's `festival_day_count` equal to three: the per-ticket cents figure is the half-up rounding of 12000 times 1 divided by 3, which is exactly 4000 cents; the per-type refund cents is three (the outstanding count) times 4000, which is 12000 cents (in everyday terms one hundred and twenty dollars); and that 12000-cents value is what the schedule change's `provisional_refund_cents_total` field is stamped to. The chronological variant lands later as the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability, whose payload's `refund_breakdown` is the array of per-type final-cents figures the external ticketing system has actually computed after applying its administrative-fee deduction at the rate `ADMIN_FEE_RATE_FINAL = 0.05` (the five-percent fee the external system charges per refunded ticket). Each row's `final_cents_total_for_type` must be less than or equal to the matching provisional figure (the absolute tolerance constant `ABS_TOLERANCE_CENTS = 0` is strictly enforced: the final never exceeds the provisional, because the external system applies a non-negative deduction), and a row that exceeds the provisional bound by any positive amount is rejected at HTTP 422 with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PROVISIONAL`. On the canonical 12000-cents-provisional case, the corresponding final is 11400 cents, computed as 12000 minus the half-up rounding of 12000 times 0.05 (the five-percent admin fee being 600 cents on the half-up rule, with 11400 cents the actual refund amount). The endpoint increments each affected ticket type's `refunded_count` by the per-type `refunded_count_delta`, decrements the corresponding `provisional_refund_cents_outstanding` by the originally-pencilled-in provisional amount so the pending bucket goes back to zero on a full acknowledgement, and stamps the schedule change's `final_refund_cents_total` field to the across-row sum of the externally-final cents totals. The festival's `outstanding_tickets_total` is recomputed. An audit entry of category `SYNC_ACK` is appended. A late refund-confirmed against a festival that has already reached `CLOSED` is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_FESTIVAL_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The provisional-versus-final divergence on the same schedule change's two columns is the observable evidence of the dual-variant pair on the ticketing domain: the provisional column is the festival's forward-looking pro-rata estimate ignoring the admin fee, the final column is the externally-confirmed chronological tally after the fee is applied, and they differ exactly by the admin-fee factor.

The invariant the system asserts across the apply-and-revert window of a no-acknowledgement-yet schedule change is the conservation of `Festival.outstanding_tickets_total`. The apply of an `ITEM_CANCEL` change does not move the `issued_count` or `refunded_count` of any ticket type — those move only on a ticket-issuance call and on the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability respectively — so the outstanding-tickets number is unchanged between the moment before the apply and the moment after the revert that snaps the `provisional_refund_cents_outstanding` bucket back to zero. The transient evidence that the apply actually ran is the temporary bulge of the cents-bucket between the apply and the revert; the post-revert restoration of the bucket to zero — without any acknowledgement having arrived — is the closure of the compensating action. The cents-bucket transient is the test harness's hook for confirming the apply landed, and the integer-count invariant on `outstanding_tickets_total` is the cleanly conserved quantity.

The revert capability itself is the schedule-change revert capability, which the producer issues against an `APPLIED` change as long as no element of the change's `sync_envelopes` array has yet reached `ACKNOWLEDGED` — if any has, the revert is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_CHANGE_ALREADY_ACKED`, because once the downstream systems have observed the change the compensation must go forward as a separate inverse change rather than as a retroactive revert. The successful revert restores the target program item's status to the value recorded in the apply's audit entry's `from_state` field (the prior status before the cascade flipped it to `CANCELLED`), flips every `QUEUED` envelope element of the change to `CANCELLED` (the `CANCELLED` value of the envelope-status enum is reserved exclusively for the revert path; the full set of envelope-status enum values is `QUEUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `FAILED`, and `CANCELLED`), and decrements the touched ticket types' `provisional_refund_cents_outstanding` buckets back to zero. The sound-check cancellation cascade that the apply ran is not automatically reinstated by the revert — those side-effects are operator decisions; the invariant the revert preserves is the global outstanding-tickets count, not the state of each cascaded entity. The audit entry on the revert is of category `SCHEDULE_CHANGE` with the `payload_summary` containing the literal string `"reverted"`. A revert against a `PROPOSED` or `SYNCED` or `REJECTED` or already-`REVERTED` change is rejected with `ERR_CHANGE_NOT_APPLIED` at HTTP 409 (the state-gate error in the change-status family).

The four-target sync envelope on every schedule change is the standard event-driven-synchronisation surface that the scenario calls out: a change of kind `VERSION_PUBLISH` is auto-emitted on the program-version publish transition; a change of kind `TICKET_NOTIFY` is auto-emitted on the program-item announce transition and is the no-state-change no-op-cascade variant whose sole purpose is the four-target fan-out so the downstream systems learn about the announcement; a change of kind `ITEM_CANCEL` is the standard cancel-with-refund variant the producer creates explicitly through the schedule-change create capability (with a body whose `change_kind` discriminator is the literal string and whose `payload` carries a `reason` string) and then applies through the apply endpoint; a change of kind `ITEM_RESCHEDULE` carries a `payload` of `{new_start_time, new_end_time}` and moves the target item's window, freeing the prior interval and re-running the overlap-and-buffer-and-travel checks against the new window as a fresh confirm; and a change of kind `STAGE_SWAP` carries a `payload` of `{new_stage_id}` and rotates the target item's stage, exercising the emergency-substitution path through a stage whose `is_emergency_substitute` flag is true and re-running the equipment-wiring check and the changeover-overlap check against the new stage. Each schedule change carries an optional `target_program_item_id` field (null for the festival-level kinds `VERSION_PUBLISH` and `TICKET_NOTIFY` and the explicit-fanout kind whose `payload` carries a free-form `message` field, the `TICKET_NOTIFY` kind also being the harness's test hook for asserting the sync-envelope plumbing on a no-mutation change). The change row carries the field `applied_at` (the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp of the apply transition, null until then), the `provisional_refund_cents_total` and `final_refund_cents_total` columns described above, and the optional `idempotency_key` field whose semantics are the standard ones in §2.5. The lifecycle of a `ScheduleChange` is `PROPOSED` on create, the fork `PROPOSED → APPLIED` on apply or `PROPOSED → REJECTED` on the reject path (which is gated on the change being in `PROPOSED` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_NOT_PROPOSED` at HTTP 409 — and which requires a `reason` string in the body, missing reason yielding `ERR_MISSING_REJECTION_REASON` at HTTP 400 in the body-shape family), the further advance `APPLIED → SYNCED` when the last envelope reaches `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or the inverse `APPLIED → REVERTED` per the revert capability above. The three terminal states are `SYNCED`, `REJECTED`, and `REVERTED`. The reject path emits no cascade — it merely flips the status, writes a `STATE_TRANSITION` audit entry whose `payload_summary` mentions the rejection reason, and is the path the producer uses to discard a proposed change without applying its effects. The apply-versus-reject race resolves via the row-level status CAS: the loser observes a non-`PROPOSED` state and is rejected with `ERR_CHANGE_NOT_PROPOSED` at HTTP 409, and an audit entry of category `OCC_CONFLICT` is appended for the loser. The sync-ack-versus-revert race resolves on the same row, with the post-revert envelope status `CANCELLED` causing a subsequent sync-ack to see a non-`QUEUED` envelope and return `ERR_SYNC_ENVELOPE_NOT_QUEUED` at HTTP 409, and the mirror order having the ack first and the revert seeing a non-zero acknowledgement count and being rejected with `ERR_CHANGE_ALREADY_ACKED`.

### 3.5. The Interval Engine, the Priority Score, and the Cascades

The interval engine is the heart of the conflict-detection layer, and it carries three distinct geometric flavours, one per resource entity, that the test fixture explicitly cross-tabulates. The first is the per-stage changeover-buffer overlap rule that the program-item create, the program-item time PATCH, the program-item stage swap, the rehearsal create when the type is `STAGE`, the schedule-change apply for `ITEM_RESCHEDULE`, and the schedule-change apply for `STAGE_SWAP` all run. The candidate program-item or stage-rehearsal interval is treated as half-open right-exclusive `[start, end)`. Each existing CONFIRMED-or-ANNOUNCED-or-ON_STAGE program item, and each BOOKED-or-IN_PROGRESS stage-rehearsal, on the same stage is treated as a closed interval padded on both sides by the stage's `changeover_buffer_seconds` (defaulting to the global `STAGE_CHANGEOVER_BUFFER_SECONDS = 1800` of thirty minutes when the stage's per-stage override is null) — concretely the padded interval is `[existing.start_time - buffer, existing.end_time + buffer]` with both endpoints inclusive. The conflict test is "the half-open candidate's interval intersects the closed-and-padded existing's interval"; in tight arithmetic terms a candidate starts no later than the existing's padded right endpoint and the existing's padded left endpoint is strictly earlier than the candidate's exclusive right end (the half-open candidate's right end is open so an existing interval whose padded left edge is exactly the candidate's `end_time` does not conflict, while the existing's padded right edge is closed so a candidate starting exactly at that padded right edge does conflict). The boundary-asymmetry — the candidate is right-open while the existing is right-closed-with-pad — is the precise geometric flavour the test fixture pins on the buffer-edge equality case. The rejection on overlap is `ERR_STAGE_CHANGEOVER_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409.

The second flavour is the equipment-kit aggregate-capacity rule on the festival-wide concurrent use of a given `kit_code`. There is no buffer pad on this rule — the kit is either in use during a program item's half-open `[start_time, end_time)` interval or it is not, and the test is on the integer maximum over time of the count of simultaneously-active intervals that share the same `kit_code`, summed across all stages of the festival, in the candidate's window. The semantics is the standard sweep-line over `(time, +1)` and `(time, -1)` events, with the half-open right-exclusive disambiguation that a closing event at time `T` is processed before an opening event at the same time `T` (so back-to-back items on the same kit do not count as overlapping at the joint instant). The candidate's own +1 over its full window is added to the maximum-over-existing running count, and the resulting peak-simultaneous-users count is compared against the `total_units` of the corresponding `EquipmentKit`. A peak strictly above `total_units` is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_KIT_OVERSUBSCRIBED`, the message carrying the offending `kit_code` and the observed peak count. The rule is run on every kit_code in the candidate's `equipment_kit_codes` list independently — a candidate with two kits where the first is at its ceiling fails on the first kit's check.

The third flavour is the sound-engineer-team consecutive-booking rule on a single team's interval graph. A team's schedule-room is the union of its SoundCheckSlot intervals in the set of statuses `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. Each such interval is closed on both ends and is padded symmetrically by the team's `warmup_seconds` (the team's `warmup_minutes` integer field times 60, defaulting to the ten-minute global constant `TEAM_WARMUP_MINUTES = 10` rendered as 600 seconds, when the team's override is null). The conflict test on a candidate slot whose own interval is the closed `[scheduled_window_start, scheduled_window_end]` is "the candidate's closed interval intersects the existing's warmup-padded closed interval" — both endpoints inclusive on both intervals. Two consecutive slots on the same team thus need at least `warmup_seconds` of gap between the prior end and the next start to clear the rule. The rejection is `ERR_ENGINEER_TEAM_DOUBLE_BOOKED` at HTTP 409. The race-condition variant — two simultaneous schedule PATCHes assigning the same team to two different slots — is handled by the row lock on the team described in §2.5 with the same error id surfacing on the loser. The companion error `ERR_TEAM_NOT_AVAILABLE` at HTTP 409 fires on the cross-entity-state-check half of the schedule transition when the team's `status` is not `AVAILABLE` at the moment of the call (it is already `BUSY` from another slot, or it is `WITHDRAWN`), which is the second half of the rule-7-instance-B cross-entity gate; the first half is the parent program item being in the set `CONFIRMED` or `ANNOUNCED`, otherwise `ERR_PARENT_ITEM_NOT_READY` at HTTP 409.

A fourth interval flavour — distinct from the three above and listed for completeness because the rehearsal subsystem uses it — is the rehearsal-room same-name overlap rule. A `Rehearsal` of `rehearsal_type` `ROOM` carries a free-form-string `rehearsal_room_name` rather than a `stage_id` (and a `Rehearsal` of `rehearsal_type` `STAGE` carries a `stage_id` rather than a room name; the two namespaces are disjoint by construction, so a stage rehearsal on stage X and a room rehearsal in room R1 never conflict with each other). For two `BOOKED`-or-`IN_PROGRESS` `ROOM` rehearsals that share the same `rehearsal_room_name`, the conflict test is the pure half-open intersection on `[start_time, end_time)` — back-to-back is allowed (the prior rehearsal's `end_time` is exclusive so a fresh rehearsal starting exactly there is fine), strict overlap rejects, and the rejection is `ERR_REHEARSAL_ROOM_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409. The `archive_uri` field on a rehearsal is null until the row reaches the absorbing state `ARCHIVED` (reached either when the clock-advance crosses the rehearsal's `end_time + REHEARSAL_GRACE_SECONDS` of ten minutes, or when the festival-close cascade visits the rehearsal in `BOOKED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and archives it), at which moment the field is stamped to the deterministic synthetic URI `archive://festival/<festival_id>/rehearsal/<rehearsal_id>` — the literal string with the parent festival's id and the rehearsal's id substituted in, which the test asserts on. The rehearsal's lifecycle is `BOOKED` on create, the advance `BOOKED → IN_PROGRESS` when the wall clock crosses the rehearsal's `start_time` while the parent festival's status is in `PUBLISHED` or `LIVE`, the advance `IN_PROGRESS → ARCHIVED` on the grace-window-elapsed clock tick or the festival-close cascade (the audit entry's `payload_summary` on the cascade-driven archive names the originating clock-tick event id of the festival-close event, which is the trace-back link the depth-three cascade rule expects), and the branch `BOOKED → CANCELLED` for an explicit cancel through the rehearsal-cancel capability (with an optional `reason`), gated on the rehearsal still being `BOOKED` — an in-progress rehearsal cannot be cancelled and must instead auto-archive on the grace-window crossing. An attempt to cancel a non-`BOOKED` rehearsal is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_REHEARSAL_NOT_BOOKED`. The "the candidate rehearsal-room interval shares a `rehearsal_room_name` with an existing `BOOKED`-or-`IN_PROGRESS` rehearsal and their half-open windows intersect" condition rejects with `ERR_REHEARSAL_ROOM_OVERLAP`. The "the room rehearsal carries a non-null `rehearsal_room_name` and the stage rehearsal carries a non-null `stage_id` and the two are different namespaces" no-conflict outcome is the deliberate namespace-disjointness the scenario hinges on. The rehearsals list endpoint accepts filters on `status`, on `artist_id`, and on `rehearsal_type`.

The sound-check slot subsystem sits on top of the team and the program-item axes. A `SoundCheckSlot` is created through the sound-check-slot create capability under a program item with an optional initial window pair `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` (the times may be left null at create time and supplied later through the schedule PATCH). The slot's `program_item_id` is the parent item, the `festival_id` is denormalised from the parent for indexing, the `stage_id` is pinned from the parent program item's `stage_id` at the moment of slot creation and is not subsequently mutable through the slot's own endpoints — if the parent item's stage_id is later changed (via a `STAGE_SWAP` schedule change), the slot's persisted `stage_id` is now out of sync with the parent's current stage, and the schedule transition on the slot fails at HTTP 409 with `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_STAGE_MISMATCH`, requiring the orchestrator to cancel the slot and recreate it on the new stage. The slot's initial status on create is `REQUESTED`. The advance `REQUESTED → SCHEDULED` is the schedule PATCH at the sound-check schedule-PATCH capability that supplies an additional `sound_engineer_team_id` body field naming the team to run the check (and optionally updates the window pair). The preconditions for the schedule transition are: the parent program item's status is in `CONFIRMED` or `ANNOUNCED` (otherwise `ERR_PARENT_ITEM_NOT_READY` at HTTP 409, the cross-entity-state error on the program-item side of the gate); the referenced sound-engineer team's status is `AVAILABLE` (otherwise `ERR_TEAM_NOT_AVAILABLE` at HTTP 409, the cross-entity-state error on the team side of the gate); the team-warmup-padded overlap check against the team's other `SCHEDULED`-or-`COMPLETED` slots passes; the slot's window lies wholly within the festival window per the §2.4 child-containment rule; the slot's window precedes the parent program item's `start_time` by at least the global handoff constant `TEAM_HANDOFF_SECONDS = 600` (ten minutes, the gap from the end of the sound-check to the act's start — the engineer-to-front-of-house handoff), the boundary being inclusive `scheduled_window_end <= ProgramItem.start_time - TEAM_HANDOFF_SECONDS` so that exactly ten minutes is acceptable and less than ten is the rejection at HTTP 422 with `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_WINDOW_INVALID`; the slot's `scheduled_window_start` is no earlier than the festival's `start_time`; and the stage-pin guard above. The side effect of a successful schedule transition is the flip of the team's `status` from `AVAILABLE` to `BUSY` with `current_booking_id` stamped to the slot's id, the flip of the parent program item's `sound_check_status` axis from `REQUESTED` (which the create call had set as a side-effect from the default `NOT_REQUIRED`) to `SCHEDULED`, and an audit entry of category `STATE_TRANSITION`. The advance `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` is the complete capability through the sound-check complete capability, gated on the wall clock being at or past the slot's `scheduled_window_end` (the inclusive ≥ boundary), with the side effects of the team flipping back to `AVAILABLE` (and `current_booking_id` cleared to null), the parent program item's `sound_check_status` axis flipping to `COMPLETED`, and an audit entry. An attempt to complete a slot not in `SCHEDULED` is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The branch `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` to the absorbing terminal `CANCELLED` is reached through the slot's own cancel endpoint the sound-check cancel capability (with an optional `reason`), which is also the cascade-target of the program-item-cancel chain. The cancel of a `SCHEDULED` slot whose team's `current_booking_id` equals the slot's id is the second link of the depth-three cascade-A chain, releasing the team back to `AVAILABLE` and triggering the team's own audit entry that is the third link. The cancel of an already-terminal slot is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_SOUND_CHECK_NOT_TERMINAL`. The cancel also flips the parent program item's `sound_check_status` axis: if at least one other non-cancelled slot remains under the same item, the axis reverts to `REQUESTED`; otherwise it reverts to `NOT_REQUIRED`. The slot-list endpoint at the sound-checks list capability accepts filters on `status`, on `program_item_id`, and on `sound_engineer_team_id`.

The slot-priority scoring formula on the program item itself is the weighted-sum that ranks `PROPOSED` items competing for similar windows. It is computed on creation and is recomputed on every PATCH that touches the artist reference or the times or the rider count or the artist's `external_commitments`, and the harness can force a fresh recompute through the recompute-priority endpoint without driving any state. The inputs are the artist's `tier` enum (mapping to the four bonuses listed below), the travel-buffer-in-minutes number defined as the gap from the closest preceding external-commitment's `end_time` to the candidate slot's `start_time` in minutes — and then clamped from below at zero (negative gaps, i.e., the commitment hasn't ended yet, contribute zero to the term rather than a negative number) and from above at the constant `TRAVEL_BUFFER_MAX_MINUTES = 1440` (a twenty-four-hour ceiling so the term cannot dominate the sum) — the integer `multi_festival_conflict_count` equal to the count of `external_commitments` whose half-open window intersects the plus-or-minus-twenty-four-hour neighbourhood of the candidate's window, and the integer `rider_count` equal to the count of all riders on the item (blocking or not), clamped from above at the constant `RIDER_CAP = 5` (so a long rider list does not run away with the bonus). The five tier-bonus constants are `HEADLINER_BONUS = 100`, `MAIN_BONUS = 50`, `OPENER_BONUS = 30`, and `SURPRISE_BONUS = 10`; the corresponding weights on the other terms are `TRAVEL_WEIGHT = 0.1` (each travel-buffer minute contributes one tenth of a point), `MULTI_FESTIVAL_CONFLICT_PENALTY = 20` (each external conflict subtracts twenty points), and `RIDER_WEIGHT = 2` (each rider, capped at five, contributes two points). The raw score is the algebraic sum: the matching tier bonus, plus the travel-buffer-minutes times the travel weight, minus the conflict count times the conflict penalty, plus the clamped rider count times the rider weight. The final stored value is the raw score floored at zero (any negative result is clamped up to zero, the non-negative floor `NON_NEGATIVE_FLOOR = 0`) and rounded half up to two decimal places. The canonical illustrative input — a HEADLINER artist with a six-hundred-minute travel buffer (ten hours), one external-commitment conflict in the ±24-hour window, and seven riders on the item — yields the tier bonus 100, the travel term 600 times 0.1 equals 60.0, the conflict term 1 times 20 equals 20 (subtracted), and the rider term `min(7, 5) * 2 = 10` for an unclamped raw of 100 + 60 - 20 + 10 = 150, max with zero is 150, and rounded to two decimals is 150.00. The three independent non-linear pieces — the indicator-function selection over `tier`, the upper-clamp at 1440 on the travel-buffer-minutes term, and the upper-clamp at 5 on the rider count — give the formula the combinatorial structure the scenario calls a weighted scoring rule.

The artist's travel-buffer threshold check — separate from the priority-score's travel term — is the explicit gate that fires on the program-item-create capability (advisorily, where the create still succeeds but the item is then unable to confirm) and authoritatively on the `confirm` transition and on the apply of an `ITEM_RESCHEDULE` schedule change. The gate is "the gap in seconds from the latest preceding external commitment's `end_time` to the candidate's `start_time` is at least the artist's `minimum_travel_buffer_hours` (defaulting to the global `TRAVEL_HOURS_MIN = 4` of four hours when the per-artist override is null) converted to seconds by multiplying by 3600". The boundary is inclusive — equality on the gap is acceptable, strict less-than is the rejection at HTTP 409 with `ERR_ARTIST_TRAVEL_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. The sibling overlap-subset case — where the candidate's half-open window actually intersects one of the artist's external commitments' half-open windows rather than merely lying close to it — is the multi-festival temporal-conflict rejection `ERR_ARTIST_MULTI_FESTIVAL_CONFLICT` at HTTP 409, which the priority-conflict pinning in §2.6 placed behind the cross-entity state check on the program-version axis. The sibling-on-the-self-overlap-of-the-same-artist case is `ERR_ARTIST_SELF_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409 when the candidate intersects another of the same artist's program items in `CONFIRMED`, `ANNOUNCED`, or `ON_STAGE` status in any festival (the visibility across festivals goes through the global artist row's accumulated `external_commitments` array, which the scenario takes to be the canonical cross-festival ledger, plus the system's own per-festival program items for the same artist).

The cascading-side-effects layer is the chain of consequences that fan out from each of the three "originating" triggers the scaffold pins as the test recipes' canonical entry points. The first chain, originating from the apply of a `ScheduleChange` of kind `ITEM_CANCEL` (the apply endpoint hit, see §3.4 for the change-row mechanics), proceeds in three causally-ordered links. Link one is the target program item flipping from its prior status (one of `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `ANNOUNCED`) to the absorbing terminal `CANCELLED`, which is the apply's primary state effect. Link two, caused by the cancel of the program item and not by the apply directly, is every `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` `SoundCheckSlot` whose `program_item_id` is the cancelled item also flipping to its own absorbing terminal `CANCELLED`. The cause-chain semantics is precise here: the slot's cancel is a consequence of the parent program-item's cancel, which is a consequence of the schedule-change's apply, which is the originating trigger. Link three, caused by the slot cancel and not by the program-item cancel directly, is every `SoundEngineerTeam` whose `current_booking_id` was the now-cancelled slot's id flipping back from `BUSY` to `AVAILABLE` with `current_booking_id` cleared to null, and an audit entry on the team row of category `STATE_TRANSITION` recording the team's status flip. The three links are caused strictly in sequence — schedule-change apply causes program-item cancel, program-item cancel causes sound-check cancel, sound-check cancel causes team release — and that strict causation is the depth-three transitive-cascade definition the test recipe asserts on by walking the audit-log entries' `created_at` ordering. A parallel chain that fires alongside the cascade-A chain but is rooted on a different originating trigger — namely a the program-item direct-cancel capability directly, not wrapped in a schedule change — is cascade B: link one is the same program-item cancel writing a row to the audit log, and link two is every `ExposureAllocation` whose `program_item_id` is the cancelled item flipping from `PLANNED` or `DELIVERED` to the absorbing `VOIDED` state, appending a `FulfilmentLog` row of `event_type` `PROGRAM_VOIDED` whose `units` equals the allocation's current `delivered_units` (so the inverse balances cleanly per the rule below), and triggering the parent `SponsorTier`'s `tier_remaining_quota` and `projected_fulfilment_pct` to be recomputed. Cascade B is depth two — the originating cancel is the parent of the allocation void and the parent's parent's audit entry is the side-effect of the cancel itself, not a third causal link in the chain — so cascade B does not by itself reach the depth-three bar that cascade A clears. A third chain, cascade C, originates from a clock-tick at the clock-advance operator capability that crosses the festival's `end_time + FESTIVAL_CLOSE_GRACE_SECONDS` boundary: link one is the festival's status flipping `LIVE → CLOSED`, link two (caused by the festival's close, not by the clock tick directly) is every `BOOKED` or `IN_PROGRESS` rehearsal under the festival auto-archiving to `ARCHIVED` with its synthetic `archive_uri` stamped to the `archive://festival/{festival_id}/rehearsal/{rehearsal_id}` literal, and link three (caused by each rehearsal's archive and not by the festival close directly) is each archived rehearsal's audit entry of category `CLOCK_TICK` whose `payload_summary` names the originating clock-tick event's id, completing the fan-out trace. The three chains live in distinct entity neighbourhoods — cascade A through the sound subsystem, cascade B through the sponsor subsystem, cascade C through the rehearsal subsystem — and the scenario's cascading-side-effects layer is the union of the three. The fan-out from a `Festival`-level cancel (the festival-level hard-cancel capability) is broad rather than deep — every non-terminal child of every kind under the festival is visited in one transaction — and is therefore not counted as a transitive cascade because the originating trigger fan-outs in parallel to many siblings rather than chaining link-by-link.

A schedule change of kind `ITEM_RESCHEDULE` carries a payload of `{new_start_time, new_end_time}` and, on apply, frees the prior interval the target item occupied and runs the full overlap-buffer-travel-multi-festival check stack against the new window as if it were a fresh confirm — the same error ids surface on rejection. A change of kind `STAGE_SWAP` carries `{new_stage_id}` and rotates the item to a new stage, re-running the equipment-wiring check and the changeover-overlap check on the new stage (a swap whose target stage's `equipment_kits` array does not cover the item's `equipment_kit_codes` is rejected with `ERR_STAGE_LACKS_EQUIPMENT_KIT` on the apply). A change of kind `TICKET_NOTIFY` is the no-state-change explicit-fanout variant the harness uses to assert the sync-envelope plumbing without mutating any schedule data — the body's `payload` carries a free-text `message` field and the apply just seeds the four envelopes and writes the audit entry of category `SCHEDULE_CHANGE`. A change of kind `VERSION_PUBLISH` is the auto-emitted variant on the program-version publish transition described in §3.3 and is not normally created by the client directly through the schedule-changes POST. The list endpoint the schedule-changes list capability accepts filters on `status`, on `change_kind`, and on `target_program_item_id`.

### 3.6. The Sponsor Waterfall and the Exposure Fulfilment Ledger

The festival's sponsorship economics live in the `SponsorTier` table — one row per sponsor — and the `ExposureAllocation` table that hangs under it, with the immutable append-only `FulfilmentLog` event-log under each allocation. A `SponsorTier` is created by `SponsorshipAdmin` (or `FestivalProducer`) through the sponsor-tiers POST endpoint. Each tier carries a `tier_code` enum drawn from the three marketing tiers `TITLE` (the apex, named-on-the-festival-marquee tier), `GOLD`, and `SILVER`; a `tier_name` free-text marketing label (such as the literal string "Title — Aurora Communications"); an integer `priority_rank` that is the intra-tier tiebreak (a lower numerical rank wins the next available unit when the pool is short — rank 1 beats rank 2); a free-text `sponsor_external_id` that is the opaque identifier the external sponsor-management system minted for this sponsor (which is the literal that appears as the lexicographic tiebreak when two sponsors in the same tier have identical `priority_rank`, with the lexicographically smaller `sponsor_external_id` winning); a free-text `sponsor_display_name` marketing string; an integer `contracted_units` total that is the across-the-catalog ceiling the sponsor's contract pays for; a `contract_kinds_allowed` array of strings drawn from the four-element subset of the catalog kinds `{stage_name, screen_time, artist_mention, branded_zone}` that the sponsor's contract entitles them to consume units of; and three computed fields — the forward-looking `projected_fulfilment_pct`, the post-event-actual `actual_fulfilment_pct` (null until the post-event sweep stamps it), and the `tier_remaining_quota` which is the `contracted_units` minus the across-tier sum of allocation `delivered_units` and is the conserved quantity the deliver-and-void invariant pins. The tier-list endpoint accepts a `tier_code` filter.

The festival-wide exposure catalog described in §2.8 is the dictionary `{kind: total_units}` that bounds the total disbursable units of each kind across all sponsors. The waterfall is the deterministic allocator that walks the catalog and the allocations and writes the resulting `actual_units` field on each allocation in the canonical order `WATERFALL_TIER_ORDER = ["TITLE", "GOLD", "SILVER"]` — title tier first, gold second, silver third, regardless of any sponsor's `priority_rank` value. Within a tier the ordering is `priority_rank` ascending then `sponsor_external_id` lexicographic ascending. The disbursement is straightforward: for each `kind` in the four-element set, the running `pool_remaining` is initialised to the catalog's `total_units` for that kind, the tier-ordered-then-rank-ordered allocations of that kind that are in `PLANNED` or `DELIVERED` status are stepped through in order, and each allocation's `actual_units` is set to the minimum of its `requested_units` and the current `pool_remaining`, then `pool_remaining` is decremented by the just-disbursed amount. The pool may end the kind's iteration with a positive remainder if total requested is less than the catalog, and an allocation that arrives when the pool is already drained at its position is given a zero `actual_units` (a zero-units row is a valid record that surfaces in the post-event report as a zero-percent delivery line for the under-served sponsor, rather than being rejected outright). The waterfall is run on demand by the sweep endpoint the sponsor-allocation-sweep capability, which is idempotent — running it a second time with no new allocations or no catalog change produces the same `actual_units` vector — and which also writes an audit entry of category `EXPOSURE` whose `payload_summary` carries the per-kind disbursement vector for the test fixture to assert on. The race conditions around the sweep — the advisory-lock-against-fresh-allocation and the same-tier-last-unit race — are the two errors `ERR_CONCURRENT_SWEEP_IN_PROGRESS` and `ERR_TIER_POOL_EXHAUSTED` described in §2.5. The canonical illustrative input for the waterfall is the case where the catalog's `branded_zone` total is 10 units, one Title sponsor requests 5 units (gets 5; pool falls to 5), two Gold sponsors each request 2 units (the lower-`priority_rank` Gold drawn first, both succeed, pool falls 5 - 2 - 2 = 1), and three Silver sponsors each request 1 unit in `priority_rank` ascending order — the first Silver gets the last unit, the other two get zero each because the pool is drained, and the precise outcome that the test asserts is "the first Silver's `actual_units` is exactly 1 and the others' are exactly 0", which a global-priority-rank-only allocator that ignored the tier ordering would not produce.

An `ExposureAllocation` row is created under a sponsor tier through the create endpoint at the exposure-allocation create capability under a sponsor tier. The body carries the `kind` string from the four-kind set, the integer `requested_units` non-negative count of how many catalog units the sponsor is asking for, an optional `program_item_id` if the exposure is tied to a specific item (a stage-name exposure tied to the headliner's set on a specific stage, for example) or null if it is festival-wide (a branded-zone exposure that runs across the whole festival, for example), and an optional `pinned_rate_cents_per_unit` integer in mills (thousandths of a cent) per unit that is the contractual rate at which the sponsor pays the festival per delivered exposure unit. When the rate is null on creation the parent-tier's default `SPONSOR_PAYOUT_RATE_MILS_DEFAULT = 25` (twenty-five mills per unit, equivalent to two-and-a-half cents per unit) applies. The row's initial state is `PLANNED` with the computed counters `actual_units` (initially zero, the next sweep populates it) and `delivered_units` (initially zero, the deliver/void log defines it as a function of the log rows) starting at zero. The status enum is `PLANNED`, `DELIVERED`, `REPORTED`, and `VOIDED`, with `REPORTED` and `VOIDED` the two terminal-absorbing states. The validation cascade on the create is: the referential check that the `kind` is a member of the parent tier's `contract_kinds_allowed` array — when it is not, the generic `ERR_EXPOSURE_KIND_NOT_CONTRACTED` at HTTP 422 fires, unless the offending kind is the literal string `artist_mention` and the parent's allowed set does not contain it, in which case the more specific `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_TIER` at HTTP 422 fires in its place; the cross-festival reference guard on the optional `program_item_id`, which when non-null must reference a program item whose `festival_id` matches the allocation's, otherwise `ERR_EXPOSURE_PROGRAM_ITEM_CROSS_FESTIVAL` at HTTP 422; and the per-tier contract-units cap, which is the sum of the new allocation's `requested_units` plus the tier's existing allocations of the same kind being no greater than the tier's `contracted_units` field, otherwise `ERR_TIER_CONTRACT_UNITS_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 422. The priority-conflict pinning between the referential check (priority 16 in §2.6) and the fiscal-cap check (priority 19) is the one stated above: when both would fail simultaneously, the client sees the referential error (`ERR_ARTIST_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_TIER` on the artist-mention case, `ERR_EXPOSURE_KIND_NOT_CONTRACTED` otherwise) rather than the fiscal error. The successful create writes the row with `status` `PLANNED`, `actual_units` zero, `delivered_units` zero, and an audit entry of category `EXPOSURE`. The list endpoint the festival-wide exposure-allocations list capability is the festival-wide list across all sponsor tiers (cross-tier rather than nested under a specific tier in the URL because the harness needs to walk the whole set in one page), with filters on `status`, on `kind`, and on `sponsor_tier_id`. The detail endpoint the per-allocation detail read returns the allocation joined with its `FulfilmentLog` tail array — the synthetic `ExposureAllocationDetail` shape that nests the log under the row.

The deliver-and-void event-log under an allocation is the append-only `FulfilmentLog` table. Each row carries an `id`, the parent `exposure_allocation_id`, an optional `program_item_id` discriminator (when the delivery happened during a specific program item the field carries that item's id, otherwise it is null), an `event_type` enum from the four values `DELIVERED` (a unit was delivered to the audience), `VOIDED` (a previously-delivered unit was retracted manually through the void-delivery endpoint), `REPORTED` (the closing-tally event written by the post-event sweep on the `DELIVERED → REPORTED` transition, with the `units` field always zero on a `REPORTED` row), and `PROGRAM_VOIDED` (the cascade-from-a-program-item-cancel variant of `VOIDED`, distinguished so the audit reader can tell apart an operator-issued retraction from a parent-cancel cascade), a non-negative integer `units` count that the row adjusts the allocation's `delivered_units` by (the sign comes from the `event_type`, not from a signed integer, so the `units` field on a `VOIDED` row is the positive magnitude of the retraction), a free-text `note`, and the timestamp `created_at`. The allocation's computed `delivered_units` field is the sum of the `units` of `DELIVERED` log rows minus the sum of the `units` of `VOIDED` and `PROGRAM_VOIDED` log rows under the same allocation (the `REPORTED` row's `units` is always zero and contributes nothing). The deliver endpoint the exposure-allocation deliver capability takes a positive-integer `units` body field, an optional free-text `note`, and an optional `If-Match`. Its preconditions are the cross-entity gate that the tied program item's status is `PERFORMED` for an allocation whose `program_item_id` is non-null (otherwise the cross-entity-state error `ERR_EXPOSURE_ALLOCATION_NOT_DELIVERABLE` at HTTP 409 fires), or that the parent festival's status is in the set `LIVE` or `CLOSED` for an allocation whose `program_item_id` is null (the festival-wide exposure case — the audience-area branded zone runs during the festival proper and may be delivered against from the moment the festival goes `LIVE`); the cap check that the sum of all existing `DELIVERED`-log-rows' `units` on this allocation plus the new `units` does not exceed the allocation's `actual_units` (the post-waterfall ceiling) — an overage there is rejected at HTTP 409 with the same `ERR_EXPOSURE_ALLOCATION_NOT_DELIVERABLE` family error or, where the allocation's status is already a terminal `VOIDED` or `REPORTED`, with `ERR_EXPOSURE_ALLOCATION_TERMINAL` at HTTP 409; and the parent-tier-quota gate that the tier's `tier_remaining_quota` is at least the new `units`. On success the endpoint appends a `FulfilmentLog` row of `event_type` `DELIVERED` with the supplied `units` and `note`, recomputes the allocation's `delivered_units` from the log (the running sum described above), recomputes the parent tier's `tier_remaining_quota` and `projected_fulfilment_pct` from the across-allocations aggregate, flips the allocation's status from `PLANNED` to `DELIVERED` on the first non-zero delivery (subsequent deliveries on an already-`DELIVERED` allocation are an explicit self-loop on the state machine — the state name does not change but a fresh `DELIVERED` log row is appended), and writes an audit entry of category `EXPOSURE`. The void-delivery endpoint at the exposure-allocation void-delivery capability is the operator's inverse: the body requires a `reason` string (missing reason returns `ERR_MISSING_VOID_REASON` at HTTP 400) and optionally a `units` integer that defaults to the allocation's current `delivered_units` (the default ensures the inverse cleanly cancels the running total). The endpoint appends a `FulfilmentLog` row of `event_type` `VOIDED` with the supplied or defaulted `units` and the `reason` in the note. The cascade variant — when the cascade-B chain from a program-item cancel reaches the allocation — uses the `event_type` `PROGRAM_VOIDED` instead of the operator-issued `VOIDED`, the discriminator letting the audit reader tell apart the two routes. The void's side effect is the parent tier's recomputation of `tier_remaining_quota` (which returns to its pre-deliver value when the void's `units` matches the prior `DELIVERED` magnitudes — the conservation property the rule pins) and `projected_fulfilment_pct`, the flip of the allocation's status from the current non-terminal state to the absorbing terminal `VOIDED`, and the audit entry. A void on an allocation that is already in a terminal status `VOIDED` or `REPORTED` is rejected at HTTP 409 with `ERR_EXPOSURE_ALLOCATION_TERMINAL`.

The conservation invariant the system asserts across a deliver-then-void round trip on a single allocation is that the parent `SponsorTier`'s `tier_remaining_quota` returns to its pre-deliver value. The mechanic is the `units` field of the `VOIDED` or `PROGRAM_VOIDED` log row matching the magnitude of the prior `DELIVERED` log row, so the running sum that defines `delivered_units` returns to zero (or, in the general case where multiple deliveries preceded the void, returns to its pre-final-deliver value), and the across-tier sum the quota depends on similarly snaps back. The two conserved quantities at the system level — the festival's `outstanding_tickets_total` across an apply-and-revert of an `ITEM_CANCEL` change in §3.4, and the sponsor tier's `tier_remaining_quota` across a deliver-and-void round trip here — sit on different entity domains (the festival and the sponsor tier respectively) and conserve different quantities (the integer count of unrefunded tickets and the integer-units remainder of the sponsor's contractual catalog quota).

The projected-versus-actual fulfilment-percentage dual-variant on the sponsor domain is the second piece. The projected `projected_fulfilment_pct` field on the `SponsorTier` row is the forward-looking percentage computed eagerly on every relevant mutation — a program-item state change that affects whether a tied allocation counts, an exposure-allocation creation or void, and the explicit synchronous sweep. The numerator is the sum across the tier's allocations of each allocation's projected contribution. An allocation tied to a specific program item (non-null `program_item_id`) contributes its full `actual_units` when the tied item's status is in the "will-or-did-happen" set `CONFIRMED`, `ANNOUNCED`, `ON_STAGE`, or `PERFORMED`, and contributes zero when the tied item's status is the terminal `CANCELLED`. An allocation that is festival-wide (null `program_item_id`) contributes the product of its `actual_units` and the window-fraction-remaining number defined in §2.7, the fraction being 1.0 when the festival has not yet started, falling linearly toward 0.0 as the logical wall clock crosses the festival window, and clamped at the endpoints. The denominator is the tier's `contracted_units`, the numerator-over-denominator times 100 is the percentage, and the final number is rounded half up to the two-decimal-place precision `PERCENT_PRECISION = 2`. The actual `actual_fulfilment_pct` field is null while the festival is still open and is stamped only by the post-event sweep the post-event sweep operator capability while the festival is `CLOSED`. The actual computation walks the immutable `FulfilmentLog` event stream rather than the live schedule: for each allocation the sweep computes its `delivered_units` as the standard running difference, the across-the-tier sum is the numerator, the denominator is again `contracted_units`, the percentage is the same percentage formula, and the rounding rule is the same half-up to two decimals. The two columns can legitimately diverge. The canonical illustrative case is a `GOLD` sponsor tier whose `contracted_units` is 10, with 8 of those tied to specific program items that all transition into the "will-happen" set ahead of the festival and 2 that are tied to a festival-wide branded-zone allocation whose `window_fraction_remaining` is 1.0 at projection time (because the festival has not yet started), so the projected numerator is 10 and the projected percentage is 100.00 — though if one of the eight tied items is day-of cancelled, the post-event actual numerator is 9 and the actual percentage is 90.00, the divergence between the two columns being the observable mark of the dual variant. More precisely on the scenario's canonical literal: 8 of the 10 contracted units are tied to confirmed items and 2 are festival-wide. The projected numerator is 8 + 2 × 1.0 = 10 — but the worked example collapses to a simpler form by taking the 2-unit festival-wide piece as not-yet-counted-into-its-tied-items, so the projected numerator picks up only the 8 confirmed-tie units' contribution plus a fractional-window 2-unit contribution, summing to 8 when the window has not yet opened on the festival-wide piece (the difficulty design's specific arithmetic on the projection-vs-actual divergence is the worked example the harness tests against). The salient property the test asserts is "projected and actual are different non-zero numbers on the same `SponsorTier` row, and the actual is derived strictly from the `FulfilmentLog` stream while the projected is derived strictly from the live program-item state".

The sponsor payout total in cents is the per-tier financial line the festival owes the sponsor at festival close, computed on the post-event sweep's `compute_payout` flag (when the body's flag is true on the post-event sweep operator capability) and surfaced through the read endpoint the per-tier fulfilment-report read's `sponsor_payout_total_cents` field. The formula is the across-the-tier sum of each `DELIVERED`-or-`REPORTED` `ExposureAllocation`'s `delivered_units` times its `pinned_rate_cents_per_unit` (in mills, the thousandths-of-a-cent unit, defaulting to `SPONSOR_PAYOUT_RATE_MILS_DEFAULT = 25` when the field is null), with the integer-multiplication kept exact in the unbounded-precision intermediate, the sum in mills divided by 1000 at the very end of the sum to convert mills to cents, and the single quotient rounded half up to integer cents. The rounding-order is pinned as "sum the mills across all lines first, then divide once at the end, then round once at the end" rather than "round each line's mills-to-cents conversion first and then sum the rounded cents", and the two orderings can diverge by exactly one cent on the canonical input vector of three exposure-allocation lines each carrying one delivered unit at the default rate of twenty-five mills per unit. Under the pinned order the unrounded total is the sum of the three lines' mills — one plus one plus one — multiplied by the rate of twenty-five mills per unit, with that sum-of-mills divided once by one thousand to convert to cents, which gives the rational number 0.075 cents that rounds half up at the single final rounding step to 0.08 cents — and on the integer-cents output convention the implementation reports this as 8 cents (the half-up rule on the 0.005-cents-fraction boundary picks the upper integer). Under the alternative per-line rounding, each line's one-unit-times-twenty-five-mills-divided-by-one-thousand is 0.025 cents, which rounds half up to 0.03 cents per line; the three rounded-per-line figures sum to 0.09 cents and the integer-cents output is 9 cents. The system pins 8 as the right answer, the test fixture asserts the literal 8, and any implementation that does per-line rounding fails the assertion. The mills-to-cents step is the single floating-point-equivalent step in the entire calculation surface, and the implementation does the arithmetic in exact-decimal precision to avoid any binary-floating-point drift on the 0.005-cents-fraction boundary. The post-event sweep also writes a closing `REPORTED` log row on each `DELIVERED` allocation, flipping the allocation's state from `DELIVERED` to the terminal `REPORTED`, and the multiple-invocation idempotency of the sweep (multiple the post-event sweep operator capability calls after the festival has closed) is the property that the `REPORTED` row's append is a one-time effect per allocation — a sweep that finds an allocation already in `REPORTED` is a no-op on that allocation.

### 3.7. The Audit Log and the Read-Only Reporting Surface

Every state-changing operation in the system writes a row to the `AuditEntry` append-only table for the parent festival. The audit row's `event_category` enum is exactly the six-element set described in §2.8, the `subject_entity_type` is the string class-name of the entity the operation targeted, the `subject_entity_id` is that row's `id`, the `from_state` and `to_state` fields hold the prior and posterior status enum values for state-transition categories and are null for the categories without a state-change semantic (`SYNC_ACK`, `CLOCK_TICK` when the tick did not itself transition a row, and `EXPOSURE` on the sweep), and the `payload_summary` is the short human-readable English string that the implementation composes per the scenario's cascade-cross-references the audit log on. The `actor_user_id` is the `X-User-ID` of the operator who originated the call, with the literal string `"system"` standing in for the system-actor of the cascade hops, the clock-tick-driven auto-transitions, and the sweep. The `actor_role` mirrors `X-User-Role` on the originating call, again with `"system"` for system-actors. The audit rows are ordered strictly by `created_at` per festival and the audit-list endpoint returns them in that order; the list endpoint's filters are on `event_category`, on `subject_entity_type`, and on a `created_at_after` ISO 8601 UTC timestamp string. The audit-log read is gated to the audit-viewer allowlist of `AuditViewer`, `FestivalProducer`, and `PlatformAdmin`, returning `ERR_AUDIT_LOG_FORBIDDEN` to any other role per §2.1.

The post-event sponsor-fulfilment report is the synthetic view at the per-tier fulfilment-report read, open to `SponsorshipAdmin`, `FestivalProducer`, and `AuditViewer`. The shape returned in the `data` envelope of the success response is the synthetic `FulfilmentReportView` whose fields are the tier's `projected_fulfilment_pct`, the tier's `actual_fulfilment_pct` (null while the festival is still open, populated by the post-event sweep), an array of per-allocation `delivered_units` figures keyed by `exposure_allocation_id`, the tier's `tier_remaining_quota` snapshot, and the `sponsor_payout_total_cents` integer computed by the sweep when the sweep was invoked with `compute_payout = true`. The report is read-only — there is no PATCH on it — and the values are the live derived numbers from the underlying entities and the immutable log stream.

The clock-state read at the clock-state read capability, open to `PlatformAdmin` and `AuditViewer`, returns the synthetic `ClockView` with the per-festival `clock_offset_seconds` and the implied logical-wall-clock-of-record. The companion clock-advance endpoint at the clock-advance operator capability and the auxiliary read are the only two surfaces that touch the per-festival `clock_offset_seconds` field. The two sync-event ingress endpoints the external-system sync-acknowledgement ingress capability and the external-ticketing-system refund-confirmed ingress capability and the sweep endpoint the post-event sweep operator capability round out the operator-only surface that the harness drives the system from. The four-target sync-envelope status semantics, the refund-magnitude check, and the festival-close-frozen-counters rule all live on those three endpoints and have been described in §2.8 and §3.4.

The hard cap on the number of program items per festival is the global constant `MAX_PROGRAM_ITEMS_PER_FESTIVAL = 500`, beyond which a fresh program-item create is rejected as a malformed-input case (the catch-all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_BODY` family) — the cap exists to bound the input space the test harness can drive in a single transaction and is not normally hit by any real-world festival in the scenario's scope.

Accepts an `idempotency_key`. | `FestivalProducer` |
| `GET` | `/festivals/{festivalId}/schedule-changes` | List schedule changes, filterable by `status`, `change_kind`, and `target_program_item_id`. | `Any` |

Flips the ticketing envelope element to `ACKNOWLEDGED`. | `PlatformAdmin` |
| `POST` | `/_sweep/run` | Run the post-event sweep across the festival. Pre-close it refreshes projected percentages only. Post-close it flips every `DELIVERED` allocation to `REPORTED` with a closing `REPORTED` log row, stamps the actual percentages, recomputes the `tier_remaining_quota` figures, and — when the body's `compute_payout` is true — computes the per-tier `sponsor_payout_total_cents`. Idempotent. | `PlatformAdmin`, `SponsorshipAdmin` |
