# Certificate Authority Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A publicly trusted certificate authority lives or dies by its ability to prove, months after the fact, that every certificate it signed was preceded by a complete and correctly sequenced chain of controls: a policy profile that was in force, an applicant whose organization was independently vetted, domain control that was demonstrated by an approved technique inside a still-valid reuse window, a key ceremony that two auditors witnessed and a third party attested, and a transparency record that the public can inspect. The Certificate Authority Operations API is the system of record for that chain. It is not a signing library; it is the control plane that decides whether signing is permitted at all, and it refuses issuance the moment any one of the five gating aggregates — profile posture, subscriber account standing, organizational vetting, domain-authorization freshness, and hardware-security-module attestation — falls out of compliance.

The second half of the mandate is the part that browser root programs actually audit: what happens after something goes wrong. Revocation in this system is not a flag on a row. Every executed revocation enters a conserved queue attached to its certificate profile, and that queue must be drained exactly — never partially, never twice — by a certificate revocation list publication whose validity window overlaps its predecessor. A profile whose queue grows past its own configured trigger stops issuing entirely, and resumes only when the list is published. Misissuance incidents carry a hard disclosure clock, mass revocation runs in deterministic batches with a per-certificate remediation penalty, and the annual audit against each browser root program can retire the CA's entire extended-validation product line with a single adverse opinion.

Every mutating operation in this specification is therefore written twice: once as the business act an operator performs, and once as the set of counters, timestamps and dependent states that act is obliged to move. Implementers should read the two as inseparable. All business resources are mounted under `/ca`, and a single test-driving endpoint under `/_clock` controls the server's logical clock, because a compliance system whose deadlines depend on wall-clock time cannot be verified deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, resource identity, concurrency, precision, and the order in which failures are reported.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the individual human operator performing the act, and `X-User-Role` declares the operational capacity that operator is acting in. Six roles exist, spelled exactly: `POLICY_ADMIN`, `VALIDATION_OFFICER`, `VETTING_APPROVER`, `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR`, `REVOCATION_OFFICER`, and `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR`.

`POLICY_ADMIN` owns the CA's policy objects — certificate profiles, subscriber accounts, and the hardware-security-module partition inventory — and never touches an individual issuance, revocation or audit. `VALIDATION_OFFICER` performs domain control validation and the three organizational vetting checks, and drives a certificate request from creation through validation clearance; it may never approve the vetting file it checked nor issue the certificate it cleared. `VETTING_APPROVER` is the sole approver of organizational vetting files and holds no other write capability, which is precisely what makes the vetting segregation-of-duties rule enforceable by user identity rather than by role. `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` runs the issuance systems: it schedules and aborts key ceremony windows, issues certificates under an attested ceremony, submits pre-certificates to transparency logs, and activates certificates. `REVOCATION_OFFICER` owns revocation and the status services: it raises, executes and denies revocation requests, publishes revocation lists, and runs mass-revocation batches for disclosed incidents. `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` attests key ceremonies and repudiates attestations, manages misissuance incidents and root program correspondence, and runs the annual root program audit; it may not attest a ceremony it witnessed. Witnessing a key ceremony is the one write capability shared between two roles: both `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` and `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` may record themselves as a witness. Advancing the logical clock and every read (`GET`) endpoint are available to all six roles, so that a client may read back any state regardless of the capacity it is acting in.

Identity semantics are uniform across all six roles: `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier and is never compared against any persisted entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `POLICY_ADMIN`, `VALIDATION_OFFICER`, `VETTING_APPROVER`, `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR`, `REVOCATION_OFFICER` or `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR`, the server may record the supplied `X-User-ID` on the resource the act touched — as `registry_checked_by_user_id`, `callback_checked_by_user_id`, `authority_checked_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `validation_cleared_by_user_id`, `attested_by_user_id`, an entry of `witness_user_ids`, `requested_by_user_id`, `published_by_user_id`, `opined_by_user_id` or `sent_by_user_id` — and it may compare that recorded value against the `X-User-ID` of a later act to enforce segregation of duties, but it never resolves it to a subscriber, an operator record, or any other stored resource. There is no ownership filtering in this API: a `VALIDATION_OFFICER` may act on any subscriber's request, and role membership alone determines authorization.

Header enforcement is unconditional and precedes everything else. A header whose value is present but consists only of whitespace counts as empty, and both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before they are matched or recorded. A request missing or empty `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_HEADER` (HTTP 401) on every endpoint except the read endpoints, which do not require it. A request missing or empty `X-User-Role` is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER` (HTTP 401) on every endpoint without exception. A role string that is not one of the six names above is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401), and a syntactically valid role that is simply not permitted on the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Values outside those bounds are silently clamped rather than rejected — a `limit` below 1 is raised to 1, a `limit` above 100 is lowered to 100, and a negative `offset` becomes 0 — and a value that cannot be parsed as an integer falls back to that parameter's default, so no list endpoint ever answers with a pagination error. When a list endpoint responds, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`; `total_count` reports the size of the filtered result set before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true whenever unreturned rows remain after the current window.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by the order in which the records were created (a server-internal insertion sequence) and never by `id`. Three collections deviate because their domain order is normative rather than chronological: a profile's revocation-list publications are returned in ascending `crl_number` order, a partition's key ceremonies in ascending `window_start` order, and root program communications in ascending `sent_at` order. A certificate's transparency-log submissions are returned in ascending `created_at` order.

Filtering is additive: every filter parameter a collection accepts narrows the set, and combining two filters applies both. Each filter parameter is named identically to the record field it tests and its value is compared as an exact string against that field, so an unrecognised value simply yields an empty page rather than an error; a parameter supplied empty is ignored altogether. The one boolean-valued filter, `disclosure_breached`, accepts `true`, `1` or `yes` for true and `false`, `0` or `no` for false, case-insensitively and after trimming, and any other spelling is ignored rather than rejected. Certificate profiles may be filtered by `status`, `validation_type` and `status_service_posture`. Subscribers may be filtered by `account_status` and `jurisdiction_code`. A subscriber's vetting files may be filtered by `vetting_state`. Domain authorizations may be filtered by `subscriber_id`, `profile_id`, `domain_name`, `state` and `method`. Certificate requests may be filtered by `subscriber_id`, `profile_id`, `issued_certificate_id`, `state` and `blocked_reason`. Certificates may be filtered by `subscriber_id`, `profile_id`, `request_id`, `ceremony_id`, `supersedes_certificate_id`, `lifecycle_status` and `revocation_status`. Hardware-security-module partitions and their ceremonies may be filtered by `state`. Transparency-log submissions may be filtered by `outcome`. Revocation requests may be filtered by `certificate_id`, `incident_id`, `state`, `origin` and `reason_code`. Misissuance incidents may be filtered by `subscriber_id`, `scope_profile_id`, `state`, `category` and `disclosure_breached`. Root program audits may be filtered by `root_program` and `state`, and the communications attached to an incident or an audit may be filtered by `kind`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every resource in this system is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat them as opaque. Alongside `id`, every resource carries a `created_at` timestamp, and every resource that can be modified after creation additionally carries `updated_at` and `version`. All timestamps — `created_at`, `updated_at`, and every domain timestamp such as `validated_at`, `not_after` or `disclosure_due_at` — are ISO 8601 instants in UTC rendered with exactly three fractional digits. A timestamp arriving in a request body must be a string parseable as an ISO 8601 instant, where a trailing `Z` or an explicit UTC offset is accepted and a value carrying no offset at all is read as UTC; anything else is rejected as a malformed field with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

`version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly by a client or applied indirectly by a cascade. `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock on the same occasions. A cascade that touches five certificates increments five `version` counters, not one; a cascade that touches a resource twice within the handling of a single request increments it twice only if it genuinely mutated it twice. Two consequences of that rule are observable. A recomputation that lands on the value the resource already carried is not a mutation at all: recomputing a subscriber's `risk_score_operational` to the number it already held leaves both `version` and `updated_at` untouched. Conversely, a single act that first moves a resource's counters and then, as a consequence of those counters, changes one of its states does mutate it twice — an executed revocation that both moves a profile's revocation counters and flips that profile's `status_service_posture` raises the profile's `version` by two, and a revocation that both moves a subscriber's `revocation_pending_count` and suspends the account raises the subscriber's `version` by two.

Three record types in this system are append-only audit artifacts and are never modified after creation. A transparency-log submission (`CtLogSubmission`), a revocation-list publication (`CrlPublication`) and a root program communication (`RootProgramCommunication`) therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations entirely. Every other resource — certificate profiles, subscribers, organizational vettings, domain authorizations, certificate requests, certificates, partitions, key ceremonies, revocation requests, misissuance incidents and root program audits — carries the full set of four.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Certificates and Profiles

Two write operations in this API are protected by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a second, later write from a client working off a stale read would succeed on business grounds and silently destroy information.

The first is raising a revocation request. That operation requires the non-optional input field `expected_certificate_version`, carrying the `version` of the target certificate as the client last observed it. If the value does not equal the certificate's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a newer revocation request legitimately supersedes any prior request still in `REQUESTED` state for the same certificate, so no state precondition would stop the second create; the version check is the only thing that makes a stale writer observable and deterministic.

The second is publishing a revocation list for a profile. That operation requires the non-optional input field `expected_profile_version`, carrying the `version` of the target certificate profile as the client last observed it. A mismatch against the profile's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROFILE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each publication legitimately supersedes the profile's previous list and drains whatever is pending at the instant it runs, so a publication issued from a stale view would otherwise succeed and publish a list omitting revocations recorded in between.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one has no defined effect.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and its Three Advancement Sweeps

Time in this system is explicit. The server holds a logical clock that starts at the fixed default `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to wall-clock time. Every timestamp the server writes, and every deadline comparison it makes, reads that clock. The clock may only move forward: a submitted `current_utc` earlier than the server's current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), while a `current_utc` exactly equal to the current clock is accepted and is a no-op advance. Advancing the clock is available to all six roles.

Each successful advance runs three sweeps, in this exact order. The endpoint answers 200 with an empty `data` object — it returns no clock resource — and within each sweep the affected records are visited in ascending `created_at` order.

Sweep one expires reusable domain control validations: every domain authorization in state `VALIDATED` whose `expires_at` is at or before the new clock moves to `EXPIRED`, and its `version` increments. The comparison is inclusive, so an authorization whose `expires_at` is exactly the new clock **is** expired — the reuse window is half-open.

Sweep two expires certificates: every certificate whose `lifecycle_status` is `PREISSUANCE` or `ISSUED` and whose `not_after` is at or before the new clock moves to `lifecycle_status` `EXPIRED`, and its `version` increments. This sweep is entirely independent of the certificate's `revocation_status`; an already `REVOKED` certificate still ages into `EXPIRED`, and a certificate that was never activated ages out of `PREISSUANCE` directly.

Sweep three flags missed disclosure deadlines: every misissuance incident still in state `OPEN` whose `disclosure_due_at` is strictly earlier than the new clock has `disclosure_breached` set to true, and its `version` increments. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the first two sweeps: this comparison is strict, so an incident whose `disclosure_due_at` is exactly the new clock is **not** breached, whereas an authorization or certificate sitting exactly on its boundary **is** expired. Once set, `disclosure_breached` is never cleared, not even by a later disclosure.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties Across Three Duty Pairs

This CA operates under mandatory segregation of duties, enforced by `X-User-ID` rather than by role, so that a single person holding two roles cannot self-approve. Three duty pairs are enforced.

First, the operator who recorded any vetting check may not approve that vetting file. At approval time the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against every non-null value among `registry_checked_by_user_id`, `callback_checked_by_user_id` and `authority_checked_by_user_id`; a match on any one of them is rejected with `ERR_VETTING_SOD_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Second, the operator who cleared validation on a certificate request may not issue the resulting certificate: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the request's `validation_cleared_by_user_id`, issuance is refused with `ERR_ISSUANCE_SOD_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). Third, an operator who witnessed a key ceremony may not attest it: if the acting `X-User-ID` appears anywhere in that ceremony's `witness_user_ids`, attestation is refused with `ERR_ATTESTER_WAS_WITNESS` (HTTP 409). A related uniqueness rule guards the witness quorum itself — a user already present in `witness_user_ids` who tries to witness again is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WITNESS` (HTTP 409), so a quorum of two always means two distinct people.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence: One Global, Strictly Ordered Ladder

When a single request violates several rules at once, the server reports exactly one error: the one belonging to the highest-precedence violated rule, and it stops there. Precedence is global across the whole API, not per endpoint, and it runs in the following bands from highest to lowest.

1. **Authentication and authorization.** `ERR_MISSING_USER_HEADER`, then `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER`, then `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, then `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, in that order.
2. **Request schema.** First, every required input field must be present and of the declared JSON type, or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — this applies to every creating and modifying endpoint. A request body that is absent, empty or unparseable is treated as an empty JSON object, so its required fields report as missing under this rule, whereas a body that parses to something other than a JSON object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an optional field supplied as explicit `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and takes its default. A boolean-typed input supplied as a non-boolean, and an integer-typed input supplied as a boolean or a non-integer, are both `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than range errors. Then every enum-typed input must hold one of the declared values, or `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (HTTP 422). Then integer inputs must lie inside their declared ranges, or `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422); this range check is exercised when creating a certificate profile, registering a subscriber, creating a certificate request, and scheduling a key ceremony. Finally array-valued inputs must satisfy their content rules, or `ERR_INVALID_ARRAY_CONTENT` (HTTP 422); this applies when creating a certificate request and when creating a certificate profile.
3. **Existence, resolved left to right.** Path identifiers are resolved before body identifiers, and within each group in the order they appear. The catalogue is `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VETTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404, and that listed order is also their relative precedence when two of them could fire on the same request. A profile referenced through `profile_id` or `scope_profile_id`, a subscriber referenced through `subscriber_id`, a certificate referenced through `certificate_id` or `supersedes_certificate_id`, a ceremony referenced through `ceremony_id` and an incident referenced through `incident_id` are all checked under this band exactly as if they had been path segments. Consequently a request naming both a nonexistent profile and a nonexistent subscriber reports the profile error.
4. **Optimistic concurrency.** `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATE_VERSION` on raising a revocation request, then `ERR_STALE_PROFILE_VERSION` on publishing a revocation list. Both sit above every state precondition, so a stale version is reported even when the target is also in the wrong state.
5. **State preconditions.** This is the largest band. One entry deserves special attention: on certificate activation, the check that the certificate's `not_after` is strictly after the logical clock (`ERR_CERTIFICATE_ALREADY_EXPIRED`) is deliberately evaluated **before** the check that its `lifecycle_status` is `PREISSUANCE` (`ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_PREISSUANCE`). Because sweep two flips a `PREISSUANCE` certificate to `EXPIRED` the moment its `not_after` is reached, the more specific expiry error must win; `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_PREISSUANCE` remains reachable by trying to activate a certificate that is already `ISSUED`. One further local exception applies on revocation-request execution: there the request's own `ERR_REVOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN` is evaluated first, ahead of both `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED_NOT_REVOCABLE` and the target certificate's `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_REVOCABLE`, so an already-executed or denied request reports its own state rather than the certificate's. The remainder of the band, in precedence order, is `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PROFILE_STATUS_SERVICE_OVERDUE`, `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_VETTING_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_VETTING_NOT_AWAITING_APPROVAL`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_VALIDATABLE`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_VALIDATED`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_REJECTABLE`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_READY`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_PREISSUANCE`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_REVOCABLE`, `ERR_REVOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_PARTITION_QUARANTINED`, `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_WITNESSED`, `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_ATTESTED`, `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_ABORTABLE`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_DISCLOSED`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_CLOSEABLE`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_FINDINGS`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_IN_FIELDWORK` and `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_OPINED`.
6. **Segregation of duties and ceremony compliance.** `ERR_VETTING_SOD_VIOLATION`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_WITNESS`, then `ERR_ATTESTER_WAS_WITNESS`, then `ERR_CEREMONY_WINDOW_OPEN`, then `ERR_ISSUANCE_SOD_VIOLATION`. All of these rank below the state band, so a wrong-state target is reported before a duty conflict.
7. **Issuance compliance gates.** `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_ATTESTED_FOR_ISSUANCE`, then `ERR_CEREMONY_ATTESTATION_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_DCV_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED`, then the validation-clearance chain in this exact order: `ERR_DOMAIN_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`, `ERR_DOMAIN_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_DCV_REUSE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_ORG_VETTING_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ORG_VETTING_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_ORG_VETTING_REUSE_EXPIRED`, `ERR_CSR_KEY_TOO_WEAK`.
8. **Compliance interlocks.** `ERR_INCIDENT_DISCLOSURE_BREACHED`, then `ERR_CASCADE_REVOCATION_NOT_DENIABLE`, then `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED_NOT_REVOCABLE`.
9. **Uniqueness.** `ERR_PROFILE_NAME_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_REGISTRY_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_PARTITION_SERIAL_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_VETTING_ALREADY_OPEN`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_ALREADY_OPEN`.
10. **Temporal geometry.** `ERR_INVALID_CEREMONY_WINDOW`, `ERR_CEREMONY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_INVALID_CRL_WINDOW`, `ERR_CRL_COVERAGE_GAP`, `ERR_CRL_WINDOW_NOT_ADVANCING`, `ERR_CRL_VALIDITY_TOO_LONG`, `ERR_INVALID_SCOPE_WINDOW`, `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_PERIOD`, `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_OPEN`, `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`. Note that on a revocation-list publication the plain window sanity check outranks the coverage-overlap check, which outranks the series-advance check, which outranks the maximum-validity-span check.
11. **Referential coherence.** `ERR_SUPERSEDES_SUBSCRIBER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SCOPE_METHOD_NOT_IN_PROFILE`, `ERR_INCIDENT_CERTIFICATE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_MISMATCH`.
12. **Capacity and aggregate constraints.** `ERR_NO_CERTIFICATES_IN_SCOPE`, then `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SCTS`, then `ERR_INCIDENT_REVOCATIONS_OUTSTANDING`, then `ERR_AUDIT_NO_FINDINGS`.
13. **Fiscal.** `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) is the very last check in the system. On issuance it is evaluated only after every compliance gate above has passed, so a request that is both non-compliant and over its credit limit reports the compliance failure.

### 2.9. Precision, Rounding and the Conserved Money Ledger

All money in this system is expressed in integer cents and never as a decimal fraction of a currency unit. `base_fee_cents`, `credit_limit_cents`, `outstanding_balance_cents`, `projected_charge_cents`, `charged_amount_cents` and `penalty_total_cents` are all integers. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent — that is, a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, upward. The specific steps at which rounding is applied are pinned per calculation and stated in §3; rounding at a different step yields a different, incorrect answer, so implementers must follow the stated order rather than rounding only at the end.

One non-monetary quantity is a decimal number rather than an integer: `risk_score_audit_final` is carried with exactly two decimal places, produced by rounding half up once over the summed terms, never per term. Its operational sibling `risk_score_operational` is a plain integer. Timestamp arithmetic throughout the system is performed at whole-second resolution, and day-, hour- and minute-valued constants are converted to seconds before comparison.

The subscriber's `outstanding_balance_cents` is a conserved ledger, not a free-form counter. It is defined at all times as the sum of `charged_amount_cents` over that subscriber's certificates whose `refunded_at` is null. Issuance adds a charge to it; the one compensating event in the system removes a charge from it. Nothing else may move it, and the recorded balance must always equal that sum recomputed from the certificates.

### 2.10. The Revocation Conservation Invariant and Status-Service Posture

Each certificate profile maintains three counters over its own revocations, and they are bound by an invariant that must hold before and after every publication: `total_crl_entries_published` plus `pending_crl_entry_count` always equals `revoked_certificate_count`. A revocation raises `revoked_certificate_count` and `pending_crl_entry_count` together by one; a publication moves whatever is pending into `total_crl_entries_published` and resets `pending_crl_entry_count` to zero. No path in the system may drop a revocation from the queue without publishing it, and no publication may carry the same certificate twice — a certificate's `crl_published_at` and `crl_number` are stamped by the first list that carries its serial and are never rewritten.

Layered on top of that ledger is the profile's `status_service_posture`, a second, independent state dimension distinct from `status`. Its two values are `NOMINAL` and `CRL_OVERDUE`. When an executed revocation drives `pending_crl_entry_count` to a value at or above the profile's own `crl_trigger_threshold` while the posture is `NOMINAL`, the posture flips to `CRL_OVERDUE`. The comparison is inclusive of the threshold, so a profile configured with a threshold of 1 flips on its very first unpublished revocation. A `CRL_OVERDUE` posture blocks issuance on that profile, and the flip immediately propagates: every certificate request of that profile sitting in `READY_TO_ISSUE` moves to `ISSUANCE_BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `PROFILE_STATUS_SERVICE_OVERDUE`. When a publication drains the queue so that `pending_crl_entry_count` is zero, the posture returns to `NOMINAL` and every `ISSUANCE_BLOCKED` request of that profile returns to `READY_TO_ISSUE` with `blocked_reason` cleared to null. The cycle may repeat any number of times.

### 2.11. Deterministic Ordering of Cascades and Batches

Because a single request in this system can mutate dozens of rows, every multi-row effect has a pinned processing order, and implementations must follow it because the resulting counters and identifiers are observable.

Requests released or blocked by a profile posture change, and requests rolled back by a domain-authorization invalidation, are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Certificates pushed into pending revocation by an attestation repudiation are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Mass-revocation candidates are processed in ascending `created_at` order with ties broken by ascending `serial_number`. Revocation-list entries are ordered by ascending `revoked_at` with ties broken by ascending `serial_number`. In none of these orderings does a resource's `id` participate — identifiers are UUIDs and carry no order.

Two sequences in this system are monotonic and deterministic rather than random. A certificate's `serial_number` is a decimal string: the first certificate ever issued receives `"1000001"`, and each subsequent issuance receives the previous serial plus one. A profile's `crl_number` is a per-profile integer sequence whose first publication is 1 and which advances by one on every publication, including a publication that drains nothing.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The subsections below describe the CA's operational workflows in the order an operator lives them: policy first, then the applicant, then the two independent validation tracks, then the request pipeline, the signing infrastructure, issuance, revocation, and finally the compliance machinery that watches all of it.

### 3.1. Certificate Profiles: The Policy That Is in Force

Nothing in this system can happen without a **certificate profile**, the policy object that fixes what a class of certificates is allowed to be. A `POLICY_ADMIN` drafts a profile by supplying a `name` of between 1 and 80 characters that is unique across all profiles after case-folding — a second profile whose name differs only in letter case is rejected with `ERR_PROFILE_NAME_DUPLICATE` — together with a `validation_type` of `DV`, `OV` or `EV`, which is the validation tier the profile issues under and the single most consequential choice on the record.

The remaining policy inputs are all numeric or enumerated. `max_validity_days` is a hard cap on certificate lifetime, between 1 and 398 days. `dcv_reuse_days`, also between 1 and 398, is how long a successful domain control validation may be reused before it must be redone. `max_dcv_reuse_count`, between 1 and 50, is how many certificate requests a single validated domain authorization may be consumed by. `org_vetting_reuse_days` is the organizational vetting reuse period; it is the one policy input that may be omitted, defaulting to 0, and its permitted range depends on the tier: it must be exactly 0 for a `DV` profile, where organizational vetting is not performed at all, and between 1 and 825 for an `OV` or `EV` profile; violating either bound is an out-of-range input, so an `OV` or `EV` profile that omits the field fails on the default 0 with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. `allowed_dcv_methods` enumerates the approved domain control validation techniques for the profile as a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `DNS_TXT`, `HTTP_TOKEN`, `EMAIL_APPROVAL` and `TLS_ALPN`, so it holds between one and four distinct methods. `required_sct_count`, between 0 and 5, is the minimum number of distinct certificate transparency logs that must have accepted a certificate before it may be activated. `base_fee_cents`, between 1 and 10,000,000, is the per-domain base issuance fee before the tier multiplier. `crl_trigger_threshold`, between 1 and 1000, is the number of unpublished revocations that flips the profile's revocation-status-service posture. `max_crl_validity_hours`, between 1 and 240, caps how long a single published revocation list may claim to be valid. When two of these inputs are simultaneously out of range the one reported is the first in this evaluation order: `max_validity_days`, `dcv_reuse_days`, `max_dcv_reuse_count`, `org_vetting_reuse_days`, `required_sct_count`, `base_fee_cents`, `crl_trigger_threshold`, `max_crl_validity_hours`. Because the enum band outranks the range band, a member of `allowed_dcv_methods` that is not one of the four approved technique names is reported as `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` before any range or array-content rule is considered, while an empty or duplicate-bearing list survives to the array-content rule and reports `ERR_INVALID_ARRAY_CONTENT`.

Two fields on a freshly drafted profile are derived rather than supplied. `required_vetting_level` is computed directly from the tier: a `DV` profile requires `NONE`, an `OV` profile requires `OV`, and an `EV` profile requires `EV`. `status_service_posture` starts at `NOMINAL`. The four ledger counters `pending_crl_entry_count`, `revoked_certificate_count`, `total_crl_entries_published` and `last_crl_number` all start at 0, while `last_crl_this_update` and `last_crl_next_update` start null because no list has been published; `activated_at` and `retired_at` are likewise null.

A new profile is born in `status` `DRAFT` and is inert. Activation by a `POLICY_ADMIN` moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`; the profile must actually be in `DRAFT` (a second activation attempt earns `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_DRAFT`), and the tier consistency condition must hold, meaning the profile is either a `DV` profile or one whose `org_vetting_reuse_days` is at least 1 — a condition the creation-time range check already guarantees. Activation stamps `activated_at` with the logical clock and increments the profile's `version`. Only an `ACTIVE` profile may be named by any operational act: opening a domain authorization, validating one, submitting a certificate request, clearing its validation, issuing from it, or publishing a revocation list for it. All six of those operations reject a `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` profile with `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Retirement is never requested by a human. A profile leaves `ACTIVE` for the terminal `RETIRED` state only as a consequence of an adverse annual audit opinion, and then only if its `validation_type` is `EV`: extended-validation issuance is suspended wholesale while `DV` and `OV` profiles continue untouched. That cascade stamps `retired_at` with the logical clock, increments the profile's `version`, and sets the opining audit's `retired_profile_count` to the number of profiles it retired. Retirement never rolls the profile's revocation counters, CRL series state or `status_service_posture` back; a `RETIRED` profile keeps every number it had.

### 3.2. Subscribers: Accounts, Credit and Account Posture

A **subscriber** is the applicant organization that certificates are issued to. A `POLICY_ADMIN` registers one with its `legal_name` (1 to 160 characters), the `jurisdiction_code` of incorporation used for registry verification (2 to 6 uppercase characters), the `registry_id` the applicant asserts in that registry (1 to 40 characters), a `contact_email` of at least 1 character with no upper bound that must contain an at-sign and which is the address used for `EMAIL_APPROVAL` domain validation, a `callback_phone` of 5 to 24 characters that must be the registry-listed number used for the vetting callback, and optionally a `credit_limit_cents` between 0 and 100,000,000 which defaults to 500,000 when omitted. The uppercase rule on `jurisdiction_code` is enforced by requiring the value to be identical to its own upper-cased form, so digits and other caseless characters are accepted while any lowercase letter is rejected as a malformed field with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the missing at-sign in `contact_email` is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The pair of `jurisdiction_code` and `registry_id` must be unique across all subscribers; a duplicate pair is rejected with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_REGISTRY_DUPLICATE`.

Registration puts the account in `account_status` `ACTIVE` with `outstanding_balance_cents`, `issued_certificate_count`, `revocation_pending_count` and `risk_score_operational` all at 0, `risk_score_audit_final` null until the first audit opinion covers the subscriber, and `suspended_at` null. A `POLICY_ADMIN` may later amend `credit_limit_cents`, `contact_email` and `callback_phone`, each optionally and independently; that amendment increments the subscriber's `version` unconditionally, so an amendment naming no field at all is accepted and still moves `version` and `updated_at`. An amended `credit_limit_cents` is re-checked against the same 0 to 100,000,000 range and an amended `contact_email` against the same at-sign rule, and on this one endpoint the range check is deliberately evaluated *after* the subscriber has been resolved, so amending a nonexistent subscriber with an out-of-range limit reports `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_FOUND`. Neither `account_status` nor any computed counter — `outstanding_balance_cents`, `issued_certificate_count`, `revocation_pending_count`, `risk_score_operational`, `risk_score_audit_final` — is ever writable from a request body. `issued_certificate_count` in particular is a lifetime tally that rises on every issuance and is never decremented, not even by a revocation or a refund.

Account posture moves only by cascade. When `revocation_pending_count` reaches 2 or more while the account is `ACTIVE`, the account moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, `suspended_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `version` increments. Suspension is reversible: when pending revocations are executed or denied and `revocation_pending_count` falls below 2, a `SUSPENDED` account returns to `ACTIVE`, `suspended_at` is cleared back to null, and `version` increments again. The counter itself is floored at 0, so a decrement can never drive it negative, and because moving the counter is one mutation and flipping `account_status` is a second, the single act that does both raises the subscriber's `version` by two. An account may oscillate between the two as often as its revocation backlog does.

Suspension is purely a gate — it never mutates the subscriber's own requests or certificates. What it blocks is participation: an account that is not `ACTIVE` cannot have a vetting file opened for it, cannot have a domain authorization created or validated, cannot have a certificate request created or submitted, cannot have a request's validation cleared, and cannot have a certificate issued. All seven of those operations reject a non-`ACTIVE` subscriber with `ERR_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Termination, by contrast, is permanent. When a mass-revocation batch runs under an incident whose `category` is `KEY_COMPROMISE` and whose attributed `subscriber_id` names this subscriber, and the batch revoked at least one of that subscriber's certificates, the account moves to the terminal `TERMINATED` state from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. That transition stamps `suspended_at` with the logical clock and increments `version`. A terminated account never returns.

### 3.3. Organizational Vetting: Three Independent Checks and a Separate Approver

Organizational vetting is the track that establishes that the applicant is who it says it is. A `VALIDATION_OFFICER` opens a vetting file against a subscriber — the file takes its `subscriber_id` from the subscriber it is opened under and never from the request body — and supplies exactly one input, `required_level`, which is either `OV` or `EV`. The file begins in `vetting_state` `PENDING` with all three check dimensions at `NOT_STARTED` and `approved_at`, `approved_by_user_id` and `rejected_at` all null. A subscriber may have only one file in flight: if it already holds a vetting whose `vetting_state` is `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `AWAITING_APPROVAL`, opening another is rejected with `ERR_VETTING_ALREADY_OPEN`. The subscriber must also be `ACTIVE`.

The file is compound: three orthogonal outcome dimensions plus a separate approval act. `registry_check_status` records the business-registry verification of the subscriber's `legal_name`, `registry_id` and `jurisdiction_code`. `callback_check_status` records the confirmation call placed to the registry-listed `callback_phone`. `authority_check_status` records the applicant-authority check, which establishes that the individual requesting the certificate is entitled to bind the organization. Each dimension holds `NOT_STARTED`, `PASSED` or `FAILED`. Which dimensions are *required* depends on the depth: an `OV` file requires the registry and callback dimensions only, while an `EV` file additionally requires the authority dimension. For an `OV` file the authority dimension is ignored entirely — recording it neither advances nor rejects the compound state, though the dimension's own status and the identity of the officer who recorded it are still stamped.

A `VALIDATION_OFFICER` records one dimension at a time, naming the `dimension` as `REGISTRY`, `CALLBACK` or `AUTHORITY`, the `outcome` as `PASSED` or `FAILED`, and optionally an `evidence_ref` pointing at the supporting evidence package. Recording stamps that dimension's status and writes the acting `X-User-ID` into the matching `registry_checked_by_user_id`, `callback_checked_by_user_id` or `authority_checked_by_user_id`, and increments the file's `version` — including on the `OV`-file `AUTHORITY` recording that moves no state at all. The `evidence_ref` is validated as an optional string but is not persisted anywhere on the file. Recording is only permitted while the file is still open, meaning `vetting_state` is `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `AWAITING_APPROVAL`; a file already `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` rejects any further check with `ERR_VETTING_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The response is 200 with the updated file.

The compound state moves as follows; it is always re-derived from the three dimension statuses as they stand *after* the recording rather than from the transition itself, so re-recording a dimension with the outcome it already carried simply re-derives the state the file is already in, while still stamping the officer and moving `version`. A `PASSED` result on a dimension that was still `NOT_STARTED`, recorded while at least one required dimension remains `NOT_STARTED` afterwards, moves the file `PENDING → IN_PROGRESS`. A `PASSED` result that leaves every dimension required by `required_level` at `PASSED` moves the file `IN_PROGRESS → AWAITING_APPROVAL`; for an `OV` file this fires as soon as the registry and callback dimensions are both `PASSED`, even while `authority_check_status` is still `NOT_STARTED`. A `FAILED` result on any dimension that `required_level` requires drives the whole file to the terminal `REJECTED` state and stamps `rejected_at` with the logical clock — and it does so from `PENDING`, from `IN_PROGRESS`, and even from `AWAITING_APPROVAL`, where it overwrites a dimension that had already been recorded `PASSED`. One failed required dimension rejects the file regardless of how the other two stand.

Approval is the act of a different person in a different role. A `VETTING_APPROVER` approves a file that is in `AWAITING_APPROVAL` — any other state is refused with `ERR_VETTING_NOT_AWAITING_APPROVAL` — and the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from every non-null value among the three `*_checked_by_user_id` fields, or the approval is refused with `ERR_VETTING_SOD_VIOLATION`. Approval moves the file `AWAITING_APPROVAL → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock and `approved_by_user_id` with the approver's `X-User-ID`, and increments `version`. That `approved_at` instant is what starts the organizational vetting reuse window, whose length each profile bounds independently with its own `org_vetting_reuse_days`.

### 3.4. Domain Control Validation: Challenges, Attempts and the Reuse Window

The second validation track proves control of each domain name. A `VALIDATION_OFFICER` opens a **domain authorization** by naming the `subscriber_id` it is for, the `profile_id` whose policy governs it, the `domain_name` under validation, and the `method` to be used. The domain name must be lowercase, between 4 and 253 characters, contain at least one dot, and carry neither a leading nor a trailing dot. The method must be one of `DNS_TXT`, `HTTP_TOKEN`, `EMAIL_APPROVAL` or `TLS_ALPN`, and it must additionally appear in the named profile's `allowed_dcv_methods`; a technique the profile has not approved is rejected with `ERR_DCV_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED`. Both the profile and the subscriber must be `ACTIVE`. Finally, the triple of subscriber, profile and domain name may have at most one live challenge: if an authorization for the same triple already sits in state `PENDING` or `VALIDATED`, the new one is rejected with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_ALREADY_OPEN`, while triples whose only prior authorizations are `FAILED`, `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` are free to be reopened.

Creation mints a `challenge_token` — a server-generated value, deterministic per authorization, of the form `dcv-challenge-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded counter that advances by one for every token the server mints (including every token minted by a later invalidation), so the first token issued after boot is `dcv-challenge-000001` — which is returned in the response and which the applicant must echo back, and initialises `state` to `PENDING` with `attempt_count`, `reuse_count` and `invalidation_count` all at 0 and `validated_at`, `expires_at` and `invalidated_at` all null.

Validation is attempt-limited, and the limit is three attempts in total. A `VALIDATION_OFFICER` submits a `challenge_token`; the authorization must be in `PENDING` or `FAILED` (anything else, including an already `VALIDATED`, `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` authorization, is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_VALIDATABLE`), and both the subscriber and the profile must still be `ACTIVE`. Every attempt, successful or not, increments `attempt_count` by one and the authorization's `version` by one. When the submitted token equals the stored one, the authorization moves to `VALIDATED` — from `PENDING` on a first-time success and equally from `FAILED` after one or two earlier misses, because a failed attempt is forgiven as long as the authorization has not been exhausted; the token match is tested before the attempt ceiling, so a success on the third and last attempt validates rather than exhausting. Success stamps `validated_at` with the logical clock and sets `expires_at` to `validated_at` plus the profile's `dcv_reuse_days` in days. A success does **not** reset `attempt_count`.

When the submitted token does not match, the outcome depends on how many attempts have already been spent, and in neither case is the response an error: the endpoint answers 200 with the updated authorization. If the incremented `attempt_count` is still below 3, the authorization lands in `FAILED` — moving `PENDING → FAILED` on the first miss and remaining `FAILED → FAILED` on the second. If the incremented `attempt_count` reaches exactly 3, the authorization moves `FAILED → EXHAUSTED`, which is terminal: an exhausted authorization can never be validated, and the operator must open a fresh one.

A validated authorization is a reusable asset with two independent limits. The first is temporal: the reuse window is the half-open interval running from `validated_at` up to but not including `expires_at`, so an authorization is usable while the logical clock is strictly before `expires_at` and unusable the instant the clock reaches it. Clock sweep one makes that lapse visible by moving such an authorization `VALIDATED → EXPIRED`, which is terminal. The second limit is volumetric: `reuse_count` counts the certificate requests that have consumed the authorization at validation clearance, and it may never reach the profile's `max_dcv_reuse_count`.

Domain control may also be repudiated. A `VALIDATION_OFFICER` invalidates an authorization by supplying a `reason`, which is a required string that is validated and then discarded rather than persisted on the record; the authorization must currently be `VALIDATED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_VALIDATED`. Invalidation returns it `VALIDATED → PENDING` and reopens it for a fresh proof: `invalidated_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `invalidation_count` increments, `attempt_count` resets to 0, `validated_at` and `expires_at` are cleared to null, and a new `challenge_token` is minted. Because a repudiated domain proof cannot underwrite a pending issuance, the invalidation cascades: every certificate request whose `consumed_authorization_ids` contains this authorization and whose `state` is `READY_TO_ISSUE` or `ISSUANCE_BLOCKED` is walked back to `SUBMITTED` in ascending `created_at` order, with `validation_cleared_by_user_id`, `validation_cleared_at`, `projected_not_before`, `projected_not_after`, `projected_charge_cents` and `blocked_reason` all cleared to null and `consumed_authorization_ids` emptied to an empty array. Every authorization that those rolled-back requests were holding — the invalidated one included — has its `reuse_count` decremented by one, so the volumetric budget is genuinely returned rather than leaked. The invalidated authorization's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every request the cascade touched. The cycle `VALIDATED → PENDING → VALIDATED` may repeat any number of times.

### 3.5. The Certificate Request Pipeline: Drafting, Returning and Clearing Validation

A **certificate request** is the unit of work that travels through the CA. A `VALIDATION_OFFICER` creates one for an `ACTIVE` subscriber, naming the `subscriber_id` of the applicant and the `profile_id` of the governing policy — creation checks only that the subscriber is `ACTIVE` and does not require the named profile to have left `DRAFT`, since the profile-status gate first bites at submission — and supplying `requested_domains`, `requested_validity_days`, `csr_key_bits` and optionally `expedited`. `requested_domains` holds between 1 and 10 lowercase fully-qualified domain names with no duplicate after case-folding; violating either the count bounds or the duplicate rule, or supplying an entry that is not a JSON string at all, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ARRAY_CONTENT`. Those array-content rules are evaluated before the shape of the individual names, so a payload whose entries collide after case-folding reports `ERR_INVALID_ARRAY_CONTENT` even when one of the colliding spellings is not lowercase; an entry that survives the array-content rules and then fails the per-name shape rules of §3.4 — lowercase, 4 to 253 characters, at least one dot, neither a leading nor a trailing dot — is rejected as a malformed field with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `requested_validity_days` lies between 1 and 398 and will be capped later by the profile. `csr_key_bits` is the RSA modulus size asserted in the signing request and must be exactly 2048, 3072 or 4096; because it is an integer input rather than an enum, any other integer is reported as `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, and `requested_validity_days` is range-checked before it. `expedited` is a boolean that defaults to false when omitted and, when true, adds an expedite fee to the issuance charge. The new request starts in `state` `DRAFT` with `return_count` at 0, `consumed_authorization_ids` an empty array, and `submitted_at`, `validation_cleared_by_user_id`, `validation_cleared_at`, `projected_not_before`, `projected_not_after`, `projected_charge_cents`, `issued_certificate_id`, `rejected_reason` and `blocked_reason` all null.

Submission moves the request `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock. The request must be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`, and both the subscriber and the profile must be `ACTIVE`.

A submitted request can be sent back for more information, and the return counter is what makes that loop finite. Returning a request requires a `return_reason` and requires the request to be `SUBMITTED`, or `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SUBMITTED` is raised. While `return_count` is below 2, the return moves the request `SUBMITTED → DRAFT` and increments `return_count` — so the request may bounce `SUBMITTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED` twice. When `return_count` is already exactly 2, the third return is not a return at all: it drives `return_count` to 3, moves the request to the terminal `REJECTED` state, and copies the supplied `return_reason` value into the request's `rejected_reason`. The endpoint still answers 200 with the request in `REJECTED`, not an error.

Outright rejection is the other exit. It carries a `rejected_reason` and is permitted only while the request is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_REJECTABLE`. The destination state depends on where the request was: a `SUBMITTED` request becomes `REJECTED`, while a `DRAFT` request — on which no validation work was performed — becomes `WITHDRAWN` instead. Both are terminal, and both stamp `rejected_reason` from the body.

**Clearing validation** is the pivotal act of the pipeline, and it is where the two validation tracks and the policy come together. A `VALIDATION_OFFICER` clears a `SUBMITTED` request (`ERR_REQUEST_NOT_SUBMITTED` otherwise), with the subscriber and profile both `ACTIVE`, and the operation takes no input fields at all: everything it needs is already on the request. It moves the request `SUBMITTED → READY_TO_ISSUE`.

Clearance first selects one domain authorization per requested domain, walking `requested_domains` in array order. For a given domain, the candidate set is every authorization belonging to the same `subscriber_id`, naming the same `profile_id`, carrying that exact `domain_name`, in state `VALIDATED`, whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, and whose `reuse_count` is strictly below the profile's `max_dcv_reuse_count`. Authorizations in `PENDING`, `FAILED`, `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` are never candidates. From the candidate set the server picks the one with the greatest `validated_at`; ties are broken by the greatest `created_at`, then by the greatest `reuse_count`, and any remaining tie by the latest of the two records to have been created (the server-internal insertion sequence), never by `id`.

When a domain has no candidate, the failure is diagnosed precisely, and the three diagnoses are reported in a fixed precedence that is applied across the whole `requested_domains` list rather than per domain: the server first tests every requested domain for the missing-authorization condition, then every requested domain for the lapsed-window condition, and only then every requested domain for the reuse-budget condition. A later domain's missing authorization therefore outranks an earlier domain's exhausted reuse budget, and within each of the three passes the domains are examined in array order. If no authorization exists for that triple in either `VALIDATED` or `EXPIRED` state at all, the failure is `ERR_DOMAIN_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`. If one does exist but its reuse window has lapsed — either it has already been flipped to `EXPIRED` by clock sweep one, or it is still nominally `VALIDATED` with `expires_at` at or before the clock — the failure is `ERR_DOMAIN_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`; because the sweep normally runs first, `EXPIRED` is the form usually observed. If a fresh `VALIDATED` authorization exists but every candidate's `reuse_count` has already reached `max_dcv_reuse_count`, the failure is `ERR_DCV_REUSE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Organizational vetting is checked next, and only when the profile's `required_vetting_level` is not `NONE` — a `DV` profile skips this block entirely. The subscriber must hold at least one vetting file in `vetting_state` `APPROVED`, or `ERR_ORG_VETTING_REQUIRED` is raised. The three gates are evaluated over the whole set of the subscriber's approved files rather than over a single chosen one, so a subscriber holding several approved files passes as long as some file satisfies each gate in turn. At least one approved file's `required_level` must cover the profile's `required_vetting_level` under the ordering where `NONE` is weaker than `OV`, which is weaker than `EV`; an `OV` vetting therefore never satisfies an `EV` profile, and the mismatch is `ERR_ORG_VETTING_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT`. Among just those covering files, at least one `approved_at` must be strictly later than the logical clock minus the profile's `org_vetting_reuse_days` in days, so a vetting exactly at the edge of its reuse period has already lapsed; a stale vetting is `ERR_ORG_VETTING_REUSE_EXPIRED`. Finally the request's `csr_key_bits` must meet the tier's key-strength minimum: `DV` and `OV` profiles require at least 2048 bits, while an `EV` profile requires at least 3072, so a 2048-bit key against an `EV` profile is rejected with `ERR_CSR_KEY_TOO_WEAK`. These four gates are evaluated in exactly the order just given, after the three domain-authorization diagnoses.

A successful clearance writes a great deal onto the request. `validation_cleared_by_user_id` takes the acting `X-User-ID` and `validation_cleared_at` the logical clock — together these become the segregation-of-duties anchor for issuance. `consumed_authorization_ids` receives the selected authorization identifiers in `requested_domains` order, one per domain, and every selected authorization has its `reuse_count` incremented by one. The request's `version` increments, as does the `version` of each consumed authorization.

Clearance also computes two projections. The **projected validity window** is the anchored-at-clearance variant of the validity calculation: `projected_not_before` is the logical clock at clearance, and `projected_not_after` is that same instant plus the capped validity span, where the capped span in days is the lesser of the request's `requested_validity_days` and the profile's `max_validity_days`. This projection deliberately ignores both the domain-authorization reuse floor and the ceremony attestation cap; it is a forecast of what the request could get, not a promise.

The **projected charge** is where the money is decided. `projected_charge_cents` is computed in six pinned steps. First, the per-domain unit price is the profile's `base_fee_cents` multiplied by the validation-tier multiplier — 1.0 for a `DV` profile, 1.5 for an `OV` profile and 2.5 for an `EV` profile — with the product rounded half up to a whole cent at this point, before any multiplication by the domain count. Second, the subtotal is that unit price multiplied by the number of entries in `requested_domains`. Third, a volume discount percentage is read from the subtotal: a subtotal from 0 through 49,999 cents earns no discount, a subtotal from 50,000 through 149,999 cents earns 10 percent, and a subtotal of 150,000 cents or more earns 20 percent. The band is keyed on this pre-discount, pre-expedite subtotal and is never re-derived afterwards, even when the discounted subtotal or the final total would fall in a different band. Fourth, the discount amount is the subtotal multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the discounted subtotal is the subtotal minus the discount. Fifth, if `expedited` is true the expedite fee is 25 percent of the discounted subtotal, rounded half up to a whole cent; if `expedited` is false the expedite fee is zero. Sixth, the total is the discounted subtotal plus the expedite fee. Rounding happens at the first, fourth and fifth steps only — rounding the base-fee-times-multiplier-times-domains product in one shot instead yields a different and incorrect subtotal whenever the unit price has a fractional part. Nothing about the subscriber enters this computation: the volume band depends on the subtotal of this one request and never on the subscriber's prior issuance history.

A cleared request is not yet safe. Two things can push it back. If a consumed authorization is later invalidated, the request falls back to `SUBMITTED` as described in §3.4, discarding both projections and its clearance stamps. And if the governing profile's `status_service_posture` flips to `CRL_OVERDUE`, the request moves `READY_TO_ISSUE → ISSUANCE_BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `PROFILE_STATUS_SERVICE_OVERDUE`, returning to `READY_TO_ISSUE` with `blocked_reason` cleared to null once the posture returns to `NOMINAL`. That pair may oscillate as often as the profile's revocation-list backlog does.

### 3.6. Signing Infrastructure: Partitions, Ceremony Windows and Attestation

Signing keys live in hardware. A `POLICY_ADMIN` registers an **HSM partition** with an operator-facing `label` of 1 to 60 characters and a hardware `serial` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique across all partitions; a repeat serial is rejected with `ERR_PARTITION_SERIAL_DUPLICATE`. The partition starts in `state` `OPERATIONAL` with `quarantined_at` null and `attested_ceremony_count` at 0.

An `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` schedules a **key ceremony** on a partition, which supplies the ceremony's `partition_id`; the operator provides `window_start`, `window_end` and `attestation_valid_days`. The gates run in the order the global ladder dictates: `attestation_valid_days` must lie between 1 and 365 first; then the partition must be `OPERATIONAL`, scheduling on a quarantined partition being refused with `ERR_PARTITION_QUARANTINED`; and only then must the window be non-degenerate, with `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, or `ERR_INVALID_CEREMONY_WINDOW` is raised.

Ceremony windows on a single partition must not merely avoid overlapping — they must keep a positive separation buffer of one hour, because an operator needs the partition physically clear before the next ceremony begins. The occupation rule treats the windows as closed intervals inflated by that buffer: the proposed ceremony conflicts with an existing one whenever the proposed `window_start` is at or before the existing `window_end` plus 60 minutes **and** the existing `window_start` is at or before the proposed `window_end` plus 60 minutes. Because both comparisons are inclusive, a gap of exactly 60 minutes between two windows still conflicts, while a gap of 60 minutes and one second does not; adjacency, likewise, is a conflict. Only ceremonies in `SCHEDULED`, `WITNESSED` or `ATTESTED` block — an `ABORTED` or `ATTESTATION_REVOKED` ceremony releases its window and never blocks again. A conflict is reported as `ERR_CEREMONY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A new ceremony starts in `SCHEDULED` with `witness_user_ids` an empty array, `witness_count` at 0, `issued_certificate_count` at 0, and `attested_at`, `attested_by_user_id`, `attestation_expires_at` and `attestation_revoked_at` all null.

Witnessing is a two-person join, and it is the one act both `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` and `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` may perform. The ceremony must be `SCHEDULED`, or `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_SCHEDULED` is raised, and the acting `X-User-ID` must not already appear in `witness_user_ids`, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_WITNESS` is raised. The first witness appends its `X-User-ID` to `witness_user_ids`, sets `witness_count` to 1, and deliberately leaves the ceremony in `SCHEDULED` — one signature is not a quorum. The second, distinct witness appends its `X-User-ID`, sets `witness_count` to 2, and moves the ceremony `SCHEDULED → WITNESSED`. Both attempts increment the ceremony's `version`, and `witness_user_ids` preserves the order in which the witnesses were recorded.

Attestation is the auditor's independent sign-off, and it is what turns a ceremony into something a certificate may be signed under. A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` attests a ceremony under four checks that run in this order: the partition must still be `OPERATIONAL`, or `ERR_PARTITION_QUARANTINED`; the ceremony must be `WITNESSED`, or `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_WITNESSED`; its `witness_user_ids` must not contain the attester's own `X-User-ID`, since an auditor may not attest a ceremony it witnessed, or `ERR_ATTESTER_WAS_WITNESS`; and its window must have closed — the logical clock must be at or after `window_end`, and attesting while the window is still open is refused with `ERR_CEREMONY_WINDOW_OPEN`. Attestation moves the ceremony `WITNESSED → ATTESTED`, stamps `attested_at` with the logical clock and `attested_by_user_id` with the attester's `X-User-ID`, and sets `attestation_expires_at` to `attested_at` plus `attestation_valid_days` in days. That expiry is not decoration: it becomes a hard cap on the `not_after` of every certificate signed under this ceremony. The partition's `attested_ceremony_count` and `version` both increment, as does the ceremony's `version`.

Repudiating an attestation is the most destructive single act in this API, and it is a four-hop transitive cascade in which each hop is caused by the previous hop rather than by the original request. A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` supplies a `reason` — a required string that is validated and then discarded rather than stored on the ceremony — against a ceremony that must be `ATTESTED` (`ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_ATTESTED` otherwise), and the endpoint answers 200 with the repudiated ceremony. Hop one: the ceremony moves `ATTESTED → ATTESTATION_REVOKED`, `attestation_revoked_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the partition's `attested_ceremony_count` decrements by one. Hop two: because that ceremony lost its attestation, its partition moves `OPERATIONAL → QUARANTINED`, `quarantined_at` is stamped, `attested_ceremony_count` is recomputed as the number of that partition's ceremonies still in state `ATTESTED`, and the partition's `version` increments; quarantine is terminal, and a quarantined partition can never sign again. Repudiating a second attested ceremony on a partition that is already `QUARANTINED` leaves `state` and the original `quarantined_at` exactly as they stand, while still recomputing `attested_ceremony_count` and incrementing the partition's `version`. Hop three: because the partition is now quarantined, every certificate whose `ceremony_id` names *any* ceremony of that partition — not only the repudiated one — and whose `revocation_status` is `NONE` moves to `REVOCATION_PENDING`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and each receives a system-raised revocation request whose `origin` is `SYSTEM_CASCADE`, whose `reason_code` is `KEY_COMPROMISE`, whose `evidence_ref` is the literal string `ceremony:` followed by the repudiated ceremony's identifier, and whose `requested_by_user_id` is the literal string `system`. Hop four: because those certificates entered `REVOCATION_PENDING`, each affected subscriber's `revocation_pending_count` rises, and any subscriber whose count reaches 2 moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. The ceremony's `version` increments along with the `version` of every partition, certificate and subscriber the cascade touched.

A ceremony that never gets that far can be abandoned. An `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` aborts a ceremony that is `SCHEDULED` or `WITNESSED`, supplying a `reason` that is likewise required, validated and then discarded rather than stored; any other state is refused with `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_ABORTABLE`. The abort moves the ceremony to the terminal `ABORTED` state, leaves `witness_user_ids` and `witness_count` exactly as they stand, increments its `version`, and frees its occupation window so a replacement ceremony may be scheduled over it.

### 3.7. Issuance, Transparency Logging and Activation

Issuance is performed by an `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` against a request in `READY_TO_ISSUE`, and it is the point at which every gate in the system is evaluated at once. The operator supplies the `ceremony_id` of the attested ceremony whose partition will sign, and optionally a `supersedes_certificate_id` naming the certificate this one replaces.

The gates, in the order they are evaluated, follow the global ladder of §2.8 exactly, and that order is these. The profile must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_ACTIVE`, and must be at `status_service_posture` `NOMINAL`, or `ERR_PROFILE_STATUS_SERVICE_OVERDUE`. The subscriber must be `ACTIVE`. The request must then be in `READY_TO_ISSUE`, so a request still `SUBMITTED`, or one sitting in `ISSUANCE_BLOCKED`, is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_READY`. The named ceremony's partition must be `OPERATIONAL`, or `ERR_PARTITION_QUARANTINED`. Only then does the acting `X-User-ID` have to differ from the request's `validation_cleared_by_user_id`, or `ERR_ISSUANCE_SOD_VIOLATION`. The named ceremony must be in state `ATTESTED`, or `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_ATTESTED_FOR_ISSUANCE`; and its `attestation_expires_at` must be present and strictly after the logical clock, or `ERR_CEREMONY_ATTESTATION_EXPIRED`. Every authorization named in `consumed_authorization_ids` must still be in state `VALIDATED` with `expires_at` strictly after the logical clock, and an issuance attempted after a consumed authorization's reuse window has lapsed — or naming an authorization that no longer resolves at all — is refused with `ERR_DOMAIN_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED`. When `supersedes_certificate_id` is supplied, the certificate it names must belong to the same `subscriber_id` as the issuing request, or `ERR_SUPERSEDES_SUBSCRIBER_MISMATCH`. Last of all comes the fiscal gate: the subscriber's `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the settlement total for this issuance must be at most `credit_limit_cents`, and exceeding it is `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 402.

A successful issuance mints a **certificate** and answers 201 with it. The new record carries `lifecycle_status` `PREISSUANCE` and `revocation_status` `NONE`, a fresh `serial_number` taken as the previous serial plus one (the very first certificate the system ever issues being `"1000001"`), the `request_id` of the originating request, and `subscriber_id` and `profile_id` copied from it, along with the `ceremony_id` and any `supersedes_certificate_id` supplied in the body. `dcv_methods_used` records the distinct `method` values of the authorizations the originating request consumed, sorted ascending alphabetically. `sct_count` starts at 0, and `refunded_at`, `activated_at`, `revoked_at`, `revocation_reason_code`, `crl_number` and `crl_published_at` all start null.

The certificate's **final validity window** is the anchored-at-issuance variant of the validity calculation, and unlike the projection it respects every cap. `not_before` is the logical clock at issuance. Three candidate end instants are then computed: the profile cap, which is the issuance clock plus the capped span in days, where the capped span is again the lesser of the request's `requested_validity_days` and the profile's `max_validity_days`; the domain-authorization floor, which is the earliest `expires_at` among all the authorizations named in `consumed_authorization_ids`; and the attestation cap, which is the named ceremony's `attestation_expires_at`. `not_after` is the earliest of those three. Because the issuance clock is at or after the clearance clock and because two extra caps now bind, `not_after` is strictly earlier than the request's `projected_not_after` whenever either extra cap is the binding one. `validity_days_final` is then the number of whole days in the final window: the total seconds from `not_before` to `not_after` divided by 86,400 seconds per day, with any fraction discarded downward.

`charged_amount_cents` is the settlement total, computed at issuance by exactly the same six-step tiered calculation described in §3.5 that produced `projected_charge_cents`. Issuance then debits the subscriber: `outstanding_balance_cents` rises by `charged_amount_cents` and `issued_certificate_count` rises by one, with the subscriber's `version` incrementing. The originating request moves `READY_TO_ISSUE → ISSUED`, its `issued_certificate_id` is set to the new certificate, and its `version` increments. The signing ceremony's `issued_certificate_count` increments by one.

A freshly minted certificate is not yet publicly usable, because the CA still owes the public a transparency record. An `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` records a **transparency-log submission** against the certificate by naming the `log_operator` — one of `GOOGLE_ARGON`, `CLOUDFLARE_NIMBUS`, `DIGICERT_YETI` or `SECTIGO_SABRE` — and the `outcome`, either `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`. A `rejection_reason` carrying the log's reported cause must be present exactly when the outcome is `REJECTED` and must be omitted or null when it is `ACCEPTED`; either mismatch is rejected with `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_MISMATCH`. The target certificate must have `lifecycle_status` `PREISSUANCE` or `ISSUED` and `revocation_status` `NONE`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_REVOCABLE`. The submission's `certificate_id` comes from the certificate it is recorded against, `sct_timestamp` is stamped with the logical clock, and the record is immutable thereafter — it carries `id` and `created_at` and no `updated_at` or `version` of its own. The endpoint answers 201 with the submission record, not with the certificate, and a certificate's submissions are listed in ascending `created_at` order (the `sct_timestamp` of every submission made at one clock instant is identical, so the list order is the order the submissions were recorded) and may be filtered by `outcome`.

Signed-certificate-timestamp counting deduplicates by log. `counted_toward_sct` on a new submission is true only when its `outcome` is `ACCEPTED` and no earlier submission for the same certificate and the same `log_operator` was already `ACCEPTED`. The certificate's `sct_count` is then recomputed as the number of distinct `log_operator` values that have ever returned `ACCEPTED` for it, so a second acceptance from a log already represented raises nothing, and a `REJECTED` submission neither raises nor lowers the count. Recording a submission increments the certificate's `version`.

Activation is the final step: an `ISSUANCE_OPERATOR` moves the certificate `PREISSUANCE → ISSUED`, which is what makes it publicly usable. The certificate must not have aged out — the expiry test is made directly against `lifecycle_status` being `EXPIRED` rather than by re-comparing `not_after` with the clock, which is equivalent because clock sweep two flips an unactivated certificate to `EXPIRED` the instant its `not_after` arrives and because issuance never mints a certificate whose `not_after` is already reached — and an expired certificate is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_ALREADY_EXPIRED`, evaluated ahead of the state check. The `lifecycle_status` must then be `PREISSUANCE`, so activating an already-`ISSUED` certificate earns `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_PREISSUANCE`, and `revocation_status` must still be `NONE` — a certificate in `REVOCATION_PENDING` or `REVOKED` reports that same `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_PREISSUANCE`, the two conditions sharing one error. Finally `sct_count` must be at least the profile's `required_sct_count`; the comparison is inclusive, so a count exactly equal to the requirement passes, while anything lower is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SCTS`. Activation stamps `activated_at` with the logical clock, increments the certificate's `version`, and answers 200 with the updated certificate.

The two status dimensions on a certificate evolve independently for the rest of its life. `lifecycle_status` tracks `PREISSUANCE`, `ISSUED` and the terminal `EXPIRED`; `revocation_status` tracks `NONE`, `REVOCATION_PENDING` and the terminal `REVOKED`. Any combination is reachable, including a pre-issuance revocation sitting at `PREISSUANCE` with `REVOKED`, and a certificate revoked before it aged out sitting at `EXPIRED` with `REVOKED`.

### 3.8. Revocation Processing and the Status Services

A `REVOCATION_OFFICER` raises a **revocation request** by naming the `certificate_id` of the target, the RFC 5280 `reason_code` to be recorded on the eventual list entry, and `expected_certificate_version` carrying the certificate's `version` as last read. The reason code is one of `UNSPECIFIED`, `KEY_COMPROMISE`, `AFFILIATION_CHANGED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `CESSATION_OF_OPERATION` or `PRIVILEGE_WITHDRAWN`. Two optional inputs shape the request further: `incident_id` links it to a misissuance incident, and `evidence_ref` points at the compromise evidence package. `evidence_ref` is conditionally mandatory — when `reason_code` is `KEY_COMPROMISE` a revocation request without it is rejected as a missing required field with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The target certificate must be revocable, meaning its `lifecycle_status` is `PREISSUANCE` or `ISSUED` and its `revocation_status` is `NONE`; anything else is `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_REVOCABLE`. When `incident_id` is supplied, the target certificate's `profile_id` must equal that incident's `scope_profile_id`, or the link is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_CERTIFICATE_MISMATCH`. The presence of the link also decides provenance: `origin` is set to `SUBSCRIBER` when `incident_id` is absent and to `COMPLIANCE` when it is present. `requested_by_user_id` takes the acting `X-User-ID` and `requested_at` the logical clock, and the request is born in `state` `REQUESTED` with `deadline_breached` false and `executed_at`, `denied_at`, `denial_reason` and `superseded_at` all null. The response is 201.

**Revocation deadlines** are computed at creation, for every revocation request regardless of how it came into being, and three inputs interact non-linearly. The base allowance comes from the reason code: 24 hours for `KEY_COMPROMISE` and for `PRIVILEGE_WITHDRAWN`, and 120 hours for `AFFILIATION_CHANGED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `CESSATION_OF_OPERATION` and `UNSPECIFIED`. An incident-linked cap of 24 hours applies when the request carries an `incident_id`; when it does not, the incident cap contributes the base allowance itself and so never binds. A tier cap of 48 hours applies when the target certificate's profile has `validation_type` `EV`; for `DV` and `OV` profiles the tier cap likewise contributes the base allowance. The effective allowance is the smallest of those three values, and then raised to a floor of 4 hours if it somehow came out lower. `deadline_at` is `requested_at` plus the effective allowance in whole hours.

Raising a request also moves the certificate. Its `revocation_status` goes `NONE → REVOCATION_PENDING`, its `version` increments, and the owning subscriber's `revocation_pending_count` rises by one — which suspends the account the moment that count reaches 2, as described in §3.2. Because a newer request legitimately displaces an older one, every other revocation request for the same certificate still sitting in `REQUESTED` moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state, processed in ascending `created_at` order, with `superseded_at` stamped at the logical clock and its `version` incremented. This is exactly why the operation carries the optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.5 rather than relying on a state precondition: nothing else would make a stale second writer visible.

**Execution** is what actually revokes, and its three preconditions are evaluated in exactly this order. A `REVOCATION_OFFICER` executes a request that must first be in `REQUESTED` (`ERR_REVOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN` otherwise, and this check outranks both certificate checks that follow it), whose target certificate's `lifecycle_status` must not be `EXPIRED` — an expired certificate is never added to a revocation list, and its open request must be denied instead, so the attempt is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_EXPIRED_NOT_REVOCABLE` — and whose target certificate must be in `revocation_status` `REVOCATION_PENDING`, or `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_REVOCABLE`. No additional check compares the request against its certificate's other requests: because raising supersedes every older open request, at most one `REQUESTED` request exists per certificate, and execution simply takes the one it was called on. Execution stamps `executed_at` with the logical clock and sets `deadline_breached` to true when the logical clock is strictly after `deadline_at`, and false otherwise, so an execution landing exactly on the deadline is on time.

Execution then runs a three-hop cascade. Hop one: the certificate moves `REVOCATION_PENDING → REVOKED`, `revoked_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the request's `reason_code` is copied onto the certificate's `revocation_reason_code`. The owning subscriber's `revocation_pending_count` decrements by one, which returns a `SUSPENDED` account to `ACTIVE` once the count is below 2. Hop two: because the certificate became `REVOKED`, the governing profile's `revoked_certificate_count` and `pending_crl_entry_count` each rise by one. Hop three: because `pending_crl_entry_count` has now reached `crl_trigger_threshold`, the profile's `status_service_posture` moves `NOMINAL → CRL_OVERDUE`, which in turn moves every `READY_TO_ISSUE` request of that profile to `ISSUANCE_BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `PROFILE_STATUS_SERVICE_OVERDUE`, in ascending `created_at` order. One compensating effect rides along: when the revoked certificate was still `PREISSUANCE` and the `reason_code` is `SUPERSEDED`, the issuance charge is credited back — `refunded_at` is stamped with the logical clock and the subscriber's `outstanding_balance_cents` is reduced by that certificate's `charged_amount_cents`. The `version` of the revocation request, the certificate, the profile and the subscriber all increment, and the endpoint answers 200 with the updated revocation request rather than with the certificate.

**Denial** is the other exit. A `REVOCATION_OFFICER` denies a request in `REQUESTED` state by supplying a `denial_reason`, and the request's `origin` must not be `SYSTEM_CASCADE` — a cascade-raised revocation reflects a real key compromise and may never be waved away, so the attempt is refused with `ERR_CASCADE_REVOCATION_NOT_DENIABLE`. Denial moves the request to the terminal `DENIED` state, stamps `denied_at` with the logical clock and records the `denial_reason`. The target certificate returns `REVOCATION_PENDING → NONE`, but only when no other revocation request for it remains in `REQUESTED`; the subscriber's `revocation_pending_count` decrements by one, un-suspending the account once it is below 2. The `version` of the request, the certificate and the subscriber all increment, and the endpoint answers 200 with the updated revocation request. A certificate may be pushed to `REVOCATION_PENDING` and released back to `NONE` repeatedly.

**Publishing a revocation list** is how the CA discharges the queue, and it is the only operation that can. A `REVOCATION_OFFICER` publishes for an `ACTIVE` profile, supplying `this_update` and `next_update`, the bounds of the half-open validity interval the list covers, plus the `expected_profile_version` guard of §2.5. The window geometry is checked in four steps, in this order. First, `next_update` must be strictly after `this_update`, or `ERR_INVALID_CRL_WINDOW`. Second, when the profile has published before, `this_update` must be at or before the previous list's `last_crl_next_update` minus 30 minutes, so consecutive lists overlap by at least half an hour and status service never shows a coverage gap; an overlap of exactly 30 minutes is accepted while 29 minutes and 59 seconds is a violation reported as `ERR_CRL_COVERAGE_GAP`. Third, again only when a previous list exists, `next_update` must be strictly after `last_crl_next_update`, so the series genuinely advances rather than nesting a new window inside the old one; a non-advancing window is `ERR_CRL_WINDOW_NOT_ADVANCING`. Fourth, the span from `this_update` to `next_update` must be at most the profile's `max_crl_validity_hours` in hours, or `ERR_CRL_VALIDITY_TOO_LONG`. Note the deliberate contrast with ceremony windows: there, overlap is forbidden and separation is mandatory; here, overlap is mandatory and a gap is the violation. A profile's very first publication is bound only by the non-degenerate-window rule and the maximum-span rule, since there is no predecessor to overlap.

The publication then drains the queue exactly. The drain set is every certificate of that profile whose `revocation_status` is `REVOKED` and whose `crl_published_at` is still null, ordered by ascending `revoked_at` with ties broken by ascending `serial_number`. `crl_number` on the new publication is the profile's `last_crl_number` plus one, `entry_count` is the size of the drain set, and `revoked_serials` is the list of those certificates' `serial_number` values in that same order. Each drained certificate has its `crl_published_at` stamped with the logical clock and its `crl_number` set to the publication's number. On the profile, `total_crl_entries_published` grows by `entry_count`, `pending_crl_entry_count` is reset to 0, `last_crl_number` becomes the new number, and `last_crl_this_update` and `last_crl_next_update` take the submitted `this_update` and `next_update`. `published_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. A publication with an empty drain set is perfectly legal: `entry_count` is 0, `revoked_serials` is an empty array, and `crl_number` still advances by one. The publication record itself is immutable, carrying `id` and `created_at` and neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and the response is 201. Because the queue is now empty, the profile's posture returns `CRL_OVERDUE → NOMINAL` and every `ISSUANCE_BLOCKED` request of the profile returns to `READY_TO_ISSUE` with `blocked_reason` cleared, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and the profile's `version` increments — once for the drain itself, and a second time when the posture actually moves, so a publication that clears a `CRL_OVERDUE` posture raises the profile's `version` by two while a publication made while the posture is already `NOMINAL` raises it by one and releases nothing.

### 3.9. Compliance Operations: Misissuance Incidents, Mass Revocation and Root Program Audits

A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` opens a **misissuance incident** with a `title` of 1 to 120 characters, a `category` of `KEY_COMPROMISE`, `DOMAIN_MISISSUANCE`, `ORG_MISISSUANCE` or `PROCEDURAL`, and a scope. The scope is a `scope_profile_id` naming the profile whose certificates form the candidate pool, a `scope_issued_from` and `scope_issued_to` pair bounding the pool by certificate `not_before`, and two optional narrowings: `scope_dcv_method`, one of `DNS_TXT`, `HTTP_TOKEN`, `EMAIL_APPROVAL` or `TLS_ALPN`, which keeps only certificates whose `dcv_methods_used` contains that method, and `subscriber_id`, which attributes the incident to one subscriber. Both may be null; a null `scope_dcv_method` means no method narrowing, and a null `subscriber_id` means the incident is CA-wide and no subscriber's risk score moves because of it. `scope_issued_to` must be strictly after `scope_issued_from` (`ERR_INVALID_SCOPE_WINDOW`), and when `scope_dcv_method` is supplied it must appear in the scoped profile's `allowed_dcv_methods` (`ERR_SCOPE_METHOD_NOT_IN_PROFILE`).

Creation stamps `discovered_at` with the logical clock and sets `disclosure_due_at` to exactly 24 hours later, the root program disclosure deadline. The incident is born in `state` `OPEN` with `disclosure_breached` false, `disclosed_at` and `closed_at` null, and `revoked_certificate_count`, `remaining_scope_count` and `penalty_total_cents` all initialised to 0. When `subscriber_id` is present the attributed subscriber's `risk_score_operational` is recomputed immediately, so opening the incident is itself a scoring event; because a recomputation that lands on the value already held is not a mutation, a subscriber already sitting at the cap of 100 has neither its `version` nor its `updated_at` moved. The response is 201.

**Disclosure** is the only exit from `OPEN`. A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` discloses an incident in `state` `OPEN` — anything else is `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_OPEN` — naming the `root_program` addressed (`MOZILLA`, `CHROME`, `APPLE` or `MICROSOFT`) and supplying the narrative `body`. The incident moves `OPEN → DISCLOSED`, `disclosed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and a root program communication is recorded with `kind` `INCIDENT_DISCLOSURE`, its `incident_id` set, its `audit_id` null, its `sent_at` at the logical clock, its `sent_by_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, and `within_deadline` true when `sent_at` is at or before `disclosure_due_at` — a disclosure landing exactly on the deadline instant is on time. The attributed subscriber's `risk_score_operational` is recomputed and the incident's `version` increments; the endpoint answers 200 with the updated incident rather than with the communication it filed, and the communication takes its `root_program` from the body.

The deadline itself is policed by the third clock sweep of §2.6, and that sweep is strict: an `OPEN` incident gets `disclosure_breached` set to true only when the new clock is strictly after `disclosure_due_at`, so an advance landing exactly on `disclosure_due_at` leaves the flag false. Once set, `disclosure_breached` is never cleared, not even by a later disclosure. A breached incident is not dead, but it may not be remediated silently: before any mass revocation may run on an incident whose `disclosure_breached` is true, a communication with `kind` `BREACH_ESCALATION` must already exist for it, or the batch is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_DISCLOSURE_BREACHED`. A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` files that escalation for an incident already in `DISCLOSED` or `MASS_REVOCATION_IN_PROGRESS` (otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_DISCLOSED`) by supplying `root_program` and `body`; the record carries `kind` `BREACH_ESCALATION`, its `incident_id` set, its `audit_id` null, and `within_deadline` always false, because a breach escalation is by definition late. The response is 201. Once one escalation exists, further mass-revocation batches on that incident are unblocked permanently.

**Mass revocation** is the remediation engine and it is deliberately batched. A `REVOCATION_OFFICER` runs a batch against an incident whose `state` is `DISCLOSED` or `MASS_REVOCATION_IN_PROGRESS`, after the breach-escalation gate above. The candidate pool is every certificate whose `profile_id` equals the incident's `scope_profile_id`, whose `revocation_status` is `NONE`, whose `lifecycle_status` is `PREISSUANCE` or `ISSUED`, whose `not_before` falls in the half-open window from `scope_issued_from` inclusive to `scope_issued_to` exclusive, and — when `scope_dcv_method` is set — whose `dcv_methods_used` contains that method. Certificates already `REVOKED` or sitting in `REVOCATION_PENDING` are outside the pool. An empty pool is refused with `ERR_NO_CERTIFICATES_IN_SCOPE`. The pool is ordered by ascending `created_at` with ties broken by ascending `serial_number`, never by `id`, and the batch takes the first 50 entries of that order.

Each certificate in the batch is revoked in a single step that skips the pending state entirely: `revocation_status` moves `NONE → REVOKED` directly, `revoked_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and a revocation request is created already in `state` `EXECUTED` with `origin` `MASS_REVOCATION`, its `incident_id` pointing at the incident and its `deadline_at` computed by the same deadline rules as any other request — so the incident-linked 24-hour cap always applies to it. That request takes `requested_by_user_id` and `requested_at` from the acting operator and the logical clock exactly as a hand-raised one does, has its `executed_at` stamped with the same clock instant, carries a null `evidence_ref`, and is born with `deadline_breached` false and `denied_at`, `denial_reason` and `superseded_at` null; the batch never supersedes any pre-existing request. Because this path never touches `REVOCATION_PENDING`, the owning subscriber's `revocation_pending_count` is left alone and no account is suspended by a mass revocation. The governing profile's `revoked_certificate_count` and `pending_crl_entry_count` each rise by one per certificate, applied certificate by certificate as the batch walks its pinned order, which will push the profile's `status_service_posture` to `CRL_OVERDUE` and block its ready requests the moment the queue reaches `crl_trigger_threshold` — mid-batch if that is where the threshold falls. The profile's `version` therefore rises once per certificate revoked, and twice on the certificate whose revocation trips the threshold. The `revocation_reason_code` written onto each certificate follows the incident's `category`: `KEY_COMPROMISE` for a `KEY_COMPROMISE` incident, `PRIVILEGE_WITHDRAWN` for `DOMAIN_MISISSUANCE`, `AFFILIATION_CHANGED` for `ORG_MISISSUANCE`, and `UNSPECIFIED` for `PROCEDURAL`.

The batch also prices the damage. For each certificate actually revoked by the batch, in the pinned selection order, the residual fraction of life is the seconds from the logical clock to that certificate's `not_after` divided by the seconds from its `not_before` to its `not_after`, floored at 0 so an already-elapsed certificate contributes nothing, and taken as 0 outright whenever that denominator is not strictly positive; this uses the certificate's own final validity window, never the request's projection. The per-certificate penalty is 1000 cents multiplied by the category penalty weight — 3.0 for `KEY_COMPROMISE`, 2.0 for `DOMAIN_MISISSUANCE`, 1.5 for `ORG_MISISSUANCE`, 0.5 for `PROCEDURAL` — multiplied by that residual fraction. The rounding order is pinned: each per-certificate penalty is rounded half up to whole cents *before* entering the sum, and the batch total is the sum of those rounded amounts. Summing the unrounded terms and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer; for a two-certificate batch the two orders can differ by a full cent. The batch total is added to the incident's `penalty_total_cents`, which therefore accumulates across every batch.

After the batch, `revoked_certificate_count` and `remaining_scope_count` are set from the recomputed pool: `remaining_scope_count` is the size of the pool before the batch minus the size of the batch, and `revoked_certificate_count` is the running total revoked under this incident across all batches. The incident's state then follows the drain. A batch that leaves `remaining_scope_count` above 0 moves the incident to `MASS_REVOCATION_IN_PROGRESS` — from `DISCLOSED` on the first such batch, and from `MASS_REVOCATION_IN_PROGRESS` back to itself on each subsequent one, so an incident with 130 in-scope certificates cycles through that state twice before draining on the third batch. A batch that drains the pool to zero moves the incident to `REMEDIATED`, whether it arrived there from `MASS_REVOCATION_IN_PROGRESS` or straight from `DISCLOSED` in a single batch that cleared the whole pool. One further effect fires on the worst category: when the incident's `category` is `KEY_COMPROMISE`, `subscriber_id` is not null and at least one certificate in this batch belonged to that attributed subscriber, that subscriber's `account_status` moves to the terminal `TERMINATED` and `suspended_at` is stamped with the logical clock; an account already `TERMINATED` is left alone entirely, its `version` unmoved. The attributed subscriber's `risk_score_operational` is recomputed, the incident's `version`, the profile's `version` and every touched certificate's `version` increment, and the endpoint answers 200 with the updated incident.

**Closing** an incident requires the work to be genuinely finished. A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` closes an incident whose `state` is `DISCLOSED`, `MASS_REVOCATION_IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATED` — anything else, including an `OPEN` incident, is `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_CLOSEABLE` — and whose `remaining_scope_count` is 0 with every revocation request carrying this `incident_id` resolved to `EXECUTED` or `DENIED`; either failing condition is reported as the single error `ERR_INCIDENT_REVOCATIONS_OUTSTANDING`. The incident moves to the terminal `CLOSED` state, `closed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the endpoint answers 200 with the closed incident, and the attributed subscriber's `risk_score_operational` is recomputed — it drops, because closed incidents leave the operational input set. A procedural incident whose scope window matched no certificate from the very beginning goes `DISCLOSED → CLOSED` directly, with no mass revocation ever run against it, since its `remaining_scope_count` was already 0.

**Operational risk scoring** runs on every incident state change: on opening an incident, on disclosing one, on each mass-revocation batch and on closing one. The score is the sum of category weights — 40 for `KEY_COMPROMISE`, 25 for `DOMAIN_MISISSUANCE`, 15 for `ORG_MISISSUANCE`, 5 for `PROCEDURAL` — over every incident attributed to that subscriber whose `state` is anything other than `CLOSED`, capped at 100 and carried as a whole integer in `risk_score_operational`. There is no date filtering and no decay: a subscriber all of whose incidents are closed scores 0.

**Root program audits** are the other half of compliance operations. They run on their own annual rhythm rather than in reaction to a single event, they accumulate findings before reaching a two-way opinion fork, and their opinion is the only thing in the system that writes a subscriber's final audited risk score or retires a live profile.

A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` schedules an audit against one browser `root_program` for a closed period bounded by `period_start` inclusive and `period_end` inclusive. `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start` and the period may span at most 366 days measured as an exact elapsed duration, so a span of exactly 366 days is accepted; either violation is reported as the single error `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_PERIOD`. Only one audit per root program may be in flight at a time: no audit in `state` `SCHEDULED` or `FIELDWORK` may already exist for the same `root_program`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_OPEN`. This open-audit rule is what enforces the intent that audit periods for one root program not overlap; a new period may be scheduled as soon as the previous audit has been opined. The audit is born in `state` `SCHEDULED` with `finding_count`, `critical_finding_count`, `assessed_subscriber_count` and `retired_profile_count` all 0 and `opinion_at` and `opined_by_user_id` null. The response is 201.

A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` records a **finding** against an audit whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `FIELDWORK`, supplying a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` and a `summary`, which is required as a string but is not persisted anywhere. Each finding raises `finding_count` by one and, when `severity` is `CRITICAL`, raises `critical_finding_count` by one as well. The first finding moves the audit `SCHEDULED → FIELDWORK`; every subsequent finding is a self-transition `FIELDWORK → FIELDWORK`, so the audit may accumulate findings indefinitely while remaining in fieldwork. A finding recorded against an audit that already carries an opinion is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_FINDINGS`. A finding is not itself a stored, addressable resource — it is recorded purely as counter and state movement on the audit — so this endpoint answers 200 with the updated audit rather than 201 with a finding record.

**The opinion** is a single operation with two mutually exclusive outcomes, and the fork is decided entirely by the accumulated counters rather than by any input. A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` opines on an audit whose `state` is still `SCHEDULED` or `FIELDWORK` — an audit already in `QUALIFIED` or `UNQUALIFIED` can never be opined again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_IN_FIELDWORK`, while a still-`SCHEDULED` audit gets past this check only to fail the findings check below — and whose `finding_count` is at least 1, since an audit with no fieldwork at all cannot carry an opinion (`ERR_AUDIT_NO_FINDINGS`). The outcome is `QUALIFIED` when `critical_finding_count` is at least 1 or `finding_count` is at least 3, and `UNQUALIFIED` when `critical_finding_count` is 0 and `finding_count` is below 3. Both are terminal.

The two branches share two side effects and differ in exactly two. Both stamp `opinion_at` with the logical clock and `opined_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Both write the final audit score onto every subscriber with at least one incident discovered inside the audit period and set `assessed_subscriber_count` to the size of that assessed population, incrementing each assessed subscriber's `version`; the subscribers are walked in ascending `created_at` order, and a subscriber whose new score equals the one it already carried is still counted as assessed and still has its `version` moved, because the score is written unconditionally rather than recomputed for change. They differ in that a `QUALIFIED` opinion retires every certificate profile whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `validation_type` is `EV`, in ascending `created_at` order, moving each to `RETIRED`, incrementing each retired profile's `version` and setting `retired_profile_count` to the number retired, and files a communication with `kind` `AUDIT_QUALIFIED_NOTICE`; whereas an `UNQUALIFIED` opinion leaves every profile untouched with `retired_profile_count` remaining 0, and files a communication with `kind` `AUDIT_CLEAN_NOTICE`. Either notice carries its `audit_id` set, its `incident_id` null, its `root_program` copied from the audit rather than supplied, its `body` null because it is system-generated rather than authored, its `sent_at` at the logical clock, `within_deadline` always true, and `sent_by_user_id` the literal `system`. The audit's `version` increments exactly once on either branch, and the endpoint answers 200 with the opined audit rather than 201 with the notice.

**The final audit score** is a deliberately different computation from the operational one, over deliberately different data. Its input set is every incident attributed to the subscriber whose `discovered_at` falls inside the closed interval from `period_start` to `period_end` inclusive, regardless of state — so incidents the operational score discarded for being `CLOSED` re-enter here. Each incident contributes its category weight multiplied by a closed multiplier of 0.5 when its `state` is `CLOSED` and 1.0 otherwise, multiplied by an age decay computed from the age in whole days between the incident's `discovered_at` and the logical clock at the moment of the opinion: the decay is 1 minus 0.1 for each completed 30-day step of that age, floored at 0.4, so an incident 89 days old decays to 0.8 and anything 180 days old or older sits at the 0.4 floor. `risk_score_audit_final` is the sum of those terms, rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places once over the whole sum and never per term, then capped at 100.00. The same incident data therefore yields a different number in `risk_score_audit_final` than in `risk_score_operational`.

**Follow-up correspondence** closes the loop. A `COMPLIANCE_AUDITOR` files a response for an audit whose `state` is `QUALIFIED` or `UNQUALIFIED` — correspondence is only sent after an opinion exists, so an audit still in `SCHEDULED` or `FIELDWORK` is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_OPINED` — supplying the `root_program` addressed and the `body`. The record carries `kind` `ROOT_PROGRAM_RESPONSE`, its `audit_id` set, its `incident_id` null, its `sent_at` at the logical clock and its `sent_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and `within_deadline` is true when `sent_at` is at or before `opinion_at` plus 14 days — a response filed exactly on the fourteenth day is within the deadline. The response is 201. Communications are immutable records carrying only `id` and `created_at`; both an incident's and an audit's communications are listed in ascending `sent_at` order and may be filtered by `kind`.
