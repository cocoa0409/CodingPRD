# Vineyard Management & Custom Farming API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A custom-farming company does not own the vines it farms. It signs an agreement with a ranch owner, takes over the whole viticultural program for a crop year, and then bills the owner for a management fee, a share of the direct cost it incurred, and — when the fruit arrives at the winery above an agreed sugar level — a quality incentive. Everything the company does in between is simultaneously an agronomic decision and a billable event. A pruning crew booked onto a block is a labor commitment against a plan budget; a fungicide application is a material cost, a regulatory record, and a legal prohibition on entering that block for a stated number of hours; a truckload of Cabernet delivered to a winery is a tonnage draw on a receiving slot and a line on the client's invoice. Today those five views live in five spreadsheets that disagree with one another by the end of every August.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that holds all of them at once. The service tracks block inventory down to variety, clone, rootstock, trellis and vine geometry; it holds the annual farming plan and enforces its per-acre budgets; it schedules labor crews under both piece-rate and hourly pay structures with statutory minimum-wage and reporting-time guarantees; it computes deficit-irrigation recommendations from evapotranspiration and soil-moisture readings against a seasonal water cap; it maintains the pesticide compliance log, including restricted-entry and pre-harvest intervals that actively cancel field work already on the calendar; it converts cluster counts and berry samples into a governing yield estimate; and it books, dispatches and settles harvest loads into winery delivery windows. The single hardest requirement is that these subsystems are not independent — logging one spray application can retire a block's re-entry window, cancel two scheduled work orders, release their cost estimates from a plan ledger, and flip that plan's budget posture, all inside one request.

Two audiences read this API. Farming-company staff — managers, viticulturists, applicators, compliance auditors, controllers and crew foremen — drive the operational workflow. The ranch-owning client sees a deliberately narrow slice: its own ranch, its own contract, and the settlement statements produced against it. The specification below is written so that a reader who has never seen the underlying data model can reconstruct every stored field, every derived figure, every state transition and every rejection condition from the prose.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this API is mounted at the URL root; there is no versioned or namespaced prefix. Every path placeholder is spelled `{id}` and always carries a server-minted UUID. Every collection starts out empty on a freshly started server — nothing is seeded or preloaded, and every row the API ever reports was built through the endpoints of §4.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request must carry `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, blank, or `X-User-Role` does not name one of the seven roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is checked before authorisation, and both are checked before anything else about the request is examined.

The seven roles are `VineyardManager`, `Agronomist`, `Applicator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Controller`, `CrewSupervisor` and `ClientOwner`.

A `VineyardManager` is the farming company's operations lead and owns the widest surface: ranch registration, block planting, contract drafting and block enrollment, crew registration, work-order scheduling and cancellation, delivery-window creation, and the booking, receipt and rejection of harvest loads. An `Agronomist` is the viticulturist: pest scouting and spray recommendation are theirs exclusively, and they share farming-plan drafting, yield-estimate filing and irrigation scheduling with the `VineyardManager`. An `Applicator` is a licensed pesticide handler whose sole write capability is logging an application. A `ComplianceOfficer` is a regulatory auditor whose sole write capability is countersigning an applied record. A `Controller` handles money and governance: plan approval, plan return-for-revision, payroll release and contract settlement. A `CrewSupervisor` is the field foreman of exactly one labor crew and starts, completes and dispatches that crew's work; the supervisor shares the recording of applied irrigation water with the `VineyardManager`. A `ClientOwner` is the ranch-owning client, countersigns the custom-farming agreement, and reads its own ranch and contracts — and nothing else. Every role, without exception, may drive the logical clock.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against stored data:

- When `X-User-Role` is `CrewSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `LaborCrew`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `LaborCrew` compare the resource's crew foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, starting or completing a work order, and dispatching a harvest load, require that `X-User-ID` equal the `crew_id` recorded on that work order or load; a mismatch is `ERR_CREW_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). Recording applied irrigation water is the one shared `CrewSupervisor` capability that references no crew at all, so the ownership requirement is vacuously satisfied there and never rejects it. No read endpoint is crew-ownership-gated at all: a `CrewSupervisor` may read any crew, block, work order, irrigation event, delivery window or harvest load, and any listing of them, not merely the rows belonging to its own crew.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClientOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vineyard`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Vineyard` compare the resource's `vineyard_id` to `X-User-ID`. Reading a ranch, reading or listing contracts, and countersigning a contract all require that the reached `Vineyard` identifier equal `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is `ERR_VINEYARD_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `VineyardManager`, `Agronomist`, `Applicator`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `Controller`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a workflow demands an audit trail — as `scout_user_id` on a filed pest observation, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a farming plan, and as `recommended_by_user_id`, `applied_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a spray record — and it compares those recorded values against each other for segregation of duties, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it is read from the server's real wall clock and is never affected by the logical clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; supplied values must be integers no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0; supplied values must be integers no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the permitted range, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A supplied value is read as an optionally signed run of decimal digits and may be padded with surrounding whitespace, so `+25` and ` 25 ` are both the integer 25 while `25.0`, an empty value and any value carrying a non-digit are non-integers and are rejected.

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two listings order themselves by their own business chronology instead: the scouting observations filed against a block come back with the most recent `observed_at` first, and the yield estimates filed for a block come back with the most recent `sample_date` first. Both of those break a tie on their leading key by `created_at` descending, so of two rows sharing an `observed_at` or a `sample_date` the more recently filed one comes first.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Each documented filter parameter is matched by comparing the supplied text against the stored value's own textual form, so `plan_year=2026` and `farming_status=BEARING` both narrow as expected; no filter value is checked against a declared enumeration or against existence, so a value matching no row narrows the result to zero rows rather than being rejected as invalid, and a query parameter an endpoint does not document is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. `version` is 1 the moment the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation is caused by a direct request against the resource or by a cascade fired from somewhere else in the system; `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. Both `created_at` and `updated_at` are read from the server's real wall clock at millisecond resolution, deliberately not from the logical clock described two paragraphs below, so moving that clock never moves them; successive stamps are strictly increasing, which is what makes `created_at` ascending ordering and every `created_at` tie-break total. `Vineyard`, `Block`, `CustomFarmingContract`, `FarmingPlan`, `LaborCrew`, `WorkOrder`, `IrrigationEvent`, `SprayRecord`, `WineryDeliveryWindow` and `HarvestLoad` all behave this way.

Two resources are immutable event records rather than mutable resources, and they are deliberately leaner: a filed `PestObservation` and a filed `YieldEstimate` each carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field — those keys are absent from their representations entirely, not merely null. Nothing in the system ever edits a filed observation or a filed estimate; corrections are made by filing a newer one.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. The server's logical clock starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and stays there until a client moves it; it never falls back to the machine's real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and is open to all seven roles; it answers with a `data` object whose single `current_utc` key echoes the submitted string back verbatim. Every instant-valued input field in this API — `current_utc`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `window_start`, `window_end` and `delivery_eta` — is accepted in ISO 8601 form with or without seconds and with or without fractional seconds, and bearing a trailing `Z`, a numeric UTC offset, or no offset at all; a value bearing no offset is read as UTC, and one bearing an offset is converted to UTC before any comparison or storage decision is made against it. Where such a field is stored on a resource it is echoed back exactly as the client wrote it rather than in a normalised form, so the server compares the converted UTC instant while the client reads back its own representation. Every timestamp the server stamps as a side effect of a workflow — `observed_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `applied_at` on an irrigation event, `applied_at` on a spray record, `dispatched_at`, `received_at` and `rejected_at` — is read from this logical clock, and every re-entry-interval and pre-harvest-interval comparison is made against it. Those stamped instants, and the clearance instants derived from them, are rendered in ISO 8601 UTC to whole seconds when the instant carries no sub-second component and with exactly three fractional digits when it does — their precision therefore follows whatever precision the clock was last set to, and at the default clock instant they read to whole seconds. Moving the clock does not rewrite any stored row, but it does change what the next read of a block's `compliance_posture` returns.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity Order

Nested paths resolve strictly left to right. A request against a block's scouting observations resolves the block segment first; if no such block exists the answer is `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and nothing further about the request is inspected. Each entity has its own 404 code: a missing `Vineyard` yields `ERR_VINEYARD_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `Block` yields `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `CustomFarmingContract` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `FarmingPlan` yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `LaborCrew` yields `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `WorkOrder` yields `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `SprayRecord` yields `ERR_SPRAY_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `IrrigationEvent` yields `ERR_IRRIGATION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `WineryDeliveryWindow` yields `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND` and a missing `HarvestLoad` yields `ERR_HARVEST_LOAD_NOT_FOUND`. All ten are HTTP 404.

References that arrive inside a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved, and they too are resolved in a fixed order rather than in the order the client happened to serialise them: `vineyard_id` first, then `replaces_block_id`, then `block_id`, then `contract_id`, then `farming_plan_id`, then `crew_id`, then `delivery_window_id`, and finally `pest_observation_id`, whose failure yields `ERR_PEST_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A body-supplied `vineyard_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_VINEYARD_NOT_FOUND`; a `block_id` or `replaces_block_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`; a `farming_plan_id` yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; a `crew_id` yields `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`; a `delivery_window_id` yields `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`. Because the order is fixed, a request naming both a non-existent block and a non-existent crew always reports the block.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

Rejections are not evaluated in an arbitrary order, and the order is observable: exactly one error is reported per request, and it is the first one that fails in the ladder below. Later checks are never reached, so a request that violates three rules always reports the earliest of them. Within a single rung, sub-checks fire in the order written here.

1. **Authentication** — headers present, non-blank, and naming a declared role, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's permitted-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Body shape** (all write endpoints) — no unknown fields, which is checked first, before anything else about the body is examined and including on those endpoints that declare no body fields at all; then, field by field in a fixed server-side order, every required field present, every field of the declared JSON type, every enum value inside its declared set, and every number inside its declared range. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An absent or empty request body counts as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose fields are all optional accepts a request with no body; a body that is not valid JSON, or is valid JSON but not a JSON object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field declared as a number rejects a quoted numeric string and a JSON boolean, and a field declared as a whole number additionally rejects a JSON float, so `3.0` is not an acceptable integer. An explicit JSON `null` supplied for an optional field is treated as though the field were absent, so the field takes its documented default and any absence requirement on it is satisfied; an explicit `null` supplied for a required field is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Interval sanity** — `scheduled_end` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start` when scheduling a work order or an irrigation set; `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start` when opening a delivery window; `period_end` must be the same as or later than `period_start` when drafting a contract. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Method-conditional yield fields** — when filing a yield estimate whose `method` is `CLUSTER_COUNT`, `avg_cluster_weight_lb` is required and `berries_per_cluster`, `avg_berry_weight_g` and `days_to_harvest` must all be absent; when `method` is `BERRY_SAMPLE`, all three of those are required and `avg_cluster_weight_lb` must be absent. Either violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. **Pay-structure coherence** — when registering a crew whose `pay_structure` is `PIECE_RATE`, `piece_rate_per_vine` must be greater than zero; when it is `HOURLY`, `piece_rate_per_vine` must be exactly zero. Either violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. **Weigh-tag sanity** — when recording a winery receipt, `tare_tons` must be strictly less than `gross_tons` and `mog_deduct_pct` must be no less than 0 and no greater than 1, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. **Pagination bounds** on every list endpoint, as described in §2.3, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
9. **Path existence**, left to right, as described in §2.5.
10. **Body reference existence**, in the fixed order of §2.5.
11. **Ranch ownership** for a `ClientOwner`, else `ERR_VINEYARD_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.
12. **Crew ownership** for a `CrewSupervisor`, else `ERR_CREW_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.
13. **Stale-block rejection** on a spray application, else `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION`.
14. **Stale-window rejection** on a harvest-load booking, else `ERR_STALE_DELIVERY_WINDOW_VERSION`.
15. **State preconditions**, in this internal order: contract-must-be-`DRAFT` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`), contract-must-be-`ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`), plan-must-be-`DRAFT` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`), plan-must-be-`SUBMITTED` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_SUBMITTED`), revision cap (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`), cited-plan-must-be-`APPROVED` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`), block-must-not-be-`REMOVED` (`ERR_BLOCK_REMOVED`), work-order-must-be-`SCHEDULED` (`ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULED`), work-order-must-be-`IN_PROGRESS` (`ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`), work-order-must-not-be-terminal (`ERR_WORK_ORDER_TERMINAL`), payroll-axis coherence (`ERR_PAYROLL_STATE_INVALID`), spray-must-be-`RECOMMENDED` (`ERR_SPRAY_NOT_RECOMMENDED`), spray-must-be-`APPLIED` (`ERR_SPRAY_NOT_APPLIED`), irrigation-must-be-`SCHEDULED` (`ERR_IRRIGATION_NOT_SCHEDULED`), load-must-be-`PLANNED` (`ERR_LOAD_NOT_PLANNED`), load-must-be-`IN_TRANSIT` (`ERR_LOAD_NOT_IN_TRANSIT`), and finally crew-must-be-`ACTIVE` (`ERR_CREW_INACTIVE`).
16. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
17. **Safety and compliance**, in this internal order: crew certification for a booking inside an open re-entry interval (`ERR_CREW_NOT_PEST_CERTIFIED`), re-entry clearance at start (`ERR_REENTRY_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`), pre-harvest clearance at booking and at dispatch (`ERR_PREHARVEST_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`), and the pest action threshold behind a recommendation (`ERR_ACTION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`).
18. **Temporal conflicts** — crew double-booking (`ERR_CREW_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`) before delivery-window containment (`ERR_DELIVERY_ETA_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`).
19. **Referential uniqueness and coherence**, in this internal order: duplicate plan for a block and crop year (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_FOR_BLOCK_YEAR`), duplicate block code within a ranch (`ERR_DUPLICATE_BLOCK_CODE`), plan-block mismatch (`ERR_PLAN_BLOCK_MISMATCH`), ranch mismatch (`ERR_VINEYARD_MISMATCH`), block already enrolled (`ERR_BLOCK_ALREADY_ENROLLED`), contract with no enrolled block (`ERR_NO_ENROLLED_BLOCKS`), block not enrolled (`ERR_BLOCK_NOT_ENROLLED`), block with no yield estimate (`ERR_NO_YIELD_ESTIMATE`), and crew task capability (`ERR_CREW_TASK_MISMATCH`).
20. **Fiscal and capacity ceilings**, last of all: plan budget (`ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`), then delivery-window tonnage (`ERR_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), then block harvest tolerance (`ERR_HARVEST_EXCEEDS_ESTIMATE`).

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. Because the stale-version checks sit at rungs 13 and 14 — after existence and ownership but before every state, safety, referential and fiscal check — a client holding an outdated view of a block or a window learns that fact before it learns anything about capacity or compliance. And because the fiscal ceilings sit last, a work order that is both over budget and booked for an incapable crew reports the capability problem, never the budget.

### 2.7. Money, Tonnage and Water Precision

Currency amounts, tonnages and acreages are decimal numbers carried to two decimal places, and every rounding in this specification is half-up: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Water depths — every figure measured in acre-inches — are carried to three decimal places, likewise rounded half-up. Vine counts are integers. Every one of these figures is carried in the JSON body as a bare number rather than as a quoted string, and the quantised value is rendered in the shortest form that represents it, so trailing zeros do not survive serialisation: a `net_tons` of 12.40 appears as `12.4`, and a `managed_acres` of 0.00 appears as `0.0`.

Where a formula rounds at an intermediate step, the rounding is part of the specification and must be reproduced. The labor-cost calculation rounds gross pay first and then derives the payroll burden from the already-rounded gross, not from the raw product. The irrigation recommendation rounds at every step exactly as it is written out in §3.5. The period direct-cost roll-up rounds each block's contribution before adding it to the total and does not re-round the total afterwards. Conversely, two figures are deliberately compared without rounding: a farming plan's budget utilisation ratio and a delivery window's tonnage utilisation ratio are both evaluated on the raw quotient, never on a rounded one.

Vine geometry is the one place where rounding is replaced by truncation. Both derived vine figures are produced by discarding the fractional part, never by rounding: a computation of 691.428 vines per acre stores 691, and 13612.5 vines stores 13612.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Blocks and Delivery Windows

Two write operations sit downstream of derived data that other writers can move, and both are guarded by an explicit version check rather than by a state precondition. In each case the guard is a required, non-optional input field carrying the `version` the client last observed on the parent resource, declared as a whole number of at least 1.

Logging a spray application requires `expected_block_version`, carrying the `version` of the target block as the `Applicator` last read it. If the block's `version` has advanced since that read, the application is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Applying a spray is a legitimately repeatable operation — a second, different `RECOMMENDED` record on the same block would succeed on its own merits — so no state precondition covers this hazard. The hazard is specific: each application rewrites the block's re-entry and pre-harvest clearance instants and auto-cancels work orders derived from them, so an applicator holding a stale view of the block would be mixing a new application into a re-entry picture that no longer exists. The remedy is to re-read the block and resubmit with the observed `version`.

Booking a harvest load requires `expected_delivery_window_version`, carrying the `version` of the referenced window as the client last read it. If the window's `version` has advanced since that read, the booking is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DELIVERY_WINDOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Booking additional tonnage into a window is likewise a legitimately repeatable operation that succeeds whenever remaining capacity allows it; the hazard is that the client's capacity arithmetic was computed against a committed-tonnage figure that an intervening booking, receipt or rejection has already moved. The remedy is to re-read the window and resubmit with the observed `version`.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may be rejected for a version mismatch.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three approval chains in this system require that two different people be involved, and all three are enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against user identifiers already stored on the record. Approving a farming plan requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the plan's `submitted_by_user_id`. Logging a spray application requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the record's `recommended_by_user_id`. Countersigning an applied record requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from both the `recommended_by_user_id` and the `applied_by_user_id`. All three violations report the same code, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409), and all three are evaluated after every state precondition on the same operation has passed — so a self-approval attempt on a plan that is not `SUBMITTED` reports the plan state, not the duty conflict.

### 2.10. Ledger Propagation and Cascade Discipline

Almost every derived figure in this system is a state-filtered roll-up over child rows, and the recomputation is immediate: by the time a request returns, every aggregate it disturbed already reflects the change. A ranch's `block_count` and `managed_acres` count only its blocks that are `ESTABLISHING` or `BEARING`. A plan's `committed_cost` counts open work orders at their frozen estimate but completed ones at their reported actual. A window's `committed_tons` counts only bookings that have not yet been resolved at the winery. There is no batch job and no eventual convergence anywhere in the specification.

Cascades follow the parent-child edges of the domain and can run several levels deep. Filing a yield estimate touches the block's harvest ledger. Recording a winery receipt touches the block's harvest ledger, then the contract's accrued incentive, then the contract's projected billing, and separately the delivery window's tonnage. Logging a spray application reaches deepest of all: from the spray record to the block's compliance clearances, from those clearances to the execution state of scheduled work orders, from those cancellations to the plan's committed cost, and from that cost to the plan's budget posture. Every entity a cascade mutates has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, including entities the client never named in the request — and a cascade that does not fire increments nothing. Recomputing a roll-up is not by itself such a mutation: the rows whose `version` a cascade advances are exactly the ones the §3 narratives name, and a resource whose only involvement is that one of its own live roll-ups now reads differently is left untouched. Concretely, a `FarmingPlan`'s `version` moves only on its own submission, return and approval, never when a work order, spray application or irrigation record moves its `committed_cost`, `remaining_budget` or `budget_posture`; and no operation anywhere in this API mutates a `LaborCrew` or a `Vineyard` row at all, so each of those keeps `version` 1 and an `updated_at` equal to its `created_at` for its whole life however far its `scheduled_work_order_count`, `block_count` or `managed_acres` may travel. When a conditional cascade's guard fails (a plan approved too early in a block's life, a work order safely outside a new re-entry window, a crew that holds pest certification), the untouched resource keeps its existing `version`, and that absence of a bump is itself part of the contract.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Ranch Registration and Block Inventory

Onboarding begins with the ranch. A `VineyardManager` registers a client ranch by supplying its `name` (a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters, as it appears on the client contract), the `owner_legal_name` of the legal entity that owns the ranch and will sign the custom-farming agreement (non-blank, 1 to 160 characters), the `ava_region` naming the American Viticultural Area the ranch sits in (non-blank, 1 to 80 characters), and a `client_contact_email` for plan and settlement correspondence, which must be a non-blank string containing exactly one at-sign and which, alone among the string fields here, carries no maximum length. A freshly registered ranch reports a `block_count` of 0 and `managed_acres` of 0.00.

Those two ranch figures are live roll-ups, not stored counters. `block_count` is the number of blocks belonging to the ranch whose `farming_status` is either `ESTABLISHING` or `BEARING`, and `managed_acres` is the sum of `acres` over that same set, rounded to two decimal places. Both are recomputed whenever a block is planted for the ranch and whenever any of its blocks changes `farming_status` — which means a block driven to `REMOVED` silently leaves both aggregates.

Planting a block is the `VineyardManager`'s single richest create operation. The payload names the owning ranch through `vineyard_id` and carries the operator-facing `block_code` label (1 to 24 characters), the `variety` planted — one of `CABERNET_SAUVIGNON`, `CHARDONNAY`, `PINOT_NOIR`, `ZINFANDEL` or `SYRAH` — the certified clonal selection identifier `clone` (a non-blank string of 1 to 24 characters, such as FPS-08), the `rootstock` the scion is grafted onto — one of `ROOTSTOCK_1103P`, `ROOTSTOCK_110R`, `ROOTSTOCK_SO4`, `RIPARIA_GLOIRE` or `ST_GEORGE` — and the `trellis_system` installed, which is one of `VSP`, `SPRAWL`, `QUADRILATERAL_CORDON`, `HEAD_TRAINED` or `GDC`. The planting geometry arrives as `planting_year` (a calendar year between 1900 and 2100 inclusive), the in-row `vine_spacing_ft` and between-row `row_spacing_ft` in feet, and the planted `acres`; all three measurements must be strictly greater than zero. The `block_code` must be unique within the owning ranch: a second block carrying a code another block on the same ranch already uses is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BLOCK_CODE`. That uniqueness scan spans every block on the ranch whatever its `farming_status`, so retiring a block never frees its code for reuse.

From the spacing pair the server derives vine density once and then freezes it. There are 43,560 square feet in an acre; dividing that by the product of the in-row and between-row spacings and discarding any fraction gives `vines_per_acre`. Multiplying `vines_per_acre` by the block's `acres` and again discarding any fraction gives `vine_count`. Both steps truncate rather than round, and neither figure is ever recomputed after create even if the block is later re-measured — a 5-foot by 8-foot planting on 12.5 acres is stored as 1089 vines per acre and 13,612 vines, not 13,613. A new block opens with `farming_status` `ESTABLISHING`, `compliance_posture` `CLEAR` and `harvest_posture` `NOT_ESTIMATED`, and its `contract_id`, `contracted_acres` and `per_acre_fee_override` are all null until the block is enrolled under an agreement. The supplied `vine_spacing_ft` and `row_spacing_ft` are stored exactly as given, never quantised, and are echoed back on the block as bare JSON numbers rendered the way §2.7 renders one — a spacing supplied as `5` reads back as `5.0` and one supplied as `5.25` reads back as `5.25` — while `vines_per_acre` and `vine_count` come back as bare integers.

Replants are recorded through the optional `replaces_block_id`, which names the predecessor block the new planting supersedes and stays null for an original planting. When it is supplied, the predecessor must belong to the same ranch as the new block — a cross-ranch lineage claim is refused with `ERR_VINEYARD_MISMATCH` — and the predecessor must not already be retired, so naming a block whose `farming_status` is already `REMOVED` is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_REMOVED`. On success the predecessor is driven from whichever live state it was in to `REMOVED`, whether it was `ESTABLISHING` or `BEARING`; its `version` is incremented by 1; and the ranch's `block_count` and `managed_acres` are recomputed so that the retired block drops out of both. `REMOVED` is terminal — no operation ever brings a block back — and it closes the block to almost everything downstream: farming-plan drafting, submission and approval, work-order scheduling, irrigation scheduling and recording, spray recommendation and application, pest-observation filing, yield-estimate filing and harvest-load booking are all refused against a `REMOVED` block with `ERR_BLOCK_REMOVED`.

A block's stored representation is also the system's compliance and harvest dashboard. Reading one block returns its `rei_clear_at` and `phi_clear_at` clearance instants, its derived `compliance_posture`, its water total `season_applied_inches`, its harvest ledger of `governing_estimate_tons`, `committed_harvest_tons`, `received_tons`, `remaining_estimated_tons` and `harvest_posture`, and the `version` an `Applicator` will need to quote back. Blocks can also be listed and narrowed by `vineyard_id`, by `contract_id`, by `replaces_block_id` (to find the successor of a retired planting) and by `farming_status`.

### 3.2. Custom-Farming Agreements: Drafting, Enrollment and Countersignature

A custom-farming agreement is drafted by a `VineyardManager` against a ranch named by `vineyard_id` and opens in `DRAFT`. Its commercial terms are `per_acre_annual_fee`, the default management fee charged per contracted acre per settlement period (zero or more); `cost_pass_through_pct`, the fraction of direct farming cost billed back to the client on top of that fee (no less than 0 and no more than 1); `quality_incentive_per_ton`, paid to the farming company for each received ton whose sugar reading clears the bar (zero or more); `quality_brix_threshold`, the degrees brix a received load must reach or exceed to earn that incentive (strictly greater than zero); and `period_overhead_pool`, a fixed dollar pool allocated across enrolled blocks by contracted-acre share on every settlement run (zero or more). Coverage dates arrive as `period_start` and `period_end`, both written as YYYY-MM-DD, with `period_end` required to be the same as or later than `period_start`. A newly drafted agreement reports `settled_period_count` 0, `enrolled_block_count` 0, `total_contracted_acres` 0.00, `accrued_quality_incentive` 0.00, `projected_total_billable` 0.00, a null `client_signature_name` and a null `last_settlement_total`.

Blocks join the agreement one at a time while it is still `DRAFT`, and enrollment is the only operation that writes a block's contract fields. The `VineyardManager` names the `block_id`, states the `contracted_acres` being enrolled (zero or more), and may supply a `per_acre_fee_override` (also zero or more) — a block-specific per-acre management fee that displaces the agreement's `per_acre_annual_fee` for that block alone; omitting it leaves the field null, which means the contract rate applies. Enrollment is the one block-scoped write that is not gated on the block's `farming_status`, so even a block already driven to `REMOVED` may be enrolled. The operation is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT` once the agreement has left `DRAFT`, with `ERR_VINEYARD_MISMATCH` when the block belongs to a different ranch than the agreement covers, and with `ERR_BLOCK_ALREADY_ENROLLED` when the block's `contract_id` is already set — a block may only ever be enrolled under one agreement, and `contract_id` is never cleared once written. Enrollment sets the block's `contract_id`, `contracted_acres` and `per_acre_fee_override`, increments the block's `version` by 1, and then recomputes the agreement's `enrolled_block_count` (the number of blocks pointing at it) and `total_contracted_acres` (the sum of their `contracted_acres`, rounded to two decimal places), refreshes its `projected_total_billable`, and increments the agreement's own `version` by 1. The updated block is what comes back to the caller.

Underneath `projected_total_billable` sits the **management fee base**, the figure that both the projection and every settlement run are built on. For each block enrolled under the agreement, take that block's `per_acre_fee_override` when it is set and the agreement's `per_acre_annual_fee` when it is not, multiply by that block's `contracted_acres`, and add the results together; the single sum is rounded to two decimal places at the end. Three blocks at 12.5, 4.0 and 3.6 contracted acres against a `per_acre_annual_fee` of 950.00 therefore contribute 11,875.00, 3,800.00 and 3,420.00 for a base of 19,095.00. Enrolled blocks stay in this sum even after their `farming_status` becomes `REMOVED`: the fee follows the contracted acreage, not the living vines. `projected_total_billable` is that base plus the agreement's `accrued_quality_incentive`, rounded to two decimal places — pass-through and volume discount are excluded from the projection by design, and only a settlement run computes them.

Countersignature is the client's move and the client's alone. A `ClientOwner` whose `X-User-ID` matches the agreement's `vineyard_id` supplies a `client_signature_name` (a non-blank string of 1 to 160 characters), which drives the agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, stores the supplied name, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1. An agreement with nothing enrolled cannot be activated: when `enrolled_block_count` is 0 the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_ENROLLED_BLOCKS`. An agreement that has already left `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`. Because enrollment is confined to `DRAFT`, the enrolled block set is frozen at the instant of activation and the acreage the client signs for is the acreage that will be billed.

### 3.3. The Annual Farming Plan: Budgets, Review Loop and Approval

Every block gets one farming program per crop year, and nothing operational may happen on a block until that program is approved. Either a `VineyardManager` or an `Agronomist` may draft it, naming the `block_id` it covers and the `plan_year` it governs (a year between 2000 and 2100 inclusive). Only one plan may exist for a given block and crop year: a second draft for the same pairing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_FOR_BLOCK_YEAR`. The plan's `quality_target` — `PREMIUM`, `STANDARD` or `BULK` — declares the fruit quality tier the block will be farmed to, and it is consulted far downstream, because it selects the deficit factor every irrigation recommendation on the block is scaled by. Four per-acre budget lines arrive with the draft, each of which must be zero or more: `pruning_budget_per_acre` for the pruning program, `canopy_budget_per_acre` for suckering and leafing, `irrigation_budget_per_acre` for the water program, and `pest_budget_per_acre` for pest and disease control. A fifth figure, `seasonal_water_cap_inches`, sets the applied-water ceiling in acre-inches for the crop year and must also be zero or more.

The plan's `total_budget` is computed once at draft time and never revised: add the four per-acre lines together and multiply the sum by the block's planted `acres` — the block's planted acreage, deliberately not its contracted acreage — then round to two decimal places. Per-acre lines of 420.00, 310.00, 180.00 and 265.00 on a 12.5-acre block therefore give a `total_budget` of 14,687.50. A freshly drafted plan sits in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0, `committed_cost` 0.00, `remaining_budget` equal to `total_budget`, `budget_posture` `WITHIN_BUDGET`, and both `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null.

`committed_cost` is the plan's live consumption ledger and it is state-filtered on three child collections at once. Its labor component walks every work order drawing on the plan: an order that is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` contributes its frozen `estimated_labor_cost`; an order that is `COMPLETED` contributes its `actual_labor_cost` instead, so the estimate is displaced rather than added to; and an order that is `CANCELLED` contributes its `actual_labor_cost` when one was written and contributes nothing at all when that field is null. Its material component is the sum of `material_cost` over the plan's spray records whose `spray_status` is `APPLIED` or `VERIFIED` — a merely `RECOMMENDED` record contributes nothing. Its water component is the sum of `water_cost` over the plan's irrigation events whose `irrigation_status` is `APPLIED` — a `SCHEDULED` event contributes nothing. The three components are added and the total rounded to two decimal places; `remaining_budget` is then `total_budget` less `committed_cost`, rounded to two decimal places, and may be negative. To make the arithmetic concrete: against a `total_budget` of 14,687.50, two scheduled work orders carrying estimates of 10,858.25 and 2,505.75 give a `committed_cost` of 13,364.00 and a `remaining_budget` of 1,323.50; if the first order is then auto-cancelled by a spray application whose `material_cost` is 812.50, the ledger falls to a `committed_cost` of 3,318.25 and a `remaining_budget` of 11,369.25.

`budget_posture` is recomputed in the same breath as `committed_cost`, from the unrounded ratio of `committed_cost` to `total_budget`. When the ratio is at most 0.80 the posture is `WITHIN_BUDGET`; when it is greater than 0.80 but at most 1.00 the posture is `AT_RISK`; when it is greater than 1.00 the posture is `EXHAUSTED`. Both upper edges are inclusive, so a plan whose consumption lands exactly on its budget — 1,028.00 committed against 1,028.00 budgeted, a ratio of exactly 1.00 — is `AT_RISK` and not `EXHAUSTED`. A plan whose `total_budget` is exactly zero is handled separately, since the ratio is undefined: it is `EXHAUSTED` when any cost at all has been committed and `WITHIN_BUDGET` when `committed_cost` is still zero. Reaching `EXHAUSTED` at all requires a cost that is not budget-gated, which in practice means recorded irrigation water.

Submission is the `VineyardManager`'s act. A plan may be submitted only from `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT` — and only while its block is not `REMOVED`, otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_REMOVED`. The submitter may attach an optional `submission_note`. That note, the `revision_note` a return requires and the optional `approval_note` an approval may carry are each strings of at most 1,000 characters — the two optional ones may be blank as well as omitted, while `revision_note` must be non-blank — and none of the three is persisted: each is validated and then discarded, so no note ever appears in a plan's representation. The transition `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` stores the submitter's `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id` and increments the plan's `version` by 1.

Review belongs to the `Controller`, who has two mutually exclusive answers. Sending the plan back for rework requires a `revision_note` and moves it `SUBMITTED → DRAFT`, incrementing `revision_count` by 1 and clearing `submitted_by_user_id` back to null so that the next submission records whoever actually resubmits. The loop is capped at two completed rounds: a return attempt made when `revision_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` and the plan remains `SUBMITTED`, which is what forces the review cycle to terminate. Either answer requires the plan to be in `SUBMITTED` to begin with; a plan in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_SUBMITTED`.

Approval is the other answer, may carry an optional `approval_note`, and moves the plan `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`. The approving `Controller`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from the plan's `submitted_by_user_id`; a self-approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The plan's block must not be `REMOVED`. On success the approver's identifier is stored as `approved_by_user_id` and the plan's `version` is incremented by 1. Approval also carries a guarded cascade into the block itself: a young block bears fruit in its third leaf, so when the plan's `plan_year` is at least three greater than the block's `planting_year`, the block is driven `ESTABLISHING → BEARING` and the block's `version` is incremented by 1. When the plan year falls short of that mark the block stays `ESTABLISHING` and its `version` is not touched at all. A block already `BEARING` is likewise unaffected. `APPROVED` is terminal — submission, return and approval are all refused afterwards — and it is the gate everything operational waits on: work orders, irrigation events and spray recommendations may only cite a plan whose `plan_status` is `APPROVED`, and citing one in any other state is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`. Plans may be listed and narrowed by `block_id`, by `plan_year` and by `plan_status`, and reading one plan returns its live `committed_cost`, `remaining_budget` and `budget_posture`.

### 3.4. Crews, Work Orders and the Two Independent Axes of a Task

Labor is organised into crews, registered by a `VineyardManager`. A crew carries a `crew_name` for schedules and payroll (non-blank, 1 to 80 characters), a `pay_structure` of either `PIECE_RATE` or `HOURLY`, a `crew_size` of at least one worker, a straight-time `base_hourly_rate` per worker (zero or more), a `piece_rate_per_vine` in dollars per vine worked (zero or more, and required to be exactly zero for an `HOURLY` crew), and a `vines_per_hour_standard` productivity reference that must be strictly greater than zero. That last figure is planning metadata for operators sizing a job; no calculation in this system consumes it. A crew also declares `task_capabilities`, a non-empty list drawn without duplicates from `PRUNING`, `SUCKERING`, `LEAFING` and `HARVEST` and echoed back in exactly the order it was supplied rather than normalised or sorted, and a `pest_certified` flag — a required boolean carrying no default — that is true only when every member holds a handler certification — the flag that decides whether the crew may enter a block during an open re-entry interval. `crew_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` and defaults to `ACTIVE` when the field is omitted; only an `ACTIVE` crew may be scheduled or dispatched, and referencing an `INACTIVE` one is refused with `ERR_CREW_INACTIVE`. Exactly four operations consult that flag — scheduling a work order, starting one, booking a harvest load and dispatching one; reporting finished work, cancelling an order, moving the payroll axis and every irrigation operation proceed whatever the crew's `crew_status`. A new crew reports `scheduled_work_order_count` 0; thereafter that figure is the live count of the crew's work orders whose `execution_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and it is recomputed on every event that adds to or removes from that set.

Scheduling a work order is a `VineyardManager` operation that ties three things together: the `block_id` being worked, the `farming_plan_id` whose budget the work draws against, and the `crew_id` doing the work. The payload states the `task_type` — `PRUNING`, `SUCKERING`, `LEAFING` or `HARVEST` — the half-open interval bounded by `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, the `planned_vines` the crew is expected to work (zero or more) and the `planned_hours` it is expected to spend (strictly greater than zero). Several coherence rules bite here. The cited plan must be `APPROVED`, or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`. The plan must belong to the very block named in the payload — a plan may not be charged for work on a different block — and a mismatch is `ERR_PLAN_BLOCK_MISMATCH`. The block must not be `REMOVED`, or `ERR_BLOCK_REMOVED`. The crew must be `ACTIVE`, and the requested `task_type` must appear in that crew's `task_capabilities`, or `ERR_CREW_TASK_MISMATCH`. A successful create freezes the order's cost estimate, sets `execution_status` to `SCHEDULED` and `payroll_status` to `NOT_PAYABLE`, leaves `actual_vines`, `actual_hours`, `actual_labor_cost`, `cancel_reason`, `started_at` and `completed_at` null, then recomputes the plan's `committed_cost`, `remaining_budget` and `budget_posture` and the crew's `scheduled_work_order_count`.

The labor-cost calculation is the system's most-used formula and it runs in two variants over identical arithmetic. The variant used at scheduling time consumes `planned_vines` and `planned_hours` and writes `estimated_labor_cost`, which is frozen on the row and never recomputed for the life of the order. The variant used when the work is reported consumes `actual_vines` and `actual_hours` and writes `actual_labor_cost`. Because the formula is the same, the same crew reporting more or fewer vines than planned yields a genuinely different figure, and it is that difference that moves the plan ledger at completion. The arithmetic itself: for a `PIECE_RATE` crew, the structural gross is the vine count for the active variant multiplied by the crew's `piece_rate_per_vine`. For an `HOURLY` crew, hours are split at the eight-hour standard shift — regular hours are the lesser of the reported hours and eight, and overtime hours are the excess above eight, or zero when there is no excess — and the structural gross is the crew's `crew_size` multiplied by its `base_hourly_rate` multiplied by the sum of the regular hours plus one and a half times the overtime hours. Independently of pay structure, a minimum-wage make-whole floor is computed as the `crew_size` multiplied by a statutory rate of 16.50 dollars per worker-hour multiplied by the hours for the active variant. Gross pay is the greater of the structural gross and that floor, rounded to two decimal places. The payroll burden is 28.50 percent of that already-rounded gross pay, itself rounded to two decimal places. The labor cost is gross pay plus burden, rounded to two decimal places. Two worked cases show why the floor matters: a twelve-worker `PIECE_RATE` crew at 0.65 dollars per vine reporting 13,000 vines over 10.0 hours has a structural gross of 8,450.00 against a floor of 1,980.00, so gross pay is 8,450.00, burden 2,408.25 and labor cost 10,858.25 — but the same crew reporting only 1,000 vines over the same 10.0 hours has a structural gross of just 650.00, the 1,980.00 floor wins, and the labor cost is 2,544.30. An eight-worker `HOURLY` crew at 22.00 dollars per hour working 10.0 hours earns eight regular and two overtime hours for a gross of 1,936.00 and a labor cost of 2,487.76; the same crew working exactly 8.0 hours costs 1,809.28.

Two gates guard the calendar and the wallet, and they are checked in that order. The crew-conflict test compares the new half-open interval against every existing work order for the same crew whose `execution_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; orders belonging to other crews are irrelevant, and `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` orders release their interval entirely. Two orders on the same block need no separation at all, so back-to-back work where one interval ends exactly when the next begins is permitted; two orders on different blocks require one hour of travel separation, and the pair conflicts until a full 60 minutes lies between them. Formally, the new interval conflicts with an existing one when the new `scheduled_start` is strictly earlier than the existing `scheduled_end` extended forward by the applicable gap, and simultaneously the existing `scheduled_start` is strictly earlier than the new `scheduled_end` extended forward by the same gap; the gap is zero minutes for a same-block pair and 60 minutes for a different-block pair. A conflict is refused with `ERR_CREW_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Only after the calendar is clear does the budget gate run: the plan's current `committed_cost` plus this order's freshly computed `estimated_labor_cost`, rounded to two decimal places, must not exceed the plan's `total_budget`. Equality is permitted; anything above is refused with `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. This gate is the reason a plan cannot be pushed past its budget by labor alone.

Once scheduled, an order advances along two entirely independent state dimensions, and confusing them is the most common modelling error in this domain. The **execution** dimension runs `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from either of the first two states; both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. The **payroll** dimension runs `NOT_PAYABLE → PENDING → APPROVED → PAID`, with `PAID` terminal. Neither dimension is derivable from the other, and each is driven by a distinct set of operations.

The crew's own `CrewSupervisor` starts the task, moving it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamping `started_at` from the logical clock. Starting requires that the order actually be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULED`, and that the crew be `ACTIVE`. It also requires re-entry clearance, discussed in §3.6. Starting deliberately leaves `payroll_status` at `NOT_PAYABLE`: no pay is owed merely because work began. The same supervisor reports the finished work, supplying `actual_vines` (zero or more) and `actual_hours` (strictly greater than zero) under exactly the bounds their planned counterparts carry; this requires the order to be `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, and requires nothing else — neither the crew's `crew_status` nor the block's `farming_status` is consulted at completion. Completion moves execution `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at`, writes `actual_labor_cost` from the reported-work variant of the labor formula, advances payroll `NOT_PAYABLE → PENDING`, and recomputes the plan's `committed_cost`, `remaining_budget` and `budget_posture` — where the frozen estimate is now displaced by the actual — along with the crew's `scheduled_work_order_count`.

Cancellation is the `VineyardManager`'s lever and its consequences differ sharply depending on where the order was. It requires a `cancel_reason` and is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_TERMINAL` on an order that is already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. An operator supplies `WEATHER`, `CLIENT_REQUEST` or `CREW_UNAVAILABLE`; the fourth permitted value, `REENTRY_RESTRICTION`, is written only by the compliance cascade of §3.6. Cancelling from `SCHEDULED` reverses the commitment outright: `actual_labor_cost` stays null and `payroll_status` stays `NOT_PAYABLE`, because no pay is owed for work never started; the plan's ledger releases the estimate; the crew's `scheduled_work_order_count` is recomputed; and the crew's half-open interval is freed for re-booking. Cancelling from `IN_PROGRESS` substitutes reporting-time pay instead: the crew showed up, so a flat statutory guarantee is owed regardless of what was planned or performed. That guarantee is the crew's `crew_size` multiplied by the same 16.50 dollars per worker-hour multiplied by a four-hour call-in minimum, rounded to two decimal places, plus the same 28.50 percent burden on that rounded gross, itself rounded, with the two added and rounded again — a twelve-worker crew therefore yields a gross of 792.00, a burden of 225.72 and an `actual_labor_cost` of 1,017.72. Neither `planned_vines`, `planned_hours`, `actual_vines` nor `actual_hours` participates in that figure. Cancelling from `IN_PROGRESS` also advances payroll `NOT_PAYABLE → PENDING`, since money is now owed, and both cancellation paths recompute the plan ledger and the crew's scheduled count. An order cancelled from `SCHEDULED` is permanently stuck at `NOT_PAYABLE`, so the combined position of `CANCELLED` execution and `NOT_PAYABLE` payroll is terminal on both axes.

The payroll axis is moved only by a `Controller`, and only through the payroll operation, which takes a `payroll_action` of `APPROVE` or `PAY` and an optional `payroll_note` — a string of at most 1,000 characters that may be blank as well as omitted and that is validated and then discarded rather than persisted, exactly like the three plan notes of §3.3. `APPROVE` moves `PENDING → APPROVED` and `PAY` moves `APPROVED → PAID`; each increments the order's `version` by 1 and neither touches `execution_status` in any way. Attempting `APPROVE` on an order whose `payroll_status` is not `PENDING`, or `PAY` on one whose `payroll_status` is not `APPROVED` — including the tempting shortcut of paying a `PENDING` order without approving it first — is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_STATE_INVALID`. Reading one order returns both state dimensions and both cost figures side by side; the collection can be narrowed by `block_id`, `crew_id`, `farming_plan_id`, `execution_status` and `payroll_status`. A crew's own record can be read back at any time to see its live `scheduled_work_order_count`.

### 3.5. Irrigation Scheduling and Deficit Water Accounting

Deficit irrigation is how a vineyard buys quality: the vines are deliberately given less water than they transpire, and how much less depends on the fruit tier the block is being farmed to. Either a `VineyardManager` or an `Agronomist` schedules an irrigation set against a `block_id` and a `farming_plan_id`, bounded by `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` with the end strictly later than the start. The cited plan must be `APPROVED` and must belong to the named block, and the block must not be `REMOVED`; the corresponding refusals are `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_PLAN_BLOCK_MISMATCH` and `ERR_BLOCK_REMOVED`. The payload carries the atmospheric and soil readings for the interval: `et0_inches`, the reference evapotranspiration in inches (zero or more); `crop_coefficient`, the phenology-stage crop coefficient (strictly greater than zero); `soil_moisture_pct`, the measured volumetric soil moisture as a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive; `field_capacity_pct`, the soil's field capacity on the same 0-to-100 scale; `root_zone_depth_in`, the managed root-zone depth in inches (strictly greater than zero); `effective_rain_inches`, rainfall credited against the interval (zero or more); and `water_unit_cost_per_acre_inch`, the dollar cost of an acre-inch of applied water (zero or more).

Every derived water figure is computed at create and frozen on the row, so a recommendation is a historical artefact that later events cannot silently rewrite. The chain runs as follows, with each step rounded to three decimal places as written before feeding the next. The `deficit_factor` is copied from a fixed table keyed on the plan's `quality_target`: a `PREMIUM` plan uses 0.55, a `STANDARD` plan 0.75, and a `BULK` plan 1.00. `et_demand_inches` is `et0_inches` multiplied by `crop_coefficient`. `target_inches` is `et_demand_inches` multiplied by the `deficit_factor`. `soil_shortfall_inches` is the difference between `field_capacity_pct` and `soil_moisture_pct`, converted from a percentage to a fraction by dividing by 100, multiplied by `root_zone_depth_in`, multiplied by an available-water fraction of 0.16, with a floor of zero applied so that soil at or above field capacity yields no shortfall. `refill_inches` is half of `soil_shortfall_inches` — the programme only refills the profile part-way in any single set. `gross_requirement_inches` is `target_inches` plus `refill_inches` less `effective_rain_inches`, floored at 0.000 so that a wet interval never produces a negative requirement. `seasonal_allowance_inches` is the plan's `seasonal_water_cap_inches` less the block's `season_applied_inches` as it stands **before** this event exists, again floored at 0.000. Finally `recommended_inches` is the smallest of three quantities: `gross_requirement_inches`, a per-set hardware ceiling of 1.500 acre-inches, and `seasonal_allowance_inches`.

The `quality_target` alone can change the answer. With a reference evapotranspiration of 1.40 inches, a crop coefficient of 0.72, soil moisture of 42 percent against a field capacity of 60 percent, a 36-inch root zone, 0.10 inches of effective rain, a seasonal cap of 9.000 inches and nothing yet applied, the demand is 1.008 inches, the soil shortfall 1.037 inches and the refill 0.519 inches; a `PREMIUM` plan targets 0.554 inches and recommends 0.973 inches, while a `STANDARD` plan on byte-identical readings targets 0.756 inches and recommends 1.175 inches. The two ceilings bite in their own right: a hot `BULK` interval with a gross requirement of 3.622 inches is clipped to the 1.500-inch per-set maximum, and a block that has already consumed most of its seasonal cap so that only 0.500 inches of allowance remains is clipped to 0.500 regardless of demand. Fully charged soil with a soaking rain — soil moisture equal to field capacity and an inch of effective rain against a modest demand — produces a shortfall of zero, a refill of 0.000 and a gross requirement of 0.000, so the recommendation is 0.000 inches and the set is worth cancelling rather than running.

A scheduled event opens with `irrigation_status` `SCHEDULED` and with `applied_inches`, `water_cost` and `applied_at` all null. Recording the water actually applied is available to both the `VineyardManager` and the block's `CrewSupervisor`, takes `applied_inches` (zero or more), and requires that the event still be `SCHEDULED` — otherwise `ERR_IRRIGATION_NOT_SCHEDULED` — and that the block not be `REMOVED`. Note carefully that the recorded figure is what actually went on the ground; it is not required to equal `recommended_inches`, which remains on the row as the recommendation it always was. The transition `SCHEDULED → APPLIED` stamps `applied_at` from the logical clock and computes `water_cost` as `applied_inches` multiplied by the block's `acres` multiplied by `water_unit_cost_per_acre_inch`, rounded to two decimal places. It then raises the block's `season_applied_inches` — the sum of `applied_inches` across the block's `APPLIED` events, rounded to three decimal places — and increments the block's `version` by 1. Finally it folds `water_cost` into the plan's `committed_cost` and recomputes `remaining_budget` and `budget_posture`.

That last cascade is deliberately unguarded. Unlike work-order scheduling, recording applied water carries **no** budget check: the water is already in the soil, so the cost is a recorded actual rather than a commitment the system may refuse. This is precisely how a plan's `budget_posture` becomes `EXHAUSTED` — no other operation in the system can push the utilisation ratio past 1.00. It also has a compounding effect on planning: because `seasonal_allowance_inches` is measured against the block's `season_applied_inches` at the moment an event is created, every recorded application shrinks the allowance available to every irrigation set scheduled afterwards, and therefore shrinks their `recommended_inches` once the seasonal ceiling becomes the binding constraint. Irrigation events can be read individually with every derived water figure intact, and listed with narrowing by `block_id`, `farming_plan_id` and `irrigation_status`.

### 3.6. Pest Scouting, Spray Applications and Interval Compliance

Nothing may be sprayed on a hunch. An `Agronomist` walking a block files a scouting observation against it, naming the `pest_code` scouted — `POWDERY_MILDEW`, `VINE_MEALYBUG`, `BOTRYTIS` or `LEAFHOPPER` — the `incidence_pct` of scouted units showing the pest (a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive), and a `severity_index` giving the ordinal severity of the infestation as a whole number between 0 and 5 inclusive. An optional free-text `scouting_note` of up to 500 characters may accompany it and defaults to null. The server stamps `observed_at` from the logical clock and records the filing agronomist's `X-User-ID` as `scout_user_id`. It also copies the registered action threshold for the observed pest onto the observation as `action_threshold_pct` — the threshold is a property of the pest, keyed on `pest_code`, and is frozen onto the observation so that a later revision of the threshold table cannot retroactively change what a scout saw. The registered thresholds are fixed at 5.0 for `POWDERY_MILDEW`, 2.0 for `VINE_MEALYBUG`, 3.0 for `BOTRYTIS` and 10.0 for `LEAFHOPPER` — and it sets `exceeds_threshold` to true exactly when the observed `incidence_pct` is greater than or equal to that `action_threshold_pct`, and to false otherwise. A filed observation is immutable, so the way to correct a bad count is to file another one. Filing one touches nothing else in the system: unlike a yield estimate, a scouting filing does not increment the block's `version`. Observations against a block can be listed, newest `observed_at` first, by the `VineyardManager`, the `Agronomist`, the `Applicator` and the `ComplianceOfficer`.

A spray recommendation is the `Agronomist`'s exclusive act and it must cite its evidence. The payload names the `block_id` to be treated, the `farming_plan_id` whose pest budget will absorb the cost, and the `pest_observation_id` justifying the application, together with the `product_name` (the registered trade name, non-blank, 1 to 120 characters), the `active_ingredient` recorded for the compliance log (non-blank, 1 to 120 characters), the label's `rei_hours` restricted-entry interval in whole hours (zero or more), the label's `phi_days` pre-harvest interval in whole days (zero or more), the `rate_per_acre` application rate in product units (strictly greater than zero) and the `material_cost` in dollars (zero or more). The cited observation must have `exceeds_threshold` true — a spray may not be recommended below the pest's action threshold, and citing a sub-threshold observation is refused with `ERR_ACTION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. That threshold test is the only coherence check made on the citation: the observation is not required to have been filed against the `block_id` being treated, so an observation filed on one block may lawfully justify a recommendation on another. The plan must be `APPROVED`, must belong to the named block, and the block must not be `REMOVED`. A created record opens in `RECOMMENDED` with the recommending agronomist's `X-User-ID` stored as `recommended_by_user_id` and with `applied_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id`, `applicator_license_no`, `wind_speed_mph`, `applied_at`, `rei_clear_at` and `phi_clear_at` all null. Critically, a `RECOMMENDED` record leaves the plan's `committed_cost` untouched: `material_cost` joins the ledger only when the spray is actually applied.

Logging the application is the `Applicator`'s exclusive act and the single most consequential write in this API. The payload carries the `applicator_license_no` captured in the field (a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters), the `wind_speed_mph` logged at the time of application (zero or more), and `expected_block_version` as described in §2.8. The record must be `RECOMMENDED`, otherwise `ERR_SPRAY_NOT_RECOMMENDED`; the block must not be `REMOVED`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `recommended_by_user_id`, because the person who wrote the recommendation may not be the person who signs for having carried it out. On success the record moves `RECOMMENDED → APPLIED`, stores the licence number, the wind reading and the applying `X-User-ID` as `applied_by_user_id`, and stamps `applied_at` from the logical clock.

From `applied_at` two clearance instants are derived on the record itself, both expressed in ISO 8601 UTC in the format §2.4 fixes — to whole seconds unless `applied_at` itself carried a sub-second component, in which case to three fractional digits, since advancing by a whole number of hours or days preserves whatever sub-second component the clock held: `rei_clear_at` is `applied_at` advanced by `rei_hours` hours, and `phi_clear_at` is `applied_at` advanced by `phi_days` multiplied by 24 hours. The block then republishes its own clearances as the latest of any qualifying record's: the block's `rei_clear_at` is the maximum `rei_clear_at` over the block's spray records whose `spray_status` is `APPLIED` or `VERIFIED`, and the block's `phi_clear_at` is the maximum `phi_clear_at` over that same set; when that set is empty, both block-level fields are null. Because the block takes the maximum, a later application carrying a short interval never shortens a longer restriction already in force, and a `RECOMMENDED` record — having no clearances at all — imposes no restriction whatever. Both comparisons that consume these instants are inclusive of the instant itself: entry is permitted once the logical clock reads at or after the block's `rei_clear_at`, and harvest is permitted once it reads at or after the block's `phi_clear_at`. An application at 06:00:00Z on 2 August 2026 with a 72-hour re-entry interval and a 21-day pre-harvest interval clears re-entry at 06:00:00Z on 5 August and pre-harvest at 06:00:00Z on 23 August; starting a work order at exactly 06:00:00Z on 5 August is permitted, and one second earlier is refused with `ERR_REENTRY_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`.

The block's `compliance_posture` is read straight off those clearances against the logical clock and is re-evaluated on every read and after every move of the clock. It is `REENTRY_RESTRICTED` when the block's `rei_clear_at` is not null and the clock is strictly earlier than it; failing that, it is `PHI_RESTRICTED` when the block's `phi_clear_at` is not null and the clock is strictly earlier than it; otherwise it is `CLEAR`. Re-entry restriction therefore masks pre-harvest restriction while both are open, because a block nobody may walk into is not usefully described as merely un-harvestable.

Field work is governed by re-entry in two places, and a certified crew is exempt in both. At scheduling time, a work order whose `scheduled_start` falls before the block's current `rei_clear_at` may only be booked for a crew whose `pest_certified` flag is true; booking an uncertified crew into an open re-entry window is refused with `ERR_CREW_NOT_PEST_CERTIFIED`. At start time, the logical clock must read at or after the block's `rei_clear_at` unless the crew is `pest_certified`; a block with a null `rei_clear_at` imposes no restriction at all. A start that fails this test is refused with `ERR_REENTRY_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`.

Applying a spray also reaches sideways into work already on the calendar, and this is the deepest cascade in the specification. Immediately after the block's clearances and `compliance_posture` are recomputed and the block's `version` is incremented by 1, the system sweeps the block's work orders that are still `SCHEDULED`. An order is auto-cancelled — driven `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to `REENTRY_RESTRICTION` — when its `scheduled_start` falls strictly earlier than the block's newly recomputed `rei_clear_at` **and** the crew holding it has `pest_certified` false. Orders whose `scheduled_start` is at or after the new clearance instant stay `SCHEDULED`, and orders held by a certified crew stay `SCHEDULED` no matter when they start. The sweep processes candidates in ascending `scheduled_start` order, breaking ties by ascending `created_at`, and increments each cancelled order's `version` by 1. Each auto-cancellation behaves exactly like an operator cancellation from `SCHEDULED`: `actual_labor_cost` stays null, `payroll_status` stays `NOT_PAYABLE`, and the frozen estimate leaves the plan ledger. The plan's `committed_cost`, `remaining_budget` and `budget_posture` are then recomputed once with both movements folded in — the new `material_cost` joining the ledger and the cancelled estimates leaving it — the `scheduled_work_order_count` of every affected crew is recomputed, and the spray record's own `version` is incremented by 1. A single application can therefore move the figures reported by a spray record, a block, several work orders, several crews and a farming plan; but only the spray record, the block and each auto-cancelled work order are rows the request actually rewrites, so those alone carry an incremented `version` when the request returns, while the affected crews and the farming plan keep the `version` they already had exactly as §2.10 requires.

Verification closes the compliance loop. A `ComplianceOfficer` countersigns an `APPLIED` record, optionally attaching a `verification_note` — a string of at most 1,000 characters that may be blank as well as omitted and that is validated and then discarded rather than persisted — which moves it `APPLIED → VERIFIED` and stores the officer's `X-User-ID` as `verified_by_user_id`. The record must be `APPLIED`, otherwise `ERR_SPRAY_NOT_APPLIED`, and the verifying `X-User-ID` must differ from both `recommended_by_user_id` and `applied_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — three distinct people must touch a regulated application before it is fully attested. Verification is an audit stamp and nothing more: the block's clearances were established when the spray was applied, both `APPLIED` and `VERIFIED` records feed the block-level maxima, and moving from one to the other changes no clearance, no ledger and no work order. Spray records may be read individually — returning the derived clearances and the full three-signature trail — and listed with narrowing by `block_id`, `farming_plan_id`, `pest_observation_id` and `spray_status`.

### 3.7. Yield Estimation and the Block Harvest Ledger

Harvest planning rests on an estimate of how much fruit is actually hanging, and the vineyard revises that estimate as the season progresses. Either a `VineyardManager` or an `Agronomist` files an estimate against a block, choosing a `method` of `CLUSTER_COUNT` or `BERRY_SAMPLE`, stating the `sample_date` on which the sample was taken as a YYYY-MM-DD date, and reporting the `avg_clusters_per_vine` mean cluster count from the sample (zero or more). The remaining inputs are method-conditional and are enforced strictly, as set out in §2.6: a `CLUSTER_COUNT` estimate requires `avg_cluster_weight_lb`, the historical mean cluster weight in pounds, and must omit the three berry fields entirely; a `BERRY_SAMPLE` estimate requires all three of `berries_per_cluster` (the mean berry count per cluster, itself a whole number), `avg_berry_weight_g` (the mean berry weight in grams) and `days_to_harvest` (the whole number of days from `sample_date` to the planned pick), and must omit `avg_cluster_weight_lb`. Each supplied berry or cluster-weight figure must be strictly greater than zero; `days_to_harvest` must be zero or more. The block must not be `REMOVED`.

At create the server freezes `vine_count_at_estimate` from the block's current `vine_count`, so a later replant cannot retroactively change what a filed estimate means. The total cluster count is `vine_count_at_estimate` multiplied by `avg_clusters_per_vine`. Under the `CLUSTER_COUNT` method, `estimated_tons` is that total cluster count multiplied by `avg_cluster_weight_lb` and then divided by the 2,000 pounds in a ton. Under the `BERRY_SAMPLE` method, the cluster weight is first derived rather than supplied — `berries_per_cluster` multiplied by `avg_berry_weight_g`, divided by the 453.592 grams in a pound — and then inflated by a growth factor of one plus four thousandths per day of `days_to_harvest`, on the reasoning that berries continue to gain weight between sampling and picking; `estimated_tons` is the total cluster count multiplied by that derived cluster weight, multiplied by that growth factor, and divided by 2,000. In both branches the rounding to two decimal places is applied exactly once, to the final tonnage: there is no intermediate rounding of the derived cluster weight or the growth factor, and rounding the derived cluster weight to four decimal places first is observably wrong. `estimated_tons_per_acre` is then `estimated_tons` divided by the block's `acres`, rounded to two decimal places. A filed estimate's representation always carries all four method-conditional keys whatever `method` produced it: the ones the chosen method does not consume are present and null rather than absent, so a `CLUSTER_COUNT` estimate reads back `berries_per_cluster`, `avg_berry_weight_g` and `days_to_harvest` as null while a `BERRY_SAMPLE` estimate reads back a null `avg_cluster_weight_lb`. The absence requirements of §2.6 bind the request body alone and say nothing about the response.

The two methods can disagree substantially on the same vines, which is the whole point of filing both. On a block with 13,612 frozen vines across 12.5 acres and 32 clusters per vine, a `CLUSTER_COUNT` estimate using a 0.42-pound historical cluster weight yields 435,584 clusters and an `estimated_tons` of 91.47, or 7.32 tons per acre. A `BERRY_SAMPLE` estimate on the same block with 95 berries per cluster, a 1.35-gram mean berry weight and 30 days to harvest — a growth factor of 1.120 — yields an `estimated_tons` of 68.97, or 5.52 tons per acre. Identical vines and identical cluster counts, 22.50 tons apart.

Because a block accumulates estimates over a season, exactly one of them governs at any moment, and the rule is chronological rather than optimistic or conservative: among all estimates filed for the block regardless of `method`, the governing estimate is the one with the latest `sample_date`, and when two share the same `sample_date` the one created later wins. The tie-break is `created_at` and never `id`, which means filing a `CLUSTER_COUNT` estimate after a `BERRY_SAMPLE` estimate bearing the same `sample_date` moves the block's governing tonnage back up. The block's `governing_estimate_tons` is that estimate's `estimated_tons`, and it is 0.00 when the block has no estimate at all. Filing an estimate recomputes `governing_estimate_tons`, `remaining_estimated_tons` and `harvest_posture` on the block and increments the block's `version` by 1.

Alongside the governing figure the block maintains a three-line harvest ledger. `committed_harvest_tons` is the sum of `estimated_tons` over the block's harvest loads whose `load_status` is `PLANNED` or `IN_TRANSIT`, rounded to two decimal places — tonnage that is booked but not yet resolved at the winery. `received_tons` is the sum of `net_tons` over the block's loads whose `load_status` is `RECEIVED`, rounded to two decimal places. `remaining_estimated_tons` is `governing_estimate_tons` less `committed_harvest_tons` less `received_tons`, rounded to two decimal places; it may go negative, but only by way of the harvest tolerance described in §3.8. `harvest_posture` summarises the ledger in one word: it is `NOT_ESTIMATED` when the block has no yield estimate at all; failing that, it is `COMPLETE` when `remaining_estimated_tons` is zero or negative; otherwise it is `OPEN`. Estimates filed for a block can be listed, newest `sample_date` first, by the `VineyardManager`, the `Agronomist` and the `Controller`.

### 3.8. Winery Delivery Windows, Load Booking and Weigh-Tag Settlement

Wineries accept fruit in slots, and a slot has both a clock and a scale. A `VineyardManager` opens a receiving slot by naming the `winery_name` that will receive the fruit (non-blank, 1 to 120 characters), the `window_start` and `window_end` instants that bound it — the end strictly later than the start — and the `capacity_tons` the winery will take in that slot, which must be strictly greater than zero. A new window reports `committed_tons` 0.00, `remaining_tons` equal to its `capacity_tons` and `window_posture` `OPEN`.

The window's booking figures are state-filtered on the booking states alone. `committed_tons` is the sum of `estimated_tons` over the loads booked into the window whose `load_status` is `PLANNED` or `IN_TRANSIT`, rounded to two decimal places; `net_tons` never enters this figure, and neither does the tonnage of a load the winery has already resolved. `remaining_tons` is `capacity_tons` less `committed_tons`, rounded to two decimal places, and the invariant that `committed_tons` plus `remaining_tons` equals `capacity_tons` holds at every observable instant. `window_posture` is derived from the unrounded ratio of `committed_tons` to `capacity_tons`: it is `FULL` when that ratio is at least 1.00, `NEARLY_FULL` when the ratio is at least 0.85 but below 1.00, and `OPEN` otherwise. A 40.00-ton window carrying one 12.40-ton booking therefore reports `committed_tons` 12.40, `remaining_tons` 27.60 and a posture of `OPEN`. Dispatching a load does not move `committed_tons` at all — the tonnage is still booked and still on its way — while receiving or rejecting it releases the booking, which is why both winery outcomes free window capacity even though only one of them credits the block.

Booking a load is a `VineyardManager` operation naming the `block_id` being picked, the `crew_id` responsible for the picking, the `delivery_window_id` the load is booked into, the `estimated_tons` being committed (strictly greater than zero), the `delivery_eta` planned arrival instant, and `expected_delivery_window_version` as described in §2.8. The gauntlet a booking must pass is the longest in the specification, and §2.6 fixes the order in which it is run. The governing agreement — reached from the block's `contract_id` — must be `ACTIVE`; a block whose contract is still `DRAFT` or has already been `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The block must not be `REMOVED`, and the crew must be `ACTIVE`. Pre-harvest compliance is checked against the planned arrival rather than the present moment: the `delivery_eta` must be at or after the block's `phi_clear_at`, and an earlier arrival is refused with `ERR_PREHARVEST_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`; a null `phi_clear_at` imposes no restriction. The `delivery_eta` must also fall inside the window's closed interval, at or after `window_start` and at or before `window_end`; both ends are inclusive, and an arrival outside them is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_ETA_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. Note that this is a containment test on a single instant and nothing more — any number of loads may share the same `delivery_eta`, because the slot is rationed by tonnage rather than by arrival time. The block must be enrolled under an agreement, so a null `contract_id` is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_ENROLLED`, and unenrolled fruit therefore cannot be sold through this system at all. The block must also have at least one yield estimate on file — a `harvest_posture` of `NOT_ESTIMATED` is refused with `ERR_NO_YIELD_ESTIMATE` — and the picking crew's `task_capabilities` must include `HARVEST`, or `ERR_CREW_TASK_MISMATCH`.

Two ceilings then close the booking out, in this order. The window's tonnage ceiling requires that the window's current `committed_tons` plus the new `estimated_tons`, rounded to two decimal places, not exceed its `capacity_tons`; equality is permitted, and anything above is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The block's harvest ceiling then requires that the block's `committed_harvest_tons` plus its `received_tons` plus the new `estimated_tons`, rounded to two decimal places, not exceed the block's `governing_estimate_tons` multiplied by a harvest tolerance of 1.10, that product likewise rounded to two decimal places. Equality is permitted; anything above is refused with `ERR_HARVEST_EXCEEDS_ESTIMATE`. The ten-percent tolerance exists because estimates are estimates, and it is the only mechanism by which a block's `remaining_estimated_tons` can legitimately go negative. A successful booking opens the load in `PLANNED` with every weigh-tag and outcome field present and null rather than absent — that set is exactly `gross_tons_at_dispatch`, `dispatched_at`, `gross_tons`, `tare_tons`, `mog_deduct_pct`, `brix_at_receipt`, `net_tons`, `quality_incentive_amount`, `received_at`, `rejected_at` and `rejection_reason` — recomputes the window's `committed_tons`, `remaining_tons` and `window_posture` and increments the window's `version` by 1, and recomputes the block's `committed_harvest_tons` and `remaining_estimated_tons` and increments the block's `version` by 1.

Dispatch belongs to the crew that is picking. The load's own `CrewSupervisor` — whose `X-User-ID` must equal the load's `crew_id` — reports the field scale reading as `gross_tons_at_dispatch` (zero or more), which moves the load `PLANNED → IN_TRANSIT` and stamps `dispatched_at` from the logical clock. The load must be `PLANNED`, otherwise `ERR_LOAD_NOT_PLANNED`, and the crew must be `ACTIVE`. Here the pre-harvest check is against the present rather than the plan: the logical clock must read at or after the block's `phi_clear_at`, and an earlier dispatch is refused with `ERR_PREHARVEST_INTERVAL_ACTIVE` — the clearance instant itself is permitted, and a null `phi_clear_at` imposes no restriction. Dispatch leaves the window's `committed_tons` untouched and increments only the load's own `version`.

At the winery the load resolves one of two ways, and the two outcomes are deliberately asymmetric. Both require the load to be `IN_TRANSIT`, otherwise `ERR_LOAD_NOT_IN_TRANSIT`, and both are the `VineyardManager`'s to record.

Recording a receipt supplies the winery weigh tag and the quality reading: `gross_tons` from the winery scale (zero or more), `tare_tons` for the bins and truck (zero or more), a `mog_deduct_pct` deduction fraction for material other than grapes, and `brix_at_receipt` in degrees brix (zero or more). As §2.6 requires, `tare_tons` must be strictly less than `gross_tons` and `mog_deduct_pct` must lie between 0 and 1 inclusive. The payable tonnage is then `gross_tons` less `tare_tons`, multiplied by one minus `mog_deduct_pct`, rounded to two decimal places, and stored as `net_tons` — a 13.10-ton gross against a 0.70-ton tare with no deduction gives 12.40 net tons, and the same weigh tag with a two-percent deduction gives 12.15. Neither the load's booked `estimated_tons` nor its `gross_tons_at_dispatch` participates in that figure. The quality incentive is then decided by comparing `brix_at_receipt` against the agreement's `quality_brix_threshold`, reached through the block's `contract_id`: when the reading is at or above the threshold, `quality_incentive_amount` is `net_tons` multiplied by the agreement's `quality_incentive_per_ton`, rounded to two decimal places; when it falls below, `quality_incentive_amount` is 0.00. The boundary is inclusive, so a reading of exactly 24.5 degrees against a 24.5 threshold earns the incentive — 12.40 net tons at 45.00 dollars per ton yields 558.00. Receipt then cascades four levels: the load moves `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED` with `received_at` stamped; the block's `committed_harvest_tons`, `received_tons`, `remaining_estimated_tons` and `harvest_posture` are recomputed and the block's `version` incremented by 1; the agreement's `accrued_quality_incentive` — the sum of `quality_incentive_amount` over all `RECEIVED` loads from blocks enrolled under it, rounded to two decimal places — and its `projected_total_billable` are recomputed and the agreement's `version` incremented by 1; and the window's `committed_tons`, `remaining_tons` and `window_posture` are recomputed with the window's `version` incremented by 1. The load's own `version` is incremented too. Receipt is gated on nothing but the load's own `load_status`, so a load still `IN_TRANSIT` when its agreement was driven `CLOSED` is received normally and still credits that closed agreement's `accrued_quality_incentive` and bumps its `version`.

Recording a rejection takes only a `rejection_reason`, which is one of `MOG_EXCESS`, `LOW_BRIX`, `ROT_DAMAGE` or `LATE_ARRIVAL`, and moves the load `IN_TRANSIT → REJECTED` with `rejected_at` stamped. A rejected load's `net_tons` is set to 0.00 and its `quality_incentive_amount` to 0.00 — not left null — while its `gross_tons`, `tare_tons`, `mog_deduct_pct`, `brix_at_receipt` and `received_at` all stay null, just as a received load's `rejected_at` and `rejection_reason` stay null. The block's `committed_harvest_tons` and `remaining_estimated_tons` are recomputed, so the booked tonnage returns to the block's available estimate and the fruit can be re-picked and re-booked; the block's `received_tons` is deliberately unchanged, because nothing was received. The block's `version` is incremented by 1. The agreement's `accrued_quality_incentive` is left exactly as it was — a rejected load earns nothing and takes nothing away. The window's `committed_tons`, `remaining_tons` and `window_posture` are recomputed and its `version` incremented by 1, releasing the slot for another load, and the load's own `version` is incremented. Loads can be read individually, returning the full weigh tag alongside `net_tons` and `quality_incentive_amount`, and listed with narrowing by `block_id`, `crew_id`, `delivery_window_id` and `load_status`.

### 3.9. Period Settlement and Contract Closure

Settlement is the `Controller`'s exclusive act, and it is where the whole season is converted into one invoice. The agreement must be `ACTIVE`; running a settlement against one still in `DRAFT` or already `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The request carries a `settlement_mode` of either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` and a `period_end_date` marking the end of the span being settled. That date narrows nothing in the roll-up below — every qualifying child row is counted however old it is, and re-settling the same stored data reproduces the same figures — and it is consulted only by the closure test in the last paragraph of this section. The response is a full statement rather than the contract row: it echoes `contract_id`, `settlement_mode` and `period_end_date`, and reports `direct_cost_total`, `management_fee_base`, `pass_through`, `tier_basis`, `volume_discount_pct`, `volume_discount`, `quality_incentive`, `total_billable` and the resulting `contract_status`.

The first stage rolls up direct cost, and it does so block by block rather than in one aggregate sweep, because the rounding boundary sits at the block. Working through the agreement's enrolled blocks in ascending `created_at` order, each block contributes four things. Its labor is the sum of `actual_labor_cost` over that block's work orders whose `execution_status` is `COMPLETED` — a cancelled order's reporting-time pay is a cost the farming company bears and never reaches the client. Its materials are the sum of `material_cost` over that block's spray records whose `spray_status` is `APPLIED` or `VERIFIED`. Its water is the sum of `water_cost` over that block's irrigation events whose `irrigation_status` is `APPLIED`. And its overhead share is the agreement's `period_overhead_pool` multiplied by that block's `contracted_acres` and divided by the agreement's `total_contracted_acres`, computed without any rounding; when that divisor is itself zero the share is taken as 0.00 rather than being undefined, so an agreement whose enrolled blocks all carry zero contracted acreage still settles. Those four quantities are added and the block's subtotal rounded to two decimal places, and only then does the subtotal join `direct_cost_total`; the running total is not re-rounded at the end. An enrolled block with no qualifying activity still absorbs its overhead share, and a wholly inactive agreement with a `period_overhead_pool` of 0.00 settles to a `direct_cost_total` of 0.00 rather than an error. To see why the rounding boundary matters: a 1,000.00-dollar pool spread over contracted acreages of 12.5, 4.0 and 3.6 — a `total_contracted_acres` of 20.1 — gives unrounded shares of 621.8905…, 199.0049… and 179.1044…; with the first block carrying 4,343.30 of labor, 812.50 of materials and 472.50 of water and the other two carrying no activity at all, the per-block figures are 6,250.19, 199.00 and 179.10 and `direct_cost_total` is 6,628.29. Summing the raw values first and rounding once at the end would give 6,628.30, and that one cent is a divergence the settlement must not produce.

The second stage prices the invoice, and the order of operations is load-bearing at two points. `management_fee_base` is the figure defined in §3.2. `pass_through` is `direct_cost_total` multiplied by the agreement's `cost_pass_through_pct` and multiplied again by the share the mode selects, rounded to two decimal places: a `FINAL` settlement passes through the full amount, while a `PROVISIONAL` settlement passes through exactly half of it, since an interim invoice should not front-load the client with costs the period may yet revise. `tier_basis` is `management_fee_base` plus that already-rounded `pass_through`, rounded to two decimal places — and it deliberately excludes both the volume discount, which has not been computed yet, and the quality incentive, which is not a cost at all. From `tier_basis` the volume discount rate is selected: `volume_discount_pct` is 0.00 when `tier_basis` is below 15,000, 0.03 when it is at least 15,000 but below 20,000, and 0.05 when it reaches 20,000. `volume_discount` is then that rate applied to `management_fee_base` alone — never to `tier_basis` — rounded to two decimal places. `quality_incentive` is the agreement's `accrued_quality_incentive` for a `FINAL` settlement and is forced to 0.00 for a `PROVISIONAL` one, because fruit quality is not yet known mid-period. `total_billable` is `management_fee_base` plus `pass_through`, less `volume_discount`, plus `quality_incentive`, rounded to two decimal places.

The two modes diverge sharply on byte-identical stored data. With a `management_fee_base` of 19,095.00, a `direct_cost_total` of 6,628.29, a `cost_pass_through_pct` of 0.08 and an `accrued_quality_incentive` of 558.00, a `FINAL` run passes through 530.26, giving a `tier_basis` of 19,625.26, a `volume_discount_pct` of 0.03, a `volume_discount` of 572.85 and a `total_billable` of 19,610.41. A `PROVISIONAL` run on the same data passes through 265.13, giving a `tier_basis` of 19,360.13, the same 0.03 rate and the same 572.85 discount, a `quality_incentive` of 0.00 and a `total_billable` of 18,787.28 — 823.13 apart. Two traps are worth naming explicitly: folding the quality incentive into `tier_basis` would have lifted it to 20,183.26 and jumped the rate to 0.05 for a discount of 954.75, and applying the discount to `tier_basis` instead of to `management_fee_base` would have produced 588.76. Neither is correct. At the small end, an agreement with a single enrolled 5.0-acre block, a `period_overhead_pool` of 0.00 and no activity at all settles to a `direct_cost_total` of 0.00, a `pass_through` of 0.00, a `management_fee_base` of 4,750.00, a `tier_basis` of 4,750.00, a `volume_discount_pct` of 0.00 and a `total_billable` of 4,750.00.

Every settlement run, whichever mode it used, increments the agreement's `settled_period_count` by 1, stores its `total_billable` in `last_settlement_total`, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1. Closure is narrower: the agreement is driven `ACTIVE → CLOSED` only when `settlement_mode` is `FINAL` **and** the supplied `period_end_date` is at or after the agreement's own `period_end`. A `PROVISIONAL` run, or a `FINAL` run for a `period_end_date` earlier than the agreement's `period_end`, leaves `contract_status` at `ACTIVE` while still bumping the counter and the last-settlement figure — mid-season invoices are expected to be numerous. `CLOSED` is terminal, and its most visible downstream consequence is that booking a harvest load against any block enrolled under a closed agreement is thereafter refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A `ClientOwner` whose `X-User-ID` matches the agreement's `vineyard_id` may read the agreement at any time to see its live `accrued_quality_incentive`, `projected_total_billable`, `settled_period_count` and `last_settlement_total`, and may list its own agreements narrowed by `vineyard_id` and `contract_status` — that listing is scoped to the client's own ranch whether or not `vineyard_id` is supplied, and a `ClientOwner` naming any other ranch in `vineyard_id` is refused with `ERR_VINEYARD_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` rather than shown an empty page.
