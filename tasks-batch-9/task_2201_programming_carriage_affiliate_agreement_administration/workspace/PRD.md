# Carriage Affiliate Admin API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A cable programming network earns almost all of its distribution revenue through carriage agreements: long-dated contracts with cable operators, satellite providers, telephone-company video services and virtual multichannel operators that specify what the network is paid per subscriber, how broadly it must be packaged, and what happens when the operator falls short. In practice, the administration of those agreements is spread across four organisations that do not trust each other's numbers. Affiliate sales negotiates terms and executes contracts. The distributor's own liaison files monthly subscriber counts. Revenue accounting decides whether those counts are acceptable, bills them, and chases the cash. An audit group periodically tests the counts against the operator's own systems and recovers the difference. Meanwhile a rights group has to make sure that every linear, video-on-demand, streaming and international window the agreement grants is actually clear of exclusivity conflicts and territorial blackouts.

This service is the system of record for that whole apparatus. It holds the commercial terms — base per-subscriber license fees, annual escalators, penetration floors, packaging tiers, launch fees and marketing commitments — and it holds the derived consequences of those terms: the escalated rate for the current contract year, the rate after any most-favored-nations benchmark caps it, the accepted subscriber counts that become recognised revenue, the invoices that carry that revenue into the receivable, and the credit posture that results when an operator stops paying. Every one of those derived values is computed by the server from pinned inputs in a pinned order, because the entire point of the system is that two parties who disagree about a number can be shown exactly how it was produced.

Two design constraints shape the whole specification. First, **time is explicit**: expiry, window activation, interest accrual, forfeiture deadlines and holdback measurements all read a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so a reviewer can replay a contract year deterministically. Second, **duties are segregated**: the person who executed an agreement may not be the person who accepts its subscriber reports, and the auditor who issues findings may not be the accountant who bills the recovery. These are not advisory workflow hints — they are enforced request-by-request from the caller's identity headers.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All business resources are mounted under the `/carriage` prefix. A single additional namespace, `/_clock`, exists purely to drive the server's logical time during testing and administration.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `AffiliateSalesExec`, `DistributorLiaison`, `RevenueAccountant`, `AuditManager`, `RightsAdministrator` and `NetworkAdmin`.

- An `AffiliateSalesExec` negotiates: drafting and amending agreements, driving the lifecycle and renewal dimensions, attaching and resetting most-favored-nations clauses, opening marketing commitment lines and recording proof of performance against them, and running renewal leverage models. This role never accepts a subscriber report and never moves money.
- A `DistributorLiaison` acts on behalf of exactly one distributing operator. It files that operator's monthly subscriber reports and reads that operator's agreements, invoices and commitments.
- A `RevenueAccountant` owns revenue recognition and receivables: reviewing subscriber reports, drafting invoices and driving them through issuance, payment, write-off and void, and placing or releasing credit holds. It may also file a subscriber report. It never negotiates terms and never clears rights.
- An `AuditManager` exercises audit rights: opening engagements, recording findings and issuing them. It is deliberately barred from raising the recovery invoice.
- A `RightsAdministrator` administers content: registering titles, setting their rights status, planning and clearing platform windows, and scheduling, enforcing and waiving territorial blackouts.
- A `NetworkAdmin` is the platform operator: onboarding distributors, publishing rate-card rows, adjusting an operator's subscriber base or credit limit, and advancing the logical clock. It is deliberately **not** granted agreement, report, audit, rights or invoice write powers.

Role identity binds to resources as follows. When `X-User-Role` is `DistributorLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Distributor` compare the resource's resolved distributor identity to `X-User-ID`. For an agreement that means its `distributor_id`; for a subscriber report, audit engagement, rights window, blackout rule, marketing commitment or renewal analysis it means the `distributor_id` of the agreement named by the resource's `agreement_id`; for an invoice it means the invoice's own `distributor_id`. A `DistributorLiaison` acting on a resource that resolves to any other operator is rejected with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `AffiliateSalesExec`, `RevenueAccountant`, `AuditManager`, `RightsAdministrator` or `NetworkAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it retains the identifier that executed an agreement, and the identifier that issued an engagement's findings — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Those two recorded identifiers exist to enforce **segregation of duties**, which is checked before any other authorisation-shaped rule other than header validity and endpoint role membership. Two prohibitions apply. The `X-User-ID` that executed a given agreement may not be the `X-User-ID` that reviews a subscriber report belonging to that agreement. The `X-User-ID` that issued a given audit engagement's findings may not be the `X-User-ID` that creates the audit-recovery invoice for that same engagement. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The rule is scoped to exactly two operations: the subscriber-report review endpoint and the invoice-create endpoint.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific narrative states otherwise, and that ordering is stable across pages.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current page. Filter query parameters combine conjunctively: supplying both an agreement filter and a status filter returns only records satisfying both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`. Mutable resources additionally carry `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation the server applies to that resource — including mutations the caller did not directly address, such as a counter refreshed by a cascade originating on a different resource. Whenever `version` increments, `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant.

Four record types are deliberately immutable and therefore carry a reduced property set. A published rate-card row is addressable by `id` alone: it has no `created_at`, no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no endpoint can modify or delete it once published. An audit finding and a renewal analysis each carry `id` and `created_at` but neither `updated_at` nor `version` — both are append-only records of a judgement made at a point in time, and both are consulted in `created_at` order by downstream calculations. The logical clock is a singleton holding `current_utc` and `set_count`; it is not a versioned resource.

### 2.5. Logical Time

The server holds its own notion of "now" in `current_utc`. At startup that value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time for any decision: contract expiry, window activation, blackout expiry, holdback measurement, interest accrual, commitment forfeiture, write-off ageing and renewal-window tests all read `current_utc`.

A `NetworkAdmin` moves the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`. Time may only move forward: a value less than or equal to the current logical time is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_MOVE_FORWARD` (HTTP 422). A successful advance increments `set_count` by one and then runs a single sweep whose step order is pinned and observable:

1. Agreements whose `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `term_end_date` is at or before the new `current_utc` move to `EXPIRED`, processed in `term_end_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. An agreement whose `renewal_status` was `RENEWAL_ANALYSIS` or `RENEWAL_OFFERED` at that moment is forced to `LAPSED`.
2. Rights windows in `CLEARED` whose `window_start` is at or before `current_utc` and whose `window_end` is strictly after it move to `ACTIVE`, processed in `window_start` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
3. Rights windows in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` whose `window_end` is at or before `current_utc` move to `EXPIRED`.
4. Blackout rules in `SCHEDULED` whose `blackout_end` is strictly before `current_utc` move to `EXPIRED`.
5. Marketing commitments whose `performance_deadline` is strictly before `current_utc` and whose `remaining_cents` is still positive forfeit their remainder, processed in `performance_deadline` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.
6. Every content title touched by steps 2 and 3 re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`.

### 2.6. Rates, Money and Rounding

Two precisions coexist. Per-subscriber **rates** — `base_license_fee_cents_per_sub`, `escalated_rate_cents_per_sub`, `effective_rate_cents_per_sub`, `published_rate_cents_per_sub`, `rate_floor_cents`, `benchmark_rate_cents` and `rate_applied_cents_per_sub` — are carried to four decimal places of a cent and rounded half up at that precision. **Amounts** are integers of whole cents; wherever a monetary amount is produced from a multiplication it is rounded half up to a whole cent.

The order of rounding is itself a contract. A subscriber report's fee is rounded to whole cents *before* it can participate in any invoice sum, and an invoice sums already-rounded per-report integers; summing unrounded products across reports and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the specified behaviour and produces different cents. The same discipline applies inside an audit engagement: each finding's recovery is rounded on its own, and the engagement accumulates rounded integers.

Other precisions used by specific derivations: penetration is carried to four decimal places, and both sides of a penetration comparison are rounded to four decimal places before being compared; blackout durations are carried to two decimal places of an hour; a renewal analysis carries its leverage score to two decimal places, its subscriber share to six, and its intermediate ratios and scores to four. Instants are compared at whole-second resolution.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Revisions

Three write operations require the client to declare the revision of the parent record it believes it is acting on. This is not an optional field, and no other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind.

- `POST /carriage/subscriber-reports` requires `expected_agreement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `CarriageAgreement` named by `agreement_id`. If it does not equal that agreement's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Filing a report is a consequential act — a restatement supersedes the report it names, and an accepted filing shifts the agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents`, its `penetration_compliance_status` and possibly its `lifecycle_status` — so a filer holding a stale view of the agreement is refused rather than allowed to bill against terms that no longer hold.
- `POST /carriage/mfn-clauses/{mfnClauseId}/reset` also requires `expected_agreement_version`, matched against the current `version` of the agreement named by the clause's `agreement_id`; a mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A reset rewrites the agreement's `effective_rate_cents_per_sub` and re-prices unbilled report fees, so running it from a stale view would silently re-price periods the client never observed.
- `POST /carriage/invoices` requires `expected_distributor_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Distributor` named by `distributor_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DISTRIBUTOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a fresh draft legitimately supersedes an earlier unissued draft's claim on the same reports and the same offset pool, this check is what makes the outcome deterministic when the operator's `receivable_balance_cents`, credit posture or `audit_recovery_balance_cents` has moved underneath the drafter.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

Path parameters are resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable segment decides the response. The path identifiers used across the API are `distributorId`, `agreementId`, `rateCardEntryId`, `mfnClauseId`, `reportId`, `engagementId`, `findingId`, `titleId`, `windowId`, `blackoutRuleId`, `commitmentId`, `invoiceId` and `analysisId`, and each has its own not-found code: `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_CARD_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MFN_CLAUSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLACKOUT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.

Body-supplied references are resolved after path parameters, in this declaration order: `distributor_id`, `agreement_id`, `content_title_id`, `substitute_content_title_id`, `subscriber_report_id`, `engagement_id`, `audit_engagement_id`, `restatement_of_report_id`. Each unresolved reference produces the not-found code of the entity type it points at — a bad `content_title_id` or `substitute_content_title_id` yields `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, a bad `subscriber_report_id` or `restatement_of_report_id` yields `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, a bad `audit_engagement_id` or `engagement_id` yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Operations that drive a state machine are expressed as a single transition endpoint per entity, carrying the transition's name in an `action` field together with whichever optional companion field that transition needs — a `reason` for a credit-posture action, a `suspension_reason` for an agreement suspension, a `note` for a report review, a `block_reason` for a window block, or an `amount_cents` for a payment. An `action` value that is not one of the declared transition names for that endpoint is a request-shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an `action` that is a declared name but is not legal from the entity's current state is a state failure with that entity's own invalid-state code.

Validation is a single ordered gauntlet. **The first rule that fails decides the response, and no side effect of the requested operation fires.** The order, from first evaluated to last, is:

1. Header validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Endpoint role membership — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
4. Distributor scope for a `DistributorLiaison` — `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
5. Request shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every required field of the endpoint must be present and non-null; enum fields must carry a declared member; integer fields must be integers; monetary and subscriber fields must not be negative; a `period_month`, `sample_period_start` or `sample_period_end` must match a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month between `01` and `12`; a `territory_code`, `home_territory_code` or the territory of a blackout must be exactly two uppercase letters; a `term_end_date` at or before `term_start_date` is malformed; and no unknown field may be present.
6. Existence of the path-addressed resource (rule 10 in the internal numbering).
7. Existence of body-supplied references (rule 11).
8. Optimistic-concurrency match on `expected_agreement_version` or `expected_distributor_version` (rule 20).
9. Own-entity state admissibility (rules 30 through 37): agreement lifecycle, agreement renewal, report review, engagement, rights window, invoice, blackout rule and MFN clause, each with its own conflict code.
10. Forward-only logical time (rule 38) — `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_MOVE_FORWARD`.
11. Cross-entity state admissibility (rules 40 through 42): the referenced agreement's lifecycle admissibility, the resolved operator's `ACTIVE` credit posture, and the referenced title's cleared rights.
12. Territorial and blackout compliance (rules 50 through 53).
13. Rights, packaging, uniqueness and evidence constraints (rules 60 through 68).
14. Temporal constraints (rules 70 through 75).
15. Billing referential integrity (rules 80 through 85).
16. Fiscal constraints (rules 90 through 93).

Where several rules sit in the same band, the lower internal number is evaluated first; the individual rules and their codes are stated in the workflow narratives of §3. The one structural consequence worth stating up front: because state admissibility (band 9) precedes every business check, an action that is not a declared transition out of the target's current state always surfaces as the relevant invalid-state conflict, never as the business rule that would also have failed.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Compensation

Many operations in this domain mutate more than one record. The rule is that every entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been addressed directly, and that cascades run to completion within the originating request — there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this system.

Cascade ordering is pinned wherever more than one record of the same type is affected. Agreements swept by an operator-level event are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Rights windows blocked by an agreement-level event are processed in `window_start` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Marketing commitments forfeited by a clock advance are processed in `performance_deadline` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Audit findings are priced in `period_month` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Marketing offsets are consumed in commitment-type order — co-op advertising first, then marketing support, then launch fee — and within a type in `created_at` ascending order.

Three operations are **compensating**: unblocking a rights window, voiding an invoice, and voiding the recovery invoice of an audit engagement. Each is specified to restore the exact pre-event values of the counters it reversed, and the relevant narrative in §3 states the invariant it must preserve. A compensation never re-stamps an original timestamp: an engagement whose recovery invoice is voided keeps its `findings_issued_at`.

### 2.10. Two Different Interval Semantics

The system deliberately uses two incompatible interval conventions, and confusing them is the most common source of wrong answers.

A **licensing window** occupies the half-open interval from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive. Two windows overlap only when each one's start is strictly before the other's end, which means adjacency is legal: a window ending at exactly the instant another begins does not conflict, and there is no separation buffer on this resource at all.

A **blackout** occupies the closed interval from `blackout_start` through `blackout_end`, both ends inclusive, and it additionally carries a mandatory separation buffer of 24 hours on each side. Two blackouts on the same key conflict when each one's start, pulled back by the buffer, is at or before the other's end — so adjacency is illegal here, and a gap of zero hours, or even of 23 hours, is refused where the window resource would have accepted it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding an Operator, Publishing the Card, and Credit Posture

An operator enters the system as a `Distributor` registered by a `NetworkAdmin` or an `AffiliateSalesExec`. Registration supplies a `legal_name` of between 1 and 200 characters that must be unique across all operators, a `distributor_type` of `CABLE_MSO`, `DBS`, `TELCO` or `VMVPD`, a `rate_card_class` of `CLASS_A`, `CLASS_B` or `CLASS_C` identifying which published card column this operator negotiates against, and a `total_subscribers` count of at least 1 covering the operator's whole video base across all tiers. That count is the denominator of every penetration calculation in the system. A `credit_limit_cents` may be supplied; when it is omitted the operator receives the standard ceiling of 100,000,000 cents — one million dollars — and it may never be negative. A newly registered operator starts `ACTIVE` with `credit_hold_reason` of `NONE`, with `receivable_balance_cents`, `audit_recovery_balance_cents` and `executed_agreement_count` all at zero, and with `version` 1.

Three of those numbers are maintained by the server and never written by a client. `receivable_balance_cents` is the sum, across the operator's invoices in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` status, of each invoice's `net_due_cents` less its `paid_cents`. `audit_recovery_balance_cents` is the same sum restricted to invoices whose `invoice_kind` is `AUDIT_RECOVERY`. `executed_agreement_count` is the number of that operator's agreements whose `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED` or `SUSPENDED`.

Only a `NetworkAdmin` may adjust an operator's `total_subscribers` or `credit_limit_cents` after registration, and either adjustment bumps the operator's `version` by one. Such an adjustment is explicitly **not** retroactive: subscriber reports already accepted keep the `billable_subscribers` and `license_fee_due_cents` they were stamped with, and the new subscriber base applies only to penetration and floor computations performed afterwards.

Credit posture is driven separately, by a `NetworkAdmin` or a `RevenueAccountant`, through a posture action naming one of `PLACE_HOLD`, `RELEASE_HOLD`, `SUSPEND`, `TERMINATE` or `REACTIVATE`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `reason`. Placing a hold moves an `ACTIVE` operator to `CREDIT_HOLD` with `credit_hold_reason` set to `MANUAL_REVIEW`, and then runs the operator-level cascade in full: every agreement of that operator whose `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; each of those agreements' rights windows in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` move to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED`; and each content title touched by those windows re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`. Releasing a hold is only permitted when `receivable_balance_cents` is at or below `credit_limit_cents` **and** `audit_recovery_balance_cents` is exactly zero; the operator returns to `ACTIVE` with `credit_hold_reason` back to `NONE`. Release does not un-suspend anything — agreements suspended for `DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD` stay suspended until someone reinstates them explicitly. `SUSPEND`, `TERMINATE` and `REACTIVATE` set `status` to `SUSPENDED`, `TERMINATED` and `ACTIVE` respectively, and a termination is refused with `ERR_OPEN_RECEIVABLE_BLOCKS_TERMINATION` while any invoice in the operator's scope sits in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`. Every posture action increments the operator's `version` by one. The complete set of hold reasons the field can carry is `NONE`, `RECEIVABLE_LIMIT_BREACH`, `AUDIT_RECOVERY_UNPAID` and `MANUAL_REVIEW`; the first two are written by the billing cascade described in §3.8, the third records a hold placed because an audit recovery is outstanding, and `MANUAL_REVIEW` is the reason attached to a hold a human places directly.

Alongside operators, a `NetworkAdmin` publishes the network's rate card as immutable rows. Each row states the `rate_card_class` and `tier` it prices — the tier being one of `BASIC`, `EXPANDED_BASIC`, `DIGITAL_TIER` or `PREMIUM` — a `published_rate_cents_per_sub` strictly greater than zero, an inclusive `effective_from` instant and an optional `effective_to` instant that is exclusive and must be strictly later than `effective_from` when supplied; a null `effective_to` means the row is open-ended. A row may also carry `min_subscribers`, defaulting to 0, below which an operator's `total_subscribers` is too small for the row to be quotable. Rate-card rows are reference data for negotiators: they are never mutated, never deleted, and never consulted automatically by the billing engine, which always prices from the agreement's own negotiated terms.

### 3.2. Drafting, Negotiating and Executing a Carriage Agreement

An `AffiliateSalesExec` drafts an agreement by naming the operator it binds through `distributor_id`, a `contract_code` that must be unique among that operator's agreements (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTRACT_CODE`), the `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` bounding the term, a `base_license_fee_cents_per_sub` strictly greater than zero representing the year-one monthly per-subscriber fee, an `escalator_pct` between 0 and 0.15 inclusive expressing the annual compounding increase (0.04 meaning four percent per contract year), a `penetration_floor_pct` strictly greater than 0 and at most 1 giving the minimum share of the operator's subscriber base that must receive the network, and a `committed_tier` naming the packaging tier the operator has committed to carry the network on. Two optional amounts complete the commercial picture: `launch_fee_cents` and `marketing_commitment_cents`, each defaulting to 0 and neither permitted to be negative. A fresh draft is stamped `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT`, `renewal_status` `NOT_DUE`, `negotiation_round` 0, `penetration_compliance_status` `UNKNOWN`, `penetration_breach_count` 0, `consecutive_compliant_periods` 0, `recognized_revenue_cents` 0 and `version` 1, and both rate fields are stamped immediately from the terms as described in §3.3.

The agreement carries **two independent state dimensions**. `lifecycle_status` runs through `DRAFT`, `NEGOTIATING`, `EXECUTED`, `SUSPENDED` and terminally `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`. `renewal_status` runs through `NOT_DUE`, `RENEWAL_ANALYSIS`, `RENEWAL_OFFERED` and terminally `RENEWED` or `LAPSED`. The renewal dimension is driven by its own action set and never writes `lifecycle_status`; the lifecycle dimension writes `renewal_status` only in the two forced-lapse cases described below. An action that is not a declared transition out of the current `lifecycle_status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`; an action that is not a declared transition out of the current `renewal_status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RENEWAL_STATE`. Both are HTTP 409.

Commercial terms may be amended only while the agreement is still pre-execution. An amendment may revise `base_license_fee_cents_per_sub`, `escalator_pct`, `penetration_floor_pct`, `committed_tier`, `term_end_date`, `launch_fee_cents` and `marketing_commitment_cents`, re-stamps both rate fields, and increments `version` by one. Once `lifecycle_status` has reached `EXECUTED`, `SUSPENDED`, `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`, an amendment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`.

Submitting for negotiation moves `DRAFT → NEGOTIATING`. It requires that the term has not already run out — `term_end_date` must be strictly later than the current logical instant — and that the owning operator is `ACTIVE` or `CREDIT_HOLD`, since a `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` operator cannot open negotiations; failing the operator test yields `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD`. The transition increments `negotiation_round` by one and re-stamps both rate fields. Returning to draft moves `NEGOTIATING → DRAFT` and deliberately leaves `negotiation_round` untouched, because that counter records entries into negotiation, not exits from it; a term revised three times therefore reports a `negotiation_round` of 3 and the round count is externally observable.

Execution moves `NEGOTIATING → EXECUTED` and is the heaviest transition in the lifecycle. It requires the operator to be strictly `ACTIVE` — an operator on `CREDIT_HOLD` cannot have a new agreement executed, and the rejection is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD` — that the current logical instant lies at or after `term_start_date` and strictly before `term_end_date`, and that the penetration floor actually binds at least one subscriber: `penetration_floor_pct` multiplied by the operator's `total_subscribers` must be at least 1, or the request is refused with `ERR_PENETRATION_FLOOR_UNENFORCEABLE`. On success the server stamps `executed_at` with the current logical instant, re-stamps `escalated_rate_cents_per_sub` and `effective_rate_cents_per_sub`, increments the operator's `executed_agreement_count` by one, and opens the network's marketing obligations: when `launch_fee_cents` is positive a commitment line of type `LAUNCH_FEE` is created with `committed_cents` equal to `launch_fee_cents` and a `performance_deadline` 180 days after `executed_at`, and when `marketing_commitment_cents` is positive a second line of type `MARKETING_SUPPORT` is created with `committed_cents` equal to `marketing_commitment_cents` and a `performance_deadline` equal to `term_end_date`. Both lines open in `OPEN` status.

Suspension moves `EXECUTED → SUSPENDED` and arrives by three routes: an `AffiliateSalesExec` suspending deliberately, in which case the caller may pass a `suspension_reason` and the recorded reason is `MANUAL_SUSPENSION`; the compliance engine reaching the breach threshold on report acceptance, which records `PENETRATION_BREACH`; and the owning operator entering `CREDIT_HOLD`, which records `DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD`. Whichever route fires, the same cascade follows: every rights window of the agreement in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED`, processed in `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, and every content title those windows belonged to re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`.

Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → EXECUTED`. It requires the operator to be `ACTIVE` again (otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD`) and the term to still be live, with `term_end_date` strictly later than the current logical instant. When the suspension carried `suspension_reason` of `PENETRATION_BREACH`, reinstatement additionally requires that `penetration_breach_count` has already been driven back to zero — by three consecutive compliant accepted periods, or by a credit review that cleared it — and a reinstatement attempted while that counter is still positive is refused with `ERR_BREACH_COUNT_NOT_CLEARED`. On success `suspension_reason` returns to `NONE` and the compensation runs: every rights window of this agreement sitting in `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED` returns to `CLEARED`, provided re-clearing still satisfies the occupancy rules of §3.6; a window that would now collide stays `BLOCKED`, with its `block_reason` rewritten to `BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`. Each content title touched by an un-blocked window re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`.

Expiry is never requested; it is swept. On a clock advance, an agreement in `EXECUTED` whose `term_end_date` has been reached moves to `EXPIRED` provided `renewal_status` is not `RENEWED`, and an agreement in `SUSPENDED` whose `term_end_date` has been reached moves to `EXPIRED` unconditionally. Both forms decrement the operator's `executed_agreement_count` by one. The `EXECUTED → EXPIRED` form forces `renewal_status` to `LAPSED` when it stood at `RENEWAL_ANALYSIS` or `RENEWAL_OFFERED`, moves every rights window of the agreement in `PLANNED`, `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`, and forfeits every marketing commitment of the agreement with positive `remaining_cents` by adding that remainder to `forfeited_cents` and setting the line to `FORFEITED`. The `SUSPENDED → EXPIRED` form likewise decrements `executed_agreement_count` and expires the agreement's rights windows, and because a suspended agreement's windows may already sit in `BLOCKED`, its sweep covers windows in `PLANNED`, `CLEARED`, `ACTIVE` **and** `BLOCKED`.

Termination is available from two states, with markedly different conditions. Terminating from `NEGOTIATING` needs nothing beyond the state itself, and forces `renewal_status` to `LAPSED` when it stood at `RENEWAL_ANALYSIS` or `RENEWAL_OFFERED`. Terminating from `SUSPENDED` additionally requires that no invoice of this agreement sits in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` — open receivables must be resolved first, and the refusal is `ERR_OPEN_RECEIVABLE_BLOCKS_TERMINATION` — and it runs the full unwind: every rights window in `PLANNED`, `CLEARED`, `ACTIVE` or `BLOCKED` moves to `EXPIRED`, every marketing commitment with positive `remaining_cents` forfeits that remainder and becomes `FORFEITED`, and the operator's `executed_agreement_count` decrements by one.

### 3.3. Escalators, Most-Favored-Nations Caps and the Rate Actually Billed

Every agreement carries two rate fields, and the distinction between them is the spine of the pricing model. `escalated_rate_cents_per_sub` is the contractual rate before any peer comparison; `effective_rate_cents_per_sub` is the rate the network actually bills. `mfn_capped` is a boolean flag that is true exactly when the effective rate came out strictly below the escalated rate because a peer benchmark bound it.

The escalated rate is produced as follows, for a nominated pricing month. Take the first day of that month at midnight UTC. Count the whole days from the agreement's `term_start_date` to that instant, discarding any fractional day. Divide that day count by 365 and discard the fraction to obtain the contract-year index, and treat any negative result as zero. The escalated rate is then `base_license_fee_cents_per_sub` multiplied by one plus `escalator_pct`, raised to the power of that contract-year index, rounded half up to four decimal places of a cent. So a contract in its first 365 days prices at the base fee unchanged; a four-percent escalator in the second contract year multiplies the base by 1.04, and in the third by 1.04 squared. When the rate is being stamped on the agreement itself, the pricing month is the month containing the current logical instant; when the rate is being applied to a subscriber report, the pricing month is that report's own `period_month`, which is why an escalating agreement prices an old period differently from a current one.

The effective rate is the escalated rate after the most-favored-nations cap. An MFN clause is attached by an `AffiliateSalesExec`, naming the `agreement_id` it caps, an `mfn_scope` of either `DOMESTIC` or `GLOBAL`, a `rate_floor_cents` of at least zero below which the capped rate may never fall, and optionally a `min_peer_subscribers` threshold (default 0) that excludes small peers from the comparison. A clause is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, `benchmark_status` `NOT_EVALUATED`, and `benchmark_rate_cents`, `benchmark_peer_agreement_id` and `last_reset_at` all null; creating it does **not** change the agreement's effective rate, because an unevaluated clause has nothing to cap with. An agreement may hold at most one clause in `ACTIVE` status, and attaching a second is refused with `ERR_MULTIPLE_ACTIVE_MFN_CLAUSES`.

The cap itself works like this. If the agreement has an `ACTIVE` clause whose `benchmark_status` is `BENCHMARKED`, take the lesser of the escalated rate and the clause's `benchmark_rate_cents`, then take the greater of that result and the clause's `rate_floor_cents`, round half up to four decimal places, and use it as `effective_rate_cents_per_sub`; `mfn_capped` becomes true exactly when that value is strictly less than the escalated rate. If there is no `ACTIVE` clause, or the `ACTIVE` clause's `benchmark_status` is `NOT_EVALUATED` or `NO_COMPARABLE`, the effective rate equals the escalated rate and `mfn_capped` is false. A clause in `WAIVED` status never caps anything.

Re-running the benchmark is an explicit act by an `AffiliateSalesExec`, guarded by `expected_agreement_version` as described in §2.7, and available only while the clause is `ACTIVE` — a reset or waiver aimed at a clause that is already `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MFN_STATE`. A reset first assembles the comparison set. A peer agreement qualifies when it is not this agreement itself, its `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED` or `SUSPENDED`, its `committed_tier` equals this agreement's `committed_tier` so the comparison is like-for-like on packaging, its `term_start_date` is at or before the current logical instant while its `term_end_date` is strictly after it, its operator is a different operator from this agreement's (so nobody benchmarks against themselves), that operator's `total_subscribers` is at least the clause's `min_peer_subscribers`, and that operator's `distributor_type` is admitted by the clause's scope. `DOMESTIC` admits `CABLE_MSO`, `DBS` and `TELCO`; `GLOBAL` admits those three plus `VMVPD`. Agreements in `DRAFT`, `NEGOTIATING`, `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED` are never candidates.

Each candidate is then converted to a comparable figure. Start from that peer agreement's own `effective_rate_cents_per_sub`. Multiply it by a volume-band factor chosen from the peer operator's `total_subscribers`: 1.05 for an operator below 2,000,000 subscribers, 1.00 for one from 2,000,000 up to but not including 10,000,000, and 0.94 for one at 10,000,000 or above. Then, if the peer operator's `distributor_type` is `VMVPD`, reduce the result by a further eight percent — that is, multiply by 0.92; otherwise leave it unchanged. Round each peer's adjusted figure half up to four decimal places of a cent **before** any comparison is made. `benchmark_rate_cents` is the smallest of those adjusted figures, and `benchmark_peer_agreement_id` identifies the peer agreement that produced it; where two candidates tie on the adjusted figure, the benchmark peer is the one whose agreement has the earlier `created_at`, and the record `id` is never used as a tiebreaker. `benchmark_status` becomes `BENCHMARKED` and `last_reset_at` is stamped with the current logical instant.

When the comparison set comes out empty, the outcome is explicit rather than an error: `benchmark_rate_cents` and `benchmark_peer_agreement_id` are both set to null, `benchmark_status` becomes `NO_COMPARABLE`, `last_reset_at` is still stamped, the agreement keeps its `escalated_rate_cents_per_sub` as its effective rate, and `mfn_capped` is false.

Because the effective rate is an input to every unbilled fee, a successful reset cascades in four linked steps. The owning agreement re-stamps `effective_rate_cents_per_sub` and `mfn_capped`. Every subscriber report of that agreement whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` and whose `invoice_id` is still null re-stamps `rate_applied_cents_per_sub` and `license_fee_due_cents` under the final fee variant of §3.4 — reports already claimed by an invoice are left alone. The agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents` is then recomputed as the sum of `license_fee_due_cents` across its `ACCEPTED` and `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` reports. Finally, every invoice of that agreement still sitting in `DRAFT` re-runs the six-step net-due evaluation of §3.8 and re-stamps `gross_license_fee_cents`, `coop_cap_pct`, `coop_offset_cents`, `launch_fee_offset_cents` and `net_due_cents`. The reset increments both the clause's `version` and the agreement's `version` by one.

Waiving a clause instead moves its `status` from `ACTIVE` to `WAIVED`, at which point the agreement's `effective_rate_cents_per_sub` reverts to `escalated_rate_cents_per_sub` and `mfn_capped` returns to false.

### 3.4. Monthly Subscriber Reporting and the Two Fee Variants

Each month the operator declares how many subscribers received the network. A `DistributorLiaison` files that declaration for its own operator, and a `RevenueAccountant` may also file one on the operator's behalf. The filing names the `agreement_id` being reported against, a `period_month` written as a four-digit year, a hyphen and a two-digit month, a `reported_subscribers` count of zero or more, and a `reported_tier` naming the tier the network was actually packaged on during the period. A filing that replaces an earlier one additionally carries `restatement_of_report_id`; an original filing leaves that field null. Every filing carries `expected_agreement_version`. A new report opens in `SUBMITTED` with `license_fee_due_cents` 0, `audit_adjustment_cents` 0, `invoice_id` null, `accepted_at` null and `version` 1, and it increments the agreement's `version` by one.

When `restatement_of_report_id` is supplied it must name a report of the *same* `agreement_id` and the *same* `period_month` whose `status` is `RESTATED`; anything else — a different agreement, a different month, or a report in any other status — is refused with `ERR_RESTATEMENT_TARGET_INVALID`. A valid restatement drives the named report from `RESTATED` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state as part of the same request. No fee is ever recognised against a superseded report, and the agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents` is unaffected by the supersession, because a report that reached `RESTATED` never contributed revenue in the first place.

At submission the server stamps four derived values. `penetration_pct` is `reported_subscribers` divided by the operator's `total_subscribers`, rounded half up to four decimal places. `rate_applied_cents_per_sub` is the agreement's effective rate evaluated for this report's own `period_month`, using the escalator and MFN machinery of §3.3 with the first day of that month as the pricing instant. `tier_placement_breach` is true exactly when the tier actually used was narrower than the tier committed: rank the tiers with `BASIC` at 4, `EXPANDED_BASIC` at 3, `DIGITAL_TIER` at 2 and `PREMIUM` at 1, where a higher rank means broader carriage, and set the flag when the rank of `reported_tier` is strictly below the rank of the agreement's `committed_tier`. And `provisional_fee_cents` is stamped as the provisional fee variant.

The fee itself has **two variants that differ in exactly one input**, and both use the same arithmetic afterwards. Compute the floor subscriber count as the agreement's `penetration_floor_pct` multiplied by the operator's `total_subscribers`, discarding any fraction. Then select the tier multiplier from the tier actually reported: `BASIC` and `EXPANDED_BASIC` both multiply by 1.00, `DIGITAL_TIER` by 0.85, and `PREMIUM` by 0.60. The fee is the billable subscriber count multiplied by `rate_applied_cents_per_sub` multiplied by that tier multiplier, rounded half up to a whole number of cents.

- Under the **provisional** variant, stamped at submission into `provisional_fee_cents`, the billable count is `reported_subscribers` verbatim; the penetration floor is ignored entirely. This figure exists for accrual visibility only and is never billed.
- Under the **final** variant, stamped at acceptance into `billable_subscribers` and `license_fee_due_cents`, the billable count is the greater of `reported_subscribers` and the floor subscriber count. Whenever the operator reported fewer subscribers than the floor requires, the final fee therefore strictly exceeds the provisional fee for identical inputs — the network bills the floor it negotiated, not the shortfall the operator delivered.

Because each report's fee is rounded to whole cents at this point, and invoices later sum those already-rounded integers, the report is the unit of rounding. Reports sitting in `DISPUTED`, `RESTATED` or `SUPERSEDED` never receive a `license_fee_due_cents` value at all.

A `RevenueAccountant` reviews a filed report with one of four actions — accept, dispute, request restatement, or accept adjustment — optionally accompanied by a `note`. The review endpoint enforces the state machine first: acceptance and dispute both require `SUBMITTED`, requesting a restatement requires `DISPUTED`, and accepting an adjustment requires `AUDIT_ADJUSTED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`.

Acceptance carries the most conditions, and their evaluation order matters because several can fail at once. After the state check, the server tests the owning agreement's lifecycle: acceptance is admissible while `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED` **or** `SUSPENDED`, and is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED` for `DRAFT`, `NEGOTIATING`, `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`. Admitting a suspended agreement here is deliberate: a suspended operator keeps settling the periods it reports, and this is the only path by which a penetration-breach suspension can ever be cured, since reinstatement demands a cleared breach counter and only report acceptance can clear it. Next comes period uniqueness: if any other report for the same `agreement_id` and `period_month` already sits in `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED`, the acceptance is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCEPTED_PERIOD`. Last comes the term test: `period_month` must lie within the month of `term_start_date` through the month of `term_end_date` inclusive, or the response is `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM`. A report that is simultaneously a duplicate period *and* outside the term therefore surfaces the duplicate-period conflict, not the term error.

A successful acceptance stamps `billable_subscribers`, `rate_applied_cents_per_sub` and `license_fee_due_cents` under the final variant, sets `accepted_at` to the current logical instant, and moves the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`. Four linked effects follow. First, the agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents` increases by the newly stamped `license_fee_due_cents`, and `penetration_compliance_status` is re-derived as described in §3.5. Second, if that re-derivation drives `penetration_breach_count` up to the suspension threshold while the agreement is `EXECUTED`, the agreement auto-transitions `EXECUTED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `PENETRATION_BREACH`. Third, because the agreement entered `SUSPENDED`, each of its rights windows in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED`, in `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending order. Fourth, each content title touched by those windows re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`. The report's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every entity the cascade mutated.

Disputing a report moves it `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED`, and is admissible while the owning agreement's `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED` or `SUSPENDED`. A dispute leaves `license_fee_due_cents` at 0 while retaining `provisional_fee_cents` for accrual reporting. Requesting a restatement then moves the report `DISPUTED → RESTATED`, which is purely an eligibility change: it makes the report a legitimate target for a later filing's `restatement_of_report_id`, and that later filing is what finally drives it to `SUPERSEDED`. This is the first of the report's two cycles — `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → RESTATED → SUPERSEDED`, with the replacement filing re-entering the machine at `SUBMITTED`.

The second cycle is `ACCEPTED → AUDIT_ADJUSTED → ACCEPTED`, and its outbound leg is driven by the audit process of §3.7 rather than by a reviewer. Accepting the adjustment returns the report `AUDIT_ADJUSTED → ACCEPTED`, and it requires that the money has already been billed: the owning audit engagement must be in `RECOVERY_INVOICED` or `COLLECTED`. The transition retains `audit_adjustment_cents`, leaves `license_fee_due_cents` unchanged, and leaves the agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents` unchanged, because the adjustment was folded into recognised revenue at the moment the report became `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` and must not be counted twice.

### 3.5. Penetration Compliance, Breach Escalation and Cure

Immediately after a report's final fee is stamped on acceptance, the agreement's penetration position is re-derived. The comparison is **inclusive**: the period is compliant when the report's `penetration_pct` is greater than or equal to the agreement's `penetration_floor_pct`, with both values rounded to four decimal places before being compared, so an operator that lands exactly on its floor is compliant.

A compliant period increments `consecutive_compliant_periods` by one and sets `penetration_compliance_status` to `COMPLIANT`. When that run reaches three, the agreement's slate is wiped: `penetration_breach_count` is reset to 0 and `consecutive_compliant_periods` is itself reset to 0, so the reward pays out once and the operator must build a fresh run of three to earn it again.

A non-compliant period does the reverse. `consecutive_compliant_periods` drops to 0, `penetration_breach_count` increments by one, and `penetration_compliance_status` becomes `BREACH`. When `penetration_breach_count` reaches two while the agreement's `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED`, the agreement is automatically suspended with `suspension_reason` of `PENETRATION_BREACH`, dragging the rights-window and content-title cascade behind it. Two missed periods, in other words, are enough to pull the network off the operator's lineup.

One ordering subtlety governs these counters: they advance in **acceptance order, not period order**. A reviewer who accepts March after accepting May updates the counters in the sequence the acceptances happened, and the pinned sequence is the acceptance sequence. Consider an agreement that accepts a compliant January, then a compliant February, then a breaching March, then a compliant April: after March the run is broken and the breach count stands at 1, and April starts a new run at 1 rather than completing the old one.

A second subtlety concerns audited periods. When an audit flips an accepted report to `AUDIT_ADJUSTED`, `consecutive_compliant_periods` is forced back to 0 — a period whose numbers turned out to be wrong no longer counts toward a compliant run — while `penetration_breach_count` is left exactly as it was. Because the breach count is unchanged, `penetration_compliance_status` re-derives to `BREACH` whenever that count is still positive, and an operator part-way to its three-period cure loses that progress the moment an audit lands on one of the periods in the run.

### 3.6. Content Rights, Platform Windows and Territorial Blackouts

A `RightsAdministrator` registers each programme or series as a content title, supplying a `title` of 1 to 200 characters, a `home_territory_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the ISO territory of first release, and a `linear_premiere_at` instant. That premiere instant is the anchor for every windowing holdback rule in the system. A newly registered title opens with `rights_status` of `PENDING_CLEARANCE`, `cleared_window_count` of 0, `distribution_coverage_status` of `UNDISTRIBUTED` and `version` 1.

The `rights_status` of a title moves only by explicit administrative act, to `OWNED_CLEARED`, `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSED`, `EXPIRED_RIGHTS` or back to `PENDING_CLEARANCE`. Only `OWNED_CLEARED` permits a window on that title to be cleared; `PENDING_CLEARANCE`, `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSED` and `EXPIRED_RIGHTS` all block clearance with `ERR_TITLE_RIGHTS_NOT_CLEARED`. Moving a title to `EXPIRED_RIGHTS` is destructive: every rights window of that title in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `RIGHTS_LAPSED`, and the title then re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`. Every rights-status change increments the title's `version` by one.

`cleared_window_count` is the number of that title's windows whose `status` is `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE`. `distribution_coverage_status` is derived from the *set of distinct platforms* among exactly those windows, by a pinned ladder evaluated top to bottom and stopping at the first arm that matches: if the set is empty the status is `UNDISTRIBUTED`; otherwise if the set is exactly `LINEAR` and nothing else the status is `LINEAR_ONLY`; otherwise if the set holds two or more distinct platforms the status is `MULTIPLATFORM`; otherwise the set holds exactly one platform which is not `LINEAR`, and the status is `EXCLUSIVE_UNCLEARED`, meaning a downstream platform is cleared while the linear window that is supposed to precede it is not. The ladder is total — every title matches exactly one arm — and it is re-evaluated in full, from the current window set, after every event that changes that set.

A window is planned before it is cleared. Planning one names the `agreement_id` granting it, the `content_title_id` being licensed, a `platform` of `LINEAR`, `VOD`, `STREAMING` or `INTERNATIONAL`, a `territory_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `window_start` and a `window_end` strictly later than the start, and an `exclusivity` of either `EXCLUSIVE` or `NON_EXCLUSIVE`. The new window opens in `PLANNED` with `block_reason` of `NONE`, `concurrent_licensee_count` of 0 and `version` 1. Its `windowing_holdback_days` is stamped immediately, as the whole number of days from the title's `linear_premiere_at` to the window's `window_start`, discarding any fraction; a window that opens before the linear premiere therefore carries a negative holdback. No occupancy check runs at planning time, because a `PLANNED` window occupies nothing.

Clearing a window is where the constraints bite, and they are evaluated in a fixed sequence. The window must be in `PLANNED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_STATE`). The granting agreement's `lifecycle_status` must be strictly `EXECUTED` — a suspended agreement cannot clear new rights, and the refusal is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`. The title's `rights_status` must be `OWNED_CLEARED`. Then no `SCHEDULED` or `ENFORCED` blackout rule of the same agreement and the same `territory_code` may intersect the window's half-open interval, or the clearance is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Then the exclusivity test, then the aggregate-concurrency test, then the holdback test, and finally the containment test: the window's half-open interval must lie inside the interval from `term_start_date` inclusive to `term_end_date` exclusive, or the response is `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM`. Because the territorial rule is a regulatory obligation to a local broadcaster while exclusivity is a private contractual matter, a window that conflicts with both a blackout and an exclusive neighbour reports the blackout conflict.

Occupancy is computed over the neighbours that share the window's `content_title_id`, `platform` and `territory_code`, excluding the window itself, whose `status` is `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE`, and whose interval overlaps this one under the half-open rule of §2.10 — each side's start strictly before the other side's end. Windows in `PLANNED`, `BLOCKED` or `EXPIRED` never occupy the resource, which is why a blocked window's slot is immediately reusable by someone else. The rule then splits on this window's own `exclusivity`:

- An `EXCLUSIVE` grant tolerates no overlap whatsoever, of any exclusivity. If even one neighbour exists, the clearance is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`.
- A `NON_EXCLUSIVE` grant tolerates non-exclusive company but not exclusive company. If any neighbour is itself `EXCLUSIVE`, the clearance is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`. Otherwise the aggregate cap applies: counting this window together with its neighbours, at most three simultaneous non-exclusive licensees may hold the same title, platform and territory, and a fourth is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_CONCURRENCY_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Either way, a window that clears successfully has `concurrent_licensee_count` stamped with the number of neighbours found — not including itself.

The holdback test compares the window's `windowing_holdback_days` against the minimum for its platform, with an inclusive threshold: `LINEAR` requires 0 days, `VOD` requires 1 day, `STREAMING` requires 7 days, and `INTERNATIONAL` requires a full 30 days after the linear premiere. Because the holdback is measured in whole days with the fraction discarded, a streaming window opening exactly seven days after the premiere passes, while one opening six days and twenty-three hours after it floors to 6 and is refused with `ERR_HOLDBACK_NOT_SATISFIED`.

Clearing a window moves it `PLANNED → CLEARED`, increments the title's `cleared_window_count` by one and re-derives `distribution_coverage_status`. Thereafter the clock takes over: a `CLEARED` window whose start has arrived and whose end has not moves `CLEARED → ACTIVE` on a clock advance, provided the granting agreement is still `EXECUTED`; and a window in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` whose `window_end` has been reached moves to `EXPIRED`, decrementing the title's `cleared_window_count` and re-deriving coverage. A window still in `PLANNED` when its end passes, or when its granting agreement reaches `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`, moves straight to `EXPIRED`; a `BLOCKED` window moves to `EXPIRED` when its granting agreement reaches `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`.

Blocking is available from both `CLEARED` and `ACTIVE`, and arrives either from a `RightsAdministrator` acting deliberately — in which case a `block_reason` may accompany the action and the recorded reason is `MANUAL_BLOCK` — or from a system event: the granting agreement entering `SUSPENDED` records `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED`, a blackout being enforced over the window records `BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`, and a title losing its rights records `RIGHTS_LAPSED`. Whichever route fires, the title's `cleared_window_count` decrements by one and `distribution_coverage_status` is re-derived from the remaining window set. Unblocking moves a window `BLOCKED → CLEARED`, requires the granting agreement to be `EXECUTED` and the title to be `OWNED_CLEARED`, and re-runs both the occupancy check and the blackout-intersection check — a slot that was taken over while the window sat blocked cannot be reclaimed. On success `block_reason` returns to `NONE` and the title's `cleared_window_count` increments by one, restoring exactly the value it held before the block: a block followed by an unblock must leave both `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status` identical to their pre-block values. The holdback and containment tests are not re-run on an unblock; they belong to the initial clearance only.

Territorial restrictions are administered separately as blackout rules, also by a `RightsAdministrator`. A rule names the `agreement_id` whose restriction it enforces, the `territory_code` blacked out, and the `blackout_start` and `blackout_end` bounding it, with the end strictly later than the start. `substitution_required` defaults to true and states whether the operator must run substitute programming during the blackout; when it is true, `substitute_content_title_id` must be present and must name a title whose `rights_status` is `OWNED_CLEARED`, or the rule is refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_TITLE_REQUIRED`. A new rule opens in `SCHEDULED` with `enforced_at` null and `version` 1.

Two aggregate checks run at scheduling time, and both are keyed on the *operator* rather than the agreement: rules are compared across every agreement of the operator resolved through the naming agreement's `distributor_id`, restricted to the same `territory_code` and to rules in `SCHEDULED` or `ENFORCED` status. Rules that are `WAIVED` or `EXPIRED` neither conflict nor count. The separation check comes first: applying the closed-interval convention and the 24-hour buffer of §2.10, a proposed rule that comes within a day of an existing rule on the same key — including touching it exactly — is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_SEPARATION_VIOLATION`. The monthly cap comes second. The rule's own `blackout_hours` is the elapsed time from `blackout_start` to `blackout_end` expressed in hours and rounded half up to two decimal places. `monthly_blackout_hours` is that figure plus the `blackout_hours` of every other qualifying rule on the same key whose `blackout_start` falls in the same UTC calendar month, and if the total exceeds 72 hours — three full days of blackout in one operator territory in one month — the rule is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_MONTHLY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The substitution-title check is evaluated after both interval checks, so a rule that both violates separation and omits its substitute title reports the separation violation.

A scheduled rule can be enforced or waived, and either action requires the rule to still be in `SCHEDULED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_BLACKOUT_STATE`. Enforcement moves `SCHEDULED → ENFORCED`, stamps `enforced_at` with the current logical instant, and then blocks the programming it restricts: every rights window of the same agreement and the same `territory_code` in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` whose half-open interval intersects the rule's closed interval moves to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`, after which each affected content title re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`. Waiving instead moves `SCHEDULED → WAIVED`, after which the rule stops conflicting with anything and stops counting toward `monthly_blackout_hours` for its month. A rule still in `SCHEDULED` when its `blackout_end` has passed is swept to `EXPIRED` on the next clock advance.

### 3.7. Audit Rights, Findings and Underreporting Recovery

The network's audit rights let an `AuditManager` test an operator's declared counts against the operator's own systems. An engagement names the `agreement_id` under audit, the `sample_period_start` and `sample_period_end` bounding the audited months inclusively — both written in the same year-and-month form as a report's `period_month`, with the end at or after the start — and a `rate_basis` of either `PERIOD_RATE` or `CLOSING_RATE`, which selects how the recovery will be priced. A new engagement opens in `OPENED`, with `total_variance_subscribers`, `total_reported_subscribers`, `underreporting_pct`, `base_recovery_cents`, `interest_cents`, `audit_cost_shift_cents` and `recovery_amount_cents` all at zero, `penalty_multiplier` at 1.0, `recovery_invoice_id` and `findings_issued_at` null, and `version` 1.

Audit rights outlive the contract but not indefinitely. Fieldwork on an agreement that has already reached `EXPIRED` may only begin within 24 months of its `term_end_date`, and `sample_period_end` may never reach past the month containing `term_end_date`; either violation is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`, both when the engagement is opened and when fieldwork is started.

Starting fieldwork moves the engagement `OPENED → FIELDWORK`. The agreement being audited must be in `EXECUTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` — those are the three states in which audit rights exist — and the sample must actually contain something to audit: at least one subscriber report of that agreement in `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` status must carry a `period_month` inside the sample range, or the request is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_NOT_REPORTED`.

Findings are recorded one at a time while the engagement is in `FIELDWORK`; recording one against an engagement in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`. A finding names the `subscriber_report_id` it contradicts and the `audited_subscribers` count the auditor substantiated, which may not be negative. The named report must belong to the engagement's agreement, must be in `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED`, and must have a `period_month` inside the engagement's sample range; otherwise the finding is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_NOT_REPORTED`. On creation the server snapshots the report's `period_month` and its `reported_subscribers` onto the finding, so a later restatement cannot rewrite audit history, and computes `variance_subscribers` as the audited count less the reported count, floored at zero — an operator that over-reported produces a finding of zero variance, never a negative one. The finding's own `engagement_id` records its engagement, while its `rate_applied_cents_per_sub` and `finding_recovery_cents` stay at zero until the engagement issues findings. A finding is immutable thereafter: nothing updates or deletes it.

From `FIELDWORK` the engagement **forks**, and the two branches have disjoint conditions and disjoint effects.

The **issue-findings** branch moves `FIELDWORK → FINDINGS_ISSUED` and requires at least one finding with a positive `variance_subscribers`; attempting it on a sample that turned up no shortfall is refused with `ERR_NO_AUDIT_FINDINGS`. The **close-with-no-finding** branch moves `FIELDWORK → CLOSED_NO_FINDING` and requires the opposite: no finding of the engagement may have positive `variance_subscribers`, and attempting it while one does is refused with `ERR_FINDINGS_PRESENT_CANNOT_CLOSE`. Closing with no finding sets `total_variance_subscribers` to 0, `underreporting_pct` to 0, `penalty_multiplier` to 1.0, and `base_recovery_cents`, `interest_cents`, `audit_cost_shift_cents` and `recovery_amount_cents` all to 0; it touches no subscriber report and leaves the agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents` exactly as it was. `CLOSED_NO_FINDING` is terminal.

Issuing findings stamps `findings_issued_at` with the current logical instant and prices the whole sample. Findings are processed in a pinned chronological order: `period_month` ascending, and within a month `created_at` ascending. The record `id` is never used for ordering. For each finding in that order:

1. Recompute its variance as the audited count less the reported count, floored at zero.
2. Select the rate. Under `PERIOD_RATE` — the contractual reading — each audited month is re-priced at the agreement's effective rate for that month's own `period_month`, escalator and MFN cap included. Under `CLOSING_RATE` — the settlement reading — every finding in the sample uses one single figure: the agreement's `effective_rate_cents_per_sub` as it stands at the findings-issuance instant. On an escalating agreement the closing-rate reading produces a strictly larger recovery than the period-rate reading for the same findings, because early periods are re-priced at the later, higher rate. The selected figure is stamped on the finding as its `rate_applied_cents_per_sub`.
3. Select the tier multiplier from the `reported_tier` of the report the finding contradicts, using the same multipliers as the license fee: 1.00 for `BASIC` and `EXPANDED_BASIC`, 0.85 for `DIGITAL_TIER`, 0.60 for `PREMIUM`.
4. Stamp `finding_recovery_cents` as the variance multiplied by the selected rate multiplied by that tier multiplier, rounded half up to a whole cent. This rounding happens per finding, before anything is accumulated.
5. Compute the interest on that finding. Count the whole calendar months from the **last day of the finding's `period_month`** to the current logical instant, treating any negative count as zero, and multiply `finding_recovery_cents` by 0.0075 — three quarters of one percent per month, simple, not compounding — and by that month count, rounding half up to a whole cent.
6. Add the finding's recovery to the running base total, its interest to the running interest total, its variance to the running variance total, and its snapshotted `reported_subscribers` to the running reported total.

The engagement's `base_recovery_cents` and `interest_cents` are those two accumulated totals, `total_variance_subscribers` is the accumulated variance and `total_reported_subscribers` the accumulated reported count. `underreporting_pct` is `total_variance_subscribers` divided by `total_reported_subscribers`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is exactly 0 when the denominator is 0. Note that a zero-variance finding still contributes its `reported_subscribers` to the denominator while contributing nothing to the base or interest totals, so including clean periods in a sample **dilutes** the underreporting percentage and can pull the engagement into a lower penalty band.

`penalty_multiplier` is then selected from `underreporting_pct` by a ladder with inclusive lower bounds: 1.00 below 0.0500, 1.10 from 0.0500 up to but not including 0.1000, and 1.25 at 0.1000 and above. Separately, `audit_cost_shift_cents` is 2,500,000 cents — the network's flat 25,000-dollar cost of audit, shifted onto the operator — whenever `underreporting_pct` is at least 0.0500, and 0 otherwise; the shift threshold and the first penalty step deliberately coincide. Finally, `recovery_amount_cents` is the sum of the base total and the interest total, multiplied by `penalty_multiplier` and rounded half up to a whole cent, and then the `audit_cost_shift_cents` is added on top — the cost shift is never itself penalty-multiplied. An engagement with no findings at all has a zero denominator and every one of these outputs is 0.

Issuing findings then cascades in three links. Each cited report with positive variance flips `ACCEPTED → AUDIT_ADJUSTED`, taking `audit_adjustment_cents` equal to that finding's `finding_recovery_cents` and adding that amount to its own `license_fee_due_cents`. The agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents` increases by the sum of the applied adjustments, and its `consecutive_compliant_periods` is forced to 0. Because the breach count is untouched while the compliant run is destroyed, `penetration_compliance_status` re-derives and remains `BREACH` whenever `penetration_breach_count` is still positive. Every entity the cascade touched has its `version` incremented.

The recovery is billed by the accountant, not the auditor, and the money's path back closes the engagement. When an audit-recovery invoice is drafted naming this engagement, the engagement moves `FINDINGS_ISSUED → RECOVERY_INVOICED` and its `recovery_invoice_id` is set to the new invoice — permitted only while `recovery_invoice_id` is null and `recovery_amount_cents` is strictly positive. When that invoice is fully settled, the engagement moves `RECOVERY_INVOICED → COLLECTED`, which is terminal: the operator's `audit_recovery_balance_cents` falls by the invoice's `net_due_cents`, and if that balance has returned to zero while `receivable_balance_cents` sits at or below `credit_limit_cents`, the operator leaves `CREDIT_HOLD` for `ACTIVE` with `credit_hold_reason` back to `NONE`. Should the recovery invoice instead be voided with nothing collected against it, the engagement is compensated back `RECOVERY_INVOICED → FINDINGS_ISSUED` with `recovery_invoice_id` cleared to null, so the same `recovery_amount_cents` can be re-invoiced; `findings_issued_at` is deliberately **not** re-stamped, and both `audit_recovery_balance_cents` and `receivable_balance_cents` fall by the voided `net_due_cents`.

### 3.8. Marketing Support, Invoicing and Cash Application

The network's spend commitments are tracked as a conserved-quantity ledger. An `AffiliateSalesExec` opens a commitment line under an agreement, naming its `commitment_type` — `LAUNCH_FEE`, `COOP_ADVERTISING` or `MARKETING_SUPPORT` — a `committed_cents` amount strictly greater than zero, and a `performance_deadline` after which anything unearned is forfeited. A new line opens `OPEN` with `earned_cents`, `earned_unbilled_cents`, `billed_cents` and `forfeited_cents` all zero, `remaining_cents` equal to `committed_cents`, and `version` 1. The launch-fee and marketing-support lines that agreement execution creates automatically (§3.2) are ordinary commitment lines of exactly this shape.

Two identities hold after **every** mutation of a commitment line, and they are the ledger's correctness condition: `committed_cents` always equals `earned_cents` plus `remaining_cents` plus `forfeited_cents`, and `earned_unbilled_cents` always equals `earned_cents` less `billed_cents`.

Recording accepted proof of performance supplies the amount as `earned_cents` together with a `proof_reference` identifying the evidence. The operation requires the funding agreement to be strictly `EXECUTED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`; requires the line's `performance_deadline` not to have passed at the current logical instant, refusing otherwise with `ERR_COMMITMENT_DEADLINE_PASSED`; and requires that the already-earned total plus the requested amount does not exceed `committed_cents`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_MARKETING_OVEREARN`. On success the amount is added to the line's earned total, `earned_unbilled_cents` is recomputed as earned less billed, and `remaining_cents` is recomputed as `committed_cents` less earned less forfeited. The line's `status` becomes `EARNED` when `remaining_cents` reaches 0, and `PARTIALLY_EARNED` while the earned total is above zero but still below `committed_cents`.

Two other events move the same ledger. Invoice issuance consumes part of the earned-but-unbilled pool as an offset: the consumed amount is added to `billed_cents`, `earned_unbilled_cents` is recomputed, and the line becomes `SETTLED` when both `remaining_cents` and `earned_unbilled_cents` are zero — nothing left to earn and nothing left to bill against. Voiding that invoice subtracts the same consumed amount back out of `billed_cents`, recomputes `earned_unbilled_cents`, and re-derives `status` by the same ladder the earn branch uses, restoring the line to exactly its pre-issue shape. And when a clock advance finds the `performance_deadline` passed with `remaining_cents` still positive, that remainder is added to `forfeited_cents`, `remaining_cents` drops to 0, and `status` becomes `FORFEITED` — permanently, since nothing un-forfeits a commitment.

Invoices are drafted by a `RevenueAccountant`, naming the `distributor_id` billed, the `agreement_id` billed, the `period_month` covered, an `invoice_kind` of `LICENSE_FEE` or `AUDIT_RECOVERY`, and `expected_distributor_version`. For an audit-recovery invoice, `audit_engagement_id` is required and names the engagement whose recovery is being billed; for a license-fee invoice it must be null. On an audit-recovery invoice the `period_month` is the month in which the findings were issued rather than a reported month. A draft opens in `DRAFT` with `paid_cents` 0, `issued_at` null and `version` 1, and drafting increments the operator's `version` by one.

Three referential checks guard the draft, in this order. First, none of the subscriber reports a license-fee invoice would bill may already carry a non-null `invoice_id`; if one does, the draft is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_BILLED`. Second, a license-fee invoice must find at least one unbilled report in `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` status for the given `agreement_id` and `period_month`, and an audit-recovery invoice must name an engagement whose `status` is `FINDINGS_ISSUED`; failing either is `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_REPORTS`. Third, the named engagement's `recovery_invoice_id` must still be null, or the draft is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_ALREADY_INVOICED`. Recall also from §2.1 that the identity which issued the engagement's findings may not draft its recovery invoice.

Drafting runs the **six-step net-due waterfall**, and its evaluation order is pinned because it looks circular: the co-op offset reduces what the operator owes, and the co-op ceiling is a percentage band, so one has to decide which figure selects the band. It is always the step-1 gross, never the post-offset payable. Evaluating it the other way lands in a lower band and yields a strictly larger net due for identical inputs.

1. **Gross.** For a `LICENSE_FEE` invoice, `gross_license_fee_cents` is the sum of `license_fee_due_cents` over the subscriber reports of the named agreement whose `period_month` equals the invoice's `period_month`, whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED`, and whose `invoice_id` is still null — each of those figures already rounded per report. For an `AUDIT_RECOVERY` invoice, the gross is the named engagement's `recovery_amount_cents`.
2. **Co-op ceiling band.** `coop_cap_pct` is selected from that gross: 0.05 when the gross is below 5,000,000 cents, 0.08 from 5,000,000 cents up to but not including 25,000,000 cents, and 0.12 at 25,000,000 cents and above. The ceiling itself is the gross multiplied by `coop_cap_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent — the only rounded intermediate in the waterfall.
3. **Co-op offset.** Sum `earned_unbilled_cents` across the agreement's commitment lines whose `commitment_type` is `COOP_ADVERTISING` or `MARKETING_SUPPORT` and whose `status` is `PARTIALLY_EARNED` or `EARNED`; lines in `OPEN`, `FORFEITED` or `SETTLED` contribute nothing. `coop_offset_cents` is the lesser of that pool and the step-2 ceiling. On an `AUDIT_RECOVERY` invoice it is 0.
4. **Launch-fee offset.** Sum `earned_unbilled_cents` across the agreement's `LAUNCH_FEE` lines in `PARTIALLY_EARNED` or `EARNED` status. `launch_fee_offset_cents` is the lesser of that pool and what the co-op offset left of the gross — that is, the gross less `coop_offset_cents`, floored at zero. The launch-fee pool is deliberately not subject to the co-op ceiling; it is bounded only by the remaining payable.
5. **Recovery adjustment.** `recovery_adjustment_cents` is 0. Audit adjustments are already folded into report fees by §3.7 and must not be counted a second time.
6. **Floor and carryforward.** The raw net is the gross less `coop_offset_cents` less `launch_fee_offset_cents` plus `recovery_adjustment_cents`. `net_due_cents` is that raw net floored at zero, and `carryforward_cents` is however much the raw net went below zero, expressed as a positive amount — the offset value that could not be applied. When the raw net is at or above zero, `carryforward_cents` is 0.

When offsets are actually consumed at issuance, commitment lines are drawn in a pinned order: co-op advertising lines first, then marketing support, then launch fee, and within a type in `created_at` ascending order.

Issuing an invoice moves it `DRAFT → ISSUED` and requires two things beyond the state itself. For a `LICENSE_FEE` invoice the operator must be `ACTIVE`; an operator on `CREDIT_HOLD`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` cannot be handed a new license-fee bill, and the refusal is `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD`. An `AUDIT_RECOVERY` invoice is exempt from that test, precisely so that a held operator can still be billed for what the audit found. And `net_due_cents` must be strictly greater than zero: a fully-offset invoice cannot be issued and must be voided instead, with the refusal `ERR_INVOICE_ZERO_NET_DUE`.

Issuance stamps `issued_at`, consumes the marketing offsets it projected, and stamps each folded report's `invoice_id` with this invoice's identifier. It then runs a five-link cascade. The operator's `receivable_balance_cents` increases by `net_due_cents`, and additionally its `audit_recovery_balance_cents` increases by the same amount when `invoice_kind` is `AUDIT_RECOVERY`. If `receivable_balance_cents` now exceeds `credit_limit_cents`, the operator moves `ACTIVE → CREDIT_HOLD` with `credit_hold_reason` of `RECEIVABLE_LIMIT_BREACH`. Because the operator entered `CREDIT_HOLD`, every agreement of that operator in `EXECUTED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD`, in `created_at` ascending order. Because those agreements entered `SUSPENDED`, their rights windows in `CLEARED` or `ACTIVE` move to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` of `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED`. And because those windows left the occupying states, each affected content title re-derives `cleared_window_count` and `distribution_coverage_status`. One issued invoice can therefore darken a title's coverage.

Cash is applied by recording a payment with an `amount_cents`. The amount must be strictly positive and must not exceed `net_due_cents` less `paid_cents`, or the payment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. A payment that leaves a residue moves the invoice `ISSUED → PARTIALLY_PAID`; a payment that closes the balance exactly moves it `ISSUED → PAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID`. Either way `paid_cents` increases by the amount and the operator's `receivable_balance_cents` decreases by the same amount, with `audit_recovery_balance_cents` decreasing identically when `invoice_kind` is `AUDIT_RECOVERY`. On full settlement of an audit-recovery invoice the owning engagement moves `RECOVERY_INVOICED → COLLECTED`, and whenever `receivable_balance_cents` has fallen to at or below `credit_limit_cents` while `audit_recovery_balance_cents` is exactly zero, the operator leaves `CREDIT_HOLD` for `ACTIVE` with `credit_hold_reason` of `NONE`.

An uncollectable invoice can be written off from either `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, but not before it has aged: the current logical instant must be at or after 120 days from `issued_at`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_WRITE_OFF_TOO_EARLY`. A write-off reduces the operator's `receivable_balance_cents` by the uncollected remainder — `net_due_cents` less `paid_cents` — and adjusts `audit_recovery_balance_cents` identically for an audit-recovery invoice. Critically, a write-off does **not** reduce the agreement's `recognized_revenue_cents`: it is a credit-loss event, not a revenue reversal. `WRITTEN_OFF` is terminal.

Voiding is the compensating path, available from `DRAFT` and from `ISSUED`, and from `ISSUED` only while `paid_cents` is exactly zero — a partially collected invoice must be written off, never voided, and attempting the void is `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`. Voiding a `DRAFT` restores nothing, and that is the point: a draft only *projects* its offsets, so `billed_cents` and `earned_unbilled_cents` were never touched at drafting and are not touched at void, and the conserved identity of §3.8 holds unchanged across both calls. Likewise a draft never claims its source reports — their `invoice_id` stays null — which is why two drafts for the same period can coexist, why only the first one to issue actually claims the reports, and why any later draft naming those now-claimed reports is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_BILLED`. And because a draft never entered the receivable, voiding one leaves `receivable_balance_cents` untouched.

Voiding an `ISSUED` invoice does the full unwind. The operator's `receivable_balance_cents` decreases by `net_due_cents`, restoring the pre-issue balance exactly, with `audit_recovery_balance_cents` adjusted identically for an audit-recovery invoice. Every marketing commitment line whose offset this invoice consumed has its `billed_cents` and `earned_unbilled_cents` restored, so that both ledger identities hold and `earned_unbilled_cents` returns to its pre-issue value. Every report this invoice billed has its `invoice_id` cleared back to null, making those reports billable again. If the receivable now sits at or below `credit_limit_cents` and `audit_recovery_balance_cents` is zero, the operator leaves `CREDIT_HOLD` for `ACTIVE` with `credit_hold_reason` of `NONE` — but agreements suspended for `DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD` are **not** auto-reinstated and still require an explicit reinstatement. And for an audit-recovery invoice, the owning engagement returns `RECOVERY_INVOICED → FINDINGS_ISSUED` with its `recovery_invoice_id` cleared. `VOID` and `PAID` are both terminal.

A per-agreement settlement read rolls all of this up for reconciliation. It reports the `agreement_id`; the agreement's stored `recognized_revenue_cents`; `accepted_report_fee_total_cents`, the sum of `license_fee_due_cents` across that agreement's reports in `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` status; `invoiced_gross_total_cents`, the sum of `gross_license_fee_cents` across that agreement's invoices other than those in `VOID`; `open_receivable_cents`, the sum of `net_due_cents` less `paid_cents` across that agreement's invoices in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; and four marketing roll-ups across the agreement's commitment lines — `marketing_committed_cents`, `marketing_earned_cents`, `marketing_remaining_cents` and `marketing_forfeited_cents`, each the sum of the corresponding per-line field. It closes with two booleans: `marketing_conserved_identity_holds`, true when every one of those lines satisfies committed equals earned plus remaining plus forfeited, and `revenue_identity_holds`, true when `recognized_revenue_cents` equals `accepted_report_fee_total_cents`.

### 3.9. Renewal Leverage and the Second Lifecycle Dimension

`renewal_status` is the agreement's other, independent dimension (§3.2). It begins at `NOT_DUE` and advances only through deliberate negotiation events, never as a side effect of billing or reporting.

Analysis opens the negotiation. An `AffiliateSalesExec` moves the agreement `NOT_DUE → RENEWAL_ANALYSIS`, which requires the `lifecycle_status` to be `EXECUTED` or `SUSPENDED` — an operator under suspension is still a renewal candidate — and requires the renewal window to be open: `term_end_date` must be no more than 270 days beyond the current logical instant. Opening analysis earlier is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The transition stamps `renewal_analysis_started_at` with the current logical instant and leaves `negotiation_round` at 0.

Within that window the exec runs **renewal leverage models**. Each model names the `agreement_id` and a `variant` of `BASE_CASE` or `DOWNSIDE`, and is run against an agreement whose `lifecycle_status` is `EXECUTED` or `SUSPENDED`. A model is an immutable snapshot: it records what the numbers said at one logical instant, carries `id` and `created_at` only, and is never updated or deleted. Running one does not move `renewal_status`; the analysis state and the analyses themselves are separate things.

A model first selects its evidence. It looks for the agreement's most recent substantiated report — the subscriber report whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` with the greatest `period_month`, and, when two such reports share that month, the one with the later `created_at`, so that a restatement accepted after the original supersedes it as the basis of the model. That report's identifier is recorded as `latest_accepted_report_id`. If the agreement has no such report at all, `latest_accepted_report_id` is null, `penetration_ratio` is 0, and `distribution_loss_subscribers` and `annualized_revenue_at_risk_cents` are both 0.

From there the model computes four normalised inputs and one score.

- `subscriber_share` is the operator's `total_subscribers` divided by 90,000,000 — the assumed national addressable universe — rounded half up to six decimal places.
- `penetration_ratio` is the selected report's `penetration_pct` divided by the agreement's `penetration_floor_pct`, capped at 2.0, rounded half up to four decimal places. It measures how far above its contractual floor the operator actually carries the network.
- `payment_score` is 1 less the operator's `receivable_balance_cents` divided by its `credit_limit_cents`, where that ratio is first clamped into the range 0 to 1 inclusive and the divisor is treated as at least 1 to keep it defined, rounded half up to four decimal places. A clean payer scores 1; an operator at or beyond its limit scores 0.
- `compliance_score` is 1 less the agreement's `penetration_breach_count` divided by 3, that ratio likewise clamped into 0 to 1 inclusive, rounded half up to four decimal places. Three or more breaches score 0.
- `leverage_score` is 100 multiplied by the weighted sum of four normalised terms — 0.40 times `subscriber_share` divided by 0.15 and capped at 1.0, plus 0.25 times `penetration_ratio` divided by 1.5 and capped at 1.0, plus 0.20 times `payment_score`, plus 0.15 times `compliance_score` — rounded half up to two decimal places. The weights total 1.00, so the score runs from 0 to 100.

The variant then selects a single behavioural assumption, `recapture_rate`: 0.35 under `BASE_CASE` and 0.10 under `DOWNSIDE` — the fraction of subscribers assumed to follow the network to a competing distributor if this agreement lapses. `distribution_loss_subscribers` is the operator's `total_subscribers` multiplied by the selected report's `penetration_pct` multiplied by one less `recapture_rate`, rounded half up to a whole subscriber. `annualized_revenue_at_risk_cents` is that loss count multiplied by the agreement's `effective_rate_cents_per_sub` multiplied by 12, rounded half up to a whole cent.

Note what the variant does and does not touch. `leverage_score` is computed entirely from the operator's structural position and payment history, so it is **identical** for the two variants of the same agreement at the same instant; only `distribution_loss_subscribers` and `annualized_revenue_at_risk_cents` differ, and the `DOWNSIDE` variant — assuming less recapture — always yields the larger loss and the larger revenue at risk. Both figures are also stamped with the `variant` and `recapture_rate` that produced them, so two models of the same agreement can be compared directly.

Once at least one model exists, the exec can table terms, moving the agreement `RENEWAL_ANALYSIS → RENEWAL_OFFERED` and stamping `renewal_offer_sent_at`. Offering with no model on file is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_REQUIRES_ANALYSIS`: the system will not let terms go out unpriced. An offer can be pulled back for rework, moving `RENEWAL_OFFERED → RENEWAL_ANALYSIS` and incrementing `negotiation_round` by one — the counter therefore records how many times the offer was withdrawn and re-tabled, and it never decreases.

An accepted offer moves the agreement `RENEWAL_OFFERED → RENEWED`. This requires the `lifecycle_status` to be exactly `EXECUTED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED` — a suspended agreement may be analysed and may be offered terms, but may not be renewed until it is reinstated — and requires the operator to be `ACTIVE`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_CREDIT_HOLD`. Renewal extends `term_end_date` by 36 months and leaves `lifecycle_status` at `EXECUTED`, so the agreement keeps running without interruption. Because the contract-year index of §3.3 is measured from `term_start_date`, the extended term means later periods fall into higher escalator years, and `effective_rate_cents_per_sub`, `escalated_rate_cents_per_sub`, `applied_escalator_years` and `mfn_capped` are recomputed accordingly. `RENEWED` is terminal on this dimension.

An offer that is declined or allowed to expire moves the agreement `RENEWAL_OFFERED → LAPSED`, and an agreement still under analysis lapses too when its `term_end_date` passes on a clock advance or when it is terminated outright. `LAPSED` is likewise terminal. Neither `RENEWED` nor `LAPSED` alters `lifecycle_status` by itself: an agreement can sit in `EXPIRED` with `renewal_status` of `LAPSED`, or in `EXECUTED` with `renewal_status` of `RENEWED`, and every one of the resulting pairs is a legal state of the record.
