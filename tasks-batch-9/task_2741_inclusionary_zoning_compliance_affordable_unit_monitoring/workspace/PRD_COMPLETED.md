# Inclusionary Zoning Compliance & Affordable Unit Monitoring API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A city housing department does not lose affordable units to bad intentions; it loses them to bookkeeping. An ordinance is amended and nobody can say which vintage governed a development entitled three years earlier. A developer withdraws a studio from the plan set and the substitute in-lieu payment is never assessed. A rent ceiling is recomputed against a newer area-median-income table and a tenant who was compliant last year is suddenly, on paper, in violation. A restricted condominium resells at an unrestricted price because the resale formula lived in a filing cabinet. Every one of these failures is a records failure, and every one of them permanently removes a household from the affordable stock.

This document specifies the Inclusionary Zoning Compliance & Affordable Unit Monitoring API, the system of record the department will use to carry a development from entitlement screening through permanent affordability monitoring. The API pins the governing ordinance vintage to the approval date at the moment of screening and never silently re-prices what it has already assessed. It computes the set-aside obligation in unit equivalents rather than whole units, so a fractional residual can be discharged with money instead of being rounded away. It runs marketing lotteries with a deterministic, reproducible ranking. It freezes cure deadlines at the instant a finding is issued so that lateness is measured against a fixed target. And it treats the housing trust fund as a conserved quantity: every cent that enters through an in-lieu payment or a shared-appreciation recapture is accounted for as unencumbered, encumbered, or disbursed, at every observable instant.

Two audiences read this system. Compliance staff need to know, for any unit, on any day, whether it is occupied, whether its rent is lawful, and whether an open finding is running against a deadline. External funders need a defensible ratio of delivered affordability against the obligation, on whichever accounting basis their grant agreement names. The requirements below are written so that both readings are computable from stored state rather than reconstructed from narrative.

All business resources are mounted under the `/iz-compliance` path prefix. A single test-driving capability, described in §2.5, is mounted separately under `/_clock`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — including every read, and excluding only the unauthenticated `GET /health` liveness probe described in §4 — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the five recognised roles, the request is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). Only once both headers are well formed does the system consider whether the role is permitted to perform the requested operation; a well-formed request from a role that the endpoint does not list is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `HousingComplianceOfficer`, `Developer`, `Resident`, and `FundAuditor`.

A `ProgramAdministrator` is the city housing department's program lead: this role publishes ordinance vintages and area-median-income schedules, assesses in-lieu fees, opens and runs lottery rounds, decides the city's right of first refusal, and administers the housing trust fund. A `HousingComplianceOfficer` is the monitoring and certification officer: this role screens projects, records regulatory agreements and deed restrictions, reviews marketing plans, certifies household incomes, issues and resolves monitoring findings, and approves resale prices. A `Developer` is the project sponsor: this role registers and amends developments, elects the compliance path, creates and withdraws restricted units, submits marketing plans, pays in-lieu assessments, and cures monitoring findings — but only for developments it owns. A `Resident` is an applicant household or the owner of a restricted unit: this role applies to lottery rounds and initiates the resale of the unit it occupies. A `FundAuditor` is the external funder's auditor: this role reads the trust-fund ledger, the draw register, and the program-compliance reports, and holds no write capability whatsoever — every write endpoint refuses a `FundAuditor` with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership checks are performed against `X-User-ID` directly.

- When `X-User-Role` is `Resident`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Household`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Household` compare the resource's household reference to `X-User-ID`. Applying on behalf of a different household, or submitting a resale of a unit whose `occupant_household_id` is not `X-User-ID`, is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Developer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `developer_user_id` on a `Project` at registration time and thereafter compares it to that stored value: every `Developer` write against a project, one of its restricted units, one of its marketing plans, one of its in-lieu assessments, or one of its monitoring reviews is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` unless `X-User-ID` equals the owning project's `developer_user_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `HousingComplianceOfficer`, or `FundAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a marketing plan, `drawn_by_user_id` on a lottery round, `certified_by_user_id` on an income certification, `issued_by_user_id` and `cleared_by_user_id` on a monitoring review, `price_approved_by_user_id` on a resale transaction, or `requested_by_user_id` on a trust-fund draw — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two operations additionally enforce **segregation of duties**, comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against an identifier the system recorded on an earlier step of the same workflow. The officer whose `X-User-ID` was captured as `issued_by_user_id` when a monitoring review was created may not clear that review's cure. The officer whose `X-User-ID` was captured as `price_approved_by_user_id` when a resale price was approved may not certify that transaction's buyer. Either attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Both checks compare identifiers, not roles: a second officer holding the same `HousingComplianceOfficer` role is exactly who is expected to perform the second step.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters *before* the `limit`/`offset` window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned in this page is strictly less than `total_count`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Collection reads are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100, and a value outside that range is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and must not be negative, and it carries no upper bound — an `offset` at or past the end of the collection returns an empty `data` array with `has_more` false. Either parameter supplied with an empty value (`?limit=`) is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, and a value that is not a base-10 integer is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both parameters, along with `total_count` and `has_more`, are echoed in the `meta` object of the response envelope.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every collection is sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with the order in which the rows were written — an internal insertion sequence never exposed as a response field — as the stable tie-break whenever two rows carry the same `created_at` value — and because that stamp is only millisecond-precise, the tie-break is routinely load-bearing. Every other rule in this specification that orders by `created_at` ascending resolves its own ties the same way. This ordering is not cosmetic — several rules in this specification break ties by creation order, and the list reads are the client's window onto that same ordering.

Collections also accept equality filters drawn from the resource's own attributes, and those filters double as the reverse-lookup mechanism for a foreign key. Ordinance vintages and AMI schedules can be narrowed by `status`. Projects can be narrowed by `ordinance_id`, `state`, `applicability`, or `compliance_posture`. Restricted units can be narrowed by `project_id`, `agreement_id`, `occupant_household_id`, `occupancy_status`, `compliance_status`, `restriction_state`, or `tenure`. Regulatory agreements accept `project_id` and `state`; marketing plans accept `project_id` and `state`; lottery rounds accept `marketing_plan_id` and `state`; households accept `lottery_event_id` and `state`; income certifications accept `household_id`, `unit_id`, `state`, and `is_recertification`; monitoring reviews accept `unit_id`, `state`, and `review_year`; resale transactions accept `unit_id`, `prior_transaction_id`, `seller_household_id`, `buyer_household_id`, `buyer_certification_id`, and `state`; in-lieu assessments accept `project_id`, `ordinance_id`, `superseded_by_fee_id`, `basis`, and `state`; trust-fund draws accept `source_fee_id`, `eligible_use`, and `state`. Filter matching is exact equality against the stored value: a query parameter not listed above for that collection is silently ignored rather than refused; a filter naming an attribute that is currently null matches only when the supplied value is the literal `null` or empty; an integer-valued attribute is compared numerically and a boolean one accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`; a filter naming a list-valued attribute matches rows whose list contains the supplied value; and a value that cannot be read as the stored attribute's type simply matches nothing rather than raising `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource in this system — ordinance vintages, AMI schedules, projects, restricted units, marketing plans, lottery rounds, households, income certifications, regulatory agreements, monitoring reviews, resale transactions, in-lieu assessments and trust-fund draws alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. There is no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) recording when the row was first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotonically increasing counter that increments by exactly one on every mutation of the row. A freshly inserted row is written with `version` 1 and with `updated_at` equal to its own `created_at`.

No entity in this system is exempt: all thirteen resource families carry `updated_at` and `version`. These row-lifecycle stamps are the one place the system reads real time: `created_at`, `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` are taken from the host's actual UTC clock, so advancing the logical clock of §2.5 never moves them. Every business-time field — `screened_at`, `due_date`, `last_payment_at`, `drawn_at`, `finding_issued_at`, `cure_deadline`, `cure_submitted_at`, `price_approved_at`, `rofr_deadline`, `closed_at`, `disbursed_at` and the rest — reads the logical clock instead.

The `version` counter is the observable trace of the cascade behaviour described throughout §3, and it is deliberately noisy. When one operation mutates several rows, **each mutated row independently increments its own `version` and refreshes its own `updated_at`** — a marketing-plan approval that flips six planned units into marketing bumps the plan's `version` once and each of the six units' `version` once. When a single operation mutates the same row more than once because a cascade re-enters it (the withdrawal chain in §3.3 recomputes the parent project's aggregates twice), the project's `version` advances once per recomputation, not once per request. Derived attributes count as mutations for this purpose: recomputing a project's `compliance_posture`, refreshing a unit's `max_allowed_rent_cents`, or resetting a unit's `original_purchase_price_cents` at a resale close all bump the owning row's `version`.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier in the path that does not resolve decides the response. Because every business collection in this system is a flat top-level collection under `/iz-compliance`, most paths carry exactly one identifier; the rule still governs, and it also governs foreign keys supplied in a request body. The thirteen path identifiers are `ordinance_id`, `ami_schedule_id`, `project_id`, `agreement_id`, `unit_id`, `marketing_plan_id`, `lottery_event_id`, `household_id`, `certification_id`, `monitoring_review_id`, `resale_transaction_id`, `fee_id` and `disbursement_id`, one per resource family. Each entity family has its own not-found code — `ERR_ORDINANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMI_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKETING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOTTERY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MONITORING_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESALE_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_FOUND` — all carrying HTTP 404. A body-supplied `project_id`, `agreement_id`, `marketing_plan_id`, `lottery_event_id`, `household_id`, `unit_id`, `prior_transaction_id`, `source_fee_id`, `buyer_household_id`, or `certification_id` that names no existing row yields the corresponding not-found code from the same catalogue.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Deterministic Nightly Sweep

Every deadline, window and expiry rule in this specification reads a **logical UTC clock** rather than the machine's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real time. Any of the five roles may advance the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability described in §4. That field is required and must be a string parseable as an ISO 8601 instant, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a value carrying no zone offset is read as UTC, and the instant is echoed back canonicalised to three fractional digits. No monotonicity is enforced: an instant equal to or earlier than the current logical now is accepted, becomes the new logical now, and runs the same four-step sweep against it.

Advancing the clock is not a passive assignment — it runs a deterministic batch sweep, and the four steps execute in this fixed order:

1. The new instant is stored as the logical now.
2. Every `IncomeCertification` still in state `CERTIFIED` whose `expires_at` is now strictly in the past transitions to `EXPIRED`, and each such expiry vacates its unit: the unit's `occupancy_status` moves from `OCCUPIED` to `VACANT`, its `current_rent_cents` is reset to 0, its `occupant_household_id` is cleared to null, and the owning project's `compliance_posture` is recomputed. Only rental certifications carry an `expires_at`, so only rental tenancies are ever swept out this way.
3. Every `InLieuFee` in state `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` whose `due_date` falls strictly before the calendar date of the new logical now transitions to `DELINQUENT`.
4. Every `ResaleTransaction` still in state `PRICE_APPROVED` whose `rofr_deadline` falls on a calendar day strictly earlier than the calendar day of the new logical now transitions to `ROFR_WAIVED` with `rofr_auto_waived` set to true.

The response to a clock advance reports the new `current_utc` together with three counters: `certifications_expired`, `fees_marked_delinquent`, and `rofr_windows_auto_waived`.

Step 4 uses a deliberately coarser granularity than the interactive right-of-first-refusal check, and the difference is observable. The interactive check compares instants and treats the deadline instant itself as inclusive, so a decision arriving exactly at `rofr_deadline` is accepted while one arriving a millisecond later is refused with `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The sweep compares calendar dates and acts only once the deadline's own calendar day has fully elapsed. Between the deadline instant and midnight of that same day the transaction therefore remains `PRICE_APPROVED`, and an explicit late decision arriving in that gap surfaces `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Once the day has elapsed the sweep auto-waives the transaction, after which a decision attempt fails the resale state guard instead. An implementation that swept instant-for-instant would make `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_EXPIRED` unreachable.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Two unit systems run through this specification and they must not be confused.

**Money is integer cents.** Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is a whole number of cents, never a decimal fraction and never a string. Because the underlying business quantities are dollar amounts with two decimal places, an amount is fully determined by its cent count; the API neither accepts nor emits fractional cents. Where a formula produces a non-integer intermediate, that intermediate is rounded to a whole cent using **round half up** (a value ending in exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, upward). Each rounding named in §3 is a distinct, ordered step: intermediate results are rounded where the specification says they are, and the rounded intermediate — not the unrounded one — feeds the next step. Collapsing a multi-step pipeline into one expression and rounding once at the end is not an equivalent implementation and will produce different cent values.

**Rates and proportions are basis points.** Every field whose name ends in `_bps` is an integer count of basis points, where 10000 basis points is exactly 100 percent, 5000 is 50 percent, and 1 basis point is one hundredth of one percent. Set-aside rates, affordability levels, administrative surcharges, discount rates, appreciation shares, loan-to-value ceilings and compliance ratios are all expressed this way, so no percentage is ever stored as a floating-point number. Unit obligations use the same integer basis-point scale to express fractions of a single dwelling: 10000 basis points is one whole affordable unit, 5000 is half a unit.

Where a formula divides by 10000 to convert a basis-point rate into an amount, the division is performed against the already-computed cent amount and the result is rounded half up to the whole cent, unless §3 explicitly says the step truncates (drops any fraction toward zero) or floors the result at zero. Calendar arithmetic is exact and integral: days are whole days, and a date plus a day count is a date.

### 2.7. Ordinance and AMI Schedule Vintages

Two reference tables govern every computation in the system, and both are versioned by publication rather than edited in place.

An **ordinance vintage** is a published `Ordinance` row carrying an `effective_date`. Publishing a vintage sets its `status` to `ACTIVE` and, as a side effect, transitions every existing `Ordinance` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_date` is strictly earlier than the new vintage's `effective_date` to `SUPERSEDED`, each such row incrementing its own `version`. Because only *strictly* earlier `ACTIVE` rows are touched, a vintage published with an `effective_date` equal to or earlier than an existing `ACTIVE` one leaves that row `ACTIVE` too, and both then read `ACTIVE`. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. Critically, a `SUPERSEDED` vintage has not stopped mattering: it still governs every development whose planning approval fell inside its period, and superseding a vintage never re-prices an in-lieu assessment that has already been booked, because each assessment copies the governing vintage onto its own `ordinance_id` at the moment it is assessed.

An **AMI schedule** is a published `AmiSchedule` row carrying an `effective_year`. Publishing one sets its `status` to `ACTIVE` and transitions every existing `ACTIVE` schedule with a strictly earlier `effective_year` to `SUPERSEDED`, each incrementing its own `version`. So long as schedules are published in ascending year order, at most one schedule is `ACTIVE` at any instant and it is the one with the greatest `effective_year`; publishing out of order, with an `effective_year` earlier than an existing `ACTIVE` schedule's, supersedes nothing and leaves both rows `ACTIVE`. Ties on `effective_year` cannot arise because `effective_year` is unique across all schedules.

Whenever this specification says a computation reads "the active AMI schedule", it means: consider all published `AmiSchedule` rows, order them by `effective_year` descending and, for safety against equal years, by `created_at` ascending, and take the first. If no schedule has been published at all, the operation that needed one is refused with `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE`. Publishing a newer schedule therefore re-prices every *future* certification, recertification, resale submission, refinance review and funder report, but it never rewrites the `ami_schedule_year`, `income_limit_cents` or `max_rent_cents` already frozen onto an existing `IncomeCertification`.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations in this system legitimately supersede an existing row belonging to a parent record, which makes acting on a stale read of that parent dangerous. Both are therefore guarded by an explicit optimistic-concurrency check against the parent's `version`.

Assessing an in-lieu fee — `POST /iz-compliance/in-lieu-fees` — requires a non-optional `expected_project_version` field in the request body carrying the `version` of the target `Project` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the project's current `version`, the assessment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The guard exists because a new assessment supersedes the project's open assessment: a writer holding a stale view might not know that the obligation has been re-screened, or that a substitution assessment has already been booked, so it must re-read the project before assessing.

Submitting a resale transaction — `POST /iz-compliance/resale-transactions` — requires a non-optional `expected_unit_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `AffordableUnit` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a fresh submission supersedes the unit's open submitted transaction, and because a monitoring cascade may have changed the unit's `compliance_status` since the client read it.

Both fields are plain integers of at least 0, and that range check is part of the payload rung: an `expected_project_version` or `expected_unit_version` that is absent, explicitly null, non-integer or negative is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before any comparison against the stored `version` is attempted, so the 412 codes are reachable only for a well-formed but stale token. A supplied 0 is well formed and simply always mismatches, because every row is written at `version` 1. No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Both guards are evaluated early — ahead of every state and business rule, as §2.9 sets out — so any request that intends to exercise a lower-priority rule on either of these two endpoints must still supply a currently-correct expected version.

### 2.9. The Order of Refusals

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is decided by the **first** violated rule in the ordering below, and only that rule's error is returned. The ordering is normative; implementations that check in a different order will return the wrong code for requests that break several rules at once.

The ladder runs as follows.

1. `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
4. The payload is well formed, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This covers required fields being present, types being correct, enumerated values lying inside their declared domains, integer cent and basis-point fields lying inside their declared ranges, `limit` lying between 1 and 100 inclusive with `offset` not negative, `contract_rent_cents` being exactly 0 when the certified unit's `tenure` is `OWNERSHIP`, and `discount_tiers` arriving ascending by `min_pre_discount_cents` with a leading element whose `min_pre_discount_cents` is exactly 0. A field supplied explicitly as `null` counts as absent: a required field present as `null` fails this rung, and an optional one present as `null` falls back to its documented default. A request body that is absent or empty is read as an empty JSON object, so required fields then fail as missing, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object fails this rung directly. Project screening and close-out are the only two write operations that read no body at all and therefore ignore whatever body they are handed.
5. Every identifier appearing in the URL path resolves, checked left to right, else the matching 404 code from §2.4.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, else the matching 404 code from §2.4.
7. Ownership holds: a `Developer` acting on a project, restricted unit, marketing plan, in-lieu assessment or monitoring review matches the owning project's `developer_user_id`; a `Resident` acting on a household matches that household's `id`; a `Resident` submitting a resale matches the unit's `occupant_household_id`. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This applies to project amendment, path election, unit creation, unit withdrawal, unit reinstatement, marketing-plan submission, marketing-plan resubmission, in-lieu payment, cure submission, lottery application, refinance review, and resale submission.
8. The optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.8 holds, else `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION`.
9. The project's `state` permits the operation, else `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. Screening is permitted only from `DRAFT`; amendment only from `DRAFT`, `SCREENED_SUBJECT`, `SCREENED_EXEMPT`, `PATH_ELECTED` or `AGREEMENT_RECORDED`; path election only from `SCREENED_SUBJECT`; agreement recording only from `PATH_ELECTED`; unit creation only from `PATH_ELECTED`, `AGREEMENT_RECORDED` or `MONITORING`; unit withdrawal from any project state except `CLOSED_OUT`; close-out only from `MONITORING`.
10. The regulatory agreement's `state` permits the operation — `DRAFT` for recording the agreement, `RECORDED` before any unit deed restriction may be recorded against it — else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`.
11. The unit's `restriction_state` permits the operation — `NOT_RECORDED` to record a restriction, `RECORDED` to release one, to run a refinance review, to submit a resale, or to approve a submitted resale's price — else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_RESTRICTED`. Only those two resale steps consult the restriction: the right-of-first-refusal decision, buyer certification and close do not re-read it.
12. The unit's `occupancy_status` permits the operation — withdrawal only from `PLANNED` or `MARKETING`, reinstatement only from `WITHDRAWN`, and deed-restriction recording from any status other than `WITHDRAWN` — else `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_STATE_INVALID`.
13. The marketing plan's `state` permits the operation — review only from `SUBMITTED`, resubmission only from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and opening a lottery round only against an `APPROVED` plan — else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`.
14. The lottery round's `state` permits the operation — drawing only from `PENDING`, redrawing only from `DRAWN`, and accepting an application only while `PENDING` — else `ERR_LOTTERY_STATE_INVALID`.
15. The household's `state` permits the operation — applying only from `REGISTERED`, and being certified only from `REGISTERED` or `SELECTED`, since an already `CERTIFIED` household holds a live placement and is not certified again — else `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_STATE_INVALID`.
16. The certification targeted by a recertification is in state `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`.
17. The monitoring review's `state` permits the operation — cure submission only from `FINDING_ISSUED` or `ESCALATED`, cure clearing and escalation only from `CURE_SUBMITTED` — else `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`.
18. The resale transaction's `state` permits the operation — price approval from `SUBMITTED`, the right-of-first-refusal decision from `PRICE_APPROVED`, buyer certification from `ROFR_WAIVED`, closing from either `BUYER_CERTIFIED` or `ROFR_EXERCISED` — else `ERR_RESALE_STATE_INVALID`.
19. The in-lieu assessment being paid is in state `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `DELINQUENT`; a `PAID` or `SUPERSEDED` assessment accepts no further payment and yields `ERR_FEE_NOT_PAYABLE`.
20. The trust-fund draw's `state` permits the operation — disbursement only from `REQUESTED`, voiding from either `REQUESTED` or `DISBURSED` — else `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.
21. Segregation of duties holds on cure clearing and buyer certification, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
22. The unit is not under enforcement — its `compliance_status` is anything other than `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` — else `ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`. This guard applies to four operations: resale submission, resale price approval, refinance review, and restriction release.
23. A cure being cleared actually fixes the violation, meaning `corrected_rent_cents` is at most the unit's `max_allowed_rent_cents`, else `ERR_CURE_INSUFFICIENT`.
24. The per-unit comparability standard holds for a unit being created, else `ERR_COMPARABILITY_STANDARD_FAILED`.
25. The project-wide bedroom distribution floor holds at close-out, else `ERR_BEDROOM_DISTRIBUTION_FAILED`.
26. An ordinance vintage exists, else `ERR_NO_GOVERNING_ORDINANCE`. At screening this means a vintage effective on or before the project's `approval_date`; when an in-lieu assessment is booked — whether through the assessment endpoint or through the withdrawal cascade — it means the project already carries a pinned `ordinance_id` that still resolves, which an unscreened or freshly amended project does not.
27. At least one AMI schedule has been published, else `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE`. This guard applies to income certification, recertification, deed-restriction recording, refinance review, resale submission, and both the lottery draw and the lottery redraw — the redraw needs a schedule to re-size the pool it re-checks. It does **not** apply to recording a monitoring review, which degrades gracefully when no schedule exists rather than refusing.
28. The elected `compliance_path` is `ONSITE`, or appears in the governing vintage's `alternative_paths_allowed`, else `ERR_PATH_NOT_PERMITTED`.
29. Uniqueness holds, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`: `code` is unique across all ordinance vintages, `effective_year` across all AMI schedules, `unit_number` within a project, `review_year` within a unit, and `doc_number` across all regulatory agreements.
30. The eligible applicant pool for a draw or redraw is non-empty, else `ERR_EMPTY_ELIGIBLE_POOL`.
31. A reinstatement finds an open substitution assessment to supersede, else `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTION_FEE`.
32. `lottery_open_at` is strictly before `lottery_close_at` on plan submission and resubmission, else `ERR_INVALID_MARKETING_WINDOW`.
33. The marketing window does not overlap another live plan of the same project, else `ERR_MARKETING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
34. An application arrives inside the plan's window, else `ERR_APPLICATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
35. For a rental unit, the new certification period does not overlap an existing `CERTIFIED` period for the same unit, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; ownership certifications carry no term and are never scanned.
36. The recertification renewal window is open, else `ERR_RECERT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. This rule is scoped to predecessors that actually carry an `expires_at`, that is, rental certifications; an ownership predecessor has no term, is skipped by this rung, and is caught by rung 57 instead.
37. The right-of-first-refusal window has not lapsed, else `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
38. The regulatory agreement supplied at unit creation belongs to the same project as the unit, else `ERR_UNIT_PROJECT_MISMATCH`.
39. `prior_transaction_id`, when supplied, names a `CLOSED` resale of the same unit; when omitted, the unit has no closed resale history. Otherwise `ERR_PRIOR_TRANSACTION_MISMATCH`.
40. The buyer's certification is `CERTIFIED`, is for this transaction's unit, and belongs to the named buyer household, else `ERR_BUYER_CERTIFICATION_MISMATCH`.
41. The household being certified has income within the unit's income limit, and when the unit's `occupancy_status` is `RESERVED` the household being certified is that unit's `occupant_household_id`. Otherwise `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
42. The project still owes unit equivalents that the requested `basis` can price, else `ERR_NO_REMAINING_OBLIGATION`.
43. A payment does not exceed the assessment's outstanding balance, else `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`.
44. `proposed_price_cents` is within the frozen `max_resale_price_cents`, else `ERR_RESALE_PRICE_EXCEEDS_MAX`.
45. A requested refinance balance is within the loan-to-restricted-value ceiling, else `ERR_REFINANCE_EXCEEDS_CAP`.
46. The source assessment behind a draw is `PAID` before its proceeds may leave the fund, else `ERR_SOURCE_FEE_NOT_PAID`.
47. The substitution assessment targeted by a reinstatement has received no payment, else `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_FEE_PAID`.
48. An administrative draw stays inside the fund-use cap, else `ERR_ADMIN_USE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
49. The fund can cover the commitment, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUND_BALANCE`; this covers both a draw request and an exercised right of first refusal.
50. Approving a marketing plan requires its project to be in state `AGREEMENT_RECORDED`, else `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_READY_FOR_MARKETING`.
51. An agreement is recorded only for a project whose `compliance_path` is `ONSITE` or `OFFSITE`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_REQUIRED`.
52. `recorded_at` is on or after the project's `approval_date`, else `ERR_RECORDING_PREDATES_APPROVAL`.
53. Only an `OWNERSHIP` unit carries a recorded deed restriction, else `ERR_RESTRICTION_TENURE_MISMATCH`.
54. No resale transaction of the unit is live — that is, none is in `SUBMITTED`, `PRICE_APPROVED`, `ROFR_WAIVED`, `ROFR_EXERCISED` or `BUYER_CERTIFIED` — when a restriction is released, else `ERR_RESALE_IN_PROGRESS`.
55. The application window has closed before a draw is run, else `ERR_APPLICATION_WINDOW_OPEN`.
56. No household selected by the round being redrawn has already reached `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_REDRAW_AFTER_CERTIFICATION`.
57. Only a rental certification, one carrying a non-null `expires_at`, may recertify, else `ERR_RECERT_NOT_APPLICABLE`.
58. The project's `compliance_posture` is `COMPLIANT_ONSITE` or `COMPLIANT_BY_FEE` at close-out, else `ERR_OBLIGATION_UNSATISFIED`.
59. Every in-lieu assessment of the project is `PAID` or `SUPERSEDED` at close-out, else `ERR_FEE_OUTSTANDING`.
60. No monitoring review on any unit of the project sits in `FINDING_ISSUED`, `CURE_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED` at close-out, else `ERR_OPEN_FINDING_BLOCKS_CLOSEOUT`.

Two of these orderings are counter-intuitive enough to call out, because they deliberately let a state error mask a fiscal or referential one. First, rung 19 outranks rung 43: paying an over-balance amount into a `SUPERSEDED` assessment violates both rules, and the response must be `ERR_FEE_NOT_PAYABLE`, not `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Second, rung 22 outranks rung 39: submitting a resale on an `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` unit while also naming another unit's closed transaction as the prior link violates both, and the response must be `ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`, not `ERR_PRIOR_TRANSACTION_MISMATCH`.

### 2.10. Interval Conventions: Two Resources, Two Rules

Two families of date-and-time intervals live in this system, and they use **deliberately different** endpoint conventions. Confusing them is a defect, not a rounding difference.

A **certification period** on a restricted unit is a **closed** interval running from `effective_date` through `expires_at`, both endpoints inclusive. Two such periods collide if they share even a single day, which means a new period beginning exactly on an existing period's `expires_at` *is* a collision. That is precisely why an annual recertification places its successor at the predecessor's `expires_at` plus one day.

A **marketing window** on a project is a **half-open** interval running from `lottery_open_at` inclusive to `lottery_close_at` exclusive. Two such windows collide only if they genuinely overlap, which means a new window whose `lottery_open_at` equals an existing window's `lottery_close_at` is exact adjacency and is *accepted*. The same touching-endpoint arrangement that is rejected for certification periods is permitted for marketing windows.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

The system maintains two independent conservation invariants, and both are checkable from stored state at any instant.

The first is denominated in **unit equivalents**. For a subject project that has reached a compliant posture, the sum of `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` and `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` is at least `total_obligation_bps` — and equals it exactly whenever the development delivered no more than it owed, since nothing caps over-delivery and a project that builds more units than required simply carries a larger onsite term. Withdrawing a unit removes 10000 basis points from the onsite term and the cascaded substitution assessment adds exactly 10000 basis points to the fee term; reinstating the unit adds 10000 back to the onsite term and supersedes that assessment, removing 10000 from the fee term. Each compensating pair restores the sum exactly, so the quantity survives withdrawal-and-reinstatement cycles of any length.

The second is denominated in **money**, and it governs the housing trust fund. At every observable instant, the fund's unencumbered balance plus its encumbered total plus its disbursed total equals its total inflow. Requesting a draw moves cents from unencumbered to encumbered; disbursing that draw moves the same cents from encumbered to disbursed and leaves the unencumbered balance untouched; voiding a draw from either state returns the cents, so the unencumbered balance comes back to exactly its pre-request value. The two conserved quantities are unrelated to each other — one counts dwellings, the other counts cents — and neither is ever reconciled against the other.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Publishing the Regulatory Baseline

Nothing in this system can be computed until a `ProgramAdministrator` has published the two reference tables the rest of the program reads.

An **ordinance vintage** is the legal instrument itself, reduced to the parameters the department must apply. It is published with a citation `code` of between 1 and 40 characters, unique across all vintages, and an `effective_date` given as a plain calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form — the earliest project approval date this vintage governs. `min_project_units_threshold`, an integer of at least 1, is the project-size trigger: a development is subject to the ordinance only when its unit count reaches this value. `set_aside_bps_by_district` maps a zoning district code to the set-aside rate in basis points that district owes; its keys are drawn from `R-2`, `R-4` and `C-2`, its values are integers between 1 and 10000 inclusive, and a district that simply does not appear in the map is exempt under that vintage. `gap_basis_cents_by_level` maps an affordability level, given as the string form of its basis-point value and drawn from `"5000"`, `"8000"` and `"12000"`, to the affordability gap per unit in cents, which is the base rate every in-lieu assessment is priced from; each value is an integer of at least 1. `fee_gap_level_bps` names which of those levels prices in-lieu fees under this vintage and must be a key present in `gap_basis_cents_by_level`. Both maps are required fields, and each may carry any subset of its allowed keys — `set_aside_bps_by_district` may even be empty, in which case every district is exempt under the vintage, while `gap_basis_cents_by_level` must at least contain the level `fee_gap_level_bps` names. A key outside the allowed set is a malformed request.

Three further parameters shape the money. `surcharge_bps` is the administrative surcharge rate, an integer from 0 through 10000, applied to the fee base. `surcharge_cap_cents` is a hard ceiling in cents on that surcharge, an integer of at least 0. `discount_tiers` is an ordered scale-discount schedule; each element carries a `min_pre_discount_cents` threshold and a `discount_bps` rate between 0 and 10000, the elements must arrive ascending by `min_pre_discount_cents`, and the first element must have a `min_pre_discount_cents` of exactly 0 so that every possible total falls into some tier. A malformed schedule is rejected as an invalid request. `fee_due_days`, an integer of at least 1, is the number of days from the assessment instant to the resulting assessment's `due_date`. Finally, `alternative_paths_allowed` lists the compliance paths other than on-site construction that this vintage permits; it is a duplicate-free subset of `OFFSITE`, `IN_LIEU_FEE` and `LAND_DEDICATION` — a repeated entry is a malformed request — it may be omitted or empty, in which case it is stored as the empty list, and its contents are what the path-election check in §3.2 consults.

A newly published vintage carries `status` `ACTIVE`. Publishing it transitions every `ACTIVE` vintage with a strictly earlier `effective_date` from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, each superseded row incrementing its own `version`. Republishing a `code` that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

An **AMI schedule** is the income table. It is published with an `effective_year` — a calendar year between 2000 and 2100, unique across all schedules, again refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` on collision — plus two lookup tables. `median_income_cents_by_size` maps household size, as the string keys `"1"` through `"5"` (all five required), to the 100 percent area-median annual income for that size in cents, each an integer of at least 1. `utility_allowance_cents_by_bedrooms` maps bedroom count, as the string keys `"0"` through `"3"` (all four required), to the monthly utility allowance in cents for a unit of that size, each an integer of at least 0. A new schedule's `status` is `ACTIVE`, and publishing it transitions every `ACTIVE` schedule with a strictly earlier `effective_year` from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, each bumping its own `version`.

Selecting the governing vintage for a development is a chronological lookup performed at screening, and it deliberately ignores `status`. Consider every published `Ordinance` whose `effective_date` is on or before the project's `approval_date`; if that candidate set is empty, screening is refused with `ERR_NO_GOVERNING_ORDINANCE`. Otherwise order the candidates by `effective_date` descending and break any tie by `created_at` ascending — so that when two vintages share an `effective_date`, the one created first wins — and take the first. That row's identity is pinned onto the project. A vintage's own `id` never participates in the decision. Because a superseded vintage remains a candidate, a development approved in 2024 continues to be screened against the 2024 rules no matter how many later vintages exist.

### 3.2. Screening a Development, Electing a Path, Recording the Agreement

A `Developer` registers a development by supplying a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `zoning_district` of `R-2`, `R-4` or `C-2`, a `total_units` count of at least 1, and an `approval_date` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date. The system stamps the acting `X-User-ID` onto `developer_user_id`, places the project in `state` `DRAFT` with `applicability` `PENDING` and `compliance_posture` `PENDING`, and leaves `ordinance_id`, `compliance_path` and `screened_at` null, `screen_count` at 0, and all four obligation figures at 0.

**Screening** is performed by a `HousingComplianceOfficer` or a `ProgramAdministrator` and is permitted only while the project is `DRAFT`. It pins the governing vintage as described in §3.1, then sizes the obligation. Two thresholds interact and both boundaries are inclusive. First, look up the project's `zoning_district` in the vintage's `set_aside_bps_by_district`. If the district is absent from that map, **or** the project's `total_units` is strictly below the vintage's `min_project_units_threshold`, the verdict is exemption: `applicability` becomes `EXEMPT`, `required_affordable_units`, `fractional_obligation_bps` and `total_obligation_bps` are all set to 0, `compliance_posture` becomes `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and the project lands in `SCREENED_EXEMPT`. Otherwise the verdict is `SUBJECT`, and the project lands in `SCREENED_SUBJECT`.

On the subject branch the raw obligation, in basis points of one dwelling, is the project's `total_units` multiplied by the district's set-aside rate in basis points. Divide that raw figure by 10000 discarding any remainder to obtain a whole-unit count, and take the remainder — the raw figure minus ten thousand times that whole count — as the residual fraction in basis points. If the residual reaches 5000 basis points, that is, half a dwelling or more, the residual rounds up into a whole unit: `required_affordable_units` becomes the whole count plus one and `fractional_obligation_bps` becomes 0. If the residual is strictly below 5000 basis points it survives as a priceable fraction: `required_affordable_units` becomes the whole count and `fractional_obligation_bps` becomes the residual. Either way `total_obligation_bps` is `required_affordable_units` multiplied by 10000 plus `fractional_obligation_bps`. Screening also sets `screened_at` to the current logical instant, increases `screen_count` by one, recomputes both equivalent aggregates and then `compliance_posture`, and increments the project's `version` exactly once. A project fresh from a subject screening has no units and no assessments, so its posture is `DEFICIENT`.

Both branches of screening share the same trigger and the same actor but write **disjoint** result sets, and the exempt branch is not a degenerate case of the subject branch: it explicitly zeroes all four obligation figures and explicitly sets the posture to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, which no other transition in the system produces.

Entitlements change, and the system models that as a **revision loop**. The owning `Developer` may amend `name`, `zoning_district`, `total_units` or `approval_date` — any subset, all optional — while the project is in `DRAFT`, `SCREENED_SUBJECT`, `SCREENED_EXEMPT`, `PATH_ELECTED` or `AGREEMENT_RECORDED`. The amendment always returns the project to `DRAFT`: from `SCREENED_SUBJECT` to `DRAFT`, from `SCREENED_EXEMPT` to `DRAFT`, from `PATH_ELECTED` to `DRAFT`, from `AGREEMENT_RECORDED` to `DRAFT`, and from `DRAFT` to `DRAFT` as a legal self-transition. In every case the screening outputs are cleared: `applicability` returns to `PENDING`, `required_affordable_units`, `fractional_obligation_bps` and `total_obligation_bps` return to 0, `compliance_path` and `ordinance_id` are cleared to null, `compliance_posture` returns to `PENDING`, and the project's `version` increments — including on the self-transition, where the clears are no-ops on a project that was never screened but the version bump still happens, which is exactly what a subsequent re-screen depends on. Note what amendment does **not** do. It does not zero the two equivalent aggregates: `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` and `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` are recomputed from the units and assessments that still exist, so a project carrying inventory keeps its onsite term straight across the amendment. It does not reset `screened_at` or `screen_count` either, both of which survive as the record of the previous pass. And it does not supersede existing in-lieu assessments. Re-pricing after an amendment is an explicit, separate assessment carrying a freshly-read `expected_project_version`.

Because screening runs only from `DRAFT`, a second screening always requires an amendment first, and `screen_count` is the visible record of how many times the development has been through the mill.

**Electing a path** is the owning `Developer`'s decision and is permitted only from `SCREENED_SUBJECT`, moving the project `SCREENED_SUBJECT → PATH_ELECTED`. The submitted `compliance_path` must be `ONSITE`, or must appear in the governing vintage's `alternative_paths_allowed`; anything else — `OFFSITE`, `IN_LIEU_FEE` or `LAND_DEDICATION` under a vintage that does not permit it — is refused with `ERR_PATH_NOT_PERMITTED`. On success `compliance_path` is stored, the project's equivalent aggregates and `compliance_posture` are recomputed, and its `version` increments once.

A **regulatory agreement** is the instrument that carries the long-term restrictions. A `HousingComplianceOfficer` or `ProgramAdministrator` drafts one against a `project_id`, supplying a `term_years` of at least 30 and at most 99, a `resale_formula` of either `AMI_INDEXED` or `SHARED_APPRECIATION`, an `owner_appreciation_share_bps` between 0 and 10000 giving the seller's share of appreciation with the city recapturing the remainder, and a `max_refinance_ltv_bps` between 0 and 10000 giving the maximum loan-to-restricted-value ratio permitted on a refinance. A fresh agreement sits in `state` `DRAFT` with `doc_number` and `recorded_at` unset. Drafting is gated on nothing but the `project_id` resolving: the project may be in any `state`, and a project may hold more than one agreement.

Recording the agreement with the county recorder is exclusive to a `HousingComplianceOfficer`, is permitted only from `DRAFT`, and requires the project to be in `PATH_ELECTED`. Three further conditions apply. The project's `compliance_path` must be `ONSITE` or `OFFSITE`, because a development discharging its obligation entirely through fees or a land dedication records no agreement at all; an attempt on an `IN_LIEU_FEE` or `LAND_DEDICATION` project is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_REQUIRED`. The supplied `doc_number`, a string of between 1 and 120 characters, must be unique across every agreement ever recorded — compared against every other agreement that carries a non-null `doc_number`, whatever its own state — else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. And the supplied `recorded_at`, accepted either as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date or as a full ISO 8601 instant and stored verbatim as it was supplied, must fall on or after the project's `approval_date` when the two are compared as calendar dates, else `ERR_RECORDING_PREDATES_APPROVAL`. On success the agreement moves `DRAFT → RECORDED` with `doc_number` and `recorded_at` stored and its `version` incremented, and the recording cascades into the project: the project moves `PATH_ELECTED → AGREEMENT_RECORDED` and its `version` increments too.

The project's own lifecycle therefore runs `DRAFT → SCREENED_SUBJECT` (or `DRAFT → SCREENED_EXEMPT`) `→ PATH_ELECTED → AGREEMENT_RECORDED → MONITORING → CLOSED_OUT`, with the amendment loop able to pull the project back to `DRAFT` from any of the first five states. The step into `MONITORING` is not a request in its own right: the first income certification recorded against any unit of a project sitting in `AGREEMENT_RECORDED` cascades the project `AGREEMENT_RECORDED → MONITORING` and increments its `version`. `CLOSED_OUT` is terminal and is described in §3.9.

### 3.3. Building and Adjusting the Restricted Inventory

The owning `Developer` builds the restricted inventory once the project has reached `PATH_ELECTED`, and may keep adding to it while the project is in `AGREEMENT_RECORDED` or `MONITORING`. Each restricted unit is created against a `project_id` and an `agreement_id`; the named agreement must belong to the same project as the unit, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_UNIT_PROJECT_MISMATCH`. The agreement named here is the one whose resale formula and refinance ceiling will govern this unit for the whole affordability term.

A unit's descriptive attributes are supplied at creation. `unit_number` is the designation of between 1 and 16 characters, unique within its project, and it is also the deterministic pairing key at lottery draw time. `bedrooms` is an integer from 0 through 3, where 0 denotes a studio. `sqft` is net livable square feet, at least 200. `tenure` is either `RENTAL` — rent-and-income monitored annually — or `OWNERSHIP` — deed-restricted and resale-controlled. `affordability_level_bps` is the assigned affordability level and must be exactly one of 5000, 8000 or 12000 basis points of area median income. `market_comparable_sqft`, also at least 200, is the floor area of the market-rate comparable unit against which the **comparability standard** is measured.

That comparability standard is a per-unit gate enforced at creation. Take the unit's `market_comparable_sqft`, multiply by 8000 basis points — four fifths of the comparable — and round half up to a whole square foot; call that the minimum. If the unit's own `sqft` is strictly below that minimum, creation is refused with `ERR_COMPARABILITY_STANDARD_FAILED`. A unit exactly at the minimum passes.

A new unit begins with three independent state dimensions, all set at once: `occupancy_status` `PLANNED`, `compliance_status` `COMPLIANT`, and `restriction_state` `NOT_RECORDED`. Every money-valued attribute of that fresh unit — `current_rent_cents`, `max_allowed_rent_cents`, `original_purchase_price_cents`, `ami_income_at_purchase_cents`, `last_refinance_balance_cents` and `approved_refinance_cap_cents` — begins at integer 0 rather than null, and each stays 0 until some later operation writes it; an operation refused before it reaches that write leaves the field, and the unit's `version`, exactly where they were. The unit's three nullable attributes — `occupant_household_id`, `restriction_recorded_at` and `withdrawn_reason` — begin null rather than 0. These three dimensions live on the same row and **never gate one another**. A unit can be `OCCUPIED` and simultaneously `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`; it can be `VACANT` and `COMPLIANT`; occupancy transitions never consult the compliance dimension and compliance transitions never consult the occupancy dimension. Only ownership units are ever `RECORDED` on the restriction dimension.

Creating a unit also touches the parent. The project's `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` is recomputed as 10000 basis points multiplied by the number of that project's units whose `occupancy_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`; its `compliance_posture` is recomputed; and its `version` increments. Separately, the project's `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` is always the sum of `unit_equivalent_bps` over that project's in-lieu assessments in state `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `DELINQUENT` or `PAID` — superseded assessments contribute nothing. Neither aggregate involves any rounding: every term is a whole multiple of one basis point.

**Compliance posture** is the derived gate that governs several downstream operations, and its clauses are evaluated strictly top to bottom with the first match winning:

1. If the project's `state` is `DRAFT`, the posture is `PENDING`.
2. Otherwise, if `applicability` is `EXEMPT`, the posture is `NOT_APPLICABLE`.
3. Otherwise, if any non-withdrawn unit of the project has `compliance_status` `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`, the posture is `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT`.
4. Otherwise, if `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` plus `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` is strictly less than `total_obligation_bps`, the posture is `DEFICIENT`.
5. Otherwise, if `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` is greater than 0, the posture is `COMPLIANT_BY_FEE`.
6. Otherwise the posture is `COMPLIANT_ONSITE`.

Clause order is normative, and clause 3 is the one that surprises people: enforcement outranks deficiency, so a project that is both short of its obligation *and* holding an out-of-compliance unit reports `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT`, not `DEFICIENT`. Because every input to this evaluation is a stored aggregate rather than the posture itself, one pass always reaches a fixed point.

**Withdrawing a unit** is the owning `Developer`'s escape hatch, permitted only while the unit's `occupancy_status` is `PLANNED` or `MARKETING`, and only while the parent project is not yet `CLOSED_OUT` — every other project state, `DRAFT` and `MONITORING` included, permits a withdrawal. The request carries a required `reason` of between 1 and 500 characters. The transition is `PLANNED → WITHDRAWN` or `MARKETING → WITHDRAWN`; both branches behave identically. What follows is the longest cascade in the system, and each link is caused by the one before it:

- The unit's `occupancy_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, its `withdrawn_reason` stores the supplied `reason`, and its `version` increments.
- Because the unit no longer counts as delivered on site, the project's `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` is recomputed and drops by exactly 10000 basis points, its `compliance_posture` is recomputed, and its `version` increments. The posture becomes `DEFICIENT` only when the recomputed onsite and fee terms no longer reach `total_obligation_bps`; a project that had delivered more than it owed stays compliant, and one holding an out-of-compliance unit reports `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` under clause 3 instead. The remaining two links fire only on the `DEFICIENT` outcome, so on any other outcome the withdrawal stops here with a single project mutation.
- Because the posture has become `DEFICIENT` while the project's `compliance_path` is `ONSITE`, the system immediately assesses a substitution in-lieu fee on the project's behalf: a fresh assessment with `basis` `SUBSTITUTION`, `unit_equivalent_bps` of exactly 10000, `ordinance_id` copied from the project's pinned vintage, a `due_date` of the current logical date plus the vintage's `fee_due_days`, and `state` `ASSESSED`. Its money figures are computed by the same pricing pipeline described in §3.4, but this cascaded assessment is booked *without* superseding anything: the supersession of the project's other open assessments described in §3.4 belongs to the explicit assessment endpoint alone, and no `expected_project_version` is read or required here. If the project holds no pinned vintage at this point the withdrawal is refused with `ERR_NO_GOVERNING_ORDINANCE`.
- Because that assessment now exists, the project's `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` is recomputed and rises by exactly 10000 basis points, its `compliance_posture` is recomputed once more and settles at `COMPLIANT_BY_FEE` — or stays `DEFICIENT` if the project was short by more than the single substituted dwelling, since exactly one 10000-basis-point assessment is booked no matter how large the gap — and its `version` increments again.

The net effect is that a withdrawal converts a dwelling into money without ever letting the conserved sum of §2.11 drift: 10000 basis points leave the onsite term and 10000 basis points enter the fee term.

**Reinstating a unit** is the compensating action, again the owning `Developer`'s, and permitted only from `WITHDRAWN`, moving the unit `WITHDRAWN → PLANNED`. It carries no payload. Before anything is written the system selects the substitution assessment to unwind: among that project's assessments whose `basis` is `SUBSTITUTION` and whose `state` is `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `DELINQUENT`, order by `created_at` ascending and take the oldest. Oldest-first is the sole selector; an assessment's own `id` is never consulted. If there is no such assessment the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTION_FEE`. If the selected assessment's `paid_amount_cents` is greater than 0 — any payment at all has landed on it — the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_FEE_PAID` rather than silently stranding the money, which is exactly what keeps the unit-equivalents quantity conserved.

On success: the unit's `occupancy_status` returns to `PLANNED`, its `withdrawn_reason` is cleared to null, and its `version` increments. The selected assessment transitions to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_fee_id` left null — there is no replacement assessment, this is an unwind — and its `version` increments. The project's `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` and `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` are both recomputed, its `compliance_posture` is recomputed, and its `version` increments. Ten thousand basis points return to the onsite term and ten thousand leave the fee term, restoring the sum exactly.

### 3.4. Pricing the Alternative: In-Lieu Assessment and Payment

An in-lieu assessment converts an unbuilt obligation into a payable amount. Assessment is available to a `ProgramAdministrator` or a `HousingComplianceOfficer` and takes three inputs: the `project_id` of the development, the `basis` on which the assessment is drawn, and the `expected_project_version` optimistic-concurrency token described in §2.8. The assessment copies the project's pinned vintage onto its own `ordinance_id` at that instant, and that copy is permanent: later vintages never re-price an assessment already booked. The vintage is taken from the project's stored `ordinance_id` and is never re-selected from the `approval_date`, so a `SUPERSEDED` vintage prices the assessment whenever that is the one screening pinned; if the project carries no pinned vintage at all — it has never been screened, or an amendment cleared it per §3.2 — the assessment is refused with `ERR_NO_GOVERNING_ORDINANCE`, and that refusal is decided ahead of the remaining-obligation test below. The project's own `state` is otherwise never consulted: an assessment may be booked against a project in any state, `MONITORING` and even `CLOSED_OUT` included, so long as the requested `basis` still prices something.

The `basis` decides **what quantity** is priced, and the three branches are genuinely different rules rather than variations on one:

- `FULL` prices everything still outstanding: the project's `total_obligation_bps` minus its `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` minus its `fee_covered_equivalents_bps`.
- `FRACTIONAL` prices only the residual fraction left over from screening: the project's `fractional_obligation_bps`, and nothing else.
- `SUBSTITUTION` prices exactly one withdrawn dwelling: a flat 10000 basis points. This is the basis the withdrawal cascade of §3.3 uses.

Whichever branch runs, the resulting figure is stored as the assessment's `unit_equivalent_bps`. If that figure is zero or negative — the project owes nothing the requested basis can price — the assessment is refused with `ERR_NO_REMAINING_OBLIGATION`.

The money is then computed in four ordered steps, each rounding feeding the next.

**Step one, the base.** Look up the gap rate: take the vintage's `fee_gap_level_bps`, use its string form as the key into the vintage's `gap_basis_cents_by_level`, and read the affordability gap per unit in cents. Multiply that gap by the assessment's `unit_equivalent_bps`, divide by 10000, and round half up to the whole cent. The result is `base_amount_cents`. A full-unit assessment therefore costs exactly the published gap; a half-unit assessment costs half of it.

**Step two, the surcharge, clamped.** Multiply `base_amount_cents` by the vintage's `surcharge_bps`, divide by 10000, and round half up to the whole cent. Then take the smaller of that figure and the vintage's `surcharge_cap_cents`. The result is `surcharge_cents`. Adding it to `base_amount_cents` gives `pre_discount_total_cents`.

**Step three, tier selection.** The scale discount is selected on `pre_discount_total_cents` — the total **after** the surcharge — and not on `base_amount_cents`. Walk the vintage's `discount_tiers` in ascending order of `min_pre_discount_cents`, and for every tier whose `min_pre_discount_cents` is at most `pre_discount_total_cents`, adopt that tier's `discount_bps`; the last tier that qualifies wins. Because the schedule always begins at a threshold of 0, some tier always qualifies. The adopted rate is stored as `discount_bps_applied`.

**Step four, the discount and the total.** Multiply `pre_discount_total_cents` by `discount_bps_applied`, divide by 10000, and round half up to the whole cent, giving `discount_cents`. Subtract that from `pre_discount_total_cents` to obtain `assessed_amount_cents`, the amount actually owed.

These three inputs interact and none of them may be dropped or reordered. In particular, the surcharge cap can pull the pre-discount total back below a tier boundary, so a capped surcharge and an uncapped one can select **different** discount tiers from the same base amount — which is precisely why the cap must be applied before the tier scan rather than after it.

The assessment's `due_date` is the calendar date of the current logical instant plus the vintage's `fee_due_days` days. `paid_amount_cents` starts at 0, `last_payment_at` starts null, and `state` starts at `ASSESSED`. Booking a new assessment **through this endpoint** also supersedes whatever the project had open — the cascaded substitution assessment of §3.3 supersedes nothing, so this sentence is not a property of every assessment that comes into existence: any other assessment of the same project in `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `DELINQUENT` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` — `ASSESSED → SUPERSEDED` and `DELINQUENT → SUPERSEDED` alike — with its `superseded_by_fee_id` pointing at the new assessment and its own `version` incremented, and the project's `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed. The project's `version` increments as well.

**Payments** are recorded by the owning `Developer` against an open assessment, one `amount_cents` at a time, and the balance is `assessed_amount_cents` minus `paid_amount_cents`. If the payment is strictly less than that balance, the assessment moves `ASSESSED → PARTIALLY_PAID` on the first such payment and `PARTIALLY_PAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` on subsequent ones; either way `paid_amount_cents` grows by the payment, `last_payment_at` records the current logical instant, and the assessment's `version` increments. If the payment exactly equals the balance the assessment is settled: `paid_amount_cents` becomes `assessed_amount_cents`, `state` becomes `PAID` — reachable as `ASSESSED → PAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` or `DELINQUENT → PAID` — `last_payment_at` is stamped, `version` increments, and the trust fund's paid-fee inflow rises, which in turn raises both the fund's unencumbered balance and its administrative cap. If the payment exceeds the balance it is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Payment against an assessment that is already `PAID` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_FEE_NOT_PAYABLE`, and per §2.9 that state refusal outranks the balance refusal when a request breaks both.

Lateness is detected by the nightly sweep rather than by any request: an assessment in `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` whose `due_date` has passed transitions `ASSESSED → DELINQUENT` or `PARTIALLY_PAID → DELINQUENT` and increments its `version`. A delinquent assessment is still payable, and paying it in full still settles it. `PAID` and `SUPERSEDED` are the two terminal states.

### 3.5. Marketing, Outreach and Lottery Administration

Before any household can be placed, the owning `Developer` submits a marketing and outreach plan against a `project_id`. The plan declares its application window as `lottery_open_at`, the inclusive start, and `lottery_close_at`, the exclusive end, both ISO 8601 UTC instants; each is parsed on arrival and **stored canonicalised** to three fractional digits with a `Z` suffix rather than verbatim as supplied, so `2026-09-01T00:00:00+00:00` comes back as `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z`. `lottery_open_at` must be strictly earlier than `lottery_close_at`, else the submission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MARKETING_WINDOW`. The plan may also declare `preference_categories`, an ordered list drawn without duplicates from `LIVE_WORK`, `DISPLACED` and `PUBLIC_EMPLOYEE` and stored in the order supplied; the field is optional and may be empty, and when it is omitted or supplied as `null` it is stored as the empty list, in which case the plan awards no preferences at all. A fresh plan sits in `state` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at 0 and `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` unset. Submission is gated on nothing beyond the `project_id` resolving and the acting `Developer` owning that project: the project's own `state` and `compliance_posture` are not consulted at submission time — those two are read only when the plan is approved — and a project may hold any number of plans so long as their windows do not collide.

Windows are half-open, per §2.10, and the system refuses to let two live windows of the same project overlap. Scan every other plan of that project whose `state` is `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` — a `CLOSED` plan releases its window — and reject with `ERR_MARKETING_WINDOW_OVERLAP` if the proposed `lottery_open_at` is strictly earlier than that plan's `lottery_close_at` *and* that plan's `lottery_open_at` is strictly earlier than the proposed `lottery_close_at`. Exact adjacency, where the proposed window opens at the very instant an existing window closes, satisfies neither half of that test and is therefore accepted.

A `HousingComplianceOfficer` reviews a `SUBMITTED` plan with a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, optionally attaching a `review_note` of at most 1000 characters, the empty string included. Both branches store the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`, store the note as `review_note` — a note omitted or supplied as `null` is stored as `null`, overwriting whatever an earlier review round had left there — and increment the plan's `version` — and there the resemblance ends, because the branches carry disjoint cascades.

On `APPROVE` the plan moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`. Approval is gated on the project: the project must be in `state` `AGREEMENT_RECORDED`, and its `compliance_posture` must be anything other than `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT`; either failure is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_READY_FOR_MARKETING`. Approval then cascades into the inventory: every restricted unit of that project whose `occupancy_status` is `PLANNED` transitions `PLANNED → MARKETING`, and each such unit independently increments its own `version`.

On `REQUEST_REVISION` the plan moves `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and **no unit changes occupancy at all**; the revision branch consults neither the project's `state` nor its `compliance_posture`, so a plan can always be sent back for revision even for a project that is nowhere near ready to market. The plan then re-enters the pipeline through a resubmission by the owning `Developer`, permitted only from `REVISION_REQUESTED` and moving the plan `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. A resubmission supplies a fresh `lottery_open_at`, a fresh `lottery_close_at` and an optional fresh `preference_categories`, all three replacing what the plan previously held — omitting `preference_categories` replaces the stored list with the empty list rather than preserving the previous one; both window rules — strict ordering and non-overlap against the project's other live plans, the plan being resubmitted always excluded from its own overlap scan — are re-enforced. `revision_round` increases by one on every resubmission and is the loop counter for the review cycle. The `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` written by the review that sent the plan back survive the resubmission untouched, so a `SUBMITTED` plan carrying a non-null `reviewed_by_user_id` is one that has been round-tripped at least once. `CLOSED` is the plan's terminal state and is reached only by the draw cascade below.

**Households** are registered by a `ProgramAdministrator` or a `HousingComplianceOfficer` with an `applicant_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `household_size` from 1 through 5 which drives every income-limit lookup, and a self-reported `gross_annual_income_cents` of at least 0. A registered household starts in `state` `REGISTERED` with `preference_score` 0, `rank_position` null, `lottery_event_id` null and `preference_claims` an empty list. The `id` minted for the household is what that applicant will present as `X-User-ID` when acting as a `Resident`. Reading a household is not scoped to the reader: a `Resident` may read any household, and may page the whole household collection, because the self-ownership rule of rung 7 binds only the application endpoint.

A **lottery round** is opened by a `ProgramAdministrator` against a `marketing_plan_id` whose plan is `APPROVED`, declaring `units_offered` — the number of units this round places, at least 1 and carrying no upper bound, and never reconciled against how many units the project actually holds in `MARKETING` — and `eligibility_level_bps`, the affordability level whose income limit defines the eligible pool, again exactly one of 5000, 8000 or 12000 and not required to match the `affordability_level_bps` of any particular unit. A plan may carry more than one round, and the round records no reference to the project except through its plan. A new round sits in `state` `PENDING` with `round_seq` at 1, empty `selected_household_ids` and `waitlisted_household_ids`, and null `drawn_at`, `drawn_by_user_id` and `redraw_reason`.

A `Resident` applies to a round while it is `PENDING`, moving that household `REGISTERED → APPLIED`. The application names a `lottery_event_id` and may carry `preference_claims`, a duplicate-free subset of `LIVE_WORK`, `DISPLACED` and `PUBLIC_EMPLOYEE`, which is stored as the empty list when omitted or supplied as `null`. The plan behind the round must be `APPROVED`, and the current logical instant must sit at or after the plan's `lottery_open_at` and strictly before its `lottery_close_at`; an application outside that half-open window is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. On acceptance the household stores the `lottery_event_id` and the `preference_claims`, and its `preference_score` is computed as 100 points for each claimed category that also appears in the plan's `preference_categories` — a claim the plan does not list scores nothing. The household's `version` increments.

**Income limits** are the eligibility yardstick and are computed the same way everywhere they appear. Take the active AMI schedule, read `median_income_cents_by_size` at the string form of the relevant household size, multiply that median by the relevant affordability level in basis points, and divide by 10000. Then publish the figure to the whole dollar: divide by 100, round half up, and multiply by 100 again, so the stored limit is always a whole multiple of 100 cents. Eligibility is inclusive — an income exactly equal to the limit qualifies, and the limit plus one cent does not. If the active schedule's `median_income_cents_by_size` carries no entry for the household size the computation needs — or, for the rent ceiling of §3.6, no `utility_allowance_cents_by_bedrooms` entry for the unit's `bedrooms` — the operation is refused with `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE`, exactly as though no schedule had been published at all; a household is never silently dropped from a pool because its size is missing from the table. An `OWNERSHIP` certification is the one case that never runs the rent-ceiling pipeline, so it never needs the utility-allowance entry.

**Running the draw** is a `ProgramAdministrator` action permitted only from `PENDING`, and it requires the round's plan to be `APPROVED` and the application window to be over: if the current logical instant is strictly earlier than the plan's `lottery_close_at` the draw is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_WINDOW_OPEN`. The ranking is total and deterministic. Assemble the pool from every household attached to this round whose `state` is `APPLIED`, `SELECTED` or `WAITLISTED` and whose `gross_annual_income_cents` is at most the income limit for that household's `household_size` at the round's `eligibility_level_bps`. If the pool is empty the draw is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_ELIGIBLE_POOL`. These refusals fire in the ladder order of §2.9 rather than the order they are narrated here: the AMI-schedule guard (rung 27) and the empty-pool guard (rung 30) both outrank the still-open-window guard (rung 55), so a draw attempted before the window closes reports `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE` when no schedule exists and `ERR_EMPTY_ELIGIBLE_POOL` when the pool is empty, and only reaches `ERR_APPLICATION_WINDOW_OPEN` once a schedule exists and the pool is non-empty. Recompute each pooled household's `preference_score` against the plan's `preference_categories`, then order the pool by `preference_score` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending — a household's own `id` never participates in the ordering — and assign each household its 1-based `rank_position` in that order. The first `units_offered` households become `selected_household_ids` in rank order; everyone ranked beyond that becomes `waitlisted_household_ids`, also in rank order.

The draw's cascade reaches four kinds of row. The round itself moves `PENDING → DRAWN`, records the two id lists, stamps `drawn_at` with the current logical instant and `drawn_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, leaves `round_seq` unchanged, and increments its `version`. Each selected household moves `APPLIED → SELECTED` and each waitlisted household moves `APPLIED → WAITLISTED`, both with `rank_position` set, each bumping its own `version`. The project's units whose `occupancy_status` is `MARKETING`, taken in ascending `unit_number` order — `unit_number` is a string and the comparison is lexicographic on that string, so unit `10` sorts ahead of unit `9` — are paired rank-for-rank with the selected households: each such unit moves `MARKETING → RESERVED` with `occupant_household_id` set to the household at the matching rank, incrementing its own `version`. Pairing simply stops at the shorter of the two lists and neither surplus is an error: selected households beyond the supply of `MARKETING` units stay `SELECTED` with no unit reserved for them, and `MARKETING` units beyond the number of selected households stay `MARKETING` with `occupant_household_id` still null and their `version` untouched. And the marketing plan behind the round moves `APPROVED → CLOSED`, incrementing its `version`.

**Redrawing** exists because a draw sometimes has to be voided, and it is a genuine loop rather than a rollback to nothing. A `ProgramAdministrator` redraws only from `DRAWN`, supplying a `reason` of between 1 and 500 characters, and only while no household in `selected_household_ids` has yet reached `CERTIFIED`; a redraw attempted after any selected household has been certified is refused with `ERR_REDRAW_AFTER_CERTIFICATION`. A redraw re-checks the eligible pool by the same rule the draw uses and therefore needs an AMI schedule of its own: with none published it is refused with `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE`, and with a schedule but an empty pool with `ERR_EMPTY_ELIGIBLE_POOL` — both of which outrank the certification guard, so a round whose pool has emptied reports the empty pool rather than `ERR_REDRAW_AFTER_CERTIFICATION`. On success the round moves `DRAWN → PENDING`, `round_seq` increases by one, both `selected_household_ids` and `waitlisted_household_ids` are emptied, `redraw_reason` stores the supplied reason, and the round's `version` increments; `drawn_at` and `drawn_by_user_id` keep the stamps of the voided draw rather than returning to null. Every household attached to this round whose `state` is `SELECTED` or `WAITLISTED` returns to `APPLIED` — `SELECTED → APPLIED` and `WAITLISTED → APPLIED` — with `rank_position` cleared to null and its own `version` bumped, while `preference_score` is left at whatever the voided draw computed. Every unit whose `occupancy_status` is `RESERVED` and whose `occupant_household_id` was in the cleared selection returns `RESERVED → MARKETING` with `occupant_household_id` cleared to null. What a redraw does **not** do is reopen the marketing plan: the plan stays `CLOSED`, because `CLOSED` is terminal and nothing ever moves a plan out of it. A round returned to `PENDING` therefore cannot in fact be drawn a second time and cannot receive a further application — both operations fail the plan-state rung and are refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` — so the redraw's own pool guard is evaluated against the pool exactly as it stands at the redraw instant, and nothing can be added to that pool afterwards.

### 3.6. Certifying a Household and Freezing the Rent Ceiling

Certification is where a household, a unit and a vintage of the AMI table meet, and it is the single act that turns a paper obligation into a monitored placement. A `HousingComplianceOfficer` records a certification naming a `household_id`, a `unit_id`, the officer-verified `certified_income_cents` of at least 0, the `contract_rent_cents` of at least 0 that will actually be charged, and an `effective_date` given as an ISO 8601 calendar date `YYYY-MM-DD` — the first day of the certification period. For a unit whose `tenure` is `OWNERSHIP` the `contract_rent_cents` must be exactly 0; a non-zero figure is a payload-shape failure and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That rung can only fire once the tenure is knowable, so a request naming a `unit_id` that resolves to nothing is answered with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` instead, whatever `contract_rent_cents` it carried. Of the two body foreign keys, `household_id` is resolved before `unit_id`: a request whose household and unit are both unknown reports `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`. At least one AMI schedule must exist, else `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE`. The household must be in `state` `REGISTERED` or `SELECTED`; a household already `CERTIFIED` holds a live placement and is not certified again, which is refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_STATE_INVALID`.

Recording the certification pins five computed figures onto the row, and once pinned they are never rewritten by a later schedule.

- `ami_schedule_year` takes the `effective_year` of the schedule chosen by the active-schedule rule of §2.7.
- `income_limit_cents` is the income limit for the household's `household_size` at the unit's `affordability_level_bps`, computed exactly as in §3.5: median times level in basis points divided by 10000, then divided by 100, rounded half up and multiplied by 100 again.
- `max_rent_cents` is the monthly rent ceiling, computed by the pinned four-step pipeline described below. For an `OWNERSHIP` unit it is 0.
- `ami_pct_bps` records where the household actually landed on the AMI curve: multiply `certified_income_cents` by 10000, divide by the median income at that `household_size` from the same schedule, and round half up.
- `expires_at` is the last day of the certification period: for a `RENTAL` unit it is the `effective_date` plus 365 days; for an `OWNERSHIP` unit it is null, because ownership qualification is a point-in-time event with no term and no annual renewal.

Eligibility is then tested against the pinned limit: the `certified_income_cents` must be at most `income_limit_cents`, and if the unit's `occupancy_status` is `RESERVED` the household being certified must be the very household recorded as that unit's `occupant_household_id` — certifying somebody else into a unit the lottery reserved is refused. Both failures surface as `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

**The rent ceiling is a four-step pipeline and its step order is pinned.** Step one: take the household size implied by the unit itself — the unit's `bedrooms` plus one — and compute the annual income limit for that size at the unit's `affordability_level_bps`, which includes the whole-dollar half-up rounding. Step two: divide that annual figure by 12 and truncate toward zero to the cent, discarding any fraction. Step three: multiply the truncated monthly figure by 3000 basis points, divide by 10000 and round half up to the cent; 3000 basis points is the assumed share of monthly income a household should spend on housing. Step four: subtract the `utility_allowance_cents_by_bedrooms` entry for the unit's `bedrooms` from the same active schedule, and if that subtraction would go below zero publish 0 instead. The result is `max_rent_cents`. Each step's rounding is an input to the next, so the pipeline may not be algebraically collapsed into a single expression — a collapsed formula returns a figure one cent away from the correct answer, and that cent is observable in the stored value and in every downstream rent comparison.

**Certification periods are closed intervals** for `RENTAL` units, per §2.10. Before accepting a certification, derive the proposed period as `effective_date` through `effective_date` plus 365 days, then scan every existing certification of the same `unit_id` whose `state` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `expires_at` is non-null. The proposed period collides if its start is on or before the existing period's `expires_at` *and* the existing period's `effective_date` is on or before the proposed end; a collision is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because both endpoints are inclusive, a new period beginning exactly on an existing period's `expires_at` **is** a collision. `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` certifications are invisible to the scan, and so is every `OWNERSHIP` certification: an `OWNERSHIP` unit skips the overlap scan entirely, which is precisely what allows the incoming buyer of a pending resale to be certified while the departing seller's certification is still `CERTIFIED`.

A successful certification then cascades outward, each affected row incrementing its own `version` independently.

1. **The unit becomes occupied.** From `RESERVED` — permitted only when the certification's `unit_id` and `household_id` match the unit and its `occupant_household_id` — the unit moves `RESERVED → OCCUPIED`, setting `current_rent_cents` to the certification's `contract_rent_cents` and `max_allowed_rent_cents` to its `max_rent_cents`. From `PLANNED` the unit moves `PLANNED → OCCUPIED` — direct placement without a lottery, which is the normal path for `OWNERSHIP` units — additionally setting `occupant_household_id` from the certification. From `VACANT` the unit moves `VACANT → OCCUPIED` with the same side effects as the `PLANNED` branch. A unit already `OCCUPIED` has no occupancy transition available and its `occupancy_status` simply stays `OCCUPIED`, though its rent fields are refreshed. A unit sitting in `MARKETING` or in `WITHDRAWN` behaves the same way — no occupancy transition and no `occupant_household_id` written — because certification never guards on the occupancy dimension beyond the `RESERVED` eligibility rule above; on every branch alike the two rent fields are written and the unit's `version` increments exactly once.
2. **The household becomes certified.** `SELECTED → CERTIFIED` for a lottery placement, or `REGISTERED → CERTIFIED` for a direct placement or a resale buyer. `CERTIFIED` is the household's terminal state.
3. **A fully placed lottery round completes.** If the round is `DRAWN` and every id in its `selected_household_ids` now belongs to a household in `state` `CERTIFIED`, the round moves `DRAWN → COMPLETED`.
4. **A project enters monitoring.** The first certification recorded against any unit of a project sitting in `AGREEMENT_RECORDED` moves that project `AGREEMENT_RECORDED → MONITORING`, and that same transition recomputes both of the project's equivalent aggregates and then its `compliance_posture`, the project's `version` incrementing exactly once for the whole step. A project in any other `state` is not touched at all — `MONITORING` included, which is where every certification after the first one finds it — so later certifications leave the project's `version`, aggregates and posture entirely alone.

**Recertification** renews a rental term without ever letting two live periods touch. A `HousingComplianceOfficer` recertifies an existing certification, supplying only a fresh `certified_income_cents` and `contract_rent_cents`. The predecessor must be in `state` `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. It must have a non-null `expires_at`: an `OWNERSHIP` certification carries no term and never recertifies, which is refused with `ERR_RECERT_NOT_APPLICABLE`. And the renewal window must be open — the current logical instant must be at or after the predecessor's `expires_at` minus 30 days. Because `expires_at` is a calendar date it is read as midnight UTC of that day, so the window opens at exactly `00:00:00.000Z` on the day 30 days earlier and an instant sitting precisely on that boundary is already inside it; anything earlier is refused with `ERR_RECERT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. That window rule is scoped to predecessors with a term, so an `OWNERSHIP` predecessor is skipped by it and is caught by the not-applicable rule instead. Being rung 57, the not-applicable rule is the lowest of the four guards this endpoint runs, so an `OWNERSHIP` predecessor still reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when the submitted `contract_rent_cents` is non-zero (rung 4), `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE` when it is not `CERTIFIED` (rung 16), and `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE` when no schedule has been published (rung 27), reaching `ERR_RECERT_NOT_APPLICABLE` only once all three are satisfied.

On success the predecessor moves `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED` and a successor certification is created with `is_recertification` set to true, carrying the same `household_id` and `unit_id`, and with `effective_date` set to the predecessor's `expires_at` plus one day — the successor abuts the predecessor without touching it, which is exactly what the closed-interval overlap scan demands. All five computed figures are recomputed for the successor from whichever schedule is active at the recertification instant, so a schedule published in the meantime raises the successor's limits while leaving the predecessor's pinned figures untouched. The unit's `current_rent_cents` and `max_allowed_rent_cents` are refreshed from the successor and the unit's `version` increments by one. Nothing else moves: the household stays `CERTIFIED` untouched, the lottery round keeps whatever state it had reached, the unit's `occupancy_status` and `occupant_household_id` are left as they are, and the project's `state`, aggregates and `compliance_posture` are not recomputed. Recertification is a creation endpoint despite living under a nested path — it answers HTTP 201 carrying the newly minted successor certification in `data`, never the superseded predecessor.

Two forces retire a certification other than recertification. Sweep step 2 expires any `CERTIFIED` certification with a non-null `expires_at` strictly earlier than the new logical instant, moving it `CERTIFIED → EXPIRED` and cascading the unit `OCCUPIED → VACANT` with `current_rent_cents` reset to 0 and `occupant_household_id` cleared to null, after which the project's `compliance_posture` is recomputed. And closing a resale retires the seller's certification, moving the certification of the unit whose `household_id` equals the transaction's `seller_household_id` from `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED`. `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal.

### 3.7. Annual Monitoring, Cure and Enforcement

Every restricted unit is reviewed once a year. A `HousingComplianceOfficer` records a review against a `unit_id` for a `review_year` between 2000 and 2100, reporting the `reported_rent_cents` the owner says it is charging and the `reported_income_cents` the owner says the sitting household now earns, both integers of at least 0, and a required boolean `occupancy_verified` recording whether tenancy or owner-occupancy documentation was produced. Only one review may exist per `review_year` per `unit_id`; a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. A new review starts in `state` `RECORDED`, but that state is transient: findings are evaluated at creation and the review lands in one of two states before the response is returned. A new review's `penalty_final_cents` is null, and it stays null on either landing state until a cure submission computes it; nothing else — neither clearing a cure nor escalating one — ever writes that field. `cure_submitted_at`, `corrected_rent_cents` and `cleared_by_user_id` likewise start null on both landings, and on the compliant landing `finding_issued_at` and `issued_by_user_id` stay null too.

**Findings are emitted in a fixed order**, so `finding_types` is a deterministic array rather than a set.

1. `RENT_OVERCHARGE` — appended when the unit's `tenure` is `RENTAL` and `reported_rent_cents` is strictly greater than the unit's `max_allowed_rent_cents` as frozen by its last certification. An `OWNERSHIP` unit is never rent-monitored, so this finding is structurally impossible for one.
2. `INCOME_OVER_LIMIT` — appended when `reported_income_cents` is strictly greater than the recertification ceiling. That ceiling is the income limit for the occupant household's `household_size` at the unit's `affordability_level_bps`, multiplied by 14000 basis points, divided by 10000 and rounded half up: a sitting household is allowed to grow to 140 percent of its entry limit before the finding is issued. This test is evaluated only when the unit's `occupant_household_id` actually resolves to a household **and** at least one AMI schedule has been published. With either missing there is no ceiling to compare against, the finding is silently skipped, and the review is still recorded — recording a review is not one of the operations rung 27 guards, so it is never refused with `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE`. The occupant consulted is the unit's *current* `occupant_household_id`, not whichever household its certification names.
3. `OCCUPANCY_UNVERIFIED` — appended when `occupancy_verified` is false.

Both numeric comparisons are strict, so a reported figure exactly at the ceiling is compliant.

If `finding_types` comes out empty the review moves `RECORDED → COMPLIANT`, its `cure_deadline` stays null, its `penalty_at_base_cents` stays 0, and **the unit's `compliance_status` does not change at all**. `COMPLIANT` is a terminal review state.

If `finding_types` is non-empty the review moves `RECORDED → FINDING_ISSUED`, stamping `finding_issued_at` with the current logical instant and `issued_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and cascades the unit `COMPLIANT → FINDING_OPEN` with the unit's own `version` incremented. That unit cascade is conditional on the unit actually reading `COMPLIANT`: a unit already sitting in `FINDING_OPEN`, `CURE_PENDING` or `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` from an earlier year's review is left exactly where it is and its `version` does not move. Recording a review never touches the project on either landing — no `compliance_posture` is recomputed when a finding is issued, and the project's `version` does not advance. Issue also freezes the penalty and deadline figures, and the way they are frozen matters, because the penalty depends on how long the cure takes while the time the owner is *allowed* to take depends on the penalty. That circularity is broken by pinning the first evaluation at one month.

- `overcharge_monthly_cents` is the `reported_rent_cents` minus the unit's `max_allowed_rent_cents`, floored at 0 — a unit charging under its ceiling carries no overcharge even when other findings fire. That subtraction is unconditional: it is evaluated and stored for every review the moment the review is recorded, on the compliant landing as much as on the finding landing, and it never consults the unit's `tenure`. An `OWNERSHIP` unit's `max_allowed_rent_cents` is 0 — its certifications pin `max_rent_cents` at 0 — so a review reporting a non-zero rent on such a unit records that whole reported rent as its `overcharge_monthly_cents` even though `RENT_OVERCHARGE` itself cannot fire for it, and if any other finding does fire that figure is what prices `penalty_at_base_cents` and therefore what buys the cure extension.
- `penalty_at_base_cents` is `overcharge_monthly_cents` multiplied by 3, plus 25000 cents for exactly one month. This is the penalty *as if* the cure landed inside the first month, and it is the sole input to the extension decision.
- `cure_extension_days` is 15 when `penalty_at_base_cents` is at least 100000 cents, and 0 otherwise — a serious violation earns more time to fix, not less. The threshold is inclusive.
- `cure_deadline` is `finding_issued_at` plus 30 days plus `cure_extension_days`.

`cure_extension_days` and `cure_deadline` are decided exactly once, at issue, from `penalty_at_base_cents` alone. They are **never** re-derived afterwards, because re-deriving the extension from the larger final penalty would move the very deadline that lateness is measured against.

**Submitting a cure** is the owning `Developer`'s action, supplying a `corrected_rent_cents`. It is permitted from `FINDING_ISSUED` or `ESCALATED` only; any other review state is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. Every submission stamps `cure_submitted_at` with the current logical instant, stores `corrected_rent_cents`, and computes `penalty_final_cents` at that instant: count the elapsed whole days from `finding_issued_at` to `cure_submitted_at`, divide by 30 and round *up* to the next whole number, take the greater of that and 1, and that is the number of penalty months; `penalty_final_cents` is then `overcharge_monthly_cents` multiplied by 3, plus 25000 cents for each such month. Because the month count uses a ceiling, day 31 is already the second month. `cure_deadline` and `cure_extension_days` are left exactly as they were.

From `FINDING_ISSUED` the submission forks on the clock alone, and the two branches carry disjoint cascades. Submitted at or before `cure_deadline`, the review moves `FINDING_ISSUED → CURE_SUBMITTED` and the unit moves `FINDING_OPEN → CURE_PENDING` with its `current_rent_cents` overwritten by the `corrected_rent_cents`; the compliance transition there is conditional on the unit reading `FINDING_OPEN`, but the rent overwrite and the unit's single `version` bump are not, so a unit found anywhere else on the compliance dimension still records the corrected rent. This on-time branch never touches the project row and never recomputes its `compliance_posture`. Submitted after `cure_deadline`, the review moves `FINDING_ISSUED → ESCALATED` and then, only if the unit reads `FINDING_OPEN`, the unit moves `FINDING_OPEN → OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` and the project's `compliance_posture` is recomputed with the project's `version` incremented — the recomputation runs the whole clause list of §3.9, which lands on `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` for a project that is past `DRAFT` and not `EXEMPT`, and on `PENDING` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` when one of those two earlier clauses matches instead. If the unit was not `FINDING_OPEN` the late branch touches neither the unit nor the project at all. The late branch does not touch `current_rent_cents` in either case.

A `HousingComplianceOfficer` then disposes of a `CURE_SUBMITTED` review one of two ways.

- **Clearing the cure** moves the review `CURE_SUBMITTED → CURED`, storing `cleared_by_user_id`, and cascades the unit `CURE_PENDING → COMPLIANT` with the project's `compliance_posture` recomputed. Both halves of that cascade are conditional on the unit actually reading `CURE_PENDING`: when it does not, only the review row changes and neither the unit nor the project advances its `version`. Clearing is not ownership-scoped — any `HousingComplianceOfficer` other than the issuer may clear, whatever project the unit belongs to. Two guards apply, in this order: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the review's `issued_by_user_id`, since the officer who issued a finding may not also clear it, refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the submitted `corrected_rent_cents` must be at most the unit's `max_allowed_rent_cents`, because a cure that still overcharges is no cure, refused with `ERR_CURE_INSUFFICIENT`. `CURED` is the review's other terminal state.
- **Escalating** moves the review `CURE_SUBMITTED → ESCALATED` and, again only if the unit reads `CURE_PENDING`, cascades the unit `CURE_PENDING → OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` and recomputes the project's `compliance_posture` by the same clause list, normally settling on `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT`. A unit that is not `CURE_PENDING` leaves both the unit and the project untouched. Escalation reads no body fields and writes no acting-user field onto the review, and like clearing it is not ownership-scoped.

Enforcement is not a dead end. An `ESCALATED` review re-enters the pipeline when the owning `Developer` submits a further cure, moving the review `ESCALATED → CURE_SUBMITTED` and the unit `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE → CURE_PENDING` with `current_rent_cents` refreshed and the project's posture recomputed. The compliance transition is once more conditional on the unit reading `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`, but the rent refresh, the unit's `version` bump and the project's posture recomputation are unconditional on this branch — which is the asymmetry worth remembering, since an `ESCALATED → CURE_SUBMITTED` submission always mutates the project row while a `FINDING_ISSUED → CURE_SUBMITTED` one never does. `penalty_final_cents` is recomputed at the new `cure_submitted_at` and therefore keeps growing with each month of delay, while `cure_deadline` remains the figure frozen at issue. That loop can run as many times as needed, and it is the only route out of `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` on the compliance dimension — which matters because a unit whose `compliance_status` is `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` is barred from resale submission, price approval, refinance review and restriction release by `ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`.

### 3.8. Deed Restrictions, Refinancing and Resale

An ownership unit carries its affordability in a recorded deed restriction, and that restriction is what makes every downstream price control enforceable. A `HousingComplianceOfficer` records the restriction by supplying the `original_purchase_price_cents` the household actually paid, an integer of at least 0, and the `recorded_at` instant, accepted either as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date or as a full ISO 8601 instant and stored verbatim as supplied. The unit's `tenure` must be `OWNERSHIP` — a rental unit has no deed restriction to record and is refused with `ERR_RESTRICTION_TENURE_MISMATCH`. The unit's governing agreement must itself be `RECORDED`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`. The unit's `restriction_state` must be `NOT_RECORDED`, else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_RESTRICTED`. The unit must not be `WITHDRAWN`, else `ERR_UNIT_OCCUPANCY_STATE_INVALID`, and at least one AMI schedule must exist, else `ERR_NO_AMI_SCHEDULE`. Those five conditions are listed here for exposition, not in precedence order: they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.9, so the agreement guard (rung 10), the restriction-state guard (rung 11), the withdrawn-unit guard (rung 12) and the AMI-schedule guard (rung 27) all outrank the tenure guard (rung 53) — a `RENTAL` unit whose agreement is still `DRAFT` therefore reports `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`, not `ERR_RESTRICTION_TENURE_MISMATCH`. Recording moves the unit `NOT_RECORDED → RECORDED` on the restriction dimension, stores `restriction_recorded_at` and `original_purchase_price_cents`, and freezes `ami_income_at_purchase_cents` from the active schedule's `median_income_cents_by_size` entry for the unit's `bedrooms` plus one. Those two frozen numbers are the basis of every resale ceiling and refinance ceiling the unit will ever see.

Releasing the restriction is the reverse and is permitted only from `RECORDED`, moving the unit `RECORDED → RELEASED`. Release is blocked while any resale of that unit is live — that is, in `SUBMITTED`, `PRICE_APPROVED`, `ROFR_WAIVED`, `ROFR_EXERCISED` or `BUYER_CERTIFIED` — refused with `ERR_RESALE_IN_PROGRESS`, and it is blocked while the unit's `compliance_status` is `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`, refused with `ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`. Per §2.9 the compliance guard (rung 22) outranks the live-resale guard (rung 54), so a unit that is both out of compliance and mid-resale reports `ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`. `RELEASED` is terminal on that dimension.

**Refinance review** lets an owner borrow against a restricted unit without borrowing against value the restriction does not permit. Either the owning `Developer` or a `HousingComplianceOfficer` submits a `requested_balance_cents` of at least 0; the unit's `restriction_state` must be `RECORDED` and its `compliance_status` must not be `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`, and an AMI schedule must exist. Read `current_median` as the active schedule's median income for the unit's `bedrooms` plus one. Multiply the unit's `original_purchase_price_cents` by `current_median`, divide by the unit's frozen `ami_income_at_purchase_cents`, and round half up: that is the unit's restricted value today. Multiply that restricted value by the agreement's `max_refinance_ltv_bps`, divide by 10000 and round half up again: that is `approved_refinance_cap_cents`, which is stored on the unit. If `requested_balance_cents` exceeds the cap the review is refused with `ERR_REFINANCE_EXCEEDS_CAP`; the cap is inclusive, so a request exactly equal to it is approved and the cap plus one cent is not. On approval `last_refinance_balance_cents` records the requested balance and the unit's `version` increments exactly once for the pair of writes. Because a `RECORDED` restriction only ever exists on an `OWNERSHIP` unit, a refinance review is unreachable for a rental one, and it consults neither the project's `state` nor its `compliance_posture`. Because the restricted value is recomputed from whichever schedule is active at review time, publishing a newer AMI schedule raises the same unit's cap.

**Submitting a resale** is done by the selling `Resident` — who must be the unit's `occupant_household_id` — or by a `ProgramAdministrator` or `HousingComplianceOfficer` on that household's behalf. The submission names the `unit_id` and the `expected_unit_version` optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.8, and carries the commercial terms: `proposed_price_cents` of at least 1, the unrestricted market appraisal `appraised_value_cents` of at least 1, the `capital_improvements_cents` submitted for credit, the `deferred_maintenance_cents` assessed against the seller, and the `broker_fee_cents` that will be deducted to reach net proceeds, all three of at least 0. It may carry a `prior_transaction_id` linking to an earlier closed resale of the same unit. That link is checked strictly: when supplied it must reference a `CLOSED` resale of the *same* unit — any one of them, since the check is state-and-unit only and never insists on the most recent — and when omitted the unit must have no closed resale history at all; either failure is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_TRANSACTION_MISMATCH`. The chain is therefore mandatory in one direction only: once a unit has closed even one resale, every later submission must name one. The unit's `restriction_state` must be `RECORDED` and its `compliance_status` must not be `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` — and when a submission both names a foreign unit's closed transaction and targets an out-of-compliance unit, the safety refusal `ERR_UNIT_OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` is the one returned, because it sits higher on the ladder of §2.9 than the referential one. Only `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` blocks a submission; a unit sitting at `FINDING_OPEN` or `CURE_PENDING` may still be sold. The unit's `occupancy_status` is not consulted at all, so a `ProgramAdministrator` or a `HousingComplianceOfficer` may submit against a `RECORDED` unit that is `PLANNED`, `MARKETING` or `VACANT`, in which case the `seller_household_id` copied from the unit is null — which changes what the close cascade retires, as set out below. A `Resident` never reaches that case, because the occupant check of rung 7 already requires the unit to be occupied by that household.

Submission freezes the lawful ceiling onto the row, and this is one of the two places where a single named calculation has two genuinely different variants. First, common to both: `resale_formula_applied` is copied from the agreement's `resale_formula`, pinning which variant runs for the life of this transaction, and `improvement_credit_cents` is the lesser of the submitted `capital_improvements_cents` and the credit cap — the unit's `original_purchase_price_cents` multiplied by 1000 basis points, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. Then the variant decides `indexed_base_cents`:

- Under `AMI_INDEXED`, multiply the unit's `original_purchase_price_cents` by the active schedule's median income for `bedrooms` plus one, divide by the unit's frozen `ami_income_at_purchase_cents`, and round half up. The price tracks AMI growth and the submitted `appraised_value_cents` is ignored entirely.
- Under `SHARED_APPRECIATION`, take the appreciation as `appraised_value_cents` minus `original_purchase_price_cents`, floored at 0, multiply it by the agreement's `owner_appreciation_share_bps`, divide by 10000 and round half up, then add that to `original_purchase_price_cents`. The price tracks the market appraisal and the AMI schedule is ignored entirely by the arithmetic — though rung 27 still requires at least one schedule to have been published before the submission is accepted, so an unpublished schedule refuses a shared-appreciation resale that would never have read it.

`max_resale_price_cents` is then `indexed_base_cents` plus `improvement_credit_cents` minus `deferred_maintenance_cents`. That final subtraction is **not** floored at zero and submission never validates its result, so a `deferred_maintenance_cents` exceeding the indexed base plus the credit is accepted and frozen as a negative `max_resale_price_cents` — a ceiling that no `proposed_price_cents` of at least 1 can meet, which surfaces not at submission but later, as `ERR_RESALE_PRICE_EXCEEDS_MAX` at price approval. The same unit with the same submitted numbers under two different agreements yields two different ceilings. Submission also copies `seller_household_id` from the unit's `occupant_household_id`, sets `state` to `SUBMITTED`, moves any other `SUBMITTED` resale of the same unit `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED`, and increments the unit's `version` by one — which is why a second submission must supply a freshly read `expected_unit_version`. Only `SUBMITTED` siblings are retired: a transaction of the same unit that has already advanced to `PRICE_APPROVED`, `ROFR_WAIVED`, `ROFR_EXERCISED` or `BUYER_CERTIFIED` is left exactly as it stands, so one unit can legitimately carry two live transactions at once, and each of them keeps the ceiling frozen at its own submission. The unit row itself is only version-bumped; none of its stored fields change at submission. A transaction that has not yet closed carries all five proceeds-waterfall figures — `net_proceeds_cents`, `seller_equity_cents`, `seller_improvement_credit_cents`, `seller_appreciation_share_cents` and `city_recapture_cents` — as integer 0 rather than null; the close described below is the only step that ever writes them, so a transaction in `SUBMITTED`, `PRICE_APPROVED`, `ROFR_WAIVED`, `ROFR_EXERCISED`, `BUYER_CERTIFIED` or `SUPERSEDED` reports 0 in all five. The stamps and links the later steps fill in — `price_approved_at`, `price_approved_by_user_id`, `rofr_deadline`, `buyer_household_id`, `buyer_certification_id` and `closed_at` — are each null on a fresh submission, while `rofr_auto_waived` is written as boolean `false` and turns `true` only in sweep step 4, never on an explicit `WAIVE` decision.

**Approving the price** is a `HousingComplianceOfficer` action from `SUBMITTED` only. The `proposed_price_cents` must be at most the frozen `max_resale_price_cents`, else `ERR_RESALE_PRICE_EXCEEDS_MAX`; the ceiling is inclusive. The unit must still be `RECORDED` and not `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`. Approval moves the transaction `SUBMITTED → PRICE_APPROVED`, stamps `price_approved_at` with the current logical instant and `price_approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and opens the city's right of first refusal by setting `rofr_deadline` to `price_approved_at` plus 30 days. Price approval is the last step that reads the unit at all: neither the right-of-first-refusal decision, nor buyer certification, nor close re-checks the unit's `restriction_state` or its `compliance_status`, so a unit that a monitoring finding pushes to `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` after its price has been approved still runs the remaining three steps to completion.

**The right-of-first-refusal decision** is a `ProgramAdministrator` action from `PRICE_APPROVED`, carrying a `decision` of either `WAIVE` or `EXERCISE`. The window is enforced as an instant comparison and the deadline instant itself is inclusive: a decision arriving at exactly `rofr_deadline` is accepted, and one arriving later is refused with `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. `WAIVE` moves the transaction `PRICE_APPROVED → ROFR_WAIVED` and the private sale proceeds. `EXERCISE` means the city itself buys the unit, so it additionally requires the trust fund's `unencumbered_balance_cents` to be at least the `proposed_price_cents`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUND_BALANCE`; it moves the transaction `PRICE_APPROVED → ROFR_EXERCISED`. That comparison is a solvency test and nothing more: exercising encumbers no cents, writes no draw, and leaves all eight figures of §3.9 exactly where they were — both at the decision and at the city-purchase close that follows — so the same balance stays available to trust-fund draws, and the test is never repeated at close even if the fund has been emptied in between. This is the fork: the two decisions lead to two different terminal states with different money and occupancy outcomes.

The window also closes by itself, and the two closings are deliberately different in granularity. Sweep step 4 auto-waives a transaction only once the `rofr_deadline`'s own calendar day has *fully* elapsed, that is when the deadline's calendar date is strictly earlier than the new logical instant's calendar date, moving it `PRICE_APPROVED → ROFR_WAIVED` and setting `rofr_auto_waived` to true. Between the deadline instant and the end of that calendar day the transaction is therefore still `PRICE_APPROVED`, yet an explicit decision arriving in that gap is refused with `ERR_ROFR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. After the sweep has auto-waived it, a late decision fails the resale state guard `ERR_RESALE_STATE_INVALID` instead.

**Certifying the buyer** is a `HousingComplianceOfficer` action from `ROFR_WAIVED` only, naming a `buyer_household_id` and a `certification_id`. The officer who approved the price may not also certify the buyer: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to `price_approved_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The referenced certification must be in `state` `CERTIFIED`, must be for this transaction's `unit_id`, and must belong to the household named in the payload; any mismatch is refused with `ERR_BUYER_CERTIFICATION_MISMATCH`. Success stores `buyer_household_id` and `buyer_certification_id` and moves the transaction `ROFR_WAIVED → BUYER_CERTIFIED`. On the exercised branch these two fields stay null, because the city, not a household, is the buyer.

**Closing** is a `HousingComplianceOfficer` action from `BUYER_CERTIFIED` or `ROFR_EXERCISED`; any other state is refused with `ERR_RESALE_STATE_INVALID`. Close stamps `closed_at`, moves the transaction `BUYER_CERTIFIED → CLOSED` or `ROFR_EXERCISED → CITY_PURCHASE_CLOSED`, and runs the proceeds waterfall. Start from `net_proceeds_cents`, the `proposed_price_cents` minus the `broker_fee_cents`, and pour that figure through four buckets in order, each bucket taking what it can from what is left.

1. `seller_equity_cents` — the lesser of the remaining proceeds and the unit's `original_purchase_price_cents` as the unit carries it at the instant of close, not as it stood when this transaction was submitted; the two differ whenever another transaction of the same unit closed in between and reset the basis. It returns the seller's restricted equity basis.
2. `seller_improvement_credit_cents` — the lesser of what now remains and the transaction's `improvement_credit_cents`, already capped at submission.
3. `seller_appreciation_share_cents` — on the exercised branch the seller takes the entire residual, because the city is the buyer. Otherwise multiply the residual by the agreement's `owner_appreciation_share_bps`, divide by 10000 and round half up.
4. `city_recapture_cents` — exactly what is left after bucket 3, taken by subtraction and never rounded independently. On the exercised branch it is 0.

The four buckets always sum to `net_proceeds_cents` exactly, for every input including an underwater sale where buckets 2, 3 and 4 all collapse to 0, because bucket 4 absorbs the remainder rather than rounding on its own. Neither `net_proceeds_cents` nor bucket 1 is floored at zero: when `broker_fee_cents` exceeds `proposed_price_cents` the net is negative and bucket 1 records that whole negative figure as the seller's equity, leaving exactly nothing for the three buckets behind it — which is precisely what keeps the sum equal to the net instead of manufacturing cents the sale never produced.

Close then cascades in three layers, and the two terminal states differ in the middle one. Because the transaction closed, the unit's price basis is reset: `original_purchase_price_cents` becomes the `proposed_price_cents` and `ami_income_at_purchase_cents` is re-captured from the active schedule's median for `bedrooms` plus one, so the next resale is indexed from the new basis. On the `CLOSED` branch the unit's `occupant_household_id` becomes the `buyer_household_id` and its `occupancy_status` becomes `OCCUPIED`; on the `CITY_PURCHASE_CLOSED` branch the `occupant_household_id` is cleared to null and the `occupancy_status` becomes `VACANT`, because the city holds an empty unit awaiting a new placement. Because the unit's price basis changed, the seller's `CERTIFIED` certification for that unit moves `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED` on both branches. The scan looks at every certification of this unit, keeps only those in state `CERTIFIED`, and among those retires the ones belonging to the `seller_household_id`; when that field is null — the unit was unoccupied when the resale was submitted — the household filter drops away and *every* `CERTIFIED` certification of the unit is retired, the buyer's freshly attached one included. The project row is not part of this cascade at all: a resale close leaves the project's obligation aggregates, its `compliance_posture` and its `version` untouched even though the unit's `occupancy_status` has moved. And because `city_recapture_cents` was booked, the trust fund's `total_recapture_cents` rises on the `CLOSED` branch, which lifts both `unencumbered_balance_cents` and `admin_cap_cents`. `CLOSED`, `CITY_PURCHASE_CLOSED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal.

### 3.9. The Housing Trust Fund, Funder Reporting and Close-Out

Every cent an in-lieu payment or a city recapture brings in lands in one pooled housing trust fund, and the fund is a computed read rather than a stored balance. Its eight figures are derived, on every read, from the rows that produced them.

- `total_paid_fees_cents` is the sum of `paid_amount_cents` across **every** in-lieu assessment regardless of `state` — money already received is not un-received by a later supersession.
- `total_recapture_cents` is the sum of `city_recapture_cents` across resale transactions in `state` `CLOSED` only.
- `total_inflow_cents` is those two added together.
- `encumbered_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` across draws in `state` `REQUESTED`.
- `disbursed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` across draws in `state` `DISBURSED`.
- `unencumbered_balance_cents` is `total_inflow_cents` minus `encumbered_cents` minus `disbursed_cents`.
- `admin_cap_cents` is `total_inflow_cents` multiplied by 1000 basis points, divided by 10000 and rounded half up — administration may consume at most a tenth of everything the fund has ever taken in.
- `admin_committed_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` across draws whose `state` is `REQUESTED` or `DISBURSED` and whose `eligible_use` is `ADMIN`.

Draws in `state` `VOIDED` contribute nothing to any of these figures.

**Requesting a draw** is a `ProgramAdministrator` action naming the `source_fee_id` of the assessment the draw is earmarked against — at request time that assessment need only resolve, because its `state` is examined when the draw is disbursed and not before, so a draw may be booked against an assessment still `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `DELINQUENT` or already `SUPERSEDED` — an `eligible_use` of exactly one of `AFFORDABLE_HOUSING_PRODUCTION`, `RENTAL_SUBSIDY`, `HOMEBUYER_ASSISTANCE` or `ADMIN`, and an `amount_cents` of at least 1. Two fiscal guards apply. For an `ADMIN` draw, `admin_committed_cents` plus the requested `amount_cents` must be at most `admin_cap_cents`, else `ERR_ADMIN_USE_CAP_EXCEEDED`; both bounds are inclusive. And for every draw, the `amount_cents` must be at most `unencumbered_balance_cents`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FUND_BALANCE` — the same refusal that blocks a right-of-first-refusal exercise the fund cannot afford. A new draw sits in `state` `REQUESTED` with `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and the requested cents are immediately encumbered, so `unencumbered_balance_cents` falls the moment the request is accepted.

**Disbursing** moves a draw `REQUESTED → DISBURSED` and stamps `disbursed_at` with the current logical instant. It requires the earmarked assessment to be in `state` `PAID`: drawing against an assessment that is still `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `DELINQUENT` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_SOURCE_FEE_NOT_PAID`. Disbursement moves the cents from the encumbered bucket to the disbursed bucket and therefore leaves `unencumbered_balance_cents` completely unchanged.

**Voiding** is the compensating action and is permitted from either live state, moving the draw `REQUESTED → VOIDED` or `DISBURSED → VOIDED` and storing the supplied `void_reason`, a mandatory string of 1 to 500 characters. A `disbursed_at` stamp the draw already carries is left exactly as it was rather than cleared, so a voided draw still records when its money went out. A void from either state removes the cents from whichever bucket held them, so `unencumbered_balance_cents` returns to exactly its pre-request value and `admin_committed_cents` returns to exactly its pre-request value too. `VOIDED` is terminal; a voided draw cannot be revived, only replaced by a fresh request. Any other state combination — disbursing something already disbursed or voided, voiding something already voided — is refused with `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Across all of this the money invariant of §2.11 holds at every observable instant: `unencumbered_balance_cents` plus `encumbered_cents` plus `disbursed_cents` always equals `total_inflow_cents`.

**Funder reporting** answers a different question for a different audience, and it is the second place where one named calculation has two real variants. A `FundAuditor`, `ProgramAdministrator` or `HousingComplianceOfficer` requests a report for one `project_id` on a stated `basis`. Both arrive as query parameters and both are mandatory: an absent or empty `project_id`, an absent or empty `basis`, or a `basis` outside `ENTITLEMENT` and `OCCUPANCY` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because that payload rung outranks existence, a bad `basis` paired with an unknown `project_id` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`. The result is a single computed object, not a collection, so it carries no pagination fields in `meta`; `limit` and `offset` are nevertheless parsed and range-checked if supplied, which means a `limit` outside 1 to 100 is still `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` here even though neither parameter changes the answer. The report returns that `project_id`, the `basis` it was computed on, and seven figures. `total_obligation_bps` is the project's obligation as screened. `delivered_equivalents_bps` is `onsite_unit_equivalents_bps` plus `fee_covered_equivalents_bps` — everything discharged on paper. `occupied_equivalents_bps` is 10000 basis points for each unit of the project that is *both* `OCCUPIED` and `COMPLIANT` — everything discharged in fact. `open_findings_count` counts the project's monitoring reviews in `state` `FINDING_ISSUED`, `CURE_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`. `total_fees_assessed_cents` sums `assessed_amount_cents` across the project's assessments excluding those in `state` `SUPERSEDED`, while `total_fees_paid_cents` sums `paid_amount_cents` across all of them.

The `basis` decides only `compliance_ratio_bps`, and it decides it completely.

- On the `ENTITLEMENT` basis the ratio is 10000 multiplied by `delivered_equivalents_bps`, divided by `total_obligation_bps`, rounded half up. The funder is crediting obligations discharged on paper: an assessed in-lieu fee counts fully, and a unit counts the moment it exists whether anyone lives in it or not.
- On the `OCCUPANCY` basis the ratio is 10000 multiplied by `occupied_equivalents_bps`, divided by `total_obligation_bps`, rounded half up. The funder is crediting households actually housed: in-lieu fees contribute nothing at all, and a unit counts only while it is both occupied and compliant, so a finding on an occupied unit lowers this ratio without moving the entitlement ratio by a single basis point.

A project whose `total_obligation_bps` is 0 reports `compliance_ratio_bps` as 10000 on either basis rather than dividing by zero. `WITHDRAWN` units are excluded from every metric in the report, `open_findings_count` included — a finding on a withdrawn unit is invisible here. A `SUPERSEDED` assessment is excluded from `total_fees_assessed_cents` and contributes nothing to `fee_covered_equivalents_bps`, but the cents it actually collected before it was superseded still count in `total_fees_paid_cents`, exactly as they still count in the trust fund's `total_paid_fees_cents`; the report can therefore show `total_fees_paid_cents` exceeding `total_fees_assessed_cents`.

**Close-out** retires a project's inclusionary obligation, and it is deliberately the hardest gate in the system. A `ProgramAdministrator` or `HousingComplianceOfficer` closes out a project in `state` `MONITORING` only; any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. Four substantive gates then apply in this order.

1. **The bedroom distribution floor.** Count the project's units whose `occupancy_status` is not `WITHDRAWN` and whose `bedrooms` is at least 2 — the family-sized units. Multiply that count by 10000 and compare it against the project's `required_affordable_units` multiplied by 5000 basis points: if the family-unit side is strictly smaller the close-out is refused with `ERR_BEDROOM_DISTRIBUTION_FAILED`. At least half the required units must be family-sized. This is an aggregate rule, so every unit can have passed its own comparability check at creation while the collection still fails here, and withdrawing a single two-bedroom unit can flip a project from passing to failing.
2. **The obligation itself.** The project's `compliance_posture` must be `COMPLIANT_ONSITE` or `COMPLIANT_BY_FEE`; a `DEFICIENT`, `UNDER_ENFORCEMENT` or `PENDING` posture is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_UNSATISFIED`.
3. **The money.** Every in-lieu assessment of the project must be in `state` `PAID` or `SUPERSEDED`; an `ASSESSED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `DELINQUENT` assessment is refused with `ERR_FEE_OUTSTANDING`.
4. **The open findings.** No monitoring review on any unit of the project may be in `state` `FINDING_ISSUED`, `CURE_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`; any of those is refused with `ERR_OPEN_FINDING_BLOCKS_CLOSEOUT`. A `COMPLIANT` or `CURED` review is no obstacle.

When all four gates pass, the project moves `MONITORING → CLOSED_OUT` and every regulatory agreement of that project still in `state` `RECORDED` moves `RECORDED → RELEASED`, each incrementing its own `version`; the project's aggregates and `compliance_posture` are recomputed after the state change and its own `version` increments exactly once. `CLOSED_OUT` is the project's terminal state and `RELEASED` is the agreement's, and at that point the development's inclusionary obligation is discharged for the record.
