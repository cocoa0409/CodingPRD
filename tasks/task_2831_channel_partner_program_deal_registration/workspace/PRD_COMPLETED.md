# Channel Partner Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Software vendors that sell through a channel do not sell through one channel. They sell through distributors who buy inventory and resell it, through resellers who never take stock, and through their own field sales force — and all three of them will, sooner or later, walk into the same end customer on the same week. The Channel Partner Program service is the vendor's system of record for that collision: it tiers partners on measurable attainment, lets them stake a claim on a deal before they invest in it, arbitrates the claims that overlap, prices the resulting orders against the tier and the claim, and funds the marketing that created the pipeline in the first place.

Three pressures shape every rule in this document. The first is **temporal**: a deal registration confers a protection window that begins the instant the channel director approves it and ends a tier-dependent number of days later, and almost every interesting rejection in the system is a question about whether some instant falls inside some interval. The second is **fiscal**: margin points and market development fund cents are real money, so the service resolves margin through a strictly ordered pipeline and moves fund cents only between named buckets that must always sum back to what was allocated. The third is **governance**: channel programs are audited, so the party that prepares a figure may never be the party that approves it, and a partner whose compliance findings have gone unremediated is frozen out of the program until it acts.

This document specifies the complete external contract. Section 2 fixes the parts that every client depends on structurally — headers, envelopes, paging, identity, rounding, window geometry, stale-version guards, and the order in which rejections are evaluated. Section 3 narrates the business itself, following a partner from enrolment through registration, conflict, pricing, funding and annual review. Sections 4 and 5 summarise the HTTP surface and the error vocabulary. All routes are mounted at the service root; there is no versioning or tenancy prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock capability described in §2.4 must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. Exactly four roles exist, spelled precisely as follows: `Partner`, `ChannelOpsAnalyst`, `ChannelDirector`, and `DirectSalesRep`. If either header is absent — a header present but carrying the empty string counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` carries any string other than those four, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The four roles divide along preparation-versus-decision lines. A `ChannelOpsAnalyst` is channel operations: it enrols partners, records certifications, maintains the account coverage map, proposes account mapping agreements, reviews deal registrations, prices and invoices orders, opens and closes market development fund periods, verifies proof documents, validates claims, and prepares performance reviews. A `ChannelDirector` is the approval authority: it decides deal registrations, decides special pricing requests, resolves channel conflict cases, advances claim settlements, and decides performance reviews. A `DirectSalesRep` is the vendor's own field seller, whose only write capability is declaring direct pursuit of an account and escalating a conflict case to the second rung. A `Partner` is the channel partner company itself.

Only the `Partner` role is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Partner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Partner` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Partner` compare that resource's `partner_id` foreign key to `X-User-ID`. For a deal registration the compared field is the registration's own `partner_id`; for an account mapping agreement it is the mapping's `partner_id`; for a performance review it is the review's `partner_id`; for a market development fund claim it is the `partner_id` of the fund period the claim draws from; for a conflict escalation it is the `partner_id` of the case's subject registration; and for a create request it is the `partner_id` supplied in the body. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope check guards eight write operations: drafting a deal registration, submitting or resubmitting one, responding to an account mapping agreement, escalating a conflict case, creating a special pricing request, creating a fund claim, attaching a proof document to a claim, and remediating a performance review. It fires only when the acting role is `Partner`: the same operation performed by another role the endpoint authorises — channel operations countering, accepting or terminating an account mapping agreement, for instance — is not scope-checked at all. And `X-User-ID` is never itself resolved against the `Partner` collection, so a `Partner` identifier that names no partner row is not an authentication or existence failure of any kind; it is simply an identifier that fails every one of these ownership comparisons.

When `X-User-Role` is `ChannelOpsAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands an audit trail — as `prepared_by_user_id` on a `PerformanceReview`, as `validated_by_user_id` on an `MdfClaim`, and as `verified_by_user_id` on a `ProofOfPerformance` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same is true of `ChannelDirector`, whose opaque identifier is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` on a `PerformanceReview` or a `SpecialPricingRequest`, as `resolved_by_user_id` on a `ConflictCase`, and as `settled_by_user_id` on an `MdfClaim`; and of `DirectSalesRep`, whose opaque identifier is recorded as `direct_pursuit_owner_id` on an `Account`. Because these three identifiers are opaque, the segregation-of-duties comparisons in §2.8 are string comparisons between two recorded operator identifiers, not entity lookups.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the only instant in the entire contract that is read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, because it describes when the response was produced rather than any business instant; it therefore bears no relation to the logical clock's current value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0, may be no smaller than 0, and has no upper bound at all — an `offset` past the end of the result set is accepted and simply yields an empty page with the true `total_count`. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422); "an integer" means a run of decimal digits with an optional leading `+` or `-`, so an empty value and a value carrying a decimal point (`10.0`) are both rejected even where the number they denote would have been in range.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first — with ties broken by insertion order, so rows minted at the same logical instant come back in the order they were created. List responses carry `"response_type": "array"`, and their `meta` object carries the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before paging is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this service carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Every resource also carries `created_at`, the instant the row was inserted, and `updated_at`, the instant the row was last mutated; both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits, and on a freshly created row `updated_at` equals `created_at`. Every resource further carries `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. All twelve resource types — `Partner`, `CertificationRecord`, `Account`, `AccountMapping`, `DealRegistration`, `ConflictCase`, `SpecialPricingRequest`, `MdfAllocation`, `MdfClaim`, `ProofOfPerformance`, `OrderInvoice` and `PerformanceReview` — carry all four of these properties; none of them omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Version bumps and `updated_at` refreshes are not confined to the row named in the request path. When an operation cascades into another row — an account whose coverage changes because a registration was approved, a partner whose standing changes because a claim was clawed back, a fund period whose buckets move because a claim was settled — that other row's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed as well. One qualification applies to the hops whose whole content is a recomputation of derived fields — the partner-standing hops that recompute `open_conflict_count`, `marketing_qualified_spend_cents`, `marketing_attainment_pct`, `cert_credit_total`, `tier_status`, `standing_reason` and `portal_entitlement_level`: such a hop advances the partner's `version` and `updated_at` only when the recomputation actually changes one of those figures. A recomputation that lands on the values already stored is a no-op and leaves the partner's `version` and `updated_at` exactly as they were, so a cascade that visits a partner nothing has changed for is not observable on that partner's audit fields at all. Hops that write a non-derived field — a status, a window instant, a closure reason, a coverage field, a fund bucket — always bump, unconditionally. No read ever bumps anything: a GET leaves every row's `version` and `updated_at` untouched even though the derived fields of a `Partner` and the `available_cents` of an `MdfAllocation` are recomputed from the current rows at the moment they are serialised, which is why a read can surface a fresher derived figure than the last `version` bump would suggest. Section 2.9 states the discipline; §3 states each individual cascade.

Time in this service is entirely logical. A test-driving capability sets the server's logical clock to an arbitrary instant supplied as `current_utc`; it writes no entity, and every subsequent request observes that instant as "now". Before the first such call the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A freshly launched process starts from that default with all twelve collections completely empty: nothing is seeded, and no state survives a restart. Every window, deadline, cooldown, expiry sweep and elapsed-day computation in this document reads only that clock. This capability is the one endpoint that does not require the identity headers of §2.1, and because it bypasses §2.1 altogether it does not inspect them either: a call carrying no headers, or carrying an `X-User-Role` that is not one of the four declared roles, is accepted rather than rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. `current_utc` is required and must be an instant in the form §2.6 accepts, and the call returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object.

### 2.5. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; the API never accepts or emits a fractional cent. All rates, margins and attainment figures are decimal fractions rather than percentage points, so a margin of eighteen percent is carried as `0.1800`. Every one of them — and every score — travels over the wire as a JSON number rather than a string, so the stated decimal precision is a rounding rule and not a rendering rule: a `margin_pct` rounded to `0.1800` reads back as `0.18`, a `marketing_attainment_pct` of `1.0000` reads back as `1.0`, and a `provisional_score` of `25.00` reads back as `25.0`. Clients must compare these fields numerically, never as text.

Rounding is always half-up, never banker's rounding, and it is applied at the precisions the individual rules name. Margin fractions — `margin_pct`, `registration_margin_uplift_pct`, `requested_extra_margin_pct` and `approved_extra_margin_pct` — carry four decimal places. `marketing_attainment_pct` also carries four decimal places and is constrained to the closed range from 0.0 to 1.0 inclusive. Weighted review scores — `provisional_score` and `final_score` — carry two decimal places. Certification credits, conflict counters and escalation levels are plain integers with no rounding at all, and portal entitlement is an enumerated level involving no arithmetic.

Two rounding disciplines are load-bearing and must not be "optimised". First, where a rule sums several rounded lines, each line is rounded to integer cents **before** the summation and the summation itself is exact; summing the raw inputs and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer whenever two or more lines produce a half-cent product. Second, where a rule derives a residual, the residual is obtained by subtraction from the total rather than by a second multiplication, so that no cent is ever created or destroyed. Weighted-score components are the mirror image of the first rule: the components are held as exact fractions, the weighted sum is computed from those exact fractions, and the rounding to two decimal places happens once at the very end — rounding each component before weighting would drift the score.

### 2.6. Window Geometry: Three Different Intervals

This service uses three interval shapes, deliberately different from one another, and confusing them is the single most common way to get the temporal rules wrong.

An **exclusivity window** on a deal registration is half-open. A registration protects a given account-and-product-line pair at an instant when that instant is at or after `exclusivity_start_at` and strictly before `exclusivity_end_at`. One millisecond before `exclusivity_end_at` the protection is still live and a competing submission is rejected with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`; at exactly `exclusivity_end_at` the protection is over, the expiry sweep of §3.3 flips the incumbent to `EXPIRED`, and the competing submission succeeds. At most one registration may hold a live window on a given account-and-product-line pair at any instant; windows are never merged or unioned.

An **account mapping window** is closed at both ends. A mapping is in force from `effective_from` through `effective_until` inclusive. For conflict testing the incumbent's window is additionally extended by a cooldown buffer of fourteen days added after `effective_until` — a one-sided buffer, with no equivalent buffer before `effective_from` — and the intersection test between two windows is inclusive at every endpoint. Section 3.2 states the exact consequences.

A **fiscal or review period** — the `period_start` through `period_end` of an `MdfAllocation` or a `PerformanceReview` — is likewise closed at both ends, and two periods that merely touch at a single endpoint do intersect.

All instants are compared at millisecond precision. An instant is *accepted* on input in the form `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS`, followed by an optional fractional part of one to nine digits, followed by a mandatory UTC designator that is `Z`, `+00:00` or `+0000`; surrounding whitespace is tolerated. Fewer than three fractional digits, or none at all, is therefore perfectly acceptable — `2026-08-10T00:00:00Z` and `2026-08-10T00:00:00.1Z` are both valid — while a date without a time, a time without a UTC designator, and any non-zero offset such as `-05:00` are all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). An instant is *rendered* on output always with exactly three fractional digits and a `Z`, and fractional digits supplied beyond the third are kept for every comparison but **truncated, not rounded**, in that rendering, so an input of `...T00:00:00.999999Z` reads back as `...T00:00:00.999Z`. Wherever a rule adds a number of days to an instant, the addition is an exact multiple of twenty-four hours; there is no calendar or timezone arithmetic. Every declared interval must satisfy its end-after-start rule: `expires_at` must be strictly greater than `issued_at` on a certification record, `effective_until` strictly greater than `effective_from` on a mapping, and `period_end` strictly greater than `period_start` on a fund period and on a performance review. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Two Create Operations

Two create operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row, because in both cases every state precondition would legitimately pass and the write would nonetheless be made against a picture of the world the client never observed.

`POST /special-pricing-requests` requires a non-optional `expected_registration_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `DealRegistration`. If that value does not equal the registration's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters because the program deliberately allows a newer request on the same registration to supersede a pending one, so a second create would otherwise succeed and silently void a request the client had not seen.

`POST /account-mappings` requires a non-optional `expected_account_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Account`. If that value does not equal the account's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters because the account's coverage fields — `coverage_state`, `protected_until`, `protected_partner_id` and `direct_pursuit_owner_id` — are exactly the inputs a mapping agreement is negotiated against, and a proposed mapping is legitimately allowed to coexist with any coverage state, so nothing else would catch the staleness.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field of any kind. Clients read the current `version` of a registration or an account from the corresponding single-resource read before issuing either create. Both guard fields must be non-negative integers, and neither is persisted: they are request-only, so an `AccountMapping` never carries `expected_account_version` and a `SpecialPricingRequest` never carries `expected_registration_version` in any response body. Because request shape sits at priority 5 of §2.8 while the guards themselves sit at 7 and 8, a guard field that is absent, `null`, negative or not an integer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) and never the 412.

### 2.8. The Order of Rejection

When a single request would fail more than one check, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the ascending priority order below, and the first failing check wins. This ordering is part of the contract: a suspended partner submitting a registration that also conflicts on exclusivity must see the suspension, not the conflict.

1. **Authentication.** Both identity headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the four declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock capability.
2. **Authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Existence.** Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and only then are body foreign keys resolved in the order they are declared. A body foreign key is put to the existence check only when its value is shape-plausible, meaning a string that is not blank: a body foreign key that is present but holds a non-string, a blank string, or JSON `null` cannot be resolved against any collection and so falls through to the request-shape check (5), which rejects it with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) rather than with a 404. It is only a present, non-blank string naming no row that produces the not-found errors enumerated below. A missing `Partner` named by a path segment or by a body `partner_id` yields `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND` (404) — this guards reading a partner, patching a partner, recording and listing that partner's certifications, opening a fund period, pricing an order, preparing a performance review, proposing an account mapping, and drafting a deal registration. A missing `CertificationRecord` yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single certification read. A missing `Account` named by a path segment or by a body `account_id` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` (404) — guarding the single account read, the direct-pursuit declaration, the account's registration list, the account's conflict-case list, drafting a deal registration, and proposing an account mapping. A missing `AccountMapping` yields `ERR_MAPPING_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single mapping read and on the mapping response action. A missing `DealRegistration` named by a path segment, by a body `registration_id`, or by a body `supersedes_registration_id` yields `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` (404) — guarding the single registration read, submit, review, decision and convert actions, the registration's special-pricing-request list, the registration's order list, drafting a registration, creating a special pricing request, and pricing an order. A missing `ConflictCase` yields `ERR_CONFLICT_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single case read, the escalation and the resolution. A missing `SpecialPricingRequest` yields `ERR_SPR_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single request read and its decision. A missing `MdfAllocation` named by a path segment or by a body `allocation_id` yields `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` (404) — guarding the single fund-period read, the period close, the period's claim list, and creating a claim. A missing `MdfClaim` yields `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` (404) — guarding the single claim read, validate, settlement, proof attachment, proof list, and proof verification. A `ProofOfPerformance` that does not exist under the supplied `proof_id`, **or that exists but belongs to a claim other than the one named earlier in the same path**, yields `ERR_PROOF_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the verification action; the same error id guards the standalone single proof-document read, which resolves `proof_id` against the whole collection with no parent claim to cross-check. A missing `OrderInvoice` yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single order read, the invoice action and the credit-memo action. A missing `PerformanceReview` yields `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single review read, the remediation and the decision.
4. **Partner scoping.** The eight-operation ownership check of §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
5. **Request shape.** Every required field present with its declared type; every enum field holding one of its declared values; integer money fields not negative; fraction fields inside the closed range from 0 to 1; every datetime field an ISO 8601 UTC instant in one of the forms §2.6 declares acceptable; every declared interval satisfying end strictly after start; every required free-text note (`reviewer_note`, `reject_reason`, `resolution_note`, `remediation_note`, `rejection_reason`, `terminated_reason`) present and non-empty; and a partial special-pricing decision supplying `approved_extra_margin_pct` strictly above 0 and strictly below `requested_extra_margin_pct`. Any violation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every endpoint. Five conventions make "present with its declared type" precise, and they hold on every endpoint alike. First, a field explicitly carrying JSON `null` is treated exactly as an absent field: a required field set to `null` is reported as missing, and an optional field set to `null` takes the default this document names for it. Second, non-empty means non-blank — a string of nothing but whitespace is empty, and a declared maximum length is counted in characters. Third, where an integer is declared the value must be a JSON number: a number whose fractional part is zero (`1000.0`) is accepted as the integer it denotes, a number with a non-zero fractional part is rejected as a fractional cent, a numeric *string* (`"1000"`) is always rejected, and `true`/`false` are never integers. Fourth, where a decimal fraction is declared the value must likewise be a JSON number and never a string, and where a boolean is declared the value must be a JSON boolean. Fifth, the request body must be a JSON object sent under a JSON content type: an omitted or wholly blank body is read as the empty object `{}`, so what is reported is the first missing required field, whereas a body that is present but is not a JSON object — a JSON array, a bare scalar, unparseable text, or even well-formed JSON sent without a JSON content type — is rejected outright as a body that is not a JSON object.
6. **Partner-type eligibility.** An order with `order_type` of `STOCKING` is accepted only when the partner's `partner_type` is `DISTRIBUTOR`; a `RESELLER` may place `DROP_SHIP` orders only. Otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422), on order pricing.
7. **Registration stale-version guard.** `expected_registration_version` equals the registration's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` (412), on special-pricing-request creation.
8. **Account stale-version guard.** `expected_account_version` equals the account's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (412), on account-mapping creation.
9. **State precondition.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared in §3, including the three loop caps: at most two completed revision rounds on a registration, at most two counter rounds on a mapping, and at most two proof rounds on a claim. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
10. **Fund period open.** The `MdfAllocation` behind the operation has `status` of `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_CLOSED` (409). This guards six operations: creating a claim, a `VALIDATE` or `REQUEST_PROOF` claim decision, attaching a proof document, verifying a proof document, the `APPROVE_PAYMENT` and `PAY` settlement actions, and closing the period itself (a period already `CLOSED` cannot be closed again). A `REJECT` claim decision is deliberately not among them: it is never gated on the period being open, so an attempt to reject or claw back a claim on a closed period is answered by the state precondition (9) — cascade C having already driven every claim of that period out of every rejectable state.
11. **Claim validated before settlement.** A settlement action requires `validation_state` of `VALIDATED` — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VALIDATED` (409), on the settlement action.
12. **Duplicate registration.** No other `DealRegistration` with the same partner, account and product line is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `CONFLICT_HELD` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION` (409), on registration submit.
13. **Program standing.** The partner's `tier_status` is not `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED` (403). This guards seven operations: registration submit, an approving registration decision, order pricing, order invoicing, fund-period creation, claim creation, and special-pricing-request creation.
14. **Audit remediation.** The partner has no `PerformanceReview` whose `status` is `FINDINGS_OPEN` and whose `remediation_due_at` is at or before the current logical instant — otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_REMEDIATION_OVERDUE` (409). This guards four operations: registration submit, claim creation, the `ACCEPT` action on an account mapping agreement, and the partner's own remediation of a review (where the check is against that review's own deadline rather than a scan of the partner's other reviews).
15. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the operator who performed the segregated earlier step — `validated_by_user_id` for a settlement action, `prepared_by_user_id` for a review decision. Otherwise `ERR_SOD_SAME_ACTOR` (409). This guards two operations: claim settlement and review decision.
16. **Certification requirement.** The partner's `cert_credit_total` meets the program minimum, with the stricter platform-suite requirement where it applies — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET` (422), on registration submit. Section 3.1 states the thresholds.
17. **Exclusivity conflict.** No other approved registration holds a live exclusivity window on the same account-and-product-line pair, and the account's `coverage_state` is not `DIRECT_LOCKED` — otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` (409). This guards two operations: registration submit and an approving registration decision.
18. **Mapping window conflict.** The mapping window clears every other non-terminated mapping's window plus its cooldown buffer on an overlapping scope — otherwise `ERR_MAPPING_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). This guards three operations: account-mapping creation, the mapping response action, and registration submit (where the collision is against another partner's live active mapping).
19. **Special-pricing validity window.** The current logical instant is strictly before the request's `expires_at` — otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), on the special-pricing decision.
20. **Margin ceiling.** The resolved margin implied by a special-pricing grant or by a credit memo stays at or below the partner tier's margin ceiling — otherwise `ERR_MARGIN_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (422). This guards two operations: the special-pricing decision and the credit-memo action.
21. **Credit size.** `credit_memo_cents` is strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than the invoice's current `invoice_amount_cents` — otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE` (422), on the credit-memo action.
22. **Verified proof present.** At least one of the claim's proof documents has `verified` set to true — otherwise `ERR_NO_VERIFIED_PROOF` (409), on a validating claim decision.
23. **Verification not repeatable.** The proof document being verified currently has `verified` set to false — otherwise `ERR_PROOF_ALREADY_VERIFIED` (409), on proof verification. A second verification would double-count the document's spend into the partner's qualified marketing spend.
24. **Supersede target.** When `supersedes_registration_id` is supplied it names a registration with the same `partner_id`, the same `account_id` and the same `product_line`, whose `status` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (422). This guards two operations: drafting a registration and an approving registration decision.
25. **Registration approved and live.** A special pricing request targets a registration whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose exclusivity window is live at the current logical instant — otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409), on special-pricing-request creation.
26. **Escalation ladder cap.** The case's `escalation_level` is strictly below 2 — otherwise `ERR_CONFLICT_ESCALATION_LIMIT` (409), on conflict escalation, and nothing is written.
27. **Fund availability.** `requested_cents` is at or below the fund period's `available_cents` — otherwise `ERR_MDF_ALLOCATION_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on claim creation.
28. **Annual fund capacity.** The new period's `allocated_cents` plus the `allocated_cents` of every existing period of the same partner whose inclusive interval intersects the new one stays at or below the partner tier's annual cap — otherwise `ERR_MDF_PERIOD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), on fund-period creation.

Note the two consequences that fall out of the ordering above. Because program standing (13) and audit remediation (14) sit above every temporal and fiscal check, a frozen partner always sees the freeze rather than a downstream conflict. Because segregation of duties (15) sits below the claim-validated check (11), a director who validated a claim and then tries to settle it while it is still un-validated sees `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VALIDATED` rather than `ERR_SOD_SAME_ACTOR`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Propagation

Five multi-hop cascades exist in this service, and each one is atomic with the request that triggered it: either every hop lands or none does, and the response body reflects the completed cascade. Atomicity is in fact wider than the cascade — it covers the whole request. Any request that mutates state and then returns an error response, whatever the error and whatever priority rung of §2.8 produced it, leaves the database exactly as it stood before the request arrived. This matters most for the sweeps, because several endpoints deliberately run a sweep *before* a precondition that may still reject the request: the registration expiry sweep of §3.3, the mapping expiry sweep of §3.2 and the certification expiry sweep of §3.9 all run early, and if a later check then rejects the request, the sweep's transitions, closure reasons and `version` bumps are discarded along with everything else the request touched, exactly as though the sweep had never run. A rejected mapping proposal therefore leaves a lapsed incumbent still sitting in `ACTIVE`, and the very next proposal that clears the window test is the one that finally moves it to `EXPIRED`. Read requests never write, so nothing needs unwinding on them. Cascade A fires when a channel director approves a deal registration and runs four hops deep — the account becomes protected, sibling registrations on the same account-and-product-line pair are held, a conflict case is raised for each held registration, and each held registration's partner may be placed on probation. Cascade B fires when a validated but still unpaid claim is clawed back and also runs four hops — the claim is rejected, the fund commitment is released, the partner's qualified marketing spend falls, and the partner may be placed on probation. Cascade C fires when a fund period is closed and runs in a pinned four-step order described in §3.8. Cascade D fires when a direct sales representative declares pursuit of a protected account. Cascade E fires when preparing a performance review sweeps lapsed certifications and recomputes the partner's credit total.

Derived fields are always full recomputations from the current rows, never incremental deltas applied to a previously stored figure. This is what makes the sweeps idempotent: two sweeps that visit the same row can never double-subtract. The one deliberate exception is the fund-bucket ledger of §3.8, whose four counters are moved by explicit transfers precisely so that the conservation identity is observable.

### 2.10. Path Resolution, Sub-Resource Reads and List Filters

Entities in this service are peer collections joined by declared foreign keys; nothing is nested for identity. A sub-resource read such as an account's registrations or a claim's proof documents is a reverse-reachability channel only, and the child is always addressable in its own right as well. Path segments are nonetheless resolved strictly left to right, so a request naming a nonexistent claim and a nonexistent proof document reports the claim as missing; and a proof document that exists but hangs off a different claim than the one named earlier in the path is reported as missing rather than verified.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, the list endpoints expose the following filters, all optional and all combinable with one another. Every one of them matches by exact, case-sensitive string equality against the row's stored value, and a row whose value for the filtered field is null never matches — so `?supersedes_registration_id=` cannot be used to find first-time registrations, and `?partner_type=distributor` finds nothing. A filter value that is not a legal value of the field is not a request-shape failure and never yields a 422: it simply matches no row, so the response is a well-formed empty page with `total_count` 0 and `has_more` false. A query parameter this section does not declare for the endpoint is ignored outright. The one non-string filter, `verified` on a claim's proof documents, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case, and any other value is ignored as though the parameter had not been supplied at all rather than rejected. Partners may be filtered by `tier`, `tier_status` and `partner_type`. Certification records may be filtered by `partner_id`, `status` and `track`; the per-partner certification list accepts `status`. Accounts may be filtered by `region_code`, `coverage_state` and `protected_partner_id` — the last of which is how a caller finds every account a given partner currently protects. Account mapping agreements may be filtered by `partner_id`, `account_id`, `mapping_status` and `product_line`. Deal registrations may be filtered by `partner_id`, `account_id`, `status`, `product_line` and `supersedes_registration_id`, the last being the reverse-reachability filter for the entity's self-referential foreign key; the per-account registration list accepts `status` and `product_line`. Conflict cases may be filtered by `account_id`, `subject_registration_id`, `counter_registration_id`, `status` and `conflict_type`; the per-account case list accepts `status`. Special pricing requests may be filtered by `registration_id` and `status`, and the per-registration list accepts `status`. Fund periods may be filtered by `partner_id` and `status`. Claims may be filtered by `allocation_id`, `validation_state`, `settlement_state` and `activity_type`; the per-period claim list accepts `validation_state` and `settlement_state`. A claim's proof documents may be filtered by `verified`. Orders may be filtered by `partner_id`, `registration_id`, `status` and `order_type`, and the per-registration order list accepts `status`. Performance reviews may be filtered by `partner_id` and `status`.

Because conflict cases are only ever created by cascade and never by a client request, the case list and the per-account case list are the only channels through which a partner or a direct sales representative discovers that a case exists.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrolling a Partner, Earning Credits and Unlocking the Portal

Channel operations enrols a partner company by supplying its `company_name` — a non-empty string of at most 160 characters — and its `partner_type`, which is either `DISTRIBUTOR` or `RESELLER`. The distinction is not cosmetic: only a `DISTRIBUTOR` may later be given inventory orders, while a `RESELLER` may only be given orders that ship to the end customer. The enrolling analyst may also supply `marketing_commitment_cents`, the annual marketing spend the partner contractually commits to, which must not be negative and defaults to 0 when omitted. A newly enrolled partner starts at `tier` `REGISTERED` with `tier_status` `COMPLIANT` and `standing_reason` null, with `portal_entitlement_level` `BASIC`, and with `cert_credit_total`, `trailing_revenue_cents`, `marketing_qualified_spend_cents` and `open_conflict_count` all at 0. Because a commitment of 0 makes the partner vacuously compliant on marketing, `marketing_attainment_pct` reads exactly 1.0000 on such a partner.

The partner's contractual record can later be amended by channel operations, which may revise `company_name`, `marketing_commitment_cents`, or both. Amending the commitment does not merely store a number: `marketing_attainment_pct` is immediately recomputed against the new denominator, `portal_entitlement_level` is recomputed from the resulting standing, and the partner's `version` advances by 1. Both fields are optional, and the amendment is accepted — and the `version` advanced by exactly 1 — even when it supplies neither of them or supplies values identical to the stored ones, so an amendment carrying an empty body is a well-formed no-op write rather than an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A partner's `tier`, by contrast, is never written by this amendment or by any other operation except an approving performance-review decision, and `tier_status` is only ever written by the standing rules described later in this document.

**Enablement credits.** Each certification a partner's engineers earn is recorded by channel operations against that partner, carrying the engineer's `engineer_name` (non-empty), the `track` earned, and the two instants `issued_at` and `expires_at`, where `expires_at` must be strictly later than `issued_at`. The `track` is one of `SALES_PRO`, `TECH_ASSOCIATE` or `TECH_EXPERT`, and it fixes the record's `credit_weight` at creation and forever after: a `SALES_PRO` record is worth 1 credit, a `TECH_ASSOCIATE` record 2 credits, and a `TECH_EXPERT` record 4. Every new record starts with `status` `ACTIVE`; the only path to `status` `EXPIRED` is the certification sweep described in §3.9, and a record in `ACTIVE` is the only kind that counts for anything.

Recording a certification triggers a full recomputation of the partner's `cert_credit_total`, which is the sum of `credit_weight` across every certification record belonging to that partner whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Records in `EXPIRED` contribute nothing. This rollup is always a complete recomputation from the surviving rows rather than an increment applied to the stored figure, which is what makes the sweep safe to run repeatedly. The recomputation is followed by a recomputation of `portal_entitlement_level`, and the partner's `version` advances by 1.

**Portal entitlement.** The portal ladder has four rungs in ascending order: `BASIC`, `STANDARD`, `PREMIUM`, `ELITE`. A partner's starting rung is read straight off its `tier` — a `REGISTERED` partner starts at the first rung, `SILVER` at the second, `GOLD` at the third and `PLATINUM` at the fourth. If the partner's `tier_status` is `SUSPENDED`, entitlement short-circuits to `BASIC` immediately and no further reasoning is performed. Otherwise the service counts demotions: one demotion if `tier_status` is `PROBATION`, and one further demotion if `cert_credit_total` has fallen below the certification floor for the partner's tier. That floor is 0 credits for `REGISTERED`, 2 for `SILVER`, 6 for `GOLD` and 12 for `PLATINUM`. Demotions accumulate, and the resulting rung is the starting rung stepped down by the number of demotions, never falling below the first rung. A `GOLD` partner on probation therefore reads `STANDARD`; a `GOLD` partner on probation that has also let its credits lapse below 6 reads `BASIC`; and a `REGISTERED` partner on probation stays `BASIC` because the ladder has no lower rung. Entitlement is a pure function of the partner's own `tier`, `tier_status` and `cert_credit_total`, with no cross-entity scan, and it is recomputed every time any one of those three changes.

**Registration eligibility.** Credits are also the gate on staking a deal claim at all. A partner must hold at least 2 enablement credits before any registration of theirs can be submitted. A registration whose `product_line` is `PLATFORM_SUITE` demands more: at least 6 credits, **and** at least one certification record in `ACTIVE` whose `track` is `TECH_EXPERT`. Failing either form of the requirement is `ERR_CERTIFICATION_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET`. Because a single `TECH_EXPERT` record is worth 4 credits, a partner with one expert and one associate clears the general minimum and the platform-suite credit total alike.

**Marketing attainment.** The partner's `marketing_qualified_spend_cents` is the sum of `spend_amount_cents` over every proof-of-performance document whose `verified` flag is true, whose parent claim currently has `validation_state` `VALIDATED`, and whose parent claim draws on a fund period belonging to that partner. Verified evidence hanging off a claim that is merely `SUBMITTED`, still `PROOF_REQUESTED`, or already `REJECTED` contributes nothing, so the very same document can enter and leave the numerator as its claim is validated and later clawed back. From that figure, `marketing_attainment_pct` is the smaller of 1.0 and the qualified spend divided by `marketing_commitment_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places — except that a partner whose `marketing_commitment_cents` is 0 reads exactly 1.0000, since there is nothing to fall short of. A partner whose attainment sits at or above 0.75 is compliant on the marketing dimension; falling below that fraction is what puts an otherwise healthy partner on probation with `standing_reason` `MARKETING_ATTAINMENT_SHORTFALL`, and recovering to at or above it is what clears exactly that probation. Clearing a probation that way rewrites `tier_status` back to `COMPLIANT` but leaves `standing_reason` holding the value that put the partner there, so a partner can legitimately read `COMPLIANT` alongside a non-null `standing_reason`; an approving performance-review decision is the only writer in the service that ever returns `standing_reason` to null.

### 3.2. The Coverage Map: Accounts, Direct Pursuit and Mapping Agreements

An end-customer account enters the coverage map with an `account_name` (non-empty, at most 160 characters) and a `region_code` of `NA`, `EMEA` or `APAC`. It begins life in `coverage_state` `OPEN_CHANNEL`, with `protected_until`, `protected_partner_id`, `protected_product_line`, `direct_pursuit_owner_id` and `direct_pursuit_declared_at` all null. Coverage is a four-state machine: `OPEN_CHANNEL`, `CHANNEL_PROTECTED`, `NAMED_DIRECT`, and the terminal `DIRECT_LOCKED`. Nothing ever leaves `DIRECT_LOCKED`, and no registration may be submitted against an account in that state.

A `DirectSalesRep` declares direct pursuit of an account, optionally attaching a `pursuit_note`, which must be a string when supplied but is allowed to be empty and carries no length limit, and which the service accepts without keeping: an `Account` has no field for it, so it is neither stored nor echoed in any response. What that declaration means depends entirely on the account's current coverage, and only `OPEN_CHANNEL` and `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` admit it at all: declaring pursuit of an account already in `NAMED_DIRECT`, or of one in the terminal `DIRECT_LOCKED`, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Whichever of the two admissible branches applies, `direct_pursuit_owner_id` and `direct_pursuit_declared_at` are written first and unconditionally, overwriting any values they already held. Declaring pursuit on an account in `OPEN_CHANNEL` moves it `OPEN_CHANNEL → NAMED_DIRECT`, writing the representative's opaque identifier into `direct_pursuit_owner_id` and the current logical instant into `direct_pursuit_declared_at`; the account's `version` advances by 1. Declaring pursuit on an account in `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` leaves the coverage state exactly where it is — the transition is `CHANNEL_PROTECTED → CHANNEL_PROTECTED` — but it does not leave the world where it is, because it opens channel conflict against the protecting partner. Cascade D runs three hops. First, a conflict case is raised with `conflict_type` `PARTNER_VS_DIRECT`, `subject_registration_id` set to the protecting registration (the registration in `APPROVED` whose `partner_id` equals the account's `protected_partner_id` and whose `product_line` equals the account's `protected_product_line` — the earliest such registration by `created_at`, with insertion order breaking a tie, where more than one somehow qualifies), `counter_registration_id` left null, `account_id` set to this account, `detected_at` set to the current logical instant, `status` `OPEN`, `escalation_level` 0, and all three recommendation fields computed as described in §3.5. Second, because that case now exists, the protecting registration's `closure_reason` is set to `DIRECT_PURSUIT_CONTESTED` while its `status` remains `APPROVED` and its exclusivity window remains intact until the case resolves; that registration's `version` advances by 1. Third, because the case was created in `OPEN`, the protecting partner's `open_conflict_count` is recomputed as the number of conflict cases whose subject registration belongs to that partner and whose `status` is `OPEN`, `ESCALATED_TIER1` or `ESCALATED_TIER2`; when the recomputed count reaches 2 and the partner's `tier_status` is `COMPLIANT`, the partner is moved to `PROBATION` with `standing_reason` `OPEN_CONFLICT_THRESHOLD` and its `portal_entitlement_level` is recomputed one ladder step down. That third hop is a recomputation of derived fields, so it advances the protecting partner's `version` and `updated_at` only when one of those recomputed figures actually moves, exactly as §2.4 qualifies. Cascade D may also find no protecting registration at all — an account still reading `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` whose protector has since been marked `CONVERTED` is the ordinary way that happens — and in that case all three hops are skipped: no conflict case is created, no registration's `closure_reason` is written, and no partner's standing is recomputed, while the declaration itself still succeeds and still records the pursuit fields. The account's own `version` advances by 1 in either branch, and in the protected branch it advances by exactly 1 whether or not cascade D found anything to contest.

**Account mapping agreements.** A mapping agreement is the negotiated, bilateral cousin of a deal registration: it names a `partner_id` and an `account_id`, a `product_line` of `PLATFORM_SUITE`, `ANALYTICS_ADDON` or `SUPPORT_SERVICES`, an `exclusivity_scope` of either `FULL` or `PRODUCT_LINE`, and a closed date window running from `effective_from` through `effective_until` inclusive, where `effective_until` must be strictly later than `effective_from`. A scope of `FULL` blocks every product line on the account; a scope of `PRODUCT_LINE` blocks only the named `product_line`. Channel operations proposes the agreement, and the proposal must carry `expected_account_version` as described in §2.7. A fresh proposal is stored with `mapping_status` `PROPOSED`, `counter_round` 0, `activated_at` null and `closure_reason` null, and the account's `version` advances by 1.

Before the proposed window is tested against anything, a mapping sweep runs: every agreement whose `mapping_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_until` lies strictly before the current logical instant moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` with `closure_reason` `WINDOW_ELAPSED` and its `version` advanced by 1. Only then is the candidate window tested.

**The window test.** The candidate window is the closed interval from `effective_from` through `effective_until`. It is tested against every other agreement on the same account whose `mapping_status` is `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`, restricted to those whose scope overlaps the candidate's — which means either agreement having `exclusivity_scope` `FULL`, or both naming the same `product_line`. Agreements in `TERMINATED` are excluded from the test entirely. For each incumbent so selected, the blocked interval runs from that incumbent's `effective_from` through its `effective_until` extended by a cooldown of fourteen days, and the candidate conflicts with it unless the candidate starts strictly after the blocked interval ends or the candidate ends strictly before the blocked interval starts. Every endpoint of that comparison is inclusive, which produces four consequences worth stating outright: a candidate starting exactly at the end of a blocked interval conflicts; a candidate starting one millisecond later is allowed; a candidate ending exactly at an incumbent's `effective_from` conflicts; and a candidate ending one millisecond before that instant is allowed, because there is no cooldown buffer in front of an incumbent's start. A terminated agreement contributes no cooldown at all, while an expired one contributes its full fourteen days. Any conflict is `ERR_MAPPING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**The negotiation cycle.** An agreement is advanced by a response action, which is one of `COUNTER`, `ACCEPT` or `TERMINATE`. A `COUNTER` re-proposes the window and therefore requires both `effective_from` and `effective_until` to be present and to satisfy the end-after-start rule; the countered window replaces the stored one, `counter_round` advances by 1, and the new window is re-tested by the window test above against agreements in `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACTIVE`. The cycle alternates sides: the partner counters an agreement sitting in `PROPOSED`, moving it `PROPOSED → COUNTERED`, and the vendor's channel operations counters an agreement sitting in `COUNTERED`, moving it `COUNTERED → PROPOSED`. Both sides share the same `counter_round` counter, and at most two counters may ever be applied — a third is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and writes nothing.

An `ACCEPT` activates the stored window without changing it. The partner accepts from `PROPOSED` and channel operations accepts from `COUNTERED`; either way the agreement moves to `ACTIVE`, `activated_at` is set to the current logical instant, and the `version` advances by 1. Acceptance re-runs the window test, but narrowed: at acceptance the stored window is checked only against agreements already in `ACTIVE`. Acceptance is also one of the four operations frozen by an overdue compliance audit, so a partner with an overdue finding cannot activate an agreement until it remediates. That freeze is evaluated against the agreement's own `partner_id` whichever authorised role acts, so channel operations accepting from `COUNTERED` on behalf of a partner carrying an overdue finding is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_REMEDIATION_OVERDUE` exactly as the partner itself would be.

A `TERMINATE` abandons the agreement and requires a non-empty `terminated_reason`, which is recorded as the agreement's `closure_reason`. Channel operations may terminate an agreement from `ACTIVE` or from `PROPOSED`, and no other role may terminate at all: a `Partner` supplying `TERMINATE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, because the endpoint authorises the role but not this action, and the same error answers a termination attempted from `COUNTERED`. Both permitted paths land in the terminal `TERMINATED` state, and from that moment the abandoned window stops contributing any cooldown buffer to future window tests. The other terminal state, `EXPIRED`, is reached only by the sweep, carries `closure_reason` `WINDOW_ELAPSED`, and — unlike termination — keeps contributing its cooldown.

Live agreements reach back into the registration flow. When a partner submits a deal registration, the service also checks for an agreement in `ACTIVE` belonging to a **different** partner, on the same account, whose scope covers the registration's `product_line` (either the agreement's `exclusivity_scope` is `FULL` or its `product_line` matches), and whose window contains the current logical instant inclusively at both ends. Such an agreement blocks the submission with `ERR_MAPPING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

### 3.3. Staking a Claim: Drafting, Submission and the Revision Loop

A deal registration is the partner's claim on a specific opportunity. The partner drafts it by naming its own `partner_id`, the `account_id` being pursued, the `product_line` being registered (`PLATFORM_SUITE`, `ANALYTICS_ADDON` or `SUPPORT_SERVICES`), the forecast value `expected_amount_cents`, and the forecast close instant `expected_close_date`. The program will not accept a claim on trivial business: `expected_amount_cents` must be at least 100000 cents, which is the one-thousand-dollar minimum registration size. A renewal names the predecessor it intends to replace in `supersedes_registration_id`; a first-time registration leaves that field null.

Drafting validates almost nothing about the world. A new registration is stored with `status` `DRAFT`, `revision_round` 0, `registration_margin_uplift_pct` 0.0, and `submitted_at`, `approved_at`, `exclusivity_start_at`, `exclusivity_end_at`, `protection_days` and `closure_reason` all null. The single referential check applied at draft time is on the renewal pointer: when `supersedes_registration_id` is supplied it must name a registration of the same `partner_id`, the same `account_id` and the same `product_line`, whose `status` is `APPROVED`, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`. The full lifecycle vocabulary is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `CONFLICT_HELD`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `CONVERTED`, of which the last four are terminal.

**Submission is where the coverage map is consulted.** Submitting moves the registration `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and only the owning partner may do it. Before any window is inspected, an expiry sweep runs — and it runs first, deliberately, so that a claim whose protection has already elapsed cannot block a newcomer. The sweep visits every registration on the same account-and-product-line pair whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `exclusivity_end_at` is at or before the current logical instant, moves each `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, sets its `closure_reason` to `WINDOW_ELAPSED`, sets its `registration_margin_uplift_pct` to 0.02, and advances its `version` by 1. A second hop follows for any swept registration that was the account's protector: when the account's `coverage_state` is `CHANNEL_PROTECTED`, its `protected_partner_id` matches the swept registration's `partner_id`, its `protected_product_line` matches the swept registration's `product_line`, and its `protected_until` is at or before the current logical instant, the account moves `CHANNEL_PROTECTED → OPEN_CHANNEL` with `protected_until`, `protected_partner_id` and `protected_product_line` all cleared to null, and the account's `version` advances by 1. This sweep is the only producer of a registration in `EXPIRED` and the only path an account has back to `OPEN_CHANNEL`; because it reads nothing but the logical clock, it is fully deterministic.

With the sweep complete, submission then requires all of the following, in the order §2.8 fixes. The partner's `tier_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`. The partner must have no performance review sitting in `FINDINGS_OPEN` whose `remediation_due_at` has already been reached, or `ERR_AUDIT_REMEDIATION_OVERDUE`. The partner's certification credits must clear the thresholds of §3.1, or `ERR_CERTIFICATION_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET`. The account must not be in `DIRECT_LOCKED`, and no **other** registration on the same account-and-product-line pair may be in `APPROVED` holding a live exclusivity window at the current logical instant — with one carve-out: an incumbent is ignored when it is precisely the registration this one supersedes and it belongs to the same partner. Either failure is `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`. No other registration of the **same** partner on the same account and product line may be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `CONFLICT_HELD`, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`. And no other partner's live active mapping agreement may cover the account and product line, per §3.2, or `ERR_MAPPING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. On success, `submitted_at` is set to the current logical instant and the registration's `version` advances by 1.

**The revision loop.** The review step carries an `action` that is either `START_REVIEW` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`; any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. With `START_REVIEW`, channel operations picks up a submitted claim and starts its assessment, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; the only precondition is that the registration is in `SUBMITTED`, and the only effect is a `version` bump. With `REQUEST_CHANGES` the analyst sends the deal back to the partner instead of escalating it to a decision, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED`. That path requires a `reviewer_note` that is present and non-empty — an empty note is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the note is recorded as the registration's `closure_reason`.

The partner then resubmits, moving the registration `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. Resubmission is not a shortcut: it re-runs the expiry sweep and re-evaluates every precondition of the first submission against the state of the world as it now stands, so a claim that was clear on its first pass can be rejected on its second. It additionally requires `revision_round` to be strictly below 2; the third resubmission attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. On success `revision_round` advances by 1, `submitted_at` is rewritten to the current logical instant, and the `version` advances by 1. The `closure_reason` carrying the analyst's `reviewer_note` is deliberately left in place: neither resubmission nor the approval that may follow it clears the field, so a registration can sit in `SUBMITTED` or even in `APPROVED` still showing the note of the round that sent it back. That rewriting has a consequence the partner should understand: `submitted_at` is the chronological key by which competing claims are ranked in §3.5, so every trip through the revision loop pushes the partner's claim later in the queue. A partner that registered first but revised twice can lose a conflict to a rival that never revised.

### 3.4. Approval, the Exclusivity Window and the Protection Cascade

The channel director is the sole approval authority on a registration under review, and the decision carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. Rejection is the simpler branch: it requires a present and non-empty `reject_reason` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), moves the registration `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, records the supplied reason as `closure_reason`, resets `registration_margin_uplift_pct` to 0.0, and advances the `version` by 1.

Approval moves the registration `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and is gated on four things: the registration is in `UNDER_REVIEW`; the partner's `tier_status` is not `SUSPENDED` (otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`); no other registration on the same account-and-product-line pair is in `APPROVED` holding a live exclusivity window at the current logical instant, unless that incumbent is precisely the registration this one supersedes (otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`); and, when `supersedes_registration_id` is set, the predecessor belongs to the same partner, matches on account and product line, and is itself in `APPROVED` (otherwise `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`, or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` where the pointer is structurally unusable).

**The window itself.** On approval, `approved_at` is set to the current logical instant. The window length `protection_days` is read from the approving partner's `tier` as it stands at that moment: 30 days for `REGISTERED`, 60 for `SILVER`, 90 for `GOLD`, and 120 for `PLATINUM`. `exclusivity_start_at` is the current logical instant — the same instant as `approved_at` — and `exclusivity_end_at` is that instant plus `protection_days` days, each day an exact twenty-four hours. The window is half-open as §2.6 describes. `registration_margin_uplift_pct` is set to 0.06, the full in-window uplift. Registrations on a different `product_line` of the same account never interact with this window, and windows are never merged: a renewal replaces the incumbent's window rather than extending it.

**Cascade A** then runs four hops deep from that single approval. Hop one: because the registration became `APPROVED`, the account's `coverage_state` is set to `CHANNEL_PROTECTED`, its `protected_until` to the new registration's `exclusivity_end_at`, its `protected_partner_id` to the registration's `partner_id` and its `protected_product_line` to the registration's `product_line`, with the account's `version` advanced by 1. Hop two: because the account is now protected for that account-and-product-line pair, every **other** registration on the same pair whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` moves to `CONFLICT_HELD` with `closure_reason` `ACCOUNT_PROTECTED_BY_OTHER_PARTNER` and its `version` advanced by 1 — whatever partner it belongs to, the approving partner's own competing claims included, despite what the closure reason's name suggests, and taken in ascending `created_at` order with insertion order breaking a tie. Hop three: because each of those registrations entered `CONFLICT_HELD`, a conflict case is created for each, with `conflict_type` `PARTNER_VS_PARTNER`, `subject_registration_id` set to the held registration, `counter_registration_id` set to the registration just approved, `account_id` set to the shared account, `detected_at` set to the current logical instant, `status` `OPEN`, `escalation_level` 0, `resolution_note` null, `resolved_by_user_id` null, and the three recommendation fields computed per §3.5. Hop four: because each of those cases was created in `OPEN`, the held registration's partner has `open_conflict_count` recomputed, and when the recomputed count reaches 2 while that partner's `tier_status` is `COMPLIANT`, the partner moves to `PROBATION` with `standing_reason` `OPEN_CONFLICT_THRESHOLD` and `portal_entitlement_level` recomputed — one ladder step down, so a `GOLD` partner drops to `STANDARD` while a `REGISTERED` partner stays at `BASIC`. Being a recomputation of derived fields, that hop advances the held partner's `version` and `updated_at` only when one of the recomputed figures actually moves, as §2.4 qualifies. Hops two, three and four are applied registration by registration rather than as three separate phases: each held registration is flipped, its case minted and its partner's standing recomputed before the next held registration is visited at all. Where two held registrations belong to the same partner, that partner is therefore visited twice — picking up one new open case on the first visit and the other on the second, with the probation applying on whichever visit first carries the count to 2 — so its `version` advances once for each of those two visits, both of which moved a figure, rather than once for the cascade as a whole. And where no registration on the pair sits in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` at all, hops two through four write nothing whatsoever: no case exists, no partner is visited, and hop one is the approval's only cascade effect.

A renewal approval carries one extra effect. When `supersedes_registration_id` is set, the predecessor moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED` with `closure_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_RENEWAL` and its `version` advanced by 1. The predecessor is deliberately exempt from hop two, because hop two only touches registrations in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and the predecessor is in none of those. The approved registration's own `version` advances by 1 in every branch.

Three states feed `CONFLICT_HELD`, and all three behave identically: `SUBMITTED → CONFLICT_HELD`, `UNDER_REVIEW → CONFLICT_HELD` and `CHANGES_REQUESTED → CONFLICT_HELD`. In each case the trigger is a system cascade — either a competing registration on the same account-and-product-line pair reaching `APPROVED`, or a direct sales representative declaring pursuit on a protected account — the registration's `closure_reason` is set to the cascade's reason, a conflict case is created as described above, and the `version` advances by 1. No client can drive a registration into `CONFLICT_HELD` directly.

**Conversion closes the loop.** Once at least one order priced against an approved registration has reached `INVOICED` or `CREDITED`, channel operations marks the registration `APPROVED → CONVERTED` with `closure_reason` `CONVERTED` and the `version` advanced by 1. Attempting conversion while no such order exists is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, as is attempting it from any state other than `APPROVED`.

### 3.5. Channel Conflict: Detection, Advice, Escalation and Resolution

A conflict case is never created by a client. It exists only as the product of cascade A hop three or cascade D hop one, which is why the case list and the per-account case list of §2.10 are the only ways a partner or a direct sales representative learns that one has been raised. Every case carries the contested `account_id` copied from its subject registration, the `subject_registration_id` whose claim is contested (the case is always raised on behalf of that registration's partner), a `counter_registration_id` naming the competing registration — which is null exactly when `conflict_type` is `PARTNER_VS_DIRECT` — a `conflict_type` of either `PARTNER_VS_PARTNER` or `PARTNER_VS_DIRECT`, a `detected_at` instant, and a `status` drawn from `OPEN`, `ESCALATED_TIER1`, `ESCALATED_TIER2`, `RESOLVED_PARTNER`, `RESOLVED_DIRECT` and `RESOLVED_SPLIT`, of which the last three are terminal. `escalation_level` is 0 while the case is `OPEN`, 1 at `ESCALATED_TIER1` and 2 at `ESCALATED_TIER2`.

**Two doctrines, both computed.** At detection the service computes an advisory claimant recommendation under **two competing doctrines** and stores both, unconditionally, on every case. The recommendation vocabulary is `GRANT_SUBJECT`, `GRANT_COUNTER`, `RELEASE_TO_DIRECT` and `DISMISS`. Every window and coverage comparison in both doctrines is evaluated at `detected_at`.

The first doctrine, recorded in `recommended_resolution_registration_first`, is the channel's registration-order rule: whoever staked a claim earliest should win. The service assembles a list of claimants. The subject registration always contributes a claimant labelled `GRANT_SUBJECT` whose claim instant is the subject's `submitted_at` and whose tiebreaker is the subject's `created_at`. When `counter_registration_id` is not null, the competing registration contributes a claimant labelled `GRANT_COUNTER` whose claim instant is the counter registration's `submitted_at` and whose tiebreaker is the counter registration's `created_at`. When the account's `direct_pursuit_owner_id` is not null, direct sales contributes a claimant labelled `RELEASE_TO_DIRECT` whose claim instant is the account's `direct_pursuit_declared_at` and whose tiebreaker is the account's `created_at`. The claimant with the earliest claim instant wins; if two claim instants are identical the earlier tiebreaker instant wins; identifiers are never used to break a tie. When two claimants tie on the claim instant and on the tiebreaker alike — a routine occurrence, because the logical clock only moves when it is set — the claimant assembled earlier wins, and they are assembled in the fixed order `GRANT_SUBJECT`, then `GRANT_COUNTER`, then `RELEASE_TO_DIRECT`. Because every trip through the revision loop rewrites `submitted_at`, a partner that registered first but revised its deal can lose under this doctrine.

The second doctrine, recorded in `recommended_resolution_coverage_first`, is the account-coverage rule: whoever holds the live protection should win, regardless of who claimed first. It is evaluated as an ordered ladder, first match winning. If the account's `coverage_state` is `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` and `detected_at` falls strictly before the account's `protected_until`, then the recommendation is `GRANT_SUBJECT` when the account's `protected_partner_id` equals the subject registration's `partner_id`, or `GRANT_COUNTER` when a counter registration exists and the account's `protected_partner_id` equals that counter registration's `partner_id`, or `DISMISS` when the protection belongs to neither party. Otherwise, if the account's `direct_pursuit_owner_id` is not null, the recommendation is `RELEASE_TO_DIRECT`. Otherwise, if the subject registration's `status` is `APPROVED`, the recommendation is `GRANT_SUBJECT`. Otherwise the recommendation is `DISMISS`.

Which of the two stored figures is authoritative is recorded in `applied_variant`, whose value is `REGISTRATION_FIRST` on a `PARTNER_VS_PARTNER` case and `COVERAGE_FIRST` on a `PARTNER_VS_DIRECT` case, and `recommended_resolution` is a copy of whichever of the two doctrine fields `applied_variant` names. On the same case data the two doctrines can name different winners, which is exactly why both are exposed side by side. All of it is advisory: the recommendation moves no state by itself, and the channel director is free to resolve against it.

**Escalation.** The ladder has two rungs and different owners. The subject registration's partner escalates a case sitting in `OPEN`, moving it `OPEN → ESCALATED_TIER1` and setting `escalation_level` to 1; the acting partner's `X-User-ID` must equal the subject registration's `partner_id`, or `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. A direct sales representative escalates a case sitting in `ESCALATED_TIER1`, moving it `ESCALATED_TIER1 → ESCALATED_TIER2` and setting `escalation_level` to 2. Either escalation requires an `escalation_note` — which must be present and must be a string, but which, unlike the free-text notes §2.8 lists at priority 5, is allowed to be empty or blank — and records it as the case's `resolution_note`, overwriting whatever that field held, and advances the case's `version` by 1. Escalating a case whose `escalation_level` is already 2 is rejected with `ERR_CONFLICT_ESCALATION_LIMIT` and writes nothing whatsoever. A case already sitting in one of the three terminal statuses cannot be escalated from any rung either, and because the state precondition sits at priority 9 while the ladder cap sits at 26, such a case is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` even when its `escalation_level` had already reached 2.

**Resolution.** Only the channel director resolves a case, and the vocabulary supplied is a `resolution` of `PARTNER`, `DIRECT` or `SPLIT` — deliberately *not* the advisory recommendation vocabulary above. A present and non-empty `resolution_note` is required on every resolution, or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A case may be resolved from `OPEN`, from `ESCALATED_TIER1` or from `ESCALATED_TIER2`; the outcome is identical whichever rung the case sat on. Every resolution records the acting director's opaque identifier as `resolved_by_user_id`, advances the case's `version` by 1, and finishes by recomputing the subject registration's partner's `open_conflict_count`: when the recomputed count has fallen below 2 while that partner's `tier_status` is `PROBATION` with `standing_reason` `OPEN_CONFLICT_THRESHOLD`, the partner returns to `COMPLIANT` and its `portal_entitlement_level` is recomputed. `standing_reason` is not cleared by that return — it keeps reading `OPEN_CONFLICT_THRESHOLD` on a partner now back in `COMPLIANT` — and because the whole hop is a recomputation of derived fields, it advances the partner's `version` and `updated_at` only when one of those recomputed figures actually moves, exactly as §2.4 qualifies.

Resolving in favour of the partner drives the case to `RESOLVED_PARTNER`, and what happens to the subject registration depends on the conflict's type. On a `PARTNER_VS_PARTNER` case the subject registration moves `CONFLICT_HELD → SUBMITTED` so that channel operations can review it again; its `closure_reason` is cleared to null and — importantly — its `submitted_at` is **not** rewritten, so the original chronological key survives resolution. On a `PARTNER_VS_DIRECT` case the subject registration keeps `status` `APPROVED`, its `closure_reason` is cleared to null, and the account's `direct_pursuit_owner_id` is cleared to null with the account's `version` advanced by 1; the account's `coverage_state` and its `direct_pursuit_declared_at` are both left exactly as they stood, so the account stays `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` still carrying the instant the contested pursuit was declared. In both branches the subject registration's `version` advances by 1.

Resolving in favour of direct sales drives the case to `RESOLVED_DIRECT` and is the harshest outcome. The subject registration transitions to `REJECTED` — from `CONFLICT_HELD` on a `PARTNER_VS_PARTNER` case, from `APPROVED` on a `PARTNER_VS_DIRECT` case — with `closure_reason` `RESOLVED_DIRECT` and `registration_margin_uplift_pct` reset to 0.0, and its `version` advanced by 1. The account moves to the terminal `DIRECT_LOCKED` from whatever coverage state it was in — `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` or `NAMED_DIRECT` ordinarily, but `OPEN_CHANNEL` just as well where the expiry sweep of §3.3 had already released it — with `protected_until`, `protected_partner_id` and `protected_product_line` all cleared to null, `direct_pursuit_owner_id` deliberately left as it stands, and its `version` advanced by 1.

Resolving by splitting the account drives the case to `RESOLVED_SPLIT` and is available only on a `PARTNER_VS_PARTNER` case; a direct-sales conflict cannot be split, and attempting it is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The subject registration moves `CONFLICT_HELD → APPROVED`, but on reduced terms: `approved_at` is set to the current logical instant, `protection_days` is the tier's normal window length halved and rounded down to a whole number of days — 15 days for `REGISTERED`, 30 for `SILVER`, 45 for `GOLD`, 60 for `PLATINUM` — `exclusivity_start_at` is the current logical instant, `exclusivity_end_at` is that instant plus the halved `protection_days`, and `registration_margin_uplift_pct` is set to 0.02 rather than 0.06, because a split award never earns the full uplift. The account keeps `coverage_state` `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` and its `protected_until` becomes the later of its existing value and the split registration's new `exclusivity_end_at` — the new instant outright where the account carried no `protected_until` at all — with the account's `version` advanced by 1 whether or not that instant actually moved. Its `protected_partner_id` and `protected_product_line` are left pointing wherever they already pointed, because a split award does not move the account's protection onto the split registration's partner; and the subject registration's `closure_reason` is likewise left as it stands rather than cleared, so an `ACCOUNT_PROTECTED_BY_OTHER_PARTNER` reason survives on a registration now back in `APPROVED`. The subject registration's `version` advances by 1 as well.

There is one other way an account already in `CHANNEL_PROTECTED` stays there while its protection instant moves: a renewal approval that supersedes the current protector overwrites `protected_until` with the successor registration's `exclusivity_end_at`, and the account's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.6. Pricing an Order: Margin Resolution, Special Pricing and Ship-and-Debit

Channel operations prices a partner order by naming the buying `partner_id`, an `order_type` of either `STOCKING` (distributor inventory purchase) or `DROP_SHIP` (shipped to the end customer), the vendor list value `list_amount_cents` (which must be strictly greater than 0), and optionally the `registration_id` of the deal registration the order is priced against — left null for unregistered business. That registration need only exist: it is never cross-checked against the buying `partner_id`, so an order may legitimately name another partner's registration and will draw whatever uplift that registration's status and window imply. A `STOCKING` order is accepted only for a partner whose `partner_type` is `DISTRIBUTOR`; giving a `RESELLER` a stocking order is `ERR_PARTNER_TYPE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Pricing is also blocked for a suspended partner. A new order is stored with `status` `PENDING`, `invoiced_at` null, `credit_memo_cents` 0 and `credit_reason` null, and its entire margin structure is resolved and frozen at that moment. Later tier changes never re-derive it.

**The six pricing steps are strictly ordered, and the order is what makes the calculation well defined.** The volume band is a function of the preliminary net amount, and the final margin is a function of the band, so re-deriving the band from the final net would produce a different invoice. Implementations must follow the sequence exactly.

*Step one — the preliminary margin.* Start from the base margin for the partner's `tier` as it stands at creation: 0.10 for `REGISTERED`, 0.14 for `SILVER`, 0.18 for `GOLD`, 0.22 for `PLATINUM`. To that, add three uplifts. The **registration uplift** is 0.06 when the linked registration is in `APPROVED` and the current logical instant falls at or after its `exclusivity_start_at` and strictly before its `exclusivity_end_at`; it is 0.02 when a registration is linked and its `status` is `APPROVED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CONVERTED` but the in-window test above did not hold; and it is 0.00 when no registration is linked, when the linked registration is `REJECTED`, or when the linked registration is still in flight. The **special pricing uplift** is the `approved_extra_margin_pct` of the newest special pricing request on that same registration — newest by `created_at`, with a tie on `created_at` broken by insertion order so that the most recently created row wins — whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` and whose `expires_at` is strictly later than the current logical instant; if no such request exists the uplift is 0.00. The **stocking allowance** is 0.02 when the partner's `partner_type` is `DISTRIBUTOR` and the `order_type` is `STOCKING`, and 0.00 otherwise. The preliminary margin is the base plus all three uplifts, held as an exact fraction.

*Step two — the preliminary net.* Multiply `list_amount_cents` by one minus the preliminary margin and round half-up to a whole number of cents. This figure exists only to select the band; it is never stored.

*Step three — the volume band.* Read `volume_band` off the preliminary net alone: `HIGH` when the preliminary net is at least 20000000 cents (two hundred thousand dollars), `MID` when it is at least 5000000 cents (fifty thousand dollars) but below the high threshold, and `NONE` otherwise. The band contributes a volume uplift of 0.03 for `HIGH`, 0.015 for `MID`, and 0.0 for `NONE`.

*Step four — the final margin.* Add the volume uplift to the preliminary margin, raise the result to the program margin floor of 0.05 if it fell below it, then cap it at the ceiling for the partner's `tier` — 0.18 for `REGISTERED`, 0.24 for `SILVER`, 0.30 for `GOLD`, 0.36 for `PLATINUM` — and round the outcome half-up to four decimal places. The floor is applied before the ceiling. The result is stored as `margin_pct`.

*Step five — the invoice amount.* Multiply `list_amount_cents` by one minus the stored `margin_pct` and round half-up to a whole number of cents. The result is stored as `invoice_amount_cents`.

*Step six — the margin value.* `margin_value_cents` is `list_amount_cents` minus `invoice_amount_cents`, obtained by subtraction and never by a second multiplication, so that not a single cent is created or destroyed. The band never looks at any other order; the only inputs to the whole pipeline are the order's own `list_amount_cents` and `order_type`, the partner's `tier` and `partner_type` at creation, the linked registration's status and window, and the newest un-expired granted special pricing request on that registration.

**Requesting extra margin.** A partner may ask for additional margin points on a specific approved registration by supplying that `registration_id`, a `requested_extra_margin_pct` strictly greater than 0.0 and at most 0.1000 carried to four decimal places, a non-empty `justification`, and the `expected_registration_version` guard of §2.7. The target registration must be in `APPROVED` with a live exclusivity window at the current logical instant, or the create is rejected with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_APPROVED`; a suspended partner cannot file one at all. The new request is stored with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `approved_extra_margin_pct` null, `decided_by_user_id` null, and an `expires_at` set to the earlier of the registration's `exclusivity_end_at` and the current logical instant plus 30 days — the request confers no uplift at or after that instant. Creating a request also supersedes its predecessors: every other request on the same `registration_id` still sitting in `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` with `approved_extra_margin_pct` set to 0.0 and its `version` advanced by 1, its `decided_by_user_id` staying null because a supersession is not a decision and its `expires_at` staying exactly as it was. The registration's own `version` advances by 1 as a result of the create, which is precisely why the stale-version guard exists: without it a client would silently void a pending request it had never seen.

The channel director decides a submitted request with a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `PARTIAL` or `DENY`, and each branch is gated differently. `APPROVE` moves the request `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, requires the current logical instant to be strictly before `expires_at` (otherwise `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_EXPIRED`), requires that the base margin for the partner's `tier` plus the full in-window registration uplift of 0.06 plus `requested_extra_margin_pct` stays at or below the tier's margin ceiling (otherwise `ERR_MARGIN_CEILING_EXCEEDED`), and sets `approved_extra_margin_pct` equal to `requested_extra_margin_pct`. `PARTIAL` moves the request `SUBMITTED → PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, requires the same strictly-before-`expires_at` condition, requires a supplied `approved_extra_margin_pct` that is present, strictly greater than 0.0 and strictly less than `requested_extra_margin_pct` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), requires that the base margin for the tier plus 0.06 plus that supplied value stays at or below the tier's ceiling (otherwise `ERR_MARGIN_CEILING_EXCEEDED`), and stores the supplied value as `approved_extra_margin_pct`. `DENY` moves the request `SUBMITTED → DENIED` with `approved_extra_margin_pct` set to 0.0; a denial is accepted whenever the request is in `SUBMITTED` and is not gated on the validity window or on any ceiling, since it grants nothing. None of the three branches re-examines the target registration: only the request's own `expires_at` gates a grant, so a request whose registration has meanwhile moved to `SUPERSEDED` or `REJECTED` while that instant still lies ahead is decided exactly as though the registration were untouched, and the ceiling arithmetic always adds the full in-window uplift of 0.06 whatever the registration's status has become. Nor does any branch write the registration: a decision advances only the request's own audit fields. All three branches record the deciding director's opaque identifier as `decided_by_user_id` and advance the request's `version` by 1. `APPROVED`, `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal.

**Invoicing turns an order into revenue.** Channel operations moves a priced order `PENDING → INVOICED`, which requires the order to be in `PENDING` and the partner's `tier_status` not to be `SUSPENDED`. `invoiced_at` is set to the current logical instant, the order's `version` advances by 1, and the partner's `trailing_revenue_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` across that partner's orders whose `status` is `INVOICED` or `CREDITED`; because an invoicing always moves that figure, the partner's own `version` advances by 1 alongside the order's. Nothing else on the partner is touched — an invoicing recomputes revenue alone and never its tier, standing, marketing figures or entitlement. Orders still in `PENDING` are excluded from that rolling figure entirely — they matter only to the provisional scoring variant of §3.9. Because `invoice_amount_cents` is already net of every credit memo applied so far, the rollup must not subtract `credit_memo_cents` a second time.

**Ship-and-debit credits.** When an order turns out to have been priced too high for the deal that closed, channel operations issues a credit memo against it, supplying `credit_memo_cents` and a `credit_reason` — the reason is required and must be a string, but unlike the free-text notes §2.8 lists at priority 5 it is allowed to be empty or blank and carries no length limit. Two checks apply to the credit, and they are evaluated in the priority order of §2.8 — the margin ceiling (20) first, the credit size (21) second — so when a single credit would fail both, the ceiling error is the one returned. The credit must leave the order inside the tier's margin ceiling: the effective margin after the credit — the list amount minus the reduced invoice amount (the order's current `invoice_amount_cents` less the supplied `credit_memo_cents`, a figure that is simply carried through even when a large credit drives it to or below zero), divided by the list amount, rounded half-up to four decimal places — must be at or below the ceiling for the partner's `tier`, or `ERR_MARGIN_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. The credit must then be strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than the order's **current** `invoice_amount_cents`, or `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE`. Because every tier ceiling is well below 1.0000, a credit at or above the current `invoice_amount_cents` drives the effective margin to at least 1.0000 and therefore trips the ceiling check first, which leaves the lower bound — a `credit_memo_cents` of exactly 0 — as the practical way to observe `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE`; a negative `credit_memo_cents` never reaches either check, being already refused by the request-shape check (5) of §2.8. On success the order moves `INVOICED → CREDITED`: `credit_memo_cents` records the credit, `invoice_amount_cents` is reduced by it, `margin_value_cents` is re-derived as `list_amount_cents` minus the new `invoice_amount_cents`, `margin_pct` is re-derived as `margin_value_cents` divided by `list_amount_cents` rounded half-up to four decimal places, `credit_reason` is recorded, `volume_band` alone is left exactly as pricing froze it and is never re-derived from the reduced amount, the partner's `trailing_revenue_cents` is recomputed with the partner's own `version` advancing by 1 as that figure moves, and the order's `version` advances by 1. Further credits are permitted on an order already in `CREDITED`, a self-loop `CREDITED → CREDITED`, in which `credit_memo_cents` accumulates while every other field is recomputed exactly as above; each successive credit must independently satisfy both the size check against the then-current `invoice_amount_cents` and the tier ceiling check on the cumulative effective margin.

**The conservation identity.** At every observable instant, and after order pricing, invoicing and every credit memo alike, four things hold on each order: `list_amount_cents` equals `invoice_amount_cents` plus `margin_value_cents`; `margin_pct` equals `margin_value_cents` divided by `list_amount_cents` rounded half-up to four decimal places; `margin_value_cents` is never below the list amount multiplied by the 0.05 margin floor and rounded half-up to whole cents; and `margin_pct` never exceeds the ceiling for the partner's tier. A credit memo is a pure transfer of cents out of `invoice_amount_cents` and into `margin_value_cents` that never touches `list_amount_cents`. At the partner level, `trailing_revenue_cents` always equals the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` over that partner's orders in `INVOICED` and `CREDITED`.

### 3.7. Funding the Channel: Fund Periods, Claims and Proof of Performance

A market development fund period is opened by channel operations for a named `partner_id` over a closed fiscal interval running from `period_start` through `period_end` inclusive, with `period_end` strictly later than `period_start`. Suspended partners cannot have a period opened for them.

**Sizing the fund.** The period's `basis_revenue_cents` is the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` over that partner's orders whose `status` is `INVOICED` or `CREDITED` and whose `invoiced_at` falls **strictly before** `period_start` — a period is funded by the business already booked when it opens, never by business booked inside it. The accrual is that basis multiplied by the partner tier's accrual rate and rounded half-up to whole cents: 0.0 for `REGISTERED`, 0.01 for `SILVER`, 0.02 for `GOLD`, 0.03 for `PLATINUM`. The accrual alone is then capped at the tier's per-period ceiling — 0 cents for `REGISTERED`, 500000 for `SILVER`, 2000000 for `GOLD`, 5000000 for `PLATINUM` — and the tier's flat base grant is added **after** that cap, so the cap bounds the accrual only: 0 cents for `REGISTERED`, 50000 for `SILVER`, 100000 for `GOLD`, 200000 for `PLATINUM`. The sum is stored as `allocated_cents`. A `REGISTERED` partner accrues nothing and receives no base grant, so its period is sized at 0 and no claim can ever be paid from it.

**Annual capacity.** A period is only created if it fits under the partner's annual ceiling. The candidate period's `allocated_cents`, added to the `allocated_cents` of every existing period of the same partner whose inclusive interval intersects the candidate's, must stay at or below the tier's annual cap: 0 cents for `REGISTERED`, 400000 for `SILVER`, 900000 for `GOLD`, 2000000 for `PLATINUM`. Two periods that merely touch at a single endpoint do intersect. A candidate that would breach the cap is rejected with `ERR_MDF_PERIOD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and nothing is created. A period that clears the cap is stored with `committed_cents`, `paid_cents` and `expired_cents` all 0, `available_cents` equal to `allocated_cents`, `status` `OPEN` and `closed_at` null.

**Four buckets, one identity.** A fund period's money always sits in exactly one of four buckets. `available_cents` is unspoken-for money and is always `allocated_cents` less `committed_cents` less `paid_cents` less `expired_cents`; `committed_cents` is money reserved by claims that are neither settled nor rejected; `paid_cents` is money actually disbursed to the partner; and `expired_cents` is money burned, either by closing the period or by writing a claim off. At every observable instant `allocated_cents` equals the sum of the four buckets, and neither `available_cents` nor `committed_cents` may ever go negative. Every operation described below moves cents between exactly two buckets; nothing is ever rounded during a transfer, and the period's `version` advances by 1 on any single request that moves its buckets — once per request rather than once per transfer, so the several successive transfers cascade C makes in §3.8 together advance it by exactly 1, and a request that moves no cents at all leaves the period's `version` and `updated_at` untouched.

**Filing a claim.** A partner files a claim against an open period by naming the `allocation_id`, an `activity_type` of `EVENT`, `DIGITAL_CAMPAIGN` or `CONTENT_SYNDICATION`, and the `requested_cents` it wants reimbursed, which must be strictly greater than 0. The period must be `OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_CLOSED`), the partner must not be suspended, the partner must have no overdue compliance audit, and `requested_cents` must be at or below the period's current `available_cents` (otherwise `ERR_MDF_ALLOCATION_INSUFFICIENT` — which is also what a claim against a `REGISTERED` partner's zero-sized period always produces). The claim is stored with `validation_state` `SUBMITTED`, `settlement_state` `UNPAID`, `proof_round` 0, and `matched_total_cents`, `approved_cents`, `validated_by_user_id`, `settled_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` all null. The full `requested_cents` is committed against the pool immediately: `committed_cents` increases by that amount and `available_cents` is recomputed.

A claim carries **two independent state dimensions**. `validation_state` is the proof-of-performance review dimension and runs over `SUBMITTED`, `PROOF_REQUESTED`, `VALIDATED` and the terminal `REJECTED`. `settlement_state` is the payment dimension and runs over `UNPAID`, `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` and the terminal `PAID` and `WRITTEN_OFF`. The two evolve separately and are stored and reported separately; the only coupling is that `settlement_state` may only advance while `validation_state` is `VALIDATED`.

**Attaching evidence.** A partner attaches a proof-of-performance document to its own claim by supplying a `doc_type` of `INVOICE`, `ATTENDEE_LIST`, `SCREENSHOT` or `LEAD_REPORT`, the `spend_amount_cents` the document evidences (strictly greater than 0), and optionally the `audience_reach` — the audience or lead count — which must not be negative and which is stored as null when it is omitted. The period must still be `OPEN`, and the acting partner must own the period the claim draws on, or `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. A new document is stored with its `claim_id` set to the claim named in the path — the document's only foreign key, and the field a proof read reports its parentage through — with `verified` false, `verified_at` null, `verified_by_user_id` null and `matched_line_cents` null, and the parent claim's `version` advances by 1.

Attaching evidence is allowed from two claim states, and they behave differently. From `validation_state` `SUBMITTED` the attachment is a self-loop: the claim stays `SUBMITTED` and `proof_round` is **not** incremented, which is how a partner assembles a multi-line claim out of several documents without consuming any round. From `validation_state` `PROOF_REQUESTED` the attachment returns the claim `PROOF_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, closing the loop the analyst opened.

Channel operations then verifies a document individually. Verification sets `verified` to true, `verified_at` to the current logical instant and `verified_by_user_id` to the acting analyst's opaque identifier, and advances the document's `version` by 1; the parent period must still be `OPEN`. The parent claim's own `validation_state` is not examined at all, so a document hanging off a claim still in `SUBMITTED`, one in `PROOF_REQUESTED`, or one already terminally `REJECTED` is verifiable just the same, and the claim's own `version` is never advanced by a verification. Verification is not repeatable — a document whose `verified` is already true is rejected with `ERR_PROOF_ALREADY_VERIFIED`, because a second verification would double-count its `spend_amount_cents` into the partner's qualified marketing spend. When the parent claim is already `VALIDATED` at the moment of verification, the partner's `marketing_qualified_spend_cents` and `marketing_attainment_pct` are recomputed as §3.1 describes — and the recomputation stops there. A verification never drives a standing transition: it leaves `tier_status`, `standing_reason` and `portal_entitlement_level` exactly as they stood, so a partner whose attainment climbs back to 0.75 through a verification alone stays on probation until a validating claim decision applies the recovery. Being a recomputation of derived fields, the hop advances that partner's `version` and `updated_at` only when one of the two recomputed figures actually moves, and when the parent claim is not yet `VALIDATED` the partner is not visited at all.

**Validating a claim.** Channel operations resolves the validation dimension with a `decision` of `VALIDATE`, `REQUEST_PROOF` or `REJECT`, and may supply a `rejection_reason`.

`REQUEST_PROOF` moves the claim `SUBMITTED → PROOF_REQUESTED`, requires the period to be `OPEN`, requires `proof_round` to be strictly below 2, and advances `proof_round` by 1. Only the analyst's request consumes a round; the partner's answering attachment does not. A third proof request is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

`VALIDATE` moves the claim `SUBMITTED → VALIDATED`. It requires the period to be `OPEN` and requires at least one of the claim's documents to have `verified` true, or `ERR_NO_VERIFIED_PROOF`. Matching is then computed **per line and rounded per line**: taking the claim's verified documents in ascending `created_at` order, each document's `matched_line_cents` is its `spend_amount_cents` multiplied by the match rate for the partner's `tier` — 0.25 for `REGISTERED`, 0.40 for `SILVER`, 0.50 for `GOLD`, 0.60 for `PLATINUM` — and rounded half-up to whole cents. `matched_total_cents` is the exact sum of those already-rounded line figures; summing the raw spend and rounding once at the end is a different and wrong answer whenever two or more lines produce a half-cent product. Unverified documents contribute nothing and keep `matched_line_cents` null. Writing `matched_line_cents` onto a verified document is not itself treated as a mutation of that document: its `version` and `updated_at` stay exactly as verification left them, which is why a read can surface a line figure the document's audit fields give no hint of. `approved_cents` is then the smaller of `requested_cents` and `matched_total_cents`; no availability term enters this comparison, because `requested_cents` was already committed against the pool when the claim was filed, so the pool can always cover what is approved. The unmatched remainder — `requested_cents` less `approved_cents` — is released back: `committed_cents` decreases by that remainder and `available_cents` is recomputed; when that remainder is exactly 0, because the matched total reached or exceeded the request, no cents move and the period is not written at all, so its `version` and `updated_at` are left untouched by the validation. The acting analyst's opaque identifier is recorded as `validated_by_user_id`, and the claim's `version` advances by 1. Validation finally recomputes the partner's `marketing_qualified_spend_cents` and `marketing_attainment_pct`; when the recomputed attainment reaches or exceeds 0.75 while the partner's `tier_status` is `PROBATION` with `standing_reason` `MARKETING_ATTAINMENT_SHORTFALL`, the partner returns to `COMPLIANT` and `portal_entitlement_level` is recomputed.

`REJECT` requires a present and non-empty `rejection_reason` and is reachable from three places. From `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and from `PROOF_REQUESTED → REJECTED` the behaviour is identical: `approved_cents` is set to 0, the reason is recorded, `validated_by_user_id` is set to the acting analyst, `committed_cents` decreases by the claim's full `requested_cents`, `available_cents` is recomputed, and the claim's `version` advances by 1. The third path, `VALIDATED → REJECTED`, is a claw-back and is only permitted while `settlement_state` is still `UNPAID`; a claim already `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` or `PAID` can no longer be clawed back, and attempting it is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

**Cascade B** is that claw-back, and it runs four hops from a different trigger than cascade A. Hop one: the claim's `validation_state` becomes `REJECTED`, `approved_cents` is set to 0, the `rejection_reason` is recorded and `validated_by_user_id` is set to the acting analyst. `matched_total_cents` is deliberately **not** cleared — it keeps the figure validation computed, as do the documents' own `matched_line_cents` — so the record of what was once matched survives the claw-back; and `settlement_state` stays `UNPAID`, since only the validation dimension moved. Hop two: because the claim left `VALIDATED`, `committed_cents` decreases by the claim's **previous** `approved_cents` — the amount that was still committed after validation, not the original request — and `available_cents` is recomputed, so a file-validate-reject round trip restores `available_cents` to its pre-claim value with zero drift. Hop three: because the claim is no longer `VALIDATED`, the partner's `marketing_qualified_spend_cents` drops by the sum of `spend_amount_cents` over this claim's verified documents and `marketing_attainment_pct` is recomputed. Hop four: when that recomputed attainment has fallen below 0.75 while the partner's `tier_status` is `COMPLIANT`, the partner moves to `PROBATION` with `standing_reason` `MARKETING_ATTAINMENT_SHORTFALL` and `portal_entitlement_level` is recomputed. The claim's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.8. Settling Claims and Closing the Period

The channel director owns the settlement dimension and advances it with an `action` of `APPROVE_PAYMENT`, `PAY` or `WRITE_OFF`. Every settlement action records the acting director's opaque identifier as `settled_by_user_id`, advances the claim's `version` by 1, and is barred to the operator who validated the claim: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `validated_by_user_id` the action is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SAME_ACTOR`. Segregation of duties is the whole point — the party that measured the spend may not be the party that releases the money.

`APPROVE_PAYMENT` moves the claim `UNPAID → APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT`. It requires `validation_state` to be `VALIDATED` (otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VALIDATED`), `settlement_state` to be `UNPAID`, and the fund period to be `OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_CLOSED`). No cents move: the claim's `approved_cents` was already sitting in `committed_cents`.

`PAY` moves the claim `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT → PAID`. It requires `settlement_state` to be `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` and the period to be `OPEN`. The claim's `approved_cents` transfers from `committed_cents` into `paid_cents`, and `available_cents` is recomputed.

`WRITE_OFF` abandons the money without paying it. It is reachable from `UNPAID → WRITTEN_OFF`, which additionally requires `validation_state` to be `VALIDATED` (otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VALIDATED`), and from `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT → WRITTEN_OFF`. Both paths behave identically: the claim's `approved_cents` transfers from `committed_cents` into `expired_cents` — compensating the commitment without returning the money to the pool — `available_cents` is recomputed, and `rejection_reason` is set to `WRITTEN_OFF`. The second of those two transitions is also the one the period-close cascade drives on the system's behalf — though the cascade stamps `PERIOD_CLOSED` rather than `WRITTEN_OFF` into `rejection_reason` and records no `settled_by_user_id`, because no director acted.

**Closing a period, in a pinned order.** Channel operations closes a fund period that is currently `OPEN`; closing one already `CLOSED` is `ERR_ALLOCATION_CLOSED`. Cascade C then runs four steps, and the order is pinned because running the balance sweep before the claim sweeps would burn a smaller balance and break the conservation identity. Step one, first: every claim of this period whose `validation_state` is `SUBMITTED` or `PROOF_REQUESTED` moves to `validation_state` `REJECTED` with `approved_cents` set to 0 and `rejection_reason` set to `PERIOD_CLOSED`, and the period's `committed_cents` decreases by that claim's `requested_cents`; that claim's `settlement_state` stays `UNPAID` and its `validated_by_user_id` is left exactly as it stood — null on a claim no analyst had yet decided — because the system rather than an operator drove the rejection. Step two, second: every claim of this period whose `validation_state` is `VALIDATED` and whose `settlement_state` is `UNPAID` or `APPROVED_FOR_PAYMENT` moves to `settlement_state` `WRITTEN_OFF` with `rejection_reason` set to `PERIOD_CLOSED`, the period's `committed_cents` decreases by that claim's `approved_cents`, and the period's `expired_cents` increases by the same `approved_cents`; that claim's `validation_state` stays `VALIDATED`, its `approved_cents` and `matched_total_cents` are left as validation computed them, and its `settled_by_user_id` stays null for the same reason. Step three, third, operating on the balance the first two steps have produced: the period's `expired_cents` increases by its current `available_cents`, and `available_cents` is then set to 0. Step four: `status` becomes `CLOSED` and `closed_at` is set to the current logical instant. Every claim touched in steps one and two has its own `version` advanced by 1, and the period's `version` advances by 1 in total across all four steps rather than once per bucket movement. No hop of the cascade reaches the partner: step one's claims were never `VALIDATED` and step two leaves `validation_state` at `VALIDATED`, so a close moves nothing in the partner's `marketing_qualified_spend_cents`, `marketing_attainment_pct`, `tier_status`, `standing_reason` or `portal_entitlement_level`, and the partner's `version` and `updated_at` are untouched by it.

Read as a ledger, the whole fund system has exactly six movements, each a pure transfer between two buckets: filing a claim moves `requested_cents` from available to committed; validating moves the unmatched remainder from committed back to available; rejecting moves back to available either the previous `approved_cents` (when the claim had been validated) or the full `requested_cents` (when it had not); paying moves `approved_cents` from committed to paid; writing off moves `approved_cents` from committed to expired; and closing the period moves what remains from committed to available to expired in cascade C's pinned order. Nothing else touches the buckets, and no rounding occurs in any transfer.

### 3.9. The Annual Reckoning: Compliance Audits and Tier Scoring

The performance review is the program's periodic checkpoint, and it is the only place a partner's `tier` is ever rewritten. Channel operations prepares one for a named `partner_id` over a closed period from `period_start` through `period_end` (end strictly after start), optionally recording the number of program-compliance audit findings as `findings_count`, which must not be negative and defaults to 0 when omitted. Review lifecycle states are `PREPARED`, `FINDINGS_OPEN`, `REMEDIATED`, and the terminal `APPROVED` and `REJECTED`.

**Preparation is also the enablement checkpoint.** Before anything is scored, cascade E sweeps the partner's certifications: every certification record of that partner whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the current logical instant moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` with its `version` advanced by 1, and then — because those records left `ACTIVE` — the partner's `cert_credit_total` is recomputed by the full rollup of §3.1 and `portal_entitlement_level` is recomputed from the result. That closing hop is a recomputation of derived fields, so it advances the partner's `version` and `updated_at` only when the credit total or the entitlement level actually moves, exactly as §2.4 qualifies; a sweep that finds no lapsed record leaves the partner's audit fields alone. The sweep visits only that partner's own records, and it recomputes nothing else on the partner — not its revenue, not its conflict count. Preparing a review is the **only** producer of a certification record in `EXPIRED`; certifications never lapse on their own, and in particular the review decision does not re-run the sweep, so a record that lapses between preparation and decision is still `ACTIVE` when the final variant reads it.

The prepared review then records the acting analyst's opaque identifier as `prepared_by_user_id`, snapshots the partner's `marketing_attainment_pct` onto the review as the scoring's own recomputation of that figure has just left it — not as it stood before the request, so the snapshot is always the fresh value — and computes the provisional half of the scoring, writing `provisional_cert_credit`, `provisional_revenue_cents`, `provisional_score` and `recommended_tier`. `final_cert_credit`, `final_revenue_cents`, `final_score`, `approved_tier`, `decided_by_user_id` and `remediation_note` are all left null until the decision. If `findings_count` is 0 the review is stored with `status` `PREPARED`, `remediation_due_at` null, and is immediately decidable. If `findings_count` is greater than 0 the review is instead moved `PREPARED → FINDINGS_OPEN` in the very same response that created it, with `remediation_due_at` set to the current logical instant plus 30 days. The row is minted directly in `FINDINGS_OPEN` rather than written and then rewritten, so it is created with `version` 1 and with `updated_at` equal to `created_at` exactly as a clean review is. The `PREPARED` state is therefore observable only on a clean audit. Across the whole preparation the partner's own `version` advances by at most 1, however many of its derived figures the certification sweep and the scoring's marketing recomputation moved between them, and not at all when none of them moved.

**The scoring formula, and why there are two of it.** Both halves share one arithmetic. The certification component is the smaller of 1 and the variant's certification-credit basis divided by a target of 12 credits. The revenue component is the smaller of 1 and the variant's period revenue divided by a target of 50000000 cents (five hundred thousand dollars). The marketing component is the partner's `marketing_attainment_pct`, already a fraction between 0 and 1, recomputed immediately before use and taken exactly as that field stores it — that is, already quantised to four decimal places by §3.1's rule, and never re-derived here as an exact quotient of qualified spend over commitment. The score is 40 times the certification component plus 35 times the revenue component plus 25 times the marketing component, rounded half-up to two decimal places **once, at the end**; the certification and revenue components are held as exact unrounded fractions, because rounding either of them before weighting would drift the score.

The tier is then read off a gate ladder evaluated top-down, first match winning, in which every gate is a conjunction of the score **and** the raw un-normalised inputs — so a partner can max out the score and still be blocked by a raw floor. The first gate awards `PLATINUM` when the score is at least 85 and the certification basis is at least 12 credits and the period revenue is at least 50000000 cents. The second awards `GOLD` when the score is at least 70 and the certification basis is at least 6 credits. The third awards `SILVER` when the score is at least 45 and the certification basis is at least 2 credits. Failing all three yields `REGISTERED`.

The two variants read the same database rows and differ only in two filters. The **provisional** variant, computed at preparation and producing `recommended_tier`, is forward-looking: its certification basis is the sum of `credit_weight` over every certification record still in `ACTIVE`, regardless of how soon it lapses, and its period revenue is the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` over the partner's orders whose `status` is `PENDING`, `INVOICED` or `CREDITED` and whose window instant falls inside the review period inclusively at both ends — where that window instant is `invoiced_at` for an order in `INVOICED` or `CREDITED` and `created_at` for an order still in `PENDING`. The **final** variant, computed at the decision and producing `approved_tier`, is coverage-tested: its certification basis counts only records in `ACTIVE` whose `expires_at` is at or after `period_end` plus 90 days, and its period revenue counts only orders whose `status` is `INVOICED` or `CREDITED` and whose `invoiced_at` falls inside the review period inclusively at both ends, with `PENDING` orders excluded entirely. Consequently `final_score` is at or below `provisional_score` whenever either dropped set is non-empty, and identical data can imply two different tiers. Rows belonging to other partners never contribute to either variant.

**Remediation.** A partner whose review sits in `FINDINGS_OPEN` closes out the findings itself, moving the review `FINDINGS_OPEN → REMEDIATED`. It must be the review's own partner (`ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` otherwise), it must supply a present and non-empty `remediation_note` which is recorded on the review, and it must act at or before the review's own `remediation_due_at` — acting after that instant is `ERR_AUDIT_REMEDIATION_OVERDUE`. The review's `version` advances by 1. While the deadline is unmet and the review stays `FINDINGS_OPEN`, that overdue finding freezes the partner out of registration submission, claim filing and mapping acceptance, as §2.8 sets out.

**The decision.** Only the channel director decides a review, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and in both cases the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the review's `prepared_by_user_id` or the request is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SAME_ACTOR`. Both branches compute the final variant, writing `final_cert_credit`, `final_revenue_cents` and `final_score`, record the acting director as `decided_by_user_id`, and advance the review's `version` by 1. The partner the decision writes has its own `version` advanced by at most 1 in total, whichever branch ran and however many of its fields the branch rewrote — and not at all when nothing among its `tier`, `tier_status`, `standing_reason`, derived rollups and `portal_entitlement_level` actually moved, which an approval that re-awards the tier a compliant partner already holds is the ordinary way to observe.

`APPROVE` is reachable from `PREPARED → APPROVED` and from `REMEDIATED → APPROVED`; a review still in `FINDINGS_OPEN` must be remediated first, and approving it directly is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Approval writes `approved_tier` from the final variant's gate ladder and copies it onto the partner's `tier` — the one and only writer of that field in the whole service. It then settles the partner's standing: if the partner is in `PROBATION` with `standing_reason` `MARKETING_ATTAINMENT_SHORTFALL`, that probation is left untouched, because a tier award does not excuse a marketing shortfall; otherwise `tier_status` becomes `COMPLIANT` and `standing_reason` is cleared to null. Finally `portal_entitlement_level` is recomputed against the newly written `tier`, which is where a promotion actually reaches the partner's portal access.

`REJECT` is reachable from `PREPARED → REJECTED`, from `REMEDIATED → REJECTED`, and from `FINDINGS_OPEN → REJECTED` — the last of these only once the deadline has passed, meaning the current logical instant is strictly after `remediation_due_at`; rejecting a `FINDINGS_OPEN` review whose deadline has not yet passed is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than an overdue-remediation error. Rejection leaves `approved_tier` null and leaves the partner's `tier` untouched, but sets the partner's `tier_status` to `SUSPENDED` with `standing_reason` `PROGRAM_REVIEW_REJECTED` and recomputes `portal_entitlement_level`, which for a suspended partner short-circuits to `BASIC`. A suspended partner is frozen out of registration submission and approval, order pricing and invoicing, fund-period creation, claim filing and special pricing requests until a later review approval restores it.

Every other successful call — reads, list reads, state-advancing actions and partial updates alike — returns HTTP 200, and an action returns the resource it mutated as named in the description below.
