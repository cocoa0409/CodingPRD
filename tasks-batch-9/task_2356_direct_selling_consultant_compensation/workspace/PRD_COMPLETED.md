# Direct Selling Consultant Compensation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A direct selling company earns its licence to operate twice over: once from the consultants who build its field organisation, and once from the regulators who audit how that organisation is paid and described to the public. Both audiences ask the same question in different words — *is the money right, and can you prove it?* Today the company answers with spreadsheets. Enrollment paperwork, starter-kit sales tax, genealogy edits after a termination, marginal volume bonuses, multi-level overrides with compression, buyback obligations, the seventy percent anti-inventory-loading rule, disciplinary sanctions and the annual income disclosure statement are each maintained by a different team on a different tool, and every reconciliation between them is manual. The result is a compensation cycle that cannot be replayed, an income disclosure nobody will sign, and a genealogy that quietly diverges from the volume that was actually paid on it.

This document specifies the Direct Selling Consultant Compensation API, the single system of record that replaces all of it. The API is mounted under `/direct-selling`. It owns the whole arc: a prospect's enrollment application with its three independent gates (agreement acceptance, identity verification, starter-kit payment); the consultant genealogy and the volume that flows up it; qualification periods that freeze volume, evaluate rank and stamp sell-through; commission runs that compute retail profit, personal volume bonuses and multi-level overrides and then move through a segregated approval to disbursement; statutory inventory buybacks that reverse volume and claw back commission already paid; compliance cases that escalate a disciplinary ladder; terminations that rewrite the genealogy and reinstatements that partially restore it; and income disclosure statements whose published distribution figures must match the run they were drawn from.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **money is never approximate**: all monetary amounts are integer cents, all volume is integer points, all rates are integer basis points, and every rounding step names its direction and its position in the calculation. Second, **every figure is attributable**: the analyst who computes a number may never be the controller who approves it or the reviewer who publishes it, corrections to closed volume are visible as flags on the affected period and run rather than as silent restatements, and any write that depends on a parent figure the client read earlier must prove that the figure has not moved.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint. Where §3 describes what a workflow means, this section describes how every request is authenticated, shaped, ordered, rounded and versioned.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty — and a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the body is parsed. Seven roles exist, spelled exactly: `Applicant`, `Consultant`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `CommissionAnalyst`, `FinanceController`, `LegalReviewer`, and `PlatformAdmin`. If `X-User-Role` is not one of those seven, or is one of them but is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The per-endpoint authorisation lists are given in §4.

Two of the seven roles are entity-backed, which is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Consultant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consultant` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a consultant compare that resource's `consultant_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` identifies the acting prospect by their email address: the caller owns an `EnrollmentApplication` when the application's `applicant_email` equals `X-User-ID`. A `Consultant` or `Applicant` caller that addresses a resource they do not own is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE` (HTTP 403), and this row-level check runs before any body validation. List endpoints address no single resource, so neither the ownership check nor any owner-scoped filter applies to them: a `Consultant` or `Applicant` caller listing rows sees every row that matches the query filters, whoever owns it.

The remaining five roles are opaque operator identifiers not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `CommissionAnalyst`, `FinanceController`, `LegalReviewer` or `PlatformAdmin`, the server may record `X-User-ID` on a resource as an audit trail — as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a compliance case, `calculated_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a commission run, `decided_by_user_id` on a buyback request, and `submitted_by_user_id` and `legal_reviewer_user_id` on an income disclosure statement — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`, and no ownership filter applies to those roles.

Duties are deliberately segregated rather than merely partitioned. A `CommissionAnalyst` runs the qualification and commission cycle and prepares disclosures but can neither approve nor pay a run nor publish a disclosure. A `FinanceController` approves, pays and reverses runs and decides and executes buybacks but never calculates one. A `LegalReviewer` holds exactly one write capability in the entire domain — the legal decision on a disclosure. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns identity decisions, enrollment approval, the disciplinary process and reinstatement, but never touches money. A `PlatformAdmin` configures plans, jurisdictions, products and periods, mints genealogy roots, adjusts ranks administratively, reopens closed periods, drives fulfillment and sets the logical clock, and is deliberately excluded from the money path: an administrator may not calculate, approve, pay or reverse a run, may not decide a compliance case, and may not publish a disclosure. Accepting the consultant agreement is the one enrollment act a `PlatformAdmin` cannot perform on an applicant's behalf, because acceptance is a personal legal act reserved to `Applicant`; filing an appeal against a sanction is likewise reserved to `Consultant`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a shape error and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`, as is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-ten integer literal. Filters are matched for exact equality and combined conjunctively; a filter drawn from an enumerated set is validated as an enum, so an undeclared member is `ERR_VALIDATION`, whereas identifier and code filters accept any string and simply match no row.

Unless a list endpoint's own description in §4 says otherwise, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Three lists override or refine that default: qualification periods are ordered by `period_start` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; product orders break a `created_at` tie by `merchandise_subtotal_cents` ascending — the refinement matters for orders that share a consultant and a period, but it is applied to any two orders whose `created_at` agrees; and commission statements break a `created_at` tie by `calculation_sequence` ascending. Any tie surviving those keys is resolved by the server's own insertion order for the rows. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, `total_count` counting every row that matched the filters before pagination and `has_more` being true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows returned is below `total_count`.

Where an ordering fallback is needed anywhere else in the system — choosing the earliest of several statements, or the newest published disclosure of a run — the tie-break is `created_at` ascending. The UUID `id` of a row never decides a business outcome.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource the API exposes carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Clients never supply either.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, a system-managed integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a downline consultant's payment posts volume that changes an ancestor's `group_volume_points`, that ancestor's qualification row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as a client-initiated write would.

`TaxJurisdiction` is the single exception. A jurisdiction row is immutable after creation, has no update endpoint, and carries only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this system — period intervals, buyback windows, appeal deadlines, sanction cooling-off buffers and the reinstatement window — reads a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock is readable by all seven roles and its value is exposed as `current_utc`; both the read and the write answer with an object whose only member is `current_utc`. Before it is ever set, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it by supplying a new `current_utc`, which makes long windows reachable in testing without waiting; the submitted value is stored and echoed back verbatim rather than reformatted.

Setting the clock is inert by itself: it triggers no business transition, expires no sanction and closes no period. Each temporal rule reads the clock at the moment its own operation is invoked. Timestamps the server stamps on resources — `agreement_accepted_at`, `placed_at`, `locked_at`, `closed_at`, `evaluated_at`, `paid_at`, `reversed_at`, `refunded_at`, `resolved_at`, `published_at`, `enrollment_completed_at`, `terminated_at` and `reinstated_at` — all take the logical clock's value at the instant of the transition that stamps them, copied verbatim in whatever spelling the clock currently holds rather than renormalised. The server's own `created_at` and `updated_at` bookkeeping is the exception: those two read wall-clock time, not the logical clock.

### 2.6. Money, Volume and Basis Points

Three numeric kinds appear throughout the domain, and none of them is a floating-point quantity.

Monetary amounts are integer **cents**; every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer number of cents. Sales volume is integer **points**; every field whose name ends in `_points` is an integer point count. Rates are integer **basis points**; every field whose name ends in `_bp` is a rate in basis points, where 10,000 basis points is one hundred percent, so a rate of 825 basis points is a tax of 8.25 percent.

Applying a rate to an amount always means: multiply the amount by the rate in basis points, divide by 10,000, and round the exact rational result to the nearest whole cent, with a result whose fractional part is exactly one half rounded away from zero. This is the system's default rounding and it is referred to below as rounding half up. Two properties of it are normative and must not be optimised away. Rounding is applied at the position the calculation names — per order line, per bonus band, per paid downline node — and the rounded results are then summed; a rate is never applied to a pre-aggregated basis to save a step, because doing so produces a different total. And rounding is never applied to an intermediate value that the calculation does not name as rounded; the exact rational value is carried forward.

Exactly one ratio in this domain does not round half up. The sell-through ratio behind the anti-inventory-loading rule truncates toward zero, because a consultant may never be credited with sell-through they did not achieve. That exception is stated again where the ratio is defined.

### 2.7. Version Preconditions on Derived Financial Writes

Two write operations produce a figure that is derived from a parent row the client must have read first. Both are guarded by an explicit version precondition, and in both cases the precondition field is a required part of the request body rather than an optional refinement.

Opening a commission run requires `expected_qualification_period_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `QualificationPeriod` the run will pay. The run is created only when that value equals the period's current `version`; otherwise nothing is written and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments the period's `version` by 1, so replaying the identical request with the same expected value is rejected. The rule exists because a period that was reopened and re-locked, or that absorbed a buyback correction, no longer holds the volume the client believed it was paying.

Drafting an income disclosure statement requires `expected_commission_run_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `CommissionRun` being disclosed. The statement row is written only when that value equals the run's current `version`; otherwise no row is created and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A successful create increments the run's `version` by 1, so a second draft attempt reusing the stale expected value is rejected and the caller must re-read the run. The rule exists because a run that has been reversed, or whose statements were replaced by a newer calculation generation, would produce a disclosure that misstates the earnings distribution.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection take no `expected_..._version` input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Order of Rejection

Validation is strictly ordered. The first rule a request violates decides the response, the request short-circuits at that point, and nothing at all is written — not even the parts of the operation that would have succeeded.

Every request is evaluated against the same prefix, in this order:

1. **Credentials present** — both headers non-empty, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role permitted** — `X-User-Role` is one of the seven roles and appears in the endpoint's authorised list, or `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Row ownership** — for endpoints whose authorised roles include `Consultant` or `Applicant`, the acting caller owns the addressed resource, or `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE`. A path identifier that resolves to no row cannot fail this check, because there is no owner to compare against, so such a request is answered with that entity's 404 from step 5 instead. On the two creation endpoints that carry `consultant_id` in the body, by contrast, the check is a plain comparison of the submitted value against `X-User-ID`, so a `Consultant` naming any other value — even one that resolves to no consultant — is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE` before the body is validated.
4. **Request shape** — every required field present; every enum value a declared member; every integer an integer inside its declared range; every array inside its declared length bounds; every datetime an ISO 8601 value with a `Z` offset; `limit` between 1 and 100 and `offset` at or above 0. Any violation is the single generic `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). Unknown body keys, and any attempt to supply a server-computed field, are silently ignored rather than rejected.
5. **Path identifiers resolve** — each identifier in the URL path resolves to a live row, or the 404 named for that entity (see §2.9).
6. **Body references resolve** — each foreign key in the request body, including a state code looked up against `TaxJurisdiction`, resolves to a live row, or the 404 named for that entity. Body references are resolved left to right in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint.
7. **Nested line references resolve** — each `product_id` and `product_order_id` inside a `lines` element resolves to a live row, resolved in submitted line order, so the first bad line decides the error.
8. **Version precondition** — where §2.7 applies, the expected version matches, or `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` / `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION`.

Only then are the domain rules of the specific operation evaluated. Their relative order is not uniform across the system and is therefore stated explicitly in the §3 narrative for each operation that carries more than one. Three of those orderings are worth flagging here because they are counter-intuitive and each is the difference between two plausible error codes:

- On enrollment application creation, sponsor eligibility is a referential concern and outranks the duplicate-email constraint. An application that both reuses an email already tied to an open application **and** names an ineligible sponsor is rejected with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, never with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION_EMAIL`.
- On order placement, the consultant's own status is a state concern and outranks the anti-inventory-loading rule. A `SUSPENDED` consultant who is also in breach of the seventy percent rule is rejected with `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_ORDER_ELIGIBLE`, never with `ERR_INVENTORY_LOADING_VIOLATION`.
- On a compliance decision, the disciplinary escalation rule outranks the sanction-window rules. A decision that both repeats a sanction level the consultant has already received **and** collides with an existing sanction window is rejected with `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_ESCALATION_REQUIRED`, never with `ERR_SANCTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

### 2.9. Reference Resolution and the Not-Found Catalogue

Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the response names the first identifier that fails. The addressed entity determines the error: `plan_id` yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `jurisdiction_id` yields `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `product_id` yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `application_id` yields `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `consultant_id` yields `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_FOUND`, `order_id` yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `period_id` yields `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `period_qualification_id` yields `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `run_id` yields `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `statement_id` addressing a commission statement yields `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND` while `statement_id` addressing an income disclosure statement yields `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`, `buyback_id` yields `ERR_BUYBACK_NOT_FOUND`, and `case_id` yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`. Every one of these carries HTTP 404.

The same catalogue serves body references. A `compensation_plan_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; a `sponsor_consultant_id` or `consultant_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_FOUND`; a `starter_kit_product_id`, or a `product_id` inside a line, yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`; an `applicant_state_code` or `ship_to_state_code` for which no jurisdiction is registered yields `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`; a `posted_period_code` or `qualification_period_id` that names no period yields `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`; a `product_order_id` inside a buyback line yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; and a `commission_run_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Genealogy Integrity and Cascading Recomputation

The consultant genealogy is a single tree per plan, held by a self-referential upline pointer, and it is the substrate every group-volume and override figure is computed over. Two derived fields keep it navigable: `upline_path`, an ordered array of consultant identifiers running from the root down to the direct sponsor and excluding the consultant themselves, and `genealogy_depth`, the number of entries in that array. A root consultant has an empty `upline_path` and a `genealogy_depth` of 0. Whenever a consultant's sponsor pointer is rewritten, `upline_path` and `genealogy_depth` are recomputed not only for that consultant but transitively for every descendant of that consultant, and `direct_downline_count` — the number of non-terminated consultants pointing directly at a given consultant — is recomputed on both the losing and the receiving end of the move.

Volume aggregates ride the same tree, and they are live only while the period they belong to is accepting volume. `group_volume_points` and `active_leg_count` on a qualification row are maintained incrementally while the row's period is `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, and are frozen while it is `LOCKED` or `CLOSED`. Consequently, any event that moves volume or moves a subtree — an order payment, a buyback refund, a termination reassignment — recomputes those two fields on the acting consultant's row and on the rows of every ancestor in the acting consultant's `upline_path`, but only for periods whose status is `OPEN` or `REOPENED`. Periods that are already `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` are never silently restated; a correction reaching them raises the visible flags described in §3.6 instead.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties on Money and Disclosure

Two figures in this system are prepared by one person and blessed by another, and in both cases the system enforces that they are different people rather than trusting policy. The controller taking an approval decision on a commission run must present an `X-User-ID` different from the run's `calculated_by_user_id`. The reviewer taking a legal decision on an income disclosure statement must present an `X-User-ID` different from the statement's `submitted_by_user_id`. Either collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). The check bites even though no single role can perform both halves, because the same human may hold two role headers under one `X-User-ID`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Plan, the Tax Map and the Catalogue

Everything downstream is parameterised by a **compensation plan**, which a `PlatformAdmin` creates by supplying a `plan_code` — a business identifier of 1 to 32 characters, unique across all plans, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_CODE` — and a display `name`. Every other plan field has a default, so a minimal call yields the canonical plan described here. The plan tracks `consultant_count`, the number of consultants enrolled on it whose status is not `TERMINATED`, which starts at 0 and moves as consultants are minted, terminated and reinstated.

The plan's economic settings fall into families. **Volume valuation**: `point_value_cents` is the cash value of one volume point when a bonus is paid, defaulting to 100 cents — one dollar per point — and must be at least 1. **Qualification thresholds**: `payline_min_pv_points` (default 200) is the minimum net personal volume a downline consultant must post in a period to occupy a payline level at all; `pv_bonus_min_points` (default 100) is the separate and lower floor below which no personal volume bonus is computed; and `active_leg_min_points` (default 300) is the minimum subtree volume — a leg's root plus all of its descendants — for one of a consultant's direct legs to count as active. Those three floors are deliberately distinct numbers and must not be conflated. **Bonus shaping**: `pv_bonus_bands` is an ordered list of marginal bands, each carrying an `upper_points` ceiling and a `rate_bp` rate, whose final element must carry a null `upper_points` to mean "everything above"; the default ladder pays 500 basis points on the first 500 points, 900 basis points on points 501 through 1,500, and 1,300 basis points on every point beyond 1,500. `senior_rank_bump_bp` (default 200) is the extra rate applied to the whole personal volume once a consultant is paid as `SENIOR_CONSULTANT` or higher, `pv_bonus_cap_cents` (default 50,000 cents, or five hundred dollars) is the ceiling on the bonus after the bump is added, and `probation_keep_bp` (default 5,000, meaning one half) is the fraction of the bonus a consultant on probation keeps. **Payout policy**: `withhold_tier_threshold_cents` (default 20,000) splits the chargeback withholding rate between `withhold_low_bp` (default 1,000) at or below the threshold and `withhold_high_bp` (default 2,500) strictly above it, and `min_payout_cents` (default 2,500 cents, twenty-five dollars) is the floor below which a statement is carried forward instead of disbursed. **Rank tenure**: `consecutive_qualifying_periods` (default 2) is how many consecutive closed periods a consultant must qualify at a higher rank before their held rank advances, and `maintenance_grace_periods` (default 1) is how many consecutive closed periods they may hold a rank above the one they qualified for before a one-step demotion. **Inventory and returns**: `seventy_rule_bp` (default 7,000, which is seventy percent) is the minimum prior-period sell-through required before another personal-inventory order is accepted, `buyback_rate_bp` (default 9,000, ninety percent) is the fraction of eligible wholesale cost refunded on an approved buyback, `buyback_window_days` (default 365 days, one year) is the maximum age of an order eligible for buyback, and a restocking fee of `restock_fee_bp` (default 500) applies only when the gross refund strictly exceeds `restock_free_limit_cents` (default 45,000). **Discipline and re-entry**: `sanction_cooloff_days` (default 30) is the buffer appended after a sanction window during which another sanction window may not begin, `appeal_window_days` (default 14, two weeks) is how long a consultant has to appeal a sanction, `reinstatement_window_days` (default 180 days exactly) bounds reinstatement after termination, and `max_identity_attempts` (default 2) is how many identity submissions an applicant gets. **Shipping**: `flat_shipping_cents` (default 995) is charged when the merchandise subtotal is below `free_shipping_threshold_cents` (default 25,000), and nothing is charged at or above it. Every numeric plan field named above is an optional integer that falls back to its stated default; each must be at least 0, except `point_value_cents` which must be at least 1, and every field whose name ends in `_bp` — including each bonus band's `rate_bp` — is additionally capped at 10,000, while a band's `upper_points` must be a non-negative integer or null. Any violation of those bounds is the generic `ERR_VALIDATION`.

The plan also carries `compression_mode`, which selects which multi-level override algorithm runs and is either `DYNAMIC` (the default) or `STATIC`, and `rank_ladder`, the ordered list of rank definitions. Each rung of the ladder carries a `rank_code`, a `rank_order`, thresholds `required_pv_points`, `required_gv_points` and `required_active_legs`, a count of `override_levels`, and `override_bp_by_level` — a one-indexed list of per-level override rates whose length must be exactly `override_levels`. A ladder must have at least one rung, every `rank_code` must be unique within it, the three `required_*` thresholds must be integers of at least 0, each `override_bp_by_level` rate must be an integer from 0 to 10,000, and the `rank_order` values must be the contiguous integers from 1 to the number of rungs; a supplied ladder is stored — and echoed back — sorted by `rank_order` ascending whatever order its rungs arrived in. The default ladder has four rungs: `CONSULTANT` at `rank_order` 1, requiring nothing and paying no override levels; `SENIOR_CONSULTANT` at 2, requiring 300 personal volume points, 1,200 group volume points and 1 active leg, paying 1 override level at 500 basis points; `DIRECTOR` at 3, requiring 500 personal points, 3,000 group points and 1 active leg, paying 3 levels at 800, 400 and 200 basis points; and `EXECUTIVE_DIRECTOR` at 4, requiring 800 personal points, 10,000 group points and 3 active legs, paying 5 levels at 900, 500, 300, 200 and 100 basis points.

Plan policy is partially mutable. An administrator may revise `compression_mode`, `payline_min_pv_points`, `min_payout_cents`, `seventy_rule_bp`, `restock_free_limit_cents`, `restock_fee_bp`, `probation_keep_bp`, `withhold_tier_threshold_cents`, `pv_bonus_cap_cents`, `sanction_cooloff_days`, `appeal_window_days` and `reinstatement_window_days`; a patch body containing none of those fields is refused with `ERR_NO_MUTABLE_FIELDS` rather than silently accepted, and a successful patch increments the plan's `version`. Changing `compression_mode` affects only runs calculated afterwards: a run that has already been calculated keeps the mode it recorded until it is recalculated. Plans are listable and filterable by `plan_code` and `compression_mode`.

Sales tax treatment is registered per state as a **tax jurisdiction**, again by a `PlatformAdmin`, carrying a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters and unique across all jurisdictions — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION` — a `tax_rate_bp` between 0 and 2,000 inclusive, and a `facilitator_agreement_active` boolean recording whether the company holds a marketplace-facilitator agreement in that state — a boolean with no default, so omitting it, or supplying a `state_code` that is not exactly two uppercase letters, is a shape error rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Two fields are derived from that boolean and are not supplied by the caller: `tax_basis_mode` is `SRP` when a facilitator agreement is active and `NET_PRICE` otherwise, and `remitter` is `COMPANY` when a facilitator agreement is active and `CONSULTANT` otherwise. Jurisdictions are listable and filterable by `state_code` and `facilitator_agreement_active`.

The catalogue holds **products**, each with a `sku` unique across the catalogue — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` — a `name`, a `wholesale_price_cents` of at least 0 that a consultant pays per unit, and an `srp_cents` suggested retail price per unit that must be at least the wholesale price — a lower one is a shape error rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` — and that becomes the taxable basis under a facilitator agreement. Each product also carries `volume_points_per_unit` (default 0, at least 0), a `commissionable` flag (default true) which when false keeps the unit's points out of commissionable volume entirely, a `buyback_eligible` flag (default true) which when false forbids the product from ever appearing on a buyback line, and an `is_starter_kit` flag (default false) which when true is what makes the product usable as an enrollment starter kit. Products are listable and filterable by `sku`, `is_starter_kit` and `buyback_eligible`.

### 3.2. Enrollment: Three Independent Gates and One Compound Status

A prospect enters through an **enrollment application**, created either by the `Applicant` themselves or by a `PlatformAdmin`. The creation payload carries `applicant_email`, `applicant_full_name`, `applicant_state_code`, `compensation_plan_id`, `starter_kit_product_id`, an optional `sponsor_consultant_id`, an optional `agreement_version` defaulting to `AGR-2026-01`, and an optional `identity_document_reference`.

Four business rules guard creation, evaluated in this order after the shared prefix of §2.8 has passed. First, the product named by `starter_kit_product_id` must have `is_starter_kit` true; an ordinary catalogue item is refused with `ERR_STARTER_KIT_INVALID`. Second, if `sponsor_consultant_id` is supplied, that consultant's status must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; a `SUSPENDED`, `INACTIVE` or `TERMINATED` sponsor is refused with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Third, `sponsor_consultant_id` may be omitted or null only by a `PlatformAdmin`, because a sponsorless application enrolls a genealogy root; any other caller omitting it is refused with `ERR_ROOT_ENROLLMENT_FORBIDDEN`. Fourth, no other application with the same `applicant_email` may currently be in `DRAFT`, `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED`; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION_EMAIL`. As §2.8 notes, the sponsor rule outranks the email rule.

At creation the starter kit is priced once and never repriced. `kit_charge_cents` is the starter kit product's `wholesale_price_cents`. The taxable basis is that product's `srp_cents` when the jurisdiction registered for `applicant_state_code` has a facilitator agreement active, and its `wholesale_price_cents` otherwise. `kit_tax_cents` is that basis multiplied by the jurisdiction's `tax_rate_bp`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up on that single multiplication. `kit_total_cents` is `kit_charge_cents` plus `kit_tax_cents`. Under a facilitator agreement the applicant therefore pays wholesale for the merchandise but is taxed on the higher suggested retail price — the two amounts are intentionally different bases in the same calculation.

An application advances along three independent dimensions. `agreement_status` runs `NOT_ACCEPTED` to `ACCEPTED`. `identity_status` runs across `UNVERIFIED`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `VERIFIED` and `FAILED`. `kit_status` runs `UNPAID` to `PAID`. At creation, `agreement_status` is `NOT_ACCEPTED` and `kit_status` is `UNPAID`; if `identity_document_reference` was supplied in the creation payload, `identity_status` starts at `PENDING_REVIEW` with `identity_attempt_count` at 1, and if it was omitted, `identity_status` starts at `UNVERIFIED` with `identity_attempt_count` at 0.

Over the three dimensions sits a compound `status`, re-derived on every write rather than set directly. If `status` is already one of the terminal values `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, it is frozen and no re-derivation occurs. Otherwise, if `agreement_status` is `ACCEPTED` and `identity_status` is `VERIFIED` and `kit_status` is `PAID`, the compound `status` is `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`; in every other combination it is `DRAFT`. The dimensions otherwise advance freely and in any order, with one coupling: the kit may not be paid before the agreement is accepted, because an applicant may not be charged before signing.

Two state guards apply to all four dimension writes and are evaluated before the dimension's own rules. A write against an application whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_TERMINAL`; and a write against an application that has already rolled up to `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DRAFT`. The terminal check runs first.

**Accepting the agreement** is reserved to the `Applicant` and takes a single `agreement_version`. After the two state guards, `agreement_status` must still be `NOT_ACCEPTED` — acceptance is not idempotent, and a second attempt is `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_ACCEPTED` — and the submitted `agreement_version` must equal the version recorded on the application, so the applicant accepts exactly the text they were shown; a mismatch is `ERR_AGREEMENT_VERSION_MISMATCH`. On success `agreement_status` becomes `ACCEPTED`, `agreement_accepted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the compound `status` is re-derived.

**Submitting identity documents**, available to the `Applicant` or a `PlatformAdmin`, takes a replacement `identity_document_reference`. After the two state guards, `identity_status` must be `UNVERIFIED` or `FAILED` — resubmission after a failure is the intended cycle, whereas a submission already under review or already verified may not be replaced and is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE` — and `identity_attempt_count` must be strictly below the plan's `max_identity_attempts`, or the request is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. On success the stored `identity_document_reference` is replaced, `identity_attempt_count` is incremented by 1, `identity_status` becomes `PENDING_REVIEW`, and `identity_failure_reason` is cleared to null.

**Deciding identity** is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `decision` of either `VERIFY` or `FAIL` plus an optional `failure_reason`. After the two state guards, `identity_status` must be `PENDING_REVIEW`, or the request is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_PENDING` because there is nothing to decide. A `VERIFY` decision sets `identity_status` to `VERIFIED` and re-derives the compound `status`. A `FAIL` decision taken while `identity_attempt_count` is still below `max_identity_attempts` sets `identity_status` to `FAILED` and records `identity_failure_reason` from the payload, leaving the application in `DRAFT` so the applicant may resubmit. A `FAIL` decision taken once `identity_attempt_count` has reached `max_identity_attempts` does the same and additionally drives the application to `REJECTED` in the same request, with no separate call, setting `rejection_reason` to `IDENTITY_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`.

**Paying the starter kit**, available to the `Applicant` or a `PlatformAdmin`, takes `amount_cents` and a `payment_reference`. After the two state guards, the agreement test runs first: an unaccepted agreement is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED`, and only then is an already-paid kit refused with `ERR_KIT_ALREADY_PAID`. Finally the submitted `amount_cents` must equal `kit_total_cents` exactly, sales tax included; anything else is `ERR_KIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. On success `kit_status` becomes `PAID`, `kit_payment_reference` records the supplied reference, and the compound `status` is re-derived.

**Withdrawing** an application, available to the `Applicant` or a `PlatformAdmin`, takes a `reason` and is permitted while the compound `status` is `DRAFT` or `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`. After the terminal guard, `kit_status` must still be `UNPAID`: once the kit has been paid the applicant must complete enrollment and recover the merchandise through the buyback route instead, so withdrawal is refused with `ERR_KIT_PAID_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`. The starter-kit charge is never refunded by withdrawal. On success the compound `status` becomes `WITHDRAWN` and `rejection_reason` records the supplied reason.

**Approving** an application is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` and takes no payload. Sponsor eligibility is re-checked here, and it is checked before the state guards, because the sponsor's status can change while the application sits in `DRAFT`: a sponsor who is no longer `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` yields `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Then the terminal guard applies, and then the compound `status` must be `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`; an application still in `DRAFT` — because any one of agreement, identity or kit payment is outstanding — is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_INCOMPLETE`. Approval is the only way a consultant comes into existence, and it returns the newly created consultant rather than the application.

Minting a consultant copies `applicant_full_name` into `full_name`, `applicant_email` into `email`, `applicant_state_code` into `state_code`, and the application's `compensation_plan_id` onto the new row, and records `enrollment_application_id` pointing back at the application that minted it. The new consultant's `sponsor_consultant_id` is copied from the application; `upline_path` is the sponsor's own `upline_path` with the sponsor's identifier appended, or the empty list for a root; and `genealogy_depth` is the length of that path. `status` starts at `ACTIVE`, `rank_code` and `rank_order` are set to the ladder rung whose `rank_order` is 1, and `rank_source` is `ENROLLMENT` — the enumerated ways a rank can be set are `ENROLLMENT`, `EARNED`, `ADMIN_ADJUSTED`, `MAINTENANCE_DEMOTION` and `REINSTATEMENT`. `enrollment_completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Every derived counter starts at its default: `direct_downline_count`, `consecutive_qualified_count`, `grace_periods_used`, `lifetime_commission_paid_cents`, `carry_forward_cents`, `clawback_balance_cents`, `reinstatement_count`, `reassignment_count`, `open_case_count` and `highest_sanction_order` all at 0; `payout_hold` false; and `terminated_at`, `termination_reason`, `reinstated_at`, `reassigned_from_sponsor_consultant_id`, `active_sanction_level` and `active_sanction_until` all null. Approval also increments the sponsor's `direct_downline_count` by 1 and the plan's `consultant_count` by 1, and writes `created_consultant_id` back onto the application, whose `status` becomes `APPROVED`.

Applications are listable and filterable by `sponsor_consultant_id`, `compensation_plan_id`, `starter_kit_product_id`, `created_consultant_id`, `applicant_email`, `status` and `identity_status` — which is how a sponsor's pending applications and an approved application's minted consultant are both reachable. Consultants are listable and filterable by `compensation_plan_id`, `enrollment_application_id`, `sponsor_consultant_id`, `reassigned_from_sponsor_consultant_id`, `status`, `rank_code` and `payout_hold`; filtering by `sponsor_consultant_id` yields a consultant's direct downline, and filtering by `reassigned_from_sponsor_consultant_id` yields everyone moved by one particular termination.

### 3.3. Placing Orders: Per-Line Tax, Shipping and the Seventy Percent Rule

Orders are placed by the owning `Consultant` or by a `PlatformAdmin`, and the payload carries `consultant_id`, an `order_type` of `PERSONAL_INVENTORY`, `RETAIL_CUSTOMER` or `BUSINESS_SUPPLY`, a `posted_period_code` naming the period the volume must post to — resolved among the periods of the ordering consultant's own compensation plan, so a code that names no period of that plan is `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` — a `ship_to_state_code` selecting the tax jurisdiction, and `lines` — between 1 and 20 elements, each supplying a `product_id` and a `quantity` of at least 1. Line order is preserved exactly as submitted, and the server appends the priced members of each line rather than accepting them from the client: `unit_wholesale_price_cents`, `unit_srp_cents`, `volume_points_per_unit`, `commissionable`, `line_wholesale_subtotal_cents`, `line_srp_subtotal_cents`, `line_tax_cents`, `line_volume_points` and `returned_quantity`.

Four domain rules guard placement, in this order. No line may name a product whose `is_starter_kit` is true, because starter kits are sold only through the enrollment flow; a violation is `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`. Then the ordering consultant's status must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; `SUSPENDED`, `INACTIVE` and `TERMINATED` consultants are refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_ORDER_ELIGIBLE`. Then the period named by `posted_period_code` must have status `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, since a `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` period accepts no new volume; otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ACCEPTING_VOLUME`. Then the logical clock must fall inside that period's half-open interval, at or after its `period_start` and strictly before its `period_end`, so volume cannot be posted to a period the order was not placed in; otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_CODE_MISMATCH`. Finally, and only when `order_type` is `PERSONAL_INVENTORY`, the anti-inventory-loading rule described below must pass, or the order is refused with `ERR_INVENTORY_LOADING_VIOLATION`. As §2.8 notes, the consultant-status rule outranks the inventory-loading rule.

Pricing an admitted order begins by snapshotting the jurisdiction registered for `ship_to_state_code`: `tax_basis_mode`, `tax_remitter` and `tax_rate_bp` on the order are copies of that jurisdiction's basis mode, remitter and rate at the moment of creation, and later edits to the jurisdiction never reprice a placed order. Then, for each line in submitted order: `unit_wholesale_price_cents` and `unit_srp_cents` are copied from the product, `line_wholesale_subtotal_cents` is the unit wholesale price multiplied by the line's `quantity`, `line_srp_subtotal_cents` is the unit suggested retail price multiplied by the same quantity, the line's taxable basis is its suggested-retail subtotal when the snapshotted `tax_basis_mode` is `SRP` and its wholesale subtotal when the mode is `NET_PRICE`, `line_tax_cents` is that basis multiplied by the snapshotted `tax_rate_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up **on the line**, `line_volume_points` is the product's `volume_points_per_unit` multiplied by the quantity, and `returned_quantity` starts at 0.

The order-level roll-ups follow. `merchandise_subtotal_cents` is the sum of the lines' wholesale subtotals and `srp_basis_cents` the sum of their suggested-retail subtotals. `tax_total_cents` is the sum of the already-rounded `line_tax_cents` values; the rate is never applied to the order-level basis, because summing rounded lines and rounding a summed basis give different answers and only the former is correct here. `shipping_cents` is the plan's `flat_shipping_cents` when `merchandise_subtotal_cents` is strictly below the plan's `free_shipping_threshold_cents`, and 0 when it is at or above that threshold. `total_due_cents` is `merchandise_subtotal_cents` plus `tax_total_cents` plus `shipping_cents`. `total_volume_points` is the sum of every line's `line_volume_points`, while `commissionable_volume_points` sums `line_volume_points` only over lines whose `commissionable` flag is true. `placed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `returned_volume_points` and `refunded_cents` start at 0, `payment_reference` starts null, and `volume_posted` starts false.

An order carries two independent status dimensions. `payment_status` moves across `PENDING`, `PAID`, `REFUNDED_PARTIAL` and `REFUNDED_FULL`, starting at `PENDING`. `fulfillment_status` moves across `NEW`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED`, `RETURNED_PARTIAL` and `RETURNED_FULL`, starting at `NEW`. The two advance independently — an order may be `PAID` and still `NEW`, or `PENDING` and `NEW`, or `PAID` and `DELIVERED` — with one coupling: shipping requires payment, so the combination of `PENDING` payment with `SHIPPED` fulfillment is unreachable.

**Paying an order** is done by the owning `Consultant` or a `PlatformAdmin` and takes `amount_cents` and a `payment_reference`. The consultant's status must still be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, or the payment is refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_ORDER_ELIGIBLE`; the posted period must still be `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, or `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ACCEPTING_VOLUME`; the submitted `amount_cents` must equal `total_due_cents` exactly, per-line tax and shipping included, or `ERR_ORDER_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; and only then is `payment_status` required to be `PENDING`, since payment is not idempotent and a repeat is `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_PAID`. Note the order of those last two: an incorrect amount on an already-paid order surfaces as the amount mismatch, not as the already-paid conflict.

Payment is where volume enters the compensation system. `payment_status` becomes `PAID`, `payment_reference` is recorded, and `volume_posted` becomes true. The qualification row for the pairing of this consultant and this posted period is created if it does not yet exist, and then that row's `personal_volume_points`, `retail_volume_points`, `inventory_volume_points` and `net_personal_volume_points` are each increased by this order's contribution — retail volume taking the share sourced from `RETAIL_CUSTOMER` orders and inventory volume the share sourced from `PERSONAL_INVENTORY` orders, while a `BUSINESS_SUPPLY` order feeds neither split. That contribution is the order's `total_volume_points`, counting every line whether or not it is `commissionable`; `commissionable_volume_points` is carried for the commission side and never narrows what posts to the qualification row. The period's `total_net_personal_volume_points` rises by the same contribution. Finally, per §2.10, `group_volume_points` and `active_leg_count` are recomputed on the paying consultant's qualification row and on the rows of every ancestor named in their `upline_path`.

**Advancing fulfillment** is reserved to a `PlatformAdmin` and takes an `event` of `SHIP` or `DELIVER`. A `SHIP` event requires `fulfillment_status` `NEW` and `payment_status` `PAID`, and moves fulfillment `NEW → SHIPPED`. A `DELIVER` event requires `fulfillment_status` `SHIPPED` and moves it `SHIPPED → DELIVERED`. Any other combination — shipping from a state other than `NEW`, shipping an unpaid order, or delivering from a state other than `SHIPPED` — is refused with `ERR_FULFILLMENT_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. The fulfillment dimension is otherwise linear; the `RETURNED_PARTIAL` and `RETURNED_FULL` values are reached only through a buyback refund, described in §3.6.

Orders are listable and filterable by `consultant_id`, `order_type`, `posted_period_code`, `payment_status` and `fulfillment_status`.

### 3.4. The Qualification Cycle: Volume In, Rank Out

A **qualification period** is a half-open window over a plan's timeline, opened by a `CommissionAnalyst` or a `PlatformAdmin` with a `compensation_plan_id`, a `period_code` label, an inclusive `period_start` and an exclusive `period_end`. Three rules guard creation, in this order: `period_code` must be unique within the plan, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD_CODE`; `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, since empty and inverted periods are meaningless, or `ERR_PERIOD_BOUNDS_INVALID`; and the new interval must not intersect any existing period of the same plan, or `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Periods tile the timeline and adjacency is legal: a new period whose `period_start` equals an existing period's `period_end` does not overlap it, because each interval excludes its own end instant. Both bounds are stored and echoed back exactly as submitted rather than renormalised, and each must be a well-formed instant in the `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS[.sss]Z` spelling of §2.5 or the request is `ERR_VALIDATION`.

A period's `status` cycles through `OPEN`, `LOCKED`, `CLOSED` and `REOPENED`, starting at `OPEN`, and no value is absorbing: `CLOSED` is exitable through `REOPENED`, and `REOPENED` re-enters `LOCKED`. The period tracks `locked_at`, `closed_at`, `reopen_count`, a `correction_pending` flag, `qualification_count` (the number of qualification rows attached to it) and `total_net_personal_volume_points` (the sum of `net_personal_volume_points` across those rows).

A **qualification row** is the per-consultant, per-period record of volume and standing. It is server-created rather than client-created, and carries `consultant_id`, `qualification_period_id` and a denormalised `period_code`. Its volume fields are `personal_volume_points`, the sum of `line_volume_points` over the consultant's paid orders posted to this period; `returned_volume_points`, the points removed from the period by refunded buybacks; `net_personal_volume_points`, the personal points minus the returned points; and the two channel splits `retail_volume_points` and `inventory_volume_points`. `group_volume_points` is this consultant's own net personal volume plus the net personal volume of every descendant in the genealogy at unlimited depth, and `active_leg_count` is the number of direct downline consultants whose own subtree volume reaches the plan's `active_leg_min_points`; both are live while the period is `OPEN` or `REOPENED` and frozen while it is `LOCKED` or `CLOSED`, per §2.10. The row also carries `attested_personal_use_points`, `sell_through_bp`, `qualified_rank_code`, `qualified_rank_order`, `paid_as_rank_code` and `evaluated_at`. A freshly created row starts with every points field, `group_volume_points`, `active_leg_count`, `sell_through_bp` and `qualified_rank_order` at 0 and with `qualified_rank_code`, `paid_as_rank_code` and `evaluated_at` null; `sell_through_bp` therefore reads 0, not 10,000, until a lock stamps it.

**Attesting personal use** is available to the owning `Consultant` or a `PlatformAdmin` and sets `attested_personal_use_points` on a row, recording the points the consultant declares they personally consumed in the period. The row's period must be `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, since a `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` period accepts no new attestation either, or the request is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ACCEPTING_VOLUME`; and the attested figure may not exceed the row's `inventory_volume_points`, because a consultant cannot attest to consuming more than they purchased, or the request is refused with `ERR_ATTESTATION_EXCEEDS_INVENTORY`. The attested figure feeds the sell-through ratio at the next lock.

**Locking a period**, reserved to a `CommissionAnalyst`, freezes its volume and evaluates everyone. It requires `status` `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, and a period that is already `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`; the transitions it drives are `OPEN → LOCKED` and `REOPENED → LOCKED`. The lock creates a qualification row for every consultant of the plan whose status is not `TERMINATED` and who does not yet have one, so that non-producers are represented with zeroes. For every row it then finalises `net_personal_volume_points`, `group_volume_points` and `active_leg_count`, stamps `sell_through_bp`, sets `qualified_rank_code` and `qualified_rank_order`, sets `paid_as_rank_code`, and stamps `evaluated_at` from the logical clock. It also expires lapsed discipline, and only within this period's own plan: every consultant of that plan whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` and whose `active_sanction_until` is a non-null instant strictly before the logical clock is returned to `ACTIVE` with `active_sanction_level` and `active_sanction_until` cleared to null, while `highest_sanction_order` is deliberately **not** reduced, because the escalation ladder is permanent. Finally the period's `status` becomes `LOCKED`, `locked_at` is stamped, and `qualification_count` and `total_net_personal_volume_points` are refreshed. From this point `group_volume_points` and `active_leg_count` stay frozen until a reopen.

Rank evaluation at lock works over the genealogy, not over one row in isolation. The subtree volume of a consultant is the sum of `net_personal_volume_points` across that consultant's own row for this period and the rows of every descendant at unlimited depth, counting 0 for any descendant with no row. `group_volume_points` is the subtree volume of the row's own consultant. `active_leg_count` is the number of direct children whose status is not `TERMINATED` and whose subtree volume — the leg root plus all of its descendants, not the leg root's personal volume alone — is at least the plan's `active_leg_min_points`. `qualified_rank_code` is then the rung of the plan's `rank_ladder` with the greatest `rank_order` for which all three of the following hold: the row's `net_personal_volume_points` is at least the rung's `required_pv_points`, its `group_volume_points` is at least the rung's `required_gv_points`, and its `active_leg_count` is at least the rung's `required_active_legs`. All three comparisons are inclusive, so a consultant sitting exactly on a threshold satisfies it. If no rung matches at all, `qualified_rank_code` is null and `qualified_rank_order` is 0; otherwise `qualified_rank_order` is the matched rung's `rank_order`. `paid_as_rank_code` is the rank the consultant will actually be paid at: when `qualified_rank_order` is 0 it is null, and otherwise it is the rung whose `rank_order` is the smaller of the consultant's held `rank_order` and the row's `qualified_rank_order` — a consultant is never paid above what they qualified for in the period, nor above the rank they hold.

The sell-through ratio behind the seventy percent rule is stamped at the same moment. Purchased points are the row's `inventory_volume_points`. Credited points are the smaller of the purchased points and the sum of `retail_volume_points` and `attested_personal_use_points`, so a consultant can never be credited with more than they bought. When purchased points are 0, `sell_through_bp` is 10,000 by definition — a consultant who bought no inventory has nothing to sell through. Otherwise `sell_through_bp` is the credited points multiplied by 10,000, divided by the purchased points, and **truncated toward zero**; this is the one ratio in the domain that floors rather than rounding half up, precisely so that a shortfall is never rounded away. The admission test for a new `PERSONAL_INVENTORY` order then reads the consultant's qualification row for the most recent `CLOSED` period of the plan, taking periods in `period_start` descending order: if no such row exists — a consultant with no closed period yet — the order is admitted unconditionally, and otherwise the order is admitted only when that row's `sell_through_bp` is at or above the plan's `seventy_rule_bp`.

**Closing a period**, also reserved to a `CommissionAnalyst`, requires `status` `LOCKED` and refuses anything else with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_LOCKED`, driving `LOCKED → CLOSED`. Closing is where tenure is settled, consultant by consultant. Advancement: when a consultant's `qualified_rank_order` for the closing period exceeds their held `rank_order`, `consecutive_qualified_count` is incremented, and once it reaches the plan's `consecutive_qualifying_periods` the consultant's `rank_code` and `rank_order` advance by exactly one rung — never two, however far above their rank they qualified, and never past the ladder's top rung, so a consultant already holding the highest rank keeps it while the counter still resets — with `rank_source` set to `EARNED` and the counter reset to 0. Any closing period in which the consultant falls short of that advancement candidate resets `consecutive_qualified_count` to 0, so the qualifying periods must be consecutive. Maintenance: when `qualified_rank_order` is below the held `rank_order`, `grace_periods_used` is incremented, and once it exceeds the plan's `maintenance_grace_periods` the rank is demoted by one rung with `rank_source` set to `MAINTENANCE_DEMOTION` — never below the `rank_order` 1 rung, and `grace_periods_used` is left standing rather than reset, so each further shortfall demotes again until the floor is reached. Activity flips are applied in the same pass: a consultant who is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, whose row for the closing period has a null `qualified_rank_code`, and whose `grace_periods_used` has already reached `maintenance_grace_periods` — the count as it stood when this closing period's pass began, before that period's own increment — transitions `ACTIVE → INACTIVE` or `PROBATION → INACTIVE`, with `rank_code` and `rank_order` reset to the `rank_order` 1 rung, `rank_source` set to `MAINTENANCE_DEMOTION` and `grace_periods_used` reset to 0; conversely a consultant who is `INACTIVE` and whose row for the closing period has a non-null `qualified_rank_code` transitions `INACTIVE → ACTIVE`. The period's `status` then becomes `CLOSED`, `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `correction_pending` is set to false.

**Reopening a period** is reserved to a `PlatformAdmin`, takes a `reason`, and exists so a post-close correction can be absorbed honestly. It requires `status` `CLOSED`, refusing anything else with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`, and it is additionally blocked while any commission run of the period is in `DRAFT`, `CALCULATED`, `APPROVED` or `PAID`: a paid period must have its run reversed before its volume can be corrected, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_RUN_BLOCKS_REOPEN`. On success `status` becomes `REOPENED` in the transition `CLOSED → REOPENED`, `reopen_count` is incremented by 1, `correction_pending` is set to false, and `group_volume_points` and `active_leg_count` become live again and are recomputed immediately from the current genealogy and volume rather than from whatever was frozen at the last lock, the period's `qualification_count` and `total_net_personal_volume_points` being refreshed in the same pass. The `locked_at` and `closed_at` stamps of the cycle just undone are retained as historical record rather than cleared; a later lock and close overwrite them.

Periods are listable and filterable by `compensation_plan_id`, `period_code` and `status`. Qualification rows are listable and filterable by `consultant_id`, `qualification_period_id`, `period_code` and `qualified_rank_code`; supplying a consultant together with a period addresses exactly one row.

### 3.5. Commission Runs: Calculate, Approve, Pay, Reverse

A **commission run** pays exactly one closed period. A `CommissionAnalyst` opens it by supplying `qualification_period_id` together with the version precondition `expected_qualification_period_version` described in §2.7. After the version check, the referenced period's `status` must be `CLOSED`, since commissions are only ever calculated on closed volume, or the request is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`; and no other run of the same period may be in `DRAFT`, `CALCULATED`, `APPROVED` or `PAID`, since at most one live run exists per period, or the request is refused with `ERR_RUN_ALREADY_EXISTS`. A `REVERSED` run does not block a fresh one. Creation denormalises `period_code` from the period and increments the period's `version` by 1. A new run opens with `calculation_sequence` and all eight roll-up totals at 0, `statement_count` 0, `correction_flagged` false, and `compression_mode_snapshot`, `calculated_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `paid_at` and `reversed_at` all null.

A run's `status` moves across `DRAFT`, `CALCULATED`, `APPROVED`, `PAID` and `REVERSED`, starting at `DRAFT`, and its lifecycle state is checked before anything else that operation cares about: calculating requires `DRAFT` or fails with `ERR_RUN_NOT_DRAFT`, taking an approval decision requires `CALCULATED` or fails with `ERR_RUN_NOT_CALCULATED`, paying requires `APPROVED` or fails with `ERR_RUN_NOT_APPROVED`, and reversing requires `PAID` or fails with `ERR_RUN_NOT_PAID`. Alongside `status` the run carries `compression_mode_snapshot`, `calculation_sequence`, `calculated_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `statement_count`, `paid_at`, `reversed_at`, a `correction_flagged` boolean, and the roll-up totals `gross_total_cents`, `forfeited_to_company_cents`, `distributable_pool_cents`, `withheld_total_cents`, `net_payable_total_cents`, `paid_total_cents`, `carry_forward_total_cents` and `reversed_total_cents`.

**Calculating** the run, reserved to a `CommissionAnalyst`, requires `DRAFT` status and requires the period to still be `CLOSED`; it drives `DRAFT → CALCULATED`. Every statement of the run that is currently `ACTIVE` is first set to `SUPERSEDED` with its `payout_status` reset to `PENDING`, so a recalculation never leaves two live generations behind. Then one `ACTIVE` **commission statement** is created per qualification row of the period whose consultant's status is not `TERMINATED`. Each statement records `commission_run_id`, `consultant_id`, the denormalised `period_code`, the run's `calculation_sequence` as it stood when the generation began — the value **before** this calculation increments it, so the statements of a first calculation all record 0 while the run afterwards reads 1 — and copies `paid_as_rank_code` and `net_personal_volume_points` from the consultant's qualification row for the period. Its `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED`, and its `payout_status` is one of `PENDING`, `PAYABLE`, `CARRIED_FORWARD`, `PAID` or `VOIDED`, starting at `ACTIVE` and `PENDING`, with `paid_cents` and `carry_forward_cents` both starting at 0. The run then records `compression_mode_snapshot` as the plan's current `compression_mode`, increments `calculation_sequence` by 1, sets `calculated_by_user_id` to the calling analyst's `X-User-ID`, refreshes `gross_total_cents`, `forfeited_to_company_cents`, `distributable_pool_cents`, `withheld_total_cents`, `net_payable_total_cents` and `statement_count`, and moves to `CALCULATED`. The roll-ups are defined over the run's `ACTIVE` statements: `gross_total_cents` sums their `gross_commission_cents`, `withheld_total_cents` their `withholding_cents`, `net_payable_total_cents` their `net_payable_cents`, and `statement_count` counts them; `distributable_pool_cents` is `gross_total_cents` plus `forfeited_to_company_cents`.

Three earnings components are computed per statement, and they are summed into `gross_commission_cents`.

**Retail profit** is the consultant's own margin on customer sales. `retail_profit_cents` is computed over the orders whose `consultant_id` is the statement's consultant, whose `posted_period_code` equals the run's `period_code`, whose `order_type` is `RETAIL_CUSTOMER`, and whose `payment_status` is `PAID` or `REFUNDED_PARTIAL`. Over those orders and all of their lines, it sums the difference between the line's `unit_srp_cents` and its `unit_wholesale_price_cents`, multiplied by the line's `quantity` minus its `returned_quantity`. Every term is an integer, so no rounding occurs. Personal-inventory and business-supply purchases produce no retail profit at all, and an order still `PENDING` payment contributes nothing until it is paid.

**The personal volume bonus** rewards the consultant's own net personal volume in four steps whose order is normative. If `net_personal_volume_points` is below the plan's `pv_bonus_min_points`, `pv_bonus_cents` is 0 and the calculation stops. Step one walks the plan's `pv_bonus_bands` in order, maintaining a running lower bound that starts at 0: for each band, the band's effective ceiling is its `upper_points`, or the consultant's volume itself for the final band whose `upper_points` is null; the points falling in the band are the greater of 0 and the smaller of the volume and that ceiling, minus the running lower bound; the band's contribution is those band points multiplied by the plan's `point_value_cents`, multiplied by the band's `rate_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up **per band**; the contributions are summed; the running lower bound advances to the band's ceiling; and the walk stops once the lower bound reaches the volume. Step two adds a rank bump computed on the **whole** volume rather than on the top band alone: when `paid_as_rank_code` is not null and its `rank_order` is at least 2 — that is, `SENIOR_CONSULTANT` or higher — the bump is the full `net_personal_volume_points` multiplied by `point_value_cents`, multiplied by the plan's `senior_rank_bump_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up once; otherwise the bump is 0. Step three caps the result: the capped amount is the smaller of the plan's `pv_bonus_cap_cents` and the sum of the banded total and the bump. Step four applies the probation haircut **after** the cap: when the earning consultant's `status` is `PROBATION`, `pv_bonus_cents` is the capped amount multiplied by the plan's `probation_keep_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up; for every other status `pv_bonus_cents` is the capped amount unchanged.

**Multi-level overrides** pay the earner a share of each downline consultant's net personal volume, level by level, and this is where the plan's compression mode matters. If the earner's `paid_as_rank_code` is null, `override_total_cents` is 0 and `override_breakdown` is empty. Otherwise the earner's paid-as rung supplies a count of `override_levels` and a one-indexed list of `override_bp_by_level` rates; if `override_levels` is 0, as it is for `CONSULTANT`, `override_total_cents` is 0 and `override_breakdown` is empty. A downline node counts as **payline qualified** when its consultant's status is not `TERMINATED`, it has a qualification row in this period, and that row's `net_personal_volume_points` is at least the plan's `payline_min_pv_points`. The amount a node generates at a given level is that node's `net_personal_volume_points` multiplied by the plan's `point_value_cents`, multiplied by the rate for that level, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up per paid node. Every descendant of the earner is considered at unlimited depth. For each descendant, its genealogical level relative to the earner is the descendant's `genealogy_depth` minus the earner's `genealogy_depth`, which is 1 for a direct child, and its skipped count is the number of nodes strictly between the earner and that descendant on the genealogy path that are not payline qualified.

Under `DYNAMIC` compression the paid level of a payline-qualified descendant is its genealogical level minus its skipped count: the descendant is paid at the level it would occupy if every non-qualified ancestor between it and the earner were removed from the tree, so deep volume is pulled up into a nearer, better-paying band. A compressed level still has to land inside the earner's ladder: a descendant whose paid level exceeds `override_levels` generates no payment and no breakdown element, exactly as under `STATIC`. A non-qualified node generates neither a payment nor a forfeiture, so `DYNAMIC` contributes nothing to the run's `forfeited_to_company_cents`.

Under `STATIC` compression the paid level of a payline-qualified descendant is its literal genealogical level, with no compression at all, so deep qualified volume pays at a worse rate — and pays nothing once its genealogical level exceeds the earner's `override_levels`. In exchange, the entitlement attached to a gap does not vanish: for every **non**-qualified descendant whose genealogical level is at or below the earner's `override_levels`, the amount that node would have generated at its own genealogical level is added to the run's `forfeited_to_company_cents` instead of being paid to anyone. This is why `distributable_pool_cents` — gross plus forfeited — is the honest measure of what the plan distributed, and why a `STATIC` run and a `DYNAMIC` run over identical volume disagree on both the statement totals and the company's share.

In both modes the statement records one `override_breakdown` element per paid downline node, each carrying `source_consultant_id`, `paid_level`, `volume_points` and `amount_cents`, ordered by `paid_level` ascending and then by the source consultant's `created_at` ascending. `override_total_cents` is the sum of the breakdown's `amount_cents` values. Descendants that generate no payment produce no breakdown element.

**Withholding** turns gross into net. `gross_commission_cents` is `retail_profit_cents` plus `pv_bonus_cents` plus `override_total_cents`. `withhold_rate_bp` is the plan's `withhold_high_bp` when `gross_commission_cents` is strictly above the plan's `withhold_tier_threshold_cents`, and the plan's `withhold_low_bp` when it is at or below that threshold — a statement sitting exactly on the threshold takes the low rate. The tier amount is `gross_commission_cents` multiplied by `withhold_rate_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up once. `withholding_cents` is the smaller of that tier amount and the consultant's outstanding `clawback_balance_cents`, because withholding exists only to recover that balance and never to create a surplus; a consultant who owes nothing has `withholding_cents` of 0 whatever the rate. `net_payable_cents` is `gross_commission_cents` minus `withholding_cents`.

**The approval decision** is reserved to a `FinanceController` and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `decision_note`. The run must be `CALCULATED`; then the segregation rule of §2.11 applies, and a caller whose `X-User-ID` equals `calculated_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; then, for an `APPROVE` decision only, no consultant holding an `ACTIVE` statement of the run may have status `SUSPENDED`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_SUSPENDED_EARNER_BLOCKS_APPROVAL` — a suspended earner must be resolved before the money moves. A `REJECT` decision is never blocked by a suspended earner. `APPROVE` drives `CALCULATED → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id`, and sets every `ACTIVE` statement's `payout_status` to `PAYABLE`. `REJECT` drives `CALCULATED → DRAFT` so the analyst can recalculate, sets every `ACTIVE` statement to `SUPERSEDED` with `payout_status` back to `PENDING`, resets all run totals and `statement_count` to 0, and clears `compression_mode_snapshot` to null.

**Paying** an approved run is reserved to a `FinanceController` and takes no payload; it drives `APPROVED → PAID`. Per `ACTIVE` statement, the disbursement test is evaluated against the consultant's `payout_hold` **as it stood at the start of the transition**, before any recovery from this run has been applied. The clawback recovery itself happens for every statement regardless of whether the net amount is disbursed: the consultant's `clawback_balance_cents` is decreased by the statement's `withholding_cents`, and `payout_hold` is then re-derived as true exactly when `clawback_balance_cents` is greater than 0. Then, when the statement's `net_payable_cents` is at or above the plan's `min_payout_cents` and the consultant's `payout_hold` was false at the start of the transition, `paid_cents` is set to `net_payable_cents`, `carry_forward_cents` is 0, `payout_status` becomes `PAID`, and the consultant's `lifetime_commission_paid_cents` rises by the paid amount. Otherwise — a net amount below the minimum payout, or a consultant who was on hold — `paid_cents` is 0, `carry_forward_cents` is set to `net_payable_cents`, `payout_status` becomes `CARRIED_FORWARD`, and the consultant's own `carry_forward_cents` rises by that amount. The run then refreshes `paid_total_cents` and `carry_forward_total_cents` over its `ACTIVE` statements and stamps `paid_at` from the logical clock.

**Reversing** a paid run is reserved to a `FinanceController`, requires a `reversal_reason`, and drives `PAID → REVERSED`. Every `ACTIVE` statement of the run moves `ACTIVE → VOIDED` with `payout_status` also set to `VOIDED`. For each statement whose `payout_status` was `PAID`, the consultant's `lifetime_commission_paid_cents` is decreased by that statement's `paid_cents` and their `clawback_balance_cents` is increased by the same amount, after which `payout_hold` is set to true when `clawback_balance_cents` is above 0 — disbursed money becomes a receivable. For each statement whose `payout_status` was `CARRIED_FORWARD`, the consultant's `carry_forward_cents` is decreased by that statement's `carry_forward_cents`. The run's `reversed_total_cents` is set to what `paid_total_cents` had been and `reversed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Reversal also reaches the disclosure side: every `PUBLISHED` income disclosure statement of this run moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` with `supersede_reason` set to `RUN_REVERSED`, because a reversed run's earnings distribution may no longer stand as published.

Runs are listable and filterable by `qualification_period_id`, `period_code` and `status`. Statements are listable and filterable by `commission_run_id`, `consultant_id`, `period_code`, `status`, `payout_status` and `paid_as_rank_code`; supplying a run together with a consultant addresses exactly one `ACTIVE` statement per calculation generation.

### 3.6. Inventory Buybacks, Volume Reversal and Clawback

The company's statutory obligation to buy back unsold inventory is exercised through a **buyback request**, raised by the returning `Consultant` or by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `consultant_id` and `lines` of 1 to 20 elements. Each line the client supplies carries a `product_order_id` naming the original purchase, a `product_id`, a `quantity` of at least 1, and a `condition` of `RESALABLE`, `OPENED` or `DAMAGED`; the server appends `unit_wholesale_price_cents`, `volume_points_per_unit`, `line_wholesale_cents` and `line_volume_points`.

Eligibility is unusual in one respect: a consultant whose status is `TERMINATED` may still raise a buyback, because the statutory right to sell inventory back survives termination. Only `SUSPENDED` is refused here, with `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_ORDER_ELIGIBLE` — which is the same code the order endpoints use for the wider set of ineligible statuses. After that status check the line rules run in order. Every line's `product_order_id` must name an order belonging to the same consultant, whose `order_type` is `PERSONAL_INVENTORY`, whose `payment_status` is `PAID` or `REFUNDED_PARTIAL`, and whose lines actually contain the referenced `product_id`; any of those failing is `ERR_BUYBACK_LINE_INVALID`, since only the consultant's own paid inventory purchases may be returned. Then every referenced product must have `buyback_eligible` true, or `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_BUYBACK_ELIGIBLE`, because consumables and literature are excluded from the obligation. Then every referenced order's `placed_at` must be at most the plan's `buyback_window_days` before the logical clock, or `ERR_BUYBACK_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Then, line by line in submitted order, the line's `quantity` must be at or below the corresponding order line's `quantity` minus its `returned_quantity`, or `ERR_BUYBACK_QUANTITY_EXCEEDED`; and the line's `condition` must be `RESALABLE`, or `ERR_BUYBACK_CONDITION_INVALID`. The quantity test is evaluated before the condition test on each line.

The settlement is priced immediately at request time, in five ordered steps, so the consultant sees the restocking fee and the pro rata commission clawback before the company decides. Step one: `eligible_wholesale_cents` is the sum over lines of `unit_wholesale_price_cents` multiplied by `quantity`, `requested_volume_points` is the sum over lines of `volume_points_per_unit` multiplied by `quantity`, and `gross_refund_cents` is `eligible_wholesale_cents` multiplied by the plan's `buyback_rate_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. Step two: `restocking_fee_bp` is the plan's `restock_fee_bp` when `gross_refund_cents` is strictly above the plan's `restock_free_limit_cents`, and 0 when it is at or below that limit; `restocking_fee_cents` is `gross_refund_cents` multiplied by `restocking_fee_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. The tier is keyed on the **gross** refund of step one, before anything is netted; re-deriving the tier from the net refund is the classic error and produces a different fee. Step three: let the reference statement be the consultant's commission statement for the period named by `volume_reversal_period_code` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `VOIDED`, taking the most recent by `created_at` when several exist, and let the reference row be the consultant's qualification row for that same period. `commission_clawback_cents` is 0 when no such statement exists, when the consultant has no qualification row for that period at all, or when the reference row's `net_personal_volume_points` is at or below 0; otherwise it is the statement's `pv_bonus_cents` multiplied by `requested_volume_points`, divided by the row's `net_personal_volume_points`, rounded half up, and then capped at the statement's `pv_bonus_cents` so the company never recovers more bonus than it paid. Step four: `net_refund_cents` is the greater of 0 and `gross_refund_cents` minus `restocking_fee_cents` minus `commission_clawback_cents`, so a refund is never negative. Step five: `unrecovered_clawback_cents` is the greater of 0 and the sum of `restocking_fee_cents` and `commission_clawback_cents` minus `gross_refund_cents` — the shortfall the refund could not absorb, which becomes a receivable against the consultant. Steps four and five are exact; steps one, two and three each round half up. At creation `volume_reversal_period_code` is set to the `posted_period_code` of the referenced orders — taken from the order named by the first submitted line when the lines draw on orders posted to different periods — which is the period the returned volume will be subtracted from.

A request's `status` runs `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `REFUNDED`, starting at `REQUESTED`. **The buyback decision** is reserved to a `FinanceController` and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` plus an optional `decision_reason`. The request must be `REQUESTED`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_BUYBACK_NOT_REQUESTED`; and a `DENY` decision must supply a `decision_reason`, since denials must be justified in writing whereas approvals need no reason, or it is refused with `ERR_DECISION_REASON_REQUIRED`. `APPROVE` drives `REQUESTED → APPROVED` and records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_reason`; `DENY` drives `REQUESTED → DENIED` and records the same two fields, leaving every settlement figure computed but never disbursed.

**Executing the refund** is reserved to a `FinanceController`, requires `status` `APPROVED` — anything else is `ERR_BUYBACK_NOT_APPROVED` — and drives `APPROVED → REFUNDED`, stamping `refunded_at` from the logical clock. Its cascade is the widest in the system and every branch of it is required. On the order side, each referenced order line's `returned_quantity` is increased by the buyback quantity, and the order's `returned_volume_points` — the sum over its lines of `volume_points_per_unit` multiplied by `returned_quantity` — and `refunded_cents` — the cumulative `net_refund_cents` of refunded buybacks drawn against it — are recomputed; the order's `payment_status` becomes `REFUNDED_FULL` when every line is fully returned and `REFUNDED_PARTIAL` otherwise, and its `fulfillment_status` becomes `RETURNED_FULL` or `RETURNED_PARTIAL` on the same test. On the volume side, the consultant's qualification row for the period named by `volume_reversal_period_code` — created first if the consultant has none — has its `returned_volume_points` increased by `requested_volume_points` and its `net_personal_volume_points` recomputed, and the period's `qualification_count` and `total_net_personal_volume_points` are refreshed from its rows whatever its status, since those two totals are defined over the rows and are not among the aggregates §2.10 freezes. What happens next depends on that period's status. When it is `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, `group_volume_points` and `active_leg_count` are recomputed for the consultant and every ancestor, per §2.10. When it is `LOCKED` or `CLOSED`, the frozen aggregates are left alone and the correction is made visible instead: the period's `correction_pending` is set to true, every commission run of that period whose `status` is `PAID` has `correction_flagged` set to true, and every `PUBLISHED` income disclosure statement of such a run moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` with `supersede_reason` set to `PERIOD_CORRECTION`. Finally, on the ledger side, the consultant's `clawback_balance_cents` is increased by the request's `unrecovered_clawback_cents` and `payout_hold` is re-derived as true exactly when that balance is above 0.

Buyback requests are listable and filterable by `consultant_id`, `status` and `volume_reversal_period_code`.

### 3.7. Compliance, the Disciplinary Ladder and Appeals

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a **compliance case** against a consultant with a `consultant_id`, a `claim_type` of `INCOME_CLAIM`, `PRODUCT_CLAIM`, `PRICING_VIOLATION` or `CROSS_RECRUITING`, and an `evidence_reference` pointing at the evidence bundle. The subject consultant's status must not be `TERMINATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_ALREADY_TERMINATED`; a terminated consultant may not be made the subject of a new case. Opening a case increments the consultant's `open_case_count` by 1.

A case's `status` runs `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `SANCTIONED`, `DISMISSED` and `APPEALED`, starting at `OPEN`, and the path from `SANCTIONED` back through `APPEALED` into `UNDER_REVIEW` makes the machine cyclic. **Moving a case into review** is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`, requires `status` `OPEN` or `APPEALED` — anything else is `ERR_CASE_NOT_REVIEWABLE` — drives `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEALED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and sets `reviewed_by_user_id` to the caller's `X-User-ID`.

**Deciding a case** is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` and carries a `decision` of `SANCTION` or `DISMISS`, plus `sanction_level`, `effective_from`, `effective_until` and `decision_reason` as conditionally required or optional inputs. The case must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. For the `SANCTION` branch the remaining checks then run in this order. All three of `sanction_level`, `effective_from` and `effective_until` must be supplied, or `ERR_SANCTION_FIELDS_REQUIRED`. The requested sanction's ladder position must be strictly greater than the consultant's `highest_sanction_order`, or `ERR_DISCIPLINARY_ESCALATION_REQUIRED`: the ladder runs `WARNING` at position 1, `PROBATION` at 2, `SUSPENSION` at 3 and `TERMINATION` at 4, and it may neither repeat a level nor step down. `effective_until` must be strictly after `effective_from`, or `ERR_SANCTION_WINDOW_INVALID`. The closed window from `effective_from` through `effective_until` must neither intersect any other sanctioned window of the same consultant nor begin within the plan's `sanction_cooloff_days` after such a window's `effective_until`, or `ERR_SANCTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; "other sanctioned window" means another case of that consultant whose `status` is `SANCTIONED` at this instant, so a window carried by a case since `DISMISSED` or `APPEALED` no longer conflicts. And where the sanction level is `TERMINATION`, the termination safety rule of §3.8 applies as well, refusing the decision with `ERR_APPROVED_RUN_BLOCKS_TERMINATION` while an approved-but-unpaid run covers a period in which the consultant holds an `ACTIVE` statement. As §2.8 notes, the escalation rule outranks both window rules.

A `SANCTION` decision drives `UNDER_REVIEW → SANCTIONED` and records `sanction_level`, `sanction_order` (the ladder position of that level, 0 when unsanctioned), `effective_from`, `effective_until` and `decision_reason`. It sets `appeal_deadline_at` to `effective_from` plus the plan's `appeal_window_days`, rendered as a computed instant with exactly three fractional digits whatever spelling `effective_from` arrived in, stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock, and decrements the consultant's `open_case_count` by 1 without ever taking it below 0. It then updates the consultant per the sanction level, and in every branch `highest_sanction_order` only ever rises to the ladder position of the level imposed. A `WARNING` sanction changes nothing else about the consultant: status, `active_sanction_level` and `active_sanction_until` are all left as they were, so a warning is recorded on the ladder without restricting anything. A `PROBATION` sanction on an `ACTIVE` consultant drives `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, sets `active_sanction_level` to `PROBATION` and `active_sanction_until` to the case's `effective_until`, raises `highest_sanction_order` to 2 if it was lower, and has the standing effect that future personal volume bonuses are multiplied by the plan's `probation_keep_bp` while the consultant remains `PROBATION`. A `SUSPENSION` sanction on an `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` consultant drives `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` or `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, sets `active_sanction_level` to `SUSPENSION` and `active_sanction_until` to the case's `effective_until`, raises `highest_sanction_order` to 3 if it was lower, and bars the consultant from creating orders or buyback requests while suspended. Those two status transitions are the only ones either level drives: a consultant standing on any other status — `INACTIVE`, for instance — keeps it, while `active_sanction_level`, `active_sanction_until` and `highest_sanction_order` are recorded exactly as described. A `TERMINATION` sanction drives the consultant to `TERMINATED` with the full cascade described in §3.8 and a `termination_reason` of `DISCIPLINARY`, sets `active_sanction_level` to `TERMINATION` and `active_sanction_until` to null, and raises `highest_sanction_order` to 4.

A `DISMISS` decision drives `UNDER_REVIEW → DISMISSED`, stamps `resolved_at`, records `decision_reason`, and decrements `open_case_count` by 1. Where the case being dismissed had previously imposed `PROBATION` or `SUSPENSION`, the consultant's status returns to `ACTIVE` and both `active_sanction_level` and `active_sanction_until` are cleared to null. Two things a dismissal deliberately does not do: `highest_sanction_order` is not reduced, because the escalation ladder is permanent, and a `TERMINATION` is not automatically undone, because restoring a terminated consultant remains the separate act described in §3.8.

**Appealing** is the one write a `Consultant` may perform on a compliance case, and only on their own: the acting consultant's identity must equal the case's `consultant_id`. The case must be `SANCTIONED`, or the appeal is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_SANCTIONED`; the logical clock must be at or before `appeal_deadline_at`, or `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`; and `appeal_count` must be 0, since one appeal is permitted per case, or `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED`. The payload carries an `appeal_statement`. Filing drives `SANCTIONED → APPEALED`, increments `appeal_count` by 1, and increments the consultant's `open_case_count` by 1. The sanction is expressly **not** stayed while the appeal is pending: there is no suspension of execution, and the consultant remains under whatever status the sanction imposed until a further review and decision says otherwise.

Cases are listable and filterable by `consultant_id`, `status`, `claim_type` and `sanction_level`.

### 3.8. Termination, Downline Reassignment, Reinstatement and Rank Overrides

A consultant's `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED`, `INACTIVE` and `TERMINATED`, and no value is unconditionally terminal: `TERMINATED` becomes absorbing only once the plan's `reinstatement_window_days` have elapsed since `terminated_at`, and inside that window reinstatement is the single exit edge. `SUSPENDED` also exits automatically, back to `ACTIVE`, when a period lock finds `active_sanction_until` strictly before the logical clock, as described in §3.4.

**Termination** may be performed by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin` with a `termination_reason` of `VOLUNTARY`, `ADMINISTRATIVE` or `DISCIPLINARY`, and it is also reached as the fourth rung of the disciplinary ladder. Two checks guard it, in this order: the consultant must not already be `TERMINATED`, or `ERR_CONSULTANT_ALREADY_TERMINATED`; and no commission run in status `APPROVED` may cover a period in which this consultant holds an `ACTIVE` statement, or `ERR_APPROVED_RUN_BLOCKS_TERMINATION`, because an approved-but-unpaid run must be settled before the earner's genealogy is rewritten under it.

Termination's cascade is the point at which the genealogy changes shape, and every branch matters. The consultant's `status` becomes `TERMINATED` — from `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE` — `terminated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `termination_reason` is recorded. Every direct downline consultant whose status is not `TERMINATED` is then reassigned upward: their `sponsor_consultant_id` is set to the terminated consultant's own `sponsor_consultant_id`, which is null when the terminated consultant was a root; their `reassigned_from_sponsor_consultant_id` is set to the terminated consultant's identifier, retained so that a later reinstatement can find the edge again; and their `reassignment_count` is incremented by 1. `upline_path` and `genealogy_depth` are recomputed for every reassigned consultant and, transitively, for every descendant of a reassigned consultant. `direct_downline_count` is recomputed on both ends: it falls to 0 on the terminated consultant and rises on the receiving grandparent by the number of children it inherited. For every qualification period whose status is `OPEN` or `REOPENED`, the qualification rows of every ancestor of the moved subtree have `group_volume_points` and `active_leg_count` recomputed. When the termination arrived through a sanction, `active_sanction_level` is set to `TERMINATION` and `active_sanction_until` to null. And the plan's `consultant_count` is decremented by 1.

**Reinstatement** is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`, takes an optional `reinstatement_note`, and is guarded by four checks in this order: the consultant's status must be `TERMINATED`, or `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_TERMINATED`; the logical clock must be at or before `terminated_at` plus the plan's `reinstatement_window_days`, or `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, after which the consultant must re-enroll from scratch; `clawback_balance_cents` must be 0, or `ERR_CLAWBACK_OUTSTANDING`, since outstanding receivables must be settled first; and no compliance case of the consultant may be in `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEALED`, or `ERR_OPEN_CASE_BLOCKS_REINSTATEMENT`.

Reinstatement drives `TERMINATED → ACTIVE`, stamps `reinstated_at` from the logical clock, and increments `reinstatement_count` by 1. The upline edge is restored asymmetrically and deliberately: `sponsor_consultant_id` is set back to `reassigned_from_sponsor_consultant_id` when that consultant's status is not `TERMINATED`; failing that, to the nearest non-terminated ancestor of that consultant; and failing that, to null. The restored sponsor's `direct_downline_count` is incremented by 1. The downline that was reassigned away at termination is **not** returned — reassignment is permanent, and the reinstated consultant rebuilds their organisation from the edge up. `rank_code` and `rank_order` are reset to the `rank_order` 1 rung with `rank_source` set to `REINSTATEMENT`, and `consecutive_qualified_count` and `grace_periods_used` are reset to 0. `active_sanction_level` and `active_sanction_until` are cleared, while `terminated_at` is retained for audit. The plan's `consultant_count` is incremented by 1.

**An administrative rank override** is reserved to a `PlatformAdmin` and takes a `rank_code` plus an optional `adjustment_reason`. It exists to recognise ranks earned on a legacy plan and to make any rung's override structure reachable without waiting out the plan's `consecutive_qualifying_periods` closed periods. Two checks guard it: the consultant's status must be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `TERMINATED`, or `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_ADJUSTABLE`, since ranks are not adjusted under suspension or after termination; and the submitted `rank_code` must exist in the consultant's plan `rank_ladder`, since ranks are plan-scoped, or `ERR_RANK_CODE_UNKNOWN`. On success `rank_code` and `rank_order` are set to the requested rung, `rank_source` becomes `ADMIN_ADJUSTED`, and `consecutive_qualified_count` and `grace_periods_used` are reset to 0. The adjustment changes no other dimension: a consultant who was `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `INACTIVE` stays in that same status, and the new rank changes future `paid_as_rank_code` outcomes without ever rewriting a statement that has already been calculated.

### 3.9. Income Disclosure: Publishing What the Field Actually Earned

The income disclosure statement is the company's public account of what its consultants earn, and it is drafted from a run that has already been paid. A `CommissionAnalyst` creates one with a `commission_run_id`, a `population_basis` of either `ALL_ENROLLED` or `PAID_ONLY`, and the version precondition `expected_commission_run_version` described in §2.7. After the version check, the referenced run's `status` must be `PAID`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_RUN_NOT_PAID`; and the population the chosen basis selects must be non-empty, or the draft is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_POPULATION_EMPTY` — a real possibility under `PAID_ONLY` when every statement of the run was carried forward. Creation denormalises `period_code` from the run, computes the full metric set immediately, and increments the run's `version` by 1; a new draft carries `revision_number` 0 and null `submitted_by_user_id`, `legal_reviewer_user_id`, `published_at`, `superseded_by_id`, `supersede_reason` and `revision_note`.

Earnings in a disclosure mean **gross** commission: each population member contributes the `gross_commission_cents` of their `ACTIVE` statement in the run, before withholding and before the minimum-payout carry-forward. The two population bases are genuinely different policies and disagree on every published figure. Under `ALL_ENROLLED`, the population is every consultant of the run's plan whose status is not `TERMINATED` and whose `enrollment_completed_at` is strictly before the period's `period_end`; a member with no `ACTIVE` statement in the run contributes earnings of 0, so `zero_earner_count` can be positive and the mean and median are pulled down. This is the basis that includes non-earners. Under `PAID_ONLY`, the population is only those consultants whose `ACTIVE` statement in the run has `payout_status` `PAID`; statements carried forward below the minimum payout and consultants with no statement at all are excluded entirely, so `population_count` is smaller, `zero_earner_count` is normally 0, and the mean, median and ninetieth percentile all come out higher. The two bases therefore disagree on `population_count`, `mean_earnings_cents`, `median_earnings_cents`, `top_decile_share_bp` and every bucket count for one and the same run.

Given the selected population, let the earnings values be sorted in ascending order and let the population size be `population_count`. `total_earnings_cents` is the sum of every member's earnings. `zero_earner_count` is the number of members whose earnings are exactly 0. `mean_earnings_cents` is `total_earnings_cents` divided by `population_count`, rounded half up. `median_earnings_cents` is, when the population size is odd, the middle value of the ascending list — the value at the zero-based position obtained by halving one less than the size and discarding any remainder — and, when the size is even, the average of the two central values, that is their sum divided by two and rounded half up. `p90_earnings_cents` is the nearest-rank ninetieth percentile: take ninety times the population size, divide by one hundred, round that quotient up to a whole number, subtract 1, and read the value at that zero-based position in the ascending list. For the top decile, let the decile size be the greater of 1 and the population size divided by ten rounded up; the top decile total is the sum of that many largest earnings values, and `top_decile_share_bp` is 0 when `total_earnings_cents` is 0 and otherwise the top decile total multiplied by 10,000, divided by `total_earnings_cents`, rounded half up.

`distribution_buckets` is always four bands in exactly this order: earnings of exactly 0; earnings from 1 through 9,999 cents; earnings from 10,000 through 49,999 cents; and earnings of 50,000 cents and above with no upper limit. Each element carries its `lower_cents` and `upper_cents` bounds — the fourth band's `upper_cents` being null — together with a `consultant_count` of members whose earnings fall inside the inclusive band and a `share_bp` equal to that count multiplied by 10,000, divided by `population_count`, rounded half up.

A disclosure's `status` runs `DRAFT`, `LEGAL_REVIEW`, `PUBLISHED` and `SUPERSEDED`, starting at `DRAFT`. **Submitting for legal review** is reserved to a `CommissionAnalyst`, requires `status` `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_DRAFT` — drives `DRAFT → LEGAL_REVIEW`, and records `submitted_by_user_id` as the caller's `X-User-ID` for the segregation test.

**The legal decision** is reserved to a `LegalReviewer` and carries a `decision` of `PUBLISH` or `REQUEST_REVISION` plus an optional `revision_note`. Its checks run in this order: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — a null `submitted_by_user_id`, as on a draft never submitted, matches nobody and so passes this check straight through to the next one; the underlying run must still be `PAID`, since a reversed run may not be disclosed, or `ERR_RUN_NOT_PAID`; the statement's `status` must be `LEGAL_REVIEW`, or `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_IN_REVIEW`; and a `REQUEST_REVISION` decision must supply a `revision_note`, since the analyst must be told what to change, or `ERR_REVISION_NOTE_REQUIRED`.

A `PUBLISH` decision drives `LEGAL_REVIEW → PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` from the logical clock and records `legal_reviewer_user_id`. It also retires the previous publication: any other `PUBLISHED` statement of the same run moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` with `supersede_reason` set to `NEWER_PUBLICATION` and `superseded_by_id` pointing at the statement just published. A `REQUEST_REVISION` decision drives `LEGAL_REVIEW → DRAFT`, increments `revision_number` by 1, records the `revision_note`, clears `submitted_by_user_id` to null so the segregation test is re-armed against whoever resubmits, and recomputes every distribution metric from current data rather than reusing the figures from the previous round trip.

A published disclosure reaches `SUPERSEDED` by exactly three routes, and `supersede_reason` names which one: `NEWER_PUBLICATION` when a newer statement of the same run is published, in which case `superseded_by_id` is set and points at that newer statement; `RUN_REVERSED` when the underlying run is reversed, per §3.5; and `PERIOD_CORRECTION` when a post-close volume correction reaches the run's period through a buyback refund, per §3.6. `superseded_by_id` is set only in the `NEWER_PUBLICATION` case and remains null for the other two.

Disclosures are listable and filterable by `commission_run_id`, `superseded_by_id`, `period_code`, `population_basis` and `status`; filtering by `superseded_by_id` finds the statement a given publication replaced.
