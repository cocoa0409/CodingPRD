# Design System Governance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A shared design system only pays for itself when the organisation can answer three questions on demand: what is in the inventory, how mature is each piece of it, and who is actually using it. Today those answers live in spreadsheets, in the heads of two or three long-serving designers, and in screenshots pasted into chat threads. The design system team cannot tell a product group whether the button it is about to fork already exists at production quality on iOS; the accessibility lead cannot prove which components have a current manual assistive-technology pass; and nobody can say whether last quarter's "adoption push" moved any real numbers. This document specifies the Design System Governance API, the system of record that replaces all of that guesswork with enforced process.

The API models the full governance surface of a mature design system. Components are inventoried with two independent state dimensions — a **maturity** dimension that certifies quality and a **lifecycle** dimension that tracks deprecation — and each component's realisation on the web, iOS and Android platforms is tracked separately so that "coverage" becomes a computed fact rather than an opinion. Contributions flow through a mandatory pipeline of proposal, design critique, engineering review and acceptance, with segregation of duties enforced so that no single operator can shepherd their own change through every gate. Accessibility conformance is scored from filed evidence rather than asserted, and the score that governs promotion is deliberately harsher than the score an engineer sees on their own implementation. Breaking changes propagate through a declared dependency graph and are absorbed by a versioning policy that switches behaviour once a release crosses a batching threshold. Deprecations must serve a notice period computed from the component's maturity and platform footprint, and every deprecation is reversible with exact compensation of the blocks it imposed.

Underneath all of it sits a technical-debt ledger. Teams that want to ship a one-off component, fork a system component, or hard-code a raw colour instead of a token may do so — but only through an approved exception that charges debt points against a team budget, and a team that exhausts its budget is frozen out of the contribution pipeline until it repays. Every deadline, expiry and notice window in the system reads from a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so governance behaviour is exactly reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and versioning, precision, validation precedence, and the cascade discipline that governs multi-entity writes. Everything here applies across the whole API unless a later section narrows it.

The API is mounted under `/design-system`, with the sole exception of the logical-clock test-driving capability described in §2.4.

### 2.1. Governance Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it acts. A header that is absent, sent as an empty string, or sent as whitespace only is treated as absent, and the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The one carve-out is the logical-clock capability of §2.4, which does not reject a request for absent or blank headers; when headers are supplied to it they must still name `SystemAdmin`.

Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- `SystemAdmin` — the design-system platform operator. Owns team registration, token-set provisioning, release cutting and publication, and the logical clock. A `SystemAdmin` never reviews or accepts a contribution and cannot approve an exception grant.
- `DesignSystemLead` — steward of the design system. Owns the component inventory, the token architecture, maturity promotion, deprecation planning and migration guides, proposal acceptance and exception-grant decisions. A `DesignSystemLead` can neither cut nor publish a release, and cannot file a review verdict.
- `DesignCritiqueReviewer` — the design-critique gate reviewer. May file review verdicts whose `stage` is `DESIGN_CRITIQUE`, and only while the proposal sits at that gate.
- `EngineeringReviewer` — the engineering-review gate reviewer. May file review verdicts whose `stage` is `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, and only after the design critique has approved. Cannot approve at the design-critique gate and cannot accept a proposal.
- `ContributingTeam` — a product team that consumes and contributes to the design system. Files its own adoption scans, opens and submits its own proposals, declares and ships implementations of components it owns, and requests and repays its own exception grants. Cannot promote maturity, accept proposals, decide exceptions, or publish releases.

If `X-User-Role` is not one of those five strings, or is a valid role that does not appear in the called endpoint's authorised-role list in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `ContributingTeam`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Team`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Team` compare the resource's team foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `DesignSystemLead`, `DesignCritiqueReviewer` or `EngineeringReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a transition demands an audit trail — as `reviewer_user_id` on a review record, as `design_critique_reviewer_user_id` or `engineering_reviewer_user_id` on a proposal, and as `decided_by_user_id` on an exception grant — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

**Ownership enforcement.** On every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `ContributingTeam`, a caller acting as `ContributingTeam` must own the target resource. Ownership is resolved as follows: for a team-scoped operation the `Team`'s own `id`; for a component the component's `owning_team_id`; for a platform implementation the `owning_team_id` of the component named by its `component_id`; for a dependency edge the `owning_team_id` of the component named by its `consumer_component_id`; for a contribution proposal its `author_team_id`; for an exception grant its `requesting_team_id`; and for an adoption scan its `team_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Callers acting in the other four roles are not ownership-checked — an adoption scan filed by a `SystemAdmin` on a team's behalf, for instance, is accepted without any ownership comparison.

**Segregation of duties.** The two review gates on one proposal must be filed by different operators, and the operator who accepts a proposal must be a third party. Concretely: a reviewer filing the `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` verdict must present an `X-User-ID` different from the proposal's recorded `design_critique_reviewer_user_id`; a reviewer filing the `DESIGN_CRITIQUE` verdict must present an `X-User-ID` different from the recorded `engineering_reviewer_user_id`; and the `DesignSystemLead` who accepts the proposal must present an `X-User-ID` equal to neither of those two recorded reviewer ids. Any of these collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The authoring team never files a review on its own proposal, because the reviewer roles and `ContributingTeam` are disjoint.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints paginate through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the first returned row; it defaults to 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific list endpoint declares otherwise, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Three lists declare otherwise and are documented with their own ordering in §3: a team's adoption scans come back newest measurement window first, an implementation's accessibility evidence comes back in ascending `audit_sequence` order, and a proposal's review records come back in ascending `created_at` order.

Every list response includes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching those filters remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock as the Sole Time Source

Nothing in this system reads the host's wall clock. A server-held **logical clock** is the only time source: every deadline comparison, notice window, expiry check, and every system-stamped instant — `submitted_at`, `accepted_at`, `shipped_at`, `audited_at`, `scanned_at`, `decided_at`, `repaid_at`, `published_at`, `rescinded_at`, `docs_last_reviewed_at`, `conformance_baseline_at` — is taken from it. The clock's fixed default value at server start is `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it must never fall back to real time.

A `SystemAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`. The clock may move backwards as well as forwards; no monotonicity is assumed. The response reports the new value as `current_utc`, together with `expired_exception_grant_ids` — the ids of the exception grants that this move expired — and `expired_count`, the length of that list. Moving the clock to or past an `APPROVED` exception grant's `expires_at` transitions that grant to `EXPIRED`; when a single move expires several grants they are processed in ascending `created_at` order, so that any budget arithmetic within the same request observes a deterministic sequence. Expiry does not reduce a team's outstanding debt — see §3.9.

All datetime values, whether supplied by a client or stamped by the server, are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits. A value that is not a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource in the system carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources also carry the full audit triple:

- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only rewrite computed roll-up fields.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. This is the value that optimistic concurrency control reads (§2.6) and that the contribution process pins (§3.6).

The resources carrying all four fields are teams, components, platform implementations, dependency edges, token sets, design tokens, contribution proposals, releases, deprecation plans and exception grants.

Three resources are append-only evidence records and are never mutated after creation: review records, accessibility audits and adoption scans. **These three carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all** — the fields are absent from their payloads, not merely unchanging. When a computed value derived from one of these records changes, the change lands on the mutable parent (the proposal, the implementation, the component, the team), and it is that parent whose `updated_at` and `version` move.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Governance Parents

Two write operations act on a child row while depending on a parent row that other operators are concurrently editing. Both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the parent's `version`, and in both cases the version field is a **required** input, not an optional one — omitting it is a shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

**Accepting a contribution proposal.** A request to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/accept` must carry `expected_component_version` matching the current `version` of the `Component` named by the proposal's `target_component_id`. If the value does not match that component's current `version`, the accept is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMPONENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). No state check can substitute for this guard: two different proposals may each legitimately reach `READY_FOR_ACCEPTANCE` against the same component and each accept is individually valid, so the second accept is refused solely because the accepting client's view of the component's major version and blocked-dependency picture is out of date.

**Writing a design token.** A request to `POST /design-system/tokens` and a request to `PUT /design-system/tokens/{id}` must each carry `expected_token_set_version` matching the current `version` of the `TokenSet` the token belongs to. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TOKEN_SET_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Creating a second token with a different name is perfectly legitimate under the product semantics, so no state precondition would refuse it; the guard exists purely so that a brand or theming layer added by another author cannot be silently overwritten by an author who never observed it.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Callers read a parent's current `version` from that parent's read endpoint before attempting a guarded write.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Pinned Evaluation Order

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero.

- Technical-debt point values (`debt_budget_points`, `debt_points`, `outstanding_debt_points`, `lifetime_debt_points`) are numbers with 2 decimal places and are never negative.
- Conformance scores (`conformance_score`, `governance_conformance_score`) are numbers with 2 decimal places on the closed interval from 0.00 to 100.00. They are floored at 0.00: a computation that would yield a negative number yields 0.00 instead.
- Adoption ratios and indices (`adoption_ratio`, `adoption_index_latest`, `adoption_index_trailing`, and the indices surfaced on the team dashboard) are numbers with 2 decimal places on the closed interval from 0.00 to 100.00, rounded at each stage of their computation.
- `token_compliance_ratio` is a number with 2 decimal places on the closed interval from 0.00 to 1.00.
- `breaking_ratio` is a number with **4** decimal places on the closed interval from 0.0000 to 1.0000.
- Notice windows (`required_notice_days`, `actual_notice_days`) and every other count are integers. Day counts are floored toward zero, and every threshold comparison on them is inclusive.
- `resolved_value` is the leaf token's `raw_value` string reproduced verbatim; it is never re-formatted, re-cased, or numerically normalised.

Two evaluation orders are **pinned**, meaning an implementation that reorders the steps produces different stored numbers and is wrong even though its arithmetic is individually correct.

The first pinned order governs technical-debt roll-up: each grant's points are rounded to two decimal places once, at approval, and the team roll-up then sums those already-rounded per-grant values and rounds the sum again. Summing the unrounded products and rounding only at the end is explicitly *not* the rule, and the two orders diverge. Three exception grants that each price out at five base points multiplied by 1.25 for a single mobile platform and 0.75 for a `BETA` substitute component each freeze at 4.69 points, giving a team total of 14.07 under the pinned order; summing the unrounded products of 4.6875 and rounding once would give 14.06.

The second pinned order governs release version bumping, described fully in §3.7: the breaking-change ratio and the resulting policy tier are computed from the maturity-adjusted change classes and are **never** recomputed after the tier has escalated individual proposals. The evaluation is a single forward pass and no fixed point is sought, even though the escalation step can change the very population the ratio measured. Consider a release carrying two breaking-change proposals — one of them against an `EXPERIMENTAL` component — plus one enhancement that touches the public API: the pinned order yields a ratio of 0.3333, an `INCREMENTAL` tier and a breaking-change count of 1, whereas naively re-deriving the ratio from the unadjusted classes or iterating the steps to stability would yield 0.6667, a `BATCHED` tier and a breaking-change count of 3.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must report the violation with the **lowest** priority number in the list below. The ordering is normative: a request that both lacks permission and names a non-existent component reports the permission failure, and a request whose payload is malformed reports the shape failure rather than any downstream business rule. Implementations evaluate strictly in ascending priority order and stop at the first violation.

1. `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-empty (a header sent as an empty string or as whitespace only counts as absent) — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles **and** appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Request body and query shape: every required input field is present; every enum value is a member of its declared set; integer counts are non-negative; numbers respect their declared scale and range; datetimes are ISO 8601 UTC; semver strings match the three-part dotted numeric form; `limit` is within 1 to 100 and `offset` is at least 0; exactly one of a design token's `raw_value` and `alias_of_token_id` is non-null; a ship request's `token_compliance_ratio` is at least 0.90; a deprecation plan's `announced_at` is strictly before its `sunset_at`, which is strictly before its `removal_at`; an adoption scan's `window_start_at` is strictly before its `window_end_at`; a dependency edge's `consumer_component_id` differs from its `provider_component_id`; and a token's alias target lives in the same `token_set_id`. Any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint.
4. Every id appearing in the URL path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right — otherwise the matching 404: `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_DEPENDENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLATFORM_IMPLEMENTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOKEN_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOKEN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPRECATION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_EXCEPTION_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that carries a path id.
5. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, validated in the field order declared by that endpoint's input list — otherwise `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLATFORM_IMPLEMENTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOKEN_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOKEN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `POST /design-system/components`, `POST /design-system/components/{id}/dependencies`, `POST /design-system/tokens`, `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals`, `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/accept`, `POST /design-system/components/{id}/deprecation-plans` and `POST /design-system/exception-grants`.
6. When `X-User-Role` is `ContributingTeam`, `X-User-ID` equals the owning team id of the target resource as resolved in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `ContributingTeam`.
7. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROPOSAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_MATURITY_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_IMPLEMENTATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DEPRECATION_PLAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EXCEPTION_GRANT_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE` (409). Applies to every state-changing write.
8. The owning component's `lifecycle_state` is `ACTIVE` or `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED` when shipping an implementation, and is not `REMOVED` when approving an exception grant against it — otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_LIFECYCLE_BLOCKS_SHIP` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/platform-implementations/{id}/ship` and `POST /design-system/exception-grants/{id}/decision`.
9. The optimistic-concurrency field matches its parent's current `version`: `expected_component_version` against the target component on acceptance, `expected_token_set_version` against the token set on every token write — otherwise `ERR_STALE_COMPONENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_TOKEN_SET_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/accept`, `POST /design-system/tokens` and `PUT /design-system/tokens/{id}`.
10. The review's `stage` matches the gate implied by the proposal's current `state` — `DESIGN_CRITIQUE` only from `DESIGN_CRITIQUE`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` only from `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` — the acting role is the reviewer role for that stage, and a `BLOCK` verdict is accepted only at the `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` stage. Otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_STAGE_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/reviews`.
11. Segregation of duties: the reviewer's `X-User-ID` differs from the user id already recorded for the *other* review stage on that proposal — `design_critique_reviewer_user_id` when filing `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `engineering_reviewer_user_id` when filing `DESIGN_CRITIQUE`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/reviews`, and the same code guards the accepting lead's identity on `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/accept`.
12. `governance_conformance_score` is at least 70.00 for a `BETA` promotion and at least 90.00 for a `STABLE` promotion, inclusive at both boundaries — otherwise `ERR_A11Y_CONFORMANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/components/{id}/promote-maturity`.
13. The `AUTOMATED` `AA` accessibility audit with the greatest `audit_sequence` for the implementation exists and reports `violations_critical` equal to 0 — otherwise `ERR_A11Y_CRITICAL_VIOLATIONS_OPEN` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/platform-implementations/{id}/ship`.
14. The token set named by the proposal's `pinned_token_set_id` still has a `version` equal to the proposal's `pinned_token_set_version` recorded at the most recent submit — otherwise `ERR_PINNED_TOKEN_SET_STALE` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/accept`.
15. No deprecation plan of the component is in state `ANNOUNCED` or `SUNSET` — otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_DEPRECATION_IN_PROGRESS` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/components/{id}/promote-maturity`. The same code rejects a submit whose target component's `lifecycle_state` is neither `ACTIVE` nor `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED`.
16. The relevant component's `blocked_dependency_count` is 0 — the component being promoted, or the component owning the implementation being shipped — otherwise `ERR_BLOCKED_DEPENDENCY` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/components/{id}/promote-maturity` and `POST /design-system/platform-implementations/{id}/ship`.
17. The requested alias edge does not close a cycle inside the token set — otherwise `ERR_TOKEN_ALIAS_CYCLE` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/tokens` and `PUT /design-system/tokens/{id}`.
18. The requested dependency edge does not close a cycle in the component dependency graph, measured over the transitive closure of all dependency edges regardless of their `compatibility_state` — otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_CYCLE` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/components/{id}/dependencies`.
19. Uniqueness: a team's `slug`, a component's `slug` and a token set's `slug` are each unique across all rows of their kind; the pair of `component_id` and `platform` is unique per platform implementation; the pair of `consumer_component_id` and `provider_component_id` is unique per dependency edge; and the four-part tuple of `token_set_id`, `name`, `brand` and `mode` is unique per design token. A collision yields `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT` (409), except for the design-token tuple, which yields `ERR_TOKEN_NAME_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/teams`, `POST /design-system/components`, `POST /design-system/token-sets`, `POST /design-system/tokens`, `POST /design-system/components/{id}/platform-implementations` and `POST /design-system/components/{id}/dependencies`.
20. The alias walk from the written token terminates within 5 hops — otherwise `ERR_TOKEN_ALIAS_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/tokens` and `PUT /design-system/tokens/{id}`.
21. Platform coverage is sufficient for the requested maturity: `shipped_platform_count` is at least 1 for `BETA`, and `coverage_state` is `PARTIAL` or `FULL` for `STABLE` — otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/components/{id}/promote-maturity`.
22. On a resubmit, the proposal's `revision_round` incremented by 1 is at most 3 — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/submit`.
23. The set of `submitted_evidence_kinds` contains every member of `required_evidence_kinds` — otherwise `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/accept`.
24. A migration guide is present wherever the governance policy demands one: `migration_guide_state` is `PUBLISHED` before an `ANNOUNCED` plan may advance to `SUNSET`; a `STABLE` component may not be announced with `publish` set to false; and every carried proposal whose `effective_change_class` resolves to `MAJOR` at publish time carries `MIGRATION_GUIDE` in its `submitted_evidence_kinds`. Otherwise `ERR_MIGRATION_GUIDE_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/deprecation-plans/{id}/migration-guide`, `POST /design-system/deprecation-plans/{id}/advance` and `POST /design-system/releases/{id}/publish`.
25. `actual_notice_days` is at least `required_notice_days`, **and** the whole days from `sunset_at` to `removal_at` number at least 30 — otherwise `ERR_DEPRECATION_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/components/{id}/deprecation-plans`.
26. The new plan's half-open window from `announced_at` up to but excluding `removal_at` does not intersect the equivalent window of any deprecation plan of the same component whose state is `DRAFT`, `ANNOUNCED` or `SUNSET`; exact adjacency, where the new plan's `announced_at` equals an existing plan's `removal_at`, is permitted. Otherwise `ERR_DEPRECATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/components/{id}/deprecation-plans`.
27. The new scan's closed window from `window_start_at` through `window_end_at` inclusive does not intersect the window of any existing adoption scan with the same `team_id` and `scan_kind`; adjacency is **not** permitted here, so a new `window_start_at` exactly equal to an existing `window_end_at` is a collision. Otherwise `ERR_SCAN_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/teams/{id}/adoption-scans`.
28. The logical clock has reached the due instant for the requested advance: at or past `sunset_at` for the step into `SUNSET`, at or past `removal_at` for the step into `REMOVED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPRECATION_ADVANCE_NOT_DUE` (409). Applies to `POST /design-system/deprecation-plans/{id}/advance`.
29. The authoring team's `debt_state` is `CLEAR` — otherwise `ERR_TEAM_DEBT_FROZEN` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals` and `POST /design-system/contribution-proposals/{id}/submit` only; exception-grant requests stay open to a frozen team so that it can document the debt it already carries.
30. The requesting team's `outstanding_debt_points` plus the grant's computed points is at most that team's `debt_budget_points` — otherwise `ERR_TEAM_DEBT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /design-system/exception-grants/{id}/decision`.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Reference Existence

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the response names the **first** identifier that fails to resolve. A request against a platform implementation nested under a component reports `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND` before it ever attempts to locate the implementation. Each resource kind has its own dedicated 404: teams report `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, components report `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, dependency edges report `ERR_COMPONENT_DEPENDENCY_NOT_FOUND`, platform implementations report `ERR_PLATFORM_IMPLEMENTATION_NOT_FOUND`, token sets report `ERR_TOKEN_SET_NOT_FOUND`, design tokens report `ERR_TOKEN_NOT_FOUND`, contribution proposals report `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, releases report `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND`, deprecation plans report `ERR_DEPRECATION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` and exception grants report `ERR_EXCEPTION_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND` covers every component reference regardless of its role in the payload — a proposal's `target_component_id`, a dependency's `provider_component_id`, an exception grant's `substitute_for_component_id` and a deprecation plan's `replacement_component_id` all report the same code. `ERR_TOKEN_NOT_FOUND` likewise covers a token referenced as an `alias_of_token_id` target.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Compensating Actions

Several writes in this system mutate more than one entity. The governing rule is that a cascade proceeds in **hops**, and each hop fires because of the preceding hop's mutation rather than because of the originating call. Every row a hop touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the server performs these transitions without any client request naming the affected rows. Two multi-hop cascades exist — the four-hop cascade released by accepting a breaking change (§3.6) and the three-hop cascade released by sunsetting a provider component (§3.8) — and both are specified hop by hop in their own subsections.

Two operations in this API are **compensating actions**, meaning their job is to give back exactly what an earlier operation took. Rescinding a deprecation plan returns to `COMPATIBLE` precisely the dependency edges its sunset cascade flipped to `SUNSET_PROVIDER`, and decrements each affected consumer's `blocked_dependency_count` by precisely the amount that cascade added; the conserved quantity is the dependency-block total, recorded on the plan as `blocked_dependent_count`. Repaying an exception grant returns a team's `outstanding_debt_points` to precisely its pre-approval value while leaving `lifetime_debt_points` untouched. In both cases the compensation is exact rather than a recomputation from scratch, and in neither case is a *governance fact* reversed: a consumer component demoted from `STABLE` to `BETA` because its provider was sunset is not automatically promoted back when the sunset is rescinded, and a repaid grant's `debt_points` value stays on the row as the historic amount.

### 2.11. The Two Independent Component State Dimensions

A component carries two orthogonal state machines that advance independently of one another and never gate each other's transitions.

The **maturity dimension**, `maturity_level`, certifies quality. Its values are `EXPERIMENTAL`, `BETA` and `STABLE`; a newly registered component starts at `EXPERIMENTAL`. It advances one step at a time under explicit `DesignSystemLead` action and can be demoted from `STABLE` back to `BETA` by a governance cascade. `EXPERIMENTAL` is never re-entered once left. Whenever the dimension is demoted, `maturity_demotion_reason` records why: `UPSTREAM_SUNSET` or `CONFORMANCE_REGRESSION`. The field reads `NONE` on a component that has never been demoted, and is reset to `NONE` by every successful promotion.

The **lifecycle dimension**, `lifecycle_state`, tracks the component's place in the deprecation timeline. Its values are `ACTIVE`, `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED`, `SUNSET` and `REMOVED`; a newly registered component starts at `ACTIVE` and `REMOVED` is terminal. Every transition on this dimension is driven by a deprecation plan rather than by a direct call on the component, and two of them are reversals back to `ACTIVE` (§3.8).

Because the dimensions are independent, a `STABLE` component may be `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED`, and an `EXPERIMENTAL` component may be `SUNSET`. Promotion gates read the lifecycle dimension only through the deprecation-in-progress check of §2.8 rule 15, and deprecation gates read the maturity dimension only through the notice-window arithmetic of §3.8.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Teams, Adoption Measurement and the Team Dashboard

Everything in this system is ultimately attributed to a team. A `SystemAdmin` registers each product or design group by supplying a human-readable `name` of between 1 and 120 non-blank characters and a URL-safe `slug` in lowercase kebab-case that must be unique across every registered team; a collision is rejected with `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`. Two further fields are optional at registration. `platform_scope` declares which platforms the team ships on and is one of `WEB_ONLY`, `MOBILE_ONLY` or `ALL`, defaulting to `ALL`. `debt_budget_points` caps the outstanding technical debt the team may carry, is expressed to two decimal places, may not be negative, and defaults to 40.00. Registration initialises the whole computed surface of the row: `outstanding_debt_points` and `lifetime_debt_points` at 0.00, `debt_state` at `CLEAR`, `blocked_implementation_count` at 0, and both `adoption_index_latest` and `adoption_index_trailing` at 0.00. New teams are created with a 201 response.

Three of those computed fields are maintained by machinery described later: `blocked_implementation_count` counts the platform implementations belonging to components this team owns whose `status` is `BLOCKED` (§3.3), and the debt fields track exception grants (§3.9). The remaining pair is adoption, and it is measured rather than declared.

**Filing a measurement.** Adoption is observed through two channels: static analysis of shipped code, and linting of design source files. Either a `ContributingTeam` acting on itself or a `SystemAdmin` acting on any team files one measurement at a time, declaring the channel as `scan_kind` — `CODE_SCAN` or `DESIGN_LINT` — together with the closed measurement interval bounded inclusively by `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, where the start must fall strictly before the end. The three census counts are `system_instance_count`, the occurrences that resolved to a design-system component or token; `legacy_instance_count`, the occurrences of pre-system markup and styles; and `one_off_instance_count`, the occurrences of one-off components whether or not an exception covers them. All three must be non-negative integers. The server stamps `scanned_at` from the logical clock, computes the row's `adoption_ratio`, and increments the team's `version`. A measurement is created with a 201 response and is thereafter immutable: adoption scans are append-only evidence and carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Measurement windows may not overlap within a channel. A new scan collides with any existing scan of the same `team_id` and the same `scan_kind` whose closed window shares even a single instant with the new one, and because both endpoints of the interval are inclusive, exact adjacency is a collision too: a new `window_start_at` equal to an existing `window_end_at` is refused with `ERR_SCAN_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Different channels never collide with each other.

**The per-scan ratio.** For a single scan, add `system_instance_count`, `legacy_instance_count` and `one_off_instance_count` together to obtain the total observed population. When that total is zero the scan's `adoption_ratio` is 0.00. Otherwise the ratio is `system_instance_count` divided by that total, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places — so it is a percentage on the closed interval from 0.00 to 100.00, not a fraction.

**Recency ordering.** Within one team-and-channel group, scans are ordered most recent first by `window_end_at` descending; ties are broken by `scanned_at` descending, and any remaining tie by `created_at` descending. No row `id` ever participates in this ordering.

**The two index views.** A team's adoption index is a weighted blend of its two channels, in which `CODE_SCAN` carries a weight of 0.70 and `DESIGN_LINT` a weight of 0.30. Two variants of the blend exist and both are recomputed and stored on every scan filing.

The **latest-scan** view takes, for each of the two channels, the `adoption_ratio` of the single most recent scan in that channel's recency order, treating a channel with no scans at all as 0.00. It multiplies the code-scanning figure by 0.70 and the design-linting figure by 0.30, adds the two products, and rounds half up to two decimal places. The result is stored as `adoption_index_latest`. Under this view a single good scan moves the number immediately.

The **trailing-window** view takes, for each channel, the first two rows of that channel's recency order — or the single row available when only one exists, or nothing when the channel is empty. It averages the `adoption_ratio` values of those rows by summing them and dividing by how many were taken, rounding that per-channel average half up to two decimal places; a channel with no rows contributes 0.00. It then multiplies the code-scanning average by 0.70 and the design-linting average by 0.30, adds the two products, and rounds half up to two decimal places again. The result is stored as `adoption_index_trailing`. Because the average drags a fresh improvement backwards, a team that has just improved reads materially lower here than under the latest-scan view on exactly the same rows.

**The dashboard.** A team-level dashboard read surfaces one of those two views, selected by the optional `view` field, whose accepted values are `LATEST_SCAN` and `TRAILING_WINDOW` and which defaults to `LATEST_SCAN` when omitted. The dashboard's field semantics are pinned. `team_id` echoes the team and `view` echoes the resolved variant. `adoption_index` is that variant's weighted index. `code_scan_ratio` and `design_lint_ratio` are that same variant's per-channel inputs — the single most recent scan's ratio under the latest-scan view, the two-row average under the trailing-window view — and each reads 0.00 when its channel has no scan. `outstanding_debt_points`, `debt_state` and `blocked_implementation_count` mirror the team row verbatim. `open_exception_count` counts this team's exception grants whose `state` is `REQUESTED`. `scanned_kind_count` counts how many distinct `scan_kind` values have at least one scan for this team, and is therefore 0, 1 or 2.

A team's scan history is listable, newest measurement window first, and can be narrowed to one channel with a `scan_kind` filter alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`. That listing is also the only way to walk from a team back to the scans that reference it through `team_id`.

### 3.2. The Token Architecture: Layers, Aliases and Resolution

The visual foundations of the system live in token sets. A `SystemAdmin` or a `DesignSystemLead` provisions a **TokenSet** — the container for one coherent token architecture spanning colour, typography and spacing — with a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters and a globally unique lowercase kebab-case `slug`; a duplicate slug is `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`. Two optional fields fix the fallback layers used when a resolution read omits them: `default_brand`, one of `BASE`, `BRAND_A` or `BRAND_B`, defaulting to `BASE`; and `default_mode`, one of `LIGHT` or `DARK`, defaulting to `LIGHT`. Provisioning returns 201 and initialises `token_count` and `max_alias_depth` at 0.

Only a `DesignSystemLead` writes tokens. Each **DesignToken** names its container through `token_set_id` and carries a dotted `name` such as `color.action.primary`, a `category` of `COLOR`, `TYPOGRAPHY` or `SPACING`, and two layer coordinates: `brand`, one of `BASE`, `BRAND_A` or `BRAND_B` and defaulting to `BASE`; and `mode`, one of `LIGHT`, `DARK` or `ANY` and defaulting to `ANY`, where `ANY` means the token is eligible for both light and dark reads. A token is either a **leaf**, in which case its literal `raw_value` is set and its `alias_of_token_id` is null, or an **alias**, in which case `alias_of_token_id` names another token and `raw_value` is null. Exactly one of the two must be non-null; supplying both or neither is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An alias may only point at a token in the same `token_set_id`, so each token set is an independent graph; a cross-set alias target is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every token write also carries `expected_token_set_version` as described in §2.6.

The four-part combination of `token_set_id`, `name`, `brand` and `mode` must be unique; a second token at the same coordinates is refused with `ERR_TOKEN_NAME_CONFLICT`. A successful write returns 201, recomputes the set's `token_count` and `max_alias_depth`, and increments the token set's `version` by 1.

**Resolving an alias chain.** From a chosen token, follow `alias_of_token_id` one hop at a time, counting hops, until a token with no alias target is reached. That terminal token is the chain's leaf. The starting token's `alias_depth` is the number of hops taken — 0 for a leaf, which resolves to itself — and its `resolved_value` is the leaf's `raw_value` reproduced exactly. Two guards bound the walk. If the walk would exceed 5 hops, the write is refused with `ERR_TOKEN_ALIAS_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). If the walk returns to the token it started from, including the degenerate case of a token aliasing itself, or reaches any token it has already visited on the same walk, the write is refused with `ERR_TOKEN_ALIAS_CYCLE` (HTTP 409). Both refusals happen **before any row is written**, so a rejected token creation leaves `token_count`, `max_alias_depth` and the token set's `version` untouched. A set's `max_alias_depth` is the greatest `alias_depth` among its tokens, and 0 when the set is empty.

**Re-pointing a token.** During a brand refresh a `DesignSystemLead` re-points an existing token in place, supplying `expected_token_set_version` together with exactly one of a replacement `raw_value` or a new `alias_of_token_id` target. The resulting alias graph is re-walked in full before anything is written, and the same cycle and depth guards apply with the same two error codes. On success the operation returns 200, rewrites this token's `alias_of_token_id` or `raw_value` along with its `alias_depth` and `resolved_value`, and then recomputes `alias_depth` and `resolved_value` for **every** token whose own alias walk passes through the re-pointed token — a downstream refresh that is easy to overlook and that is the whole point of the operation. It finally recomputes the set's `max_alias_depth` and increments both the token's `version` and the token set's `version` by 1.

**Reading resolved values.** A computed read resolves every distinct token `name` in a set for one brand-and-mode combination. The read accepts an optional `brand` and an optional `mode`; an omitted `brand` falls back to the set's `default_brand` and an omitted `mode` to its `default_mode`. For each distinct name, the layer to use is the first present entry in this strictly ordered precedence list: the requested brand at the requested mode; then the requested brand at `ANY`; then `BASE` at the requested mode; then `BASE` at `ANY`. Because the four-part uniqueness rule allows at most one row per layer, no tie-break is ever needed, and a name with no row in any of the four layers simply does not appear in the result. Layer selection happens once, on the chosen token; alias targets are then resolved by explicit id and never re-enter layer selection, so a `BRAND_A` token aliasing a `BASE` token resolves to that `BASE` leaf's value regardless of the brand the read asked for. The response echoes `token_set_id`, the resolved `brand` and `mode`, the set's `max_alias_depth`, a `resolved_count` giving the number of names resolved, and the `resolved` array of resolved entries.

Tokens are also listable with filters on `token_set_id`, `alias_of_token_id`, `category`, `brand` and `mode` beside `limit` and `offset`; filtering by `alias_of_token_id` is how a lead discovers which tokens depend on a token before re-pointing it. Individual tokens and token sets are readable one at a time, and the token set read is where a caller obtains the `version` it must echo as `expected_token_set_version`, and that a proposal pins at submit time.

### 3.3. The Component Inventory, Platform Implementations and Coverage

A `DesignSystemLead` or a `SystemAdmin` registers a component with a display `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a globally unique lowercase kebab-case `slug`, and an `owning_team_id` naming the team accountable for the component's governance. An optional `docs_state` declares the state of the component's page on the documentation site and is one of `MISSING`, `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, defaulting to `MISSING`. Registration returns 201 and seeds a large computed surface: `maturity_level` at `EXPERIMENTAL`, `lifecycle_state` at `ACTIVE`, `maturity_demotion_reason` at `NONE`, `current_major_version` at 1, `shipped_platform_count` at 0, `coverage_state` at `NONE`, `governance_conformance_score` at 0.00, `blocked_dependency_count` at 0, `pending_breaking_change_count` at 0 and `one_off_exception_count` at 0. It also stamps `conformance_baseline_at` from the logical clock — this is the opening of the component's accessibility-evidence window, discussed in §3.4 — and sets `docs_last_reviewed_at` to the logical clock when the supplied `docs_state` is `PUBLISHED`, and to null otherwise. `docs_last_reviewed_at` is the instant at which the documentation page most recently became `PUBLISHED`, and it stays null while the page is `MISSING` or `DRAFT`.

The inventory is listable with filters on `owning_team_id`, `maturity_level`, `lifecycle_state` and `coverage_state` alongside `limit` and `offset`; the `owning_team_id` filter is how a caller walks from a team to the components it owns. A single component read returns both state dimensions and every governance roll-up on the row.

**Declaring a platform implementation.** A component's realisation on a given platform is a separate resource. A `ContributingTeam` that owns the component, or a `DesignSystemLead`, declares one by naming the `platform` — `WEB`, `IOS` or `ANDROID` — together with the distributed `package_name` of 1 to 160 non-blank characters and a `package_version` that must be a three-part dotted numeric semantic version such as `2.4.0`. An optional `token_compliance_ratio` records the fraction of the package's styling declarations that resolve through design tokens rather than raw literals, as reported by code scanning; it is a number with two decimal places on the closed interval from 0.00 to 1.00 and defaults to 0.00. The implementation takes its `component_id` from the component in the request path. Declaration returns 201 with `status` at `PLANNED`, `conformance_score` at 0.00, `last_audit_sequence` at 0, `blocked_reason` at `NONE` and `shipped_at` null. A component may have at most one implementation per platform: a second declaration for the same `component_id` and `platform` pair is `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`. A `PLANNED` implementation contributes nothing to coverage.

**Shipping.** Only the owning `ContributingTeam` ships. The request supplies the `package_version` actually released and the `token_compliance_ratio` measured against it, and both values overwrite what the row previously held. Shipping is available from exactly two positions on the implementation's state machine — `PLANNED → SHIPPED` for a first release and `BLOCKED → SHIPPED` for a recovery — and attempting it from `SHIPPED` or from the terminal `RETIRED` is `ERR_INVALID_IMPLEMENTATION_STATE`. Four business gates then apply. The owning component's `lifecycle_state` must be `ACTIVE` or `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED`; a component already at `SUNSET` or `REMOVED` refuses the ship with `ERR_COMPONENT_LIFECYCLE_BLOCKS_SHIP`. The `AUTOMATED` `AA` accessibility audit with the greatest `audit_sequence` for this implementation must exist and must report `violations_critical` equal to 0; a missing audit or any open critical finding is `ERR_A11Y_CRITICAL_VIOLATIONS_OPEN`. The supplied `token_compliance_ratio` must be at least 0.90, and a lower figure is refused as a shape violation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Finally the owning component's `blocked_dependency_count` must be 0, otherwise `ERR_BLOCKED_DEPENDENCY` — and for a recovery from `BLOCKED` this is the substantive gate, because the consumer must first re-pin its dependency edge to the provider's new major version before it can ship again.

A successful ship returns 200, sets `status` to `SHIPPED`, stamps `shipped_at` from the logical clock, resets `blocked_reason` to `NONE`, recomputes the component's `shipped_platform_count` and `coverage_state`, recomputes the component's `governance_conformance_score` under the governance-sweep view of §3.4, and — when the ship cleared a previously `BLOCKED` row — recomputes the owning team's `blocked_implementation_count` downward. It increments both the implementation's `version` and the component's `version` by 1.

**Computing coverage.** Coverage is derived from the set of *distinct* platforms among the component's implementations whose `status` is exactly `SHIPPED`. Implementations in `PLANNED`, `BLOCKED` or `RETIRED` are excluded, which is precisely why a breaking-change cascade that blocks an implementation immediately downgrades the component's coverage. `shipped_platform_count` is the size of that platform set, an integer from 0 to 3, and `coverage_state` reads:

- `NONE` when the set is empty;
- `WEB_ONLY` when the set is exactly the single platform `WEB`;
- `MOBILE_ONLY` when the set is non-empty and does not contain `WEB` — that is, one or both of `IOS` and `ANDROID` and nothing else;
- `PARTIAL` when the set contains `WEB` and exactly one of `IOS` and `ANDROID`;
- `FULL` when the set contains `WEB` and both `IOS` and `ANDROID`.

These two derived values are the platform gates on maturity promotion: reaching `BETA` requires `shipped_platform_count` of at least 1, and reaching `STABLE` requires `coverage_state` to be `PARTIAL` or `FULL`.

**Blocking, recovery and retirement.** An implementation leaves `SHIPPED` for `BLOCKED` only under a server-driven cascade, never by direct request, and only when the owning component's `blocked_dependency_count` became positive during that same request; the transition sets `blocked_reason` to `UPSTREAM_BREAKING_CHANGE` and recomputes the component's `shipped_platform_count` and `coverage_state` downward. `blocked_reason` is one of `NONE`, `UPSTREAM_BREAKING_CHANGE` or `PROVIDER_SUNSET`, and it records why the row was last blocked. Recovery from `BLOCKED` back to `SHIPPED` closes the cycle and is the ordinary re-ship described above. Separately, when a component's `lifecycle_state` reaches `REMOVED`, every one of its implementations moves to the terminal `RETIRED` from wherever it stood — `PLANNED → RETIRED`, `SHIPPED → RETIRED` or `BLOCKED → RETIRED` — and where the retired row had been `BLOCKED`, the owning team's `blocked_implementation_count` is recomputed downward as part of the same request. `RETIRED` is terminal; nothing leaves it.

Implementations are listable under their component with filters on `status` and `platform` beside `limit` and `offset`, and each is individually readable, returning the latest-evidence `conformance_score` described next.

### 3.4. Accessibility Evidence, Conformance Scoring and Maturity Promotion

Accessibility is governed by filed evidence, not by assertion. A `ContributingTeam` or a `DesignSystemLead` files one immutable audit record against a platform implementation, declaring the testing `method` — `AUTOMATED` for a scanner run or `MANUAL` for an assistive-technology pass — the `wcag_level` the audit was run at, one of `A`, `AA` or `AAA`, the three non-negative finding counts `violations_critical`, `violations_serious` and `violations_moderate`, and an `evidence_url` of 1 to 500 non-blank characters linking the stored report or manual test script run. Filing returns 201. The server mints `audit_sequence` as the implementation's `last_audit_sequence` plus 1, stores it on the row, advances `last_audit_sequence` to match, stamps `audited_at` from the logical clock, and computes the row's `defect_load`. The record takes its `platform_implementation_id` from the implementation named in the request path rather than from the payload. Audit records are append-only: they carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and `audit_sequence` — never the row `id` — is the deterministic recency key for every selection below. Evidence is listable per implementation in ascending `audit_sequence` order, with optional `method` and `wcag_level` filters beside `limit` and `offset`.

A row's `defect_load` weights findings by severity: ten points for each critical finding, four for each serious finding, and one for each moderate finding, summed. Only audits whose `wcag_level` is `AA` ever enter a score; `A`-level and `AAA`-level audits are accepted, stored and listed, but are invisible to both scoring views.

**The shared arithmetic.** Both scoring views reduce the qualifying `AA` evidence to at most one defect load per method, and then apply identical arithmetic. Count how many of the two methods, `AUTOMATED` and `MANUAL`, have no selected load at all; call that the missing-method count. Take the selected `AUTOMATED` load, or zero if that method is missing, and multiply it by 0.4. Take the selected `MANUAL` load, or zero if that method is missing, and multiply it by 0.6. Add the two products. Subtract that sum from 100, then subtract a further 25.0 for each missing method. Finally take the larger of the result and 0.00, and round half up to two decimal places. A score is therefore never negative, and an implementation with no `AA` evidence at all scores 50.00 rather than 100.00 — the missing-method penalty is what makes `ERR_A11Y_CONFORMANCE_INSUFFICIENT` fire when a required testing method is simply absent from the window.

**The latest-evidence view** is what an implementing engineer sees. For each method it selects the single qualifying `AA` audit with the greatest `audit_sequence` and ignores every earlier one. Its result is written to the implementation's `conformance_score` on every audit filing, so remediating a bad audit by filing a good one immediately raises the number.

**The governance-sweep view** is deliberately harsher, and it is the only view any promotion gate reads. For each method it restricts the qualifying `AA` audits to those whose `audited_at` is at or after the component's `conformance_baseline_at`, and then selects the **worst** of them — the greatest `defect_load` in the window, not the most recent. An earlier bad audit therefore keeps depressing the component's score until the baseline moves. On identical evidence, the governance sweep yields a strictly lower number than the latest-evidence view whenever any superseded audit was worse than the latest.

**Rolling up to the component.** The governance-sweep view is then rolled up. Consider the component's implementations whose `status` is `SHIPPED` or `BLOCKED`. When that set is empty, the component's `governance_conformance_score` is 0.00. Otherwise it is the **minimum** swept score across that set: the component is only as accessible as its worst live platform. The roll-up is recomputed whenever an audit is filed, whenever an implementation ships, and whenever a maturity promotion resets the baseline.

Filing an audit therefore mutates three rows. It writes the new evidence record, refreshes the implementation's `conformance_score` and `last_audit_sequence` and increments the implementation's `version` by 1, and refreshes the component's `governance_conformance_score` and increments the component's `version` by 1. One further effect is easy to miss: if the component's `maturity_level` is `BETA` and the newly swept score falls below 70.00, the component's `maturity_demotion_reason` is set to `CONFORMANCE_REGRESSION`. The `maturity_level` itself stays `BETA` — this is the `BETA → BETA` self-transition of the maturity dimension, and `EXPERIMENTAL` is never re-entered.

**Promotion.** A `DesignSystemLead` advances the maturity dimension exactly one step at a time, naming the destination as `target_maturity_level`. Only `EXPERIMENTAL → BETA` and `BETA → STABLE` are declared; asking for any other destination — `EXPERIMENTAL` straight to `STABLE`, or a request to move down — is `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_MATURITY_TRANSITION`. Each step has its own gate set, and both sets are enforced in full.

Promotion to `BETA` requires four things: `shipped_platform_count` of at least 1, otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` — and note that a component with no shipped implementation at all falls here, since its governance score would also be 0.00; a `governance_conformance_score` of at least 70.00, inclusive at the boundary, otherwise `ERR_A11Y_CONFORMANCE_INSUFFICIENT`; a `blocked_dependency_count` of 0, otherwise `ERR_BLOCKED_DEPENDENCY`; and no deprecation plan for this component in state `ANNOUNCED` or `SUNSET`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_DEPRECATION_IN_PROGRESS`.

Promotion to `STABLE` requires five: a `coverage_state` of `PARTIAL` or `FULL`, otherwise `ERR_PLATFORM_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`; a `governance_conformance_score` of at least 90.00, inclusive at the boundary, otherwise `ERR_A11Y_CONFORMANCE_INSUFFICIENT`; a `docs_state` of `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_MATURITY_TRANSITION`; a `blocked_dependency_count` of 0, otherwise `ERR_BLOCKED_DEPENDENCY`; and again no deprecation plan for this component in state `ANNOUNCED` or `SUNSET`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_DEPRECATION_IN_PROGRESS`.

A successful promotion returns 200. It sets `maturity_level` to the requested destination, clears `maturity_demotion_reason` back to `NONE`, resets `conformance_baseline_at` to the logical clock, recomputes `governance_conformance_score` against that new window — dropping every audit older than the reset out of the sweep entirely, which typically raises the score in the same request — and increments the component's `version` by 1.

Demotion works differently in one important respect. When the maturity dimension is demoted from `STABLE` to `BETA` by a provider sunset, `conformance_baseline_at` is **not** reset; only a successful promotion moves the baseline. That demotion closes the maturity cycle `STABLE → BETA → STABLE`, and its cause is recorded as `UPSTREAM_SUNSET` in `maturity_demotion_reason`.

### 3.5. The Dependency Graph and Compatibility Pinning

Components consume one another, and the system records those relationships explicitly so that breaking changes can be propagated rather than discovered. A `DesignSystemLead`, or a `ContributingTeam` that owns the consuming component, declares an edge from the component in the request path — which becomes the edge's `consumer_component_id` — to a `provider_component_id` supplied in the payload, at a `pinned_provider_major` that must be an integer of at least 1 and that records which major version of the provider's public API the consumer is built against. A component may not depend on itself: a `provider_component_id` equal to the `consumer_component_id` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The same ordered pair may not be declared twice, and a repeat is `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`. The graph must stay acyclic: an edge that would close a cycle anywhere in the transitive closure of existing edges is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENCY_CYCLE`, and that closure is taken over **all** edges regardless of their `compatibility_state`, so an already-`INCOMPATIBLE` edge still counts as a path when cycle-checking. Declaration returns 201.

Each edge carries a `compatibility_state` of `COMPATIBLE`, `INCOMPATIBLE` or `SUNSET_PROVIDER`, and the server sets it rather than the client. At declaration the edge is `COMPATIBLE` when `pinned_provider_major` equals the provider's `current_major_version` and the provider's `lifecycle_state` is not `SUNSET`; it is `SUNSET_PROVIDER` when the provider is at `SUNSET`; and it is `INCOMPATIBLE` otherwise — which in practice means the consumer pinned a major version the provider has moved past. The consumer component's `blocked_dependency_count` is then recomputed as the number of its outgoing edges whose `compatibility_state` is `INCOMPATIBLE` or `SUNSET_PROVIDER`, and the consumer component's `version` is incremented by 1.

Edges touching a component are listable from that component, with a `direction` filter accepting `ALL`, which is the default, `CONSUMES` for the edges where the path component is the consumer, and `PROVIDES` for the edges where it is the provider. A `compatibility_state` filter narrows further, alongside `limit` and `offset`. This listing is the only way to walk the graph in either direction.

**Clearing a block.** When an upstream breaking change has left an edge `INCOMPATIBLE`, the consumer re-pins it. A `DesignSystemLead` or the owning `ContributingTeam` supplies a fresh `pinned_provider_major` for the edge, and the server recomputes `compatibility_state` by the same three-way rule: `COMPATIBLE` when the new pin equals the provider's `current_major_version` and the provider is not at `SUNSET`, `SUNSET_PROVIDER` when the provider is at `SUNSET`, and `INCOMPATIBLE` otherwise. When the edge becomes `COMPATIBLE` the consumer's `blocked_dependency_count` is recomputed downward. The operation returns 200 and increments both the edge's `version` and the consumer component's `version` by 1. Re-pinning is the mandatory first step of a recovery: until the count reaches 0, the consumer's implementations cannot leave `BLOCKED`.

### 3.6. The Contribution Process: Draft to Acceptance

Contributions travel a cyclic pipeline. A proposal's `state` is one of `DRAFT`, `DESIGN_CRITIQUE`, `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `READY_FOR_ACCEPTANCE`, `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, with `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` terminal. The cycle runs `CHANGES_REQUESTED → DESIGN_CRITIQUE → ENGINEERING_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` across numbered revision rounds, and the engineering gate branches three ways.

**Opening a proposal.** A `ContributingTeam` opens one with a `title` of 1 to 160 non-blank characters, an `author_team_id` that must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, a `target_component_id` naming the component the change touches, and a `pinned_token_set_id` naming the token set the design was authored against. `proposal_kind` classifies the contribution as `NEW_COMPONENT`, `ENHANCEMENT` or `BREAKING_CHANGE`, and the optional boolean `touches_public_api` declares whether the change alters the component's documented public props or API surface, defaulting to false. Opening is refused with `ERR_TEAM_DEBT_FROZEN` when the authoring team's `debt_state` is `FROZEN`. A successful open returns 201 with `state` at `DRAFT`, `revision_round` at 0, both `required_evidence_kinds` and `submitted_evidence_kinds` as empty arrays, and `released_in_release_id`, `pinned_token_set_version`, `raw_change_class`, `effective_change_class`, `design_critique_reviewer_user_id`, `engineering_reviewer_user_id`, `submitted_at` and `accepted_at` all null.

**Submitting and resubmitting.** The author submits the proposal into `DESIGN_CRITIQUE` with no payload at all, and the same action serves both the first submit, `DRAFT → DESIGN_CRITIQUE`, and every resubmit, `CHANGES_REQUESTED → DESIGN_CRITIQUE`. Four preconditions apply to both: the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal `author_team_id`; the authoring team's `debt_state` must be `CLEAR`, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_DEBT_FROZEN`; the target component's `lifecycle_state` must be `ACTIVE` or `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_DEPRECATION_IN_PROGRESS`; and the round the submit would produce must not exceed 3, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The revision cap is a refusal, not a silent clamp: a fourth round is rejected rather than capped at three.

The two submits differ in their bookkeeping. Out of `DRAFT`, `revision_round` is set to 1 and `submitted_evidence_kinds` is cleared to an empty array, because no evidence can exist in round 1. Out of `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `revision_round` is incremented by 1 and `submitted_evidence_kinds` is **pruned** to only those kinds that survive a round change — see the carry-over rule below. Both submits stamp `submitted_at` from the logical clock, pin `pinned_token_set_version` to the current `version` of the token set named by `pinned_token_set_id` (a re-pin on every resubmit; the staleness re-validation happens later, at acceptance), recompute `required_evidence_kinds`, increment the proposal's `version` by 1, and return 200.

**The required evidence set.** Acceptance criteria are computed, not negotiated. The base requirement follows `proposal_kind`: a `NEW_COMPONENT` requires `A11Y_AUTOMATED`, `A11Y_MANUAL`, `DOC_PAGE` and `TOKEN_COMPLIANCE`; an `ENHANCEMENT` requires only `A11Y_AUTOMATED` and `TOKEN_COMPLIANCE`; and a `BREAKING_CHANGE` requires `A11Y_AUTOMATED`, `A11Y_MANUAL`, `DOC_PAGE`, `MIGRATION_GUIDE` and `TOKEN_COMPLIANCE`. To that base, `PLATFORM_PARITY` is added when — and only when — the target component's `coverage_state` at the moment of the submit is `PARTIAL` or `FULL`. The union is stored in `required_evidence_kinds` as an ascending-sorted array with no duplicates, and its members are drawn from exactly six kinds: `A11Y_AUTOMATED`, `A11Y_MANUAL`, `DOC_PAGE`, `MIGRATION_GUIDE`, `PLATFORM_PARITY` and `TOKEN_COMPLIANCE`. The requirement set is frozen per round: coverage measured at a later instant is irrelevant until the next submit recomputes it.

**The carry-over rule.** On a resubmit out of `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `submitted_evidence_kinds` is reduced to its intersection with the two round-durable kinds, `DOC_PAGE` and `MIGRATION_GUIDE`. Everything else — the accessibility evidence, the token-compliance figure, the platform-parity claim — must be re-filed against the new `revision_round`. This is why a proposal that fully satisfied its evidence gate in round 1 can fail acceptance with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` in round 2 without any requirement having changed.

**Filing evidence.** The authoring `ContributingTeam` attaches evidence for the current round by supplying `evidence_kinds`, one or more of the six kinds, together with an `evidence_url`. The supplied kinds are unioned into `submitted_evidence_kinds`, which is then re-sorted ascending, the proposal's `version` is incremented by 1, and the operation returns 200 with the updated proposal.

**The two review gates.** A reviewer files one immutable verdict at a time, supplying the `stage` — `DESIGN_CRITIQUE` or `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` — the `verdict` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_CHANGES` or `BLOCK`, and `notes` of 1 to 2000 non-blank characters giving the rationale. The verdict is stored as a review record that captures its `proposal_id`, the `stage`, the `verdict`, the `notes`, the `reviewer_user_id` taken from the caller's `X-User-ID`, the `revision_round` the proposal was in when the verdict was filed (an integer from 1 to 3), and the `resulting_proposal_state` — the proposal's state immediately after the verdict was applied, which is one of `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `READY_FOR_ACCEPTANCE` or `REJECTED`. Review records are append-only and carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`; each is created with a 201 response and each increments the proposal's `version` by 1. A proposal's verdicts are listable in ascending `created_at` order with filters on `stage` and `revision_round` beside `limit` and `offset`.

The `stage` in the payload must match the gate implied by the proposal's current `state` and the acting role must be that gate's reviewer role, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_STAGE_MISMATCH`. Five branches exist, and each carries a distinct side-effect set:

- A `DesignCritiqueReviewer` filing `DESIGN_CRITIQUE` with `APPROVE` advances the proposal `DESIGN_CRITIQUE → ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, records `resulting_proposal_state` as `ENGINEERING_REVIEW`, and sets the proposal's `design_critique_reviewer_user_id` to the caller's `X-User-ID`.
- A `DesignCritiqueReviewer` filing `DESIGN_CRITIQUE` with `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the proposal `DESIGN_CRITIQUE → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, records `resulting_proposal_state` as `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, and likewise sets `design_critique_reviewer_user_id`.
- An `EngineeringReviewer` filing `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` with `APPROVE` advances the proposal `ENGINEERING_REVIEW → READY_FOR_ACCEPTANCE`, records `resulting_proposal_state` as `READY_FOR_ACCEPTANCE`, and sets `engineering_reviewer_user_id`.
- An `EngineeringReviewer` filing `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` with `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the proposal `ENGINEERING_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, records `resulting_proposal_state` as `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, and sets `engineering_reviewer_user_id`.
- An `EngineeringReviewer` filing `ENGINEERING_REVIEW` with `BLOCK` moves the proposal `ENGINEERING_REVIEW → REJECTED`, records `resulting_proposal_state` as `REJECTED`, sets `engineering_reviewer_user_id`, and additionally clears both `required_evidence_kinds` and `submitted_evidence_kinds` to empty arrays. `BLOCK` is available only at the engineering gate; a `BLOCK` offered at the design-critique gate is `ERR_REVIEW_STAGE_MISMATCH`.

Segregation of duties applies at both gates, as specified in §2.1: the engineering verdict's `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `design_critique_reviewer_user_id`, and the design-critique verdict's `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `engineering_reviewer_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

**Acceptance.** A `DesignSystemLead` accepts a `READY_FOR_ACCEPTANCE` proposal into a `DRAFT` release, naming that release as `release_id` and echoing the target component's current version as `expected_component_version`. Four checks guard the write beyond the state and role rules: `expected_component_version` must equal the target component's `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_COMPONENT_VERSION`; the token set named by `pinned_token_set_id` must still have a `version` equal to the proposal's `pinned_token_set_version`, otherwise `ERR_PINNED_TOKEN_SET_STALE`, because the design was reviewed against tokens that have since moved; the named release must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE`; and `submitted_evidence_kinds` must contain every member of `required_evidence_kinds`, otherwise `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`. The accepting lead's `X-User-ID` must additionally differ from both recorded reviewer ids, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A successful acceptance returns 200, moves the proposal `READY_FOR_ACCEPTANCE → ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock, writes `released_in_release_id` to the named release, and increments the proposal's `version` by 1. When `DOC_PAGE` is among the `submitted_evidence_kinds`, it also sets the target component's `docs_state` to `PUBLISHED` and its `docs_last_reviewed_at` to the logical clock.

**The four-hop breaking-change cascade.** When — and only when — `proposal_kind` is `BREAKING_CHANGE`, acceptance releases a cascade whose hops each fire because of the previous hop's mutation rather than because of the accept itself.

1. **Hop 1** increments the target component's `current_major_version` by 1 and its `pending_breaking_change_count` by 1, and increments that component's `version` by 1.
2. **Hop 2** fires because hop 1 changed `current_major_version`. Every dependency edge whose `provider_component_id` is this component and whose `pinned_provider_major` is now less than the new `current_major_version` flips from `COMPATIBLE` to `INCOMPATIBLE`, and each affected consumer component's `blocked_dependency_count` is recomputed upward.
3. **Hop 3** fires because hop 2 raised those consumers' `blocked_dependency_count`. Every platform implementation of an affected consumer component whose `status` is `SHIPPED` moves `SHIPPED → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `UPSTREAM_BREAKING_CHANGE`, and that consumer's `shipped_platform_count` and `coverage_state` are recomputed — typically downward, and potentially all the way to `NONE`.
4. **Hop 4** fires because hop 3 changed those implementations' `status`. For each affected consumer, the `blocked_implementation_count` of the team named by its `owning_team_id` is recomputed upward.

That chain is the serialisation point between a breaking change and everything downstream of it, and it is also why `expected_component_version` exists: a lead who accepted a second proposal against the same component without observing the first one's major-version bump would be reasoning about a dependency picture that no longer exists.

Proposals are listable with filters on `author_team_id`, `target_component_id`, `pinned_token_set_id`, `released_in_release_id` and `state` beside `limit` and `offset` — the reverse walk from a team, a component, a token set or a release to the proposals referencing it — and each is individually readable with its evidence arrays, `revision_round` and `pinned_token_set_version` on the row.

### 3.7. Releases, Semantic Versioning and the Breaking-Change Policy

Accepted contributions ship in releases, and only a `SystemAdmin` cuts and publishes them. Cutting a release takes a `semver_base` — the currently published design-system version this release bumps from, expressed as a three-part dotted numeric string such as `4.7.2` — and an optional `channel` of `STABLE` or `NEXT`, defaulting to `STABLE`. The release is created with a 201 response in `state` `DRAFT`, with `release_notes_state` at `PENDING`, `breaking_change_count` and `escalated_proposal_count` at 0, and `semver`, `policy_tier`, `breaking_ratio` and `published_at` all null. Its `state` has only two values, `DRAFT` and the terminal `PUBLISHED`.

Publication is the moment every deferred version decision is made at once. It takes no payload. Three preconditions apply: the release must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE`; at least one contribution proposal must have its `released_in_release_id` pointing at this release with `state` `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE` again, because an empty release has nothing to version; and — evaluated **after** the version arithmetic below has run — every carried proposal whose `effective_change_class` came out as `MAJOR` must carry `MIGRATION_GUIDE` in its `submitted_evidence_kinds`, otherwise `ERR_MIGRATION_GUIDE_REQUIRED`. That last check is the one that surprises people: a proposal classified `MINOR` at accept time, whose own acceptance gate therefore never demanded a migration guide, can be escalated to `MAJOR` by the batching policy and fail here.

**The carried set** is exactly those proposals whose `released_in_release_id` names this release and whose `state` is `ACCEPTED`. Proposals in any other state, and proposals attached to a different release, are excluded. The number of carried proposals is the denominator used below. The whole computation is a set computation and does not depend on the order in which proposals are visited.

**Step 1 — the raw class.** Each carried proposal gets a `raw_change_class`, drawn from the three semantic-version change classes `MAJOR`, `MINOR` and `PATCH`: a `BREAKING_CHANGE` proposal is `MAJOR`; a `NEW_COMPONENT` proposal is `MINOR`; an `ENHANCEMENT` whose `touches_public_api` is true is `MINOR`; and an `ENHANCEMENT` whose `touches_public_api` is false is `PATCH`.

**Step 2 — the maturity adjustment.** A proposal whose step-1 class is `MAJOR` is de-escalated to `MINOR` when the `maturity_level` of the component named by its `target_component_id`, **read at publish time rather than at accept time**, is `EXPERIMENTAL` — an experimental component carries no stability contract, so breaking it is not a breaking change. Every other proposal keeps its step-1 class.

**Step 3 — the breaking ratio.** Count the carried proposals whose *step-2* class is `MAJOR`, divide by the number of carried proposals, and round half up to four decimal places. That is `breaking_ratio`. It is computed from the step-2 classes — never from the step-1 classes, and never from the step-5 classes.

**Step 4 — the policy tier.** `policy_tier` is `BATCHED` when `breaking_ratio` is at least 0.50, and `INCREMENTAL` otherwise.

**Step 5 — the effective class.** Under an `INCREMENTAL` tier, each proposal's `effective_change_class` is simply its step-2 class. Under a `BATCHED` tier, a proposal whose step-2 class is `MINOR` **and** whose `touches_public_api` is true is escalated to `MAJOR`, on the reasoning that a batched breaking window may as well absorb public-API minors; every other proposal keeps its step-2 class.

**Step 6 — the pinned finish.** `breaking_ratio` and `policy_tier` are **not** recomputed from the step-5 classes. The evaluation is a single forward pass and no fixed point is sought, even though step 5 can change the very population step 3 measured. What remains is arithmetic. `breaking_change_count` is the number of carried proposals whose `effective_change_class` is `MAJOR`. `escalated_proposal_count` is the number of carried proposals whose `effective_change_class` differs from their `raw_change_class`, and it therefore counts both the step-2 maturity de-escalations and the step-5 batching escalations. The new `semver` is derived from `semver_base` by parsing it into its major, minor and patch integers and then: raising the major by 1 and setting minor and patch to 0 when at least one carried proposal is effectively `MAJOR`; otherwise keeping the major, raising the minor by 1 and setting patch to 0 when no proposal is effectively `MAJOR` but at least one is effectively `MINOR`; and otherwise keeping the major and minor and raising the patch by 1.

Publication returns 200. It writes `raw_change_class` and `effective_change_class` onto every carried proposal, writes `semver`, `policy_tier`, `breaking_ratio`, `breaking_change_count` and `escalated_proposal_count` onto the release, moves the release `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` — the only transition its `state` machine has — flips `release_notes_state` to `PUBLISHED` — release communication is a side effect of publishing, not a separate action — and stamps `published_at` from the logical clock. It also resets `pending_breaking_change_count` to 0 on every component that was the target of a carried `BREAKING_CHANGE` proposal, which is what that counter means: accepted breaking changes not yet carried by a published release. Finally it increments the release's `version` by 1 and each carried proposal's `version` by 1.

### 3.8. Deprecation Timelines, Sunset Cascades and Rescission

Retiring a component is a scheduled, announced, and reversible process owned entirely by the `DesignSystemLead`. A plan is drafted against a component with three instants: `announced_at`, the moment the deprecation becomes public; `sunset_at`, after which the component may be moved out of service; and `removal_at`, after which it may be deleted from the inventory. They must be strictly increasing — `announced_at` before `sunset_at` before `removal_at` — or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Optionally the plan names a `replacement_component_id`, which must resolve to an existing component other than the one being deprecated, and a `migration_guide_url`. The plan takes its `component_id` from the request path, is created with a 201 response in `state` `DRAFT` with `blocked_dependent_count` at 0 and `rescinded_at` null, and its `migration_guide_state` starts at `DRAFT` when a `migration_guide_url` was supplied and at `MISSING` otherwise. A plan's `state` runs through `DRAFT`, `ANNOUNCED`, `SUNSET` and the two terminal states `REMOVED` and `RESCINDED`. Plans of a component are listable with a `state` filter beside `limit` and `offset`.

**The notice window.** Two integer day counts are computed once, at plan creation, and frozen onto the plan. `required_notice_days` is a base determined by the component's `maturity_level` plus a surcharge determined by its `coverage_state`. The base is 14 days for an `EXPERIMENTAL` component, 45 days for a `BETA` component and 90 days for a `STABLE` one. The surcharge is 0 days when `coverage_state` is `NONE`, `WEB_ONLY` or `MOBILE_ONLY`, 15 days when it is `PARTIAL`, and 30 days when it is `FULL` — the more platforms a component reaches, the longer its consumers get. `actual_notice_days` is the number of whole days from `announced_at` to `sunset_at`, obtained by taking the elapsed seconds and dividing by 86400 seconds per day with the fractional part discarded. Two gates then apply, and both report `ERR_DEPRECATION_NOTICE_TOO_SHORT`. The first requires `actual_notice_days` to be at least `required_notice_days`, inclusive at the boundary, so a plan that serves exactly the required number of days passes. The second requires the whole days from `sunset_at` to `removal_at`, computed the same way, to be at least 30.

**Window exclusivity.** A plan occupies the half-open interval that begins at its `announced_at` and ends just before its `removal_at`, on its component. A new plan collides with an existing plan of the same component whose `state` is `DRAFT`, `ANNOUNCED` or `SUNSET` exactly when the new plan's `announced_at` falls strictly before the existing plan's `removal_at` **and** the existing plan's `announced_at` falls strictly before the new plan's `removal_at`. Because the interval excludes its own end, touching windows do not collide: a new `announced_at` exactly equal to an existing `removal_at` is permitted. Plans that have reached `REMOVED` or `RESCINDED` release their window entirely and never collide. A collision is `ERR_DEPRECATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

**Announcing.** Attaching the guide is what announces the plan. The lead supplies a `migration_guide_url`, which must be non-blank or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, together with a boolean `publish`. The plan must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DEPRECATION_PLAN_STATE`, and the component's `lifecycle_state` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise the same code. One asymmetry matters: when the component's `maturity_level` is `STABLE`, `publish` must be true — a `STABLE` component may not be announced without a published guide — and a `publish` of false in that situation is `ERR_MIGRATION_GUIDE_REQUIRED`. With `publish` false on a non-`STABLE` component, the operation records the `migration_guide_url`, sets `migration_guide_state` to `DRAFT`, leaves the plan's `state` at `DRAFT`, increments the plan's `version` by 1, and touches nothing else. With `publish` true, it sets `migration_guide_state` to `PUBLISHED`, advances the plan `DRAFT → ANNOUNCED`, drives the component's lifecycle dimension `ACTIVE → DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED`, and increments both the plan's `version` and the component's `version` by 1. Either way the operation returns 200.

**Advancing, and the three-hop sunset cascade.** Advancing moves the plan one step along `ANNOUNCED → SUNSET → REMOVED` and takes no payload. In both steps the logical clock must have reached the due instant — at or past `sunset_at` for the first, at or past `removal_at` for the second — otherwise `ERR_DEPRECATION_ADVANCE_NOT_DUE`. Advancing a plan that is in `DRAFT`, or one that has already reached `REMOVED`, is `ERR_INVALID_DEPRECATION_PLAN_STATE`.

The step into `SUNSET` additionally requires `migration_guide_state` to be `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_MIGRATION_GUIDE_REQUIRED`. On success the plan's `state` becomes `SUNSET`, and three hops follow, each caused by the one before it. **Hop 1** drives the component's lifecycle dimension `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED → SUNSET` and increments that component's `version` by 1. **Hop 2** fires because hop 1 changed the component's `lifecycle_state`: every dependency edge whose `provider_component_id` is this component and whose `compatibility_state` is `COMPATIBLE` flips to `SUNSET_PROVIDER`; each affected consumer component's `blocked_dependency_count` is recomputed upward; and the plan's `blocked_dependent_count` is set to the number of consumer components whose count rose. **Hop 3** fires because hop 2 raised those counts: every affected consumer component whose `maturity_level` is `STABLE` is demoted to `BETA` with `maturity_demotion_reason` set to `UPSTREAM_SUNSET`, and its `conformance_baseline_at` is left untouched. The plan's own `version` increments by 1.

The step into `REMOVED` sets the plan's `state` to `REMOVED` and drives the component's lifecycle dimension `SUNSET → REMOVED`. That component transition retires every platform implementation of the component that was in `PLANNED`, `SHIPPED` or `BLOCKED`, recomputes the component's `shipped_platform_count` to 0 and its `coverage_state` to `NONE`, and increments the component's `version` by 1; the plan's `version` increments by 1 as well.

**Rescinding.** Rescission is the compensating action, available from `ANNOUNCED` and from `SUNSET` and refused from anywhere else with `ERR_INVALID_DEPRECATION_PLAN_STATE`. It takes a `reason`. In both cases — `ANNOUNCED → RESCINDED` and `SUNSET → RESCINDED` — the plan's `state` becomes `RESCINDED`, `rescinded_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the plan's half-open occupancy window on the component is released so that a future plan may reuse those dates, and the plan's `version` and the component's `version` each increment by 1. The two branches differ in how far back they have to unwind.

Rescinding from `ANNOUNCED` drives the component's lifecycle dimension `DEPRECATION_ANNOUNCED → ACTIVE`. No dependency edges were ever touched, so nothing else changes.

Rescinding from `SUNSET` drives the component's lifecycle dimension `SUNSET → ACTIVE` and gives back exactly what hop 2 of the sunset cascade took: every dependency edge whose `provider_component_id` is this component and whose `compatibility_state` is `SUNSET_PROVIDER` returns to `COMPATIBLE`, and each affected consumer's `blocked_dependency_count` falls by precisely the amount that hop added. The plan's `blocked_dependent_count` is then reset to 0. What is **not** given back is maturity: a consumer demoted to `BETA` with `maturity_demotion_reason` `UPSTREAM_SUNSET` stays demoted, because a demotion is a governance fact rather than a bookkeeping entry. The conserved quantity is the dependency-block total, not `maturity_level`, and a consumer that wants `STABLE` back must earn it through the promotion gates of §3.4.

### 3.9. Governance Exceptions and the Technical-Debt Ledger

Not every product need can wait for the design system, so a team may ask formal permission to deviate — and pay for it in tracked debt. A `ContributingTeam` requests an exception grant naming a `requesting_team_id`, which must equal the requesting team's own `X-User-ID` or the request is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; a `substitute_for_component_id` identifying the system component being bypassed, forked or worked around; an `exception_kind` of `ONE_OFF_COMPONENT`, `FORK_OF_SYSTEM_COMPONENT` or `RAW_TOKEN_USAGE`; a `platform_scope` of `WEB`, `IOS`, `ANDROID` or `ALL`; a `justification` between 1 and 2000 characters that may not be blank; and an `expires_at` instant at which the deviation is meant to be gone. The grant is created with a 201 response in `state` `REQUESTED`, with `debt_points` at 0.00, and with `pinned_maturity_level`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `repaid_at` all null. Requesting is deliberately available even to a team whose `debt_state` is `FROZEN`: a frozen team may still ask, it simply cannot get an approval that would push it further over budget. Grants are listable with filters on `requesting_team_id`, `substitute_for_component_id`, `state` and `exception_kind` beside `limit` and `offset`, and are individually readable. Their `state` machine has exactly five transitions: `REQUESTED → APPROVED`, `REQUESTED → DENIED`, `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, `APPROVED → REPAID` and `EXPIRED → REPAID`, with `DENIED` and `REPAID` terminal.

**Deciding.** Only a `DesignSystemLead` decides, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and optional `decision_notes`. The grant must be in `REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_EXCEPTION_GRANT_STATE`. The component named by `substitute_for_component_id` must not have a `lifecycle_state` of `REMOVED`, otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_LIFECYCLE_BLOCKS_SHIP` — there is no point granting an exception against something that no longer exists. And on approval only, the requesting team's `outstanding_debt_points` plus the debt this grant would carry must not exceed the team's `debt_budget_points`; exceeding it is `ERR_TEAM_DEBT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The comparison is inclusive, so a grant that lands exactly on the budget is approved and immediately puts the team into `FROZEN`.

An approval pins `pinned_maturity_level` to the `maturity_level` that the substitute component holds at the moment of decision, computes and freezes `debt_points`, sets `state` to `APPROVED`, records `decided_by_user_id` from the deciding lead's `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the logical clock, recomputes the requesting team's debt roll-ups, recomputes the substitute component's `one_off_exception_count` upward, and increments both the grant's `version` and the team's `version` by 1. A denial sets `state` to `DENIED` and records `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, leaves `debt_points` at 0.00, changes no team roll-up and no component counter, and increments only the grant's `version` by 1. Either decision returns 200.

**Pricing a deviation.** A grant's `debt_points` is a base figure for the kind of deviation, multiplied by a platform-reach factor, multiplied by a factor for the maturity of the component being bypassed, with the single product rounded half up to two decimal places. The base is 12 points for `ONE_OFF_COMPONENT`, 8 points for `FORK_OF_SYSTEM_COMPONENT` and 5 points for `RAW_TOKEN_USAGE`. The platform factor is 1.00 for `WEB`, 1.25 for `IOS`, 1.25 for `ANDROID` and 1.75 for `ALL`. The maturity factor reads the `pinned_maturity_level` and is 0.50 for `EXPERIMENTAL`, 0.75 for `BETA` and 1.50 for `STABLE` — deviating from a stable, committed component is the expensive case, while deviating from an experimental one is nearly free. Rounding happens once, on the final product, and never on the intermediate multiplications. Because the maturity level is pinned at decision time, a later promotion or demotion of the substitute component never reprices an already-decided grant.

**The ledger.** A team's `outstanding_debt_points` is the sum of `debt_points` across that team's grants in `APPROVED` or `EXPIRED`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Its `lifetime_debt_points` is the sum across grants in `APPROVED`, `EXPIRED` or `REPAID`, rounded the same way; because repayment only moves a grant from one member of that set to another, `lifetime_debt_points` never decreases. Grants in `REQUESTED` and `DENIED` carry 0.00 and enter neither sum. The team's `debt_state` is `FROZEN` when `outstanding_debt_points` is at least `debt_budget_points` and `CLEAR` otherwise, and it is recomputed on every event that moves either figure. A `FROZEN` team is barred from opening or submitting contribution proposals with `ERR_TEAM_DEBT_FROZEN`, as described in §3.6. Separately, each component's `one_off_exception_count` is the number of grants naming it as `substitute_for_component_id` whose `state` is `APPROVED` or `EXPIRED` — this is the figure that the accessibility-conformance score of §3.4 penalises, so unpaid deviations quietly erode the score of the very component they bypass.

**Expiry.** Expiry is driven by the logical clock and by nothing else. When the clock is set, every grant in `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` is at or before the new instant moves to `EXPIRED`, in ascending `created_at` order, as described in §2.4. The transition is pure bookkeeping of status: the grant keeps its `debt_points` in full, `EXPIRED` remains inside the outstanding sum, and so `outstanding_debt_points` does **not** fall when a grant expires. An expired grant is an overdue debt, not a forgiven one, and a team cannot escape `FROZEN` by waiting.

**Repaying.** Only the owning `ContributingTeam` repays, and an `X-User-ID` other than the grant's `requesting_team_id` is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The grant must be in `APPROVED` or `EXPIRED` — both are repayable, so an overdue deviation can still be remediated — and any other state is `ERR_INVALID_EXCEPTION_GRANT_STATE`. The team supplies `remediation_notes` describing how the deviation was removed. Repayment sets `state` to `REPAID`, stamps `repaid_at` from the logical clock, and retains `debt_points` unchanged as the historic amount that was owed. Because `REPAID` is outside the outstanding set, `outstanding_debt_points` falls by exactly this grant's `debt_points`, `debt_state` is recomputed and may return to `CLEAR`, and the substitute component's `one_off_exception_count` falls by one. Because `REPAID` is inside the lifetime set, `lifetime_debt_points` does not move. Both the grant's `version` and the team's `version` increment by 1, and the operation returns 200.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is exposed under the `/design-system` prefix, with the sole exception of the clock capability, which is mounted at the server root. Operations that create a new resource respond with HTTP 201; operations that read or modify an existing resource respond with HTTP 200. All responses, successful or otherwise, use the envelope of §2.2. Where the Authorized Role(s) column reads **All roles**, the endpoint accepts any of the five roles of §2.1 — `SystemAdmin`, `DesignSystemLead`, `DesignCritiqueReviewer`, `EngineeringReviewer` and `ContributingTeam` — subject to the ownership rules stated in §2.1 and in the workflow that governs the resource.
