# Chargemaster Governance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A hospital's chargemaster — the master list of every billable item, its internal number, its UB-04 revenue code mapping, its CPT or HCPCS code and its published gross charge — is simultaneously a pricing artifact, a compliance artifact and an operational one. Revenue integrity teams are asked to keep thousands of line items internally consistent while the ground shifts underneath them: coding authorities retire and replace codes every year, departments petition for new items and price increases, payer contracts turn over on their own calendars, and federal price-transparency rules require a machine-readable file that must be internally coherent at the instant it is published. The failure modes are expensive and quiet. A duplicated item silently doubles a patient's bill. An item whose price was never rebuilt after its governing methodology changed publishes a stale number to the world. A charge that was performed but never posted disappears from revenue entirely.

This document specifies the Chargemaster Governance API, a system of record that makes each of those failure modes an explicit, enforced state rather than an accident. Every system capability is mounted under the `/chargemaster` path prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described below. The design rests on three ideas. First, **segregation of duties**: the person who requests a change is never the person who reviews it, and neither is the person who approves it. Second, **explicit posture**: a facility whose chargemaster is mid-transition — a transparency file being assembled, an annual code update being crosswalked — advertises that fact and refuses the writes that would corrupt the transition. Third, **conserved compensating actions**: rolling back an annual code update restores the facility's aggregate charge exactly, and resolving a missing-charge finding moves money between two departmental counters without changing their sum.

The reader should treat pricing arithmetic in this specification as literal. All monetary amounts are integer cents, all ratios and multiples are integer basis points, and every rounding step is pinned to a specific position in a specific order of operations. Two implementations that round in different places produce different published prices, and a published price is a legal representation to patients and payers.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This is the very first check performed on any request, before path resolution, before schema validation, before anything else.

The six roles are `SystemAdmin`, `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`, `PricingCommitteeApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ContractManager` and `DepartmentManager`. Immediately after authentication, the server checks that the acting role appears in the authorized-role list for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4); a role that is not listed receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The division of labour among them is deliberate. A `SystemAdmin` configures the environment — facilities, the national revenue-code reference table, departments, and the logical clock — and never touches item pricing or approvals. A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` owns day-to-day chargemaster maintenance: drafting items, repricing them, authoring price rules, retiring duplicates and inactive items, reviewing department requests, mapping annual code-update entries, and reconciling charge capture batches. A `PricingCommitteeApprover` holds the approval authority: item approval, item activation and reactivation, change-request approval, and annual crosswalk approval. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns price-transparency publication, impact-analysis sign-off, and the activation, rollback and cancellation of annual code update cycles. A `ContractManager` owns payer contract terms and may run payer impact analyses of proposed price changes. A `DepartmentManager` speaks for exactly one department: requesting chargemaster changes for it and submitting its charge capture batches.

Five of the six roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`, `PricingCommitteeApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `ContractManager`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `approved_by_user_id` on a charge item or a change request, as `reviewed_by_user_id` or `submitted_by_user_id` on a change request, as `mapped_by_user_id` on a code-update entry, as `signed_off_by_user_id` on an impact analysis — and it compares recorded operator identifiers against each other to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`.

`DepartmentManager` is different: its identity is backed by the `Department` entity. When `X-User-Role` is `DepartmentManager`, `X-User-ID` is the opaque user identifier recorded as `manager_user_id` on exactly one `Department` row, and ownership checks compare the department owning the target resource against that value. Concretely: submitting a change request, resubmitting one, withdrawing one, ingesting a charge capture batch, appending a line to a batch, and disputing a reconciled batch all require that `X-User-ID` equal the `manager_user_id` of the department named by the request's `department_id` (directly on the body, or indirectly via the `ChangeRequest` or the `ChargeCaptureBatch` being acted upon). A mismatch is `ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), evaluated third — after authentication and role admission, and before any resource is looked up. The same error code covers the narrower ownership rule that a withdrawal or a resubmit may only be performed by the user recorded in `submitted_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. The `offset` parameter defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint's narrative in §3 pins a different order, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints override this: the duplicate-candidate report ranks by similarity score descending and breaks ties by `created_at` ascending, and the ordering rules embedded in the transparency file build and the impact analysis apply to the contents of those resources rather than to a list response.

The four pagination fields — `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` — appear inside `meta` on list responses. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most entities additionally carry `updated_at` (same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation) and `version` (an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row). The entities with the full complement — `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` — are `Facility`, `Department`, `ChargeItem`, `PriceRule`, `ChangeRequest`, `CodeUpdateCycle`, `CodeUpdateEntry`, `PayerContract`, `ChargeCaptureBatch`, `MissingChargeFinding`, `PriceChangeImpactAnalysis` and `TransparencyFile`.

Two entities are deliberately narrower. `RevenueCode` is national reference data and `CapturedCharge` is an append-only capture line; both carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field — those two keys are absent from their representations entirely, and no client may pass a version expectation for them. That a `CapturedCharge` is nonetheless rewritten by each reconciliation pass (its `match_status`, `expected_charge_cents`, `line_variance_bp` and resolved `charge_item_id` are recomputed) does not give it a version counter; reconciliation is idempotent per pass by construction.

A version increment is not cosmetic. When a mutation cascades — and in this system it almost always does — every touched row's own `version` advances by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshes. A single change-request implementation can therefore advance the `version` of the change request, the charge item, the facility, a previously published transparency file, and every sibling change request that was voided by the implementation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Interval Conventions

Time in this system is a logical clock, never wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that is the fixed startup default and the server never falls back to real time. A `SystemAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-control endpoint, and every subsequent request evaluates "now" against the new value. Moving the clock has one business consequence: every `PayerContract` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `term_end_date` is earlier than the new logical now transitions to `EXPIRED`.

Three interval conventions coexist in this specification, and confusing them produces wrong answers:

- **Half-open intervals** govern `PriceRule` effectivity. A rule is in force when its `effective_from` is at or before now and, if `effective_to` is present, when now is strictly earlier than `effective_to`. A null `effective_to` means open-ended. Consequently two rules for the same scope may abut — one ending exactly where the next begins — without colliding.
- **Closed intervals** govern `PayerContract` terms and `ChargeCaptureBatch` service-date windows. A contract covers an instant when `term_start_date` is at or before it and `term_end_date` is at or after it; `term_end_date` is never null. A batch covers a service date when `window_start_date` is at or before it and `window_end_date` is at or after it. Because both endpoints are inclusive, abutting payer terms *do* overlap.
- **Inclusive hour windows** govern companion-charge pairing, described in §3.9.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary values are integer cents; the API never exchanges fractional currency. All multiples, markups, ratios and percentages are integer basis points, where 10000 basis points is exactly one times the underlying quantity — so 12000 basis points is 1.20 times, 40000 basis points is 4.00 times, and 10500 basis points is 1.05 times.

Wherever a basis-point factor is applied, the product is divided by 10000 and the result is rounded to the nearest whole cent using **round half up**, meaning a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero. This rounding happens **after each multiplication**, not once at the end of a chain. Basis-point outputs — the facility's aggregate markup ratio, a change request's projected markup ratio, a captured line's variance, a batch's mean variance — are likewise whole integers produced by a round-half-up on the final division.

One score in the system is not an integer: the duplicate similarity score is a decimal rounded half up to four decimal places, and its description-similarity component is rounded to four decimal places *before* it is multiplied by its weight.

### 2.7. Chargemaster Posture: The Facility-Wide Governance Gate

Each `Facility` publishes a computed field `chargemaster_posture` whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is never written directly; it is derived, and it is re-derived at the end of every mutation that touches any child of the facility — specifically after every transparency-file transition, every code-update-cycle transition, every change to a charge item's `price_status`, and every price-rule supersession. If re-deriving it changes any field on the `Facility` row, the facility's `version` increases by 1.

The derivation is evaluated in this exact precedence order, stopping at the first match:

1. `FROZEN`, when at least one `TransparencyFile` belonging to this facility has `status` of either `BUILDING` or `VALIDATED`.
2. `RESTRICTED`, when at least one `CodeUpdateCycle` belonging to this facility has `status` of either `MAPPING` or `CROSSWALK_APPROVED`.
3. `RESTRICTED`, when the facility's `stale_price_item_count` is 5 or more (five exactly qualifies).
4. `OPEN`, otherwise.

A `FROZEN` posture rejects six write operations with `ERR_FACILITY_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): creating a charge item, driving a charge item's lifecycle, repricing a charge item, creating a price rule, implementing an approved change request, and — for the code-update cycle — the `ACTIVATE` and `ROLL_BACK` actions. A `RESTRICTED` posture rejects exactly two things with `ERR_FACILITY_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409): creating a charge item whose `code_type` is `CPT` or `HCPCS`, and approving a change request whose `request_type` is `REVENUE_CODE_REMAP`. An `OPEN` posture imposes no gate. Note the asymmetry deliberately: a `RESTRICTED` facility still accepts `REV_ONLY` and `CDM_INTERNAL` item creation, and still accepts approval of every other request type.

Alongside posture, the facility maintains `transparency_status`, which takes the value `CURRENT` or `NEEDS_REFRESH` and defaults to `NEEDS_REFRESH`. It is `CURRENT` only while `published_transparency_file_id` is non-null *and* no `ACTIVE` charge item has been mutated since that file was published; any such mutation drops it back to `NEEDS_REFRESH` and clears `published_transparency_file_id` to null.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Rejection

Two write operations in this system can legitimately be attempted against a parent whose state the client has not re-read, and in both the correct outcome is refusal rather than a blind overwrite. Both use optimistic concurrency control on the parent's `version`.

The first is change-request approval. A `POST /chargemaster/change-requests/{change_request_id}/approve` request must carry `expected_charge_item_version` as a required, non-optional body field, holding the `version` of the target `ChargeItem` that the approver last observed (readable from the item's own read endpoint). Two change requests may legitimately target the same charge item and both may reach `UNDER_REVIEW`; approving one is a valid supersede of the other's view of that item, because approving a `PRICE_CHANGE` or `NEW_ITEM` request advances the item's `version` when it moves `price_status` to `REPRICE_PENDING`. If the supplied `expected_charge_item_version` does not equal the live `ChargeItem.version`, the approval is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The second is transparency-file construction. A `POST /chargemaster/transparency-files` request must carry `expected_facility_version` as a required, non-optional body field, holding the `Facility.version` the client last observed. A new build legitimately supersedes an earlier published file for the same facility, so state alone does not block a second build; instead, a build whose `expected_facility_version` does not equal the live `Facility.version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the facility's inclusion set has moved since the client last observed it. The same field and the same error code apply to the facility configuration update endpoint, `PATCH /chargemaster/facilities/{facility_id}`, which also requires `expected_facility_version`.

No other endpoint takes a version-expectation field. In particular, driving an item's lifecycle, repricing an item, dispositioning a code-update entry, reconciling a batch and dispositioning a finding all take no expected-version input. Two related but distinct staleness mechanisms exist elsewhere and must not be conflated with these two: `ChangeRequest.pinned_charge_item_version`, checked at implementation time and reported as `ERR_PINNED_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 409); and `PriceChangeImpactAnalysis.analysed_charge_item_version`, checked at sign-off and reported as `ERR_ANALYSIS_SIGNOFF_STALE` (HTTP 409). Neither is client-supplied.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and Validation Ordering

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, and the outermost missing segment determines the error. The full evaluation order for any request is fixed, and the **first** failing check determines the returned `error_id` — later checks are never allowed to mask an earlier one:

1. Header presence and role legality — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Endpoint role admission — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. Department scope for a `DepartmentManager` — `ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
4. `facility_id`, wherever it appears in a path or body, resolves to a `Facility` — `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` (404).
5. `charge_item_id` in the path resolves — `ERR_CHARGE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. `change_request_id` in the path resolves — `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` (404).
7. `cycle_id` resolves to a `CodeUpdateCycle` and `entry_id` resolves to a `CodeUpdateEntry` — `ERR_CODE_UPDATE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
8. `batch_id` resolves to a `ChargeCaptureBatch`, `captured_charge_id` to a `CapturedCharge` and `finding_id` to a `MissingChargeFinding` — `ERR_CAPTURE_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
9. `file_id` resolves to a `TransparencyFile`, `analysis_id` to a `PriceChangeImpactAnalysis`, `price_rule_id` to a `PriceRule`, `payer_contract_id` to a `PayerContract`, `department_id` to a `Department` and `revenue_code_id` to a `RevenueCode` — `ERR_GOVERNANCE_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
10. Body foreign keys resolve, checked left to right in this declaration order: `department_id`, then `revenue_code_id` or `proposed_revenue_code_id`, then `charge_item_id`, then `companion_required_item_id`, then `duplicate_of_item_id`, then `recovery_captured_charge_id` — `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
11. Payload schema conformance — `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). This covers required fields being present, enum members being legal, integers being non-negative, `quantity` being at least 1, `justification` being at least 10 characters long, datetimes being well-formed ISO 8601 UTC, `markup_tiers` being sorted ascending with a first tier whose `min_cost_basis_cents` is 0, `effective_to` being either null or strictly later than `effective_from`, `term_end_date` being no earlier than `term_start_date`, `window_end_date` being no earlier than `window_start_date`, and a non-empty `defer_reason` or `disposition_note` wherever one is required.
12. Pagination bounds on list endpoints — `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).
13. Entity-state admissibility, one rank shared by nine mutually exclusive rules distinguished by target entity, so exactly one can fire for a given request: `ERR_INVALID_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_STATE` (409) for item lifecycle and reprice; `ERR_INVALID_ITEM_PRICE_STATE` (409) for change-request implementation; `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_STATE` (409) for change-request decisions and resubmits; `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` (409) for cycle lifecycle actions and entry creation; `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE` (409) for entry disposition; `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE` (409) for batch reconcile, batch lifecycle and line append; `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE` (409) for finding disposition; `ERR_INVALID_FILE_STATE` (409) for transparency-file lifecycle; and `ERR_INVALID_ANALYSIS_STATE` (409) for analysis sign-off.
14. `FROZEN` posture gate — `ERR_FACILITY_FROZEN` (409).
15. `RESTRICTED` posture gate — `ERR_FACILITY_RESTRICTED` (409).
16. Charge-item stale-version rejection — `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (412).
17. Facility stale-version rejection — `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (412).
18. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409).
19. Review-before-approval — `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_REVIEWED` (409).
20. Transparency publication blackout — `ERR_TRANSPARENCY_BLACKOUT` (409).
21. Unmapped code-update entries at crosswalk approval — `ERR_UNMAPPED_CODE_ENTRIES` (409).
22. Analysis signability — `ERR_ANALYSIS_SIGNOFF_STALE` (409).
23–31. Business referential integrity, in this order: `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_MAPPING_INVALID` (422), `ERR_DEPARTMENT_REVENUE_CODE_NOT_ALLOWED` (422), `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_RETIRED` (409), `ERR_COMPANION_ITEM_INVALID` (422), `ERR_DUPLICATE_TARGET_INVALID` (422), `ERR_CROSSWALK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND` (404), `ERR_CROSSWALK_TARGET_AMBIGUOUS` (409), `ERR_PINNED_VERSION_STALE` (409), `ERR_RECOVERY_LINE_INVALID` (422).
32–38. Temporal rules, in this order: `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST` (422), `ERR_ITEM_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE` (409), `ERR_PRICE_RULE_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409), `ERR_BATCH_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (422).
39–52. Business constraints, in this order: the natural-key uniqueness rank, which is one logical rank split into `ERR_DUPLICATE_CDM_NUMBER` (409) for charge items and `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409) for every other natural key; then `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409); then the empty-collection rank, one logical rank split by the collection involved into `ERR_BATCH_HAS_NO_LINES` (422), `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_NO_ENTRIES` (422), `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_NO_MAPPED_ENTRIES` (422) and `ERR_NOTHING_TO_DISPUTE` (422); then `ERR_NO_PUBLISHABLE_ITEMS` (422), `ERR_FINDINGS_OPEN` (409), `ERR_DEPARTMENT_CAPTURE_HOLD` (409), `ERR_ITEM_IN_OPEN_BATCH` (409), `ERR_ITEM_HAS_OPEN_REQUEST` (409), `ERR_ITEM_PENDING_CODE_UPDATE` (409), `ERR_DUPLICATE_SIMILARITY_TOO_LOW` (422), `ERR_ITEM_PRICE_NOT_CURRENT` (409), `ERR_ITEM_PRICE_FROZEN` (409), `ERR_ITEM_RETIRED_AS_DUPLICATE` (409), and the batch-state rule for finding dispositions which also reports `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE` (409).
53–54. Fiscal rules, last of all: `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_RULE` (422), then `ERR_AGGREGATE_MARKUP_LIMIT` (422).

One further rule governs batch finalization, which is guarded by `ERR_BATCH_LINES_UNRECONCILED` (409) when any line of the batch still carries `match_status` of `UNRECONCILED`; it is evaluated with the batch's other finalization constraints.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Four distinct identity comparisons share the single error code `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409), and all four are enforced at the same priority rank:

- The reviewer of a change request must differ from its submitter: moving a request to `UNDER_REVIEW` requires that `X-User-ID` differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. The same comparison guards a rejection decision.
- The approver of a change request must differ from both its submitter and its reviewer: `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id` *and* from `reviewed_by_user_id`.
- The crosswalk approver of an annual code update cycle must differ from every entry mapper: `X-User-ID` must not equal the `mapped_by_user_id` recorded on any entry of that cycle.
- Only the reviewer of record may bounce a request back for changes: requesting changes requires that `X-User-ID` equal the recorded `reviewed_by_user_id`.

The resubmit path is scoped by the same principle from the other direction — only the original submitter may answer a change request, and that comparison is reported as `ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

### 2.11. Cascade and Recomputation Discipline

Aggregates in this system are always derived from persisted rows, never from projections. The `Facility` row carries `active_item_count` (the number of its charge items whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE`), `aggregate_gross_charge_cents` (the sum of `gross_charge_cents` over those same `ACTIVE` items), `aggregate_cost_basis_cents` (the sum of `cost_basis_cents` over those same `ACTIVE` items), `aggregate_markup_ratio_bp` (the aggregate gross charge multiplied by 10000, divided by the aggregate cost basis, rounded half up — and defined as 0 when the aggregate cost basis is 0), and `stale_price_item_count` (the number of its charge items that are simultaneously `ACTIVE` in the lifecycle dimension and `PRICE_STALE` in the pricing dimension). Items in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `INACTIVE` or `ARCHIVED` never contribute to any facility aggregate.

Similarly the `Department` row carries `unresolved_finding_amount_cents` (the sum of `estimated_lost_charge_cents` over its `CONFIRMED` missing-charge findings), `recovered_charge_cents` (the same sum over its `RESOLVED` findings), `open_request_count` (the number of its change requests whose `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`), and the boolean `capture_hold`, which defaults to false and becomes true once `unresolved_finding_amount_cents` reaches 5,000 dollars — that is, 500000 cents — or more.

The most consequential cascade chain in the system links item mutation to transparency supersession, and it fires identically no matter which operation triggers it. Whenever an `ACTIVE` charge item of a facility is mutated — by a reprice, by a change-request implementation, by an annual code-cycle activation or rollback, or by a lifecycle transition — four things happen in order: the item's own `version` advances; every `TransparencyFile` of that facility whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`; the facility's `published_transparency_file_id` is cleared to null and its `transparency_status` set to `NEEDS_REFRESH`; and the facility's aggregates and `chargemaster_posture` are recomputed with `Facility.version` advancing by 1. When the supersession was caused by an upstream item mutation rather than by a newer file being published, the superseded file's `superseded_by_file_id` remains null; when a newer file's publication caused it, `superseded_by_file_id` names that newer file.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Facility, Its Departments and the Revenue-Code Table

A `SystemAdmin` begins by creating the governance root. A facility is created with a `name` and a `cms_certification_number` (its CMS CCN, which must be unique across all facilities), and with six pricing-governance knobs that all have defaults so that a minimal payload is legal. `rounding_mode` defaults to `NEAREST_DOLLAR` and may instead be `NEAREST_CENT` or `PSYCHOLOGICAL_99`; it controls the final rounding step of every gross-charge computation. `aggregate_markup_limit_bp` defaults to 40000 basis points — a facility-wide ceiling of four times cost. `volume_growth_bp` defaults to 10500 basis points, a projected annual volume growth of five percent, and is consulted only by the forward-projected impact variant. `impact_lookback_days` defaults to 90 days and is consulted only by the historical-replay impact variant. `duplicate_detection_mode` defaults to `STRICT_CODE` and may instead be `FUZZY_DESCRIPTION`; it selects which duplicate-scoring variant the cleanup workflow applies. A newly created facility has `version` 1, all aggregates at 0, `chargemaster_posture` of `OPEN` and `transparency_status` of `NEEDS_REFRESH`; `published_transparency_file_id` is null.

All six knobs may be adjusted later by a `SystemAdmin`, and any such adjustment must carry `expected_facility_version`; a successful adjustment advances `Facility.version` by 1 and re-derives `chargemaster_posture`. A collision on `cms_certification_number` is reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

The national revenue-code reference table is also `SystemAdmin` territory. Each `RevenueCode` row carries a four-digit UB-04 `code` string that is globally unique (for example `'0300'`), a human-readable `description`, a boolean `requires_hcpcs`, an `allowed_code_types` array drawn from the four values `CPT`, `HCPCS`, `REV_ONLY` and `CDM_INTERNAL`, a `pricing_class` of `SUPPLY`, `PHARMACY`, `PROCEDURE` or `ROOM_AND_BOARD` that forms part of the scope key used when selecting a price rule, and a `status` that defaults to `ACTIVE` and may be `RETIRED`. Retiring a revenue code is a one-way flip of `status` from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED`; items already `ACTIVE` under it keep billing, but no item mapped to a retired code may thereafter be activated or reactivated, which surfaces as `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_RETIRED`. Revenue codes are reference data and carry only `id` and `created_at`. A duplicate `code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Departments sit under a facility. Each is created with `facility_id`, a `name` such as "Cardiac Cath Lab", a `cost_center_code` unique within the facility, the `manager_user_id` of the `DepartmentManager` entitled to act for it, and an optional `allowed_revenue_code_values` array of four-digit revenue-code strings. That array is a whitelist: when it is non-empty, an item in this department may only map to a revenue code whose `code` appears in it, and a violation is `ERR_DEPARTMENT_REVENUE_CODE_NOT_ALLOWED`. An empty array means unrestricted. A fresh department has `capture_hold` false, `unresolved_finding_amount_cents`, `recovered_charge_cents` and `open_request_count` all at 0. A duplicate `cost_center_code` within the same facility is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

### 3.2. Price-Setting Methodology, Gross Charge and Markup Compliance

Pricing methodology is expressed as `PriceRule` rows authored by a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`. A rule is scoped to a `(facility_id, revenue_code_id)` pair and declares a `pricing_method` of `COST_PLUS_MARKUP`, `MEDICARE_MULTIPLE` or `FLAT_FEE`. For cost-plus pricing it carries `markup_tiers`, an array of objects each holding `min_cost_basis_cents` and `markup_bp`, sorted ascending by `min_cost_basis_cents` with a first tier whose `min_cost_basis_cents` is exactly 0; a payload violating either ordering condition is `ERR_VALIDATION`. When `markup_tiers` is omitted the rule adopts the standard three-band ladder: a 3.20-times markup (32000 basis points) from zero cost, stepping down to 2.40 times (24000 basis points) once the cost basis reaches 500 dollars, and to 1.80 times (18000 basis points) once it reaches 2,500 dollars. Every rule also carries `min_medicare_multiple_bp`, defaulting to 12000 basis points, and `market_cap_multiple_bp`, defaulting to 11000 basis points. `flat_fee_cents` holds the charge used verbatim under `FLAT_FEE` pricing and is null otherwise.

Effectivity is half-open: `effective_from` is the inclusive start and `effective_to` the exclusive end, with null meaning open-ended. Creating a rule triggers a scope-key overlap check against other rules of the same `(facility_id, revenue_code_id)` whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; if the new interval intersects an existing one the create is refused with `ERR_PRICE_RULE_OVERLAP`. Because the intervals are half-open, an existing rule whose `effective_to` equals the new rule's `effective_from` abuts rather than overlaps and is entirely legal.

Deliberate replacement is expressed through an optional `supersede_rule_id` on the create payload. Naming a rule there excludes that rule from the overlap check, and on success four things follow. The named rule transitions `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, its `effective_to` is truncated to the new rule's `effective_from`, and its `superseded_by_rule_id` is set to the new rule's identifier. Every `ChargeItem` whose `applied_price_rule_id` names the superseded rule transitions in the pricing dimension from `PRICED_CURRENT` to `PRICE_STALE`, with each such item's `version` advancing by 1. The facility's `stale_price_item_count` is then recomputed, and if it has reached 5 the facility's `chargemaster_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`; `Facility.version` advances by 1. A rule may also sit in `RETIRED` status, in which case, like `SUPERSEDED`, it is never selectable; `superseded_by_rule_id` is null while a rule is `ACTIVE` or `RETIRED`. Each rule exposes `applied_item_count`, the number of charge items whose `applied_price_rule_id` names it.

**Rule selection** runs at the start of every gross-charge computation. The candidate set is every `PriceRule` whose `facility_id` matches the item's facility, whose `revenue_code_id` matches the item's revenue code, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `effective_from` is at or before the logical now, and whose `effective_to` is either null or strictly later than the logical now. `SUPERSEDED` and `RETIRED` rules are never candidates. If the candidate set is empty, the operation fails with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_RULE` (HTTP 422). Otherwise candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending — never by `id` — and the first ranked candidate is selected.

**The gross charge** is then built in three pinned steps from the selected rule, the single charge item under evaluation, and the owning facility's `rounding_mode`. No other item participates.

*Step one — method dispatch produces a base amount.* Under `FLAT_FEE`, the base is the rule's `flat_fee_cents` exactly. Under `MEDICARE_MULTIPLE`, the base is the item's `medicare_rate_cents` multiplied by the rule's `min_medicare_multiple_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Under `COST_PLUS_MARKUP`, the applicable tier is the last tier in the ascending `markup_tiers` array whose `min_cost_basis_cents` is at or below the item's `cost_basis_cents` — the boundary is inclusive, so a cost basis exactly equal to a tier's threshold selects *that* tier rather than the one below it — and the base is the item's `cost_basis_cents` multiplied by the selected tier's `markup_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up.

*Step two — the Medicare floor is applied first, then the market cap.* The floor is the item's `medicare_rate_cents` multiplied by `min_medicare_multiple_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The cap is the item's `market_p75_cents` multiplied by `market_cap_multiple_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The base is raised to the floor if it is below it — the larger of base and floor is taken — and the result is then lowered to the cap if it is above it — the smaller of that intermediate value and the cap is taken. The order is pinned and it matters: when the floor exceeds the cap, the cap wins, and the item's `price_conflict` flag is set to true (it is false by default and whenever the floor is at or below the cap). Applying the cap first and the floor second would yield the floor amount instead and is wrong.

*Step three — the facility's rounding mode is applied exactly once, last.* Under `NEAREST_CENT` the constrained amount stands unchanged. Under `NEAREST_DOLLAR` the amount is divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole number of dollars, and multiplied back by 100. Under `PSYCHOLOGICAL_99` the amount is divided by 100, rounded *up* to the next whole dollar, multiplied by 100, and then reduced by one cent — so every price ends in 99 cents.

**Markup compliance** is a second, facility-level gate on the same arithmetic, and it is evaluated at four moments: when a change request is created, when it is resubmitted, when it is approved, and when a charge item is approved in the lifecycle dimension. It looks circular at first glance — the facility ratio depends on the item's gross charge while the item's admissibility depends on the facility ratio — so the evaluation order is pinned. First, the item under evaluation is *excluded* from the facility snapshot: the snapshot numerator is the facility's `aggregate_gross_charge_cents` less the item's own `gross_charge_cents` if and only if the item's `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` (and less nothing otherwise), and the snapshot denominator is the facility's `aggregate_cost_basis_cents` less the item's own `cost_basis_cents` under the same condition. Second, the proposed post-change values are added back: the proposed gross charge is the request's `projected_gross_charge_cents`, and the proposed cost is `proposed_cost_basis_cents` when `request_type` is `NEW_ITEM` or `PRICE_CHANGE` and the item's existing `cost_basis_cents` otherwise. Third, `projected_markup_ratio_bp` is the sum of snapshot and proposed gross charge, multiplied by 10000, divided by the sum of snapshot and proposed cost, rounded half up — and defined as 0 when that denominator is 0. The check passes when `projected_markup_ratio_bp` is at or below the facility's `aggregate_markup_limit_bp`; equality passes. Failure is `ERR_AGGREGATE_MARKUP_LIMIT` (HTTP 422). Excluding the item first is what stops a repriced `ACTIVE` item from being counted twice; naively adding proposed values on top of the live aggregate inflates the ratio and would reject legitimate changes. The ratio is computed before any field is written, and once the write does occur the stored facility aggregates are recomputed from the persisted items rather than from this projection.

### 3.3. Creating a Chargemaster Item and Walking It Into Service

A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` drafts new chargemaster items. The creation payload names the owning `facility_id` and `department_id`, the `revenue_code_id` that maps the item to a UB-04 revenue code, an internal `cdm_number` that must be unique within the facility, a free-text `description`, a `code_type` of `CPT`, `HCPCS`, `REV_ONLY` or `CDM_INTERNAL`, the three pricing inputs `cost_basis_cents` (fully-loaded acquisition cost), `medicare_rate_cents` (the Medicare allowable) and `market_p75_cents` (the seventy-fifth-percentile market charge), and an `effective_from` instant from which the item may be billed. Optionally it carries `code_value`, the CPT or HCPCS code string, which may be null only when `code_type` is `REV_ONLY` or `CDM_INTERNAL`. Optionally it also carries `companion_required_item_id` and `companion_window_hours`, discussed in §3.9; `companion_window_hours` defaults to 24 when omitted, and `companion_required_item_id` is null when no pairing applies.

Creation is refused outright while the facility's posture is `FROZEN` (`ERR_FACILITY_FROZEN`), and refused for a `CPT` or `HCPCS` item while the posture is `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_FACILITY_RESTRICTED`). Beyond posture, four content rules apply. The revenue-code mapping must be legal: the item's `code_type` must appear in the revenue code's `allowed_code_types`, and if that revenue code has `requires_hcpcs` set to true then the item's `code_type` must be `CPT` or `HCPCS` and its `code_value` must be non-null — otherwise `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_MAPPING_INVALID` (422). The owning department's `allowed_revenue_code_values` must be empty or must contain the mapped revenue code's `code`, otherwise `ERR_DEPARTMENT_REVENUE_CODE_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). A supplied `companion_required_item_id` must reference a *different* charge item in the *same* facility whose `lifecycle_status` is not `ARCHIVED`; pointing at the item itself, at another facility's item, or at an archived item is `ERR_COMPANION_ITEM_INVALID` (422). And `effective_from` must be at or after the logical now at creation time — a back-dated item is `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST` (422). A colliding `cdm_number` within the facility is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CDM_NUMBER` (409).

A newly created item lands in `DRAFT` in the lifecycle dimension and `PRICE_STALE` in the pricing dimension, with `gross_charge_cents` at 0, `prior_gross_charge_cents` at 0, `price_conflict` false, `applied_price_rule_id` null (nothing has been priced yet), `duplicate_of_item_id` null, `approved_by_user_id` null, `last_code_cycle_id` null, and `version` 1.

The lifecycle dimension is driven by a single action-bearing operation whose `action` selects among `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW`, `RETURN_TO_DRAFT`, `APPROVE`, `ACTIVATE`, `DEACTIVATE`, `REACTIVATE`, `ARCHIVE` and `MARK_DUPLICATE`. Every one of those actions accepts an optional free-text `note`; `duplicate_of_item_id` is supplied in the payload only for `MARK_DUPLICATE`. Every one of them is gated on the facility not being `FROZEN`, and every one of them advances the item's `version` by 1. An action that is not declared out of the item's current `lifecycle_status` — archiving directly from `ACTIVE`, approving from `DRAFT`, and so on — is `ERR_INVALID_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_STATE` (409). `ARCHIVED` is the only terminal lifecycle state.

`DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW` is the analyst's submission for review. Beyond posture it re-checks the revenue-code mapping legality (`ERR_REVENUE_CODE_MAPPING_INVALID`) and the department whitelist (`ERR_DEPARTMENT_REVENUE_CODE_NOT_ALLOWED`) — the same two rules enforced at creation, re-evaluated because reference data may have moved in between. It sets `lifecycle_status` to `PENDING_REVIEW` and touches nothing in the pricing dimension: the two dimensions are independent and this transition does not read or write `price_status`.

`PENDING_REVIEW → DRAFT`, the analyst's return-to-draft, has no precondition beyond posture and simply sets `lifecycle_status` back to `DRAFT`. An item may cycle `DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW → DRAFT` any number of times.

`PENDING_REVIEW → APPROVED` is a `PricingCommitteeApprover` action, and here the two dimensions finally meet. Two additional preconditions apply beyond posture, evaluated in this order: the pricing dimension must already be settled, meaning `price_status` must equal `PRICED_CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_PRICE_NOT_CURRENT` (409); and the markup-compliance projection of §3.2 must land at or below the facility's `aggregate_markup_limit_bp`, otherwise `ERR_AGGREGATE_MARKUP_LIMIT` (422). On success `lifecycle_status` becomes `APPROVED` and `approved_by_user_id` records the approving `X-User-ID`.

`APPROVED → ACTIVE`, also a `PricingCommitteeApprover` action, is where the item joins the facility's published aggregates, and it carries three preconditions beyond posture. The mapped revenue code's `status` must still be `ACTIVE` — a cross-entity dependency on reference data that may have been retired since the item was drafted — otherwise `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_RETIRED` (409). The item's `effective_from` must be at or before the logical now, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE` (409). And no `CodeUpdateEntry` whose `target_charge_item_id` names this item may sit in `MAPPED` status inside a cycle that has reached `CROSSWALK_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_PENDING_CODE_UPDATE` (409) — an item about to be rewritten by an approved crosswalk must not be activated under its old code first. On success `lifecycle_status` becomes `ACTIVE`; the facility's `active_item_count` increases by 1, its `aggregate_gross_charge_cents` increases by the item's `gross_charge_cents`, its `aggregate_cost_basis_cents` increases by the item's `cost_basis_cents`, `aggregate_markup_ratio_bp` is recomputed, `transparency_status` is set to `NEEDS_REFRESH`, `chargemaster_posture` is re-derived, and `Facility.version` advances by 1.

`INACTIVE → ACTIVE`, the reactivation that closes the cleanup loop `ACTIVE → INACTIVE → ACTIVE`, is a `PricingCommitteeApprover` action with three preconditions beyond posture. The item's `duplicate_of_item_id` must be null — an item retired as a duplicate can never come back, and attempting it is `ERR_ITEM_RETIRED_AS_DUPLICATE` (409). The mapped revenue code's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_RETIRED`. And `price_status` must be `PRICED_CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_PRICE_NOT_CURRENT`. On success `lifecycle_status` returns to `ACTIVE`, the facility's aggregates and `chargemaster_posture` are recomputed, `transparency_status` is set to `NEEDS_REFRESH`, and `Facility.version` advances by 1.

`INACTIVE → ARCHIVED` is the analyst's final retirement and is gated on two collection emptiness rules beyond posture. No `ChangeRequest` targeting this item may sit in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` status, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_HAS_OPEN_REQUEST` (409). And no `CapturedCharge` referencing this item may sit in a `ChargeCaptureBatch` whose `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_IN_OPEN_BATCH` (409) — note that archival demands *fully settled* batches, a stricter test than the deactivation rule described in §3.8. On success `lifecycle_status` becomes `ARCHIVED`, and as a cascade every `CodeUpdateEntry` whose `target_charge_item_id` names this item and whose `status` is `PENDING` or `MAPPED` transitions to `DEFERRED` with `defer_reason` set to `'TARGET_ARCHIVED'`.

Items are discoverable by filter on `facility_id`, `department_id`, `revenue_code_id`, `duplicate_of_item_id`, `companion_required_item_id`, `applied_price_rule_id`, `last_code_cycle_id`, `lifecycle_status`, `price_status`, `code_value` and `cdm_number` — the last two being how a crosswalk analyst locates a retiring code, and the self-referential filters being how a reviewer walks the duplicate and companion graphs.

### 3.4. The Pricing Dimension: Repricing, Staleness and the Publication Freeze

Every charge item carries a second, fully independent state dimension, `price_status`, whose values are `PRICED_CURRENT`, `PRICE_STALE`, `REPRICE_PENDING` and `PRICE_FROZEN`. It begins at `PRICE_STALE` and has no terminal state — an item's price is never finished. Because the two dimensions are independent, a `DRAFT` item and an `ACTIVE` item are both repriceable, and the lifecycle actions of §3.3 never write `price_status` except where explicitly stated.

Repricing is the analyst's core act, and it moves `PRICE_STALE → PRICED_CURRENT`. Beyond the facility not being `FROZEN`, two preconditions apply: the item's `lifecycle_status` must not be `ARCHIVED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_STATE` (409); and the rule selector of §3.2 must find at least one candidate, otherwise `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_RULE` (422). A reprice attempted while `price_status` is `PRICE_FROZEN` is refused with `ERR_ITEM_PRICE_FROZEN` (409). On success, in order: `prior_gross_charge_cents` takes the item's outgoing `gross_charge_cents` before it is overwritten; `gross_charge_cents` takes the computed output; `applied_price_rule_id` records the selected rule; `price_conflict` is set according to whether the Medicare floor exceeded the market cap; `price_status` becomes `PRICED_CURRENT`; and the item's `version` advances by 1.

Two further cascades ride on a reprice. If — and only if — the item's `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE`, the facility's `aggregate_gross_charge_cents` and `aggregate_markup_ratio_bp` are recomputed, `stale_price_item_count` is recomputed, `transparency_status` is set to `NEEDS_REFRESH`, `chargemaster_posture` is re-derived, any `PUBLISHED` transparency file for that facility transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, and `Facility.version` advances by 1. Independently of lifecycle state, every `PriceChangeImpactAnalysis` whose `status` is `COMPUTED` and whose change request targets this item transitions to `STALE` — the price the analysis measured against no longer exists.

Moving in the other direction, `PRICED_CURRENT → PRICE_STALE` is never a client action. It fires when the `PriceRule` named by the item's `applied_price_rule_id` transitions `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`; the precondition is precisely that the item's `applied_price_rule_id` equals the superseded rule's identifier. `price_status` becomes `PRICE_STALE` and the item's `version` advances by 1; the facility's `stale_price_item_count` is recomputed, and once that count reaches 5 the facility's `chargemaster_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`, with `Facility.version` advancing by 1.

Two symmetric transitions carry an item into `REPRICE_PENDING`, both driven by a `PricingCommitteeApprover` approving a change request whose `request_type` is `PRICE_CHANGE` or `NEW_ITEM`, and both requiring that the approving request target this item and satisfy every one of the approval preconditions of §3.5. From `PRICED_CURRENT`, the transition `PRICED_CURRENT → REPRICE_PENDING` sets `price_status` to `REPRICE_PENDING` and advances the item's `version` by 1 — and that version bump is exactly what a second, stale approval attempt observes as a version mismatch. From `PRICE_STALE`, the transition `PRICE_STALE → REPRICE_PENDING` behaves identically: `price_status` becomes `REPRICE_PENDING` and the item's `version` advances by 1. The pricing dimension therefore reaches `REPRICE_PENDING` from either settled or stale, with no difference in effect.

`REPRICE_PENDING → PRICED_CURRENT` is the analyst's implementation of the approved request. Its precondition is that the request's `pinned_charge_item_version` still equal the live `ChargeItem.version`, otherwise `ERR_PINNED_VERSION_STALE` (409). On success the item's `cost_basis_cents`, `description` and `revenue_code_id` are updated according to the request's `request_type`, `prior_gross_charge_cents` takes the outgoing `gross_charge_cents`, `gross_charge_cents` is recomputed from the rule selector and pricing arithmetic, `price_status` becomes `PRICED_CURRENT`, and the item's `version` advances by 1. Attempting to implement a `PRICE_CHANGE` request against an item whose `price_status` is not `REPRICE_PENDING` is `ERR_INVALID_ITEM_PRICE_STATE` (409).

The last pair of pricing transitions belongs to the transparency workflow and is likewise system-driven. When a facility's transparency file moves `BUILDING → VALIDATED`, every charge item of that facility whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` — only the publishable inclusion set is frozen — and whose `price_status` is `PRICED_CURRENT` transitions `PRICED_CURRENT → PRICE_FROZEN`, each advancing its own `version` by 1. While an item is `PRICE_FROZEN` its price cannot be rebuilt, which is where `ERR_ITEM_PRICE_FROZEN` comes from. The thaw, `PRICE_FROZEN → PRICED_CURRENT`, fires when the file moves `VALIDATED → PUBLISHED` or `VALIDATED → FAILED`, on the condition that the facility has no *other* transparency file still sitting in `BUILDING` or `VALIDATED`; each thawed item's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.5. Department Change Requests: Submission, Review, Approval, Implementation

A `DepartmentManager` is the only actor who may open a `ChangeRequest`. The submission names the `department_id` the manager owns, the `charge_item_id` being targeted — always an existing item, and for a `NEW_ITEM` request that means the `DRAFT` item the analyst has just created — the `request_type`, and a `justification` of at least 10 characters. Three optional proposal fields carry the substance and which of them is read depends entirely on `request_type`: `proposed_cost_basis_cents` and `proposed_description` are read only for `NEW_ITEM` and `PRICE_CHANGE`, with a null `proposed_description` leaving the item's `description` unchanged; `proposed_revenue_code_id` is read only for `REVENUE_CODE_REMAP`; and a `DEACTIVATION` request reads none of them. The four legal `request_type` values are `NEW_ITEM`, `PRICE_CHANGE`, `REVENUE_CODE_REMAP` and `DEACTIVATION`.

Submission creates the request in `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at 0, `submitted_by_user_id` set from `X-User-ID`, `version` 1, and `reviewed_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `pinned_charge_item_version` and `decision_note` all null. Two dry-run figures are computed immediately: `projected_gross_charge_cents`, from the gross-charge arithmetic of §3.2 applied to the proposed inputs, and `projected_markup_ratio_bp`, from the markup-compliance projection. Because the dry run runs the rule selector, a submission against an item with no applicable rule fails with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_RULE`. The owning department's `open_request_count` is then recomputed.

Review is a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` decision that moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. Its two preconditions are that `X-User-ID` differ from `submitted_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`) and that the target item's `lifecycle_status` not be `ARCHIVED` (`ERR_INVALID_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_STATE`). It records `reviewed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and copies the payload's `decision_note` onto the request.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` the reviewer of record may bounce the request back, `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, on the single precondition that `X-User-ID` equal the recorded `reviewed_by_user_id` — only the reviewer who took the request may return it, and anyone else receives `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The payload's `decision_note` is recorded.

The revision loop closes with the manager's resubmit, `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. Two preconditions guard it: `X-User-ID` must equal the owning department's `manager_user_id` (`ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), and `revision_round` must be strictly less than 3 (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409). Read that boundary carefully — a request already at `revision_round` 3 is refused, while a request at `revision_round` 2 succeeds and lands at 3; the third resubmit is the last accepted one. On success `revision_round` increases by 1, the payload's `justification` and any supplied `proposed_cost_basis_cents`, `proposed_revenue_code_id` and `proposed_description` overwrite the stored values, `projected_gross_charge_cents` and `projected_markup_ratio_bp` are recomputed, `reviewed_by_user_id` is cleared to null so that a fresh review decision is mandatory, and `status` returns to `SUBMITTED`. The full cycle is therefore `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`.

Approval, `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, is reserved to the `PricingCommitteeApprover` and is the most heavily guarded transition in the system. Its preconditions are evaluated in this order. First — after the generic state admissibility check and before every business rule — the supplied `expected_charge_item_version` must equal the live `ChargeItem.version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Second, `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id` *and* from `reviewed_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Third, `reviewed_by_user_id` must not be null — a request cannot jump review — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_REVIEWED` (409). Fourth, when `request_type` is `REVENUE_CODE_REMAP`, the facility's `chargemaster_posture` must not be `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_FACILITY_RESTRICTED`, 409). Fifth, still only for `REVENUE_CODE_REMAP`, the `proposed_revenue_code_id` must resolve to a revenue code whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `allowed_code_types` contains the target item's `code_type`, otherwise `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_MAPPING_INVALID` (422). Sixth and last, `projected_markup_ratio_bp` must be at or below the facility's `aggregate_markup_limit_bp`, otherwise `ERR_AGGREGATE_MARKUP_LIMIT` (422). An optional `decision_note` may accompany the approval.

On success the request's `status` becomes `APPROVED` and `approved_by_user_id` records the approving `X-User-ID`. What happens to `pinned_charge_item_version` then differs by branch, and the two branches must be read as a matched pair. For `PRICE_CHANGE` and `NEW_ITEM`, the target item's `price_status` moves to `REPRICE_PENDING` and `ChargeItem.version` advances by 1; `pinned_charge_item_version` is set to the *post-bump* version so that the later implementation can match it. For `DEACTIVATION` and `REVENUE_CODE_REMAP`, the item is not mutated at all and `pinned_charge_item_version` simply equals the version that was observed. Either way the pin is what implementation later checks.

Rejection, `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, is also an approver action, guarded only by `X-User-ID` differing from `submitted_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). It sets `status` to `REJECTED`, leaves `approved_by_user_id` null, and records the payload's `decision_note`. It also carries one compensating cascade: when the target item's `price_status` is `REPRICE_PENDING` and no *other* `APPROVED` change request targets that item, the item's `price_status` reverts to `PRICE_STALE` and `ChargeItem.version` advances by 1.

Withdrawal is the submitter's own escape hatch and is reachable from two states with identical treatment. `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` and `CHANGES_REQUESTED → WITHDRAWN` are both `DepartmentManager` decisions, both require that `X-User-ID` equal `submitted_by_user_id` (`ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), both set `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, both advance the request's `version` by 1, and both trigger a recomputation of the department's `open_request_count`.

Implementation, `APPROVED → IMPLEMENTED`, returns the request to the `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` and accepts an optional `implementation_note`. Three preconditions apply: the facility must not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_FACILITY_FROZEN`); `pinned_charge_item_version` must still equal the live `ChargeItem.version`, otherwise `ERR_PINNED_VERSION_STALE` (409) — and when that fails the pin is *not* refreshed and the request remains `APPROVED`, with nothing mutated; and for a `DEACTIVATION` request the target item must additionally satisfy the deactivation preconditions of §3.8, which surface here as `ERR_ITEM_IN_OPEN_BATCH` (409).

The implementation branch is chosen by `request_type`. For `PRICE_CHANGE` and `NEW_ITEM`, the item's `cost_basis_cents` takes `proposed_cost_basis_cents`, its `description` takes `proposed_description` when that field is non-null, `prior_gross_charge_cents` takes the outgoing `gross_charge_cents`, `gross_charge_cents` is recomputed, `price_status` moves to `PRICED_CURRENT`, and `ChargeItem.version` advances by 1. For `REVENUE_CODE_REMAP`, the item's `revenue_code_id` takes `proposed_revenue_code_id`, `price_status` moves to `PRICE_STALE` and `ChargeItem.version` advances by 1 — deliberately *no* reprice, because the remapped item must be repriced explicitly under its new revenue code's rule. For `DEACTIVATION`, the item runs the full `ACTIVE → INACTIVE` transition with that transition's complete cascade.

Four further cascade links fire on every successful implementation regardless of branch. The charge item has been mutated and its `version` has advanced. Every `TransparencyFile` of the facility whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`. The facility's `published_transparency_file_id` is cleared to null, its `transparency_status` set to `NEEDS_REFRESH`, its aggregates and `chargemaster_posture` recomputed, and `Facility.version` advanced by 1. And every *other* change request with `status` `APPROVED` that targets the same `charge_item_id` and whose `pinned_charge_item_version` no longer equals the item's new version transitions `APPROVED → VOIDED_STALE`, advancing its own `version` by 1 and prompting a recomputation of the department's `open_request_count`. Separately, every `PriceChangeImpactAnalysis` for this request whose `status` is `COMPUTED` transitions to `STALE`.

`IMPLEMENTED`, `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `VOIDED_STALE` are the four terminal states of a change request. Any decision, resubmit or implementation attempted from a state that does not declare it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_STATE` (409). Requests are discoverable by filter on `department_id`, `charge_item_id`, `proposed_revenue_code_id`, `status` and `request_type` — the `proposed_revenue_code_id` filter being the reverse channel by which a reference-data owner finds the remaps pointed at a code.

### 3.6. Payer Contracts and the Impact of a Price Change on Reimbursement

Payer terms are the `ContractManager`'s domain. A `PayerContract` is created against a `facility_id` with a `payer_code` such as `'BCBS_STATE'`, a `product_line` of `COMMERCIAL`, `MEDICARE_ADVANTAGE` or `MEDICAID_MCO`, a `reimbursement_method` of `PERCENT_OF_CHARGE`, `LESSER_OF_FEE_SCHEDULE` or `CASE_RATE`, and a closed term bounded by `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` (the latter never null). Which money field is required depends on the method: `percent_of_charge_bp` is required unless `reimbursement_method` is `CASE_RATE`; `fee_schedule_cents` is required when the method is `LESSER_OF_FEE_SCHEDULE` and null otherwise; `case_rate_cents` is required when the method is `CASE_RATE` and null otherwise. Two optional fields tune downstream behaviour: `trailing_units`, defaulting to 0, records the payer's trailing-period utilization and is read only by the forward-projected impact variant; and `renegotiation_notice_days`, defaulting to 30, sets the clear gap this term demands before a successor term of the same scope may begin. A contract's `status` starts at `ACTIVE` and may become `EXPIRED` (when the logical clock passes `term_end_date`) or `TERMINATED`.

Two temporal checks run at creation, in this order. First, no other contract of the same `(facility_id, payer_code, product_line)` scope key whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` may have a `[term_start_date, term_end_date]` interval intersecting the new one; because both endpoints are inclusive, a prior term ending exactly on the new term's start date *does* collide, and the create is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409). Second, for the same scope key, the gap between the prior term's `term_end_date` and the new term's `term_start_date` must be **strictly greater** than the prior term's `renegotiation_notice_days` in days; a gap of exactly `renegotiation_notice_days` days fails with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409). Creating a contract also transitions every `PriceChangeImpactAnalysis` of that facility whose `status` is `COMPUTED` to `STALE`, since the payer mix has changed.

A `ContractManager` may end a term early. Termination, which accepts an optional `termination_note`, sets `status` to `TERMINATED` and advances the contract's `version` by 1, and likewise transitions every `COMPUTED` impact analysis of the facility to `STALE`. Contracts are discoverable by filter on `facility_id`, `payer_code`, `product_line` and `status`.

Before a committee signs off on a price move, someone must answer the question "what does this do to what we actually get paid?" That is a `PriceChangeImpactAnalysis`, computed by either a `ContractManager` or a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` against a named `change_request_id` under a chosen `variant` of `FORWARD_PROJECTED` or `HISTORICAL_REPLAY`. Computation snapshots three values on the analysis row — `analysed_charge_item_version` (the target item's `version` at computation time), `old_gross_charge_cents` (the item's current `gross_charge_cents`) and `new_gross_charge_cents` (the request's `projected_gross_charge_cents`) — and lands the analysis in `COMPUTED`.

Both variants share one reimbursement helper. Given a contract, a charge amount and a unit count, the per-unit reimbursement is computed as follows. Under `PERCENT_OF_CHARGE`, it is the charge multiplied by the contract's `percent_of_charge_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Under `LESSER_OF_FEE_SCHEDULE`, that same percentage amount is computed and the smaller of it and the contract's `fee_schedule_cents` is taken. Under `CASE_RATE`, it is the contract's `case_rate_cents` — which is charge-independent, so a case-rate payer's delta is always zero. The per-unit amount is rounded **first** and only then multiplied by the unit count; multiplying before rounding gives a different answer.

Under **`FORWARD_PROJECTED`**, the contract set is every `PayerContract` of the item's facility whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose closed term contains the logical now (its `term_start_date` at or before now and its `term_end_date` at or after now), ordered by `payer_code` ascending then `product_line` ascending, never by `id`. For each contract, the unit count is that contract's `trailing_units` multiplied by the facility's `volume_growth_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The helper is then applied twice — once with the old gross charge and once with the new — and a line is emitted holding `payer_code`, `product_line`, `units`, `old_reimbursement_cents`, `new_reimbursement_cents` and `delta_cents`, the last being the new reimbursement less the old. A payer whose `trailing_units` is zero still emits a line, with `units` of 0.

Under **`HISTORICAL_REPLAY`**, the input is real captured volume rather than a projection. The line set is every `CapturedCharge` whose `charge_item_id` is the target item, whose owning batch has `status` of `RECONCILED` or `CLOSED`, whose `match_status` is `MATCHED` or `PRICE_VARIANCE`, and whose `service_datetime` falls inside the lookback window — from the logical now less the facility's `impact_lookback_days` days up to the logical now, with **both** ends inclusive. Those lines are replayed in order of `service_datetime` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, never by `id`. For each line the applicable contract is the one for the item's facility whose `payer_code` equals the line's `payer_code`, whose closed term contains the line's `service_datetime`, and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` — the term in force on the *service* date, not today. When no such contract exists the line is skipped and contributes zero. Otherwise the line accumulates into a bucket keyed by `(payer_code, product_line)`: the bucket's `units` increases by the line's `quantity`, its old reimbursement increases by the helper applied to the old gross charge at that quantity, and its new reimbursement increases by the helper applied to the new gross charge at that quantity. One line is emitted per bucket, ordered by `payer_code` ascending then `product_line` ascending. A payer with no captured lines in the window emits no line at all.

`payer_lines` holds the emitted array in that order, and `total_reimbursement_delta_cents` is the sum of the per-line `delta_cents` values — each delta already rounded, so the rule is round per line and then sum. The two variants deliberately disagree on identical data: the forward projection prices every unit under today's contract, while the historical replay prices each unit under the contract that governed its service date, so a payer whose term expired mid-window contributes different numbers and the two totals differ.

Sign-off belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer` alone and moves `COMPUTED → SIGNED_OFF`, accepting an optional `signoff_note`. Two preconditions apply: the `status` must be `COMPUTED`, and `analysed_charge_item_version` must still equal the target item's live `ChargeItem.version`. A sign-off attempted on an analysis whose `status` is `STALE`, or on a `COMPUTED` analysis whose pinned item version has moved, is refused with `ERR_ANALYSIS_SIGNOFF_STALE` (409); an attempt against an analysis in any other status surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_ANALYSIS_STATE` (409). On success `signed_off_by_user_id` records the officer's `X-User-ID`; that field is null unless the analysis is `SIGNED_OFF`. `SIGNED_OFF` is terminal. The system-driven transition `COMPUTED → STALE` fires whenever the target item is mutated or a payer contract for the facility is created or terminated. Analyses are discoverable by filter on `change_request_id`, `variant` and `status`.

### 3.7. The Annual CPT/HCPCS Code Update Cycle

Once a year the coding authorities publish additions, deletions, revisions and crosswalks, and the chargemaster must absorb them atomically. A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` opens a `CodeUpdateCycle` against a `facility_id` with a `cycle_year`, a `code_set` of `CPT` or `HCPCS`, and an optional `cost_inflator_bp` defaulting to 10300 basis points — a three percent cost inflation applied when a crosswalk also moves the revenue code. The triple `(facility_id, code_set, cycle_year)` must be unique; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. A new cycle sits in `LOADED` with `entry_count`, `unmapped_entry_count` and `applied_entry_count` at 0, `pre_activation_active_item_count` and `pre_activation_aggregate_gross_charge_cents` null, and `activated_at` and `rolled_back_at` null. Cycles are discoverable by filter on `facility_id`, `code_set`, `cycle_year` and `status`.

Entries are added by the analyst while the cycle is in `LOADED` or `MAPPING`; adding an entry to a cycle past `MAPPING` is `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` (409). Each `CodeUpdateEntry` declares an `action` of `ADD`, `DELETE`, `REVISE` or `CROSSWALK`, an `old_code_value` (the retiring code string, null when the action is `ADD`), a `new_code_value` (the replacement, null when the action is `DELETE`), and an optional `new_revenue_code_id` naming a remapped revenue code, left null when the revenue code is unchanged. An entry is created in `PENDING`; the cycle's `entry_count` and `unmapped_entry_count` are recomputed and `CodeUpdateCycle.version` advances by 1. Entries are discoverable by filter on `cycle_id`, `target_charge_item_id`, `new_revenue_code_id`, `prior_revenue_code_id`, `status` and `action` — and because entries are mutated by cascade rather than by direct client action, that filtered listing is their only discovery channel.

`LOADED → MAPPING`, the analyst's start-mapping action, requires that `entry_count` be at least 1 — a cycle with no entries cannot start mapping, and the attempt is `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_NO_ENTRIES` (422). On success the cycle's `status` becomes `MAPPING`, and re-deriving the facility's posture yields `RESTRICTED` by rule 2 unless a transparency file is holding it at `FROZEN`; `Facility.version` advances by 1.

Each `PENDING` entry is then dispositioned by the analyst as either a map or a defer, both of which require the cycle to be in `MAPPING` (`ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`) and the entry itself to be in `PENDING` (`ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE`, 409). The `PENDING → DEFERRED` path requires a non-empty `defer_reason`, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`; it sets `status` to `DEFERRED`, records `mapped_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and prompts a recomputation of the cycle's `unmapped_entry_count` with `CodeUpdateCycle.version` advancing by 1. `DEFERRED` is terminal for an entry.

The `PENDING → MAPPED` path is where the real work happens. Unless the entry's `action` is `ADD`, exactly one `ChargeItem` in the cycle's facility must have `code_value` equal to the entry's `old_code_value` with `lifecycle_status` in `ACTIVE`, `INACTIVE` or `APPROVED`: zero matches is `ERR_CROSSWALK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND` (404) and more than one is `ERR_CROSSWALK_TARGET_AMBIGUOUS` (409). When `new_revenue_code_id` is non-null it must resolve to a revenue code whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `allowed_code_types` contains the target item's `code_type`, otherwise `ERR_REVENUE_CODE_MAPPING_INVALID` (422). On success `target_charge_item_id` records the resolved item, `mapped_by_user_id` records `X-User-ID`, `carried_gross_charge_cents` is computed as described next, `status` becomes `MAPPED`, and the cycle's `unmapped_entry_count` is recomputed with `CodeUpdateCycle.version` advancing by 1.

**The crosswalk price carry** determines what a mapped item's charge becomes when the cycle activates, and it is computed at map time rather than at activation time. When the entry's `action` is `DELETE`, the carry is the target item's current `gross_charge_cents` unchanged — the item will simply be deactivated at apply time. When `new_revenue_code_id` is null, or when it names the same revenue code the item already has, the carry is again the target item's current `gross_charge_cents`: a pure code substitution carries the price forward. Only when the revenue code genuinely changes is the price rebuilt, and then in a pinned order. First the target item's `cost_basis_cents` is multiplied by the cycle's `cost_inflator_bp`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to whole cents. Then the full gross-charge computation of §3.2 is run against the target item as if its cost basis were that inflated amount and its revenue code were the entry's `new_revenue_code_id`, selecting the price rule that applies to the **new** revenue code at map time. Because the inflation is rounded *before* tier selection, an inflated cost basis can cross a markup tier boundary and pick up a different `markup_bp` than the pre-inflation cost would have; inflating the resulting charge instead of the cost basis produces a different number and is wrong. If no rule applies to the new revenue code, the map fails with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_RULE`.

`MAPPING → CROSSWALK_APPROVED` is a `PricingCommitteeApprover` action carrying three preconditions. The cycle's `unmapped_entry_count` must be 0 — every entry must be either `MAPPED` or `DEFERRED` — otherwise `ERR_UNMAPPED_CODE_ENTRIES` (409). At least one entry must be in `MAPPED` status, so an all-deferred cycle cannot be approved, and that failure is `ERR_CYCLE_HAS_NO_MAPPED_ENTRIES` (422). And `X-User-ID` must differ from the `mapped_by_user_id` recorded on *every* entry of the cycle, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). On success `status` becomes `CROSSWALK_APPROVED`; the facility's posture remains `RESTRICTED`, since the derivation's second rule covers `CROSSWALK_APPROVED` just as it covers `MAPPING`.

`CROSSWALK_APPROVED → ACTIVATED` belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer` and is the atomic application of the whole cycle. Two preconditions apply: the facility must not be `FROZEN` — no transparency file may be sitting in `BUILDING` or `VALIDATED` — otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_FROZEN` (409); and no `ChangeRequest` in `APPROVED` status may target any item referenced by a `MAPPED` entry of this cycle, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_HAS_OPEN_REQUEST` (409). On success the cycle first snapshots the facility's state *before any child is mutated*: `pre_activation_active_item_count` takes the facility's `active_item_count` and `pre_activation_aggregate_gross_charge_cents` takes its `aggregate_gross_charge_cents`. Then `status` becomes `ACTIVATED`, `activated_at` takes the logical now, and the cycle's `version` advances by 1.

Four cascade links then run in sequence. First, every entry whose `status` is `MAPPED` is processed **in order of `created_at` ascending**, transitions `MAPPED → APPLIED`, and snapshots four values from its target item for later rollback: `prior_code_value` from the item's `code_value`, `prior_revenue_code_id` from its `revenue_code_id`, `prior_gross_charge_cents` from its `gross_charge_cents`, and `prior_lifecycle_status` from its `lifecycle_status`; each entry's `version` advances by 1. An entry may only apply if its `target_charge_item_id` still resolves to an item whose `lifecycle_status` is not `ARCHIVED`. Second, each applied entry mutates its target item: for `CROSSWALK` and `REVISE`, the item's `code_value` takes `new_code_value`, its `revenue_code_id` takes `new_revenue_code_id` when that field is non-null, its `gross_charge_cents` takes the entry's `carried_gross_charge_cents`, and its `price_status` moves to `PRICE_STALE`; for `DELETE`, the item's `lifecycle_status` moves to `INACTIVE`. In every case the item's `version` advances by 1 and its `last_code_cycle_id` records this cycle. Third, those item mutations force every `TransparencyFile` of the facility whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` to transition `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`. Fourth, that supersession clears the facility's `published_transparency_file_id` to null, sets `transparency_status` to `NEEDS_REFRESH`, recomputes `active_item_count`, `aggregate_gross_charge_cents`, `aggregate_cost_basis_cents`, `aggregate_markup_ratio_bp`, `stale_price_item_count` and `chargemaster_posture`, and advances `Facility.version` by 1.

`ACTIVATED → ROLLED_BACK` is the compensating action, again a `ComplianceOfficer` prerogative. Its two preconditions are that the facility not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_FACILITY_FROZEN`) and that no `ChargeCaptureBatch` of the facility currently in `RECONCILING` reference an item touched by this cycle, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_IN_OPEN_BATCH` (409). Rollback walks every entry whose `status` is `APPLIED` **in order of `created_at` descending** — the exact reverse of the apply order — and restores its target item verbatim from the snapshots: `code_value` from `prior_code_value`, `revenue_code_id` from `prior_revenue_code_id`, `gross_charge_cents` from `prior_gross_charge_cents`, `lifecycle_status` from `prior_lifecycle_status`, `price_status` set to `PRICE_STALE`, and `last_code_cycle_id` cleared to null, with each item's `version` advancing by 1. Each entry transitions `APPLIED → REVERTED`, which requires that its `prior_code_value` be non-null, and advances its own `version` by 1. `REVERTED` is terminal for an entry. The cycle's `status` becomes `ROLLED_BACK` and `rolled_back_at` takes the logical now. The facility's aggregates are then recomputed, and the conservation invariant must hold: `active_item_count` equals `pre_activation_active_item_count` and `aggregate_gross_charge_cents` equals `pre_activation_aggregate_gross_charge_cents`. Finally `chargemaster_posture` is re-derived and `Facility.version` advances by 1.

`MAPPING → CANCELLED` abandons a cycle before it applies. The `ComplianceOfficer` may cancel only while `applied_entry_count` is 0; a cancel attempted after entries have applied is `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` (409). Cancellation sets `status` to `CANCELLED` and transitions every entry whose `status` is `PENDING` or `MAPPED` to `DEFERRED` with `defer_reason` set to `'CYCLE_CANCELLED'`, then re-derives the facility's posture — the second derivation rule no longer holds for this cycle — with `Facility.version` advancing by 1. `ROLLED_BACK` and `CANCELLED` are the cycle's two terminal states. Every cycle lifecycle action accepts an optional `note`.

Two entry transitions are purely system-driven and share the `MAPPED → DEFERRED` shape. When a mapped entry's target item transitions `INACTIVE → ARCHIVED`, or when the owning cycle is cancelled, the entry moves to `DEFERRED` with `defer_reason` set to `'TARGET_ARCHIVED'` or `'CYCLE_CANCELLED'` respectively, its `target_charge_item_id` cleared to null, its `version` advanced by 1, and the cycle's `unmapped_entry_count` recomputed; this is permitted while the cycle's `status` is `MAPPING`, `CROSSWALK_APPROVED` or `CANCELLED`.

### 3.8. Duplicate and Inactive Item Cleanup

Two paths lead an `ACTIVE` item to `INACTIVE`, and they are the same transition seen from two motives: a plain deactivation, and a duplicate retirement. Both are `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` actions, both require the facility not to be `FROZEN`, and both are blocked while any `CapturedCharge` referencing the item sits in a `ChargeCaptureBatch` whose `status` is `INGESTED` or `RECONCILING`, which surfaces as `ERR_ITEM_IN_OPEN_BATCH` (409). Note this is looser than the archival rule of §3.3, which demands that *no* non-`CLOSED` batch reference the item: deactivation tolerates a `RECONCILED` or `DISPUTED` batch, archival does not.

The duplicate-retirement path adds two preconditions of its own. The `duplicate_of_item_id` supplied in the payload must reference a *different* item in the *same* facility whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` and which is not itself flagged as a duplicate — pointing at the item itself, at another facility's item, at a non-`ACTIVE` item, or at an already-flagged duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TARGET_INVALID` (422). And the similarity score between the item being retired and the surviving item must reach the threshold of 0.8000 under the facility's `duplicate_detection_mode`; a score below it is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SIMILARITY_TOO_LOW` (422).

On success `lifecycle_status` becomes `INACTIVE` and the item's `version` advances by 1; on the duplicate path, `duplicate_of_item_id` is set to the surviving item's identifier. The facility then loses the item from its aggregates: `active_item_count` decreases by 1, `aggregate_gross_charge_cents` decreases by the item's `gross_charge_cents`, `aggregate_cost_basis_cents` decreases by the item's `cost_basis_cents`, and `aggregate_markup_ratio_bp` is recomputed, with `Facility.version` advancing by 1. Three further cascade links follow: every `TransparencyFile` of the facility whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`; the facility's `published_transparency_file_id` is cleared to null and `transparency_status` set to `NEEDS_REFRESH`; and `chargemaster_posture` is re-derived.

**Scoring similarity.** The candidate set for any subject item is every `ChargeItem` of the same facility other than the subject itself whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `duplicate_of_item_id` is null; `INACTIVE`, `ARCHIVED` and `DRAFT` items and items already flagged as duplicates are excluded. Candidates are ranked by score descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, never by `id`. Three component signals feed the score. The code signal is 1 when the two items share the same `code_type` and the same `code_value` and that `code_value` is non-null, and 0 otherwise. The revenue signal is 1 when the two items share the same `revenue_code_id` and 0 otherwise. The description signal is a Jaccard similarity over description tokens, where tokenizing a `description` means upper-casing it, splitting on every non-alphanumeric character, discarding tokens shorter than three characters, and deduplicating the remainder into a set; the similarity is the size of the intersection of the two token sets divided by the size of their union, rounded half up to four decimal places, and defined as 0 when the union is empty.

The two variants then combine those signals very differently. Under **`STRICT_CODE`**, the score is exactly 1.0000 when the code signal is 1 *and* the revenue signal is 1 *and* the two items share the same `department_id`, and exactly 0.0000 in every other case; two items with identical descriptions but different `code_value` strings can therefore never be marked duplicates. Under **`FUZZY_DESCRIPTION`**, the score is the weighted sum of the code signal at weight 0.5, the description similarity at weight 0.3, and the revenue signal at weight 0.2, rounded half up to four decimal places. The consequences are worth spelling out: a pair with different codes but a perfect description match and the same revenue code scores 0.5000 and fails; a pair with identical codes but disjoint descriptions scores 0.7000 and fails; and a pair with identical codes and the same revenue code scores 0.7000 plus 0.3 times the description similarity, crossing the 0.8000 threshold only once that similarity reaches 0.3334. The same pair of items can therefore be a duplicate under one facility's mode and not under another's. The threshold comparison is inclusive: a score of exactly 0.8000 passes.

The same scoring is also available as a read-only report over a subject item's `ACTIVE`, non-duplicate siblings, ranked the same way, and accepting an optional `min_score` filter alongside the standard pagination parameters. That report is available to `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`, `PricingCommitteeApprover` and `ComplianceOfficer` only — a `SystemAdmin`, a `ContractManager` and a `DepartmentManager` may read every other collection but not this one.

### 3.9. Charge Capture Reconciliation and Missing-Charge Detection

A `DepartmentManager` ingests a `ChargeCaptureBatch` covering a closed service-date window for one department, supplying `facility_id`, `department_id`, `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`. Two rules gate ingestion. The department's `capture_hold` must be false, otherwise `ERR_DEPARTMENT_CAPTURE_HOLD` (409) — a department drowning in unresolved leakage is cut off from submitting more work until it cleans up. And capacity is bounded per calendar day: no single day inside the new closed window may end up covered by more than two `ChargeCaptureBatch` rows of the same department whose `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`. A third overlapping batch on any one day is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). A new batch is created in `INGESTED` with `reconcile_pass_count` at 0 and every counter — `line_count`, `matched_line_count`, `exception_line_count`, `open_finding_count`, `total_expected_charge_cents`, `total_posted_charge_cents` — at 0, and `mean_line_variance_bp` null until the first reconciliation pass.

Lines are appended by the same manager while the batch's `status` is `INGESTED`; appending to a batch in any other status is `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE` (409). Each `CapturedCharge` line supplies `submitted_cdm_number` (the CDM number as the ancillary system posted it), `encounter_id` (the patient encounter, which is the boundary within which companion pairing is evaluated), `payer_code` (the payer on the encounter), `service_datetime`, `quantity` (at least 1) and `posted_charge_cents` (the charge the source system actually posted). The `service_datetime` must fall inside the batch's closed window — at or after `window_start_date` and at or before `window_end_date` — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (422). A line is created with `match_status` of `UNRECONCILED`, `charge_item_id` null, `expected_charge_cents` null and `line_variance_bp` null; the batch's `line_count` and `total_posted_charge_cents` are recomputed and `ChargeCaptureBatch.version` advances by 1. Lines carry only `id` and `created_at` — no `updated_at`, no `version`.

Reconciliation is the analyst's pass over the batch, and it is reachable from two states with identical effect: `INGESTED → RECONCILING` and `DISPUTED → RECONCILING`. Both require `line_count` to be at least 1, since an empty batch cannot be reconciled, and both report `ERR_BATCH_HAS_NO_LINES` (422) when it is 0. Both set `status` to `RECONCILING`, increase `reconcile_pass_count` by 1, advance the batch's `version` by 1, and re-execute the complete set of line-level and finding-level side effects described below. The dispute cycle `RECONCILED → DISPUTED → RECONCILING → RECONCILED` may therefore be walked repeatedly.

**Per-line classification** processes every line of the batch in order of `service_datetime` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, never by `id`. Five tests are applied and the **first** match wins:

1. If `submitted_cdm_number` matches no `ChargeItem`'s `cdm_number` within the batch's facility, `charge_item_id` stays null and `match_status` becomes `UNKNOWN_CODE`.
2. Otherwise `charge_item_id` records the resolved item; if that item's `lifecycle_status` is `INACTIVE` or `ARCHIVED`, `match_status` becomes `INACTIVE_ITEM`.
3. Otherwise, if another line of the *same* batch shares this line's `encounter_id`, `charge_item_id` and `service_datetime` and has an earlier `created_at`, `match_status` becomes `DUPLICATE_LINE`.
4. Otherwise `expected_charge_cents` is computed as the resolved item's `gross_charge_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity`, and `line_variance_bp` as the posted charge less the expected charge, multiplied by 10000, divided by the expected charge, rounded half up. If the absolute value of `line_variance_bp` is greater than 100 basis points — that is, more than one percent away from expected — `match_status` becomes `PRICE_VARIANCE`. The boundary is exact: an absolute variance of exactly 100 basis points is `MATCHED`, and 101 is `PRICE_VARIANCE`.
5. Otherwise `match_status` becomes `MATCHED`.

One degenerate case needs its own statement: when the expected charge computes to 0 because the item was never repriced, `line_variance_bp` is null, and the line is `PRICE_VARIANCE` whenever `posted_charge_cents` is greater than 0 and `MATCHED` otherwise. `expected_charge_cents` is also null whenever `charge_item_id` is null.

**Batch roll-up** then follows, and the order of rounding is pinned. The scored set is those lines whose `match_status` is `MATCHED` or `PRICE_VARIANCE` and whose `line_variance_bp` is not null. If the scored set is empty, `mean_line_variance_bp` is 0. Otherwise `mean_line_variance_bp` is the sum of the scored lines' already-rounded `line_variance_bp` values divided by the count of scored lines, rounded half up to whole basis points. Rounding each line first and averaging those rounded values is the specification; summing raw amounts and dividing once at the aggregate level produces a different number for the same lines and is wrong. Alongside the mean, `total_expected_charge_cents` is the sum of `expected_charge_cents` over every line whose `match_status` is not `UNKNOWN_CODE`, and `total_posted_charge_cents` is the sum of `posted_charge_cents` over **all** lines in the batch including the unknown-code and duplicate ones. `matched_line_count` is the count of `MATCHED` lines and `exception_line_count` the count of lines whose `match_status` is `PRICE_VARIANCE`, `UNKNOWN_CODE`, `INACTIVE_ITEM` or `DUPLICATE_LINE`.

**Missing-charge detection** runs immediately after per-line classification, on the same pass. Every line of the batch is considered as a possible trigger, in order of `service_datetime` ascending then `created_at` ascending. A line is skipped unless its `match_status` is `MATCHED` or `PRICE_VARIANCE`. Its resolved item is then examined: if the item's `companion_required_item_id` is null, the line is skipped. If the named companion item's `lifecycle_status` is `INACTIVE` or `ARCHIVED`, the line is skipped as well — there is nothing capturable to demand. Otherwise the pass looks for a companion line in the same batch satisfying **all** of four conditions: its `charge_item_id` equals the companion item's identifier; its `encounter_id` equals the trigger's `encounter_id`; its `match_status` is not `DUPLICATE_LINE`; its `quantity` is at least the trigger's `quantity`; and its `service_datetime` falls between the trigger's `service_datetime` and the trigger's `service_datetime` plus the triggering item's `companion_window_hours`, with **both** bounds inclusive. A companion posted exactly `companion_window_hours` after the trigger counts as present; a companion posted *before* the trigger does not count at all. Lines from other batches and other encounters never satisfy the pairing.

When a companion is present and an `OPEN` `MissingChargeFinding` already exists for that trigger line, the finding transitions `OPEN → DISMISSED` with `disposition_note` set to `'COMPANION_PRESENT_ON_REPASS'`. When no companion is present and no finding with that `trigger_captured_charge_id` already sits in `OPEN`, `CONFIRMED` or `RESOLVED`, a new `MissingChargeFinding` is created in `OPEN` with `batch_id` and `department_id` copied from the batch, `trigger_captured_charge_id` naming the unpaired line, `expected_charge_item_id` naming the companion item that should have been captured, and `estimated_lost_charge_cents` computed as the companion item's `gross_charge_cents` multiplied by the trigger line's `quantity`. That amount is **frozen at creation** and is never recomputed, even if the companion item is later repriced. A new finding's `disposition_note` is null while it is `OPEN`, as is its `recovery_captured_charge_id`.

Once the pass is done the analyst finalizes the batch, `RECONCILING → RECONCILED`, on the single precondition that no line still carries `match_status` of `UNRECONCILED`; a residual unreconciled line is `ERR_BATCH_LINES_UNRECONCILED` (409). From `RECONCILED` the department manager may dispute — `RECONCILED → DISPUTED` — which requires that `X-User-ID` equal the department's `manager_user_id` (`ERR_DEPARTMENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`) and that `exception_line_count` be at least 1, since there must be something to argue about; a clean batch yields `ERR_NOTHING_TO_DISPUTE` (422). Also from `RECONCILED`, the analyst may close — `RECONCILED → CLOSED` — which requires that `open_finding_count` be 0, meaning every finding of the batch is `CONFIRMED`, `DISMISSED` or `RESOLVED` (otherwise `ERR_FINDINGS_OPEN`, 409), and that the department's `capture_hold` be false (otherwise `ERR_DEPARTMENT_CAPTURE_HOLD`, 409). Closing recomputes the department's aggregates but deliberately does not touch `open_request_count`, which tracks change requests rather than batches. `CLOSED` is the batch's terminal state, and the three lifecycle actions are named `FINALIZE`, `DISPUTE` and `CLOSE`, each accepting an optional `note`.

**Dispositioning findings** is the analyst's follow-up work, driven by an `action` of `CONFIRM`, `DISMISS` or `RESOLVE`. Confirming, `OPEN → CONFIRMED`, requires the owning batch's `status` to be `RECONCILING`, `RECONCILED` or `DISPUTED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, 409); it records the payload's `disposition_note`, then adds the finding's `estimated_lost_charge_cents` to the department's `unresolved_finding_amount_cents` with `Department.version` advancing by 1, and — the second cascade link — sets the department's `capture_hold` to true once `unresolved_finding_amount_cents` reaches 500000 cents or more, which blocks that department from ingesting any further capture batch. The batch's `open_finding_count` is recomputed and `ChargeCaptureBatch.version` advances by 1.

Dismissing, `OPEN → DISMISSED`, requires the same three batch statuses (`ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`) and additionally a non-empty `disposition_note` (`ERR_VALIDATION`); it sets `status` to `DISMISSED` and prompts a recomputation of the batch's `open_finding_count`. Resolving is the compensating counterpart to confirmation and runs `CONFIRMED → RESOLVED`. It requires that the supplied `recovery_captured_charge_id` resolve to a `CapturedCharge` in the **same** batch whose `charge_item_id` equals the finding's `expected_charge_item_id`, otherwise `ERR_RECOVERY_LINE_INVALID` (422), and that the batch's `status` not be `CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. On success `recovery_captured_charge_id` is stored, and the *same literal amount* — the finding's frozen `estimated_lost_charge_cents` — is subtracted from the department's `unresolved_finding_amount_cents` and added to its `recovered_charge_cents`, so that the department's total identified leakage is conserved: the sum of `unresolved_finding_amount_cents` and `recovered_charge_cents` always equals the sum of `estimated_lost_charge_cents` over all findings whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `RESOLVED`. The department's `capture_hold` is then recomputed — which is how a department digs itself out of a hold — and `Department.version` advances by 1. `DISMISSED` and `RESOLVED` are the finding's terminal states, and a disposition not declared from the finding's current status (resolving an `OPEN` finding, confirming a `DISMISSED` one) is `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE` (409).

Because reconciliation mutates lines and creates findings by cascade rather than by direct client request, filtered listings are the discovery channel for both. Captured lines are filterable by `batch_id`, `charge_item_id`, `encounter_id` and `match_status`; findings are filterable by `batch_id`, `department_id`, `trigger_captured_charge_id`, `expected_charge_item_id`, `recovery_captured_charge_id` and `status`. Batches themselves are filterable by `facility_id`, `department_id` and `status`, and departments by `facility_id` and `capture_hold`.

### 3.10. Building and Publishing the Price Transparency File

Federal transparency rules require a machine-readable file of standard charges, and this system treats its production as a governed lifecycle rather than an export. A `ComplianceOfficer` initiates a build for a `facility_id` in a chosen `file_format` of `CSV` or `JSON`, and must carry `expected_facility_version` as described in §2.8. Initiating the build creates a `TransparencyFile` in `BUILDING`, computes its contents, advances `Facility.version` by 1 and re-derives the facility's posture — which by the first derivation rule becomes `FROZEN`, sealing the chargemaster against the writes listed in §2.7 for the duration of the build. `published_at` and `superseded_by_file_id` are null on a new file.

**The build** captures `as_of_datetime` from the logical clock, and the inclusion set is evaluated at that instant: every `ChargeItem` of the file's facility whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `price_status` is either `PRICED_CURRENT` or `PRICE_FROZEN`, and whose `duplicate_of_item_id` is null. Rows are ordered by the mapped revenue code's `code` ascending, then by `code_value` ascending with null `code_value` sorting after every non-null value, then by `cdm_number` ascending — never by `id`. From that ordered set, `included_item_count` is the row count and `total_gross_charge_cents` is the sum of `gross_charge_cents` over the rows. `payer_column_count` is the number of distinct `payer_code` and `product_line` pairs among the facility's `PayerContract` rows whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose closed term contains `as_of_datetime`; each such payer column's negotiated charge for a row is the reimbursement helper of §3.6 applied to that item's `gross_charge_cents` at a unit count of 1. `content_hash` is a SHA-256 digest over the ordered rows, each serialized as its `cdm_number`, its revenue code, its `code_value` (or an empty string when null) and its `gross_charge_cents` joined by vertical bars, with rows joined by newlines — deterministic for a given inclusion set and ordering, and recomputed on every build.

`validation_errors` is an array of strings assembled during the build and re-assembled on every validation attempt. It gains the string `'NO_PUBLISHABLE_ITEMS'` when `included_item_count` is 0, the string `'NO_ACTIVE_PAYER_COLUMNS'` when `payer_column_count` is 0, and the string `'STALE_PRICED_ITEMS'` when any charge item of the facility whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` has `price_status` of `PRICE_STALE`. Note the asymmetry there: a stale-priced `ACTIVE` item is excluded from the file's inclusion set but still blocks validation, because a facility cannot honestly publish while part of its live chargemaster is unpriced.

Validation is where the file branches. The `VALIDATE` action routes `BUILDING → VALIDATED` when the recomputed `validation_errors` array is empty, and `BUILDING → FAILED` when it is non-empty — the two outcomes are the same request with opposite verdicts, and the non-empty case arises when at least one `ACTIVE` item is `PRICE_STALE` or when `payer_column_count` is 0. On the `VALIDATED` branch, every charge item of the facility whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `price_status` is `PRICED_CURRENT` transitions to `PRICE_FROZEN` with each item's `version` advancing by 1, and the facility's posture is re-derived to `FROZEN` with `Facility.version` advancing by 1. On the `FAILED` branch, no item is frozen; the file's `status` becomes `FAILED`, the facility's posture is re-derived (this file no longer holds it `FROZEN`) and `Facility.version` advances by 1.

Publication, `VALIDATED → PUBLISHED`, carries two preconditions. No `CodeUpdateCycle` of the facility may be in `MAPPING` or `CROSSWALK_APPROVED` status — publishing during a crosswalk blackout is refused with `ERR_TRANSPARENCY_BLACKOUT` (409) — and `included_item_count` must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_NO_PUBLISHABLE_ITEMS` (422). On success `status` becomes `PUBLISHED` and `published_at` takes the logical now. Any *other* transparency file of the facility still in `PUBLISHED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_file_id` set to this file's identifier. Every charge item of the facility whose `price_status` is `PRICE_FROZEN` thaws back to `PRICED_CURRENT`, each advancing its own `version` by 1. And the facility records the outcome: `published_transparency_file_id` takes this file's identifier, `transparency_status` becomes `CURRENT`, `chargemaster_posture` is re-derived, and `Facility.version` advances by 1.

A validated file may also be abandoned rather than published. The `FAIL` action from `VALIDATED` requires only that the file be in `VALIDATED`; it sets `status` to `FAILED`, thaws every `PRICE_FROZEN` item of the facility back to `PRICED_CURRENT` with each item's `version` advancing by 1, re-derives the facility's posture and advances `Facility.version` by 1, leaving the facility without a published file. Every transparency lifecycle action accepts an optional `note`, and an action not declared from the file's current status — publishing straight from `BUILDING`, validating something already `PUBLISHED` — is `ERR_INVALID_FILE_STATE` (409).

`FAILED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the file's terminal states. Supersession also arrives from outside: a `PUBLISHED` file transitions to `SUPERSEDED` whenever any `ACTIVE` charge item of its facility is mutated by a reprice, a change-request implementation, a code-cycle activation or rollback, or a lifecycle change, and in that case `superseded_by_file_id` stays null while the facility's `published_transparency_file_id` is cleared and `transparency_status` drops to `NEEDS_REFRESH`. Files are discoverable by filter on `facility_id`, `superseded_by_file_id` and `status`.
