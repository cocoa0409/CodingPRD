# Workplace Violence Prevention API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employers operating public-facing intake desks, cash-handling counters, night-shift warehouses and lone-worker field routes are obliged to run a documented workplace-violence prevention programme rather than a collection of ad-hoc reactions. In practice the obligation splits into two halves that most organisations keep in separate systems: a *planning* half — hazard identification by work area and job function, role-specific training with an annual refresh, drills, periodic plan review — and a *response* half — reporting channels that protect the reporter from retaliation, a multidisciplinary threat assessment team that evaluates behavioural risk, protective measures such as access restriction and security escort, and a violent incident log whose regulatory data elements are retained for years. Keeping the halves apart is what causes inspection findings: the plan says an area is controlled while the log shows three incidents there, and nobody can prove which version of the plan was in force when the third one happened.

This API unifies both halves behind a single derived-state model. A worksite's prevention plan carries live compliance metrics — hazard coverage, training compliance, log completeness, drill credit, overdue post-incident actions — that are recomputed on every write that could move them, and those metrics roll up into a single inspection-readiness score whose threshold tightens automatically when the worksite becomes high-risk or the plan falls due for review. Threat cases, protective measures and log entries all point back at the plan that governed them, so the audit trail is a consequence of ordinary operation rather than a reporting exercise bolted on afterwards.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule below. First, **segregation of duties**: the safety officer who authors a hazard assessment may not validate it, and the officer who submits a plan revision may not activate it. Second, **anti-retaliation primacy**: once an employee alleges retaliation on a report they filed, adverse protective measures naming that employee are refused ahead of every scheduling, capacity and window rule in the system. Both commitments are enforced server-side, in a fixed evaluation order, so that two implementations of this specification reject the same request with the same code.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identifier resolution, rejection precedence, precision, concurrency, the logical clock, and the cascade discipline that keeps derived fields honest.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. The system recognises exactly ten roles: `SafetyOfficer`, `SiteManager`, `ThreatTeamLead`, `ThreatTeamMember`, `SecurityCoordinator`, `HRPartner`, `Employee`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `Anonymous`, and `ProgramAdmin`. A request that omits either header, or whose `X-User-Role` is not one of those ten strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The single exception is the anonymous reporting channel: a caller presenting `X-User-Role: Anonymous` may omit `X-User-ID` entirely. Once the headers are accepted, the role is matched against the endpoint's authorised role list from §4; a role that is not listed is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).

Seven of the ten roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `SafetyOfficer`, `SiteManager`, `ThreatTeamLead`, `ThreatTeamMember`, `SecurityCoordinator`, `HRPartner`, or `Employee`, the value of `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee` row, and ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employee` compare that resource's employee-valued field to `X-User-ID`. For all seven, the identifier must resolve to an existing `Employee` whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`; a separated worker's identifier can no longer act, and such a request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This is how the segregation-of-duties rules are evaluated — activation compares `X-User-ID` against the plan's `submitted_by_employee_id`, validation compares it against the assessment's `authored_by_employee_id` — and how the two stamping side effects are written, since `submitted_by_employee_id`, `activated_by_employee_id`, `triaged_by_employee_id` and `validated_by_employee_id` are all copied from the caller's `X-User-ID`.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAuditor`, `Anonymous`, or `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may log it but never compares it to any row's `id`, and the ACTIVE-employee resolution check above is skipped for these three. `ComplianceAuditor` is a read-only regulatory inspector with visibility into every collection and no write capability at all. `Anonymous` may file an incident report and do nothing else. `ProgramAdmin` bootstraps organisational reference data — worksites, work areas, employees, training modules — and drives the logical clock and the nightly sweep, but holds no clinical, HR or threat-assessment authority.

Three narrower ownership rules layer on top of the role gate. An actor whose role is `Employee` may complete only a training assignment whose `employee_id` equals their `X-User-ID`, and may allege retaliation only on a report whose `reporter_employee_id` equals their `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). An `HRPartner` is exempt from the second of those two checks, because HR drives the retaliation review on behalf of the claimant.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be below 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` sets the number of rows skipped and defaults to 0; negative values are not accepted. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape error and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`.

Beyond pagination, list endpoints accept equality filters on the fields named in §4's description column. Worksite listings filter on `state_code` and `high_risk_flag`; work areas on `worksite_id`, `job_function` and `lone_worker_flag`; prevention plans on `worksite_id`, `status`, `submitted_by_employee_id`, `activated_by_employee_id` and `inspection_ready`; hazard assessments on `plan_id`, `work_area_id`, `status`, `control_tier`, `authored_by_employee_id` and `validated_by_employee_id`; employees on `worksite_id`, `work_area_id`, `job_function`, `team_discipline`, `employment_status` and `training_current`; training modules on `required_for_escort`; training assignments on `employee_id`, `module_id` and `status`; incident reports on `worksite_id`, `work_area_id`, `reporter_employee_id`, `subject_employee_id`, `triaged_by_employee_id`, `retaliation_claimant_employee_id`, `channel`, `severity_class`, `triage_status` and `retaliation_status`; threat cases on `incident_report_id`, `plan_id`, `lead_employee_id`, `subject_employee_id`, `protected_employee_id`, `status`, `risk_band` and `protection_status`; team seats on `case_id`, `member_employee_id`, `discipline`, `role_on_team`, `status` and `ballot_round`; case actions on `case_id`, `subject_employee_id`, `work_area_id`, `escort_employee_id`, `assigned_employee_id`, `action_class`, `action_type`, `status` and `auto_generated`; violent incident log entries on `worksite_id`, `incident_report_id`, `case_id`, `supersedes_entry_id`, `superseded_flag` and `retention_status`; and drills on `plan_id`, `scenario_type`, `status` and `credited`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Append-Only Log

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix. Clients never supply it, and supplying one has no effect.

Almost every resource — worksites, work areas, prevention plans, hazard assessments, employees, training modules, training assignments, incident reports, threat cases, team assignments, case actions and drills — additionally carries three system-managed fields:

- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. It is read from real server time and not from the logical clock of §2.9, so it does not echo the clock value; successive writes always receive strictly increasing values, which is what makes the "created earlier than this row" comparisons of §3.2 and §3.7 unambiguous.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only change derived fields, taken from the same real-time source and rendered in the same three-fractional-digit shape.
- `version` (integer): an integer that increments by one on every mutation of the row.

The **violent incident log entry** is the sole exception and is deliberately so. Log entries are append-only regulatory records: they carry `id` and `created_at` only. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a log entry, because a log entry is never mutated in place. The one narrow exception to "never mutated" is the `superseded_flag` bookkeeping described in §3.9, which is derived from the existence of a successor entry rather than from an edit to the entry itself.

Recomputation counts as mutation. When a work area's `current_hazard_score` or `restricted_employee_count` is refreshed, when a plan's `hazard_coverage_pct` or `readiness_score` is refreshed, or when an employee's `escort_committed_minutes` or `training_current` is refreshed, that row's `updated_at` moves and its `version` increments. This matters directly: the two version guards in §2.7 are read against exactly these counters, so a derived-field refresh on a plan or an employee is enough to invalidate a client's stale expectation.

### 2.5. Request Rejection Precedence

Checks are evaluated strictly in the order below, and the **first** violated check determines the response. No later check runs, so a request that breaks several rules always reports the earliest one. The ordering is a contract, not an optimisation.

1. Headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This runs ahead of path existence, so an unauthenticated request against a non-existent identifier still returns 401.
2. The caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised role list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This outranks every existence, state and business rule, so a wrong-role call against a non-existent identifier returns 403 and not 404.
3. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `Employee` whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `SafetyOfficer`, `SiteManager`, `ThreatTeamLead`, `ThreatTeamMember`, `SecurityCoordinator`, `HRPartner` or `Employee`. The three opaque roles skip it.
4. Request shape: the body and query string parse, every statically required field is present, every enum value is in range, every number respects its declared bounds, and pagination stays within its limits — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This step touches no stored data. Unknown extra body fields are ignored rather than rejected, and computed or system-managed fields supplied by a client are ignored.
5. Every templated identifier in the path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right — one of the thirteen not-found codes (404) listed in §2.6.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the field order declared for that endpoint — the matching not-found code (404). Path identifiers are always checked before body identifiers, so a request carrying two unresolvable foreign keys reports whichever comes first in the endpoint's declared input order.
7. Resource ownership for the `Employee` role — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Ownership is checked after existence, so an employee acting on somebody else's row learns it exists before being refused.
8. Version guards — `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` (412), per §2.7. These run ahead of every state and business rule on the two endpoints that carry them, so a stale version against a plan in the wrong state still returns 412.
9. The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — the matching invalid-state code (409): `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_HAZARD_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RETALIATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ACTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TRAINING_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DRILL_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_EMPLOYEE_INVALID_STATE`. This also covers idempotent replays: submitting an already `IN_REVIEW` plan, closing an already `CLOSED` case, completing an already `COMPLETED` assignment.
10. Log-entry immutability — `ERR_LOG_ENTRY_IMMUTABLE` (409). An amendment may target only an entry whose `superseded_flag` is false and whose `retention_status` is `ACTIVE`.
11. The governing plan's `compliance_posture` is not `FROZEN` — `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409). Guards case-action creation and team convening.
12. Interval shape on a case action — `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422).
13. The anti-retaliation hold — `ERR_ANTI_RETALIATION_HOLD` (409). This outranks every scheduling, capacity and window rule below it, so a request that is both retaliatory and outside the protection window returns the anti-retaliation code.
14. Escort qualification — `ERR_ESCORT_NOT_QUALIFIED` (409).
15. Team-seat conflict of interest — `ERR_SUBJECT_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST` (409).
16. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Checked before the coverage and training gates, so a self-approval attempt on an under-trained plan reports the segregation failure.
17. Action type belongs to its declared class — `ERR_ACTION_TYPE_CLASS_MISMATCH` (422).
18. The field set required by the specific action type is complete — `ERR_ACTION_FIELD_MISSING` (422).
19. Cross-aggregate referential integrity — `ERR_WORK_AREA_WORKSITE_MISMATCH` or `ERR_MODULE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (409). Every foreign key resolves, but they point into different worksites or incompatible job functions.
20. Reporting-channel conditional validation — `ERR_REPORTER_REQUIRED` (422).
21. Uniqueness — `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_OPEN_FOR_REPORT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_LEAD` (409).
22. Team quorum — `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (409). Runs after the state check at step 9, so convening an already-`ASSESSING` case with an incomplete roster reports the invalid state rather than the quorum failure.
23. Ballot eligibility for the current round — `ERR_BALLOT_ROUND_CLOSED` (409).
24. Reassessment round ceiling — `ERR_MAX_ASSESSMENT_ROUNDS` (409).
25. Disposition matches the consensus band mapping — `ERR_DISPOSITION_BAND_MISMATCH` (409).
26. Override legitimacy — `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED` (409).
27. Training score reaches the module's passing score — `ERR_TRAINING_SCORE_BELOW_PASSING` (409).
28. Drill participation bounds — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_COUNT_EXCEEDS_HEADCOUNT` (422).
29. A protective measure overlaps the case's protection window — `ERR_MEASURE_OUTSIDE_PROTECTION_WINDOW` (409).
30. Escort double-booking — `ERR_ESCORT_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409).
31. Work-area restriction capacity — `ERR_AREA_RESTRICTION_CAPACITY` (409).
32. Full hazard coverage on activation — `ERR_HAZARD_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` (409). Checked before the training gate, so a plan failing both reports the coverage failure.
33. Training compliance on activation — `ERR_TRAINING_COMPLIANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (409).
34. No open post-incident actions on case closure — `ERR_OPEN_ACTIONS_BLOCK_CLOSE` (409).
35. Every live log entry of the case is regulatorily complete — `ERR_LOG_ENTRY_REQUIRED_ELEMENT_MISSING` (409). Runs after the open-action gate, so a case with both problems reports the open-action failure.

### 2.6. Path and Body Identifier Resolution

Templated path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the 404 code. The path variables in use are `worksite_id`, `work_area_id`, `plan_id`, `hazard_assessment_id`, `employee_id`, `module_id`, `training_assignment_id`, `incident_report_id`, `threat_case_id`, `team_assignment_id`, `case_action_id`, `log_entry_id`, and `drill_id`. Each maps to one code: `ERR_WORKSITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREVENTION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HAZARD_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAINING_MODULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAINING_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_THREAT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOG_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_DRILL_NOT_FOUND`.

The same catalogue serves body foreign keys, which are resolved after all path segments. Every employee-valued field — `employee_id`, `lead_employee_id`, `member_employee_id`, `subject_employee_id`, `reporter_employee_id`, `escort_employee_id`, and `assigned_employee_id` — reports `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND` when it names no employee. A `case_id` in a body that names no threat case reports `ERR_THREAT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`. `ERR_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` has a second trigger: it is also returned when a balloting caller holds no team seat at all on the case they are trying to vote in.

Only two read/write paths in this API are nested — the team-seat and ballot operations scoped under a threat case, and drills, which reference their plan by body field rather than by path. Everything else is a flat top-level collection joined by explicit foreign-key fields in the request body.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Superseding Writes

Two creation endpoints legitimately *supersede* an existing row, and both are therefore guarded against a client acting on a view of the parent it has not refreshed.

Creating a hazard assessment for a given plan and work area supersedes that pair's prior live assessment. The request must therefore carry `expected_plan_version`, a non-optional integer holding the `version` of the target `PreventionPlan` as the client last observed it. If `expected_plan_version` does not equal the plan's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (412): the client's view of the plan is stale, so the supersede it is asking for would discard an assessment it has never seen.

Creating a training assignment for a given employee and module supersedes that pair's prior assignment as the annual refresh. The request must carry `expected_employee_version`, holding the `version` of the target `Employee` as the client last observed it. If it does not equal the employee's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` (412), because the learner record has advanced — through an earlier refresh, a completion, or a separation — since the client last read it.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two carry no expected-version input, and none is inferred.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

All derived numeric values are rounded half-up. Unless a specific rule below says otherwise, the target is **two decimal places**: hazard scores, both behavioural risk scores, every one of the four plan compliance percentages, the drill participation percentage, the readiness subtotal, penalty points and final readiness score, each ballot's rounded share, and the protective coverage gap in hours.

Three precision rules are load-bearing and are restated where they apply:

- **Severity points are integers.** The incident severity classification produces a whole-number point total and its band comparisons are exact integer comparisons.
- **Discipline weights carry three decimal places** and are never rounded during the consensus computation; only the per-ballot share is rounded, and it is rounded *before* the shares are added together.
- **The readiness subtotal is rounded before it selects a penalty band.** Rounding at that intermediate step is not cosmetic; the rounded value is the band-selection key, and banding on the unrounded sum can land one band lower and produce a different final score.

Percentages are always computed from integer counts, never from other percentages. Every quotient is formed once, from its own numerator and denominator, and rounded once.

### 2.9. The Logical Clock and the Nightly Compliance Sweep

The server reads all deadlines, expiries, retention dates and protection windows from a **logical clock**, never from real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `ProgramAdmin` may move it by supplying `current_utc`; the call changes no stored rows and returns the new clock value. An instant the server writes straight off the clock — `activated_at`, `separated_at`, a training assignment's `assigned_at` and `completed_at`, `ballot_submitted_at`, `protection_window_start`, a drill's `executed_at`, a case action's `completed_at` and `revoked_at` — is stored exactly as the clock currently reads, character for character. An instant the server computes by advancing some other instant — a plan's `review_due_date`, a training assignment's `expires_at`, `protection_window_end`, an automatically generated action's `due_at`, and a log entry's `retention_expiry_date` — is rendered in that same shape: it carries a three-digit fractional part only when the computed instant actually has one, so from the initial clock a 365-day `review_due_date` reads `2027-08-01T00:00:00Z` and not `2027-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. The three-fractional-digit rendering that §2.4 requires of `created_at` and `updated_at` does not extend to these domain instants.

A `ProgramAdmin` also drives the nightly compliance sweep, which performs eight steps in a **fixed order that never varies**:

1. Training assignments lapse: an `ASSIGNED` assignment whose `due_at` is strictly before the clock becomes `EXPIRED`, and a `COMPLETED` assignment whose `expires_at` is at or before the clock becomes `EXPIRED`. Every affected employee's `training_current` is then refreshed.
2. Drills lapse: a `SCHEDULED` drill whose `scheduled_at` is strictly before the clock becomes `MISSED`.
3. Protective measures advance: a `SCHEDULED` protective action whose `starts_at` is at or before the clock while its `ends_at` is still in the future becomes `ACTIVE`; any protective action in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` whose `ends_at` is at or before the clock becomes `EXPIRED`, releasing its escort minutes and its area restriction as described in §2.11.
4. Post-incident actions fall behind: a `PENDING` post-incident action whose `due_at` is strictly before the clock becomes `OVERDUE`, incrementing the `overdue_action_count` of the plan governing its case.
5. Coverage is re-measured: the protective coverage gap and protection status are recomputed for every case that is not `CLOSED`.
6. Retention expires: a log entry whose `retention_expiry_date` is at or before the clock has its `retention_status` moved from `ACTIVE` to `RETENTION_EXPIRED`.
7. Plan review falls due: an `ACTIVE` plan whose `review_due_date` is at or before the clock becomes `REVIEW_DUE`, and its `compliance_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`.
8. Everything is rescored: the compliance metrics and the inspection readiness score are recomputed for every plan that is not `SUPERSEDED`.

Because steps 7 and 8 never reorder, a plan that falls due in step 7 is always rescored in step 8 against the stricter `RESTRICTED` readiness threshold within the same sweep. The sweep reports what it did through eight counters: `training_expired_count`, `drills_missed_count`, `protective_activated_count`, `protective_expired_count`, `actions_overdue_count`, `log_retention_expired_count`, `plans_review_due_count`, and `plans_rescored_count`.

### 2.10. Compliance Posture, Cascades and Derived-Field Reconciliation

A prevention plan's `compliance_posture` is not an input; it is derived from the plan's own `status` together with the risk state of its worksite, and it recomputes whenever either moves. It is `FROZEN` when the plan's `status` is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `SUPERSEDED`. It is `RESTRICTED` when the plan's `status` is `REVIEW_DUE`, and also whenever the worksite's `high_risk_flag` is true. Otherwise — an `ACTIVE` plan on a worksite that is not high-risk — it is `OPEN`. A `FROZEN` posture is a hard stop on operational writes: no case action may be recorded against a case whose plan is frozen, and no team may convene on such a case, both refused with `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN`.

Consequences propagate. A single decision on a threat case can reach four levels: the case's own status moves, the underlying incident report becomes substantiated, a violent incident log entry is appended for the worksite, the worksite's rolling incident count and `high_risk_flag` move, and the worksite's active plan is pushed from `ACTIVE` to `REVIEW_DUE` with its posture tightening to `RESTRICTED` and its readiness re-evaluated against the stricter threshold. Each link runs because the previous one changed a value the next one reads — not because the caller asked for it. Every touched row's `updated_at` and `version` move accordingly, and §3 describes each chain link where it belongs in the workflow.

Cascades also run in reverse. Revoking a protective measure, separating an employee, or closing a case all *remove* commitments, and each removal triggers the same recomputation path in the opposite direction: the conserved counters are credited back, the coverage gap widens, protection status may flip, and in two of those cases a compensating post-incident action is generated automatically.

### 2.11. Conserved Commitments: The Escort-Minute Ledger and the Restriction Counters

Three counters in this system are **conserved**: whatever a write adds, some later write removes, and the counter must return to where it started once no live commitment remains.

`Employee.escort_committed_minutes` is the sum of `duration_minutes` over every security-escort action naming that employee as escort whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`. Scheduling such an action adds its duration; the action later expiring, being revoked, or being revoked as part of a case closure or an employee separation subtracts exactly the same duration. `WorkArea.restricted_employee_count` is the number of access-restriction actions on that area whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`, and `Employee.active_restriction_count` is the number of such actions naming that employee as subject. Both increment by one when the restriction is recorded and decrement by one when it expires or is revoked.

`SCHEDULED` and `ACTIVE` are both inside the conserved set. The nightly advance from `SCHEDULED` to `ACTIVE` therefore moves no counter at all — only the exits to `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` do.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Programme: Worksites, Work Areas and People

A `ProgramAdmin` bootstraps the reference data. A **worksite** is registered with a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters (the jurisdiction whose retention rules the violent incident log will follow), and an `employee_count` of at least 1 — the declared headcount that later serves as the denominator for drill participation. A fresh worksite starts with `violent_incident_count_12mo` at 0 and `high_risk_flag` at false. That count is not a stored tally maintained by hand: it is the number of violent incident log entries for the worksite whose `retention_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `superseded_flag` is false, and whose `incident_at` falls within the trailing 365 days of the logical clock. `high_risk_flag` is true as soon as that count reaches one — a single substantiated violent incident in a rolling year is enough to make a worksite high-risk.

**Work areas** subdivide a worksite. Each is created against a `worksite_id` with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `job_function` drawn from `PATIENT_CARE`, `CASH_HANDLING`, `FIELD_SERVICE`, `WAREHOUSE`, `SECURITY` and `ADMIN`, and an `occupancy_cap` of at least 1 giving the maximum staff headcount the area may hold at any instant. Three exposure flags shape the area's hazard profile and all three default to false: `public_access_flag` when members of the public can physically enter, `lone_worker_flag` when staff routinely work there alone, and `night_shift_flag` when the area is staffed between 22:00 and 06:00. Two derived fields ride along: `restricted_employee_count`, starting at 0, and `current_hazard_score`, starting null. The latter is the `hazard_score` of the area's non-`SUPERSEDED` hazard assessment belonging to the worksite's plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE`; when the worksite has no such plan, or that plan has no live assessment for the area, `current_hazard_score` stays null. Adding a work area is a compliance event in its own right, because it enlarges the hazard-coverage denominator: the compliance metrics and readiness score of every non-`SUPERSEDED` plan of that worksite are recomputed immediately, and the plan's `hazard_coverage_pct` drops.

**Employees** are registered against a `worksite_id` and, for staff with a fixed post, a `work_area_id` — roving staff leave it null. Each carries a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `job_function` from the same six-value enumeration, which governs both training scope and escort eligibility. An employee who may represent a discipline on the threat assessment team is additionally given a `team_discipline` of `HR`, `SECURITY`, `LEGAL`, `CLINICAL` or `OPERATIONS`; the field is null for everyone else. New employees start with `employment_status` `ACTIVE`, `separated_at` null, `escort_committed_minutes` at 0, `active_restriction_count` at 0 and `training_current` false. Registering an employee introduces new training obligations, so the compliance metrics and readiness score of every non-`SUPERSEDED` plan of the worksite are recomputed on creation.

Separation is the most far-reaching single write in the system. An `HRPartner` separates a worker by supplying a `separation_reason`, moving `employment_status` from `ACTIVE` to `SEPARATED` and recording `separated_at` from the logical clock; separating someone already `SEPARATED` is rejected with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_INVALID_STATE`. Five consequences follow in one transaction. First, every security-escort action naming the departing worker as escort whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `REVOKED`, crediting `escort_committed_minutes` back until it reaches 0. Second, because those escort intervals have left the conserved set, the protective coverage gap is recomputed for each affected case, and `protection_status` may flip from `COVERED` to `GAP`. Third, any case that flips to `GAP` while it is `MONITORING` receives an automatically generated safety-plan-review post-incident action assigned to the case lead and due 24 hours after the logical clock, which raises that case's `open_action_count`. Fourth, because `open_action_count` is now above zero, closing that case is refused with `ERR_OPEN_ACTIONS_BLOCK_CLOSE` until the generated action is completed. Fifth, every team seat held by the departing worker whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` becomes `RECUSED` with its ballot fields cleared, and each affected case's `quorum_met` and `ballot_count_current_round` are recomputed — a recusal can therefore break a quorum that was previously met. Finally, the departing worker leaves the training-compliance denominator, so every non-`SUPERSEDED` plan of the worksite is recomputed.

### 3.2. The Prevention Plan Review Cycle

A `SafetyOfficer` drafts a **prevention plan** for a worksite by supplying `worksite_id`, an `effective_date` on which the plan text takes effect, and optionally a `review_interval_days` giving the number of days after activation until periodic review falls due. That interval defaults to 365 — a year — and must lie between 30 and 1095 days inclusive. The server computes `plan_revision` as one more than the number of plans already recorded for the same `worksite_id` whose `created_at` precedes the new row's, so the first plan for a worksite is revision 1. A new plan starts in `status` `DRAFT` with `compliance_posture` `FROZEN` and `inspection_ready` false; `submitted_by_employee_id`, `activated_by_employee_id`, `activated_at` and `review_due_date` are all null. Its metric block starts at `hazard_coverage_pct` 0, `training_compliance_pct` 0, `log_completeness_pct` 100, `drills_completed_12mo` 0, `unmitigated_hazard_count` 0, `overdue_action_count` 0, `readiness_subtotal` 0, `readiness_penalty_band` `WEAK`, `readiness_penalty_points` 0, `readiness_score` 0 — and the metrics and readiness score are then computed for real from whatever data already exists.

Submission moves a plan from `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW`, optionally accompanied by a `submission_note`. The plan's worksite must already have at least one work area — a plan describing no work areas has nothing to assess, and a submit against a worksite with no work areas is refused with `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`. Submission stamps `submitted_by_employee_id` with the caller's identifier, which is the baseline the later segregation-of-duties check reads, and recomputes `hazard_coverage_pct`, `training_compliance_pct`, `log_completeness_pct`, `unmitigated_hazard_count`, `overdue_action_count` and the whole readiness computation. Because the plan is now `IN_REVIEW`, `compliance_posture` remains `FROZEN` and `inspection_ready` is forced false regardless of how good the numbers look.

A `SiteManager` may push an `IN_REVIEW` plan back with a mandatory `revision_reason`, returning it to `DRAFT` and **clearing** `submitted_by_employee_id` so that the next submission re-stamps the segregation baseline from scratch. A plan may travel `DRAFT` → `IN_REVIEW` → `DRAFT` any number of times.

Activation, also a `SiteManager` action and optionally carrying an `activation_note`, moves the plan `IN_REVIEW` → `ACTIVE` and is guarded by three gates evaluated in this order. First, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_employee_id`; a submitter attempting to activate their own submission is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Second, `hazard_coverage_pct` must equal exactly 100.00 — every work area of the worksite must carry a live hazard assessment — or the request is refused with `ERR_HAZARD_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. Third, `training_compliance_pct` must be at least 80.00, an inclusive boundary where exactly 80.00 passes and 79.99 fails, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_COMPLIANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. Because the gates run in that order, a plan failing both the coverage and training gates reports the coverage failure. Activation is otherwise rejected with `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE` when the plan is in any state other than `IN_REVIEW`.

A successful activation writes `activated_by_employee_id` from the caller, `activated_at` from the logical clock, and `review_due_date` as `activated_at` advanced by `review_interval_days` — recomputed afresh on every activation. It then cascades. Every *other* plan for the same worksite whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`; because a superseded plan's `compliance_posture` becomes `FROZEN`, its `inspection_ready` becomes false and cases still pointing at it can no longer receive case actions, which surface as `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. The newly active plan's posture becomes `OPEN`, `current_hazard_score` is recomputed for every work area of the worksite (because the live plan has changed, and with it which assessments count), and the plan's own readiness score and posture are recomputed.

Two independent events move an `ACTIVE` plan to `REVIEW_DUE`. The nightly sweep does it when `review_due_date` is at or before the logical clock. The substantiation cascade does it when the worksite's `high_risk_flag` flips from false to true. Either way, `compliance_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED` and `inspection_ready` is re-evaluated against the tighter threshold of 85.00 rather than the ordinary 75.00. From `REVIEW_DUE`, the safety officer submits again — closing the periodic-review cycle — which stamps a new `submitted_by_employee_id`, **clears** `activated_by_employee_id` and `review_due_date`, and moves the posture from `RESTRICTED` all the way to `FROZEN`, at which point case actions against cases governed by that plan are refused with `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. A `REVIEW_DUE` plan can equally be superseded by the activation of a newer plan for the same worksite, again taking its posture to `FROZEN` and its `inspection_ready` to false. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

### 3.3. Hazard Identification, Scoring and Control Tiering

Hazard assessment is the paper trail behind every control decision, and it is deliberately two-staged: a safety officer screens, a site manager validates, and the score is computed differently at each stage.

A `SafetyOfficer` records a screening assessment against a `plan_id` and a `work_area_id`, together with `reported_incident_count` (prior incidents reported in that area over the last twelve months, between 0 and 50), a `proximity_factor` grading public-proximity exposure as `LOW`, `MODERATE` or `HIGH`, `cash_on_hand_cents` giving the typical cash held in the area in cents and never negative, `min_staff_per_shift` giving the minimum staff scheduled per shift and never negative, and the `expected_plan_version` guard of §2.7. The work area named must belong to the plan's worksite; a cross-worksite reference is refused with `ERR_WORK_AREA_WORKSITE_MISMATCH`. The new row starts in `status` `SCREENING`, its `authored_by_employee_id` stamped from the caller, `validated_by_employee_id` and `walkthrough_note` null, `mitigation_credits` at 0 and `barrier_installed` false.

Creating an assessment for a plan-and-area pair that already has a live one is the intended way to revise: the prior assessment, whether `SCREENING` or `VALIDATED`, becomes `SUPERSEDED`, stops counting toward `hazard_coverage_pct` and `unmitigated_hazard_count`, and the area's `current_hazard_score` switches to the new screening row's score. The create also increments the plan's `version` by one — which is precisely why the guard exists, since two clients each holding the same observed version cannot both supersede.

**Six risk terms build the score, and all six are always evaluated.** The incident term is the reported incident count, capped at 8, multiplied by 5 points each — so nine or more prior incidents earn the same 40 points as eight. The proximity term contributes 0 points for `LOW`, 8 points for `MODERATE` and 20 points for `HIGH`. The cash term counts complete blocks of 5,000,000 cents — that is, complete fifty-thousand-dollar blocks — of `cash_on_hand_cents`, discarding any partial block, awards 4 points per block, and is then capped at 20 points, so anything from 250,000 dollars upward scores the same 20. The staffing term is 12 points when `min_staff_per_shift` is 1 or 0, 6 points when it is exactly 2, and 0 points when it is 3 or more. The exposure term adds 10 points when the area's `public_access_flag` is true and nothing otherwise. The night term adds 8 points when `night_shift_flag` is true and nothing otherwise.

The six terms are added into a raw total, and then **two clamps are applied in a fixed order: the floor first, the cap second.** If the area's `lone_worker_flag` is true, the raw total is replaced by whichever is larger, itself or 45 points — a lone-worker area can never score below 45 no matter how benign its other inputs, which is often enough on its own to lift the area out of the lowest control tier. The result is then replaced by whichever is smaller, itself or 100 points. That clamped value is the **base**.

Under the `SCREENING` variant the `hazard_score` is the base rounded half-up to two decimal places, `score_variant` is recorded as `SCREENING`, and `mitigation_verified` is forced false — mitigation claims are not trusted until somebody has walked the area.

Validation is a `SiteManager` action and requires a mandatory `walkthrough_note` capturing the validation narrative, plus the two mitigation inputs the screening variant ignored: `mitigation_credits`, the number of verified administrative controls credited, between 0 and 10 and defaulting to 0, and `barrier_installed`, true when an engineering barrier is present, defaulting to false. The validator must not be the author: an identical `X-User-ID` and `authored_by_employee_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The assessment must still be `SCREENING`; validating one already `VALIDATED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_HAZARD_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The governing plan must not be `SUPERSEDED` — validation is permitted while the plan is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE`, and refused with `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE` once the plan is superseded.

Under the `VALIDATED` variant the same six terms and the same two clamps produce the same base, and then a **mitigation credit is subtracted**. The credit is `mitigation_credits`, capped at 4, multiplied by 5 points each, plus a further 10 points when `barrier_installed` is true — so the maximum achievable credit is 30 points, and a claim of six credits earns the same 20 points as a claim of four. The `hazard_score` is the base less the credit, floored at 0 so it can never go negative, rounded half-up to two decimal places. `score_variant` becomes `VALIDATED`, `validated_by_employee_id` is stamped from the caller, and `mitigation_verified` becomes true when the assessment is validated and either an engineering barrier is installed or at least one mitigation credit was recorded.

The `control_tier` follows the score directly and is refreshed at both stages: `TIER_1_ENGINEERING` when `hazard_score` is at least 60, `TIER_2_ADMINISTRATIVE` when it is at least 35 but below 60, and `TIER_3_TRAINING_ONLY` below 35. A `TIER_1_ENGINEERING` assessment whose `mitigation_verified` is false is an **unmitigated engineering hazard**, and the plan's `unmitigated_hazard_count` is the number of such assessments among its non-`SUPERSEDED` rows. Every screening create and every validation therefore recomputes the area's `current_hazard_score`, the plan's `unmitigated_hazard_count`, the rest of the plan compliance metrics, and the readiness score.

### 3.4. Training: Modules, Assignments and the Annual Refresh

A `ProgramAdmin` publishes a **training module** with a `code` of 1 to 40 characters, unique across all modules, a `job_function_scope` — a non-empty array of job functions for which the module is mandatory — and a `scenario_set_version` naming the role-specific scenario pack learners will be shown. Three further inputs have defaults: `refresh_interval_days`, the number of days a completion stays current before the annual refresh falls due, defaults to 365 and must lie between 30 and 1095; `passing_score` defaults to 80 and must lie between 0 and 100; and `required_for_escort` defaults to false, becoming true for modules whose completion is a precondition for acting as a security escort. Publishing a module widens the training obligation across the organisation, so the compliance metrics and readiness score of every affected plan are recomputed as new employee-and-module pairs enter the denominator.

An `HRPartner` assigns a module to a learner by supplying `employee_id`, `module_id`, a `due_at` deadline, and the `expected_employee_version` guard of §2.7. The learner's `job_function` must be one of the values in the module's `job_function_scope`; assigning a warehouse worker a patient-care module is refused with `ERR_MODULE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The new row starts in `status` `ASSIGNED` with `assigned_at` set from the logical clock, `attempt_count` at 0, and `completed_at`, `score`, `expires_at` and its own pinned `scenario_set_version` all null. The same call is also the **annual refresh**: any prior assignment for the same learner and module — whether `ASSIGNED`, `COMPLETED` or `EXPIRED` — becomes `SUPERSEDED` and stops counting toward `training_compliance_pct`, and the create increments the learner's `Employee` `version` by one.

Completing an assignment is done by the learner themselves or on their behalf by an `HRPartner`, and carries a single input: `score`. An `Employee` caller may complete only their own assignment, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The assignment must be `ASSIGNED`; completing one that is `COMPLETED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_INVALID_STATE`. The submitted `score` must be at least the module's `passing_score`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_SCORE_BELOW_PASSING` — but a failed attempt is not free: `attempt_count` counts every completion call the assignment has seen, whether accepted or rejected for score, and the row stays `ASSIGNED` so the learner may try again.

A successful completion moves the assignment `ASSIGNED` → `COMPLETED`, writes `completed_at` from the logical clock, sets `expires_at` to `completed_at` advanced by the module's `refresh_interval_days`, **pins** `scenario_set_version` by copying the module's current value onto the assignment (so republishing a scenario pack never rewrites history), and increments `attempt_count`. It then recomputes the learner's `training_current`, and the compliance metrics and readiness score of every non-`SUPERSEDED` plan of the learner's worksite.

An employee's `training_current` is true only when, for **every** module whose `job_function_scope` contains that employee's `job_function`, there exists an assignment in `status` `COMPLETED` whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock. One lapsed module is enough to make the whole flag false. The nightly sweep is what causes lapses: an `ASSIGNED` assignment whose `due_at` has passed becomes `EXPIRED`, and a `COMPLETED` assignment becomes `EXPIRED` once `expires_at` is at or before the clock — the annual refresh falling due. Both refresh `training_current` and both re-run the worksite plans' `training_compliance_pct` and readiness. The lapse has teeth beyond the metrics: an employee whose escort-required module has lapsed to `EXPIRED` can no longer be named as escort on a new protective measure, which is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_NOT_QUALIFIED`, even though nothing about the employee row itself changed.

`training_compliance_pct` is measured over **pairs**. The denominator is the number of distinct combinations of an `ACTIVE` employee of the plan's worksite with a module whose `job_function_scope` contains that employee's `job_function`. If there are no such pairs at all the percentage is 100.00 — vacuously compliant. Otherwise the numerator is the number of those pairs for which some assignment exists with matching `employee_id` and `module_id`, `status` `COMPLETED`, and `expires_at` **strictly after** the logical clock; an assignment expiring at exactly the current instant is stale and does not count. The percentage is the numerator divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, rounded half-up to two decimals. Five pairs of which four are completed and unexpired therefore yield exactly 80.00 — landing precisely on the activation gate.

### 3.5. Reporting Channels, Severity Classification and Triage

Anyone may raise an incident or threat report: an `Employee`, a `SiteManager`, a `SafetyOfficer`, or an unidentified reporter using the `Anonymous` role. The report names the `worksite_id` and the `work_area_id` where the event occurred, carries a `narrative` of between 10 and 4000 characters, an `occurred_at` timestamp that must not be after the logical clock (a future-dated event is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), an `incident_type` from `TYPE_1_CRIMINAL`, `TYPE_2_CLIENT`, `TYPE_3_COWORKER` and `TYPE_4_DOMESTIC`, and a `channel` from `WEB_FORM`, `HOTLINE`, `SUPERVISOR` and `ANONYMOUS_DROPBOX`. Two optional descriptors carry defaults: `weapon_involved` defaults to false, and `injury_level` defaults to `NONE`, with `FIRST_AID`, `MEDICAL_TREATMENT`, `LOST_TIME` and `FATALITY` recording progressively worse outcomes. Where the aggressor is internal staff, `subject_employee_id` names the person of concern; it stays null when the aggressor is not staff.

The `reporter_employee_id` field is where the channel rule bites. For every channel other than `ANONYMOUS_DROPBOX` a reporter must be named, and for `ANONYMOUS_DROPBOX` it must be absent; either mistake is refused with `ERR_REPORTER_REQUIRED`. The consequence is worth stating plainly, because it constrains a later workflow: an anonymous report can never support a retaliation claim, because there is no claimant to protect.

**Severity is classified on filing from three inputs.** `severity_points` is the sum of three contributions: 30 points when `weapon_involved` is true and nothing when it is false; an injury contribution of 0 points for `NONE`, 8 for `FIRST_AID`, 18 for `MEDICAL_TREATMENT`, 28 for `LOST_TIME` and 40 for `FATALITY`; and a typology contribution of 10 points for `TYPE_1_CRIMINAL`, 6 for `TYPE_2_CLIENT`, 12 for `TYPE_3_COWORKER` and 8 for `TYPE_4_DOMESTIC`. The total is a whole number and its bands are exact: `severity_class` is `CRITICAL` when `severity_points` is at least 40, `SERIOUS` when it is at least 20 but below 40, and `MINOR` below 20. A weapon and no injury on a criminal-typology event scores exactly 40 and is therefore `CRITICAL`; an unweaponed first-aid coworker event scores exactly 20 and is therefore `SERIOUS`.

`log_required` is a separate, deliberately broader test: it is true when `severity_class` is anything other than `MINOR`, **or** `injury_level` is anything other than `NONE`, **or** `weapon_involved` is true. Any one of the three suffices. It is `log_required` that later decides whether a substantiated report must produce a violent incident log entry.

Every new report starts with both status dimensions at their initial values — `triage_status` `NEW` and `retaliation_status` `NOT_ALLEGED` — and with `triaged_by_employee_id` and `retaliation_claimant_employee_id` null. These two dimensions are **independent**: a report may sit in `triage_status` `SUBSTANTIATED` while its `retaliation_status` is still `NOT_ALLEGED`, and equally may have a substantiated retaliation claim while its triage disposition is `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`.

A `SafetyOfficer` triages a `NEW` report by supplying a `disposition` of either `ESCALATE` or `NO_ACTION`, optionally with a `triage_note`. Both branches stamp `triaged_by_employee_id` from the caller and re-run the severity classification from the stored inputs, but their consequences are disjoint. `ESCALATE` moves `triage_status` from `NEW` to `ESCALATED`, which is the only state from which a threat case may be opened. `NO_ACTION` moves it from `NEW` to `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` and forecloses the case entirely — no threat case may be opened afterwards — and it is permitted **only** when `severity_class` is `MINOR`. Requesting `NO_ACTION` on a `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL` report is refused with `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`, as is any triage of a report whose `triage_status` is not `NEW`.

### 3.6. The Retaliation Dimension and the Anti-Retaliation Hold

The second status dimension runs on its own track and is driven by a single operation taking an `action` and an optional `statement`. The four actions are `ALLEGE`, `OPEN_REVIEW`, `SUBSTANTIATE` and `DISMISS`, and an action that is not declared from the report's current `retaliation_status` is refused with `ERR_RETALIATION_INVALID_STATE`.

`ALLEGE` is the claimant's own act. It moves `retaliation_status` from `NOT_ALLEGED` to `ALLEGED` and requires that the report actually name a reporter — an anonymous report has no `reporter_employee_id` and so cannot host a claim, which is refused with `ERR_RETALIATION_INVALID_STATE`. When the caller's role is `Employee`, their `X-User-ID` must equal the report's `reporter_employee_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; an `HRPartner` may raise the allegation on the claimant's behalf. The transition copies `reporter_employee_id` into `retaliation_claimant_employee_id` and **engages the anti-retaliation hold**.

The hold is the sharpest rule in the system. While any report has a `retaliation_status` of `ALLEGED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, no adverse protective measure — a schedule change or an access restriction — may name that report's `retaliation_claimant_employee_id` as its subject. Such a request is refused with `ERR_ANTI_RETALIATION_HOLD`, and it is refused **ahead of** every interval-window, escort-booking and area-capacity rule, so a measure that is simultaneously retaliatory and scheduled entirely outside the case's protection window reports the retaliation code rather than the window code.

An `HRPartner` drives the review from there. `OPEN_REVIEW` moves `ALLEGED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` with the hold still engaged. `SUBSTANTIATE` moves `UNDER_REVIEW` → `SUBSTANTIATED` and makes the hold **permanent**: adverse measures against that claimant remain refused in every later state. `DISMISS` **releases** the hold, and it is available from either of two states — `ALLEGED` → `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `UNDER_REVIEW` → `UNSUBSTANTIATED` — after which adverse measures naming the former claimant become creatable again. `SUBSTANTIATED` and `UNSUBSTANTIATED` are both terminal on this dimension.

### 3.7. Convening the Team, Deliberating and Deciding

A threat case is opened by a `ThreatTeamLead` or a `SafetyOfficer` naming an `incident_report_id` and a `lead_employee_id`. The report's `triage_status` must be `ESCALATED` and no non-`CLOSED` case may already exist for that report; either violation is refused with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_OPEN_FOR_REPORT`. Creation copies three references off the report and its worksite: `plan_id` is taken from the worksite's prevention plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE`, `subject_employee_id` is copied from the report's `subject_employee_id`, and `protected_employee_id` is copied from the report's `reporter_employee_id` — leaving it null for anonymous reports, which is what makes protection tracking inapplicable on those cases. The case starts in `status` `OPEN` with `assessment_round` at 1, `quorum_met` false, `ballot_count_current_round` at 0, `interim_risk_score`, `consensus_risk_score`, `risk_band`, `disposition`, `override_justification`, `protection_window_start`, `protection_window_end` and `closure_summary` all null, `coverage_gap_hours` at 0, `protection_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and `open_action_count` at 0.

Seating the team is the lead's exclusive job. Each seat names a `member_employee_id`, the `discipline` it represents — `HR`, `SECURITY`, `LEGAL`, `CLINICAL` or `OPERATIONS` — and a `role_on_team` of `LEAD`, `VOTING_MEMBER` or `ADVISOR`. A `LEAD` or `VOTING_MEMBER` seat both votes and counts toward quorum; an `ADVISOR` seat does neither, though it does satisfy a discipline requirement. Seats start in `status` `ASSIGNED` with `ballot_value`, `ballot_round`, `ballot_weight`, `ballot_share`, `ballot_submitted_at` and `rationale` all null. Three integrity rules apply. Nobody evaluates their own case: a seat naming the case's `subject_employee_id`, its `protected_employee_id`, or the `reporter_employee_id` of the underlying report is refused with `ERR_SUBJECT_CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`. An employee may hold at most one `ASSIGNED` seat per case, or the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT`. And a case may hold at most one `ASSIGNED` seat whose `role_on_team` is `LEAD`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEAM_LEAD`. Every seating recomputes `quorum_met`.

**Quorum is conditional on the incident, not fixed.** Three disciplines are always required: `HR`, `SECURITY` and `OPERATIONS`. `CLINICAL` becomes required as well whenever the underlying report's `injury_level` is anything other than `NONE`. `LEGAL` becomes required whenever the report's `weapon_involved` is true or its `incident_type` is `TYPE_4_DOMESTIC`. The seated disciplines are those of the case's seats whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, advisors included. `quorum_met` is true only when three conditions hold together: no required discipline is missing from the seated set, the number of `ASSIGNED` seats whose `role_on_team` is `LEAD` or `VOTING_MEMBER` is at least three, and there is exactly one `ASSIGNED` `LEAD` seat. When disciplines are missing they are reported in alphabetical order, which makes the outcome deterministic. The same three-seat roster is therefore a quorum for an unweaponed, uninjured client-typology incident and not a quorum for an otherwise identical domestic-typology one, where `LEGAL` also becomes mandatory.

Convening moves the case `OPEN` → `ASSESSING`, optionally recording a `convene_note`. It requires that `quorum_met` be true — otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` — and that the governing plan not be frozen, so a case whose plan is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Convening from any state other than `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`, and because the state check precedes the quorum check, convening an already-`ASSESSING` case with a broken roster reports the invalid state. A successful convene sets `assessment_round` to 1 and computes the **interim** behavioural risk score.

The interim score looks only at the incident and the subject's history. A derived **prior case count** is the number of *other* threat cases whose `subject_employee_id` equals this case's non-null `subject_employee_id` and whose `created_at` precedes this case's `created_at` — ordering is by creation timestamp alone and never by identifier. The score is then the sum of three terms: the report's `severity_points` multiplied by 0.60, plus 8 points for each prior case with the prior-case count capped at 4, plus 12 points when the report's `weapon_involved` is true. That sum is capped at 100 and rounded half-up to two decimal places into `interim_risk_score`, and `risk_band` is set from it. A weaponed first-aid coworker report with 50 severity points and no prior cases therefore yields exactly 42.00.

Balloting is open to a `ThreatTeamMember` or the `ThreatTeamLead` and carries a `ballot_value` of `NO_CONCERN`, `MONITOR`, `ELEVATED` or `IMMINENT` together with a `rationale`. The caller must hold an `ASSIGNED` seat on the case whose `role_on_team` is `LEAD` or `VOTING_MEMBER`, must not already have balloted in the case's current `assessment_round`, and the case must still be `ASSESSING`; a second ballot in the same round, a ballot from an `ADVISOR` seat, a ballot from a `RECUSED` seat, and a ballot arriving after the case has already advanced are all refused with `ERR_BALLOT_ROUND_CLOSED`. A caller holding no seat at all on the case is refused with `ERR_TEAM_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`. An accepted ballot writes `ballot_value`, `rationale`, `ballot_round` set to the case's current `assessment_round`, and `ballot_submitted_at` from the logical clock, and increments `ballot_count_current_round` — which is itself the count of the case's `ASSIGNED` seats with a voting role whose `ballot_round` equals the current `assessment_round`. The resource returned by a ballot call is the caller's own updated team seat, not the threat case, so a caller that needs the case's refreshed counters — or the automatic advance described next — reads the case back.

The case's own state is a **function of its seats**. The moment `ballot_count_current_round` equals the number of `ASSIGNED` seats whose `role_on_team` is `LEAD` or `VOTING_MEMBER` — that is, the moment every voting seat has balloted in the current round — the case advances `ASSESSING` → `DELIBERATION` automatically on that same ballot call, with no separate request. No score is computed at that point: `consensus_risk_score` stays null until the decision.

From `DELIBERATION` the lead has two ways forward. Reassessment, carrying a mandatory `reassess_reason`, returns the case `DELIBERATION` → `ASSESSING` for a fresh round: `assessment_round` increments by one, and `ballot_value`, `ballot_round`, `ballot_weight`, `ballot_share`, `ballot_submitted_at` and `rationale` are cleared on **every** seat of the case, so `ballot_count_current_round` falls back to 0. `interim_risk_score` is left untouched and `consensus_risk_score` stays null. The cycle may repeat, but `assessment_round` must be below 3 to reassess; a third-round case asking for a fourth is refused with `ERR_MAX_ASSESSMENT_ROUNDS`, which is what stops the loop running forever.

The other way forward is the decision, which carries a `disposition` and an optional `override_justification`. Deciding first computes the **consensus** score. The counted seats are those whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, whose `role_on_team` is `LEAD` or `VOTING_MEMBER`, and whose `ballot_round` equals the case's `assessment_round`. Each discipline carries a weight: `SECURITY` 1.325, `CLINICAL` 1.150, `HR` 1.000, `LEGAL` 0.875 and `OPERATIONS` 0.775. The **total weight** is the exact, unrounded sum of the weights of the counted seats. Each ballot value carries points: `NO_CONCERN` 5, `MONITOR` 35, `ELEVATED` 65 and `IMMINENT` 95. For each counted seat, `ballot_weight` is pinned to that seat's discipline weight, and `ballot_share` is that seat's ballot points multiplied by its `ballot_weight`, divided by the total weight, and **rounded half-up to two decimal places at that point**. The rounded shares are then added together. The order is mandatory and not interchangeable: each share is rounded first and the rounded shares are summed, because summing the unrounded shares and rounding once at the end produces a different number and is wrong. Finally, `consensus_risk_score` is 0.35 multiplied by the case's `interim_risk_score` plus 0.65 multiplied by the sum of rounded shares, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and `risk_band` is refreshed from it.

`risk_band` is `IMMINENT` when `consensus_risk_score` is at least 70, `HIGH` when it is at least 45 but below 70, `MODERATE` when it is at least 20 but below 45, and `LOW` below 20. The band maps one-to-one onto a required disposition: `LOW` requires `NO_THREAT`, `MODERATE` requires `MONITOR`, `HIGH` requires `ELEVATED_RISK`, and `IMMINENT` requires `IMMINENT_RISK`. Whenever `consensus_risk_score` exists it, and not `interim_risk_score`, is the authority for the band. A requested `disposition` that does not match the mapping and arrives without an `override_justification` is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_BAND_MISMATCH`.

Overrides are permitted, but only in the safe direction. An `override_justification` must be at least 40 characters long, and may raise the disposition by **at most one step** above the band mapping; it may never lower a disposition below the mapping, and it may never skip more than one band. A justification that is too short, skips more than one step, or attempts to de-escalate is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Escalation with a written rationale is a legitimate professional judgement; de-escalation of a machine-computed risk never is.

The two decision branches then diverge sharply. A `disposition` of `MONITOR`, `ELEVATED_RISK` or `IMMINENT_RISK` moves the case `DELIBERATION` → `MONITORING`, writes `protection_window_start` from the logical clock and `protection_window_end` 14 days later, and recomputes the coverage gap — which, since no protective measure exists yet, makes the entire window a gap and sets `protection_status` to `GAP`. It then runs a four-link cascade. The linked incident report moves `triage_status` `ESCALATED` → `SUBSTANTIATED`. Because the report is now substantiated and its `log_required` is true, exactly one violent incident log entry is appended, carrying six of the eight required regulatory elements. Because that entry now exists, the worksite's `violent_incident_count_12mo` is recomputed upward and `high_risk_flag` flips to true at the threshold of one. And because `high_risk_flag` flipped, the worksite's `ACTIVE` plan moves to `REVIEW_DUE`, its `compliance_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`, and `inspection_ready` is re-evaluated against 85.00.

A `disposition` of `NO_THREAT` moves the case `DELIBERATION` → `CLOSED` and **skips that entire cascade**. The linked report moves `ESCALATED` → `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, no log entry is appended, the worksite's `violent_incident_count_12mo` is unchanged, no protection window is opened, `protection_status` stays `NOT_APPLICABLE` and `coverage_gap_hours` stays 0.00. Deciding from any state other than `DELIBERATION` is refused with `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`.

Closing a `MONITORING` case requires a `closure_summary` and passes two gates in order. First, `open_action_count` must be 0, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_ACTIONS_BLOCK_CLOSE`; counselling referrals, debriefings, law-enforcement coordination and safety-plan reviews must all be finished, and an automatically generated safety-plan review created by a coverage gap counts exactly like a hand-created one. Second, every live violent incident log entry belonging to the case must carry all eight required elements, or the request is refused with `ERR_LOG_ENTRY_REQUIRED_ELEMENT_MISSING` — and since the auto-appended entry carries only six, the log must be amended before any case can close. Because the open-action gate runs first, a case failing both reports the open-action code. A successful close records the `closure_summary`, transitions every protective measure of the case still in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED` — crediting `escort_committed_minutes` back and decrementing both `restricted_employee_count` and `active_restriction_count` — then recomputes `coverage_gap_hours` and sets `protection_status` to `NOT_APPLICABLE`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Protective Measures and Post-Incident Actions

Case actions are a single entity holding two very different shapes, discriminated by `action_class`. A `PROTECTIVE` action occupies an interval; a `POST_INCIDENT` action carries a deadline. A `SecurityCoordinator` or an `HRPartner` records one against a `case_id`, declaring the `action_class` and a specific `action_type`, plus a `subject_employee_id` naming the employee protected or restricted by the measure and an optional free-text `note`.

The type must belong to its class. `PROTECTIVE` admits `ACCESS_RESTRICTION`, `SCHEDULE_CHANGE`, `SECURITY_ESCORT` and `RESTRAINING_ORDER_SUPPORT`; `POST_INCIDENT` admits `COUNSELING_REFERRAL`, `DEBRIEFING`, `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_COORDINATION` and `SAFETY_PLAN_REVIEW`. A crossed pairing is refused with `ERR_ACTION_TYPE_CLASS_MISMATCH`. Beyond the class, the specific type decides which further fields are mandatory rather than a static required list: `ACCESS_RESTRICTION` requires a `work_area_id` alongside its `subject_employee_id`, `SECURITY_ESCORT` requires an `escort_employee_id` and a protected-employee context, and every `POST_INCIDENT` type requires an `assigned_employee_id`. An incomplete set for the declared type is refused with `ERR_ACTION_FIELD_MISSING`. Fields not required by the type stay null — `work_area_id` is null on anything but an access restriction, `escort_employee_id` is null on anything but an escort, and `assigned_employee_id` is null on every protective measure. Where a `work_area_id` is given, that area must belong to the same worksite as the case's incident report, or the request is refused with `ERR_WORK_AREA_WORKSITE_MISMATCH`.

Interval shape is next, and it is checked before any overlap or window arithmetic so that a reversed interval never reaches the merge logic. A `PROTECTIVE` action must carry both `starts_at` and `ends_at` with `starts_at` strictly earlier than `ends_at`; a `POST_INCIDENT` action must carry `due_at` and must carry neither `starts_at` nor `ends_at`. Any of those violations is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. No case action of either class may be recorded at all while the plan governing its case has a `compliance_posture` of `FROZEN`, which is refused with `ERR_PLAN_POSTURE_FROZEN`.

Three further guards apply to protective measures, each after the anti-retaliation hold of §3.6 and in this order. A protective interval must overlap the case's protection window: `starts_at` must be strictly before `protection_window_end` and `ends_at` strictly after `protection_window_start`. An interval lying wholly at or after the window's end, or wholly at or before its start, is refused with `ERR_MEASURE_OUTSIDE_PROTECTION_WINDOW`; an interval that only partially overlaps is accepted and clipped later. Next, a `SECURITY_ESCORT` may not double-book its escort: the requested interval, widened by 30 minutes on **each** side to allow for travel, must not overlap any other `SECURITY_ESCORT` in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` status naming the same `escort_employee_id`. The check is scoped across all cases, not just the current one, and because the buffer is symmetric two escorts that merely touch endpoint-to-endpoint still conflict. A collision is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Finally, `ACCESS_RESTRICTION` respects an area ceiling: the number of *other* access restrictions in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` status on the same `work_area_id` whose half-open interval overlaps the requested one must stay **below** one quarter of the area's `occupancy_cap` with any fraction discarded, subject to a hard minimum of one — so an area with an `occupancy_cap` of 4 or fewer tolerates no concurrent overlapping restriction at all, one with a cap of 8 tolerates one, and one with a cap of 12 tolerates two. Reaching the ceiling is refused with `ERR_AREA_RESTRICTION_CAPACITY`. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the escort rule: this check uses plain half-open overlap with no buffer, so a new restriction starting at exactly the instant a previous one ends does **not** conflict, whereas two escorts in that position do.

A `SECURITY_ESCORT` also demands a qualified escort. The named `escort_employee_id` must be an `ACTIVE` employee holding, for every module whose `required_for_escort` is true, an assignment in `status` `COMPLETED` whose `expires_at` is after the logical clock. A separated escort, or one whose escort-required refresh has lapsed to `EXPIRED`, is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_NOT_QUALIFIED`.

An accepted `PROTECTIVE` action starts in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `duration_minutes` computed as the whole number of minutes between `starts_at` and `ends_at`, `auto_generated` false, and `completed_at`, `late_flag`, `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` all at their initial values. A `SECURITY_ESCORT` immediately increments the escort's `escort_committed_minutes` by `duration_minutes`; an `ACCESS_RESTRICTION` immediately increments both the area's `restricted_employee_count` and the subject's `active_restriction_count` by one. An accepted `POST_INCIDENT` action starts in `status` `PENDING` with `duration_minutes` at 0, `auto_generated` false, and increments the case's `open_action_count`. Either way, the case's coverage gap and `protection_status` are recomputed.

**The coverage gap measures uncovered time inside the protection window.** When `protection_window_start` is null the gap is 0.00 and `protection_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and nothing further is computed. Otherwise the window runs from `protection_window_start` up to but not including `protection_window_end` — half-open — which for the standard 14-day window is 20,160 minutes, or 336.00 hours. The candidate measures are the case's `PROTECTIVE` actions whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`, **excluding** `RESTRAINING_ORDER_SUPPORT`, which is legal assistance rather than physical coverage and never counts as time covered. Each candidate is clipped to the window — its start becomes the later of its own `starts_at` and the window start, its end becomes the earlier of its own `ends_at` and the window end — and any candidate that collapses to nothing after clipping is dropped. The surviving intervals are sorted by start and then end, and merged: an interval whose start is at or before the running end of the interval before it extends that interval, taking whichever end is later. **Adjacent intervals merge**, because a zero-length gap is no gap — two measures where one ends exactly when the next begins together cover the whole span. The covered minutes are the total length of the merged intervals; the gap minutes are the window length less the covered minutes; and `coverage_gap_hours` is the gap minutes divided by 60, rounded half-up to two decimal places. `protection_status` is `COVERED` when `coverage_gap_hours` is exactly 0.00 and `GAP` otherwise, except that it remains `NOT_APPLICABLE` whenever `protected_employee_id` is null or `protection_window_start` is null. A case with no protective measures at all therefore shows the full 336.00 hours as a gap, and a single measure covering only the window's first day shows 312.00.

Revoking a protective measure is a `SecurityCoordinator` action carrying a mandatory `revocation_reason`. It applies to `PROTECTIVE` actions in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` status — both arms behave identically — and writes `revoked_at` from the logical clock together with the `revocation_reason`, taking the row to `REVOKED`. Revoking anything else, or completing a protective measure, is refused with `ERR_ACTION_INVALID_STATE`. The compensation is exact: a `SECURITY_ESCORT` credits `escort_committed_minutes` back by precisely `duration_minutes`, and an `ACCESS_RESTRICTION` decrements both `restricted_employee_count` and `active_restriction_count` by one. Because the interval has left the conserved set, the coverage gap is recomputed and `protection_status` may flip from `COVERED` to `GAP`. And if it does flip to `GAP` while the case is `MONITORING`, the system appends a compensating `POST_INCIDENT` action of type `SAFETY_PLAN_REVIEW` with `auto_generated` true, `assigned_employee_id` set to the case's `lead_employee_id` and `due_at` 24 hours after the logical clock — raising `open_action_count` and thereby blocking closure with `ERR_OPEN_ACTIONS_BLOCK_CLOSE` until somebody attends to the newly uncovered time.

Completing a post-incident action is available to an `HRPartner` or a `SecurityCoordinator` and takes an optional `completion_note`. It applies from `PENDING` or from `OVERDUE`; both arms write `completed_at` from the logical clock and set `late_flag` to true exactly when `completed_at` is after `due_at`, which is always the case on the `OVERDUE` arm. Both decrement the case's `open_action_count`, and both re-run the governing plan's readiness score. The `OVERDUE` arm additionally decrements the plan's `overdue_action_count`, since the plan was being penalised for the lateness and that penalty now lifts.

The nightly sweep drives the remaining transitions. A `SCHEDULED` protective measure whose window has opened becomes `ACTIVE` with no ledger movement, since both states are inside the conserved set. A measure in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` whose `ends_at` has arrived becomes `EXPIRED`, releasing exactly the same credits as a revocation would — escort minutes decremented by `duration_minutes`, area and subject restriction counters decremented by one — and recomputing the owning case's `coverage_gap_hours` and `protection_status`. A `PENDING` post-incident action whose `due_at` has passed becomes `OVERDUE`, incrementing the governing plan's `overdue_action_count` and re-running the readiness score.

### 3.9. The Violent Incident Log, Drills and Inspection Readiness

The violent incident log is a regulatory register, not an editable table. Entries are written by the system, never posted directly by a client, and each carries a `worksite_id`, the `incident_report_id` of the substantiated report, and the `case_id` of the case whose decision produced it.

**Eight regulatory data elements are required on every entry, in this order:** `incident_at`, copied from the report's `occurred_at`; `work_area_name`, a snapshot of the work area's `name` taken at write time so that later renames do not rewrite history; `incident_type`, copied from the report; `perpetrator_class`; `injury_level`, copied from the report; `law_enforcement_notified`; `consequence_narrative`, describing the outcome and any lost-time consequences; and `controls_summary`, describing the controls implemented after the incident. `perpetrator_class` is derived from the report's typology: `STRANGER` for `TYPE_1_CRIMINAL`, `CLIENT` for `TYPE_2_CLIENT`, `DOMESTIC_PARTNER` for `TYPE_4_DOMESTIC`, and for `TYPE_3_COWORKER` either `COWORKER` when the report names a `subject_employee_id` or `UNKNOWN` when it does not. `law_enforcement_notified` is true exactly when, at the moment the entry is written, a law-enforcement-coordination action exists for the entry's `case_id` in `PENDING`, `OVERDUE` or `COMPLETED` status. The entry also copies `weapon_involved` from the report as contextual detail — useful to a reader, but explicitly **not** one of the eight required elements and never counted as one.

`required_element_count_present` is simply how many of those eight elements are non-null on the row. An automatically appended entry therefore carries **6**: `consequence_narrative` and `controls_summary` are unknown at decision time and stay null until an amendment supplies them. Each entry also carries `retention_expiry_date`, computed as `incident_at` advanced by five years, and `retention_status`, which starts `ACTIVE` and is flipped to `RETENTION_EXPIRED` by the nightly sweep once the expiry date is reached. Entries carry `supersedes_entry_id`, null on an original, `amendment_reason`, null on an original, and `superseded_flag`, which is derived on read: it is true exactly when some other entry's `supersedes_entry_id` equals this entry's `id`.

Correction works by **appending a successor, never by editing**. A `SafetyOfficer` amends a live entry by supplying a mandatory `amendment_reason` plus whichever of `perpetrator_class`, `law_enforcement_notified`, `consequence_narrative` and `controls_summary` the correction supplies. A new entry is created with `supersedes_entry_id` pointing at the target, the target's `superseded_flag` becomes true, and `required_element_count_present` is recomputed on the new entry from the eight elements now present — which is how a six-element entry becomes an eight-element one. Amendment is only permitted against an entry whose `superseded_flag` is false and whose `retention_status` is `ACTIVE`; amending an already-superseded entry, or one past retention, is refused with `ERR_LOG_ENTRY_IMMUTABLE`, so corrections always chain from the newest live entry. Every amendment recomputes the worksite plan's `log_completeness_pct` and readiness score.

`log_completeness_pct` measures how complete the register is. The live entries are those of the plan's worksite whose `superseded_flag` is false. With no live entries the percentage is 100.00. Otherwise it is the total of `required_element_count_present` across the live entries, divided by eight times the number of live entries, multiplied by 100, and rounded half-up to two decimals. A single live entry holding six of eight elements therefore reads 75.00, and two live entries holding six and eight respectively read 87.50.

`hazard_coverage_pct` is measured the same way from counts. The denominator is the number of work areas belonging to the plan's worksite; the numerator is the number of **distinct** work areas covered by the plan's non-`SUPERSEDED` hazard assessments. A worksite with no work areas yields 100.00; otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, multiplied by 100, rounded half-up to two decimals — so one live assessment across three areas reads 33.33 and two across three reads 66.67. `overdue_action_count` on a plan is the number of `POST_INCIDENT` case actions in `OVERDUE` status whose case points at that plan. All five derived aggregates — the four percentages plus `unmitigated_hazard_count` — are recomputed together on every write that could move any of them, because each is an aggregate over rows that may later be created, superseded or expired.

**Drills** exercise the plan. A `SafetyOfficer` schedules one against a `plan_id` with a `scenario_type` of `ACTIVE_ASSAILANT`, `DE_ESCALATION`, `LOCKDOWN` or `EVACUATION` and a `scheduled_at` planned execution time. It starts in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `credited` false, and `executed_at`, `participant_count`, `findings_count` and `participation_pct` all null. Execution records a `participant_count` between 0 and the worksite's `employee_count` and a `findings_count` between 0 and 50; a participant count above the declared headcount, or more than 50 findings, is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_COUNT_EXCEEDS_HEADCOUNT`, which is what protects the participation denominator. Executing a drill that is not `SCHEDULED`, or one whose plan has been `SUPERSEDED`, is refused with `ERR_DRILL_INVALID_STATE`. A successful execution moves the drill `SCHEDULED` → `EXECUTED`, writes `executed_at` from the logical clock, and computes `participation_pct` as `participant_count` divided by the worksite's `employee_count`, multiplied by 100, rounded half-up to two decimals. `credited` becomes true when the drill is `EXECUTED` and `participation_pct` is at least 60.00 — an inclusive boundary. A credited drill increments the plan's `drills_completed_12mo` and re-runs the readiness score. A `SCHEDULED` drill whose `scheduled_at` passes without execution is moved to `MISSED` by the nightly sweep; `drills_completed_12mo` does not move, but the drill contribution to readiness may fall on the next recompute. `drills_completed_12mo` itself is the number of the plan's drills that are `EXECUTED`, `credited`, and whose `executed_at` falls within the trailing 365 days of the logical clock.

**Inspection readiness rolls everything up, in four labelled steps.** Five weighted components are formed first. The hazard component is 30 points scaled by `hazard_coverage_pct` as a proportion of 100. The training component is 25 points scaled by `training_compliance_pct` as a proportion of 100, with the percentage first capped at 100 so that an over-100 figure cannot inflate the score. The log component is 20 points scaled by `log_completeness_pct` as a proportion of 100. The drill component is 15 points multiplied by the smaller of 1 and the ratio of `drills_completed_12mo` to the two drills required per year — so two credited drills in the trailing year earn the full 15 points and one earns half. The action component is 10 points when `overdue_action_count` is 0, 4 points when it is exactly 1, and 0 points when it is 2 or more.

Step 1 adds the five components and rounds the sum half-up to two decimal places into `readiness_subtotal`. Step 2 selects `readiness_penalty_band` **from the rounded subtotal, never from the unrounded sum and never from the final score**: `STRONG` when `readiness_subtotal` is at least 90, `ADEQUATE` when it is at least 75 but below 90, and `WEAK` below 75. The band fixes a penalty rate of 0.02 for `STRONG`, 0.06 for `ADEQUATE` and 0.12 for `WEAK`. The rounding is decisive here rather than cosmetic — a raw sum of 74.999 rounds to a subtotal of exactly 75.00 and therefore bands as `ADEQUATE` at 0.06, whereas banding on the raw figure would have given `WEAK` at 0.12 and a materially different final score. Step 3 computes `readiness_penalty_points` as `readiness_subtotal` multiplied by the penalty rate multiplied by `unmitigated_hazard_count`, rounded half-up to two decimals, and `readiness_score` as `readiness_subtotal` less `readiness_penalty_points`, floored at 0 and rounded half-up to two decimals. A plan with no unmitigated engineering hazards therefore pays no penalty at all, whatever its band.

Step 4 sets `inspection_ready`, and the bar depends on the plan's posture. It is always false whenever `compliance_posture` is `FROZEN`, regardless of the score. When the posture is `RESTRICTED` — a `REVIEW_DUE` plan, or any plan on a high-risk worksite — `inspection_ready` requires `readiness_score` to be at least 85.00. When the posture is `OPEN`, it requires at least 75.00. Both boundaries are inclusive: a score of exactly 85.00 on a `RESTRICTED` plan is ready. `unmitigated_hazard_count` influences readiness only through `readiness_penalty_points` and is never a separate gate — a plan with unmitigated hazards but a high enough score after penalty is still ready.
