# Furniture Bank Distribution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A local furniture bank sits at the junction of three flows that rarely line up: donated household goods arriving from the public on the donor's schedule, referred households arriving from partner social-service agencies on the caseworker's schedule, and a small fleet of trucks and volunteer movers that has to carry the first to the second. Every one of those flows is scarce and every one of them is governed by rules that were written after something went wrong — a recalled crib was handed to a family, a bed-bug infestation moved from one residence into a warehouse and then into six more homes, an agency booked twenty deliveries and its clients answered the door for three of them. This API is the operational system of record for that work.

The system tracks a **PartnerAgency** and the quarterly approval quota it may spend, the **Client** households it refers, the **Referral** that carries a caseworker's request through adjudication, and the **Voucher** that redeems an approved referral into a concrete list of household-goods lines. On the supply side it tracks a **DonationOffer** and the individual **DonationItem** pieces on it, the acceptance criteria applied to each piece, the warehouse **InventoryItem** stock units minted from accepted pieces, and the bed-bug quarantine protocol that decides when those units may be touched. Distribution binds the two together: a **DeliveryRun** on a **Truck**, crewed by **Volunteer** movers through **CrewAssignment** invitations, calls at a **DeliveryStop** for each booked voucher. Demand that cannot be met becomes a **WaitlistEntry**, and the unmet-need report over those entries is what the executive director takes to the board when asking for more beds.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, the system runs on a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that quarantine dwell periods, referral expiry, delivery bans and volunteer rest buffers are all exercised deterministically. Second, money and stock are treated as conserved quantities: an approval that consumes a quota slot must be matched by an expiry that returns it, and a stock unit reserved against a voucher line must be matched by a cancellation that releases it. Where a single operation touches several entities, this document states every one of those effects, because an implementation that applies only the obvious one leaves the ledger silently wrong.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, common resource properties, the logical clock, numeric precision, concurrency control, and the fixed order in which requests are rejected.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is missing or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `CaseManager`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `WarehouseLead`, `DispatchPlanner`, and `Volunteer`. If `X-User-Role` is not one of those six, or if it is one of them but that role is not listed as authorized for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role, and this is what ties a request to the resources it is permitted to touch.

- When `X-User-Role` is `CaseManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PartnerAgency`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `PartnerAgency` compare the resource's agency foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a case manager may only read an agency whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`, may only create a `Client` or a `Referral` whose `agency_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only read or act on a client, referral or voucher whose owning agency — reached directly through `agency_id`, or through the voucher's referral and that referral's `agency_id` — equals `X-User-ID`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scoping guards household registration, household reads, referral drafting, referral reads, referral submission, voucher issuance, voucher reads, voucher cancellation, and agency reads. Every one of those nine is a single-resource operation, and the list is exhaustive: neither the collection listings of households, referrals and vouchers nor the nested collections hanging off a voucher — its lines and its booked stops — carry any ownership comparison, so a case manager reading them sees every matching row on file.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Volunteer` row; ownership checks compare the `volunteer_id` of the crew assignment being answered, and the `id` of the volunteer record being read, to `X-User-ID`. Answering someone else's crew invitation, or reading another volunteer's record, is rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Those two operations are the whole of it: the nested listing of a volunteer's crew assignments carries no ownership comparison at all, so any role authorized for that endpoint — a volunteer included — may read any volunteer's assignment list.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and is never compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `DispatchPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and is never compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `WarehouseLead`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does record it — the warehouse inspection stamps it onto `DonationOffer.inspector_user_id` — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`. It is compared to one thing only: the stored `inspector_user_id`, when a later quarantine release is attempted. A release performed by the same `X-User-ID` that recorded the intake inspection on that offer is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Two different people must sign off on an offer that came into the building under bed-bug suspicion.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer within 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer greater than or equal to 0, is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, and rows that share an identical `created_at` — which every row minted inside a single request does, the logical clock not having moved between them — are ordered by the sequence in which they were inserted, so a create request's array order survives into the listing. Exactly one endpoint says otherwise: the stop list of a delivery run comes back in routing order, ascending `sequence`; the stop list of a voucher keeps the common `created_at` order. The optional filters offered by a list endpoint are exact string comparisons against the stored field, so a filter value that matches nothing — including one outside the field's declared member set — yields an empty page rather than a rejection, and a query parameter the endpoint does not offer is ignored. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of matching rows before the page window is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource in this system — `PartnerAgency`, `Client`, `Referral`, `Voucher`, `VoucherLine`, `DonationOffer`, `DonationItem`, `InventoryItem`, `Truck`, `DeliveryRun`, `DeliveryStop`, `Volunteer`, `CrewAssignment` and `WaitlistEntry` alike — carries the same four common properties, with no exceptions:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client.
- `created_at` — the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits at which the row was created.
- `updated_at` — the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits at which the row was last mutated. At creation it equals `created_at`.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations applied indirectly as a side effect of an operation aimed at a different resource.

The `version` field is not decorative: it is the observable evidence that a cascade actually fired. When this document says that an operation "bumps the version" of a secondary entity, it means both that `version` increases by 1 on that entity and that its `updated_at` is refreshed. When an operation touches several entities, each touched entity's `version` and `updated_at` move independently. A single request nonetheless moves any one row by at most 1, however many of that row's fields it writes and however many cascade steps arrive at it: `version` is the evidence that the request touched the row, not a tally of individual writes. And a recompute that leaves every value it writes exactly as it found it is not a touch at all — an agency `standing` pass that lands on the standing already stored, a run whose `load_cubic_feet`, `confirmed_crew_count`, `has_confirmed_driver` and `stop_count` all come out unchanged, a waitlist entry rescored or topped up to the numbers it already held, and a voucher line whose `status` and `quantity_allocated` both survive a round untouched each keep the `version` they had.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Nested Reads

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error. A read of the lines belonging to a voucher, for example, resolves the voucher first; if no voucher carries that `id`, the answer is `ERR_VOUCHER_NOT_FOUND` and the request never reaches the line collection. The same discipline applies to the stops of a voucher, the items of a donation offer, the stops and crew of a delivery run, and the crew assignments of a volunteer. Each entity has its own not-found identifier: `ERR_AGENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOUCHER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DONATION_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVENTORY_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_WAITLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, all returned with HTTP 404.

The identical set of identifiers governs foreign keys supplied in a request body. Whenever a payload names an agency, a client, a referral, a voucher, a truck, a delivery run or a volunteer that does not resolve to an existing row, the corresponding not-found identifier is returned with HTTP 404. This body-level resolution applies to household registration, referral drafting, voucher issuance, pickup-truck assignment on a donation offer, run planning (including the optional `rescheduled_from_run_id` back-reference, which is resolved after the `truck_id` and yields `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND` when it does not resolve), stop booking, and crew invitation.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and the Operational Cycle

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time for business purposes. A logical clock supplies the instant referred to throughout this document as "now". Before any operator sets it, that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must use that startup default rather than falling back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; moving the clock mutates no entity and runs no expiry of its own, and it answers `200 OK` with an empty object in `data`.

Expiry, rescoring, promotion and household reinstatement are instead driven by an explicit operational cycle, also `PlatformAdmin`-only, which accepts an optional boolean `advance_quota_epoch` that defaults to false when it is omitted. The cycle runs its passes in exactly this pinned order, and an implementation that reorders them will produce different results. The whole cycle is one request, so the at-most-one-bump rule of §2.4 applies across all six passes together: an agency whose quota is refunded in pass 1, whose standing changes in pass 3 and whose epoch advances in pass 6 comes out of the call with its `version` up by exactly 1, and a household remediated in 3a and reinstated in 3b likewise moves by 1:

1. **Referral expiry.** Every `Referral` in status `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` is strictly earlier than now moves to `EXPIRED`.
2. **Voucher expiry.** The vouchers of those referrals expire and their reserved stock is released (detailed in §3.3 and §3.6). Only a voucher standing in `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` or `ALLOCATED` is expired by this pass; a voucher already booked onto a run — one in `SCHEDULED` — is passed over untouched, as are the terminal states. The pass reads the referral's `expires_at` directly, so a voucher expires here whatever status pass 1 left its referral in.
3. **Standing recompute.** Every agency's `standing` is recomputed.
   - **3a. Bed-bug remediation.** Every `Client` whose `bed_bug_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `bed_bug_confirmed_at` is at least 60 days old moves to `bed_bug_status` `REMEDIATED`.
   - **3b. Household reinstatement.** Every `Client` in status `ON_HOLD` whose `delivery_ban_until` is null or has already passed — a stamp falling exactly on now counts as passed — and whose `bed_bug_status` is no longer `CONFIRMED`, returns to status `ACTIVE` with `no_show_strikes` reset to 0. The `delivery_ban_until` stamp itself is left standing at whatever instant it holds; reinstatement does not clear it. Because 3a runs before 3b, a household remediated in this very cycle is reinstated in the same call.
4. **Waitlist rescoring.** Every `WaitlistEntry` in status `WAITING` has its `priority_score` recomputed using the waitlist variant of the need score.
5. **Promotion pass.** Waiting demand is re-run against available stock. The candidates are exactly those vouchers carrying at least one `WAITING` entry, taken in ascending `created_at` order, and each candidate whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` is put through a full allocation round — which bumps that voucher's `version` and, unless it is a zero-yield round on a still-`ISSUED` voucher, recomputes its `fill_ratio` and provisional money fields, whether or not the round found anything to reserve.
6. **Quota epoch advance.** Only when `advance_quota_epoch` is true, every agency's `quota_epoch` is incremented and its `quota_used` is reset to 0.

The cycle answers with a summary object rather than an entity: `expired_referral_count`, `expired_voucher_count`, `released_inventory_count`, `promoted_waitlist_count`, `restanding_count`, `reinstated_client_count`, and the boolean `quota_epoch_advanced`. Each is counted as follows. `expired_referral_count` is the referrals pass 1 moved to `EXPIRED`. `expired_voucher_count` is the vouchers pass 2 expired, and `released_inventory_count` the individual stock units those expiries handed back to `AVAILABLE`. `promoted_waitlist_count` is the number of waitlist entries moved `WAITING → PROMOTED` by pass 5, not the number of vouchers that pass touched. `restanding_count` is the number of agencies the standing pass visited, which is every agency on file whether or not its `standing` came out any different. `reinstated_client_count` is the households pass 3b returned to `ACTIVE`. And `quota_epoch_advanced` simply echoes the request flag, false whenever the flag was false or absent.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding, and Derived Numbers

All money in this system is expressed in **integer cents** — `gross_fee_cents`, `subsidy_cents`, `client_due_cents`, `amount_paid_cents`, `chargeback_cents` and `no_show_credits_owed_cents` are integers, never decimals. Wherever rounding is required, the method is **round half up**, and the number of times rounding is applied is itself part of the contract:

- In the delivery fee, rounding is applied exactly once, to the subsidy amount. The household's share is then a pure subtraction and is never re-rounded.
- In the no-show chargeback, rounding is applied once per voucher line, before the line charges are summed. Summing unrounded line charges and rounding the total once is wrong and will differ by a cent or more.
- `priority_score` on both `Referral` and `WaitlistEntry` is carried to one decimal place, rounded half up, and floored at 0.0.
- `declared_soft_ratio` on a donation offer is carried to three decimal places, rounded half up.
- `bed_bug_risk_score` on a donation offer is carried to one decimal place, rounded half up, on a 0.0 to 100.0 scale.
- `fill_ratio` on a voucher is carried to three decimal places, rounded half up like every other number here.
- `fill_rate_pct` in the unmet-need report is carried to one decimal place, rounded half up, or is null when its denominator is zero.
- The no-show rate that drives agency `standing` is compared as an exact rational value with no rounding at all before comparison.
- Cubic feet — `cubic_feet`, `load_cubic_feet`, `capacity_cubic_feet` — are summed as declared and compared exactly, with no tolerance.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Repeatable Writes

Two write operations in this system are legitimately repeatable against an unchanged parent — running a second allocation round against a voucher succeeds on its own merits, and a run legitimately carries many stops — so neither can be protected by a state precondition alone. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency on the parent's `version`.

A `POST /vouchers/{id}/allocate` request must carry `expected_voucher_version` as a required, non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the target `Voucher` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the voucher's current `version`, the allocation round is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VOUCHER_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so a warehouse lead cannot reserve stock against a picture of the voucher that another writer has already moved past.

A `POST /delivery-runs/{id}/stops` request must carry `expected_run_version` as a required, non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the target `DeliveryRun` as the planner last observed it. If that value does not equal the run's current `version`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), pinning the planner's view of the run's load and stop list to the one they actually read.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, cancellation, dispatch, fee settlement, crew invitation, crew response, triage, pickup scheduling, intake, quarantine release and outcome recording carry no expected-version input of any kind.

### 2.9. Rejection Precedence

When a request would fail several checks at once, exactly one identifier comes back, and which one is fixed. Checks are evaluated top to bottom in the order below and the first failure wins.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the six roles and that role is authorized for this endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The request body conforms to the endpoint's declared input schema: every required field present — a field supplied with an explicit `null` counts as absent, so a required field sent as `null` fails while an optional one sent as `null` falls back to its default — no unknown field, and, for the action endpoints that declare no input field at all, an absent or empty body with any field whatsoever rejected as unknown, every enum value drawn from its declared member set, every integer within its declared range, every datetime a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, and every window's end strictly after its start — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every endpoint that accepts a body.
4. `limit` is an integer within 1 through 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer greater than or equal to 0 — otherwise `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
5. Every path-parameter resource exists, validated left to right — otherwise the matching not-found identifier from §2.5 (404). Applies to every endpoint with a path id.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — otherwise the matching not-found identifier (404). Applies to household registration, referral drafting, voucher issuance, pickup-truck assignment, run planning, stop booking and crew invitation.
7. Agency scoping for a `CaseManager` — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403), on the nine operations listed in §2.1.
8. Self-scoping for a `Volunteer` — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403), on crew-invitation response and volunteer read.
9. Segregation of duties on quarantine release — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. The supplied expected-version field equals the parent entity's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VOUCHER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` (412), on the allocation round and stop booking respectively.
11. The target resource is in a state that has an outgoing transition for this trigger — otherwise one of `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_VOUCHER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FEE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_STOP_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_CREW_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
12. A `RETURNED` referral is only resubmittable while its `revision_count` is strictly less than 2 — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), on referral submission.
13. The household's `bed_bug_status` is not `CONFIRMED` — otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_BED_BUG_BLOCK` (422). This guards five operations: referral submission, referral approval (the approve decision only), voucher issuance, the allocation round, and stop booking.
14. The household is not under a delivery ban, meaning its `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `delivery_ban_until` is either null or no later than now — otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_DELIVERY_BANNED` (422), on stop booking, and on referral submission too, where the household-status half of that test carries the same identifier.
15. The truck is not under a bed-bug lockout covering the window in question, meaning `bed_bug_lockout_until` is either null or no later than the window start being scheduled or dispatched — otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_BED_BUG_LOCKOUT` (409). This guards two operations: pickup scheduling and run dispatch.
16. Quarantine dwell time has elapsed, meaning now is at or after `quarantine_release_due_at` — otherwise `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_ELAPSED` (409), on quarantine release.
17. The referral has not expired, meaning now is at or before `expires_at` — otherwise `ERR_REFERRAL_EXPIRED` (409). This guards three operations: voucher issuance, the allocation round, and stop booking.
18. The truck's resource window is free — otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on pickup scheduling.
19. The volunteer's rest window is respected — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on crew invitation.
20. At most one voucher exists per referral — otherwise `ERR_VOUCHER_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409), on voucher issuance.
21. The volunteer does not already hold an `INVITED` or `CONFIRMED` assignment on this same run — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREW_ASSIGNMENT` (409), on crew invitation.
22. The requested `sequence` is not already occupied by another `SCHEDULED` stop on this run — otherwise `ERR_STOP_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT` (409), on stop booking.
23. A `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` voucher is filled to at least the scheduling floor — otherwise `ERR_VOUCHER_UNDERFILLED` (422), on stop booking.
24. A `DRIVER` seat is only offered to a volunteer whose `lift_certified` is true — otherwise `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422), on crew invitation.
25. The run's `load_cubic_feet` is no greater than the truck's `capacity_cubic_feet` — otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), on dispatch.
26. The run's `confirmed_crew_count` is at least the truck's `crew_minimum` and `has_confirmed_driver` is true — otherwise `ERR_CREW_BELOW_MINIMUM` (409), on dispatch.
27. The referring agency's `standing` is not `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED` (409). This guards two operations: referral submission and referral approval (the approve decision only).
28. The agency's `quota_used` is strictly less than its `quarterly_voucher_quota` — otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` (409), on referral approval (the approve decision only).
29. A payment record's `amount_cents` equals the voucher's `client_due_cents` exactly — otherwise `ERR_FEE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422), on fee settlement.

Note the consequence of ordering the state gate (11) ahead of the safety gates: a bed-bug-blocked household attempting an operation from a state that has no outgoing transition receives the state identifier, not `ERR_CLIENT_BED_BUG_BLOCK`. Note equally that authorization (2) precedes scoping (7 and 8): a role that is not on an endpoint's list never reaches the ownership comparison.

### 2.10. Cascade Integrity and Conserved Quantities

Two quantities in this system are conserved by paired forward and compensating actions, and both must hold after every request.

The first is the agency's approval budget. `PartnerAgency.quota_used` must always equal the number of that agency's referrals whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `FULFILLED` and whose `approved_epoch` equals the agency's current `quota_epoch`. Approving a referral is the forward action and raises `quota_used` by 1 while stamping `approved_epoch`; expiring an approved referral in the operational cycle is the compensating action and lowers `quota_used` by 1. Moving a referral from `APPROVED` to `FULFILLED` must **not** touch `quota_used`, because both statuses sit inside the counted set and the identity already holds across fulfilment.

The second is physical stock. `VoucherLine.quantity_allocated` must always equal the number of inventory units whose `reserved_for_voucher_line_id` points at that line and whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `STAGED` or `DELIVERED`. Reserving a unit during an allocation round is the forward action and raises `quantity_allocated` by 1; cancelling the voucher is the compensating action, which returns each unit to `AVAILABLE`, clears its `reserved_for_voucher_line_id`, lowers `quantity_allocated` by 1, and moves the line to `CANCELLED`. For every category, the count of `AVAILABLE` units therefore returns to its pre-allocation value once every unit reserved for the line has been released — although `available_since` is restamped on the way back, so the returned unit re-enters the allocation ranking at the back of its grade's queue. The unit count is conserved; the ordering is not. A failed delivery is deliberately **not** a compensator here: it parks the stock in `RESERVED` and leaves `quantity_allocated` untouched.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Partner Agencies, Households, and the Quota Epoch

A `PlatformAdmin` registers each referring social-service agency by supplying its legal `name` — a non-empty string of 1 to 120 characters — the `county` it operates in, which is one of `NORTH`, `CENTRAL` or `SOUTH`, and a `quarterly_voucher_quota` of at least 1. That quota is the maximum number of referrals the agency may hold in an approved state within a single quota accounting period. A newly registered agency opens with `quota_used` at 0, `quota_epoch` at 1, `standing` at `GOOD`, and `no_show_credits_owed_cents` at 0. The quota epoch counter is advanced only by the operational cycle, and only when that cycle is asked to do so; nothing else moves it.

Households are registered by a `CaseManager`, who supplies the `agency_id` of their own agency, a `household_size` of at least 1 and at most 12, the `county` the household lives in, and an optional `has_infant` flag that defaults to false and means the household includes a child under 24 months. A newly registered household opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `bed_bug_status` `CLEAR`, `bed_bug_confirmed_at` null, `no_show_strikes` at 0, `delivery_ban_until` null, and `lifetime_vouchers_delivered` at 0. The household eligibility gate has only two values, `ACTIVE` and `ON_HOLD`; the bed-bug protocol status of the residence has three, `CLEAR`, `CONFIRMED` and `REMEDIATED`. Households may be listed with optional filters on `agency_id`, `status` and `county`.

Agency compliance `standing` is a live derived field, not a latch — it can move back to `GOOD` on a later recompute. It is recalculated on every delivery outcome that produces a `NO_SHOW` or a `REFUSED`, and for every agency during the standing pass of the operational cycle. The recompute looks at the delivery stops belonging to that agency, reached from stop to voucher to referral and then to the referral's agency, whose `status` is one of `DELIVERED`, `NO_SHOW` or `REFUSED`. Those stops are ordered by `created_at` descending and the first ten are kept; if the stop sitting at the tenth position ties on `created_at` with stops behind it, every tied stop is kept as well, so the window may exceed ten rows but is never broken by an arbitrary field. Stops that are `CANCELLED` or still `SCHEDULED` never enter the window. If fewer than two stops are in the window, `standing` is `GOOD`. Otherwise the system takes the count of `NO_SHOW` stops in the window divided by the total count of stops in the window, as an exact rational value with no rounding, and sets `standing` to `SUSPENDED` when that share is at least one half, otherwise to `PROBATION` when it is at least one quarter, otherwise to `GOOD`. A share of exactly 0.25 is therefore `PROBATION`, and a share of exactly 0.50 is `SUSPENDED`.

Standing has teeth in three places. A `SUSPENDED` agency cannot submit a referral and cannot have one approved, both rejected with `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`. A `PROBATION` agency's households lose part of their delivery subsidy, and a `SUSPENDED` agency's households lose all of it, as described in §3.9. Finally, chargebacks from failed deliveries accumulate on the agency's `no_show_credits_owed_cents` ledger, which only ever grows as outcomes are recorded — and a failed stop whose computed chargeback comes out at 0 adds nothing to that ledger and does not bump the agency's `version` at all.

### 3.2. Referral Intake, Revision Rounds, and Adjudication

A case manager drafts a referral by naming the `client_id` of the household and the `agency_id` of their own agency, which must equal the household's own `agency_id`; the two foreign keys are resolved in that written order, client first, and a mismatch on either — a supplied `agency_id` that is not the acting user, or a household that belongs to somebody else — is an `ERR_AGENCY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The draft carries `requested_categories`, a non-empty array whose elements are drawn from `BED`, `SOFA`, `CRIB`, `TABLE_SET`, `DRESSER`, `KITCHEN_KIT` and `LINENS`; an `urgency` banding of `ROUTINE`, `PRIORITY` or `EMERGENCY`; an optional `home_visit_completed` boolean that defaults to false and records whether the case manager physically visited the residence; and a `bed_bug_attestation` boolean by which the case manager attests that there is no active infestation at the residence. A fresh referral is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0, `priority_score` at 0.0, and `approved_at`, `expires_at`, `approved_epoch` and `review_note` all null. Referrals may be listed with optional filters on `client_id`, `agency_id` and `status`.

Submitting a referral moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Five conditions must hold: the referral is in `DRAFT`, the household's `status` is `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_DELIVERY_BANNED`, the same identifier stop booking uses for an ineligible household), the household's `bed_bug_status` is not `CONFIRMED` (otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_BED_BUG_BLOCK`), the referral's `bed_bug_attestation` is true (otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_BED_BUG_BLOCK` as well, an unattested residence being treated as an infestation risk), and the agency's `standing` is not `SUSPENDED` (otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_SUSPENDED`). They are tested in exactly that written order, with the revision-limit check of the `RETURNED` edge sitting between the state gate and the bed-bug status. Submission recomputes `priority_score` using the intake variant of the need score and bumps the referral's `version` by 1.

The same submission action also closes the revision loop, moving a referral `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`. That edge requires the referral to be in `RETURNED`, requires `revision_count` to be strictly less than 2, and requires the same household and agency conditions as a first submission: household `status` `ACTIVE`, household `bed_bug_status` not `CONFIRMED`, `bed_bug_attestation` true, agency `standing` not `SUSPENDED`. A revision round raises `revision_count` by 1, then recomputes `priority_score` with the intake variant — and because the raised revision count feeds the penalty term, each round lowers the score by six points. A third resubmission attempt, where `revision_count` already equals 2, is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Submitting from any status other than `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` is rejected with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_SUBMITTED`.

**The need score.** Both `Referral.priority_score` and `WaitlistEntry.priority_score` come from the same weighted calculation, differing only in two inputs. Urgency contributes 10 points for `ROUTINE`, 25 for `PRIORITY`, and 40 for `EMERGENCY`. Household size contributes 5 points per person, counting at most six people even in a larger household — so a household of nine contributes 30 points, the same as a household of six. A household with `has_infant` true contributes a further 15 points. Tenure contributes 4 points for each whole 30-day month waited, capped at 20 points, which is reached after five whole months. From that sum two penalties are subtracted: 6 points for each revision round already recorded on the referral, and a strike penalty equal to the household's `no_show_strikes` multiplied by a per-variant weight. The result is floored at 0.0 and then rounded half up to one decimal place. In the **intake variant**, written to `Referral.priority_score` when a referral is submitted (including every revision round), the tenure input is zero — the queue clock has not started — and the strike weight is 8 points per strike. In the **waitlist variant**, written to `WaitlistEntry.priority_score` during the scoring pass of the operational cycle over every `WAITING` entry, the tenure input is the number of whole 24-hour days elapsed between the entry's `waiting_since` and now, rounded down, and the strike weight is 16 points per strike. The same household therefore scores differently as a waiting line than it did as an intake referral. Referrals whose status is `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` or `FULFILLED` are never rescored.

**Adjudication.** A `PlatformAdmin` records the furniture bank's verdict by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `RETURN` or `DENY`, plus an optional `note` that only the `RETURN` and `DENY` decisions store: an `APPROVE` leaves `review_note` exactly as it found it and discards any note sent with it, while a `RETURN` or `DENY` arriving without one writes `review_note` null. A `RETURN` decision requires the referral to be in `SUBMITTED` and moves it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, storing the supplied note in `review_note` and bumping the referral's `version`. A `DENY` decision is accepted from either of the two live review states — `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and `RETURNED → DENIED` — and likewise stores `review_note` and bumps `version`. An `APPROVE` decision moves the referral `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`. It requires the referral to be in `SUBMITTED`, requires the agency's `standing` not to be `SUSPENDED`, requires `quota_used` to be strictly less than `quarterly_voucher_quota` (otherwise `ERR_AGENCY_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`), and requires the household's `bed_bug_status` not to be `CONFIRMED`. Approval stamps `approved_at` with the current instant and `expires_at` with that instant plus 45 days, captures the agency's current `quota_epoch` into the referral's `approved_epoch`, raises the agency's `quota_used` by 1, and bumps both the referral's and the agency's `version` by 1. Attempting a review from a status that has no matching outgoing edge is rejected with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_SUBMITTED`.

Two further edges leave an approved referral. During the expiry pass of the operational cycle, a referral in `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` is strictly earlier than now moves `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, lowers the agency's `quota_used` by 1 as the compensating half of the quota identity, never taking it below 0, and bumps both the referral's and the agency's `version`. Separately, when the voucher redeeming this referral reaches `DELIVERED`, the referral moves `APPROVED → FULFILLED` and its `version` is bumped — but `quota_used` is deliberately left alone here, because `APPROVED` and `FULFILLED` both count toward the quota and the total is unchanged.

Finally, a referral can be denied by the system rather than by an adjudicator. When a household is placed `ON_HOLD` by the no-show contagion described in §3.8, every referral of that household sitting in `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, and every referral sitting in `RETURNED` moves `RETURNED → DENIED`. Both system-driven edges write the literal string `auto-denied: household placed ON_HOLD` into `review_note` and bump the referral's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. Voucher Issuance, Line Composition, and Withdrawal

A case manager redeems an approved referral by issuing a voucher against its `referral_id`, together with `lines`, an array in which each element declares a `category` drawn from the seven household-goods categories, a `quantity_requested` of at least 1 and at most 6, and a `min_condition` of `A`, `B` or `C` naming the worst condition grade the household will accept on that line. Issuance requires the referral to be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_APPROVED`; requires that no voucher already exists for that referral, otherwise `ERR_VOUCHER_ALREADY_EXISTS`; requires the referral not to have expired, otherwise `ERR_REFERRAL_EXPIRED`; and requires the household's `bed_bug_status` not to be `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_BED_BUG_BLOCK`.

The new voucher opens on two independent state axes. Its fulfilment axis, `status`, starts at `ISSUED` and moves through `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, `ALLOCATED`, `SCHEDULED` and finally `DELIVERED`, `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED`. Its billing axis, `fee_status`, starts at `PENDING` and moves to `WAIVED`, `INVOICED`, and from there to `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. The two advance on different triggers and, apart from one deliberate coupling described in §3.9, do not read each other. At issuance `gross_fee_cents`, `subsidy_cents`, `client_due_cents` and `amount_paid_cents` are all 0, `fill_ratio` is 0.0, and `fee_basis` is null — it becomes `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` only once a fee calculation has actually run. One `VoucherLine` is created per element of the supplied array, each carrying the owning `voucher_id`, its `quantity_allocated` at 0, and its `status` at `OPEN`. A line's fill state is one of `OPEN`, `PARTIAL`, `FILLED`, `WAITLISTED` or `CANCELLED`. Vouchers may be listed with optional filters on `referral_id`, `status` and `fee_status`; a voucher's lines and its booked stops are each readable as their own collections, filterable by `status`.

Withdrawal is available to a `CaseManager` or a `PlatformAdmin`, who must supply a `reason` — any string satisfies it, and the value is recorded nowhere on the voucher. Cancellation is accepted from three fulfilment states. From `ISSUED → CANCELLED` no stock is held, so the effect is limited to moving every `VoucherLine` to `CANCELLED`, moving every `WAITING` waitlist entry belonging to those lines to `CANCELLED`, and bumping the voucher's `version`. From `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → CANCELLED` and from `ALLOCATED → CANCELLED` the effects are identical to each other and add stock release: every inventory unit reserved against those lines moves `RESERVED → AVAILABLE`, its `reserved_for_voucher_line_id` is cleared, its `available_since` is restamped to the current instant, and the owning line's `quantity_allocated` drops by one per released unit; then every line moves to `CANCELLED`, every `WAITING` waitlist entry for those lines moves to `CANCELLED`, and the voucher's `version` is bumped. Cancelling from any other fulfilment state is rejected with `ERR_VOUCHER_INVALID_STATE`.

Expiry follows the referral. During step 2 of the operational cycle's expiry pass, a voucher whose referral's `expires_at` is strictly earlier than now expires from whichever live fulfilment state it holds. From `ISSUED → EXPIRED` there is no reserved stock, so every line moves to `CANCELLED` and every `WAITING` waitlist entry for those lines moves to `EXPIRED`. From `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → EXPIRED` and from `ALLOCATED → EXPIRED` the same line and waitlist effects apply, and in addition every reserved unit moves `RESERVED → AVAILABLE` with `reserved_for_voucher_line_id` cleared and `available_since` restamped to now. All three edges bump the voucher's `version` by 1. Neither expiry nor cancellation rewrites the voucher's money fields, its `fee_basis` or its `fill_ratio`: those keep whatever the last allocation round left on them, even though the lines they were computed from have just been emptied and cancelled.

### 3.4. Donation Offers and the Item Acceptance Criteria

An `IntakeCoordinator` logs a donor's offer with the donor's `donor_name` and `donor_phone`, both non-empty; the `pickup_county` the goods must be collected from; and a pickup availability window given by an inclusive `pickup_window_start` and an exclusive `pickup_window_end` that must be strictly later than the start. Alongside these comes `items`, a non-empty array in which each element declares a `category`, a `declared_condition` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D` as claimed by the donor, a `soft_surface` boolean marking upholstered or fabric-covered pieces that carry bed-bug risk, and a `cubic_feet` stowage volume that must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than 200. One `DonationItem` is created per element, in the order supplied, each carrying the owning `offer_id`, a `decision` of `PENDING`, a null `reject_reason` and a null `graded_condition`. The offer itself opens in `status` `OFFERED` with `truck_id` null, `accepted_item_count` and `rejected_item_count` at 0, `declared_soft_ratio` at 0.0, `bed_bug_risk_score` at 0.0, `inspector_user_id` null, and both `quarantine_started_at` and `quarantine_release_due_at` null. Offers may be listed with optional filters on `status`, `truck_id` and `pickup_county`, and an offer's pieces may be listed filtered by `decision`.

**Triage** applies the acceptance criteria to every piece on the offer, and its three steps must be evaluated in exactly this order.

Step one takes a pre-decision snapshot over *all* submitted pieces of the offer: `declared_soft_ratio` is the number of pieces whose `soft_surface` is true divided by the total number of pieces, rounded half up to three decimal places.

Step two decides each piece, walking the pieces in ascending `created_at` order, with ties keeping the order in which they appeared in the create request's array. Before the walk begins the system measures the warehouse: current occupancy is the sum of `cubic_feet` over every inventory unit whose `status` is `QUARANTINED`, `AVAILABLE`, `RESERVED` or `STAGED` — units that are `DELIVERED` or `DISPOSED` have left the building and are excluded — and headroom is the warehouse's total floor space of 400 cubic feet minus that occupancy. A running total of accepted volume starts at zero. For each piece the reject-reason ladder is a strict first match, and only the first matching rung is reported:

1. If the piece's `category` is `CRIB` and its `declared_condition` is anything other than `A`, it is refused with `reject_reason` `RECALLED_CRIB`. A crib that is both non-A-grade and D-grade therefore reports `RECALLED_CRIB`, never `CONDITION_BELOW_MIN`.
2. Otherwise, if its `declared_condition` is `D`, it is refused with `reject_reason` `CONDITION_BELOW_MIN`.
3. Otherwise, if its `soft_surface` is true and the offer's `declared_soft_ratio` from step one is at least 0.5, it is refused with `reject_reason` `SOFT_SURFACE_RISK`. An offer that is half or more upholstered loses all of its upholstery.
4. Otherwise, if the running total of accepted volume plus this piece's `cubic_feet` would strictly exceed the headroom, it is refused with `reject_reason` `CAPACITY`.
5. Otherwise the piece is accepted, its `decision` becomes `ACCEPTED`, and its `cubic_feet` is added to the running total.

Step three computes the risk score only after every decision is final. If no piece was accepted, `bed_bug_risk_score` is 0.0. Otherwise it is 100 multiplied by the accepted soft-surface volume divided by the total accepted volume, rounded half up to one decimal place — that is, the share of accepted cubic feet that is upholstered, expressed on a 0.0 to 100.0 scale. Cubic feet are compared exactly throughout, with no tolerance.

The outcome of triage also decides the offer's own state, inside the same request rather than through a second call. If at least one piece was accepted, the offer moves `OFFERED → TRIAGED` and `declared_soft_ratio`, `accepted_item_count`, `rejected_item_count` and `bed_bug_risk_score` are all recomputed. If nothing was accepted, every piece is refused with a reason and the offer moves `OFFERED → REJECTED` instead, with `declared_soft_ratio` and `rejected_item_count` recomputed and `bed_bug_risk_score` set to 0.0. Both edges bump the offer's `version` by 1, and every piece walked by step two — accepted and refused alike — has its own `version` bumped as its `decision` is written. Triaging an offer that is not in `OFFERED` is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`.

**Booking the pickup** moves a triaged offer `TRIAGED → PICKUP_SCHEDULED` by naming the `truck_id` that will collect it, which writes that value onto the offer and bumps the offer's `version`. Two guards apply. First, the truck must not be under a bed-bug lockout covering the collection: its `bed_bug_lockout_until` must be null or no later than the offer's `pickup_window_start`, otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_BED_BUG_LOCKOUT`. Second, the truck's calendar must be free. Under half-open interval semantics, where an interval includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, the requested pickup window must not overlap any other donation offer in `PICKUP_SCHEDULED` on the same truck, nor any delivery run on that truck whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `CREWED` or `DISPATCHED`. Touching endpoints do not overlap: a window that begins at the exact instant another one ends is accepted. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Once booked, the truck's window is itself occupied for the purpose of every later overlap test.

### 3.5. Warehouse Intake, Grading, and the Bed-Bug Quarantine Protocol

When the truck comes back, a `WarehouseLead` records the inspection by supplying `inspections`, an array in which each element names the `item_id` of a piece, the `graded_condition` the lead assigns it — `A`, `B`, `C` or `D` — and a `live_pest_observed` boolean. The array must cover exactly the `ACCEPTED` pieces of that offer: no accepted piece may be omitted, no other piece may be included, and no `item_id` may appear twice; an array that fails this is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The inspection is only accepted while the offer is in `PICKUP_SCHEDULED`; from anywhere else it is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`. That coverage test is the one deliberate exception to the precedence of §2.9: although it answers with `ERR_VALIDATION`, it is evaluated *after* the state gate, so an inspection that names the wrong pieces of an offer that is also in the wrong state comes back `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`. The shape of the array — its elements, their fields and their types — is still checked ahead of everything, exactly as rule 3 requires.

Grading is what mints warehouse stock. Working through the accepted pieces in their own ascending `created_at` order — which is the order they were supplied in on the offer — every inspection graded `A`, `B` or `C` produces one `InventoryItem`, which copies its `category` and its `cubic_feet` from the originating piece, copies the assigned `graded_condition` into its own `condition`, and records the piece it came from in `donation_item_id`. A stock unit's `condition` is therefore always one of `A`, `B` or `C`: D-graded pieces never become stock at all. Instead, any inspection graded `D` writes that `D` into the piece's `graded_condition` all the same and then flips its `decision` to `REJECTED` with `reject_reason` `CONDITION_BELOW_MIN`, minting nothing; every graded piece has its `version` bumped whichever way it goes. Because those flips move pieces out of the accepted set, the intake also recomputes the offer's `accepted_item_count` and `rejected_item_count` from the pieces as they now stand, while `declared_soft_ratio` and `bed_bug_risk_score` — both of them triage numbers — are left exactly as triage wrote them. Every inspection also stamps the offer's `inspector_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, which is what the segregation-of-duties rule will later compare against.

Which branch the inspection takes depends on bed-bug risk. On the **clean branch** — no inspection reported `live_pest_observed` true *and* no accepted piece on the offer has `soft_surface` true — the offer moves `PICKUP_SCHEDULED → INSPECTED` and every minted unit is created directly in `status` `AVAILABLE` with its `available_since` set to the current instant and its `reserved_for_voucher_line_id` null. Because a unit's canonical starting state is `QUARANTINED`, this is the `QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE` edge applied inside the very request that mints the unit, and it bumps the unit's `version`.

On the **risk branch** — at least one inspection reported `live_pest_observed` true, *or* at least one accepted piece has `soft_surface` true, where "accepted" on both branches is read from the triage decision as it stood when the inspection arrived, so an upholstered piece that this very inspection is about to grade `D` still puts the offer on the risk branch — the offer moves `PICKUP_SCHEDULED → QUARANTINED`. Units are still minted for every `A`, `B` or `C` grade, but their landing state differs: a unit whose originating piece has `soft_surface` true *and* which comes from an offer where `live_pest_observed` fired anywhere is created directly in `status` `DISPOSED` — the `QUARANTINED → DISPOSED` edge, applied in the same request — while every other unit is created in `status` `QUARANTINED`, where it sits at `version` 1 with a null `available_since` — that stamp is written only by a move onto the floor, so a `DISPOSED` unit never receives one either. Both edges bump the unit's `version`. The offer's `quarantine_started_at` is set to the current instant, and `quarantine_release_due_at` is set to that instant plus 21 days when a live pest fired anywhere on the offer, or plus 14 days when the risk came only from upholstery. Both branches bump the offer's `version` by 1.

**The bed-bug contagion chain.** When and only when a live pest was observed, the intake fires a five-step cascade — last, once the pieces are graded, the units minted and the offer itself moved to `QUARANTINED` — whose steps are applied in order:

- **Step 1.** The collecting truck's `bed_bug_lockout_until` is stamped with the current instant plus 72 hours, and the truck's `version` is bumped by 1.
- **Step 2.** Every delivery run on that truck whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `CREWED` and whose `service_date` equals the UTC calendar date of the current instant is cancelled — the `PLANNED → CANCELLED` and `CREWED → CANCELLED` edges — each bumping that run's `version`. Runs on later dates that still fall inside the lockout window are deliberately **not** cancelled; they survive but cannot be dispatched until the lockout lapses, and an attempt to dispatch them earlier is refused with `ERR_TRUCK_BED_BUG_LOCKOUT`.
- **Step 3.** Every delivery stop on those cancelled runs whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, with its `chargeback_cents` staying at 0 and its `version` bumped.
- **Step 4.** Each of those cancelled stops releases its voucher back off the road: the voucher leaves `SCHEDULED` for `ALLOCATED` when every one of its lines is fully filled, or for `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` when at least one line is still short. The reserved stock is retained exactly as it was, because the run never dispatched and nothing was ever staged.
- **Step 5.** Every crew assignment on those cancelled runs whose `status` is `INVITED` moves `INVITED → RELEASED`, and every one whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` moves `CONFIRMED → RELEASED`, the latter also returning 4.0 hours to that volunteer's `assigned_hours_month`, floored at 0.0. Both edges bump the assignment's `version`.

**Releasing quarantine** is a separate act by a *different* `WarehouseLead`. Its three gates are read in the precedence order of §2.9 rather than in narrative order: first the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the offer's stored `inspector_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — a comparison skipped outright while that stamp is still null, and one that fires ahead of the state gate, so the inspecting lead is turned away with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even on an offer that was never quarantined; then the offer must be in `QUARANTINED`, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`; then the current instant must be at or after `quarantine_release_due_at`, otherwise `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_ELAPSED`. Release moves the offer `QUARANTINED → RELEASED` and moves every inventory unit sourced from that offer still sitting in `QUARANTINED` to `AVAILABLE`, restamping each unit's `available_since` to the release instant and bumping each unit's `version` along with the offer's. Units created straight into `DISPOSED` are never revived.

Warehouse stock may be read one unit at a time or listed with optional filters on `status`, `category`, `condition`, `donation_item_id` and `reserved_for_voucher_line_id`. A stock unit's `status` is one of `QUARANTINED`, `AVAILABLE`, `RESERVED`, `STAGED`, `DELIVERED` or `DISPOSED`, and the last two are terminal.

### 3.6. Allocation Rounds, Stock Selection, and the Unmet-Need Waitlist

Allocation is the act of a `WarehouseLead` walking a voucher's unfilled lines and reserving physical stock against them. It is deliberately repeatable — a top-up round against a half-filled voucher is a normal operation, not an error — which is exactly why the round carries `expected_voucher_version` and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VOUCHER_VERSION` when that value does not match the voucher's live `version`. A round is refused with `ERR_VOUCHER_INVALID_STATE` unless the voucher is in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, with `ERR_REFERRAL_EXPIRED` when the underlying referral has expired, and with `ERR_CLIENT_BED_BUG_BLOCK` when the household's `bed_bug_status` is `CONFIRMED`.

**The waterfall selector.** Within a round, lines are processed in a fixed order: first by category priority, where `BED` comes first, then `CRIB`, `LINENS`, `TABLE_SET`, `DRESSER`, `SOFA`, and `KITCHEN_KIT` last; and within a category, by ascending `created_at`. Only lines whose `status` is `OPEN`, `PARTIAL` or `WAITLISTED` participate. For each such line the shortfall is its `quantity_requested` minus its `quantity_allocated`; a line with no shortfall is skipped.

Candidate stock for a line is drawn from the whole warehouse, not from any single donation offer, and the eligibility filter is exact: a unit qualifies when its `status` is `AVAILABLE`, its `category` equals the line's `category`, and its `condition` is no worse than the line's `min_condition` — where `A` ranks 1, `B` ranks 2 and `C` ranks 3, and "no worse" means the unit's rank is less than or equal to the line's rank. Units sitting in `QUARANTINED` are invisible to allocation; a unit becomes selectable only once a clean intake or a quarantine release has moved it to `AVAILABLE`.

Qualifying units are then ranked so that the worst acceptable grade is spent first, keeping A-grade stock for households that demand it: units are ordered by condition rank descending, so `C` before `B` before `A`; then by ascending `available_since`, so the unit that has been on the floor longest goes first; and ties are broken by ascending `created_at`. The selector takes the first units in that order up to the line's shortfall. Each taken unit moves `AVAILABLE → RESERVED`, records the line in its `reserved_for_voucher_line_id`, raises the line's `quantity_allocated` by 1, and has its `version` bumped. Because `available_since` is the instant a unit *most recently* entered `AVAILABLE`, a unit that bounced back from a failed delivery goes to the back of its grade's queue.

Finding nothing is never an error. If units still remain unfilled on a line after selection, the line's `status` becomes `WAITLISTED` when nothing at all was allocated to it, or `PARTIAL` when some units were, and a `WaitlistEntry` is created or topped up for that line. A waitlist entry carries the `voucher_line_id` of the line, the `client_id` of the waiting household, a `category` and a `county` copied from the line and from the household respectively, a `quantity_unmet` equal to the units still short, a `priority_score` computed with the waitlist variant of the need score evaluated at that moment (tenure is zero at creation; the score is then recomputed with that same waitlist variant on every later top-up as well as by the operational cycle's scoring pass, always measuring tenure from the original `waiting_since`, and the entry's `version` moves only when the recomputed score or the `quantity_unmet` actually differs from what was stored), and a `waiting_since` that is set to the current instant on first creation and is **never** reset by a later top-up. Its `status` is one of `WAITING`, `PROMOTED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED`, and it starts at `WAITING`. If instead the line ends the round fully filled, its `status` becomes `FILLED` and any `WAITING` entry for that line moves `WAITING → PROMOTED` with `quantity_unmet` set to 0.

**What a round does to the voucher.** Five outcomes are possible, and the branch is decided by comparing total allocated units against total requested units after the selector has run. A round that yields nothing at all leaves the voucher `ISSUED → ISSUED`: every unfilled line becomes `WAITLISTED` with a `WAITING` entry recorded, `fill_ratio` stays 0.0, and because no stock is held the money fields stay at 0 and `fee_basis` stays null. This is a successful response, not an error — the shortfall is recorded on the waitlist instead. A round that fills some but not all requested units moves the voucher `ISSUED → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, reserving the selected units, recomputing every line's `quantity_allocated` and `status`, creating or topping up a waitlist entry for every line still short, recomputing `fill_ratio`, and recomputing the money fields with the provisional fee variant so that `fee_basis` becomes `PROVISIONAL`. A round that fills every line completely moves the voucher `ISSUED → ALLOCATED`, reserving the selected units, setting every line's `status` to `FILLED`, and recomputing `fill_ratio` and the provisional money fields. A top-up round against an already partly filled voucher that completes it moves it `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → ALLOCATED`, reserves the selected units, moves the matching waitlist entries to `PROMOTED`, and recomputes the provisional money fields. A top-up round that still leaves a shortfall keeps the voucher at `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, reserves what it found, reduces each affected entry's `quantity_unmet` by the number of units just filled, and recomputes the provisional money fields. All five edges bump the voucher's `version` by 1. The promotion pass of the operational cycle drives the same selector and can produce the same `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → ALLOCATED` and `WAITING → PROMOTED` movements without a warehouse lead being involved.

A voucher's `fill_ratio` is the sum of `quantity_allocated` across its lines divided by the sum of `quantity_requested` across its lines, carried to three decimals.

Waitlist entries leave `WAITING` by three routes. They move `WAITING → PROMOTED` when a promotion round or a top-up round reduces their `quantity_unmet` to 0. They move `WAITING → EXPIRED` when the owning voucher expires in step 2 of the operational cycle's expiry pass. They move `WAITING → CANCELLED` when the owning voucher is cancelled, or when the entry's household is placed `ON_HOLD` by the no-show contagion. Each of those edges bumps the entry's `version` by 1. Entries may be read individually or listed with optional filters on `voucher_line_id`, `client_id`, `status`, `category` and `county`.

**The unmet-need report.** The report that goes to the board reads only `WAITING` entries — the moment an entry is promoted, expires or is cancelled it leaves the report. It accepts two optional filters, `county` and `category`, each narrowing the source set to entries carrying that value. The surviving entries are grouped by the pair of `county` and `category`, one row per group that has at least one entry, ordered by county in the literal sequence `NORTH`, then `CENTRAL`, then `SOUTH`, and within a county by the same category priority the allocator uses, so `BED` first and `KITCHEN_KIT` last. Each row reports `unmet_quantity`, the sum of `quantity_unmet` across the group's entries; `households_waiting`, the number of distinct `client_id` values in the group; `max_wait_days`, the largest number of whole 24-hour days elapsed between an entry's `waiting_since` and now, rounded down; `top_priority_score`, the highest `priority_score` in the group; `requested_units`, the total demand ever raised in that county and category, being the sum of `quantity_requested` over every voucher line of that category whose household's `county` matches the group and whose `status` is not `CANCELLED`; `allocated_units`, the sum of `quantity_allocated` over those same lines; and `fill_rate_pct`, which is 100 multiplied by `allocated_units` divided by `requested_units`, rounded half up to one decimal place, or null when `requested_units` is zero. Alongside the rows the response carries `generated_at`, which is the logical clock's current instant and not wall time, the array `rows`, `total_unmet_quantity` as the sum of `unmet_quantity` across all rows, and `total_households_waiting` as the count of distinct `client_id` values across the entire filtered source set — which is not the sum of the per-row counts, because one household can wait in several categories. When nothing is waiting, the answer is an empty `rows` array with `total_unmet_quantity` and `total_households_waiting` both 0; it is never a 404 and never a row with zeroed fields. The report is not a list endpoint: it answers as a single object, its `meta` carries no pagination block, and `limit` and `offset` are neither honoured nor validated on it.

### 3.7. Fleet, Volunteers, Run Planning, and Dispatch

A `PlatformAdmin` adds a truck to the fleet by supplying a non-empty `label` that is unique across trucks, a duplicate being rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`, a `capacity_cubic_feet` strictly greater than 0, a `home_county`, and a `crew_minimum` of at least 1 and at most 4 naming the number of confirmed crew a run on that truck needs before it can leave the planning state. A new truck's `bed_bug_lockout_until` is null. A `PlatformAdmin` likewise registers a moving-labor volunteer with a non-empty `name`, a `county` they serve, and an optional `lift_certified` boolean, defaulting to false, that marks them cleared to drive the box truck and handle heavy lifts. A new volunteer opens with `reliability_score` at 100.0 and `assigned_hours_month` at 0.0.

A `DispatchPlanner` plans a run by naming the `truck_id`, a `service_date` as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, a road window given by an inclusive `window_start` and an exclusive `window_end` that must be strictly later than the start, the `county` the run will actually serve, and optionally a `rescheduled_from_run_id` pointing at a cancelled run this one replaces — null for an original run. Note that the run's own `county` is a routing fact and need not equal the truck's `home_county`; it is the county the truck actually drives to, and §3.9 explains why that distinction shows up on the invoice. A new run opens in `status` `PLANNED` with `load_cubic_feet` at 0.0, `confirmed_crew_count` at 0, `has_confirmed_driver` false and `stop_count` at 0. Runs may be listed with optional filters on `truck_id`, `status`, `service_date` and `rescheduled_from_run_id`, and a run's stops and its crew are each readable as their own collections filterable by `status`.

**Crew invitation.** A planner invites a volunteer onto a run by naming the `volunteer_id` and the `role_on_crew`, which is either `DRIVER` or `LIFTER`. The assignment is created in `status` `INVITED`. The run must first be in `PLANNED` or `CREWED`, an invitation onto a run anywhere else being refused with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`; beyond that state gate three guards apply, in the precedence order of §2.9. The volunteer must be rested: they must not already hold an assignment in `INVITED` or `CONFIRMED` on another run whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `CREWED` or `DISPATCHED` and whose window comes within one hour of the new run's window. The buffer is applied on a closed interval — a conflict exists when the new run's `window_start` is at or before the other run's `window_end` plus 60 minutes *and* the other run's `window_start` is at or before the new run's `window_end` plus 60 minutes — so a gap of exactly 60 minutes still conflicts while a gap of 61 minutes does not, and the rejection is `ERR_VOLUNTEER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The volunteer must not already hold an `INVITED` or `CONFIRMED` assignment on this same run, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREW_ASSIGNMENT`. And a `DRIVER` seat may only be offered to a volunteer whose `lift_certified` is true, otherwise `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

**Crew response.** A volunteer answers their own invitation — and only their own — by supplying a `response` of `CONFIRM`, `DECLINE` or `WITHDRAW`. Confirming requires the assignment to be `INVITED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the assignment's `volunteer_id`, and requires the run to be in `PLANNED`, a run standing anywhere else being refused with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`; it moves the assignment `INVITED → CONFIRMED`, adds 4.0 hours to that volunteer's `assigned_hours_month`, raises the run's `confirmed_crew_count` by 1, recomputes `has_confirmed_driver`, and bumps the assignment's `version`. Declining requires the assignment to be `INVITED` and the acting user to own it; it moves the assignment `INVITED → DECLINED` and bumps its `version`, with no effect on hours or crew counts and without recomputing or touching the run at all. Withdrawing requires the assignment to be `CONFIRMED`, requires the acting user to own it, and requires the run to be in `PLANNED` or `CREWED`, again `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` otherwise; it moves the assignment `CONFIRMED → RELEASED`, returns 4.0 hours from that volunteer's `assigned_hours_month` with the result floored at 0.0, lowers the run's `confirmed_crew_count` by 1, recomputes `has_confirmed_driver`, and bumps the assignment's `version`. A response that has no outgoing edge from the assignment's current state — confirming or declining something that is not `INVITED`, or withdrawing something that is not `CONFIRMED` — is rejected with `ERR_CREW_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

Crew responses also move the run itself, automatically. After a confirmation, a run in `PLANNED` moves `PLANNED → CREWED` when its `confirmed_crew_count` has reached at least the truck's `crew_minimum` *and* its `has_confirmed_driver` is true. After a withdrawal, a run in `CREWED` falls back `CREWED → PLANNED` when its `confirmed_crew_count` has dropped below the truck's `crew_minimum` or its `has_confirmed_driver` has become false. Crew churn can therefore promote and demote a run any number of times before it leaves the yard; each such movement bumps the run's `version`.

**Booking a stop.** A planner books a voucher onto a run by supplying the `voucher_id`, a routing `sequence` of at least 1 and at most 40 that must be unique among that run's `SCHEDULED` stops, and `expected_run_version`. The run must be in `PLANNED` or `CREWED`, and that gate is read before the voucher's own, so a booking that is wrong on both counts answers `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_VOUCHER_INVALID_STATE`. The household must be eligible: its `status` must be `ACTIVE`, its `bed_bug_status` must not be `CONFIRMED`, and its `delivery_ban_until` must be null or no later than the current instant, otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_DELIVERY_BANNED`. A voucher in `ALLOCATED` books straight through, moving `ALLOCATED → SCHEDULED`. A voucher in `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` may also be booked, moving `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → SCHEDULED`, but only when its `fill_ratio` is at least 0.5 — a half-empty truckload is worth a trip, a nearly empty one is not, and anything below that inclusive floor is rejected with `ERR_VOUCHER_UNDERFILLED`. A duplicate `sequence` is rejected with `ERR_STOP_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT`. Either edge creates a `DeliveryStop` in `status` `SCHEDULED` carrying the owning `run_id` and the served `voucher_id`, with `pest_observed_at_residence` false, `chargeback_cents` at 0 and `outcome_recorded_at` null; raises the run's `stop_count` by 1; recomputes the run's `load_cubic_feet`; and bumps both the voucher's and the run's `version` by 1.

**Load and crew feasibility.** A run's derived numbers are recomputed whenever a stop is booked, whenever a crew member responds, and whenever the bed-bug cascade fires, and they are read as preconditions at dispatch, where they are refreshed on the run before the gates are tested but without a `version` bump, so a dispatch that is refused leaves no trace on the run. `load_cubic_feet` is the sum of `cubic_feet` over every inventory unit whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `STAGED` and whose `reserved_for_voucher_line_id` points at a line of a voucher served by a `SCHEDULED` stop on this run — so a stop cancelled by the cascade drops out of the sum immediately. `confirmed_crew_count` is the number of crew assignments on the run whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`. `has_confirmed_driver` is true exactly when at least one of those confirmed assignments has `role_on_crew` `DRIVER`. `stop_count` is the number of stops on the run whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`. A run is feasible to dispatch when `stop_count` is at least 1, `load_cubic_feet` is no greater than the truck's `capacity_cubic_feet`, `confirmed_crew_count` is at least the truck's `crew_minimum`, and `has_confirmed_driver` is true. Both numeric comparisons are inclusive: a load exactly equal to capacity passes, and a confirmed crew exactly equal to the minimum passes.

**Dispatch.** Sending a run on the road moves it `CREWED → DISPATCHED`. The state gate accepts a run in `PLANNED` or `CREWED` and refuses a run in `DISPATCHED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`; a run carrying zero `SCHEDULED` stops is likewise refused with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. A run that passes the state gate while still in `PLANNED` then always fails the crew gate with `ERR_CREW_BELOW_MINIMUM`, which is precisely how that rejection becomes observable. Beyond those, the truck must not be locked out — `bed_bug_lockout_until` null or no later than the run's `window_start`, otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_BED_BUG_LOCKOUT` — and the load must fit, otherwise `ERR_TRUCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Dispatch has two cascading effects beyond the run's own state change. Every inventory unit sitting in `RESERVED` for a voucher with a `SCHEDULED` stop on this run moves `RESERVED → STAGED`, bumping each unit's `version`. And every crew assignment on the run still sitting in `INVITED` at that moment moves `INVITED → NO_SHOW`, deducting 15.0 points from that volunteer's `reliability_score` with the result floored at 0.0 and bumping each assignment's `version`. Volunteers who never answered are marked absent, not quietly forgotten.

### 3.8. Delivery Outcomes, Chargebacks, and the No-Show Contagion Chain

A `DispatchPlanner` closes a stop by supplying an `outcome` of `DELIVERED`, `NO_SHOW` or `REFUSED`, plus an optional `pest_observed_at_residence` boolean that defaults to false and records that the crew saw live bed-bug evidence at the residence. Every outcome requires the stop to be in `SCHEDULED` and its run to be in `DISPATCHED`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_STOP_INVALID_STATE`. Each outcome sets `outcome_recorded_at` to the current instant, records the supplied `pest_observed_at_residence` on the stop whichever of the three outcomes was chosen, and bumps the stop's `version` by 1. Within a failed outcome the effects land in a pinned order: the chargeback is computed and posted to the agency ledger, the staged stock reverts, the voucher comes back off the road, the no-show chain fires, the stop itself is written, then the pest cascade, then the agency standing recompute — so the stop just closed is already inside its own standing window — and only then the run's derived numbers and its possible move to `COMPLETED`. In all three cases the run's `stop_count` drops by 1, and when the stop just closed was the run's last `SCHEDULED` stop the run moves `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED` automatically, bumping the run's `version`.

**A successful delivery** moves the stop `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED` and leaves its `chargeback_cents` at 0. Every inventory unit held for that voucher moves `STAGED → DELIVERED`, bumping each unit's `version`. The voucher moves `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED`, which recomputes its money fields with the final fee variant and sets `fee_basis` to `FINAL`. The household's `lifetime_vouchers_delivered` rises by 1. The owning referral moves `APPROVED → FULFILLED`, but only if it is in fact still sitting in `APPROVED`; a referral that has moved on is left alone. The voucher's `version` is bumped.

**A no-show** — nobody home — moves the stop `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW` and bills the agency. Every staged unit reverts `STAGED → RESERVED`, keeping its `reserved_for_voucher_line_id` and leaving the line's `quantity_allocated` untouched, and its `available_since` is deliberately **not** restamped: the furniture rides back to the warehouse still earmarked for that household. The voucher moves `SCHEDULED → ALLOCATED` when every line is still fully filled, or `SCHEDULED → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` when at least one line is short, so a half-filled voucher never silently claims to be complete. Either way the voucher becomes bookable onto a new run with no further allocation round, and this pair of edges may fire any number of times; `fill_ratio` is unchanged because the stock never left the voucher.

**A refusal at the door** moves the stop `SCHEDULED → REFUSED` and behaves like a no-show in every respect but two: the chargeback is computed at the lower refusal rate, and the household's `no_show_strikes` is **not** incremented, because a refusal is not an absence. The same stock reversion applies — staged units return to `RESERVED`, still earmarked — and the voucher makes the same choice between `ALLOCATED` and `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` on the same test. Both failure outcomes recompute the agency's `standing`.

**The chargeback.** For a failed stop the charge is assembled line by line. The rate is 0.45 for a no-show and 0.25 for a refusal. Each voucher line of the stop's voucher whose `status` is `OPEN`, `PARTIAL`, `FILLED` or `WAITLISTED` is charged; lines in `CANCELLED` are charged nothing. Walking those lines in ascending `created_at` order, each line's charge is its category's base amount multiplied by its `quantity_allocated` — read as it stood *before* this request touched any stock — multiplied by the rate, and that product is rounded half up to whole cents *at the line*. The category base amounts are 2500 cents for `BED`, 3000 for `SOFA`, 1500 for `CRIB`, 1150 for `TABLE_SET`, 950 for `DRESSER`, 650 for `KITCHEN_KIT` and 250 for `LINENS`. The stop's `chargeback_cents` is then the sum of those already-rounded line charges, and that same total is added to the `no_show_credits_owed_cents` of the agency reached from the stop through its voucher and that voucher's referral. Rounding the untouched total instead of the individual lines is wrong and will differ by a cent or more.

**The no-show contagion chain.** A no-show — and only a no-show — fires a cascade in this order. Step one raises the household's `no_show_strikes` by 1. Step two fires whenever that raised count stands at 2 or more, so the third and every later no-show re-runs it: the household's `status` becomes `ON_HOLD` and its `delivery_ban_until` is set to the current instant plus 90 days. Step three fires only because the household has just gone `ON_HOLD`: every referral of that household in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED` is denied with the auto-denial note described in §3.2, and every `WAITING` waitlist entry of that household moves to `CANCELLED`. A household under a ban cannot be booked onto a run at all, and only the operational cycle's reinstatement pass will lift it.

**Pest at the residence.** Independently of the outcome chosen, if `pest_observed_at_residence` is true on a `DELIVERED` or a `REFUSED` stop, the residence itself is quarantined: the household's `bed_bug_status` becomes `CONFIRMED`, its `bed_bug_confirmed_at` is stamped with the current instant, its `status` becomes `ON_HOLD`, every `WAITING` waitlist entry of that household moves to `CANCELLED`, and the truck that made the call has its `bed_bug_lockout_until` set to the current instant plus 72 hours. Nothing beyond that list moves: the residence cascade leaves `no_show_strikes` and `delivery_ban_until` untouched and, unlike the no-show chain, does not auto-deny the household's `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED` referrals even though the household has just been placed `ON_HOLD`. Nor does it fire on a `NO_SHOW` stop, which records the flag on the stop and stops there. A `CONFIRMED` household is blocked from referral submission, referral approval, voucher issuance, allocation rounds and stop booking alike, all with `ERR_CLIENT_BED_BUG_BLOCK`, until the operational cycle's remediation pass has moved it to `REMEDIATED` 60 days later.

### 3.9. Delivery Fees, Subsidy Tiers, and Billing Settlement

The household's delivery fee is computed twice over the life of a voucher, once as a quote and once as an invoice, by one calculation with two variants. The **provisional** variant runs on every allocation round and on every operational-cycle promotion that touches the voucher — every round bar the zero-yield one that leaves a voucher standing in `ISSUED`, which writes no money and no `fee_basis` at all — and writes `fee_basis` `PROVISIONAL`. The **final** variant runs when a stop closes as `DELIVERED`, and writes `fee_basis` `FINAL`. Only two inputs differ between them.

The first differing input is the service county. The provisional quote uses the household's own `county` — where the family lives, which is what the case manager was quoted. The final invoice uses the `county` of the delivery run that actually served the stop — where the truck actually drove. Identical stock delivered out of a differently-countied run therefore produces a different `gross_fee_cents`, `subsidy_cents` and `client_due_cents` than the quote did.

The second differing input is the stock filter. The stock basis is always the set of inventory units whose `reserved_for_voucher_line_id` points at a line of *this* voucher; the provisional variant counts those whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `STAGED`, while the final variant counts only those whose `status` is `DELIVERED`. Lines in `CANCELLED` contribute nothing, because their units have already gone back to `AVAILABLE` and fail the filter either way.

From that basis the gross fee is assembled in a strict order. First, a base amount is taken from the service county: 4000 cents for `NORTH`, 3000 cents for `CENTRAL`, and 5000 cents for `SOUTH`. Second, a bulk charge is added: the total `cubic_feet` of the basis is divided by 50, that quotient is rounded **up** to a whole number, and the result is multiplied by 1200 cents — so a basis of zero cubic feet adds nothing, a basis of 50 cubic feet adds one increment, and a basis of 51 adds two. Third, a lift surcharge of 1500 cents is added when the basis contains at least one unit whose `category` is `BED` or `SOFA`, and nothing otherwise. Fourth, the sum of those three components is capped: `gross_fee_cents` is the lesser of that sum and 9000 cents.

The subsidy percentage is then assembled, also in a strict order. It starts from the household's size tier: 0.50 for a `household_size` of 2 or fewer, 0.70 for a `household_size` of 3 or 4, and 0.90 for a `household_size` of 5 or more. If the household's `has_infant` is true, 0.10 is added, with the result capped at 1.00. If the referring agency's `standing` is `PROBATION`, 0.20 is then subtracted, with the result floored at 0.00. If instead the agency's `standing` is `SUSPENDED`, the percentage is set outright to 0.00 regardless of everything above it. The agency and the household are both reached from the voucher through its referral, and both are read at the instant the calculation runs.

Money is then settled with exactly one rounding step: `subsidy_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents, and `client_due_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` minus `subsidy_cents`, never re-rounded.

**The billing freeze.** `gross_fee_cents`, `subsidy_cents`, `client_due_cents` and `fee_basis` are written only while the voucher's `fee_status` is still `PENDING`. Once the billing axis has left `PENDING` — to `WAIVED`, `INVOICED`, `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` — those four fields are frozen: later allocation rounds, waitlist promotions and even the final variant still perform all of their state changes, but they must not overwrite the four money fields. This is the only coupling between the voucher's two axes.

**Settlement.** A `PlatformAdmin` advances the billing axis by supplying an `action` of `WAIVE`, `INVOICE`, `RECORD_PAYMENT` or `WRITE_OFF`, with an `amount_cents` accompanying a payment record. That field is optional in the schema but required in fact when the `action` is `RECORD_PAYMENT`: its absence is an `ERR_VALIDATION` raised with the rest of rule 3, and therefore ahead of the path lookup, while on the other three actions an `amount_cents` supplied anyway is accepted and ignored. Waiving requires `fee_status` `PENDING` and requires the voucher's fulfilment `status` not to be `CANCELLED`; it moves `PENDING → WAIVED`, sets `subsidy_cents` equal to `gross_fee_cents`, sets `client_due_cents` to 0, leaves `gross_fee_cents`, `fee_basis` and `amount_paid_cents` exactly as they stand, and bumps the voucher's `version`. Note that waiving is otherwise independent of the fulfilment axis — a voucher may be waived while it is still `ISSUED`. Invoicing is the single fee edge that reads the fulfilment axis: it requires `fee_status` `PENDING` *and* requires the voucher's `status` to be `DELIVERED`, so that the final fee variant has already run, and it moves `PENDING → INVOICED` and bumps `version`. Recording a payment requires `fee_status` `INVOICED` and requires the supplied `amount_cents` to equal `client_due_cents` exactly, otherwise `ERR_FEE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; it moves `INVOICED → PAID`, writes the supplied amount into `amount_paid_cents`, and bumps `version`. Writing off requires `fee_status` `INVOICED`; it moves `INVOICED → WRITTEN_OFF`, sets `client_due_cents` to 0, leaves `amount_paid_cents` as it stands, and bumps `version`. Any settlement action with no outgoing edge from the voucher's current `fee_status` — including an invoice attempt while the voucher is not `DELIVERED` — is rejected with `ERR_FEE_STATE_INVALID`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that mint a brand-new stand-alone resource — registering an agency, a truck, a volunteer, a household, a referral, a voucher, a donation offer or a delivery run, and adding a stop or a crew invitation to a run — answer with `201 Created`. Every other successful operation, including all reads, all state-advancing actions on an existing resource, the logical-clock move and the operational cycle, answers with `200 OK`. Where an operation moves several entities at once, the body carries the single entity the operation is named for: the booked `DeliveryStop` for a stop booking, the invited `CrewAssignment` for a crew invitation and the answering one for a crew response, the `Voucher` for an issuance — without its lines, which are read back through their own collection — and for an allocation round, a cancellation and a fee settlement, the `DonationOffer` for triage, pickup booking, intake and quarantine release, the `DeliveryRun` for a dispatch, the `DeliveryStop` for an outcome, and the `Referral` for a submission or a review.
