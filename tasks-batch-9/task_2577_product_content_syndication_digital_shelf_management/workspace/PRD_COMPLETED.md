# Product Content Syndication & Digital Shelf API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A brand's digital shelf team lives with a structural problem: the product information it authors internally is never the product information a shopper sees. Between the two sit a dozen retailer accounts, each with its own attribute taxonomy expectations, its own character ceilings, its own image minimums, its own approval gate for enhanced content, and its own feed intake calendar with a finite number of slots. Content that is complete by house standards is routinely rejected by one retailer over a description that runs eight characters long, while a competitor's listing outranks it because two secondary images were never approved. The team's day is therefore not "write content" but "prove content is acceptable to this retailer, get it transmitted, get it confirmed, and then watch what the retailer's page actually renders."

This document specifies the API for a platform that closes that loop. It carries a product information repository whose revisions are version-controlled and governed, a per-retailer requirement layer that turns each retailer's content contract into machine-checkable completeness scoring, a syndication engine that reserves feed slots and windows on the retailer's intake calendar and tracks acceptance, partial acceptance and per-row rejection, a rich-media and enhanced-content deployment pipeline that respects retailer approval flags and placement calendars, and a digital shelf monitoring surface that ingests crawl observations on search rank, availability, content accuracy and ratings, raises alerts, and routes them to the account team that owns the relationship.

Two design commitments run through everything below and are worth stating up front. First, **the same content payload is scored twice under two different inclusion sets** — a *declared* view that answers "what has the author claimed?" and a *certified* view that answers "what would this retailer actually accept?" — and only the certified view gates distribution. Second, **feed slots and version pin references are conserved quantities**: every forward action that consumes one has a compensating action that returns it, and the ledgers must show zero drift across a full cycle. The API is mounted under `/digital-shelf`, is fully deterministic, and derives every timestamp from an explicitly settable logical clock so that rush-window pricing and window-overlap arithmetic are reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity semantics, concurrency control, rounding discipline, and the order in which the platform evaluates a request before it is allowed to mutate anything.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the seven roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other check runs. A header holding nothing but whitespace counts as empty; otherwise both header values are stripped of surrounding whitespace before use, so the role is matched — case-sensitively, with no aliases or abbreviations accepted — against the seven names after stripping, and the stripped `X-User-ID` is what gets recorded wherever provenance is demanded. If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ShelfAdmin`, `ContentAuthor`, `LegalReviewer`, `BrandGovernanceApprover`, `SyndicationOperator`, `RetailerIntegration` and `AccountTeamLead`.

- A `ShelfAdmin` is the digital shelf platform operator. It owns the retailer roster, the global attribute taxonomy, the crawl ingestion feed, the logical clock, and the sole authority to waive a blocking feed error.
- A `ContentAuthor` registers products, drafts content revisions and attribute values, registers rich media, opens retailer listings, raises and submits change requests, and deploys and withdraws enhanced content.
- A `LegalReviewer` decides the `LEGAL` review track of a change request and nothing else.
- A `BrandGovernanceApprover` decides the `BRAND` review track of a change request and nothing else.
- A `SyndicationOperator` queues, transmits, requeues and cancels syndication jobs, and resolves rejection errors by fixing content.
- A `RetailerIntegration` account is the retailer's own integration principal. It confirms or rejects transmitted feeds and approves rich media and enhanced-content modules for its own listings.
- An `AccountTeamLead` owns shelf-alert follow-up and the suspend-or-clear decision on a listing.

Read operations under `/digital-shelf` are open to all seven roles. Every write is scoped to the role that owns the operation, as enumerated in §4.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `RetailerIntegration`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Retailer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Retailer` compare the resource's retailer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a retailer feed response is scoped by the `retailer_id` of the job's listing, an enhanced-content decision by the `retailer_id` of the deployment's listing, and a media decision by requiring that a `RetailerListing` exists for the pair formed by the asset's `product_id` and `X-User-ID`. A violation of any of these is `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

For the other six roles — `ShelfAdmin`, `ContentAuthor`, `LegalReviewer`, `BrandGovernanceApprover`, `SyndicationOperator` and `AccountTeamLead` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands provenance (as `author_user_id` on a content revision and on a change request, as `legal_reviewer_user_id` or `brand_reviewer_user_id` on a decided review track, as `resolved_by_user_id` on a closed rejection error, as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on an acknowledged shelf alert, and as `account_team_user_id` and `assigned_account_team_user_id` on listings and the alerts they raise) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

**Segregation of duties.** Review authority is deliberately split. The acting role must match the track it decides: a `LegalReviewer` may only submit a decision on track `LEGAL`, and a `BrandGovernanceApprover` may only submit a decision on track `BRAND`; the mismatch is `ERR_TRACK_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Beyond that, two independent person-level rules apply, both reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): the `X-User-ID` recorded as the change request's `author_user_id` may never decide either track, and the `X-User-ID` deciding one track may never equal the `X-User-ID` already recorded as the other track's reviewer. Waiver authority is split the same way: a `SyndicationOperator` may repair any rejection error and may waive a `WARNING` row, but a waiver against a row whose `severity` is `BLOCKING` is reserved to `ShelfAdmin` and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_WAIVE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. This one field is the sole exception to §2.4's logical-clock rule: it is taken from real wall-clock time rather than from the settable logical clock, so it does not move when a `ShelfAdmin` repositions the clock and it never equals a persisted `created_at`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three failures are answered by the transport layer rather than by any endpoint's own pipeline, and because no view function runs they are reported in this same envelope *before* the authentication check of §2.8 step 1, regardless of what headers the request carried: a path that matches no route in §4 returns `error_id` `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, a known path called with a method §4 does not define returns `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405, and a request body that is not parseable JSON returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. Any unforeseen server-side failure returns `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500. Trailing slashes are not significant: `/digital-shelf/products` and `/digital-shelf/products/` reach the same operation.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); both parameters must be integers, and a value that is not an integer at all — an empty string, a decimal, or any other non-numeric text — draws the same `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Only a parameter that is absent from the query string falls back to its default; supplying it obliges it to be valid.

Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order unless a specific endpoint's narrative states otherwise, and because the logical clock is settable many rows routinely share a `created_at`: ties are broken by insertion order, so the row created earlier in the request sequence sorts first. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit` (the effective page size applied), `offset` (the effective starting position applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page — that is, when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is still less than `total_count`). An `offset` past the end of the collection is not an error; it simply returns an empty `data` array with the true `total_count`.

Every filter query parameter named in §3 and §4 is matched by exact string equality against the stored field and is never itself validated against the enum it draws from: a filter value that is not a member of the enum — or is any other unrecognised string — is accepted and matches nothing, yielding an empty page rather than an error. Supplying several filters on one read narrows the result by all of them together. Query parameters the endpoint does not define are ignored.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a random version-4 UUID string in the canonical hyphenated form, with no type prefix; clients never supply it, and ids are not sequential, so nothing may be inferred about creation order from an `id`. Resources also carry `created_at` and `updated_at` as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits — both read from the logical clock, so a freshly created row has `updated_at` exactly equal to `created_at` — and a `version` integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Any client-supplied value for a computed or system-owned field is silently ignored rather than rejected. Refreshing a purely derived figure does not count as a mutation: the single-retailer read of §3.1 recomputes `feed_slots_reserved` from the ledger before rendering the row and leaves both `updated_at` and `version` untouched, and the same holds for the score recomputes described in §2.10.

Serialisation is by fixed field set, not by presence: every response object for a given entity kind carries the same keys in the same order on every call, and a field that has no value is rendered as JSON `null` rather than omitted. Server-side bookkeeping such as the insertion sequence used for tie-breaking is never exposed.

`ShelfObservation` is the single exception. It is an append-only event row: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and it has **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version`. There is no update or delete operation whose output is a `ShelfObservation`. Its `created_at` exists specifically because two calculations — the shelf projection and the conversion-lift analysis — break ties between observations sharing the same `observed_at` by insertion order.

All time in the platform comes from a deterministic logical clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never advances on its own — it holds whatever instant it was last set to for as many requests as follow, which is what makes two rows created in separate requests share a `created_at`. A `ShelfAdmin` may reposition it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, mandatory and given as a non-empty string; a string that does not parse as an ISO 8601 instant is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), not `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. The clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards, and setting it to the instant it already holds is a successful no-op. The response is HTTP 200 whose `data` carries the single field `current_utc`, the newly set instant rendered with three fractional digits; no entity is mutated by the call, and in particular no score, ledger or alert is re-evaluated as a consequence of time having moved. Every subsequent system stamp — `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `transmitted_at`, `responded_at`, `retailer_decision_at`, `resolved_at`, `last_pinned_at`, `last_review_at`, and the observation's own `created_at` — derives from the clock's current value, as do the rush-window classification used in feed pricing and the interval arithmetic used for window contention.

### 2.5. Flat Collections and Left-to-Right Reference Resolution

The entity graph here is genuinely a graph, not a tree: a `RetailerListing` is the junction of a product and a retailer, a `RetailerRequirement` is the junction of a retailer and a taxonomy attribute key, a content revision points at the revision it supersedes, and an enhanced-content deployment joins a listing, a change request and a media asset simultaneously. Because no resource is reachable through exactly one ownership chain, the URL space is a set of flat collections under `/digital-shelf` rather than a deep nested tree, and cross-references travel in request bodies.

Where a path does nest — a retailer's requirements, a revision's attribute values, a listing's observations, a job's rejection errors — path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and each such child records the identifier of the parent that produced it — a rejection error, for instance, records the `syndication_job_id` of the transmission whose response raised it, which is why it can be read back through that job's own collection. The first path segment that cannot be resolved produces its own not-found error before any deeper segment is examined, and before any body-carried reference is looked up. Path resolution does **not**, however, precede the body-shape check: the §2.8 pipeline is authoritative, and its step 8 sits ahead of every existence step, so a write whose body is malformed reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the identifier in its path names nothing — the path row is only fetched once the body has been found well-formed. The one class of exception is an operation whose step-4 retailer-scope test is itself defined against a field of the path row: there the row has to be fetched before step 4 can be evaluated at all, and therefore before step 8, so its own not-found error outranks a malformed body. The media retailer decision of §3.3 is the case in point, and so are the other two step-4 operations: the retailer feed response of §3.7 and the retailer deployment decision of §3.8 each fetch the job or the deployment their path names, and through it the listing whose `retailer_id` step 4 compares against `X-User-ID`, before any body is parsed — so an unknown job id or deployment id outranks a missing or malformed `outcome` or `decision`, while a foreign retailer's `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` outranks both. A path identifier that is empty or is not a string is treated exactly like one that names no row, producing that segment's not-found error. Body-carried references are resolved in the fixed order given in §2.8 so that a request naming three nonexistent rows always reports the same one.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write operations reach into a parent aggregate whose state is shared by many children, and each of them requires the client to prove it has observed that parent's current state. In all three cases the field is a mandatory input, not an optional one, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

- Queueing a syndication job must carry `expected_retailer_version` matching the current `version` of the retailer that owns the target listing. Feed slots are a shared pool, so an earlier queue, cancel or invalidation cascade may already have moved `feed_slots_reserved` since the client last read the retailer; a second queue attempt would otherwise be perfectly legal and capacity might well admit it, which is exactly why the stale-version check is the only thing that distinguishes it. The requeue operation carries the same field, but there it is **optional**: supplied, it is checked identically and a mismatch is the same `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; omitted — or sent as an explicit `null` — it disables the concurrency check for that call entirely, and the requeue proceeds against whatever `version` the retailer now holds.
- Recording a review-track decision must carry `expected_product_version` matching the current `version` of the change request's product. The `LEGAL` and `BRAND` tracks are independently decidable, so a second decision on the other track is legitimate business flow; what this check catches is a reviewer deciding from a product view that predates the other track's decision, the pin cascade, or an alert recompute.
- Creating an enhanced-content deployment must carry `expected_retailer_listing_version` matching the current `version` of the target `RetailerListing`. Two deployments on non-intersecting windows are both legal, so nothing in the window arithmetic rejects the second writer; the stale version is what reveals that the writer did not see the placement calendar as it now stands.

Adjusting a retailer's requirement envelope also carries `expected_retailer_version` and is subject to the same rejection. No endpoint other than these five takes a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.7. Score Precision and Rounding Discipline

All four percentage-shaped figures in the system — the two completeness variants, the listing readiness score, the product shelf health index, and the conversion lift — are stored and returned with exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up. They are carried as exact decimals internally and emitted as JSON **numbers**, never as quoted strings, which means the 2-decimal quantisation governs the value and not its printed form: a score quantised to `85.00` serialises as `85.0` and one quantised to `0.00` serialises as `0.0`, because JSON number rendering drops insignificant trailing zeros. A consumer comparing scores must compare numerically rather than by string. Monetary output is different: the syndication service fee is an integer number of cents and is never fractional.

Decimal-valued *inputs* are a separate matter and are not quantised on the way in. A weight supplied as `1.005` or `0.333` is accepted and stored exactly as supplied, and is echoed back unchanged; the platform neither rounds it to 2 decimal places nor rejects it for carrying more precision. Where §3 describes a configuration decimal as "expressed to 2 decimal places" it is describing the intended usage, not a validated constraint — the only input constraints actually enforced on those fields are their sign and range bounds.

Two rounding disciplines deserve to be called out because getting them wrong changes the last cent or the last hundredth:

- **Round once, at the end, unless told otherwise.** For completeness and readiness, numerator and denominator are carried as exact values and the half-up rounding to 2 decimal places is applied a single time, to the final percentage. No intermediate component is rounded first.
- **The shelf health index rounds twice, in a pinned order.** Each qualifying retailer's subscore is rounded half up to 2 decimal places **first**; the already-rounded subscores are then averaged; and the average is rounded half up to 2 decimal places again. Averaging the unrounded subscores and rounding once at the end is incorrect and will produce a different hundredth.

The feed fee applies half-up rounding to whole cents at two specific points — once when the volume discount is applied and once when the rush surcharge amount is computed — and never to the raw multiplication product. The two ledger quantities (reserved feed slots and version pin references) are exact integers with no rounding at all.

### 2.8. Request Evaluation Order

The platform evaluates a request as a fixed pipeline. Order matters because several checks can be simultaneously true, and the response must be deterministic: the first check that fails produces the error, and no later check is consulted. The pipeline runs in exactly this sequence.

1. Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. On a review-track decision, the acting role matches the requested `track` — otherwise `ERR_TRACK_ROLE_MISMATCH`. This step and step 6 are both evaluated only where `track` is present and is literally `LEGAL` or `BRAND`: a `track` that is absent, null, non-string or any other word cannot be matched against a role or against the other track's reviewer, so both steps are skipped and the request falls through to step 8's `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. When the acting role is `RetailerIntegration`, `X-User-ID` equals the retailer that owns the target resource — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Applies to exactly three operations: the retailer feed response on a syndication job, the retailer decision on an enhanced-content deployment, and the retailer decision on a media asset.
5. On a review-track decision, `X-User-ID` differs from the change request's `author_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. On a review-track decision, `X-User-ID` differs from the other track's already-recorded reviewer — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
7. `limit` and `offset` are in range — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Applies to every collection read that accepts them.
8. Request-body shape: every required field present, types matching their declarations, every enum value a member of its declared set, and no decimal negative where the field forbids it — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `slot_count` below 1 falls here too. Applies to every create, submit-style and patch operation. Values supplied for computed or system-owned fields are ignored rather than rejected. An operation that declares no body field at all sits outside this step altogether and never parses a body: transmitting a syndication job, cancelling one, and acknowledging a shelf alert accept anything sent with them — a JSON array, a bare string, even unparseable bytes — and ignore it rather than reporting `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Four conventions apply throughout this step. A request that carries no body at all, or a body that is empty or whitespace-only, is read as the empty object `{}`, so an operation with required fields reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` while an operation whose every field is optional succeeds on an empty body. A body that parses to anything other than a JSON object — an array, a bare string, a number — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An explicit JSON `null` is indistinguishable from omission: for a required field it is a shape failure, and for an optional field it selects the declared default. Type checking is strict — a boolean is never accepted where an integer is declared, a numeric string is never accepted where a number is declared, and an integer field rejects a fractional value — while an integer *is* accepted where a decimal is declared. String bounds are measured in characters on the value exactly as supplied, before any stripping the operation later performs. A uniqueness collision on a client-supplied natural key also lands here rather than under a dedicated 409, as noted at each such field in §3. The `message` of an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` names the offending field, but clients must key on `error_id` alone.
9. `window_end` is strictly after `window_start`, and both bounds parse as ISO 8601 UTC instants — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. Applies to queueing a syndication job, requeueing one, and creating an enhanced-content deployment. The split against step 8 is precise: a bound that is required but absent, or is present as a non-string, is a step-8 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a bound that is a string but does not parse as an instant is this step's `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. A bound carrying a non-UTC numeric offset such as `+02:00` parses and is converted to the equivalent UTC instant before any comparison; a bound with no zone designator at all is read as UTC.
10. The referenced retailer exists — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND`.
11. The referenced product exists — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`.
12. The referenced content revision exists, whether it is named as a target, as a superseded predecessor, or as the payload of a transmission — otherwise `ERR_CONTENT_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`.
13. The referenced retailer listing exists — otherwise `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`. The same code covers the case where an operation implies a listing for a given product-and-retailer pair and no such listing exists.
14. The referenced media asset exists — otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to the media retailer decision, the single-asset read, and deployment creation.
15. The referenced change request exists — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every change-request operation and to deployment creation.
16. The referenced syndication job exists — otherwise `ERR_SYNDICATION_JOB_NOT_FOUND`.
17. The referenced rejection error exists — otherwise `ERR_REJECTION_ERROR_NOT_FOUND`.
18. The referenced deployment exists — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
19. The referenced shelf alert exists — otherwise `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`.
20. Every supplied `attribute_key` resolves to a row in the global taxonomy — otherwise `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 422). Applies to declaring a retailer requirement, writing an attribute value, and any `attribute_key` carried inside a retailer feed response's error rows. Wherever a request supplies an `attribute_key` for lookup it is first shape-checked at step 8 as a string of 1 to 40 characters with no pattern requirement, so a key that is empty or longer than 40 characters reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and never reaches this step; the taxonomy match itself is exact and case-sensitive.
21. No listing already exists for the product-and-retailer pair being opened — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING`.
22. No requirement row already exists for the retailer-and-attribute-key pair being declared — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT`.
23. The supplied concurrency field equals the current `version` of its named parent — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. This position is where the concurrency check genuinely runs, and it is early: it precedes every status, state, scope, threshold and capacity guard numbered below it, so a queue attempt that is simultaneously stale and over feed capacity reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, and a deployment creation that is simultaneously stale and overlapping reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION` too. Where the concurrency field is optional — requeue alone, per §2.6 — an absent field skips this step and nothing else about the ordering changes.
24. The retailer's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Applies to six operations: opening a listing, submitting a change request, queueing a job, transmitting a job, requeueing a job, and creating a deployment.
25. The target content revision's `state` is `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. Applies to three operations: writing an attribute value, raising a change request, and submitting one.
26. The change request's `state` is `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`. Applies to submission.
27. The change request's `state` is `IN_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_IN_REVIEW`. Applies to a review-track decision.
28. The named review track is still `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_TRACK_ALREADY_DECIDED`.
29. The job's current `state` admits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`. Transmission requires `QUEUED`; a retailer feed response requires `TRANSMITTED`; requeue requires `REJECTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`; cancellation requires `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`.
30. The rejection error's `state` is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_REJECTION_ERROR_ALREADY_CLOSED`.
31. Every rejection error of the job whose `severity` is `BLOCKING` has reached `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED` — otherwise `ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED`. Applies to requeue.
32. The deployment is in an actionable state — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A retailer decision requires `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL`; a withdrawal requires `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`.
33. The alert is not already `RESOLVED`, and acknowledgement additionally requires that it is still `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.
34. The listing's `shelf_state` is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED` — otherwise `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED`. Applies to queueing a job, requeueing one, and creating a deployment.
35. The stripped attribute value contains no prohibited claim token — otherwise `ERR_PROHIBITED_CLAIM` (HTTP 422). Applies to writing an attribute value.
36. The job's content revision is `PINNED` and its `product_id` equals the listing's `product_id` — otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PINNED` (HTTP 422). Applies to queueing and requeueing.
37. The cited change request's `state` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422). Applies to deployment creation.
38. The cited change request's retailer and product both match the target listing, and the cited media asset's product matches it too — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
39. The cited media asset's `approval_state` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422).
40. The stripped attribute value's `char_count` is at most the global taxonomy ceiling — otherwise `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_LENGTH_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Applies to writing an attribute value.
41. The count of approved image-role assets on the product is at least the retailer's `min_image_count` — otherwise `ERR_IMAGE_COUNT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). Applies to deployment creation.
42. The listing's certified completeness score is at least 85.00 — otherwise `ERR_COMPLETENESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422). Applies to queueing and requeueing.
43. The new deployment window does not intersect an active deployment window on the same listing — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
44. The retailer's concurrent-window allowance is not exceeded — otherwise `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Applies to queueing and requeueing.
45. Reserved slots plus the requested slots do not exceed the retailer's capacity — otherwise `ERR_FEED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Applies to queueing and requeueing.
46. A waiver against a `BLOCKING` row is being issued by a `ShelfAdmin` — otherwise `ERR_WAIVE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to resolving a rejection error.

Note the deliberate consequence of steps 43 through 45 sitting last: a request that is simultaneously over capacity and in window conflict reports the window conflict, because window contention is evaluated before slot capacity.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities: Feed Slots and Pin References

Two integers in this system are ledgers, not counters, and both are recomputed from their source rows rather than incremented in place.

`Retailer.feed_slots_reserved` is the sum of `slot_count` over every syndication job belonging to any listing of that retailer whose `state` is `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`. Those are the only two holding states; a job that has reached `ACCEPTED`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` or `INVALIDATED` releases its slots entirely. The ledger is recomputed on every job state write — creation, cancellation, a retailer feed response, a requeue, and the system's invalidation cascade — and the retailer's `version` increments on each of those writes. Transmission is deliberately absent from that list: `QUEUED → TRANSMITTED` moves a job from one holding state to the other, so the sum does not change, and the retailer row is not written at all — neither its `feed_slots_reserved`, nor its `updated_at`, nor its `version` moves when a job is transmitted. The invariant that matters: queueing a job and then cancelling it, or queueing and then invalidating it, or queueing and then receiving any retailer response, must leave `feed_slots_reserved` at exactly its pre-queue value. The `version` moves; the sum does not drift.

`ContentVersion.pin_count` is the number of references currently held on a revision, and it counts two different kinds of reference: the number of retailer listings whose `pinned_content_version_id` equals the revision's `id`, plus the number of enhanced-content deployments in state `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE` whose governing change request's `target_content_version_id` equals that same `id`. Deployments in `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` contribute nothing. It is recomputed on the change-request approval join, on deployment creation, on a retailer deployment decision, and on withdrawal. Its compensating pairs: creating a deployment and then withdrawing it returns the count to its pre-creation value, as does creating one and having the retailer reject it; and pinning a new revision onto a listing costs the previously pinned revision exactly the one listing reference it held. Globally, the sum of `pin_count` across all revisions always equals the number of listings with a non-null `pinned_content_version_id` plus the number of deployments in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations mutate more than one aggregate. Where that happens, the cascade is specified as an ordered sequence of hops, each hop caused by the write the previous hop performed, and each hop must complete within the same request. Two cascades are four hops deep — the change-request approval join and the rejection outcome of a retailer feed response — and both are described hop by hop in §3.

Two general rules govern them. First, whenever a cascade writes a **state or reference** field on an entity, that entity's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1, and this applies to entities the client did not name in the request: a review decision advances the product's `version`, a job state change that alters the slot ledger's holding set advances the retailer's `version` — transmission, which moves a job between the two holding states, is the one job state write that leaves the retailer row untouched — and a deployment write advances the listing's `version`. This rule is deliberately confined to state and reference writes. Refreshing a **derived** field does not advance a `version` or an `updated_at` at all: the two completeness variants, the readiness score, `open_blocking_error_count`, the projected snapshot fields, a revision's `pin_count`, a product's `shelf_health_index`, `conversion_lift_pct`, `open_alert_count` and `governance_flag`, and a retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` are all recomputed silently in place. The practical consequences are worth naming, because they are easy to get backwards: opening a content revision, writing an attribute value, declaring a retailer requirement or adjusting a retailer's envelope all change scores on every affected listing while leaving each of those listings' `version` and `updated_at` exactly where they were, and they change `shelf_health_index` on every affected product while leaving that product's `version` alone. Where the narrative in §3 explicitly says a `version` increments — the retailer's on an envelope adjustment or a job state write, the listing's on a state-dimension move or a deployment write, the product's on a review decision, an observation ingest, an alert closure or a shelf-status change — that increment happens because of the state write, once per request, not once per derived field touched. A listing's `syndication_state` or `shelf_state` being re-derived to the value it already holds is not a write at all, so it advances nothing. Second, where a cascade processes a set of rows, the processing order is ascending `created_at` with insertion order breaking ties, so that the outcome of a multi-row cascade is reproducible.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Retailer Roster and the Attribute Taxonomy

Everything downstream depends on configuration a `ShelfAdmin` puts in place first. Onboarding a retailer account records its `name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters, and its `retailer_code`, a short identifier printed on feed transmissions that must be unique across all retailers and between 3 and 8 characters long using only uppercase letters and digits. The uniqueness collision on `retailer_code` is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — there is no dedicated duplicate-retailer code — and because it is a step-8 body-shape failure it is detected before any of the envelope defaults below take effect. The comparison is exact and case-sensitive, which in practice means literal equality since the field's own pattern already forces uppercase. The rest of the payload is the retailer's **content requirements envelope**, and each part of it is optional at onboarding because each has a default. `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and is one of `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `OFFBOARDED`. `min_image_count`, the minimum number of approved image-role assets this retailer demands per product, defaults to 1 and may be zero or any larger whole number. `feed_slot_capacity`, the total number of feed slots the retailer will hold reserved at any one moment across all of its listings, defaults to 10 and must be at least 1. `max_concurrent_feed_windows`, the number of a retailer's in-flight transmission windows that may cover a single instant, defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. `enhanced_content_requires_approval` defaults to true; when true a newly created enhanced-content deployment begins life awaiting a retailer decision, and when false it begins life already published. A freshly onboarded retailer has `feed_slots_reserved` initialised to 0 and `version` initialised to 1, and the reserved figure is thereafter always between 0 and `feed_slot_capacity` inclusive.

Retailers can be listed with an optional `status` filter alongside the usual page controls, and a single retailer read always reflects its live reserved-slot figure rather than a cached one — recomputed from the job ledger at read time, without advancing the retailer's `version` or `updated_at`, so the read is idempotent. The list read does not perform that refresh; it renders each retailer's stored figure, which the ledger discipline of §2.9 keeps correct in any case.

Adjusting a retailer's envelope after onboarding is the one configuration write that is concurrency-guarded, because the same `version` field that a caller would use to prove it has seen the envelope is also the field that carries the feed-slot ledger. The adjustment accepts `expected_retailer_version` — a mandatory integer; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at step 8, before the retailer is even looked up — plus any subset of `status`, `min_image_count`, `feed_slot_capacity`, `max_concurrent_feed_windows` and `enhanced_content_requires_approval`. Each supplied field is bound-checked exactly as at onboarding, and each omitted field is left at its current stored value rather than reset to its onboarding default. The subset may be empty: an adjustment that carries nothing but a matching `expected_retailer_version` is a legitimate, successful write with all the consequences below, so it is the cheapest way to advance the retailer's `version`. Its consequences reach further than the retailer row: `feed_slots_reserved` is refreshed from the ledger, the retailer's `version` increments by 1, then `completeness_score_declared`, `completeness_score_certified` and `readiness_score` are recomputed for every listing belonging to that retailer — because `min_image_count` feeds the image component of the certified variant — and then `shelf_health_index` is recomputed once for every distinct product touched by those listings, in the order those products were first encountered walking the retailer's listings in ascending `created_at`. `feed_slot_capacity` may be lowered below the currently reserved figure; nothing rejects that and no job is released, so `feed_slots_reserved` may temporarily exceed `feed_slot_capacity` until reservations drain, and the next queue attempt simply fails its capacity gate. Moving `status` away from `ACTIVE` likewise leaves existing jobs, listings and deployments untouched; it only closes the gates §2.8 step 24 guards. The response is the updated retailer row.

The **global attribute taxonomy** is the vocabulary all content is written against. Adding an attribute records its `attribute_key`, which must be unique across the taxonomy and written in snake_case between 2 and 40 characters — concretely, one or more segments of lowercase letters and digits joined by single underscores, so no uppercase letter, no leading or trailing underscore, no doubled underscore and no other punctuation, and a duplicate key is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than any dedicated duplicate code; its `data_type`, one of `STRING`, `INTEGER`, `DECIMAL` or `ENUM`; its `taxonomy_group`, one of `CORE`, `MARKETING`, `VARIANT` or `COMPLIANCE`; its `max_chars`, the house-wide character ceiling of at least 1 that acts as a hard write-time limit on any value authored against the key; and its `base_weight`, a mandatory completeness weight strictly greater than zero, nominally expressed to 2 decimal places but accepted and stored at whatever precision is supplied per §2.7, used whenever a retailer's own requirement row supplies no weight of its own. A `base_weight` of exactly zero or a negative one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An optional `is_global_required` flag defaults to false and marks house-standard attributes for documentation purposes only — on its own it has no effect on any score, and in particular it neither enters a completeness denominator nor creates a requirement row for any retailer. The taxonomy can be listed with an optional `taxonomy_group` filter; there is no single-attribute read, and a taxonomy row, once added, is never updated or deleted.

Each retailer's content contract is then expressed as a set of requirement rows, one per attribute key. A requirement row is scoped to a retailer — the row echoes that owner as `retailer_id`, taken from the path segment and never from the body — and carries `attribute_key`, which must name an existing taxonomy attribute or the write fails with `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN`; a `requirement_level` of `REQUIRED`, `RECOMMENDED` or `OPTIONAL`; an optional `max_chars_override`, which must be at least 1 when supplied and defaults to null meaning "fall back to the taxonomy's `max_chars`"; and an optional `weight_override`, which must be strictly greater than zero when supplied — nominally to 2 decimal places, but accepted at whatever precision is given per §2.7 — and defaults to null meaning "fall back to the taxonomy's `base_weight`". An override may be *larger* than the value it replaces; nothing constrains `max_chars_override` to sit at or below the taxonomy's `max_chars`, so a retailer may declare a ceiling more generous than the house one, in which case the write-time house limit of §3.2 is still the binding constraint on authors. Note the ordering these two guards sit in: the retailer must resolve (step 10) before the attribute key does (step 20), and the duplicate check (step 22) runs after both. A retailer may hold at most one requirement row per attribute key; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT`. There is no operation that updates or removes a requirement row once declared, and no single-requirement read. Declaring a requirement recomputes both completeness variants and the readiness score of every listing of that retailer, and then the shelf health index of every product those listings touch, exactly as an envelope adjustment does — and, exactly as there, without advancing any listing's or product's `version`. The declaring retailer's own `version` is **not** advanced either: unlike the envelope adjustment, this write is not concurrency-guarded and takes no `expected_retailer_version`. A retailer's requirement rows can be read back as a list, in ascending `created_at` order.

The distinction between the three requirement levels is the single most consequential piece of taxonomy configuration. `REQUIRED` and `RECOMMENDED` rows both enter the completeness denominator and can both earn credit in the numerator; they differ only in operational emphasis, not in arithmetic. `OPTIONAL` rows are excluded from the numerator **and** from the denominator, so declaring an attribute optional does not depress a score — it removes the attribute from the calculation entirely.

### 3.2. Product Registration and the Content Revision Chain

A `ContentAuthor` registers a product with four author-supplied identifiers, all four mandatory: `sku`, unique across all products and between 3 and 40 characters, whose uniqueness collision — like `retailer_code`'s — is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) and is compared exactly and case-sensitively, so `ABC-1` and `abc-1` are two different SKUs; `gtin`, the trade item number printed on the pack, between 8 and 14 digits, meaning a string of nothing but decimal digits of that length, not a number, and leading zeros are preserved as written; `category_code`, the merchandising category, and `brand_code`, the owning brand inside the portfolio, each any non-empty string with no upper length bound and no format or referential constraint — neither is validated against any registry, and neither need be unique. Everything else on a product is rolled up by the platform. There is no operation that edits a product's four identifiers after registration, and none that deletes a product. A new product begins with `status` `DRAFT`, `shelf_health_index` 0.00, `conversion_lift_pct` 0.00, `open_alert_count` 0, `governance_flag` `NONE`, and both `last_pinned_at` and `last_review_at` null. `status` is one of only two values, `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, and it flips to `ACTIVE` the moment any content revision of the product first becomes `PINNED`; it never flips back. Products can be listed with optional `status` and `governance_flag` filters.

Content is authored as an explicit revision chain rather than by editing a product in place. Opening a revision requires only the `product_id`; the author may additionally name `supersedes_content_version_id` to record which existing revision this one is drafted to replace (left null on the first revision of a product), and may declare `image_count`, the number of images the author claims the revision ships, which defaults to 0 and may be zero or larger. When `supersedes_content_version_id` is supplied it must resolve to an existing revision or the write fails with `ERR_CONTENT_VERSION_NOT_FOUND` — checked after the product, per §2.8 steps 11 and 12 — but nothing more is demanded of it: it need not belong to the same product, need not be in any particular state, and need not be unpointed-at, so several revisions may legitimately declare the same predecessor and a chain may fork. The field is a recorded assertion only; supplying it supersedes nothing and mutates no other row, since the actual `PINNED → SUPERSEDED` transition belongs exclusively to the approval join of §3.5. The platform stamps `revision_no` as one more than the number of revisions the product already has at creation time — so numbering starts at 1 and is unique within a product, which means "the latest revision" is never ambiguous — sets `state` to `DRAFT`, records `author_user_id` from the calling `X-User-ID`, and leaves `pin_count` at 0. Opening a revision immediately recomputes `completeness_score_declared` for every listing of the product, and *only* that figure: the certified variant and the readiness score are deliberately left alone, because a brand-new draft cannot be any listing's certified source. Since no listing `version` moves for a derived write, opening a revision leaves every listing row's `version` and `updated_at` untouched. Revisions can be listed filtered by `product_id`, by `supersedes_content_version_id` to walk the chain forward, or by `state`. Revisions are never edited or deleted through their own endpoints; the only writable thing about a revision after creation is its attribute-value collection, and its `state`, `pin_count` and `version` move only as consequences described elsewhere.

A revision's lifecycle has four states — `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `PINNED` and `SUPERSEDED` — and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: a superseded revision can never be pinned again and can never be transmitted again. Movement between them is never requested directly; it is always a consequence of governance, described in §3.5.

Attribute values are written one key at a time onto a revision, and only while that revision is `DRAFT`. Writing against a revision in `IN_REVIEW`, `PINNED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT` — this is the content freeze that makes a review meaningful. The payload is an `attribute_key`, which must resolve in the global taxonomy, and a `value` of between 1 and 2000 characters, both mandatory. Note the order these interact in: the revision must resolve first (`ERR_CONTENT_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`), then the attribute key (`ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN`, step 20), and only then is the revision's `DRAFT` state tested (step 25) — so writing an unknown key against a `PINNED` revision reports the unknown key, not the frozen state. The 1-to-2000 bound is measured on the string exactly as submitted, *before* stripping, which has a deliberate consequence: a value consisting only of whitespace clears the shape check, clears both content screens, and is stored with a `char_count` of 0 — a stored row that earns no completeness credit under either variant, since both require `char_count` at least 1. Writes are upserts: at most one row exists per attribute key per revision, and a second write on the same key replaces the existing row rather than creating a second one, keeping that row's `id` and `created_at` while refreshing its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Both the inserting write and the replacing write answer with HTTP 201 and the attribute-value row, so 201 here does not imply a row was newly brought into existence. The platform derives two fields from the payload, and stores `value` verbatim as submitted — surrounding whitespace and all — so the echoed `value` may be longer than the `char_count` beside it. `char_count` is the length of `value` after leading and trailing whitespace has been stripped; interior whitespace is never collapsed. `type_valid` is true when that same stripped value parses as the `data_type` declared for the key in the taxonomy, and false otherwise; a row with `type_valid` false can never earn certified completeness credit, though it is still stored. What "parses as" means is fixed per data type: an `INTEGER` key demands nothing but decimal digits with an optional leading `+` or `-` sign, so `42` and `-7` pass while `4.0`, `1e3` and `12abc` do not; a `DECIMAL` key additionally admits a single decimal point with digits on either side or on just one of them, so `3`, `3.5`, `.5` and `3.` pass while exponent notation does not; and a `STRING` or `ENUM` key imposes no format test at all, so any value passes — an `ENUM` key carries no member list anywhere in the taxonomy for a value to be checked against. A stripped value that is empty is `type_valid` false whatever the data type, which is why the whitespace-only write above stores `type_valid` false alongside its `char_count` of 0. There is no operation that deletes an attribute value, so a key once written can only be overwritten, never withdrawn from the revision.

Two content screens run at write time, and both are keyed on the stripped value. A prohibited-claim screen performs a case-insensitive substring match for the tokens *cures*, *fda approved* and *100% guaranteed*; a match anywhere in the stripped value is rejected with `ERR_PROHIBITED_CLAIM`. A length screen then requires `char_count` to be at most the `max_chars` declared on the taxonomy attribute, rejecting with `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_LENGTH_EXCEEDED` otherwise. Note carefully what the length screen does **not** do: a retailer's `max_chars_override` is not a write-time limit. An author is free to write a value that exceeds a particular retailer's ceiling; the consequence is that the value silently withholds certified credit for that retailer, not that the write fails. Because prohibited claims are caught here, at authoring time, no additional content screen runs later when the revision is submitted for review. A successful attribute-value write recomputes `completeness_score_declared`, `completeness_score_certified` and `readiness_score` for every listing of the revision's product, and then the product's `shelf_health_index`. A revision's attribute values can be read back as a list.

### 3.3. Rich Media Assets and Retailer Media Decisions

Rich media is registered against a product, not against a revision, because the same hero shot serves every revision and every retailer. A `ContentAuthor` registers an asset with its `product_id`, its `asset_role` — one of `HERO_IMAGE`, `SECONDARY_IMAGE`, `VIDEO` or `A_PLUS_MODULE` — and its `source_uri`, the brand-side URI of the master file, between 1 and 500 characters. Only `HERO_IMAGE` and `SECONDARY_IMAGE` count as image roles for the purposes of the certified completeness image component and the retailer's `min_image_count` gate; `VIDEO` and `A_PLUS_MODULE` assets are deployable as enhanced content but never satisfy an image minimum. A newly registered asset has `approval_state` `PENDING`, `approving_retailer_id` null and `decided_at` null. Assets can be listed filtered by `product_id`, by `approval_state`, or by `approving_retailer_id`.

Each asset then needs a retailer's blessing before it counts for anything. A `RetailerIntegration` principal supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` against a `PENDING` asset, moving `approval_state` to `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` respectively, recording `approving_retailer_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and stamping `decided_at` from the logical clock. `REJECTED` is terminal for an asset. Two guards apply. First, a `RetailerListing` must already exist for the pair formed by the asset's `product_id` and the acting `X-User-ID` — a retailer that does not carry the product cannot express an opinion on its media, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; this is step 4 of §2.8 and so runs before the request body is examined. Because that pair test is defined against the asset's own `product_id`, this one operation must fetch the asset named in its path before anything else can be evaluated — ahead of both the scope test and the body-shape check — so a decision naming an unknown asset id reports `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` even when the acting retailer carries no listing whatsoever and even when the body is itself malformed. Second, the asset must still be `PENDING`: an asset whose `approval_state` has already moved to `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` cannot be decided again, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409). That second guard is a state check on the target row, so it is evaluated after the body-shape check of §2.8 step 8, alongside the other state guards of steps 24 through 34.

The approval branch and the rejection branch differ in exactly one respect, and it is worth stating both sides explicitly. On `APPROVE`, the newly approved asset raises the approved image-role count for the product, so `completeness_score_certified` and `readiness_score` of the listing joining that product to the acting retailer are recomputed on the spot — and nothing else is: that listing's `completeness_score_declared` is left where it stood, no other retailer's listing of the same product is refreshed even though its own certified image component has moved, and the product's `shelf_health_index` is not recomputed either. The asset row's own `version` increments by 1 on either branch, because the approval-state write is a state write. On `REJECT`, `approving_retailer_id` and `decided_at` are recorded exactly as on approval, but no certified score changes, because a rejected asset never counts toward anything.

### 3.4. Retailer Listings and the Two Completeness Variants

A `RetailerListing` is the junction row that says "this product is carried by this retailer". A `ContentAuthor` opens one by naming `product_id` and `retailer_id`, and may optionally name `account_team_user_id`, the opaque identifier of the `AccountTeamLead` who owns follow-up for this relationship — any non-empty string, with no upper length bound and no check that it names anything, so an empty string is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` while an explicit `null` selects the default; it defaults to null, and whatever it holds is copied onto every shelf alert the listing later raises, whatever that alert's type. At most one listing may exist per product-and-retailer pair, and a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING`. A listing may only be opened against a retailer whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; against a `PAUSED` or `OFFBOARDED` retailer the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A new listing starts with `syndication_state` `NOT_SYNDICATED`, `shelf_state` `OK`, `pinned_content_version_id` null, `in_stock` true, `open_blocking_error_count` 0, and `search_rank_latest`, `content_accuracy_pct` and `rating_avg` all null because nothing has been observed yet. Both completeness variants and the readiness score are computed immediately at creation, which in practice yields 0.00 for the certified variant while nothing is pinned. Listings can be listed filtered by either side of the junction — `product_id` or `retailer_id` — or by `pinned_content_version_id` to find every listing certified on a given revision, or by either state dimension via `syndication_state` and `shelf_state`. Unlike the single-retailer read of §3.1, neither the single-listing read nor the list read refreshes anything: both render the three stored scores and the stored snapshot fields as the last write left them.

The two state dimensions on a listing are genuinely independent and never write each other. `syndication_state` moves through `NOT_SYNDICATED`, `PENDING`, `LIVE` and `ERROR_HOLD` and is driven entirely by the syndication job lifecycle and by the closure of rejection errors. `shelf_state` moves through `OK`, `OOS_WATCH`, `UNAUTHORIZED` and `SUSPENDED` and is driven entirely by crawl observations and by account-team decisions. A listing can perfectly well be `LIVE` on the feed dimension and `UNAUTHORIZED` on the shelf dimension at the same time.

**The two completeness variants.** The heart of the platform is that the same content is scored twice, against the same retailer requirement set but with two different inclusion tests. Both variants begin by assembling the scoring set: every requirement row belonging to the listing's retailer whose `requirement_level` is `REQUIRED` or `RECOMMENDED`, with `OPTIONAL` rows discarded. Each row in that set contributes a weight, which is its `weight_override` when that is present and the taxonomy's `base_weight` for its attribute key otherwise. The denominator both variants divide by is the sum of those weights plus a fixed **image weight of 20**, which is always present in the denominator whether or not the retailer demands any images at all, so the denominator itself is never zero. What does short-circuit the calculation is an empty scoring set: when the retailer has declared no `REQUIRED` or `RECOMMENDED` requirement row at all, both variants are 0.00 and no further work is done — the image weight standing alone earns nothing, however many approved images or claimed images there are — while the diagnostic read described below still reports `requirement_weight_total` as the bare image weight of 20.00 and reports both image components as 0.00.

Where the variants part company is the revision each reads and the test each applies:

- The **declared** variant is the author-facing view. Its source is the content revision of the listing's product with the highest `revision_no`, regardless of what state that revision is in — a `DRAFT`, an `IN_REVIEW`, a `PINNED` or even a `SUPERSEDED` revision is equally eligible, and because `revision_no` is unique within a product there is never a tie to break. When the product has no revision at all, the declared score is 0.00. A requirement row earns full credit for its weight when the source revision carries an attribute value on that key whose `char_count` is at least 1 — presence alone, nothing more. Every retailer character ceiling is ignored, and the type check is ignored.
- The **certified** variant is the retailer-facing view, and it is the one that gates distribution. Its source is strictly the revision the listing has pinned, named by `pinned_content_version_id`; when nothing is pinned the certified score is 0.00. A requirement row earns credit only when the pinned revision carries an attribute value on that key with `char_count` at least 1, **and** that row's `type_valid` is true, **and** its `char_count` is at most the effective retailer ceiling for the key — the requirement row's `max_chars_override` when that is present, and the taxonomy attribute's `max_chars` otherwise.

The image weight is the one component that earns partial credit, and both variants share the same shape for it while counting images differently. The declared variant's image count is simply the source revision's claimed `image_count`. The certified variant's image count is the number of media assets on the listing's product whose `asset_role` is `HERO_IMAGE` or `SECONDARY_IMAGE` and whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED`. Given a count, the image component is the full image weight of 20 when the retailer's `min_image_count` is zero; otherwise it is 20 multiplied by the smaller of 1 and the ratio of the count to `min_image_count`. So three approved images against a minimum of three earn the whole 20, two against a minimum of three earn two thirds of it, and five against a minimum of three still earn only 20 — the ratio is capped at 1 and never rewards overshoot.

Each variant's numerator is then the sum, over the scoring set, of each row's weight multiplied by that variant's credit for the row, plus that variant's image component. The score is 100 multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Numerator and denominator are carried exactly; nothing is rounded before the final division. The declared result is stored as `completeness_score_declared` and the certified result as `completeness_score_certified`, and both are recomputed on every write that can change any of their inputs: opening a revision (which per §3.2 refreshes the declared variant alone), writing an attribute value, declaring a retailer requirement, adjusting a retailer's envelope, a retailer's media decision (which per §3.3 refreshes the certified variant alone, on the acting retailer's listing alone), and the pin write performed by a change-request approval.

A dedicated diagnostic read puts the two variants side by side for one listing and explains the gap. It echoes the `retailer_listing_id`, reports `declared_score` and `certified_score`, names the revision each variant read as `declared_source_content_version_id` and `certified_source_content_version_id` (either of which is null when that variant had no source), reports `requirement_weight_total` as the shared denominator described above — the summed weights of the scoring set plus the image weight of 20 — and reports `declared_image_component` and `certified_image_component` as the two image contributions actually credited. Those three figures are rendered to 2 decimal places rounded half up, exactly as the two scores are, so a scoring set whose weights sum to `0.333` reports a `requirement_weight_total` of 20.33 and a two-thirds image credit reports 13.33. The read is a pure computation over current state: it writes neither score back onto the listing, refreshes nothing, and moves no `version`. It also reports two diagnostic arrays, both computed against the certified source and both sorted in ascending alphabetical order of attribute key: `missing_required_keys` lists the keys of `REQUIRED` requirement rows for which the pinned revision carries no attribute value with at least one character, and `over_limit_keys` lists the keys in the scoring set whose pinned value exceeds the effective retailer ceiling. When nothing is pinned, `missing_required_keys` contains every `REQUIRED` key of the retailer and `over_limit_keys` is empty.

**Readiness.** Alongside the two completeness variants each listing carries a `readiness_score`, the single number a feed operator looks at before transmitting. It blends 60 percent of the listing's stored, already-quantised `completeness_score_certified` with 40 percent of a floored accuracy figure, then subtracts a penalty for unresolved blocking feed errors and adds a bonus for live enhanced content, clamps the exact blended result to the range 0 to 100, and rounds that clamped value half up to 2 decimal places in a single step. The accuracy figure is the listing's `content_accuracy_pct`, treated as 0.00 while no observation has ever been recorded; and it is floored — meaning it is replaced by the smaller of itself and 60.00 — whenever the listing's `shelf_state` is anything other than `OK`. A listing sitting on `OOS_WATCH`, `UNAUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED` therefore cannot present better than 60.00 accuracy to the blend no matter what the crawler saw. The penalty tiers keyed on `open_blocking_error_count` are given in §3.7 and the enhanced-content bonus in §3.8. Readiness is recomputed on every write that touches any of those inputs: a completeness recompute, an observation ingest, a shelf-status change, the closure of a rejection error, and any deployment state change.

### 3.5. Content Change Governance: Two Review Tracks, One Join, Four Cascade Hops

No revision ever becomes certified for a retailer by an author's own act. A `ContentAuthor` raises a governed change request naming the `product_id`, the single `retailer_id` whose listing the request will certify, the `target_content_version_id` of the revision proposed for certification, and a `rationale` of between 1 and 500 characters recorded for audit. The target revision must be in `DRAFT` at this point or the request is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. Nothing else about the three references is checked here: they are resolved in the §2.8 order — retailer, then product, then revision — and then recorded exactly as supplied, so the target revision need not belong to the named product, no listing need yet exist for the product-and-retailer pair, and the retailer need not be `ACTIVE`. All of that is deferred to submission. A newly raised request sits in `state` `DRAFT` with `legal_review_state` and `brand_review_state` both `PENDING`, `legal_reviewer_user_id` and `brand_reviewer_user_id` both null, `submitted_at` and `decided_at` both null, and `author_user_id` recorded from the calling `X-User-ID` — the value that will later bar this user from deciding either of its two tracks. Change requests can be listed filtered by `product_id`, by `retailer_id`, by `target_content_version_id`, or by `state`.

Submission is the author's last act on the request. Its four preconditions are evaluated strictly in the order fixed by §2.8, which is the order they are listed here: a listing must already exist for the request's product-and-retailer pair (otherwise `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, §2.8 step 13) — there is no point certifying content for a retailer that does not carry the product; the named retailer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`, step 24); the target revision must still be `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`, step 25); and the request itself must still be `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`, step 26). The consequence of step 25 preceding step 26 is worth spelling out: because a first submission simultaneously freezes the target revision, resubmitting a request that is already `IN_REVIEW` reports `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT` rather than `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`, and step 26 is reached only where the revision is `DRAFT` while the request is not — as it is for a request the reject branch has left in `REJECTED` with its revision reopened. Submission moves the request `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and **forks** the review into two independently decidable dimensions by leaving both `legal_review_state` and `brand_review_state` at `PENDING`. It simultaneously moves the target revision `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, which is the content freeze: from this moment any further attribute-value write against that revision is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. No fresh content screen runs at submission, because prohibited claims were already rejected at attribute-value write time. Submission declares no body fields of its own and never inspects the request body at all, so nothing a client sends alongside it can be rejected; it answers HTTP 200 with the updated change request, and both the request row and the frozen revision row have their `version` incremented by 1.

A review decision names the `track` — `LEGAL` or `BRAND` — the `decision` — `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` — and `expected_product_version`. Six conditions guard it, in the order given in §2.8: the acting role must own the named track, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `author_user_id`, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from whichever reviewer identifier the other track has already recorded, `expected_product_version` must equal the product's current `version`, the request must be `IN_REVIEW`, and the named track must still be `PENDING` (otherwise `ERR_TRACK_ALREADY_DECIDED`). The position of the concurrency check in that list is not incidental: it is §2.8 step 23 and therefore sits ahead of both state checks, so a decision that is simultaneously stale and aimed at a request no longer `IN_REVIEW`, or at a track already decided, reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Whichever way the decision goes, the acting identity is recorded as `legal_reviewer_user_id` or `brand_reviewer_user_id` according to the track, the product's `last_review_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the product's `version` increments by 1.

**The self-loop.** When the decision is `APPROVED` and the other track is still `PENDING`, the request's aggregate `state` stays `IN_REVIEW`. Only the named dimension moves to `APPROVED`. Nothing is pinned, nothing is superseded, no job is invalidated, and no revision's `pin_count` changes. This is the case operators most often misread: the request looks decided from the perspective of one reviewer and is not decided at all.

**The join.** When a decision is `APPROVED` and, after applying it, both `legal_review_state` and `brand_review_state` are `APPROVED`, the request advances `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED` — a terminal state — and a four-hop cascade fires inside the same request.

- *Hop 1 — pin.* The request's `state` becomes `APPROVED` and its `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The target revision transitions `IN_REVIEW → PINNED`. The listing joining the request's product and retailer has its `pinned_content_version_id` repointed to that revision; that is a reference write, so the listing's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1. The product's `last_pinned_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `status` is set to `ACTIVE` — written unconditionally, so a product already `ACTIVE` simply stays there, and the product's single `version` increment for the request is the one every review decision performs.
- *Hop 2 — supersede, caused by hop 1's pin write.* Whatever revision that listing's `pinned_content_version_id` previously named, if any, transitions `PINNED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `pin_count` recomputed downward, losing exactly the one listing reference it held. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal; that revision can never be pinned or transmitted again.
- *Hop 3 — invalidate, caused by hop 2's supersede write.* Every syndication job still in `QUEUED` whose `content_version_id` is the newly superseded revision transitions `QUEUED → INVALIDATED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Jobs already in `TRANSMITTED` are deliberately **not** invalidated — the retailer already holds the payload.
- *Hop 4 — release, caused by hop 3's invalidation writes.* Each invalidated job releases its `slot_count`, so the owning retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed from the ledger and the retailer's `version` increments by 1 — once per invalidated job, so a retailer that owns two of them sees its `version` advance twice inside the one request. The listings those jobs belonged to are then settled once each, in the order they were first encountered walking the invalidated jobs: each has its `open_blocking_error_count` and its `readiness_score` recomputed, and where a listing is left with no job in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED` and an `open_blocking_error_count` of zero, its `syndication_state` returns to `NOT_SYNDICATED` — a listing left with a positive blocking count keeps whatever `syndication_state` it already had rather than being forced onto `ERROR_HOLD` here. Their completeness variants are not recomputed, since nothing the invalidation touched feeds them. Because a job's revision always belongs to its listing's product, this reach is confined to listings of the same product, but it does extend to that product's listings at other retailers.

Alongside the four hops, the join recomputes `completeness_score_certified`, `completeness_score_declared` and `readiness_score` on the one listing whose pin it repointed — not on every listing the cascade touched — recomputes the newly pinned revision's `pin_count` upward, recomputes the product's `shelf_health_index`, and — like every review decision — stamps `last_review_at` and increments the product's `version`.

**The reject branch.** When a decision is `REJECTED` on either track, the outcome is deliberately asymmetric to the join, and it short-circuits: the named dimension becomes `REJECTED` and the request's aggregate `state` becomes `REJECTED` immediately, **even while the other track is still `PENDING`**. `decided_at` is stamped. The target revision transitions `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, reopening it for editing. Nothing else moves: the listing's `pinned_content_version_id` is left exactly as it was, no revision is superseded, no job is invalidated, and no `pin_count` changes on any revision. `REJECTED` is terminal for the request itself, but not for the revision — the very same revision can be proposed again through a brand-new change request, which is why the revision's own lifecycle is a cycle of `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` rather than a one-way path.

### 3.6. Feed Syndication: Queueing, Window Contention, Slots and Fees

A `SyndicationOperator` queues a transmission by naming the `retailer_listing_id` it targets, the `content_version_id` whose content will be sent, the `slot_count` of feed rows the transmission occupies (an integer from 1 to 10 inclusive), the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the transmission window, and `expected_retailer_version`. The new job begins in `state` `QUEUED` with `attempt_no` 1, `transmitted_at` and `responded_at` both null, and it drives the target listing's `syndication_state` from `NOT_SYNDICATED` to `PENDING` — but only from there. The write is guarded on the listing's current value, so a listing already sitting on `PENDING`, on `LIVE` or on `ERROR_HOLD` when a further job is queued against it is left exactly where it is, and because nothing was written its `updated_at` and `version` do not move either. (Requeue, by contrast, drives the listing to `PENDING` from whatever value it holds; see §3.7.) A job's `state` is one of `QUEUED`, `TRANSMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `INVALIDATED` or `CANCELLED`, of which `ACCEPTED`, `INVALIDATED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Jobs can be listed filtered by `retailer_listing_id`, by `content_version_id`, or by `state`.

Queueing is the most heavily guarded write in the platform, and its guards run in the order fixed by §2.8. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. The retailer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The listing's `shelf_state` must be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED` — a listing under unauthorized-seller enforcement is not a listing you push more content to. The named revision must be `PINNED` and its `product_id` must equal the listing's `product_id`, otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PINNED`; only certified content goes on a feed, and a `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `SUPERSEDED` revision is refused. The listing's `completeness_score_certified` must be at least 85.00, otherwise `ERR_COMPLETENESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; a listing sitting at exactly 85.00 passes.

Two capacity guards then apply, and their geometries are different in a way that matters. The **concurrent-window** guard counts the retailer's jobs — across all of its listings, not just this one — that are in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED` and whose window intersects the requested window, and requires that count to be strictly fewer than the retailer's `max_concurrent_feed_windows`; exceeding it is `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. This comparison treats both windows as **closed intervals**, including both endpoints, so a new window that begins at the exact instant an existing window ends **does** count as intersecting. The **slot capacity** guard requires that the retailer's current `feed_slots_reserved` plus the requested `slot_count` is at most `feed_slot_capacity`; landing exactly on capacity is allowed, and exceeding it is `ERR_FEED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. `expected_retailer_version` must of course match the retailer's current `version`, or the queue is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` — but that check is emphatically **not** last. Per §2.8 step 23 it runs as soon as the named revision and listing have been resolved and the owning retailer located, ahead of the retailer's `status`, ahead of the `shelf_state`, `PINNED` and certified-score gates, and ahead of both capacity guards; a queue attempt that is stale *and* shelf-blocked *and* over capacity therefore reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

On success, the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` grows by the job's `slot_count` and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. Two figures are frozen onto the job at this moment: `readiness_at_queue` captures the listing's readiness score as it stands, and `service_fee_cents` captures the feed fee computed below. The revision's `pin_count` is deliberately **not** touched — a job references a revision, it does not pin it. Nothing else is recomputed by a successful queue: `readiness_at_queue` copies the `readiness_score` the listing already carries rather than triggering a fresh recompute of it, the two completeness variants are left alone, and neither the product's `shelf_health_index` nor its roll-up fields nor any alert is touched, because neither `NOT_SYNDICATED` nor `PENDING` is inside the product's health input set and the queue therefore cannot change that set. The response is HTTP 201 carrying the new job row.

**The feed service fee.** The fee is computed in five ordered steps, and the order is pinned; it must not be iterated to a fixed point. First, the subtotal in cents is the per-slot rate of 2500 cents — that is, 25 dollars per feed slot — multiplied by `slot_count`. Second, a volume discount percentage is selected **from that subtotal**: no discount when the subtotal is below 5000 cents, 5 percent when the subtotal is at least 5000 and below 15000 cents, 10 percent when it is at least 15000 and below 25000 cents, and 15 percent when it is 25000 cents or more. Third, the discounted amount is the subtotal multiplied by one minus the discount percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, a rush surcharge percentage is selected: the transmission counts as **rush** when `window_start` minus the logical clock's current instant is strictly less than 24 hours, and that is a signed comparison rather than an absolute gap — a `window_start` sitting exactly on the clock, or already in the clock's past, is therefore rush, and only a window opening a full 24 hours or more ahead of the clock escapes the surcharge; a non-rush transmission carries no surcharge, a rush transmission carries 20 percent when the discounted amount is below 20000 cents, and a rush transmission carries 12 percent when the discounted amount is 20000 cents or more. The surcharge tier is keyed on the **discounted amount before any surcharge**, never on the final total — keying it on the total would be circular, because the surcharge itself can push the total across the 20000-cent boundary. Fifth, the surcharge amount is the discounted amount multiplied by the surcharge percentage, rounded half up to whole cents, and the fee written to `service_fee_cents` is the discounted amount plus that surcharge amount.

Transmitting a job is a plain state advance available to a `SyndicationOperator`: the job must be in `QUEUED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`, and the retailer's `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE` — and the §2.8 order puts the retailer-status check first, so a job that is both no longer `QUEUED` and owned by a retailer suspended in the meantime reports `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE` rather than the job-state error. The operation takes no request body and reads none. The job moves `QUEUED → TRANSMITTED`, `transmitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the job's own `version` increments by 1. Slots stay reserved, because `TRANSMITTED` is still a holding state of the slot ledger — which means this is the one job state write that writes nothing at all onto the retailer: `feed_slots_reserved` is unchanged and the retailer's `updated_at` and `version` do not move. Nothing on the listing or the product is recomputed either, so the job row is the only row this call mutates. No completeness or image gate runs at transmission — the certified-score threshold was already enforced at queue time.

Cancellation is available while a job is in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED` and moves it to the terminal `CANCELLED`; from any other state the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`. It reads no request body, and — unlike queueing, transmitting and requeueing — it carries no retailer-status guard at all, so a job whose retailer has since been suspended is still cancellable. A job cancelled out of `TRANSMITTED` keeps the `transmitted_at` it was stamped with; `responded_at` stays null, and `attempt_no`, `slot_count`, the window bounds, `readiness_at_queue` and `service_fee_cents` are all left exactly as they stood. Cancellation releases the whole reservation: the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed from the ledger, dropping the job's `slot_count`, and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. Every `OPEN` rejection error still attached to the cancelled job — rows a previous attempt's response left behind, since a job can carry them across a requeue — is moved to `WAIVED`, in ascending `created_at` order with insertion order breaking ties, with `resolution_mode` set to `WAIVE`, `resolution_note` set to the literal string `CANCELLED_WITH_JOB`, `resolved_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `resolved_by_user_id` left **null**, because no human closed these rows; each closed row's own `version` increments by 1. Rows already `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED` are left alone. The listing's `syndication_state` then settles — and because the waivers land first, the blocking rows this very cancellation just waived no longer count toward the recomputed `open_blocking_error_count`: it becomes `ERROR_HOLD` when the listing still has a positive `open_blocking_error_count`, becomes `NOT_SYNDICATED` when the listing has no other job in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`, and is otherwise left unchanged. The listing's `readiness_score` is then recomputed whatever the settle decided, and the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed **only** where that settle actually changed `syndication_state` — a cancellation that leaves the listing on `PENDING` because another job of the same listing is still holding, or that re-derives `ERROR_HOLD` onto a listing already there, touches nothing on the product at all. No shelf alert is raised, resolved or re-rolled-up by a cancellation, so the product's `open_alert_count`, `governance_flag` and `version` never move on this path, and the response is HTTP 200 carrying the cancelled job row.

### 3.7. Retailer Feed Responses, Rejection Errors and the Requeue Cycle

Confirmation comes from the retailer's own integration account, acting on a job that is in `TRANSMITTED`; from any other state the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`, and from an account whose `X-User-ID` is not the retailer owning the job's listing it is `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The response carries an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, and optionally an `errors` array of per-row complaints. Each element of `errors` carries a `retailer_error_code` of 1 to 40 characters as returned by the retailer's feed processor, an optional `attribute_key` naming the attribute the retailer flagged, which defaults to null and must resolve in the global taxonomy when supplied (otherwise `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN`), a `severity` of `BLOCKING` or `WARNING`, and a `message` of 1 to 500 characters carrying the retailer's human-readable reason. The three outcomes are mutually exclusive in the shape of `errors` they admit: `ACCEPTED` requires `errors` to be absent or empty, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` requires at least one element with every element carrying `severity` `WARNING`, and `REJECTED` requires at least one element with at least one carrying `severity` `BLOCKING`. The whole of that shape checking is step 8, and it runs in one pinned sequence inside the step: `outcome` is resolved against its enum first, so a bogus `outcome` is reported without `errors` being looked at; then `errors` itself must be a JSON array when present, a non-array value being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` while an absent one and an explicit `null` both read as the empty list; then every element is shape-checked in request order, so a bad `severity` on the second element is reported even though the first element was fine and even though the outcome-to-`errors` consistency rule above would also have failed; then that consistency rule is applied to the fully parsed array; and only then, last of all, is each supplied `attribute_key` resolved against the taxonomy. The consequence is that a `REJECTED` response carrying a single `WARNING` row that also names an unknown attribute key reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN`. Only once the whole body has passed is the job's `state` consulted, so a well-shaped response against a job still in `QUEUED` is `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE` while a malformed one against the same job is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each element then becomes one rejection error row, created in request order, each a fresh row at `version` 1 starting in `state` `OPEN` with `resolution_mode`, `resolved_by_user_id`, `resolution_note` and `resolved_at` all null. A job's rejection error rows can be read back as a list, optionally filtered by `severity` and by `state` — each an exact string match, with pagination validated ahead of the job's own existence, so bad paging against an unknown job id reports `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Whichever outcome arrives, `responded_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the reservation is released — the job leaves the two holding states, so the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed from the ledger and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. From there the three branches diverge, and each deserves its own accounting:

- On `ACCEPTED` the job reaches its terminal `ACCEPTED` state and the listing's `syndication_state` moves `PENDING → LIVE`. The listing's `pinned_content_version_id` is emphatically **not** touched — pinning is owned exclusively by the change-request join. That `LIVE` write is unconditional on the listing's prior value rather than a `PENDING`-only transition: a listing sitting on `ERROR_HOLD` because a *different* job of the same listing still carries `OPEN` blocking rows is driven to `LIVE` all the same, and a listing already on `LIVE` is a no-op that moves no `version`. Because a `LIVE` listing now qualifies for the product's health input set, the listing's `open_blocking_error_count` is re-derived, then its `readiness_score`, then the product's `shelf_health_index`, in that order. Neither the `HEALTH_DEGRADED` rule nor the alert roll-up is evaluated on this branch, so the product's `open_alert_count`, `governance_flag` and `version` stay exactly where they were.
- On `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` the job lands in `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, one `OPEN` rejection error row is created per element in request order, and the listing's `syndication_state` still moves `PENDING → LIVE`, because by construction no `BLOCKING` row exists — and, exactly as on `ACCEPTED`, unconditionally on the listing's prior value. The listing's `open_blocking_error_count` is re-derived and therefore unchanged, and the branch then closes identically to `ACCEPTED`: the listing's `readiness_score` and the product's `shelf_health_index` are recomputed, no `HEALTH_DEGRADED` evaluation or alert roll-up runs, and the product's `version` does not move. The one asymmetry with `ACCEPTED` is that `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` is not terminal — it is one of the two states requeue accepts.
- On `REJECTED` a four-hop cascade fires. *Hop 1:* the job's `state` becomes `REJECTED`, `responded_at` is stamped, one rejection error row is created per element of `errors` in request order, the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` drops by the job's `slot_count` and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. *Hop 2, caused by hop 1's `BLOCKING` rows:* the listing's `open_blocking_error_count` is recomputed and is now positive, so the listing's `syndication_state` becomes `ERROR_HOLD` and its `readiness_score` is recomputed with the new penalty tier. *Hop 3, caused by hop 2's `ERROR_HOLD` write, which admits the listing into the health input set:* the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed, and when the recomputed index falls below 60.00 a shelf alert with `alert_type` `HEALTH_DEGRADED`, `severity` `MEDIUM` and a null `source_observation_id` is created on that listing — suppressed when a `HEALTH_DEGRADED` alert is already `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` there. *Hop 4, caused by hop 3's alert row:* the product's `open_alert_count` and `governance_flag` are re-derived from the open-alert set under the §3.9 precedence — so the flag becomes `LISTING_RISK` when an `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` alert is open, `CONTENT_AT_RISK` when the set is otherwise non-empty, and `NONE` when the set is empty, because hop 4 is a re-derivation of the roll-up rather than an assertion that the flag must rise. Whether the product's `version` increments is decided by hop 3 alone: it increments by 1 **only where hop 3 actually created a `HEALTH_DEGRADED` row**. A rejection whose recomputed `shelf_health_index` is still 60.00 or above, or one where a `HEALTH_DEGRADED` alert of that listing was already `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` so the de-duplication suppressed a second row, still runs the roll-up recompute but leaves the product's `updated_at` and `version` untouched — the roll-up fields are derived, and per §2.10 derived writes move neither.

**Blocking-error penalty tiers.** The `open_blocking_error_count` a listing carries is the number of rejection errors with `severity` `BLOCKING` and `state` `OPEN` across every syndication job of that listing, not merely the most recent one. It selects the readiness penalty described in §3.4 in four bands: no penalty at all when the count is zero, 5 points when the count is 1 or 2, 12 points when the count is 3, 4 or 5, and 25 points once the count reaches 6 or more.

Closing a rejection error is how a listing digs itself out. The row must be in `OPEN`, otherwise the attempt is `ERR_REJECTION_ERROR_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The caller supplies a `resolution_mode` of `FIX` — meaning the underlying content was repaired — or `WAIVE` — meaning the defect was accepted as-is — together with a `resolution_note` of at least 1 character and no upper bound. Both fields are mandatory, and both are shape-checked before the row the path names is fetched, so a call supplying neither against an unknown rejection-error id reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_REJECTION_ERROR_NOT_FOUND`. A `SyndicationOperator` may `FIX` any row and may `WAIVE` a `WARNING` row, but a `WAIVE` against a row whose `severity` is `BLOCKING` is reserved to `ShelfAdmin` and otherwise fails with `ERR_WAIVE_NOT_PERMITTED`. A `FIX` moves the row `OPEN → RESOLVED` and a `WAIVE` moves it `OPEN → WAIVED`; both are terminal, both record `resolved_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and stamp `resolved_at` from the logical clock alongside the supplied `resolution_note`, and — this is the important symmetry — both have exactly the same downstream effect. The listing's `open_blocking_error_count` is recomputed and its `readiness_score` is recomputed, possibly dropping to a gentler penalty tier. A waived blocking row stops blocking just as completely as a fixed one. When the closing call takes `open_blocking_error_count` to zero and no job of the listing is in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`, the listing's `syndication_state` moves `ERROR_HOLD → NOT_SYNDICATED` and its readiness is recomputed with no penalty at all, which in turn changes whether the listing qualifies for the product's health input set. All three conditions are required and the third is literally the listing's current value: a listing that is not on `ERROR_HOLD` when the last blocking row closes — one left on `LIVE` by a partially accepted attempt, say — is left exactly where it is. The product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed only where that state move actually happened; a closure that moves no state leaves the product untouched. Either way the product's `version` never moves on this operation, and no shelf alert is raised or closed by closing a rejection error. The response is HTTP 200 carrying the closed rejection-error row.

**The requeue cycle.** A job that ended in `REJECTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` is not dead; it cycles. A `SyndicationOperator` requeues it, optionally supplying a fresh `slot_count` — again an integer from 1 to 10 inclusive — a fresh `window_start`, a fresh `window_end` and `expected_retailer_version`; each of the first three, where supplied, overwrites the corresponding field on the job, and each omitted one leaves the existing value in place, while `expected_retailer_version` is not a job field at all but the optional concurrency proof of §2.6. Every gate below is then evaluated against that **effective** window — the supplied bound where one was supplied, the retained bound where one was not — so supplying only a `window_start` that falls at or after the job's retained `window_end` is `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` just as a crossed pair would be. The two cases differ only in when they are caught: a supplied pair is compared before the job named in the path is fetched, so two crossed bounds against an unknown job id report `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`, whereas a single bound can only be compared against the retained one after the job has been located, so there an unknown job id reports `ERR_SYNDICATION_JOB_NOT_FOUND`. Requeue is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE` from any state other than `REJECTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, and with `ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED` while any `BLOCKING` rejection error of the job is still `OPEN` rather than `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED`. It then re-runs every gate the original queue ran, against the new window and slot figures: `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, the retailer still `ACTIVE`, the listing's `shelf_state` neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED`, the job's content revision still `PINNED` — a revision superseded in the meantime can never be requeued, so `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PINNED` is the correct outcome there — the listing's `completeness_score_certified` still at least 85.00, the closed-interval concurrent-window count still below the retailer's `max_concurrent_feed_windows`, and reserved slots plus the requested slots still within `feed_slot_capacity` — where "reserved" no longer includes this job's own slots, since a `REJECTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` job left the holding set when its response landed, so the requeue competes for capacity exactly as a brand-new job would. `expected_retailer_version`, where supplied, is checked where §2.8 step 23 puts it — after the listing and retailer have been located but before the retailer-status, job-state, blocking-error, shelf-state, `PINNED`, certified-score, window-conflict and capacity gates, and therefore first of all of them, not last.

A successful requeue moves the job back to `QUEUED`, increments `attempt_no` by 1, resets `transmitted_at` and `responded_at` to null, and re-reserves the slots so the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed upward and its `version` increments by 1. Both frozen figures are re-frozen at the current clock position: `readiness_at_queue` recaptures the listing's readiness as it now stands, and `service_fee_cents` is recomputed from scratch — which is how a requeue into a window less than 24 hours away picks up a rush surcharge the original attempt never paid. The listing's `syndication_state` becomes `PENDING`, coming from `NOT_SYNDICATED` when the previous attempt was rejected and its blocking errors were subsequently cleared, or from `LIVE` when the previous attempt was partially accepted. Unlike the write that queueing performs, this one is unconditional on the prior value: the listing is driven to `PENDING` from `NOT_SYNDICATED`, from `ERROR_HOLD`, or from `LIVE` alike, and only a listing already on `PENDING` makes it a no-op that moves no `version`. The listing's `readiness_score` is then recomputed on every successful requeue. The `LIVE` path has a consequence worth spelling out: a `LIVE → PENDING` move takes the listing out of the product's health input set until the new attempt resolves, so the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed on the way through. The product recompute is gated on that prior value being exactly `LIVE` and on nothing else: an `ERROR_HOLD → PENDING` requeue — the shape you get when the blocking rows were all closed while another job of the listing was still holding, so the listing never settled to `NOT_SYNDICATED` — also leaves the health input set, yet the product's `shelf_health_index` is deliberately **not** recomputed on that path. The product's `version` never moves on a requeue in any case, no alert is raised or rolled up, and the response is HTTP 200 carrying the requeued job row.

### 3.8. Enhanced Content Deployment Windows

Enhanced content — an A+ page, a comparison chart, a video block — is published onto a listing for a bounded display period rather than indefinitely. A `ContentAuthor` creates a deployment by naming the `retailer_listing_id` whose placement calendar it occupies, the `change_request_id` of the approved governance record authorising it, the `media_asset_id` of the approved rich-media asset being deployed, the `module_kind` — one of `A_PLUS_PAGE`, `COMPARISON_CHART` or `VIDEO_BLOCK` — the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the placement, and `expected_retailer_listing_version`. The window is **half-open**: `window_start` is inclusive and `window_end` is exclusive, and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start` or the request fails with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. Deployments can be listed filtered by `retailer_listing_id`, by `change_request_id`, by `media_asset_id`, or by `state`.

Six substantive gates guard creation, evaluated in the order given in §2.8. The retailer must be `ACTIVE`. The listing's `shelf_state` must be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED`. The cited change request must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_APPROVED` — enhanced content rides on governance that has already cleared both review tracks. The cited change request's `retailer_id` and `product_id` must both match the listing's, and the cited asset's `product_id` must match the listing's `product_id` too; any of those three mismatching yields `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The cited asset's `approval_state` must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED`. And the number of media assets on the product whose `asset_role` is `HERO_IMAGE` or `SECONDARY_IMAGE` and whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED` must be at least the retailer's `min_image_count`, otherwise `ERR_IMAGE_COUNT_BELOW_MINIMUM`.

The placement calendar itself is guarded by a pairwise interval test, and its geometry is half-open rather than closed. The new window may not intersect the window of any deployment on the **same listing** that is currently in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`; deployments in `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` are ignored entirely. Because both intervals are half-open, two windows that merely touch — where an existing deployment's `window_end` equals the new `window_start` — do **not** intersect and are allowed, so a listing can carry back-to-back placements with no gap. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. `expected_retailer_listing_version` must equal the listing's current `version`, or the creation fails with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` — and despite being described last here, that check is the *first* of the substantive gates to run, per §2.8 step 23: it is evaluated as soon as the listing, the media asset and the change request named in the body have been resolved, ahead of the retailer's `status`, ahead of all six gates above, and ahead of the overlap test, so a creation that is both stale and overlapping reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

The initial state of a successful deployment is decided by the retailer's configuration, not by the request. When the retailer's `enhanced_content_requires_approval` is true, the deployment is created in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` and awaits a decision. When it is false, the very same request creates the deployment directly in `LIVE` with `retailer_decision_at` left null, because no decision was ever required. Either way the listing's `version` increments by 1, and the `pin_count` of the revision named by the change request's `target_content_version_id` is recomputed upward, because an active deployment is a second kind of live reference to a revision. When the deployment lands directly in `LIVE`, the listing's `readiness_score` is additionally recomputed with the enhanced-content bonus and the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed.

That bonus is a flat **5 points** added to the readiness blend described in §3.4, and it is earned by the existence of at least one deployment of the listing in `LIVE`. A deployment sitting in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` earns nothing — waiting for approval is not the same as being published.

A `RetailerIntegration` principal decides a pending deployment by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. The deployment must be in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL`, otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be the retailer owning the deployment's listing, otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On `APPROVE` the deployment moves `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL → LIVE`, `retailer_decision_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the listing's `readiness_score` is recomputed now carrying the 5-point bonus, the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed, and the listing's `version` increments by 1; the revision's `pin_count` is deliberately left alone on this branch, because `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` and `LIVE` count alike as active references and the reference therefore never moved. On `REJECT` the deployment moves `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL → REJECTED`, which is terminal; `retailer_decision_at` is stamped exactly as on approval, the revision's `pin_count` is recomputed downward because that reference is gone, and the placement window is freed for another deployment. A rejected deployment never earns the readiness bonus, so no bonus is removed — and nothing is recomputed to remove it: neither the listing's `readiness_score` nor the product's `shelf_health_index` is touched on the reject branch. The listing's `version`, however, increments by 1 on **both** branches: the decision is a deployment write on the listing's placement calendar whichever way it goes. The product's `version` moves on neither, and the response is HTTP 200 carrying the decided deployment row.

Withdrawal is the author's compensating action and is available from either `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`; from `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` the attempt is `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. An optional `withdrawn_reason` is recorded on the row and defaults to null; supplied, it must be a string of at least 1 character with no upper bound, so an empty string is a step-8 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` while omitting the field — or sending it as an explicit `null` — writes null. That body-shape check runs before the deployment named in the path is fetched, so an empty `withdrawn_reason` against an unknown deployment id reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Withdrawal carries no retailer-status guard, so a deployment whose retailer has since been suspended is still withdrawable. The deployment moves to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, the revision's `pin_count` is recomputed downward — returning the pin ledger to precisely its pre-creation value, which is the point of calling withdrawal a compensating action — the listing's `readiness_score` is recomputed with the bonus removed, the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed, and the listing's `version` increments by 1. Withdrawal from `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` and withdrawal from `LIVE` are specified identically; the only practical difference is that in the pending case there was never a bonus in the score to remove.

### 3.9. Digital Shelf Monitoring, Alerting and Performance Correlation

Monitoring enters the platform through the crawl. A `ShelfAdmin` ingests one observation against one listing, carrying `observed_at` — the instant the crawl saw the shelf — `in_stock` as observed on the retailer's product page, `content_accuracy_pct` as the percentage of certified attributes rendered correctly (between 0.00 and 100.00 to 2 decimal places), `rating_avg` as the average star rating observed (between 0.00 and 5.00 to 2 decimal places), `rating_count` as the number of ratings observed (zero or more), `unauthorized_seller_count` as the number of unauthorized sellers seen on the listing (zero or more), `conversion_rate_pct` as the conversion rate for the period ending at `observed_at` (between 0.00 and 100.00 to 2 decimal places), `units_sold` for that same period (zero or more), and an optional `search_rank` giving the keyword search position seen on the retailer's site, which must be at least 1 when supplied and defaults to null to mean the product did not appear at all. Every one of those fields except `search_rank` is mandatory, and each stated numeric range is inclusive at both ends. `observed_at` is supplied as a string: one that is absent or is not a string is a step-8 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and so is a string that does not parse as an ISO 8601 instant — `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` belongs exclusively to the three window-bearing operations of §2.8 step 9 and is never raised here. As there, a numeric zone offset is converted to the equivalent UTC instant and a value with no zone designator is read as UTC. Observations are append-only and can be read back as the listing's chronological log; the ingest answers HTTP 201 with the observation row, which per §2.4 carries only `id` and `created_at` beside its payload.

**Projection.** Before any alert rule is evaluated, the listing's shelf snapshot is re-projected from its observation log. The latest observation is determined by sorting the listing's observations by `observed_at` ascending and breaking ties on `created_at` ascending — never by `id` — and taking the last row. That row's values are copied verbatim onto the listing with no rounding: `search_rank` into `search_rank_latest`, and `content_accuracy_pct`, `rating_avg` and `in_stock` into the like-named listing fields. Because ties are broken by insertion order, two observations sharing the same `observed_at` resolve in favour of the later-inserted row. The consequence for backfill is deliberate: a late-arriving observation whose `observed_at` is older than the current latest leaves all four projected fields exactly as they were. It is not inert, though. The alert rules below are evaluated against the values of the observation just ingested rather than against the refreshed snapshot, so a backfilled row can still move `shelf_state` and still raise or auto-resolve alerts even where it changed nothing the listing renders — and it participates fully in the conversion-lift analysis below.

**Alert rules.** With the snapshot refreshed, the listing's `shelf_state` is re-derived under a fixed precedence — `SUSPENDED` outranks `UNAUTHORIZED`, which outranks `OOS_WATCH`, which outranks `OK` — so an observation that is simultaneously out of stock and shows unauthorized sellers lands the listing on `UNAUTHORIZED`, and an observation of any shape leaves a listing that an account team has already `SUSPENDED` exactly where it is. A listing already on `SUSPENDED` is skipped by the whole availability-and-authorisation branch rather than merely spared the state write: no `OUT_OF_STOCK` and no `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` alert is raised against it, and no `OUT_OF_STOCK` alert of its own is auto-resolved either, however the crawl found the shelf. Three monitoring rules then fire, and every alert they raise is de-duplicated: a rule never creates a second alert of the same `alert_type` on the same listing while one of that type is already `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`.

- An observation with `in_stock` false and `unauthorized_seller_count` zero moves an `OK` listing to `OOS_WATCH` and raises an alert with `alert_type` `OUT_OF_STOCK`, `severity` `MEDIUM`, and `source_observation_id` pointing at the observation that triggered it. The alert is bound to that transition and to no other: an out-of-stock observation against a listing already on `OOS_WATCH`, or against one sitting on `UNAUTHORIZED`, moves nothing and raises nothing at all.
- An observation with `unauthorized_seller_count` of 1 or more moves the listing to `UNAUTHORIZED` — provided it is not already `SUSPENDED` — and raises an alert with `alert_type` `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` and `severity` `HIGH`, whose `assigned_account_team_user_id` is copied from the listing's `account_team_user_id` and is null when the listing has no named owner. The product's `governance_flag` becomes `LISTING_RISK`. This rule, unlike the out-of-stock one, is not bound to a transition: an observation still showing unauthorized sellers against a listing already on `UNAUTHORIZED` raises a fresh alert whenever no `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` alert of that listing is currently `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and the state write it attempts is simply a no-op. From this point on, queueing a job or creating a deployment against the listing is refused with `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED` until an account team clears it.
- An observation whose `content_accuracy_pct` is below 85.00 raises an alert with `alert_type` `CONTENT_DRIFT` and `severity` `LOW`. The comparison is strict, so an observation landing exactly on 85.00 raises nothing, and the alert carries the ingested observation as its `source_observation_id`. Content drift does not move `shelf_state`; it is a content-quality signal, not an availability one — and because it sits outside the availability-and-authorisation branch it is evaluated whatever the listing's `shelf_state` is, `SUSPENDED` included.

A fourth alert type is not raised by an observation's own values but by the health recompute that follows it. An alert with `alert_type` `HEALTH_DEGRADED` and `severity` `MEDIUM` is raised on the listing only when the listing is actually inside the product's health input set — that is, its `syndication_state` is `LIVE` or `ERROR_HOLD` — **and** the recomputed `shelf_health_index` is below 60.00. A `NOT_SYNDICATED` or `PENDING` listing never raises it, which is precisely why the empty-input-set index of 0.00 is not an alert condition. Like the `HEALTH_DEGRADED` alert of §3.7 it carries a null `source_observation_id`, because no single observation raised it. Its position inside the ingest is fixed: it is evaluated after the `shelf_health_index` recompute and before the conversion-lift recompute, so the alert it raises is already in place when `open_alert_count` and `governance_flag` are rolled up at the close of the same request, while the listing's `readiness_score` — recomputed just before the health index — is not revisited afterwards.

Recovery through observation is automatic in one specific case. An observation with `in_stock` true and `unauthorized_seller_count` zero moves an `OOS_WATCH` listing back to `OK`, and every `OUT_OF_STOCK` alert of that listing still in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` is auto-transitioned to `RESOLVED` with `resolution_note` set to the literal string `AUTO_CLEARED_BY_OBSERVATION` and `resolved_at` stamped from the logical clock. Unauthorized-seller alerts are never cleared this way; they require a human.

Every ingest closes by recomputing the listing's `readiness_score`, the product's `shelf_health_index`, the product's `conversion_lift_pct` under the default variant, the product's `open_alert_count` and `governance_flag`, and incrementing the product's `version` by 1.

**Account-team follow-up.** An `AccountTeamLead` owns the human half of the loop. The shelf-status action supplies a target `shelf_state` of either `SUSPENDED` or `OK`. Neither target is ever refused on state grounds: this operation carries no shelf-state guard at all, so every one of the eight state-and-target combinations succeeds, and the listing's `updated_at` and `version` move only where the `shelf_state` value actually changes. Clearing to `OK` is therefore accepted from `UNAUTHORIZED`, `OOS_WATCH`, `SUSPENDED` and from `OK` itself, where it is an idempotent no-op on the state field; it leaves `syndication_state` untouched, and resolves every `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` alert of the listing still in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` with `resolution_note` set to the literal string `AUTO_CLEARED_BY_ACCOUNT_TEAM` — those closures and everything after them run on the already-`OK` path too; it then recomputes the listing's `readiness_score`, the product's `open_alert_count` and `governance_flag`, and increments the product's `version` by 1. Suspending is accepted from any state, `SUSPENDED` included, leaves `syndication_state` untouched, closes no alert, and recomputes `readiness_score` — which now floors the accuracy input at 60.00, because the listing is no longer `OK`; unlike the clearing branch it leaves the product's roll-up fields and the product's `version` entirely alone. Either way the response is HTTP 200 carrying the updated listing row.

Alerts themselves have a three-state lifecycle. Every alert, of whatever `alert_type` and whatever raised it, is stamped at creation with `assigned_account_team_user_id` copied from its listing's `account_team_user_id` and null where the listing named no owner, and no operation in the platform ever reassigns it. Acknowledging one takes no request body, moves it `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED` and records `acknowledged_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` alongside — never over — that assignment; the alert's own `updated_at` and `version` move, and nothing else in the system is written at all: the product's `open_alert_count`, `governance_flag`, `updated_at` and `version` are deliberately **unchanged**, because an acknowledged alert is still open follow-up. Resolving one requires a `resolution_note` of 1 to 500 characters, shape-checked before the alert the path names is fetched, so a resolve carrying no note against an unknown alert id reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`. It is permitted from `OPEN` or from `ACKNOWLEDGED`, moves the alert to the terminal `RESOLVED`, records that note, stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock and increments the alert's own `version` by 1, then recomputes the product's `open_alert_count` downward and its `governance_flag`, and increments the product's `version` by 1 — that product increment is unconditional on this path, unlike the derived-only recomputes elsewhere. Resolving straight from `OPEN` leaves `acknowledged_by_user_id` null; resolving from `ACKNOWLEDGED` preserves whatever it already held. Acknowledging an already-acknowledged alert, or acting on a `RESOLVED` one, is rejected with `ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. Alerts can be listed filtered by `retailer_listing_id`, by `source_observation_id`, by `alert_type`, or by `state`.

**Product roll-up.** The product's `open_alert_count` is the number of alerts across every listing of the product whose `state` is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Its `governance_flag` is derived from that same set under a pinned precedence, evaluated top to bottom: it is `LISTING_RISK` when any alert in the set has `alert_type` `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING`; otherwise `CONTENT_AT_RISK` when the count is 1 or more; otherwise `NONE`. So resolving the only unauthorized-listing alert on a product that still has a drift alert open drops the flag from `LISTING_RISK` back to `CONTENT_AT_RISK` rather than to `NONE`.

**Cross-retailer shelf health.** The product's `shelf_health_index` aggregates only those of its listings whose `syndication_state` is `LIVE` or `ERROR_HOLD`; `NOT_SYNDICATED` and `PENDING` listings are excluded, and when no listing qualifies the index is 0.00 and the per-retailer breakdown is empty. Qualifying listings are processed in ascending `created_at` order, and each contributes exactly one subscore regardless of how many observations it has accumulated. A listing's subscore blends four components. Its accuracy component is `content_accuracy_pct`, treated as 0 when null. Its rank component is 0 when `search_rank_latest` is null, and otherwise 100 reduced by 5 points for every position below the first — so rank 1 scores 100, rank 6 scores 75, rank 11 scores 50 — with the result clamped to the range 0 to 100, meaning rank 21 and everything worse scores 0. Its availability component is 100 when `in_stock` is true and 0 otherwise. Its rating component is 20 multiplied by `rating_avg`, treated as 0 when null, which maps a perfect 5.00 rating to 100. The subscore is 0.45 of the accuracy component plus 0.25 of the rank component plus 0.20 of the availability component plus 0.10 of the rating component, **rounded half up to 2 decimal places at this point**. The index is then the arithmetic mean of those already-rounded subscores, rounded half up to 2 decimal places again. The two-stage rounding order is mandatory and is not the same as averaging the unrounded subscores.

**Content-to-conversion correlation.** The last analytical piece answers whether better content actually sells more. The input set is every observation belonging to any listing of the product, with no filter on listing state and no cap on observation age, ordered by `observed_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Those observations are split into a HIGH group and a BASE group by the selected variant. If either group is empty the lift is 0.00 — the group sizes reported alongside it are still the true sizes of the split that produced that short circuit, not zeroes. Otherwise the mean `conversion_rate_pct` of each group is taken as an exact value, and if the BASE group's mean is zero the lift is 0.00. The lift is then 100 multiplied by the difference between the HIGH mean and the BASE mean, divided by the BASE mean, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — a single rounding, applied to the final percentage; the group means are never rounded.

The two variants split the same rows differently. The default variant, named `post_pin_window`, splits on pin time: observations whose `observed_at` is at or after the product's `last_pinned_at` form HIGH, and those strictly before it form BASE. When `last_pinned_at` is still null, every observation falls into BASE, HIGH is empty, and the lift is therefore 0.00. The alternative variant, named `quality_split`, ignores pin time entirely and splits on measured accuracy: observations whose `content_accuracy_pct` is at least 90.00 form HIGH and those below 90.00 form BASE. Because the same rows regroup, the two variants generally report different lifts from identical data. The `post_pin_window` figure is the one stored on the product as `conversion_lift_pct`, refreshed on every observation ingest for any listing of the product. Unlike every other score in the system, a lift figure is not clamped to a non-negative range: when the HIGH group converts worse than the BASE group the stored value is negative, carried to the same 2 decimal places.

Both the health index and the lift are also available as an on-demand computed read for a single product, which accepts an optional `lift_variant` query parameter defaulting to `post_pin_window`. This is the one query parameter in the platform that *is* validated against its enum: a `lift_variant` that is neither `post_pin_window` nor `quality_split` — including an explicitly empty one — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and that rejection is evaluated before the product itself is looked up, so a bad variant against a nonexistent product reports the variant rather than `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`. The read computes everything it returns on the spot and persists nothing: neither the product's stored `shelf_health_index` nor its stored `conversion_lift_pct` is written by it, so requesting `quality_split` here never disturbs the `post_pin_window` figure stored on the product, and the product's `version` does not move. The response echoes `product_id`, reports the two-stage-rounded `shelf_health_index` together with `qualifying_listing_count` and a `retailer_subscores` array giving the per-listing breakdown in the same ascending-`created_at` order used by the aggregation — each entry of that array identifies its listing with `retailer_listing_id` and `retailer_id`, carries the one already-rounded blended subscore in a field named `subscore`, and additionally spells out the four components that subscore blends as `accuracy_component`, `rank_component`, `availability_component` and `rating_component`, each of them the component value described above rendered to 2 decimal places rounded half up (so a listing observed in stock shows an `availability_component` of `100.00` regardless of what its own `subscore` came to) — and reports `conversion_lift_pct` alongside the `lift_variant` actually applied and the group sizes `high_group_size` and `base_group_size` that produced it.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The table below is the complete HTTP surface. Status codes follow one rule: an operation that brings a new resource into existence responds with 201 Created, and every other successful operation — including every state transition, every configuration adjustment, and every read — responds with 200 OK. The status is a property of the operation rather than of the outcome, so the attribute-value upsert answers 201 on every success, including the write that merely replaces an existing row. Request and response payloads are deliberately not repeated here; the fields each operation accepts, the values it computes, and the cascades it triggers are specified in §2 and §3. Where the "Authorized Role(s)" column reads *All roles*, the operation is open to all seven roles named in §2.1.
