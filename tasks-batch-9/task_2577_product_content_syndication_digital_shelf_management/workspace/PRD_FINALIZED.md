# Product Content Syndication & Digital Shelf API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A brand's digital shelf team lives with a structural problem: the product information it authors internally is never the product information a shopper sees. Between the two sit a dozen retailer accounts, each with its own attribute taxonomy expectations, its own character ceilings, its own image minimums, its own approval gate for enhanced content, and its own feed intake calendar with a finite number of slots. Content that is complete by house standards is routinely rejected by one retailer over a description that runs eight characters long, while a competitor's listing outranks it because two secondary images were never approved. The team's day is therefore not "write content" but "prove content is acceptable to this retailer, get it transmitted, get it confirmed, and then watch what the retailer's page actually renders."

This document specifies the API for a platform that closes that loop. It carries a product information repository whose revisions are version-controlled and governed, a per-retailer requirement layer that turns each retailer's content contract into machine-checkable completeness scoring, a syndication engine that reserves feed slots and windows on the retailer's intake calendar and tracks acceptance, partial acceptance and per-row rejection, a rich-media and enhanced-content deployment pipeline that respects retailer approval flags and placement calendars, and a digital shelf monitoring surface that ingests crawl observations on search rank, availability, content accuracy and ratings, raises alerts, and routes them to the account team that owns the relationship.

Two design commitments run through everything below and are worth stating up front. First, **the same content payload is scored twice under two different inclusion sets** — a *declared* view that answers "what has the author claimed?" and a *certified* view that answers "what would this retailer actually accept?" — and only the certified view gates distribution. Second, **feed slots and version pin references are conserved quantities**: every forward action that consumes one has a compensating action that returns it, and the ledgers must show zero drift across a full cycle. The API is mounted under `/digital-shelf`, is fully deterministic, and derives every timestamp from an explicitly settable logical clock so that rush-window pricing and window-overlap arithmetic are reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity semantics, concurrency control, rounding discipline, and the order in which the platform evaluates a request before it is allowed to mutate anything.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the seven roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other check runs. If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ShelfAdmin`, `ContentAuthor`, `LegalReviewer`, `BrandGovernanceApprover`, `SyndicationOperator`, `RetailerIntegration` and `AccountTeamLead`.

- A `ShelfAdmin` is the digital shelf platform operator. It owns the retailer roster, the global attribute taxonomy, the crawl ingestion feed, the logical clock, and the sole authority to waive a blocking feed error.
- A `ContentAuthor` registers products, drafts content revisions and attribute values, registers rich media, opens retailer listings, raises and submits change requests, and deploys and withdraws enhanced content.
- A `LegalReviewer` decides the `LEGAL` review track of a change request and nothing else.
- A `BrandGovernanceApprover` decides the `BRAND` review track of a change request and nothing else.
- A `SyndicationOperator` queues, transmits, requeues and cancels syndication jobs, and resolves rejection errors by fixing content.
- A `RetailerIntegration` account is the retailer's own integration principal. It confirms or rejects transmitted feeds and approves rich media and enhanced-content modules for its own listings.
- An `AccountTeamLead` owns shelf-alert follow-up and the suspend-or-clear decision on a listing.

Read operations under `/digital-shelf` are open to all seven roles. Every write is scoped to the role that owns the operation, as enumerated in §4.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `RetailerIntegration`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Retailer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Retailer` compare the resource's retailer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a retailer feed response is scoped by the `retailer_id` of the job's listing, an enhanced-content decision by the `retailer_id` of the deployment's listing, and a media decision by requiring that a `RetailerListing` exists for the pair formed by the asset's `product_id` and `X-User-ID`. A violation of any of these is `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

For the other six roles — `ShelfAdmin`, `ContentAuthor`, `LegalReviewer`, `BrandGovernanceApprover`, `SyndicationOperator` and `AccountTeamLead` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands provenance (as `author_user_id` on a content revision and on a change request, as `legal_reviewer_user_id` or `brand_reviewer_user_id` on a decided review track, as `resolved_by_user_id` on a closed rejection error, as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on an acknowledged shelf alert, and as `account_team_user_id` and `assigned_account_team_user_id` on listings and the alerts they raise) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

**Segregation of duties.** Review authority is deliberately split. The acting role must match the track it decides: a `LegalReviewer` may only submit a decision on track `LEGAL`, and a `BrandGovernanceApprover` may only submit a decision on track `BRAND`; the mismatch is `ERR_TRACK_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Beyond that, two independent person-level rules apply, both reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): the `X-User-ID` recorded as the change request's `author_user_id` may never decide either track, and the `X-User-ID` deciding one track may never equal the `X-User-ID` already recorded as the other track's reviewer. Waiver authority is split the same way: a `SyndicationOperator` may repair any rejection error and may waive a `WARNING` row, but a waiver against a row whose `severity` is `BLOCKING` is reserved to `ShelfAdmin` and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_WAIVE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); both parameters must be integers.

Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order unless a specific endpoint's narrative states otherwise. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit` (the effective page size applied), `offset` (the effective starting position applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Resources also carry `created_at` and `updated_at` as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Any client-supplied value for a computed or system-owned field is silently ignored rather than rejected.

`ShelfObservation` is the single exception. It is an append-only event row: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and it has **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version`. There is no update or delete operation whose output is a `ShelfObservation`. Its `created_at` exists specifically because two calculations — the shelf projection and the conversion-lift analysis — break ties between observations sharing the same `observed_at` by insertion order.

All time in the platform comes from a deterministic logical clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `ShelfAdmin` may reposition it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new `current_utc`, and no entity is mutated by the call. Every subsequent system stamp — `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `transmitted_at`, `responded_at`, `retailer_decision_at`, `resolved_at`, `last_pinned_at`, `last_review_at`, and the observation's own `created_at` — derives from the clock's current value, as do the rush-window classification used in feed pricing and the interval arithmetic used for window contention.

### 2.5. Flat Collections and Left-to-Right Reference Resolution

The entity graph here is genuinely a graph, not a tree: a `RetailerListing` is the junction of a product and a retailer, a `RetailerRequirement` is the junction of a retailer and a taxonomy attribute key, a content revision points at the revision it supersedes, and an enhanced-content deployment joins a listing, a change request and a media asset simultaneously. Because no resource is reachable through exactly one ownership chain, the URL space is a set of flat collections under `/digital-shelf` rather than a deep nested tree, and cross-references travel in request bodies.

Where a path does nest — a retailer's requirements, a revision's attribute values, a listing's observations, a job's rejection errors — path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and each such child records the identifier of the parent that produced it — a rejection error, for instance, records the `syndication_job_id` of the transmission whose response raised it, which is why it can be read back through that job's own collection. The first path segment that cannot be resolved produces its own not-found error before any deeper segment or any body field is examined. Body-carried references are resolved in the fixed order given in §2.8 so that a request naming three nonexistent rows always reports the same one.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write operations reach into a parent aggregate whose state is shared by many children, and each of them requires the client to prove it has observed that parent's current state. In all three cases the field is a mandatory input, not an optional one, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

- Queueing a syndication job must carry `expected_retailer_version` matching the current `version` of the retailer that owns the target listing. Feed slots are a shared pool, so an earlier queue, cancel or invalidation cascade may already have moved `feed_slots_reserved` since the client last read the retailer; a second queue attempt would otherwise be perfectly legal and capacity might well admit it, which is exactly why the stale-version check is the only thing that distinguishes it. The requeue operation carries the same field and applies the same check.
- Recording a review-track decision must carry `expected_product_version` matching the current `version` of the change request's product. The `LEGAL` and `BRAND` tracks are independently decidable, so a second decision on the other track is legitimate business flow; what this check catches is a reviewer deciding from a product view that predates the other track's decision, the pin cascade, or an alert recompute.
- Creating an enhanced-content deployment must carry `expected_retailer_listing_version` matching the current `version` of the target `RetailerListing`. Two deployments on non-intersecting windows are both legal, so nothing in the window arithmetic rejects the second writer; the stale version is what reveals that the writer did not see the placement calendar as it now stands.

Adjusting a retailer's requirement envelope also carries `expected_retailer_version` and is subject to the same rejection. No endpoint other than these five takes a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.7. Score Precision and Rounding Discipline

All four percentage-shaped figures in the system — the two completeness variants, the listing readiness score, the product shelf health index, and the conversion lift — are stored and returned with exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up. Monetary output is different: the syndication service fee is an integer number of cents and is never fractional.

Two rounding disciplines deserve to be called out because getting them wrong changes the last cent or the last hundredth:

- **Round once, at the end, unless told otherwise.** For completeness and readiness, numerator and denominator are carried as exact values and the half-up rounding to 2 decimal places is applied a single time, to the final percentage. No intermediate component is rounded first.
- **The shelf health index rounds twice, in a pinned order.** Each qualifying retailer's subscore is rounded half up to 2 decimal places **first**; the already-rounded subscores are then averaged; and the average is rounded half up to 2 decimal places again. Averaging the unrounded subscores and rounding once at the end is incorrect and will produce a different hundredth.

The feed fee applies half-up rounding to whole cents at two specific points — once when the volume discount is applied and once when the rush surcharge amount is computed — and never to the raw multiplication product. The two ledger quantities (reserved feed slots and version pin references) are exact integers with no rounding at all.

### 2.8. Request Evaluation Order

The platform evaluates a request as a fixed pipeline. Order matters because several checks can be simultaneously true, and the response must be deterministic: the first check that fails produces the error, and no later check is consulted. The pipeline runs in exactly this sequence.

1. Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. On a review-track decision, the acting role matches the requested `track` — otherwise `ERR_TRACK_ROLE_MISMATCH`.
4. When the acting role is `RetailerIntegration`, `X-User-ID` equals the retailer that owns the target resource — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Applies to exactly three operations: the retailer feed response on a syndication job, the retailer decision on an enhanced-content deployment, and the retailer decision on a media asset.
5. On a review-track decision, `X-User-ID` differs from the change request's `author_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
6. On a review-track decision, `X-User-ID` differs from the other track's already-recorded reviewer — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
7. `limit` and `offset` are in range — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Applies to every collection read that accepts them.
8. Request-body shape: every required field present, types matching their declarations, every enum value a member of its declared set, and no decimal negative where the field forbids it — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `slot_count` below 1 falls here too. Applies to every create, submit-style and patch operation. Values supplied for computed or system-owned fields are ignored rather than rejected.
9. `window_end` is strictly after `window_start`, and both bounds parse as ISO 8601 UTC instants — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. Applies to queueing a syndication job, requeueing one, and creating an enhanced-content deployment.
10. The referenced retailer exists — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND`.
11. The referenced product exists — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`.
12. The referenced content revision exists, whether it is named as a target, as a superseded predecessor, or as the payload of a transmission — otherwise `ERR_CONTENT_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`.
13. The referenced retailer listing exists — otherwise `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`. The same code covers the case where an operation implies a listing for a given product-and-retailer pair and no such listing exists.
14. The referenced media asset exists — otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to the media retailer decision, the single-asset read, and deployment creation.
15. The referenced change request exists — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every change-request operation and to deployment creation.
16. The referenced syndication job exists — otherwise `ERR_SYNDICATION_JOB_NOT_FOUND`.
17. The referenced rejection error exists — otherwise `ERR_REJECTION_ERROR_NOT_FOUND`.
18. The referenced deployment exists — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
19. The referenced shelf alert exists — otherwise `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`.
20. Every supplied `attribute_key` resolves to a row in the global taxonomy — otherwise `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 422). Applies to declaring a retailer requirement, writing an attribute value, and any `attribute_key` carried inside a retailer feed response's error rows.
21. No listing already exists for the product-and-retailer pair being opened — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING`.
22. No requirement row already exists for the retailer-and-attribute-key pair being declared — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT`.
23. The supplied concurrency field equals the current `version` of its named parent — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.
24. The retailer's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Applies to six operations: opening a listing, submitting a change request, queueing a job, transmitting a job, requeueing a job, and creating a deployment.
25. The target content revision's `state` is `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. Applies to three operations: writing an attribute value, raising a change request, and submitting one.
26. The change request's `state` is `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`. Applies to submission.
27. The change request's `state` is `IN_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_IN_REVIEW`. Applies to a review-track decision.
28. The named review track is still `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_TRACK_ALREADY_DECIDED`.
29. The job's current `state` admits the requested transition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`. Transmission requires `QUEUED`; a retailer feed response requires `TRANSMITTED`; requeue requires `REJECTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`; cancellation requires `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`.
30. The rejection error's `state` is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_REJECTION_ERROR_ALREADY_CLOSED`.
31. Every rejection error of the job whose `severity` is `BLOCKING` has reached `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED` — otherwise `ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED`. Applies to requeue.
32. The deployment is in an actionable state — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A retailer decision requires `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL`; a withdrawal requires `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`.
33. The alert is not already `RESOLVED`, and acknowledgement additionally requires that it is still `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.
34. The listing's `shelf_state` is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED` — otherwise `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED`. Applies to queueing a job, requeueing one, and creating a deployment.
35. The stripped attribute value contains no prohibited claim token — otherwise `ERR_PROHIBITED_CLAIM` (HTTP 422). Applies to writing an attribute value.
36. The job's content revision is `PINNED` and its `product_id` equals the listing's `product_id` — otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PINNED` (HTTP 422). Applies to queueing and requeueing.
37. The cited change request's `state` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422). Applies to deployment creation.
38. The cited change request's retailer and product both match the target listing, and the cited media asset's product matches it too — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
39. The cited media asset's `approval_state` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422).
40. The stripped attribute value's `char_count` is at most the global taxonomy ceiling — otherwise `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_LENGTH_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Applies to writing an attribute value.
41. The count of approved image-role assets on the product is at least the retailer's `min_image_count` — otherwise `ERR_IMAGE_COUNT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). Applies to deployment creation.
42. The listing's certified completeness score is at least 85.00 — otherwise `ERR_COMPLETENESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422). Applies to queueing and requeueing.
43. The new deployment window does not intersect an active deployment window on the same listing — otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
44. The retailer's concurrent-window allowance is not exceeded — otherwise `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Applies to queueing and requeueing.
45. Reserved slots plus the requested slots do not exceed the retailer's capacity — otherwise `ERR_FEED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Applies to queueing and requeueing.
46. A waiver against a `BLOCKING` row is being issued by a `ShelfAdmin` — otherwise `ERR_WAIVE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to resolving a rejection error.

Note the deliberate consequence of steps 43 through 45 sitting last: a request that is simultaneously over capacity and in window conflict reports the window conflict, because window contention is evaluated before slot capacity.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities: Feed Slots and Pin References

Two integers in this system are ledgers, not counters, and both are recomputed from their source rows rather than incremented in place.

`Retailer.feed_slots_reserved` is the sum of `slot_count` over every syndication job belonging to any listing of that retailer whose `state` is `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`. Those are the only two holding states; a job that has reached `ACCEPTED`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` or `INVALIDATED` releases its slots entirely. The ledger is recomputed on every job state write — creation, cancellation, a retailer feed response, a requeue, and the system's invalidation cascade — and the retailer's `version` increments on each of those writes. The invariant that matters: queueing a job and then cancelling it, or queueing and then invalidating it, or queueing and then receiving any retailer response, must leave `feed_slots_reserved` at exactly its pre-queue value. The `version` moves; the sum does not drift.

`ContentVersion.pin_count` is the number of references currently held on a revision, and it counts two different kinds of reference: the number of retailer listings whose `pinned_content_version_id` equals the revision's `id`, plus the number of enhanced-content deployments in state `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE` whose governing change request's `target_content_version_id` equals that same `id`. Deployments in `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` contribute nothing. It is recomputed on the change-request approval join, on deployment creation, on a retailer deployment decision, and on withdrawal. Its compensating pairs: creating a deployment and then withdrawing it returns the count to its pre-creation value, as does creating one and having the retailer reject it; and pinning a new revision onto a listing costs the previously pinned revision exactly the one listing reference it held. Globally, the sum of `pin_count` across all revisions always equals the number of listings with a non-null `pinned_content_version_id` plus the number of deployments in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations mutate more than one aggregate. Where that happens, the cascade is specified as an ordered sequence of hops, each hop caused by the write the previous hop performed, and each hop must complete within the same request. Two cascades are four hops deep — the change-request approval join and the rejection outcome of a retailer feed response — and both are described hop by hop in §3.

Two general rules govern them. First, whenever a cascade writes a field on an entity, that entity's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1, and this applies to entities the client did not name in the request: a review decision advances the product's `version`, a job state change advances the retailer's `version`, a deployment write advances the listing's `version`. Second, where a cascade processes a set of rows, the processing order is ascending `created_at`, so that the outcome of a multi-row cascade is reproducible.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Retailer Roster and the Attribute Taxonomy

Everything downstream depends on configuration a `ShelfAdmin` puts in place first. Onboarding a retailer account records its `name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters, and its `retailer_code`, a short identifier printed on feed transmissions that must be unique across all retailers and between 3 and 8 characters long using only uppercase letters and digits. The rest of the payload is the retailer's **content requirements envelope**, and each part of it is optional at onboarding because each has a default. `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and is one of `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `OFFBOARDED`. `min_image_count`, the minimum number of approved image-role assets this retailer demands per product, defaults to 1 and may be zero or any larger whole number. `feed_slot_capacity`, the total number of feed slots the retailer will hold reserved at any one moment across all of its listings, defaults to 10 and must be at least 1. `max_concurrent_feed_windows`, the number of a retailer's in-flight transmission windows that may cover a single instant, defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. `enhanced_content_requires_approval` defaults to true; when true a newly created enhanced-content deployment begins life awaiting a retailer decision, and when false it begins life already published. A freshly onboarded retailer has `feed_slots_reserved` initialised to 0 and `version` initialised to 1, and the reserved figure is thereafter always between 0 and `feed_slot_capacity` inclusive.

Retailers can be listed with an optional `status` filter alongside the usual page controls, and a single retailer read always reflects its live reserved-slot figure rather than a cached one.

Adjusting a retailer's envelope after onboarding is the one configuration write that is concurrency-guarded, because the same `version` field that a caller would use to prove it has seen the envelope is also the field that carries the feed-slot ledger. The adjustment accepts `expected_retailer_version` plus any subset of `status`, `min_image_count`, `feed_slot_capacity`, `max_concurrent_feed_windows` and `enhanced_content_requires_approval`. Its consequences reach further than the retailer row: the retailer's `version` increments by 1, then `completeness_score_declared`, `completeness_score_certified` and `readiness_score` are recomputed for every listing belonging to that retailer — because `min_image_count` feeds the image component of the certified variant — and then `shelf_health_index` is recomputed for every product touched by those listings.

The **global attribute taxonomy** is the vocabulary all content is written against. Adding an attribute records its `attribute_key`, which must be unique across the taxonomy and written in snake_case between 2 and 40 characters; its `data_type`, one of `STRING`, `INTEGER`, `DECIMAL` or `ENUM`; its `taxonomy_group`, one of `CORE`, `MARKETING`, `VARIANT` or `COMPLIANCE`; its `max_chars`, the house-wide character ceiling of at least 1 that acts as a hard write-time limit on any value authored against the key; and its `base_weight`, a completeness weight greater than zero expressed to 2 decimal places, used whenever a retailer's own requirement row supplies no weight of its own. An optional `is_global_required` flag defaults to false and marks house-standard attributes for documentation purposes only — on its own it has no effect on any score. The taxonomy can be listed with an optional `taxonomy_group` filter.

Each retailer's content contract is then expressed as a set of requirement rows, one per attribute key. A requirement row is scoped to a retailer and carries `attribute_key`, which must name an existing taxonomy attribute or the write fails with `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN`; a `requirement_level` of `REQUIRED`, `RECOMMENDED` or `OPTIONAL`; an optional `max_chars_override`, which must be at least 1 when supplied and defaults to null meaning "fall back to the taxonomy's `max_chars`"; and an optional `weight_override`, which must be greater than zero to 2 decimal places when supplied and defaults to null meaning "fall back to the taxonomy's `base_weight`". A retailer may hold at most one requirement row per attribute key; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUIREMENT`. Declaring a requirement recomputes both completeness variants and the readiness score of every listing of that retailer, and then the shelf health index of every product those listings touch. A retailer's requirement rows can be read back as a list.

The distinction between the three requirement levels is the single most consequential piece of taxonomy configuration. `REQUIRED` and `RECOMMENDED` rows both enter the completeness denominator and can both earn credit in the numerator; they differ only in operational emphasis, not in arithmetic. `OPTIONAL` rows are excluded from the numerator **and** from the denominator, so declaring an attribute optional does not depress a score — it removes the attribute from the calculation entirely.

### 3.2. Product Registration and the Content Revision Chain

A `ContentAuthor` registers a product with four author-supplied identifiers: `sku`, unique across all products and between 3 and 40 characters; `gtin`, the trade item number printed on the pack, between 8 and 14 digits; `category_code`, the merchandising category; and `brand_code`, the owning brand inside the portfolio. Everything else on a product is rolled up by the platform. A new product begins with `status` `DRAFT`, `shelf_health_index` 0.00, `conversion_lift_pct` 0.00, `open_alert_count` 0, `governance_flag` `NONE`, and both `last_pinned_at` and `last_review_at` null. `status` is one of only two values, `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, and it flips to `ACTIVE` the moment any content revision of the product first becomes `PINNED`; it never flips back. Products can be listed with optional `status` and `governance_flag` filters.

Content is authored as an explicit revision chain rather than by editing a product in place. Opening a revision requires only the `product_id`; the author may additionally name `supersedes_content_version_id` to record which existing revision this one is drafted to replace (left null on the first revision of a product), and may declare `image_count`, the number of images the author claims the revision ships, which defaults to 0 and may be zero or larger. The platform stamps `revision_no` as one more than the number of revisions the product already has at creation time — so numbering starts at 1 and is unique within a product, which means "the latest revision" is never ambiguous — sets `state` to `DRAFT`, records `author_user_id` from the calling `X-User-ID`, and leaves `pin_count` at 0. Opening a revision immediately recomputes `completeness_score_declared` for every listing of the product, since the new revision becomes the declared source the instant it exists. Revisions can be listed filtered by `product_id`, by `supersedes_content_version_id` to walk the chain forward, or by `state`.

A revision's lifecycle has four states — `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `PINNED` and `SUPERSEDED` — and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: a superseded revision can never be pinned again and can never be transmitted again. Movement between them is never requested directly; it is always a consequence of governance, described in §3.5.

Attribute values are written one key at a time onto a revision, and only while that revision is `DRAFT`. Writing against a revision in `IN_REVIEW`, `PINNED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT` — this is the content freeze that makes a review meaningful. The payload is an `attribute_key`, which must resolve in the global taxonomy, and a `value` of between 1 and 2000 characters. Writes are upserts: at most one row exists per attribute key per revision, and a second write on the same key replaces the existing row rather than creating a second one. The platform derives two fields from the payload. `char_count` is the length of `value` after leading and trailing whitespace has been stripped. `type_valid` is true when that same stripped value parses as the `data_type` declared for the key in the taxonomy, and false otherwise; a row with `type_valid` false can never earn certified completeness credit, though it is still stored.

Two content screens run at write time, and both are keyed on the stripped value. A prohibited-claim screen performs a case-insensitive substring match for the tokens *cures*, *fda approved* and *100% guaranteed*; a match anywhere in the stripped value is rejected with `ERR_PROHIBITED_CLAIM`. A length screen then requires `char_count` to be at most the `max_chars` declared on the taxonomy attribute, rejecting with `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_LENGTH_EXCEEDED` otherwise. Note carefully what the length screen does **not** do: a retailer's `max_chars_override` is not a write-time limit. An author is free to write a value that exceeds a particular retailer's ceiling; the consequence is that the value silently withholds certified credit for that retailer, not that the write fails. Because prohibited claims are caught here, at authoring time, no additional content screen runs later when the revision is submitted for review. A successful attribute-value write recomputes `completeness_score_declared`, `completeness_score_certified` and `readiness_score` for every listing of the revision's product, and then the product's `shelf_health_index`. A revision's attribute values can be read back as a list.

### 3.3. Rich Media Assets and Retailer Media Decisions

Rich media is registered against a product, not against a revision, because the same hero shot serves every revision and every retailer. A `ContentAuthor` registers an asset with its `product_id`, its `asset_role` — one of `HERO_IMAGE`, `SECONDARY_IMAGE`, `VIDEO` or `A_PLUS_MODULE` — and its `source_uri`, the brand-side URI of the master file, between 1 and 500 characters. Only `HERO_IMAGE` and `SECONDARY_IMAGE` count as image roles for the purposes of the certified completeness image component and the retailer's `min_image_count` gate; `VIDEO` and `A_PLUS_MODULE` assets are deployable as enhanced content but never satisfy an image minimum. A newly registered asset has `approval_state` `PENDING`, `approving_retailer_id` null and `decided_at` null. Assets can be listed filtered by `product_id`, by `approval_state`, or by `approving_retailer_id`.

Each asset then needs a retailer's blessing before it counts for anything. A `RetailerIntegration` principal supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` against a `PENDING` asset, moving `approval_state` to `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` respectively, recording `approving_retailer_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and stamping `decided_at` from the logical clock. `REJECTED` is terminal for an asset. Two guards apply. First, a `RetailerListing` must already exist for the pair formed by the asset's `product_id` and the acting `X-User-ID` — a retailer that does not carry the product cannot express an opinion on its media, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; this is step 4 of §2.8 and so runs before the request body is examined. Second, the asset must still be `PENDING`: an asset whose `approval_state` has already moved to `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` cannot be decided again, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409). That second guard is a state check on the target row, so it is evaluated after the body-shape check of §2.8 step 8, alongside the other state guards of steps 24 through 34.

The approval branch and the rejection branch differ in exactly one respect, and it is worth stating both sides explicitly. On `APPROVE`, the newly approved asset raises the approved image-role count for the product, so `completeness_score_certified` and `readiness_score` of the listing joining that product to the acting retailer are recomputed on the spot. On `REJECT`, `approving_retailer_id` and `decided_at` are recorded exactly as on approval, but no certified score changes, because a rejected asset never counts toward anything.

### 3.4. Retailer Listings and the Two Completeness Variants

A `RetailerListing` is the junction row that says "this product is carried by this retailer". A `ContentAuthor` opens one by naming `product_id` and `retailer_id`, and may optionally name `account_team_user_id`, the opaque identifier of the `AccountTeamLead` who owns follow-up for this relationship; it defaults to null, and whatever it holds is copied onto every shelf alert the listing later raises. At most one listing may exist per product-and-retailer pair, and a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LISTING`. A listing may only be opened against a retailer whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; against a `PAUSED` or `OFFBOARDED` retailer the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A new listing starts with `syndication_state` `NOT_SYNDICATED`, `shelf_state` `OK`, `pinned_content_version_id` null, `in_stock` true, `open_blocking_error_count` 0, and `search_rank_latest`, `content_accuracy_pct` and `rating_avg` all null because nothing has been observed yet. Both completeness variants and the readiness score are computed immediately at creation, which in practice yields 0.00 for the certified variant while nothing is pinned. Listings can be listed filtered by either side of the junction — `product_id` or `retailer_id` — or by `pinned_content_version_id` to find every listing certified on a given revision, or by either state dimension via `syndication_state` and `shelf_state`.

The two state dimensions on a listing are genuinely independent and never write each other. `syndication_state` moves through `NOT_SYNDICATED`, `PENDING`, `LIVE` and `ERROR_HOLD` and is driven entirely by the syndication job lifecycle and by the closure of rejection errors. `shelf_state` moves through `OK`, `OOS_WATCH`, `UNAUTHORIZED` and `SUSPENDED` and is driven entirely by crawl observations and by account-team decisions. A listing can perfectly well be `LIVE` on the feed dimension and `UNAUTHORIZED` on the shelf dimension at the same time.

**The two completeness variants.** The heart of the platform is that the same content is scored twice, against the same retailer requirement set but with two different inclusion tests. Both variants begin by assembling the scoring set: every requirement row belonging to the listing's retailer whose `requirement_level` is `REQUIRED` or `RECOMMENDED`, with `OPTIONAL` rows discarded. Each row in that set contributes a weight, which is its `weight_override` when that is present and the taxonomy's `base_weight` for its attribute key otherwise. The denominator both variants divide by is the sum of those weights plus a fixed **image weight of 20**, which is always present in the denominator whether or not the retailer demands any images at all. If that denominator comes out as zero — which happens only when the retailer has declared no scoring requirement rows and the image weight has therefore been added to nothing — the score is 0.00 and no further work is done.

Where the variants part company is the revision each reads and the test each applies:

- The **declared** variant is the author-facing view. Its source is the content revision of the listing's product with the highest `revision_no`, regardless of what state that revision is in — a `DRAFT`, an `IN_REVIEW`, a `PINNED` or even a `SUPERSEDED` revision is equally eligible, and because `revision_no` is unique within a product there is never a tie to break. When the product has no revision at all, the declared score is 0.00. A requirement row earns full credit for its weight when the source revision carries an attribute value on that key whose `char_count` is at least 1 — presence alone, nothing more. Every retailer character ceiling is ignored, and the type check is ignored.
- The **certified** variant is the retailer-facing view, and it is the one that gates distribution. Its source is strictly the revision the listing has pinned, named by `pinned_content_version_id`; when nothing is pinned the certified score is 0.00. A requirement row earns credit only when the pinned revision carries an attribute value on that key with `char_count` at least 1, **and** that row's `type_valid` is true, **and** its `char_count` is at most the effective retailer ceiling for the key — the requirement row's `max_chars_override` when that is present, and the taxonomy attribute's `max_chars` otherwise.

The image weight is the one component that earns partial credit, and both variants share the same shape for it while counting images differently. The declared variant's image count is simply the source revision's claimed `image_count`. The certified variant's image count is the number of media assets on the listing's product whose `asset_role` is `HERO_IMAGE` or `SECONDARY_IMAGE` and whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED`. Given a count, the image component is the full image weight of 20 when the retailer's `min_image_count` is zero; otherwise it is 20 multiplied by the smaller of 1 and the ratio of the count to `min_image_count`. So three approved images against a minimum of three earn the whole 20, two against a minimum of three earn two thirds of it, and five against a minimum of three still earn only 20 — the ratio is capped at 1 and never rewards overshoot.

Each variant's numerator is then the sum, over the scoring set, of each row's weight multiplied by that variant's credit for the row, plus that variant's image component. The score is 100 multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Numerator and denominator are carried exactly; nothing is rounded before the final division. The declared result is stored as `completeness_score_declared` and the certified result as `completeness_score_certified`, and both are recomputed on every write that can change any of their inputs: opening a revision, writing an attribute value, declaring a retailer requirement, adjusting a retailer's envelope, a retailer's media decision, and the pin write performed by a change-request approval.

A dedicated diagnostic read puts the two variants side by side for one listing and explains the gap. It echoes the `retailer_listing_id`, reports `declared_score` and `certified_score`, names the revision each variant read as `declared_source_content_version_id` and `certified_source_content_version_id` (either of which is null when that variant had no source), reports `requirement_weight_total` as the shared denominator described above — the summed weights of the scoring set plus the image weight of 20 — and reports `declared_image_component` and `certified_image_component` as the two image contributions actually credited. It also reports two diagnostic arrays, both computed against the certified source and both sorted in ascending alphabetical order of attribute key: `missing_required_keys` lists the keys of `REQUIRED` requirement rows for which the pinned revision carries no attribute value with at least one character, and `over_limit_keys` lists the keys in the scoring set whose pinned value exceeds the effective retailer ceiling. When nothing is pinned, `missing_required_keys` contains every `REQUIRED` key of the retailer and `over_limit_keys` is empty.

**Readiness.** Alongside the two completeness variants each listing carries a `readiness_score`, the single number a feed operator looks at before transmitting. It blends 60 percent of the listing's `completeness_score_certified` with 40 percent of a floored accuracy figure, then subtracts a penalty for unresolved blocking feed errors and adds a bonus for live enhanced content, clamps the result to the range 0 to 100, and rounds half up to 2 decimal places. The accuracy figure is the listing's `content_accuracy_pct`, treated as 0.00 while no observation has ever been recorded; and it is floored — meaning it is replaced by the smaller of itself and 60.00 — whenever the listing's `shelf_state` is anything other than `OK`. A listing sitting on `OOS_WATCH`, `UNAUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED` therefore cannot present better than 60.00 accuracy to the blend no matter what the crawler saw. The penalty tiers keyed on `open_blocking_error_count` are given in §3.7 and the enhanced-content bonus in §3.8. Readiness is recomputed on every write that touches any of those inputs: a completeness recompute, an observation ingest, a shelf-status change, the closure of a rejection error, and any deployment state change.

### 3.5. Content Change Governance: Two Review Tracks, One Join, Four Cascade Hops

No revision ever becomes certified for a retailer by an author's own act. A `ContentAuthor` raises a governed change request naming the `product_id`, the single `retailer_id` whose listing the request will certify, the `target_content_version_id` of the revision proposed for certification, and a `rationale` of between 1 and 500 characters recorded for audit. The target revision must be in `DRAFT` at this point or the request is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. A newly raised request sits in `state` `DRAFT` with `legal_review_state` and `brand_review_state` both `PENDING`, `legal_reviewer_user_id` and `brand_reviewer_user_id` both null, `submitted_at` and `decided_at` both null, and `author_user_id` recorded from the calling `X-User-ID` — the value that will later bar this user from deciding either of its two tracks. Change requests can be listed filtered by `product_id`, by `retailer_id`, by `target_content_version_id`, or by `state`.

Submission is the author's last act on the request. Its four preconditions are evaluated strictly in the order fixed by §2.8, which is the order they are listed here: a listing must already exist for the request's product-and-retailer pair (otherwise `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, §2.8 step 13) — there is no point certifying content for a retailer that does not carry the product; the named retailer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`, step 24); the target revision must still be `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`, step 25); and the request itself must still be `DRAFT` (otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`, step 26). The consequence of step 25 preceding step 26 is worth spelling out: because a first submission simultaneously freezes the target revision, resubmitting a request that is already `IN_REVIEW` reports `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT` rather than `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_DRAFT`, and step 26 is reached only where the revision is `DRAFT` while the request is not — as it is for a request the reject branch has left in `REJECTED` with its revision reopened. Submission moves the request `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and **forks** the review into two independently decidable dimensions by leaving both `legal_review_state` and `brand_review_state` at `PENDING`. It simultaneously moves the target revision `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, which is the content freeze: from this moment any further attribute-value write against that revision is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_DRAFT`. No fresh content screen runs at submission, because prohibited claims were already rejected at attribute-value write time.

A review decision names the `track` — `LEGAL` or `BRAND` — the `decision` — `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` — and `expected_product_version`. Six conditions guard it, in the order given in §2.8: the acting role must own the named track, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `author_user_id`, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from whichever reviewer identifier the other track has already recorded, the request must be `IN_REVIEW`, the named track must still be `PENDING` (otherwise `ERR_TRACK_ALREADY_DECIDED`), and `expected_product_version` must equal the product's current `version`. Whichever way the decision goes, the acting identity is recorded as `legal_reviewer_user_id` or `brand_reviewer_user_id` according to the track, the product's `last_review_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the product's `version` increments by 1.

**The self-loop.** When the decision is `APPROVED` and the other track is still `PENDING`, the request's aggregate `state` stays `IN_REVIEW`. Only the named dimension moves to `APPROVED`. Nothing is pinned, nothing is superseded, no job is invalidated, and no revision's `pin_count` changes. This is the case operators most often misread: the request looks decided from the perspective of one reviewer and is not decided at all.

**The join.** When a decision is `APPROVED` and, after applying it, both `legal_review_state` and `brand_review_state` are `APPROVED`, the request advances `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED` — a terminal state — and a four-hop cascade fires inside the same request.

- *Hop 1 — pin.* The request's `state` becomes `APPROVED` and its `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The target revision transitions `IN_REVIEW → PINNED`. The listing joining the request's product and retailer has its `pinned_content_version_id` repointed to that revision. The product's `last_pinned_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `status` becomes `ACTIVE`.
- *Hop 2 — supersede, caused by hop 1's pin write.* Whatever revision that listing's `pinned_content_version_id` previously named, if any, transitions `PINNED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `pin_count` recomputed downward, losing exactly the one listing reference it held. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal; that revision can never be pinned or transmitted again.
- *Hop 3 — invalidate, caused by hop 2's supersede write.* Every syndication job still in `QUEUED` whose `content_version_id` is the newly superseded revision transitions `QUEUED → INVALIDATED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Jobs already in `TRANSMITTED` are deliberately **not** invalidated — the retailer already holds the payload.
- *Hop 4 — release, caused by hop 3's invalidation writes.* Each invalidated job releases its `slot_count`, so the owning retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed from the ledger and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. Where a listing is left with no job in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED` and an `open_blocking_error_count` of zero, its `syndication_state` returns to `NOT_SYNDICATED`.

Alongside the four hops, the join recomputes `completeness_score_certified`, `completeness_score_declared` and `readiness_score` on each touched listing, recomputes the newly pinned revision's `pin_count` upward, recomputes the product's `shelf_health_index`, and — like every review decision — stamps `last_review_at` and increments the product's `version`.

**The reject branch.** When a decision is `REJECTED` on either track, the outcome is deliberately asymmetric to the join, and it short-circuits: the named dimension becomes `REJECTED` and the request's aggregate `state` becomes `REJECTED` immediately, **even while the other track is still `PENDING`**. `decided_at` is stamped. The target revision transitions `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, reopening it for editing. Nothing else moves: the listing's `pinned_content_version_id` is left exactly as it was, no revision is superseded, no job is invalidated, and no `pin_count` changes on any revision. `REJECTED` is terminal for the request itself, but not for the revision — the very same revision can be proposed again through a brand-new change request, which is why the revision's own lifecycle is a cycle of `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` rather than a one-way path.

### 3.6. Feed Syndication: Queueing, Window Contention, Slots and Fees

A `SyndicationOperator` queues a transmission by naming the `retailer_listing_id` it targets, the `content_version_id` whose content will be sent, the `slot_count` of feed rows the transmission occupies (an integer from 1 to 10 inclusive), the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the transmission window, and `expected_retailer_version`. The new job begins in `state` `QUEUED` with `attempt_no` 1, `transmitted_at` and `responded_at` both null, and it drives the target listing's `syndication_state` from `NOT_SYNDICATED` to `PENDING`. A job's `state` is one of `QUEUED`, `TRANSMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `INVALIDATED` or `CANCELLED`, of which `ACCEPTED`, `INVALIDATED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Jobs can be listed filtered by `retailer_listing_id`, by `content_version_id`, or by `state`.

Queueing is the most heavily guarded write in the platform, and its guards run in the order fixed by §2.8. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. The retailer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The listing's `shelf_state` must be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED` — a listing under unauthorized-seller enforcement is not a listing you push more content to. The named revision must be `PINNED` and its `product_id` must equal the listing's `product_id`, otherwise `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PINNED`; only certified content goes on a feed, and a `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `SUPERSEDED` revision is refused. The listing's `completeness_score_certified` must be at least 85.00, otherwise `ERR_COMPLETENESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; a listing sitting at exactly 85.00 passes.

Two capacity guards then apply, and their geometries are different in a way that matters. The **concurrent-window** guard counts the retailer's jobs — across all of its listings, not just this one — that are in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED` and whose window intersects the requested window, and requires that count to be strictly fewer than the retailer's `max_concurrent_feed_windows`; exceeding it is `ERR_FEED_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. This comparison treats both windows as **closed intervals**, including both endpoints, so a new window that begins at the exact instant an existing window ends **does** count as intersecting. The **slot capacity** guard requires that the retailer's current `feed_slots_reserved` plus the requested `slot_count` is at most `feed_slot_capacity`; landing exactly on capacity is allowed, and exceeding it is `ERR_FEED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Finally `expected_retailer_version` must match the retailer's current `version`, or the queue is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

On success, the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` grows by the job's `slot_count` and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. Two figures are frozen onto the job at this moment: `readiness_at_queue` captures the listing's readiness score as it stands, and `service_fee_cents` captures the feed fee computed below. The revision's `pin_count` is deliberately **not** touched — a job references a revision, it does not pin it.

**The feed service fee.** The fee is computed in five ordered steps, and the order is pinned; it must not be iterated to a fixed point. First, the subtotal in cents is the per-slot rate of 2500 cents — that is, 25 dollars per feed slot — multiplied by `slot_count`. Second, a volume discount percentage is selected **from that subtotal**: no discount when the subtotal is below 5000 cents, 5 percent when the subtotal is at least 5000 and below 15000 cents, 10 percent when it is at least 15000 and below 25000 cents, and 15 percent when it is 25000 cents or more. Third, the discounted amount is the subtotal multiplied by one minus the discount percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, a rush surcharge percentage is selected: the transmission counts as **rush** when the gap between `window_start` and the logical clock's current instant is strictly less than 24 hours; a non-rush transmission carries no surcharge, a rush transmission carries 20 percent when the discounted amount is below 20000 cents, and a rush transmission carries 12 percent when the discounted amount is 20000 cents or more. The surcharge tier is keyed on the **discounted amount before any surcharge**, never on the final total — keying it on the total would be circular, because the surcharge itself can push the total across the 20000-cent boundary. Fifth, the surcharge amount is the discounted amount multiplied by the surcharge percentage, rounded half up to whole cents, and the fee written to `service_fee_cents` is the discounted amount plus that surcharge amount.

Transmitting a job is a plain state advance available to a `SyndicationOperator`: the job must be in `QUEUED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`, and the retailer's `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The job moves `QUEUED → TRANSMITTED` and `transmitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Slots stay reserved, because `TRANSMITTED` is still a holding state of the slot ledger. No completeness or image gate runs at transmission — the certified-score threshold was already enforced at queue time.

Cancellation is available while a job is in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED` and moves it to the terminal `CANCELLED`; from any other state the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`. Cancellation releases the whole reservation: the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed from the ledger, dropping the job's `slot_count`, and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. Every `OPEN` rejection error still attached to the cancelled job is moved to `WAIVED` with `resolution_note` set to `CANCELLED_WITH_JOB`. The listing's `syndication_state` then settles: it becomes `ERROR_HOLD` when the listing still has a positive `open_blocking_error_count`, becomes `NOT_SYNDICATED` when the listing has no other job in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`, and is otherwise left unchanged.

### 3.7. Retailer Feed Responses, Rejection Errors and the Requeue Cycle

Confirmation comes from the retailer's own integration account, acting on a job that is in `TRANSMITTED`; from any other state the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE`, and from an account whose `X-User-ID` is not the retailer owning the job's listing it is `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The response carries an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED`, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, and optionally an `errors` array of per-row complaints. Each element of `errors` carries a `retailer_error_code` of 1 to 40 characters as returned by the retailer's feed processor, an optional `attribute_key` naming the attribute the retailer flagged, which defaults to null and must resolve in the global taxonomy when supplied (otherwise `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_KEY_UNKNOWN`), a `severity` of `BLOCKING` or `WARNING`, and a `message` of 1 to 500 characters carrying the retailer's human-readable reason. The three outcomes are mutually exclusive in the shape of `errors` they admit: `ACCEPTED` requires `errors` to be absent or empty, `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` requires at least one element with every element carrying `severity` `WARNING`, and `REJECTED` requires at least one element with at least one carrying `severity` `BLOCKING`. Each element becomes one rejection error row, created in request order, each starting in `state` `OPEN` with `resolution_mode` and `resolved_by_user_id` null. A job's rejection error rows can be read back as a list, optionally filtered by `severity` and by `state`.

Whichever outcome arrives, `responded_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the reservation is released — the job leaves the two holding states, so the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed from the ledger and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. From there the three branches diverge, and each deserves its own accounting:

- On `ACCEPTED` the job reaches its terminal `ACCEPTED` state and the listing's `syndication_state` moves `PENDING → LIVE`. The listing's `pinned_content_version_id` is emphatically **not** touched — pinning is owned exclusively by the change-request join. Because a `LIVE` listing now qualifies for the product's health input set, both the listing's `readiness_score` and the product's `shelf_health_index` are recomputed.
- On `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` the job lands in `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, one `OPEN` rejection error row is created per element in request order, and the listing's `syndication_state` still moves `PENDING → LIVE`, because by construction no `BLOCKING` row exists. The listing's `open_blocking_error_count` is therefore unchanged.
- On `REJECTED` a four-hop cascade fires. *Hop 1:* the job's `state` becomes `REJECTED`, `responded_at` is stamped, one rejection error row is created per element of `errors` in request order, the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` drops by the job's `slot_count` and the retailer's `version` increments by 1. *Hop 2, caused by hop 1's `BLOCKING` rows:* the listing's `open_blocking_error_count` is recomputed and is now positive, so the listing's `syndication_state` becomes `ERROR_HOLD` and its `readiness_score` is recomputed with the new penalty tier. *Hop 3, caused by hop 2's `ERROR_HOLD` write, which admits the listing into the health input set:* the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed, and when the recomputed index falls below 60.00 a shelf alert with `alert_type` `HEALTH_DEGRADED`, `severity` `MEDIUM` and a null `source_observation_id` is created on that listing — suppressed when a `HEALTH_DEGRADED` alert is already `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` there. *Hop 4, caused by hop 3's alert row:* the product's `open_alert_count` is recomputed and its `governance_flag` becomes `CONTENT_AT_RISK`, or `LISTING_RISK` when an `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` alert is also open, and the product's `version` increments by 1.

**Blocking-error penalty tiers.** The `open_blocking_error_count` a listing carries is the number of rejection errors with `severity` `BLOCKING` and `state` `OPEN` across every syndication job of that listing, not merely the most recent one. It selects the readiness penalty described in §3.4 in four bands: no penalty at all when the count is zero, 5 points when the count is 1 or 2, 12 points when the count is 3, 4 or 5, and 25 points once the count reaches 6 or more.

Closing a rejection error is how a listing digs itself out. The row must be in `OPEN`, otherwise the attempt is `ERR_REJECTION_ERROR_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The caller supplies a `resolution_mode` of `FIX` — meaning the underlying content was repaired — or `WAIVE` — meaning the defect was accepted as-is — together with a `resolution_note`. A `SyndicationOperator` may `FIX` any row and may `WAIVE` a `WARNING` row, but a `WAIVE` against a row whose `severity` is `BLOCKING` is reserved to `ShelfAdmin` and otherwise fails with `ERR_WAIVE_NOT_PERMITTED`. A `FIX` moves the row `OPEN → RESOLVED` and a `WAIVE` moves it `OPEN → WAIVED`; both are terminal, both record `resolved_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and stamp `resolved_at` from the logical clock alongside the supplied `resolution_note`, and — this is the important symmetry — both have exactly the same downstream effect. The listing's `open_blocking_error_count` is recomputed and its `readiness_score` is recomputed, possibly dropping to a gentler penalty tier. A waived blocking row stops blocking just as completely as a fixed one. When the closing call takes `open_blocking_error_count` to zero and no job of the listing is in `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED`, the listing's `syndication_state` moves `ERROR_HOLD → NOT_SYNDICATED` and its readiness is recomputed with no penalty at all, which in turn changes whether the listing qualifies for the product's health input set.

**The requeue cycle.** A job that ended in `REJECTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` is not dead; it cycles. A `SyndicationOperator` requeues it, optionally supplying a fresh `slot_count`, a fresh `window_start`, a fresh `window_end` and `expected_retailer_version`; any value supplied overwrites the corresponding field on the job, and any value omitted leaves the existing one in place. Requeue is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_JOB_STATE` from any state other than `REJECTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED`, and with `ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED` while any `BLOCKING` rejection error of the job is still `OPEN` rather than `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED`. It then re-runs every gate the original queue ran, against the new window and slot figures: `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, the retailer still `ACTIVE`, the listing's `shelf_state` neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED`, the job's content revision still `PINNED` — a revision superseded in the meantime can never be requeued, so `ERR_VERSION_NOT_PINNED` is the correct outcome there — the listing's `completeness_score_certified` still at least 85.00, the closed-interval concurrent-window count still below the retailer's `max_concurrent_feed_windows`, reserved slots plus the requested slots still within `feed_slot_capacity`, and `expected_retailer_version` matching the retailer's current `version`.

A successful requeue moves the job back to `QUEUED`, increments `attempt_no` by 1, resets `transmitted_at` and `responded_at` to null, and re-reserves the slots so the retailer's `feed_slots_reserved` is recomputed upward and its `version` increments by 1. Both frozen figures are re-frozen at the current clock position: `readiness_at_queue` recaptures the listing's readiness as it now stands, and `service_fee_cents` is recomputed from scratch — which is how a requeue into a window less than 24 hours away picks up a rush surcharge the original attempt never paid. The listing's `syndication_state` becomes `PENDING`, coming from `NOT_SYNDICATED` when the previous attempt was rejected and its blocking errors were subsequently cleared, or from `LIVE` when the previous attempt was partially accepted. That second path has a consequence worth spelling out: a `LIVE → PENDING` move takes the listing out of the product's health input set until the new attempt resolves, so the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed on the way through.

### 3.8. Enhanced Content Deployment Windows

Enhanced content — an A+ page, a comparison chart, a video block — is published onto a listing for a bounded display period rather than indefinitely. A `ContentAuthor` creates a deployment by naming the `retailer_listing_id` whose placement calendar it occupies, the `change_request_id` of the approved governance record authorising it, the `media_asset_id` of the approved rich-media asset being deployed, the `module_kind` — one of `A_PLUS_PAGE`, `COMPARISON_CHART` or `VIDEO_BLOCK` — the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the placement, and `expected_retailer_listing_version`. The window is **half-open**: `window_start` is inclusive and `window_end` is exclusive, and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start` or the request fails with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. Deployments can be listed filtered by `retailer_listing_id`, by `change_request_id`, by `media_asset_id`, or by `state`.

Six substantive gates guard creation, evaluated in the order given in §2.8. The retailer must be `ACTIVE`. The listing's `shelf_state` must be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `UNAUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED`. The cited change request must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_APPROVED` — enhanced content rides on governance that has already cleared both review tracks. The cited change request's `retailer_id` and `product_id` must both match the listing's, and the cited asset's `product_id` must match the listing's `product_id` too; any of those three mismatching yields `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The cited asset's `approval_state` must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_MEDIA_ASSET_NOT_APPROVED`. And the number of media assets on the product whose `asset_role` is `HERO_IMAGE` or `SECONDARY_IMAGE` and whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED` must be at least the retailer's `min_image_count`, otherwise `ERR_IMAGE_COUNT_BELOW_MINIMUM`.

The placement calendar itself is guarded by a pairwise interval test, and its geometry is half-open rather than closed. The new window may not intersect the window of any deployment on the **same listing** that is currently in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`; deployments in `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` are ignored entirely. Because both intervals are half-open, two windows that merely touch — where an existing deployment's `window_end` equals the new `window_start` — do **not** intersect and are allowed, so a listing can carry back-to-back placements with no gap. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Finally `expected_retailer_listing_version` must equal the listing's current `version`, or the creation fails with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

The initial state of a successful deployment is decided by the retailer's configuration, not by the request. When the retailer's `enhanced_content_requires_approval` is true, the deployment is created in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` and awaits a decision. When it is false, the very same request creates the deployment directly in `LIVE` with `retailer_decision_at` left null, because no decision was ever required. Either way the listing's `version` increments by 1, and the `pin_count` of the revision named by the change request's `target_content_version_id` is recomputed upward, because an active deployment is a second kind of live reference to a revision. When the deployment lands directly in `LIVE`, the listing's `readiness_score` is additionally recomputed with the enhanced-content bonus and the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed.

That bonus is a flat **5 points** added to the readiness blend described in §3.4, and it is earned by the existence of at least one deployment of the listing in `LIVE`. A deployment sitting in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` earns nothing — waiting for approval is not the same as being published.

A `RetailerIntegration` principal decides a pending deployment by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. The deployment must be in `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL`, otherwise `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be the retailer owning the deployment's listing, otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On `APPROVE` the deployment moves `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL → LIVE`, `retailer_decision_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the listing's `readiness_score` is recomputed now carrying the 5-point bonus, the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed, and the listing's `version` increments by 1. On `REJECT` the deployment moves `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL → REJECTED`, which is terminal; `retailer_decision_at` is stamped exactly as on approval, the revision's `pin_count` is recomputed downward because that reference is gone, and the placement window is freed for another deployment. A rejected deployment never earns the readiness bonus, so no bonus is removed.

Withdrawal is the author's compensating action and is available from either `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` or `LIVE`; from `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` the attempt is `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. An optional `withdrawn_reason` is recorded on the row and defaults to null. The deployment moves to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, the revision's `pin_count` is recomputed downward — returning the pin ledger to precisely its pre-creation value, which is the point of calling withdrawal a compensating action — the listing's `readiness_score` is recomputed with the bonus removed, the product's `shelf_health_index` is recomputed, and the listing's `version` increments by 1. Withdrawal from `PENDING_RETAILER_APPROVAL` and withdrawal from `LIVE` are specified identically; the only practical difference is that in the pending case there was never a bonus in the score to remove.

### 3.9. Digital Shelf Monitoring, Alerting and Performance Correlation

Monitoring enters the platform through the crawl. A `ShelfAdmin` ingests one observation against one listing, carrying `observed_at` — the instant the crawl saw the shelf — `in_stock` as observed on the retailer's product page, `content_accuracy_pct` as the percentage of certified attributes rendered correctly (between 0.00 and 100.00 to 2 decimal places), `rating_avg` as the average star rating observed (between 0.00 and 5.00 to 2 decimal places), `rating_count` as the number of ratings observed (zero or more), `unauthorized_seller_count` as the number of unauthorized sellers seen on the listing (zero or more), `conversion_rate_pct` as the conversion rate for the period ending at `observed_at` (between 0.00 and 100.00 to 2 decimal places), `units_sold` for that same period (zero or more), and an optional `search_rank` giving the keyword search position seen on the retailer's site, which must be at least 1 when supplied and defaults to null to mean the product did not appear at all. Observations are append-only and can be read back as the listing's chronological log.

**Projection.** Before any alert rule is evaluated, the listing's shelf snapshot is re-projected from its observation log. The latest observation is determined by sorting the listing's observations by `observed_at` ascending and breaking ties on `created_at` ascending — never by `id` — and taking the last row. That row's values are copied verbatim onto the listing with no rounding: `search_rank` into `search_rank_latest`, and `content_accuracy_pct`, `rating_avg` and `in_stock` into the like-named listing fields. Because ties are broken by insertion order, two observations sharing the same `observed_at` resolve in favour of the later-inserted row. The consequence for backfill is deliberate: a late-arriving observation whose `observed_at` is older than the current latest updates nothing at all on the listing, yet it still participates fully in the conversion-lift analysis below.

**Alert rules.** With the snapshot refreshed, the listing's `shelf_state` is re-derived under a fixed precedence — `SUSPENDED` outranks `UNAUTHORIZED`, which outranks `OOS_WATCH`, which outranks `OK` — so an observation that is simultaneously out of stock and shows unauthorized sellers lands the listing on `UNAUTHORIZED`, and an observation of any shape leaves a listing that an account team has already `SUSPENDED` exactly where it is. Three monitoring rules then fire, and every alert they raise is de-duplicated: a rule never creates a second alert of the same `alert_type` on the same listing while one of that type is already `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`.

- An observation with `in_stock` false and `unauthorized_seller_count` zero moves an `OK` listing to `OOS_WATCH` and raises an alert with `alert_type` `OUT_OF_STOCK`, `severity` `MEDIUM`, and `source_observation_id` pointing at the observation that triggered it.
- An observation with `unauthorized_seller_count` of 1 or more moves the listing to `UNAUTHORIZED` — provided it is not already `SUSPENDED` — and raises an alert with `alert_type` `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` and `severity` `HIGH`, whose `assigned_account_team_user_id` is copied from the listing's `account_team_user_id` and is null when the listing has no named owner. The product's `governance_flag` becomes `LISTING_RISK`. From this point on, queueing a job or creating a deployment against the listing is refused with `ERR_LISTING_SHELF_BLOCKED` until an account team clears it.
- An observation whose `content_accuracy_pct` is below 85.00 raises an alert with `alert_type` `CONTENT_DRIFT` and `severity` `LOW`. Content drift does not move `shelf_state`; it is a content-quality signal, not an availability one.

A fourth alert type is not raised by an observation's own values but by the health recompute that follows it. An alert with `alert_type` `HEALTH_DEGRADED` and `severity` `MEDIUM` is raised on the listing only when the listing is actually inside the product's health input set — that is, its `syndication_state` is `LIVE` or `ERROR_HOLD` — **and** the recomputed `shelf_health_index` is below 60.00. A `NOT_SYNDICATED` or `PENDING` listing never raises it, which is precisely why the empty-input-set index of 0.00 is not an alert condition.

Recovery through observation is automatic in one specific case. An observation with `in_stock` true and `unauthorized_seller_count` zero moves an `OOS_WATCH` listing back to `OK`, and every `OUT_OF_STOCK` alert of that listing still in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` is auto-transitioned to `RESOLVED` with `resolution_note` set to the literal string `AUTO_CLEARED_BY_OBSERVATION` and `resolved_at` stamped from the logical clock. Unauthorized-seller alerts are never cleared this way; they require a human.

Every ingest closes by recomputing the listing's `readiness_score`, the product's `shelf_health_index`, the product's `conversion_lift_pct` under the default variant, the product's `open_alert_count` and `governance_flag`, and incrementing the product's `version` by 1.

**Account-team follow-up.** An `AccountTeamLead` owns the human half of the loop. The shelf-status action supplies a target `shelf_state` of either `SUSPENDED` or `OK`. Clearing to `OK` is permitted from `UNAUTHORIZED`, `OOS_WATCH` or `SUSPENDED`, leaves `syndication_state` untouched, and resolves every `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING` alert of the listing still in `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` with `resolution_note` set to the literal string `AUTO_CLEARED_BY_ACCOUNT_TEAM`; it then recomputes the listing's `readiness_score`, the product's `open_alert_count` and `governance_flag`, and increments the product's `version` by 1. Suspending is permitted from any state other than `SUSPENDED`, leaves `syndication_state` untouched, and recomputes `readiness_score` — which now floors the accuracy input at 60.00, because the listing is no longer `OK`.

Alerts themselves have a three-state lifecycle. Acknowledging one moves it `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED` and records `acknowledged_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`; the product's `open_alert_count` is deliberately **unchanged**, because an acknowledged alert is still open follow-up. Resolving one is permitted from `OPEN` or from `ACKNOWLEDGED`, moves it to the terminal `RESOLVED`, records the supplied `resolution_note`, stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock, then recomputes the product's `open_alert_count` downward and its `governance_flag`, and increments the product's `version` by 1. Acknowledging an already-acknowledged alert, or acting on a `RESOLVED` one, is rejected with `ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. Alerts can be listed filtered by `retailer_listing_id`, by `source_observation_id`, by `alert_type`, or by `state`.

**Product roll-up.** The product's `open_alert_count` is the number of alerts across every listing of the product whose `state` is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Its `governance_flag` is derived from that same set under a pinned precedence, evaluated top to bottom: it is `LISTING_RISK` when any alert in the set has `alert_type` `UNAUTHORIZED_LISTING`; otherwise `CONTENT_AT_RISK` when the count is 1 or more; otherwise `NONE`. So resolving the only unauthorized-listing alert on a product that still has a drift alert open drops the flag from `LISTING_RISK` back to `CONTENT_AT_RISK` rather than to `NONE`.

**Cross-retailer shelf health.** The product's `shelf_health_index` aggregates only those of its listings whose `syndication_state` is `LIVE` or `ERROR_HOLD`; `NOT_SYNDICATED` and `PENDING` listings are excluded, and when no listing qualifies the index is 0.00 and the per-retailer breakdown is empty. Qualifying listings are processed in ascending `created_at` order, and each contributes exactly one subscore regardless of how many observations it has accumulated. A listing's subscore blends four components. Its accuracy component is `content_accuracy_pct`, treated as 0 when null. Its rank component is 0 when `search_rank_latest` is null, and otherwise 100 reduced by 5 points for every position below the first — so rank 1 scores 100, rank 6 scores 75, rank 11 scores 50 — with the result clamped to the range 0 to 100, meaning rank 21 and everything worse scores 0. Its availability component is 100 when `in_stock` is true and 0 otherwise. Its rating component is 20 multiplied by `rating_avg`, treated as 0 when null, which maps a perfect 5.00 rating to 100. The subscore is 0.45 of the accuracy component plus 0.25 of the rank component plus 0.20 of the availability component plus 0.10 of the rating component, **rounded half up to 2 decimal places at this point**. The index is then the arithmetic mean of those already-rounded subscores, rounded half up to 2 decimal places again. The two-stage rounding order is mandatory and is not the same as averaging the unrounded subscores.

**Content-to-conversion correlation.** The last analytical piece answers whether better content actually sells more. The input set is every observation belonging to any listing of the product, with no filter on listing state and no cap on observation age, ordered by `observed_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Those observations are split into a HIGH group and a BASE group by the selected variant. If either group is empty the lift is 0.00. Otherwise the mean `conversion_rate_pct` of each group is taken as an exact value, and if the BASE group's mean is zero the lift is 0.00. The lift is then 100 multiplied by the difference between the HIGH mean and the BASE mean, divided by the BASE mean, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — a single rounding, applied to the final percentage; the group means are never rounded.

The two variants split the same rows differently. The default variant, named `post_pin_window`, splits on pin time: observations whose `observed_at` is at or after the product's `last_pinned_at` form HIGH, and those strictly before it form BASE. When `last_pinned_at` is still null, every observation falls into BASE, HIGH is empty, and the lift is therefore 0.00. The alternative variant, named `quality_split`, ignores pin time entirely and splits on measured accuracy: observations whose `content_accuracy_pct` is at least 90.00 form HIGH and those below 90.00 form BASE. Because the same rows regroup, the two variants generally report different lifts from identical data. The `post_pin_window` figure is the one stored on the product as `conversion_lift_pct`, refreshed on every observation ingest for any listing of the product. Unlike every other score in the system, a lift figure is not clamped to a non-negative range: when the HIGH group converts worse than the BASE group the stored value is negative, carried to the same 2 decimal places.

Both the health index and the lift are also available as an on-demand computed read for a single product, which accepts an optional `lift_variant` query parameter defaulting to `post_pin_window`. The response echoes `product_id`, reports the two-stage-rounded `shelf_health_index` together with `qualifying_listing_count` and a `retailer_subscores` array giving the per-listing breakdown in the same ascending-`created_at` order used by the aggregation — each entry of that array identifies its listing with `retailer_listing_id` and `retailer_id`, carries the one already-rounded blended subscore in a field named `subscore`, and additionally spells out the four components that subscore blends as `accuracy_component`, `rank_component`, `availability_component` and `rating_component`, each of them the component value described above rendered to 2 decimal places rounded half up (so a listing observed in stock shows an `availability_component` of `100.00` regardless of what its own `subscore` came to) — and reports `conversion_lift_pct` alongside the `lift_variant` actually applied and the group sizes `high_group_size` and `base_group_size` that produced it.
