# Research Model Production & Welfare Oversight API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Producing research animal models at institutional scale is a compliance business as much as a breeding business. A production site operates barrier rooms whose health-status categorization determines which customer barriers may receive which animals; breeding colonies must be genetically monitored on a fixed surveillance cadence and institutionally authorized under an animal-care protocol whose approved quota bounds every animal ever placed under it; customer orders arrive with exacting per-sex quantities, age and weight windows, minimum health-status floors, and required delivery dates; and every shipment must preserve barrier integrity on dedicated, freshly sanitized vehicles whose transit windows cannot overlap. Around that operational core sit the oversight functions — protocol review with segregation of duties, welfare inspection of both production and customer sites with corrective-action escalation, custom model generation and cryopreservation services with intellectual-property terms — and the regulatory registration lifecycle that can suspend a facility mid-pipeline and cascade cancellations through scheduled transport.

This document specifies the REST API for that platform. It is written for the engineer who will implement the service: Section 2 fixes the system-wide contracts (authentication, response envelope, pagination, the logical clock, the barrier rank order, validation priority, optimistic concurrency, and numeric precision), Section 3 narrates the business workflows in which every rule, threshold, cascade, and calculation lives, Section 4 summarizes the endpoint surface, and Section 5 catalogs the error identifiers. All URL paths are mounted under the `/api` prefix with exactly one exception — the logical-clock control, documented in Section 2.5 — and all time semantics in the system are driven by that logical clock rather than by wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every API request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. The declared roles are exactly seven, spelled as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `FacilityManager`, `Veterinarian`, `IACUCReviewer`, `Customer`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, and `WelfareInspector`. A request in which either header is missing, or whose `X-User-Role` value is not one of these seven declared roles, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is authenticated but is not listed among the roles authorized for the target endpoint is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles divide the work as follows. A `PlatformAdmin` is the central operator: it registers facilities, strains, and customers; drives account status, facility registration status, protocol suspension and reinstatement, and contract milestones; and owns the logical clock. A `FacilityManager` runs a production site: rooms, colonies, cohorts, sourcing and allocation decisions, and protocol drafting and submission. A `Veterinarian` holds veterinary authority: health surveillance screens and their results, genetic SNP panels, quarantine imposition and lifting, and culls. An `IACUCReviewer` sits on the institutional animal care and use committee and decides protocol reviews; by segregation of duties it may not decide a protocol it submitted. A `Customer` is a research-institution account holder that places and cancels its own orders, executes its own contracts, and performs receiving-protocol acceptance on its own shipments. A `LogisticsCoordinator` plans transport: it creates shipments on dedicated barrier-certified vehicles and drives them through loading, departure, and arrival. A `WelfareInspector` inspects production and customer sites; by segregation of duties it may not verify corrective closure of a report it filed. Any authenticated role may call any read (GET) endpoint, except where a customer-scoped collection restricts the `Customer` role to its own records as described in Section 3.

Role identity binds to resources in two distinct ways. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — for orders and contracts directly against their `customer_id`, and for shipments transitively through the shipment's order. A `Customer`-role caller whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the target resource's owning customer (its own customer record and sub-collections, its orders and their allocations and shipments, its contracts, its site inspections) is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `FacilityManager`, `Veterinarian`, `IACUCReviewer`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, or `WelfareInspector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on an `IACUCProtocol` when the protocol is submitted, or as `reported_by_user_id` on a `WelfareInspection` when the report is filed — but it does not compare it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers nonetheless ground the segregation-of-duties rules: the operator who submitted a protocol may not approve or reject it, and the inspector who filed a report may not verify its corrective closure; either attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10 when omitted, and any supplied value must lie between 1 and 100 inclusive. The `offset` parameter defaults to 0 when omitted. List results are always sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest record of the collection appears first. The `meta` object of a list response carries exactly the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`, where `total_count` reports the full size of the filtered collection and `has_more` indicates whether records exist beyond the returned window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and Versioning

Every persisted resource carries an `id` field: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, assigned at creation and never reused. Most resources additionally carry the common audit triple: `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded at creation; `updated_at`, the same timestamp format refreshed on every mutation; and `version`, an integer starting at 1 that increments on every mutation. Four resource types are deliberately lighter — `ModelStrain`, `HealthScreen`, `Allocation`, and `WelfareInspection` carry only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent on those four, and no statement in this document about `version` or `updated_at` applies to them.

Version increments follow one global rule regardless of how dramatic the write is: a single mutating request increments the `version` of every entity it touches by exactly 1, no matter how many cascade hops or automatic transitions fire inside that one request. When, for example, an allocation creation both reserves animals on a cohort and auto-transitions the parent order to fully covered, the order's `version` rises by exactly 1 — the automatic transition shares the same single increment.

Resources are addressed through nested URL paths built from `{id}` path parameters, and identifiers resolve left to right along the path before any request-body foreign keys are examined. If any path identifier, or any foreign key actually present in the request body (`room_id`, `strain_id`, `protocol_id`, `colony_id`, `order_id`, `cohort_id`, `facility_id`, `customer_id`, `source_strain_id`), does not resolve to an existing record, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A field that is required but entirely absent from the body is not a 404 case at all — it is a malformed request caught later by the validation ladder as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The platform never reads wall-clock time. All temporal semantics — cohort ages, protocol validity, sanitization windows, transit durations, surveillance cadence, corrective deadlines — are evaluated against a server-side logical clock. At server startup the logical clock is initialized to the fixed default instant `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to wall-clock time, not even on first boot or after a restart without an explicit set.

The logical clock is advanced through a dedicated administrative capability whose path lives outside the `/api` prefix: `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. This is the one and only endpoint not mounted under `/api`; every other endpoint in Section 4 carries the `/api` prefix exactly as tabulated. The capability is restricted to `PlatformAdmin`, accepts a single input field `to_iso8601` carrying an ISO 8601 instant, and returns success with HTTP 200 and no resource payload.

Every advance of the logical clock runs a time sweep over the persisted state, and the sweep's three steps execute in a pinned order. First, protocol expiry: any `IACUCProtocol` in `APPROVED` whose `expiration_date` is strictly before the new clock instant transitions to `EXPIRED`. Second, genetic surveillance aging: any `Colony` whose `genetic_status` is `CLEAR` and for which the new clock instant is strictly later than `last_snp_panel_at` plus `snp_panel_interval_weeks` transitions to `PANEL_DUE`. Third, corrective-action escalation: any `WelfareInspection` in `CORRECTIVE_ACTION` whose `corrective_deadline` is strictly before the new clock instant transitions to `ESCALATED`, and each such escalation fires its site cascade — a facility-site escalation suspends the facility's registration (with the full shipment-cancellation cascade described in Section 3.1), while a customer-site escalation suspends the customer account. All three comparisons are strict: an instant exactly equal to a deadline or expiry does not trigger the corresponding transition.

### 2.6. The Barrier Health-Status Rank Order

Health-status categories form a single ordered rank that rooms, cohorts, strains, customers, orders, and vehicles all speak: `CONVENTIONAL` is rank 1, `MPF` is rank 2, `VF` is rank 3, and `GERM_FREE` is rank 4, so that `CONVENTIONAL` < `MPF` < `VF` < `GERM_FREE`. A room's `barrier_level`, a cohort's `health_status`, a strain's `min_barrier_level`, a customer's `accepted_min_health_status`, an order's `min_health_status`, and a shipment vehicle's `vehicle_barrier_cert` all draw from this same four-value enumeration, and every cross-entity comparison in Section 3 is a comparison of these ranks — "at least" means rank greater than or equal to, and "below" means strictly lower rank. Barrier standing is monotonic downward: a positive finding on a room's exclusion list downgrades the room exactly one rank (`GERM_FREE` to `VF`, `VF` to `MPF`, `MPF` to `CONVENTIONAL`, with `CONVENTIONAL` unchanged because no lower rank exists), and no operation in the system ever upgrades a barrier level or a cohort health status.

### 2.7. Validation Priority and Error Selection

A single request can violate several rules at once, and the platform resolves that ambiguity with a fixed priority ladder. Validation categories are ordered: authentication first (priority 1), then endpoint authorization (priority 2), then resource ownership (priority 3), then identifier existence (priority 4), then state-machine legality including optimistic-concurrency freshness (priority 5), then business safety rules such as quarantine, registration, genetic, and protocol gates (priority 6), then business temporal rules such as lead times, transit windows, and sanitization chronology (priority 7), then business constraint rules such as required fields, enum membership, uniqueness, quantity minima, and capacity headroom (priority 8), then business referential rules such as barrier sufficiency, strain matching, and spec windows (priority 9), and finally business fiscal rules such as account standing and credit exposure (priority 10). When several rules are violated by the same request, the reported error is always the one with the lowest priority number; a missing-authentication failure therefore masks every business rule, and a quarantine violation surfaces before a weight-window mismatch even if both hold.

Within this ladder, three generic checks apply to every endpoint. Missing or mistyped required fields — including the inspection site rule that exactly one of `facility_id` or `customer_id` may be set — are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). An enum field carrying a value outside its declared member set is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 400). And any transition or action requested against a resource whose current state does not declare that transition — including any further transition out of a terminal state — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Order Writes

Two write endpoints mutate state derived from a `CustomerOrder` and are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the order's `version`: creating an allocation, and creating a shipment. Each such request must carry a non-optional `expected_order_version` field equal to the target order's current `version` as the client last observed it. If the order's `version` has advanced since that observation — for example because another allocation was created or released against the same order in the meantime, each a legitimate mutation under the order state machine — the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and no part of it is applied. Endpoints other than those two creation operations do not take any version field; in particular, order, shipment, and contract transitions are guarded purely by their state machines and role checks, not by version tokens.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integer cents — prices, quotes, credit limits, exposures, balances, and fees are never fractional. Where a pricing step produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half-up to the nearest integer cent at that step before the next step consumes it; the pinned rounding points of the order quote are given in Section 3.6. Ages are integer weeks computed by flooring: elapsed days are floored to whole days first, then floored again to whole weeks. Cage and box occupancies are integers obtained by ceiling division, and the ceiling is always taken per individual line — per cohort for cage occupancy, per allocation-and-sex for shipment boxes — before any summation; summing animals first and applying one ceiling to the total is a different, rejected algorithm. Time comparisons use strictly-later or strictly-earlier semantics wherever Section 3 says "strictly"; a comparison stated without that qualifier is inclusive.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Facility Registration and Barrier Housing

A production network begins with its sites. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each production facility with a display `name`, a short `site_code` that must be unique across all facilities, the oversight authority's `registration_number`, and a `registration_expires_at` instant marking when the current registration lapses. A facility is born with `registration_status` set to `REGISTERED`, and its `suspension_reason` is null until a suspension records one. Registering a facility whose `site_code` collides with an existing facility is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (HTTP 409).

Registration is a lifecycle, not a flag. A `PlatformAdmin` may move a `REGISTERED` facility to `UNDER_INSPECTION` while regulators examine it, and back to `REGISTERED` once satisfied — but reinstatement carries a payload `registration_expires_at` that must be strictly after the current logical clock, and the facility's `version` increments on each such move. Suspension is graver. A facility in `REGISTERED` flips to `SUSPENDED` either by explicit `PlatformAdmin` action carrying a payload `reason`, or by system cascade when a welfare inspection at that facility escalates, in which case `suspension_reason` is set to the literal `OVERDUE_CORRECTIVE_ACTION`. A facility in `UNDER_INSPECTION` reaches `SUSPENDED` only through that same inspection-escalation cascade, with identical effects. Either way, suspension fires a transport cascade: every shipment still in `PLANNED` or `LOADED` whose order draws any allocation from a cohort housed in this facility's rooms is cancelled, and each such cancelled shipment's order falls back from `SCHEDULED` to `ALLOCATED` with its allocations remaining `RESERVED`. Reinstatement from `SUSPENDED` to `REGISTERED` is allowed only when no welfare inspection at this facility currently stands in `ESCALATED`, and again requires a payload `registration_expires_at` strictly after the logical clock; a successful reinstatement clears `suspension_reason`. Finally, a facility in `UNDER_INSPECTION` may be moved to the terminal `REVOKED` state, which fires the same shipment-cancellation cascade as suspension. From `REVOKED`, no further transition exists — any attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Within a facility, animals live in barrier rooms. A `FacilityManager` or `PlatformAdmin` creates a room with its `facility_id`, a `room_code` unique within that facility, the room's `barrier_level` from the four-value rank enumeration, a non-empty `excluded_agents` array naming the infectious agents that must never be present at this barrier level, and a `cage_capacity` of at least 1 total cage slots. The room's `occupied_cages` is never written directly — it is derived by the cage-accounting rule of Section 3.4 and must never exceed `cage_capacity`. A room's `status` begins as `ACTIVE`.

Rooms answer to a small operational machine. Any `Veterinarian` may manually quarantine an `ACTIVE` room, which records `quarantined_at` at the current logical clock and increments the room's `version` — note that manual quarantine does not change `barrier_level`; only the positive-screen cascade of Section 3.5 both quarantines and downgrades. Lifting a quarantine requires evidence: some health screen of this room must exist in `RESULTED_NEGATIVE` whose `resulted_at` is strictly after the room's `quarantined_at`, and a successful lift clears `quarantined_at`. A `FacilityManager` may decommission an `ACTIVE` room only when no colony housed there has a `lifecycle_status` other than `RETIRED` and no cohort of those colonies remains in `WEANED`, `HOLD`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, or `FULLY_ALLOCATED`; `DECOMMISSIONED` is terminal. Creating colonies or cohorts in, or drawing allocations through, a room that is `QUARANTINED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` is rejected with `ERR_ROOM_QUARANTINED` (HTTP 409).

### 3.2. Strain Catalog, Colony Founding, and Genetic Monitoring

The strain catalog is the product catalog of the business. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each research model strain with a unique `strain_code` (a collision with an existing strain is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, HTTP 409), its `species` — `MOUSE` or `RAT` — its `immune_class` of `IMMUNOCOMPETENT` or `IMMUNOCOMPROMISED`, its `breeding_type` of `INBRED`, `OUTBRED`, or `HYBRID`, and the minimum `min_barrier_level` rank any room must hold to house it. Reproductive and commercial parameters complete the record: `gestation_days` and `wean_age_days`, both strictly positive integers used by production-planning feasibility; `typical_litter_size`, at least 1 expected pups per breeding pair per litter; and `base_price_cents`, the non-negative list price per animal in cents. A strain record carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Founding a breeding colony is the most heavily gated creation in the system, because it commits physical space, regulatory quota, and barrier integrity at once. A `FacilityManager` founds a colony by naming its `room_id`, `strain_id`, and authorizing `protocol_id`, together with the `breeding_pair_count` (at least 1). Two optional fields tune the stock: `generation_number`, the filial generation of the breeding stock, defaulting to 0; and `snp_panel_interval_weeks`, the number of weeks allowed between genetic-monitoring panels, defaulting to 12. The gates, in the order the validation ladder surfaces them: the target room must be `ACTIVE`, since a `QUARANTINED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` room rejects with `ERR_ROOM_QUARANTINED` (HTTP 409); the room's facility must hold `registration_status` equal to `REGISTERED`, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 409); the colony's protocol must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409); the protocol must have quota headroom, else `ERR_PROTOCOL_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); and the room must have cage headroom, else `ERR_CAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Referential rules follow: the room's `barrier_level` rank must be at least the strain's `min_barrier_level` rank, else `ERR_BARRIER_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422); and immunocompromised housing is restricted — an `IMMUNOCOMPROMISED` strain may only be housed in a room of rank `VF` or higher, and a room hosts colonies of at most one `IMMUNOCOMPROMISED` strain, with violations rejected as `ERR_IMMUNOCOMPROMISED_MIXING` (HTTP 422).

Each breeding pair consumes exactly 1 cage, and the cage-capacity check compares the room's current occupancy plus the incoming `breeding_pair_count` against `cage_capacity` — equality passes, so a room may be filled exactly to capacity. Protocol quota behaves the same way at twice the rate: founding a colony adds 2 animals per breeding pair to the protocol's cumulative usage, and the check `animals_used` plus twice the new `breeding_pair_count` must remain at or below `approved_animal_quota`, with equality passing. On successful founding, the protocol's `animals_used` increases by twice the pair count, the colony's `last_snp_panel_at` is initialized to the current logical clock, and the room's derived occupancy is recomputed.

A colony evolves along two independent state dimensions. The production dimension, `lifecycle_status`, runs `FOUNDING` → `EXPANDING` → `PRODUCTION` → `PHASING_OUT` → `RETIRED`, with one sanctioned revert: a colony in `PHASING_OUT` may be reactivated to `PRODUCTION`, but only while its `genetic_status` is not `DRIFT_DETECTED`. Advancing from `EXPANDING` to `PRODUCTION` requires that at least one cohort of the colony stand in `WEANED`, `HOLD`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, or `FULLY_ALLOCATED` — a colony with no live cohorts is not yet in production. Retirement from `PHASING_OUT` requires that no cohort of the colony remain in one of those four live states and that no allocation in `RESERVED` exists on any of its cohorts; retiring drops the colony's breeding pairs from the room's occupancy. Every lifecycle move is a `FacilityManager` decision and increments the colony's `version`.

The genetic dimension, `genetic_status`, evolves independently. A colony begins `CLEAR`. When the clock sweep finds the logical clock strictly later than `last_snp_panel_at` plus `snp_panel_interval_weeks`, the colony slips to `PANEL_DUE` — surveillance is owed. A `Veterinarian` records a SNP panel by submitting a `result` of `CLEAR` or `DRIFT_DETECTED`. Recording `CLEAR` on a `PANEL_DUE` colony returns it to `CLEAR`; recording `DRIFT_DETECTED` — from either `CLEAR` or `PANEL_DUE` — moves the colony to the terminal `DRIFT_DETECTED` state, and if the colony's `lifecycle_status` was `EXPANDING` or `PRODUCTION` at that moment it is forced into `PHASING_OUT`, because drifted stock must not remain in production. Every panel recording refreshes `last_snp_panel_at` to the current logical clock and increments the colony's `version`.

Colony reads expose two availability projections computed from the same cohort set — the colony's cohorts in `HOLD` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` (cohorts in `WEANED`, `FULLY_ALLOCATED`, `SHIPPED`, or `CULLED` never contribute). The `confirmed_available_count` sums, over those cohorts, each cohort's live headcount minus its reservations — that is, `count_male` plus `count_female` minus `reserved_male` minus `reserved_female`. The `projected_pipeline_count` adds a forward-looking term: when the colony's `lifecycle_status` is `PRODUCTION` and its `genetic_status` is not `DRIFT_DETECTED`, the expected next-litter yield is the floor of `breeding_pair_count` multiplied by the strain's `typical_litter_size` multiplied by the survival rate 0.8; in any other lifecycle or genetic state the term is 0. The confirmed figure is the `CONFIRMED_ONLY` variant and counts only physically present, unreserved animals; the projected figure is the `PROJECTED` variant used by order sourcing feasibility, and identical inputs always yield a projected value greater than or equal to the confirmed one.

### 3.3. Cohort Production and the Cohort Lifecycle

A cohort is a weaned group of animals born together under one colony. A `FacilityManager` records a cohort with its `colony_id`, the `birth_date` instant — which must not lie after the current logical clock — the live headcounts `count_male` and `count_female` (each at least 0), and the average weights `avg_weight_g_male` and `avg_weight_g_female` in grams, both strictly positive. Creation is gated much like colony founding, evaluated in ladder order: the colony's `lifecycle_status` must be `EXPANDING` or `PRODUCTION` — a `FOUNDING`, `PHASING_OUT`, or `RETIRED` colony cannot accept new cohorts and the attempt is rejected as an illegitimate state with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); the room housing the colony must not be `QUARANTINED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` (`ERR_ROOM_QUARANTINED`, HTTP 409); the colony's `genetic_status` must be `CLEAR`, so a `PANEL_DUE` or `DRIFT_DETECTED` colony rejects cohort creation with `ERR_GENETIC_DRIFT` (HTTP 409); the colony's protocol must be `APPROVED` (`ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_APPROVED`, HTTP 409) with sufficient quota headroom (`ERR_PROTOCOL_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422); and the room must retain cage headroom for the newcomers (`ERR_CAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422). The headroom arithmetic for both quota and cages is defined in Section 3.4.

Creation freezes three facts. The `initial_count_total` field snapshots `count_male` plus `count_female` at creation and never changes afterward — the protocol's monotonic usage accumulator reads this snapshot, never the live counts. The cohort's `health_status` is initialized to the housing room's current `barrier_level`, and thereafter can only be downgraded by the surveillance cascade, never upgraded. And the cohort's `status` begins as `WEANED`.

A cohort's age is always derived, never stored. Its `age_weeks` is computed by taking the difference between an as-of instant and `birth_date`, flooring that difference to whole days (a day is 86400 seconds), then flooring the days to whole weeks (7 days per week). On plain cohort reads the as-of instant is the current logical clock; when the platform judges allocation eligibility for an order, the as-of instant is that order's `required_delivery_date`, so eligibility is always evaluated at the age the animals will be at delivery.

Releasing a cohort for sale is a deliberate act: a `FacilityManager` moves a `WEANED` cohort to `HOLD`, but only while the colony's `lifecycle_status` is `EXPANDING` or `PRODUCTION` and its `genetic_status` is `CLEAR`. From `HOLD`, the market takes over through system transitions: the first allocation created against the cohort moves it to `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`; when an allocation leaves `available_male` at 0 and `available_female` at 0 while at least one allocation stands in `RESERVED`, the cohort rises to `FULLY_ALLOCATED`; a release that reopens availability drops it back from `FULLY_ALLOCATED` to `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`; and when the last `RESERVED` allocation is released, the cohort reverts to `HOLD` — the compensation path. Here and below, `available_male` means `count_male` minus `reserved_male`, and `available_female` means `count_female` minus `reserved_female`, where `reserved_male` and `reserved_female` sum the per-sex quantities of the cohort's allocations still in `RESERVED`. All of these status moves increment the cohort's `version`. The terminal `SHIPPED` state arrives only through departure consumption: when a shipment departs, the consumed quantities are subtracted from `count_male` and `count_female`, and any touched cohort whose live counts both reach 0 — whether it stood in `HOLD`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, or `FULLY_ALLOCATED` — transitions to `SHIPPED`.

Culling is a `Veterinarian` prerogative and comes in two forms. A full cull moves a cohort from `WEANED`, `HOLD`, or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` to the terminal `CULLED` state; it demands that no allocation in `RESERVED` exists on the cohort and that the payload `cull_male` and `cull_female` exactly equal the current `count_male` and `count_female`, whereupon both counts become 0 and the room's occupancy is recomputed. A partial cull — cull counts smaller than the current counts — is permitted from the same three states provided `cull_male` does not exceed `available_male` and `cull_female` does not exceed `available_female`, because reserved animals can never be culled; it decrements `count_male` and `count_female` without changing `status`, then recomputes availability — a partial cull from `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` that removes every available animal flips the cohort to `FULLY_ALLOCATED`, not to `CULLED`. Crucially, culling is consumption without refund: the protocol's `animals_used` is not decremented by culls, just as it is never decremented by releases, shipments, or retirements.

After any shipment departure, every touched cohort is recomputed in a fixed sequence: first the consumed per-sex quantities are subtracted from `count_male` and `count_female`; then, if both counts are 0, the cohort is `SHIPPED`; otherwise, if `available_male` is 0 and `available_female` is 0 while reservations remain, it is `FULLY_ALLOCATED`; otherwise, if any `RESERVED` allocation remains, it is `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`; otherwise it returns to `HOLD`.

### 3.4. Cage Accounting and the Protocol Quota

Two accumulators police the physical and regulatory capacity of production, and both are computed the same way everywhere they are consulted — on reads, at creation gates, and after every mutation that could change them.

A room's `occupied_cages` is the sum of two terms. The first term counts breeding stock: for every colony housed in the room whose `lifecycle_status` is anything other than `RETIRED`, one cage per breeding pair — that is, the colony's `breeding_pair_count`. The second term counts weaned animals: for every cohort, of any colony in the room, whose `status` is one of `WEANED`, `HOLD`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, or `FULLY_ALLOCATED` (cohorts already `SHIPPED` or `CULLED` no longer occupy the room), the ceiling of that cohort's live headcount — `count_male` plus `count_female` — divided by the cage density of 5 animals per cage. The ceiling is taken per cohort before the per-cohort results are summed; rounding only a grand total once would undercount mixed cohorts. The two creation checks built on this occupancy both admit equality: founding a colony requires `occupied_cages` plus the incoming `breeding_pair_count` to be less than or equal to `cage_capacity`, and recording a cohort requires `occupied_cages` plus the ceiling of the new cohort's total headcount divided by 5 to be less than or equal to `cage_capacity`; exceeding capacity in either case is `ERR_CAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

A protocol's `animals_used` is the regulatory mirror. It accumulates, across every colony referencing the protocol whatever its lifecycle state, 2 animals per breeding pair, plus, across every cohort of those colonies whatever its status, the cohort's immutable `initial_count_total`. The accumulator is strictly monotonic: culls, allocation releases, shipment departures, and colony retirements never refund it, which is exactly why `initial_count_total` exists as a creation-time snapshot decoupled from the mutable live counts. The quota gate consults this accumulator at both creation points with equality passing: founding a colony requires `animals_used` plus twice the incoming `breeding_pair_count` to be less than or equal to `approved_animal_quota`, and recording a cohort requires `animals_used` plus the incoming total headcount (`count_male` plus `count_female`) to be less than or equal to `approved_animal_quota`; exceeding the quota at either gate is `ERR_PROTOCOL_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

### 3.5. Diagnostic Surveillance and the Barrier-Downgrade Cascade

Barrier integrity is policed by scheduled diagnostic surveillance. A `Veterinarian` schedules a health screen for a room by supplying its `room_id`, a `panel_type` of `QUARTERLY_SENTINEL`, `OUTBREAK_DIAGNOSTIC`, or `PRE_SHIPMENT`, and a `scheduled_at` sampling instant on the logical clock. The screen begins in `SCHEDULED` with an empty `results` array. When samples come back, the veterinarian records the results: a `results` array whose entries each name an `agent` and whether it tested `positive`. Recording results is only possible while the room's `status` is anything other than `DECOMMISSIONED`, and it stamps `resulted_at` with the current logical clock. The outcome is mechanical: if at least one positive entry names an agent on the room's `excluded_agents` list, the screen flips to `RESULTED_POSITIVE`; otherwise it settles as `RESULTED_NEGATIVE`. Both resulted states are terminal. A negative screen has evidentiary value beyond itself — it is the instrument that lifts a quarantine, provided its `resulted_at` is strictly after the room's `quarantined_at`.

A positive screen fires the widest cascade in the platform, four hops from a single laboratory finding. Hop one hits the room: it transitions to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantined_at` set to the current logical clock, and its `barrier_level` is downgraded exactly one rank — `GERM_FREE` to `VF`, `VF` to `MPF`, `MPF` to `CONVENTIONAL`, while a `CONVENTIONAL` room stays `CONVENTIONAL` because no lower rank exists. Hop two hits the cohorts: every cohort of every colony in that room whose `health_status` rank now exceeds the room's downgraded rank is pulled down to the room's new `barrier_level` — cohorts already at or below the new rank are untouched. Hop three hits the allocations: every allocation still in `RESERVED` on a downgraded cohort whose order's `min_health_status` rank exceeds the cohort's new `health_status` rank transitions to `RELEASED`, and the bookkeeping follows — the cohort's `reserved_male` and `reserved_female` and the order's `allocated_male` and `allocated_female` are decremented by the released quantities. Hop four hits the orders: for each order whose allocated totals now fall short of its requested quantities, an order in `ALLOCATED` reverts to `SOURCING`, and an order in `SCHEDULED` first has its `PLANNED` or `LOADED` shipment cancelled and then reverts to `SOURCING`; orders in `SOURCING` or `BACKORDERED` simply remain where they are; and orders already in `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED`, or `CLOSED` are untouched, because their allocations are already `CONSUMED`. Throughout the cascade the global version rule of Section 2.4 applies — one request, one increment per touched entity, however many hops fire.

### 3.6. Customer Accounts, Credit Positions, and Order Intake

Demand enters the system through research-institution accounts. A `PlatformAdmin` onboards a customer with its `institution_name`, a billing `account_number` that must be unique across all customers (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, HTTP 409), a `credit_limit_cents` bounding gross credit exposure (at least 0), the institution's `accepted_min_health_status` — the minimum barrier rank its receiving barrier accepts, carried as an informational default for its orders — and an `is_commercial` flag distinguishing commercial accounts from academic ones, defaulting to false; the flag later governs exclusive intellectual-property terms in Section 3.10. The account opens in `account_status` `ACTIVE`. Account standing is a small machine: `ACTIVE` and `CREDIT_HOLD` alternate freely under `PlatformAdmin` control; `SUSPENDED` is reachable from either, manually or by system cascade when a customer-site welfare inspection escalates; and reinstatement from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` is blocked while any inspection of this customer stands in `ESCALATED`. Both `CREDIT_HOLD` and `SUSPENDED` block new order submission and order sourcing, and the violation is fiscal in kind: `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 403).

Two credit figures are derived on every customer read, from the same order data but different status sets. The `outstanding_balance_cents` figure — the NET_BILLED variant — sums `quoted_total_cents` over the customer's orders in `DELIVERED` only; it is the billed pipeline. The `credit_exposure_cents` figure — the GROSS variant — sums `quoted_total_cents` over orders in `SOURCING`, `BACKORDERED`, `ALLOCATED`, `SCHEDULED`, `SHIPPED`, and `DELIVERED`; it is the committed pipeline. Orders in `SUBMITTED`, `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED`, or `REJECTED` count toward neither variant, and a `BACKORDERED` order counts toward GROSS only because its quote is locked on entry into `BACKORDERED`. Identical order data therefore always yields a NET_BILLED figure less than or equal to the GROSS figure.

Ordering itself is a `Customer`-role act, and the account identity comes from the request, not the payload: the order's `customer_id` is taken from the caller's `X-User-ID` header and is never accepted from the request body. The order names its `strain_id`, the requested `quantity_male` and `quantity_female` (each at least 0, and their sum must be at least 1 — a zero-quantity order is `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`, HTTP 422), the inclusive age window `min_age_weeks` to `max_age_weeks` (a lower bound above the upper bound is `ERR_INVALID_AGE_WINDOW`, HTTP 422), the weight window `min_weight_g` to `max_weight_g` in grams, where the lower bound must be strictly below the upper bound (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WEIGHT_WINDOW`, HTTP 422) yet animal eligibility later treats both bounds as inclusive, the `required_delivery_date` instant, and optionally a `min_health_status` floor defaulting to `MPF`. Submission requires the delivery date to be at least 14 days after the current logical clock — the platform's minimum order lead time — and an earlier date is rejected with `ERR_DELIVERY_WINDOW_INFEASIBLE` (HTTP 422). Submission also requires the account to be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 403). A valid order is born in `SUBMITTED` with `quoted_total_cents` still null and both `allocated_male` and `allocated_female` at 0.

The price of an order is locked the moment it leaves `SUBMITTED` for `SOURCING` or `BACKORDERED`, and the quote arithmetic is pinned step for step. Let the total quantity be `quantity_male` plus `quantity_female`. The subtotal is that quantity multiplied by the strain's `base_price_cents`. A volume tier discount applies to the subtotal: 20 percent when the total quantity is at least 250 animals, otherwise 10 percent when it is at least 100 animals, otherwise no discount; the discounted figure is rounded half-up to integer cents. Then surcharges apply to the discounted figure: 25 percent when the strain's `immune_class` is `IMMUNOCOMPROMISED` (nothing for `IMMUNOCOMPETENT`), plus a barrier premium of 40 percent when the order's `min_health_status` is `GERM_FREE`, or 20 percent when it is `VF`, or nothing otherwise; the result is again rounded half-up to integer cents to produce `quoted_total_cents`. The evaluation order is fixed — tier discount first, surcharges second — and each step rounds to integer cents before the next consumes it, so reordering the steps or deferring rounding yields a different, non-conforming price.

Moving a `SUBMITTED` order into `SOURCING` is a `FacilityManager` decision guarded by checks in ladder order. The order must currently stand in `SUBMITTED` — any other state makes the transition illegitimate (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`, HTTP 409). Supply feasibility comes next among the business rules: the `PROJECTED` availability of Section 3.2, summed over the eligible colonies of the requested strain, must be at least the requested total quantity, where an eligible colony is one whose room is `ACTIVE`, whose room's facility holds `registration_status` `REGISTERED`, whose `genetic_status` is not `DRIFT_DETECTED`, and whose room's `barrier_level` rank is at least the order's `min_health_status` rank; when the summed projection falls short, the sourcing attempt is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLY_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422) — the correct disposition for such an order is `BACKORDERED`, not `SOURCING`. The fiscal checks close the gate: the customer's `account_status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 403), and the credit check compares figures — the customer's current `credit_exposure_cents` plus this order's freshly computed quote must not strictly exceed `credit_limit_cents`; equality passes, and a strictly greater result is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A successful sourcing transition computes and stores `quoted_total_cents`, raises the customer's GROSS exposure by that amount, and increments the order's `version`. A `FacilityManager` may instead route a `SUBMITTED` order directly to `BACKORDERED` — requiring only that the account be `ACTIVE` — which likewise computes and stores the quote and raises exposure; and a `SUBMITTED` order may also be moved to the terminal `REJECTED` state.

### 3.7. Sourcing, Allocation, and the Supply Loop

An allocation reserves specific animals from a specific cohort for a specific order. A `FacilityManager` creates one by naming the `order_id` and `cohort_id`, the per-sex quantities `qty_male` and `qty_female` (their sum must be at least 1, else `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`, HTTP 422), and — because allocation creation is one of the two OCC-guarded writes of Section 2.8 — an `expected_order_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target order. If the order's `version` has advanced since that observation, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is reserved.

The business gates then apply in ladder order. Safety first: the room housing the cohort must not be `QUARANTINED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` (`ERR_ROOM_QUARANTINED`, HTTP 409); the producing colony's genetic standing must be clean in two distinct senses — a `DRIFT_DETECTED` colony rejects the allocation with `ERR_GENETIC_DRIFT` (HTTP 409), while a colony merely in `PANEL_DUE`, its surveillance overdue but no drift found, rejects with the distinct `ERR_PANEL_OVERDUE` (HTTP 409). Then the temporal spec: the cohort's age, evaluated per Section 3.3 at the order's `required_delivery_date`, must lie within the order's `min_age_weeks` to `max_age_weeks` window, both bounds inclusive, else `ERR_AGE_SPEC_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Then the referential rules: the cohort's colony must produce the very strain the order requests — a differing strain is `ERR_STRAIN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); for each sex allocated, the cohort's average weight (`avg_weight_g_male` for males, `avg_weight_g_female` for females) must lie within the order's `min_weight_g` to `max_weight_g` window, both bounds inclusive, else `ERR_WEIGHT_SPEC_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); the cohort must stand in `HOLD` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` with sufficient free headcount — `available_male` at least `qty_male` and `available_female` at least `qty_female` — else `ERR_COHORT_NOT_ALLOCATABLE` (HTTP 409); and the order itself must stand in `SOURCING` or `BACKORDERED`, else `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SOURCING` (HTTP 409). The allocation is born in `RESERVED`.

Creation rewires both sides of the reservation. On the cohort, `reserved_male` and `reserved_female` rise by the allocated quantities, and the status machine advances: a `HOLD` cohort becomes `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, and a cohort whose availability now reaches zero in both sexes while at least one allocation stands `RESERVED` becomes `FULLY_ALLOCATED`. On the order, `allocated_male` and `allocated_female` rise likewise, and when the allocated totals first equal the requested `quantity_male` and `quantity_female`, the order auto-transitions to `ALLOCATED` — from `SOURCING` or from `BACKORDERED` alike. Per the global version rule, the order's `version` rises by exactly 1 for the whole request, the automatic coverage transition sharing that single increment.

Releasing is the compensating act. A `FacilityManager` may release any allocation in `RESERVED`, moving it to `RELEASED`; the cohort's reserved counts fall, and its status recomputes — `FULLY_ALLOCATED` relaxes to `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, and a `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` cohort whose last reservation disappears returns to `HOLD` — while the order's allocated totals fall, and an order that loses full coverage drops from `ALLOCATED` back to `SOURCING`. The order's `version` increments. Releases also arrive unbidden from the two cascades already described — order cancellation below, and the barrier-downgrade cascade of Section 3.5 — with identical bookkeeping.

The supply loop ties the order states together. A `FacilityManager` may park a `SOURCING` order into `BACKORDERED` and later retry sourcing from `BACKORDERED` back to `SOURCING`; the back-edge keeps every `RESERVED` allocation in place. Coverage motion is symmetric: allocation creation completes coverage into `ALLOCATED`, and any release or cascade that breaks coverage returns the order to `SOURCING`. Cancellation is the customer-facing escape hatch, open to the owning `Customer` (whose `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `customer_id`, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`, HTTP 403) and to `PlatformAdmin`, from any of `SUBMITTED`, `SOURCING`, `BACKORDERED`, `ALLOCATED`, or `SCHEDULED`. Cancelling from `SOURCING`, `BACKORDERED`, or `ALLOCATED` releases all `RESERVED` allocations — decrementing cohort reserved counts and recomputing cohort statuses — and decreases the customer's `credit_exposure_cents` by the order's `quoted_total_cents`, the GROSS compensation. Cancelling from `SCHEDULED` additionally cancels the order's `PLANNED` or `LOADED` shipment before the same release and compensation run. Cancelling from `SUBMITTED` touches neither allocations nor credit, since no quote has been locked yet. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Transport Logistics and the Receiving Protocol

Animals move between barriers on dedicated, certified vehicles, and a shipment is the unit of that motion. A `LogisticsCoordinator` plans a shipment for an order by supplying the `order_id`, the `vehicle_code` identifying the dedicated barrier-certified vehicle, the `vehicle_barrier_cert` rank the vehicle is certified to maintain, the vehicle's `sanitized_at` timestamp, the planned `departure_date` and `planned_arrival_date`, and — the second OCC-guarded write — an `expected_order_version` matching the order's current `version`, with a mismatch rejected as `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Shipment creation requires the order to stand exactly in `ALLOCATED` — any other state, including `SOURCING` or `BACKORDERED`, is `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SOURCING` (HTTP 409) — and an order admits at most one non-`CANCELLED` shipment, so creating a second while one stands in `PLANNED`, `LOADED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `ARRIVED`, `ACCEPTED`, or `REJECTED_AT_RECEIPT` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SHIPMENT` (HTTP 409). The vehicle's certification must match the cargo: a `vehicle_barrier_cert` rank below the order's `min_health_status` rank is rejected with `ERR_BARRIER_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

Three temporal rules then discipline the plan, all evaluated against the logical clock. The transit window itself must be feasible: `departure_date` must not precede the current logical clock, the difference between `planned_arrival_date` and `departure_date` must not exceed 48 hours — the platform's maximum transit duration — and `planned_arrival_date` must fall on or before the order's `required_delivery_date`; any violation is `ERR_TRANSIT_DURATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Sanitization must be fresh and sequenced: `sanitized_at` must lie within the 72 hours preceding departure — no earlier than 72 hours before `departure_date` and no later than `departure_date` itself — and, for every prior non-`CANCELLED` shipment on the same `vehicle_code` whose planned arrival falls at or before this departure, `sanitized_at` must be at or after that earlier shipment's `planned_arrival_date`, guaranteeing a fresh sanitization after the previous tour; a stale or mis-sequenced timestamp is `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_SANITIZED` (HTTP 422). And the vehicle cannot be in two places at once: the half-open transit window from `departure_date` inclusive to `planned_arrival_date` exclusive must not overlap the window of any other non-`CANCELLED` shipment on the same `vehicle_code` standing in `PLANNED`, `LOADED`, or `IN_TRANSIT`; a departure scheduled exactly at a predecessor's planned arrival is allowed, but any true overlap is `ERR_VEHICLE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Planning computes the cargo's boxing. For every allocation in `RESERVED` on the order, and for each sex with a positive reserved quantity, the animals-per-box capacity is chosen from the cohort's average weight for that sex: 10 per box when the average weight is below 15 grams (light animals), 4 per box when it is 25 grams or more (heavy animals), and 6 per box otherwise (standard animals). Each such allocation-and-sex line contributes the ceiling of its quantity divided by its per-box capacity, and the shipment's `box_count` is the sum of those per-line ceilings. The rounding order is pinned — ceiling per line first, then summation — so pooling animals across lines before applying one ceiling is a different algorithm and yields a non-conforming, smaller box count. Planning also moves the order from `ALLOCATED` to `SCHEDULED`, with the order's `version` rising by exactly 1 for the whole request.

The shipment then drives through its own machine, and the order follows one step behind. A `LogisticsCoordinator` loads a `PLANNED` shipment — permitted only while its order stands in `SCHEDULED` — moving it to `LOADED`. Departure moves it from `LOADED` to `IN_TRANSIT` and is gated on the source facility's standing: the facility housing the source cohorts' rooms must hold `registration_status` `REGISTERED`, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 409). Departure is the consumption point: every `RESERVED` allocation of the order transitions to `CONSUMED`, the consumed per-sex quantities are subtracted from each source cohort's `count_male` and `count_female` with the post-consumption recompute of Section 3.3 applied (a cohort drained to zero reaches `SHIPPED`), the order's `allocated_male` and `allocated_female` are decremented by the consumed quantities, and the order advances from `SCHEDULED` to `SHIPPED`. Arrival at the customer site moves the shipment from `IN_TRANSIT` to `ARRIVED` and records `actual_arrival_at` at the current logical clock.

Receipt is the customer's receiving-protocol fork, and only the owning `Customer` — `X-User-ID` equal to the shipment's order's `customer_id`, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) — may call it. Acceptance moves the shipment from `ARRIVED` to `ACCEPTED` and requires the declared counts `received_male` and `received_female`, which are recorded on the shipment; the order then advances from `SHIPPED` to `DELIVERED`, and billing posts there — the customer's `outstanding_balance_cents` rises by the order's `quoted_total_cents`, the NET_BILLED event. Refusal moves the shipment from `ARRIVED` to `REJECTED_AT_RECEIPT`; the order then falls from `SHIPPED` to `CLOSED`, no billing is posted, and the customer's `credit_exposure_cents` decreases by `quoted_total_cents` since a `CLOSED` order no longer counts toward GROSS. Both receipt outcomes are terminal for the shipment. A shipment still in `PLANNED` or `LOADED` may be cancelled by a `LogisticsCoordinator` or `PlatformAdmin`, or by the system cascades of Sections 3.1 and 3.5; cancellation moves the order from `SCHEDULED` back to `ALLOCATED` — unless the same cascade already forced the order to `SOURCING` — leaving its allocations `RESERVED`. `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED_AT_RECEIPT`, and `CANCELLED` are all terminal.

One settlement step remains with the customer: from `DELIVERED`, the owning `Customer` may close the order. Closing decreases `credit_exposure_cents` by `quoted_total_cents` — a `CLOSED` order exits the GROSS pipeline — while `outstanding_balance_cents` is unchanged, because the billed amount remains owed. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.9. Protocol Review and Welfare Inspection

No colony exists without institutional authorization. A `FacilityManager` drafts an `IACUCProtocol` with its `facility_id`, a `protocol_number` unique across all protocols (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, HTTP 409), the responsible principal investigator's `pi_name`, the covered `species` (`MOUSE` or `RAT`), the `approved_animal_quota` of at least 1 that bounds the monotonic usage accumulator of Section 3.4, and the approval window from `effective_date` to `expiration_date`. The protocol opens in `DRAFT`, and the review machine runs with a strict division of hands. The drafting `FacilityManager` submits — `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` — and the system records the caller's `X-User-ID` in `submitted_by_user_id`. An `IACUCReviewer` then takes the protocol `UNDER_REVIEW`, and from there may approve it, reject it, or request revisions. Approval and rejection carry the segregation-of-duties guard: the deciding caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, and a self-decision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Revision requests loop — a protocol in `REVISION_REQUESTED` returns to `SUBMITTED` on resubmission, which refreshes `submitted_by_user_id` to the resubmitting caller. A `PlatformAdmin` may suspend an `APPROVED` protocol and later reinstate it to `APPROVED`. The drafting manager may withdraw a protocol from `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` into the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state, and `REJECTED` is likewise terminal. Time is the final reviewer: when the clock sweep finds the logical clock strictly past an `APPROVED` protocol's `expiration_date`, the protocol transitions to the terminal `EXPIRED` state, and colonies referencing it can no longer create cohorts or accept allocations — any such attempt meets `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409), the same gate that bars colony and cohort creation under a protocol not currently `APPROVED`.

Welfare oversight extends to both ends of the supply chain. A `WelfareInspector` schedules an inspection naming exactly one site — a `facility_id` for a production site or a `customer_id` for a customer site; setting both or neither is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The inspection carries an `inspection_type` of `ROUTINE`, `FOR_CAUSE`, or `PRE_PLACEMENT`, and a `scheduled_date`. Each site admits at most one open inspection per UTC calendar day: if another inspection of the same site in `SCHEDULED` or `CORRECTIVE_ACTION` already occupies that calendar day, the scheduling is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_DAY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The inspection opens in `SCHEDULED`.

Reporting closes the loop — or opens corrective action. When the inspector files the report, the payload `findings` array lists each deficiency with a severity of `MINOR` or `SIGNIFICANT`, and the caller's `X-User-ID` is stored as `reported_by_user_id`. An empty `findings` array closes the inspection immediately as `CLOSED_VERIFIED` — no deficiencies, no corrective phase. A non-empty array moves the inspection to `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, and if any finding is `SIGNIFICANT` the payload must carry a `corrective_deadline`, else the report is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Verification of the corrective work belongs to a different pair of hands: the verifying `WelfareInspector`'s `X-User-ID` must differ from `reported_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). An `outcome` of `VERIFIED_OK` closes the inspection as `CLOSED_VERIFIED`. An `outcome` of `VERIFIED_FAILED` escalates it to `ESCALATED` — and time can escalate it too, for the clock sweep moves any `CORRECTIVE_ACTION` inspection whose `corrective_deadline` is strictly past to `ESCALATED`. Escalation is the point where oversight bites the business: an escalated facility-site inspection flips that facility's `registration_status` to `SUSPENDED`, firing the full shipment-cancellation cascade of Section 3.1 with `suspension_reason` set to `OVERDUE_CORRECTIVE_ACTION`; an escalated customer-site inspection flips that customer's `account_status` to `SUSPENDED`, blocking new orders and sourcing until reinstatement — which, per Section 3.6, is barred while the escalation stands. `CLOSED_VERIFIED` and `ESCALATED` are both terminal.

### 3.10. Custom Model Services and Intellectual Property

Beyond catalog production, the platform sells two contract services, both opened by a `PlatformAdmin` against a `customer_id`: a `CUSTOM_GENERATION` contract derives a brand-new research model strain from a background strain, and a `CRYOPRESERVATION` contract banks germplasm of an existing strain and scores its post-thaw viability. Every contract names its `contract_type`, the `source_strain_id` — the background strain for generation, or the strain being banked — and its `ip_ownership` terms of `CUSTOMER_EXCLUSIVE`, `SHARED`, or `VENDOR_RETAINED`. The type-specific payload rules are strict and are enforced both at creation and again at execution, with any violation rejected as `ERR_IP_TERMS_INVALID` (HTTP 422). A `CUSTOM_GENERATION` contract must carry a `new_strain_spec` — a complete strain definition object with `strain_code`, `species`, `immune_class`, `breeding_type`, `min_barrier_level`, `gestation_days`, `wean_age_days`, `typical_litter_size`, and `base_price_cents` — and must not carry `straw_count`. A `CRYOPRESERVATION` contract must carry a `straw_count` of at least 1 banked straw and must not carry a `new_strain_spec`.

The intellectual-property terms have their own arithmetic. `CUSTOMER_EXCLUSIVE` terms are reserved for commercial accounts — the customer's `is_commercial` flag must be true — and require a one-time `exclusivity_fee_cents` of at least 1, while `royalty_rate_pct` must be absent or 0, since exclusivity replaces royalties. `SHARED` or `VENDOR_RETAINED` terms instead require a `royalty_rate_pct` strictly greater than 0 and at most 100 — the downstream-sales royalty percentage. Any combination that breaks these rules — exclusive terms without a commercial customer or without the fee, a royalty attached to exclusive terms, or a missing or out-of-range royalty on shared or vendor-retained terms — is `ERR_IP_TERMS_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

A contract's `milestone_status` advances through a supervised pipeline. The contracting `Customer` executes a `DRAFT` contract — `X-User-ID` must equal the contract's `customer_id`, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) — and execution re-validates the IP terms above before moving to `EXECUTED`. A `PlatformAdmin` then drives the work: `EXECUTED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, and `IN_PROGRESS` to `VALIDATION`. Completion forks on the contract type. For `CUSTOM_GENERATION`, completion requires the contract's `new_strain_spec` to be present; completing mints a new `ModelStrain` from that spec — whose `strain_code` must not collide with any existing strain, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (HTTP 409) — and stores the new strain's identifier in `generated_strain_id`, which had been null until then. For `CRYOPRESERVATION`, completion requires a payload `viability_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive — a value outside that range is `ERR_IP_TERMS_INVALID` (HTTP 422) — and the outcome hinges on the viability floor of 70: a `viability_pct` of at least 70 completes the contract and stores the figure, while a value below 70 routes the contract to `VALIDATION_FAILED` instead, still storing the figure. A failed validation is not the end: the contract may be moved from `VALIDATION_FAILED` back to `IN_PROGRESS` to retry. A `COMPLETED` contract is eventually moved to the terminal `CLOSED` state by a `PlatformAdmin`, and a contract still in `DRAFT` or `EXECUTED` may be cancelled to the terminal `CANCELLED` state by the owning `Customer` or a `PlatformAdmin`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The table below summarizes the full API surface. Endpoints that create a new resource respond with HTTP 201 and the created resource in the envelope's `data` field; endpoints that modify, transition, or act upon an existing resource respond with HTTP 200 and the updated resource; read endpoints respond with HTTP 200. The sole exception to envelope resource payloads is the logical-clock control, which returns HTTP 200 with no resource payload. All list endpoints accept the `limit` and `offset` query parameters of Section 2.3 and return the paginated envelope. The colony list additionally accepts optional `room_id`, `strain_id`, `protocol_id`, and `lifecycle_status` filters, and the per-customer order list accepts an optional `status` filter.
