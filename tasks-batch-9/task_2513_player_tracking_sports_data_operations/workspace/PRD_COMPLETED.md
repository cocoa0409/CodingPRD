# Player Tracking Data Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Professional league tracking data is no longer a broadcast garnish; it is a regulated asset. Every position sample a league sells has to be traceable back to a rig whose measurement accuracy somebody signed for, to a game whose statistical record somebody certified, to a player whose biometric consent somebody countersigned under the collective bargaining agreement, and to a contractual window that somebody in the league office allocated out of a finite pool of licensable minutes. Today those four chains of custody live in four disconnected systems, and the seams between them are exactly where the league loses money and credibility: rigs drift out of tolerance mid-game and nobody notices until a broadcaster complains, a withdrawn consent leaves biometric attributes flowing on a commercial feed for another week, and two rights holders discover they were both sold the same territory for the same window.

The Player Tracking Data Ops API is the single system of record that closes those seams. It governs venue sensor and optical tracking installations from deployment through calibration, accuracy validation and certification; it drives game data collection through both a real-time and a post-game pipeline; it runs the statistician tagging workflow with its quality-control acceptance step and its formal correction procedure; it allocates data rights among the league, its clubs, its broadcasters and its commercial and developer licensees; it enforces player consent and collective-bargaining constraints on every biometric payload; it holds licensees to their latency SLAs and their unit quotas; and it normalizes historical metrics across tracking-system generations so that a distance figure captured three seasons ago can be compared to one captured last night.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as non-negotiable. The first is **segregation of duties**: the engineer who measured a rig may not be the operator who certifies it, the statistician who tagged an event may not be the reviewer who accepts it, and the author of a correction request may not be the reviewer who resolves it. The second is **conservation**: installation censuses, tag censuses, licensable minutes and licensee units are all conserved quantities, and every operation that moves one of them must move the counterpart in the same request so that no ledger is ever observably out of balance.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint in the API: authentication, response shape, pagination, audit fields, temporal semantics, numeric precision, concurrency control, and the order in which rejections are evaluated. All resources are addressed from the root of the service; there is no additional URL prefix in front of the collection names.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity that principal is acting in. The API recognises exactly six roles: `LeagueDataOps`, `CalibrationEngineer`, `Statistician`, `QualityReviewer`, `ClubDataOfficer`, and `Licensee`. A request that omits either header, that sends either header with an empty value — which is treated exactly as if the header were absent — or that presents an `X-User-Role` value outside those six spellings, matched exactly and case-sensitively, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose headers are well-formed but whose role is not among the roles listed for the target operation in §4 is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles divide as follows. `LeagueDataOps` is the league data-operations desk: it registers venues, rigs, clubs, games and licensees, certifies rigs and games, ratifies consent, allocates and revokes rights windows, records normalization runs, and drives the logical clock. `CalibrationEngineer` is the field engineer who records accuracy-validation runs and flags measured drift, and who may never certify a rig they themselves measured. `Statistician` is the event tagger who creates and submits tags and may open corrections. `QualityReviewer` is the quality-control desk that accepts tags and resolves corrections, and never authors the work it reviews. `ClubDataOfficer` is the club-side data officer who files and withdraws player consent and may dispute tags of its own club's games. `Licensee` is the third-party data licensee or broadcaster consuming feeds under a rights window at its contracted access tier.

Four of the six roles carry **opaque** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `LeagueDataOps`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it — as `certified_by_user_id` on a certified installation and as `ratified_by_user_id` on a ratified consent record — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `CalibrationEngineer`, whose `X-User-ID` is recorded as `recorded_by_user_id` on a calibration run and mirrored onto the rig as `latest_run_engineer_user_id`; for `Statistician`, whose `X-User-ID` is recorded as `tagged_by_user_id` on an event tag and as `submitted_by_user_id` on a correction request it authors; and for `QualityReviewer`, whose `X-User-ID` is recorded as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an accepted tag and on a resolved correction request. None of these four identifiers is resolved against a stored row; they are compared only to each other, and only for the segregation-of-duties checks described in §2.9 and for the single opaque-identity ownership check in the system — the comparison of a submitting statistician's `X-User-ID` against the tag's `tagged_by_user_id`, described in §3.5.

The remaining two roles carry **entity-backed** identities. When `X-User-Role` is `ClubDataOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Club`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Club` compare the resource's club reference to `X-User-ID` — the addressed player's `club_id` for consent filing and withdrawal, and the game's `home_club_id` or `away_club_id` for opening a correction request. Those are the only two club-ownership checks in the system; registering a player is deliberately not one of them, so a `ClubDataOfficer` may register a player against any `club_id` that resolves. The value is also stamped onto a filed consent record as `submitted_by_club_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Licensee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DataLicensee`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DataLicensee` compare the resource's `licensee_id` to `X-User-ID`, which is how feed-session usage reporting and termination are restricted to the licensee that opened the session. A `Licensee` never supplies its own identifier in a request body: the `licensee_id` of a new feed session is taken from `X-User-ID`. An entity-backed role that acts on a resource owned by another club or another licensee is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits, as the example above shows.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the server's real wall clock, not from the logical clock of §2.6: the envelope is transport metadata, and the logical clock governs only the domain timestamps §2.6 enumerates.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Transport-level rejections are rendered in this same envelope rather than in the framework's own format: a path matching no declared route and a method the matched path does not declare both carry an `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with the transport's own status — 404 and 405 respectively — and a trailing slash on any declared path is accepted as equivalent to the path without it.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the accepted range, a negative `offset`, or either parameter carrying anything but a plain integer literal, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either parameter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent and falls back to its default; an `offset` past the end of the result set yields an empty page rather than an error; and query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected. Query-parameter validation occupies the same position as body-schema validation in the ladder of §2.10 — rank 4, after path resolution — so on a sub-resource path an unresolvable parent still surfaces its 404 ahead of a bad `limit`.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with insertion order as the tie-break between rows sharing a `created_at`, unless a specific endpoint states otherwise; two endpoints deliberately do. The calibration-run history of a rig is returned newest first — `created_at` descending, with insertion order descending as the tie-break, so the run currently governing the rig's accuracy claim is the first row a reader sees. A session's usage records are returned in the chronological order of the samples themselves, ascending by the client-supplied `sampled_at_utc` with `created_at` and then insertion order as tie-breaks, which is exactly the replay order settlement uses; the licensee-wide audit trail, by contrast, keeps the default `created_at` ascending. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the returned page.

Filtering parameters, where a list endpoint offers them, are combined conjunctively with each other and with pagination. The venue installation list accepts `status`; the player list accepts `club_id`; the consent-record list of a player accepts `status`; the game list accepts `venue_id`, `home_club_id`, `away_club_id` and `collection_status`; the event-tag list accepts `game_id`, `player_id` and `review_status`; the correction-request list of a tag accepts `status`; the rights-window list of a game accepts `rights_class` and `status`; the feed-session list accepts `game_id`, `licensee_id`, `rights_window_id` and `status`; the licensee audit trail accepts `feed_session_id` and `sla_breach`; and the normalization-run list of a game accepts `basis` and `metric_code`. A filter value that is not a member of the enumeration it narrows is not a schema error; it simply matches no rows. A filter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent. The one non-string filter, `sla_breach`, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and rejects any other value with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity and Audit Fields

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`, which is a server-generated UUID string in the canonical lower-case hyphenated 8-4-4-4-12 form of a version-4 UUID. Identifiers never carry a type prefix; a game id and a rights-window id are indistinguishable by inspection and must be resolved against the collection the caller addressed. Clients never supply `id` values for resources they are creating.

Most resources additionally carry three system-managed audit fields. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first persisted. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the most recent mutation of the row. Both are read from the server's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.6, so they keep advancing no matter where a caller has set the logical clock, and they never coincide with the domain stamps of §2.6. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every subsequent mutation of the row, including mutations that touch only derived fields — when a venue's installation census is recomputed, when a game's tag census or `quality_index` moves, or when a licensee's unit ledger is adjusted by a cascade, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented exactly as if a client had edited it directly. What counts is that a field actually moved: a venue-census re-derivation that leaves the posture and all five counts exactly as they were is not a mutation, and such a no-op re-derivation refreshes neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Two record types are immutable once written and therefore carry a narrower shape: a `CalibrationRun` and a `LicenseUsageRecord` expose only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resources. Every other resource in the system — venues, tracking installations, clubs, players, consent records, games, event tags, correction requests, rights windows, licensees, feed sessions and normalization runs — carries the full complement of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Reverse-Lookup Channels

Resources are addressed by their own top-level collections. Parent linkage is expressed by identifiers inside request bodies rather than by nested creation paths, and the sub-resource read paths that do exist — a venue's installations, a rig's calibration runs, a player's consent records, a tag's correction requests, a game's rights windows and normalization runs, a session's usage records, and a licensee's usage-record audit trail — exist purely as reverse-lookup channels onto a foreign key.

When a path contains more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first unresolvable one determines the rejection. The resolution errors are `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLUB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GAME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_TAG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORRECTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RIGHTS_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSEE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FEED_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404. The same catalogue serves body-borne references: a `venue_id`, `home_club_id`, `away_club_id`, `club_id`, `game_id`, `player_id`, `rights_window_id`, `holder_id` or `proposed_player_id` that does not resolve to an existing row is a 404 naming the missing resource type, and an unresolvable `holder_id` under a licensee holder is reported as `ERR_LICENSEE_NOT_FOUND`. An identifier is never inspected for shape before it is looked up: a string that is not a UUID at all is simply an identifier that resolves to nothing, so it draws the same 404 as a well-formed identifier for a row that was never created, rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. (A body-borne reference whose JSON type is not a string is a different matter and still fails the type check at rank 4.)

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this is a fixed startup default and the server never falls back to real time. Every timestamp the domain writes from "now" — a calibration run's `recorded_at_utc`, an installation's `certified_at_utc`, a consent record's `terminated_at_utc`, a game's `certified_at_utc`, a correction's `resolved_at_utc`, a rights window's `revoked_at_utc` — is the logical clock value at the moment of the write, and every temporal comparison the domain makes is made against the logical clock. Each of those stamps carries the clock's value in exactly the spelling it was last set with: the string is stored verbatim rather than re-rendered into the three-fractional-digit form the audit fields of §2.4 use, so before any client sets the clock they all read `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` with no fractional part, and after a set they read back character-for-character whatever `to_iso8601` carried.

A `LeagueDataOps` operator advances the clock through an administrative test-driving capability that takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the new instant; it is the only field the request accepts, it is required, and it must parse as an ISO 8601 instant. The new instant is then adopted unconditionally, with no comparison against the value the clock already held, so the clock may equally be moved backwards or set to the instant it already reads. Advancing the clock is not inert: it is the mechanism by which consent lapses. Every `ConsentRecord` in `ACTIVE` status whose `expires_at_utc` is at or before the new clock value is materialised into `EXPIRED`, and the records are processed in ascending `expires_at_utc` order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, so that the observable order of the resulting cascades is deterministic. Each expiry runs the same downstream cascade as a voluntary withdrawal, described in §3.3, with `halt_reason` set to `CONSENT_EXPIRED`. That sweep runs on every set, including one that moves the clock backwards or leaves it where it was; the comparison is against the new value alone and never against the direction of the move. The response is an ordinary HTTP 200 `object` envelope whose `data` carries exactly three keys and no resource fields: the resulting `current_utc`, echoing the supplied spelling verbatim, the `expired_consent_record_count` and the `suspended_feed_session_count`, the last being the number of feed sessions the sweep's cascades moved to `SUSPENDED` across all lapsed records.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Ceiling Discipline

Three distinct numeric disciplines coexist in this system and must not be confused with one another.

**Rounding half up.** Wherever a derived decimal figure is stored, it is rounded half up to a fixed number of decimal places. A calibration run's `composite_rms_mm` is rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is the *rounded* value that the accuracy thresholds compare against — a raw composite of 90.004 becomes 90.00 and therefore passes a ceiling of 90.00. A normalization run's `normalized_value` is rounded half up to two decimal places. A game's `quality_index` is rounded half up to four decimal places, and the certification floor is compared against that four-decimal figure. Charges expressed in cents — `reserved_charge_cents`, `settled_charge_cents` on both the session and the licensee, and `sla_credit_cents` — are integers produced by rounding half up.

**Ceiling.** Wherever a continuous quantity is converted into a billable or allocable integer, the conversion rounds *up* to the next whole unit, never to nearest. The conversion is carried out in exact rational arithmetic, so a quantity that is mathematically already a whole number ceilings to itself and never one unit higher. Rights-window minutes and licensee units are both ceiling quantities. The granularity at which the ceiling is applied is load-bearing and differs per context: rights-window minutes are ceilinged once per window and never once over a sum of windows, while feed-session units are ceilinged once over the whole booked window when a session is reserved but once per usage record when a session is metered. §3.7 and §3.8 spell out both cases.

**Exact arithmetic.** Some intermediate quantities are deliberately not rounded at all. A feed session's `duration_minutes` is the exact number of seconds between its start and end divided by sixty, kept unrounded so that the ceiling that follows is applied to the true duration. Integer censuses — installation counts, tag counts, allocated and remaining minutes, unit balances — are exact integers with no rounding involved.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Game Aggregate

One write in this system carries an optimistic-concurrency guard: the creation of an event tag. The guard exists because a tag created on a key that an existing tag already occupies legitimately supersedes that existing tag, so a client working from an outdated view of the game would silently retire a colleague's freshly accepted tag instead of failing.

Creating an event tag therefore requires `expected_game_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Game`. If `expected_game_version` does not equal the game's current `version` at request time — because any other writer has advanced it since the client last read the game — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GAME_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and no tag, no supersession and no census movement occurs. Note that the game's `version` is advanced by a great many operations besides tagging: opening, closing, resuming and certifying collection, every tag submission and acceptance, every correction opened and resolved, and every census or `quality_index` recomputation. A client that reads a game, waits, and then tags without re-reading should expect this rejection.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Certifying a rig, ratifying consent, revoking a rights window, terminating a session and every other mutation are unguarded in this sense and must not be sent an expected-version input.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three operations compare the acting `X-User-ID` against a counterparty identifier already recorded on the resource and refuse to proceed when they are equal. Certifying a tracking installation compares `X-User-ID` against the rig's `latest_run_engineer_user_id`; accepting an event tag compares `X-User-ID` against the tag's `tagged_by_user_id`; approving or rejecting a correction request compares `X-User-ID` against the request's `submitted_by_user_id`. In all four operations — certify, accept, approve and reject — an equal comparison is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

These checks are about the *identifier*, not the role. Two `QualityReviewer` operators reviewing each other's work is fine; one `QualityReviewer` accepting a tag they created while acting as a `Statistician` under the same `X-User-ID` is not.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

Many operations are guarded by several checks at once, and which error a caller sees when more than one condition is violated is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Checks are evaluated in the following fixed order, and the first violated check determines the response. A caller that fixes one problem may therefore see a different error surface on the next attempt.

1. Authentication: both headers present and `X-User-Role` among the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. Role authorisation: the acting role is listed for the operation in §4 — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Path resolution: every path-parameter resource exists, resolved left to right — otherwise the matching 404 from §2.5. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
4. Body schema: required fields present, types correct, enum members among the declared values, arrays declared non-empty actually non-empty (`allowed_scopes` on a rights window and `scope` on a feed session), numeric ranges honoured, no unknown fields — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every creating or acting operation, including the acting operations that declare no input at all — those tolerate an absent or empty body and reject *any* field as unknown. A field explicitly present as JSON `null` is treated exactly as an omitted field: `null` in a required field is a missing-field rejection, and `null` in an optional field takes the declared default. One deliberate exception: an empty `segment_values` array on a normalization run *passes* this schema check, so that the domain-specific refusal at rank 38 survives to reach the caller.
5. Body reference resolution: every foreign key supplied in the body resolves — `venue_id`, `home_club_id`, `away_club_id`, `club_id`, `game_id`, `player_id`, `rights_window_id`, `holder_id`, `proposed_player_id` — otherwise the matching 404. Applies to deploying an installation, registering a player, scheduling a game, creating an event tag, allocating a rights window, opening a feed session, and opening a correction request.
6. Resource ownership: an entity-backed role acts only within its own club or licensee — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Applies to filing a consent record, withdrawing a consent record, opening a correction request as a `ClubDataOfficer`, reporting a usage record, terminating a feed session, and — as the one case where the counterparty is an opaque identifier rather than a club or licensee — submitting an event tag, where the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the tag's `tagged_by_user_id`. No read endpoint carries this check at all: a `ClubDataOfficer` or `Licensee` presenting any resolvable identifier may read any club, any licensee ledger, any feed session and any usage-record page.
7. Segregation of duties per §2.9 — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to certifying an installation, accepting a tag, approving a correction and rejecting a correction.
8. Optimistic concurrency per §2.8 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_GAME_VERSION`. Applies to creating an event tag.
9. Accuracy claim: the rig's `latest_run_result` is `PASS` — otherwise `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_PASSING`. Applies to certifying an installation. This check sits deliberately *ahead* of the installation lifecycle gate at rank 10: the accuracy claim is the thing being certified, so a rig with no passing run is refused on accuracy grounds even when its lifecycle state would also have been wrong.
10. Installation lifecycle: the rig's `status` is in the requested transition's allowed source set — recording a run allows `DEPLOYED`, `CALIBRATING` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`; certifying allows only `VALIDATED`; flagging drift allows only `CERTIFIED`; retiring allows `CERTIFIED` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INSTALLATION_STATE`, which is also what any attempt to act on a `RETIRED` rig returns.
11. Live collection: the game's `collection_status` is `LIVE` — otherwise `ERR_GAME_NOT_COLLECTING`. Applies to creating an event tag and to opening a feed session whose `delivery_mode` is `REALTIME`.
12. Closed collection: the game's `collection_status` is `COLLECTED` or `ARCHIVED` — otherwise `ERR_GAME_NOT_COLLECTED`. Applies to opening a feed session whose `delivery_mode` is `POST_GAME`, to recording a normalization run, and to certifying a game. `ARCHIVED` is accepted on certification on purpose: the first passage to `OFFICIAL` archives the game, so every later re-certification cycle necessarily runs against an already-`ARCHIVED` row, and an already-official game must be allowed to reach rank 14 rather than being deflected here.
13. Collection transition source: `SCHEDULED` for opening collection, `SUSPENDED_COLLECTION` for resuming collection, `LIVE` for closing collection — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_GAME_COLLECTION_STATE`.
14. Not already official: the game's `certification_status` is not `OFFICIAL` — otherwise `ERR_GAME_ALREADY_OFFICIAL`. Applies to certifying a game. A game still in `PENDING` is *not* deflected here; it is refused later, at rank 36.
15. Tag lifecycle: the tag's `review_status` is in the requested transition's allowed source set — `DRAFT` for submission, `SUBMITTED` for acceptance, `ACCEPTED` for opening a correction — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TAG_STATE`, which is also what any attempt to act on a `SUPERSEDED` tag returns.
16. Correction lifecycle: the correction request's `status` is `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_CORRECTION_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. Applies to approving and rejecting a correction.
17. Consent lifecycle: `PENDING` for ratification and `ACTIVE` for withdrawal — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE`.
18. Rights-window lifecycle: the window's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_RIGHTS_WINDOW_REVOKED`. Applies to opening a feed session and to revoking a window.
19. Session lifecycle: `ACTIVE` for usage reporting, and `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` for termination — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_ACTIVE`.
20. Venue posture: the game's venue has `data_posture` equal to `CERTIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Applies to opening collection and resuming collection.
21. Biometric consent: a tag whose `biometric_payload_present` is true names a player whose `biometric_release_state` is `GRANTED` — otherwise `ERR_BIOMETRIC_CONSENT_MISSING` (HTTP 422).
22. Collective bargaining version: the consent record's `cba_agreement_version` equals the current agreement version, whose fixed value is `CBA-2026A` — otherwise `ERR_CBA_VERSION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Applies both to filing a consent record and to ratifying one.
23. Biometric entitlement: a session requesting `BIOMETRIC` scope belongs to a licensee whose `biometric_entitled` is true — otherwise `ERR_BIOMETRIC_SCOPE_NOT_LICENSED` (HTTP 422).
24. Consent temporal sanity: `effective_from_utc` is strictly earlier than `expires_at_utc`, and `expires_at_utc` is strictly later than the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRY_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). Applies both to filing a consent record and to ratifying one.
25. Interval sanity: `window_end_utc` is strictly after `window_start_utc` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422). Applies to allocating a rights window and to opening a feed session.
26. Calibration freshness: the elapsed time from the rig's `latest_run_recorded_at_utc` to the logical clock is at most 72 hours — otherwise `ERR_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). Applies to certifying an installation.
27. Rights containment: the session's occupation interval falls inside the rights window's interval — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_RIGHTS` (HTTP 422).
28. Rights exclusivity: no colliding active window already covers the candidate — otherwise `ERR_RIGHTS_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Applies to allocating a rights window.
29. Consent uniqueness: the player holds no other consent record in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` status for the same `scope` — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_PENDING` (HTTP 409).
30. Tier scope: every requested scope member is permitted at the licensee's `tier` — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_IN_TIER` (HTTP 422).
31. Window scope: every requested scope member appears in the rights window's `allowed_scopes` — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_IN_RIGHTS_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
32. Latency floor: `requested_latency_ms` is greater than or equal to the venue's `min_achievable_latency_ms`, equality allowed — otherwise `ERR_LATENCY_BELOW_VENUE_FLOOR` (HTTP 422).
33. Latency ceiling: `requested_latency_ms` is less than or equal to the rights window's `max_latency_ms`, equality allowed — otherwise `ERR_LATENCY_SLA_UNSATISFIABLE` (HTTP 422).
34. Venue transport: the candidate session's bandwidth fits within the venue's egress capacity at every instant it occupies — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_TRANSPORT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
35. No open corrections: the game's `under_correction_tag_count` is zero — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_CORRECTIONS` (HTTP 409). Applies to certifying a game.
36. Accepted tags exist: the game's `accepted_tag_count` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_TAGS` (HTTP 422). Applies to certifying a game.
37. Quality floor: the game's `quality_index` is at least 0.8000, equality allowed — otherwise `ERR_QUALITY_INDEX_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422). Applies to certifying a game.
38. Normalization input: `segment_values` is a non-empty array — otherwise `ERR_NORMALIZATION_SEGMENTS_EMPTY` (HTTP 422).
39. Window/game agreement: the referenced rights window's `game_id` equals the session's `game_id` — otherwise `ERR_RIGHTS_WINDOW_GAME_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).
40. Roster membership: the attributed player's `club_id` is the game's `home_club_id` or `away_club_id` — otherwise `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_IN_GAME` (HTTP 409). Applies to creating an event tag and to approving a correction that proposes a replacement player.
41. Holder resolution: `holder_id` matches the declared `holder_type` — otherwise `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). Applies to allocating a rights window.
42. Allocation pool: the candidate window's minutes fit in the pool it draws from, equality allowed — otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409).
43. Licensee quota: the reservation the session needs is at most the licensee's `available_units`, equality allowed — otherwise `ERR_LICENSEE_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 402).
44. Session reservation: the session's already-metered units plus the new record's charge is at most the session's reserved units, equality allowed — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_RESERVATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Census Invariants

Several operations in this system reach well beyond the row the caller addressed, and the reach is transitive: a single write can move three entity types in one request. Three cascade chains carry that weight, and each is described in full in its §3 subsection: a venue losing its certified posture suspends the games it is hosting, which in turn suspends the feed sessions on those games; a consent loss masks the affected player's biometric-bearing tags, which in turn suspends the biometric-scope sessions on the affected games; and a revoked rights window settles and terminates every session held under it, which in turn releases each licensee's unconsumed reservation and returns the window's minutes to the pool it drew from. A cascade is not optional and not deferred — by the time the request that triggered it returns, every affected row is already in its new state, with `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented on every row the cascade touched.

Alongside the cascades, four censuses are maintained as conserved quantities and must balance after every request that touches them.

- **Installation census.** A venue's `certified_installation_count`, `recalibrating_installation_count` and `uncertified_installation_count` sum to exactly its `active_installation_count`; `retired_installation_count` stands outside that sum and outside every posture decision.
- **Tag census.** A game's `draft_tag_count`, `submitted_tag_count`, `accepted_tag_count`, `under_correction_tag_count` and `superseded_tag_count` sum to exactly its `total_tag_count`. Every tag transition moves a row from one bucket to another and leaves `total_tag_count` untouched; only the creation of a new tag increases it.
- **Minute ledger.** A game's `league_reserve_minutes` plus its `net_allocatable_minutes` equal its `licensable_minutes`; its `allocated_minutes_total` plus its `remaining_allocatable_minutes` equal its `net_allocatable_minutes`; and its `league_reserve_allocated_minutes` never exceeds `league_reserve_minutes`.
- **Unit ledger.** A licensee's `available_units`, `reserved_units` and `consumed_units` sum to exactly its `granted_units` at all times.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Venues and the Installation Census

Everything in this system ultimately rests on a **venue** — the arena whose sensors produce the tracking output. A `LeagueDataOps` operator registers one by supplying its `name`, the `city` it sits in, and the `tracking_tech` family installed there, which is one of `OPTICAL`, `SENSOR_UWB` or `HYBRID`. Three further inputs describe the venue's operational envelope and are referenced by rules far away from venue registration. `required_certified_installations` is the number of certified rigs the venue needs before it may be considered fully certified, and must be at least 1. `transport_capacity_mbps` is the total egress bandwidth the venue can sustain across all concurrent feed sessions, and must be greater than zero; §3.8 explains how candidate sessions are admitted against it. `min_achievable_latency_ms` is the physical latency floor of the venue's transport path, and must be at least 1; no feed session may ask for a delivery latency below it. `required_certified_installations` and `min_achievable_latency_ms` are whole numbers — a fractional value in either is a rank-4 schema rejection — while `transport_capacity_mbps` may legitimately carry a fractional value.

A freshly registered venue starts with `data_posture` of `UNCERTIFIED` and with `active_installation_count`, `certified_installation_count`, `recalibrating_installation_count`, `uncertified_installation_count` and `retired_installation_count` all zero.

Rigs are deployed into a venue as **tracking installations**. A `LeagueDataOps` operator deploys one with a payload naming the `venue_id` it belongs to, a `rig_label` for the operators on site, and a `modality` of `OPTICAL_CAMERA`, `UWB_ANCHOR` or `LIDAR`. A `rig_label` is expected to be unique among that venue's installations whose status is not `RETIRED`; retiring a rig frees its label for reuse. That expectation is an operational convention rather than a validated constraint — the server never tests it, so a duplicate `rig_label` at the same venue is accepted like any other. A new rig enters the world in `DEPLOYED` status, with `latest_run_result`, `latest_run_composite_rms_mm`, `latest_run_recorded_at_utc`, `latest_run_engineer_user_id`, `certified_at_utc` and `certified_by_user_id` all null, and with `calibration_run_count` and `drift_flag_count` at zero. Deployment lifts the venue's `active_installation_count` and `uncertified_installation_count` by one each and re-derives the venue's posture.

The venue's **data posture** is not an input; it is a derived gate over the venue's rigs, and it is re-derived from scratch on every single installation status change — when a calibration run is recorded, when a passing run auto-validates a rig, when a rig is certified, when drift is flagged, and when a rig is retired. The derivation partitions the venue's installations by `status` and counts them: the certified count is the number of rigs in `CERTIFIED`; the recalibrating count is the number in either `CALIBRATING` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`; the uncertified count is the number in either `DEPLOYED` or `VALIDATED`; the retired count is the number in `RETIRED`. Those four counts are written to `certified_installation_count`, `recalibrating_installation_count`, `uncertified_installation_count` and `retired_installation_count` respectively, and `active_installation_count` is the sum of the first three — retired rigs are excluded from the active count and from every gating count.

The posture itself follows from those counts, tested in this order. If the certified count is zero, `data_posture` is `UNCERTIFIED` — and that outcome holds regardless of how many rigs are mid-calibration. Otherwise, if the certified count is greater than or equal to the venue's `required_certified_installations` *and* the recalibrating count is exactly zero, `data_posture` is `CERTIFIED`. In every remaining case — some rigs certified but not enough of them, or enough of them but at least one rig back in calibration — `data_posture` is `RESTRICTED`. The practical consequence is that a single drift flag on a single rig is enough to pull a fully certified venue down to `RESTRICTED`, which is precisely the safety property the league wants.

Only a venue at `CERTIFIED` posture may have collection opened or resumed on its games; anything else is refused with `ERR_VENUE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Both `LeagueDataOps` and `CalibrationEngineer` may read a venue and list its installations, optionally narrowing that list by `status`; unnarrowed, that list carries the venue's rigs of every status, `RETIRED` ones included.

### 3.2. Calibration Runs, Accuracy Verdicts and Rig Certification

Accuracy is established by measurement, not by assertion. A `CalibrationEngineer` records a **calibration run** against a rig, supplying the run's three axis errors — `axis_rms_x_mm`, `axis_rms_y_mm` and `axis_rms_z_mm`, each a non-negative RMS positional error in millimetres — together with `sample_count`, the number of tracking samples the fit was computed over, and `reference_point_count`, the number of surveyed reference points used, both of which must be non-negative. The server stamps the run with the acting `X-User-ID` as `recorded_by_user_id` and the logical clock as `recorded_at_utc`, and echoes the rig's identifier onto the row as `installation_id`. A run is immutable once recorded, which is why it carries no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Two derived values are written onto the run before anything else happens. `composite_rms_mm` is the quadratic mean of the three axis errors: each of the three axis values is squared, the three squares are added together, that sum is divided by three, and the square root of the result is rounded half up to two decimal places. `result` is the accuracy verdict, and it is decided in exactly this order. First the sufficiency gate: if `sample_count` is below 5,000 *or* `reference_point_count` is below 12, the verdict is `INVALID` and no accuracy claim is recorded at all, whatever the composite figure happens to be — the composite is still computed, written onto the run and mirrored onto the rig, it simply establishes nothing. Otherwise, if `composite_rms_mm` is at most 90.00 millimetres — nine centimetres, inclusive, so a composite of exactly 90.00 passes — the verdict is `PASS`. Otherwise, if `composite_rms_mm` is at most 140.00 millimetres, again inclusive, the verdict is `PROVISIONAL`. Anything above that is a `FAIL`. Only the run being recorded participates in this evaluation; no prior run of the rig contributes to it.

Recording a run is permitted while the rig is in `DEPLOYED`, `CALIBRATING` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, and any other source state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSTALLATION_STATE`. The recording always moves the rig into `CALIBRATING`, whether it came from `DEPLOYED` or from `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, and in both cases the same effects follow: the run row is created, the rig's `calibration_run_count` rises by one, and its four mirror fields — `latest_run_result`, `latest_run_composite_rms_mm`, `latest_run_recorded_at_utc` and `latest_run_engineer_user_id` — are refreshed from the run that now has the greatest `recorded_at_utc` (with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, so the last-written of two runs sharing an instant wins). The venue's census and posture are then re-derived. Immediately afterwards, and without any further call, a rig whose just-recorded verdict was `PASS` advances `CALIBRATING → VALIDATED`, and the venue census and posture are re-derived again to reflect the move. A verdict of `PROVISIONAL`, `FAIL` or `INVALID` leaves the rig sitting in `CALIBRATING`, awaiting another attempt. Both status writes are real mutations of the rig row, so a passing run refreshes the rig's `updated_at` and increments its `version` twice — once for the move into `CALIBRATING` and once for the move into `VALIDATED` — while a non-passing run increments it once, even when the rig was already in `CALIBRATING` and the first write therefore changed no field. Recording a run re-derives the census and posture and does nothing more: it never fires the game-and-session degradation cascade described below, so a run that drops a venue from `CERTIFIED` to `RESTRICTED` by putting a rig back into `CALIBRATING` leaves every `LIVE` game at that venue live and `CLEAN`.

Certification is the league's counter-signature on somebody else's measurement, and it is reserved to `LeagueDataOps`. A rig may be certified only from `VALIDATED`; its `latest_run_result` must be `PASS`; the elapsed time from `latest_run_recorded_at_utc` to the current logical clock must be at most 72 hours — three days of validity, after which the measurement is stale and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`, the comparison being signed rather than absolute, so a run stamped *later* than the current clock (the clock having since been wound back) yields a negative elapsed time and satisfies the check; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the rig's `latest_run_engineer_user_id`, so that the engineer who measured the rig cannot be the operator who certifies it. A rig whose latest verdict is not `PASS` is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_PASSING` ahead of any state complaint, per §2.10. On success the rig moves `VALIDATED → CERTIFIED`, `certified_at_utc` is stamped with the logical clock and `certified_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, the venue's certified count rises by one while its uncertified count falls by one, and the venue's posture is re-derived — which is the moment a venue can finally flip from `RESTRICTED` to `CERTIFIED`.

Drift is the reverse move, and it belongs to the engineer, not the league. A `CalibrationEngineer` who measures drift on a certified rig flags it, moving the rig `CERTIFIED → RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED` and raising its `drift_flag_count` by one. What follows is the system's most consequential cascade, and all three of its links fire inside the same request. The first link re-derives the venue's census — certified count down one, recalibrating count up one — and with it the `data_posture`, which necessarily leaves `CERTIFIED`. The second link, caused by the first, reaches every game hosted at that venue whose `collection_status` is `LIVE`: each such game moves `LIVE → SUSPENDED_COLLECTION` and its `data_quality_flag` moves from `CLEAN` to `DEGRADED`. The third link, caused by the second, reaches every feed session on those games whose `status` is `ACTIVE`: each moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `halt_reason` set to `VENUE_DEGRADED`. The second and third links are conditional on the posture genuinely departing `CERTIFIED`: they fire only when the posture read `CERTIFIED` immediately before the flag, so a venue already standing at `RESTRICTED` or `UNCERTIFIED` when drift is flagged moves no game and no session at all, however many `LIVE` games it happens to be hosting. Critically, a suspension of this kind leaves the licensee's quota completely untouched — neither `reserved_units` nor `available_units` moves, because the session has not been settled, merely paused.

Retirement is permanent and is reserved to `LeagueDataOps`. A rig may be retired from either `CERTIFIED` or `RECALIBRATION_REQUIRED`, and `RETIRED` is a terminal state from which no transition is possible. Retiring a certified rig lowers the venue's certified count by one; retiring a rig awaiting recalibration lowers the recalibrating count by one; in both cases `active_installation_count` falls by one and `retired_installation_count` rises by one, and the posture is re-derived. When that re-derivation causes the posture to leave `CERTIFIED`, retirement fires exactly the same live-game suspension and active-session suspension cascade as a drift flag, with the same `VENUE_DEGRADED` halt reason and the same untouched quotas. Retirement rewrites nothing else on the rig: its `certified_at_utc`, `certified_by_user_id` and four latest-run mirror fields stand exactly as they were, so a retired rig still reads back the certification and the measurement it once held. Both `LeagueDataOps` and `CalibrationEngineer` may read a rig and page through its calibration-run history.

### 3.3. Clubs, Rosters and the Consent Chain

Clubs are the counterparties in every game and the custodians of their players' consent. A `LeagueDataOps` operator registers a **club** with its `name` and its `market_code`, a three-letter market code expected to be unique across the league — an expectation the server enforces in neither respect, so any string is accepted as a `market_code`, whatever its length and however many clubs already carry it. A club's `roster_size` is derived, starting at zero and rising by one for every player registered against it, which refreshes the club row's `updated_at` and `version`; nothing ever lowers it. Both `LeagueDataOps` and any `ClubDataOfficer` may read a club — the read carries no ownership check, so a club officer may read a club other than its own.

Players are registered either by the league or by the club itself, with a payload naming the `club_id` holding the registration, the player's `full_name` and a `jersey_number` between 0 and 99 inclusive, expected to be unique within the club though that expectation, like the club's `market_code`, is not enforced. A new player begins with `biometric_release_state` of `NONE`, no `active_consent_record_id`, and a `masked_tag_count` of zero. The player list may be narrowed by `club_id`, and `LeagueDataOps`, `ClubDataOfficer`, `Statistician` and `QualityReviewer` may all read players — the tagging and review desks need to see `biometric_release_state` before they attach biometric attributes to anything.

**Consent records** are the collective-bargaining artefacts that make biometric tracking lawful, and only a `ClubDataOfficer` may file one, against a player on its own roster. Filing requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the addressed player's `club_id`; anything else is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The payload declares the `scope` covered — either `BIOMETRIC_TRACKING` or `POSITIONAL_ONLY` — the `cba_agreement_version` the club countersigned against, and the interval the consent covers, given as `effective_from_utc` and `expires_at_utc`. The agreement version is not free text: it must equal the current collective bargaining agreement version, whose fixed value is `CBA-2026A`, and any other value is refused with `ERR_CBA_VERSION_MISMATCH`. The interval must be coherent and forward-looking — `effective_from_utc` strictly earlier than `expires_at_utc`, and `expires_at_utc` strictly later than the current logical clock — with `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRY_IN_PAST` covering both failures. A player may hold at most one live record per scope: if the player already has a record in `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` status covering the same `scope`, the filing is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_PENDING`. A successfully filed record is created in `PENDING`, with `submitted_by_club_id` stamped from the filing officer's `X-User-ID`, and with `ratified_by_user_id` and `terminated_at_utc` still null. The record echoes its `player_id`, and it carries `effective_from_utc` and `expires_at_utc` back in exactly the spelling the client supplied rather than re-rendered into any canonical form. Both `LeagueDataOps` and any `ClubDataOfficer` may page a player's consent records, narrowing by `status`; the ownership check governs filing and withdrawal only and never the read, so a club officer may page another club's player.

Ratification is the league's side of the agreement and belongs to `LeagueDataOps`. A record may be ratified only from `PENDING` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE` — and the agreement-version and expiry checks are re-applied at ratification time, so a record filed days ago whose `expires_at_utc` has since fallen behind the logical clock is refused at this step rather than silently activated. Ratification moves the record `PENDING → ACTIVE`, stamps `ratified_by_user_id` with the ratifying operator's `X-User-ID`, and points the player's `active_consent_record_id` at the record. That pointer holds exactly one record at a time and is overwritten whichever `scope` the newly ratified record covers, so it always names the player's most recently ratified record rather than the biometric one specifically. Whether the player's `biometric_release_state` moves depends on the scope: ratifying a `BIOMETRIC_TRACKING` record moves it to `GRANTED` from whatever it held before, including from `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED`, while ratifying a `POSITIONAL_ONLY` record leaves `biometric_release_state` exactly as it was. Only a player at `GRANTED` may carry biometric payloads on their tags. Ratification restores the release state but never reverses an earlier masking: tags whose `biometric_masked` was set by a previous consent loss stay masked for good, and neither the player's nor a game's `masked_tag_count` ever falls.

Withdrawal is the club's unilateral right and belongs to the `ClubDataOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the player's `club_id`. A record may be withdrawn only from `ACTIVE`. Withdrawal moves the record `ACTIVE → WITHDRAWN`, stamps `terminated_at_utc` with the logical clock, and clears the player's `active_consent_record_id` to null when that pointer named the withdrawn record — leaving it untouched when it had since been re-pointed at some later ratification. Then the consent cascade runs, in three linked steps within the same request. The first link moves the player's `biometric_release_state` from `GRANTED` to `WITHDRAWN`. The whole cascade is keyed on that release state rather than on the withdrawn record's `scope`: a player standing at `GRANTED` loses the grant whichever scope's record was withdrawn, and a player not at `GRANTED` — the ordinary case for a `POSITIONAL_ONLY` withdrawal — stops the cascade right here, with no tag masked and no session suspended, though the withdrawal still refreshes the player row's `updated_at` and `version`. The second link, caused by the first, reaches every event tag attributed to that player whose `biometric_payload_present` is true and whose `review_status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`: each such tag has `biometric_masked` set to true, suppressing the biometric attributes from every downstream read, and the player's `masked_tag_count` together with each affected game's `masked_tag_count` rises accordingly — counting only the tags this cascade actually flips, since a tag already carrying `biometric_masked` from an earlier consent loss is skipped and never counted a second time. The third link, caused by the second, reaches every feed session in `ACTIVE` status that is running on a game which just gained a masked tag and whose requested `scope` includes `BIOMETRIC`: each such session moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `halt_reason` set to `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN`, and, as with venue degradation, the licensee's quota is left untouched because nothing has been settled.

Lapse is the same story with a different trigger and a different label. When the logical clock advances past an active record's `expires_at_utc`, that record moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` with `terminated_at_utc` set to the new clock value and `active_consent_record_id` cleared under the same only-if-it-named-this-record rule, the player's `biometric_release_state` moves from `GRANTED` to `EXPIRED`, and the identical tag-masking and biometric-session-suspension cascade runs under the identical `GRANTED` guard — the only difference being that the suspended sessions carry `halt_reason` of `CONSENT_EXPIRED` rather than `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN`. Both `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` are terminal: neither record can be revived, and a club that wants consent back must file a fresh record, which the uniqueness rule now permits because no live record remains for the scope.

### 3.4. Scheduling Games and the Two Collection Dimensions

A **game** is the unit of data production, and it carries two entirely independent state dimensions on the same row. `collection_status` tracks the data-collection pipeline and is driven by league operations and by the venue-posture cascade. `certification_status` tracks the statistical record and is driven by the tagging and quality-control workflow described in §3.5 and §3.6. Neither dimension gates the other's transitions except where explicitly stated, and readers should not assume that a game whose collection has closed has a certified record, or vice versa.

`LeagueDataOps` schedules a game by naming the `venue_id` that will host it, the `home_club_id` and `away_club_id` contesting it, its `scheduled_start_utc` tip-off instant, the `tracking_generation` of the system that will produce its data — one of `G1`, `G2` or `G3`, which §3.9 uses to normalize historical metrics — and `licensable_minutes`, the total minutes of tracking output that may be licensed out of this game, which must be at least 1 and forms the allocation pool §3.7 draws from. The two clubs are not required to differ — the server never compares `home_club_id` against `away_club_id`, so a game naming the same club on both sides is scheduled like any other — and `scheduled_start_utc` is stored and read back in exactly the spelling the client supplied rather than re-rendered into any canonical form. The new game opens with `collection_status` of `SCHEDULED` and `certification_status` of `PENDING`, `data_quality_flag` of `CLEAN`, every tag-census counter (`draft_tag_count`, `submitted_tag_count`, `accepted_tag_count`, `under_correction_tag_count`, `superseded_tag_count`, `total_tag_count`), correction counter (`approved_correction_count`, `rejected_correction_count`) and `masked_tag_count` at zero, `quality_index` at 0.0, and `certified_at_utc` null. Its minute ledger is seeded at this moment: `league_reserve_minutes` and `net_allocatable_minutes` are computed from `licensable_minutes` as §3.7 describes, `allocated_minutes_total` and `league_reserve_allocated_minutes` start at zero, and `remaining_allocatable_minutes` starts equal to `net_allocatable_minutes`.

Opening collection is the operation that turns a scheduled fixture into a live data source. It is permitted only from `SCHEDULED` — any other source state is `ERR_INVALID_GAME_COLLECTION_STATE` — and only when the hosting venue's `data_posture` reads `CERTIFIED` at that instant, which is the single hard link from the calibration subsystem into game operations. Success advances `collection_status` `SCHEDULED → LIVE`, sets `data_quality_flag` to `CLEAN`, and increments the game's `version`. While a game is `LIVE`, statisticians may tag it and licensees may open real-time feed sessions on it; outside `LIVE`, both are refused with `ERR_GAME_NOT_COLLECTING`.

Suspension is never requested; it is inflicted. A game moves `LIVE → SUSPENDED_COLLECTION` only as the second link of the venue-degradation cascade of §3.2, and when it does, `data_quality_flag` flips to `DEGRADED` and every `ACTIVE` feed session on the game moves to `SUSPENDED` with `halt_reason` of `VENUE_DEGRADED`. Recovery is requested: once the venue's `data_posture` reads `CERTIFIED` again — which requires the offending rig to have been recalibrated and re-certified, or retired — `LeagueDataOps` resumes the game. Resumption is permitted only from `SUSPENDED_COLLECTION`, re-checks the venue posture and is refused with `ERR_VENUE_NOT_CERTIFIED` when the posture has not recovered, advances `collection_status` `SUSPENDED_COLLECTION → LIVE`, returns `data_quality_flag` to `CLEAN`, increments the game's `version`, and reactivates precisely those sessions that the degradation paused: every feed session on the game in `SUSPENDED` status whose `halt_reason` is `VENUE_DEGRADED` moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `halt_reason` cleared to null. Sessions suspended for any other reason — a consent loss or a repeated SLA breach — are deliberately left where they are, because resuming collection says nothing about consent or delivery quality.

Closing collection hands the game to the post-game pipeline. It is permitted only from `LIVE`, advances `collection_status` `LIVE → COLLECTED`, and increments the game's `version`. Two effects accompany it. Every feed session on the game in `ACTIVE` status whose `delivery_mode` is `REALTIME` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `halt_reason` of `CLIENT_REQUEST`, since there is no longer a live stream to consume; post-game sessions are untouched, and — this being a suspension rather than a settlement — no licensee's unit ledger moves and each suspended session still counts towards its rights window's `active_feed_session_count`. And the game's `quality_index` is recomputed, so that the statistical picture presented to the certification desk reflects the final tag population. Once collection is `COLLECTED`, post-game feed sessions and normalization runs become available; attempting either before collection closed is refused with `ERR_GAME_NOT_COLLECTED`.

The last collection move is not a call at all. When the game's `certification_status` reaches `OFFICIAL`, the system advances `collection_status` `COLLECTED → ARCHIVED` in the same request, with no effect beyond the status write and the accompanying `updated_at` and `version` refresh. `ARCHIVED` is the terminal collection state; a re-certification of an already-archived game leaves `collection_status` at `ARCHIVED`. Reading a game — both dimensions, the tag census, the `quality_index` and the whole minute ledger — is open to `LeagueDataOps`, `Statistician`, `QualityReviewer`, `ClubDataOfficer` and `Licensee`, and the game list may be narrowed by `venue_id`, `home_club_id`, `away_club_id` and `collection_status`.

### 3.5. Event Tagging, Supersession and Quality Review

Statisticians turn tracking output into a statistical record by attaching **event tags** to a live game. A `Statistician` creates one with a payload naming the `game_id` being tagged and the `player_id` the event is attributed to, a `tag_type` of `SHOT`, `PASS`, `FOUL` or `SUBSTITUTION`, a `clock_ms` game-clock offset in milliseconds which must be non-negative, and a `confidence` figure between 0 and 1 inclusive expressing how sure the statistician is of the call. Two further inputs carry the sensitive and the concurrency-critical parts of the request. `biometric_payload_present` is optional and defaults to false; when true, it declares that the tag carries biometric-derived attributes such as heart rate or player load. `expected_game_version` is mandatory and carries the client's last observed `version` of the game, as §2.8 requires.

Three domain gates guard tag creation beyond the schema and reference checks. The game's `collection_status` must be `LIVE`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_GAME_NOT_COLLECTING`. A tag whose `biometric_payload_present` is true requires the attributed player's `biometric_release_state` to be `GRANTED`, and a player at `NONE`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED` yields `ERR_BIOMETRIC_CONSENT_MISSING` — this is the point where the consent chain of §3.3 becomes an operational constraint rather than paperwork. And the attributed player must actually be in the game: their `club_id` must equal the game's `home_club_id` or its `away_club_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_IN_GAME`.

A successful create yields a tag in `DRAFT` `review_status`, with `tagged_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and with `reviewed_by_user_id`, `superseded_by_tag_id` and `corrected_clock_ms` null, `correction_count` at zero, and `biometric_masked` false. The game's `version` is incremented by one, its `total_tag_count` and `draft_tag_count` each rise by one, and the tag's `game_version_at_tag` is stamped with the game's `version` *after* that increment — it is the audit anchor a reviewer uses to reconstruct which concurrency generation a disputed tag was written against.

Creation is also the only way a tag leaves the workflow involuntarily. The triple of `game_id`, `tag_type` and `clock_ms` identifies one event, and a new tag on a triple that an existing non-superseded tag already occupies **supersedes** that existing tag. Supersession is permitted out of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`: the older tag moves to `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_tag_id` is set to the new tag's id, and the game's census moves that row out of whichever bucket held it and into `superseded_tag_count`, leaving `total_tag_count` unchanged for the superseded row. A tag sitting in `UNDER_CORRECTION` falls outside that source set and is therefore *not* superseded: the new tag is created and enters the census as an ordinary `DRAFT` row while the disputed tag stays exactly where it is, so a triple legitimately carries two live tags until the correction resolves. When the superseded row came out of `ACCEPTED`, the game's `accepted_tag_count` falls by one and the game's `quality_index` is recomputed, because the scored tag population has genuinely changed. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: no submission, acceptance or correction may be attempted against such a tag, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TAG_STATE`.

Submission is the statistician's hand-off, and it is personal: only the statistician whose `X-User-ID` matches the tag's `tagged_by_user_id` may submit it, and another operator's attempt is refused as an ownership failure with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A tag may be submitted only from `DRAFT`. Submission advances the tag `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, lowers the game's `draft_tag_count` by one, raises its `submitted_tag_count` by one, and increments the game's `version`.

Acceptance is the quality-control desk's counter-signature and belongs exclusively to `QualityReviewer`. A tag may be accepted only from `SUBMITTED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the tag's `tagged_by_user_id` — a reviewer accepting their own tagging work is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Acceptance advances the tag `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewer's `X-User-ID`, lowers the game's `submitted_tag_count` by one and raises its `accepted_tag_count` by one, increments the game's `version`, and recomputes the game's `quality_index`. It also carries one cross-dimension effect: when this is the game's first acceptance, so that `accepted_tag_count` becomes at least 1 while `certification_status` still reads `PENDING`, the game's `certification_status` advances `PENDING → PROVISIONAL` in the same request. A game therefore never sits in `PENDING` with accepted work behind it.

Tags are readable by `LeagueDataOps`, `Statistician`, `QualityReviewer` and `ClubDataOfficer`, and the tag list may be narrowed by `game_id`, `player_id` and `review_status`.

### 3.6. Corrections, the Quality Index and Game Certification

Accepted work is not frozen work. When a statistician or a club believes an accepted tag is wrong, they open a **correction request** against it, which is the only mechanism by which an accepted tag's content may change. Both `Statistician` and `ClubDataOfficer` may open one; a `ClubDataOfficer` may only dispute tags of its own club's games, so its `X-User-ID` must equal the game's `home_club_id` or `away_club_id`, and anything else is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

The request declares a `reason_code` of `WRONG_PLAYER`, `WRONG_CLOCK`, `WRONG_TYPE` or `BIOMETRIC_REMOVAL`, and carries at most one replacement value. `proposed_clock_ms` is required when the reason is `WRONG_CLOCK` — and, like the tag's own `clock_ms`, must be a non-negative integer, a negative value being a rank-4 schema rejection — and must be omitted otherwise; `proposed_player_id` is required when the reason is `WRONG_PLAYER` and must be omitted otherwise; a request that supplies the wrong pairing is a malformed body and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A correction may be opened only against a tag in `ACCEPTED` status. Because opening a correction immediately moves the tag to `UNDER_CORRECTION`, a second correction against the same tag while the first is still unresolved necessarily fails that same state gate and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TAG_STATE` — at most one unresolved correction can exist per tag by construction.

Opening a correction creates the request in `SUBMITTED` status, with `submitted_by_user_id` stamped from the author's `X-User-ID`, `submitted_by_role` recording whether the author acted as `Statistician` or as `ClubDataOfficer`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `resolved_at_utc` still null, and the disputed tag's identifier echoed as `event_tag_id`. The tag moves `ACCEPTED → UNDER_CORRECTION`, the game's `accepted_tag_count` falls by one and its `under_correction_tag_count` rises by one while `total_tag_count` stays put, the game's `version` is incremented, and the `quality_index` is recomputed. Opening a correction also reaches the certification dimension: if the game's `certification_status` was `OFFICIAL`, it moves `OFFICIAL → DISPUTED`, with no effect beyond that status write — the tag's own move into `UNDER_CORRECTION` is the substantive event. Correction requests filed against a tag are listable by `LeagueDataOps`, `Statistician`, `QualityReviewer` and `ClubDataOfficer`, narrowable by `status`.

Resolution belongs exclusively to `QualityReviewer`, must act on a request still in `SUBMITTED` status — `ERR_CORRECTION_ALREADY_RESOLVED` otherwise — and must come from someone other than the author, since the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against `submitted_by_user_id` and equality yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. From there the two branches diverge sharply, and the divergence is the point of the whole mechanism.

**Approval** accepts the dispute. When the reason is `WRONG_PLAYER`, the proposed replacement must itself be eligible: the player named by `proposed_player_id` must have a `club_id` equal to the game's `home_club_id` or `away_club_id`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_IN_GAME`. Roster membership is the *only* thing re-checked about the replacement: the biometric gate of §3.5 is not re-applied here, so a `WRONG_PLAYER` approval may legitimately re-attribute a tag whose `biometric_payload_present` is true to a player holding no granted biometric release, and it neither masks the tag nor moves any `masked_tag_count` on either player. On success the request moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, `reviewed_by_user_id` is stamped with the reviewer's `X-User-ID` and `resolved_at_utc` with the logical clock. The tag moves `UNDER_CORRECTION → ACCEPTED` with the corrected values applied — a `WRONG_CLOCK` approval writes the request's `proposed_clock_ms` into the tag's `corrected_clock_ms`, and a `WRONG_PLAYER` approval overwrites the tag's `player_id` with the request's `proposed_player_id` — and the tag's `correction_count` rises by one. A `WRONG_TYPE` or `BIOMETRIC_REMOVAL` approval carries no replacement value and therefore writes no field back onto the tag, but still raises `correction_count`, because the correction genuinely happened. The game's `approved_correction_count` rises by one, its census is restored (`under_correction_tag_count` down one, `accepted_tag_count` up one), its `quality_index` is recomputed, and its `version` is incremented. Finally the certification dimension moves `DISPUTED → PROVISIONAL`: the official record has been altered, so the game must be certified again from scratch.

**Rejection** upholds the tag. The request moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with the same `reviewed_by_user_id` and `resolved_at_utc` stamps, and the tag moves `UNDER_CORRECTION → ACCEPTED` with every field unchanged and `correction_count` unchanged. The game's `rejected_correction_count` rises by one, its census is restored exactly as on approval, its `quality_index` is recomputed — with the lighter rejected-dispute weight, so a rejected challenge costs the game far less than an upheld one — and its `version` is incremented. The certification dimension moves `DISPUTED → OFFICIAL` directly, provided the game's `under_correction_tag_count` is zero once the tag has returned to `ACCEPTED`, and the game's existing `certified_at_utc` is preserved untouched. No re-certification is needed, because nothing about the official record actually changed. Both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

The **quality index** is the single number the certification desk trusts, and it is recomputed on every event that could move it: a tag acceptance, a tag supersession, the opening of a correction, the resolution of a correction either way, and the closing of collection. It is derived as follows. The scored population is the set of the game's event tags whose `review_status` is either `ACCEPTED` or `UNDER_CORRECTION`; `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` and `SUPERSEDED` tags are excluded. If that population is empty, the index short-circuits to 0.0000 and no weighting is applied at all. Otherwise three components are formed. The first is mean confidence: the `confidence` values of the scored tags are added together and divided by the number of scored tags. The second is the correction penalty: the game's `approved_correction_count` is taken at full weight of 1.0 and its `rejected_correction_count` at a quarter weight of 0.25, those two products are added, the sum is divided by the greater of 1 and the game's `accepted_tag_count`, and the result is capped at 1.0. The third is completeness: the game's `accepted_tag_count` is divided by the four accepted tags a fully-tagged game is expected to carry, and the result is capped at 1.0. The index is then half the mean confidence, plus three tenths of one minus the correction penalty, plus two tenths of the completeness, rounded half up to four decimal places and written to `quality_index`.

Certifying a game is `LeagueDataOps` work and is the gate everything upstream has been feeding. It is permitted only while `certification_status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `DISPUTED`; an already-official game is refused with `ERR_GAME_ALREADY_OFFICIAL`, and a game still in `PENDING` reaches the accepted-tags gate instead and is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_TAGS`. The game's `collection_status` must be `COLLECTED` or `ARCHIVED`, and `ARCHIVED` is accepted deliberately, since the first passage to `OFFICIAL` archives the game and every later dispute-and-recertify cycle therefore runs against an archived row. Beyond that, three substantive conditions apply, evaluated in the order §2.10 fixes: no correction may be open, so `under_correction_tag_count` must be zero or the attempt is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CORRECTIONS`; `accepted_tag_count` must be at least 1; and `quality_index` must be at least 0.8000 inclusive, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_QUALITY_INDEX_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. On success `certification_status` advances to `OFFICIAL`, `certified_at_utc` is stamped with the logical clock, `collection_status` advances `COLLECTED → ARCHIVED` (or stays `ARCHIVED` on a re-certification), and the game's `version` is incremented.

### 3.7. Allocating Data Rights Out of a Finite Pool

A game's tracking output is sold in **rights windows**, and the league office allocates them out of a pool that is fixed the moment the game is scheduled. Only `LeagueDataOps` may allocate. The payload declares the `rights_class` being granted — `BROADCAST_LIVE`, `IN_VENUE_DISPLAY`, `COMMERCIAL_LICENSE` or `LEAGUE_ARCHIVE` — the `holder_type` of the party receiving it (`LEAGUE`, `CLUB` or `LICENSEE`), an optional `holder_id` resolving that party, a `territory`, the covered interval as `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, a `max_latency_ms` delivery SLA ceiling of at least 1 millisecond, and `allowed_scopes`, a non-empty subset of `POSITIONAL`, `EVENT` and `BIOMETRIC` that sessions under the window may request. A new window is created in `ACTIVE` status with `revoked_at_utc` null and `active_feed_session_count` at zero, and echoes the game's identifier as `game_id`. `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` are stored and read back in exactly the spelling the client supplied, not re-rendered into any canonical form.

The holder reference is polymorphic and is resolved against the declared `holder_type`. A `LEAGUE` window must omit `holder_id` entirely. A `CLUB` window must carry a `holder_id` that is a club identifier equal to the game's `home_club_id` or `away_club_id` — the league does not grant a club rights over a game it is not playing in. A `LICENSEE` window must carry a `holder_id` that resolves to an existing data licensee. Any mismatch of this contract — a league window that supplies a holder, a club window naming an uninvolved club, a licensee window with no licensee — is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_MISMATCH`, while a `holder_id` that simply does not exist is a 404 naming the missing type — but that 404 only arises under a `CLUB` or a `LICENSEE` holder, since those are the only two types whose `holder_id` is resolved at all. A `LEAGUE` window's `holder_id` is never looked up, so any non-null value there draws `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_MISMATCH` whether or not it names a real row.

Exclusivity is the commercial promise behind a rights window, and it is enforced on three axes at once: the same game, the same `rights_class`, and a colliding `territory`. A territory is expected to be either the literal `GLOBAL` or a two-letter country code, but that expectation is an operational convention rather than a validated constraint: the server checks neither the length nor the membership of the string it is handed and accepts any `territory` whatsoever, using the value for nothing but the collision rule. In that rule the literal `GLOBAL` collides with every territory including itself, while any two other values collide only when they are exactly equal. When those axes coincide, the candidate window may not intersect any active window already on the game. Intervals are **closed** on both ends, so two windows that merely touch — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — do collide. For `BROADCAST_LIVE` windows a further safety margin applies: both the candidate interval and each existing interval are first expanded by 300 seconds, five minutes, on each side before intersection is tested, so consecutive live broadcast windows must be separated by more than ten minutes of real distance. A collision is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Independently of exclusivity, the interval itself must be coherent: `window_end_utc` strictly after `window_start_utc`, or `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW_BOUNDS`.

The **minute ledger** is what makes rights allocation a zero-sum exercise, and its steps are evaluated in a fixed order that resolves what would otherwise look like a circular dependency. Step one, on the gross basis: the league's archival reserve, `league_reserve_minutes`, is fifteen percent of the game's `licensable_minutes`, rounded up to a whole minute. The reserve is always a share of the *gross* licensable figure, never of the net pool it creates. Step two: `net_allocatable_minutes` is `licensable_minutes` less `league_reserve_minutes`. Step three establishes two disjoint pools, each drawn down in window creation order from oldest to newest: the reserve pool is drawn only by windows whose `rights_class` is `LEAGUE_ARCHIVE`, and the net pool is drawn by windows of all three other classes. Each window's own draw, `allocated_minutes`, is the number of seconds between its `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` divided by sixty and rounded up to a whole minute; the rounding up happens once per window and is never applied to a sum of windows. `league_reserve_allocated_minutes` is the total of `allocated_minutes` across the game's active `LEAGUE_ARCHIVE` windows, `allocated_minutes_total` is the total across the game's active windows of every other class, and `remaining_allocatable_minutes` is `net_allocatable_minutes` less `allocated_minutes_total`. Revoked windows contribute nothing to either total.

Step four is the admission test for a candidate window, and it is pool-specific. A `LEAGUE_ARCHIVE` candidate is admitted when its `allocated_minutes` is less than or equal to `league_reserve_minutes` less `league_reserve_allocated_minutes`; a candidate of any other class is admitted when its `allocated_minutes` is less than or equal to `remaining_allocatable_minutes`. Equality is admitted in both cases — a window that exactly exhausts its pool is legal. A candidate that does not fit is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_POOL_EXHAUSTED`, and the practical consequence is that the archival reserve can never be cannibalised by a broadcaster and a broadcaster can never be squeezed out by the archive.

Revocation is the league's emergency brake and belongs to `LeagueDataOps`. A window may be revoked only from `ACTIVE`; a repeat attempt on an already-revoked window is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_WINDOW_REVOKED`. Revocation moves the window `ACTIVE → REVOKED` and stamps `revoked_at_utc` with the logical clock, then cascades in two linked steps. The first link reaches every feed session held under the window whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`: each is settled against its reported usage exactly as a voluntary termination would settle it, and moved to `TERMINATED` with `halt_reason` of `CLIENT_REQUEST`. Since `active_feed_session_count` tracks the sessions under the window that are `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, it falls to zero. The second link, caused by the first, settles each affected licensee's ledger: that licensee's `reserved_units` falls by the session's `reserved_units`, its `consumed_units` rises by the session's metered units, its `available_units` rises by the session's `refunded_units`, and its `settled_charge_cents` rises by the session's `settled_charge_cents`. Finally the window's minutes return to the pool they came from: for a window of any non-archive class the game's `allocated_minutes_total` falls by that window's `allocated_minutes` and `remaining_allocatable_minutes` rises by the same amount, while for a `LEAGUE_ARCHIVE` window the game's `league_reserve_allocated_minutes` falls by that amount, freeing the same number of minutes inside the reserve pool. The window row itself is mutated exactly once by the whole operation, so its `version` advances by one however many sessions the cascade settles, while each settled session, each affected licensee and the game each carry their own increment. `REVOKED` is terminal. A game's rights windows are listable by `LeagueDataOps`, `ClubDataOfficer` and `Licensee`, narrowable by `rights_class` and `status`.

### 3.8. Licensees, Feed Sessions and the Unit Quota Ledger

Third-party access is sold as a **data licensee** record, onboarded by `LeagueDataOps`. The payload names the licensee, declares its `licensee_class` — `BROADCASTER`, `COMMERCIAL_LICENSEE` or `DEVELOPER` — its access `tier` of `BRONZE`, `SILVER` or `GOLD`, the `granted_units` its licence covers for the term, which must be non-negative, and the optional `biometric_entitled` flag, which defaults to false and records whether the licence paperwork permits biometric data at all. On creation `available_units` is initialised to `granted_units`, while `reserved_units`, `consumed_units`, `settled_charge_cents`, `sla_credit_cents` and `audit_flag_count` all start at zero. A licensee's `name` is its display name for the league's own reporting.

The `tier` fixes two things at once. It fixes the unit price: a `BRONZE` licence is billed at 40 cents per unit, a `SILVER` licence at 30 cents, and a `GOLD` licence at 22 cents — volume buys a discount. And it fixes the scope ceiling: `BRONZE` may request only `POSITIONAL` data, `SILVER` may request `POSITIONAL` and `EVENT`, and only `GOLD` may request `BIOMETRIC` alongside the other two. A scope member outside the tier's set is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_IN_TIER`. Biometric access requires both conditions independently: the tier must be `GOLD` *and* `biometric_entitled` must be true, and a `GOLD` licensee whose paperwork never permitted biometrics is refused with `ERR_BIOMETRIC_SCOPE_NOT_LICENSED`. Both `LeagueDataOps` and the licensee itself may read the full quota ledger.

A **feed session** is a licensee exercising a rights window. Only a `Licensee` may open one, and the session's `licensee_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID` rather than from the body — a licensee cannot open a session in another licensee's name. That identifier must itself resolve to a `DataLicensee`, and it is resolved as the third body-reference lookup of the request, after `game_id` and after `rights_window_id`, so an `X-User-ID` naming no licensee draws `ERR_LICENSEE_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404 — but only once the game and the rights window have both resolved. The payload names the `game_id` whose output is being streamed and the `rights_window_id` the session is exercised under, declares a `delivery_mode` of `REALTIME` or `POST_GAME`, states the `bandwidth_mbps` of sustained egress the session will occupy (which must be greater than zero), fixes the occupation interval as `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, asks for a `requested_latency_ms` guarantee of at least 1 millisecond, and lists the requested `scope`, a non-empty subset of `POSITIONAL`, `EVENT` and `BIOMETRIC`.

Admission runs a long ladder of checks, in the order §2.10 fixes. The delivery mode picks the game gate: a `REALTIME` session requires the game's `collection_status` to be `LIVE` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_GAME_NOT_COLLECTING`, while a `POST_GAME` session requires `COLLECTED` or `ARCHIVED` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_GAME_NOT_COLLECTED`. The rights window must be `ACTIVE`. Every requested scope member must appear both in the tier's permitted set and in the window's `allowed_scopes`, the latter failing with `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_IN_RIGHTS_WINDOW`. The latency request is squeezed from both sides: `requested_latency_ms` must be greater than or equal to the hosting venue's `min_achievable_latency_ms`, since no amount of contract writing beats physics, and less than or equal to the rights window's `max_latency_ms`, since a session may not claim a tighter guarantee than the window actually sells; equality is acceptable at both bounds, and the two failures are `ERR_LATENCY_BELOW_VENUE_FLOOR` and `ERR_LATENCY_SLA_UNSATISFIABLE`. The occupation interval must be coherent, and it must be **contained** in the rights window's interval: the session's interval is half-open, covering its start instant and stopping short of its end instant, while the window's interval is closed on both ends, and a session reaching outside is refused with `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_RIGHTS`. The referenced window must belong to the same game the session names, or the request is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_WINDOW_GAME_MISMATCH`.

The venue transport check is the one that couples licensees to each other. At every instant inside the candidate's half-open occupation interval, the sum of `bandwidth_mbps` across all feed sessions in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status that sit on games at the same venue, plus the candidate's own `bandwidth_mbps`, must not exceed that venue's `transport_capacity_mbps`. A suspended session still holds its slice of the pipe — suspension is a pause, not a release. Two sessions where one ends at exactly the instant the other starts are adjacent rather than overlapping and do not contend. An over-subscription is refused with `ERR_VENUE_TRANSPORT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Pricing happens at the moment of admission, under the **reserved-forward** variant, and again at settlement, under the **metered-actual** variant. Both variants share two derived inputs. `duration_minutes` is the exact number of seconds between the session's `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` divided by sixty, kept unrounded. `latency_multiplier` is a premium selected from the latency the session asked for: a `requested_latency_ms` of at most 250 milliseconds — a quarter of a second — carries a multiplier of 1.50; anything above that but at most 1,000 milliseconds, a full second, carries 1.20; anything slower carries 1.00. Both derived inputs are persisted on the session row and read back on every session read, and the multiplier is frozen there at admission: every later usage record is charged against the stored `latency_multiplier` rather than against a figure recomputed from `requested_latency_ms`. As with a rights window, the session's `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` are echoed back in exactly the spelling the client supplied.

Under the reserved-forward variant the session is priced as if the entire booked window were fully consumed: `reserved_units` is the product of `bandwidth_mbps`, `duration_minutes` and `latency_multiplier`, rounded up to a whole unit with a single ceiling over the whole window, and `reserved_charge_cents` is that unit count multiplied by the licensee's per-unit tier rate and rounded half up to whole cents. The reservation must fit the licensee's remaining quota: `reserved_units` must be less than or equal to the licensee's `available_units`, equality allowed, or the session is refused with `ERR_LICENSEE_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` at HTTP 402. On success the licensee's `available_units` falls by `reserved_units` and its `reserved_units` rises by the same amount — the total of granted units never moves, only its distribution — and the rights window's `active_feed_session_count` rises by one. The session is created in `ACTIVE` status with `halt_reason` null, `metered_units`, `settled_charge_cents`, `refunded_units`, `usage_record_count` and `sla_breach_count` all at zero.

Metered consumption is reported as **usage records**, and only the owning licensee may report them, against a session in `ACTIVE` status. Each record declares the `sampled_at_utc` instant the sample covers, the measured `bandwidth_mbps` during the sample (greater than zero), the `duration_seconds` the sample spans (at least 1), and the observed `latency_p95_ms` during the sample (at least 1). The record's `units_charged` is the product of its `bandwidth_mbps`, its `duration_seconds` divided by sixty, and the parent session's `latency_multiplier`, rounded up to a whole unit — the ceiling is applied to this record alone. The record carries the parent session's identifier as `feed_session_id` and copies the parent's `licensee_id`, and it is immutable, hence without `updated_at` or `version`. Its `sampled_at_utc` is echoed back verbatim as supplied and is never required to fall inside the session's occupation interval — it orders the replay and nothing more. Before the record is accepted, the reservation is re-checked: the session's already-accumulated `metered_units` plus the new record's `units_charged` must be less than or equal to the session's `reserved_units`, equality allowed, or the report is refused with `ERR_SESSION_RESERVATION_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 402. This is why the refund at settlement can never be negative.

Accepting a record adds its `units_charged` to the session's `metered_units` and raises the session's `usage_record_count` by one. The licensee's unit ledger is deliberately not touched at this point — `available_units`, `reserved_units` and `consumed_units` all stay exactly where admission left them until settlement — so a record that does not breach its SLA leaves the licensee row entirely unmutated, with neither `updated_at` nor `version` moving. Delivery quality is judged on the same record: `sla_breach` is set to true when the record's `latency_p95_ms` is strictly greater than the governing rights window's `max_latency_ms`. A breach raises the session's `sla_breach_count` by one and the licensee's `audit_flag_count` by one, and it credits the licensee: a quarter of the record's charge — the record's `units_charged` multiplied by the tier rate, multiplied by 0.25 and rounded half up to whole cents — is added to the licensee's `sla_credit_cents`. Breaches are also a circuit breaker: on the third breach recorded against a single session, that session moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `halt_reason` of `SLA_BREACH_THRESHOLD`. Such a suspension is not undone by resuming a game's collection, which only revives sessions halted for venue degradation. A session's usage records are listable by the league and by the owning licensee, and the licensee audit trail across all of that licensee's sessions may be narrowed by `feed_session_id` and by `sla_breach`.

Termination is the licensee's own act, permitted from `ACTIVE` or from `SUSPENDED`, and restricted to the licensee whose `X-User-ID` equals the session's `licensee_id`. Termination settles the session under the metered-actual variant, which ignores the booked window entirely and prices only what was actually reported. The session's usage records are replayed in ascending `sampled_at_utc` order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; each record's charge is the per-record ceiling described above; `metered_units` is the sum of those per-record charges; and `settled_charge_cents` is `metered_units` multiplied by the tier rate and rounded half up to whole cents. There is no status filter on usage records — they are immutable and every one of them counts. `refunded_units` is the session's `reserved_units` less its metered units rounded up to a whole unit. Because the ceiling granularity differs between the two variants — once over the whole window when reserving, once per record when metering — identical total wall-clock coverage can and does yield a different unit count under each, and the difference is exactly what gets refunded.

Settlement then moves the licensee's ledger in four coordinated steps: `reserved_units` falls by the session's `reserved_units`, `consumed_units` rises by the session's metered units rounded up to a whole unit, `available_units` rises by the session's `refunded_units`, and the licensee's `settled_charge_cents` rises by the session's `settled_charge_cents`. The session moves to `TERMINATED` with `halt_reason` overwritten to `CLIENT_REQUEST` — overwritten even when the session was suspended for some other reason — and the rights window's `active_feed_session_count` falls by one. `TERMINATED` is terminal; a further termination attempt is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_ACTIVE`, as is any usage report against a non-active session. Feed sessions are readable by `LeagueDataOps` and by licensees, and the session list may be narrowed by `game_id`, `licensee_id`, `rights_window_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Normalizing History Across Tracking Generations

Tracking hardware changes between seasons, and a distance figure produced by a first-generation optical pipeline is not directly comparable to one produced three generations later. **Normalization runs** exist to make historical output comparable, and only `LeagueDataOps` records them, against a game whose `collection_status` is `COLLECTED` or `ARCHIVED`. The payload declares the `basis` to apply — either `RAW_GENERATION` or `CANONICAL_SEASON` — the `metric_code` being normalized, which is `TOTAL_DISTANCE_M` or `SPRINT_DISTANCE_M`, and `segment_values`, the per-segment raw metric values in the capturing generation's native units, in the order they were captured, every one of which must be non-negative. The run echoes the game's identifier as `game_id`, and echoes `basis`, `metric_code` and `segment_values` back exactly as supplied; recording it is a pure read of the game, mutating nothing on the game row, so the game's `updated_at` and `version` do not move.

An empty `segment_values` array is a deliberate special case. It passes the request-schema check, which is what allows the domain-specific refusal to reach the caller: an empty array is refused with `ERR_NORMALIZATION_SEGMENTS_EMPTY` at HTTP 422 rather than with a generic malformed-body error, so operators can tell "you sent nothing to normalize" apart from "you sent nonsense".

Every run stamps four derived descriptors alongside the answer. `source_generation` is copied from the game's `tracking_generation` at run time, freezing the provenance even if the game record is later amended. `scale_factor_applied` is the generation's scale constant: a `G1` capture scales by 1.042, a `G2` capture by 1.017, and a `G3` capture by 1.000, since the current generation is the reference. `drift_correction_pct` records the season drift correction that was applied, expressed as a percentage. And `mapping_version` stamps the canonical mapping table the run used, whose fixed value is `MAP-2026.1`.

The two bases differ in *when* rounding happens, and that difference is the entire point of offering both. Under the `RAW_GENERATION` basis, no drift correction is applied — `drift_correction_pct` is written as 0 — and the value is reproduced the way the capturing generation's own pipeline published it: each element of `segment_values` is multiplied by the generation scale factor and rounded half up to two decimal places, those rounded per-segment figures are added together, and the total is rounded half up to two decimal places again. Rounding each segment first accumulates a small upward bias, which is faithful to how the original pipeline reported.

Under the `CANONICAL_SEASON` basis the arithmetic is deliberately reversed: the elements of `segment_values` are added together at full precision with no intermediate rounding, that exact sum is multiplied once by the generation scale factor, the result is then multiplied by one plus the generation's drift correction expressed as a fraction — that is, the drift percentage divided by one hundred — and only then is the figure rounded half up to two decimal places, exactly once. The drift corrections are a reduction of six tenths of one percent for `G1` data and two tenths of one percent for `G2` data, and no correction at all for `G3` data, so `drift_correction_pct` is written as -0.6, -0.2 and 0.0 respectively. Because of both the reversed rounding order and the drift correction, the same `segment_values` yield a strictly different `normalized_value` under the two bases for `G1` and `G2` data, and the league relies on that: `RAW_GENERATION` is what a historical broadcast graphic must match, and `CANONICAL_SEASON` is what a cross-season leaderboard must use. A game's normalization runs are listable by `LeagueDataOps` and `ClubDataOfficer`, narrowable by `basis` and `metric_code`.
