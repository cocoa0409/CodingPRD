# Citizen Science Data Collection API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Continent-scale bird and habitat monitoring lives or dies on the credibility of volunteer-gathered records. A network of thousands of amateur observers can cover more ground in a single migration season than any professional survey team could cover in a decade, but only if every record that reaches the published dataset carries a defensible provenance chain: who observed it, under what written protocol, with what training credential, backed by what photographic evidence, adjudicated by which subject-matter experts, and at what measured confidence. This document specifies the backend service that maintains that chain — a single API, mounted under `/citizen-science`, that carries a survey protocol from drafting through activation, a volunteer from enrolment through certification and recognition, an observation from a muddy field notebook through weighted expert consensus into a citable published dataset, and a borrowed acoustic recorder from the equipment cage through quarantine and recalibration and back.

Three tensions shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **quality versus participation**: the program must be strict enough that a research partner can cite the dataset, yet forgiving enough that a well-meaning volunteer who mis-identifies one bird is coached rather than expelled — hence a bounded revision cycle, a probation tier that is reversible, and two independently computed quality scores that answer different questions. The second is **evidence versus convenience**: photographic evidence is screened mechanically against its own EXIF metadata before it counts, and an observation submitted a week after the fact scores materially lower than one filed the same evening. The third is **coordination without locking**: multiple experts adjudicate the same observation, multiple staff members book the same instrument, and multiple publications may be assembled from the same campaign, so the service uses optimistic concurrency on the shared parent rows rather than serialising the humans behind them.

Everything in this specification is deterministic and testable. There is no wall-clock dependency, no random tie-breaking, and no "best effort" cascade: every derived number has a pinned evaluation order, every state change names the rows it touches, and every rejection names a stable error identifier.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent or empty, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries any value outside the four recognised roles, the request fails with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well-formed but the acting role is not among the roles that the target operation permits, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The four roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `Volunteer`, `Expert`, and `ResearchPartner`, spelled exactly as shown.

`ProgramAdmin` is the program-staff role: it owns protocol design, campaign operations, equipment stewardship, publication management, and partner administration. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check is applied to a `ProgramAdmin` call.

The other three roles are entity-backed, meaning `X-User-ID` names a row that must exist. When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Volunteer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Volunteer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Expert`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Expert`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Expert` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ResearchPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ResearchPartner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ResearchPartner` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. For all three entity-backed roles, if `X-User-ID` does not resolve to a live row of the backing entity, the request fails with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Where an entity-backed actor attempts to write a resource it does not own, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Concretely: a `Volunteer` may only submit, photograph, or resubmit observations whose `volunteer_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only request equipment loans whose `volunteer_id` equals `X-User-ID`; an `Expert` may only complete training on a certification whose `trainer_expert_id` equals `X-User-ID`; a `ResearchPartner` may only request access grants whose `partner_id` equals `X-User-ID`. This ownership rule guards exactly six operations: observation submission, photo attachment, observation resubmission, training completion, equipment-loan creation, and access-grant creation.

Reading is broadly shared but not uniform. `ProgramAdmin` may read every collection in the system. A `Volunteer` may read protocols, campaigns, sites, observations, photo evidence, votes, equipment, loans, publications, certifications, volunteer records, and the recognition-award ledger. An `Expert` may read protocols, volunteers, certifications, campaigns, sites, observations, photo evidence, votes, and publications, plus the vote history of any expert. A `ResearchPartner` may read protocols, campaigns, sites, observations, publications, partner records, and access grants. The authoritative per-operation role list is the table in §4; any role absent from an operation's list receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Exclusivity matters as much as permission. Only a `ProgramAdmin` creates and activates protocols; registers volunteers, experts, equipment, and research partners; enrols and grants certifications; creates, opens, and closes campaigns and adds survey sites; issues and takes back equipment loans, clears quarantine, and completes calibration; retracts accepted observations; creates, releases, and withdraws publications; approves partners and approves or revokes access grants; reinstates volunteers on probation or suspension; and runs maintenance sweeps or sets the logical clock. Only a `Volunteer` submits observations, attaches photo evidence, and resubmits observations sent back for revision. Only an `Expert` completes training, starts a review, and casts verification votes. A `ResearchPartner` has no exclusive write operation of its own; it shares equipment-loan-free read access and the ability to request grants for itself, an ability it shares with `ProgramAdmin`. Equipment-loan requests may likewise be filed by either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `Volunteer`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every collection-returning operation is paginated by the same two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending, oldest row first. The pagination block returned in `meta` carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Filters are additive: supplying several narrows the result to rows matching all of them. Protocols may be filtered by `status`, by `code`, and by `supersedes_protocol_id`. Volunteers may be filtered by `status`, `recognition_tier`, and `home_region_code`. Experts may be filtered by `status`, `specialty_taxon`, and `linked_volunteer_id`. Certifications may be filtered by `volunteer_id`, `protocol_id`, `trainer_expert_id`, and `status`. Campaigns may be filtered by `protocol_id` and `status`. The site list of a campaign may be filtered by `coverage_status`. Observations may be filtered by `campaign_id`, `site_id`, `volunteer_id`, `equipment_loan_id`, `publication_id`, `review_status`, and `publication_status`. Photo evidence on an observation may be filtered by `status`, and the vote list of an observation may be filtered by `round_number`. Equipment may be filtered by `status` and `equipment_type`; equipment loans by `equipment_id`, `volunteer_id`, and `status`. Publications may be filtered by `campaign_id`, `supersedes_publication_id`, and `status`. Research partners may be filtered by `status` and `trust_tier`; access grants by `publication_id`, `partner_id`, and `status`. The recognition-award ledger may be filtered by `volunteer_id`, `observation_id`, and `award_type`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id`, a UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied value is ignored. Every record also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at insert time.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Whenever a rule in this document says a row's state, a derived count, or a computed score changed, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock and its `version` advances by one — including when the change was caused indirectly by a cascade rather than by a request aimed at that row. Two record types are exceptions: `VerificationVote` and `RecognitionAward` are append-only ledger rows. Both carry only `id` and `created_at`; neither carries `updated_at` and neither carries `version`. They are never mutated after insert, so there is nothing for those two fields to express.

Time in this system is entirely logical. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Every subsequent timestamp stamp, submission-lag computation, certification-expiry comparison, calibration comparison, quarantine comparison, embargo comparison, and grant-expiry comparison reads the new value. Any rule below that speaks of "the logical clock" or "now" means this value and nothing else.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Contested Parent Rows

Three write operations touch a parent row that several actors legitimately contend for, and each of the three requires the client to prove it has observed the parent's latest state. In each case the version field is a mandatory input, not an optional one; omitting it is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Casting a verification vote — `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/votes` — requires `expected_observation_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Observation`. If it does not equal the observation's current `version`, the vote is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OBSERVATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second expert voting on the same observation is entirely legitimate product behaviour, so it is precisely this stale-version check that forces each voter to re-read the accumulated weight tallies — and the possibly already-resolved `review_status` — before adding their own weight.

Requesting an equipment loan — `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans` — requires `expected_equipment_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Equipment`. A mismatch with the equipment's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EQUIPMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Two non-overlapping loan windows on the same unit are both legitimate, so the check exists to force the requester to re-read the unit's occupied intervals and its current quarantine window before booking.

Creating a publication — `POST /citizen-science/publications` — requires `expected_campaign_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the owning `Campaign`. A mismatch with the campaign's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a new publication legitimately supersedes the campaign's prior one, this check is what stops a creator from superseding a publication set it never observed.

No other operation in this API takes a version field, and no other operation may reject a request for a version reason.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Unless a rule states otherwise, every rounding in this specification is round-half-up on the decimal value.

Money-like and score-like quantities each carry a fixed scale. `provisional_quality_points`, `final_quality_points`, `geo_delta_meters`, `coverage_ratio`, `quality_floor`, `mean_quality`, `calibration_drift_ppm`, and `drift_added_ppm` are all carried at 2 decimal places. `quality_score`, `lifetime_quality_score`, `coverage_score`, `consensus_confidence`, `weight_applied`, `confirm_weight`, `reject_weight`, and `revision_weight` are all carried at 4 decimal places. `verification_weight` and `priority_weight` are supplied at 2 decimal places. `points_awarded`, `loan_hours`, `total_loan_hours`, `record_count`, `site_count`, `gap_count`, `version_number`, `revision_round`, `photo_count`, and all the various counters are integers.

Where a rounding *order* is specified, that order is part of the contract and not an implementation liberty. Three places in this document pin an order explicitly: the quality-point calculation carries its intermediate raw score at full precision and rounds only at the two variant endpoints; the coverage calculation rounds each site's ratio before averaging them; and the calibration-drift calculation rounds the hourly base drift before the penalties are added and rounds again afterwards. Deviating from a pinned order produces a different number and is wrong even when the deviation looks harmless.

Two integer-truncating operations appear and are not rounding: loan duration in hours is always rounded **up** to the next whole hour, and a recognition award's base point value is always rounded **down** to the next whole point.

### 2.7. The Request Admission Ladder

Most operations in this system are guarded by several checks at once, and the order in which those checks run is observable: a request that violates two rules must fail with the error belonging to the earlier rule. The complete ladder is given below in strict evaluation order by priority number. A check whose scope does not include the current operation is skipped, and evaluation stops at the first check that fails.

- **Priority 1 — authentication (all operations).** Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` must be present and non-empty, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
- **Priority 2 — role recognition (all operations).** `X-User-Role` must be one of `ProgramAdmin`, `Volunteer`, `Expert`, or `ResearchPartner`, otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
- **Priority 3 — role authorisation (all operations).** The acting role must appear in the operation's permitted-role list in §4, otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
- **Priority 4 — path resource existence (every operation with a templated path segment).** Each templated segment is resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that names no row terminates the request with that entity's not-found error at HTTP 404: `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`. Left-to-right means that when a nested path names both a parent and a child and neither exists, the parent's error is the one returned.
- **Priority 5 — actor existence (every operation called as `Volunteer`, `Expert`, or `ResearchPartner`).** `X-User-ID` must resolve to a live row of the backing entity, otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_FOUND` (404).
- **Priority 6 — payload shape (all creating and acting operations that take a body).** Every required field must be present, every value must match its declared type, every enum value must be a declared member of its enumeration, and every numeric value must fall inside its declared range; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).
- **Priority 7 — body foreign-key existence (all body-carrying operations that reference other rows).** Every foreign key supplied in the body must resolve to an existing row, otherwise the corresponding 404: `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`.
- **Priority 8 — resource ownership.** Guards exactly six operations: `POST /citizen-science/observations`, `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/photos`, `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/resubmit`, `POST /citizen-science/certifications/{id}/complete-training`, `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans`, and `POST /citizen-science/access-grants`. A `Volunteer` actor's `X-User-ID` must equal the observation's or loan's `volunteer_id`; an `Expert` actor's `X-User-ID` must equal the certification's `trainer_expert_id`; a `ResearchPartner` actor's `X-User-ID` must equal the grant's `partner_id`. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
- **Priority 9 — self-verification bar.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/start-review` and `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/votes`. The acting `Expert`'s `linked_volunteer_id` must not equal the observation's `volunteer_id`, otherwise `ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION` (403).
- **Priority 10 — trainer-conflict bar.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/votes` only. When the observation's `revision_round` is 0, the acting `Expert` must not be the `trainer_expert_id` on the observer's certification for the campaign's protocol, otherwise `ERR_TRAINER_VERIFICATION_CONFLICT` (403).
- **Priority 11 — duplicate vote.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/votes` only. No `VerificationVote` may already exist for the same combination of `observation_id`, `expert_id`, and `round_number`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE` (409).
- **Priority 12 — optimistic concurrency.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/votes`, `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans`, and `POST /citizen-science/publications`. The supplied `expected_observation_version`, `expected_equipment_version`, or `expected_campaign_version` must equal the parent row's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_OBSERVATION_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_EQUIPMENT_VERSION`, or `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` respectively (412).
- **Priority 20 — protocol draft state.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/protocols/{id}/activate`. The protocol's `status` must be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_DRAFT` (409).
- **Priority 21 — certification state.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/certifications/{id}/complete-training` (requires `ENROLLED`) and `POST /citizen-science/certifications/{id}/grant` (requires `TRAINED`). Otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID` (409).
- **Priority 22 — campaign state.** Guards five operations: `POST /citizen-science/observations` and `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/resubmit` both require the campaign's `status` to be `OPEN`; `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/open` requires `PLANNED` or `CLOSED`; `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/close` requires `OPEN`; and `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/sites` requires a campaign that is neither `PUBLISHED` nor `ARCHIVED`. Otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_OPEN` (409).
- **Priority 23 — observation review state.** Guards four operations: `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/start-review` requires `SUBMITTED`, `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/votes` requires `UNDER_REVIEW`, `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/resubmit` requires `NEEDS_REVISION`, and `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/retract` requires `ACCEPTED`. Otherwise `ERR_OBSERVATION_STATE_INVALID` (409).
- **Priority 24 — loan and equipment state.** Guards five operations: `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans`, `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans/{id}/issue`, `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans/{id}/return`, `POST /citizen-science/equipment/{id}/clear-quarantine`, and `POST /citizen-science/equipment/{id}/complete-calibration`. A loan must be `REQUESTED` to be issued and must be `ISSUED` or `OVERDUE` to be returned, otherwise `ERR_LOAN_STATE_INVALID` (409). A unit must be `QUARANTINE` to have quarantine cleared and `IN_CALIBRATION` to complete calibration, and a unit whose `status` is `RETIRED` may not be booked at all, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409).
- **Priority 25 — publication state.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/publications` (the owning campaign's `status` must be `CLOSED` or `PUBLISHED`), `POST /citizen-science/publications/{id}/release` (the publication must be `DRAFT`), and `POST /citizen-science/publications/{id}/withdraw` (the publication must be `DRAFT`, `EMBARGOED`, or `RELEASED`). Otherwise `ERR_PUBLICATION_STATE_INVALID` (409).
- **Priority 26 — grant state.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/access-grants/{id}/approve` (requires `REQUESTED`) and `POST /citizen-science/access-grants/{id}/revoke` (requires `APPROVED`). Otherwise `ERR_GRANT_STATE_INVALID` (409).
- **Priority 27 — partner standing.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/access-grants/{id}/approve`. The referenced partner's `status` must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
- **Priority 28 — protocol active.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/certifications/{id}/grant` and `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/open`. The referenced protocol's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
- **Priority 30 — volunteer standing.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations`, `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/resubmit`, and `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans`. The volunteer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING` (403).
- **Priority 31 — valid certification.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations` and `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/resubmit`. The observer must hold a certification for the campaign's protocol whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `expires_at` is strictly later than the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_VALID` (403).
- **Priority 32 — certification level.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations`. The observer's certification `level` must rank at or above the protocol's `required_certification_level`, using the ordinal ranking where `BASIC` is 1, `ADVANCED` is 2, and `EXPERT` is 3. Otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (403).
- **Priority 33 — equipment requirement.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations`. When the protocol's `required_equipment_type` is anything other than `NONE`, the submission must supply an `equipment_loan_id` whose loan has `status` `ISSUED`, whose loan `volunteer_id` equals the observer, and whose instrument's `equipment_type` equals the required type. Otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (422).
- **Priority 34 — expert standing.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/start-review` and `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/votes`. The acting expert's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_EXPERT_SUSPENDED` (403).
- **Priority 35 — exam pass mark.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/certifications/{id}/complete-training`. The supplied `exam_score` must be at least 70, otherwise `ERR_EXAM_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (422).
- **Priority 36 — site belongs to campaign.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations`. The supplied `site_id` must name a site whose `campaign_id` equals the supplied `campaign_id`, otherwise `ERR_SITE_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH` (409).
- **Priority 37 — no reviews in flight.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/close`. No observation of the campaign may have `review_status` `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `NEEDS_REVISION`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_REVIEWS_PENDING` (409).
- **Priority 38 — sensitive scope.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/access-grants/{id}/approve`. A grant whose `scope` is `SENSITIVE` requires the partner's `trust_tier` to be `TRUSTED`, otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
- **Priority 39 — data use agreement.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/access-grants/{id}/approve`. A grant whose `scope` is `FULL` or `SENSITIVE` requires the partner's `data_use_agreement_signed_at` to be non-null, otherwise `ERR_DUA_NOT_SIGNED` (403).
- **Priority 40 — observation inside season.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations`. The calendar date of `observed_at` must fall inside the campaign's season window, inclusive of both `season_start_date` and `season_end_date`, otherwise `ERR_OBSERVED_OUTSIDE_SEASON` (422).
- **Priority 41 — submission lag.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations`. The elapsed hours from `observed_at` to the logical clock must be at most the protocol's `max_submission_lag_hours`, otherwise `ERR_SUBMISSION_LAG_EXCEEDED` (422).
- **Priority 42 — instrument interval conflict.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans`. The requested window must not intersect any occupied interval of the unit under the half-open regime described in §2.9, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (409).
- **Priority 43 — season window conflict.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/campaigns`. The new campaign's season window must not intersect the window of any other non-archived campaign on the same protocol under the closed-inclusive regime described in §2.9, otherwise `ERR_SEASON_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
- **Priority 44 — calibration currency.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/equipment-loans/{id}/issue`. The unit's `calibration_valid_until` must be strictly later than the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED` (409).
- **Priority 45 — quarantine elapsed.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/equipment/{id}/clear-quarantine`. The logical clock must be at or after the unit's `quarantine_until`, otherwise `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_ELAPSED` (409).
- **Priority 46 — grant expiry in the future.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/access-grants` and `POST /citizen-science/access-grants/{id}/approve`. The grant's `expires_at` must be strictly later than the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_GRANT_EXPIRY_IN_PAST` (422).
- **Priority 47 — embargo scope.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/access-grants/{id}/approve`. A grant against an `EMBARGOED` publication may only be approved when its `scope` is `AGGREGATE`, otherwise `ERR_EMBARGO_ACTIVE` (409).
- **Priority 48 — uniqueness.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/protocols`, `POST /citizen-science/volunteers`, `POST /citizen-science/equipment`, `POST /citizen-science/publications`, `POST /citizen-science/partners`, `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/sites`, and `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/photos`. Protocol `code`, volunteer `email`, equipment `asset_tag`, publication `doi`, and partner `org_name` are each unique across all rows of their type; `site_code` is unique within its owning campaign; `file_hash` is unique within its owning observation. A collision yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409).
- **Priority 49 — revision budget.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/resubmit`. The observation's `revision_round` must be strictly less than 2, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).
- **Priority 50 — photo evidence sufficiency.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations/{id}/start-review`. The observation's `photo_count` must be at least the protocol's `min_photo_count`, otherwise `ERR_PHOTO_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
- **Priority 51 — campaign has sites.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/open` and `POST /citizen-science/campaigns/{id}/close`. The campaign's `site_count` must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_HAS_NO_SITES` (422).
- **Priority 52 — record floor.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/publications` and `POST /citizen-science/publications/{id}/release`. At creation, the candidate set must hold at least 2 observations, counted after any superseded publication has released its queued observations back into the pool; at release, the publication's `record_count` must be at least 2. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RECORDS` (422).
- **Priority 53 — site saturation.** Guards `POST /citizen-science/observations`. The target site's `accepted_observation_count` must be strictly less than twice the campaign's `target_observations_per_site` at submission time, otherwise `ERR_SITE_SATURATED` (409).

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Nested Resources

A great many operations in this program change more than the row the caller named. Those secondary changes are not incidental bookkeeping — they are part of the operation's contract, they are applied within the same request, and they are immediately visible to the next read. Five cascade chains recur throughout §3 and are referred to there by name: the **protocol supersession chain**, which retires an old protocol and revokes the certifications hanging off it; the **rejection chain**, which drives a volunteer's standing down and strips their credentials and pending loans; the **acceptance chain**, which mints recognition awards and refreshes coverage arithmetic; the **retraction chain**, which reverses an acceptance and can re-open a closed campaign; and the **withdrawal chain**, which revokes partner grants and can suspend partners. Each chain is described link by link at the point in §3 where it is triggered, and each link states which rows it touches.

Two general rules govern all of them. First, cascades are applied in the link order given, and later links may read values written by earlier links — the ordering is therefore observable and is part of the specification. Second, every row a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if it had been written directly.

Nested collections behave predictably: a request for the sites of a campaign, the photo evidence of an observation, the votes on an observation, or the votes cast by an expert returns only the children of the named parent, paginated and ordered by `created_at` ascending like any other list. The parent segment is resolved before the child filter is applied, so a nested read against a non-existent parent is a 404 naming the parent, never an empty page.

### 2.9. Two Interval Regimes: Instrument Occupancy and Season Windows

The program schedules two very different kinds of interval and deliberately treats their boundaries differently. Confusing them is the single most common source of scheduling defects, so both are stated exhaustively here.

**Instrument occupancy is half-open.** A loan request describes the window from `start_at` up to but not including `due_at`. An instrument's occupied intervals are derived from its existing loans: a loan whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `ISSUED`, or `OVERDUE` occupies the window from its `start_at` up to but not including its `due_at`; a loan whose `status` is `RETURNED` occupies the window from its `start_at` up to but not including its `returned_at` plus 48 hours, the post-return quarantine tail; and a loan whose `status` is `LOST` or `CANCELLED` occupies nothing at all. The requested window conflicts with an occupied window exactly when the requested start is strictly earlier than the occupied end **and** the occupied start is strictly earlier than the requested end. Adjacency is therefore permitted: a request whose `start_at` lands exactly on an occupied interval's end instant is accepted.

**Campaign seasons are closed on both ends.** A campaign's window runs from `season_start_date` through `season_end_date` with both dates included. Two campaigns bound to the same `protocol_id` may not hold intersecting windows. Campaigns whose `status` is `ARCHIVED` are excluded from the comparison entirely; campaigns in every other status occupy their window. Two windows intersect unless one ends strictly before the other begins — that is, they are disjoint only when the first window's `season_end_date` is strictly earlier than the second window's `season_start_date`, or the second's `season_end_date` is strictly earlier than the first's `season_start_date`. Adjacency is therefore forbidden: a new campaign whose `season_start_date` equals an existing campaign's `season_end_date` is rejected.

### 2.10. Derived Values and the Recognition Ledger Invariant

A number of stored fields are denormalized mirrors of things the service could otherwise recompute. They exist so that reads are cheap, and the contract is that they are always correct at the moment a read observes them, never eventually correct.

The recognition ledger carries the strictest of these invariants: for every volunteer, the sum of `points_awarded` across every `RecognitionAward` row bearing that volunteer's `volunteer_id` equals that volunteer's `lifetime_points` at all times. There is no separate adjustment path — points are only ever changed by appending a ledger row, and a reversal is itself a ledger row with a negative value rather than a deletion or an edit of history.

The same discipline applies to the counting fields. A volunteer's `accepted_observation_count`, `rejected_observation_count`, `consecutive_accepted`, and `consecutive_rejections` track the adjudication history described in §3.7. An expert's `vote_count` counts the `VerificationVote` rows they have cast and `retracted_confirm_count` counts the deciding-round confirmations of theirs that were later retracted. A site's `accepted_observation_count`, `coverage_ratio`, and `coverage_status`, and a campaign's `accepted_observation_count`, `site_count`, `coverage_score`, and `gap_count`, are all maintained by the coverage arithmetic in §3.3. A publication's `record_count` and `mean_quality` track its bound observations, and a partner's `active_grant_count` tracks its approved grants. An instrument's `total_loan_hours` accumulates the `loan_hours` of every loan on it that reached `RETURNED` or `LOST`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing a Survey Protocol and Maintaining the Version Chain

Everything the program measures begins as a written **survey protocol** — the methodological contract that says who may observe, how quickly they must report, how much photographic evidence is needed, and what instrument must be in their hands. A `ProgramAdmin` drafts one by supplying a `code`, a descriptive `title`, the `required_certification_level` a volunteer must hold before submitting under it (`BASIC`, `ADVANCED`, or `EXPERT`), a `min_photo_count` of at least zero, and a `max_submission_lag_hours` greater than zero. Two further inputs are optional. `required_equipment_type` names the instrument class an observation must be backed by and takes the values `NONE`, `ACOUSTIC_RECORDER`, `GPS_LOGGER`, or `SPOTTING_SCOPE`, defaulting to `NONE` when omitted. `certification_valid_days` sets how long a granted credential under this protocol stays live and defaults to 365 days when omitted; it must be greater than zero.

A new protocol lands in `DRAFT` with `activated_at` still null. Its `code` is unique across every protocol row in the system regardless of that row's status, so re-using a code — even one belonging to a long-retired protocol — is a duplicate-key collision. The `version_number` is derived rather than supplied: a first-generation protocol, created without a `supersedes_protocol_id`, gets `version_number` 1, while a protocol created with a `supersedes_protocol_id` pointing at an existing row gets that row's `version_number` plus one. This is the self-referential version chain: methodology evolves by drafting a successor that names its predecessor, not by editing a protocol that observations already reference.

Activation is where the chain actually advances. A `DRAFT` protocol moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE` only when it is genuinely still in `DRAFT` — an activation aimed at a protocol already `ACTIVE` or already `RETIRED` is refused with `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_DRAFT` — and only when no other protocol carrying the same `code` is currently `ACTIVE`, the sole exception being the very row this protocol names as its `supersedes_protocol_id`. Because `code` is globally unique across protocols, that second condition is satisfied structurally for every well-formed drafting sequence; it is stated here because it is the invariant activation is defending, namely that a code never has two live definitions at once. Activation stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock and advances the protocol's `version` by one.

Activation also fires the **protocol supersession chain**, and its three links run in order. In the first link, when the activating protocol carries a `supersedes_protocol_id` and that predecessor is currently `ACTIVE`, the predecessor transitions `ACTIVE → RETIRED`. In the second link, caused by the first, every certification whose `protocol_id` names the newly retired protocol and whose `status` is `ENROLLED`, `TRAINED`, `CERTIFIED`, or `SUSPENDED` transitions to `REVOKED` — that is, `ENROLLED → REVOKED`, `TRAINED → REVOKED`, `CERTIFIED → REVOKED`, and `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` all apply, and `REVOKED` is a terminal state from which nothing returns. In the third link, caused by the second, every volunteer who lost their last `CERTIFIED` certification in the second link, and whose own `status` was `ACTIVE`, transitions `ACTIVE → PENDING`; a volunteer who still holds at least one `CERTIFIED` certification elsewhere is untouched. Each row that moves in any of the three links has its `version` incremented, the retired protocol included. `RETIRED` is terminal for a protocol: there is no un-retire.

### 3.2. Enrolling Observers and Experts, and the Certification Lifecycle

A volunteer joins the program as a record carrying `full_name`, `email`, and `home_region_code`, registered by a `ProgramAdmin`. The `email` is unique across all volunteers. A freshly registered volunteer sits in `PENDING` with no field privileges at all: both `quality_score` and `lifetime_quality_score` seed to 0.5, `recognition_tier` seeds to `BRONZE`, and `lifetime_points`, `accepted_observation_count`, `rejected_observation_count`, `consecutive_accepted`, and `consecutive_rejections` all seed to 0. The `home_region_code` plays no gating role; it exists so campaign eligibility can be reported by region.

Experts are registered the same way, with `full_name`, the `specialty_taxon` they adjudicate, and a `verification_weight` between 0.50 and 4.00 inclusive at two decimal places — the base influence their vote will carry. An expert who also submits observations of their own may be registered with a `linked_volunteer_id` naming that volunteer account; the field is null for an expert who never submits, and it is the hook that later prevents anyone from verifying their own field work. A new expert begins `ACTIVE`, with `vote_count` and `retracted_confirm_count` at 0.

Certification is a three-party arrangement. A `ProgramAdmin` enrols a volunteer by naming the `volunteer_id`, the `protocol_id` being trained on, the `trainer_expert_id` who will run the session, and the `level` being attempted (`BASIC`, `ADVANCED`, or `EXPERT`). The row starts in `ENROLLED` with `exam_score`, `granted_at`, and `expires_at` all null and `suspended_reason` at `NONE`. Naming the trainer at enrolment time is not merely record-keeping: that expert is thereby disqualified from casting the opening-round verification vote on this volunteer's work under this protocol, a segregation-of-duties rule elaborated in §3.6.

When the training session concludes, the named trainer — and only the named trainer — records the result. The certification must still be `ENROLLED`, and the reported `exam_score` must be an integer from 0 to 100. A score of at least 70 moves the certification `ENROLLED → TRAINED` and stores the score on the row; a score below 70 is refused with `ERR_EXAM_SCORE_TOO_LOW` and leaves the certification where it was. Any expert other than the named trainer attempting this receives `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and an attempt against a certification in any state other than `ENROLLED` receives `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Granting is an administrative act, and it is the moment a credential becomes usable. A `TRAINED` certification moves `TRAINED → CERTIFIED` provided the underlying protocol is still `ACTIVE`; if the protocol has drifted out of `ACTIVE`, the grant is refused with `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_ACTIVE`. On success the service stamps `granted_at` from the logical clock and sets `expires_at` to that instant advanced by the protocol's `certification_valid_days` in days — so a protocol left at its 365-day default yields a credential good for a year to the minute. Granting carries one cross-entity effect: when the owning volunteer is still `PENDING`, that volunteer transitions `PENDING → ACTIVE` and their `version` increments alongside the certification's.

Credentials can be taken away and given back. A certification moves `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `QUALITY_PROBATION` when its holder's standing collapses, as described in §3.7. It travels back `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` with `suspended_reason` reset to `NONE` when a `ProgramAdmin` reinstates the holder — a cycle a single certification may traverse any number of times. Reinstatement itself is available only for a volunteer whose `status` is `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` and whose current `quality_score` stands at 0.50 or above; the volunteer returns `PROBATION → ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, every certification of theirs sitting in `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` `QUALITY_PROBATION` returns to `CERTIFIED` with the reason cleared to `NONE`, `consecutive_rejections` resets to 0, and the volunteer's `version` increments. Note what reinstatement does *not* touch: `consecutive_accepted` is left exactly as it was, and neither quality score is recomputed by the act of reinstating.

Two terminal exits remain. A certification moves `CERTIFIED → EXPIRED` during a maintenance sweep once the logical clock reaches or passes its `expires_at`. And a certification in `ENROLLED`, `TRAINED`, `CERTIFIED`, or `SUSPENDED` moves to `REVOKED` when the protocol it belongs to is retired by a superseding activation. `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` are both terminal; a volunteer who needs the credential again must be enrolled afresh.

### 3.3. Seasonal Campaigns, Survey Sites and Coverage Arithmetic

Field effort is organised into seasonal campaigns. A `ProgramAdmin` creates one by supplying a `name`, the `protocol_id` it runs under, the `season_start_date` and `season_end_date` bounding the sampling season as ISO calendar dates, and a `target_observations_per_site` greater than zero — the accepted-observation quota each site is expected to meet. The end date may not precede the start date. The new campaign lands in `PLANNED` with `coverage_score`, `gap_count`, `site_count`, and `accepted_observation_count` all at 0. Season windows on the same protocol may not intersect, under the closed-inclusive regime of §2.9; a collision is refused with `ERR_SEASON_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Sites are added to a campaign one at a time, each carrying a `site_code` unique within that campaign, a `latitude` and `longitude` in decimal degrees, a `habitat_type` from `WETLAND`, `FOREST`, `GRASSLAND`, `URBAN`, or `COASTAL`, and an optional `priority_weight` between 0.50 and 2.00 at two decimal places, defaulting to 1.0. The weight is not decorative: it multiplies into the quality points every accepted observation at that site earns, so a hard-to-reach coastal transect can be made worth more than a suburban feeder. Sites may be added to a campaign in any status except `PUBLISHED` or `ARCHIVED`; adding one raises the campaign's `site_count`, re-runs the coverage arithmetic, and increments the campaign's `version`. A newly added site starts with `accepted_observation_count` at 0, `coverage_ratio` at 0.0, and `coverage_status` at `GAP`.

**Coverage arithmetic** is re-run whenever an observation enters or leaves the `ACCEPTED` review state, whenever a site is added, and whenever a campaign is opened or closed. It proceeds in two stages and the stage order is pinned. In the first stage, each survey site of the campaign — every site row, with no status filter and including sites that have never received a single record — has its `accepted_observation_count` recounted as the number of that site's observations whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED`, and its `coverage_ratio` set to that count divided by the campaign's `target_observations_per_site`, capped at 1.0, and then rounded to two decimal places. Each site's `coverage_status` is then decided by a ladder evaluated in exactly this order: the site is `GAP` when its `coverage_ratio` is below 0.50; otherwise it is `SATURATED` when its `accepted_observation_count` has reached twice the campaign's `target_observations_per_site`; otherwise it is `MET` when its `coverage_ratio` is exactly 1.00; otherwise it is `UNDER`. In the second stage, the campaign's `site_count` is set to the number of site rows, its `coverage_score` is set to the arithmetic mean of the already-rounded per-site ratios rounded to four decimal places (or 0.0000 when the campaign has no sites at all), and its `gap_count` is set to the number of sites whose `coverage_ratio` is below 0.50.

The two-stage rounding is a genuine numerical commitment, not a formality. Averaging the unrounded ratios and rounding once at the end produces a different answer: a campaign with a target of 3 and two sites holding 1 and 0 accepted records yields a `coverage_score` of 0.1650 under the pinned order and 0.1667 under the single-rounding order. The pinned order is the correct one.

Opening a campaign moves it `PLANNED → OPEN` or `CLOSED → OPEN`. It requires the campaign's protocol to be `ACTIVE` and the campaign to hold at least one site, and it re-runs the coverage arithmetic before returning. The `CLOSED → OPEN` edge is the campaign revision cycle and may be traversed any number of times before the campaign is ever published. Closing moves the campaign `OPEN → CLOSED`, again requires at least one site, and additionally requires that no observation of the campaign is still in flight — nothing in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `NEEDS_REVISION` — otherwise the close is refused with `ERR_OPEN_REVIEWS_PENDING`. Closing likewise re-runs the coverage arithmetic. Both edges increment the campaign's `version`.

Beyond `CLOSED`, campaign status is driven entirely by publication and by the maintenance sweep. A campaign moves `CLOSED → PUBLISHED` as a consequence of one of its publications being released, and back `PUBLISHED → CLOSED` when a withdrawal leaves it with no released publication remaining. Finally, a `PUBLISHED` campaign moves `PUBLISHED → ARCHIVED` during a maintenance sweep once the logical clock's calendar date is strictly later than the campaign's `season_end_date` advanced by 30 days — a month of grace after the season ends before the record is frozen. `ARCHIVED` is terminal, and archived campaigns drop out of the season-window conflict check entirely, which is what lets a future season re-use the same calendar dates on the same protocol.

### 3.4. Instrument Custody: Booking, Issue, Return, Quarantine and Calibration

Acoustic recorders and GPS loggers are shared, expensive, and drift out of true with use, so the program tracks each unit's whole service cycle. A `ProgramAdmin` registers an instrument with a globally unique `asset_tag`, an `equipment_type` of `ACOUSTIC_RECORDER`, `GPS_LOGGER`, or `SPOTTING_SCOPE`, and a `calibration_valid_until` instant after which the unit may not be handed out. It starts `AVAILABLE` with `calibration_drift_ppm` at 0.0, `quarantine_until` null, and `total_loan_hours` at 0.

Booking a unit is deliberately more demanding than it looks. A `ProgramAdmin` or the volunteer themselves requests a loan by naming the `equipment_id`, the `volunteer_id` who will carry the unit, a `start_at` and a `due_at` with the end strictly after the start, and `expected_equipment_version` matching the instrument's current `version` — the stale-version guard of §2.5. The requesting volunteer must be in `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` standing, and the instrument must not be `RETIRED`; a unit in `AVAILABLE`, `ON_LOAN`, `QUARANTINE`, or `IN_CALIBRATION` may all be booked forward in time, but a retired unit is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`. The requested window is then checked against the instrument's occupied intervals under the half-open regime of §2.9, and an intersection is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`. A successful request lands the loan in `REQUESTED` with `issued_at` and `returned_at` null, `condition_on_return` at `NONE`, `loan_hours` at 0, and `drift_added_ppm` at 0.0 — and, because booking changes what the instrument's calendar looks like to the next requester, it increments the **instrument's** `version` by one.

Issuing hands the unit over. The loan must be in `REQUESTED`, the instrument must be `AVAILABLE`, its `calibration_valid_until` must be strictly later than the logical clock — an expired calibration is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED` — and the logical clock must still be before the loan's `due_at`, since there is no sense in issuing a unit whose booking window has already elapsed. On success, `issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the loan moves `REQUESTED → ISSUED`, and the instrument moves `AVAILABLE → ON_LOAN`; both rows' `version` values increment.

Taking a unit back is the busiest operation in the equipment lifecycle, and its behaviour forks on the reported `condition_on_return`. The loan must be in `ISSUED` or `OVERDUE`. In every case, `returned_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the reported condition is stored, and the loan's `loan_hours` is computed as the elapsed time from `issued_at` to `returned_at` in exact fractional hours **rounded up** to the next whole hour.

When the condition reported is `GOOD` or `DAMAGED`, the loan moves `ISSUED → RETURNED` or `OVERDUE → RETURNED` and the **calibration-drift computation** runs. The base drift is the loan's `loan_hours` multiplied by 0.75 parts per million per hour, rounded to two decimal places. A damage penalty of 200.00 ppm is added when the condition is `DAMAGED` and nothing is added when it is `GOOD`; an overdue penalty of 100.00 ppm is added when the loan was sitting in `OVERDUE` at the moment of return and nothing is added when it was still `ISSUED`. The loan's `drift_added_ppm` is the sum of the rounded base drift and those two penalties, rounded again to two decimal places — the base drift is rounded before the penalties join it, and the total is rounded after, and both roundings are observable. The instrument's `calibration_drift_ppm` becomes its previous value plus `drift_added_ppm`, rounded to two decimal places, and its `total_loan_hours` grows by the loan's `loan_hours`. The instrument itself moves `ON_LOAN → QUARANTINE` with `quarantine_until` set to the logical clock advanced by 48 hours, which is two full days in which the unit may be neither issued nor cleared. Only the loan being returned contributes to any of this; earlier loans are already folded into the instrument's accumulated drift and hours.

When the condition reported is `LOST`, the picture is different. The loan moves `ISSUED → LOST` or `OVERDUE → LOST`, `loan_hours` is still computed and still accumulates into the instrument's `total_loan_hours`, but `drift_added_ppm` stays at 0.00 — a unit that never came back cannot have its drift measured. The instrument moves `ON_LOAN → RETIRED`, and every other loan on that instrument still sitting in `REQUESTED` transitions `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`, since those bookings can never be honoured.

Clearing quarantine is a three-way fork decided purely on accumulated drift, and each branch behaves differently. The instrument must be in `QUARANTINE` and the logical clock must have reached or passed its `quarantine_until`; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_QUARANTINE_NOT_ELAPSED`. All three branches clear `quarantine_until` back to null and increment the instrument's `version`. When `calibration_drift_ppm` is below 500.00, the unit moves `QUARANTINE → AVAILABLE` and returns to the pool. When it stands at 500.00 or above but below 1000.00, the unit moves `QUARANTINE → IN_CALIBRATION` and is taken out of circulation for service. When it reaches 1000.00 or more, the unit moves `QUARANTINE → RETIRED` for good, and — exactly as with a lost unit — every loan on it still in `REQUESTED` transitions `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`.

Completing calibration closes the service cycle. An instrument in `IN_CALIBRATION` moves `IN_CALIBRATION → AVAILABLE`, its `calibration_drift_ppm` resets to 0.0, and its `calibration_valid_until` is re-stamped to the logical clock advanced by 365 days. The full loop `AVAILABLE → ON_LOAN → QUARANTINE → IN_CALIBRATION → AVAILABLE` may be traversed indefinitely; `RETIRED` is the only terminal state an instrument has.

One transition is not driven by any human action: a loan sitting in `ISSUED` whose `due_at` the logical clock has reached or passed moves `ISSUED → OVERDUE` during the next maintenance sweep. That is the state whose presence at return time triggers the overdue drift surcharge. Loans reaching `RETURNED`, `LOST`, or `CANCELLED` are terminal.

### 3.5. Filing a Field Observation and Screening its Photo Evidence

A volunteer files an observation by naming the `campaign_id` and the `site_id` within it, their own `volunteer_id`, the `observed_at` instant at which the sighting was made in the field, the `latitude` and `longitude` of the sighting in decimal degrees, the `species_code` identified, and a `count_value` of individuals that must be zero or greater. Where the protocol demands an instrument, the submission also carries the `equipment_loan_id` of the loan the observer is holding; where the protocol's `required_equipment_type` is `NONE`, the field is omitted.

The gate an observation must pass is long, and every clause of it is enforced. The campaign must be `OPEN`. The named site must belong to the named campaign, or the submission is refused with `ERR_SITE_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH`. The observer must be in `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` standing — a `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` volunteer cannot file at all. The observer must hold a certification for that campaign's protocol whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, and that certification's `level` must rank at or above the protocol's `required_certification_level` on the ordinal scale where `BASIC` is 1, `ADVANCED` is 2, and `EXPERT` is 3 — so an `ADVANCED` holder may submit under a protocol demanding `BASIC` or `ADVANCED` but not one demanding `EXPERT`. The calendar date of `observed_at` must fall inside the campaign's season window with both boundary dates included. The gap between `observed_at` and the logical clock, measured in hours, must not exceed the protocol's `max_submission_lag_hours`; a protocol set to 72 gives observers three days to write up their notes and no more. When the protocol names an instrument class, the supplied `equipment_loan_id` must resolve to a loan in `ISSUED` status whose `volunteer_id` is the observer and whose instrument's `equipment_type` matches the required class, or the submission is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`. Finally the target site must not already be saturated: its `accepted_observation_count` must be strictly below twice the campaign's `target_observations_per_site`, otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_SITE_SATURATED`.

A surviving submission is created with `review_status` `SUBMITTED` and `publication_status` `UNPUBLISHED` — the two independent state dimensions this record carries for the rest of its life — plus `revision_round` at 0, `photo_count` at 0, `publication_id` null, `final_quality_points` at 0.0, and `consensus_confidence`, `confirm_weight`, `reject_weight`, and `revision_weight` all at 0.0. Its `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `provisional_quality_points` is computed immediately.

**Quality points** are the program's single measure of how much a record is worth, and the provisional figure is what an observation shows before any expert has weighed in. It is built from four ingredients. The base is 40 points. On top of that sits a photo bonus: 5 points for each accepted photo beyond the protocol's `min_photo_count`, never less than zero and capped at 20 points, so five surplus photographs earn the same bonus as fifty. Those two are multiplied by a timeliness factor read off the reporting lag — the elapsed hours from `observed_at` to `submitted_at` in exact fractional hours — which is 1.00 when the lag is at most six hours, 0.90 when it is more than six but at most twenty-four, 0.75 when it is more than twenty-four but at most seventy-two, and 0.50 beyond three days. The product is then multiplied by a site factor equal to the site's `priority_weight` clamped into the range 0.80 to 1.50 — a weight below 0.80 is treated as 0.80 and a weight above 1.50 as 1.50. The result of those three multiplications is the observation's **raw score**, which the service carries at full precision and never rounds. `provisional_quality_points` is that raw score rounded to two decimal places. The provisional variant ignores expert confidence and the revision count entirely, so an observation on its second revision round shows exactly the same provisional figure as an otherwise identical observation that was never sent back.

Photo evidence is attached one image at a time by the observing volunteer, each upload carrying a `file_hash` unique within that observation, the `captured_at` instant from the image's EXIF metadata, and the EXIF fix as `exif_latitude` and `exif_longitude`. Every upload is screened at the moment it arrives, and the screening ladder runs in a fixed order. First the service computes `geo_delta_meters`: the planar distance between the EXIF fix and the observation's own fix, obtained by taking the difference in latitude and the difference in longitude, squaring each, summing them, taking the square root, multiplying by 111320.0 metres per degree, and rounding to two decimal places. Then the time test runs first and the geographic test second. If `captured_at` is earlier than `observed_at` by more than two hours, or later than `observed_at` by more than two hours, the photo's `status` is set to `REJECTED` and the geographic comparison is not evaluated at all on that branch. Otherwise, if `geo_delta_meters` is greater than 250.00, the `status` is `FLAGGED_MISMATCH`. Otherwise the `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Both tolerance comparisons are strict, so a photograph captured exactly two hours after `observed_at`, or sitting exactly 250.00 metres from the observation fix, is `ACCEPTED`. Only the image being uploaded is screened; images already attached are never re-screened, even if the observation is later revised.

After each upload the observation's `photo_count` is recounted as the number of its photo-evidence children whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` — flagged and rejected images are stored and readable but contribute nothing — and `provisional_quality_points` is recomputed from the new count, with the observation's `version` incrementing. This is the lever an observer pulls when they want a stronger record: attach more good photographs before review begins.

### 3.6. Expert Verification, Weighted Consensus and the Revision Cycle

Review begins when an expert picks a record out of the queue and moves it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. The acting expert must be `ACTIVE` — a suspended verifier is turned away with `ERR_EXPERT_SUSPENDED` — and must not be the observer in disguise: if the expert's `linked_volunteer_id` equals the observation's `volunteer_id`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION`. The observation must actually satisfy the protocol's evidence requirement by this point, meaning its `photo_count` is at or above the protocol's `min_photo_count`, otherwise `ERR_PHOTO_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`. Review may only start while the owning campaign is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`; a campaign that has moved on to `PUBLISHED` or `ARCHIVED` no longer admits new reviews, and a campaign still `PLANNED` has nothing to review.

Adjudication itself is a weighted, quorum-based negotiation rather than a single verdict. An expert votes by supplying a `decision` of `CONFIRM`, `REJECT`, or `REQUEST_REVISION`, a self-reported `confidence` integer from 1 to 5, the `expected_observation_version` demanded by §2.5, and optionally a `comment` carrying free-text feedback for the observer. Four bars stand in front of a vote. The observation must be `UNDER_REVIEW`. The acting expert must be `ACTIVE`. The expert must not be linked to the observer. And while the observation is still in its opening round — that is, while `revision_round` is 0 — the acting expert must not be the `trainer_expert_id` on the observer's certification for this campaign's protocol, since someone who trained an observer cannot impartially validate that observer's first attempt; a breach is refused with `ERR_TRAINER_VERIFICATION_CONFLICT`. Finally, an expert gets one vote per round: a second vote from the same expert on the same observation in the same `round_number` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`.

Each accepted vote is stored as a ledger row bearing its `observation_id`, `expert_id`, the `round_number` copied from the observation's current `revision_round` at insert time, the `decision`, the `confidence`, the `comment`, and a `weight_applied` computed as the expert's `verification_weight` multiplied by the quantity 0.6 plus 0.1 times the vote's `confidence`, rounded to four decimal places — so a weight-2.00 expert voting at confidence 5 contributes 2.2000, while the same expert voting at confidence 1 contributes 1.4000. Storing the vote also raises the acting expert's `vote_count` by one and the observation's `version` by one, whatever the outcome turns out to be.

**Consensus** is then recomputed over the current round alone. Only votes whose `round_number` equals the observation's current `revision_round` participate; votes from earlier rounds are retained for audit and are never re-summed. The observation's `confirm_weight`, `reject_weight`, and `revision_weight` are the sums of the already-rounded `weight_applied` values of the current round's `CONFIRM`, `REJECT`, and `REQUEST_REVISION` votes respectively, each carried to four decimal places. The observation's `consensus_confidence` is 0.0000 when the current round holds no `CONFIRM` vote at all; otherwise it is the weighted mean confidence of the confirming votes — the sum over confirming votes of the vote's `confidence` multiplied by that expert's `verification_weight`, divided by the sum of those same experts' `verification_weight` values, rounded to four decimal places. Note that this mean weighs by the expert's raw `verification_weight`, not by the confidence-adjusted `weight_applied`.

The resolution ladder is then evaluated in exactly this order after each vote is stored, and the first branch that matches wins. If the current round holds fewer than two votes, the outcome is a hold: the vote is stored but `review_status` stays where it is, a self-loop written `UNDER_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW`. Otherwise, if `reject_weight` has reached 3.0 and is greater than or equal to `confirm_weight`, the observation resolves `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` — ties go to rejection, which is the program's conservative default. Otherwise, if `confirm_weight` has reached 3.0 and is strictly greater than `reject_weight`, the observation resolves `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED`. Otherwise, if `revision_weight` has reached 2.0, the observation resolves `UNDER_REVIEW → NEEDS_REVISION`. Otherwise the outcome is again a hold, `UNDER_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW`, and the observation awaits further votes.

When the ladder lands on revision, the `comment` text of every `REQUEST_REVISION` vote in the current round becomes the feedback set returned to the observer — that is the whole point of the comment field. The observer may then act on it: resubmitting moves the record `NEEDS_REVISION → SUBMITTED`, and only the observer who filed it may do so. The observation's `revision_round` must still be below 2, otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`; the owning campaign must still be `OPEN`; and the observer's certification for the protocol must still be `CERTIFIED`. A resubmission may correct the record in place by supplying any of `latitude`, `longitude`, `species_code`, or `count_value`; each is optional and an omitted field keeps its existing value. On success `revision_round` increments by one, `submitted_at` is re-stamped from the logical clock, and `provisional_quality_points` is recomputed from the new `submitted_at` — which usually means the timeliness factor drops, because the clock has moved on since the sighting. Votes from the earlier round remain on file but stop counting, since consensus only ever sums the current round. The cycle `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → NEEDS_REVISION → SUBMITTED` may therefore be traversed at most twice.

That bound creates a dead end the program cleans up on its own. An observation that has already used both revision rounds and is nonetheless sent back to `NEEDS_REVISION` can never be resubmitted by its observer, so the next maintenance sweep moves it `NEEDS_REVISION → REJECTED` and runs exactly the same standing cascade a vote-driven rejection would run.

When the ladder lands on acceptance, the observation's **final quality points** are computed and the record's second state dimension begins to move. The final figure starts from the same unrounded raw score described in §3.5 — base 40 plus the capped photo bonus, multiplied by the timeliness factor and the clamped site factor — and multiplies it by two further terms before a single rounding. The first is a confidence multiplier equal to 0.60 plus 0.10 times the observation's stored four-decimal `consensus_confidence`. The second is a revision penalty equal to 1.00 minus 0.15 for each completed revision round, so a record accepted at `revision_round` 0 keeps its full value, one accepted at round 1 keeps 85 percent, and one accepted at round 2 keeps 70 percent. The product is rounded, once, to two decimal places and stored as `final_quality_points`. Because the raw score is never rounded before this multiplication, `final_quality_points` will generally differ from `provisional_quality_points` even when the confidence multiplier and revision penalty happen to work out to 1.00. `final_quality_points` stays at 0.0 for any observation that has not been accepted.

`REJECTED` and `RETRACTED` are the terminal review states. An observation that reaches `ACCEPTED` may still be pulled back by a `ProgramAdmin`, as §3.7 describes, but no vote can move it once it has resolved.

### 3.7. Observer Standing, Quality Scores, Recognition and Retraction

Every adjudication changes what the program thinks of the observer, and it does so through two scores that answer deliberately different questions. Both are computed over the same **adjudicated set** — every observation belonging to that volunteer whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, or `RETRACTED`. Records still in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `NEEDS_REVISION` are excluded from both. Within the set, an `ACCEPTED` record counts as a hit worth one and everything else counts as zero; a `RETRACTED` record stays in the set and scores as a miss, exactly like a rejection.

`quality_score` is the rolling-window variant. Take the 20 most recent members of the adjudicated set, ordered by `observed_at` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, and divide the number of hits among them by how many members the window actually holds, rounding to four decimal places. A volunteer with no adjudicated observations at all holds the neutral seed of 0.5. Because the window is capped at twenty, an old rejection that has fallen out of it is invisible to this score — which is exactly what makes it the right gate for probation, for reinstatement, and for campaign eligibility.

`lifetime_quality_score` is the lifetime-weighted variant, and nothing ever falls out of it. Every member of the adjudicated set contributes, discounted exponentially by age: a record's weight is one half raised to the power of its age in exact fractional days — measured from `observed_at` to the logical clock — divided by a half-life of 180 days, so a six-month-old record counts half as much as a fresh one and a year-old record a quarter as much. The score is the sum of each record's weight times its hit value, divided by the sum of all the weights, rounded to four decimal places, with the same neutral seed of 0.5 when the set is empty. A volunteer whose recent twenty are flawless can therefore still carry a `lifetime_quality_score` below 0.90 because of older misses, and that is intentional: the lifetime figure, not the window, is what drives recognition.

**Acceptance** runs a four-link chain, in order. In the first link the observation's `final_quality_points` are computed as described in §3.6 and its `publication_status` moves `UNPUBLISHED → QUEUED`, making it a candidate for the campaign's next publication. In the second link, caused by the first, the recognition ledger gains a row of `award_type` `OBSERVATION_ACCEPTED` carrying this observation's `observation_id`, the volunteer's `accepted_observation_count` and `consecutive_accepted` each rise by one, `consecutive_rejections` resets to 0, and the awarded points are added to `lifetime_points`. The points on that row are computed by reading the volunteer's recognition band from their `lifetime_quality_score` **as it stands before this acceptance is folded in** — a multiplier of 1.50 at 0.90 or above, 1.25 at 0.75 or above, 1.10 at 0.60 or above, and 1.00 otherwise — multiplying `final_quality_points` by that multiplier, and taking the floor of the result to a whole number of points. The ordering here is pinned: the multiplier comes from the pre-acceptance score, never from the post-acceptance one.

In the third link, caused by the second and evaluated only after `consecutive_accepted` has been incremented, a run of five earns a bonus: whenever the new `consecutive_accepted` is a positive multiple of 5, a second ledger row of `award_type` `STREAK_BONUS` worth a flat 5 points is appended against the same observation and added to `lifetime_points`. In the fourth link, also caused by the second, both quality scores are recomputed and the volunteer's `recognition_tier` is re-derived from the new `lifetime_quality_score`: `PLATINUM` at 0.90 or above, `GOLD` at 0.75 or above, `SILVER` at 0.60 or above, and `BRONZE` below that. If the tier has climbed — ranking `BRONZE` as 1, `SILVER` as 2, `GOLD` as 3, and `PLATINUM` as 4 — a third ledger row of `award_type` `MILESTONE` worth a flat 25 points is appended and added to `lifetime_points`. A milestone row is not tied to any single observation, so its `observation_id` is null. Every ledger row also carries a human-readable `reason` explaining why it was minted. Alongside the four links, the observation's site has its `accepted_observation_count` raised by one and its `coverage_ratio` and `coverage_status` recomputed, and the campaign has its `accepted_observation_count` raised by one and its `coverage_score` and `gap_count` recomputed.

**Rejection** runs a five-link chain of its own. The first link sets `review_status` to `REJECTED` and leaves `publication_status` at `UNPUBLISHED` — a rejected record never enters the publication pipeline. The second link, caused by the first, raises the volunteer's `rejected_observation_count` and `consecutive_rejections` by one each, resets `consecutive_accepted` to 0, and recomputes both quality scores. The third link, caused by the second, fires only when the recomputed `quality_score` has fallen below 0.50 and the volunteer was `ACTIVE`: the volunteer transitions `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. The fourth link, caused by the third, suspends the volunteer's serious credentials — every certification of theirs whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `level` is `ADVANCED` or `EXPERT` transitions `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `QUALITY_PROBATION`, while `BASIC` credentials survive so the observer can keep contributing simple records. The fifth link, caused by the fourth, cancels forward commitments: every equipment loan of that volunteer still sitting in `REQUESTED` transitions `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`.

A volunteer already on probation who keeps getting rejected is removed from the field. When a rejection lands on a volunteer whose `status` is `PROBATION` and whose `consecutive_rejections` has reached 3 after the increment, the volunteer transitions `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, and this time **every** certification of theirs in `CERTIFIED` is suspended with `suspended_reason` `QUALITY_PROBATION` regardless of `level`. Both `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED` are exits from which a `ProgramAdmin` reinstatement returns the volunteer to `ACTIVE`, under the conditions given in §3.2.

**Retraction** is the compensating operation, available to a `ProgramAdmin` against any observation in `ACCEPTED` and carrying a free-text `reason`. Its chain has four links plus a ledger compensation. The first link moves `review_status` `ACCEPTED → RETRACTED`. The second link, caused by the first, moves the publication dimension: `publication_status` goes `QUEUED → WITHDRAWN` or `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`, and when the observation was bound into a publication, that publication's `record_count` drops by one, its `mean_quality` is recomputed over the observations that remain bound to it, and its `version` increments. The third link, caused by the second, unwinds the coverage arithmetic: the site's `accepted_observation_count` drops by one and its `coverage_ratio` and `coverage_status` are recomputed, and the campaign's `accepted_observation_count` drops by one and its `coverage_score` and `gap_count` are recomputed. The fourth link, caused by the third, can re-open a finished campaign: when the recompute has raised the campaign's `gap_count` above the value it held before the retraction and the campaign is sitting in `CLOSED`, the campaign transitions `CLOSED → OPEN` so the sampling gap can be filled.

The ledger compensation is exact and additive. A row of `award_type` `REVERSAL` is appended whose `points_awarded` is the negation of the sum of `points_awarded` across every prior ledger row bearing this observation's `observation_id`, and that same sum is subtracted from the volunteer's `lifetime_points` — which is how the invariant of §2.10 survives a retraction. Because milestone rows carry a null `observation_id`, a tier promotion is never clawed back. The volunteer's `accepted_observation_count` drops by one, `rejected_observation_count` rises by one, `consecutive_accepted` resets to 0, and both quality scores are recomputed with the retracted record scoring as a miss. One thing pointedly does not happen: a retraction never drives a volunteer into `PROBATION`, however far the recomputed `quality_score` falls. Only a vote-driven or sweep-driven resolution into `REJECTED` can do that.

Retraction finally reaches back to the experts who got it wrong. For every expert who cast a `CONFIRM` vote in the deciding round of the retracted observation, `retracted_confirm_count` rises by one, and any expert whose count reaches 2 transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and can cast no further votes until an administrator intervenes.

### 3.8. Assembling Datasets and Granting Research Partners Access

A campaign's accepted records become citable science by being bound into a **publication**. A `ProgramAdmin` assembles one by naming the `campaign_id`, a globally unique `doi` for the released dataset, the `expected_campaign_version` demanded by §2.5, and optionally an `embargo_until` instant before which only aggregate-level access may be granted. The owning campaign must be in `CLOSED` or `PUBLISHED`; a campaign still `OPEN`, `PLANNED`, or `ARCHIVED` cannot be published from.

Assembly runs in three pinned steps. **Step one runs first, before anything is measured.** When the campaign already carries a publication in `DRAFT`, `EMBARGOED`, or `RELEASED`, that prior publication is superseded: it transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, and the new row's `supersedes_publication_id` is set to point at it. The superseded publication's bound observations are treated asymmetrically by design — those whose `publication_status` is `QUEUED` have their `publication_id` cleared back to null and their `publication_status` moved `QUEUED → UNPUBLISHED`, which puts them straight back into the candidate pool, while those whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED` move to `WITHDRAWN` and do **not** re-enter, because a record that has already been released under a DOI cannot quietly reappear in a successor dataset.

**Step two** computes the publication's metrics over the refreshed candidate pool. The candidate set is fixed before anything else is derived: it is every observation of this campaign whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED`, whose `publication_id` is still null, and whose `publication_status` is therefore either `QUEUED` (the state acceptance puts it in) or `UNPUBLISHED` (the state a record returns to when the draft that held it was withdrawn). Retracted records, records already `PUBLISHED` or `WITHDRAWN` on the publication dimension, and records already bound to another publication never enter it. From that set alone the `quality_floor` is derived: when the candidate set is empty the floor is 0.00, and otherwise the candidates are sorted by `final_quality_points` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, the lower median is taken — for an odd-sized set the middle element, and for an even-sized set the lower of the two central elements — and the floor is that element's `final_quality_points` multiplied by 0.80 and rounded to two decimal places. Inclusion is then decided against that floor and is inclusive at it: every candidate whose `final_quality_points` is greater than or equal to `quality_floor` is included. The publication's `record_count` is the number of included observations, and its `mean_quality` is the arithmetic mean of their `final_quality_points` rounded to two decimal places, or 0.00 when nothing was included. The floor is never re-derived from the included subset — evaluation stops here and there is no fixed-point iteration; recomputing the median from the survivors would be wrong.

**Step three** binds the result: every included observation has its `publication_id` set to the new publication. Those observations stay in `QUEUED`; nothing moves to `PUBLISHED` until the dataset is actually released. Assembly requires at least 2 candidate observations to exist, counted after step one has returned any superseded publication's queued records to the pool, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RECORDS`. The new publication lands in `DRAFT` with `released_at` null, and the owning campaign's `version` increments.

Releasing a `DRAFT` publication forks on the embargo. When `embargo_until` is null, or when the logical clock has reached or passed it, the publication moves `DRAFT → RELEASED`: `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, every bound observation moves `publication_status` `QUEUED → PUBLISHED`, and the owning campaign moves `CLOSED → PUBLISHED`. When `embargo_until` is set and the logical clock has not yet reached it, the publication instead moves `DRAFT → EMBARGOED` and its bound observations stay in `QUEUED`; the campaign does not change status and the dataset waits. Either branch requires `record_count` to be at least 2 and increments the publication's `version`. An `EMBARGOED` publication moves `EMBARGOED → RELEASED` on its own during the first maintenance sweep after the clock reaches `embargo_until`, with exactly the same three effects as a direct release.

Withdrawal is available against a publication in `DRAFT`, `EMBARGOED`, or `RELEASED` — the edges `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, `EMBARGOED → WITHDRAWN`, and `RELEASED → WITHDRAWN` respectively — carries a free-text `reason`, and runs a three-link chain. The first link separates the bound observations: those whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED` move to `WITHDRAWN`, and those whose `publication_status` is `QUEUED` move `QUEUED → UNPUBLISHED` with `publication_id` cleared to null, so they can be picked up by a later dataset. The second link, caused by the first, tears down access: every access grant on this publication whose `status` is `APPROVED` transitions `APPROVED → REVOKED` with `denied_reason` set to `PUBLICATION_WITHDRAWN` and the owning partner's `active_grant_count` decremented, while every grant still in `REQUESTED` transitions `REQUESTED → DENIED` with the same `denied_reason`. The third link, caused by the second, disciplines the partners: every research partner whose `active_grant_count` reached 0 as a consequence of the second link, and whose own `status` was `APPROVED`, transitions `APPROVED → SUSPENDED`. Alongside the chain, the owning campaign moves `PUBLISHED → CLOSED` once no `RELEASED` publication remains on it. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for a publication.

Research partners are registered by a `ProgramAdmin` with a globally unique `org_name`, a `contact_email`, an optional `trust_tier` of `STANDARD` or `TRUSTED` defaulting to `STANDARD`, and an optional `data_use_agreement_signed_at` instant recording when the data use agreement was signed. A new partner sits in `PENDING` with `active_grant_count` at 0 and gains nothing until approved; approval moves a partner `PENDING → APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED → APPROVED`, the latter closing the reinstatement cycle `APPROVED → SUSPENDED → APPROVED` that a publication withdrawal can open.

Access itself is granted per publication and per scope. A request names the `publication_id`, the `partner_id`, the `scope` of `AGGREGATE`, `FULL`, or `SENSITIVE`, and an `expires_at` instant strictly later than the logical clock; it lands in `REQUESTED` with `denied_reason` at `NONE`. Approval is where the gates bite, and all of them must pass together: the grant must still be `REQUESTED`, the partner must be `APPROVED`, and the publication must be `RELEASED` — or `EMBARGOED`, but then only for an `AGGREGATE`-scope grant, since an embargo exists precisely to withhold record-level data, and a `FULL` or `SENSITIVE` request against an embargoed dataset is refused with `ERR_EMBARGO_ACTIVE`. A `FULL` or `SENSITIVE` scope additionally requires the partner's `data_use_agreement_signed_at` to be recorded, and a `SENSITIVE` scope additionally requires the partner's `trust_tier` to be `TRUSTED`. The grant's `expires_at` must still be in the future at approval time. On success the grant moves `REQUESTED → APPROVED` and the partner's `active_grant_count` rises by one.

Grants leave the approved state three ways. An administrator may revoke one, moving it `APPROVED → REVOKED` with `denied_reason` set to `ADMIN_REVOKED` and the partner's `active_grant_count` decremented. A publication withdrawal revokes it with `denied_reason` `PUBLICATION_WITHDRAWN`, or denies it outright if it was still `REQUESTED`. And a maintenance sweep moves an `APPROVED` grant whose `expires_at` the clock has reached `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, with `denied_reason` set to `GRANT_EXPIRED` and `active_grant_count` decremented. `DENIED`, `REVOKED`, and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

### 3.9. The Maintenance Sweep and the Logical Clock

Several transitions in this program are driven by the passage of time rather than by anyone's request, and the program collects all of them into a single administrative sweep so their ordering is deterministic. A `ProgramAdmin` runs the sweep, and its six phases execute in exactly this pinned order against the current logical clock.

First, certifications expire: every certification in `CERTIFIED` whose `expires_at` the clock has reached or passed moves `CERTIFIED → EXPIRED`. Second, loans fall overdue: every loan in `ISSUED` whose `due_at` the clock has reached or passed moves `ISSUED → OVERDUE`. Third, exhausted revisions are cleared: every observation in `NEEDS_REVISION` whose `revision_round` has reached 2 moves `NEEDS_REVISION → REJECTED`, running the full standing cascade of §3.7 exactly as a vote-driven rejection would. Fourth, matured embargoes release: every publication in `EMBARGOED` whose `embargo_until` the clock has reached or passed moves `EMBARGOED → RELEASED`, stamping `released_at`, moving its bound observations `QUEUED → PUBLISHED`, and moving its campaign `CLOSED → PUBLISHED`. Fifth, grants expire as described in §3.8. Sixth and last, campaigns archive: every campaign in `PUBLISHED` whose `season_end_date` plus 30 days is strictly earlier than the clock's calendar date moves `PUBLISHED → ARCHIVED`.

The sweep reports what it did, returning `expired_certification_count`, `overdue_loan_count`, `released_publication_count`, `expired_grant_count`, `archived_campaign_count`, and `exhausted_revision_count`. A sweep that finds nothing to do is not an error; it simply reports zeros.

The clock itself is administrative infrastructure for driving and testing time-dependent behaviour. Setting it takes a single `to_iso8601` instant and reports back the resulting `current_utc`. Nothing else in the system reads real time.
