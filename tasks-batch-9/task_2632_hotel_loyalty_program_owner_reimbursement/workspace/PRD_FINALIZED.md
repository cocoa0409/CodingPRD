# Hotel Loyalty Owner Reimbursement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A hotel brand's loyalty programme sits between two parties whose interests never fully align. Members want points that earn quickly, award nights that price cheaply, and elite benefits that are actually honoured at the front desk. Franchised property owners want to be paid fairly when a member turns up holding an award reservation that displaces a paying guest, and they want the programme fee assessed against their room revenue to be defensible line by line. The programme itself sits in the middle, holding a point liability it must fund, a cash balance it must not overdraw, and a reputation it must defend when a property refuses a breakfast it owed.

This document specifies the API that runs that arrangement end to end. The system tracks paid stays and the points and programme fees they generate; award bookings and the reimbursement owed to the hosting property for each award night consumed; elite benefit obligations, their delivery or denial, and the cash recovery credited to the property for honouring them; the sale of points through co-brand card issuers, direct member purchases and wholesale partner channels; and the monthly settlement batches through which the programme nets what it owes a property against what that property owes in fees. Every one of those movements is expressed as a ledger entry or a monetary snapshot, because the programme's two conserved quantities — member points outstanding and programme cash on hand — must be reconstructible from primary records at any instant.

Two design commitments dominate the requirements that follow. First, **money and points are never estimated twice the same way**: the reimbursement a property accrues when an award guest checks out and the reimbursement it is finally paid at settlement are computed by the same formula from deliberately different inputs, and both figures are retained. Second, **derived state is always re-derived, never patched**: a property's fee ledger, a member's elite tier, a property's outstanding benefit recovery and the programme fund's solvency posture are all recomputed from their source rows at the end of any request that could have moved them, which is what makes a void, a refund or a batch rejection able to unwind its forward action exactly.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to every endpoint the API exposes. All business resources are mounted under `/loyalty`, alongside a single administrative clock endpoint described in §2.5.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the five roles the programme recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The five roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `HotelOwner`, `Member`, `MemberCareAgent`, and `ProgramAuditor`. When the headers are well formed but the acting role does not appear in the target endpoint's authorised list (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The two roles that are backed by persisted entities anchor every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `HotelOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Hotel`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Hotel` compare the resource's property foreign key to `X-User-ID`, whether that property is named directly in the path, in the request body's `hotel_id`, or indirectly through the stay, redemption, reimbursement batch, blackout window or rate plan the request addresses. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's member foreign key to `X-User-ID`, reached directly through `member_id` or indirectly through the stay, redemption, point purchase, ledger entry or complaint named in the path. In either case, a request that reaches a property or a member other than the one `X-User-ID` names is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining three roles are operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition demands an audit trail — as `released_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on `ReimbursementBatch` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `MemberCareAgent`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: a care agent transacts on behalf of members without being one, and the server neither compares that identifier to a `Member` record nor records it on business rows. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the auditor is a read-only assurance role over the programme's books whose sole non-read capability is forcing a revaluation of the programme fund, and every other state-changing request from this role is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Role capability is deliberately asymmetric where money moves. A `HotelOwner` may post its own stays, record or deny elite benefits, complete or mark no-show on award stays at its own property, refresh its own performance figures, draft blackout windows, register rate plans and open a reimbursement batch — but it may never release, approve, reject or pay a batch, because the party requesting money must not be the party disbursing it. A `MemberCareAgent` may grant goodwill points and refund a purchase but may never settle a purchase, release a batch, or adjudicate an escalated complaint. Requesting further information on a complaint is reserved to `MemberCareAgent` alone: the programme administrator adjudicates escalations and does not run the care queue.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Every collection endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` sets the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0, and accepts a minimum of 0 with no maximum — an `offset` that skips past the end of the collection is a legal empty page, not an error. Neither range is enforced by quiet substitution: a parameter that is supplied but is not a whole number, or whose value falls outside the range declared for it, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) under the request-shape step of §2.6, so a `limit` of 0, a `limit` of 101 and a negative `offset` are each rejected rather than clamped to the nearest accepted value or replaced by the default. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, collections are sorted by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`.

Beyond pagination, each collection accepts a fixed set of equality filters, and no others. The property collection filters on `status` and `market_tier`. Rate plans filter on `hotel_id` and `rate_category`. Award price windows filter on `hotel_id` and `season`. Blackout windows filter on `hotel_id` and `status`. Members filter on `elite_tier` and `email`. Paid stays filter on `member_id`, `hotel_id`, `rate_plan_id` and `status`. Elite benefit obligations filter on `stay_id`, `status` and `benefit_type`. Award bookings filter on `member_id`, `hotel_id`, `batch_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `reimbursement_status` — the last two being independent dimensions that may be combined. Reimbursement batches filter on `hotel_id`, `period_month` and `status`. Point ledger entries filter on `member_id`, `source_stay_id`, `source_redemption_id`, `source_purchase_id`, `reversal_of_entry_id` and `entry_type`. Partners filter on `partner_type`. Point purchases filter on `member_id`, `partner_id`, `channel` and `status`. Member complaints filter on `member_id`, `related_delivery_id`, `status` and `subject_type`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource the API returns carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation: when a paid stay's posting moves a property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents`, that is a mutation of the `Hotel` row, so the property's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments. `Hotel`, `BlackoutWindow`, `Member`, `Stay`, `EliteBenefitDelivery`, `Redemption`, `ReimbursementBatch`, `PointPurchase`, `Partner`, `ProgramFund` and `MemberComplaint` all behave this way.

Three resources are append-only records of a decision already made and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations entirely. Those three are `RatePlan`, whose earn rate and eligibility flags are fixed at creation; `RedemptionPricing`, whose published award price for a date window is never edited in place; and `PointLedgerEntry`, which is the immutable primary record of a point movement and is compensated by writing a further entry rather than by being changed.

### 2.5. The Logical Programme Clock

Every date and time comparison in this specification reads a server-held **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. At server start the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability lets any of the five roles set the clock to an explicit instant supplied as `current_utc`; the clock may be advanced or rewound, and setting it writes no entity. The response carries no resource payload.

The clock governs: whether a paid stay has ended and may therefore be posted; whether an award booking has elapsed and may be completed, or whether its arrival date has come and a no-show may be recorded; which calendar year a member's year-to-date qualifying counters cover; which calendar year a property's consumed blackout allowance covers; which calendar month a partner's month-to-date purchased volume covers; which calendar year a member's purchased-point total covers; the default enrolment date of a new member; and the instant stamped into `last_revalued_at` when the programme fund is revalued.

### 2.6. Identifier Resolution and the Order of Validation

Requests that name resources in the URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the 404 returned. The path variables the API uses are `hotelId`, `ratePlanId`, `pricingId`, `windowId`, `memberId`, `stayId`, `deliveryId`, `redemptionId`, `batchId`, `entryId`, `partnerId`, `purchaseId` and `complaintId`; a rate plan or award price window is created beneath its property and thereafter addressed by its own identifier at the top level. Identifier resolution also covers foreign keys arriving in a request body: a body-supplied `hotel_id`, `member_id`, `rate_plan_id`, `partner_id` or `related_delivery_id` is resolved the same way and yields the same 404.

Across the whole API, checks are evaluated in a fixed precedence so that a request failing several rules always reports the same one. The order is:

1. **Header authentication** — missing headers or an unrecognised role yield `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorisation** — a role absent from the endpoint's list yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Resource ownership** — an owner or member reaching someone else's resource yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
4. **Segregation of duties** on a reimbursement batch, described in §2.10.
5. **Existence** of every identifier, resolved left to right along the path and then across body foreign keys, yielding `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REDEMPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PRICING_ROW_NOT_FOUND` respectively. Because the segregation-of-duties rules read fields off the batch itself, the batch's own existence is naturally established before they can be evaluated; existence always precedes any check that reads a resource's contents.
6. **Request shape** — every field the endpoint declares required must be present and of the declared type, and every enum-valued field must carry a value from that field's declared set, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
7. **Date-range sanity** — a paid stay's and an award booking's `checkout_date` must be strictly after its `checkin_date`, and a blackout window's `end_date` and an award price window's `window_end_date` must fall on or after their respective start dates. A violation yields `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (HTTP 422) and applies to paid-stay capture, award booking, blackout drafting and award price publication.
8. **Quantity ranges** — `room_revenue_cents` and `incidental_revenue_cents` must be at least 0; `points_quantity` must be an exact multiple of 1000 and at least 1000; `program_fee_pct` must lie between 0.0 and 0.20 inclusive with at most four decimal places; `trailing_12m_occupancy_pct` must lie between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive with at most four decimal places; `rooms_count` must be at least 1; `trailing_12m_adr_cents` must be at least 0; `breakage_rate_pct` must lie between 0.0 and 0.35 inclusive; and a goodwill adjustment's `points` must not be zero. A violation yields `ERR_AMOUNT_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). This rule guards property enrolment, performance refresh, paid-stay capture, point purchase creation, point adjustment and breakage-rate restatement.
9. **Cross-reference integrity** — the mismatch rules of §3, namely `ERR_RATE_PLAN_HOTEL_MISMATCH`, `ERR_DELIVERY_MEMBER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PARTNER_CHANNEL_MISMATCH` and the award-price-window resolution failure `ERR_NO_REDEMPTION_PRICING`.
10. **Optimistic concurrency**, described in §2.8.
11. **State preconditions** on the target entity and its property.
12. **Programme safety and policy limits** — escalation and adjudication preconditions, the information-request cap and the benefit-denial cap.
13. **Temporal rules** — blackout coverage, blackout buffers, member booking overlap, and the three clock-elapse rules.
14. **Constraint satisfaction** — award inventory, tier gating, annual blackout cap, uniqueness rules, batch period uniqueness, batch item availability and partner monthly cap.
15. **Fiscal capacity, evaluated last** — point sufficiency, the member annual purchase cap, programme cash sufficiency and the two fund-impairment gates. Fiscal checks come last on purpose: a request that is malformed, unauthorised or logically impossible must never be reported as a funding problem.

### 2.7. Money, Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents; no monetary field is ever a fractional number. Where a monetary amount is derived by multiplying by a percentage, the result is rounded **half up** to the whole cent — which, since cents are the storage unit, is the two-decimal-place rounding of the underlying currency amount. Percentages themselves (`program_fee_pct`, `trailing_12m_occupancy_pct`, `volume_discount_pct`, `breakage_rate_pct`, `bonus_multiplier_applied`) are fractions between zero and one and preserve the precision supplied or derived, with `program_fee_pct` and `trailing_12m_occupancy_pct` bounded to at most four decimal places on input.

Point quantities are always whole numbers, and the direction of truncation is not uniform — it is chosen per rule and stated with each rule in §3. Earning rounds **down** to the whole point. The elite bonus multiplication rounds **down**. The elite tier discount on an award price rounds **down** before being capped. The peak-season surcharge on an award price rounds **up**. The programme fund's `solvency_ratio` is the only non-monetary derived number carrying more than two places: it is rounded half up to four decimal places.

Two ordering pins matter enough to state globally. First, when a percentage is applied across many rows and the results are then summed, each row is rounded to the cent first and the already-rounded figures are then added; the alternative — summing the raw amounts and applying the percentage once — is explicitly not the rule and gives different answers. Second, where a formula both rounds and clamps, the rounding happens first and the clamp is applied to the rounded figure.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Properties and Member Accounts

Two write operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and only those two.

The first is award booking creation. A request to book an award stay must carry `expected_hotel_version`, a required (never optional) field whose value is the `version` of the target property as the client last observed it. If `expected_hotel_version` does not equal that property's current `version`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HOTEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because award pricing inputs, blackout activation, award allotment consumption and the delinquent fee posture all live on the `Hotel` row and all advance its `version`: a request carrying an outdated `expected_hotel_version` was priced against a property state that no longer exists. Note that this cannot be reduced to a state precondition — a second booking with a refreshed version is entirely legitimate business, because award inventory routinely permits more than one reservation on the same night.

The second is the goodwill point adjustment. A request to write a point adjustment must carry `expected_member_version`, again a required field, whose value is the `version` of the target member as the operator last observed it. If `expected_member_version` does not equal that member's current `version`, the adjustment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here too, nothing in the state machine would block a repeat: two goodwill grants of the same size to the same member are both perfectly legal, so the version mismatch is the only signal available that the operator was looking at a balance and a tier which have since moved.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Where §3 describes an operation without mentioning an expected version, none is sent and none is checked.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity and the Programme's Conserved Quantities

Derived fields in this system are never incrementally patched in isolation; they are re-derived from their source rows at the end of any request that could have moved them, and the resulting write is a mutation of the owning row with all the `updated_at` and `version` consequences of §2.4. Concretely, `Hotel.fee_ledger_balance_cents`, `Hotel.reimbursement_owed_cents`, `Hotel.benefit_recovery_owed_cents`, `Hotel.benefit_denial_count` and `Hotel.blackout_days_used_current_year` are all definitionally derived: the reimbursement owed is the sum of `settled_reimbursement_cents` across that property's award bookings whose `reimbursement_status` is either `ACCRUED` or `BATCHED`; the benefit recovery owed is the sum of `cost_recovery_cents` across the elite benefit obligations of that property's stays whose `status` is `DELIVERED`; the denial count is the number of that property's benefit obligations whose `status` is `DENIED`, `ESCALATED` or `DENIED_FINAL`; and the consumed blackout allowance is the sum of `day_count` across that property's blackout windows whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `start_date` falls in the logical clock's current year. On the member side, `point_balance` is the signed sum of `points` across that member's ledger entries, `lifetime_points_earned` is the sum of `points` across that member's entries restricted to entries whose `points` is positive, `qualifying_revenue_cents_ytd` is the sum of `room_revenue_cents` across that member's stays whose `status` is `POSTED_QUALIFYING` and whose `checkin_date` falls in the logical clock's current year, and `points_purchased_current_year` is the sum of `points_quantity` across that member's point purchases whose `status` is either `PENDING` or `SETTLED` and whose `created_at` falls in the logical clock's current year. On the partner side, `purchased_points_mtd` is the sum of `points_quantity` across that partner's point purchases whose `status` is either `PENDING` or `SETTLED` and whose `created_at` falls in the logical clock's current calendar month. Both counters therefore admit a purchase from the moment it is recorded, for the reason §3.8 gives, and only a `REFUNDED` purchase leaves the counted set.

Because re-derivation is definitional rather than incremental, cascades chain: a request may cause a first entity to change, that change may cause a second entity to change, and the second change may cause a third. Every such hop is described explicitly in §3, including the hops that are caused by a preceding hop rather than by the original request. A server-initiated transition of this kind is legitimate and does not require an explicit client request against the entity it moves.

Two quantities in the programme are conserved and must reconcile exactly at all times, with no rounding participating in either identity. **Member points**: a member's `point_balance` equals the signed sum of `points` over that member's `PointLedgerEntry` rows, always. And once a forward action is paired with its compensating action, the scoped sum returns to exactly zero — after a previously posted stay is voided, the sum of `points` across ledger entries whose `source_stay_id` names that stay is zero; after an award booking is cancelled, the sum of `points` across entries whose `source_redemption_id` names that booking is zero; after a settled point purchase is refunded, the sum of `points` across entries whose `source_purchase_id` names that purchase is zero. This exactness is precisely why the rounding-down inside the earning formulas is applied once at award time and never re-derived at reversal time: a reversal negates the stored figure rather than recomputing it. **Programme cash**: `ProgramFund.cash_balance_cents` equals the sum of `cash_due_cents` over point purchases whose `status` is `SETTLED`, minus the sum of `net_payable_cents` over reimbursement batches whose `status` is `PAID`. Purchases in `PENDING` and `REFUNDED` are excluded from the first sum, and every batch status other than `PAID` is excluded from the second — which is why a refund needs no separate reversal term in the identity, it simply leaves the settled set. A negative `net_payable_cents` on a paid batch increases programme cash rather than decreasing it.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties in the Reimbursement Chain

A reimbursement batch travels from release through approval to payment, and no single operator may carry it across more than one of those boundaries. When a batch is released, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded on the batch as `released_by_user_id`; when it is approved, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `approved_by_user_id`.

The `X-User-ID` approving or rejecting a batch must differ from that batch's `released_by_user_id`; if it matches, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Both the approval and the rejection action are guarded by this rule. The `X-User-ID` paying a batch must differ from **both** `released_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id`; if it matches either, the request is refused with `ERR_PAYER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 403), so no single operator can walk a payment from release all the way to cash-out. Both of these duty checks are evaluated before the batch's own status is examined, so an operator who released a batch and immediately attempts to approve it again receives the duties refusal rather than a status complaint.

Separately, the property that requests money is never the party that disburses it: batch release, approval, rejection and payment are all reserved to `ProgramAdministrator`, while a `HotelOwner` may only open the batch.

### 2.11. Programme Fund Solvency Posture as a Programme-Wide Gate

The programme fund is a singleton resource that exists from server start with `cash_balance_cents` of 0, `breakage_rate_pct` of 0.20, `outstanding_points` of 0, `point_liability_cents` of 0, a `solvency_ratio` of 999.0000 and a `status` of `HEALTHY`. Its `status` is not set by a client; it is derived from the recomputed `solvency_ratio` from scratch at the end of any request that writes a point ledger entry, moves `cash_balance_cents`, restates the breakage assumption, or explicitly asks for a revaluation. Because the derivation starts from scratch every time, a fund that is already `IMPAIRED` and re-evaluates to `IMPAIRED` is a perfectly ordinary evaluation, as is any move between the three postures in either direction.

The thresholds are fixed. A `solvency_ratio` of at least 1.0000 yields `HEALTHY`, and peak-season award bookings are permitted. A ratio of at least 0.7500 but below 1.0000 yields `WATCH`; this posture gates no operation at all, it is reported so that the brand can see the deterioration. A ratio below 0.7500 yields `IMPAIRED`, and two gates close programme-wide: goodwill point grants are refused with `ERR_FUND_IMPAIRED` (HTTP 402), and any award booking whose resolved season is `PEAK` is refused with `ERR_FUND_IMPAIRED_PEAK_BLOCKED` (HTTP 402). The goodwill gate covers both channels through which goodwill points can be granted — the standalone point adjustment, and a complaint resolution that carries a positive goodwill grant. `STANDARD`-season award bookings remain open in every posture.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Property Into the Programme, and Taking It Out

A `ProgramAdministrator` enrols a property by supplying its `name` (a non-empty trade name of 1 to 120 characters), its `brand_code` (exactly three uppercase letters identifying the chain the property flies), its `market_tier` — one of `URBAN`, `RESORT` or `SELECT`, which is the key that later decides the peak redemption surcharge — its `program_fee_pct`, its `rooms_count` of sellable inventory, and the two trailing-twelve-month performance figures `trailing_12m_occupancy_pct` and `trailing_12m_adr_cents`. The `program_fee_pct` is the fraction of qualifying room revenue the programme assesses back to the property, and the brand caps it at twenty percent. A newly enrolled property lands in `ONBOARDING` with `fee_ledger_balance_cents`, `reimbursement_owed_cents`, `benefit_recovery_owed_cents`, `benefit_denial_count` and `blackout_days_used_current_year` all at 0, `termination_reason` null, and `version` at 1.

Before a property can trade, it must publish at least one rate plan. Either a `ProgramAdministrator` or the property's own `HotelOwner` registers a rate plan by supplying a `rate_code` of 1 to 16 characters — something like `BAR` or `CORP01` — and a `rate_category` drawn from `TRANSIENT`, `CORPORATE_NEGOTIATED`, `GROUP`, `OPAQUE_WHOLESALE`, `EMPLOYEE_COMP` and `AWARD`. The `rate_code` must be unique within the property; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATE_CODE`. A rate plan may be registered while the property is in any status other than `TERMINATED`; attempting it on a terminated property yields `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE`. Everything else on the rate plan is derived from `rate_category` alone and is never accepted from the request. The `base_earn_rate_per_dollar` — points earned per whole dollar of room revenue — is 10 for `TRANSIENT`, 8 for `CORPORATE_NEGOTIATED`, 6 for `GROUP`, and 0 for each of `OPAQUE_WHOLESALE`, `EMPLOYEE_COMP` and `AWARD`. The flag `is_qualifying` is true exactly when the category is one of `TRANSIENT`, `CORPORATE_NEGOTIATED` or `GROUP`, and false for the three excluded families. The flag `program_fee_eligible` is true for exactly the same three categories, because the programme never assesses a fee on revenue it did not reward. The flag `counts_toward_elite` is true for only `TRANSIENT` and `CORPORATE_NEGOTIATED`: group business earns points and is fee-assessed, but never accrues elite nights, and that asymmetry is the whole reason `counts_toward_elite` exists as a flag separate from `is_qualifying`. A rate plan records the property it belongs to through its `hotel_id`.

Activation is the administrator-only step that moves a property `ONBOARDING → ACTIVE` and opens it for paid-stay capture and award redemption. Two conditions are checked, in this order: the property's status must be exactly `ONBOARDING`, so a second activation attempt on an already-active property is refused with `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE` rather than silently repeated; then at least one rate plan must belong to the property, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_HOTEL_ONBOARDING_INCOMPLETE`. Activation increments the property's `version`.

The property's status is the master gate on nearly everything, and the exact status each operation demands differs on purpose. Capturing a paid stay and drafting a blackout window require status exactly `ACTIVE`; `ONBOARDING`, `FEE_DELINQUENT` and `TERMINATED` are all refused. Booking an award stay and opening a reimbursement batch require status either `ACTIVE` or `FEE_DELINQUENT`, refusing only `ONBOARDING` and `TERMINATED` — a delinquent property still owes redemption reimbursement, and its arrears are collected through the batch fee offset rather than by shutting off award business, although peak inventory is separately withheld from it as described in §3.6. Posting a paid stay likewise requires `ACTIVE` or `FEE_DELINQUENT`, as does confirming a held award booking. Registering a rate plan, refreshing performance figures, adjusting a disputed stay and releasing a reimbursement batch require any status other than `TERMINATED`. Activation requires exactly `ONBOARDING`; termination requires `ACTIVE` or `FEE_DELINQUENT`, so an onboarding property cannot be terminated and a terminated one cannot be terminated twice. Every one of these refusals carries `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE` with HTTP 409.

Either a `ProgramAdministrator` or the property's `HotelOwner` may restate the property's performance by supplying fresh values for `trailing_12m_occupancy_pct` and `trailing_12m_adr_cents`, which overwrite the stored figures and increment the property's `version`. This is a consequential operation rather than a bookkeeping one: those two numbers are the inputs to the owner-reimbursement formula, so refreshing them between the moment an award stay accrues and the moment its batch is released is exactly what makes the accrual figure and the settlement figure diverge. Importantly, a refresh **does not** restate any `estimated_reimbursement_cents` already written on an accrued award booking; the accrued estimate stands as recorded.

A property that runs up programme fees faster than it earns reimbursement falls into delinquency. At the end of any request that posts a paid stay or adjusts a disputed one, if the property's recomputed `fee_ledger_balance_cents` is strictly greater than 250,000 cents — that is, above two thousand five hundred dollars of outstanding fee — the property moves `ACTIVE → FEE_DELINQUENT` and its `version` increments. The comparison is strictly greater-than: a balance of exactly 250,000 cents leaves the property `ACTIVE`. Delinquency withdraws peak award inventory program-side, while standard bookings and reimbursement batching continue unchanged. The reverse move happens only through settlement: at the end of a batch payment, if the property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` after that settlement stands at or below zero, a property that was `FEE_DELINQUENT` returns to `ACTIVE` and its `version` increments again.

Termination is the administrator's exit, and it unwinds every obligation that has not yet been paid. The caller supplies a `termination_reason`, which is recorded on the property; before termination that field is null. The property moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` or `FEE_DELINQUENT → TERMINATED`, with an identical cascade in both cases, and any outstanding `fee_ledger_balance_cents` is retained exactly as recorded rather than being forgiven. The cascade runs as follows. Every award booking of that property whose `reimbursement_status` is `ACCRUED` moves `ACCRUED → VOIDED`; because those rows have left the owed set, the property's `reimbursement_owed_cents` is recomputed and lands at 0. Every reimbursement batch of that property whose `status` is `OPEN` or `RELEASED` moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `HOTEL_TERMINATED`, and each award booking attached to such a batch reverts `BATCHED → VOIDED` with its `batch_id` cleared to null. This write-off is not §3.7's ordinary rejection and does not borrow its resets: the batch retains every figure the release wrote onto it, so `item_count`, `gross_reimbursement_cents`, `fee_offset_base_cents`, `delinquency_surcharge_cents`, `total_fee_offset_cents` and `net_payable_cents` stand as released rather than returning to 0, and every award booking this cascade voids retains both `estimated_reimbursement_cents` and `settled_reimbursement_cents` exactly as recorded — a voided booking leaves the owed set on the strength of its `reimbursement_status` alone, so neither figure needs discarding. Every elite benefit obligation belonging to a stay of that property whose `status` is `OBLIGATED` moves to `WAIVED`. Every award booking of that property whose `fulfillment_status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and the points it charged are returned to the member by a `REVERSAL` ledger entry exactly as if the member had cancelled it. Because ledger entries were written, the fund's liability valuation is re-run and its posture re-derived. Finally the property's `version` increments.

### 3.2. Publishing Award Prices and Managing the Blackout Calendar

Award pricing is published as dated windows, and only a `ProgramAdministrator` may publish them. Each window names its `season` — `STANDARD` or `PEAK` — an inclusive `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the list award price `points_per_night` (at least 1000), the per-night `cash_copay_cents` the member pays the property directly in addition to points (at least 0, and never collected by the programme), a `min_tier_required` drawn from `BASE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` and `PLATINUM` that gates who may book this row's inventory, and an `award_allotment_per_night` (at least 0) capping how many award room-nights may be concurrently occupied on any one calendar date under this row. A window records its property through `hotel_id`. Two windows of the same property and the same season may not overlap in their inclusive date ranges; an attempt is refused with `ERR_PRICING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A standard window and a peak window covering the same dates, by contrast, is the normal configuration — §3.6 explains how the resolver chooses between them.

Blackout windows follow a three-state lifecycle of their own. Either a `ProgramAdministrator` or the property's `HotelOwner` drafts one at a property that is exactly `ACTIVE`, supplying an inclusive `start_date`, an inclusive `end_date`, and a `scope` of either `ALL_REDEMPTIONS` or `STANDARD_ONLY`. The draft lands in `DRAFT` with `revoke_reason` null, and its `day_count` is computed inclusively on both endpoints: the number of days from `start_date` to `end_date` plus one, so a window running from the first through the fifth of a month has a `day_count` of 5. A `DRAFT` window blocks nothing and consumes none of the property's annual blackout allowance.

Activation of a blackout window is administrator-only and runs three checks in a fixed order. First the window must be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`. Second, the window must stand clear of every other `ACTIVE` window at the same property by at least seven clear days. That comparison is made pairwise: for each other `ACTIVE` window of the property, order the two windows by `start_date` ascending, breaking a tie by `created_at` ascending, and count the clear days strictly between them as the number of days from the earlier window's `end_date` to the later window's `start_date`, minus one. If that count of clear days is below seven for any pair, the activation is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Because the endpoints are inclusive, a window ending on the fifth of December followed by one starting on the thirteenth leaves exactly seven clear days and is accepted, while a start on the twelfth leaves six and is refused; direct adjacency leaves zero clear days and any overlap leaves fewer than zero, so both are refused as well. Third, the property's `blackout_days_used_current_year` plus this window's `day_count` must not exceed 60 days in the logical clock's calendar year, else `ERR_BLACKOUT_ANNUAL_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Only `ACTIVE` windows participate in either check: `DRAFT` and `REVOKED` windows neither block an activation nor consume the annual allowance. On success the window moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, its `day_count` is added to the property's consumed allowance, and the window's `version` increments.

Withdrawing a window is the mirror image. An administrator revokes an `ACTIVE` window with a `revoke_reason`, moving it `ACTIVE → REVOKED`, returning its `day_count` to the property's annual allowance, and incrementing the window's `version`. Revoking anything that is not `ACTIVE` — a draft, or an already-revoked window — is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`. An `ACTIVE` window's `scope` decides how much it actually blocks: `ALL_REDEMPTIONS` closes the property's award calendar to every season, while `STANDARD_ONLY` closes it only to bookings whose resolved season is `STANDARD`, leaving peak award business open on those same dates.

### 3.3. Enrolling Members and the Paid-Stay Posting Fork

Enrolment is deliberately thin. A `ProgramAdministrator` or a `MemberCareAgent` creates a member from an `email` — which must be globally unique across the programme and must contain an at-sign — a `full_name` for display, and an optional `enrollment_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. When `enrollment_date` is omitted it defaults to the logical clock's current date, meaning the date part of the current logical UTC timestamp. A new member starts at `elite_tier` `BASE` with `qualifying_nights_ytd`, `qualifying_revenue_cents_ytd`, `point_balance`, `lifetime_points_earned` and `points_purchased_current_year` all at 0.

Paid stays enter the system already ended or in progress, captured by a `ProgramAdministrator` or by the hosting property's `HotelOwner` at a property whose status is exactly `ACTIVE`. The payload names the guest through `member_id`, the property through `hotel_id`, the commercial terms through `rate_plan_id`, and the dates through `checkin_date` and `checkout_date`. Room-only revenue arrives as `room_revenue_cents` and is the sole base for both earning and fee assessment; `incidental_revenue_cents` — food, beverage and everything else — is optional, defaults to 0, and never earns a point nor attracts a cent of programme fee under any rate category. The referenced rate plan must belong to the same property as the stay; a rate plan from a different property is refused with `ERR_RATE_PLAN_HOTEL_MISMATCH`. On creation, the system computes `nights` as the number of days from `checkin_date` to `checkout_date` — a half-open interval that includes the arrival date and excludes the departure date, so a stay arriving on the tenth and departing on the twelfth is two nights — and snapshots the guest's current `elite_tier` into `tier_at_checkin`. The stay lands in `PENDING_POSTING` with `base_points_awarded`, `bonus_points_awarded` and `program_fee_assessed_cents` at 0, `bonus_multiplier_applied` at 0.00, and `dispute_reason` and `void_reason` both null.

Posting is where the stay becomes money and points, and it is the system's most consequential fork. A `ProgramAdministrator` or the hosting `HotelOwner` posts the stay, and three preconditions are evaluated in order: the stay must be in `PENDING_POSTING`, else `ERR_STAY_INVALID_STATE`; the property must be `ACTIVE` or `FEE_DELINQUENT`, else `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the logical clock's current date must be on or after the stay's `checkout_date`, else `ERR_STAY_NOT_COMPLETED` — a stay may only be posted once it has actually ended. From `PENDING_POSTING` the stay then branches to one of two terminal-for-earning statuses on the basis of a single test, and the two branches share a trigger and an actor while having entirely disjoint consequences.

**The qualifying arm — `PENDING_POSTING → POSTED_QUALIFYING`.** This arm is taken when the stay's rate plan has `is_qualifying` true *and* the stay's `room_revenue_cents` is greater than zero. Base earning comes first: `base_points_awarded` is the stay's `room_revenue_cents` multiplied by the rate plan's `base_earn_rate_per_dollar`, divided by one hundred to convert cents into whole dollars, with the result rounded **down** to a whole point. The rounding never goes up, so a stay of 12,345 cents on a rate plan earning 10 points per dollar awards 1,234 points, not 1,235. Elite bonus earning follows: the multiplier is 0.00 at `BASE`, 0.20 at `SILVER`, 0.50 at `GOLD` and 0.75 at `PLATINUM`, and `bonus_points_awarded` is `base_points_awarded` multiplied by that multiplier, again rounded **down** to a whole point — so a base award of 123,450 points at the platinum multiplier yields 92,587 bonus points rather than 92,587.5. Whichever multiplier was used is recorded on the stay as `bonus_multiplier_applied`. At posting time the multiplier is keyed on the stay's own `tier_at_checkin` snapshot — the tier the guest held when the stay row was created — so any retroactive tier movement between arrival and posting is deliberately ignored here.

Both awards become ledger entries. One `EARN_BASE` entry is written with `points` equal to `base_points_awarded` and `source_stay_id` naming the stay; one `EARN_BONUS` entry is written with `points` equal to `bonus_points_awarded` and the same `source_stay_id`, and that second entry is emitted **only** when `bonus_points_awarded` is greater than zero. Because those entries exist, the member's `point_balance` and `lifetime_points_earned` are recomputed. Because the stay has entered `POSTED_QUALIFYING`, the member's `qualifying_revenue_cents_ytd` is recomputed, and the member's `qualifying_nights_ytd` is recomputed as well whenever the stay's rate plan has `counts_toward_elite` true. Because `qualifying_nights_ytd` moved, the member's `elite_tier` is re-derived.

Tier derivation counts nights, not revenue, and it counts them narrowly: `qualifying_nights_ytd` is the sum of `nights` across the member's stays whose `status` is exactly `POSTED_QUALIFYING`, whose rate plan has `counts_toward_elite` true, and whose `checkin_date` falls in the logical clock's calendar year. Stays in `DISPUTED`, `ADJUSTED`, `VOID`, `PENDING_POSTING` and `POSTED_NON_QUALIFYING` are all excluded. The tier is then the highest band the count reaches: 50 qualifying nights or more is `PLATINUM`, 30 or more is `GOLD`, 10 or more is `SILVER`, and anything below 10 is `BASE`. Every comparison is inclusive of its threshold, so exactly 50 nights is `PLATINUM` while 49 nights is `GOLD`. This same re-derivation runs on any request that moves a stay into or out of `POSTED_QUALIFYING` — posting, adjustment and voiding alike.

The programme fee is assessed on the same request. Per stay, the fee is the stay's `room_revenue_cents` multiplied by the property's `program_fee_pct`, rounded half up to the whole cent, and stored on the stay as `program_fee_assessed_cents`; if the rate plan has `program_fee_eligible` false or `is_qualifying` false, that figure is 0. The property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` is then re-derived as the sum of the **already-rounded** per-stay figures across all of that property's stays whose `status` is one of `POSTED_QUALIFYING`, `POSTED_NON_QUALIFYING`, `DISPUTED` or `ADJUSTED` — stays in `PENDING_POSTING` and `VOID` are excluded, and there is no monthly window, the ledger spans all time — minus the sum of `fee_offset_base_cents` across that property's reimbursement batches whose `status` is `PAID`. The rounding order is pinned and it matters: round each stay's fee to the cent first, then add the rounded figures. Three stays of 12,345 cents each at a fee rate of 0.0475 therefore produce 586 cents apiece and a ledger of 1,758 cents; summing the revenue first and applying the rate once would give 1,759 and is explicitly not the rule. Because the balance moved, the delinquency evaluation of §3.1 runs.

Finally, the qualifying arm synthesises the property's benefit obligations. The entitlement matrix reads the stay's `tier_at_checkin`: `UPGRADE` is owed at `SILVER`, `GOLD` and `PLATINUM`; `BREAKFAST` is owed at `GOLD` and `PLATINUM`; `LATE_CHECKOUT` is owed at `PLATINUM` only; and a `BASE` guest is entitled to nothing at all. One elite benefit obligation is created in status `OBLIGATED` for each entitled `benefit_type`, each recording its originating stay through `stay_id`, with at most one obligation per combination of stay and benefit type. Because the ledger changed, the fund's `outstanding_points`, `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are all recomputed at the end of the request.

**The non-qualifying arm — `PENDING_POSTING → POSTED_NON_QUALIFYING`.** This arm is taken when the stay's rate plan has `is_qualifying` false *or* the stay's `room_revenue_cents` is exactly zero. It has precisely the opposite consequences, and they are worth spelling out with the same care. The stay's `base_points_awarded` is set to 0, its `bonus_points_awarded` to 0, its `bonus_multiplier_applied` to 0.00 and its `program_fee_assessed_cents` to 0. No `EARN_BASE` entry and no `EARN_BONUS` entry is written. No elite benefit obligation is synthesised, so an opaque wholesale or employee-comp night never puts an upgrade on the property's books no matter how high the guest's tier. The member's `elite_tier` is unchanged, the member's `qualifying_nights_ytd` and `qualifying_revenue_cents_ytd` do not move, the property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` is unchanged, the delinquency evaluation is therefore moot, and the programme fund is untouched. Note that the stay still enters the fee-ledger's status filter set, which is why it appears in the balance derivation above contributing its own zero.

### 3.4. Correcting the Record: Disputes, Adjustments and Voids

A posted stay is not immutable. A `Member`, a `MemberCareAgent` or a `ProgramAdministrator` may open a dispute on a stay in either `POSTED_QUALIFYING` or `POSTED_NON_QUALIFYING` by supplying a `dispute_reason`, moving it to `DISPUTED` and incrementing the stay's `version`; a dispute raised against a stay in any other status is refused with `ERR_STAY_INVALID_STATE`. Opening a dispute freezes the earnings rather than reversing them: the awarded `base_points_awarded` and `bonus_points_awarded` stand, the ledger entries stand, `program_fee_assessed_cents` stands, the property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` is unchanged because `DISPUTED` remains inside the fee-ledger's status filter, and any elite benefit obligations already synthesised stand exactly as posted. One derived figure does move, however: because tier derivation counts only stays in exactly `POSTED_QUALIFYING`, the disputed stay's nights leave the count, so the member's `elite_tier` is re-derived on this request and may fall.

Settling a dispute is administrator-only and moves the stay `DISPUTED → ADJUSTED`, which is terminal. The property must not be `TERMINATED`, else `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE`; the stay must be in `DISPUTED`, else `ERR_STAY_INVALID_STATE`. The caller may optionally supply a restated `room_revenue_cents`; when that field is present it overwrites the stay's revenue, and when it is absent the stay keeps its posted figure. The award is then recomputed on whatever revenue now stands, and here the bonus is computed under the **final tier** reading rather than the provisional one: the multiplier is keyed on the member's `elite_tier` as read at the moment of adjustment, after every intervening posting has already re-derived it. This is the deliberate divergence between the two bonus variants — identical stay data yields two different bonus figures. A stay whose base award is 123,450 points awards 0 bonus points at posting time if the guest held `BASE` at check-in, and 92,587 bonus points at adjustment time once that same guest has climbed to `PLATINUM`.

The corrections are written as deltas, never as edits to the original entries. An additional `EARN_BASE` ledger entry is written whose `points` is the new base award minus the old base award, and it is written only when that difference is non-zero. An additional `EARN_BONUS` entry is written whose `points` is the new bonus award minus the old bonus award, again only when that difference is non-zero. Both carry `source_stay_id` naming the stay, and either may be negative. Qualification itself is re-derived on the revised data: if the rate plan has `is_qualifying` false, or the revised revenue is zero, the awards collapse to 0 and both deltas are negative. Because ledger entries were written, the member's `point_balance` and `lifetime_points_earned` are recomputed; because qualifying revenue changed, `qualifying_revenue_cents_ytd` and `elite_tier` are recomputed — and note that `ADJUSTED` also sits outside the tier-counting set, so the stay's nights do not return to the count. The stay's `program_fee_assessed_cents` is recomputed on the revised revenue and the property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` moves by the fee delta, after which the delinquency evaluation of §3.1 runs. The fund's `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are recomputed at the end.

Voiding is the full unwind, and it is administrator-only. The caller supplies a `void_reason` and the stay moves to `VOID` from any status that is not already `VOID` — `PENDING_POSTING`, `POSTED_QUALIFYING`, `POSTED_NON_QUALIFYING` and `DISPUTED` are all valid origins, and a second void is refused with `ERR_STAY_INVALID_STATE`. What the void costs depends entirely on what the stay had produced.

Voiding from `PENDING_POSTING` is trivial: the stay's `status` becomes `VOID`, its `void_reason` is recorded, and nothing else happens because no ledger entry exists to reverse and no fee was ever assessed. Voiding from `POSTED_NON_QUALIFYING` is equally trivial for exactly the same reason: the non-qualifying arm wrote no ledger entries and assessed no fee, so there is nothing to compensate.

Voiding from `POSTED_QUALIFYING` runs a four-deep transitive cascade in which each hop is caused by the hop before it, not by the original request. **Hop one:** the stay's `status` becomes `VOID` and its `void_reason` is recorded. **Hop two:** because the stay went void, one `REVERSAL` ledger entry is written for every existing entry whose `source_stay_id` names this stay, each carrying `points` equal to the negation of the original entry's `points` and `reversal_of_entry_id` naming that original entry; and the stay's `program_fee_assessed_cents` is credited back, so the property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` falls by that amount as the stay leaves the fee-ledger's status filter set. **Hop three:** because those reversal entries were written, the member's `point_balance`, `lifetime_points_earned`, `qualifying_revenue_cents_ytd` and `qualifying_nights_ytd` are all recomputed, and the recomputed night count re-derives the member's `elite_tier`, which may drop. **Hop four:** because the member's `elite_tier` dropped, every elite benefit obligation in status `OBLIGATED` belonging to **any** stay of that member whose `benefit_type` is no longer entitled at the new tier moves to `WAIVED` — a downgrade all the way to `BASE` therefore waives all three benefit types across the member's outstanding obligations. **And independently of the tier outcome:** every elite benefit obligation whose `stay_id` names this very stay and whose `status` is `OBLIGATED` or `ESCALATED` moves to `WAIVED`, because an obligation cannot outlive the stay that created it and an escalated denial loses the fact pattern it was arguing about. Waiving an `ESCALATED` obligation recomputes the property's `benefit_denial_count`, and since `WAIVED` sits outside the counted set that figure falls by one. A member complaint sitting in `ESCALATED` against a waived obligation keeps its own status, but its adjudication will now be refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_ESCALATED` — there is no live denial left to rule on. Finally, because the reversal entries were written, the fund's `outstanding_points`, `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are recomputed.

Voiding from `DISPUTED` follows whichever of those two shapes fits the stay's history: if the disputed stay had originally posted as qualifying, the full cascade above runs unchanged; if it had posted as non-qualifying, the void is the trivial one.

### 3.5. Elite Benefit Obligations, Delivery and Cost Recovery

Elite benefit obligations are never created by a client. They are synthesised by the qualifying arm of stay posting, which means a read of a single obligation or a filtered list of them is the only way a property discovers what it owes. Each obligation carries the `benefit_type` it represents, its `status`, its `cost_recovery_cents` (0 until delivered) and its `denial_reason` (null unless denied).

A `ProgramAdministrator` or the hosting `HotelOwner` records that the property honoured the benefit, moving the obligation `OBLIGATED → DELIVERED`. Two checks run and their order is fixed: first the obligation's originating stay must still be in `POSTED_QUALIFYING`, else `ERR_STAY_INVALID_STATE` — a benefit cannot be recorded against a stay that has since been disputed, adjusted or voided; then the obligation itself must be in `OBLIGATED`, else `ERR_DELIVERY_INVALID_STATE`. On success the programme credits the property a cash recovery, computed by benefit type. An `UPGRADE` recovers fifteen percent of the property's `trailing_12m_adr_cents` as read at delivery time, rounded half up to the cent and then capped at 9,000 cents; the rounding happens before the cap, and the cap is a hard ceiling, so an average daily rate of 30,000 cents recovers 4,500 while a rate of 80,000 cents recovers 9,000 rather than 12,000. A `BREAKFAST` recovers 1,800 cents for each of the originating stay's `nights`. A `LATE_CHECKOUT` recovers a flat 1,200 cents. The figure lands in `cost_recovery_cents`, the property's `benefit_recovery_owed_cents` is recomputed, and the obligation's `version` increments.

Denial is the same actors' alternative, moving the obligation `OBLIGATED → DENIED` with a `denial_reason`, leaving `cost_recovery_cents` at 0, recomputing the property's `benefit_denial_count`, and incrementing the obligation's `version`. Denial runs three checks in order: the originating stay must still be `POSTED_QUALIFYING`, else `ERR_STAY_INVALID_STATE`; the obligation must be `OBLIGATED`, else `ERR_DELIVERY_INVALID_STATE`; and the property's `benefit_denial_count` must be below 3 before this denial is recorded, else `ERR_BENEFIT_DENIAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The count is programme-lifetime, not periodic: a property gets three denials in total, and the fourth attempt is refused. What the count includes matters, because obligations move: `DENIED`, `ESCALATED` and `DENIED_FINAL` are all inside the counted set, while `DELIVERED`, `WAIVED` and `OBLIGATED` are outside it. A denial that is escalated therefore still counts against the property; a denial that adjudication overturns stops counting; and a denial waived by a stay void stops counting too.

The remaining transitions on an obligation are all server-driven and are described where their causes live. A `DENIED` obligation advances `DENIED → ESCALATED` when the member escalates the complaint that contests it (§3.9), and the property's `benefit_denial_count` is recomputed even though `ESCALATED` remains inside the counted set. An `ESCALATED` obligation advances `ESCALATED → DELIVERED` when adjudication orders delivery — in which case the cost recovery above is computed and both `benefit_denial_count` and `benefit_recovery_owed_cents` are recomputed, since the obligation both left the denied set and entered the delivered one — or `ESCALATED → DENIED_FINAL` when adjudication upholds the denial, in which case `cost_recovery_cents` stays 0 and `benefit_denial_count` is recomputed with the obligation still inside the counted set. An `OBLIGATED` obligation moves `OBLIGATED → WAIVED` from three distinct causes: a member tier drop in the stay-void cascade, a property termination, or the void of the obligation's own stay; in every case `cost_recovery_cents` stays 0 and neither `benefit_recovery_owed_cents` nor `benefit_denial_count` is affected, since `OBLIGATED` was outside both sets already. An `ESCALATED` obligation moves `ESCALATED → WAIVED` from exactly one cause: the void of its own stay.

### 3.6. Booking, Pricing and Fulfilling an Award Stay

Booking an award stay is the single most heavily guarded operation in the programme, because one request selects a price, prices an award non-linearly, debits a member, occupies inventory and bumps a property's `version` all at once. A `ProgramAdministrator`, a `MemberCareAgent` or the redeeming `Member` submits `member_id`, `hotel_id`, `checkin_date`, `checkout_date` and `expected_hotel_version`. The server computes `nights` as the number of days from `checkin_date` to `checkout_date`, an interval that is half-open — the arrival night is occupied and the departure night is not.

**Selecting the governing price window.** Before any check that depends on the season, the server resolves which published price applies. The candidate set is every award price window of the target property whose inclusive range from `window_start_date` through `window_end_date` contains the booking's `checkin_date`; the `checkout_date` plays no part in selection, so a booking that straddles two windows is priced entirely by the window containing its arrival. Candidates are ranked by `season`, with `PEAK` ranking above `STANDARD`, and the highest-ranked candidate wins — so where a peak window and a standard window both cover the arrival date, the peak price governs. When two candidates tie on rank, the one with the earlier `created_at` wins, and if `created_at` also ties, the row inserted first wins; the row identifier never participates in the choice. An empty candidate set is refused with `ERR_NO_REDEMPTION_PRICING`. The winning row is then snapshotted onto the booking: its `season` becomes the booking's `season`, its `points_per_night` becomes `points_per_night_applied`, its `award_allotment_per_night` becomes `award_allotment_applied`, and its `cash_copay_cents` multiplied by `nights` becomes the booking's `cash_copay_cents` — a figure the property collects directly from the guest and the programme never touches.

**Pricing the award in points.** The points price is computed from five interacting inputs — the snapshotted nightly price, the member's `elite_tier`, the property's `market_tier`, the resolved `season` and the night count — in a strictly pinned order of five steps. First, the tier discount per night: the nightly price is multiplied by the member's tier percentage, which is zero for `BASE` and zero for `SILVER`, five percent for `GOLD` and ten percent for `PLATINUM`; that product is floored to whole points and then capped at 3,000 points. Both directions of that step matter — a `PLATINUM` member at a nightly price of 40,000 points computes 4,000 and is then capped down to 3,000. Second, the free night: when the resolved season is `STANDARD` and the booking runs five nights or more, the chargeable night count is one less than `nights`; otherwise every night is chargeable. A peak booking never earns a free night, however long it is. Third, the gross: the discounted nightly price is multiplied by the chargeable night count. Fourth, the peak surcharge: when the resolved season is `PEAK`, the gross is multiplied by the property's market surcharge — twenty-five percent for `URBAN`, thirty-five percent for `RESORT`, fifteen percent for `SELECT` — and that product is rounded **up** to whole points, never half up; when the season is `STANDARD` the surcharge is zero. Fifth, the absolute floor: the final `points_charged` is the greater of the post-surcharge total and 6,000 points per chargeable night — the floor is applied once, after the surcharge, and against chargeable nights rather than against `nights`, so a free night is not floored. Applying the free night before the discount, or the floor before the surcharge, produces a different answer and is wrong.

**The order in which the booking may be refused.** Once the price window is resolved and the price computed, the remaining checks run in a fixed order and the first failure is the response. Optimistic concurrency comes first: a mismatched `expected_hotel_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_HOTEL_VERSION`. Then the property's status must be `ACTIVE` or `FEE_DELINQUENT`, else `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE` — a delinquent property still accepts award bookings, because it still owes reimbursement and its arrears are recovered through the batch offset of §3.7. Then, if the resolved season is `PEAK`, the property must not be `FEE_DELINQUENT`, else `ERR_PEAK_INVENTORY_WITHHELD`: peak inventory specifically is withheld from properties in arrears. Then no `ACTIVE` blackout window of the property may cover any night of the half-open stay interval, else `ERR_BLACKOUT_DATE_UNAVAILABLE` — and the window's `scope` decides what it blocks, with `ALL_REDEMPTIONS` blocking every season and `STANDARD_ONLY` blocking only bookings whose resolved season is `STANDARD`, which is why a peak booking can pass straight through a standard-only blackout. Then the member must hold no other booking in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `STAYED` at any property whose half-open interval intersects this one, else `ERR_MEMBER_REDEMPTION_OVERLAP`; because the test is strictly-less-than on both sides, a checkout on one date and a check-in the same date at a different property do not intersect, but one member cannot occupy two properties on the same night. Then award inventory: for every night of the half-open interval, count the property's bookings in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `STAYED` that occupy that night, and if adding this booking would take that count past `award_allotment_applied` on any single night, refuse with `ERR_AWARD_INVENTORY_UNAVAILABLE` — the test is a per-date count against a capacity, not a pairwise clash, and `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` bookings release their nights and are excluded from the count. Then the tier gate: the member's `elite_tier` must rank at or above the selected window's `min_tier_required`, on the ranking `BASE` below `SILVER` below `GOLD` below `PLATINUM`, else `ERR_TIER_BELOW_INVENTORY_MINIMUM`. Then the member's `point_balance` must be at least the computed `points_charged`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_POINTS`. And last, if the resolved season is `PEAK`, the programme fund's `status` must not be `IMPAIRED`, else `ERR_FUND_IMPAIRED_PEAK_BLOCKED`.

Note carefully what that ordering means for a booking that fails several tests at once. A peak booking at a delinquent property whose member cannot afford it reports `ERR_PEAK_INVENTORY_WITHHELD`, not `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_POINTS`. A sold-out night at a property whose member ranks below the gate reports `ERR_AWARD_INVENTORY_UNAVAILABLE`, not `ERR_TIER_BELOW_INVENTORY_MINIMUM`. And a peak booking blocked by an impaired fund is the very last thing checked, so every geometric and fiscal test above it has already passed.

On success the booking is created with `fulfillment_status` `HELD` and `reimbursement_status` `NOT_DUE`, both `estimated_reimbursement_cents` and `settled_reimbursement_cents` at 0, `batch_id` null and `cancel_reason` null. A `REDEEM` ledger entry is written carrying the negation of `points_charged` with `source_redemption_id` naming the booking, the member's `point_balance` falls by that amount, the property's `version` increments by one, and the fund's `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are recomputed.

**Two independent status dimensions.** A booking carries two statuses that evolve without reference to one another. `fulfillment_status` tracks what happened to the room; `reimbursement_status` tracks where the owner's money stands and never leaves `NOT_DUE` until the room dimension reaches `STAYED`.

The hosting `HotelOwner`, or an administrator, confirms a held booking, moving `fulfillment_status` `HELD → CONFIRMED`, leaving `reimbursement_status` at `NOT_DUE` and moving no points — the debit already happened at creation. Confirmation also requires the property to be `ACTIVE` or `FEE_DELINQUENT`, else `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE`. Any status other than `HELD` is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_INVALID_STATE`. Confirmation is also available to a `MemberCareAgent` and to the booking's own `Member`.

Completing the stay is available to the hosting `HotelOwner` and to an administrator, and moves `CONFIRMED → STAYED`. The booking's `checkout_date` must be on or before the logical clock's current date, else `ERR_REDEMPTION_NOT_ELAPSED`; any status other than `CONFIRMED` is `ERR_REDEMPTION_INVALID_STATE`. Because the award room was consumed, the reimbursement dimension advances `NOT_DUE → ACCRUED` in the same request and `estimated_reimbursement_cents` is computed under the **accrual** reading of the reimbursement formula, described in §3.7; the property's `reimbursement_owed_cents` is recomputed because the booking has entered the owed set — but note what that recomputation actually picks up: the owed figure of §2.9 sums `settled_reimbursement_cents`, and no settlement figure is written until the booking is batched at release, so a booking that is merely `ACCRUED` contributes 0 to it however large its `estimated_reimbursement_cents` may be. The booking's `version` increments.

Recording a no-show is likewise the hosting owner's or an administrator's action and moves `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW`. The `checkin_date` must be on or before the clock's current date, else `ERR_REDEMPTION_NOT_ARRIVED`; any status other than `CONFIRMED` is `ERR_REDEMPTION_INVALID_STATE`. A no-show is deliberately punitive and deliberately asymmetric with a cancellation: the debited points are forfeited, so **no** compensating entry is written and the member's `point_balance` does not move, and `reimbursement_status` stays at `NOT_DUE` permanently — a no-show is never reimbursable. The nights are nevertheless released, so they stop counting toward award inventory for later bookings. The booking's `version` increments.

Cancelling is the member's own action, also available to a `MemberCareAgent` and an administrator, and moves either `HELD → CANCELLED` or `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` with an identical effect set; any other status is `ERR_REDEMPTION_INVALID_STATE`. The caller supplies a `cancel_reason`, which is recorded. A `REVERSAL` ledger entry is written carrying positive `points_charged` — exactly what was debited, never more and never less — with `source_redemption_id` naming the booking and `reversal_of_entry_id` naming the original `REDEEM` entry; the member's `point_balance` rises by that amount; the nights are released from award inventory; the booking's `version` increments; and the fund's `outstanding_points`, `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are recomputed. `STAYED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal on the fulfilment dimension.

### 3.7. Owner Reimbursement, from Accrual to Disbursement

When a member redeems points for a room, the property gives up a saleable night and the programme owes it money. How much money is the single most contested number in the programme, and the answer depends on how full the property was and what its rooms were worth — figures that drift between the night the award stay ends and the month the cheque is cut. The programme therefore computes the reimbursement **twice**, from the same formula and two different sets of inputs, and keeps both figures on the booking.

**The reimbursement formula.** Given an occupancy figure and an average daily rate, the per-night reimbursement rate is banded. When occupancy is at or above 0.95 — compression, the property could certainly have sold the room — the rate per night is the full average daily rate, undiscounted. When occupancy is below 0.95 but at or above 0.80 — strong demand — the rate per night is seventy-five percent of the average daily rate, rounded half up to the cent. When occupancy is below 0.80 — soft demand, the room might well have gone empty — the rate per night is fifty percent of the average daily rate rounded half up, but never less than 3,000 cents. The reimbursement amount is that per-night rate multiplied by the booking's `nights`. Two precision points are load-bearing: the rounding happens on the per-night rate **before** the multiplication by nights, and the 3,000-cent floor applies per night, not per stay, so a three-night soft-demand award is floored at 9,000 cents in total.

**The two readings of that formula.** The **accrual** reading runs when `fulfillment_status` becomes `STAYED`, takes its occupancy from the property's `trailing_12m_occupancy_pct` and its rate from `trailing_12m_adr_cents` exactly as they read at that moment, and writes the result to `estimated_reimbursement_cents`. The **settlement** reading runs when the batch is released, takes its occupancy from the batch's `occupancy_pct_at_release` and its rate from the batch's `adr_cents_at_release` — both snapshotted from the property at release time, and therefore reflecting any performance refresh that happened in between — and writes the result to `settled_reimbursement_cents`. The same booking can legitimately carry two different figures: a two-night award at an average daily rate of 30,000 cents accrues 45,000 cents while occupancy reads 0.82, and settles at 60,000 cents once occupancy has been refreshed to 0.96. Neither figure is wrong; the estimate is what the property was told, the settlement is what the programme pays, and the batch preserves the inputs that justify the difference.

**Opening a batch.** An administrator or the hosting `HotelOwner` opens a batch by supplying `hotel_id` and `period_month` in year-and-month form. The property must not be `ONBOARDING` or `TERMINATED`, else `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE`; a `FEE_DELINQUENT` property may absolutely have a batch opened, because the batch is precisely how its arrears are recovered. No other batch for the same property and the same `period_month` may exist in `OPEN`, `RELEASED` or `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_PERIOD` — a `PAID` or `REJECTED` batch for that period does not block a new one, which is what lets a rejected period be reworked. The batch opens in `OPEN` with `item_count`, `gross_reimbursement_cents`, `fee_offset_base_cents`, `delinquency_surcharge_cents`, `total_fee_offset_cents` and `net_payable_cents` all 0, `occupancy_pct_at_release` at 0.0, `adr_cents_at_release` at 0, and `released_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` all null. Nothing is selected and nothing is priced until release.

**Releasing a batch** is administrator-only and moves it `OPEN → RELEASED`. The batch must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`; the property must not be `TERMINATED`, else `ERR_HOTEL_NOT_ACTIVE`. The server then selects the batch's items: every booking of the batch's property whose `fulfillment_status` is `STAYED` and whose `reimbursement_status` is `ACCRUED` and whose `checkout_date` falls inside the batch's calendar month, both endpoints of that month inclusive, ordered by `created_at` ascending with insertion order as the tie-break and never by identifier. A checkout on the first day of the following month belongs to the following period, not this one. Bookings in `NOT_DUE`, `BATCHED`, `PAID` or `VOIDED` are all excluded — which is what makes a no-show, permanently `NOT_DUE`, invisible to every batch forever. An empty selection is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NO_ELIGIBLE_ITEMS` and the batch stays `OPEN`.

On a non-empty selection the batch snapshots `occupancy_pct_at_release` and `adr_cents_at_release` from the property, sets `item_count` to the number of items selected, and advances each selected booking's `reimbursement_status` `ACCRUED → BATCHED`, setting its `batch_id` to this batch and computing its `settled_reimbursement_cents` under the settlement reading. The property's `reimbursement_owed_cents` is recomputed; the selected items stay inside the owed set because `BATCHED` sits there alongside `ACCRUED`, and the recomputation now picks up each item's newly written `settled_reimbursement_cents` where that field previously stood at 0.

**The net-payable waterfall.** The batch's money fields are then computed in six pinned steps. Step one: `gross_reimbursement_cents` is the sum of `settled_reimbursement_cents` across the selected items. Step two: `fee_offset_base_cents` is the property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` as read at release — the programme nets what the property owes it in fees against what it owes the property in reimbursement. Step three: a working figure, the gross less the fee offset base. Step four: the delinquency surcharge percentage is selected from that step-three figure — five percent when it is negative, two percent when it is zero or more but below 500,000 cents, and one percent when it is 500,000 cents or more — and `delinquency_surcharge_cents` is that percentage of `fee_offset_base_cents`, rounded half up once. The tiering is deliberately regressive on the working figure: a property whose fees exceed its reimbursements pays the steepest surcharge on its arrears. Step five: `total_fee_offset_cents` is `fee_offset_base_cents` plus `delinquency_surcharge_cents`. Step six: `net_payable_cents` is `gross_reimbursement_cents` less `total_fee_offset_cents`.

The circularity in that waterfall is only apparent, and the pin in step four is what resolves it in one pass: **the surcharge tier is keyed on the step-three working figure, never on the step-six result.** With a gross of 1,205,000 cents and a fee offset base of 700,000 cents, step three yields 505,000, which selects the one-percent tier, giving a surcharge of 7,000 and a `net_payable_cents` of 498,000. Keying the tier on the step-six result instead would see 498,000, select the two-percent tier, and produce 491,000 — a different and wrong answer. Note also that `net_payable_cents` is signed: a negative value means the property owes the programme money, and that is a legitimate outcome, not an error.

Release finally records `released_by_user_id` from the caller's operator header and increments the batch's `version`.

**Approval and rejection** are the two exits from `RELEASED`, and both are administrator-only. Approval moves `RELEASED → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` from the caller's operator header, increments the `version` and moves no monetary field whatsoever. Rejection moves `RELEASED → REJECTED` with a `rejection_reason`. Both are subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10: the caller must not be the operator recorded in `released_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Rejection unwinds the release completely: every booking whose `batch_id` names this batch reverts `reimbursement_status` `BATCHED → ACCRUED`, has its `batch_id` cleared to null and its `settled_reimbursement_cents` reset to 0 — the settlement figure is discarded from the booking and survives only on the batch, for audit. The batch's own `item_count`, `gross_reimbursement_cents`, `fee_offset_base_cents`, `delinquency_surcharge_cents`, `total_fee_offset_cents` and `net_payable_cents` are all reset to 0 and its `version` increments. The reimbursement dimension of every affected booking is now in exactly the state it held before release, so a later batch for the same period can pick those items up again.

**Paying an approved batch** is administrator-only, requires the batch to be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`, and is the tightest-controlled operation in the programme. The caller must differ from **both** `released_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id`, else `ERR_PAYER_CONFLICT` — three distinct operators are required to carry money from selection to cash-out. The fund must be able to cover the disbursement: its `cash_balance_cents` must be at least `net_payable_cents`, else `ERR_FUND_INSUFFICIENT_CASH`; a batch with a negative `net_payable_cents` always passes that test, because paying it collects cash rather than spending it.

Payment then runs a three-deep cascade. **Hop one:** the batch moves `APPROVED → PAID` and its `version` increments. **Hop two:** because the batch went paid, every booking whose `batch_id` names it advances `reimbursement_status` `BATCHED → PAID`; the fund's `cash_balance_cents` falls by `net_payable_cents`, so a negative net payable *increases* programme cash; the property's `reimbursement_owed_cents` is recomputed and drops those bookings, since `PAID` sits outside the owed set; and the property's `fee_ledger_balance_cents` is re-derived by the fee-ledger rule of §3.1, which now also subtracts this batch's `fee_offset_base_cents`. Because the ledger derivation subtracts the offset **base** and not the offset total, the surcharge is money the fund keeps rather than credit the property receives — a 700,000-cent ledger settled by a batch carrying a 7,000-cent surcharge lands at exactly 0, not at minus 7,000. **Hop three:** because the fee ledger has been cleared to at or below zero, a property sitting in `FEE_DELINQUENT` transitions `FEE_DELINQUENT → ACTIVE` and its `version` increments — the restoration described in §3.1, reached here transitively rather than by any request aimed at the property. And because programme cash moved, the fund's `solvency_ratio` and `status` are recomputed at the end.

`PAID` and `REJECTED` are both terminal for a batch. On the booking's reimbursement dimension, `PAID` and `VOIDED` are terminal, and `VOIDED` is reachable only through the property-termination cascade of §3.1 — either directly from `ACCRUED`, or from `BATCHED` when termination rejects the batch the booking was attached to, in which case the booking's `batch_id` is cleared to null on the way. In both cases the property's `reimbursement_owed_cents` is recomputed, and a booking in `VOIDED` is excluded from every future batch selection exactly as `PAID` is.

### 3.8. Selling Points, Partner Volume, and Valuing What the Programme Owes

Points enter the programme from two directions. They are earned, by staying; and they are bought, by members with a credit card and by commercial partners buying wholesale. The bought points are where the programme's cash comes from, and they are also the reason the programme's liability grows faster than its earnings do.

**Partners.** An administrator registers a point-sale partner with a `name`, a `partner_type` of `CO_BRAND_ISSUER`, `AIRLINE` or `RETAIL`, a `points_cost_per_1000_cents` of at least 1 — the wholesale list price the partner pays per thousand points before any volume discount — and a `monthly_cap_points` of at least 1,000. The partner's `purchased_points_mtd` opens at 0 and is thereafter derived: it is the sum of `points_quantity` over that partner's purchases in status `PENDING` or `SETTLED` whose `created_at` falls in the logical clock's current month, and the same figure is what the monthly cap is measured against. Partner records are visible only to the `ProgramAdministrator` and the `ProgramAuditor`; neither a property nor a member can enumerate the programme's wholesale price list.

**Recording a purchase.** An administrator, a `MemberCareAgent` or the buying `Member` records a point sale with `member_id`, `channel`, `points_quantity` and optionally `partner_id`. The `channel` is one of `CO_BRAND_CARD`, `DIRECT_PURCHASE` or `PARTNER_SALE`. The `points_quantity` must be a multiple of 1,000 and at least 1,000. The channel and the partner reference must agree exactly: `partner_id` must be present when the channel is `PARTNER_SALE` and absent for both `CO_BRAND_CARD` and `DIRECT_PURCHASE`, else `ERR_PARTNER_CHANNEL_MISMATCH` — a partner reference on a direct purchase is as much an error as a missing one on a partner sale.

The purchase is then priced. The volume discount is tiered on `points_quantity` alone: twelve percent at 100,000 points or more, five percent at 25,000 points or more, and nothing below that. The comparisons are inclusive, so exactly 100,000 points takes the twelve-percent tier while 99,000 takes the five-percent tier. The list price per thousand points is the referenced partner's `points_cost_per_1000_cents` for a `PARTNER_SALE`, and a flat 1,250 cents for both other channels. The `cash_due_cents` is the quantity in thousands multiplied by that list price, multiplied by one minus the discount, and rounded half up **once** at the end of that whole expression — rounding the discount separately or rounding the undiscounted price first can differ by a cent. The `volume_discount_pct` selected is recorded on the purchase. Finally the co-brand bonus: `bonus_points` is ten percent of `points_quantity`, floored to whole points, for the `CO_BRAND_CARD` channel and 0 for both others — a `DIRECT_PURCHASE` never earns a bonus however large it is.

Two caps then apply. The member's `points_purchased_current_year` plus the requested `points_quantity` must not exceed 200,000, else `ERR_PURCHASE_CAP_EXCEEDED`; note that the cap is measured against `points_quantity` and does not count `bonus_points`, so a co-brand buyer can end the year holding more than 200,000 purchased points. And for a `PARTNER_SALE` only, the partner's `purchased_points_mtd` plus `points_quantity` must not exceed that partner's `monthly_cap_points`, else `ERR_PARTNER_MONTHLY_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

The purchase is created in status `PENDING` with `refund_reason` null. Nothing has happened yet in the programme's books: **no** ledger entry is written, **no** points reach the member's balance and **no** cash reaches the fund. The `points_quantity` is nevertheless added to the member's `points_purchased_current_year` and, for a partner sale, to the partner's `purchased_points_mtd`, so both caps are consumed by the pending purchase and cannot be defeated by leaving purchases unsettled.

**Settling a purchase** is administrator-only and moves it `PENDING → SETTLED`; any other status is `ERR_PURCHASE_INVALID_STATE`. One ledger entry is written for `points_quantity` with `source_purchase_id` naming the purchase, and its `entry_type` depends on the channel: `PARTNER_SALE` for the partner channel, and `PURCHASE` for both `CO_BRAND_CARD` and `DIRECT_PURCHASE`. When `bonus_points` is greater than zero — which happens on the `CO_BRAND_CARD` channel only — one additional entry of type `EARN_BONUS` is written for the bonus amount, also carrying `source_purchase_id`. The fund's `cash_balance_cents` rises by `cash_due_cents`. Because those entries and that cash movement happened, the member's `point_balance` and `lifetime_points_earned`, the member's `points_purchased_current_year`, the partner's `purchased_points_mtd` where a partner is referenced, and the fund's `outstanding_points`, `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are all recomputed. The purchase's `version` increments.

**Refunding a purchase** is available to an administrator and a `MemberCareAgent`, moves it `SETTLED → REFUNDED` with a `refund_reason`, and is the exact compensating action for settlement; any status other than `SETTLED` is `ERR_PURCHASE_INVALID_STATE`. One `REVERSAL` entry is written for every existing entry whose `source_purchase_id` names this purchase — two entries for a co-brand purchase that carried a bonus, one otherwise — each carrying the negation of the original entry's `points` and `reversal_of_entry_id` naming it. The fund's `cash_balance_cents` falls by `cash_due_cents`. The member's `point_balance` and `points_purchased_current_year`, the partner's `purchased_points_mtd`, and the fund's `outstanding_points`, `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are all recomputed, and the purchase's `version` increments. `REFUNDED` is terminal.

**Goodwill adjustments.** An administrator or a `MemberCareAgent` may move a member's balance directly, supplying `member_id`, a signed `points` value, an `adjustment_reason` and `expected_member_version`. The operation is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the member as described in §2.8, and it is refused with `ERR_FUND_IMPAIRED` while the programme fund's `status` is `IMPAIRED` — the programme does not hand out points it cannot value. On success a `GOODWILL` ledger entry is written carrying the signed `points` and the supplied `adjustment_reason`, the member's `point_balance` moves by that amount, the member's `version` increments, and the fund's valuation and posture are recomputed. This is the only endpoint whose response body is a ledger entry rather than the resource named in the path.

**The ledger.** Every point movement in the programme is a `PointLedgerEntry`, and the seven values of `entry_type` partition every cause: `EARN_BASE` and `EARN_BONUS` for the two halves of a qualifying stay's award and for their adjustment deltas, `EARN_BONUS` again for a co-brand purchase bonus, `PURCHASE` for a co-brand or direct point sale, `PARTNER_SALE` for a wholesale one, `REDEEM` for the debit that books an award stay, `REVERSAL` for every compensating entry, and `GOODWILL` for a direct adjustment. Each entry carries the `member_id` whose balance it moves, a signed `points` delta, and whichever of `source_stay_id`, `source_redemption_id` and `source_purchase_id` identifies the document that caused it — a `REVERSAL` carries the same source reference as the entry it compensates, plus `reversal_of_entry_id` naming that entry, which is the only self-referential link in the system. `adjustment_reason` is populated on goodwill entries and null on everything the server produced by itself. Every entry also snapshots `liability_unit_cost_cents_per_1000`, which is always 600. Entries are immutable and are never created by a direct client request other than a goodwill adjustment, so the single read and the filtered list are the only way to discover them.

**Valuing the liability.** The programme fund is a singleton that exists from server start with `cash_balance_cents` 0, `outstanding_points` 0, `breakage_rate_pct` 0.20, `point_liability_cents` 0, `solvency_ratio` 999.0, `status` `HEALTHY` and `last_revalued_at` null. Its `outstanding_points` is the signed sum of `points` across **every** ledger entry of every member, with no status filter and no exclusion list — reversals already carry negative amounts, so nothing needs to be excluded and the sum is insensitive to order. The `point_liability_cents` is that outstanding total, valued at 600 cents per thousand points, multiplied by one minus `breakage_rate_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. The `solvency_ratio` is `cash_balance_cents` divided by `point_liability_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is pinned to exactly 999.0000 when the liability is zero rather than dividing by zero — so a programme with an empty ledger reads `HEALTHY`, not impaired. The posture then follows: `HEALTHY` at a ratio of 1.0000 or above, `WATCH` from 0.7500 up to but not including 1.0000, and `IMPAIRED` below 0.7500. That whole derivation re-runs from scratch at the end of every request that writes a ledger entry or moves programme cash, so the fund is never stale, and it can be re-derived on demand by an administrator or the `ProgramAuditor`, which also sets `last_revalued_at` to the clock's current instant and increments the fund's `version`. An administrator may additionally restate the assumption by supplying a new `breakage_rate_pct` between 0.0 and 0.35 inclusive; that overwrites the field, immediately re-runs the valuation and posture derivation, and increments the `version` — but it does **not** touch `last_revalued_at`, which records only an explicit revaluation.

**The two conserved quantities.** These are the identities that make the whole ledger auditable, and they must hold after every request. First, points: each member's `point_balance` equals the sum of `points` over that member's ledger entries — exactly, with no rounding anywhere in the comparison, which is precisely why the floors inside the earning and pricing formulas are applied once at award time and never re-derived when a reversal is written. Scoped to a single document, the identity is what proves a compensation was complete: after a posted stay is voided, the sum of `points` over the entries naming that stay is zero; after an award booking is cancelled, the sum over the entries naming that booking is zero; after a purchase is refunded, the sum over the entries naming that purchase is zero. Second, cash: the fund's `cash_balance_cents` equals the sum of `cash_due_cents` over purchases in status `SETTLED`, less the sum of `net_payable_cents` over reimbursement batches in status `PAID`. Pending and refunded purchases are excluded, which is why a refund needs no separate cash-reversal term to re-balance the identity — the purchase simply leaves the settled set. Every batch status other than `PAID` is excluded. And because `net_payable_cents` is signed, paying a batch with a negative net payable increases programme cash through the same subtraction.

### 3.9. Member Complaints, the Review Loop, and Programme Adjudication

When a property denies a benefit it owed, shorts a member's points, or blacks out a date the member believes should have been available, the member's recourse is a complaint. The complaint lifecycle is a bounded review loop with two exits, one of which leads to a second, independent adjudication of the underlying benefit denial.

**Filing.** A `Member`, a `MemberCareAgent` or a `ProgramAdministrator` files a complaint with `member_id`, a `subject_type` of `BENEFIT_DENIAL`, `POINT_SHORTFALL` or `BLACKOUT_DISPUTE`, a `description` between 1 and 2,000 characters, and optionally a `related_delivery_id`. When the subject is a benefit denial the `related_delivery_id` must be supplied and must name an elite benefit obligation whose originating stay belongs to the complaining member, else `ERR_DELIVERY_MEMBER_MISMATCH`; for the other two subjects the field stays null. The complaint opens in `OPEN` with `info_request_count` and `goodwill_points_granted` both 0, and `resolution` and `adjudication_outcome` both null.

**The review loop.** A `MemberCareAgent` or an administrator takes the complaint under review, moving it `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW`. From there a `MemberCareAgent` — and only a `MemberCareAgent`, since the administrator adjudicates escalations rather than running the care queue — may send it back to the member for more information, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and incrementing `info_request_count`. The member, or an agent acting for them, returns the information and the complaint moves `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, optionally overwriting `description` when a new narrative is supplied. That cycle is what bounds the loop: `info_request_count` is **never** decremented on the way back, so it accumulates. A request for information is permitted only while `info_request_count` is below 2, else `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT` — the second trip to `INFO_REQUESTED` is reachable and a third is refused, leaving the complaint stuck in `UNDER_REVIEW` until it is resolved. Returning information is subject to the member's own ownership check: a `Member` whose `X-User-ID` is not the complaint's `member_id` is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

**Resolving.** A `MemberCareAgent` or an administrator closes the review out with a mandatory `resolution` narrative and an optional `goodwill_points` figure, and that figure alone decides which of two sibling branches is taken. When `goodwill_points` is greater than zero the complaint moves `UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_GOODWILL`: `resolution` is recorded, `goodwill_points_granted` is set to the requested figure, one `GOODWILL` ledger entry is written crediting the complaining member with that many points, the member's `point_balance` and `lifetime_points_earned` are recomputed, and the fund's `outstanding_points`, `point_liability_cents`, `solvency_ratio` and `status` are recomputed. Because that arm grants points, it is blocked with `ERR_FUND_IMPAIRED` while the fund's `status` is `IMPAIRED`. When `goodwill_points` is omitted or zero the complaint moves `UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_UPHELD` instead: `resolution` is recorded, `goodwill_points_granted` stays 0, **no** ledger entry is written, the member's balance does not move, the fund is not revalued, and the fiscal gate does not apply — an upheld resolution succeeds even while the fund is impaired. `RESOLVED_GOODWILL` is terminal and admits nothing further; a member who received goodwill has no escalation path.

**Escalating.** `RESOLVED_UPHELD` is the only status from which escalation is possible, and it belongs to the `Member` — a `MemberCareAgent` may also file it on the member's behalf, but a `Member` acting for another member's complaint is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The complaint moves `RESOLVED_UPHELD → ESCALATED`. When the complaint's `subject_type` is `BENEFIT_DENIAL`, the obligation named by `related_delivery_id` must still be in `DENIED`, else `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_DENIED` — a property that has since honoured the benefit leaves nothing to argue about — and on success that obligation advances `DENIED → ESCALATED` and the property's `benefit_denial_count` is recomputed, which leaves the figure unchanged because `ESCALATED` remains inside the counted set. A `POINT_SHORTFALL` or `BLACKOUT_DISPUTE` complaint escalates without touching any obligation.

**Adjudicating.** Adjudication is exclusively the `ProgramAdministrator`'s, and it moves the complaint `ESCALATED → CLOSED` while recording an `adjudication_outcome` of either `DELIVERY_ORDERED` or `DENIAL_UPHELD`. When the complaint's `subject_type` is `BENEFIT_DENIAL`, the named obligation must still be in `ESCALATED`, else `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_ESCALATED` — the concrete way that check fires is a stay voided between escalation and adjudication, which waives the obligation out from under the escalation as described in §3.4. On `DELIVERY_ORDERED` the obligation advances `ESCALATED → DELIVERED`, the cost recovery of §3.5 is computed for its `benefit_type`, `cost_recovery_cents` is set, and both the property's `benefit_recovery_owed_cents` and its `benefit_denial_count` are recomputed — the property is paid the recovery it would have earned by honouring the benefit in the first place, and its denial count falls because the obligation has left the counted set. On `DENIAL_UPHELD` the obligation advances `ESCALATED → DENIED_FINAL`, `cost_recovery_cents` stays 0, no recovery is credited, and `benefit_denial_count` is recomputed with the obligation still counted.

Adjudication moves **no** points under any outcome. `goodwill_points_granted` is unchanged by adjudication even when the member wins, because goodwill is granted only by resolving a complaint or by an explicit point adjustment; a member whose escalation succeeds gets the benefit ordered delivered at the property's expense, not a point credit. `CLOSED` is terminal.

Every one of the six complaint actions is gated on the complaint's own status, and any mismatch is `ERR_COMPLAINT_INVALID_STATE`: review requires `OPEN`; request-info and resolve require `UNDER_REVIEW`; provide-info requires `INFO_REQUESTED`; escalate requires `RESOLVED_UPHELD`; adjudicate requires `ESCALATED`; and the two terminal statuses `RESOLVED_GOODWILL` and `CLOSED` admit nothing at all.
