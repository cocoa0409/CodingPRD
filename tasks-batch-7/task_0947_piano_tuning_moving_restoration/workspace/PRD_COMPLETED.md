# Piano Tuning, Moving & Restoration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A full-service piano shop is three businesses wearing one coat. There is a *field* business, in which technicians drive routes and tune instruments on a maintenance cycle that depends on where the piano lives and how hard it is played. There is a *bench* business, in which a grand is stripped, restrung, regulated and voiced over months, billed in stages against a deposit taken up front. And there is a *logistics and showroom* business, in which the same instrument is crated, craned out of a third-floor walk-up, held in the shop, put on the floor under a consignment agreement, and eventually sold to somebody who is not the person who brought it in. Each of these businesses has its own vocabulary and its own money, and the shop currently reconciles them by hand.

This document specifies the API that replaces that reconciliation. The system tracks clients and their addresses, the instruments at those addresses, the humidity those instruments live in, the tuning-cycle recalls that fall due, the quotes that are priced from all of the above, the service orders those quotes become, the milestones those orders bill against, the technicians and route days that execute them, the specialized moves that relocate the instruments, the warranties that follow the work, the claims filed against those warranties, the consignment agreements that sell the instruments, and the parts drawn from stock to rebuild them. Every money movement lands in one append-only client ledger, and every quantity that can be conserved — parts units, route minutes, ledger cents, sale proceeds, contracted value, deposit disposition — is asserted as an invariant rather than left to accumulate drift.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is **derivability**: prices, durations, intervals, terms and splits are computed by the server from stated inputs and stated constants, never supplied by the caller, so that two operators pricing the same instrument on the same day cannot disagree. The second is **an auditable order of events**: the server evaluates a single ordered validation ladder before it commits, it posts ledger rows in a fixed precedence within one request, and where a rule deliberately commits work *before* rejecting a request, this document says so explicitly rather than leaving the caller to discover it. The system is not a wall-clock system: it runs on a logical clock that an administrator sets, so that seasonal humidity histories, 1095-day rebuild warranties and 180-day consignment terms can be exercised deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Identity

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. There are exactly six roles, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `ShopManager`, `Technician`, `Client`, `MoveCoordinator`, and `ShowroomAgent`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (HTTP 401), as is a request that omits `X-User-ID` when the acting role is one of the two entity-backed roles (`Technician` and `Client`), and as is a request that omits `X-User-ID` on any of the four segregation-of-duties operations — releasing a milestone, adjudicating a warranty claim, creating a consignment agreement, and recording a consignment sale — regardless of which role is acting. A role name the matrix does not declare is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401). A declared role that is not among the roles the target operation authorises is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

`PlatformAdmin` is an operator identity with the full surface of the API, plus two capabilities no other role holds: setting the logical clock, and running the daily operations sweep. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `released_by_user_id` on a `Milestone`, as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `WarrantyClaim`, or as `created_by_user_id` on a `ConsignmentAgreement` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same opaque treatment applies to `ShopManager`, who runs the shop (quotes, service orders, milestone releases, warranty voids and reviews and adjudications, parts stock, route days, client records and credit adjustments); to `MoveCoordinator`, who owns physical logistics (move jobs and instrument custody, plus address creation and maintenance); and to `ShowroomAgent`, who runs the showroom and consignment desk.

The two remaining roles are entity-backed and their identity is checked. When `X-User-Role` is `Technician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician` row; ownership checks compare it to `RouteDay.technician_id` and to `ServiceOrder.assigned_technician_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Client`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client` row; ownership checks compare it to the addressed resource's `client_id`, and on a consignment agreement to `consignor_client_id` or `buyer_client_id`. An entity-backed actor that addresses a row it does not own is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This check runs on every operation whose authorised role list includes `Technician` or `Client`. On a single-row read or action the compared id is taken from the addressed row before its existence is reported, so an actor that names a row belonging to someone else gets the 403 while one that names a row that does not exist at all gets the 404 of rungs 9 through 14. On a **list** operation the check has two halves, and only the first of them is an error: an ownership filter — `client_id`, `consignor_client_id`, `buyer_client_id`, `technician_id`, `assigned_technician_id` — naming somebody other than the actor is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, while an actor that supplies no such filter is not rejected but silently narrowed. A `Client` actor's page of locations, instruments, quotes, service orders, appointments, move jobs, warranties, claims, agreements or ledger entries contains only its own rows, and `total_count` counts only those rows. The narrowing is a `Client` privilege only: a `Technician` that filters on another technician is refused, but a `Technician` that filters on nothing sees every row of the collection.

Beyond the role list, four operations enforce **segregation of duties** on the operator identity itself. The `X-User-ID` adjudicating a warranty claim must differ from every `released_by_user_id` recorded on the milestones of the covered service order, and the `X-User-ID` recording a consignment sale must differ from the `created_by_user_id` stamped on that agreement when it was drafted. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The other two of the four are the operations that *write* those identity fields — milestone release and agreement creation — which is why they demand `X-User-ID` from every role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list operation accepts `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific operation says otherwise. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`; `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` (same format, refreshed on every mutation) and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. Two entities are the exception: a `ClimateReading` and a `LedgerEntry` carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version`, because both are append-only records of something that happened and are never edited after the fact.

Three kinds of instant appear in this system and they are rendered by three different rules, which is worth pinning once because the difference is visible on the wire. **Identity stamps** — `created_at`, `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` — are minted by the server in one canonical rendering, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ` with exactly three fractional digits, and they are read from the **host's** clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so they advance with real time no matter where an administrator has put the logical clock and they are strictly increasing across rows written within one request. **Caller-supplied instants** are echoed back byte for byte exactly as they arrived, never re-rendered: `recorded_at_utc`, `scheduled_start_utc`, `scheduled_window_start_utc`, `scheduled_window_end_utc`, and the clock's own `current_utc`. **Clock-derived stamps** — `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `notified_at`, `released_at`, `accepted_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at` and `posted_at_utc` — are read off the logical clock and rendered in the *style of the string the administrator used to set it*, matching that string's fractional-digit count and its UTC suffix; before any administrator has set the clock the style is that of the default `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, meaning no fractional digits at all. A clock set with three fractional digits therefore produces three-digit business stamps, and one set with none produces none. Date-valued fields — `start_date`, `end_date`, `due_date`, `service_date`, `cycle_start_date` and `last_tuning_date` — are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings throughout, with `service_date`, `cycle_start_date` and a supplied `last_tuning_date` stored as the caller wrote them. Callers must not supply `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` on create; the server mints them. Where this document says a cascade "bumps the version" of a related row, it means the same thing: that row's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed, exactly as if the row had been written directly.

Nested paths are validated left to right. When an operation addresses a child resource beneath a parent — the readings of a location, the recalls of an instrument, the milestones or parts allocations of a service order — the parent id is resolved first and its non-existence is reported before the child id is even examined.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a **logical clock**, and every temporal decision in this specification reads it rather than the host's wall clock. Before any administrator sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrator sets it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, which must parse as an ISO-8601 instant or the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; the clock may be set backwards exactly as freely as forwards, and no operation compares the new instant to the old one. The response's `data` is not a resource but a two-key object: `current_utc`, which echoes the administrator's `to_iso8601` string **byte for byte** rather than re-rendering it, and `locations_reevaluated`, the count of addresses whose climate evaluation was re-run as a consequence. Setting the clock re-runs the out-of-band climate evaluation for every location that holds at least one reading — every such location is counted whether or not the re-run changed any of its three climate fields, and the re-run refreshes those fields **without** bumping the location's `version` or refreshing its `updated_at`.

The clock is what makes "expired", "overdue", "elapsed" and "lead time" mean something reproducible. Quote expiry, consignment expiry, warranty expiry, recall overdue tiers, appointment lead time, reschedule and cancellation fee tiers, route-day lock lead, warranty elapsed fractions and prorated transfer terms are all measured against it.

### 2.6. Money, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of **cents**, and every field that holds money is named with a `_cents` suffix. Wherever a computation produces a fractional cent, it is rounded **half up to a whole cent**, and this rounding happens at the exact points this document pins — never earlier and never later, because the same arithmetic rounded at a different point produces a different answer. The most consequential instance is quote pricing, where each priced line is rounded *after* its own discount and the rounded line amounts are then summed; summing the undiscounted lines first and rounding the discounted total once is wrong and can differ by a cent.

Durations are whole minutes and are likewise rounded half up where a multiplier produces a fraction. Day arithmetic is calendar arithmetic: adding a term in days to a date honours month lengths and leap days, and differences between dates are whole days. Where a ratio is compared to a threshold, this document states the comparison direction and whether the boundary is inclusive; a threshold written as "at least" includes the boundary and one written as "strictly greater" does not.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Five write operations reserve capacity, price, or entitlement out of a parent row that other writers can move underneath them. Each of those five requires the client's last observed `version` of that parent as a non-optional input field, and rejects the write when it no longer matches. Operations not named below take no version field at all. The comparison is a plain equality against the integer the row currently holds, with no type coercion and no range check of its own: a value of the wrong JSON type — the string `"1"` where the integer `1` was meant — simply fails to match and answers the 412 of its own rung rather than a 400 from rung 5 or 7.

Drafting a quote requires `expected_instrument_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the instrument being quoted; if the instrument's current `version` differs, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSTRUMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A quote's prices are derived from the instrument's class, its location's zone and its effective pitch tier, so a writer holding a stale view would mint a quote priced from facts the instrument no longer has — and a newly minted quote legitimately supersedes the writer's intent for the same instrument, which is precisely why the stale read must be caught rather than allowed to succeed. The re-issue operation, which mints a replacement quote, carries the same field and the same rejection.

Drafting a consignment agreement likewise requires `expected_instrument_version` and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSTRUMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) on a mismatch. A new agreement legitimately supersedes a prior withdrawn or expired one, so a second writer's create would otherwise succeed against identical-looking state; the stale version is what distinguishes an informed re-consignment from a blind one.

Booking an appointment requires `expected_route_day_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target route day, and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_DAY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the writer's capacity arithmetic was performed against a `committed_minutes` figure the day no longer reports. Rescheduling an appointment onto a route day carries the same field against the *target* day, with the same rejection.

Reserving parts against a rebuild requires `expected_parts_lot_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the lot, and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_PARTS_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A partial allocation is a legitimate outcome here, so without the check a second writer's request would quietly succeed with a smaller `allocated_qty` than it believed it was asking for.

Filing a warranty claim requires `expected_warranty_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the warranty, and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_WARRANTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second claim is legitimate up to the claim cap, and the provisional credit is computed from `term_days` and `start_date`, both of which a transfer or a prior claim can have moved under the writer.

Note the interaction that follows from all of this: drafting a quote, re-issuing a quote, and drafting a consignment agreement each bump the instrument's own `version` by 1. A caller that performs two such writes in a row must re-read the instrument between them.

### 2.8. The Request Validation Ladder and Error Precedence

The server evaluates every request against one ordered ladder and returns the **first** error it reaches; later rungs are never evaluated once an earlier one has failed. No state change is committed before the ladder completes, with exactly three deliberate exceptions described in §2.9. The ladder is stated here once, in ascending priority order, so that error precedence is derivable for any pair of failures on any operation. Where a rung names the operations it guards, that list is the complete set of operations the rung applies to.

1. **Authentication headers.** `X-User-Role` must be present and non-empty; `X-User-ID` must be present for the entity-backed roles `Technician` and `Client`, and for **any** role on the four segregation-of-duties operations `POST /milestones/{id}/release`, `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/adjudicate`, `POST /consignment-agreements` and `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/record-sale`. Otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (401). Applies to every operation.
2. **Known role.** `X-User-Role` must name a role declared in the matrix. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (401). Applies to every operation.
3. **Role authorisation.** The role must be listed among the operation's authorised roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403). Applies to every operation.
4. **Actor scope.** An entity-backed actor may only touch its own rows, as described in §2.1. Otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every operation whose authorised roles include `Client` or `Technician`.
5. **Body shape.** The request body must parse as a JSON object and every required input field of the operation must be present and non-null. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to all `POST`, `PUT` and `PATCH` operations.
6. **Enum membership.** Every enum-typed input field must carry a declared member of that enum. This routing list is exhaustive and closed: `client_type`, `home_zone_code`, `zone_code`, `instrument_class`, `usage_tier`, `service_type`, `to_condition_status`, `to_custody_status`, `to_status`, `certification_level`, `part_type`, `decision`, `void_reason`, status filters and `overdue_tier` filters route here, and **no other field does**. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). A status filter's accepted member set is the *whole* declared value set of the axis it names, including values that no row ever comes to rest in — `REQUESTED` and `RESCHEDULED` on an appointment's `scheduling_status`, and `REJECTED` on a milestone's `status`, all three of which are waypoints this system never persists. A filter naming one of them is accepted at this rung and simply matches nothing.
7. **Numeric and temporal ranges.** `stair_flights` must be an integer from 0 through 8; `relative_humidity_pct` from 0.0 through 100.0; `temperature_f` from -20.0 through 140.0; `measured_deviation_cents` from -200.0 through 200.0; `reference_pitch_hz` and `target_pitch_hz` from 415.0 through 445.0; `distance_miles` strictly greater than 0; `requested_qty` from 1 through 50; `initial_qty` from 1 through 1000; `capacity_minutes` and `daily_capacity_minutes` from 60 through 960; `reserve_price_cents` at least 50000; `sale_price_cents` strictly greater than 0; and `scheduled_window_end_utc` strictly after `scheduled_window_start_utc`. Otherwise `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (400).
8. **Pagination parameters.** As stated in §2.3. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to all list operations.
9. **Client existence.** Every path-templated id resolves, validated left to right; a `Client` id resolves here. A `client_id`, `consignor_client_id` or `buyer_client_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
10. **Location existence.** A `location_id`, `origin_location_id`, `destination_location_id` or `current_location_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_LOCATION_NOT_FOUND` (404).
11. **Instrument existence.** An `instrument_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_INSTRUMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
12. **Quote existence.** A `quote_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to the quote paths and to service-order creation.
13. **Service-order existence.** A `service_order_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_SERVICE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` (404).
14. **Everything else.** Any other referenced id that does not resolve — `Technician`, `RouteDay`, `Appointment`, `TuningRecall`, `Milestone`, `MoveJob`, `Warranty`, `WarrantyClaim`, `ConsignmentAgreement`, `PartsLot`, `PartsAllocation`, `LedgerEntry` — yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
15. **Serial uniqueness.** `serial_number` must be unique across all instruments. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER` (409). Applies to instrument creation.
16. **Lot-code uniqueness.** `lot_code` must be unique across all lots. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_CODE` (409). Applies to parts-lot creation.
17. **Address ownership.** On every operation that pairs a location with an instrument, the location's owning client must equal the instrument's owning client; additionally, an appointment's `location_id` must equal the instrument's `current_location_id`. Otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to `POST /instruments`, `POST /appointments`, `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule` and `POST /move-jobs`.
18. **Consignment ownership.** `consignor_client_id` must equal the instrument's `client_id` at agreement creation, and at sale `buyer_client_id` must differ from `consignor_client_id`. Otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to `POST /consignment-agreements` and `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/record-sale`.
19. **Client standing.** The acting or owning client's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to exactly eight operations: `POST /quotes`, `POST /quotes/{id}/issue`, `POST /quotes/{id}/approve`, `POST /appointments`, `POST /service-orders`, `POST /service-orders/{id}/resume`, `POST /warranty-claims` and `POST /tuning-recalls/{id}/notify`.
20. **Technician standing.** The technician's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /route-days`, `POST /appointments` and `POST /service-orders`.
21. **Not already terminal.** The target resource must not already sit in a terminal state of its own machine. Otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to every action operation that drives a state machine.
22. **Custody gate.** The instrument's `custody_status` must satisfy the transition's precondition: `IN_SHOP` is required to activate a consignment and to list to the showroom; `IN_TRANSIT` is required to reach `IN_SHOP` or `AT_CLIENT_SITE`; `SOLD` accepts no further custody transition. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /instruments/{id}/custody-transitions`, `POST /instruments/{id}/condition-transitions`, `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/activate` and `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/list-showroom`. The rebuild-start custody gate is **not** checked here; it is rung 37.
23. **Edge exists.** The requested transition must exist as an edge from the resource's current state in its own machine. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action operation that drives a state machine.
24. **Cross-entity gate, first pair.** A milestone may only be released while its service order is `IN_PROGRESS`, and a parts allocation may only be consumed while its service order is `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /milestones/{id}/release` and `POST /parts-allocations/{id}/consume`.
25. **Cross-entity gate, second pair.** An appointment may only be confirmed while its route day is `OPEN` or `LOCKED` — a dispatched day no longer accepts confirmations — and a milestone may only be accepted while its service order is `IN_PROGRESS`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /appointments/{id}/confirm` and `POST /milestones/{id}/accept`.
26. **Completion gate.** A service order may only be completed when every one of its milestones has status `ACCEPTED`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /service-orders/{id}/complete`.
27. **Realisation gate.** A quote may only realise a service order while its status is `APPROVED`, and at most one service order may exist per quote. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /service-orders`.
28. **Instrument version.** `expected_instrument_version` must equal the instrument's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_INSTRUMENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /quotes`, `POST /quotes/{id}/reissue` and `POST /consignment-agreements`.
29. **Route-day version.** `expected_route_day_version` must equal the route day's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_DAY_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
30. **Parts-lot version.** `expected_parts_lot_version` must equal the lot's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARTS_LOT_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /service-orders/{id}/parts-allocations`.
31. **Warranty version.** `expected_warranty_version` must equal the warranty's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_WARRANTY_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /warranty-claims`.
32. **Pitch reference.** `reference_pitch_hz` must equal the instrument's `target_pitch_hz` exactly. Otherwise `ERR_PITCH_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /instruments/{id}/pitch-assessments`.
33. **Service ladder prerequisite.** As detailed in §3.5. Otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to `POST /quotes` and `POST /quotes/{id}/reissue`.
34. **Technician certification.** The technician's certification rank must meet the service type's minimum rank. Otherwise `ERR_TECH_CERT_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /service-orders`, `POST /appointments` and `POST /route-days/{id}/status-transitions`.
35. **Move crew certification.** A move of a `CONCERT_GRAND`, or any move with `crane_required` true, requires the crew lead's `move_certified` to be true. Otherwise `ERR_MOVE_CREW_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422). Applies to `POST /move-jobs/{id}/status-transitions`.
36. **Crane requirement.** A `CONCERT_GRAND` move whose total effective stair flights is at least 2 must set `crane_required` true. Otherwise `ERR_CRANE_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /move-jobs`.
37. **Rebuild custody.** A `REBUILD` service order may only be started while its instrument's `custody_status` is `IN_SHOP`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /service-orders/{id}/start`.
38. **Adjudication segregation.** The adjudicating `X-User-ID` must differ from every `released_by_user_id` recorded on the covered order's milestones. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /warranty-claims/{id}/adjudicate`.
39. **Sale segregation.** The recording `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/record-sale`.
40. **Authorising move job.** When `authorizing_move_job_id` is supplied it must name a move job for the same instrument whose status is `DISPATCHED` (for a transition into `IN_TRANSIT`) or `IN_TRANSIT` (for a transition into `IN_SHOP` or `AT_CLIENT_SITE`). Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /instruments/{id}/custody-transitions`.
41. **Quote validity.** The logical clock must be strictly before the quote's `expires_at`. Otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /quotes/{id}/approve` and `POST /service-orders`.
42. **Consignment validity.** The logical clock must be strictly before the agreement's `expires_at`. Otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/list-showroom` and `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/record-sale`.
43. **Appointment lead time.** `scheduled_start_utc` must be at least 120 minutes after the logical clock. Otherwise `ERR_APPOINTMENT_LEAD_TIME` (422). Applies to `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
44. **Climate gate (mutate-then-reject).** The instrument's current location must not carry `out_of_band_flag` true when a warranty claim is filed. Otherwise `ERR_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND` (422). Applies to `POST /warranty-claims`. See §2.9.
45. **Appointment date.** The date part of `scheduled_start_utc` must equal the route day's `service_date`. Otherwise `ERR_APPOINTMENT_DATE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
46. **Pitch-tier conflict (mutate-then-reject).** A pitch assessment must not change the effective pitch tier of an instrument that already has a `TUNE` service order in status `IN_PROGRESS`. Otherwise `ERR_PITCH_TIER_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /instruments/{id}/pitch-assessments`. See §2.9.
47. **Lock lead time.** A route day may only be locked once the logical clock has reached 12 hours before midnight UTC at the start of its `service_date` — that is, the clock must be at or after that date's `00:00Z` minus 12 hours. Otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_DAY_LOCK_LEAD` (409). Applies to `POST /route-days/{id}/status-transitions`.
48. **Technician zone.** Either the appointment location's `zone_code` must be among the technician's `zone_codes`, or the owning client's `home_zone_code` must be among them — an out-of-zone visit for an in-zone client is permitted. Otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_ZONE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
49. **Stop overlap.** The new stop occupies the half-open interval that begins at `scheduled_start_utc` and ends `total_block_minutes` later, and must not intersect the half-open interval of any existing `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` stop on the **same** route day. Touching endpoints — one interval ending exactly where the next begins — are allowed. Otherwise `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
50. **Instrument day-booking.** An instrument may hold at most one `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` appointment per route-day `service_date`, across all technicians. Two stops on the same calendar date collide even when their minute intervals are disjoint. Otherwise `ERR_INSTRUMENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409). Applies to `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
51. **Move-window conflict.** A new move window, running from `scheduled_window_start_utc` to `scheduled_window_end_utc` and widened by 90 minutes on **both** sides, must not intersect the *unwidened* window of any move job for the same instrument whose status is `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_TRANSIT`. The interval is closed, so touching endpoints **do** collide. Otherwise `ERR_MOVE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /move-jobs`.
52. **Dispatch certification (mutate-then-reject).** On dispatch, every appointment being flipped to `EN_ROUTE` must pass the certification gate for its own `service_type`. Otherwise `ERR_TECH_CERT_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /route-days/{id}/status-transitions` with a target status of `DISPATCHED`. See §2.9.
53. **One issued quote.** At most one quote per instrument-and-service-type pair may hold status `ISSUED`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_QUOTE` (409). Applies to `POST /quotes/{id}/issue`.
54. **Milestone sequence.** A milestone may only be released when every milestone of the same order with a lower `sequence_index` has status `ACCEPTED`. Otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE` (409). Applies to `POST /milestones/{id}/release`.
55. **Capped retries.** `reschedule_count` must be below 2, `rejection_count` must be below 2, and `claim_count` must be below 2. Otherwise `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`, `POST /milestones/{id}/reject` and `POST /warranty-claims` respectively.
56. **Parts sufficiency.** The lot's `on_hand_qty` must be at least the requested quantity multiplied by 0.50 and rounded up to a whole unit; below that the request is refused rather than partially filled. Otherwise `ERR_PARTS_INSUFFICIENT` (409). Applies to `POST /service-orders/{id}/parts-allocations`.
57. **Route capacity.** The route day's `committed_minutes` plus the new stop's `total_block_minutes` must be at most the day's `capacity_minutes`. Otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
58. **Sale floor.** `sale_price_cents` must be at least the reserve price multiplied by 0.80 and rounded half up to whole cents. Otherwise `ERR_SALE_BELOW_FLOOR` (422). Applies to `POST /consignment-agreements/{id}/record-sale`.
59. **Deposit settled.** Releasing the milestone at `sequence_index` 1 requires the order's deposit to be settled, meaning the client's `account_credit_cents` is at least 0. Otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_SETTLED` (402). Applies to `POST /milestones/{id}/release`.
60. **Credit sufficiency.** Accepting a milestone requires the client's `account_credit_cents` plus the order's unapplied deposit — `deposit_held_cents` less `deposit_applied_cents`, less `deposit_refunded_cents`, less `deposit_forfeited_cents` — to be at least the milestone's `amount_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT` (402). Applies to `POST /milestones/{id}/accept`.

**Which of the two certification rungs governs a dispatch.** Rung 34 and rung 52 both raise `ERR_TECH_CERT_INSUFFICIENT` on a route-day dispatch, but rung 34 is an ordinary pre-commit check while rung 52 is a mutate-then-reject check performed per appointment as each one is flipped. On a dispatch whose route-day technician is under-certified for a service type that also appears on one of the day's stops, **rung 52 governs**. A dispatch runs no separate day-level certification check ahead of the walk: the technician's certification is examined stop by stop, against each stop's own `service_type`, as each appointment is flipped — so the appointments ordered before the offending one are already committed when the rejection lands, exactly as §2.9 describes. Rung 34's naming of `POST /route-days/{id}/status-transitions` describes that same gate on that operation and adds nothing ahead of it.

**The preconditions the ladder does not number.** Sixty numbered rungs do not exhaust what the server checks. A handful of operation-local preconditions, each described in its own §3 narrative, are enforced without a rung number of their own; every one of them answers an error already in the catalogue, and each sits at the position given here. Two of them are *format* checks that occupy rung 5's slot — before any id is resolved — and answer `ERR_VALIDATION`: `scheduled_start_utc` on `POST /appointments` and `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule`, and both window instants on `POST /move-jobs`, must parse as ISO-8601 instants; so must `to_iso8601` on `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. `email` must contain an at-sign on `POST /clients` and `PATCH /clients/{id}`, and `amount_cents` must be a JSON integer on `POST /clients/{id}/credit-adjustments`; both answer `ERR_VALIDATION` at that same slot. Two further format checks sit *later* than rung 5, after their parent has already been resolved, and are observable for it: `service_date` on `POST /route-days` is checked for ISO-date form only after the technician has resolved at rung 14, and `cycle_start_date` on `POST /tuning-recalls` only after the instrument has resolved at rung 11 — so a malformed date paired with an unknown parent answers 404, not 400. The remainder are *state* preconditions answering `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Between rungs 23 and 40, moving an instrument `IN_SHOP → IN_TRANSIT` requires that no service order of that instrument be `IN_PROGRESS`. After rung 23, driving the condition axis into `SERVICEABLE` requires `pitch_deviation_cents` to be non-null. Between rungs 23 and 37, `POST /service-orders/{id}/start` requires `assigned_technician_id` to have been set. Between rungs 23 and 35, `POST /move-jobs/{id}/status-transitions` into `SCHEDULED` requires `move_technician_id` to be set; and into `DISPATCHED` it requires the instrument's `custody_status` to be `AT_CLIENT_SITE`, `IN_SHOP` or `SHOWROOM_FLOOR` — a gate about custody that nonetheless answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and **not** `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`, which that operation never raises. Between rungs 20 and 29, `POST /appointments` requires the target route day's `status` to be `OPEN`; between rungs 23 and 29, `POST /appointments/{id}/reschedule` requires the *stop's* `fulfillment_status` to be `PENDING` and then the *target* day's `status` to be `OPEN`. After rung 23, `POST /appointments/{id}/confirm` requires `fulfillment_status` to be `PENDING` — ahead of rung 25's route-day check — and `POST /appointments/{id}/cancel` requires it to be `PENDING`, `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS`. A dispatch requires the day's `stop_count` to be at least 1, checked after rung 47's slot and before the rung-52 walk begins. And closing a client whose orders are still in flight, as §3.1 describes, answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` too.

**Two operations rung 21 does not reach, and the enum members no edge can reach.** Rung 21's "every action operation that drives a state machine" has two stated exceptions. `POST /quotes/{id}/reissue` applies the terminal gate over `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` and `SUPERSEDED` only, deliberately leaving `EXPIRED` out so that §3.5's `EXPIRED → SUPERSEDED` edge stays reachable; a `DRAFT` quote falls through to rung 23 and answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. And `POST /warranty-claims` applies no terminal gate at all: a claim may be filed against a warranty in **any** status, `VOIDED` and `EXPIRED` included, so long as rungs 31, 44 and 55 are satisfied. Separately, rung 6 admits several enum members that no edge on the addressed operation can consume, and those requests fall to rung 23's `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than to rung 6: `SHOWROOM_FLOOR` and `SOLD` as a `to_custody_status`, both of which are written only by the showroom operations of §3.10 and never requested directly; and `NEEDS_VOICING` and `RESTORED` as a `to_condition_status`, both of which are system-driven outcomes of order completion.

### 2.9. Three Deliberate Mutate-Then-Reject Points

Three rungs commit work before they reject, and none of that work is rolled back. This is intentional, and each case states exactly what survives the rejection.

At rung 44, filing a warranty claim against an instrument whose current location carries `out_of_band_flag` true: by the time the check runs, the parent warranty has **already** been moved from `ACTIVE` to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND`, its `version` incremented by 1, and the denial cascade described in §2.11 has already run against that warranty's earlier claims. The response is `ERR_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND` (422) and the new `WarrantyClaim` row is **not** created; neither is `claim_count` incremented, because rung 44 stands ahead of the counter's own rung 55. A subsequent read of the warranty observes status `VOIDED`, the `CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND` reason, and a `version` one higher than before the rejected call. The void itself is conditional in a way the rejection is not: because a claim may be filed against a warranty in any status, the gate voids the parent only when it is standing in `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFERRED`, and simply leaves an already-`VOIDED` or already-`EXPIRED` warranty untouched at its existing `version` — but it rejects the filing with `ERR_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND` either way.

At rung 46, recording a pitch assessment that would change the effective tier of an instrument with an in-flight `TUNE` order: `pitch_deviation_cents`, `raw_pitch_tier`, `pitch_raise_tier`, `planned_visit_count`, `updated_at` and `version` have **already** been written from the classification calculation. The response is `ERR_PITCH_TIER_CONFLICT` (409) and those pitch fields are **not** rolled back. The measurement is a fact of record; the conflict blocks the acknowledgement, not the reading. "Would change the effective tier" is decided by comparing the newly computed `pitch_raise_tier` against whatever the field held before the write, and a freshly registered instrument holds null there — so the very **first** assessment on an instrument always counts as a change, and rung 46 can fire on it.

At rung 52, dispatching a route day: appointments are flipped from `PENDING` to `EN_ROUTE` in `created_at` ascending order, and every appointment ordered before the offending one has **already** been flipped with its `version` incremented. The response is `ERR_TECH_CERT_INSUFFICIENT` (422) at the first failing appointment. The earlier flips are **not** rolled back — the day is partially dispatched — and the route day itself remains `LOCKED` with its `version` unchanged. Re-issuing the dispatch after fixing the certification flips only the appointments still sitting in `PENDING`.

### 2.10. Six Conservation Invariants

Six quantities in this system are conserved, and each holds after every operation that touches it, not merely at rest.

**Parts units.** For every lot, `on_hand_qty` plus `allocated_qty` plus `consumed_qty` equals `initial_qty`. The forward action is reserving units against a rebuild; the compensating action is releasing an allocation. The identity holds after every allocation, every release, every consumption, and after the release cascade fired by a service-order cancellation.

**Route minutes.** For every route day, `committed_minutes` equals the sum of `total_block_minutes` over the appointments assigned to that day whose `scheduling_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED`. The forward action is booking a stop; the compensating action is cancelling one. This figure is recomputed from scratch after every appointment creation, reschedule, cancellation and cascade cancellation — never patched incrementally. The recomputation always rewrites `committed_minutes` and `stop_count` and always bumps the day's `version` by 1, even on a pass that leaves both figures exactly where they were.

**Client money.** For every client, `account_credit_cents` equals the plain sum of `amount_cents` over **every** ledger row carrying that client's `client_id`, with no status filter of any kind. The balance is recomputed from that sum after every request that appends a row, and it is also recomputed — without any write — on `GET /clients/{id}`. Whether that recomputation *also* bumps the client's `version` is not uniform, and the split is stated once here: it does on `POST /clients/{id}/credit-adjustments`, on Cascade A hop 5 and on Cascade B hop 5; it does **not** on the `DEPOSIT_CHARGE` of order creation, the `MILESTONE_CHARGE` of milestone acceptance, the `DEPOSIT_REFUND` of order cancellation, the `CANCELLATION_FEE` of a reschedule, cancellation or no-show, the provisional `WARRANTY_CREDIT` of claim filing, or the `CONSIGNMENT_PROCEEDS` of a sale. Ledger rows are append-only and immutable: a reversal never edits or hides the original row, it appends the negation with `reverses_entry_id` pointing at the row it undoes. A forward entry paired with its reversal therefore leaves the balance exactly where it started.

**Sale proceeds.** For a sold consignment, `sale_price_cents` equals `advance_recovered_cents` plus `shop_commission_cents` plus `consignor_payout_cents`. `SOLD` is terminal, so there is no compensating action; the identity is asserted at the moment of sale. Any rounding remainder is absorbed by `shop_commission_cents`, which is computed last precisely so that it can absorb it.

**Contracted value.** For every service order, `billed_cents` plus `unbilled_cents` equals `contract_total_cents`; and the sum of `amount_cents` over all of that order's milestones — including the ones that ended up `CANCELLED` — also equals `contract_total_cents`. Accepting a milestone moves its amount from unbilled to billed; rejecting one moves it back. Milestone amounts are minted once when the order is created and never re-derived, which is what makes the second identity hold for the whole lifetime of the order.

**Deposit disposition.** At every terminal status of a service order, `deposit_held_cents` equals `deposit_applied_cents` plus `deposit_refunded_cents` plus `deposit_forfeited_cents` — with the exception the point below pins. `deposit_forfeited_cents` absorbs a cancellation fee, `deposit_applied_cents` absorbs milestone acceptance, and whatever remains is refunded.

**How the refund formula settles against that identity.** The order-cancellation refund and the rebuild-supersession refund are both specified as `deposit_held_cents` minus `deposit_applied_cents`, which does not subtract any `deposit_forfeited_cents` already booked against the order by an earlier appointment cancellation. **The formula governs, unmodified, on both paths**: neither refund subtracts `deposit_forfeited_cents`, and neither subtracts any `deposit_refunded_cents` already on the row. The consequence is a stated exception to the identity above rather than a contradiction to be repaired: on an order that books one of those two refunds after a cancellation fee has already been forfeited against it, `deposit_applied_cents` plus `deposit_refunded_cents` plus `deposit_forfeited_cents` exceeds `deposit_held_cents` by exactly the forfeited amount. The identity holds unqualified on every order that never forfeited any of its deposit.

### 2.11. Three Cascade Chains

Three operations set off multi-hop chains in which each hop is caused by the hop before it. Cascades are described here in full and referred to by letter in §3.

**Cascade A — the unauthorised move**, five hops deep, triggered when an instrument is moved into `IN_TRANSIT` without an authorizing move job. Hop 1: the instrument's `custody_status` becomes `IN_TRANSIT` and `unauthorized_move_flag` becomes true — once raised the flag is never lowered again by any operation, so a later authorised leg leaves it standing at true. Hop 2: *because* the flag was raised, every warranty on that instrument whose status is `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFERRED` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `UNAUTHORIZED_MOVE`, processed in warranty `created_at` ascending order. Hop 3: *because* a warranty was voided, every claim of that warranty whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` moves to `DENIED`, processed in claim `created_at` ascending order. Hop 4: *because* a claim was denied, a `REVERSAL` ledger row is appended carrying the negation of that claim's provisional `WARRANTY_CREDIT` amount, with `reverses_entry_id` pointing at the original row, which is never mutated; the reversal is posted to the `client_id` recorded **on that original credit row**, not to whoever owns the warranty now, so a warranty transferred between the credit and the denial still reverses against the client who received the money. Hop 5: *because* the ledger changed, that same client's `account_credit_cents` is recomputed as the plain sum over all of that client's rows and that client's `version` is incremented by 1. Hops 3 through 5 also fire whenever a warranty is voided by any other route — a manual void, the lapsed-recall sweep, or the climate gate — and hops 4 and 5 are skipped for a denied claim that never had a provisional credit, which is every claim whose resolution variant was `FREE_RESERVICE`.

**Cascade B — the rebuild supersession**, five hops deep, triggered when a `REBUILD` quote is approved. Hop 1: the quote moves from `ISSUED` to `APPROVED`. Hop 2: *because* a rebuild quote for this instrument was approved, every service order of the same instrument whose `service_type` is `TUNE`, `PITCH_RAISE`, `REGULATE` or `VOICE` and whose status is `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `ON_HOLD` moves to `VOIDED_BY_REBUILD`, processed in order `created_at` ascending. Hop 3: *because* an order was voided, every milestone of that order whose status is `PENDING`, `READY` or `RELEASED` moves to `CANCELLED`, processed in `sequence_index` ascending order, and — in the same hop, though the shop does not think of it as a separate one — every parts allocation of that order sitting `RESERVED` or `PARTIAL` is released back to its lot exactly as `POST /parts-allocations/{id}/release` would release it, in allocation `created_at` ascending order. Hop 4: *because* the last non-terminal milestone of the order reached `CANCELLED`, a `DEPOSIT_REFUND` ledger row is appended for `deposit_held_cents` minus `deposit_applied_cents`, and `deposit_refunded_cents` is **set to** that amount rather than increased by it. The trigger for hop 4 is that hop 3 found at least one cancellable milestone — never that the refund came out positive — so an order whose held deposit was 0, or whose deposit is already fully applied, still appends a `DEPOSIT_REFUND` row for exactly 0. Hop 5: *because* the ledger changed, the client's balance is recomputed and the client's `version` is incremented by 1. Hops 4 and 5 do not fire for an order that had no milestones in a cancellable status — and the allocation release of hop 3 is likewise skipped only for want of allocations, never for want of milestones — and hop 2 finds nothing to do when the instrument has no in-flight non-rebuild orders — in which case the chain stops after hop 1 and only the quote and the instrument's condition axis change.

**Cascade C — the consignment sale**, four hops deep, triggered when a sale is recorded. Hop 1: the agreement moves from `LISTED` to `SOLD` with the proceeds split stored. Hop 2: *because* the agreement sold, the instrument's `custody_status` moves from `SHOWROOM_FLOOR` to `SOLD` and its `client_id` is re-pointed to the buyer. Hop 3: *because* the instrument changed owner, every warranty on it whose status is `ACTIVE` moves to `TRANSFERRED` with a prorated term. Hop 4: *because* the warranty's beneficiary changed, every service order of that instrument whose status is `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `ON_HOLD` has its `client_id` re-pointed to the buyer. Hop 3 is skipped when the instrument carries no `ACTIVE` warranty — a warranty already `VOIDED`, `EXPIRED` or `TRANSFERRED` is left exactly as it is — and hop 4 is skipped when no in-flight order exists. The `CONSIGNMENT_PROCEEDS` row that precedes hop 1 recomputes the consignor's balance without bumping the consignor's `version`, and the buyer's balance is not touched at any hop: Cascade C bumps the `version` of the instrument, of each transferred warranty and of each re-pointed order, and of no `Client` row at all.

### 2.12. The Client Ledger and Posting Order

Every money movement in the system is a `LedgerEntry` row. Each row carries the `client_id` whose balance it moves, an `entry_type`, a signed `amount_cents` in which charges and fees are negative and credits and refunds positive, an optional free-text `memo`, and a `posted_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock. The `entry_type` is one of `DEPOSIT_CHARGE`, `MILESTONE_CHARGE`, `WARRANTY_CREDIT`, `CONSIGNMENT_PROCEEDS`, `DEPOSIT_REFUND`, `CANCELLATION_FEE`, `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` or `REVERSAL`, and it selects which of the four optional foreign keys is populated: `service_order_id` for `DEPOSIT_CHARGE`, `MILESTONE_CHARGE` and `DEPOSIT_REFUND` entries; `warranty_claim_id` for `WARRANTY_CREDIT` entries; `consignment_agreement_id` for `CONSIGNMENT_PROCEEDS` entries; and `reverses_entry_id` for `REVERSAL` entries. Two of those rows carry a second key rather than one alone. A `CANCELLATION_FEE` carries the `service_order_id` of the stop's linked order when the stop carries one and null when it does not — the fee is a row of the same shape either way. And a `REVERSAL` carries **both** `reverses_entry_id`, naming the row it undoes, and `warranty_claim_id`, naming the claim whose denial caused it. `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` populates none of the four. The `memo` is the one caller-supplied field on the entity and only `POST /clients/{id}/credit-adjustments` accepts one; every other ledger row the system writes for itself carries `memo` null.

When one request produces more than one ledger row, the rows are appended in a fixed precedence, each with a strictly increasing `created_at` and an identical `posted_at_utc`: first `REVERSAL`, then `WARRANTY_CREDIT`, then `CONSIGNMENT_PROCEEDS`, then `DEPOSIT_REFUND`, then `CANCELLATION_FEE`, then `MILESTONE_CHARGE`, then `DEPOSIT_CHARGE`, and last `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT`. The balance sum is order-independent, but the `created_at` sequence is observable through the ledger listing, which returns rows in `created_at` ascending order. That precedence is a rule the operations of this document never actually put to the test, and saying so is part of specifying it: **no** operation here appends rows of two different `entry_type`s within one request. Every request that writes more than one row writes rows of a single type — the `REVERSAL`s a void produces as it denies each open claim in turn, or the `DEPOSIT_REFUND`s Cascade B produces as it voids each superseded order — and those rows are appended in the order the cascade walks its rows, which is claim `created_at` ascending and order `created_at` ascending respectively. The eight-way precedence therefore governs vacuously today and exists to fix the answer for any operation that later appends a mixed set.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Clients, Addresses and the Climate Record

A client is registered with a `display_name`, an `email` that must contain an at-sign, a `phone` string stored verbatim, and a `home_zone_code` naming the client's default service zone as `ZONE_A`, `ZONE_B` or `ZONE_C`. The `client_type` may be supplied and defaults to `INDIVIDUAL`; the alternatives are `INSTITUTION` and `DEALER`, and the choice reaches surprisingly far — it selects the discount applied to every quote line and it selects which of three deposit branches the shop asks for. New clients begin with `status` `ACTIVE` and `account_credit_cents` of 0. An `email` missing its at-sign is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, on create and on update alike. Profile updates may revise `display_name`, `email`, `phone`, `home_zone_code` and `client_type`; every accepted `PATCH /clients/{id}` refreshes `updated_at` and bumps `version` by 1, including one that carries an empty body or re-supplies the values already stored. Clients are listed in `created_at` ascending order with optional exact-match filters on `client_type` and `status`, both enum-validated at rung 6.

The client `status` axis has three values and three edges. A `ShopManager` moves an `ACTIVE` client to `SUSPENDED` by supplying that `status` on the update, and moves a `SUSPENDED` client back to `ACTIVE` the same way; each edge bumps the client's `version` by 1. Closing an account is one-way: an `ACTIVE` client moves to `CLOSED` only when none of that client's service orders sits in `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `ON_HOLD`, and a close attempted over an in-flight order is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); `CLOSED` is terminal, so a `status` supplied against a `CLOSED` client answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Those three are the whole edge set: a `status` that names the value the client already holds is not an edge either, and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — a `PATCH` that revises profile fields must simply omit `status` rather than restate it. Neither a `SUSPENDED` nor a `CLOSED` client may originate quotes, appointments, service orders, resumptions, warranty claims or recall notifications; those eight operations reject with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Money can be moved onto or off an account directly. A credit adjustment carries a signed `amount_cents` and an optional `memo`, appends a `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` ledger row, recomputes the client's `account_credit_cents` and bumps the client's `version` by 1. This is the operator surface used to fund an account before a deposit is charged, or to debit one after the fact. `amount_cents` must be a JSON integer — a fractional or non-numeric value is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, and it carries no range of its own, so zero and any magnitude of either sign are accepted and a zero adjustment still appends a row and still bumps the version. An omitted `memo` is stored as null; a supplied one is stored verbatim and is never read by anything.

Addresses belong to clients. Creating one supplies the owning `client_id`, a human `label` such as "Main Hall" or "Residence", a `zone_code`, an `address_key`, and a `distance_miles_from_shop` that must not be negative, a negative figure being refused with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` at rung 7 even though rung 7's own list does not name the field. Three further attributes may be supplied and otherwise take their defaults: `stair_flights`, an integer from 0 through 8 that defaults to 0; `has_service_elevator`, defaulting to false; and `climate_controlled`, defaulting to false. The `address_key` is an exact-match string used for one purpose only — detecting that two stops on the same route day sit at the same physical address — and it is compared by exact string equality, never fuzzily. A service elevator is worth stating twice because it is easy to miss: when `has_service_elevator` is true, the address contributes zero stair flights to a move regardless of what `stair_flights` says. The `distance_miles_from_shop` figure is informational only; move pricing reads the distance recorded on the move itself, not this one. Addresses may later be updated in `label`, `zone_code`, `stair_flights`, `has_service_elevator`, `climate_controlled` and `distance_miles_from_shop` — `address_key` and `client_id` are fixed at creation and no operation revises them. An update bumps `version` by 1 whether or not it changes anything, and it does **not** re-run the climate evaluation even when it moves the address into or out of `climate_controlled`. A new address begins with `out_of_band_flag` false and `readings_in_window` and `out_of_band_count` both 0. Addresses are listed in `created_at` ascending order with optional exact-match filters on `client_id` and on `zone_code`, the latter enum-validated at rung 6; a `Client` actor supplying a `client_id` that is not its own is refused at rung 4, and a `Client` actor supplying no filter at all sees only its own addresses rather than everyone's.

Every address accumulates a humidity record. A reading carries the `location_id`, the `recorded_at_utc` instant at which it was taken, a `relative_humidity_pct` from 0.0 through 100.0, and a `temperature_f` from -20.0 through 140.0. The temperature is recorded for the shop's own reference and is not read by any gate in this specification. A reading is not versioned and is never edited: it carries only its identifier and its creation timestamp. The `recorded_at_utc` is stored and re-served byte-verbatim as the caller wrote it, never re-rendered. Each reading is stamped with `in_band`, true when its `relative_humidity_pct` sits within the inclusive band from 35.0 through 55.0 — a field recorded for the shop's reference and read by nothing, since the evaluation below re-derives the same judgement from the raw percentages. The readings of a location are listed in `created_at` ascending order and carry no filters.

Whenever a reading is appended — and again whenever an administrator sets the logical clock — the shop re-evaluates the address. The evaluation window is the 180 days ending at the logical clock, and both bounds are inclusive: a reading is in the window when its `recorded_at_utc` is at or after the instant 180 days before the clock and at or before the clock itself. Readings outside that window are ignored entirely. `readings_in_window` records how many readings fell inside it; `out_of_band_count` records how many of those had a `relative_humidity_pct` below 35.0 or above 55.0. When fewer than 4 readings fall in the window the address is not judged at all and `out_of_band_flag` is false. Otherwise the flag is true exactly when `out_of_band_count` divided by `readings_in_window` is **strictly greater** than 0.25 — a ratio of exactly one quarter does not trip it, so one bad reading out of four leaves the address in good standing while two out of seven does not. Setting the clock reports how many addresses were re-evaluated this way in its `locations_reevaluated` figure, and re-evaluation runs for every address holding at least one reading. Note that the daily operations sweep does **not** re-evaluate climate; only a new reading or a clock change does. The two triggers differ in one respect worth stating: appending a reading writes the three climate fields **and** bumps the address's `version` by 1, while the clock-driven re-run writes the same three fields and bumps nothing at all.

**An open point for the implementer.** A reading's `recorded_at_utc` is described as an instant that must not be after the logical clock, but the validation ladder does not name an error code for a future-dated reading. This document carries that gap forward rather than inventing a rejection: a future-dated reading is accepted, stored and returned like any other, and the window then excludes it on its own terms because it falls after the clock. A `recorded_at_utc` that does not parse as an ISO-8601 instant at all is likewise accepted and stored, and is simply passed over by every evaluation thereafter — so it counts toward neither `readings_in_window` nor `out_of_band_count`.

### 3.2. Instruments: Registration, Custody and Condition

An instrument is registered against a `client_id` and a `current_location_id` that must belong to that same client, with a `make`, a `model`, a `serial_number` unique across every instrument in the system, an `instrument_class` of `UPRIGHT`, `CONSOLE`, `BABY_GRAND`, `GRAND` or `CONCERT_GRAND`, and a `usage_tier` of `HOME`, `STUDIO`, `INSTITUTIONAL` or `PERFORMANCE`. Two optional fields round out the profile: `target_pitch_hz`, the pitch standard the instrument is maintained at, which defaults to 440.0 and must lie between 415.0 and 445.0 inclusive; and `last_tuning_date`, an ISO date recording the most recent completed tuning. A duplicate `serial_number` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER`, and a location owned by a different client is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. A new instrument begins with `consignment_agreement_id` null, `unauthorized_move_flag` false, `tuning_cycle_interval_days` null, all three pitch fields — `pitch_deviation_cents`, `raw_pitch_tier` and `pitch_raise_tier` — null, and `planned_visit_count` at the concrete value **1** rather than null; an omitted `last_tuning_date` is stored as null and is never validated as a date. Later profile edits may revise `make`, `model`, `usage_tier`, `target_pitch_hz`, `current_location_id` and `last_tuning_date`. A `current_location_id` supplied on such an edit is resolved for existence but is **not** subjected to rung 17, whose operation list does not include `PATCH /instruments/{id}` — an instrument may therefore be edited onto an address belonging to another client, and only the operations rung 17 does name will object afterwards. Instruments are listed in `created_at` ascending order with filters on `client_id`, `current_location_id`, `consignment_agreement_id`, `custody_status` and `condition_status`, the last two enum-validated at rung 6 and the first three plain exact-match; a `Client` actor sees only its own instruments whether or not it supplies a `client_id` filter.

The `instrument_class` is the single most load-bearing attribute in the system. It multiplies the price of every quoted line, it multiplies the minutes a job takes, it adjusts the tuning-cycle interval, it sets the base rate of a move, and it fixes how many action parts a rebuild consumes.

Each instrument carries **two independent status axes** that advance on their own triggers while constraining one another. The **condition axis**, `condition_status`, tracks mechanical state and starts at `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT`; its values are `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT`, `SERVICEABLE`, `NEEDS_REGULATION`, `NEEDS_VOICING`, `REBUILD_REQUIRED` and `RESTORED`. It has no terminal state, because a restored instrument eventually drifts back out of regulation. The **custody axis**, `custody_status`, tracks physical possession and starts at `AT_CLIENT_SITE`; its values are `AT_CLIENT_SITE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `IN_SHOP`, `SHOWROOM_FLOOR` and `SOLD`, and `SOLD` is terminal.

A technician drives the condition axis by supplying a `to_condition_status` and an optional `note`. Four of its edges are technician-driven and share one precondition — the instrument's `custody_status` must be `AT_CLIENT_SITE`, `IN_SHOP` or `SHOWROOM_FLOOR` — and each bumps the instrument's `version` by 1: `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT → NEEDS_REGULATION`, `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT → REBUILD_REQUIRED`, `SERVICEABLE → NEEDS_REGULATION`, and the re-entry edge `RESTORED → NEEDS_REGULATION` that makes the axis cyclic. A fifth technician-driven edge, `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT → SERVICEABLE`, carries that same custody precondition **and one more**: `pitch_deviation_cents` must not be null, meaning a pitch assessment has actually been recorded. That edge also refreshes `updated_at` alongside the version bump. A custody state outside the permitted set is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`, and that gate runs **first**, ahead of the edge test, so an instrument sitting `IN_TRANSIT` or `SOLD` answers `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE` no matter which target it was asked for; a `to_condition_status` that is not an edge from the current value is then refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the missing-pitch refusal on the `SERVICEABLE` edge answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` as well, evaluated after the edge test rather than before it. The optional `note` is accepted, ignored and stored nowhere; the `Instrument` carries no field for it and no later operation reads one.

The remaining condition edges are system-driven and are not requested directly. Completing a `REGULATE` order on an instrument sitting at `NEEDS_REGULATION` advances it to `NEEDS_VOICING` — regulating an action leaves the instrument eligible to be voiced, and this is the only producer of `NEEDS_VOICING`; the order must have been `IN_PROGRESS`. Completing a `VOICE` order advances `NEEDS_VOICING → SERVICEABLE`, again from an `IN_PROGRESS` order. Approving a `REBUILD` quote drives the instrument to `REBUILD_REQUIRED` from either `SERVICEABLE` or `NEEDS_REGULATION`. Completing a `REBUILD` order advances `REBUILD_REQUIRED → RESTORED`, which requires the instrument's `custody_status` to be `IN_SHOP` and which also moves every still-open parts allocation of that order to consumed. Each of these edges bumps the instrument's `version` by 1.

Custody is driven by a `MoveCoordinator` supplying a `to_custody_status` and, optionally, an `authorizing_move_job_id`. Moving from `AT_CLIENT_SITE` into `IN_TRANSIT` is the pivotal edge: when `authorizing_move_job_id` is supplied it must name a move job for this instrument in status `DISPATCHED`, and that job advances `DISPATCHED → IN_TRANSIT`; when it is **omitted**, `unauthorized_move_flag` is set to true and Cascade A fires, voiding the instrument's warranties as described in §2.11. The return leg `IN_SHOP → IN_TRANSIT` behaves identically with respect to the flag and the cascade, and adds a precondition of its own: no service order for this instrument may be `IN_PROGRESS`, an in-flight order being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` after the edge test and before the authorising job is examined. From `IN_TRANSIT` the instrument reaches either `IN_SHOP` or `AT_CLIENT_SITE`; on both of those edges, when a job is quoted, the job advances `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED` and the instrument's `current_location_id` is set to that job's `destination_location_id`. Every custody edge bumps the instrument's `version` by 1 — exactly once, whichever branch it takes and however many rows the cascade goes on to touch. The four edges above are the whole of what this operation can request: `SHOWROOM_FLOOR` and `SOLD` are declared members of the axis that only §3.10's showroom operations ever write, so asking for either here passes rung 6 and falls to `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rung 23.

Two further custody edges belong to the showroom. Listing an active consignment moves the instrument `IN_SHOP → SHOWROOM_FLOOR` and stamps its `consignment_agreement_id`; withdrawal or expiry of the agreement moves it `SHOWROOM_FLOOR → AT_CLIENT_SITE` and clears `consignment_agreement_id` back to null; and recording a sale moves it `SHOWROOM_FLOOR → SOLD`, re-points `client_id` at the buyer and fires Cascade C.

**An open point for the implementer, and how it is settled.** The `AT_CLIENT_SITE → IN_TRANSIT` edge states explicitly that quoting an authorizing job advances that job from `DISPATCHED` to `IN_TRANSIT`, while the `IN_SHOP → IN_TRANSIT` edge states only the unauthorized-move branch. Whether quoting a job on the shop-outbound leg advances the job the same way is not settled by the scaffold; it is settled here, in favour of symmetry. **Both** legs into `IN_TRANSIT` behave alike: an `authorizing_move_job_id` naming a `DISPATCHED` job for this instrument advances that job to `IN_TRANSIT` and bumps its `version` by 1, and it suppresses both the flag and the cascade, regardless of which of the two states the instrument left. The whole difference between the two legs remains the in-flight-order precondition the shop-outbound leg adds.

### 3.3. Pitch Assessment and the Overdue Bump

Before an instrument can be quoted intelligently, someone has to measure how far out of tune it is. A pitch assessment supplies a `reference_pitch_hz` and a `measured_deviation_cents`. The reference must equal the instrument's `target_pitch_hz` **exactly**; a technician who measured against a different standard is refused with `ERR_PITCH_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`, because a deviation measured against the wrong reference is not a deviation at all. The measured deviation is a signed number of cents between -200.0 and 200.0 inclusive, kept to one decimal place as supplied, and it is written verbatim into `pitch_deviation_cents`.

The absolute value of that deviation lands the instrument in a raw band, recorded as `raw_pitch_tier`. A magnitude of at most 5.0 cents is `TIER_0`. Above 5.0 and up to and including 15.0 is `TIER_1`. Above 15.0 and up to and including 35.0 is `TIER_2`. Anything above 35.0 is `TIER_3`, which has no upper bound.

The raw band is not the whole story. If the instrument has an outstanding tuning recall whose overdue banding has reached `OVERDUE_T2` or `LAPSED_TIER`, the tier is bumped up by one band, capped at `TIER_3`; otherwise it is not bumped at all. The result is written to `pitch_raise_tier`, the **effective** tier, and this is the field every downstream calculation reads — never `raw_pitch_tier`. The governing recall is chosen from the recalls of this instrument whose `status` is `PENDING`, `NOTIFIED` or `SCHEDULED`, ranked by `created_at` descending, breaking ties by `due_date` descending and then by `days_overdue` descending; candidates that still tie after all three are interchangeable, because `overdue_tier` — the only field the bump reads — is equal on both. When no such recall exists there is no bump. The bump reads that `overdue_tier` exactly as the recall row currently stores it, and never recomputes it from the clock at assessment time: since §3.4 refreshes `days_overdue` and `overdue_tier` only in the daily sweep, a recall that has in fact fallen ninety days past due but has not been swept since still reads `ON_TIME` and produces no bump at all. Whether an assessment bumps therefore depends on when the sweep last ran, not only on the clock. Under the un-bumped reading, `pitch_raise_tier` equals `raw_pitch_tier`; under the bumped reading it sits one band higher, so the very same measured deviation produces a different service composition, a different duration and a different quote line count.

The assessment also writes `planned_visit_count`, the number of visits a tuning needs at the effective tier: 1 visit at `TIER_0`, 1 at `TIER_1`, 1 at `TIER_2`, and 2 at `TIER_3`. Every assessment bumps the instrument's `version` by 1. And an assessment that changes the effective tier of an instrument that already has a `TUNE` order sitting `IN_PROGRESS` is rejected with `ERR_PITCH_TIER_CONFLICT` — but only *after* all the pitch fields above have been written, as §2.9 sets out in detail.

### 3.4. The Tuning Cycle, Recalls and the Daily Sweep

Every instrument is on a maintenance cycle whose length the shop derives rather than guesses. Opening a recall supplies the `instrument_id` and a `cycle_start_date`, normally the date of the last completed tuning, and the server computes the interval in three steps whose order matters. First, a base interval is read off the `usage_tier`: 182 days for a `HOME` instrument, 120 for `STUDIO`, 91 for `INSTITUTIONAL`, and 45 for a `PERFORMANCE` instrument that is played hard and expected back roughly every six weeks. Second, that base is multiplied by a climate factor read from the instrument's **current** location — 1.00 when the address is `climate_controlled`, and 0.75 when it is not, because an uncontrolled room pulls the instrument out of tune a quarter faster — and the product is rounded **down** to a whole number of days. Third, a class adjustment is added to that floored product: 0 days for an `UPRIGHT`, 0 for a `CONSOLE`, -7 for a `BABY_GRAND`, -14 for a `GRAND`, and -21 for a `CONCERT_GRAND`, each of those last three shortening the cycle by 7, 14 and 21 days respectively. The result is finally raised to a floor of 30 days, so no instrument is ever put on a cycle shorter than a month. The floor of the product is taken **before** the class adjustment is added, and the 30-day minimum is applied **last**.

That figure is written both to the instrument's `tuning_cycle_interval_days` and, as a snapshot that never moves afterwards, to the recall's own `interval_days`. The recall's `due_date` is the `cycle_start_date` advanced by `interval_days` calendar days, honouring month lengths and leap days; a `cycle_start_date` that is not an ISO date is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, checked after the instrument has resolved. Opening a recall bumps the instrument's `version` by 1. A new recall is always born at `days_overdue` 0 and `overdue_tier` `ON_TIME`, whatever `due_date` the arithmetic produced — a recall opened from a `cycle_start_date` long in the past is not overdue on paper until a sweep says so. Recalls of an instrument are listed in `created_at` ascending order with filters on `status` and `overdue_tier`, both enum-validated at rung 6.

A recall begins at `PENDING`. A `ShopManager` marks the client notified, which moves it to `NOTIFIED`, stamps `notified_at` from the logical clock and bumps the recall's `version` by 1; this requires the instrument owner's client `status` to be `ACTIVE`. Booking an appointment that quotes a `tuning_recall_id` moves the recall `NOTIFIED → SCHEDULED`; cancelling that appointment moves it back `SCHEDULED → NOTIFIED`, which is the fall-back edge that makes this axis cyclic; and completing the linked stop moves it `SCHEDULED → FULFILLED` and stamps the instrument's `last_tuning_date` with the completion date. Each of those edges bumps the recall's `version` by 1. `FULFILLED` and `LAPSED` are terminal.

`days_overdue` is the number of whole days by which the logical clock's date exceeds the `due_date`, and it is never negative — an instrument not yet due sits at 0. The `overdue_tier` bands that figure: 0 days is `ON_TIME`; 1 through 30 days is `OVERDUE_T1`; 31 through 90 days is `OVERDUE_T2`; and 91 days or more is `LAPSED_TIER`. Only the last two bands feed the pitch bump described in §3.3.

The daily operations sweep is an administrator-only checkpoint that runs four steps in a fixed order and returns a count for each. **Step 1** walks the recalls whose `status` is `PENDING`, `NOTIFIED` or `SCHEDULED` in `created_at` ascending order, recomputing `days_overdue` and `overdue_tier` for each and reporting the number touched as `evaluated_recall_count`; any recall in `PENDING` or `NOTIFIED` that has reached 91 or more days overdue moves to `LAPSED`, has its `overdue_tier` set to `LAPSED_TIER`, bumps its own `version` by 1, and voids every `ACTIVE` warranty on its instrument with `void_reason` `TUNING_CYCLE_LAPSED` — the count of lapsed recalls is `lapsed_recall_count` and the count of warranties voided this way is `voided_warranty_count`. **Step 2** moves every `ISSUED` quote whose `expires_at` the clock has reached or passed to `EXPIRED`, reporting `expired_quote_count`. **Step 3** moves every `ACTIVE` or `LISTED` consignment agreement whose `expires_at` the clock has reached or passed to `EXPIRED`, clearing the instrument's `consignment_agreement_id` and returning it from `SHOWROOM_FLOOR` to `AT_CLIENT_SITE` where applicable, reporting `expired_consignment_count`. **Step 4** moves every `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFERRED` warranty whose `end_date` the clock's date has passed — strictly after, so a warranty is still live on its final day — to `EXPIRED`, reporting `expired_warranty_count`. Each of the four steps walks its candidates in `created_at` ascending order, and the sweep's `data` is not a resource but the six-count object naming `evaluated_recall_count`, `lapsed_recall_count`, `voided_warranty_count`, `expired_quote_count`, `expired_consignment_count` and `expired_warranty_count`, answering 200. Step 1's re-evaluation is the one write in the whole sweep that leaves no trace on the row it touches: a recall that is merely re-banded has `days_overdue` and `overdue_tier` rewritten with **no** `version` bump and **no** `updated_at` refresh, and only a recall that actually lapses is versioned. A `SCHEDULED` recall is evaluated and re-banded like the others but can never lapse, because the lapse branch admits only `PENDING` and `NOTIFIED`. The voiding that a lapse performs is the full void of §3.9, denying the warranty's open claims and reversing their provisional credits; `voided_warranty_count` counts warranties, not claims or reversals.

**An open point for the implementer, and how it is settled.** The lapse step is written as voiding warranties in status `ACTIVE`, whereas the unauthorized-move cascade voids warranties in status `ACTIVE` or `TRANSFERRED`. Whether a transferred warranty survives its instrument's lapsed cycle is left as the scaffold left it, and the scaffold's narrower wording governs: **the lapse step voids `ACTIVE` warranties only**, and a `TRANSFERRED` warranty on the same instrument survives the lapse untouched, at its existing status and `version`. The asymmetry against Cascade A is deliberate rather than an oversight to be repaired — a new owner is not punished for the previous owner's neglected tuning cycle.

### 3.5. Quoting the Work

A quote is drafted against an `instrument_id` and a `service_type` — one of `TUNE`, `PITCH_RAISE`, `REGULATE`, `VOICE` or `REBUILD` — together with the `expected_instrument_version` described in §2.7. The quote copies its `client_id` from the instrument's owner at creation, starts life at `version_number` 1 with `supersedes_quote_id` null, and enters status `DRAFT` with `issued_at` and `expires_at` both null.

Before anything is priced, the shop enforces a **service ladder**: you cannot voice an action that has not been regulated, and you cannot tune an instrument that has already been condemned to a rebuild. A `VOICE` quote requires the instrument's `condition_status` to be `SERVICEABLE`, `NEEDS_VOICING` or `RESTORED`. A `REGULATE` quote requires `SERVICEABLE`, `NEEDS_REGULATION`, `NEEDS_VOICING` or `RESTORED`. A `TUNE` or `PITCH_RAISE` quote is permitted from any condition **except** `REBUILD_REQUIRED`. A `REBUILD` quote is permitted from every condition without exception, including `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT`. A violation is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`.

**Line composition** comes next. A quote normally has exactly one priced line, the service being quoted, so `line_count` is 1. There is one exception: a `TUNE` quote for an instrument whose **effective** `pitch_raise_tier` is `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` automatically acquires a second line for a pitch raise, and `line_count` becomes 2. The effective tier is read here, never the raw band, so an instrument measured at 10 cents off but bumped for a badly overdue cycle prices as a two-line quote even though the same reading on a punctual instrument would also be `TIER_1` — while an instrument sitting at `TIER_0` prices as one line regardless of how overdue it is. An instrument that has never been pitch-assessed holds `pitch_raise_tier` null, which is not one of those three, and so prices as a one-line quote exactly as a `TIER_0` instrument does; that null reads through every other tier-sensitive figure in this document — the order's duration, the stop's `service_minutes` and the stop's `estimated_price_cents` — in the same way, adding nothing.

**Pricing** then runs in seven steps, and the order of rounding is part of the specification. *Step one*: each line's undiscounted base is the service's list price multiplied by the class multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents. The list prices are 19500 cents for a `TUNE`, 9500 for a `PITCH_RAISE`, 85000 for a `REGULATE`, 42000 for a `VOICE`, and 1450000 for a `REBUILD`. The class multipliers are 1.00 for an `UPRIGHT`, 1.05 for a `CONSOLE`, 1.20 for a `BABY_GRAND`, 1.35 for a `GRAND` and 1.75 for a `CONCERT_GRAND`. The sum of the line bases is `base_price_cents`. *Step two*: the discount percentage is read off the client. A `DEALER` client sets `pricing_variant` to `DEALER_NET` at 18 percent off; an `INSTITUTION` client sets it to `INSTITUTIONAL` at 8 percent off; anyone else sets it to `RETAIL` at 0 percent off, so a retail line's net equals its base and `line_discount_cents` comes out at 0. *Step three*: **each line** is multiplied by one minus that percentage and rounded half up to whole cents **individually**, and only then are the rounded line amounts summed; `line_discount_cents` is `base_price_cents` less that sum. Summing the lines first and rounding the discounted total once is wrong and can differ by a cent. *Step four*: a zone surcharge is read from the zone of the instrument's current location — 0 cents in `ZONE_A`, 4500 cents in `ZONE_B`, 11000 cents in `ZONE_C` — and stored as `zone_surcharge_cents`. *Step five*: `subtotal_cents` is the sum of the net line amounts plus the zone surcharge. *Step six*: the loyalty discount is computed. *Step seven*: `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` less `loyalty_discount_cents`.

The **loyalty discount** is keyed on `subtotal_cents` and never on `total_cents`; keying it on the total would be circular, since the total depends on the discount. A subtotal of at least 1450000 cents earns 6 percent; a subtotal of at least 119250 cents but below 1450000 earns 3 percent; anything below 119250 earns nothing. The percentage is applied to `subtotal_cents` and rounded half up to whole cents.

The **deposit** is decided last, from three mutually exclusive branches, and recorded in both `deposit_required_cents` and `deposit_variant`. The deposit percentage for the service class is 0.00 for a `TUNE`, 0.00 for a `PITCH_RAISE`, 0.25 for a `REGULATE`, 0.25 for a `VOICE` and 0.40 for a `REBUILD`. When that percentage is zero — which is to say for tunings and pitch raises — the variant is `PERCENT_OF_CONTRACT` and the deposit is exactly 0. Otherwise, an `INSTITUTION` client takes the `NET_TERMS` branch: the deposit is 10 percent of `total_cents`, rounded half up, with no floor of any kind. Every other client takes the percentage of `total_cents`, rounded half up; if that figure reaches 25000 cents the variant is `PERCENT_OF_CONTRACT` and the figure stands, and if it falls short the variant is `FLAT_MINIMUM` and the deposit is pinned at 25000 cents regardless of how small the contract is. Two consequences are worth naming: a smaller contract can owe a *larger* deposit than its percentage suggests, and an institution can owe *less* than the 25000 a private client would owe on the identical contract.

Quotes progress through a lifecycle with four terminal states. A `ShopManager` issues a `DRAFT` quote, which stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, sets `expires_at` to 30 days after that instant, and bumps the quote's `version` by 1; issuing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_QUOTE` when another quote for the same instrument and the same `service_type` already holds status `ISSUED`. The owning client then either approves the quote — permitted only while the clock is strictly before `expires_at` and while the client is `ACTIVE`, moving it `ISSUED → APPROVED` — or declines it with an optional `reason`, moving it `ISSUED → DECLINED` — the `reason` is accepted and stored nowhere, the `Quote` carrying no field for it, and declining is not gated on the client's standing the way approving is. Approving a `REBUILD` quote additionally drives the instrument's condition axis to `REBUILD_REQUIRED` and fires Cascade B in full. The condition write is the one §3.2 describes and carries that edge's own precondition: it happens only when the instrument's `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE` or `NEEDS_REGULATION`. From any other condition — `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT`, `NEEDS_VOICING`, `RESTORED`, or `REBUILD_REQUIRED` already — the approval leaves `condition_status` exactly as it stands, while the quote still moves to `APPROVED` and Cascade B still fires. Because completing a `REBUILD` order advances the condition axis only from `REBUILD_REQUIRED`, a rebuild approved out of `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT` never reaches `RESTORED` on completion unless a technician drives the `NEEDS_ASSESSMENT → REBUILD_REQUIRED` edge directly in the meantime. The daily sweep moves an `ISSUED` quote whose `expires_at` the clock has reached or passed to `EXPIRED`.

An expired or issued quote is never edited back into life. Instead, a `ShopManager` **re-issues** it: the named quote moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its own `version` bumped by 1, and a brand-new quote row is minted in status `DRAFT` carrying `version_number` one higher than the old row, `supersedes_quote_id` pointing back at it, and freshly recomputed prices reflecting whatever has changed about the instrument, its location and its owner in the meantime. Re-issue carries `expected_instrument_version` and bumps the instrument's `version` by 1, exactly as an original draft does, and it re-runs the service ladder of the paragraph above against the instrument's *current* condition — but it re-checks neither the client's standing nor the one-issued-quote rule, so a re-issue may legitimately be minted for a suspended client and the replacement lands in `DRAFT` with `issued_at` and `expires_at` null, to be issued separately later. Only an `ISSUED` or an `EXPIRED` quote may be re-issued: `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` and `SUPERSEDED` answer `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and a `DRAFT` quote — which needs no replacement, having never been issued — answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Quotes are listed in `created_at` ascending order with filters on `instrument_id`, `client_id`, `supersedes_quote_id`, `status` and `service_type`, the last two enum-validated at rung 6, and a single quote is read back with its whole breakdown — `base_price_cents`, `line_discount_cents`, `zone_surcharge_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents`, `total_cents`, `deposit_required_cents`, `deposit_variant` and `pricing_variant`.

### 3.6. Service Orders, Milestones, Parts and Settlement

An approved quote becomes work when a service order is created against its `quote_id`, optionally naming an `assigned_technician_id`. The order refuses to exist unless the quote is `APPROVED`, unless no other order already realises that quote, and unless the clock is still strictly before the quote's `expires_at`. It copies `instrument_id`, `client_id` and `service_type` from the quote, copies `contract_total_cents` from the quote's `total_cents` — a figure that is never re-derived afterwards, no matter what happens to prices — and copies `deposit_held_cents` from the quote's `deposit_required_cents`. `billed_cents` starts at 0 and `unbilled_cents` therefore starts equal to the contract total; `deposit_applied_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` all start at 0. When `deposit_held_cents` is greater than 0, a `DEPOSIT_CHARGE` ledger row is appended for the negated deposit and the client's balance is recomputed.

The order also computes how long the work will take, in two named variants. The base figure is the service's base minutes multiplied by the class minute multiplier, rounded half up to whole minutes: 60 base minutes for a `TUNE`, 35 for a `PITCH_RAISE`, 240 for a `REGULATE`, 150 for a `VOICE` and 2400 for a `REBUILD`; multiplied by 1.00 for an `UPRIGHT`, 1.00 for a `CONSOLE`, 1.15 for a `BABY_GRAND`, 1.30 for a `GRAND` and 1.60 for a `CONCERT_GRAND`. A `TUNE` on an instrument whose effective `pitch_raise_tier` is `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` adds a further 35 minutes for the pitch-raise pass. If the base figure so obtained is **strictly greater** than 300 minutes, `visit_plan_variant` is `SPLIT_VISIT` and `estimated_minutes` is that base plus another 35 minutes, because a second visit repeats the setup pass; otherwise `visit_plan_variant` is `SINGLE_VISIT` and `estimated_minutes` equals the base exactly. A base of exactly 300 minutes is a single visit.

Creating the order also mints its **milestone schedule**, and those amounts are minted once and never re-derived. The number of milestones is fixed by the service class: 1 for a `TUNE`, 1 for a `PITCH_RAISE`, 2 for a `REGULATE`, 2 for a `VOICE` and 4 for a `REBUILD`. The contract total is divided by that count and rounded **down** to whole cents to give a share; every milestone except the last receives exactly that share, and the milestone with the **highest** `sequence_index` receives the contract total less the share multiplied by one fewer than the count — so the remainder always lands on the final stage. `sequence_index` is 1-based. The `label` of each milestone is minted from the service type: a `REBUILD` runs `DISASSEMBLY`, `STRINGING`, `ACTION_REGULATION` and `FINAL_VOICING`; a `REGULATE` runs `ACTION_PREP` then `FINAL_REGULATION`; a `VOICE` runs `HAMMER_PREP` then `FINAL_VOICING`; and a `TUNE` or `PITCH_RAISE` has the single stage `COMPLETION`. Each milestone begins at status `PENDING` with `rejection_count` 0 and `released_by_user_id`, `released_at` and `accepted_at` all null.

Work starts when a technician starts the order. This requires status `PENDING`, requires `assigned_technician_id` to have been set — an unassigned order answering `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, checked after the status test and ahead of the custody gate — and, for a `REBUILD` only, requires the instrument's `custody_status` to be `IN_SHOP`, a gate that answers with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`. Neither the technician's certification nor the client's standing is re-examined at start; both were settled when the order was created. Starting stamps `started_at` from the logical clock, bumps the order's `version` by 1, and moves **every** milestone of the order from `PENDING` to `READY` in one step, processed in `sequence_index` ascending order. Readying them all together is deliberate: the *ordering* of releases is enforced separately, at the milestone-sequence rung, which is what makes that rejection reachable from a milestone that is already `READY`. Starting a linked appointment has the same effect on a `PENDING` order and its milestones.

A `ShopManager` releases a `READY` milestone for client acceptance while the order is `IN_PROGRESS`, which stamps `released_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `released_at` from the clock and bumps the milestone's `version` by 1. Release is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE` while any lower `sequence_index` on the same order is not yet `ACCEPTED`, and refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_SETTLED` when the milestone at `sequence_index` 1 is being released while the client's `account_credit_cents` is negative.

The owning client then either accepts or rejects. **Acceptance** settles the milestone against the deposit before it touches the ledger. The order's unapplied deposit is `deposit_held_cents` less `deposit_applied_cents`, less `deposit_refunded_cents`, less `deposit_forfeited_cents`. The applied portion is the smaller of the milestone's `amount_cents` and that unapplied deposit, floored at 0 — an unapplied deposit that has already gone below zero applies nothing at all rather than clawing back an earlier application; the residual is the milestone amount less the applied portion. `deposit_applied_cents` grows by the applied portion, `billed_cents` grows by the **full** milestone amount, and `unbilled_cents` is recomputed as the contract total less `billed_cents`. When the residual is greater than 0, a `MILESTONE_CHARGE` ledger row is appended for the negated residual and the client's balance is recomputed; a residual of 0 writes no row, the deposit having covered the stage outright. Acceptance bumps two rows: the order's `version` by 1 for the billing fields, and then the milestone's by 1. Acceptance stamps `accepted_at`, requires the order to be `IN_PROGRESS`, and is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT` when the client's balance plus the unapplied deposit cannot cover the milestone amount. **Rejection** carries an optional `reason`, increments `rejection_count` by 1, clears `released_by_user_id` and `released_at` back to null, and returns the milestone to `READY` — the intermediate `REJECTED` value is a waypoint within the same request and is never the persisted result. A third rejection is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, since `rejection_count` must be below 2 to reject again. The `reason` is accepted and stored nowhere — the `Milestone` carries no field for it — and rejection, unlike acceptance, does not examine the order's status at all, so a milestone released before its order left `IN_PROGRESS` may still be rejected afterwards.

**Which reading governs each of the two gates.** The milestone acceptance edge states its credit precondition as the client's balance plus `deposit_held_cents` less `deposit_applied_cents`, while the fiscal rung on the same operation subtracts `deposit_refunded_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` as well. Likewise, the release edge describes the first milestone's gate as the order having held a deposit at all (or having required none), while the fiscal rung describes it as the client's balance being non-negative. In both pairs **the fiscal rung governs**. Acceptance compares the client's `account_credit_cents` plus the four-term unapplied deposit — `deposit_held_cents` less `deposit_applied_cents`, less `deposit_refunded_cents`, less `deposit_forfeited_cents` — against the milestone's `amount_cents`, exactly as rung 60 states. And releasing the milestone at `sequence_index` 1 looks only at the client's `account_credit_cents`, refusing with `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_SETTLED` precisely when it is negative: the order's own deposit fields are not consulted at all, so an order whose `deposit_held_cents` is 0 is refused just the same when the balance has gone negative, and an order holding a large deposit passes freely so long as the balance is at or above 0. No milestone at any other `sequence_index` is subject to that gate.

An order completes when a technician completes it from `IN_PROGRESS` with every one of its milestones `ACCEPTED`. Completion stamps `completed_at`, bumps the order's `version` by 1, mints a warranty as described in §3.9, advances the instrument's condition axis according to the service performed, stamps the instrument's `last_tuning_date` with the completion date for a `TUNE` or a `PITCH_RAISE`, and moves every parts allocation of the order still sitting `RESERVED` or `PARTIAL` to `CONSUMED` in `created_at` ascending order. The condition advance and the tuning stamp are mutually exclusive by service type — the first belongs to `REGULATE`, `VOICE` and `REBUILD`, the second to `TUNE` and `PITCH_RAISE` — so completion bumps the instrument's `version` **at most once**, and not at all when the condition edge finds the instrument standing somewhere the edge does not leave from. A `REBUILD` completed while its instrument is no longer `IN_SHOP` is a case of exactly that: the order completes, the warranty is minted and the parts are consumed, and the condition axis is left where it stands with the instrument's `version` untouched.

An order in `PENDING` or `ON_HOLD` may instead be cancelled by a `ShopManager` with an optional `reason`. Cancellation moves every milestone in `PENDING`, `READY` or `RELEASED` to `CANCELLED` in `sequence_index` ascending order, moves every parts allocation in `RESERVED` or `PARTIAL` to `RELEASED` in `created_at` ascending order, **sets** `deposit_refunded_cents` to `deposit_held_cents` less `deposit_applied_cents` rather than adding to it, appends a `DEPOSIT_REFUND` ledger row for that amount, recomputes the client's balance, and bumps the order's `version` by 1. Unlike Cascade B's hop 4, this refund is unconditional: it is appended whether or not any milestone was cancellable and whether or not the amount came out positive, so an order that held no deposit at all still leaves a `DEPOSIT_REFUND` row of exactly 0 behind it. The `reason` is accepted and stored nowhere. An order can also leave the board without anyone cancelling it: Cascade B drives a `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `ON_HOLD` order of type `TUNE`, `PITCH_RAISE`, `REGULATE` or `VOICE` to `VOIDED_BY_REBUILD` when a rebuild quote for the same instrument is approved. The one producer of `ON_HOLD` is a no-show on a linked appointment; a `ShopManager` resumes a held order back to `IN_PROGRESS` provided the client is `ACTIVE`. Orders are listed in `created_at` ascending order with filters on `quote_id`, `instrument_id`, `client_id`, `assigned_technician_id` and `status`, the last enum-validated at rung 6; a `Client` actor sees only its own orders whether or not it filters, and a `Technician` actor supplying an `assigned_technician_id` that is not its own is refused at rung 4 — though a technician that filters on nothing still sees every order, the technician scope being enforced on the filter rather than on the page. An order's milestones are listed in `sequence_index` ascending order, sorted before any `status` filter and before pagination, so a filtered page of milestones is still a window onto the sequence and not onto creation order.

Rebuilds consume **parts**. A lot is received into stock with a `part_type` of `HAMMER_SET`, `STRING_SET` or `ACTION_PARTS`, a `lot_code` unique across all lots, and an `initial_qty` between 1 and 1000. On receipt `on_hand_qty` equals `initial_qty` while `allocated_qty` and `consumed_qty` are 0, and the lot's `status` is `OPEN`. A rebuild's nominal requirement, surfaced for the shop's planning when a `REBUILD` order is created, is 1 hammer set, 1 string set, and a quantity of action parts that follows the class: 2 for an `UPRIGHT`, 2 for a `CONSOLE`, 3 for a `BABY_GRAND`, 4 for a `GRAND` and 6 for a `CONCERT_GRAND`. It is surfaced as an extra `parts_requirement` object on the serialized `ServiceOrder` — three keys, `HAMMER_SET`, `STRING_SET` and `ACTION_PARTS`, each carrying its integer — computed once from the instrument's class at order creation and never re-derived, present on every representation of a `REBUILD` order and **absent altogether** from an order of any other service type. It is a planning figure only: nothing compares it to what is actually reserved, and no gate anywhere refuses an order whose allocations fall short of it.

Reserving against an order supplies the `parts_lot_id`, a `requested_qty` between 1 and 50, and the `expected_parts_lot_version`. Although parts are a rebuild's concern, the operation itself is not restricted to one: it gates on neither the order's `service_type` nor its `status`, so units may be reserved against a `TUNE` order and against an order that has not yet started or has already been cancelled. Only *consuming* an allocation is gated on the order's status, at rung 24. The resolution has two accepting outcomes and one rejection. The partial floor is the requested quantity multiplied by 0.50 and rounded **up** to a whole unit. If the lot's `on_hand_qty` is at least the requested quantity, the allocation's `status` is `RESERVED` and its `allocated_qty` equals the full request. If `on_hand_qty` falls short of the request but is at least the partial floor, the `status` is `PARTIAL` and `allocated_qty` equals whatever remains on hand — the shortfall is absorbed silently rather than raised as an error. Below the partial floor the request is refused with `ERR_PARTS_INSUFFICIENT`. The ceiling on the floor matters: requesting 5 units with 2 on hand computes a floor of 3 and is refused, while requesting 4 units with 2 on hand computes a floor of 2 and is filled partially. A successful reservation moves `allocated_qty` out of `on_hand_qty` on the lot and bumps the lot's `version` by 1; a lot whose `on_hand_qty` reaches 0 moves to `DEPLETED`.

An allocation in `RESERVED` or `PARTIAL` may be consumed by a technician while its order is `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, which moves the units from the lot's `allocated_qty` into its `consumed_qty` and leaves `on_hand_qty` untouched, bumping the lot's `version` by 1. Alternatively a `ShopManager` releases it, which moves the units from `allocated_qty` back into `on_hand_qty`, leaves `consumed_qty` untouched, returns a `DEPLETED` lot to `OPEN` once `on_hand_qty` is greater than 0, and bumps the lot's `version` by 1. `CONSUMED` and `RELEASED` are both terminal. Allocations are listed with filters on `parts_lot_id`, `service_order_id` and `status`.

**How the two statements about depletion settle.** The lot's move from `OPEN` to `DEPLETED` is described as firing on any allocation or consumption that drives `on_hand_qty` to 0, but consumption is also described as leaving `on_hand_qty` unchanged. The second statement governs, and it disarms the first: because consuming an allocation moves units from `allocated_qty` to `consumed_qty` without touching `on_hand_qty`, a consumption can never be the operation that drives the count to 0, and so it never effects the `OPEN → DEPLETED` move. Reserving units is the only operation that does. Releasing an allocation is the only operation that returns a lot to `OPEN`, and it does so whenever the returned units leave `on_hand_qty` above 0. The net effect is that a lot's `status` is `DEPLETED` exactly while its `on_hand_qty` is 0 and `OPEN` exactly while it is above 0, on every path.

### 3.7. Technicians, Route Days and the Life of a Stop

A technician is registered with a `display_name`, a `certification_level` of `APPRENTICE`, `JOURNEYMAN` or `MASTER`, and a non-empty `zone_codes` array drawn from `ZONE_A`, `ZONE_B` and `ZONE_C`. Two optional attributes complete the record: `move_certified`, defaulting to false, and `daily_capacity_minutes`, defaulting to 480 minutes and constrained to the range 60 through 960. Technicians begin `ACTIVE`; an `INACTIVE` technician can receive neither new route days nor new appointments nor new service orders, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `zone_codes` that is not an array, or is an empty one, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; a member outside the three zones is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` at rung 6. Technicians are listed in `created_at` ascending order with filters on `certification_level` and `status`, both enum-validated, and their `certification_level`, `move_certified`, `zone_codes`, `daily_capacity_minutes` and `status` may all be revised. That revision runs no state machine of its own: `status` may be flipped between `ACTIVE` and `INACTIVE` in either direction and any number of times, `INACTIVE` is not terminal, and every accepted `PATCH /technicians/{id}` bumps `version` by 1 whether or not it changed anything. A revised `daily_capacity_minutes` reaches only route days opened afterwards; a day that already took its default keeps the `capacity_minutes` it was born with. A revised `certification_level` is likewise not applied retroactively to orders or stops already accepted — it is read afresh at each of the three gates that consult it, so lowering a technician's rank can strand a booked stop that will only fail at dispatch.

Certification is ranked and compared numerically: an `APPRENTICE` ranks 1, a `JOURNEYMAN` ranks 2 and a `MASTER` ranks 3. Each service class demands a minimum rank — 1 for a `TUNE`, 2 for a `PITCH_RAISE`, 2 for a `REGULATE`, 2 for a `VOICE` and 3 for a `REBUILD` — and the technician's rank must be at least that minimum. The gate is checked in three places: when a service order is created with an assigned technician, when an appointment is booked onto a technician's day, and when a route day is dispatched. Every failure answers `ERR_TECH_CERT_INSUFFICIENT`. A journeyman may therefore tune, raise pitch, regulate and voice, but only a master may rebuild.

A **route day** is one technician's working date. It is opened with a `technician_id` and a `service_date`, optionally overriding `capacity_minutes`, which otherwise defaults from that technician's `daily_capacity_minutes` and is likewise constrained to 60 through 960. A new day is `OPEN` with `committed_minutes` and `stop_count` both 0; a `service_date` that is not an ISO date is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`, checked only after the technician has resolved, and the date is stored and compared as the plain string the caller supplied. Nothing forbids two route days for the same technician on the same `service_date`, and nothing forbids a `service_date` in the past. Route days are listed in `created_at` ascending order with filters on `technician_id`, `service_date` and `status`, the last enum-validated at rung 6 and `service_date` matched as an exact string, and read back individually with their committed minutes and stop count.

The day's axis is driven by supplying a `to_status`, and it is cyclic. An `OPEN` day moves to `LOCKED`, which freezes bookings; this is permitted only once the logical clock has reached 12 hours before midnight UTC at the start of the day's `service_date`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_DAY_LOCK_LEAD`. A `LOCKED` day moves back to `OPEN` — the unlock edge that makes the axis cyclic. A `LOCKED` day with at least one stop moves to `DISPATCHED`, which is terminal. Each edge bumps the day's `version` by 1. Dispatch is also the event that pushes the day's work into the field: every appointment on the day whose `scheduling_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING` flips to `EN_ROUTE`, processed in appointment `created_at` ascending order, with each flipped appointment's `version` bumped by 1 — and, as §2.9 records, a certification failure part-way through that walk leaves the earlier flips standing.

**Booking a stop** supplies the `instrument_id`, the `route_day_id`, the `location_id`, the `scheduled_start_utc`, the `service_type` and the `expected_route_day_version`; it may also carry a `service_order_id` linking the visit to an order it executes, and a `tuning_recall_id` naming the recall it fulfils. The stop's location must be the instrument's `current_location_id` and must belong to the instrument's owner. The start must be at least 120 minutes after the logical clock, and its date part must equal the route day's `service_date`. Either the location's `zone_code` or the owning client's `home_zone_code` must appear in the technician's `zone_codes`; an out-of-zone visit for an in-zone client is explicitly permitted, and only a stop that satisfies neither is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_ZONE_MISMATCH`. Booking succeeds into `SCHEDULED` — the initial `REQUESTED` value is passed through within the same request and is never the persisted result — and it recomputes the day's `committed_minutes` and `stop_count` from scratch and bumps the day's `version` by 1. When a `tuning_recall_id` is supplied, that recall moves `NOTIFIED → SCHEDULED` — and only from `NOTIFIED`: a recall named while it still sits `PENDING`, or one already `SCHEDULED` against another stop, is linked to the appointment and left standing exactly where it was, silently and without error. Neither optional link is checked for coherence with the stop: the `tuning_recall_id` need not belong to the instrument being visited, and the `service_order_id` need not belong to that instrument or that client either. Both are resolved for existence only — the `service_order_id` at rung 13 and the `tuning_recall_id` at rung 14, after the route day — and are otherwise recorded as given.

Three numbers are computed for every stop. **`service_minutes`** is the service's base minutes multiplied by the instrument's class minute multiplier and rounded half up, plus a further 35 minutes when the service is a `TUNE` and the instrument's effective `pitch_raise_tier` is `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`. The split-visit adjustment that a service order applies does **not** apply to a single stop. **`travel_buffer_minutes`** follows a three-way rule. The stop is marked `same_address_chained` when an earlier-created appointment on the same route day, in `scheduling_status` `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED`, sits at a location whose `address_key` matches this one exactly; a chained stop takes a buffer of just 5 minutes, because the crew is already there. Otherwise, when the location's `zone_code` is among the technician's `zone_codes`, the buffer is 25 minutes; and when it is not — the out-of-zone visit the previous paragraph permits — the buffer is 55 minutes. **`total_block_minutes`** is `service_minutes` plus the buffer, and it is the quantity that consumes route capacity.

Capacity and collision are checked in three independent ways, and each has its own answer. The route day's `committed_minutes` plus the new stop's `total_block_minutes` must be at most the day's `capacity_minutes`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The stop's own minute interval — half-open, beginning at `scheduled_start_utc` and ending `total_block_minutes` later — must not intersect the interval of any `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` stop on the same day, though intervals that merely touch are fine; an intersection is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OVERLAP`. And the instrument itself must not already hold a `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` appointment anywhere on that calendar `service_date`, on any technician's day whatsoever; a second one is refused with `ERR_INSTRUMENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED` even when the two minute intervals do not touch at all.

The stop's `estimated_price_cents` is list pricing only — client-type discounts and zone surcharges belong to the quote, never to an appointment estimate. The list price is the service's list price multiplied by the class price multiplier and rounded half up; a `TUNE` on an instrument at effective tier `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` adds the pitch-raise list price of 9500 cents multiplied by the same class multiplier and rounded half up separately. When the stop is `same_address_chained`, 20 percent of that list price, rounded half up, is subtracted from it — so a chained stop earns both the collapsed buffer and the discount; otherwise the estimate equals the list price with nothing taken off.

**Execution** runs on the second axis, `fulfillment_status`, which starts at `PENDING` and ends at one of `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `ABORTED`. Neither axis is written directly: stops are listed in `created_at` ascending order with exact-match filters on `instrument_id`, `route_day_id`, `location_id`, `service_order_id` and `tuning_recall_id` plus enum-validated filters on `scheduling_status` and `fulfillment_status`, and a `Client` actor sees only the stops on its own instruments whether or not it filters. Dispatch moves it to `EN_ROUTE` as described above. The technician then starts the stop, which requires the acting technician's identity to equal the route day's `technician_id` and moves the stop `EN_ROUTE → IN_PROGRESS`; if the stop carries a `service_order_id` whose order is still `PENDING`, that order moves to `IN_PROGRESS` and all of its milestones move to `READY`. Completing the stop moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and, when a recall is linked **and that recall is standing in `SCHEDULED`**, moves it to `FULFILLED` and stamps the instrument's `last_tuning_date` with the clock's date. The two writes travel together: a stop whose linked recall has since been moved elsewhere, and a stop carrying no recall at all, complete without touching `last_tuning_date` — the stamp is a consequence of fulfilling the recall, not of finishing the visit. Completing a stop does not complete, or otherwise touch, any linked service order. A no-show at the door moves the stop `EN_ROUTE → NO_SHOW`, simultaneously drives `scheduling_status` to `CANCELLED`, assesses the dispatched-tier cancellation fee, appends the resulting ledger row, recomputes the day's committed minutes and stop count with a version bump, and puts any linked `IN_PROGRESS` order into `ON_HOLD` — but it does **not** hand a linked recall back to `NOTIFIED` the way an ordinary cancellation does, so a no-shown recall is left stranded in `SCHEDULED` against a stop that will never run. Both axes move under a single `version` bump on the appointment.

On the booking axis, a `SCHEDULED` stop is **confirmed** by its client, provided its `fulfillment_status` is still `PENDING` and its route day is `OPEN` or `LOCKED`. A `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` stop may be **rescheduled** onto another day and time by supplying a new `route_day_id`, a new `scheduled_start_utc` and the target day's `expected_route_day_version`. Rescheduling requires `reschedule_count` below 2, requires `fulfillment_status` to be `PENDING`, and requires the target day to be `OPEN`. The technician-certification gate is **not** re-run on the target day: rung 34 names only `POST /service-orders`, `POST /appointments` and `POST /route-days/{id}/status-transitions`, and a reschedule is none of those. A stop may therefore land legally on a day whose technician is not certified for that stop's own `service_type`, and the violation surfaces only later, at dispatch, through rung 52. The reschedule itself increments `reschedule_count` by 1, recomputes buffers, price and capacity, recomputes committed minutes and stop count on **both** the vacated day and the target day with a version bump on each, and lands the appointment back in `SCHEDULED` — which means a confirmed stop loses its confirmation. The intermediate `RESCHEDULED` value exists only inside the request and is never persisted. A third attempt is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and the cap is tested after the overlap and double-booking checks but before the capacity check. When the target day is the day the stop already sits on, the stop's own `total_block_minutes` is subtracted from the day's committed figure before the capacity comparison — a stop is never made to compete with itself — and that day's `version` is then bumped once rather than twice. The reschedule fee is 20000 cents when the stop is already `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS`; otherwise it is 0 when the stop begins at least 48 hours after the logical clock, and 7500 cents when it begins sooner. "Begins" means the **old** `scheduled_start_utc`, the one being vacated, never the new one being asked for: the fee prices how much notice the shop is being given about the booking it already holds, so a stop moved from tomorrow to next month is charged at tomorrow's lead, and one moved from next month to tomorrow is charged at next month's. A non-zero fee is posted as a `CANCELLATION_FEE` ledger row for the negated amount, and the client's balance is recomputed.

**Cancellation** takes an optional `reason` and is available from `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` while `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING`, `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS`. It drives `scheduling_status` to `CANCELLED` and `fulfillment_status` to `ABORTED`, recomputes the route day's committed minutes and stop count with a version bump, and moves any linked recall `SCHEDULED → NOTIFIED` — the `reason` being accepted and stored nowhere. The fee lands in `cancellation_fee_cents`, which is 0 for as long as the stop is not cancelled and which a reschedule never writes, however large a reschedule fee it charges; a reschedule likewise leaves the linked recall exactly where it stands. The fee comes from one of two variants. Under the **dispatched tier**, selected when `fulfillment_status` is `EN_ROUTE` or `IN_PROGRESS`, the fee is 60 percent of `estimated_price_cents` rounded half up, or 15000 cents, whichever is larger — with no lead-time consideration at all, so a cancellation ten days out but after dispatch costs more than one an hour out before dispatch. Under the **lead-time tier**, selected when `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING`, the lead is measured in hours as a real number from the logical clock to `scheduled_start_utc`: a lead of at least 72.0 hours costs nothing; a lead of at least 24.0 but under 72.0 costs 15 percent of `estimated_price_cents` rounded half up; anything under 24.0 hours costs 35 percent rounded half up, with no floor. Both boundaries are inclusive on the upper side. The no-show path always uses the dispatched-tier variant.

The fee then has to be paid. When the stop carries a `service_order_id` whose order still holds deposit money, the fee is charged against the deposit first: the forfeited portion is the smaller of the fee and the deposit still unspoken for — `deposit_held_cents` less `deposit_applied_cents` less `deposit_refunded_cents`, that difference itself floored at 0 so that an order already over-spent forfeits nothing — and it is added to `deposit_forfeited_cents`, bumping the order's `version` by 1 only when that portion came out greater than 0. Whatever is left of the fee after that is posted as a `CANCELLATION_FEE` ledger row for the negated residual, and no row at all is written when the residual is 0. When there is no linked order, the whole fee posts as a `CANCELLATION_FEE` row. Either way the client's balance is recomputed afterwards whenever the fee itself was greater than 0, and not otherwise — a free cancellation touches no money at all. The `CANCELLATION_FEE` row carries the stop's `service_order_id` when the stop has one and null when it has not.

**Two open points for the implementer, and how they are settled.** First, the cancellation edge itself states that a fee greater than 0 is appended as a `CANCELLATION_FEE` ledger row for the negated fee, while the fee calculation states that the deposit absorbs it first and only the residual is posted; the two clauses are carried forward side by side, and **the fee calculation governs**. The deposit of a linked order absorbs the fee first, exactly as the paragraph above sets out, and the ledger row is appended only for the residual — so a cancellation whose fee the deposit swallows whole leaves `deposit_forfeited_cents` raised, the order's `version` bumped, and no `CANCELLATION_FEE` row at all. The cancellation edge's clause describes what happens when there is no linked order to absorb anything, which is the case it was written for. The balance is recomputed whenever the fee was greater than 0, whether or not a row was written. Second, `same_address_chained` is determined by comparing creation timestamps, and a rescheduled appointment keeps the `created_at` it was born with; whether a stop moved onto a new day counts as "earlier" than the stops already there is not settled by the scaffold, and it is settled here by taking the timestamps at their word. On a **booking**, every `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` stop already on the day is a candidate partner, since the stop being created is by construction the newest. On a **reschedule**, only stops whose `created_at` is strictly earlier than the moved stop's own `created_at` count — so a stop moved onto a day whose occupants were all booked after it does **not** chain, keeps the full 25- or 55-minute buffer and forfeits the 20 percent discount, however long it has been standing at that address. A stop is never its own partner.

### 3.8. Specialized Moves

Moving a piano is priced from five independent components and nothing else — there is no discount and no rounding after the sum. A move job is created with the `instrument_id`, an `origin_location_id`, a `destination_location_id`, a `distance_miles` strictly greater than zero, and a `scheduled_window_start_utc` and `scheduled_window_end_utc` where the end must be strictly after the start. Three further inputs are optional: `crane_required` and `climate_controlled_transport`, both defaulting to false, and `move_technician_id` naming the crew lead, which is resolved for existence but is not certification-checked at creation — the crew-certification gate belongs to the move into `SCHEDULED`, not to the drafting. Either window instant that fails to parse as ISO-8601 is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` before any id is resolved, while an end that is not strictly after the start is refused with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` at rung 7; both instants are stored and re-served byte-verbatim. Rung 17 binds **both** endpoints here: the origin's owning client and the destination's owning client must each equal the instrument's owner, so a move cannot be used to deliver an instrument to a third party's address. The job copies its `client_id` from the instrument's owner and starts in status `DRAFT`.

The **base component**, `base_cents`, is read straight off the instrument's class: 28000 cents for an `UPRIGHT`, 32000 for a `CONSOLE`, 52000 for a `BABY_GRAND`, 68000 for a `GRAND` and 145000 for a `CONCERT_GRAND`.

The **stair component**, `stair_cents`, starts from each address's effective flights: an address with `has_service_elevator` true contributes 0, and otherwise it contributes its `stair_flights`. Adding the origin's and the destination's effective flights gives `stair_flights_total`. When that total is at most 3, the charge is 9000 cents per flight, so three flights cost 27000 cents. When it reaches 4 or more, the charge collapses to a flat bundle of 25000 cents regardless of how many flights there are — which means four flights cost **less** than three, and the stair price is deliberately not monotone in the flight count.

The **crane component**, `crane_cents`, is 185000 cents when `crane_required` is true and 0 otherwise. A `CONCERT_GRAND` whose `stair_flights_total` is at least 2 **must** set `crane_required` true; a job created without it is refused with `ERR_CRANE_REQUIRED`.

The **distance component**, `distance_cents`, bands the `distance_miles` and records both the band and the variant that produced it. At most 25.0 miles the `distance_band` is `LOCAL`, the `distance_variant` is `FLAT_LOCAL`, and the charge is a flat 6000 cents. Above 25.0 and up to 150.0 miles the band is `REGIONAL`, the variant is `PER_MILE`, and the charge is the mileage multiplied by 420 cents per mile and rounded half up — at exactly 150.0 miles that comes to 63000 cents. Above 150.0 miles the band is `LONG_HAUL`, the variant is `LONG_HAUL_FLAT`, and the charge is a flat 52000 cents — so a 151-mile move is cheaper than a 150-mile one, and this price too is deliberately not monotone.

The **climate component**, `climate_cents`, is 38000 cents when `climate_controlled_transport` is true and 0 otherwise. `total_cents` is the plain sum of the five components.

One booking rule guards the instrument: a new move's window, widened by 90 minutes on **both** sides, must not intersect the *unwidened* window of any other move for the same instrument whose status is `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED` or `IN_TRANSIT`. The comparison is a closed interval, so windows that merely touch after widening **do** collide — the opposite of the appointment-overlap rule. A collision is refused with `ERR_MOVE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The move axis is driven by supplying a `to_status` with an optional `reason` that is accepted and stored nowhere, the `MoveJob` carrying no field for it. A `DRAFT` job moves to `SCHEDULED` once `move_technician_id` is set — a job without one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, ahead of the certification gate — and, when the instrument is a `CONCERT_GRAND` or `crane_required` is true, only when that crew lead's `move_certified` is true, failing which the answer is `ERR_MOVE_CREW_NOT_CERTIFIED`. A `SCHEDULED` job moves to `DISPATCHED` provided the instrument's `custody_status` is `AT_CLIENT_SITE`, `IN_SHOP` or `SHOWROOM_FLOOR`; a custody outside that set is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and **not** with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`, which this operation never raises. `IN_TRANSIT` and `DELIVERED` are declared members of the axis that only the custody transitions of §3.2 ever write, so asking for either here answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rung 23. From there the job is advanced by the custody transitions described in §3.2 rather than directly: quoting the job on a move into `IN_TRANSIT` advances it `DISPATCHED → IN_TRANSIT`, and quoting it on the arrival leg advances it `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED` and writes the job's `destination_location_id` into the instrument's `current_location_id`. A job in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` may be cancelled. `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and every edge bumps the job's `version` by 1. Move jobs are listed with filters on `instrument_id`, `client_id`, `origin_location_id`, `destination_location_id`, `move_technician_id` and `status`, and read back individually with `base_cents`, `stair_cents`, `crane_cents`, `distance_cents`, `climate_cents` and `total_cents` all exposed.

### 3.9. Warranty Cover, Claims and Adjudication

Completing a service order mints a warranty automatically; there is no operation that creates one directly. The warranty copies `service_order_id`, `instrument_id`, `client_id` and `service_type` from the completing order, sets `start_date` to the logical clock's date at completion, sets `covered_price_cents` to the order's `contract_total_cents`, starts `claim_count` at 0 and `transferred_from_client_id` and `void_reason` at null, and enters status `ACTIVE`. The `term_days` follows the service performed: 90 days for a `TUNE`, 45 for a `PITCH_RAISE`, 365 for a `REGULATE`, 180 for a `VOICE`, and 1095 for a `REBUILD`. The `end_date` is `start_date` advanced by `term_days` calendar days. Warranties are listed with filters on `instrument_id`, `service_order_id`, `client_id` and `status`, and read back individually with their term, dates and claim count.

A warranty leaves `ACTIVE` by one of three routes. It **expires** when the daily sweep finds the clock's date strictly after `end_date`, which means it is still live throughout its final day. It becomes **transferred** when the instrument is sold off the showroom floor, as Cascade C describes. Or it is **voided**, which can happen four ways: a `ShopManager` voids it explicitly by supplying a `void_reason`; the unauthorized-move cascade voids it with reason `UNAUTHORIZED_MOVE`; the lapsed-recall sweep voids it with reason `TUNING_CYCLE_LAPSED`; and the climate gate on claim filing voids it with reason `CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND`. The fourth permitted reason, `MANUAL`, is the one an operator supplies deliberately — though `void_reason` is validated only for membership in the four, so an operator may also stamp a manual void with `UNAUTHORIZED_MOVE`, `TUNING_CYCLE_LAPSED` or `CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND` and nothing objects; the field records the reason given, not the route taken. Every void — whatever its origin — denies the warranty's open claims, as Cascade A hops 3 through 5 describe. A `TRANSFERRED` warranty can still be voided explicitly and can still expire; it keeps covering the instrument under its new owner in the meantime.

**Filing a claim** supplies the `warranty_id`, a free-text `reported_issue` describing the defect, and the `expected_warranty_version`. Filing bumps the warranty's `version` by 1 and its `claim_count` by 1, and a warranty already at 2 claims refuses a third with `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The claim copies `service_order_id` from the warranty and enters status `SUBMITTED` with `adjudicated_by_user_id` null. Filing is the one action operation in this document that applies **no** terminal gate to the row it acts on: a claim may be filed against a warranty standing in any status whatever, `VOIDED` and `EXPIRED` included, so long as the version matches, the claim cap has room and the climate gate lets it through. The elapsed fraction is then computed from that warranty's own `start_date` and `term_days` as they now stand — which, for a warranty already transferred once, are the *re-based* figures of the transfer and not the originals.

The resolution is decided at submission, from how much of the term has already run. `elapsed_fraction` is the whole days elapsed from the warranty's `start_date` to the clock's date, divided by `term_days`, clamped into the range 0.0 through 1.0, and rounded half up to **four** decimal places — and the rounding happens *before* the comparison and before the credit is multiplied, not after. Only the named warranty is read; other claims on the same warranty do not move the fraction. When `elapsed_fraction` is **strictly below** 0.50, the `resolution_variant` is `FREE_RESERVICE`, `credit_cents` is 0, and no ledger row is appended at all — the client is entitled to have the work redone rather than to money. When it is 0.50 or above, the `resolution_variant` is `PRORATED_CREDIT` and `credit_cents` is `covered_price_cents` multiplied by one minus `elapsed_fraction`, rounded half up to whole cents; a provisional `WARRANTY_CREDIT` ledger row for that positive amount is appended immediately, at submission, before anyone has looked at the claim — posted to the warranty's current `client_id` and recomputing that client's balance without bumping the client's `version`. `elapsed_fraction` is stored and served as the four-decimal number the rounding produced, trailing zeros not preserved, and `credit_cents` is stored as 0 rather than null on the `FREE_RESERVICE` branch.

A claim also cannot be filed while the instrument's current location is judged out of band; that gate answers `ERR_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_BAND` and, as §2.9 records, voids the parent warranty on its way out.

A `ShopManager` takes a `SUBMITTED` claim `UNDER_REVIEW`, then adjudicates it with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`. Both outcomes require the adjudicating `X-User-ID` to differ from every `released_by_user_id` on the covered order's milestones, and both stamp `adjudicated_by_user_id` with the acting identity. The set compared against is exactly the non-null `released_by_user_id` values standing on the milestones of the claim's own `service_order_id` at the moment of adjudication — which means a release the client subsequently *rejected* has had that field cleared back to null by §3.6 and no longer bars its author, and an order with no released milestone at all bars nobody. **Approval routes by variant**: a claim whose `resolution_variant` is `FREE_RESERVICE` lands in `APPROVED_RESERVICE`, and a claim whose variant is `PRORATED_CREDIT` lands in `APPROVED_CREDIT`, where the provisional credit simply stands and no reversal is appended. **Denial** lands the claim in `DENIED` and reverses the money: a `REVERSAL` ledger row is appended carrying the negation of the provisional `WARRANTY_CREDIT` row's amount, with `reverses_entry_id` pointing at that row and `warranty_claim_id` pointing at this claim, the original row left completely untouched; then the client's `account_credit_cents` is recomputed as the plain sum over all of that client's rows and the client's `version` is bumped by 1. The three outcomes are terminal. A claim can also reach `DENIED` without anyone adjudicating it, straight from `SUBMITTED`, when its parent warranty is voided — and by that route `adjudicated_by_user_id` stays null, which is what tells the two denials apart on a later read. Claims are listed in `created_at` ascending order with exact-match filters on `warranty_id` and `service_order_id` and an enum-validated filter on `status`; a `Client` actor sees only the claims whose warranty it owns, whether or not it filters. Warranties are listed the same way, in `created_at` ascending order, with exact-match filters on `instrument_id`, `service_order_id` and `client_id` and an enum-validated `status`.

### 3.10. Consignment, the Showroom Floor and the Sale

The showroom desk takes instruments on consignment. An agreement is drafted with the `instrument_id`, the `consignor_client_id` — which must be the instrument's current owner — a `reserve_price_cents` that must be a JSON integer of at least 50000, and the `expected_instrument_version`. Drafting is not gated on custody at all — an instrument may be put under agreement wherever it stands, and only activation and listing demand `IN_SHOP` — nor on there being no other agreement already drafted against the same instrument. Drafting stamps `created_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, bumps the instrument's `version` by 1, and enters status `DRAFT` with `buyer_client_id`, `sale_price_cents`, `split_variant` and `expires_at` all null and `advanced_restoration_cents`, `advance_recovered_cents`, `shop_commission_cents` and `consignor_payout_cents` all 0.

A `ShowroomAgent` **activates** the agreement, which requires the instrument's `custody_status` to be `IN_SHOP` — a gate that runs at rung 22, ahead of the edge test, so an agreement that is both in the wrong status *and* against an instrument out of the shop answers `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Activation sets `start_date` to the clock's date, sets `expires_at` to 180 days after the clock rendered in the clock's own style, and computes `advanced_restoration_cents` as the sum of `contract_total_cents` over that instrument's `COMPLETED` service orders whose `service_type` is `REBUILD` — the shop's own money sunk into restoring the piano, which it recovers off the top when the piano sells. That sum is taken once, at activation, and is never recomputed afterwards: a rebuild completed between activation and the sale contributes nothing to the advance the shop recovers. The agent then **lists** the instrument to the showroom, which requires the clock to be strictly before `expires_at`, moves the agreement `ACTIVE → LISTED`, moves the instrument `IN_SHOP → SHOWROOM_FLOOR` and stamps the instrument's `consignment_agreement_id`.

**Recording the sale** supplies a `buyer_client_id` and a `sale_price_cents` that must be a JSON integer greater than zero. The buyer must differ from the consignor, the agreement must be `LISTED`, the clock must still be strictly before `expires_at`, and the recording `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`. The price must also clear a floor: the reserve price multiplied by 0.80 and rounded half up to whole cents, compared inclusively, so a sale landing exactly on the floor is accepted while anything below is refused with `ERR_SALE_BELOW_FLOOR`.

The **proceeds split** then runs, and the order of its four steps is what makes the money conserve. First, `advance_recovered_cents` is the smaller of `advanced_restoration_cents` and `sale_price_cents` — the shop cannot recover more advance than the piano fetched. Second, the residual is `sale_price_cents` less `advance_recovered_cents`. Third, the commission is computed from one of two variants. When `sale_price_cents` is at most `reserve_price_cents`, `split_variant` is `AT_OR_BELOW_RESERVE` and the commission is 25 percent of the whole residual, rounded half up. When the sale beats the reserve, `split_variant` is `ABOVE_RESERVE`: the excess is `sale_price_cents` less `reserve_price_cents`; the reserve portion is the residual less that excess, floored at 0; the excess portion is the residual less the reserve portion; and the commission is 15 percent of the reserve portion rounded half up **plus** 50 percent of the excess portion rounded half up, the two legs rounded independently before being added. The marginal rate therefore jumps above reserve while the average rate can fall below 25 percent. Fourth, `consignor_payout_cents` is the residual less the commission, and `shop_commission_cents` is computed **last**, as `sale_price_cents` less `advance_recovered_cents` less `consignor_payout_cents`, so that any rounding remainder lands on the shop and the three parts add back to the sale price exactly.

A `CONSIGNMENT_PROCEEDS` ledger row for `consignor_payout_cents` is posted to the consignor, and Cascade C then re-owns everything the sale touched: the instrument moves `SHOWROOM_FLOOR → SOLD` with its `client_id` re-pointed at the buyer, every `ACTIVE` warranty on it moves to `TRANSFERRED`, and every service order of that instrument still in `PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `ON_HOLD` has its `client_id` re-pointed at the buyer too.

A transferred warranty does not simply carry on. Its remaining term is recomputed: the whole days elapsed from the warranty's `start_date` to the sale date are subtracted from its `term_days` to give the remainder, and the new `term_days` is that remainder or 30 days, whichever is **larger** — so even a nearly exhausted warranty transfers with a month of cover. The warranty's `start_date` is reset to the sale date, its `end_date` becomes the sale date advanced by the new term, its `client_id` is re-pointed at the buyer, and `transferred_from_client_id` records the consignor. That last field is retained for audit only and is never used as a lookup key or as an input to any decision. Warranties are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

An agreement can also end without a sale. A `ShowroomAgent` **withdraws** an `ACTIVE` or `LISTED` agreement with an optional `reason`, and the daily sweep **expires** an `ACTIVE` or `LISTED` agreement once the clock reaches `expires_at`. Both clear the instrument's `consignment_agreement_id` and, when the instrument is standing on the showroom floor, return its custody to `AT_CLIENT_SITE`; the withdrawal's `reason` is accepted and stored nowhere. Those two writes are independent and the instrument's `version` is bumped once if either fired and not at all if neither did — an agreement withdrawn before it was ever listed leaves the instrument entirely untouched, because the link was never stamped and the piano never left the shop. The clearing is also keyed to *this* agreement: the link is cleared only when `consignment_agreement_id` still names the agreement being ended, so a later agreement's stamp is never wiped by an earlier one's withdrawal. `SOLD`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal. Agreements are listed in `created_at` ascending order with exact-match filters on `instrument_id`, `consignor_client_id` and `buyer_client_id` and an enum-validated `status`, and read back individually with the whole proceeds split exposed; a `Client` actor sees only the agreements on which it is the consignor or the buyer, and is refused at rung 4 for filtering on anyone else's id.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Creating a new resource answers 201 Created and returns the new resource; every other successful operation answers 200 OK and returns the affected resource, or the requested resource for a read. Listing operations answer 200 OK with an array and the pagination fields in `meta`. The two are told apart by **what the response body carries**, not by the shape of the path: an operation answers 201 when the resource in its `data` is a row this very request minted, and 200 in every other case, including when the request minted rows of its own but returns something older. So `POST /clients/{id}/credit-adjustments` answers 201 with the new `LedgerEntry` it appends, and `POST /quotes/{id}/reissue` answers 201 with the freshly minted replacement quote rather than with the superseded one — while `POST /service-orders/{id}/complete` answers 200 with the completed order even though it mints a `Warranty` on the way, and every operation that merely appends `LedgerEntry` rows as a side effect answers 200 with whatever resource it was really acting on. A `POST` on a sub-collection path is no exception: `POST /instruments/{id}/pitch-assessments` mints nothing — this document declares no `PitchAssessment` entity, and §3.3 specifies the operation purely as writes onto the instrument — so it answers **200** with the updated instrument, and the four status-transition paths (`POST /instruments/{id}/custody-transitions`, `POST /instruments/{id}/condition-transitions`, `POST /route-days/{id}/status-transitions` and `POST /move-jobs/{id}/status-transitions`) likewise answer 200 with the instrument, route day or move job they drive. Two operations return no resource at all and are the only exceptions to "returns the affected resource": `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` answers 200 with the two-key object of §2.5, and `POST /_ops/daily-sweep` answers 200 with the six-count object of §3.4.
