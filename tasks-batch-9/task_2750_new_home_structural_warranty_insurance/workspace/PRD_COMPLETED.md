# New-Home Structural Warranty Insurance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A new-home structural warranty is a promise that outlives the handshake at closing. The buyer of a newly built house receives one year of workmanship coverage, two years of systems coverage and ten years of structural coverage — but the party who actually answers for a defect changes partway through that span. For the first two years the builder is on the hook and repairs in kind; after that the carrier pays cash against a policy limit and a deductible. Carriers that administer these programs live or die on their ability to keep those two periods straight, to know which builders are generating losses faster than they are generating premium, and to keep a builder's collapse from stranding the homeowners it left behind.

This document specifies the API for exactly that program. The service carries a builder enrollment file from a declared-data underwriting pass through approval, ongoing loss-ratio monitoring, reassessment on observed data, suspension, reinstatement and — in the worst case — a declaration of default that pulls every open builder-obligation claim onto the carrier's own balance sheet. Around that file it manages home enrollments priced at closing, three base coverage terms per home plus optional endorsement terms, warranty transfers to subsequent buyers, claim intake with obligor assignment, engineering assessments that decide whether damage counts as structural, a reserve ledger with payment and reversal, and a two-rung escalation ladder of non-binding mediation followed by binding arbitration.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every derived number is reproducible**: premiums, risk scores, deductibles, betterment offsets, loss ratios and transfer fees are all computed by pinned formulas with pinned evaluation order and pinned rounding, so that two implementations of this specification produce byte-identical figures. Second, **money on a claim is conserved**: the amount authorised on a claim always equals what is still reserved plus what has been disbursed plus what has been released, and the capacity of a coverage term always equals what remains plus what has been consumed. Every endpoint in this document either respects those two identities or is a bug. All resources are mounted under the `/warranty` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described in §4.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — read or write — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, empty, or consists only of whitespace, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401 before anything else about the request is examined. Beyond that blank test neither value is trimmed or normalised: the role must match one of the five names below character for character, and `X-User-ID` is carried verbatim into every ownership comparison and every actor stamp, so surrounding whitespace makes it a different principal. If both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names declared below, or is a declared role that does not appear in the authorized-roles list of the endpoint being called, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403.

The five roles are `UnderwritingOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `BuilderPrincipal` and `Homeowner`. The first three are carrier-side staff functions; the last two are external parties whose identity is anchored in persisted rows of this system.

An `UnderwritingOfficer` is the carrier's underwriter. It opens and scores builder files, records underwriting decisions, re-scores approved builders under monitoring, lifts suspensions, bills and activates home enrollments, attaches endorsement coverage terms, and administers transfer requests end to end. When `X-User-Role` is `UnderwritingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow calls for an actor stamp (for example as `reviewer_user_id` on an underwriting review) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` handles market conduct. It suspends builders, declares builders in default, curates the panel of neutrals, and is the only role permitted to reverse a payment that has already been disbursed or to move the server's logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it may be recorded as an actor stamp such as `recorded_by_user_id` on a reserve-journal row, but it is never matched against an entity `id`.

A `ClaimsAdjuster` handles claims: investigation, engineering assessment, obligation routing, adjudication, reserve authorisation, disbursement, and the recording of escalation outcomes and arbitration awards. When `X-User-Role` is `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is nevertheless significant: the server records it as `adjudicated_by_user_id` when a claim is adjudicated, as `assessed_by_user_id` on an engineering report, as `recorded_by_user_id` on reserve-journal rows, and as `outcome_recorded_by_user_id` when a session outcome is recorded, and two of those stamps are later compared against the acting `X-User-ID` by the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.10.

A `BuilderPrincipal` is the enrolling home builder acting on its own file. When `X-User-Role` is `BuilderPrincipal`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Builder` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a builder compare the resource's builder foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the addressed builder is either the builder named in the URL path, or the `builder_id` of the addressed policy, or the `builder_id` of the policy behind the addressed claim, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_BUILDER_PRINCIPAL` and HTTP 403. This check guards builder submission, home enrollment, premium payment, the builder's response to an obligation notice, and the report of a completed repair.

A `Homeowner` is the buyer who holds the warranty. When `X-User-Role` is `Homeowner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Homeowner` row; ownership checks compare the addressed policy's `current_owner_id` to `X-User-ID`, with one exception — on the resubmission of a transfer request the comparison is against that request's `requested_by_owner_id` instead, because a policy can change hands while a request is in flight. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_POLICY_OWNER` and HTTP 403. This check guards transfer requests, transfer resubmission, claim filing, and the opening of an escalation rung.

Read endpoints are shared broadly, but not uniformly, and the authorized-roles list in the endpoint table of §4 is authoritative for every read. Most reads admit all three carrier-side roles, with four narrower exceptions: the builder list, a builder's review journal and a builder's loss monitor admit only `UnderwritingOfficer` and `ComplianceOfficer`, and the neutral-panel list admits only `ComplianceOfficer`, `ClaimsAdjuster` and `Homeowner`. A `BuilderPrincipal` may read builder files, policies and their coverage terms, individual coverage terms, claims and engineering assessments. A `Homeowner` may read homeowner records, policies and coverage terms, the neutral panel, transfer requests, claims, engineering assessments and escalation cases. The reserve journal is carrier-side only: only `UnderwritingOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `ClaimsAdjuster` may read reserve entries. Registering a homeowner is shared between `UnderwritingOfficer` and `BuilderPrincipal`. The complete authorization matrix is in the endpoint table of §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is an envelope stamp rather than a domain instant, so it is read from the host wall clock and not from the logical clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside the range from 1 to 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is below 0, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 422. Integerhood is judged on the raw query text: an optional leading `+` or `-` followed by digits is an integer, and any other text — an empty value, a decimal point, a non-numeric token — fails that same check, while a parameter that is simply absent takes its default.

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, results are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first. Because `created_at` is a wall-clock stamp with only millisecond resolution, two rows can share one value; ties are broken by the order in which the server inserted the rows, so an ascending page is insertion order and a descending page is exactly its reverse. Three list endpoints override that default because their business reading order differs: the review journal of a builder is returned newest first, the coverage terms of a home are returned with `BASE` terms before `ENDORSEMENT` terms, then by `start_date` ascending, and then by `created_at` and insertion order, and the engineering assessments of a claim and the reserve journal of a claim are both returned oldest first.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the query, `total_count` reports the number of rows that match the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is below `total_count` — so an `offset` at or beyond `total_count` yields an empty `data` array with `has_more` false. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource this API persists carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. The identifier has no type prefix and no embedded meaning: clients must not parse it, and the server must not accept a client-supplied `id` on any create. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional-second digits, set once when the row is inserted and never rewritten. The audit stamps `created_at` and `updated_at` are the one family of instants in this API that is *not* read from the logical clock of the paragraphs below: they record the host wall clock at the moment the row was written, so they advance between two requests even when no operator has moved the logical clock, and they are never compared against any domain date or deadline.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 each time the row is mutated; on a freshly created row `updated_at` is identical to `created_at`. `Builder`, `Neutral`, `WarrantyPolicy`, `CoverageTerm`, `WarrantyTransfer`, `Claim` and `EscalationCase` all carry both. Four resources are deliberately immutable-once-written journal or reference rows and carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: an underwriting review, a homeowner record, an engineering assessment and a reserve-journal entry each expose neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and no request ever mutates one after creation.

A single mutating request bumps `version` on a given row by exactly one, no matter how many fields inside that row it rewrites. When a request rewrites a row twice as part of one workflow — for example the reassessment that first stores new declared inputs, then re-scores, then suspends the builder in the same call — the builder's `version` still advances by exactly 1 for the whole request. When a request cascades into other rows, each affected row's own `version` advances by 1 as well.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. The clock starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the server boots and moves only when an operator advances it; a boot is a clean slate in every other respect too, with every collection empty and no entity data of any kind seeded, so the clock and the data always restart together. Every date and instant comparison in this document — coverage windows, obligation boundaries, escalation deadlines, session windows, term expiry — reads that clock, and every date or instant the server computes for itself (a claim's `reported_date`, an assessment's `assessment_date`, a claim's `denial_date`, a builder's `default_declared_at`) is taken from it. The clock moves forward only: an instant earlier than the clock's current instant is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` and HTTP 422.

### 2.5. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in **cents**. No monetary field is ever a decimal or a string. Rates, ratios and scores are decimal numbers with a pinned number of decimal places, carried on the wire as JSON numbers in both directions: a rate supplied as a string is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because a JSON number has no notion of trailing zeros a stored `loss_ratio` of `0.0000` is serialised as the number `0.0`. Every rounding in this document is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, so 0.125 at two decimal places becomes 0.13.

The pinned precisions are as follows. A builder's `risk_score` carries 2 decimal places, and the band comparison that assigns `risk_class` is made against the already-rounded score, never against the unrounded intermediate. A builder's `loss_ratio` carries 4 decimal places, and the band comparison that assigns `monitoring_posture` is likewise made against the already-rounded ratio. A claim's `substantiation_ratio` is supplied by the adjuster with at most 2 decimal places and must lie between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive. An engineering assessment's `deflection_ratio` is supplied with at most 4 decimal places and must lie between 0.0000 and 0.5000 inclusive, and is compared against the structural threshold at 4 decimal places.

Premium arithmetic rounds **per component, not once at the end**: each of the three coverage components is rounded to whole cents independently and the three rounded integers are then added together. The flat per-state surcharge is added last, as an exact integer, and is never rounded. Claim settlement arithmetic rounds in exactly two places — when the substantiated gross is derived from the claimed amount, and when the betterment offset is derived from the provisional net; every other step of the settlement waterfall is exact integer arithmetic. The two conservation identities described in §2.9, the coverage-term limit arithmetic, and the transfer fee are all exact integer arithmetic with no rounding anywhere. Coverage-term limits derived from the purchase price are rounded to whole cents before the cap is applied. Dates are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component — exactly four digits, a hyphen, two digits, a hyphen and two digits, naming a real calendar day — and all date arithmetic is calendar-day arithmetic. Instants supplied in a request body are ISO 8601 date-times that may end in `Z` or in a numeric UTC offset and are normalised to UTC before any comparison; an instant carrying no zone designator at all is read as UTC. Every instant the server emits, whether an audit stamp or a domain instant, is rendered in the `Z`-suffixed three-fractional-digit form.

### 2.6. Nested Paths and Foreign-Key Discovery

Several endpoints address a resource through a nested path — the coverage terms of a policy, the engineering assessments of a claim, the review journal of a builder. Path parameters are resolved **strictly left to right**, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. A request naming a builder that does not exist yields `ERR_BUILDER_NOT_FOUND`; a homeowner, `ERR_HOMEOWNER_NOT_FOUND`; a policy, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`; a coverage term, `ERR_COVERAGE_TERM_NOT_FOUND`; a claim, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; a transfer request, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`; an escalation case, `ERR_ESCALATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; a neutral, `ERR_NEUTRAL_NOT_FOUND`; a reserve-journal entry, `ERR_RESERVE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. All nine are HTTP 404.

The same catalogue applies to foreign keys supplied in a request body rather than in the path. A `builder_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_BUILDER_NOT_FOUND`; an `initial_owner_id` or `new_owner_id`, `ERR_HOMEOWNER_NOT_FOUND`; a `policy_id`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`; a `neutral_id`, `ERR_NEUTRAL_NOT_FOUND`; a `prior_case_id`, `ERR_ESCALATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; a `reserve_entry_id`, `ERR_RESERVE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key resolution is checked on home enrollment, on transfer request creation, on claim filing, on the opening of an escalation rung, and on a payment reversal.

Because this domain is a graph rather than a tree — a claim points at a policy, at a coverage term and at a homeowner; an escalation case points at a claim, at a neutral and at a predecessor case; a transfer points at a policy, at a homeowner and at the request it superseded — the API guarantees a **reverse-reachability channel** for every foreign key. Each list endpoint accepts filters on the foreign keys that point into it, so a client that holds one identifier can always discover the rows that reference it. Policies are filterable by `builder_id`, `initial_owner_id`, `current_owner_id` and `pending_transfer_id` as well as by `coverage_status` and `premium_status`. Coverage terms of a policy are filterable by `coverage_type`, `term_kind` and `status`. Transfers are filterable by `policy_id`, `new_owner_id`, `prior_transfer_id` and `status`. Claims are filterable by `policy_id`, `coverage_term_id`, `filed_by_owner_id`, `status` and `obligor`. Engineering assessments of a claim are filterable by `designated_structural`. Reserve entries are filterable by `claim_id`, `reversed_entry_id` and `entry_type`, and this is the only channel through which a client can discover the identifier of a system-created payment entry that a reversal must name. Escalation cases are filterable by `claim_id`, `neutral_id`, `prior_case_id`, `stage` and `status`, and filtering by `neutral_id` is also how a client inspects a booking conflict on the panel. Builder files are filterable by `underwriting_status`, `risk_class` and `monitoring_posture`, the review journal by `decision`, and the neutral panel by `credential`, `status` and `state_code`. Every one of these list endpoints also accepts `limit` and `offset`. Filter values are matched by exact string equality against the stored field, so a filter naming a value that is not a declared enum member, or an identifier that resolves to nothing, is **not** a validation error and never a 404 — it simply matches no row and the caller receives an empty page whose `total_count` is 0. The one boolean filter, `designated_structural`, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` in any letter case and likewise matches no row on any other text.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system are dangerous precisely because they are *not* blocked by any state precondition: repeating them succeeds, and each repetition silently discards something the client never saw. Both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control on the parent row's `version`.

A `POST /warranty/policies/{id}/transfers` request must carry `expected_policy_version`, a required (never optional) integer field holding the `version` of the target `WarrantyPolicy` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the policy's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The rule exists because the product deliberately lets a new transfer request supersede whatever request is already open on the policy — the seller genuinely may have changed buyers — so nothing in the state machine stops a create that would quietly supersede a request the client never observed. Checking the policy's `version` turns that hazard into a deterministic stale-version rejection.

A `POST /warranty/claims/{id}/reserve-entries` request must carry `expected_claim_version`, a required (never optional) integer field holding the `version` of the target `Claim` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the claim's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Here the hazard is that re-reserving is a legitimate repeated operation: each entry *overwrites* the reserve target rather than adding to it, so a second entry submitted against an out-of-date reading would succeed on its own terms while discarding a reserve level the client never observed.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind. In particular, none of the premium-axis operations, none of the claim-lifecycle transitions, and none of the escalation operations take an expected-version input, and supplying one to them has no effect.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

When a request would fail more than one check, the outcome is not a matter of taste. Checks are evaluated in the ascending order below and **the first failure wins**; later checks are never reached and never mask an earlier one. Readers implementing an endpoint should treat this ladder as the endpoint's control flow.

1. **Authentication (401).** Both identity headers present and non-blank, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role admission (403).** `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles and is listed on the called endpoint, or `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Builder-principal ownership (403).** `ERR_NOT_BUILDER_PRINCIPAL`, on exactly five operations: builder submission, home enrollment, premium payment, the builder's obligation response, and the report of repair completion.
4. **Homeowner ownership (403).** `ERR_NOT_POLICY_OWNER`, on exactly four operations: transfer request creation, transfer resubmission, claim filing, and the opening of an escalation rung.
5. **Segregation of duties (403).** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on exactly two operations: claim payment and arbitration award.
6. **Path existence (404).** Every identifier in the URL resolves, left to right, per §2.6. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
7. **Body foreign-key existence (404).** Every foreign key in the request body resolves, per §2.6. Applies to home enrollment, transfer request creation, claim filing, escalation opening, and payment reversal.
8. **Request shape (422).** `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when a required body field is missing, is of the wrong type, falls outside its declared numeric range, is an enum value that is not a declared member, when an amount field is below its declared floor, or when `affected_elements` contains duplicate entries. Applies to every endpoint with a request body. A request body that is absent, unparseable or not a JSON object is treated as an empty object, so it fails on the first required field the endpoint declares and is perfectly acceptable on an endpoint that declares none; and a field supplied explicitly as JSON `null` is treated as absent, meaning `null` on an optional field takes that field's default and `null` on a required field is the missing-field error.
9. **Pagination shape (422).** `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` per §2.3. Applies to every list endpoint.
10. **Clock monotonicity (422).** `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` on the logical-clock endpoint when `to_iso8601` is earlier than the clock's current instant.
11. **Optimistic concurrency (412).** `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` on transfer request creation and `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` on reserve-entry creation, per §2.7.
12. **From-state admission (409).** The addressed row is in a declared origin state for the requested transition, or the matching state error: `ERR_INVALID_BUILDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE` for the coverage axis, `ERR_INVALID_PREMIUM_STATE` for the premium axis, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ESCALATION_STATE`, or `ERR_INVALID_NEUTRAL_STATE`. Applies to every state-transition endpoint.
13. **Cross-entity and cross-axis state guards (409),** in this internal order: `ERR_BUILDER_NOT_APPROVED` (home enrollment and policy activation), then `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_PAID` (activation), then `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ON_HOLD` (activation), then `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE` (claim filing, transfer request creation, endorsement-term creation), then `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED` (adjudication and payment), then the neutral-eligibility pair `ERR_NEUTRAL_INACTIVE` and `ERR_NEUTRAL_CREDENTIAL_MISMATCH` (escalation opening), then `ERR_MISSING_TRANSFER_DOCUMENTATION` (transfer decision), then `ERR_BUILDER_DEFAULTED` (obligation routing).
14. **Business-safety guards (409),** in this internal order: `ERR_STRUCTURAL_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` (adjudication), then `ERR_MEDIATION_NOT_EXHAUSTED` (escalation opening), then `ERR_RISK_CLASS_INELIGIBLE` (underwriting decision).
15. **Temporal guards,** in this internal order: `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED` at HTTP 409 (session outcome and award), then `ERR_COVERAGE_TERM_ELAPSED` at 422 (transfer request creation), then `ERR_NO_COVERAGE_IN_FORCE` at 422 (claim filing), then `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_ELAPSED` at 422 (escalation opening), then `ERR_NEUTRAL_WINDOW_CONFLICT` at 409 (escalation opening), then `ERR_COVERAGE_TERM_OVERLAP` at 409 (endorsement-term creation).
16. **Referential guards,** in this internal order: `ERR_TRANSFEREE_IS_CURRENT_OWNER` at 422 (transfer request creation), then `ERR_PRIOR_CASE_CLAIM_MISMATCH` at 422 (escalation opening), then `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTE_BLOCKS_TRANSFER` at 409 (transfer recording), then `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_REVERSIBLE` at 422 (payment reversal).
17. **Fiscal guards,** in this internal order: `ERR_BUILDER_POSTURE_FROZEN` at 409 (home enrollment and reinstatement), then `ERR_POSTURE_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED` at 409 (home enrollment), then `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_RESERVE` at 422 (payment), then `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` at 422 (payment), then `ERR_INITIAL_RESERVE_ALREADY_SET` at 422 (reserve entry), then `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_BELOW_DISBURSED` at 422 (reserve entry).

Three consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. Because the stale-version check sits at rung 11, ahead of every state guard, a client holding an out-of-date reading of a policy receives `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` rather than a downstream complaint about coverage status or transfer dates. And because the posture gates sit at rung 17, behind everything else, a frozen builder attempting an enrollment that is *also* malformed hears about the malformation first. And rungs 3 through 5 can only be evaluated once the row they compare against is in hand, so they precede the 404 of rung 6 only where the comparison is against an identifier the request itself supplies — builder submission, which matches `X-User-ID` against the builder id in the path, and home enrollment, which matches it against the `builder_id` in the body. On every other operation guarded by those three rungs the addressed row must resolve first, so a request that is both aimed at a row that does not exist and unauthorised receives the 404.

### 2.9. Two Conserved Quantities and Cascade Discipline

The claim ledger maintains a hard invariant that must hold after **every** request that touches it: a claim's `reserve_cents` plus its `paid_cents` plus its `released_cents` always equals its `incurred_authorized_cents`. That identity is the definition of "authorised indemnity" in this program, and each of the six movement types is designed to preserve it. Authorising a reserve sets `incurred_authorized_cents` to the sum of the claim's existing `paid_cents`, its existing `released_cents` and the newly authorised amount, and sets `reserve_cents` to that newly authorised amount. A disbursement adds its amount to `paid_cents` and subtracts the same amount from `reserve_cents`, leaving `incurred_authorized_cents` untouched. A reversal subtracts the reversed amount from `paid_cents` and adds it back to `reserve_cents`, again leaving `incurred_authorized_cents` untouched — so a payment followed by its reversal is a complete no-op on all four fields. A release adds the whole of `reserve_cents` to `released_cents` and then sets `reserve_cents` to zero, leaving `incurred_authorized_cents` untouched; releases happen on denial, on limit-exhaustion suspension and on a zero arbitration award. Reserved money never goes negative: the floor on `reserve_cents` is zero.

A parallel but distinct invariant governs coverage capacity: a coverage term's `remaining_limit_cents` plus its `consumed_limit_cents` always equals its `limit_cents`. This is a capacity ceiling rather than money in a reserve, and `limit_cents` is immutable once the term exists. A disbursement charged to a term adds its amount to `consumed_limit_cents` and subtracts the same amount from `remaining_limit_cents`; a reversal does the reverse, so a payment plus its reversal restores both derived fields exactly. The floor on `remaining_limit_cents` is zero. Only the one term named by the claim's `coverage_term_id` is charged; sibling terms on the same policy are untouched.

Cascades in this system are **synchronous and complete within the request that triggers them**. There is no deferred settlement, no background job and no eventual consistency: by the time a response is returned, every dependent row the operation implicates has been rewritten, every derived aggregate recomputed, and every affected row's `version` advanced. Two cascades in particular run several links deep and are spelled out where they occur — the builder-default cascade in §3.2 and the limit-exhaustion cascade in §3.8. Derived aggregates are always recomputed from scratch over their source populations rather than incrementally adjusted, so a recompute is idempotent and cannot drift.

### 2.10. Posture Gating and Segregation of Duties

A builder's `monitoring_posture` is a gate, not a label. `OPEN` and `WATCH` place no restriction on the program. `RESTRICTED` allows new home enrollments only when the enrolling request explicitly acknowledges the restriction. `FROZEN` blocks new home enrollments outright and also blocks the reinstatement of a suspended builder, and in addition marks the builder's not-yet-activated policies so that they cannot be put in force. The precise mechanics live in §3.2 and §3.3.

Two carrier decisions may not be made twice by the same person. First, the `ClaimsAdjuster` whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as a claim's `adjudicated_by_user_id` may not disburse money on that claim; a payment request whose `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `adjudicated_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. Second, the adjuster whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `outcome_recorded_by_user_id` on a mediation case may not issue the award on the arbitration case that succeeds it; an award request whose `X-User-ID` equals the `outcome_recorded_by_user_id` of the case named by the arbitration's `prior_case_id` is refused with the same error and status. Neither check applies anywhere else in the API.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Builder File and Scoring It on Declared Data

Everything in this program hangs off a builder. An `UnderwritingOfficer` opens a builder file by supplying six declared underwriting inputs: the builder's legal `company_name` (between 1 and 120 characters, and not blank), the `state_code` in which it is licensed (one of `TX`, `CO`, `AZ`, `FL` or `NC`), its `years_in_business` as a whole number from 0 to 100 inclusive, its `homes_closed_last_year` as a whole number from 0 to 5000 inclusive, a third-party `claims_history_score` from 0 to 100 inclusive where 100 means a spotless record, and a declared `financial_strength_tier` of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` describing its balance sheet. The new file lands in `underwriting_status` `DRAFT` with no score at all — `risk_score`, `risk_class` and `risk_basis` are all null — and with its monitoring counters at rest: `earned_premium_cents` and `incurred_loss_cents` are 0, `loss_ratio` is 0.0000, `monitoring_posture` is `OPEN`, and `default_declared_at` is null. The file also carries `obligation_period_years`, which this program fixes at 2 years for every builder; it is never accepted as request input and never varies. The `id` returned on the created file is operationally important: it is the value the builder's own staff will send as `X-User-ID` when acting as a `BuilderPrincipal`.

Nothing is scored until the builder attests to its own data. A `BuilderPrincipal` submits the file with an `attestation_signed` flag, moving `underwriting_status` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`; the file must actually be in `DRAFT`, and a submission against a file in any other underwriting state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BUILDER_STATE`. The flag is required and must be a boolean, but **either** value carries the submission: the server records no attestation field on the builder row and does not refuse a submission that declares `attestation_signed` false. The updated builder row is the response body, as it is for every other transition on the file.

Taking the file into review is the underwriter's move and it is the moment the score is computed. An `UnderwritingOfficer` may optionally attach an `intake_note` — any string, blank included, of any length, validated but not persisted anywhere on the file — and the file advances from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`. The same operation also serves the referral loop: a file sitting in `REFERRED` — sent back for revision by an earlier decision — is taken into review again by the same operation, so `REFERRED → UNDER_REVIEW` is a legal transition alongside `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. Each intake re-scores from whatever declared inputs the file currently stores, which is what makes the `REFERRED → UNDER_REVIEW → REFERRED` cycle useful rather than merely repetitive. Both intake transitions write `risk_score` and `risk_class` and set `risk_basis` to `INITIAL_UNDERWRITING`, and both advance the builder's `version` by 1.

The score itself is a weighted assessment built from five inputs, computed as follows. Start with a base of 50 points. Add a **history penalty** equal to the shortfall of the effective claims score below 100, multiplied by 0.50 — a spotless score of 100 adds nothing, a score of 60 adds 20 points. Compute a **volume factor** by dividing the effective home volume by 25 and discarding any fractional part, multiplying that whole number of 25-home buckets by 8 points per bucket, and capping the result at 40 points; then add one quarter of that capped volume factor to the running total, so a builder closing 300 homes contributes the maximum, which is 40 capped points weighted down to 10 points of score. Subtract a **tenure credit** of 2 points for each full year in business, capped at 20 points, so anything past a decade of operation earns the same credit. Subtract a **financial strength bonus** of 15 points for `TIER_1`, 8 points for `TIER_2` and 0 points for `TIER_3`. Clamp the resulting figure so that it is never below 0 and never above 100, and round it half up to two decimal places; that is `risk_score`.

`risk_class` is then a band on the rounded score, with each band's lower bound inclusive and its upper bound exclusive: class `A` for a score below 25.00, class `B` for a score from 25.00 up to but not including 45.00, class `C` for a score from 45.00 up to but not including 65.00, and class `D` for a score of 65.00 or higher. Lower is better — class `A` is the carrier's best risk and class `D` is uninsurable under this program.

With the file in `UNDER_REVIEW`, an `UnderwritingOfficer` records a verdict. The `decision` field carries `APPROVE`, `DECLINE` or `REFER`, and an optional `reviewer_notes` narrative may be supplied for the audit trail (when present it must be between 1 and 500 characters and not blank). Recording a verdict creates a permanent review row that captures who decided and what they were looking at: `builder_id` names the file, `reviewer_user_id` is copied from the acting `X-User-ID`, `risk_score_snapshot` and `risk_class_snapshot` freeze the builder's `risk_score` and `risk_class` as they stood at that moment, and `resulting_underwriting_status` records the state the builder landed in — `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` or `REFERRED` respectively. The row also echoes the `decision` it recorded and the `reviewer_notes` it was given, null when none were supplied, and it is the **review row rather than the builder row** that comes back as the response body, at HTTP 201. The builder's `version` advances by 1. A verdict may only be recorded on a file in `UNDER_REVIEW`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_BUILDER_STATE`.

One decision is not available at the underwriter's discretion: **approval of a class `D` builder is refused**. The `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` transition requires the builder's `risk_class` to be `A`, `B` or `C`, and an `APPROVE` verdict on a file whose `risk_class` is `D` is rejected with `ERR_RISK_CLASS_INELIGIBLE`. The other two verdicts have no such restriction: `DECLINE` and `REFER` are legal from `UNDER_REVIEW` for a builder in any class, and each records its review row and advances the builder's `version` by 1 exactly as `APPROVE` does. `DECLINED` is terminal — a declined file never re-enters review.

### 3.2. Loss Monitoring, Reassessment, and the Consequences of Builder Failure

A builder's `loss_ratio` is the carrier's early-warning instrument, and it is recomputed from scratch — never incrementally accumulated — every single time a premium or a claim amount that feeds it changes. The denominator, `earned_premium_cents`, is the sum of `premium_cents` over the builder's policies that are **both** in a coverage state of `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` **and** in a premium state of `PAID`; policies still in `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` and policies that were `VOIDED` are excluded, and so is an in-force policy whose premium was never paid. The numerator, `incurred_loss_cents`, is the sum of `reserve_cents` plus `paid_cents` over every claim filed against any policy of this builder whose `obligor` is `INSURER`, with **no claim-status filter whatsoever** — closed claims count exactly as open ones do. A claim's `released_cents` never enters the numerator, which is why releasing a reserve lowers a builder's incurred loss. Claims still obligated to the builder are excluded outright, which is why flipping a claim's `obligor` to `INSURER` raises the figure.

The ratio itself is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places — except when the denominator is 0, in which case `loss_ratio` is pinned at exactly 0.0000 and `monitoring_posture` at `OPEN` **even if `incurred_loss_cents` is positive**. A builder with losses and no earned premium is not treated as infinitely bad; it is treated as unrated.

`monitoring_posture` is a band on the rounded ratio with lower bounds inclusive: `OPEN` below 0.6500, `WATCH` from 0.6500 up to but not including 0.9000, `RESTRICTED` from 0.9000 up to but not including 1.2000, and `FROZEN` at 1.2000 or above. In plain terms: a builder whose insurer-funded losses have reached 65 cents on every premium dollar goes on watch, 90 cents restricts new enrollments to explicitly acknowledged exceptions, and 120 cents freezes the builder out of new business entirely.

The recompute fires on activation of a policy, on payment of an enrollment premium, on routing a claim into the insurer arm, on a builder's refusal of its repair obligation, on a claim denial, on the recording of a reserve entry, on a disbursement, on a payment reversal, on the limit-exhaustion cascade, on a mediated settlement, on an arbitration award, and inside the builder-default cascade. Every one of those writes `earned_premium_cents`, `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` afresh on the affected builder.

Carrier staff read the monitor directly through a computed view that returns the four stored figures — `earned_premium_cents`, `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` — alongside the two populations behind them. `policy_count` is the size of the denominator population: the number of this builder's policies that are in `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` coverage state and `PAID` premium state. `open_claim_count` is the number of claims on any policy of this builder whose `status` is not one of the six terminal claim states, namely `CLOSED_REPAIRED`, `CLOSED_PAID`, `CLOSED_SETTLED`, `CLOSED_AWARDED`, `CLOSED_DENIED_FINAL` and `SUSPENDED_NO_LIMIT`. The view's `data` object holds exactly those six keys and nothing else — it is a projection, not a resource, so it carries no `id`, no audit fields and no pagination — and reading it is a pure read: the two counts are tallied on the spot, while the four stored figures are echoed as the last write left them rather than being recomputed by the read.

Reassessment re-scores an approved builder on **observed** rather than declared data, and it is available only while the file's `underwriting_status` is exactly `APPROVED` — a reassessment aimed at a file in any other underwriting state, a suspended or defaulted one included, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BUILDER_STATE`. The request may optionally restate any of the four declared inputs — `years_in_business`, `homes_closed_last_year`, `claims_history_score`, `financial_strength_tier` — and any supplied value is written to the builder row *before* scoring begins. The scoring itself uses the identical five-input weighted formula of §3.1, with two substitutions. The effective claims score is no longer the declared `claims_history_score`; it is derived from realised experience as 100 minus the builder's `loss_ratio` multiplied by an observed-loss weight of 60, rounded half up to two decimal places and then clamped to lie between 0 and 100 inclusive. And the effective home volume is no longer the declared `homes_closed_last_year`; it is the count of the builder's policies whose `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`, with `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` and `VOIDED` policies excluded. Everything else — the base of 50, the 0.50 history multiplier, the 25-home buckets worth 8 points each capped at 40 and weighted at one quarter, the 2-points-per-year tenure credit capped at 20, the tier bonus, the clamp, the two-decimal rounding and the four band floors — is unchanged. Reassessment writes `risk_score` and `risk_class` and sets `risk_basis` to `RENEWAL_REASSESSMENT`. For an otherwise identical builder row the two scoring passes will generally produce different scores and can produce different bands, because declared volume and declared history have been replaced by realised enrollment count and realised loss ratio.

Reassessment has teeth. When the reassessed `risk_class` comes out as `D` and the builder was `APPROVED`, the same request applies `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` — the new score, class and `risk_basis` are written first and the suspension is applied afterwards, within the one request, and the builder's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the whole operation.

Suspension is also available to compliance directly. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a `reason` and drives `APPROVED → SUSPENDED`, which stops new enrollments and activations for that builder. The `reason` on this operation — and equally on reinstatement and on the declaration of default below — is a **required**, non-blank string of any length that the server validates and then discards: no `reason` field is written to the builder row or exposed on any response, and the response body is the updated builder file itself. The reverse move belongs to underwriting: an `UnderwritingOfficer` supplies a `reason` and drives `SUSPENDED → APPROVED`, closing the `APPROVED → SUSPENDED → APPROVED` cycle — but only while the builder's `monitoring_posture` is anything other than `FROZEN`. Attempting to reinstate a frozen builder is refused with `ERR_BUILDER_POSTURE_FROZEN`; the carrier will not put a builder back in business on a loss ratio at or above 1.2000.

The gravest operation in the API is the declaration of default. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a `reason`, and a builder in either `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `DEFAULTED` — a terminal state from which there is no return. `default_declared_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant, rendered in the same `Z`-suffixed three-fractional-digit ISO 8601 form as every other instant the server emits. Then a three-link cascade runs, synchronously, inside the same request, because the whole point of the declaration is that homeowners must not be left waiting on a builder that no longer exists.

The **first link** re-parties every stranded claim: each claim on any policy of this builder whose `status` is `BUILDER_OBLIGATION_NOTICE` transitions to `INSURER_ADJUDICATION`, and its `obligor` is rewritten from `BUILDER` to `INSURER`. The **second link** follows from the first: because those claims are now insurer-funded, the settlement waterfall of §3.7 is re-run on each of them under the insurer-backed variant, rewriting `deductible_applied_cents`, `betterment_cents` and `net_payable_cents`; then each claim's `incurred_authorized_cents` is set to the sum of its `paid_cents`, its `released_cents` and its freshly computed `net_payable_cents`, and its `reserve_cents` is set to that same `net_payable_cents`. The **third link** follows from the second: because those reserve figures moved, the builder's `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed. Separately from the three-link chain, every policy of this builder still sitting in `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` transitions to `VOIDED`, and every coverage term of such a policy whose `status` is `OPEN` transitions to `ELAPSED`; each affected policy's and term's `version` advances by 1. The builder's own `version` advances by 1. Claims in the builder arm that had already been accepted for repair, and claims already closed, are untouched.

### 3.3. Enrolling a Closed Home: Rating the Premium and Minting Coverage

Before a home can be enrolled, its buyer must exist as a party in the system. Either an `UnderwritingOfficer` or a `BuilderPrincipal` registers a home buyer with a `full_name` between 1 and 120 characters and not blank, an `email` that must contain an at-sign, and a mailing `state_code` drawn from `TX`, `CO`, `AZ`, `FL` and `NC`. The `email` is a required non-blank string with no length ceiling, and its at-sign test is applied only after `full_name` and `state_code` have themselves passed, so a payload that is wrong in both the email and the state code reports the state code. The persisted buyer row carries just those three declared fields beside its `id` and `created_at`. As with builders, the `id` returned on a new buyer record is the value that buyer will present as `X-User-ID` when acting as a `Homeowner`. Buyer records are reference data — once written, they are never mutated by any operation in this API.

Enrollment itself is the builder's act, performed at closing. A `BuilderPrincipal` supplies the `builder_id` of its own file, the `initial_owner_id` of the buyer at closing, the home's `street_address` (1 to 200 characters, not blank), the home's `state_code` (again one of `TX`, `CO`, `AZ`, `FL`, `NC`), the `purchase_price_cents` at closing — a whole number of cents from 5,000,000 to 500,000,000 inclusive, that is, a home priced between fifty thousand and five million dollars — and the `closing_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date, which may not be later than the logical clock's current date — a later `closing_date` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that comparison is made only after every other field of the payload has passed its own shape checks. An optional `posture_override_acknowledged` flag also belongs to this payload and is discussed with the posture gates below.

Three gates stand between the request and a new enrollment. The enrolling builder's `underwriting_status` must be exactly `APPROVED`, and an enrollment attempted against a builder in any other underwriting state is refused with `ERR_BUILDER_NOT_APPROVED`. The builder's `monitoring_posture` must not be `FROZEN`: a frozen builder cannot write new business at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BUILDER_POSTURE_FROZEN`. And when the builder's `monitoring_posture` is `RESTRICTED`, the request must carry `posture_override_acknowledged` set to true; a restricted-posture enrollment without that explicit acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED`. Under an `OPEN` or `WATCH` posture the acknowledgement flag is not required and is simply ignored if present.

A successful enrollment initialises the policy as follows. `risk_class_at_enrollment` is a snapshot of the builder's `risk_class` taken at that instant, and it is decisive: a later reassessment that moves the builder into a worse band **never re-rates an existing policy**. `current_owner_id` is initialised to the supplied `initial_owner_id`, `transfer_count` starts at 0, `enrollment_hold` starts at false, and `pending_transfer_id` starts as null. `coverage_start_date` is set equal to `closing_date` and is the anchor for all coverage arithmetic. The policy's two independent state axes begin at `coverage_status` `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` and `premium_status` `UNBILLED`.

Rating happens once, at enrollment, and never again. Each risk band carries a **base rate in basis points** — 38 for class `A`, 52 for class `B`, 71 for class `C` and 95 for class `D` — and a **structural loading multiplier** of 1.00 for class `A`, 1.10 for class `B`, 1.25 for class `C` and 1.45 for class `D`. The purchase price is apportioned across the three coverages by fixed shares: 18 percent to workmanship, 27 percent to systems and 55 percent to structural. `premium_component_workmanship_cents` is the purchase price multiplied by the workmanship share of 0.18, multiplied by the band's base rate in basis points, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole number of cents. `premium_component_systems_cents` is computed the same way from the systems share of 0.27. `premium_component_structural_cents` is computed from the structural share of 0.55 and then multiplied by the band's structural loading, with the rounding to whole cents performed exactly once, **after** the loading has been applied — rounding before the loading is wrong, and so is skipping the loading. `state_surcharge_cents` is a flat charge selected by the home's own `state_code`: 4,500 cents in `TX`, 9,500 cents in `FL`, 2,500 cents in `CO`, nothing at all in `AZ`, and 1,500 cents in `NC`. Finally, `premium_cents` is the sum of the three independently rounded component integers plus the surcharge, added as exact integers. Summing the three unrounded values and rounding once at the end is a different answer for some prices and is not what this program charges.

The same enrollment mints the home's coverage. Three `BASE` coverage terms are created, one per `coverage_type` and in a fixed insertion order — workmanship first, then systems, then structural — all sharing a `start_date` equal to the policy's `coverage_start_date`; because the three share that one `start_date`, that insertion order is also the order in which the coverage-term listing of §2.3 returns them. Their `end_date` values are derived by an anniversary rule: take the closing date, advance the year by the term's length in years while keeping the same month and day, and then step back one day. The workmanship term runs 1 year, the systems term runs 2 years and the structural term runs 10 years, so a home closing on 2027-03-14 has a workmanship term ending 2028-03-13, a systems term ending 2029-03-13 and a structural term ending 2037-03-13. The anniversary rule has exactly one special case: when the closing date is 29 February and the target year is not a leap year, the anniversary falls back to the last day of that month in the target year before the one-day step back is applied. The same anniversary arithmetic, applied with the builder's `obligation_period_years` of 2, yields `builder_obligation_end_date` on the policy — which is therefore the same calendar date as the systems term's `end_date`, and is the boundary that decides who answers for a claim.

Each `BASE` term also gets a limit and a deductible. `limit_cents` for the workmanship term is 10 percent of the purchase price, rounded half up to whole cents, but never more than 2,000,000 cents — a twenty-thousand-dollar ceiling. For the systems term it is 25 percent of the purchase price, rounded half up to whole cents, but never more than 6,000,000 cents. For the structural term it is the full `purchase_price_cents` with no cap at all. `deductible_cents` is 100,000 cents — one thousand dollars — on the workmanship and systems terms, and 250,000 cents on the structural term, reflecting the heavier engineering cost of a structural claim. Every new term is born with `term_kind` `BASE`, `status` `OPEN`, `consumed_limit_cents` of 0, and `remaining_limit_cents` equal to its `limit_cents`. **All coverage-term intervals in this system are closed on both ends**: a date is covered when it is on or after `start_date` and on or before `end_date`.

Coverage can be extended after the fact. An `UnderwritingOfficer` attaches an endorsement term to a policy by supplying a `coverage_type`, a `start_date`, an `end_date` that is not earlier than the `start_date`, a `limit_cents` of at least 1, and a `deductible_cents` of at least 0. The new term is born with `term_kind` `ENDORSEMENT`, `status` `OPEN`, `consumed_limit_cents` of 0 and `remaining_limit_cents` equal to its `limit_cents`, and the policy's `version` advances by 1. Endorsements are only available on a policy whose `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE`; otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE`. And an endorsement may not overlap any existing term of the same `coverage_type` on that policy — whatever that term's `term_kind` or `status`, an already `ELAPSED` or `LIMIT_EXHAUSTED` one included: both intervals are treated as closed on both ends with no buffer whatsoever, so an endorsement that merely touches an existing term of the same type — beginning on the very day the existing term ends — is a conflict, and the request is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_TERM_OVERLAP`. Terms of a *different* coverage type may overlap freely.

### 3.4. The Two Policy Axes: Billing, Activation and Expiry

A policy tracks its coverage and its money on two axes that move independently. `coverage_status` runs `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` to `ACTIVE` and thence to the terminal `EXPIRED`, or from `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` directly to the terminal `VOIDED`. `premium_status` runs `UNBILLED` to `INVOICED` to the terminal `PAID`. Neither axis waits for the other in general: `premium_status` may reach `PAID` while `coverage_status` is still `PENDING_ENROLLMENT`, and `coverage_status` may reach `EXPIRED` without `premium_status` ever leaving `UNBILLED`. There is exactly one crossing point, at activation.

Billing moves only the premium axis. An `UnderwritingOfficer`, optionally supplying an `invoice_reference` — any string, blank included and of any length, which the server validates and then discards rather than storing anywhere on the policy — drives `premium_status` from `UNBILLED` to `INVOICED`; the policy must be in `UNBILLED` on the premium axis (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PREMIUM_STATE`) and still in `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` on the coverage axis (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`), and the premium axis is tested first, so a policy that is wrong on both axes at once reports `ERR_INVALID_PREMIUM_STATE`. The policy's `version` advances by 1.

Payment is the builder's act. A `BuilderPrincipal` whose `X-User-ID` matches the policy's `builder_id` pays the invoiced enrollment premium in full, optionally supplying a `payment_reference` — likewise any string, blank included, validated and then discarded rather than stored — and `premium_status` moves from `INVOICED` to `PAID`. Nothing on the coverage axis is examined, so a policy already `VOIDED` by the builder-default cascade still accepts the payment of an `INVOICED` premium. A payment against a policy that is not `INVOICED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PREMIUM_STATE`. Payment triggers a recompute of the builder's `earned_premium_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` — but because a policy in `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` is excluded from the denominator population, nothing actually moves yet. The recompute is still performed, and the policy's `version` advances by 1.

Activation is where coverage goes in force and where the premium finally enters the builder's denominator. An `UnderwritingOfficer` drives `coverage_status` from `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` to `ACTIVE` with no request body at all, and four conditions must hold. The policy must be in `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` on the coverage axis, or `ERR_INVALID_POLICY_STATE`. Its `premium_status` must be exactly `PAID` — this is the cross-axis guard, and a policy whose premium is `UNBILLED` or `INVOICED` is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_PAID`. Its `enrollment_hold` must be false, or `ERR_ENROLLMENT_ON_HOLD`. And the enrolling builder must **still** be `APPROVED` at activation time, even though it necessarily was `APPROVED` at enrollment time — a builder suspended or defaulted in the interim blocks activation with `ERR_BUILDER_NOT_APPROVED`. On success the builder's `earned_premium_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed, this time with real effect, and the policy's `version` advances by 1.

Time passes only when an operator moves it. A `ComplianceOfficer` advances the server's logical clock to an absolute instant supplied as `to_iso8601`, and the operation runs an **epoch sweep** in the same request. The sweep does two things in order. Every coverage term whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `end_date` falls before the new clock date transitions to the terminal `ELAPSED`. Then every policy whose `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `BASE` term of type `STRUCTURAL` has an `end_date` before the new clock date transitions from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` — the structural term is the longest of the three, so its lapse is the lapse of the whole warranty. Each row the sweep touches has its `version` advanced by 1. The response reports the clock's new value as `current_utc` together with `expired_policy_count`, the number of policies moved to `EXPIRED`, and `elapsed_term_count`, the number of terms moved to `ELAPSED`. No loss-ratio recompute is needed after a sweep, because an `EXPIRED` policy remains in the denominator population exactly as an `ACTIVE` one does. The clock never regresses: an instant earlier than the current one is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION`.

### 3.5. Passing the Warranty to a Subsequent Buyer

A structural warranty follows the house, not the buyer, so the remaining coverage can be handed to a subsequent purchaser. The current owner starts the process, naming the `new_owner_id` of the subsequent buyer, the `transfer_date` on which the sale to that buyer takes effect as an ISO 8601 calendar date, optionally a `documents_reference` pointing at the deed and inspection packet, and — mandatorily — the `expected_policy_version` described in §2.7.

Four conditions govern the request. The policy's `coverage_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE`; there is nothing to transfer on a policy that never went in force or has already expired. The `transfer_date` must fall inside the closed interval of the policy's `BASE` term of type `STRUCTURAL` — on or after that term's `start_date` and on or before its `end_date` — and a date outside that window is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_TERM_ELAPSED`. The `new_owner_id` must be someone other than the policy's `current_owner_id`; naming the current holder is refused with `ERR_TRANSFEREE_IS_CURRENT_OWNER`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must be the policy's `current_owner_id`, per §2.1.

A successful request creates a transfer row in `status` `REQUESTED` against the policy named in its `policy_id`, with `requested_by_owner_id` copied from the acting `X-User-ID`, with `documents_reference` holding whatever the request supplied — null when it supplied none, and a blank string counts as supplied text — and with `decision_reason` still null. Its `transfer_fee_cents` is computed at that moment as a base fee of 7,500 cents plus 2,500 cents for each transfer already recorded on the policy — so the first hand-off costs seventy-five dollars, the second one hundred, the third one hundred and twenty-five. The multiplier is the policy's `transfer_count`, which counts only transfers that reached `RECORDED`; superseded and rejected requests never raise it and never raise the fee.

Requests supersede rather than queue. If the policy already has an open request — the row named by the policy's `pending_transfer_id`, in `status` `REQUESTED` or `NEEDS_DOCUMENTATION` — that row transitions to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` and its `version` advances by 1, and the new row's `prior_transfer_id` is set to the superseded row's `id`, forming an auditable chain of attempts. When no request was open, the new row's `prior_transfer_id` stays null. Either way the policy's `pending_transfer_id` is set to the new request's `id` and the policy's `version` advances by 1. This deliberate supersede-in-silence behaviour is precisely why the stale-version check of §2.7 guards this operation.

Underwriting administers the request from there. An `UnderwritingOfficer` who needs paperwork supplies a `reason` — a required, non-blank string of any length that the server validates and then discards, exactly as on the builder-file transitions of §3.2, rather than storing it on the request row — and sends the request from `REQUESTED` to `NEEDS_DOCUMENTATION`, advancing its `version` by 1. The requesting owner — identified against `requested_by_owner_id` rather than against the policy's current holder — answers with a `documents_reference`, which on this operation is required and must not be blank, and which overwrites whatever reference the row previously held and returns the request from `NEEDS_DOCUMENTATION` to `REQUESTED`, again advancing its `version` by 1. This `REQUESTED → NEEDS_DOCUMENTATION → REQUESTED` revision loop may run any number of times; nothing caps it. A resubmission against a request that is not in `NEEDS_DOCUMENTATION` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_STATE`, as is a documentation request against a row that is not in `REQUESTED`.

The decision is recorded with an `outcome` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `decision_reason` narrative — blank included, and written to the row as null when the request omits it, on either branch. Both branches carry equal weight. `APPROVE` moves the request from `REQUESTED` to `APPROVED`, records the `decision_reason`, and advances the request's `version` by 1; it is available **only** when the request's `documents_reference` is not null, a blank reference counting as documentation supplied, and an approval on an undocumented request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_TRANSFER_DOCUMENTATION`. `REJECT` moves the request from `REQUESTED` to the terminal `REJECTED`, records the `decision_reason`, advances the request's `version` by 1, and additionally clears the policy's `pending_transfer_id` back to null and advances the policy's `version` by 1 — a rejection frees the policy for a fresh attempt. There is no documentation precondition on rejection: a request can be turned down without ever having been documented.

Recording is the final step and the only one that touches ownership. An `UnderwritingOfficer` moves the request from `APPROVED` to the terminal `RECORDED` with no request body, and the effects reach across three rows. The policy's `current_owner_id` is rewritten to the request's `new_owner_id`, its `transfer_count` is incremented by 1, its `pending_transfer_id` is cleared to null, and its `version` advances by 1; the request's own `version` advances by 1. The policy's `initial_owner_id` is never rewritten — it permanently records the buyer at closing. One guard protects the carrier here: recording is blocked while **any** claim on that policy has a `status` of `MEDIATION` or `ARBITRATION`, because the carrier will not change the claimant of record mid-dispute. The attempt is refused with `ERR_OPEN_DISPUTE_BLOCKS_TRANSFER`.

### 3.6. Filing a Claim, Investigating It, and Deciding What Counts as Structural

The homeowner who currently holds the warranty files the claim. The payload names the `policy_id`, the `coverage_type` being invoked (`WORKMANSHIP`, `SYSTEMS` or `STRUCTURAL`), the claimant's `defect_category` characterisation of what went wrong — one of `WORKMANSHIP_DEFECT`, `SYSTEM_FAILURE`, `STRUCTURAL_CRACKING`, `LOAD_BEARING_FAILURE` or `WATER_INTRUSION` — a `damage_description` narrative between 1 and 1000 characters and not blank, and the `claimed_amount_cents` being demanded, which must be at least 1 and carries no ceiling. Those five body fields are validated in exactly the order just listed, so a payload wrong in more than one of them reports the earliest of them. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the policy's `current_owner_id`, and it is copied onto the claim as `filed_by_owner_id`; because that comparison needs the policy row in hand, a `policy_id` that is present and a string is resolved — and its owner checked — before any other field of the body is examined, while a `policy_id` that is absent or not a string is simply the missing-or-wrong-type `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Claims can only be filed against a policy whose `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE`, and an attempt against any other coverage state is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Intake resolves two things the claimant does not get to choose. First, `reported_date` is stamped from the logical clock's current date — never from the request. Second, the claim is bound to a specific coverage term, recorded as `coverage_term_id`. The candidate set is the terms of that policy whose `status` is `OPEN` or `LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`; terms already `ELAPSED` are excluded outright. From those, the terms whose `coverage_type` equals the claimed `coverage_type` and whose closed interval contains the `reported_date` — on or after `start_date` and on or before `end_date` — are ranked with `BASE` terms ahead of `ENDORSEMENT` terms, then by `start_date` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and then by insertion order, and the first is chosen. When that candidate set is legitimately empty — no term of the claimed type covers the reported date — the intake is refused with `ERR_NO_COVERAGE_IN_FORCE`.

Third, and most consequential, intake assigns the `obligor`. When the `reported_date` is on or before the policy's `builder_obligation_end_date`, the `obligor` is `BUILDER`; when it falls after that date, the `obligor` is `INSURER`. **The boundary date itself is builder-obligated** — a claim reported on exactly the last day of the obligation period belongs to the builder, and only the following day crosses over to the carrier. A fresh claim lands in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `designated_structural` false and with all four money fields — `reserve_cents`, `paid_cents`, `released_cents` and `incurred_authorized_cents` — at 0. Its settlement figures `gross_adjusted_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `betterment_cents` and `net_payable_cents` likewise all start at 0, while `substantiation_ratio`, `adjudicated_by_user_id`, `denial_date` and `escalation_deadline_date` all start null and are filled in only by the later steps that own them. Intake writes the claim row and nothing else: neither the policy nor the coverage term it selected is rewritten, so no other row's `version` moves.

Investigation is the adjuster's first move. A `ClaimsAdjuster` records a `substantiation_ratio` — the share of the claimed amount the carrier accepts as substantiated, between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive with at most two decimal places — and the claim advances from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. The ratio is stored on the claim and the first step of the settlement waterfall runs immediately so that the substantiated figure is observable before any routing decision: `gross_adjusted_cents` is the `claimed_amount_cents` multiplied by the `substantiation_ratio`, rounded half up to whole cents. The other three settlement figures stay at 0 until routing runs the rest of the waterfall. The claim's `version` advances by 1. Investigating a claim that is not in `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

Engineering assessments are filed against a claim by a `ClaimsAdjuster` and are what turn "the wall is cracked" into a formal structural designation. Each report supplies `affected_elements` — an array of between 0 and 8 entries with no duplicates, each drawn from `FOOTING`, `FOUNDATION_WALL`, `BEAM`, `LOAD_BEARING_WALL`, `ROOF_TRUSS`, `SLAB`, `NON_BEARING_PARTITION` and `FINISH` — a measured `deflection_ratio` expressing deflection as a fraction of span between 0.0000 and 0.5000 inclusive with at most four decimal places, and a `load_bearing_affected` yes-or-no finding from the engineer, those three fields being validated in exactly that order. The server stamps `assessed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `assessment_date` from the logical clock's current date; `assessment_date` is never accepted as request input. Filing a report advances the claim's `version` by 1, and an empty `affected_elements` array is a legitimate filing, not an error. Filing carries no from-state precondition of its own: an assessment may be put on file against a claim in any `status` — before the claim has been routed, while it awaits adjudication, or after it has closed.

The report's own `designated_structural` verdict requires **all three** of the following to hold at once: `load_bearing_affected` is true; at least one entry in `affected_elements` is a load-bearing element, meaning one of `FOOTING`, `FOUNDATION_WALL`, `BEAM`, `LOAD_BEARING_WALL`, `ROOF_TRUSS` or `SLAB`; and the `deflection_ratio` is 0.0033 or greater, the threshold value itself qualifying. `NON_BEARING_PARTITION` and `FINISH` are explicitly *not* load-bearing elements, so a report naming only those two fails the second conjunct. An empty `affected_elements` array likewise fails the second conjunct, and therefore yields a verdict of false even when the engineer marked `load_bearing_affected` true and measured a severe deflection.

The claim carries its own `designated_structural` flag, which is the logical OR across every assessment ever filed on it: it is true as soon as any single report reaches a true verdict, and once raised it is **never lowered** by a subsequent report that fails the test. That flag is the switch that selects which deductible schedule the settlement waterfall applies, as §3.7 explains.

### 3.7. Obligation Routing: Two Arms, Two Settlements, One Join

Once an investigated claim has a substantiated figure, the adjuster routes it. The routing request carries nothing but an optional `routing_note` — any string, blank included and of any length, which the server validates and then discards rather than storing it on the claim — and the destination is decided entirely by data already on the claim. The claim must be in `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and its `substantiation_ratio` must not be null, and then the claim's `obligor` selects the arm. The two arms have **deliberately disjoint side-effect sets**, and understanding that disjointness is the heart of this domain.

When the `obligor` is `BUILDER`, routing advances the claim `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → BUILDER_OBLIGATION_NOTICE`. The settlement waterfall runs under the **builder-obligation variant**, writing `betterment_cents` and `net_payable_cents` and forcing `deductible_applied_cents` to 0 — the builder repairs in kind, so no policy deductible is charged to the homeowner. Both `reserve_cents` and `incurred_authorized_cents` remain 0, because the builder and not the carrier funds this arm, and **no builder loss-ratio recompute occurs**. One extra guard applies only here: the builder behind the claim's policy must not already have an `underwriting_status` of `DEFAULTED`, and routing a claim into the builder arm for a defaulted builder is refused with `ERR_BUILDER_DEFAULTED`.

When the `obligor` is `INSURER`, the same routing operation advances the claim `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → INSURER_ADJUDICATION`. The waterfall runs under the **insurer-backed variant**, writing `deductible_applied_cents`, `betterment_cents` and `net_payable_cents`. The claim's `incurred_authorized_cents` is set to the sum of its `paid_cents`, its `released_cents` and its `net_payable_cents`, and its `reserve_cents` is set to that `net_payable_cents` — the carrier has now put money aside. The builder's `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed — and that recompute, wherever in this document it fires, is the one exception to the cascade rule of §2.4: it refreshes those figures on the builder row in place and never advances the builder's own `version`. Either way the claim's `version` advances by 1.

The settlement waterfall itself runs in seven pinned steps, and the order matters more than any single number in it.

1. `gross_adjusted_cents` is the `claimed_amount_cents` multiplied by the `substantiation_ratio`, rounded half up to whole cents.
2. The deductible schedule is selected by the claim's `designated_structural` flag: 250,000 cents when the flag is true, and 100,000 cents when it is false. Note that the switch is the structural *designation* from the engineering reports, not the claimant's own `defect_category` and not the term's recorded `deductible_cents`.
3. `deductible_applied_cents` is 0 under the builder-obligation variant. Under the insurer-backed variant it is the smaller of the scheduled deductible and `gross_adjusted_cents` — a small substantiated loss is fully absorbed by the deductible rather than producing a negative figure.
4. The provisional net is `gross_adjusted_cents` less `deductible_applied_cents`, computed as exact integers.
5. The betterment percentage is selected from that **provisional net, before betterment is subtracted**: nothing at all when the provisional net is below 500,000 cents, 5 percent when it is from 500,000 cents up to but not including 1,500,000 cents, and 8 percent when it is 1,500,000 cents or more. This is the step that looks circular and is not: the tier is chosen from the pre-betterment figure, and re-selecting it from the post-betterment figure is wrong.
6. `betterment_cents` is the provisional net multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents.
7. `net_payable_cents` is the smaller of two figures: the provisional net less `betterment_cents`, and the `remaining_limit_cents` of the coverage term named by the claim's `coverage_term_id`. The term's remaining capacity is a hard ceiling on what the carrier will authorise.

Because the builder variant zeroes the deductible at step 3, its provisional net is larger, which means step 5 can select a **higher** betterment tier for the builder arm than the insurer arm would select on the identical claim inputs. The two variants therefore disagree on the same claim row purely because of which period the `reported_date` fell in — and that is the intended behaviour, not an inconsistency.

The builder answers an obligation notice itself. A `BuilderPrincipal`, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the `builder_id` of the claim's policy, submits a `response` of either `ACCEPT_OBLIGATION` or `REFUSE_OBLIGATION` with an optional `response_note` — again any string, blank included and of any length, validated and then discarded rather than stored. `ACCEPT_OBLIGATION` advances the claim `BUILDER_OBLIGATION_NOTICE → BUILDER_REPAIR_ACCEPTED` and advances its `version` by 1; nothing financial moves. When the repair is finished the same builder reports a `completion_date` — a required ISO 8601 calendar date that the server validates and then discards, stored nowhere on the claim and compared against nothing, so a date in the future of the logical clock is accepted — and the claim advances `BUILDER_REPAIR_ACCEPTED → CLOSED_REPAIRED`, a terminal state reached with **zero insurer money**: `reserve_cents`, `paid_cents`, `released_cents` and `incurred_authorized_cents` are all still 0. The `version` advances by 1.

`REFUSE_OBLIGATION` is the **join point** where the builder arm rejoins the insurer arm, and its side effects are the mirror image of acceptance. The claim advances `BUILDER_OBLIGATION_NOTICE → INSURER_ADJUDICATION`; its `obligor` is rewritten from `BUILDER` to `INSURER`; the settlement waterfall is re-run under the insurer-backed variant, rewriting `deductible_applied_cents`, `betterment_cents` and `net_payable_cents` — so the deductible the builder arm had zeroed is now charged, and the betterment tier may fall as a result; `incurred_authorized_cents` is set to the sum of `paid_cents`, `released_cents` and the new `net_payable_cents`, and `reserve_cents` is set to that new `net_payable_cents`; the builder's `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed; and the claim's `version` advances by 1. The builder-default cascade of §3.2 reaches `INSURER_ADJUDICATION` from `BUILDER_OBLIGATION_NOTICE` by exactly the same route with exactly the same effects, differing only in who initiated it. In both cases a response submitted against a claim that is not in `BUILDER_OBLIGATION_NOTICE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

Adjudication closes the insurer arm's deliberation. A `ClaimsAdjuster` submits an `outcome` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` with an optional `adjudication_note` that is likewise validated, blank included, and then discarded, from a claim in `INSURER_ADJUDICATION`, and in both branches the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as the claim's `adjudicated_by_user_id` and the claim's `version` advances by 1. Both branches also share one safety guard: when the claim's `defect_category` is `STRUCTURAL_CRACKING` or `LOAD_BEARING_FAILURE`, at least one engineering assessment must already be on file for that claim, and adjudicating such a claim with no assessment is refused with `ERR_STRUCTURAL_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`. The carrier will not rule on a structural allegation it has not had an engineer look at.

`APPROVE` advances the claim `INSURER_ADJUDICATION → APPROVED` and carries one guard the denial branch does not: the coverage term resolved for the claim must have a `status` of exactly `OPEN`. A term that has been exhausted or has lapsed blocks approval with `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`. Nothing financial moves on approval itself; approval merely authorises disbursement.

`DENY` advances the claim `INSURER_ADJUDICATION → DENIED` and does considerably more. `denial_date` is stamped with the logical clock's current date. `escalation_deadline_date` is set to that denial date plus 60 days — the mediation window, and the deadline is **inclusive**, so opening the next rung exactly on `escalation_deadline_date` is allowed and one day later is not. The reserve is given back: the whole of the claim's `reserve_cents` is added to its `released_cents` and `reserve_cents` is set to 0, leaving `incurred_authorized_cents` unchanged. A reserve-journal row of `entry_type` `RELEASE` is appended for the released amount with `recorded_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` — but only when the released amount is at least 1 cent; a denial of a claim carrying no reserve appends no journal row. Finally the builder's `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed, and because releasing a reserve removes it from the numerator, a denial *lowers* the builder's loss ratio.

### 3.8. The Reserve Ledger: Authorising, Disbursing, Reversing and Running Out of Limit

Money on a claim moves through an append-only journal. Every movement made by the three operations of this section — authorisation, disbursement, reversal — appends one row, and so does every release wherever in the program it happens; the reserve rewrites that a mediated settlement and a positive arbitration award perform in §3.9 are the one exception and append no row at all. Each row records the `claim_id` it belongs to, its `entry_type`, the `amount_cents` of the movement as a positive magnitude of at least 1, an optional `memo` note explaining it — any string, blank included and of any length, stored as supplied and null when the request omits it — the `recorded_by_user_id` of whoever caused it, and two balance stamps: `balance_after_reserve_cents` and `balance_after_paid_cents`, capturing the claim's `reserve_cents` and `paid_cents` immediately after the entry was applied. The `entry_type` vocabulary is `INITIAL`, `ADJUSTMENT`, `PAYMENT`, `PAYMENT_REVERSAL` and `RELEASE`. A `reversed_entry_id` field names the payment row that a reversal undoes and is null on every other entry type. The journal never re-derives the claim's balances; it records them.

Authorising a reserve is the adjuster's operation and it accepts only two of the five entry types. The request carries an `entry_type` of `INITIAL` or `ADJUSTMENT`, the `amount_cents` being authorised, an optional `memo`, and the `expected_claim_version` required by §2.7, validated in exactly that order. The response is the newly created journal row. Two fiscal rules apply. An `INITIAL` entry is refused when the claim **already** carries an entry of type `INITIAL` or `ADJUSTMENT`, with `ERR_INITIAL_RESERVE_ALREADY_SET`: a reserve that already exists is re-authorised with `ADJUSTMENT`, never re-initialised. And an `ADJUSTMENT` entry's `amount_cents` may not be lower than the claim's `paid_cents`, because the re-authorised reserve may never fall below what has already been disbursed; the attempt is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_BELOW_DISBURSED`.

Crucially, an authorisation **overwrites the reserve target rather than adding to it**. On success the claim's `reserve_cents` becomes exactly the authorised `amount_cents`, and its `incurred_authorized_cents` becomes the sum of its existing `paid_cents`, its existing `released_cents` and that same authorised amount. The builder's loss-ratio figures are recomputed and the claim's `version` advances by 1. This overwrite semantics is why the stale-version check guards this operation: a second authorisation submitted against an out-of-date reading would succeed on its own terms and silently discard a reserve level the client never saw.

Disbursement is also the adjuster's operation, subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.10 — the adjuster recorded as the claim's `adjudicated_by_user_id` may not pay it. The request carries the `amount_cents` to disburse and an optional `memo`. The coverage term resolved for the claim must have a `status` of `OPEN`, or the payment is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_EXHAUSTED`. Two ceilings then apply, in order: the amount may not exceed the claim's `reserve_cents`, or `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_RESERVE`; and it may not exceed the `remaining_limit_cents` of the claim's coverage term, or `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Disbursement carries no from-state precondition of its own: the claim's own `status` is never examined, so any claim carrying a reserve may be paid whatever state it sits in.

A successful disbursement does five things. It appends a journal row of `entry_type` `PAYMENT` with `recorded_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`. It moves money on the claim: `amount_cents` is added to `paid_cents` and subtracted from `reserve_cents`, leaving `incurred_authorized_cents` untouched. It charges the coverage term: `amount_cents` is added to the term's `consumed_limit_cents` and subtracted from its `remaining_limit_cents`. It closes the claim when the claim is `APPROVED` and its `paid_cents` after this payment equals its `net_payable_cents`, advancing `APPROVED → CLOSED_PAID`. And it recomputes the builder's loss-ratio figures. The claim's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the whole request no matter how many of these effects fire, and the charged term's `version` advances by exactly 1 as well, whether or not the charge exhausts it. The response body is the updated claim rather than the `PAYMENT` row just appended; that row is discoverable only through the reserve journal, as §2.6 notes.

When that charge drives the term's `remaining_limit_cents` to exactly 0, the term transitions `OPEN → LIMIT_EXHAUSTED` on that same single `version` advance — and a three-link cascade fires, synchronously, in the same request.

The **first link** deals with the term's other claimants. Every *other* claim bound to that same coverage term whose `status` is `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `BUILDER_OBLIGATION_NOTICE`, `INSURER_ADJUDICATION` or `APPROVED` transitions to the terminal `SUSPENDED_NO_LIMIT`; on each such claim the whole of its `reserve_cents` is added to its `released_cents` and its `reserve_cents` is set to 0, leaving its `incurred_authorized_cents` unchanged, and a journal row of `entry_type` `RELEASE` for the released amount is appended to that claim's journal, with `recorded_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` of the paying adjuster, when the released amount is at least 1 cent. Each affected claim's `version` advances by 1. The rationale is blunt: the term has no capacity left, so authorisations against it are no longer meaningful. The **second link** follows because those reserves moved: the builder's `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed. The **third link** follows when that recompute pushes `monitoring_posture` to `FROZEN`: every policy of that builder whose `coverage_status` is `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` has its `enrollment_hold` set to true and its `version` advanced by 1 — which is exactly the flag that blocks activation in §3.4. A single payment on one claim can therefore stop an unrelated home from going in force.

Reversal is compliance's exclusive operation and it is a full reversal, never partial. A `ComplianceOfficer` names the `reserve_entry_id` of the payment row to undo, with an optional `memo`. The named row must belong to the claim in question, must have an `entry_type` of `PAYMENT`, and must not already have been reversed; a row failing any of those tests is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_REVERSIBLE`. On success a journal row of `entry_type` `PAYMENT_REVERSAL` is appended with its `reversed_entry_id` naming the undone payment. The reversed amount is subtracted from the claim's `paid_cents` and added back to its `reserve_cents`, leaving `incurred_authorized_cents` untouched; and the term is restored, with the amount subtracted from `consumed_limit_cents` and added back to `remaining_limit_cents`. A payment followed by its reversal is therefore an exact no-op on all four claim money fields and on both term capacity fields.

A reversal that lifts a term's `remaining_limit_cents` above 0 also transitions that term `LIMIT_EXHAUSTED → OPEN`, and the term's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the restoration whether or not that reopening happens — capacity is genuinely available again. But **claims already moved to `SUSPENDED_NO_LIMIT` are not resurrected**: that state is terminal, and the compensating transition on the term does not reach back into it. This is exactly why the conserved-money identity of §2.9 is asserted per claim and not per term. The reversal recomputes the builder's loss-ratio figures and advances the claim's `version` by 1. Nor does a reversal examine or rewrite the claim's own `status`: a claim already in `CLOSED_PAID` stays there even though its `paid_cents` no longer equals its `net_payable_cents`. The response body is the updated claim.

### 3.9. The Panel, Mediation, and Binding Arbitration

A denied claim is not the end of the conversation. The carrier maintains a panel of independent neutrals, added by a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `full_name` between 1 and 120 characters and not blank, a `credential` of `MEDIATOR`, `ARBITRATOR` or `BOTH`, and a `state_code` of admission drawn from `TX`, `CO`, `AZ`, `FL` and `NC`. A new neutral is born with `status` `ACTIVE` and `booked_session_count` of 0. Retiring a neutral is also compliance's move: with a `reason` — again a required non-blank string that is validated and then discarded rather than stored on the panel row — the neutral moves `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`, a terminal state, and its `version` advances by 1; a deactivation aimed at a neutral that is already `INACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NEUTRAL_STATE`. Retirement is forward-looking only — sessions already scheduled with that neutral are left completely untouched, and only new bookings are blocked.

The homeowner opens each rung of the ladder, and doing so books a neutral in one operation. The request names the `stage` being opened — `MEDIATION` or `ARBITRATION` — the `neutral_id` to book, a `window_start` and a `window_end` bounding the session as ISO 8601 UTC instants where `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`, and, for arbitration, the `prior_case_id` of the mediation case that authorises it. Those five fields are validated in exactly that order, and the strict `window_end` comparison — an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it fails — is made only after all five have passed their own shape checks. A `prior_case_id` supplied on a `MEDIATION` request is not rejected for being superfluous: it must resolve and it must belong to the same claim, and it is stored on the new case, but nothing further is asked of it; it is null when omitted. The first rung requires the claim to be in `DENIED` and advances it `DENIED → MEDIATION`. The second rung requires the claim to be in `MEDIATION` and advances it `MEDIATION → ARBITRATION`.

Six checks guard a booking, listed here by subject rather than in the order they fire — the ladder of §2.8 fixes that order, and under it the deadline test below precedes the calendar test of the next paragraph, which in turn precedes the predecessor-claim test. The acting `X-User-ID` must be the policy's `current_owner_id`. The booked neutral's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_NEUTRAL_INACTIVE`. The neutral's `credential` must admit the requested stage — `MEDIATOR` or `BOTH` for a `MEDIATION` case, `ARBITRATOR` or `BOTH` for an `ARBITRATION` case — or `ERR_NEUTRAL_CREDENTIAL_MISMATCH`. An `ARBITRATION` request must supply a `prior_case_id`, and that case must have reached `status` `IMPASSE`; a missing predecessor or one that has not deadlocked is refused with `ERR_MEDIATION_NOT_EXHAUSTED`. That predecessor must also belong to the same claim: a `prior_case_id` naming a case whose `claim_id` is a different claim is refused with `ERR_PRIOR_CASE_CLAIM_MISMATCH`. And the logical clock's current date must be on or before the claim's `escalation_deadline_date`; one day past it, the rung is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_ELAPSED`.

The sixth check is the panel's calendar. The requested session window is treated as **half-open** — it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, so a window that begins at the exact instant another ends does not by itself overlap it. Before comparing, the requested window is **widened by a turnaround allowance of 120 minutes on both sides**, giving the neutral two hours of travel and preparation either side of a session. If that widened window overlaps any other escalation-case window on the same neutral — every case ever booked on that neutral counts, whatever its own `status`, so a settled, deadlocked or awarded session still holds its slot, and the stored windows themselves are compared unwidened — the request is refused with `ERR_NEUTRAL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Clients inspect a conflict by listing escalation cases filtered on the neutral in question.

A successful booking creates an escalation case against the claim named in its `claim_id`, in `status` `SCHEDULED`, with `binding` false for a `MEDIATION` case and true for an `ARBITRATION` case — `binding` is derived from the stage and is never supplied by the client. Its `settlement_amount_cents`, `award_amount_cents` and `outcome_recorded_by_user_id` all start null, and the first two are **never accepted at case creation**. The booked neutral's `booked_session_count` is incremented by 1 and its `version` advances by 1; that counter is the number of escalation cases naming this neutral whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `SETTLED`, `IMPASSE` or `AWARD_ISSUED`. The claim's `version` advances by 1.

Recording how a session ended is the adjuster's job, and it may not be done before the session was scheduled to begin: the logical clock's current instant must be at or after the case's `window_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED`. The request carries an `outcome` and, when the parties agreed, a `settlement_amount_cents` — an optional integer with a floor of 0 that is required only on a `SETTLED` outcome, its absence there being an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` raised before the case's own state is examined, and that on an `IMPASSE` is validated if supplied and then ignored. Both outcomes require the case to be in `SCHEDULED` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_ESCALATION_STATE` — and both stamp the acting `X-User-ID` as the case's `outcome_recorded_by_user_id` and advance both the case's `version` and the claim's `version` by 1.

An outcome of `SETTLED` moves the case `SCHEDULED → SETTLED`, records the `settlement_amount_cents`, and closes the claim: `MEDIATION → CLOSED_SETTLED`, or `ARBITRATION → CLOSED_SETTLED` when an arbitration session settles before an award is issued. The claim's `incurred_authorized_cents` is set to the sum of its `paid_cents`, its `released_cents` and the settled amount, and its `reserve_cents` is set to the settled amount, which preserves the conservation identity of §2.9. A settled amount of 0 is a legal value, and no journal row is appended for this rewrite of the reserve. The builder's `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed.

An outcome of `IMPASSE` moves the case `SCHEDULED → IMPASSE` and deliberately leaves the claim's `status` alone — a deadlocked mediation keeps the claim in `MEDIATION` so the arbitration rung remains reachable. What it does change is the clock on that rung: the claim's `escalation_deadline_date` is rewritten to the logical clock's current date plus 30 days, the arbitration window. As with the mediation window, the deadline is inclusive. No money moves and no loss-ratio recompute is performed on an impasse. An impasse recorded on an arbitration-stage case takes that identical path — the claim stays in `ARBITRATION` and its `escalation_deadline_date` is pushed out by the same 30 days — even though no further rung exists to open.

The award closes the ladder. Only a `ClaimsAdjuster` may issue one, only on a case whose `stage` is `ARBITRATION` — an award requested on a mediation-stage case is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ESCALATION_STATE` — only on a case in `SCHEDULED`, only at or after the case's `window_start`, and only when the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `outcome_recorded_by_user_id` of the case named by this case's `prior_case_id`, per §2.10. The request carries the `award_amount_cents` — a required integer with a floor of 0 and no ceiling of any kind, bounded neither by the claim's reserve nor by the term's remaining limit — and an optional `award_note`, validated blank-included and then discarded; the case moves `SCHEDULED → AWARD_ISSUED` with `award_amount_cents` recorded, and the updated case, not the claim, is the response body of both this operation and the session-outcome above.

The award has two symmetric branches, and **a zero award is a legal value, not a validation error** — it is the binding final denial. When `award_amount_cents` is greater than 0, the claim advances `ARBITRATION → CLOSED_AWARDED`, its `incurred_authorized_cents` is set to the sum of its `paid_cents`, its `released_cents` and the awarded amount, and its `reserve_cents` is set to the awarded amount. When `award_amount_cents` is exactly 0, the claim advances `ARBITRATION → CLOSED_DENIED_FINAL`, the whole of its `reserve_cents` is added to its `released_cents` and its `reserve_cents` is set to 0, leaving `incurred_authorized_cents` unchanged, and a journal row of `entry_type` `RELEASE` for the released amount, carrying the acting adjuster's `X-User-ID` as its `recorded_by_user_id`, is appended when that amount is at least 1 cent. In both branches the builder's `incurred_loss_cents`, `loss_ratio` and `monitoring_posture` are recomputed, and both the case's `version` and the claim's `version` advance by 1.
