# Managed Print Services Contract & Usage Billing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A managed print services (MPS) provider does not sell printers; it sells pages. That single commercial fact is what makes the back office hard. Every page a customer prints has to be metered on a device the provider does not own, priced against a rate card that steps down as annual volume climbs, reconciled against a contracted minimum, adjusted for break/fix labour and response-time credits, and finally invoiced twice — once as a mid-period projection the customer can budget against, and once as a chronological actual at period end. When those two numbers disagree, the difference is a true-up that must be traceable to the reads that caused it. Today that reconciliation lives in spreadsheets, and the provider discovers a mis-metered device three months after it stopped reporting.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that spans the whole contract arc: fleet assessment and total-cost-of-ownership baselining, contract structure with cost-per-page tiers and minimum volumes, device placement and network integration behind secure print release, automated meter capture with explicit exception handling for offline devices, consumables auto-replenishment gated by waste and hoarding controls, service dispatch under response-time commitments and parts availability, quarterly business review with volume trends and right-sizing recommendations, and end-of-contract device removal, data sanitization and settlement. Every collection sits at the URL root: there is no shared API prefix, and resources are linked by explicit identifier fields rather than by deep URL nesting.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **money is never estimated** — every monetary field is a whole number of cents, every per-page rate is a whole number of micro-dollars, and the order in which values are rounded and summed is pinned by this document rather than left to the implementer. Second, **the provider's own staff cannot mark their own homework**: the operator who submits a meter reading may not be the operator who resolves its exception, and the account manager who authors a quarterly review may not be the one who approves it. Where those two commitments collide with convenience, they win.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below spelled exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called (see the authorization column in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are:

- **`AccountManager`** — commercial owner of the contract. Writes the contract shell and its cost-per-page rate card, activates, suspends and resumes it, registers customer sites and field technicians, authors quarterly business reviews, approves reviews authored by someone else, links refresh successors onto superseded assets, and opens and schedules end-of-contract removal orders.
- **`BillingAnalyst`** — owner of the money surface. Opens billing periods, issues the forward-looking provisional statement, closes a period into its chronological final statement, and posts the end-of-contract settlement. Never touches meters, dispatch or replenishment.
- **`FleetOpsController`** — runs the fleet. Registers devices, activates them once the hosting site's secure print release is configured, stocks the site parts locker, ingests automated meter readings, resolves read exceptions raised by someone else's submission, voids the newest accepted read, sweeps a period for silent devices, raises and cancels consumable orders, and dispatches technicians.
- **`FieldTechnician`** — the person standing at the device. Places and network-integrates hardware, submits manual meter readings, delivers cartridges, declares parts holds, records arrival onsite, resolves tickets with reported labour hours, and collects and sanitizes hardware at end of contract.
- **`CustomerAdmin`** — the customer's own site administrator. Configures secure print release for the site it represents, raises and cancels service tickets for devices at that site, and reads its own statements and reviews.

Identity semantics differ by role and are load-bearing for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Technician` compare the resource's technician identifier to `X-User-ID` — concretely, a `ServiceTicket` may only be advanced by the technician whose `id` equals the ticket's `assigned_technician_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Site`; ownership checks compare the owning site of the target resource to `X-User-ID` — the site path identifier when secure print release is being configured, and the target device's `site_id` when a service ticket is being raised or cancelled — and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_SITE_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `AccountManager`, `BillingAnalyst` or `FleetOpsController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a meter reading, and as `authored_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a quarterly review — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`. Those recorded values are compared only against each other, to enforce the segregation-of-duties rules described in §2.10.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and Collection Filters

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when more rows remain beyond the returned page).

Because the topology is flat, most parent-to-child navigation happens through filters on list endpoints rather than through nested URLs. Rate-card listings accept a `page_class` filter. Site listings accept `contract_id` and `secure_release_state`. The parts-locker listing accepts `part_sku`. Device listings accept `contract_id`, `site_id`, `replaced_by_device_id`, `removal_order_id`, `lifecycle_state` and `supply_state`. Technician listings accept `home_region` and `skill_level`. Meter-reading listings accept `device_id`, `billing_period_id`, `status` and `source`. Billing-period listings accept `contract_id` and `status`. Statement listings accept `billing_period_id` and `variant`; statement-line listings and the per-device statement-line listing accept `page_class`. Consumable-order listings accept `device_id`, `status` and `trigger_source`. Ticket listings accept `device_id`, `assigned_technician_id`, `status` and `severity`. Quarterly-review listings accept `status`. Removal-order listings accept `contract_id` and `status`. Every one of these filters is optional; when omitted, no restriction is applied on that dimension.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix, and clients must never construct or predict one. Alongside `id`, most resources expose `created_at` and `updated_at` as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, plus an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Any operation that changes a row's business fields also refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version` — including changes made as a side effect of an operation targeting some other resource.

That rendering is not special to `created_at` and `updated_at`: **every** instant-valued field a resource exposes is emitted in the same canonical form — ISO 8601, UTC, with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z` — or as `null` when the field holds no instant. This is true of instants the client supplied as much as of instants the server minted. A body-supplied instant is stored as the point in time it names and re-serialised canonically on read, so a `period_start`, `coverage_start`, `coverage_end`, `window_start`, `window_end` or `scheduled_for` sent as `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` reads back as `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and a derived stamp copied from such a value — for instance a device's `last_read_at`, taken from the accepted reading's `coverage_end` — reads back in that same form. Two spellings of the same instant therefore denote the same stored value, and every temporal comparison in this document compares points in time rather than strings.

Two entity kinds are deliberately leaner. A `CostTier` row and a `StatementLine` row carry only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resource shapes, because rate-card rows are appended rather than edited and statement lines are immutable evidence of a completed pricing run. Everything else — contracts, sites, spare-part stock rows, devices, technicians, meter readings, billing periods, billing statements, consumable orders, service tickets, quarterly reviews and removal orders — carries the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

All deadline arithmetic, all stamping of event times and all "is it due yet" comparisons read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that fixed startup default must never fall back to the host's real time. Any role may advance the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability described in §4; from that moment on, every `opened_at`, `read_at`, `delivered_at`, `generated_at` and `settled_at` stamp, and every comparison against a `respond_by`, `period_end` or `scheduled_for` boundary, uses the new value.

### 2.5. Micro-Dollars, Cents and Rounding Discipline

Per-page pricing is expressed in **micro-dollars per page**, where one cent equals 10,000 micro-dollars — a rate of 8,900 micro-dollars per page is therefore $0.0089 per page. Every monetary amount that leaves the system is a whole number of **cents**, held in an integer field. There are no floating-point money fields anywhere in the API.

Converting pages priced in micro-dollars into cents always uses the same discipline: multiply the page count by the rate in micro-dollars, divide by 10,000, and round half up to the nearest whole cent. The *order* in which that rounding happens relative to summation is pinned individually by each calculation in §3 and is never a matter of implementer preference. Where this document says "round each line, then sum the lines", accumulating micro-dollars across lines and rounding once at the end is wrong, and vice versa. The one non-monetary value carried with two decimal places is the quarterly `volume_trend_pct`, which is a percentage rounded half up to two decimal places.

Negative totals are legitimate. A billing period dominated by response-time credits may produce a negative `total_cents`, and the system must not floor it at zero.

### 2.6. Two-Dimensional Device State and Cascade Propagation

A `Device` carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The `lifecycle_state` dimension tracks placement and decommissioning and takes one of `ASSESSED`, `PLACED`, `NETWORKED`, `ACTIVE`, `SERVICE_HOLD`, `RETRIEVED` or `REMOVED`, starting at `ASSESSED` and terminating at `REMOVED`. The `supply_state` dimension tracks consumables and takes one of `SUPPLY_OK`, `SUPPLY_LOW`, `SUPPLY_ORDERED` or `SUPPLY_BLOCKED`, starting at `SUPPLY_OK` and never terminating. Neither dimension gates the other: a device on `SERVICE_HOLD` can still cycle its supply dimension, and a device in `SUPPLY_BLOCKED` is still meterable. When a transition in either dimension fires, the device's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed — once per operation, even when both dimensions move together.

Many operations in this system touch more than one row, and the cascade depth matters. A single meter-reading acceptance, for example, first writes the reading, then writes the device's counters, then may move the device's supply dimension and create a cartridge order, then adjusts the hosting site's open-cartridge tally and the contract's committed-spend counter — four hops from one call. Sanitizing a removal order likewise moves the order, then every covered device, then the contract's device count and each device's open cartridge orders, then the sites' and contract's consumables counters. Each affected row's `version` increments and `updated_at` refreshes on the hop that touches it. §3 states each cascade explicitly at the workflow that triggers it; the general rule is that derived counters are never left stale, and compensating operations (voiding a reading, cancelling an order, cancelling a ticket) must walk the same chain backwards so that reverted counters land exactly on their pre-operation values.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Nested Reads

Path identifiers are validated strictly left to right. When a path names a parent and a child, the parent must resolve before the child is looked at, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. Every failure of this kind carries HTTP 404 with the code specific to the missing resource type: `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_METER_READ_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REMOVAL_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.

The same catalogue applies to identifiers supplied inside a request body rather than in the path. A `contract_id`, `site_id`, `device_id`, `billing_period_id`, `technician_id` or successor device identifier that names no existing row produces the corresponding not-found code with HTTP 404 — a body-supplied successor device that does not exist reports `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, exactly as a path-supplied device would.

Sub-resource listings — a contract's rate-card rows, a contract's quarterly reviews, a site's parts-locker rows, a statement's lines and a device's historical statement lines — exist purely as reverse lookups. They read the child collection filtered to the parent named in the path and never create or modify anything.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Two write operations in this system are legitimate to repeat — regenerating a provisional statement supersedes the previous projection, and raising a manual cartridge order supersedes the device's existing open order — which means no state precondition can protect the caller from acting on a view that has since moved. Those two operations are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent row's `version`.

A provisional-statement request must carry `expected_billing_period_version` as a required (never optional) body field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `BillingPeriod`. If that value differs from the period's current `version` at the moment the write is applied, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so an analyst cannot publish a projection derived from a period whose read set or sweep count has since advanced.

A manual consumable-order creation must carry `expected_device_version` as a required (never optional) body field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Device`. If that value differs from the device's current `version` at the moment the write is applied, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so an operator cannot re-order toner against a stale view of the device's supply dimension.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.9. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must report the violation that comes **first** in the ordered list below, not whichever check the implementation happens to run first. The list is exhaustive and strictly ascending; a rule that appears later can never mask a rule that appears earlier.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4 — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every path-templated identifier resolves to an existing row, checked left to right — otherwise the matching 404 from §2.7. Applies to every endpoint that has a path identifier.
4. **Request shape.** Every required body field is present, every field has its declared type, every enum field carries a declared member, and every numeric field respects its declared range — including `limit` between 1 and 100 and a non-negative `offset` on list endpoints. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every endpoint.
5. **Body-supplied foreign keys exist.** Every identifier supplied in the body resolves — otherwise the matching 404 from §2.7. This applies to site creation, device creation, both meter-reading intake endpoints (`POST /_agent/meter-reads` and `POST /meter-reads`), billing-period creation, consumable-order creation, ticket creation, ticket dispatch, quarterly-review creation, removal-order creation, and refresh-replacement linkage (where an unknown `successor_device_id` reports `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`).
6. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied parent version matches the parent's current `version` per §2.8 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (412). Applies to exactly two endpoints: `POST /billing-periods/{id}/provisional-statement` and `POST /consumable-orders`.
7. **Transition existence.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEVICE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_READ_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_APPROVED` or `ERR_REMOVAL_INVALID_STATE` (all 409). Applies to every action endpoint.
8. **Governing contract is active.** Operations that consume the contract require its `status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). The guarded operations are device placement, device activation, both meter-reading intake endpoints, the meter sweep, provisional-statement generation, consumable-order creation, and ticket creation. Period close is deliberately excluded: it accepts a contract whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `TERMINATING`, so a contract already winding down can still be billed out.
9. **Period not already closed.** A meter reading may not be created, resolved or voided against a `BillingPeriod` whose `status` is `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_CLOSED` (409). Applies to four endpoints: `POST /_agent/meter-reads`, `POST /meter-reads`, `POST /meter-reads/{id}/resolve-exception` and `POST /meter-reads/{id}/void`.
10. **Only the newest accepted reading may be voided** — otherwise `ERR_READ_NOT_NEWEST` (409). Applies to `POST /meter-reads/{id}/void`.
11. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the user already recorded for the paired duty — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to two endpoints: exception resolution (compared against the reading's `submitted_by_user_id`) and review approval (compared against the review's `authored_by_user_id`).
12. **Ownership.** The acting `FieldTechnician`'s `X-User-ID` equals the ticket's `assigned_technician_id`, and the acting `CustomerAdmin`'s `X-User-ID` equals the owning `Site` identifier — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` or `ERR_SITE_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (403). Applies to six endpoints: `POST /service-tickets/{id}/arrive-onsite`, `POST /service-tickets/{id}/parts-hold`, `POST /service-tickets/{id}/resolve`, `POST /sites/{id}/secure-release-config`, `POST /service-tickets` and `POST /service-tickets/{id}/cancel`.
13. **Technician certification.** A `CRITICAL` ticket may only be dispatched to a technician whose `skill_level` is `L2` or `L3` — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422). Applies to `POST /service-tickets/{id}/dispatch`.
14. **Secure release configured.** A `NETWORKED` device may only be activated once the hosting site's `secure_release_state` is `CONFIGURED` — otherwise `ERR_SECURE_RELEASE_NOT_CONFIGURED` (409). Applies to `POST /devices/{id}/activate`.
15. **Sanitization evidence.** Sanitization requires a `sanitization_method` other than `NONE` and a non-empty `sanitization_certificate_ref` — otherwise `ERR_SANITIZATION_EVIDENCE_MISSING` (422). Applies to `POST /removal-orders/{id}/sanitize`.
16. **Dispatch region match.** The dispatched technician's `home_region` equals the `address_region` of the site hosting the ticket's device — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /service-tickets/{id}/dispatch`.
17. **Reading belongs to the period.** The reading's device belongs to the same contract as the period, and `coverage_end` falls inside the half-open window running from `period_start` up to but excluding `period_end` — otherwise `ERR_READ_OUTSIDE_PERIOD` (422). Applies to both meter-reading intake endpoints.
18. **Device is meterable.** The device's `lifecycle_state` is `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_HOLD` — otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_METERABLE` (409). Applies to both meter-reading intake endpoints.
19. **Parts availability.** A ticket that names a part may only reach `ONSITE` when the parts-locker row for the device's site and that SKU exists with `on_hand_qty` of at least 1 (otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_AVAILABLE`), and may only enter `PARTS_HOLD` when that row is missing or has an `on_hand_qty` of exactly 0 (otherwise `ERR_PART_IS_AVAILABLE`). Both are 409, and the pair applies to `POST /service-tickets/{id}/arrive-onsite` and `POST /service-tickets/{id}/parts-hold`.
20. **Reading interval sanity.** `coverage_start` is strictly earlier than `coverage_end`, and the reading's half-open interval does not intersect the interval of any other reading of the same device whose `status` is `PENDING_VALIDATION` or `ACCEPTED`; intervals that merely touch (one reading's `coverage_end` equal to the next reading's `coverage_start`) are allowed. Otherwise `ERR_READ_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to both meter-reading intake endpoints.
21. **Dispatch window sanity.** `window_start` is strictly earlier than `window_end`, `window_start` is at or after the logical clock, and the requested window does not intersect any window already occupied by the same technician after each occupied window is inflated by that technician's `travel_buffer_minutes` on both sides. Otherwise `ERR_DISPATCH_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /service-tickets/{id}/dispatch`.
22. **Temporal gates.** A period may only close once the logical clock has reached `period_end` (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`); a removal order may only be collected once the clock has reached `scheduled_for` (`ERR_COLLECTION_NOT_DUE`); a `scheduled_for` supplied at request or scheduling time must not be earlier than the logical clock (`ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST`); and a new billing-period window must have `period_start` strictly earlier than `period_end` and must not intersect another period of the same contract, touching endpoints allowed (`ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP`). All are 409, and between them they cover `POST /billing-periods/{id}/close`, `POST /removal-orders/{id}/collect`, `POST /removal-orders/{id}/schedule` and `POST /billing-periods`.
23. **No open exceptions at close.** A period may not close while its `exception_open_count` is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_OPEN_EXCEPTIONS` (409). Applies to `POST /billing-periods/{id}/close`.
24. **Uniqueness.** `asset_tag` is unique within a contract (`ERR_ASSET_TAG_DUPLICATE`); a rate-card row must not duplicate the combination of contract, `page_class` and `tier_index`, nor may its page band intersect an existing band of the same contract and `page_class` (`ERR_TIER_BAND_CONFLICT`); `quarter_label` is unique within a contract (`ERR_QUARTER_DUPLICATE`). All are 409, covering `POST /devices`, `POST /service-contracts/{id}/cost-tiers` and `POST /quarterly-reviews`.
25. **Hoarding control.** The site's `open_toner_units`, less the units of the order this request supersedes, plus the requested `quantity`, must not exceed the site's `toner_unit_cap` — otherwise `ERR_TONER_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /consumable-orders`.
26. **Rate card completeness at activation.** Activation requires a `MONO` rate-card row at `tier_index` 1 with `from_page` 1, plus the equivalent `COLOR` row when any device of the contract is `color_capable` — otherwise `ERR_RATE_CARD_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to `POST /service-contracts/{id}/activate`.
27. **Tier coverage at pricing time.** Statement generation requires a rate-card band that contains the contract-level volume of every `page_class` whose volume is greater than zero — otherwise `ERR_TIER_COVERAGE_GAP` (409). Applies to both `POST /billing-periods/{id}/provisional-statement` and `POST /billing-periods/{id}/close`.
28. **Labour ceiling.** Reported `labor_hours` must not exceed 8 hours per ticket — otherwise `ERR_LABOR_HOURS_EXCEED_LIMIT` (422). Applies to `POST /service-tickets/{id}/resolve`.
29. **Settlement requires closed periods.** Every billing period of the contract whose `period_end` is at or before the logical clock must be `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /removal-orders/{id}/settle`.
30. **Refresh linkage eligibility.** The device named in the path must carry `refresh_due` true and a null `replaced_by_device_id`, and the successor device must belong to the same contract, have a `lifecycle_state` of `ASSESSED`, `PLACED`, `NETWORKED` or `ACTIVE`, and not be the path device itself — otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_REFRESH_DUE` (409). Applies to `POST /devices/{id}/refresh-replacement`.
31. **Parts stock underflow.** A stock adjustment must supply a non-zero `qty_delta`, and the resulting `on_hand_qty` for that site and SKU must be at least zero, treating an absent row as a current quantity of zero — otherwise `ERR_PART_STOCK_UNDERFLOW` (409). Applies to `POST /sites/{id}/spare-part-stocks`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Audit Attribution

Two duties in this system are deliberately split across two humans. A meter reading records the operator who submitted it in `submitted_by_user_id`; when an exception on that reading is later resolved, the resolving operator is recorded in `resolved_by_user_id`, and the two must differ. A quarterly business review records its author in `authored_by_user_id`; when it is approved, the approver is recorded in `approved_by_user_id`, and again the two must differ. In both cases an attempt by the same `X-User-ID` to perform both halves is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. Note that the roles themselves are not split — a single `FleetOpsController` population resolves exceptions and a single `AccountManager` population approves reviews — so this is an identity check, not a role check, and it is the reason `X-User-ID` must be recorded even for the opaque roles.

The money-touching duties are separated a second way, by role rather than by identity. The rate card, the statements and the settlement are `BillingAnalyst` and `AccountManager` territory; metering, sweeping, dispatch and replenishment are `FleetOpsController` and `FieldTechnician` territory. No single role can both price a page and report the page count that gets priced.

### 2.11. Half-Open Windows and Interval Arithmetic

Every time window in this system is **half-open**: it includes its lower bound and excludes its upper bound. This applies to a billing period's window from `period_start` to `period_end`, to a meter reading's coverage window from `coverage_start` to `coverage_end`, and to a technician's onsite window from `window_start` to `window_end`. The practical consequence is that two windows which merely touch — where the earlier window's upper bound equals the later window's lower bound — do **not** overlap and are always allowed; only genuinely intersecting windows are rejected.

When a reading is assigned to a period, it is `coverage_end` that must fall inside the period window, not `coverage_start`. When a period is asked to close, the logical clock must have reached `period_end` — equality is sufficient. Durations expressed in hours are always rounded **up** to a whole number of hours before being used, whether that duration is a parts-hold interval, a lateness measurement or reported labour.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Contract Formation, the Rate Card and the Suspension Cycle

Everything in this system hangs off a **service contract**. An `AccountManager` opens one by supplying the customer's legal name in `customer_name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters), the term length in `term_months` (at least 12 and at most 60), the service envelope in `included_service_scope`, the two contracted minimum volumes `minimum_mono_pages_per_period` and `minimum_color_pages_per_period` (each zero or greater), and the fixed `monthly_service_fee_cents` (zero or greater), which doubles as the base amount for response-time credits. Four further inputs are optional and default when omitted: `response_sla_hours_critical` defaults to 4 hours and must lie between 1 and 72; `response_sla_hours_standard` defaults to a full day at 24 hours and must lie between 1 and 168; `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` defaults to 9,500 cents — $95 an hour — and must be zero or greater; and `early_termination_fee_cents` defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater. A newly created contract's `status` is `DRAFT`.

`included_service_scope` is the single most consequential commercial switch on the contract. Under `FULL`, break/fix labour sits inside the cost-per-page envelope and is never billed as a separate line — the labour charge on every ticket of that contract is exactly zero cents regardless of the hours reported. Under `BASIC`, labour is billed out at `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` and lands on the period's statement as a labour charge.

Six counters on the contract are derived, never supplied, and all start at zero on creation. `active_device_count` is the number of that contract's devices whose `lifecycle_state` is `PLACED`, `NETWORKED`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_HOLD` — assessed-but-unplaced, retrieved and removed assets are excluded. `consumables_committed_cents` is the total committed value of the contract's cartridge orders that are still open, that is, whose `status` is `ORDERED`. `lifetime_billed_cents` accumulates the total of every final statement ever issued against the contract's periods. `true_up_balance_cents` is the running sum, across closed periods, of the difference between each period's final statement total and its provisional statement total; a period that never had a provisional projection contributes exactly zero to that balance rather than contributing its full final total. `suspension_count` records how many times the contract has completed an `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` move. Alongside those, `suspension_reason` is a system-maintained enumeration that begins at `NONE` and thereafter takes one of `NONPAYMENT`, `DISPUTE` or `CUSTOMER_REQUEST`.

Before the contract can be activated it needs a **rate card**. The `AccountManager` appends rate-card rows one at a time, each naming the parent contract through its `contract_id`, the meter being priced in `page_class` (either `MONO` or `COLOR`), the row's position within that meter's ladder in `tier_index` (1-based, at least 1, and unique for a given contract and `page_class`), the inclusive lower bound of the band in `from_page` (at least 1), the inclusive upper bound in `to_page` (either null for an unbounded top band or a value no smaller than `from_page`), and the price in `rate_micro_dollars_per_page` (at least 1). Two structural rules govern the ladder: the row at `tier_index` 1 must have a `from_page` of exactly 1, and at most one row per contract and `page_class` may carry a null `to_page`. Appending a row that duplicates an existing combination of contract, `page_class` and `tier_index`, or whose band intersects an existing band of the same contract and `page_class`, is rejected with `ERR_TIER_BAND_CONFLICT`. Rate-card rows are append-only evidence: each carries an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else from the common property set.

Activation moves the contract `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. It requires a `MONO` rate-card row at `tier_index` 1 whose `from_page` is 1, and — only when at least one device of the contract has `color_capable` set true — an equivalent `COLOR` row at `tier_index` 1 with `from_page` 1. A contract that fails either test is rejected with `ERR_RATE_CARD_INCOMPLETE`; a contract that is not in `DRAFT` when activation is attempted is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`. On success the contract's `version` increases by 1. Activation is what unlocks the operational surface: until it happens, no device may be placed or activated, no meter reading may be ingested, no sweep may run, no consumable order may be raised, no provisional statement may be produced and no ticket may be opened — every one of those attempts reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Commercial reality intrudes, and an `AccountManager` may suspend an active contract by supplying a `reason` of `NONPAYMENT`, `DISPUTE` or `CUSTOMER_REQUEST`. The move `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` copies that reason into `suspension_reason`, adds 1 to `suspension_count` and increments the contract's `version`. It is refused, as an invalid contract transition reporting `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`, when the contract is not `ACTIVE` or when any billing period of that contract currently sits in `PROVISIONAL` — a published projection must be either closed out or regenerated before the contract can be frozen. Suspension blocks meter-read intake, consumable orders and billing runs but leaves device lifecycle states untouched: a suspended contract's devices remain exactly where they were. Resuming performs `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, deliberately **retaining** `suspension_reason` for audit rather than resetting it to `NONE`, and increments `version`. The pair `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` is a genuine cycle that may repeat indefinitely, with `suspension_count` accumulating across every pass. Resuming a contract that is not `SUSPENDED` reports `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`.

The remaining two contract states belong to the wind-down described in §3.9: requesting removal drives `ACTIVE → TERMINATING`, and posting the settlement drives `TERMINATING → CLOSED`, which is terminal.

### 3.2. Customer Sites, Secure Print Release and the Parts Locker

A **site** is a customer location covered by a contract. An `AccountManager` registers one with the owning `contract_id`, a human-readable `name` (non-empty) and a dispatch region code in `address_region` (non-empty and upper-case, such as `NE-01`), which is what technician assignment later matches against. Two optional inputs shape the site's consumables behaviour and its network: `toner_unit_cap` defaults to 4 and must be at least 1, and `network_vlan_tag` defaults to null and, when supplied, must lie between 1 and 4094. A new site starts with `secure_release_state` at `UNCONFIGURED`, `release_method` at `NONE` and `open_toner_units` at 0. That last field is derived rather than supplied: it is the total quantity across every still-open cartridge order — orders whose `status` is `ORDERED` — destined for a device at that site, and it is the quantity that `toner_unit_cap` bounds.

Secure print release is configured by the customer themselves. A `CustomerAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` equals the site's own identifier submits a `release_method` (`BADGE` or `PIN` for a real configuration; `NONE` is the unconfigured default the field starts at), a `probe_result`, and optionally a `network_vlan_tag`. The configuration capability may be invoked from **any** current value of `secure_release_state` — `UNCONFIGURED`, `PENDING_VALIDATION`, `FAILED` or even `CONFIGURED` — and the resulting state is decided **solely** by `probe_result`, with three symmetric outcomes:

- `probe_result` of `PASS` drives the site to `CONFIGURED`. This outcome requires `release_method` to be `BADGE` or `PIN` **and** requires `network_vlan_tag` to be supplied in the range 1 through 4094. It writes both `release_method` and `network_vlan_tag` onto the site, sets `secure_release_state` to `CONFIGURED`, and increments the site's `version` by 1. It also cascades onto the fleet: every device at that site whose `lifecycle_state` is `NETWORKED` has `secure_release_enrolled` set to true and its own `version` incremented by 1.
- `probe_result` of `DEFERRED` drives the site to `PENDING_VALIDATION`. This outcome likewise requires `release_method` to be `BADGE` or `PIN`. It writes `release_method`, writes `network_vlan_tag` only when one was supplied, sets `secure_release_state` to `PENDING_VALIDATION`, and increments the site's `version` by 1. No device is enrolled.
- `probe_result` of `FAIL` drives the site to `FAILED` regardless of which method was named. It sets `secure_release_state` to `FAILED` and increments the site's `version` by 1. No device is enrolled.

Because re-validation after a network change is a routine event, `CONFIGURED → FAILED → CONFIGURED` and `UNCONFIGURED → PENDING_VALIDATION` followed later by a passing probe are all permitted paths. A `CustomerAdmin` acting on a site other than the one its `X-User-ID` names is rejected with `ERR_SITE_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`.

Each site also keeps a **parts locker**. A `FleetOpsController` creates or adjusts a locker row by naming a `part_sku` and a signed `qty_delta`; the row for that site and SKU is created when it does not yet exist, and `on_hand_qty` moves by the supplied delta. A `qty_delta` of exactly zero is meaningless and is rejected, as is any negative delta that would drive `on_hand_qty` below zero — an absent row counts as a current quantity of zero — and both cases report `ERR_PART_STOCK_UNDERFLOW`. On success the locker row's `version` increases by 1. Alongside `on_hand_qty`, each locker row exposes a derived `reserved_qty`: the number of service tickets that name the same `part_sku`, target a device at the same `site_id`, and are still in flight with a `status` of `DISPATCHED`, `PARTS_HOLD` or `ONSITE`. Reserved quantity is informational — it is `on_hand_qty` alone that gates a technician's arrival, and stock is consumed only at the moment of arrival.

### 3.3. Fleet Assessment, Placement and the Road to Billable Service

Before a contract is signed the provider walks the customer's floor and inventories what is already there. That inventory becomes the device population, and a `FleetOpsController` records each asset with its owning `contract_id`, its hosting `site_id`, the customer's own `asset_tag` (non-empty, and unique within the contract — a repeat reports `ERR_ASSET_TAG_DUPLICATE`), the manufacturer's `model_code` (non-empty), the manufacturer's `rated_monthly_duty_cycle` (at least 1, and later the denominator of the right-sizing utilization ratio), and four total-cost-of-ownership baseline figures captured during the assessment: `assessed_monthly_mono_pages` and `assessed_monthly_color_pages` (each zero or greater) describing the volume the asset was running before the contract, `baseline_cost_micro_dollars_per_page` (at least 1) describing the customer's pre-contract cost per page, and `baseline_overhead_cents_per_month` (zero or greater) describing the fixed help-desk, storage and administration overhead allocated to that asset each month. One optional input, `color_capable`, defaults to false.

A freshly recorded asset lands at `lifecycle_state` `ASSESSED` and `supply_state` `SUPPLY_OK`, with `toner_level_pct` at 100. Its metering history is empty: `last_accepted_mono_meter` and `last_accepted_color_meter` are 0, `last_read_at` is null, `lifetime_mono_pages`, `lifetime_color_pages`, `pages_since_last_cartridge`, `offline_exception_count` and `waste_event_count` are all 0, `secure_release_enrolled` is false, `right_sizing_action` is `NONE`, `refresh_due` is false, and both `replaced_by_device_id` and `removal_order_id` are null.

Three transitions carry an asset from inventory to billable service, and each has a cross-entity guard. A `FieldTechnician` records physical installation, driving `ASSESSED → PLACED`; this requires the governing contract to be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`) and, on success, adds 1 to the contract's `active_device_count` and increments the device's `version`. The same technician then joins the placed asset to the customer network, driving `PLACED → NETWORKED`; this requires the hosting site to have a non-null `network_vlan_tag`, and a placed device at a site with no VLAN assigned is refused as an invalid device transition reporting `ERR_DEVICE_INVALID_STATE`. Network integration also settles enrolment: `secure_release_enrolled` becomes true exactly when the site's `release_method` is `BADGE` or `PIN`, and false when it is `NONE`. The device's `version` increments.

Finally a `FleetOpsController` brings the asset into billable service, driving `NETWORKED → ACTIVE` and incrementing `version`. Two guards apply here and they report different codes: the hosting site's `secure_release_state` must be exactly `CONFIGURED` — a site still `UNCONFIGURED`, still `PENDING_VALIDATION`, or sitting in `FAILED` blocks activation with `ERR_SECURE_RELEASE_NOT_CONFIGURED` — and the governing contract must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the report is `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Attempting any of these three moves from the wrong `lifecycle_state` — placing an already-active asset, activating an asset that is still merely `ASSESSED` — reports `ERR_DEVICE_INVALID_STATE`. Only once a device reaches `ACTIVE` does meter-read intake become possible.

Later in the contract's life, once a quarterly review has flagged an asset as due for refresh, an `AccountManager` links it to its replacement by supplying a `successor_device_id`, which is written onto the superseded asset as `replaced_by_device_id` and increments its `version`. The linkage is tightly guarded: the asset named in the path must carry `refresh_due` true and must not already carry a non-null `replaced_by_device_id`, and the successor must belong to the same contract, must have a `lifecycle_state` of `ASSESSED`, `PLACED`, `NETWORKED` or `ACTIVE`, and must not be the same asset as the one being replaced. Any of those failures reports `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_REFRESH_DUE`; a `successor_device_id` naming nothing at all reports `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND` instead. The `removal_order_id` field stays null until the wind-down described in §3.9 stamps it.

### 3.4. Meter Capture, Validation and Exception Handling

Meter readings arrive on two paths. The primary path is automated: a data-collection agent runs out of band on the customer's network and pushes absolute counter values, and because that agent is not itself an API client, the provider exposes a test-driving intake capability under a reserved internal namespace that a `FleetOpsController` uses to emit exactly the payload the agent would have sent, stamping `source` as `AUTOMATED`. The secondary path is manual: a `FieldTechnician` standing at an offline device reads the counter off the panel and submits it, stamping `source` as `MANUAL`. The third possible value of `source`, `SWEEP_ESTIMATE`, is never client-supplied — it marks the placeholder readings the period sweep manufactures for silent devices.

Both intake paths take the same seven inputs: `device_id`, `billing_period_id`, the absolute counter values `mono_meter` and `color_meter` (each zero or greater), the reported `toner_level_pct` (between 0 and 100 inclusive), and the coverage window `coverage_start` and `coverage_end`. Both stamp `submitted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID` and `read_at` with the logical clock, and both create the reading with `status` `PENDING_VALIDATION` and `exception_reason` `NONE`. Both return 201 — and this is important — **whether or not the reading validates**. Four gates precede validation: the governing contract must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`); the target period must not be `CLOSED` (`ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_CLOSED`); the device must belong to the same contract as the period and `coverage_end` must fall inside the period's half-open window (`ERR_READ_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`); the device must be meterable, meaning its `lifecycle_state` is `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_HOLD` (`ERR_DEVICE_NOT_METERABLE`); and `coverage_start` must be strictly earlier than `coverage_end` with the resulting interval not intersecting the interval of any other reading of the same device that is `PENDING_VALIDATION` or `ACCEPTED` (`ERR_READ_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, with touching endpoints explicitly allowed).

Validation itself compares the submitted absolute counters against the device's watermarks. The candidate monochrome delta is the submitted `mono_meter` less the device's `last_accepted_mono_meter`, and the candidate colour delta is the submitted `color_meter` less the device's `last_accepted_color_meter`. If **either** candidate delta is negative, the reading lands in `EXCEPTION` with `exception_reason` `ROLLBACK` — a counter that went backwards means the panel was replaced, the wrong asset was read, or the agent is confused. Otherwise, if the **sum** of the two candidate deltas is strictly greater than three times the device's `rated_monthly_duty_cycle`, the reading lands in `EXCEPTION` with `exception_reason` `IMPLAUSIBLE_SPIKE`; a combined delta exactly equal to three times the rated duty cycle is plausible and is accepted. The rollback test is evaluated **before** the spike test, so a reading that is simultaneously backwards on one meter and implausible on the other reports `ROLLBACK`, never `IMPLAUSIBLE_SPIKE`. Otherwise the reading is `ACCEPTED`, and `mono_delta` and `color_delta` are written with the two candidate values. A reading that is not `ACCEPTED` carries a `mono_delta` and a `color_delta` of exactly 0 and moves no device counter whatsoever.

Acceptance is where the cascade fans out, and it fans out four hops deep. The first hop writes the reading: `status` `ACCEPTED`, the two deltas, `exception_reason` back to `NONE`. The second hop writes the device: `last_accepted_mono_meter` and `last_accepted_color_meter` take the submitted absolute values, `last_read_at` takes the reading's `coverage_end`, `toner_level_pct` takes the reading's reported level, `lifetime_mono_pages` grows by `mono_delta`, `lifetime_color_pages` grows by `color_delta`, `pages_since_last_cartridge` grows by the sum of the two deltas, and the device's `version` increments. The third hop is caused by that toner write rather than by the intake call itself: when the device's newly written `toner_level_pct` is at or below 15 percent and its `supply_state` is `SUPPLY_OK`, the supply dimension moves `SUPPLY_OK → SUPPLY_LOW` and then immediately onward to either `SUPPLY_LOW → SUPPLY_ORDERED` or `SUPPLY_LOW → SUPPLY_BLOCKED`, as §3.5 describes. The fourth hop is caused by the third: if a cartridge order was created, the hosting site's `open_toner_units` grows by that order's `quantity` and the contract's `consumables_committed_cents` grows by that order's committed value. Independently of all of this, the target period's `exception_open_count` is recomputed — upward when the reading lands in `EXCEPTION`, and back down when a previously open reading becomes `ACCEPTED`.

Offline devices are the operational pain point this system exists to control, and the **period sweep** is the mechanism. A `FleetOpsController` may sweep a billing period that is `OPEN` or already `SWEPT`, provided the contract is `ACTIVE`. The sweep walks every device of the contract whose `lifecycle_state` is `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_HOLD` and which has no `ACCEPTED` reading whose `coverage_end` falls inside the period window, and manufactures one placeholder reading per such device: `source` `SWEEP_ESTIMATE`, `status` `EXCEPTION`, `exception_reason` `DEVICE_OFFLINE`, `mono_meter` and `color_meter` copied from the device's current watermarks, `toner_level_pct` copied from the device's current level, `coverage_start` set to the period's `period_start` and `coverage_end` set to the logical clock value at sweep time. Each such placeholder pushes the device's derived `offline_exception_count` up and the period's `exception_open_count` up. The period then moves `OPEN → SWEPT` (or `SWEPT → SWEPT` on a re-run), its `sweep_run_count` increases by 1 and its `version` increments. Two edges matter: when every eligible device already has an accepted reading the sweep creates zero placeholders yet **still** advances the period to `SWEPT` and **still** increments `sweep_run_count`; and on a re-run, devices that already hold a `SWEEP_ESTIMATE` reading in `EXCEPTION` for that period are skipped rather than given a duplicate.

An exception is not the end of the story. A `FleetOpsController` resolves one by supplying `corrected_mono_meter`, `corrected_color_meter`, `corrected_toner_level_pct` and a free-text `resolution_note`. The reading must currently be in `EXCEPTION` (otherwise `ERR_READ_INVALID_STATE`), its period must not be `CLOSED` (otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_CLOSED`), and the resolving `X-User-ID` must differ from the reading's `submitted_by_user_id` (otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Resolution overwrites the reading's `mono_meter`, `color_meter` and `toner_level_pct` with the three corrected values, records the `resolution_note`, stamps `resolved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, increments the reading's `version`, and moves it `EXCEPTION → PENDING_VALIDATION` — after which **exactly the same validation runs again** and lands the reading either in `ACCEPTED`, with the full four-hop cascade above, or right back in `EXCEPTION`. The cycle `EXCEPTION → PENDING_VALIDATION → EXCEPTION` may repeat as many times as it takes for the corrected counters to validate.

Sometimes a reading was simply wrong and must be withdrawn. Voiding is a `FleetOpsController` action that takes a `voided_reason` of `WRONG_DEVICE`, `DUPLICATE_CAPTURE` or `CUSTOMER_DISPUTE` (the field's default value before any void is `NONE`), and performs `ACCEPTED → VOID`, which is terminal. Only an `ACCEPTED` reading may be voided (otherwise `ERR_READ_INVALID_STATE`), only while its period is not `CLOSED` (otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_CLOSED`), and only when it is the **newest** accepted reading of its device — newest meaning greatest `coverage_end`, with `created_at` breaking a tie — failing which the request reports `ERR_READ_NOT_NEWEST`. Voiding then walks the acceptance cascade backwards. The device's `last_accepted_mono_meter`, `last_accepted_color_meter` and `last_read_at` revert to the values carried by the device's remaining accepted reading with the greatest `coverage_end` (tie broken by `created_at`), or to 0, 0 and null when no accepted reading remains. `lifetime_mono_pages` shrinks by the voided reading's `mono_delta`, `lifetime_color_pages` shrinks by its `color_delta`, and `pages_since_last_cartridge` shrinks by the sum of the two. The reading's own `mono_delta` and `color_delta` are zeroed, its `status` becomes `VOID` and its `voided_reason` is recorded. The device's `toner_level_pct` reverts to the `toner_level_pct` of that same remaining accepted reading, or to 100 when none remains. That reverted toner level triggers a second hop: when the device's `supply_state` is `SUPPLY_ORDERED`, its single open cartridge order was raised automatically (its `trigger_source` is `AUTO_THRESHOLD`), and the reverted `toner_level_pct` is now above 15 percent, that order is cancelled with `cancel_reason` `READ_VOIDED` and the device's `supply_state` returns to `SUPPLY_OK`. A third hop follows from that cancellation: the site's `open_toner_units` and the contract's `consumables_committed_cents` both fall by the cancelled order's `quantity` and committed value respectively. The device's `version` increments. The period's `exception_open_count` is deliberately **unchanged** by a void, because a voided reading is neither open nor billable.

### 3.5. Consumables: Auto-Replenishment, Hoarding Control and Waste Control

Toner replenishment is supposed to be invisible: the provider watches the toner level reported with each accepted reading and ships a cartridge before the customer notices. The trigger threshold is **15 percent remaining**. When an accepted reading leaves a device's `toner_level_pct` at or below 15 and the device's `supply_state` is `SUPPLY_OK`, the supply dimension moves `SUPPLY_OK → SUPPLY_LOW` and the device's `version` increments. Note that the supply dimension evolves entirely independently of `lifecycle_state`: a device on `SERVICE_HOLD` still reorders toner, and a device in `SUPPLY_BLOCKED` still meters pages.

From `SUPPLY_LOW` the system immediately attempts to raise the order within the same acceptance. Two conditions must both hold: the device must have **no** cartridge order currently in `ORDERED` status — the ceiling is exactly one open order per device — and the hosting site's `open_toner_units` plus one unit must not exceed the site's `toner_unit_cap`. When both hold, the supply dimension moves `SUPPLY_LOW → SUPPLY_ORDERED` and a cartridge order is created with `trigger_source` `AUTO_THRESHOLD`, a `quantity` of exactly 1, a `unit_price_cents` of 8,900 — the provider's standard automatic-replenishment unit price of $89 per cartridge — and a `part_type` chosen from the device and the reading: `TONER_COLOR` when the device is `color_capable` **and** the accepted reading's `color_delta` is greater than zero, and `TONER_BLACK` in every other case. The device's `version` increments, and the resulting order pushes the site's `open_toner_units` up by its `quantity` and the contract's `consumables_committed_cents` up by its committed value.

When either condition fails — an open order already exists, or one more unit would breach the site cap — replenishment is **suppressed** rather than forced through: the supply dimension moves `SUPPLY_LOW → SUPPLY_BLOCKED`, no cartridge order is created at all, and the device's `version` increments. The reading itself still becomes `ACCEPTED` in the ordinary way; only the replenishment is withheld. `SUPPLY_BLOCKED` is the signal that a human needs to look at the device, and it is deliberately reachable only through the automatic path.

That human is a `FleetOpsController`, who raises a manual order by supplying the target `device_id`, the `part_type` (`TONER_BLACK` or `TONER_COLOR`), the `unit_price_cents` (at least 1), the concurrency token `expected_device_version` described in §2.8, and optionally a `quantity`, which defaults to 1 and must lie between 1 and 3. The order is created with `trigger_source` `MANUAL` and a `status` of `ORDERED`; its `committed_cents` is derived as the `quantity` multiplied by the `unit_price_cents`. The device's `supply_state` must currently be `SUPPLY_LOW` or `SUPPLY_BLOCKED`, and a device in `SUPPLY_OK` or already `SUPPLY_ORDERED` is refused as an invalid device transition reporting `ERR_DEVICE_INVALID_STATE`. Because a manual order **supersedes** whatever the device already has open, the hoarding check nets out the superseded units: the site's current `open_toner_units`, minus the `quantity` of the single existing order for that device in `ORDERED` status (zero when there is none), plus the requested `quantity`, must not exceed the site's `toner_unit_cap`, and a breach reports `ERR_TONER_CAP_EXCEEDED`. On success any pre-existing order for that device in `ORDERED` status is transitioned to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `HOARDING_CONTROL`, the device's `supply_state` becomes `SUPPLY_ORDERED` by way of `SUPPLY_BLOCKED → SUPPLY_ORDERED` or `SUPPLY_LOW → SUPPLY_ORDERED`, the device's `version` increments, and the site's `open_toner_units` together with the contract's `consumables_committed_cents` are recomputed from the resulting set of open orders rather than blindly incremented.

Waste control is the mirror image of hoarding control, and it asks a different question: not *how many cartridges are open* but *how few pages the last one printed*. At the moment any order is created — automatic or manual — the device's current `pages_since_last_cartridge` is snapshotted into the order's `pages_since_last_cartridge_at_order`. The order is then flagged as waste, in the derived `waste_flagged` field, exactly when two things hold together: the device has at least one earlier cartridge order that reached `DELIVERED`, and the snapshotted page count is **strictly less than 3,000 pages**. A device's very first cartridge is therefore never a waste event however few pages it has printed, and a snapshot of exactly 3,000 pages is not waste either — the comparison is strict. Each device exposes a derived `waste_event_count`: the number of its orders that are flagged as waste and have not been cancelled. Waste events cost money at period close, as §3.7 explains.

`pages_since_last_cartridge` itself is the watermark all of this rests on. It is the total of monochrome plus colour pages across the device's accepted readings whose `coverage_end` falls after the `delivered_at` of the device's most recent delivered cartridge order; when no order has ever been delivered for that device, it is simply the device's `lifetime_mono_pages` plus its `lifetime_color_pages`.

A `FieldTechnician` closes the loop by recording delivery. Delivery requires the order to be in `ORDERED` status (otherwise `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`) and the destination device not to have been removed — a device whose `lifecycle_state` is `REMOVED` cannot take a cartridge. Delivery performs `ORDERED → DELIVERED`, stamps `delivered_at` with the logical clock, returns the device's supply dimension `SUPPLY_ORDERED → SUPPLY_OK`, resets the device's `pages_since_last_cartridge` to 0 against the new delivery watermark, releases the commitment by reducing the site's `open_toner_units` by the order's `quantity` and the contract's `consumables_committed_cents` by the order's `committed_cents`, and increments both the order's `version` and the device's `version`. The full supply cycle is therefore `SUPPLY_OK → SUPPLY_LOW → SUPPLY_ORDERED → SUPPLY_OK`.

Cancellation is the compensating path. A `FleetOpsController` cancels an order in `ORDERED` status by supplying a `cancel_reason`, which may be `HOARDING_CONTROL`, `WRONG_PART`, `DEVICE_REMOVED` or `READ_VOIDED` (the field's default before any cancellation is `NONE`). Cancellation performs `ORDERED → CANCELLED`, and both `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal — attempting to deliver or cancel an order that has already reached either reports `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. The site's `open_toner_units` falls by the order's `quantity` and the contract's `consumables_committed_cents` falls by its `committed_cents`, so that both counters land back exactly on their pre-creation values, and the order's `version` increments. The device's supply dimension then resolves on the reverted toner level, but only when no other order for that device remains in `ORDERED` status: if the device's `toner_level_pct` is still at or below 15 percent the dimension moves `SUPPLY_ORDERED → SUPPLY_LOW`, and if it is above 15 percent the dimension moves `SUPPLY_ORDERED → SUPPLY_OK`. Three other workflows cancel orders as a cascade rather than by direct request, each stamping its own reason: sanitizing a removal order cancels every open order of every removed device with `DEVICE_REMOVED`, raising a superseding manual order cancels the prior one with `HOARDING_CONTROL`, and voiding an accepted reading cancels an automatic order with `READ_VOIDED`. In all three cascade cases the site and contract consumables counters are recomputed downward and the order's `version` increments.

### 3.6. Service Dispatch: Regions, Certification, Parts and Response-Time Credits

Field service is delivered by technicians the `AccountManager` registers with a `full_name` (non-empty), a `home_region` (non-empty, upper-case) that must match a site's `address_region` for that technician to be dispatchable there, a `skill_level` of `L1`, `L2` or `L3`, and optionally a `travel_buffer_minutes` that defaults to 30 and must lie between 0 and 120. Each technician exposes a derived `open_dispatch_count`: the number of tickets assigned to them whose `status` is `DISPATCHED`, `PARTS_HOLD` or `ONSITE`.

Tickets are raised by the customer, not the provider. A `CustomerAdmin` whose `X-User-ID` equals the `site_id` of the failing device opens a break/fix ticket by supplying the `device_id`, a `severity` of `CRITICAL` or `STANDARD`, a non-empty `symptom_code`, and optionally a `part_sku` naming the spare part the repair is expected to need (null when no part is needed). A ticket raised for a device at another site is rejected with `ERR_SITE_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`, and a ticket raised against a contract that is not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The new ticket has `status` `OPEN`, a null `assigned_technician_id`, a null `window_start` and `window_end` until a dispatch reserves them, a null `hold_started_at` and `responded_at`, a `parts_hold_hours`, `hours_late`, `labor_hours`, `labor_cents` and `sla_credit_cents` of 0, `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock, and — this is the response-time commitment made concrete — a `respond_by` deadline computed as `opened_at` plus the contract's `response_sla_hours_critical` hours when `severity` is `CRITICAL`, or plus the contract's `response_sla_hours_standard` hours when `severity` is `STANDARD`.

A `FleetOpsController` dispatches the ticket by naming a `technician_id` and reserving an onsite slot with `window_start` and `window_end`, which are written onto the ticket. Four preconditions guard the assignment and they report distinct codes. The technician's `home_region` must equal the `address_region` of the site hosting the ticket's device, failing which the dispatch reports `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH`. A `CRITICAL` ticket may only go to a technician whose `skill_level` is `L2` or `L3` — an `L1` technician is not certified for critical work and the dispatch reports `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`; a `STANDARD` ticket may go to any skill level. The window must be well formed and free: `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, `window_start` at or after the logical clock, and no intersection with any window already occupied by that same technician on a ticket in `DISPATCHED`, `PARTS_HOLD` or `ONSITE` status **after each occupied window has been inflated by that technician's `travel_buffer_minutes` on both its leading and its trailing edge** — a technician carrying the default 30-minute buffer therefore needs a full hour of clear air between two adjacent jobs. Any of those window failures reports `ERR_DISPATCH_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And the ticket must be `OPEN`, failing which the report is `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`.

Successful dispatch performs `OPEN → DISPATCHED`, writes `assigned_technician_id` from the supplied `technician_id`, recomputes the technician's `open_dispatch_count` upward, increments the ticket's `version`, and cascades onto the hardware: when the ticket's device has a `lifecycle_state` of `ACTIVE`, that device moves `ACTIVE → SERVICE_HOLD` and its own `version` increments. From this point on every ticket action is restricted to the assigned technician — a `FieldTechnician` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the ticket's `assigned_technician_id` is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`.

Dispatch forks in two, and the fork is about parts. **Branch A**: the part is on the shelf, and the technician records arrival directly, performing `DISPATCHED → ONSITE`. Arrival requires either a null `part_sku` or a locker row for the device's `site_id` and that SKU with `on_hand_qty` of at least 1; a missing row or a row at zero reports `ERR_PART_NOT_AVAILABLE`. When a part is required, arrival consumes exactly one unit — the locker row's `on_hand_qty` falls by 1 and its `version` increments — and the ticket's `version` increments. On this branch no hold time accrues and `parts_hold_hours` remains 0. **Branch B**: the part is not there, and the technician declares a parts hold, performing `DISPATCHED → PARTS_HOLD`. A hold is only legitimate when the ticket actually names a `part_sku` and the locker row for that site and SKU is genuinely missing or sitting at an `on_hand_qty` of exactly 0; declaring a hold while the part is in fact available reports `ERR_PART_IS_AVAILABLE`. The hold stamps `hold_started_at` with the logical clock and increments the ticket's `version`. Once the part arrives the technician records arrival from the hold, performing `PARTS_HOLD → ONSITE`: this requires the locker row for that site and SKU to now exist with `on_hand_qty` of at least 1 (otherwise `ERR_PART_NOT_AVAILABLE`), accumulates the elapsed hold time into `parts_hold_hours` by adding the number of hours between `hold_started_at` and the current logical clock **rounded up to a whole hour**, clears `hold_started_at` back to null, consumes one locker unit and increments the locker row's `version`, and increments the ticket's `version`. Both branches converge at `ONSITE`, and stock is consumed on exactly one of them — the arrival — never twice.

Resolution is the join point and the moment every service number is computed. A `FieldTechnician` resolves an `ONSITE` ticket by reporting `labor_hours` (zero or greater, and never more than 8 hours per ticket — a larger figure reports `ERR_LABOR_HOURS_EXCEED_LIMIT`). Resolution performs `ONSITE → RESOLVED`, stamps `responded_at` from the logical clock, recomputes the technician's `open_dispatch_count` downward, increments the ticket's `version`, and cascades onto the hardware: when the device's `lifecycle_state` is `SERVICE_HOLD` it returns `SERVICE_HOLD → ACTIVE` with its own `version` increment, making `ACTIVE → SERVICE_HOLD → ACTIVE` a cycle that repeats once per dispatched ticket. Attempting resolution on a ticket that never reached `ONSITE` reports `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`.

Three derived money and time figures are written at resolution. First, `hours_late`: the number of hours between `respond_by` and `responded_at`, rounded up to a whole hour, floored at zero so that a ticket resolved on or before its deadline records exactly 0. Second, `labor_cents`: exactly 0 whenever the contract's `included_service_scope` is `FULL`, and otherwise the reported `labor_hours` **rounded up to a whole number of hours first** and then multiplied by the contract's `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` — the rounding happens before the multiplication, never after. Third, `sla_credit_cents`, the response-time breach credit, which is computed in four steps. Step one: the excused portion of the lateness is the smaller of `hours_late` and the amount by which `parts_hold_hours` exceeds an 8-hour parts grace allowance, that excess itself floored at zero — so a hold of 8 hours or less excuses nothing, and a 13-hour hold excuses at most 5 hours of lateness. Step two: the billable late hours are `hours_late` less that excused portion; if the result is zero, `sla_credit_cents` is 0 and the calculation stops. Step three: otherwise the billable late hours are divided by a 4-hour credit step and rounded **up** to a whole step, that step count is multiplied by 5 percent, and the product is doubled when `severity` is `CRITICAL` and left as it is when `severity` is `STANDARD`; the resulting factor is then capped at 50 percent. Step four: `sla_credit_cents` is the contract's `monthly_service_fee_cents` multiplied by that capped factor, rounded half up to a whole cent once, and finally capped at 25,000 cents — $250 — per ticket. The credit is a debit against the provider: §3.7 subtracts it from the period statement.

A ticket may also be abandoned. A `CustomerAdmin` — again constrained to the site that owns the device, on pain of `ERR_SITE_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` — may cancel a ticket whose `status` is `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED` or `PARTS_HOLD`, performing `OPEN → CANCELLED`, `DISPATCHED → CANCELLED` or `PARTS_HOLD → CANCELLED` with identical effects in all three cases; a ticket already `ONSITE`, `RESOLVED` or `CANCELLED` reports `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation retains `assigned_technician_id` for audit rather than nulling it, recomputes the technician's `open_dispatch_count` downward, increments the ticket's `version`, and compensates the hardware: when the device sits in `SERVICE_HOLD` **and** no other ticket of that device remains in `DISPATCHED`, `PARTS_HOLD` or `ONSITE`, the device returns `SERVICE_HOLD → ACTIVE`. No locker stock is consumed or returned by a cancellation, because stock only ever moves at arrival. Both `RESOLVED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal ticket states.

### 3.7. Billing Periods: the Provisional Projection and the Final Actual

A **billing period** is the unit of invoicing. A `BillingAnalyst` opens one for a `contract_id` with a `period_start` and a `period_end` forming a half-open window. `period_start` must be strictly earlier than `period_end`, and the window must not intersect another period of the same contract — touching endpoints are fine, so a January period ending exactly where a February period begins is legal — with any violation reporting `ERR_PERIOD_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A new period has `status` `OPEN`, a `sweep_run_count` of 0, an `exception_open_count` of 0, and both `provisional_total_cents` and `final_total_cents` null. `exception_open_count` is derived: it is the number of readings assigned to that period whose `status` is `PENDING_VALIDATION` or `EXCEPTION`. `provisional_total_cents` mirrors the total of the period's provisional statement and stays null while none exists; `final_total_cents` mirrors the total of the period's final statement and stays null until the period is `CLOSED`.

Statements come in two variants and the difference between them is the heart of this system. Every statement records which one it is in `variant` — `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` — and records the corresponding evidence basis in `projection_basis`, which is `PROJECTED` on a provisional statement and `ACTUAL` on a final one. Both variants stamp `generated_at` from the logical clock.

A **provisional statement** is a mid-period projection, produced by a `BillingAnalyst` from a period whose `status` is `OPEN`, `SWEPT` or already `PROVISIONAL`, on a contract that is `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`), and carrying the `expected_billing_period_version` concurrency token of §2.8 (a mismatch reporting `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` at HTTP 412). Regeneration is a first-class operation: any pre-existing provisional statement for that period is **superseded**, meaning it is deleted together with all of its lines and replaced by the newly generated one, and `provisional_total_cents` is overwritten with the new projection. Generation drives `OPEN → PROVISIONAL`, `SWEPT → PROVISIONAL` or `PROVISIONAL → PROVISIONAL`, sets `provisional_total_cents` to the new statement's `total_cents`, and increments the period's `version` by 1. The request returns 201 with the newly created statement.

A **final statement** is issued when a `BillingAnalyst` closes the period, which drives `SWEPT → CLOSED` or `PROVISIONAL → CLOSED`; a period still `OPEN` and never swept cannot be closed and reports `ERR_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`. Closing requires three further things. The period's `exception_open_count` must be exactly 0 — no reading of the period may still be `PENDING_VALIDATION` or `EXCEPTION` — failing which the request reports `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_OPEN_EXCEPTIONS`. The logical clock must have reached `period_end`, with equality sufficient, failing which the request reports `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. And the governing contract's `status` must be either `ACTIVE` or `TERMINATING`, which is the one place the contract-active rule is deliberately relaxed so that a contract winding down can still be billed out. Closing creates the final statement, sets `status` to `CLOSED` and `final_total_cents` to the statement's `total_cents`, and then cascades onto the contract: `lifetime_billed_cents` grows by the statement's `total_cents`, and `true_up_balance_cents` grows by the period's `final_total_cents` less its `provisional_total_cents` — treating a null `provisional_total_cents` as equal to the final total, so that a period which never carried a projection contributes exactly zero to the true-up. Both the period's `version` and the contract's `version` increment. The request returns 201 with the final statement.

Both variants price the period through the same six-step algorithm; only step one differs.

**Step one — the input set and per-device pages.** Only readings assigned to the period with `status` `ACCEPTED` contribute anything; readings in `PENDING_VALIDATION`, `EXCEPTION` or `VOID` contribute nothing at all. The devices that participate are the distinct devices referenced by those accepted readings, considered in ascending order of the device's `created_at`. For each such device, its actual monochrome pages are the sum of `mono_delta` across its accepted readings in the period, and its actual colour pages are the sum of `color_delta`. On the **FINAL** variant, the device's billable pages for each meter are exactly those actual sums. On the **PROVISIONAL** variant they are scaled up to the whole period: the period length in days is the number of seconds between `period_start` and `period_end` divided by 86,400 and rounded **up** to a whole day; the elapsed length in days is the number of seconds between `period_start` and the earlier of the current logical clock and `period_end`, divided by 86,400, rounded **down** to a whole day, and then floored at a minimum of 1 day so the scaling never divides by zero. Each device's projected pages for a meter are its actual pages for that meter multiplied by the period length in days, divided by the elapsed length in days, and rounded half up to a whole page. That rounding happens **per device and per meter, before any aggregation** — projecting the aggregate would give a different answer and is wrong.

**Step two — contract-level volume and tier selection.** The contract-level monochrome volume is the sum of every participating device's billable monochrome pages, and the contract-level colour volume is the sum of every participating device's billable colour pages. These two figures are recorded on the statement as `mono_pages_billed` and `color_pages_billed`. Each volume then selects **one** rate-card row from the rows of that contract and `page_class`: the single row whose band contains the volume, where the band runs from `from_page` to `to_page` with **both bounds inclusive** and a null `to_page` meaning unbounded above. Bands never overlap, so at most one row can match. When the volume for a `page_class` is zero, the row at `tier_index` 1 is nominated (its rate goes unused because no line will be emitted for that class); when there is no rate-card row for that `page_class` at all, the statement records a tier index of 0 and a rate of 0 for that class. When the volume is greater than zero and no band contains it, pricing fails with `ERR_TIER_COVERAGE_GAP`. The chosen indices are recorded on the statement as `mono_tier_index` and `color_tier_index`, and the chosen prices as `mono_rate_micro_dollars_per_page` and `color_rate_micro_dollars_per_page`. The ladder is **non-graduated**: the single selected rate applies to *every* page of that class for *every* device, and pages are never split across bands.

**Step three — the lines.** Lines are emitted device by device in ascending order of the device's `created_at`, and within each device the monochrome line comes before the colour line. A line is emitted only when the device's billable pages for that meter are greater than zero; a zero-page meter produces no line at all. Each `StatementLine` records its parent `statement_id`, the `device_id` it prices, the `page_class`, the `pages` it covers, the `rate_micro_dollars_per_page` copied from the statement's selected rate for that class, and an `amount_cents` computed as the pages multiplied by that rate, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The statement's `usage_subtotal_cents` is then the sum of the emitted lines' `amount_cents`. The order is pinned: **round each line to whole cents first, then sum the lines**. Accumulating micro-dollars across all lines and rounding once at the end produces a different subtotal and is incorrect. Statement lines carry only an `id` and a `created_at` from the common property set.

**Step four — the minimum-volume shortfall.** The monochrome shortfall in pages is the contract's `minimum_mono_pages_per_period` less the contract-level monochrome volume, floored at zero; the colour shortfall is `minimum_color_pages_per_period` less the contract-level colour volume, likewise floored at zero. Their sum is recorded as `shortfall_pages`. Shortfall is always priced at the **entry** rate, never at the volume-selected rate: for each class the shortfall pages are multiplied by the `rate_micro_dollars_per_page` of that class's `tier_index` 1 row (taken as 0 when no such row exists), divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent, and the two class results are then added together to give `shortfall_cents`. Each class is rounded separately before the two are summed.

**Step five — labour, credits and waste.** The tickets that matter to a period are those with `status` `RESOLVED`, belonging to a device of that contract, whose `responded_at` falls inside the period's half-open window. `service_labor_cents` is exactly 0 when the contract's `included_service_scope` is `FULL`, and otherwise the sum of `labor_cents` across those tickets. `sla_credit_cents` is the sum of `sla_credit_cents` across those same tickets regardless of service scope — response-time credits are owed under both `BASIC` and `FULL`. `waste_penalty_cents` is exactly 0 on the `PROVISIONAL` variant; on the `FINAL` variant it is 4,500 cents — a flat $45 per wasted cartridge — multiplied by the number of consumable orders belonging to a device of that contract which carry `waste_flagged` true, have a `status` other than `CANCELLED`, and were created inside the period's half-open window.

**Step six — the total.** `total_cents` is the `usage_subtotal_cents` plus `shortfall_cents` plus `service_labor_cents` plus `waste_penalty_cents`, **minus** `sla_credit_cents`. It is not floored at zero: a period in which response-time credits outweigh usage legitimately produces a negative `total_cents`, and the system must report that negative figure rather than clamping it.

Consider a period whose devices have printed steadily for ten days of a thirty-day month. The provisional statement scales each device's ten days of pages up by three, and that inflated contract-level volume may select a *cheaper* rate-card band than the actual volume eventually does; it also charges no waste penalty. The final statement prices the real pages at the band the real volume selects and adds the waste penalties. For identical reading data the two variants therefore produce different totals whenever the elapsed days are fewer than the period days, and the signed difference between them is exactly what accumulates into the contract's `true_up_balance_cents` at close.

### 3.8. The Quarterly Business Review: Savings, Trends and Right-Sizing

Once a quarter the `AccountManager` sits down with the customer, and the review that supports that conversation is produced in a single operation. Creating one takes just the `contract_id` and a `quarter_label` matching the four-digit-year-then-quarter form such as `2026-Q3`, unique within the contract on pain of `ERR_QUARTER_DUPLICATE`. The new review has `status` `DRAFT`, an `authored_by_user_id` stamped from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a null `approved_by_user_id`. Everything else on it is computed at creation time from the fleet and the closed periods.

The **fleet** for review purposes is the set of that contract's devices whose `lifecycle_state` is `PLACED`, `NETWORKED`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_HOLD`, considered in ascending order of `created_at`. Devices still merely `ASSESSED`, and devices already `RETRIEVED` or `REMOVED`, are excluded from every review computation.

Two savings figures are produced, and they answer two different questions. `baseline_savings_cents` answers *what did the assessment promise?* For each fleet device, its pre-contract monthly cost is the sum of `assessed_monthly_mono_pages` and `assessed_monthly_color_pages` multiplied by `baseline_cost_micro_dollars_per_page`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent, plus its `baseline_overhead_cents_per_month`. Its managed-print monthly cost is `assessed_monthly_mono_pages` multiplied by the `MONO` entry rate — the `rate_micro_dollars_per_page` of the contract's `tier_index` 1 `MONO` row, taken as 0 when absent — plus `assessed_monthly_color_pages` multiplied by the equivalent `COLOR` entry rate, with that combined micro-dollar figure divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent once. `baseline_savings_cents` is the sum, over the fleet, of each device's pre-contract cost less its managed-print cost. Each device's two costs are rounded before the fleet-wide summation — round per device, then sum.

`realized_savings_cents` answers *what actually happened?* It reads the contract's most recently closed period: among that contract's periods with `status` `CLOSED`, the one with the greatest `period_end`, with the earliest `created_at` breaking a tie. When no closed period exists, `realized_savings_cents` is 0. Otherwise, from that period's `FINAL` statement, each fleet device's realized pages are the sum of `pages` across the statement lines naming that device, and its realized billed amount is the sum of `amount_cents` across those same lines. The device's realized baseline is its realized pages multiplied by `baseline_cost_micro_dollars_per_page`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent, plus `baseline_overhead_cents_per_month`. `realized_savings_cents` is the sum, over the fleet, of each device's realized baseline less its realized billed amount — again rounded per device before summation. Because the two variants read different page figures on identical device rows, a fleet that under-runs or over-runs its assessment reports a realized number quite different from its baseline number, and that gap is the honest content of the review.

`volume_trend_pct` compares the two most recently closed periods. Take the contract's closed periods ordered by `period_end` descending, with earliest `created_at` breaking ties, and call the first one the current period and the second the prior period. Each period's volume is its `FINAL` statement's `mono_pages_billed` plus `color_pages_billed`. When there is no prior period, or when the prior period's volume is zero, `volume_trend_pct` is 0. Otherwise it is the current volume less the prior volume, divided by the prior volume, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places. Periods that are `OPEN`, `SWEPT` or `PROVISIONAL` are ignored entirely.

Right-sizing is where the review becomes actionable. Each fleet device's utilization is its realized pages from the most recently closed period's `FINAL` statement lines divided by its `rated_monthly_duty_cycle`, and the comparison is made as an **exact rational** — pages against duty cycle scaled by the threshold percentage — never as a rounded decimal, so that a device sitting exactly on a boundary lands on the inclusive side. The classification writes `right_sizing_action` on the device and is evaluated in this order: utilization at or below 15 percent yields `CONSOLIDATE`; otherwise utilization at or below 40 percent yields `DOWNGRADE`; otherwise utilization at or above 90 percent yields `UPGRADE`; otherwise the device yields `RETAIN`. When the contract has no closed period at all, every fleet device is classified `RETAIN`. Devices outside the fleet — those whose `lifecycle_state` is `ASSESSED`, `RETRIEVED` or `REMOVED` — keep `right_sizing_action` at `NONE`. The review then records the four tallies across the fleet in `retain_count`, `downgrade_count`, `upgrade_count` and `consolidate_count`, and records `refresh_due_device_count` as the sum of `upgrade_count` and `consolidate_count` — the devices a refresh plan should touch.

Recommendations are computed at creation but **published at approval**. A second `AccountManager` — necessarily a different `X-User-ID` from the review's `authored_by_user_id`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — approves the review, driving `DRAFT → APPROVED`, stamping `approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and incrementing the review's `version`. Approval cascades onto the fleet: every device of the contract whose `right_sizing_action` is `UPGRADE` or `CONSOLIDATE` has `refresh_due` set to true and its own `version` incremented. `APPROVED` is terminal, and approving an already-approved review reports `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_APPROVED`. Only after that publication can the refresh linkage of §3.3 record a successor asset, because that linkage tests `refresh_due`.

### 3.9. End of Contract: Removal, Data Sanitization and Settlement

The wind-down begins with a request. An `AccountManager` opens a removal order against a `contract_id` with a proposed `scheduled_for` collection instant that must not be earlier than the logical clock (otherwise `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST`). The contract must be `ACTIVE`, and the contract must not already have a removal order in flight — no existing removal order of that contract may be in `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COLLECTED` or `SANITIZED` — failing which the request reports `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`. Creation gives the removal order a `status` of `REQUESTED`, a `sanitization_method` of `NONE`, a null `sanitization_certificate_ref`, a `device_count` of 0, a null `settled_at`, and zero in each of its six money fields. It simultaneously drives the contract `ACTIVE → TERMINATING` with a contract `version` increment, and stamps `removal_order_id` onto every device of that contract whose `lifecycle_state` is `PLACED`, `NETWORKED`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_HOLD`. Those stamped devices are the order's **covered devices**, and `device_count` is derived as the number of devices carrying that order's identifier.

Confirming the date drives `REQUESTED → SCHEDULED` and writes the supplied `scheduled_for` onto the order, again refusing any instant earlier than the logical clock with `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST` and incrementing the order's `version`. A `FieldTechnician` then records physical collection, driving `SCHEDULED → COLLECTED`. Collection requires the logical clock to have reached `scheduled_for`, with equality sufficient, failing which it reports `ERR_COLLECTION_NOT_DUE`; and it requires that no covered device has a service ticket still in `DISPATCHED`, `PARTS_HOLD` or `ONSITE`, because hardware cannot be carted away while an engineer is working on it. Collection transitions every covered device to `RETRIEVED` — `PLACED → RETRIEVED`, `NETWORKED → RETRIEVED`, `ACTIVE → RETRIEVED` or `SERVICE_HOLD → RETRIEVED`, each incrementing that device's `version`, with the `SERVICE_HOLD` case additionally requiring that no in-flight ticket remains for that device — recomputes the order's `device_count`, and increments the order's `version`.

Data sanitization is the compliance gate, and it is where the deepest cascade in the system fires. A `FieldTechnician` records it by supplying a `sanitization_method` of `CRYPTO_ERASE`, `OVERWRITE_3PASS` or `PHYSICAL_DESTRUCTION` — the placeholder value `NONE` is not an acceptable method — together with a non-empty `sanitization_certificate_ref`; a missing method or missing certificate reports `ERR_SANITIZATION_EVIDENCE_MISSING`. Sanitization writes both values onto the order and drives `COLLECTED → SANITIZED`; attempting it from any other state reports `ERR_REMOVAL_INVALID_STATE`. The second hop transitions every covered device `RETRIEVED → REMOVED`, setting `secure_release_enrolled` back to false and incrementing each device's `version`; `REMOVED` is the terminal lifecycle state. The third hop follows from the removals: the contract's `active_device_count` is recomputed downward from the resulting device set, and every cartridge order of every removed device that is still in `ORDERED` status is transitioned to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `DEVICE_REMOVED`. The fourth hop follows from those cancellations: each affected site's `open_toner_units` and the contract's `consumables_committed_cents` are recomputed downward by the cancelled orders' quantities and committed amounts. The removal order's own `version` increments.

Settlement is the last act, performed by a `BillingAnalyst` and driving `SANITIZED → SETTLED`, which is terminal. It requires the contract to be in `TERMINATING`, and it requires that every billing period of the contract whose `period_end` is at or before the logical clock has `status` `CLOSED` — a period that is still genuinely running may remain `OPEN`, and its accepted pages settle as unbilled — failing which the request reports `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OPEN`. Settlement stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock, increments the order's `version`, and cascades the contract `TERMINATING → CLOSED` with a contract `version` increment.

The settlement figures are computed in a **pinned waterfall**, and the order matters because the sanitization fee's discount tier is keyed on a subtotal that excludes the fee itself — which is how the apparent circularity is resolved.

1. **Unbilled pages.** The unbilled readings are every reading with `status` `ACCEPTED` on a device of the contract whose billing period is still `OPEN`, `SWEPT` or `PROVISIONAL` — that is, any accepted page no final statement has yet billed. Their monochrome pages are the sum of `mono_delta` and their colour pages the sum of `color_delta` across that set. `unbilled_pages_charge_cents` is the monochrome pages multiplied by the `MONO` entry rate (the `rate_micro_dollars_per_page` of the contract's `tier_index` 1 `MONO` row, taken as 0 when absent), divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent, plus the colour pages multiplied by the equivalent `COLOR` entry rate, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to a whole cent. Each class is rounded once, and the two results are then summed.
2. **Early termination.** `early_termination_fee_applied_cents` is the contract's `early_termination_fee_cents` when the logical clock is strictly earlier than the contract's `created_at` advanced by `term_months` calendar months, and 0 otherwise — a contract that has run its full term pays nothing to leave.
3. **Residual credit.** `device_residual_credit_cents` is 0 when the `sanitization_method` is `PHYSICAL_DESTRUCTION`, because destroyed hardware has no residual value; otherwise it is 12,000 cents — a $120 residual credit per returned asset — multiplied by the order's `device_count`.
4. **Gross subtotal.** `gross_settlement_cents` is `unbilled_pages_charge_cents` plus `early_termination_fee_applied_cents` **minus** `device_residual_credit_cents`. It deliberately excludes the sanitization fee.
5. **Sanitization fee.** The undiscounted sanitization base is 7,500 cents — $75 per asset — multiplied by the order's `device_count`. A volume discount of 10 percent applies when `gross_settlement_cents` is at or above 500,000 cents, that is at or above $5,000; below that threshold the discount is zero. `sanitization_fee_cents` is the base multiplied by one less the discount fraction, rounded half up to a whole cent **once**, after the discount multiplication.
6. **Net.** `net_settlement_cents` is `gross_settlement_cents` plus `sanitization_fee_cents`, and it is the amount invoiced at settlement. Like a period total, it is not floored at zero: a fleet of returned assets with few unbilled pages can settle to a net credit in the customer's favour.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201; every other successful operation, including all state transitions, all reads and all list queries, returns HTTP 200. Note two deliberate exceptions to the intuition that "an action returns the thing you acted on": generating a provisional statement and closing a period both **create** a new `BillingStatement` and therefore return 201 with that statement in `data`, even though each is addressed as an action on an existing period. Meter-reading intake likewise returns 201 whether the reading validates into `ACCEPTED` or lands in `EXCEPTION`. The clock-setting capability returns 200 with an empty `data` object.
