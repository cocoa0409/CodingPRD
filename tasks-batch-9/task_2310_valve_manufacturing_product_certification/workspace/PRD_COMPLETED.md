# Valve Manufacturing & Product Certification API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An industrial valve manufacturer sells two things at once: a machined pressure-containing assembly, and the documented evidence that the assembly is what the nameplate claims. The second is harder. A single gate valve leaving the works carries a design revision, a mill heat number traceable to a qualified foundry, a shell and seat test run to a published standard, an actuator sized to a break-torque calculation, a monogram program license under which the nameplate was struck, and — for fire-safe, cryogenic or fugitive-emission service — a type test whose validity has not lapsed. When any one of those links is broken retroactively, every downstream claim built on it has to be withdrawn, and the withdrawal has to reach the customer's documentation package.

This API is the system of record for that chain. It governs product-line design and release, foundry qualification and material traceability from heat lot through rough component into a serialised unit, the test programme and its standard-derived pressure and hold-time envelopes, monogram license applications with their quality-system audits and finding registers, actuator packaging with torque sizing, engineered-to-order programmes with datasheet review and priced documentation packages, and aftermarket repair-kit reservation with field service jobs. Everything is mounted beneath the `/valve-mfg` prefix.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, quality decisions are **segregated**: the person who witnesses a test may not be the person who certifies the unit, and the auditor who leads an audit may not be the auditor who waives its findings. Second, compliance is **retroactive and cascading**: quarantining a heat lot, suspending a license or denying a monogram application all reach forward through the components, units, orders and issued document packages that depended on them, and the compensation offered when the cause is cleared is deliberately partial — material is not un-scrapped, and a decertified unit is never silently recertified.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header whose value consists only of whitespace counts as empty, and surrounding whitespace is otherwise trimmed off before the value is used. Seven roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `ProductEngineer`, `ProcurementSpecialist`, `SupplierRep`, `TestTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `MonogramAuditor`, and `FieldServiceCoordinator`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those seven, or that is one of them but is not authorised for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Authorisation is finer-grained than the endpoint table alone suggests. Several endpoints accept a family of actions in the request body, and each action carries its own role list. A `ProcurementSpecialist` may reach the component disposition endpoint but not the heat-lot disposition endpoint; a `ProductEngineer` may submit a product-line design for review or withdraw a released one, but only a `QualityManager` may release or reject it. When the caller's role is permitted on the endpoint but not for the specific `action`, `decision` or `entry_action` value supplied in the body, the response is still `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). A value that is absent, empty, or not a member of the endpoint's declared action set at all is not a role failure but a request-shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and that membership check is evaluated before the per-action role check.

Six of the seven roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProductEngineer`, `ProcurementSpecialist`, `TestTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `MonogramAuditor` or `FieldServiceCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where the workflow demands an audit trail — as `authored_by_user_id` on a product-line design, `qualified_by_user_id` on a foundry qualification, `verified_by_user_id` on a heat lot, `performed_by_user_id` on a test record, `approved_by_user_id` on an actuator package, `certified_by_user_id` on a certified unit — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

`SupplierRep` is the exception. When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Foundry`; ownership checks on any resource that references a foundry compare that resource's `foundry_id` to `X-User-ID`. A supplier representative may declare a heat lot only with a `foundry_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`, may list heat lots but sees only rows whose `foundry_id` matches — and an explicit `foundry_id` query filter naming some other foundry is rejected outright rather than silently narrowed — and may retrieve a single heat lot only when that lot belongs to it. A cross-tenant read or write is rejected with `ERR_FOUNDRY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) — deliberately distinct from `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, because the role was correct and only the tenancy was wrong. Retrieval of a foundry record by a `SupplierRep` is likewise limited to its own row; the foundry list, by contrast, is not tenancy-scoped and a `SupplierRep` sees every foundry on it. Both single-row tenancy checks are evaluated only after the addressed row has been resolved, so an unknown identifier still reports `ERR_HEAT_LOT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FOUNDRY_NOT_FOUND` rather than a scope violation. Every other write endpoint in the system — dispositions, qualifications, units, tests, licenses, orders — is closed to `SupplierRep`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and only for list endpoints. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is still less than `total_count`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Four transport-level failures answer in this same envelope with `error_id` values that sit outside the domain catalogue of §5: a path that matches no route is `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with 404, a known path addressed with an unsupported method is `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with 405, a body that is not parseable JSON is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with 400, and an unexpected server fault is `ERR_INTERNAL` with 500. A trailing slash is not significant: every path is matched with and without it.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` skips a number of leading rows and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, a negative `offset`, a non-integer value for either, or either parameter supplied with an empty value (`?limit=` or `?offset=`), is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Integer means canonical decimal form: surrounding whitespace is tolerated, but a padded, signed or decimal-pointed spelling such as `05`, `+5` or `5.0` is `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` too, and `limit` is validated before `offset`.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with rows sharing a `created_at` broken by server insertion order so the ordering is always total. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` alongside the two request-identification fields.

Lists also accept domain filters, each an exact-match filter on the named field, applied to the derived value where the field is derived (a foundry's banded `status`, a qualification's lazily expired `status`, a unit's recomputed `cert_status`, a license's `compliance_posture`). A filter supplied with an empty value, and any query parameter that is not a declared filter or pagination parameter, is ignored rather than rejected. Product-line listings accept `status`, `valve_type` and `supersedes_model_id`. Foundry listings accept `status` and `country_code`. Qualification listings accept `foundry_id`, `status`, `process` and `material_grade`. Heat-lot listings accept `foundry_id`, `status` and `material_grade`. Component listings accept `heat_lot_id`, `target_model_id`, `status` and `component_type`. Valve-unit listings accept `model_id`, `body_component_id`, `monogram_license_id`, `engineered_order_id`, `build_status`, `cert_status` and `service_status`. The per-unit test listing accepts `test_type` and `result`. Actuator listings accept `valve_unit_id`, `sizing_status` and `actuator_type`. License listings accept `license_status`, `audit_state` and `compliance_posture`. Engineered-order listings accept `status`, `model_id`, `pinned_model_id` and `doc_package_status`.

### 2.4. Record Identity and Common Properties

Every primary resource — product-line designs, foundries, foundry qualifications, heat lots, components, valve units, actuator packages, monogram licenses and engineered orders — carries a common set of server-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation. Row bookkeeping timestamps — `created_at`, `updated_at` and the `api_request_timestamp` of the envelope — are taken from real wall-clock time, not from the logical works clock of §2.5; only the domain instants named in §2.5 read the logical clock.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. Recomputation of a derived field that the server writes back — a foundry's `quality_index`, a lot's mass balance, an order's completeness percentage — counts as a mutation of the row it lands on, and the write-back happens as part of the request that changed the inputs, not as part of a later read. Recomputation that happens only in order to serve a read never bumps `version` or `updated_at`: the lazily derived values (a foundry's counters, index and band, a lot's mass balance, a design's `qualified_special_services`, a unit's recomputed `cert_status` and `provisional_emission_leak_class`, a license's open counts and posture, an order's `spec_stale`, `documentation_completeness_pct` and `certified_unit_count`) are computed at serialisation time, and a qualification whose window has lapsed reading `EXPIRED` is likewise not a mutation. The one lazy evaluation that *is* a mutation is the automatic revocation of an expired conditional license described in §3.6, because it rewrites `license_status` and runs a cascade: that bumps the license's `version` and `updated_at` on the read that discovers it. A cascade that changes a row's stored fields always counts.

Test records are the one exception. A `TestRecord` is immutable once written: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and has neither an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field.

### 2.5. The Logical Works Clock

Every date-dependent rule in this system reads a single logical clock rather than wall-clock time: foundry qualification validity windows, type-test validity, conditional license expiry, and the audit cooling-off buffer. On a fresh system the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it moves only when an operator calls the clock-setting capability with a `to_iso8601` instant. Any role may drive the clock; it exists so that time-dependent behaviour is testable. The `to_iso8601` field is required — omitting it, or supplying something that is not an ISO 8601 instant, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a bare date, a naive timestamp and an offset-bearing timestamp are all accepted and normalised to UTC, and the call answers `200 OK` with a single-key object `{"current_utc": <the new instant, ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits>}` rather than with a domain resource. The instants written from the logical clock are a design's `released_at`, a unit's `certified_at`, a license's `last_audit_closed_at`, `granted_at` and `conditional_expires_at`, and a document entry's `added_at`.

Setting the clock rewrites nothing. Expiry is evaluated lazily, at the moment a row is read or used: a foundry qualification whose window has closed is reported as `EXPIRED` on retrieval and refused as a qualification basis on use, without any sweep having run. The same laziness applies to a conditional license whose validity has elapsed.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Rounding in this domain is always **round half up**, and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity rather than global:

- Torque figures — `required_torque_nm` and `sized_torque_nm`, and equally the supplied `base_break_torque_nm`, `output_torque_nm` and `supply_pressure_bar` as they are echoed back — are held to 1 decimal place. The safety margin, `safety_margin_pct`, is stored as a fraction to 2 decimal places.
- Test envelope pressures, `required_pressure_bar`, are held to 1 decimal place, as is the applied `test_pressure_bar` on the record; `required_hold_seconds` and `hold_seconds` are integer counts of seconds. A lot's `carbon_equivalent_pct` is held to 3 decimal places and its `impact_energy_j` to 1. The measured `leak_rate_ppm` and `leak_drops`, by contrast, are echoed back exactly as supplied and are not rounded on storage; only the two derived emission figures round.
- Material masses — `qty_received_kg`, `net_weight_kg`, `qty_committed_kg`, `qty_scrapped_kg` and `qty_available_kg` — are held to 3 decimal places.
- The foundry `quality_index`, the time-weighted emission figure in parts per million, and `documentation_completeness_pct` are held to 2 decimal places.
- Money is integer cents throughout. `unit_price_cents`, `customer_credit_limit_cents`, `gross_quoted_cents`, `net_billable_cents` and `credit_issued_cents` never carry a fractional part, and every intermediate money figure is rounded to zero decimal places before the next step of a calculation consumes it.
- Finding counters, revision numbers and round counters are plain integers.

Where a calculation rounds more than once, the order of the roundings is part of the specification, not an implementation detail. Two calculations in this system are explicit about it: the actuator margin tier is selected from the **already-rounded** required torque, and the documentation completeness percentage rounds each per-category fraction **before** the weighted sum is taken.

### 2.7. Traceability Cascades and Compensating Dispositions

Because the value of a certificate is the chain behind it, a compliance event at the bottom of the chain propagates upward through every claim that rested on it, in a fixed order, within the same request. Quarantining a heat lot puts its components on hold; holding a component decertifies the units built from it; decertifying a unit voids the issued documentation package of the engineered order that unit was built against and moves that order out of production. Suspending or denying a monogram license reaches units and orders the same way. Each entity touched by a cascade is a mutation in its own right: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments.

Compensation is offered where physical reality allows it and withheld where it does not. Releasing a quarantined lot returns its held components to the status they held before the hold, and gives the foundry back the rejection it was charged — but the units the quarantine decertified stay decertified and must be re-tested. Voiding a mis-issued component returns its mass to the parent lot; scrapping it does not. Aborting a field service job restores the repair kits it had reserved; completing the job consumes them. These asymmetries are intentional: a compensating disposition undoes bookkeeping, never physics.

### 2.8. Stale-View Protection on Unit and Order Writes

Two write operations in this system are legitimate to repeat — proposing a new actuator package supersedes the previous one, and appending a datasheet revision supersedes the previous datasheet — so neither can be refused merely for having been done before. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent's `version`. Repeatability is not the same as being state-free: proposing an actuator package genuinely imposes no state precondition on the unit at all (a `BLANK`, a `SHIPPED`, a `DECERTIFIED` unit will all take a fresh package), whereas appending a datasheet revision still requires the order to be sitting at `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, and that state check is evaluated before the version check, so a revision aimed at an order in any other status reports `ERR_STATE_INVALID` even when the version it carries is also wrong.

Proposing an actuator package requires the non-optional field `expected_valve_unit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ValveUnit`. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VALVE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is what stops a writer from re-sizing an actuator against an out-of-date view of a unit that has since been decertified by a cascade.

Appending a datasheet revision to an engineered order requires the non-optional field `expected_engineered_order_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `EngineeredOrder`. A mismatch with the order's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412), preventing a revision authored against a superseded `datasheet_revision_no` from overwriting a newer one.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Both `expected_valve_unit_version` and `expected_engineered_order_version` are required inputs wherever they appear — omitting them is a request-shape failure, not an opt-out — and each must be a non-negative integer, so a null, a string, a fraction or a negative number is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before any version comparison is attempted.

### 2.9. Path Resolution for Unit-Scoped Records

Two record families are addressed beneath their parent: the test records of a valve unit and the sub-resources of an engineered order. For any nested path the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing determines the response. A request for the test records of a unit that does not exist fails with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` before any record-level check runs; a request against a sub-resource of a non-existent order fails with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. The same left-to-right precedence applies to identifiers supplied in a request body: a creation referencing several parents resolves them in the order the existence rules are ranked in §2.10 and reports the first miss.

### 2.10. The Rejection Ladder

Many operations in this system can fail several ways at once, and which error the caller sees is specified, not incidental. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the **first** violated check determines the response; no later check can mask an earlier one.

1. **Headers.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` both present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role on the endpoint.** The declared role is one of the seven and is permitted on this endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Role for the action.** On a multi-action endpoint, the role is permitted for the specific action, decision or entry-action value in the body — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. This band guards product-line actions, heat-lot dispositions, component dispositions, unit certification actions, license actions, license finding entries, order review actions, order lifecycle actions and qualification actions.
4. **Foundry tenancy.** A `SupplierRep` touches only heat lots whose `foundry_id` equals its own `X-User-ID` — otherwise `ERR_FOUNDRY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. This band guards heat-lot creation, the heat-lot list and single heat-lot retrieval, plus single-foundry retrieval. On heat-lot creation the tenancy check reads the raw `foundry_id` out of the body and so precedes the shape band; on the heat-lot list, however, the pagination band is evaluated first, so a `SupplierRep` who both asks for another foundry's lots and supplies a bad `limit` sees `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`; and on the two single-row retrievals the row is resolved first, so the existence band precedes tenancy there.
5. **Request shape.** Every input field with no declared default is present, non-null, and of the declared type; strings are non-empty; enum values are members of their declared sets; array-valued fields contain no duplicates and no unknown members; integer fields reject booleans and reject fractional numbers while accepting a float that is exactly integral — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A body that is absent or empty is treated as an empty object, so its failure surfaces as the first missing required field; a body that is malformed JSON or is a JSON value other than an object is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` directly. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored, never rejected. Applies to every write endpoint.
6. **Pagination shape.** `limit` an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` a non-negative integer — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Applies to every list endpoint.
7. **Numeric domains.** `pressure_class` and `max_pressure_class` one of 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500 or 2500; `bore_size_mm` an integer of at least 1; `base_break_torque_nm` greater than zero; `ordered_qty` at least 1; `unit_price_cents` at least 1; `customer_credit_limit_cents` at least 0; `datasheet_break_torque_nm`, wherever it is supplied, greater than zero; `qty_received_kg` greater than zero; `carbon_equivalent_pct` and `impact_energy_j` at least 0; `net_weight_kg` greater than zero; `test_pressure_bar` at least 0; `leak_rate_ppm` at least 0; `leak_drops` an integer of at least 0; `hold_seconds` an integer of at least 1; `output_torque_nm` greater than zero; `supply_pressure_bar` at least 0; `repair_kit_stock_qty` at least 0; `kit_qty` at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, on whichever write endpoint carries the field. These domain checks are part of the shape band in practice: each field is rejected as it is read, so the first offending field in the endpoint's own reading order determines the message.
8. **Interval ordering.** Supplied intervals are strictly ordered: `valid_from` strictly before `valid_until`, `audit_window_start` strictly before `audit_window_end`, `started_at` strictly before `ended_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This band guards foundry-qualification creation and qualification actions, license application and license actions, and test-record creation.
9. **Existence**, in this order of resource kind: the product-line design referenced by the path or by `model_id`, `pinned_model_id`, `supersedes_model_id` or `target_model_id` (`ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`); the foundry referenced by the path or by `foundry_id` (`ERR_FOUNDRY_NOT_FOUND`); the qualification referenced by the path (`ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`); the heat lot referenced by the path or by `heat_lot_id` (`ERR_HEAT_LOT_NOT_FOUND`); the component referenced by the path or by `body_component_id` (`ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`); the valve unit referenced by the path or by `valve_unit_id` (`ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`); the actuator package referenced by the path (`ERR_ACTUATOR_NOT_FOUND`); the monogram license referenced by the path or by `monogram_license_id` (`ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`); the engineered order referenced by the path or by `engineered_order_id` (`ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`). All nine report HTTP 404.
10. **State.** The requested action is a declared transition out of the entity's current state — otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). This is also the code for terminal-state lockdown (superseded and withdrawn designs, rejected lots, scrapped and voided components, a shipped build dimension, revoked qualifications, denied and revoked licenses, rejected and cancelled orders) and for every dependent-state precondition that has no more specific code of its own: an audit that is not closed, an order that is not a draft, a wrong `service_status`, a component sitting on hold, an order still holding a decertified unit, or a missing `datasheet_break_torque_nm` at review submission.
11. **Certification-state specialisations**, all HTTP 409: certifying a unit whose `cert_status` is already `CERTIFIED` is `ERR_UNIT_ALREADY_CERTIFIED` — a repeated certification is an error, never a no-op; certifying a unit whose `cert_status` is anything other than `TESTS_PASSED` is `ERR_UNIT_NOT_TEST_PASSED`; advancing `build_status` to `PACKAGED` or `SHIPPED` while `cert_status` is not `CERTIFIED` is `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`.
12. **Concurrency.** `expected_valve_unit_version` matches the unit's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_VALVE_UNIT_VERSION`), and `expected_engineered_order_version` matches the order's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`) — both HTTP 412.
13. **Business referential integrity**, in this order: `ERR_HEAT_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`, `ERR_FOUNDRY_NOT_QUALIFIED`, `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`, `ERR_LICENSE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, then `ERR_LICENSE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.
14. **Business safety**, in this order: `ERR_SPECIAL_SERVICE_NOT_QUALIFIED`, `ERR_REQUIRED_TESTS_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_SOD_TEST_WITNESS_CONFLICT`, `ERR_SOD_AUDIT_WAIVER_CONFLICT`, `ERR_MTR_CHEMISTRY_OUT_OF_SPEC`, `ERR_TEST_PRESSURE_BELOW_STANDARD`, `ERR_TEST_HOLD_TIME_SHORT`, `ERR_EMISSION_CLASS_FAIL`, `ERR_ACTUATOR_NOT_APPROVED`, then `ERR_ACTUATOR_TORQUE_INSUFFICIENT`.
15. **Temporal exclusivity**, in this order: `ERR_TEST_BENCH_OCCUPIED`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_AUDIT_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE`.
16. **Business constraints**, in this order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, `ERR_BUILD_SEQUENCE_INVALID`, `ERR_CLARIFICATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_OPEN_MAJOR_FINDINGS`, `ERR_MINOR_FINDING_THRESHOLD`, `ERR_PINNED_SPEC_STALE`, `ERR_DOC_PACKAGE_INCOMPLETE`, then `ERR_ORDER_UNITS_INCOMPLETE`.
17. **Fiscal and stock limits**, last of all, in this order: `ERR_LOT_INSUFFICIENT_MASS`, `ERR_REPAIR_KIT_OUT_OF_STOCK`, then `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Two collisions are worth calling out because a naive implementation inverts them. A `QualityManager` who also recorded one of a unit's tests, certifying a unit whose seat test failed, must see the state error `ERR_UNIT_NOT_TEST_PASSED` (HTTP 409) from band 11 and not the duty error `ERR_SOD_TEST_WITNESS_CONFLICT` (HTTP 403) from band 14. A component drawn from an over-committed lot that is still merely `VERIFIED` must report the referential error `ERR_HEAT_LOT_NOT_RELEASED` (HTTP 409) from band 13, not the mass error `ERR_LOT_INSUFFICIENT_MASS` (HTTP 422) from band 17.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two constraints in this system are enforced against `X-User-ID` rather than against the role, so the role check passes and the identity check still fails. The `QualityManager` who certifies a valve unit must not appear as the `performed_by_user_id` of **any** test record on that unit; a collision is `ERR_SOD_TEST_WITNESS_CONFLICT` (HTTP 403). The `MonogramAuditor` who waives an audit finding must not be the license's `lead_auditor_user_id`; a collision is `ERR_SOD_AUDIT_WAIVER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 403). Raising and closing findings remain open to the lead auditor — only waiving is restricted, because waiving is the only finding action that removes a blocker without corrective action.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Product-Line Design and Release

A `ProductEngineer` opens a product-line entry — a **design record** — by supplying a catalog `model_code`, a `valve_type` of `GATE`, `BALL`, `BUTTERFLY` or `CONTROL`, a `pressure_class` drawn from the ASME set 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500 and 2500, a `body_material_grade` of `WCB`, `CF8M`, `LCC`, `A105` or `F316`, a nominal `bore_size_mm` given as a whole number of millimetres, a `seat_type` of `SOFT` or `METAL`, and a catalog `base_break_torque_nm` measured at zero differential pressure, which is rounded half up to 1 decimal place as it is stored. Three inputs are optional: `required_special_services`, a subset of `FIRE_SAFE`, `CRYOGENIC` and `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` that the design claims, defaulting to the empty list; `repair_kit_stock_qty`, the aftermarket repair-kit units on hand for the line, defaulting to 0; and `supersedes_model_id`, naming the released design this one replaces, defaulting to null for a brand-new line — when it is supplied it must resolve to an existing design, otherwise `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, but that design's status is not constrained at creation. Creation itself applies no `model_code` uniqueness check: a design whose `model_code` is already carried by another design still opens normally, whatever status that other design holds, and the collision surfaces only when the new design is submitted for review. On creation the design opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `spec_revision` 1, an empty `qualified_special_services` list, `authored_by_user_id` set from `X-User-ID`, `released_at` null and `version` 1.

The design lifecycle runs `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → RELEASED`, with `SUPERSEDED` and `WITHDRAWN` as terminal states. Submitting for review moves `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` and is the `ProductEngineer`'s prerogative; it is available only from `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`; it requires that `base_break_torque_nm` and `bore_size_mm` are both greater than zero, a re-check of the stored values that reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a state error if it ever fails, and that no other design sharing the same `model_code` currently sits in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `RELEASED` — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. Code uniqueness is scoped to those three statuses precisely so that a withdrawn or superseded line can have its code reused.

Review is a `QualityManager` decision with two outcomes. Rejection moves `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT` and increments `spec_revision` by 1; because engineered orders pin a specific revision at approval, every order whose `pinned_model_id` is this design recomputes `spec_stale` and becomes stale as a consequence. Release moves `IN_REVIEW → RELEASED`, sets `released_at` to the current logical-clock instant, and requires that every service listed in `required_special_services` also appears in `qualified_special_services` — a design that claims a service it cannot evidence is refused with `ERR_SPECIAL_SERVICE_NOT_QUALIFIED`. Release also carries a two-link cascade: if `supersedes_model_id` is not null and that predecessor is currently `RELEASED`, the predecessor moves `RELEASED → SUPERSEDED`, and every engineered order whose `pinned_model_id` is that newly superseded predecessor recomputes `spec_stale` to true. A design that has reached `SUPERSEDED` no longer accepts new valve units; attempts are refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`.

Withdrawal is the other exit from `RELEASED`, moving `RELEASED → WITHDRAWN`, and it is the one design action open to both parties: either the `ProductEngineer` who authored the line or a `QualityManager` may withdraw it, so `WITHDRAW` is the sole `action` value on this endpoint that both roles may supply. It is permitted only when no valve unit of the design has a `cert_status` of `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS` or `TESTS_PASSED` — withdrawing a design mid-certification would strand the units, so the attempt is refused with `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. On withdrawal, every engineered order pinned to the design recomputes `spec_stale` to true.

What makes `qualified_special_services` interesting is that it is never stored as an input — it is recomputed on every read of the design. For each of `FIRE_SAFE`, `CRYOGENIC` and `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` in that order, the system gathers the test records of that same test type, with a `result` of `PASS`, taken across **every** valve unit of the design whatever that unit's own `build_status`, `cert_status` or `service_status` — a record on a unit later decertified, scrapped out or still mid-build counts exactly as much as one on a certified unit, and superseded or failed attempts are simply not in the candidate set; the service is qualified when at least one of those records has an `ended_at` that, advanced by 1,825 days — the five-year type-test validity — is at or after the current logical-clock instant. The resulting list is emitted in the fixed order `FIRE_SAFE`, then `CRYOGENIC`, then `FUGITIVE_EMISSION`, and an empty candidate set yields an empty list, never null. Comparison against the clock is at day granularity. This is also why a unit may be opened against a design that is only `IN_REVIEW`: prototype units have to exist and be type-tested before the design they belong to can be released, while certification of a production unit still demands a fully `RELEASED` design.

### 3.2. Procurement: Foundry Qualification, Heat Lots and Traceable Material

A `ProcurementSpecialist` registers each casting and forging supplier as a **foundry**, supplying a globally unique `foundry_code`, the legal `name` of the works, and the two-letter ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 `country_code` of its location. All three are simply required non-empty strings: `country_code` is stored and echoed verbatim without any length or membership check against the ISO list, so the two-letter form is a convention of the data rather than a validated constraint. A duplicate `foundry_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, compared for exact equality across every foundry whatever its band. A freshly registered foundry starts with `total_lot_count` 0, `rejected_lot_count` 0, `revoked_qualification_count` 0, a `quality_index` of 100.00 and a `status` of `ACTIVE`.

Those three counters and the index are derived, recomputed whenever the foundry is read or when anything that feeds them changes. `total_lot_count` is the number of heat lots naming this foundry, in any status. `rejected_lot_count` is the number of those lots whose status is `QUARANTINED` or `REJECTED`. `revoked_qualification_count` is the number of this foundry's qualifications whose status is `REVOKED`. The index itself is computed from a rejection rate: the rate is zero when the foundry has no lots at all — an empty history is a clean history, not a division by zero and not a zero score — and is otherwise the rejected count divided by the total count. The index is then 100 reduced by 40 times that rate, reduced further by 8 for each revoked qualification, rounded half up to 2 decimal places and finally clamped into the range 0.00 to 100.00 after the rounding. `status` is a band over the result: an index of 80 or above is `ACTIVE`, an index from 60 up to but not including 80 is `PROBATION`, and anything below 60 is `SUSPENDED`. An index of exactly 80.00 sits on the boundary and belongs to `ACTIVE`.

Qualification is scoped, not blanket. A `ProcurementSpecialist` opens a **foundry qualification** naming the `foundry_id`, a `process` of `CASTING` or `FORGING`, a `material_grade` from the same five-grade set the designs use, the highest `max_pressure_class` the qualification covers, and a validity window given by `valid_from` and `valid_until`, both of which are echoed back exactly as the client wrote them rather than renormalised. The window is a **closed** interval: both endpoints are inside it. Creation checks the interval ordering before it resolves the `foundry_id`, so a backwards window on a non-existent foundry reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and not `ERR_FOUNDRY_NOT_FOUND`. The row opens at `status` `PENDING` with `requalification_count` 0 and `qualified_by_user_id` null. Qualification states run `PENDING → QUALIFIED`, with `EXPIRED` reachable from `QUALIFIED` and `REVOKED` terminal.

Granting the qualification — moving `PENDING → QUALIFIED` — is reserved to a `QualityManager`, and is available only from `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. It requires that the current logical clock has not passed `valid_until` — the boundary instant itself still counts as inside — otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, and it requires window exclusivity: no other qualification for the same `foundry_id`, `process` and `material_grade` in either `PENDING` or `QUALIFIED` status, the latter judged after that row's own lazy expiry has been applied, may have a closed window intersecting this one. Because both windows are closed, touching endpoints **do** collide — a new window whose `valid_from` equals an existing window's `valid_until` is a conflict, reported as `ERR_QUALIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Granting writes `qualified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and triggers recomputation of the foundry's `quality_index` and `status`.

Expiry is lazy: once the clock passes `valid_until`, a `QUALIFIED` row is reported and treated as `EXPIRED` on every read and every use, and a component quoting it is refused with `ERR_FOUNDRY_NOT_QUALIFIED`. An expired row is not replaced by a fresh one — instead the `ProcurementSpecialist` requalifies it, moving `EXPIRED → PENDING` while supplying a new `valid_from` and `valid_until`. The supplied `valid_from` must be strictly later than the previous `valid_until`, otherwise `ERR_QUALIFICATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; requalification increments `requalification_count` by 1, replaces the window, and resets `qualified_by_user_id` to null so the new window must be granted afresh. Requalification is available only from `EXPIRED`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, its own `valid_from`/`valid_until` pair is mandatory and must itself be strictly ordered, and it is checked only against the row's own previous window — overlap with some *other* qualification of the same foundry, process and grade is not re-examined until the fresh window is granted. Revocation, a `QualityManager` decision, moves `QUALIFIED → REVOKED`, increments the foundry's `revoked_qualification_count` by 1, and re-bands the foundry's `quality_index` and `status`, which may drop the works to `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`. Because expiry is applied before the transition is judged, a row whose window the clock has already passed reads `EXPIRED` and can therefore no longer be revoked: the attempt is `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, and the route back is requalification.

**Heat lots** are the traceability root. Either a `SupplierRep` speaking for its own works or a `ProcurementSpecialist` declares a received lot with its `foundry_id`, a mill `heat_number` unique within that foundry, the `material_grade`, the `process`, the mass `qty_received_kg`, the material test report reference `mtr_document_ref`, and the two chemistry and toughness figures read off that report: `carbon_equivalent_pct` to 3 decimal places and `impact_energy_j` in joules to 1 decimal place. A `heat_number` repeated within one foundry is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, checked only after the foundry itself has been resolved, so the same `heat_number` under a different foundry is perfectly legal. The lot opens at `status` `RECEIVED` with `qty_committed_kg` and `qty_scrapped_kg` at 0.000, `qty_available_kg` equal to `qty_received_kg`, `verified_by_user_id` null and `quarantine_reason` null; the owning foundry's `total_lot_count` increases by 1 and its `quality_index` is recomputed.

Lot dispositions are a `QualityManager` monopoly, running `RECEIVED → VERIFIED → RELEASED` with `QUARANTINED` as the recall state and `REJECTED` terminal. Verification is the chemistry gate: `carbon_equivalent_pct` must be at or below the ceiling for the lot's `material_grade` — 0.45 for `WCB`, 0.43 for `LCC`, 0.48 for `A105`, 0.40 for `CF8M`, 0.40 for `F316` — and `impact_energy_j` must be at or above the floor for that grade — 20 joules for `WCB`, 27 for `LCC`, 20 for `A105`, 34 for `CF8M`, 34 for `F316`. Either boundary is inclusive and satisfying it is a pass; failing either is `ERR_MTR_CHEMISTRY_OUT_OF_SPEC`, with the carbon ceiling tested before the toughness floor so a lot that misses both reports the carbon failure. A successful verification records `verified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. Rejecting a `RECEIVED` lot outright moves it to `REJECTED`, increments the foundry's `rejected_lot_count` by 1 and recomputes the foundry's `quality_index` and `status`. Releasing moves `VERIFIED → RELEASED`, which is the only status in which a lot may serve as a component's traceability parent.

Quarantine is the recall lever. A `QualityManager` may quarantine a lot that is either `VERIFIED` or `RELEASED`, recording a `quarantine_reason` — an optional free-text string that is simply stored as null when it is omitted, since the recall is never refused for want of a stated reason; the foundry's `rejected_lot_count` increases by 1 and its index and band are recomputed. The quarantine then cascades in four linked steps. First, every component of the lot in `CREATED`, `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING` or `CONSUMED` records its current status into `pre_hold_status` and moves to `HOLD`. Second — caused by the first — every valve unit whose `body_component_id` is one of those held components and whose `cert_status` is `TESTS_PASSED` or `CERTIFIED` moves to `DECERTIFIED` with `decertification_reason` set to `LOT_QUARANTINE`, and any such unit sitting at `build_status` `PACKAGED` falls back to `REWORK`. Third — caused by the second — for each decertified unit that names an `engineered_order_id`, that order's documentation package, if it is `ISSUED`, moves to `VOIDED` with `held_reason` set to `DOC_PACKAGE_VOIDED`. Fourth — caused by the second and third — that same order, if its `status` is `IN_PRODUCTION`, moves to `HELD`, and if its `status` is `CLOSED`, reopens to `IN_PRODUCTION`, with `held_reason` set to `UNIT_DECERTIFIED`.

Releasing a quarantined lot — moving `QUARANTINED → RELEASED` — is the compensating disposition, and it is deliberately partial. Every component of the lot on `HOLD` returns to its `pre_hold_status` and that field is cleared, the lot's own `quarantine_reason` is cleared back to null, and the foundry's `rejected_lot_count` decreases by 1 with the index and band recomputed. The units the quarantine decertified are **not** restored: they remain `DECERTIFIED` and must be re-tested from `REWORK`. Rejecting a quarantined lot instead moves `QUARANTINED → REJECTED` and scraps the material: every component of the lot on `HOLD` moves to `SCRAPPED` with its `pre_hold_status` cleared, and the lot's `qty_scrapped_kg` and `qty_available_kg` are recomputed. `REJECT` therefore has exactly two legal origins, `RECEIVED` and `QUARANTINED`, and `RELEASE` exactly two, `VERIFIED` and `QUARANTINED`; any other origin is `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. Because the foundry's counters are derived rather than stored, the owning foundry's row is re-stamped by a disposition only when the disposition actually changes one of them — a `VERIFIED → RELEASED` release, which moves the lot between two statuses that both count as un-rejected, leaves the foundry's `version` alone.

A **component** is a rough casting or forging drawn from a lot, created by a `ProcurementSpecialist` with the parent `heat_lot_id`, the `target_model_id` the part is cut for, a `component_type` of `BODY`, `BONNET`, `BALL`, `DISC`, `STEM` or `SEAT_RING`, and the mass `net_weight_kg` drawn from the lot. Four gates apply, in the ladder order of §2.10: the parent lot must be `RELEASED` — `RECEIVED`, `VERIFIED`, `QUARANTINED` and `REJECTED` are all refused with `ERR_HEAT_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`; there must exist a qualification for the lot's foundry with `status` `QUALIFIED`, a matching `process` and `material_grade`, a `max_pressure_class` at or above the target design's `pressure_class`, and the current logical clock inside its closed window, otherwise `ERR_FOUNDRY_NOT_QUALIFIED`; and the requested `net_weight_kg` may not exceed the lot's `qty_available_kg`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_INSUFFICIENT_MASS`. Existence is resolved design-first: `target_model_id` is checked before `heat_lot_id`, so a creation naming neither reports `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`. A new component opens at `status` `CREATED` with `pre_hold_status` null, raising the lot's `qty_committed_kg` by its mass and lowering `qty_available_kg` accordingly, and re-stamping the parent lot's row.

The lot's mass balance is derived from its components at all times. `qty_committed_kg` is the total `net_weight_kg` of components in `CREATED`, `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING`, `CONSUMED` or `HOLD`. `qty_scrapped_kg` is the total `net_weight_kg` of components in `SCRAPPED`. `qty_available_kg` is `qty_received_kg` less the committed total less the scrapped total. Components in `VOIDED` contribute to neither total, which is exactly how their mass finds its way back into what is available. All three figures carry 3 decimal places, rounded half up, and the invariant that received mass equals available plus committed plus scrapped holds before and after every disposition.

Component states are `CREATED`, `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING`, `CONSUMED` and `HOLD`, with `SCRAPPED` and `VOIDED` terminal. A `ProcurementSpecialist` may issue a `CREATED` component to machining, which requires the parent lot still to be `RELEASED` — the same `ERR_HEAT_LOT_NOT_RELEASED` gate applies here as at creation, though it is evaluated after the component's own status check, so issuing an already-`CONSUMED` component out of a quarantined lot reports `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. Issuing moves no mass and so leaves the parent lot's `version` untouched, where scrapping and voiding both re-stamp it. Consumption is not a manual action: a component moves to `CONSUMED` automatically when a valve unit is opened naming it as `body_component_id`, and this is permitted from either `CREATED` or `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING`, so the machining-issue step is optional bookkeeping rather than a mandatory stage. Once consumed, the component is no longer selectable by another unit; a second attempt is `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Scrapping is permitted from `CREATED`, `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING` or `HOLD` and moves the mass from `qty_committed_kg` into `qty_scrapped_kg`, leaving `qty_available_kg` untouched — scrapped metal is gone, not returned. Voiding is the compensating disposition for a mis-issued draw: it is permitted from `CREATED` or `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING`, requires that no valve unit references the component as its `body_component_id` (otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and the reference counts whatever state that unit has since reached), removes the mass from `qty_committed_kg` and returns it to `qty_available_kg`. Scrapping likewise clears `pre_hold_status` back to null. Neither disposition is a transition out of `CONSUMED`, `SCRAPPED` or `VOIDED`: those attempts are `ERR_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.3. Serialising and Building a Valve Unit

A **valve unit** is the serialised physical article. Either a `ProductEngineer` or a `TestTechnician` opens one, naming the `model_id` of the design being built, the `body_component_id` of the traceable body casting or forging it consumes, the `monogram_license_id` of the license under which its nameplate will be struck, and a globally unique `serial_number`. Two inputs are optional: `special_services`, a subset of `FIRE_SAFE`, `CRYOGENIC` and `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` the unit is offered for, defaulting to the empty list; and `engineered_order_id`, naming the engineered-to-order programme the unit is built against, defaulting to null for a stock unit — when supplied it must resolve, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, but the order's own `status` is not constrained, so a unit may be opened against a `DRAFT` or even a `CANCELLED` programme. A repeated `serial_number` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, checked last of all, after every referential and state gate below has passed.

Creation enforces the traceability contract. The named design must be `IN_REVIEW` or `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`. The named component must be in `CREATED` or `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING`, must not already be referenced by another unit, must be of `component_type` `BODY`, and must carry a `target_model_id` equal to the unit's `model_id`; any of those four failures is `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and all four are evaluated, in that order, before the design's own status is looked at — a `BONNET` drawn for a `WITHDRAWN` design reports the component error, not the model error. The governing license must exist but is otherwise unexamined at creation: neither its `license_status`, its `compliance_posture` nor its `scope_valve_types` gates the opening of a unit, only its later certification. On success the component moves to `CONSUMED`, and the unit opens with `build_status` `BLANK`, `cert_status` `UNCERTIFIED`, `service_status` `NONE`, `reserved_kit_qty` 0, `service_engineer_user_id` null, `provisional_emission_leak_class` and `final_emission_leak_class` both null, `certified_by_user_id`, `certified_at` and `decertification_reason` all null.

A unit carries **three independent state dimensions** rather than one status. `build_status` tracks physical progress through `BLANK`, `MACHINED`, `ASSEMBLED`, `PACKAGED`, `SHIPPED` and the off-line state `REWORK`. `cert_status` tracks the certification claim through `UNCERTIFIED`, `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS`, `TESTS_PASSED`, `TESTS_FAILED`, `CERTIFIED` and `DECERTIFIED`. `service_status` tracks aftermarket and field work through `NONE`, `OPEN`, `PARTS_RESERVED`, `IN_SERVICE` and `SERVICED`. The three advance largely independently, and the couplings between them are named explicitly where they exist.

The build dimension belongs to the `TestTechnician`, who advances it one step at a time by supplying `action` `ADVANCE` together with a `to_build_status` target. `to_build_status` is mandatory on `ADVANCE` and its accepted vocabulary is the whole six-value `build_status` set, `REWORK` included; only the adjacent forward step in the sequence `BLANK → MACHINED → ASSEMBLED → PACKAGED → SHIPPED` is a legal target, so any skipped, backward or off-line target — `REWORK` among them, since it is never client-reachable — is refused with `ERR_BUILD_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. Advancing at all is refused with `ERR_STATE_INVALID` rather than a sequence error when the unit is already terminal at `SHIPPED`, or when it is sitting in `REWORK`, which must be closed out with `REWORK_COMPLETE` instead. Reaching `MACHINED` from `BLANK` additionally requires the unit's body component to be in `CONSUMED` or `ISSUED_TO_MACHINING` and specifically **not** on `HOLD`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID` — a unit cannot be machined from metal that is under quarantine.

`ASSEMBLED → PACKAGED` is the one point in the whole system where the build dimension depends on the certification dimension: it requires `cert_status` to be exactly `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and it additionally requires that if any actuator package exists for the unit, exactly one of them has `sizing_status` `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_ACTUATOR_NOT_APPROVED`. A unit with no actuator package at all clears that second gate trivially, and a unit that has packages but no single `APPROVED` one among them fails it. `PACKAGED → SHIPPED` likewise requires `cert_status` to be `CERTIFIED`. Both of these certification and actuator gates sit in bands 11 and 14 of §2.10 and are therefore evaluated **before** the band-16 adjacency check: an uncertified unit at `BLANK` asked to jump straight to `PACKAGED` reports `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`, not `ERR_BUILD_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. `SHIPPED` is terminal for the build dimension — nothing may move it afterwards, and only `service_status` may still change.

`REWORK` is where the system puts a unit whose claim has been withdrawn. The move into `REWORK` is never a client request: it happens automatically when a unit at `build_status` `ASSEMBLED` or `PACKAGED` has its `cert_status` moved to `DECERTIFIED`, or has its `service_status` moved to `IN_SERVICE`. The `TestTechnician` closes the loop by declaring rework complete with `action` `REWORK_COMPLETE`, moving `REWORK → ASSEMBLED`, which requires the unit's `service_status` to be `NONE` or `SERVICED` — a unit still in the middle of a field service job may not be handed back to assembly. A unit that re-enters `ASSEMBLED` this way carries no certification and must be re-tested from scratch.

### 3.4. The Test Programme: Bench Occupancy, Standard Envelopes and Coverage

Testing is the `TestTechnician`'s exclusive domain. A **test record** is written against a specific unit, taking its `valve_unit_id` from the path rather than the body, and captures a `test_type` of `SHELL`, `SEAT`, `FUNCTIONAL`, `FIRE_SAFE`, `CRYOGENIC` or `FUGITIVE_EMISSION`, the `test_bench_code` of the bench or bay the test occupied, the occupancy interval given by `started_at` and `ended_at`, the applied `test_pressure_bar` to 1 decimal place, the `hold_seconds` the pressure was held, and the technician's judgement in `result`, either `PASS` or `FAIL`. Two fields are conditional: `leak_rate_ppm`, the measured leak rate in parts per million by volume, is required when `test_type` is `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` and otherwise defaults to null; `leak_drops`, the observed seat leakage in drops, is required as a non-negative integer when `test_type` is `SEAT` and otherwise defaults to null. Conditional means required-when, not forbidden-otherwise: either field may be supplied on a test type that does not demand it and is then stored and echoed as given. The `standard_ref` names the standard the test was run to, is never validated against a list of standards, and defaults to `API-598`. The server writes `performed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` — the field that later blocks the same person from certifying the unit — and stores the two computed envelope figures, `required_pressure_bar` and `required_hold_seconds`, on the record itself. Records may be created only while `build_status` is `ASSEMBLED` or `PACKAGED`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. On the certification dimension the single bar is `CERTIFIED`: a certified unit may take no further test records, again `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, and every other `cert_status` — `UNCERTIFIED`, `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS`, `TESTS_PASSED`, `TESTS_FAILED` and `DECERTIFIED` alike — accepts a record, which is what lets a run be extended, re-run or reopened without any intermediate reset. The occupancy interval is checked for ordering before the addressed unit is resolved, so a backwards interval against a non-existent unit reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`.

A bench is an exclusive temporal resource, and its occupancy is a **half-open** interval: `started_at` is inside, `ended_at` is outside. A new record on the same `test_bench_code` is refused with `ERR_TEST_BENCH_OCCUPIED` when its interval overlaps an existing record's interval on that bench; intervals that merely touch, where the new record's `started_at` equals an existing record's `ended_at`, are accepted. A bench code is a global resource, matched by exact string equality across every record in the system whatever unit it belongs to. Contrast this deliberately with the closed intervals used for foundry qualification windows in §3.2, where touching endpoints do collide.

The standard fixes a minimum test envelope from the design, not from the operator's judgement. Each pressure class has a rated pressure in bar: class 150 is rated 19.6, class 300 is 51.1, class 600 is 102.1, class 900 is 153.2, class 1500 is 255.3 and class 2500 is 425.5. A shell test must reach 1.5 times that rated pressure; a seat test must reach 1.1 times it; fire-safe and cryogenic tests use the same 1.1 multiplier as the seat test. Functional and fugitive-emission tests have no pressure requirement at all — their `required_pressure_bar` is 0.0. In every case the product is rounded half up to 1 decimal place before being stored and compared. The minimum hold is banded on the design's `bore_size_mm` instead: a bore of 50 millimetres or less holds for 15 seconds, a bore above 50 up to and including 150 holds for 60 seconds, a bore above 150 up to and including 300 holds for 120 seconds, and a bore above 300 holds for 300 seconds — five minutes for the largest bodies. Each band's upper edge belongs to that band, so a bore of exactly 150 holds 60 seconds and a bore of 151 holds 120. A record whose `test_pressure_bar` falls below the computed `required_pressure_bar` is refused with `ERR_TEST_PRESSURE_BELOW_STANDARD`, and one whose `hold_seconds` falls below the computed `required_hold_seconds` is refused with `ERR_TEST_HOLD_TIME_SHORT`. Both boundaries are inclusive: meeting the minimum exactly is a pass.

Coverage — which tests a given unit actually needs — is recomputed from configuration on every evaluation and never cached, because the answer changes as the unit is configured. Every unit needs a `SHELL` test and a `SEAT` test. A `FUNCTIONAL` test joins the required set when the design's `valve_type` is `CONTROL`, or when the unit has an actuator package with `sizing_status` `APPROVED`. Each service listed in the unit's `special_services` adds the test of the same name: `FIRE_SAFE` adds a `FIRE_SAFE` test, `CRYOGENIC` adds a `CRYOGENIC` test, `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` adds a `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` test. Within each required test type, the **deciding** record is the one with the greatest `created_at` — the latest attempt, not the best one — and where two records of a type share a `created_at`, the later-written of the two decides, matching the total ordering of §2.3. `started_at` and `ended_at` play no part in deciding; only write order does.

The certification dimension then follows mechanically from the records. Writing the first record moves `cert_status` from `UNCERTIFIED`, `TESTS_FAILED` or `DECERTIFIED` to `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS`. From `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS`, the unit reaches `TESTS_PASSED` at the moment every required test type has a deciding record whose `result` is `PASS`, and it drops to `TESTS_FAILED` as soon as any deciding record's `result` is `FAIL`. Writing a further record for the same test type moves the unit back from `TESTS_FAILED` to `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS`, so a seat test that failed and was re-run with a pass leaves the unit at `TESTS_PASSED` — the later record decides. While a unit sits at `TESTS_FAILED`, certification is refused, and the diagnosis when coverage is genuinely missing rather than merely not started is `ERR_REQUIRED_TESTS_INCOMPLETE`. Note that approving an actuator package **widens** the required set retroactively: a unit already at `TESTS_PASSED` with three covered types falls back to `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS` once a fourth type, `FUNCTIONAL`, is added by the approval. That re-evaluation is lazy in exactly the sense of §2.4 — the unit's `cert_status` settles to its recomputed value the next time the unit is read or used, without the approving request stamping the unit's `version` or `updated_at` — and it runs only while `cert_status` is one of the three in-flight values `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS`, `TESTS_PASSED` or `TESTS_FAILED`. `UNCERTIFIED`, `CERTIFIED` and `DECERTIFIED` are held values that coverage arithmetic never disturbs, which is why superseding an approved package cannot quietly recertify a decertified unit and why widening the set on a certified unit does not un-certify it.

Fugitive-emission behaviour is computed twice from the same records, by two different rules, and the two answers are both retained. The input set is every test record of the unit whose `test_type` is `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` and whose `leak_rate_ppm` is not null, taken in `created_at` order, and it takes in `FAIL` records as readily as `PASS` ones — a reading is a reading; if that set is empty, both emission fields stay null and no class is asserted. Whichever rule is applied produces a figure in parts per million, and that figure is banded: at or below 50 is class `AA`; above 50 and at or below 100 is class `A`; above 100 and at or below 500 is class `B`; above 500 and at or below 1000 is class `C`; above 1000 is `FAIL`. The **peak reading** rule takes the largest `leak_rate_ppm` in the input set and is recomputed on every fugitive-emission record written, landing in `provisional_emission_leak_class` — a field that is never stored but recomputed from the live record set on every read of the unit, so it also moves when the record set changes for any other reason. The **time-weighted** rule multiplies each record's `leak_rate_ppm` by its own `hold_seconds`, sums those products, divides by the sum of the `hold_seconds`, and rounds the quotient half up to 2 decimal places before banding; it is evaluated exactly once, at certification, and frozen into `final_emission_leak_class`. The freeze is unconditional: certification writes whatever the time-weighted rule yields, so a unit that happens to carry fugitive-emission readings without claiming the service still ends up with a stored final class (and a unit with no readings at all stores null), even though only a unit whose `special_services` includes `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` has that class gated against `C` and `FAIL`. Decertification does not clear the frozen value; a later re-certification overwrites it. The two can disagree, and usually do when a short high reading is diluted by a long low one: a 40 ppm reading held 300 seconds alongside a 140 ppm reading held 100 seconds gives a provisional class of `B` from the peak of 140 and a final class of `A` from the weighted 65.00.

### 3.5. Actuator Packaging and Torque Sizing

A `ProductEngineer` proposes an **actuator package** for a unit, naming the `valve_unit_id`, an `actuator_type` of `PNEUMATIC`, `HYDRAULIC` or `ELECTRIC`, the rated `output_torque_nm` of the selected actuator to 1 decimal place, a `sizing_basis` of `CATALOG` or `DATASHEET`, and — as described in §2.8 — the mandatory `expected_valve_unit_version`. The available `supply_pressure_bar` is optional, must be at least 0 when given, and defaults to 0 whenever it is omitted — for an electric actuator that default is the expected value, for a pneumatic or hydraulic one it is a figure that will block approval later. The package opens at `sizing_status` `PROPOSED` with `approved_by_user_id` null; states run `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, with `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` terminal, and a decision aimed at a package that is no longer `PROPOSED` is `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. Proposing a new package for a unit that already has one moves every earlier package of that unit still in `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and the creating request increments the unit's `version` by 1 as part of its work — which means two proposals in a row need two different `expected_valve_unit_version` values, the second being the first plus one.

Required torque is built up from the design and the service, in a fixed order. The valve's turn class comes from its `valve_type`: `BALL` and `BUTTERFLY` are quarter-turn, `GATE` and `CONTROL` are multi-turn. The **pressure factor** is 1 plus the design's `pressure_class` divided by a turn-class divisor — 10000 for quarter-turn valves, 20000 for multi-turn valves — so a quarter-turn class-300 body carries a factor of 1.03. The **service factor** is the largest factor among the services listed in the unit's `special_services`, where `FIRE_SAFE` contributes 1.25, `CRYOGENIC` contributes 1.40 and `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` contributes 1.15; an empty list contributes 1.00. The **seat factor** is 1.00 for a `SOFT` seat and 1.30 for a `METAL` seat. The **base** break torque depends on the chosen `sizing_basis`: with `CATALOG`, the base is the design's `base_break_torque_nm` and no uncertainty allowance is added; with `DATASHEET`, which is selected when the unit belongs to an engineered order carrying a customer datasheet, the base is that order's `datasheet_break_torque_nm` multiplied by 1.10 when its `datasheet_torque_source` is `CUSTOMER_STATED`, or by 1.00 when the source is `VENDOR_MEASURED` — a customer's stated figure earns a ten per cent uncertainty allowance that a vendor-measured figure does not. `DATASHEET` on a unit that has no datasheet to read — a stock unit with no `engineered_order_id`, or one whose order has not yet been given a `datasheet_break_torque_nm` — is not an error: the basis is recorded as asked and the calculation silently falls back to the design's catalog `base_break_torque_nm` with no uncertainty allowance, giving the same answer `CATALOG` would.

`required_torque_nm` is then the base multiplied by the pressure factor, the service factor and the seat factor, rounded half up to 1 decimal place. The safety margin tier is keyed on that **already-rounded** figure, not on the unrounded product: below 500 newton-metres the margin is 0.30, from 500 up to and including 2000 it is 0.25, and above 2000 it is 0.20. This matters at the boundary — an unrounded requirement of 499.96 rounds to 500.0 and therefore takes the 0.25 tier rather than the 0.30 tier. The tier is stored in `safety_margin_pct` as a fraction to 2 decimal places, and `sized_torque_nm` is `required_torque_nm` multiplied by one plus that margin, again rounded half up to 1 decimal place. Because the same unit and design can produce different answers under the two bases, `sizing_basis` is a load-bearing input, not a label: a quarter-turn class-300 metal-seated body with a 420.0 catalog break torque sizes to 703.0 newton-metres from the catalog and to 773.3 from a customer-stated datasheet of the same 420.0.

Approval is a `QualityManager` decision. Approving moves `PROPOSED → APPROVED` and requires two things: a non-electric actuator must have a `supply_pressure_bar` greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a pneumatic or hydraulic actuator with no supply is not a sizing that can be approved; and the actuator's `output_torque_nm` must be at or above the computed `sized_torque_nm`, otherwise `ERR_ACTUATOR_TORQUE_INSUFFICIENT`. The supply-pressure check is a request-shape failure and therefore precedes the torque check per §2.10, so an under-sized pneumatic actuator with no supply pressure reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two figures are compared as stored, against the `sized_torque_nm` frozen onto the package when it was proposed, not against a fresh recomputation. Approval writes `approved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, makes the unit eligible to reach `build_status` `PACKAGED`, and — as §3.4 notes — adds `FUNCTIONAL` to the unit's required test set, though it stamps only the package's own row and leaves the unit's `version` and `updated_at` untouched. Rejecting instead moves `PROPOSED → REJECTED` with no further effect.

### 3.6. Monogram Licensing and the Quality-System Audit

A `QualityManager` applies for a **monogram license** on behalf of a facility, supplying the globally unique `license_code`, the `facility_code` the license is issued to, `scope_valve_types` as the subset of `GATE`, `BALL`, `BUTTERFLY` and `CONTROL` the license covers, the `lead_auditor_user_id` of the auditor who will lead the assessment, and the scheduled audit window as `audit_window_start` and `audit_window_end`, where the end must be strictly after the start — an empty window whose two endpoints coincide is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, exactly as band 8 of §2.10 requires, and the same strict ordering governs a re-scheduled window. `scope_valve_types` is mandatory and may be an empty array, which simply yields a license under which nothing can ever be certified. A repeated `license_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. The application opens with `license_status` `APPLIED`, `audit_state` `SCHEDULED`, `compliance_posture` `COMPLIANT`, every finding counter at 0, and `last_audit_closed_at`, `granted_at` and `conditional_expires_at` all null.

Everything after the application belongs to the `MonogramAuditor` — creating an application is closed to that role, and every audit and finding action is closed to everybody else. A license carries **two independent dimensions**. The audit dimension moves `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED` and back to `SCHEDULED`. Starting the audit requires the current logical clock to be at or after `audit_window_start`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. Closing the audit sets `last_audit_closed_at` to the current logical-clock instant. Re-scheduling from `CLOSED` back to `SCHEDULED` carries a fresh `audit_window_start` and `audit_window_end` that replace the stored window, and it enforces a cooling-off buffer: the new `audit_window_start` must be at or after `last_audit_closed_at` advanced by 30 days, compared at full instant precision rather than at day granularity, and the boundary instant itself is accepted. An earlier start is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE`. Each of the three audit moves is legal only out of its named predecessor state, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, and re-scheduling neither resets the finding register nor touches `license_status`: the lifetime counters, the open counts and the posture survive into the next audit unchanged.

Findings are recorded against the license through a register, each entry supplying an `entry_action` of `RAISE`, `CLOSE` or `WAIVE` together with a `severity` of `MAJOR` or `MINOR`. Both fields are mandatory on every entry, and the register is open in any `audit_state` — findings may be raised, closed and waived before an audit has started, during it and after it has closed. Raising increments the lifetime counter for the severity — `total_major_raised` or `total_minor_raised` — and recomputes the open counts and the posture. Closing a finding with corrective action increments `closed_major_finding_count` for a major, or the closed-minor tally for a minor; closing when the matching open count is already zero is `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, since there is nothing to close, and the counters can therefore never be driven negative. Waiving increments `waived_major_finding_count`, applies only to a major — `WAIVE` with a `severity` of `MINOR` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, checked as a shape failure before the license is even resolved — and, as §2.11 sets out, is refused with `ERR_SOD_AUDIT_WAIVER_CONFLICT` when the caller is the license's `lead_auditor_user_id`; that duty check runs before the there-is-something-to-waive check, so the lead auditor waiving against an empty register still sees the duty error. The derived counters follow directly: `open_major_finding_count` is `total_major_raised` less `closed_major_finding_count` less `waived_major_finding_count`, and `open_minor_finding_count` is `total_minor_raised` less the number of minors closed — a closed-minor tally that the server keeps internally and does not surface as a field of its own, so `total_minor_raised` and `open_minor_finding_count` are the only two minor figures in the license representation. The identity that `total_major_raised` equals the open, closed and waived major counts added together holds after every entry.

`compliance_posture` is the derived gate that the manufacturing side of the system reads. It is `FROZEN` when `open_major_finding_count` is 2 or more, or when `license_status` is `SUSPENDED`, `DENIED` or `REVOKED`. Failing that, it is `CONDITIONAL` when `open_major_finding_count` is exactly 1, or `open_minor_finding_count` is 5 or more, or `license_status` is `CONDITIONAL`. Otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. Reaching two open majors is therefore not merely a posture change: it freezes the posture, suspends the license and decertifies the license's certified units in one motion — though the automatic suspension fires only from the two suspendable statuses `GRANTED` and `CONDITIONAL`, so a second open major raised against a license still `APPLIED`, or already `SUSPENDED`, `DENIED` or `REVOKED`, changes only the derived posture and leaves `license_status` where it was. Waiving one of those two majors returns the open count to 1 and the posture to `CONDITIONAL`, but the units the freeze decertified are not restored.

The audit closeout is a three-way branch, and the three branches have deliberately divergent side-effect sets. All three require `audit_state` to be `CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, but each branch tests its own `license_status` origin first — `GRANT` from `APPLIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, `GRANT_CONDITIONAL` and `DENY` from `APPLIED` alone — so a closeout aimed at a license in the wrong status reports that as the state error even when the audit is not closed either. **Granting** moves `license_status` to `GRANTED` from either `APPLIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, and requires `open_major_finding_count` to be exactly 0 (otherwise `ERR_OPEN_MAJOR_FINDINGS`) and `open_minor_finding_count` to be 4 or fewer (otherwise `ERR_MINOR_FINDING_THRESHOLD`); it sets `granted_at` to the current logical-clock instant, clears `conditional_expires_at` to null, and recomputes the posture to `COMPLIANT`. **Granting conditionally** moves `license_status` from `APPLIED` to `CONDITIONAL`, and requires `open_major_finding_count` to be exactly 0 (otherwise `ERR_OPEN_MAJOR_FINDINGS`) and `open_minor_finding_count` to be 5 or more (otherwise `ERR_MINOR_FINDING_THRESHOLD`) — the two granting branches partition the minor-finding count exactly at five; it sets `granted_at` to the current instant, sets `conditional_expires_at` to that instant advanced by 180 days, and recomputes the posture to `CONDITIONAL`, under which certification of any unit carrying a special service is refused while plain units still certify. **Denying** moves `license_status` from `APPLIED` to `DENIED` and requires `open_major_finding_count` to be 1 or more (otherwise `ERR_OPEN_MAJOR_FINDINGS`); it sets the posture to `FROZEN` and, uniquely among the three branches, decertifies every unit under the license: each unit with this license and a `cert_status` of `CERTIFIED` moves to `DECERTIFIED` with `decertification_reason` `LICENSE_DENIED`, running the full decertification tail described in §3.7.

Suspension and reinstatement form the other loop. A `GRANTED` or `CONDITIONAL` license may be suspended, either by an explicit auditor action or automatically when `open_major_finding_count` reaches 2, and the effect cascades in three links: the posture becomes `FROZEN`; every unit with this `monogram_license_id` and `cert_status` `CERTIFIED` moves to `DECERTIFIED` with `decertification_reason` `LICENSE_SUSPENDED`; and each such unit's engineered order, if its `status` is `IN_PRODUCTION`, moves to `HELD` with `held_reason` `UNIT_DECERTIFIED`, while its documentation package, if `ISSUED`, moves to `VOIDED`. Reinstating moves `license_status` from `SUSPENDED` back to `GRANTED` and requires `open_major_finding_count` to be 0, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_MAJOR_FINDINGS`; the posture is recomputed, becoming `COMPLIANT` when `open_minor_finding_count` is 4 or fewer, and every engineered order sitting in `HELD` with `held_reason` `UNIT_DECERTIFIED` that no longer has any `DECERTIFIED` unit returns to `IN_PRODUCTION`. Decertified units are **not** auto-recertified by a reinstatement; they must be re-tested. Finally, a `CONDITIONAL` license whose grace has run out is revoked automatically: once the logical clock passes `conditional_expires_at` while `open_major_finding_count` is 1 or more, `license_status` moves to `REVOKED`, the posture becomes `FROZEN`, and every certified unit under the license moves to `DECERTIFIED` with `decertification_reason` `LICENSE_DENIED`. Both conditions are strict and both are re-tested lazily, on every read or use of the license rather than by any sweep: the expiry instant itself is still inside the grace period, and a conditional license whose majors have all been closed or waived sits past its expiry indefinitely without being revoked — it is only ever the combination of a lapsed grace and a live major that revokes. `DENIED` and `REVOKED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Certification and the Decertification Cascade

Certification is the act that turns a tested unit into a saleable, documented article, and it belongs solely to the `QualityManager`. It moves `cert_status` from `TESTS_PASSED` to `CERTIFIED` and is guarded by eight checks, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.10. The unit must be at `cert_status` `TESTS_PASSED` — a unit already `CERTIFIED` is refused with `ERR_UNIT_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`, and a unit in `UNCERTIFIED`, `TESTS_IN_PROGRESS`, `TESTS_FAILED` or `DECERTIFIED` is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_TEST_PASSED`. The design must be `RELEASED`, not merely `IN_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`. The governing license's `compliance_posture` must be `COMPLIANT` or `CONDITIONAL`; a `FROZEN` posture is `ERR_LICENSE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, and so is a `CONDITIONAL` posture when the unit's `special_services` list is not empty. The design's `valve_type` must be a member of the license's `scope_valve_types`, otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Every service in the unit's `special_services` must appear in the design's `qualified_special_services`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIAL_SERVICE_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The required test set must be fully covered by deciding `PASS` records, otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_TESTS_INCOMPLETE`. The certifying `X-User-ID` must differ from the `performed_by_user_id` of every test record on the unit, otherwise `ERR_SOD_TEST_WITNESS_CONFLICT`. And when `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` is among the unit's `special_services`, the time-weighted `final_emission_leak_class` must be `AA`, `A` or `B` — classes `C` and `FAIL` are refused with `ERR_EMISSION_CLASS_FAIL`.

A successful certification writes `certified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `certified_at` from the current logical-clock instant, clears any `decertification_reason` left over from an earlier cascade back to null, freezes `final_emission_leak_class` from the time-weighted rule of §3.4, and recomputes the owning engineered order's `certified_unit_count` and `documentation_completeness_pct` — a certificate is one of the documents the package is scored on, so certifying a unit can move its order's completeness. Because both of those order figures are derived on read rather than stored, the recompute is observable as a bookkeeping stamp: certifying a unit that belongs to an engineered order bumps that order's `version` and `updated_at` too, whatever the order's own status, and certifying a unit with no `engineered_order_id` touches nothing beyond the unit.

Decertification is the reverse, and it is the busiest cascade in the system. A `QualityManager` may decertify explicitly, and the system triggers the same tail automatically from a lot quarantine, a license suspension, a license denial, a conditional-license revocation, or the start of a field service job. The reason recorded is one of `MANUAL`, `LOT_QUARANTINE`, `LICENSE_SUSPENDED`, `LICENSE_DENIED` or `SPECIAL_SERVICE_REVOKED`; the automatic triggers each stamp their own reason, and `SPECIAL_SERVICE_REVOKED` has no automatic trigger at all, so it is only ever recorded when a caller names it explicitly. On the explicit action `decertification_reason` is optional and defaults to `MANUAL`, and any of the five values is accepted at face value whether or not the named circumstance actually holds; only a value outside the five is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The explicit action requires the unit to be at `cert_status` `CERTIFIED` and reports `ERR_STATE_INVALID` from any other cert status — including from `DECERTIFIED`, so it is not idempotent — and it carries no version field and no segregation-of-duties constraint, so the same `QualityManager` who certified a unit may decertify it. Whatever the trigger, the full tail runs: `cert_status` moves `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED`; `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` are cleared back to null; a `build_status` of `ASSEMBLED` or `PACKAGED` falls back to `REWORK`, while `BLANK`, `MACHINED`, `SHIPPED` and `REWORK` are left where they are; the unit's engineered order, if it has one, has an `ISSUED` documentation package moved to `VOIDED` with `held_reason` `DOC_PACKAGE_VOIDED`; that same order, if `IN_PRODUCTION`, moves to `HELD`, and if `CLOSED`, reopens to `IN_PRODUCTION`, with `held_reason` `UNIT_DECERTIFIED` — the status-driven reason is written second and therefore wins outright, so an `IN_PRODUCTION` order losing an `ISSUED` package to a decertification ends up `HELD` with `held_reason` `UNIT_DECERTIFIED`, while an order in any other status keeps `DOC_PACKAGE_VOIDED` as its reason and keeps its status; and the order's `certified_unit_count` and `documentation_completeness_pct` are recomputed. The owning order is stamped — `version` and `updated_at` bumped — on every decertification of one of its units, even when none of its own fields moved. A closed order is not sacred — one decertification reopens it.

### 3.8. Engineered-to-Order Programmes: Review, Pricing and Documentation

An **engineered order** is a customer programme built on a datasheet that modifies a base design. A `ProductEngineer` opens one with a globally unique `order_code`, the `model_id` of the base design, the `ordered_qty` of valves (at least 1), the agreed `unit_price_cents` per valve (at least 1, so a free-of-charge programme cannot be opened), and the `customer_credit_limit_cents` that the release-to-production check will be measured against (zero or more). Four inputs are optional: the customer's stated `datasheet_break_torque_nm` and a `datasheet_torque_source` of `CUSTOMER_STATED` or `VENDOR_MEASURED`, both of which default to null so that an order may be opened before the datasheet exists and are then supplied by a revision or refused at submission; `requested_special_services`, defaulting to the empty list; and `expedite_requested`, a boolean defaulting to false that records whether the customer bought the expedite lane. The `model_id` is resolved before the `order_code` is checked, so an order naming a missing design reports `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND` even when the code is also a duplicate; a repeated `order_code` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, and it collides against every existing order whatever its status, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` programmes included. A new order opens at `status` `DRAFT` and `doc_package_status` `ASSEMBLING`, with `datasheet_revision_no` 1, `clarification_round_count` 0, an empty `documents` list, `documentation_completeness_pct` computed from that empty list — which is 10.00 rather than zero, because the actuator-datasheet category requires nothing yet and so scores full marks under the rule below — `pinned_model_id` and `pinned_model_spec_revision` null, `spec_stale` false, `held_reason` null, and the money figures split: `gross_quoted_cents` and `net_billable_cents` are null pending approval while `credit_issued_cents` starts at the integer 0.

The review cycle runs `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, with a bounded loop out to `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED` and back, and exits to either `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`. The `ProductEngineer` submits for review; submission requires `datasheet_break_torque_nm` to be present, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID` — `datasheet_torque_source` is not checked at submission and a null source is no obstacle — and requires `requested_special_services` to be a subset of the base design's `required_special_services`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIAL_SERVICE_NOT_QUALIFIED`; the subset test is against the design's declared requirements at this point, not against what it is qualified for, and it is evaluated after the torque presence check. A `QualityManager` may then request clarification, moving `UNDER_REVIEW → CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED` and incrementing `clarification_round_count` by 1; this is permitted only while that counter is still below 3, so a fourth request is refused with `ERR_CLARIFICATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and the order must be approved or rejected instead. The `ProductEngineer` answers a clarification by appending a datasheet revision that carries a new `datasheet_break_torque_nm` and `datasheet_torque_source` together with `expected_engineered_order_version`; all three are mandatory on a revision even though the two datasheet fields are optional at creation, and the torque must again be greater than zero. A successful revision increments `datasheet_revision_no` by 1, replaces the datasheet fields, increments the order's `version` by 1, and moves `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; it leaves `clarification_round_count` alone, so the rounds already spent are never given back and the budget is consumed over the life of the order rather than per revision. Rejection, available to the `QualityManager` from either `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CLARIFICATION_REQUESTED`, moves the order to the terminal `REJECTED`.

Approval, also a `QualityManager` decision, moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and does two things that make the order commercially binding. It requires the base design to be `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`, and requires `requested_special_services` to be a subset of the design's `qualified_special_services`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIAL_SERVICE_NOT_QUALIFIED`. It then **pins the specification**: `pinned_model_id` is set to `model_id` and `pinned_model_spec_revision` captures the design's `spec_revision` as it stands at that moment. From then on, `spec_stale` is derived and becomes true whenever `pinned_model_spec_revision` no longer equals the pinned design's current `spec_revision`, or the pinned design's status has moved to `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Approval also freezes the quote.

The quote is a waterfall, and its evaluation order is load-bearing. The **subtotal** is `unit_price_cents` multiplied by `ordered_qty`. The **surcharge rate** is the sum of the rates for the services in `requested_special_services`, added together rather than compounded: `FIRE_SAFE` adds 0.06, `CRYOGENIC` adds 0.12 and `FUGITIVE_EMISSION` adds 0.04. The **surcharge** is the subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. The **pre-discount amount** is the subtotal plus the surcharge. The **volume discount rate** is keyed on that pre-discount amount, and on nothing else: a pre-discount amount of 6,000,000 cents or more earns 0.06, an amount from 2,000,000 up to but not including 6,000,000 earns 0.03, and anything below 2,000,000 earns nothing. The **discount** is the pre-discount amount multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. The **expedite fee**, charged only when `expedite_requested` is true and otherwise zero, is 0.08 of the pre-discount amount **after** the discount has been subtracted, again rounded half up to whole cents. `gross_quoted_cents` is finally the pre-discount amount, less the discount, plus the expedite fee. Approval sets `net_billable_cents` equal to `gross_quoted_cents` and `credit_issued_cents` to 0. Tiering on the grand total instead of the pre-discount amount, or charging the expedite fee before the discount, both give a different gross — for example, an order of 4 valves at 1,250,000 cents each requesting fire-safe and cryogenic service with expedite bought quotes at 6,180,840 cents, having taken the 0.03 tier on a 5,900,000 pre-discount amount rather than the 0.06 tier the 6,180,840 grand total would have crossed.

Release to production is the `ProductEngineer`'s action, moving `APPROVED → IN_PRODUCTION`. It requires the order to be at `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`; it then requires `spec_stale` to be false, otherwise `ERR_PINNED_SPEC_STALE`; and it finally requires `gross_quoted_cents` to be at or below `customer_credit_limit_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) — an equal figure passes. The stale-pin refusal is a dead end rather than a detour: no action returns an `APPROVED` order to `UNDER_REVIEW`, so there is no way to re-approve it against the design's newer revision and cancellation is the only exit left. Release also clears `held_reason` to null. Production may be interrupted: as described in §3.7, a decertified unit or a voided documentation package moves `IN_PRODUCTION → HELD` with `held_reason` set to `UNIT_DECERTIFIED` or `DOC_PACKAGE_VOIDED` respectively. The `ProductEngineer` resumes, moving `HELD → IN_PRODUCTION` and clearing `held_reason`, but only from `HELD` (otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`), and only when no unit of the order sits at `cert_status` `DECERTIFIED` (otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`) and no unit of the order sits under a license whose `compliance_posture` is `FROZEN` (otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`). Those two sweeps are separate and ordered: every unit is examined for a decertified cert status before any license posture is consulted, so an order that holds both a decertified unit and a frozen license reports `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, not the posture error. A unit whose `monogram_license_id` resolves to nothing is skipped by the posture sweep rather than treated as frozen.

The documentation package is scored continuously as documents arrive. A `QualityManager` appends an entry by supplying a `doc_type` and a non-empty `doc_ref`; the entry joins the end of the order's `documents` list recording exactly its `doc_type`, its `doc_ref` and the `added_at` instant taken from the logical works clock, and the completeness percentage is recomputed. Appending is otherwise unguarded: it imposes no precondition on the order's `status` — a `DRAFT`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED` order will all take a further document — and none on `doc_package_status`, and it never rejects a repeat, so the same `doc_type` and `doc_ref` may be appended twice and the second copy counts as a second entry toward that category's required count. Six document categories are weighted: a material test report and a test certificate each carry 0.25, a dimensional report and a non-destructive-examination report each carry 0.15, and an actuator datasheet and a declaration of conformity each carry 0.10 — the category names are `MATERIAL_TEST_REPORT`, `TEST_CERTIFICATE`, `DIMENSIONAL_REPORT`, `NDE_REPORT`, `ACTUATOR_DATASHEET` and `DECLARATION_OF_CONFORMITY`. The number of entries each category requires differs: one material test report and one test certificate **per ordered valve**, so `ordered_qty` of each; exactly one dimensional report, one non-destructive-examination report and one declaration of conformity; and one actuator datasheet only when some unit of the order has an actuator package with `sizing_status` `APPROVED`, and none at all otherwise. For each category, the fraction earned is 1.00 outright when the required count is zero; otherwise it is the smaller of the present count and the required count, divided by the required count, **rounded half up to 2 decimal places at this point**. Each category's fraction is then multiplied by its weight, the six products are added, and `documentation_completeness_pct` is 100 times that sum, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The two-stage rounding order is pinned: rounding each fraction first and summing afterwards is not the same as summing the unrounded fractions and rounding once. An order for 7 valves with no approved actuator package holding one material test report, one dimensional report and one non-destructive-examination report scores 43.50, not the 43.57 the unrounded arithmetic would give.

`doc_package_status` follows the score, but only in one direction and only from one origin. The package moves `ASSEMBLING → COMPLETE` automatically at the moment an appended entry brings `documentation_completeness_pct` to exactly 100.00; the promotion is evaluated only for a package sitting at `ASSEMBLING`, and there is no demotion, so a `COMPLETE` or `ISSUED` package whose score is still 100.00 is left exactly where it is by a further append and no fall in the score can ever pull a package back to `ASSEMBLING`. A `QualityManager` then issues it, moving `COMPLETE → ISSUED`; issuing requires the order's `status` to be `IN_PRODUCTION` or `HELD`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, then requires the package to be `COMPLETE`, otherwise `ERR_DOC_PACKAGE_INCOMPLETE`, then requires `certified_unit_count` to be at or above `ordered_qty`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_UNITS_INCOMPLETE` — that is the order the three are evaluated in. An `ISSUED` package moves to `VOIDED` when a certified unit of the order is decertified or the order is cancelled, and a `QualityManager` may also void it explicitly; the explicit void requires `doc_package_status` to be `ISSUED` and reports `ERR_STATE_INVALID` otherwise, is indifferent to the order's own `status`, sets `held_reason` to `DOC_PACKAGE_VOIDED`, and additionally drops an `IN_PRODUCTION` order to `HELD` — an order in any other status keeps its status and merely acquires the reason. Appending a further document entry after a void moves `VOIDED → ASSEMBLING` and recomputes the percentage, and because that demotion happens before the completeness test the very same append can carry the package straight back to `COMPLETE` if the score is still 100.00; neither the void nor the re-assembly touches the order's `status` or clears `held_reason`. `certified_unit_count`, for its part, is simply the number of the order's units whose `cert_status` is `CERTIFIED`.

Closing the order is a `QualityManager` action moving `IN_PRODUCTION → CLOSED`; it requires that origin status, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID`, then requires `doc_package_status` to be `ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_DOC_PACKAGE_INCOMPLETE`, and only then requires `certified_unit_count` to be at or above `ordered_qty`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_UNITS_INCOMPLETE` — the documentation package is judged before the unit count, so an order short of both reports the package. Closing leaves `held_reason` as it stands. Cancellation is the `ProductEngineer`'s compensating exit, available from `APPROVED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `HELD`, and moving the order to the terminal `CANCELLED`. The retention share depends on the status the order held **at the moment of cancellation**: a cancellation from `APPROVED` retains one tenth of `gross_quoted_cents`, while a cancellation from either `IN_PRODUCTION` or `HELD` retains thirty-five hundredths of it. `net_billable_cents` becomes that retained share, rounded half up to whole cents, and `credit_issued_cents` becomes `gross_quoted_cents` less `net_billable_cents` by subtraction rather than by a second rounding, so the identity that the gross quote equals the net billable amount plus the credit issued holds exactly, before and after. Cancellation also moves an `ISSUED` documentation package to `VOIDED` and, when it does, stamps `held_reason` `DOC_PACKAGE_VOIDED` on the now-cancelled order; a cancellation that finds the package in any other state leaves both the package and `held_reason` untouched, so a cancellation from `HELD` keeps whichever reason put it there. `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` are the order's terminal states.

### 3.9. Aftermarket Support: Repair Kits and Field Service

The field service dimension is the `FieldServiceCoordinator`'s exclusive territory, and it is deliberately decoupled from build and certification except at the two points named below. Opening a service job moves `service_status` from `NONE` or `SERVICED` to `OPEN` and requires the unit's `build_status` to be `PACKAGED`, `SHIPPED` or `REWORK`, otherwise `ERR_STATE_INVALID` — a unit that has not yet been packaged has nothing in the field to service. The service dimension is tested first: a unit already `OPEN`, `PARTS_RESERVED` or `IN_SERVICE` is refused for that reason before its `build_status` is looked at, and both refusals share `ERR_STATE_INVALID`. The request carries `engineer_user_id`, which is required on `OPEN` alone and is recorded on the unit as `service_engineer_user_id`, overwriting whatever a previous job left there; that field is null whenever `service_status` is `NONE` or `SERVICED`. `engineer_user_id` and `kit_qty` are each mandatory only for the action that consumes them — `OPEN` and `RESERVE_KIT` respectively — and are accepted and ignored when supplied to any other service action.

Reserving parts moves `OPEN → PARTS_RESERVED` and carries a `kit_qty` of at least 1 which may not exceed the design's `repair_kit_stock_qty`; an over-draw is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_KIT_OUT_OF_STOCK`. A reservation for exactly the stock on hand is allowed; only a request above it is refused. A successful reservation decreases the design's `repair_kit_stock_qty` by `kit_qty`, stamps the design's `version` and `updated_at`, and sets — not adds to — the unit's `reserved_kit_qty`, so the field carries the current job's reservation and nothing accumulated from an earlier one. Starting the job moves `PARTS_RESERVED → IN_SERVICE`, and this is the second coupling into the other two dimensions, taken as a single either-or: a unit at `cert_status` `CERTIFIED` is decertified with `decertification_reason` `MANUAL`, running the full decertification tail of §3.7 — which supplies the same `ASSEMBLED`-or-`PACKAGED` fallback to `REWORK`, along with the order hold and the documentation void — while a unit in any other cert status is not decertified and merely takes that build fallback on its own. Starting a job on a unit that is already `DECERTIFIED` therefore leaves its existing `decertification_reason` in place rather than rewriting it to `MANUAL`. Opening a valve in the field invalidates its certificate, and the system says so rather than letting the paperwork drift.

Completing the job moves `IN_SERVICE → SERVICED`: the reserved kits are consumed, `reserved_kit_qty` returns to 0, `service_engineer_user_id` is cleared to null, and stock is **not** returned to the design — the parts were fitted, and the design is not stamped at all. Completion touches neither `build_status` nor `cert_status`, so a unit that fell to `REWORK` and lost its certificate at the start of the job stays there once the job is finished. Aborting is the compensating action, available from `OPEN`, `PARTS_RESERVED` or `IN_SERVICE`, and it returns `service_status` to `NONE`: the design's `repair_kit_stock_qty` increases by the unit's `reserved_kit_qty`, `reserved_kit_qty` returns to 0, and `service_engineer_user_id` is cleared. The stock return and the design's version stamp happen only when there is something to return, so an abort from `OPEN`, with nothing reserved, leaves the design entirely untouched. Repair-kit stock is therefore conserved across a reserve-then-abort pair and permanently reduced across a reserve-then-complete pair. Because `service_status` returns to `NONE` on abort and reaches `SERVICED` on completion, a unit may be serviced repeatedly over its life, and each new job re-opens from either of those two resting states.
