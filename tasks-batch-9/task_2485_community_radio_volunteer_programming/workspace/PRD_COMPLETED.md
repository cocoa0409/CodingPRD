# Community Radio Volunteer Programming API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A licensed community radio station runs almost entirely on volunteer labour, and yet every hour it broadcasts is governed by obligations that a volunteer roster is poorly equipped to remember: a board operator must hold a live certification before touching the console, a music show owes a playlist log to the performing-rights organisations within three days of airing, a political-candidate announcement owes a public-file entry within a week of clearance, and an indecency complaint filed against a mid-afternoon airing is priced at double because it fell inside the regulator's safe-harbour window. Today all of this lives in three-ring binders, a spreadsheet of "who is trained on what", and the institutional memory of a program director who is also a volunteer. The station needs a system of record.

This document specifies the Community Radio Volunteer Programming API: a single service that carries a volunteer from enrolment through curriculum certification, carries a show idea from a draft proposal through programming-committee review into a published schedule grid, carries each airing through its playlist log into a royalty liability figure, and carries the station's regulator-facing exposure through complaint adjudication and remediation. Alongside the programming spine it runs the development side of the house — membership drives, volunteer phone banks, pledge settlement and thank-you-gift fulfilment — because the same volunteers staff both and a no-show on the phones is a disciplinary matter that changes where that volunteer's show sits in the seniority queue.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as constraints on every later section. First, the station's obligations are **clock-driven, never wall-clock driven**: certification expiry, retake cooling-off, log deadlines, public-file deadlines, calendar-quarter boundaries and drive windows are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock that an operator sets explicitly. Second, decisions that depend on a shared, contested picture of the world — which minutes of the broadcast week are free, which ballots are on the table, why a show deserves cancelling — are **guarded by version checks**, so that a client acting on a view it read earlier is turned away rather than silently overwriting a change it never saw.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every rule in this section applies across the whole API unless a later section narrows it. Where §2 and §3 describe the same rule from different angles, they are reinforcing each other, not competing.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting person, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity they are acting in. The station recognises exactly six roles, spelled precisely as follows: `ProgramDirector`, `CommitteeMember`, `Volunteer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `TrainingCoordinator`, and `DevelopmentCoordinator`. A request that omits `X-User-ID`, and a request whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint it addresses, are both rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). This check is the first thing any endpoint does, and it applies to every endpoint in §4 with a single exception: the logical-clock capability described in §2.4 is a test-driving affordance and performs no role check at all.

Three of the six roles are opaque operator identities and three are — or, more precisely, two are — backed by a persisted roster row. The distinction matters because it decides what `X-User-ID` is compared against:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Volunteer` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Volunteer` compare the resource's volunteer-bearing field to `X-User-ID`: submitting, withdrawing or amending a proposal requires `X-User-ID` to equal that proposal's `proposer_volunteer_id`, and logging or submitting a playlist requires `X-User-ID` to equal the airing's `board_operator_volunteer_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Volunteer` row — specifically one whose `committee_seat` is true. Ballot casting compares the seated member's identity against the proposal's author to enforce the station's segregation-of-duties policy.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does not compare it to any row's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does record it — as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an `UnderwritingSpot` when that officer screens its copy, and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `ComplianceIncident` when that officer rules on it — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `TrainingCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded nowhere and compared to no row's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `DevelopmentCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded nowhere and compared to no row's `id`.

Ownership is checked immediately after role membership and before any structural validation of the payload. A caller whose role is permitted but whose identity is wrong receives a more specific refusal than `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`: `ERR_NOT_PROPOSAL_OWNER` (403) when a volunteer acts on someone else's proposal, `ERR_NOT_BOARD_OPERATOR` (403) when a volunteer logs a spin or submits a log for an airing they did not board-operate, and `ERR_NOT_COMMITTEE_MEMBER` (403) when the volunteer casting a ballot does not hold a committee seat.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it reports the logical clock of §2.4 as it stands when the response is built, never the host machine's wall clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps how many rows come back; when omitted it defaults to 10, its smallest accepted value is 1 and its largest accepted value is 100. `offset` says how many rows to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0 and it may not be negative. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, or a negative `offset` is a structural failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400), as is a `limit` or `offset` that is not written as a base-ten integer. An empty or absent parameter simply falls back to its default.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. Because the logical clock only moves when an operator moves it, rows written inside one clock setting share a `created_at` exactly; that tie is broken by the order in which the server wrote them, so the row created first still comes first.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. The Station Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. The server holds a **logical clock** whose value starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and an operator-facing capability replaces it wholesale by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the capability echoes back the clock's new value, answering HTTP 200 with a `data` object whose single field is `current_utc`. A malformed or missing `current_utc` is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Because this capability exists purely to drive time-dependent behaviour deterministically, it is the one operation exempt from the header check in §2.1.

Every temporal judgement in the specification resolves against that clock: whether a certification has expired, whether the fourteen-day retake cooling-off window has elapsed, whether a playlist log has passed its deadline, whether a public-file obligation has gone unmet, which calendar quarter "quarter to date" refers to, whether a phone-bank shift falls inside its drive's window, and what instant gets stamped into fields such as `issued_on`, `last_attempt_at`, `decided_at`, `published_at`, `last_evaluated_at`, `public_file_posted_at` and `adjudicated_by_user_id`'s accompanying timestamps. Station-local time is identical to UTC, so a weekday or an hour-of-day derived from a stored instant needs no offset arithmetic.

Clock-driven obligations do not fire on their own. They are advanced by an explicit **daily sweep**, described in §2.12, which is the only operation that can move a record forward purely because time has passed.

### 2.5. Common Record Properties and Immutable Records

Most resources in the system carry a standard set of server-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply it.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the row is first written.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only touch derived fields.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. A recomputation of a derived total counts as a mutation: when a slot release rewrites a grid's `total_assigned_minutes`, that grid's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed, even though no client wrote a field on the grid directly.

`version` and `updated_at` move **at most once per row per accepted request**. When a single request mutates the same row several times — a grid whose minute figures are recomputed by each of two occupations preempted in the same placement, say, or by each of several occupations released by one cascade — that row's `version` advances by exactly 1 in total for the request, however many separate advances a later section describes for the steps inside it. A row that the request itself created is not additionally mutated by that same request: a certification record opened and graded in one call is returned on `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to its `created_at`, and the next request that changes it takes it to 2. Conversely, a request that changes no field of a row — an amendment naming none of the fields it is allowed to change — leaves that row's `version` and `updated_at` exactly as they were, while an amendment that does name an amendable field advances them even when the submitted value is identical to the stored one. Whenever a mutated row appears in the response body of the request that mutated it, that body carries the post-mutation `version` and `updated_at`, never the values the row held before the request.

A number of the derived figures §2.5 speaks of are not stored columns at all but are recomputed each time the row is rendered, so they always describe the store and the clock as they stand when the response is built: a grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct`, a drive's five money aggregates and its `attainment_pct`, a premium item's `status`, a show's `compliance_penalty_points` and `compliance_standing`, and a volunteer's `continuous_service_months`, `current_certification_count` and `needs_core_renewal`. Recomputing one of these on the way out is not itself a mutation and does not advance the row's `version` — the version movements §2.5 describes for a grid whose minutes changed come from the request that changed the occupation set, not from the later read. By contrast `Volunteer.seniority_score`, `Program.performance_score`, an airing's `featured_performance_count`, `local_featured_count`, `provisional_royalty_cents` and `final_royalty_cents`, and a pledge's `premium_status` and `tax_deductible_cents` are stored values that move only on the triggers §3 names, and a read never refreshes them.

Every instant the API emits is rendered in one and the same form — `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, converted to UTC and carrying exactly three fractional digits, so that a whole-second instant is written with a `.000` fraction. That covers `created_at` and `updated_at`; the server-stamped instants `issued_on`, `expires_on`, `last_attempt_at`, `decided_at`, `published_at`, `last_evaluated_at`, `log_due_at`, `public_file_due_at` and `public_file_posted_at`; the instants a client supplied and the server stored — `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `scheduled_start_utc`, `flight_start`, `flight_end`, `occurred_at`, and a drive's or a shift's `start_at` and `end_at`; and the logical clock's own echoed value. A client-supplied instant is therefore normalised into that form rather than echoed back character for character, and any precision finer than a millisecond is truncated. `seniority_start_date` is a calendar date rather than an instant and stays a bare `YYYY-MM-DD`.

Two records are deliberate exceptions because the station treats them as write-once evidence. A `CommitteeVote` — the committee ballot — carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no amendment operation, because the committee minutes must show what a member actually said in the round they said it. A `PlaylistEntry` — one logged spin — is likewise limited to `id` and `created_at` with no `updated_at` and no `version`, because the royalty report is built from the log as filed. Corrections to a log are made by returning the whole log to its operator, not by editing an entry.

### 2.6. Path Resolution for Sub-Resource Routes

The station's records sit at peer collections rather than in a deep ownership tree; relationships are carried by explicit identifier fields on the child row. A handful of routes nonetheless nest one identifier under another, either because creation naturally belongs to the parent (opening a certification record for a named volunteer, placing an occupation on a named grid, logging a spin against a named airing) or because the reverse lookup is convenient (listing one volunteer's certification records).

For those routes the server resolves path identifiers strictly left to right and answers on the **first** identifier that names nothing. A request for certification records under a volunteer that does not exist answers `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND` (404) and never looks at anything to its right. A request that places an occupation on a grid that does not exist answers `ERR_GRID_NOT_FOUND` (404) before it considers whether the program named in the body exists. Once every path identifier resolves, identifiers supplied in the request body are resolved next, and a body identifier naming no row answers the 404 belonging to that collection — `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRIVE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Records addressed directly at their own collection answer their own not-found code: `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAYLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. Numbers, Money and Rounding

Four kinds of number appear in this system, and each has one rule.

**Two-decimal scores and percentages.** `Volunteer.seniority_score`, `ProgramProposal.composite_score`, `ScheduleGrid.public_affairs_minutes_pct`, `Program.performance_score` and `MembershipDrive.attainment_pct` are all carried to exactly two decimal places using round-half-up. In every one of these calculations the rounding happens **once, at the end**: intermediate means, ratios and quotients are kept at full precision and are never rounded on the way through. Where a formula also clamps a value — to a floor of zero, to a ceiling of one hundred, to a cap of five — the clamp is applied before the single final rounding, except where §3 explicitly says otherwise.

**Integer cents.** `Episode.provisional_royalty_cents`, `Episode.final_royalty_cents`, `MembershipDrive.goal_cents`, the drive's five money aggregates, `Pledge.amount_cents`, `Pledge.tax_deductible_cents`, `PremiumItem.tier_threshold_cents` and `PremiumItem.fair_market_value_cents` are whole numbers of cents. No fractional cent is ever stored. Where a royalty figure is derived from a rate, the rounding rule is half-up to a whole cent, and §3.5 specifies exactly how many times that rounding is applied for each of the two royalty variants — the two answers differ, and the difference is intentional.

**Integer points and minutes.** `ComplianceIncident.penalty_points`, `Program.compliance_penalty_points`, exam scores, criterion scores, `SlotAssignment.start_minute`, `SlotAssignment.duration_minutes`, `ScheduleGrid.total_assigned_minutes` and `PlaylistEntry.duration_seconds` are exact integers with no rounding of any kind.

**Integer unit counts.** `PremiumItem.units_received_qty`, `on_hand_qty`, `reserved_qty` and `shipped_qty` are whole units. They move one unit at a time except when the vendor delivers a shipment.

Numbers are returned in response bodies as JSON numbers, not strings. A two-decimal field is emitted with its two decimals — `54.00`, not `54`.

### 2.8. Version Guards on Contested Writes

Four write operations require the client to prove it is acting on a picture of the world it actually read. Each takes a non-optional version field in its request body, whose value must equal the current `version` of the named parent record. When the values differ — because any other writer advanced that record since the client last read it — the write is refused outright and nothing is mutated.

- Creating a slot occupation on a grid requires `expected_grid_version` to equal that `ScheduleGrid`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRID_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The grid's occupation set is exactly the input to the placement decision — another writer may have added an occupation, or a seniority preemption may legitimately have displaced an incumbent — so a writer whose picture of which minutes are free is out of date cannot be trusted to place a show.
- Publishing a grid requires `expected_grid_version` to equal that `ScheduleGrid`'s current `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRID_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Publication freezes the diversity quota and flips every confirmed show to air, so a grid that gained or lost an occupation since the director's last read must be re-read before it is published.
- Deciding a proposal requires `expected_proposal_version` to equal that `ProgramProposal`'s current `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPOSAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every accepted ballot advances the proposal's `version`, and a ballot that landed after the director's read changes both the composite score and the decline-majority test, so a tally computed from a stale ballot set is refused.
- Cancelling a program requires `expected_program_version` to equal that `Program`'s current `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Cancellation is justified by the show's own review standing, and an intervening adjudication, remediation or performance review rewrites exactly that standing, so the justification must be re-established against a fresh read.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, confirming, releasing and reinstating an occupation carry none — confirmation and release do not read which minutes are free, so there is nothing for them to be stale about — and neither do exam grading, air-result recording, log submission, log certification, copy review, adjudication, remediation, drive activation, drive closure, pledge settlement, refund or premium fulfilment. A client that supplies a version field to an endpoint that does not take one has simply supplied an unrecognised key; it does not turn that endpoint into a guarded one.

### 2.9. Two-Dimensional State and Cascade Discipline

Three records in this system carry **two independent state dimensions** that advance separately and never constrain each other's vocabulary. A `Program` has a `programming_status` describing where the show sits in the programming lifecycle and a `compliance_standing` describing its regulator-facing exposure; an `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` show can be `WATCH` and keep airing, and a `RESTRICTED` show retains whatever `programming_status` it had. An `Episode` has an `air_status` describing what happened on air and a `log_status` describing where its playlist log sits. A `Pledge` has a `payment_status` describing the money and a `premium_status` describing the thank-you gift. Whenever this document describes a transition, it names the dimension it moves; the other dimension is untouched unless a side effect explicitly says so.

Cascades in this system are **immediate and complete**: a single accepted request performs every downstream mutation before it responds, and each mutated record independently refreshes its own `updated_at` and advances its own `version`. Cascades are also, in several places, deliberately **not** symmetric — an action that suspends or preempts something does not have a mirror-image restoration that fires automatically. Two consequences are worth stating up front because they are the most common source of wrong implementations. First, when a volunteer is reinstated after a suspension, the shows that were pulled into review because of that suspension are **not** returned to air automatically; they must each clear a performance review. Second, when a program's standing improves, the occupations that were preempted because of its restriction **stay** preempted; a director must reinstate each one explicitly.

Reversals that do exist are exact. Remediating a substantiated incident refunds precisely the penalty points that were charged — including the doubling applied to a safe-harbour indecency finding — so the program's `compliance_penalty_points` returns to the value it held before. Reinstating a preempted occupation returns the owning grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct` to their pre-preemption values. Refunding a paid pledge returns any shipped or reserved premium unit to stock, so the premium's unit conservation holds exactly as it did before the payment.

### 2.10. Order of Refusal

Many operations can fail for several reasons at once, and the station cares which reason it reports, because the error code drives what the operator does next. Checks are therefore evaluated in a fixed order, and the **first** failing check decides the response. The ladder, from earliest to latest, is:

1. **Role membership.** The caller's `X-User-Role` must be authorised for the endpoint, and `X-User-ID` must be present — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
2. **Ownership.** Proposal authorship (`ERR_NOT_PROPOSAL_OWNER`), then board-operator identity (`ERR_NOT_BOARD_OPERATOR`), then committee seating (`ERR_NOT_COMMITTEE_MEMBER`), in that order. Authorship and board-operator identity need a row to compare against, so when the addressed record does not exist at all the request falls through to that collection's 404 instead of reporting an ownership failure: acting on a proposal identifier that names nothing answers `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, and filing against an airing identifier that names nothing answers `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`. Committee seating is the exception — it is a property of the caller alone, so it is decided before any record is looked up and is reported even for an identifier that names nothing.
3. **Structural validation of the payload,** all of which report `ERR_VALIDATION`: required fields present and non-null first, then enum-typed inputs inside their declared domains, then numeric inputs inside their declared ranges, then pagination bounds on list requests.
4. **Interval ordering** — a window whose end is not strictly after its start reports `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.
5. **Natural-key uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`; then the one-ballot-per-round rule, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`; then the one-program-per-proposal rule, `ERR_PROGRAM_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Each of these three is scoped to a record named in the path, and none of them is reachable until that record has been found, so a path identifier that names nothing answers its own 404 first: an amendment carrying another volunteer's email against an unknown volunteer identifier answers `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, a second ballot against an unknown proposal answers `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, and a repeated `sequence_number` against an unknown airing answers `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Existence** — path identifiers first, left to right, then identifiers carried in the request body, each answering its collection's 404 code.
7. **Cross-reference agreement** — the occupation named for an airing must belong to the same show (`ERR_SLOT_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`), then the airing's scheduled instant must line up with that occupation (`ERR_EPISODE_SLOT_MISMATCH`), then a pledge's phone-bank shift must belong to the pledge's drive (`ERR_SHIFT_DRIVE_MISMATCH`), then an incident must carry the target its category requires (`ERR_INCIDENT_TARGET_REQUIRED`).
8. **Version guards** — the three stale-version refusals of §2.8.
9. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This deliberately outranks the state checks below it: an officer who cleared a spot's copy is turned away before the incident's own adjudication state is even inspected, so attempting to adjudicate an already-substantiated incident against a spot that officer cleared answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
10. **Restricted-program refusal** — `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`; then the self-vote bar, `ERR_SELF_VOTE_FORBIDDEN`; then the revocation-support check, `ERR_REVOCATION_UNSUPPORTED`.
11. **State legality.** A transition not declared for the record's current state answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a write aimed at a grid that is no longer mutable answers `ERR_GRID_NOT_MUTABLE`; these two sit at the same rung and their conditions are disjoint. Note that this rung outranks the window-collision check further down: a placement aimed at a published grid whose window *also* collides with a live occupation answers `ERR_GRID_NOT_MUTABLE`, never `ERR_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Then, in order, come the narrower state gates: `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_DRIVE_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_LOG_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_PUBLIC_FILE_NOT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_PLEDGES_PENDING`, `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_RESERVED`, `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_PAID`, and finally `ERR_NOT_EDITABLE`.
12. **Cross-entity standing** — the proposer's certification (`ERR_PROPOSER_NOT_CERTIFIED`), then the voter's roster standing (`ERR_VOTER_INELIGIBLE`), then the board operator's certification (`ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`), then the phone-bank volunteer's roster standing (`ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`).
13. **Temporal fit** — the shift-inside-drive-window check (`ERR_SHIFT_OUTSIDE_DRIVE_WINDOW`), then the occupation window collision (`ERR_SLOT_CONFLICT`), then the volunteer's own shift collision (`ERR_SHIFT_CONFLICT`), then the retake cooling-off window (`ERR_RETAKE_TOO_SOON`), then the retake attempt cap (`ERR_MAX_ATTEMPTS_REACHED`). The last pair matters: a retake requested on a record that has already been graded three times *and* is still inside its cooling-off window answers `ERR_RETAKE_TOO_SOON`, because cooling-off is checked first.
14. **Capacity and quota** — phone-line capacity (`ERR_PHONE_LINE_CAPACITY`), committee quorum (`ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`), the empty-grid publication check (`ERR_GRID_EMPTY`), the tentative-occupation publication check (`ERR_TENTATIVE_SLOTS_PRESENT`), the diversity quota (`ERR_DIVERSITY_QUOTA_UNMET`), the broadcast-week minute cap (`ERR_WEEK_MINUTES_EXCEEDED`), the music-log emptiness check (`ERR_PLAYLIST_EMPTY`), the seniority preemption margin (`ERR_SENIORITY_MARGIN_UNMET`), committee staffing (`ERR_COMMITTEE_UNDERSTAFFED`), the edited-copy cleanliness check (`ERR_EDITED_COPY_STILL_NONCOMPLIANT`), the political-copy censorship bar (`ERR_POLITICAL_COPY_UNCENSORABLE`), and finally premium stock on hand (`ERR_PREMIUM_OUT_OF_STOCK`), in exactly that order.

Publication is the clearest illustration of why the ordering is spelled out: a grid that is stale, still holds a tentative occupation, has no assigned airtime at all, and falls short of the public-affairs quota reports `ERR_STALE_GRID_VERSION` first; once re-read, it reports `ERR_GRID_EMPTY` before `ERR_TENTATIVE_SLOTS_PRESENT`, and only a non-empty, fully-confirmed grid ever gets as far as `ERR_DIVERSITY_QUOTA_UNMET`.

### 2.11. Conserved Totals

Three totals in this system are invariants rather than conveniences, and every operation that touches them is specified so that they hold after the operation completes.

The **premium unit invariant**: for every `PremiumItem`, `on_hand_qty` plus `shipped_qty` always equals `units_received_qty`. Reserving a unit moves nothing between these three — it only raises `reserved_qty`, which is an earmark against on-hand stock and not a fourth bucket. Shipping moves one unit out of `on_hand_qty` into `shipped_qty` and drops the earmark. A vendor delivery raises `units_received_qty` and `on_hand_qty` together. A return moves one unit back out of `shipped_qty` into `on_hand_qty`.

The **drive money invariant**: for every `MembershipDrive`, `gross_pledged_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every pledge on the drive whatever its `payment_status`, and it therefore only ever grows. The four narrower aggregates partition that gross by settlement state, so `fulfilled_total_cents` plus `refunded_total_cents` plus `lapsed_total_cents` plus the still-pending portion always adds back up to `gross_pledged_cents`.

The **airtime invariant**: a grid's `total_assigned_minutes` is always the exact sum of `duration_minutes` over that grid's live occupations, and its `public_affairs_minutes_pct` is always derived from the same live set. Preempting an occupation and then reinstating it returns both figures to the values they held before the preemption.

### 2.12. The Daily Sweep

Because the logical clock only moves when an operator moves it, the obligations that depend on time only advance when an operator asks them to. The **daily sweep** is that request. It takes no input, is available to the `ProgramDirector` and the `ComplianceOfficer`, and performs each of the following exactly once against the clock's current value:

- Every `CertificationRecord` in `PASSED` whose `expires_on` has been reached moves to `EXPIRED`.
- Every `Episode` whose `log_status` is still `PENDING` and whose `log_due_at` has been reached moves to `DELINQUENT`.
- Every `UnderwritingSpot` whose `public_file_status` is `REQUIRED` and whose `public_file_due_at` has been reached moves to `MISSING`, and raises the substantiated public-file incident described in §3.6.
- Every `Program` whose `programming_status` is `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` is re-evaluated against the trailing-six performance window described in §3.8.

The sweep answers with five arrays naming what it touched: `expired_certification_ids`, `delinquent_episode_ids`, `missing_public_file_spot_ids`, `created_incident_ids` and `reviewed_program_ids`. It runs those four phases in the order listed above, works through each collection in the same `created_at`-then-insertion order §2.3 gives to listings, and fills each array in that order; a show sent down for review by the fourth phase still appears in `reviewed_program_ids`, and every array is present and empty when the phase found nothing. Every cascade that a sweep-driven transition triggers runs in full inside the same request — the sweep is not a queue and leaves nothing half-finished.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrolment, Curriculum and Certification

A `TrainingCoordinator` puts a new person on the roster by supplying their `legal_name`, a contact `email`, the `role_track` they are enrolling in — one of `PROGRAMMER`, `ENGINEER` or `DEVELOPMENT` — and a `seniority_start_date` written as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date marking the beginning of their continuous service. That date is the base of every seniority computation and may not fall after the logical clock's current date. The coordinator may also seat the newcomer on the programming committee immediately by passing `committee_seat`; when the flag is omitted it is false. The `email` must be non-empty and unique across the roster, and a second enrolment reusing an address already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Every newly enrolled volunteer begins with `status` `PROSPECT`, a `strike_count` of 0, a `seniority_score` of `0.00`, and `needs_core_renewal` true — because a person who has certified in nothing is, by definition, missing all of the core curriculum. The roster standing in `status` is entirely server-driven; it is never accepted as an input, and neither are `continuous_service_months`, `current_certification_count`, `strike_count`, `seniority_score` or `needs_core_renewal`. Amending a roster entry lets the `TrainingCoordinator` or the `ProgramDirector` change `legal_name`, `email` and `committee_seat` and nothing else; any of the computed fields present in such a payload is ignored rather than honoured. A volunteer whose `status` has reached `RESIGNED` can no longer be amended at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NOT_EDITABLE`. Reusing another volunteer's `email` during an amendment is again `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, and that refusal outranks the `ERR_NOT_EDITABLE` bar on a resigned entry, so an amendment that is both aimed at a `RESIGNED` volunteer and carrying somebody else's address answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. An amendment addressed to a volunteer identifier that names nobody answers `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND` before either of those two is considered.

Training is tracked as a **certification record** per curriculum. The coordinator opens one against a named volunteer by supplying the `cert_type`, which is one of `BOARD_OPERATION`, `STATION_POLICY`, `BROADCAST_REGULATION` or `UNDERWRITING_COPY`; the record's `volunteer_id` is taken from the volunteer being addressed rather than from the body. Nothing stops a second record being opened for a curriculum the volunteer already holds — there is no uniqueness rule on the pairing of volunteer and `cert_type`, because a renewal after a lapse or a revocation is exactly a fresh record. Three of those curricula — `BOARD_OPERATION`, `STATION_POLICY` and `BROADCAST_REGULATION` — are the station's **core** set; `UNDERWRITING_COPY` is a specialism and is not core. A record opens on `status` `PENDING_EXAM` with `attempt_count` 0, a null `exam_score`, and null `issued_on`, `expires_on`, `last_attempt_at` and `revocation_reason`. The very first certification record opened for a volunteer moves that volunteer from `PROSPECT` to `IN_TRAINING`, recomputes their `seniority_score`, refreshes their `updated_at` and advances their `version` by 1. "First" means the volunteer had no certification record of any kind before this one, and the promotion only fires from `PROSPECT` — a record opened for a volunteer who is already `IN_TRAINING` or beyond leaves their roster row untouched.

Grading may happen in the same request that opens the record — the coordinator simply includes `exam_score` — or later, as a separate grading of a record sitting on `PENDING_EXAM`. Either way the score is an integer from 0 through 100 inclusive, and anything outside that range is `ERR_VALIDATION`. A score of **80 or more** passes; anything below 80 fails. On a pass the record moves `PENDING_EXAM → PASSED`, `issued_on` is stamped with the clock's current instant, `expires_on` is set to twenty-four calendar months after `issued_on`, `last_attempt_at` is stamped with the same instant, and `attempt_count` rises by 1. On a failure the record moves `PENDING_EXAM → FAILED`, `last_attempt_at` is stamped and `attempt_count` rises by 1, while `issued_on` and `expires_on` remain null. Grading a record that is not on `PENDING_EXAM` — one already `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A pass immediately recomputes the holder's `current_certification_count`, which counts that volunteer's certification records whose `status` is `PASSED` and whose `expires_on` is strictly later than the clock's current instant, and re-derives `needs_core_renewal`, which is true whenever at least one of the three core curricula is absent, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` for that volunteer. Note the deliberate asymmetry between those two derivations: `current_certification_count` is measured against the clock, so a ticket whose `expires_on` the clock has passed stops counting immediately, whereas `needs_core_renewal` is measured against record *status* alone. A core record that is still `PASSED` but whose `expires_on` the clock has overrun therefore keeps `needs_core_renewal` false until the daily sweep lapses it to `EXPIRED`; and because `UNDERWRITING_COPY` is not core, no state of that record ever moves the flag. When the pass completes the core set — that is, when for each of `BOARD_OPERATION`, `STATION_POLICY` and `BROADCAST_REGULATION` the volunteer holds a `PASSED` record whose `expires_on` is later than the clock — the volunteer advances `IN_TRAINING → CERTIFIED`, or `PROBATION → CERTIFIED` if they had fallen back, `needs_core_renewal` is set false, `seniority_score` is recomputed and `version` advances by 1. Recovery out of `PROBATION` carries one extra condition beyond a complete core set: the volunteer's `strike_count` must be **below 2**. A twice-struck volunteer who re-passes every core curriculum stays on `PROBATION`.

A failed record can be reopened for another attempt, moving `FAILED → PENDING_EXAM`, which clears `exam_score` back to null, leaves `attempt_count` exactly as it stands and advances the record's `version` by 1. Two conditions guard it. The station imposes a **fourteen-day cooling-off window**: the clock must have reached at least fourteen days past `last_attempt_at`, and an earlier request is refused with `ERR_RETAKE_TOO_SOON`. And the station caps attempts at three: a record whose `attempt_count` has reached 3 is refused with `ERR_MAX_ATTEMPTS_REACHED` and is permanently stuck on `FAILED`. Because the cooling-off check is evaluated before the cap, a record that is both exhausted and inside its window answers `ERR_RETAKE_TOO_SOON`. Reopening a record that is not on `FAILED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The cycle `PENDING_EXAM → FAILED → PENDING_EXAM` may therefore run until three attempts have been graded.

Certifications lapse on their own schedule. When the daily sweep finds a `PASSED` record whose `expires_on` the clock has reached, that record moves `PASSED → EXPIRED`. The holder's `current_certification_count` and `seniority_score` are recomputed; if the lapsed curriculum was one of the three core ones, the volunteer falls `CERTIFIED → PROBATION` and `needs_core_renewal` is set true; and every airing whose `air_status` is still `SCHEDULED` and whose `board_operator_volunteer_id` is that volunteer has its operator stripped — `board_operator_volunteer_id` is cleared to null and `needs_substitute` is set true — because the station will not put an uncertified pair of hands on the board.

Revocation is the deliberate, disciplinary counterpart to expiry, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may do it. The request names an `incident_id` and records a free-text `revocation_reason` on the record. It is supported only when the certification is currently `PASSED` **and** the named compliance incident is `SUBSTANTIATED` **and** that incident's `respondent_volunteer_id` is the same volunteer as the certification's `volunteer_id`; when the incident is in any other adjudication state, or names a different respondent, the request is refused with `ERR_REVOCATION_UNSUPPORTED`, and when no incident bears that identifier the answer is `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`. The incident-support test is applied before the record's own state is inspected, so a certification that is not on `PASSED` but whose named incident does support revocation is refused one rung later with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A supported revocation moves the record `PASSED → REVOKED` and fires a four-link cascade in one request: the holder's `current_certification_count` is recomputed, their `needs_core_renewal` is re-derived — turning true only when the revoked curriculum was one of the three core ones, so revoking the `UNDERWRITING_COPY` specialism from a volunteer whose core set is otherwise intact leaves the flag false — and they are moved to `SUSPENDED` from `CERTIFIED` or `PROBATION`, a volunteer already `SUSPENDED`, `RESIGNED`, `PROSPECT` or `IN_TRAINING` keeping the standing they had; every `Program` whose `host_volunteer_id` is that volunteer and whose `programming_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` moves to `UNDER_REVIEW`; and every airing of those now-under-review shows whose `air_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `board_operator_volunteer_id` is that volunteer has `board_operator_volunteer_id` cleared to null and `needs_substitute` set true. Both `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` are terminal for a certification record; there is no path back out of either, and a replacement curriculum must be certified from a fresh record.

The roster and the training pipeline are both browsable. The roster listing narrows by `status`, `role_track` and `committee_seat`; the station-wide certification listing narrows by `volunteer_id`, `cert_type` and `status`, and a per-volunteer listing narrows by `cert_type` and `status`.

### 3.2. Seniority, Strikes and Discipline

Seniority is the currency of a community station, and here it is an explicit number. A volunteer's `continuous_service_months` is the count of **whole** months from their `seniority_start_date` to the clock's current instant, floored: a partial month does not count, so a start date of 15 January 2024 read against a clock of 14 January 2026 yields 23 months, not 24. From that base, `seniority_score` is assembled from four terms and written to two decimal places:

- a **service term** equal to `continuous_service_months`, capped at 60 months — five years of service is as much credit as long service can earn, and further months add nothing;
- a **certification term** equal to 10 points for each live certification, that is, 10 multiplied by `current_certification_count`, counting only records that are `PASSED` with an `expires_on` later than the clock;
- a **performance term** derived from the shows the volunteer hosts. The contributing set is every `Program` whose `host_volunteer_id` is this volunteer, whose `programming_status` is not `CANCELLED`, and whose `performance_score` is not null. When that set is empty the performance term is `0.00`; otherwise it is the arithmetic mean of those shows' `performance_score` values multiplied by 0.20, rounded half up to two decimal places;
- a **strike term** equal to 15 points for each disciplinary strike, that is, 15 multiplied by `strike_count`, which is subtracted rather than added.

The score is the service term plus the certification term plus the performance term, minus the strike term, rounded half up to two decimal places, and then floored at `0.00` so that a heavily struck newcomer sits at zero rather than going negative. A volunteer with all three core certifications, twenty-four whole months of service, no strikes and no hosted shows therefore scores exactly `54.00`; the same volunteer at twenty-nine months scores exactly `59.00`. The score is recomputed whenever the volunteer's `status` changes, whenever one of their certification records reaches `PASSED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`, whenever their `strike_count` changes, and whenever a show they host has its `performance_score` rewritten.

Strikes accrue from exactly two sources, and both add 1 to `strike_count`: closing one of the volunteer's phone-bank shifts as a no-show, and a compliance incident adjudicated as substantiated whose `sanction` is `WARNING` and whose respondent is that volunteer. Each strike costs 15.00 points of seniority through the strike term above. The **second** strike is a threshold: when `strike_count` reaches 2, a `CERTIFIED` volunteer falls to `PROBATION`. `PROBATION` is a marker of standing rather than a bar — it keeps a volunteer's air privileges and blocks no operation by itself; its whole effect is the lowered `seniority_score` and the extra condition it puts on recovery.

Suspension is a real bar. A substantiated incident whose `sanction` is `SUSPENSION`, and a certification revocation, both move a volunteer from `CERTIFIED` or from `PROBATION` to `SUSPENDED`, recompute `seniority_score` and advance the volunteer's `version` by 1. The cascade that follows is the one to watch: every show whose `host_volunteer_id` is the suspended volunteer and whose `programming_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` moves to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and for shows pulled out of `SCHEDULED` this path explicitly does **not** recompute `performance_score` — the score stays exactly as it was — while each of that show's airings still on `air_status` `SCHEDULED` whose `board_operator_volunteer_id` is the suspended volunteer has that field cleared to null and `needs_substitute` set true. A `SUSPENDED` volunteer may not be rostered on the phones and may not cast a committee ballot.

Reinstatement runs through remediation of the very incident that carried the suspension: remediating a `SUBSTANTIATED` incident whose `sanction` is `SUSPENSION` and whose `respondent_volunteer_id` is that volunteer moves them `SUSPENDED → PROBATION`, recomputes `seniority_score` and advances `version`. Reinstatement stops at `PROBATION` and never returns a volunteer directly to `CERTIFIED`; and the shows left sitting in `UNDER_REVIEW` are **not** restored by the reinstatement — each must clear a performance review on its own merits.

Termination ends the relationship. A substantiated incident whose `sanction` is `TERMINATION` moves the volunteer to `RESIGNED` from `CERTIFIED`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` alike. Every show that volunteer hosts whose `programming_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `HOST_TERMINATED`; each of those cancellations releases that show's occupations whose `status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` with `release_reason` recorded as `PROGRAM_CANCELLED`, and each release recomputes the owning grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct`. Those cascade releases do not then try to send the show back to `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED` — it is already `CANCELLED`, and `CANCELLED` is terminal. `RESIGNED` is terminal: the volunteer's roster entry can no longer be amended, and they may neither vote nor be rostered.

### 3.3. Proposal Authoring and the Programming Committee

A show begins life as a **program proposal**, drafted either by the volunteer who wants to host it or by the `ProgramDirector` on their behalf. The draft names its author in `proposer_volunteer_id`, carries a working `title`, declares the `format_code` the committee will judge it against — one of `MUSIC`, `PUBLIC_AFFAIRS`, `TALK`, `NEWS` or `CULTURAL` — and states the primary broadcast `language_code` as two letters. Any two ASCII letters are accepted and the value is stored exactly as it was supplied, in whichever case it arrived; anything that is not exactly two letters — a three-letter tag, a single letter, a digit, an empty string — answers `ERR_VALIDATION`. The proposer also asks for a `requested_daypart`, one of `MORNING_DRIVE`, `MIDDAY`, `AFTERNOON_DRIVE`, `EVENING` or `OVERNIGHT`, and a `requested_duration_minutes` between 15 and 720 inclusive. The daypart is advisory only: the schedule grid may place the show anywhere. The requested duration is the figure the director will normally reuse as the show's slot length. An optional `diversity_tags` array carries the station's diversity descriptors, such as `LOCAL_ARTIST_FOCUS` or `IMMIGRANT_COMMUNITY`, and may be left empty or omitted entirely, but when it is supplied it must be a JSON array or the answer is `ERR_VALIDATION`. The `proposer_volunteer_id` must name a volunteer on the roster, and one that names nobody answers `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`; no roster standing is required to *draft* a proposal, only to submit one.

A fresh proposal sits on `status` `DRAFT` with `review_round` 1, a null `composite_score`, `decline_majority` false and a null `decided_at`. While it is `DRAFT` — or later, while it is `REVISION_REQUESTED` — its author, and only its author, may revise `title`, `format_code`, `language_code`, `requested_daypart`, `requested_duration_minutes` and `diversity_tags`. A revision attempted by anyone other than the volunteer named in `proposer_volunteer_id` answers `ERR_NOT_PROPOSAL_OWNER`; a revision attempted in any other status answers `ERR_NOT_EDITABLE`.

Submission by the author moves the proposal `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and sets `review_round` to 1. Two conditions bind: `X-User-ID` must be the proposal's `proposer_volunteer_id`, and the author's roster `status` must be exactly `CERTIFIED` — a `PROSPECT`, an `IN_TRAINING` trainee, a volunteer on `PROBATION`, a `SUSPENDED` volunteer and a `RESIGNED` one are all refused with `ERR_PROPOSER_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Submission is reachable only from `DRAFT` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and an attempt from any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — which, being a state check, is reported before the proposer's standing is examined. The author may also withdraw at any point before a decision: `WITHDRAWN` is reachable from `DRAFT`, from `SUBMITTED`, from `IN_COMMITTEE` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, always by the author alone, and always terminal.

Opening committee review is the director's move, taking the proposal `SUBMITTED → IN_COMMITTEE`. It requires the committee to actually be staffed: at least **three** volunteers must hold `committee_seat` true with a roster `status` that is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `RESIGNED`. A thinner committee than that answers `ERR_COMMITTEE_UNDERSTAFFED`.

Each seated member then casts exactly one ballot. A ballot carries the caster's `member_volunteer_id`, three criterion scores — `format_fit_score`, `diversity_score` and `quality_score`, each an integer from 1 through 5 inclusive — a `recommendation` of `APPROVE`, `REVISE` or `DECLINE`, and an optional written `rationale` retained in the committee minutes. The ballot's `proposal_id` comes from the proposal being addressed, and its `review_round` is copied from that proposal's current `review_round` at cast time, freezing the ballot to the round in which it was cast. Five refusals guard the cast, and they are evaluated in the order §2.10 sets out: a caller whose `X-User-ID` names nobody on the roster, or names a volunteer whose `committee_seat` is false, answers `ERR_NOT_COMMITTEE_MEMBER` — and because seating is a property of the caller alone it is settled before the payload is validated and before the proposal is looked up; then a second ballot bearing the same `member_volunteer_id` in the same round on the same proposal answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`; then a `member_volunteer_id` equal to the proposal's `proposer_volunteer_id` answers `ERR_SELF_VOTE_FORBIDDEN`, because the station's segregation-of-duties policy forbids a member from scoring their own show; then a proposal that is not `IN_COMMITTEE` answers `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_IN_REVIEW`; and finally a member whose roster `status` is `SUSPENDED` or `RESIGNED` answers `ERR_VOTER_INELIGIBLE`. The self-vote bar therefore outranks the not-in-review bar, exactly as §2.10 rungs 10 and 11 require. Note also that the seat test and the remaining four tests look at different people: the seat is the caller's, while the duplicate, self-vote and eligibility tests all read the body's `member_volunteer_id`, and the two need not be the same volunteer. A `member_volunteer_id` naming nobody answers `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`. Every accepted ballot advances the parent proposal's `version` by 1 — that is precisely what makes a stale tally detectable.

Tallying is the director's decision, and it is guarded by `expected_proposal_version` as described in §2.8. Only ballots whose `review_round` equals the proposal's current `review_round` are counted; ballots from an earlier round stay on file and are ignored. Before the score is computed, the tally must clear **quorum**. The eligible seat count is the number of volunteers holding `committee_seat` true whose roster `status` is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `RESIGNED` and who are *not* the proposal's author. The required quorum is the larger of 3 and sixty percent of that eligible seat count rounded up to a whole number. When the number of ballots in the current round falls short, the tally is refused with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. Note the surrounding order: because the one-program-per-proposal rule is evaluated at the duplicate-key rung, a proposal that has already produced a show answers `ERR_PROGRAM_ALREADY_EXISTS` before the version guard is even consulted, and the version guard in turn precedes both `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_IN_REVIEW` and `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`.

The `composite_score` is then assembled. The three criterion means — the mean `format_fit_score`, the mean `diversity_score` and the mean `quality_score` across the tallied ballots — are each computed at full precision and are **never** rounded at this stage. Those three means are weighted: the format-fit mean at 0.40, the diversity mean at 0.35 and the quality mean at 0.25, and the three products are added. Two bonuses may then be added on top. A **diversity bonus** of 0.50 applies when the proposal's `format_code` is `PUBLIC_AFFAIRS` or `CULTURAL` *and* the station's currently published public-affairs share is below 8.00 — that share being the `public_affairs_minutes_pct` of the single `ScheduleGrid` whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, or `0.00` when no grid is published at all. A **language bonus** of 0.25 applies when the proposal's `language_code`, compared without regard to letter case, is anything other than `EN` — so a proposal stored as `en` earns no language bonus, just as one stored as `EN` does not. The weighted sum plus whichever bonuses apply is rounded half up to two decimal places, and the result is then capped at `5.00`. Rounding the three means before weighting them instead of after is wrong and will move outcomes across the decision thresholds.

Alongside the score, the tally sets `decline_majority`, which is true exactly when twice the number of tallied ballots whose `recommendation` is `DECLINE` is strictly greater than the total number of tallied ballots — in other words, when a strict majority of the round recommended declining. Both figures, plus `decided_at` stamped with the clock, are written to the proposal, and its `version` advances by 1.

Routing follows from those two figures and from the round number. A `decline_majority` of true declines the proposal outright regardless of how high the composite climbed. Failing that, a `composite_score` of at least `4.00` moves the proposal `IN_COMMITTEE → APPROVED`; a `composite_score` of at least `3.00` but below `4.00` moves it `IN_COMMITTEE → REVISION_REQUESTED` provided the current `review_round` is below 2; and a `composite_score` below `3.00` moves it `IN_COMMITTEE → DECLINED`. The station allows at most **two** review rounds, and that cap is expressed as a third route to decline: a proposal scoring at least `3.00` but below `4.00` in `review_round` 2 is `DECLINED` rather than sent back for a second revision. `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

Re-submission is the revision cycle. An author re-submitting out of `REVISION_REQUESTED` moves the proposal `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, raises `review_round` by 1, clears `composite_score` back to null and `decline_majority` back to false, and advances `version`. `decided_at` is deliberately *not* cleared: it goes on carrying the instant of the first round's decision until the second tally overwrites it. The previous round's ballots remain stored — the minutes are not rewritten — but they are excluded from the next tally, so the committee must vote again. Because the cap is two rounds, a proposal traverses this cycle at most once.

An approval creates the show. One `Program` is written with `programming_status` `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED` and `compliance_standing` `CLEAR`, its `proposal_id` and its `host_volunteer_id` copied from the proposal (the host of record is the proposer), and its `title` and `format_code` copied across as well — the show's format is fixed at creation and is what later drives the grid's diversity quota and the local-artist term of its performance score. The new show starts with `compliance_penalty_points` 0, a null `performance_score`, no `performance_score_method` yet, a null `last_evaluated_at` and a null `cancellation_reason`. The tally nonetheless answers with the **proposal** — HTTP 200 carrying the decided `ProgramProposal` in `data`, on whichever of the four terminal or revision statuses it landed. The show it may have created is not returned by that call and is read from the program collection afterwards.

Proposals are browsable by `proposer_volunteer_id`, `status` and `format_code`; ballots are browsable by `proposal_id`, `member_volunteer_id` and `review_round`, and are readable individually but never amendable. Shows are browsable by `host_volunteer_id`, `proposal_id`, `programming_status`, `compliance_standing` and `format_code`.

### 3.4. Grid Construction, Slot Occupation and Publication

The air schedule is expressed as a **schedule grid** per station season. A `ProgramDirector` opens one with a `season_code` such as `2026-SPRING`, which must be non-empty and unique across grids — a reused code answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` — together with an `effective_from` instant and an `effective_to` instant that must be strictly later than `effective_from`, or the creation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A new grid sits on `status` `DRAFT` with `total_assigned_minutes` 0, `public_affairs_minutes_pct` `0.00` and a null `published_at`. Grids are browsable by `status` and `season_code`.

Placing a show on the grid creates a **slot occupation**. The request supplies `expected_grid_version` as §2.8 requires, the `program_id` of the show to place, a `day_of_week` from 0 through 6 where 0 is Sunday and 6 is Saturday in station-local time, a `start_minute` counting minutes after station-local midnight from 0 through 1425, and a `duration_minutes` from 15 through 720. The occupation's `grid_id` is the grid being addressed. Because a slot may not run past midnight, `start_minute` plus `duration_minutes` may not exceed 1440, and a payload that breaks that arithmetic — or any of the individual ranges — answers `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new occupation lands on `status` `TENTATIVE` with a null `preempted_by_program_id` and a null `release_reason`. The very first occupation created on a `DRAFT` grid moves that grid `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, recomputes its two derived minute figures and advances its `version` by 1. `DRAFT` and `IN_REVIEW` are both mutable; `PUBLISHED` and `SUPERSEDED` are not, and any placement, publication, confirmation, release or reinstatement aimed at a grid in either of those two states is refused with `ERR_GRID_NOT_MUTABLE`.

Windows are half-open minute ranges, and the station keeps a **two-minute station-identification buffer** on each side of every live occupation. Two occupations on the same grid and the same `day_of_week`, both with `status` `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED`, collide when the new window's start is earlier than the existing window's end plus two minutes *and* the existing window's start is earlier than the new window's end plus two minutes. A collision is normally refused with `ERR_SLOT_CONFLICT`. The optional `conflict_policy` field is what changes that: the value that alters behaviour is `SENIORITY_PREEMPT`, and omitting `conflict_policy` — or supplying the default `REJECT` — leaves the collision refusal in force.

Under `SENIORITY_PREEMPT` the station's seniority policy decides who keeps the window. The challenger's host must outrank the incumbent's host by a margin of at least `5.00` seniority points, measured as the challenger host's `seniority_score` minus the incumbent host's `seniority_score`; a smaller margin is refused with `ERR_SENIORITY_MARGIN_UNMET`. A single placement may collide with more than one live occupation, and the rule is all-or-nothing: the margin must be met against **every** colliding incumbent, one incumbent held by a host too close in seniority refuses the whole placement and displaces nobody, and when the margin is met against all of them every one of them is preempted. A challenging show whose host is no longer on the roster, or an incumbent whose is not, is treated as holding a seniority of `0.00` for this comparison. When the margin is met, each colliding incumbent moves from `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` to `PREEMPTED`, its `preempted_by_program_id` is set to the challenging show, the grid's derived minute figures are recomputed and the grid's `version` advances — and the challenger's own occupation is created on `TENTATIVE` as usual. A preemption driven by compliance restriction rather than by seniority leaves `preempted_by_program_id` null; see §3.7.

Placement also respects the size of a broadcast week. A grid's `total_assigned_minutes` may never exceed 10080 — the number of minutes in seven days — and a placement that would push it past that figure is refused with `ERR_WEEK_MINUTES_EXCEEDED`. The figure tested is the grid's current live total plus the new `duration_minutes`, measured **before** any seniority preemption gives minutes back, so a placement under `SENIORITY_PREEMPT` cannot borrow room from the incumbent it is about to displace. The cap sits between the two collision outcomes: it is tested after a plain `REJECT` collision would already have answered `ERR_SLOT_CONFLICT`, but before the seniority margin is weighed, so a `SENIORITY_PREEMPT` placement onto a full week answers `ERR_WEEK_MINUTES_EXCEEDED` rather than `ERR_SENIORITY_MARGIN_UNMET`.

The grid's two derived figures are recomputed on every occupation created, confirmed, released, preempted or reinstated on it. The **live** set is that grid's occupations whose `status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED`; `RELEASED` and `PREEMPTED` occupations are excluded from both figures. `total_assigned_minutes` is the exact sum of `duration_minutes` over the live set. The public-affairs minutes are the sum of `duration_minutes` over the live occupations whose show's `format_code` is `PUBLIC_AFFAIRS` or `NEWS` — those two formats and no others count toward the station's public-affairs obligation. When `total_assigned_minutes` is 0 the `public_affairs_minutes_pct` is `0.00` rather than an attempted division; otherwise it is one hundred multiplied by the public-affairs minutes, divided by `total_assigned_minutes`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The ratio is always taken over exact integer minutes and never over an already-rounded percentage. Sixty public-affairs minutes inside 750 assigned minutes is therefore exactly `8.00`; the same sixty minutes inside 780 assigned minutes is `7.69`. Note the direction this cuts: because releases shrink the denominator, releasing a music occupation raises the public-affairs share without any public-affairs occupation being added.

Confirming a `TENTATIVE` occupation moves it `TENTATIVE → CONFIRMED`. The owning grid must be `DRAFT` or `IN_REVIEW`; the occupying show's `compliance_standing` must not be `RESTRICTED`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`; and the show's `programming_status` must be one of `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED`, `SCHEDULED`, `ON_AIR_ACTIVE`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `PROBATION`. When this is the show's **first** confirmation it moves `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED → SCHEDULED`, advancing that show's `version` by 1. The grid's minute figures are recomputed and the grid's `version` advances. Confirmation carries no version field of its own, because it neither reads nor changes which minutes are free. Although the mutability rule is stated first here, the restricted-show refusal outranks it in the ladder of §2.10, so a confirmation aimed at a restricted show's occupation on an already-published grid answers `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`, not `ERR_GRID_NOT_MUTABLE`. Confirming an occupation that is not `TENTATIVE` — one already confirmed, released or preempted — answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as does confirming for a show whose `programming_status` is `CANCELLED`.

Releasing an occupation moves it from `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` to `RELEASED`, recording the supplied `release_reason` and recomputing the grid's minute figures and `version`. When the release leaves its show with **zero** occupations in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED`, that show moves back to `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED`. That count is taken exactly as publication takes it — occupations sitting on a `SUPERSEDED` grid are not counted as live, so a show whose only remaining occupation is stranded on a superseded grid still falls back to `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED`. The fallback applies from whatever `programming_status` the show held, `SCHEDULED`, `ON_AIR_ACTIVE`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `PROBATION` alike, and is skipped only for a show that is already `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED` or is `CANCELLED`. Releasing an occupation that is neither `TENTATIVE` nor `CONFIRMED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and `RELEASED` is terminal for an occupation. Release, like confirmation, carries no version field: giving minutes back can never conflict with another writer. A release also arrives as a cascade — from a show's cancellation, from a host's termination, and from a grid being superseded — and in each of those cases the `release_reason` is set by the cascade rather than by a client.

Reinstatement is the compensating action for preemption, moving an occupation `PREEMPTED → CONFIRMED`. It requires the owning grid to be `DRAFT` or `IN_REVIEW`, the show's `compliance_standing` to be anything other than `RESTRICTED`, and the window to still be free: if any other occupation on the same grid and `day_of_week` in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` now collides with it under the same two-minute buffer rule, the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CONFLICT`. A successful reinstatement clears `preempted_by_program_id` back to null and returns the grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct` to their pre-preemption values. It touches nothing on the show itself: a show sitting in `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED` because the preemption had emptied its live set stays in `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED`, and a director must confirm the show back onto the air through the ordinary route. An occupation that is not `PREEMPTED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Occupations are browsable by `grid_id`, `program_id`, `preempted_by_program_id`, `day_of_week` and `status`.

Publication is the act that puts the grid on air, moving it `IN_REVIEW → PUBLISHED`. It is guarded by `expected_grid_version`. Beyond the version guard and the mutability rule, four conditions apply, and §2.10 fixes the order in which they are reported: the grid must have some assigned airtime at all — a `total_assigned_minutes` of 0 answers `ERR_GRID_EMPTY`; no occupation on the grid may still be `TENTATIVE`, or the answer is `ERR_TENTATIVE_SLOTS_PRESENT`; the grid's `public_affairs_minutes_pct` must be at least `8.00`, or the answer is `ERR_DIVERSITY_QUOTA_UNMET`; and `total_assigned_minutes` must not exceed 10080. A grid that is still `DRAFT` when publication is attempted answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A successful publication stamps `published_at` with the clock and fires a four-part cascade. Every show holding a `CONFIRMED` occupation on the newly published grid moves `SCHEDULED → ON_AIR_ACTIVE`, each advancing its own `version`. Any other grid whose `status` was `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`. Every `CONFIRMED` occupation on that superseded grid moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` recorded as `GRID_SUPERSEDED`, and the superseded grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct` are recomputed and its `version` advanced. Finally, every show left with zero occupations in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` across all grids that are not `SUPERSEDED` moves back to `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED`. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a grid.

### 3.5. Airings, Playlist Logs and Royalty Reporting

An **airing** is one broadcast instance of a show against one of its recurring grid occupations. The director schedules it by naming the `program_id`, the `slot_assignment_id` of the occupation being instantiated, the `board_operator_volunteer_id` who will run the board, and the exact `scheduled_start_utc` instant. All four are required at creation — an airing may not be opened without an operator of record, and an absent or null `board_operator_volunteer_id` answers `ERR_VALIDATION` — and all three identifiers are resolved before anything else is judged, answering `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND` in that order. Two structural checks then bind the airing to its occupation. The occupation must actually belong to the named show: an occupation whose own `program_id` differs from the request's `program_id` is refused with `ERR_SLOT_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. And the instant must line up with the occupation's recurring position: the weekday of `scheduled_start_utc` must equal the occupation's `day_of_week` — counted on the same convention the grid uses, 0 for Sunday through 6 for Saturday — and its minute-of-day, its hour times sixty plus its minute, must equal the occupation's `start_minute` exactly; seconds and milliseconds in the instant play no part in that comparison. A mismatch on either is refused with `ERR_EPISODE_SLOT_MISMATCH`. A show whose `compliance_standing` is `RESTRICTED` may not be given new airings at all, and answers `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`.

The board operator must be qualified at the moment of scheduling: the named volunteer must hold a certification record of `cert_type` `BOARD_OPERATION` whose `status` is `PASSED` and whose `expires_on` is strictly after the current logical clock, and their roster `status` must be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `RESIGNED`. Any of those failures answers `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. A new airing lands on `air_status` `SCHEDULED` and `log_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, with `needs_substitute` false, `featured_performance_count` and `local_featured_count` both 0, and `provisional_royalty_cents`, `final_royalty_cents` and `log_due_at` all null. A `board_operator_volunteer_id` of null means the airing has no operator of record and still needs a substitute — that is the state the certification-expiry sweep leaves behind when it strips an operator whose ticket has lapsed and sets `needs_substitute` true.

Recording what happened on air is the director's move, supplying the resulting `air_status` and, for a substitute, a `substitute_board_operator_volunteer_id`. Three outcomes are reachable from `SCHEDULED`. `substitute_board_operator_volunteer_id` is optional in general but mandatory when the `air_status` is `SUBSTITUTE_AIRED`, and omitting it there answers `ERR_VALIDATION`; supplied against any other outcome it must still name a volunteer on the roster, answering `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND` if it names nobody, and is then ignored. Two refusals guard all three outcomes alike, in this order: a show whose `compliance_standing` is `RESTRICTED` answers `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` — including for a `MISSED` result, because the station will not go on writing air history for a restricted show — and an airing whose `air_status` is not `SCHEDULED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

An `air_status` of `AIRED` requires the airing's `board_operator_volunteer_id` to be non-null, that operator to still hold a `PASSED` `BOARD_OPERATION` certification whose `expires_on` is after the clock, that operator's roster `status` to be neither `SUSPENDED` nor `RESIGNED` — any of which failing answers `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and the show's `compliance_standing` not to be `RESTRICTED`. The log obligation then follows the show's format: when the show's `format_code` is `MUSIC` the `log_status` moves to `PENDING` and `log_due_at` is stamped at `scheduled_start_utc` plus 72 hours; for every other format the `log_status` stays `NOT_REQUIRED` and no deadline is stamped. Note that the deadline is measured from the scheduled start, not from the moment the outcome was recorded, so recording an airing late does not buy the operator more time.

An `air_status` of `SUBSTITUTE_AIRED` behaves identically on the log side but reassigns the board: the supplied `substitute_board_operator_volunteer_id` must itself clear the identical operator test — a `PASSED`, unexpired `BOARD_OPERATION` certification *and* a roster `status` that is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `RESIGNED`, any failure answering `ERR_OPERATOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` — the airing's `board_operator_volunteer_id` is overwritten with the substitute, and `needs_substitute` is set back to false. The substitute becomes the operator of record, which means the substitute — not the originally rostered volunteer — is the person entitled to file the playlist log.

An `air_status` of `MISSED` records that the show did not go out. It leaves `log_status` at `NOT_REQUIRED`, because there is nothing to log, and it asks nothing of the operator — no certification test is applied on this path, so an airing whose operator has since lapsed or been stripped to null can still be marked `MISSED` — but it counts against the show's reliability in §3.8. A fourth outcome, `CANCELLED`, is never client-supplied: it arrives only as a cascade from a show's cancellation or from a show entering `compliance_standing` `RESTRICTED`. `AIRED`, `SUBSTITUTE_AIRED`, `MISSED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal on the airing dimension.

The **playlist log** is the second, independent state dimension, and it moves without regard to which air outcome was recorded. While the log is `PENDING`, the board operator of record files one **playlist entry** per spin. An entry carries a `sequence_number` of at least 1, with no upper bound and no requirement that the numbers be contiguous or filed in order, which must be unique within its airing — a reused number answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, and the check is scoped to that airing alone, so two airings may each carry a spin numbered 1 — plus the `artist`, the `title`, a `duration_seconds` of at least 1 and again unbounded above, and a `play_source` of `LIBRARY_CD`, `LIBRARY_VINYL`, `DIGITAL`, `LIVE_PERFORMANCE` or `PRODUCTION_MUSIC`. The optional `album` and `label` complete the citation, `is_local_artist` marks a local act and defaults to false, and `is_new_release` marks a new release and also defaults to false. New releases are reported to the station's programming reports but have no effect on money: `is_new_release` never enters a royalty figure. Entries are immutable once written — they carry no `version` and no `updated_at`, and there is no way to amend or delete one. Only the volunteer whose id matches the airing's `board_operator_volunteer_id` may file entries, and anyone else — including a caller filing against an airing whose operator has been stripped to null — is refused with `ERR_NOT_BOARD_OPERATOR`, a refusal settled before the payload is validated; filing against a log that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_LOG_NOT_PENDING`. Entries are browsable by `episode_id`, `royalty_class` and `is_local_artist`; airings themselves are browsable by `program_id`, `slot_assignment_id`, `board_operator_volunteer_id`, `air_status`, `log_status` and `needs_substitute`.

Each entry's `royalty_class` is derived at write time and is never supplied. An entry is `FEATURED` when its `play_source` is `LIBRARY_CD`, `LIBRARY_VINYL` or `DIGITAL` **and** its `duration_seconds` is at least 30 — the station's threshold for a featured performance. An entry whose `play_source` is `PRODUCTION_MUSIC` is `EXEMPT`, because production library music is licensed separately. Everything else is `INCIDENTAL`: a `LIVE_PERFORMANCE`, and any library or digital spin shorter than 30 seconds. Only `FEATURED` entries carry money, so a 29-second digital spin is `INCIDENTAL` and priced at nothing while a 30-second one is `FEATURED` and priced in full.

Submitting the log moves it `PENDING → SUBMITTED`, and only the board operator of record may do it. A `MUSIC` show whose airing has zero playlist entries cannot submit, and answers `ERR_PLAYLIST_EMPTY`; a non-`MUSIC` show is never in `PENDING` in the first place. Submission does three things: it recomputes `featured_performance_count` as the number of the airing's entries whose `royalty_class` is `FEATURED`; it recomputes `local_featured_count` as the number of those `FEATURED` entries whose `is_local_artist` is true — note that a local artist logged on an `INCIDENTAL` or `EXEMPT` spin does not count; and it writes `provisional_royalty_cents` from the **SURVEY** variant of the royalty calculation.

The compliance officer then rules on the submitted log with a `decision` of `CERTIFY` or `RETURN`, optionally attaching a `review_note` — which is accepted, and must be a string when present, but is kept nowhere on the airing and appears in no response. A log that is not `SUBMITTED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `CERTIFY` moves the log `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` and writes `final_royalty_cents` from the **CENSUS** variant, leaving `provisional_royalty_cents` standing as the submission left it. `RETURN` moves it `SUBMITTED → PENDING` and clears `provisional_royalty_cents` back to null, reopening the log for correction; `featured_performance_count` and `local_featured_count` are *not* cleared by a return — they keep the figures the last submission wrote until the next submission overwrites them — and `final_royalty_cents` stays null; the operator may add or fix entries and submit again, so the log-correction cycle `PENDING → SUBMITTED → PENDING` may run any number of times. Each pass rewrites `provisional_royalty_cents` from whatever the entries then say. The daily sweep supplies the one remaining move: a log still `PENDING` when the clock reaches or passes its `log_due_at` goes `PENDING → DELINQUENT`.

The two royalty variants price the same featured set differently, and the difference is entirely a matter of where the rounding happens. Both operate on the airing's entries whose `royalty_class` is `FEATURED`, and both use the station's rate of 65 cents per minute of featured performance. When that featured set is empty — a log made entirely of short spins, live performances and production music — both variants yield the integer `0` rather than null, so a certified log of nothing but exempt spins reads `provisional_royalty_cents` 0 and `final_royalty_cents` 0.

The **SURVEY** variant, written to `provisional_royalty_cents` on submission, is the sampled estimate the station files during a survey period. It sums the `duration_seconds` of every featured entry into a single total, divides that total by 60 to get minutes, multiplies by 65 cents, multiplies the result by the survey uplift factor of 1.10, and only then rounds half up to a whole cent. The entire chain is carried at exact precision with **one** rounding at the very end.

The **CENSUS** variant, written to `final_royalty_cents` on certification, is the exhaustive per-spin accounting. Each featured spin is priced individually — its own `duration_seconds` divided by 60, multiplied by 65 cents, rounded half up to a whole cent — and the already-rounded per-spin cents are then added together. There is no uplift factor in the census variant. Because each spin is rounded before summation, the census figure can differ from the survey figure by more than the 10 percent uplift alone would explain, and implementing either variant with the other's rounding placement produces figures that are wrong by a few cents on nearly every realistic log. Both figures are stored as whole cents in an integer field, never as a decimal or a string.

### 3.6. Underwriting Copy, Political Content and the Public File

The station's development staff draft **underwriting spots** — the sponsor acknowledgements, political spots and public service announcements that run between shows. A draft names the `underwriter_name` of record, a `spot_class` of `UNDERWRITING`, `POLITICAL_CANDIDATE`, `ISSUE_ADVOCACY` or `PSA`, the proposed `copy_text` as written, and a flight window given as `flight_start` and a `flight_end` that must be strictly after it, or the draft is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. An `assigned_program_id` names the show the spot will run inside; it is **required** for `POLITICAL_CANDIDATE` and for `ISSUE_ADVOCACY`, because a political spot's public-file entry must name the show that carried it, and it is optional for `UNDERWRITING` and `PSA`. That requirement is not enforced at drafting, though — a `POLITICAL_CANDIDATE` or `ISSUE_ADVOCACY` spot may be drafted and amended with no show attached, and the requirement is applied at the moment it is submitted for review, where a missing `assigned_program_id` answers `ERR_VALIDATION`. Whenever an `assigned_program_id` *is* supplied, at drafting or in an amendment, it must name a show, and one that names nothing answers `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. A new spot sits on `status` `DRAFT` with `noncommercial_risk_score` 0, an empty `compliance_flags` array, a null `approved_copy_text`, a null `reviewed_by_user_id`, `public_file_status` `NOT_REQUIRED` and null `public_file_due_at`, `public_file_posted_at` and `public_file_reference`. Development staff may amend a `DRAFT` spot's `copy_text`, `assigned_program_id`, `flight_start` and `flight_end` — the `underwriter_name` is fixed at drafting, because it is the identity the sponsor-identification screen tests against; a spot in any other status answers `ERR_NOT_EDITABLE`. An amendment names only the fields it changes, and a field it omits — or sends as null — is left exactly as it stood, so there is no way to clear an `assigned_program_id` back to null once it has been set. An amendment that moves only one end of the flight window is checked against the stored value of the other, and a combination that leaves `flight_end` at or before `flight_start` answers `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` before the draft-only restriction is considered. Submitting for review moves the spot `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, and it re-checks the stored flight window as well: a spot whose window no longer holds answers `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, while a spot in any status other than `DRAFT` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, reported after both of those. Spots are browsable by `status`, `spot_class`, `assigned_program_id` and `public_file_status`.

Review is the compliance officer's move, and it turns on a **noncommercial risk score** computed from the copy. Scoring begins by normalizing the text: fold it to lowercase, collapse every run of whitespace to a single space, and trim leading and trailing whitespace. Four flags are then tested against the normalized copy, each contributing its weight at most once no matter how many of its phrases appear.

`CALL_TO_ACTION` contributes 40 points when the normalized copy contains any of the phrases "call now", "act now", "visit us today", "stop by today", "shop now" or "limited time offer" — a call to action is the clearest breach of noncommercial rules and is weighted accordingly. `PRICE_INFO` contributes 35 points when the copy states a price: either it contains a currency amount, meaning a dollar sign immediately followed by a digit, or it contains "% off", "percent off" or "save $". `COMPARATIVE_CLAIM` contributes 25 points when the copy contains "best", "lowest prices", "number one" or "unbeatable". `MISSING_SPONSOR_ID` contributes 10 points when the normalized copy does **not** contain the spot's `underwriter_name` normalized by the identical rule — the sponsor must be identifiable in the announcement itself, and comparing an unnormalized name against normalized copy is the classic way to raise this flag spuriously.

The `noncommercial_risk_score` is the sum of the weights of the flags that matched, capped at 100, and `compliance_flags` records the names of exactly the flags that matched, sorted in ascending alphabetical order. One class bypasses the whole screen: a `spot_class` of `POLITICAL_CANDIDATE` is recorded with `noncommercial_risk_score` 0 and an empty `compliance_flags` array regardless of what its copy says, because federal law forbids the station from censoring a qualified candidate's own words. That exemption is why the review of a political-candidate spot has no editing path at all.

Review is available only from `UNDER_REVIEW`; a spot in any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and that refusal is settled before the copy is screened at all. When an `edited_copy_text` is supplied it is screened first, and the two edit refusals below are raised on the strength of that screen alone, *before* the original copy's own band is even established — so an edit is judged whatever the original scored, and `ERR_EDITED_COPY_STILL_NONCOMPLIANT` outranks `ERR_POLITICAL_COPY_UNCENSORABLE` when both would apply.

Review resolves into one of three statuses. A `noncommercial_risk_score` below 25 moves the spot `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` with the `copy_text` accepted as-is and `approved_copy_text` set to exactly that `copy_text`; every `POLITICAL_CANDIDATE` spot, whose score is forced to 0, lands here. An `edited_copy_text` supplied against copy that already scores below 25 is screened like any other — and so can still be refused if the rewrite is itself unclean — but once it passes it is simply discarded: the spot lands on `APPROVED`, not `APPROVED_WITH_EDITS`, and `approved_copy_text` holds the original. A score of at least 60 moves the spot `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`; `approved_copy_text` stays null and `public_file_status` stays `NOT_REQUIRED`, and `REJECTED` is terminal — a rejected spot is redrafted as a new spot, never revived. Copy this far gone is beyond rescue, so an `edited_copy_text` offered against it is screened and then discarded exactly as in the below-25 case: the spot is rejected regardless of how clean the rewrite was. A score of at least 25 but below 60 is the editable band: the officer may supply an `edited_copy_text`, which is screened by the identical rule, and when that rewrite itself scores below 25 the spot moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED_WITH_EDITS` with `approved_copy_text` set to the `edited_copy_text`. A rewrite that still scores 25 or more is refused with `ERR_EDITED_COPY_STILL_NONCOMPLIANT`, and a score in the editable band with no `edited_copy_text` supplied at all moves the spot to `REJECTED`. Crucially, the stored `noncommercial_risk_score` and `compliance_flags` always describe the **original** `copy_text`; the rewrite's own score is used only to decide whether the rewrite is acceptable and never overwrites the recorded figures, because the compliance record must preserve what the sponsor originally asked to say. Whichever way the review resolves, the officer's `X-User-ID` is stored in `reviewed_by_user_id`, which is what later makes the segregation-of-duties check in §3.7 possible. An attempt to edit political-candidate copy — that is, a review of a `POLITICAL_CANDIDATE` spot carrying any `edited_copy_text` at all — is refused with `ERR_POLITICAL_COPY_UNCENSORABLE`, and because the rewrite's own cleanliness is tested first, an unclean rewrite of candidate copy answers `ERR_EDITED_COPY_STILL_NONCOMPLIANT` instead.

Clearance is what creates the **public file** obligation. When a spot whose `spot_class` is `POLITICAL_CANDIDATE` or `ISSUE_ADVOCACY` reaches `APPROVED` **or** `APPROVED_WITH_EDITS`, its `public_file_status` moves to `REQUIRED` and its `public_file_due_at` is stamped at the current logical clock plus 7 days — the station's political file window. Both cleared statuses carry the obligation, because what the political file records is that the spot ran, not whose words it ran in; an `ISSUE_ADVOCACY` spot rescued by an edit therefore owes a filing exactly as an unedited one does. The deadline is measured from the clock at the moment of clearance, not from the flight window. Spots of class `UNDERWRITING` and `PSA` incur no obligation and keep `public_file_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`.

Posting the entry is also the compliance officer's move, supplying the `public_file_reference` that identifies the filed document. A posting recorded strictly before `public_file_due_at` moves the obligation `REQUIRED → POSTED` and stamps `public_file_posted_at` with the clock. A posting recorded when the clock has already reached or passed `public_file_due_at` — the deadline has elapsed but the daily sweep has not yet been run, so the obligation is still nominally `REQUIRED` — lands on `LATE_POSTED` rather than `POSTED`, because lateness is a fact about the clock and not about whether the sweep has noticed. The `public_file_reference` is required and must be non-empty. A spot that owes no outstanding entry — one whose `public_file_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, or already `POSTED`, or already `LATE_POSTED` — answers `ERR_PUBLIC_FILE_NOT_REQUIRED`.

Missing the window is where the station's most consequential cascade begins. When the daily sweep finds a spot whose `public_file_status` is still `REQUIRED` at or after its `public_file_due_at`, the obligation moves `REQUIRED → MISSING` and the sweep raises a compliance incident on the station's own initiative. That incident is created with `category` `PUBLIC_FILE`, `severity` `MAJOR`, `status` `SUBSTANTIATED` from the outset, `penalty_points` 3, its `program_id` set to the spot's `assigned_program_id`, its `underwriting_spot_id` set to the spot, and its `occurred_at` set to the spot's `public_file_due_at` — the moment the obligation was breached, not the moment the sweep noticed. The rest of that incident's fields are exactly what a station-raised record implies: `sanction` `NONE` and `respondent_volunteer_id` null, because no individual is charged; `episode_id` null; `safe_harbor_violation` false, since the flag only ever attaches to indecency; `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `remediation_note` null, since no officer ruled on it; and a server-written `complainant_note` reading exactly `Public file posting deadline elapsed without a filing.`. Its `penalty_points` are 3, which is what the `PUBLIC_FILE`/`MAJOR` pairing prices at anyway, and a spot with no `assigned_program_id` still raises the incident — it simply carries a null `program_id` and charges nobody. Because that incident is born substantiated, its 3 points are charged to the carrying show immediately, which moves that show's `compliance_standing` to `WATCH`, or to `RESTRICTED` when the running total reaches 6. A show that reaches `RESTRICTED` then loses its air position: every occupation of that show in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `PREEMPTED`, the owning grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct` are recomputed and its `version` advanced, and every `SCHEDULED` airing of that show moves to `CANCELLED`. One missed filing deadline can therefore take a show off the air without any human having filed a complaint.

Posting after the deadline is still worth doing but does not undo the damage. A posting recorded once the obligation is already `MISSING` moves it `MISSING → LATE_POSTED` and stamps `public_file_posted_at`. It does **not** remediate the incident, does not refund the 3 points and does not restore the preempted occupations; remediating the incident is a separate, explicit act described in §3.7, and restoring a preempted occupation requires an explicit reinstatement.

One further move can take a cleared spot off the air: a substantiated compliance incident that names the spot moves it from `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_EDITS` to `SUSPENDED`.

### 3.7. Compliance Incidents, Penalty Points and Regulator-Facing Standing

A **compliance incident** is the station's record of a complaint. It may be filed by the compliance officer or by the program director, and it names a `category` of `INDECENCY`, `POLITICAL_CONTENT`, `PUBLIC_FILE`, `SPONSOR_ID` or `VOLUNTEER_CONFLICT`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, the `occurred_at` instant of the conduct complained of, and a `complainant_note` recording what was alleged.

The incident then points at whatever is at issue, and which pointers are mandatory depends on the category. A `program_id` is required for every category **except** `VOLUNTEER_CONFLICT`, because every other category is an on-air matter chargeable to a show. An `episode_id` is additionally required for `INDECENCY`, because indecency is always alleged against a specific airing. An `underwriting_spot_id` is additionally required for `SPONSOR_ID` and for `PUBLIC_FILE`. A `respondent_volunteer_id` is required for `VOLUNTEER_CONFLICT`, which is the one category directed at a person rather than at a show. Any of these omissions is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_TARGET_REQUIRED`. An optional `sanction` of `NONE`, `WARNING`, `SUSPENSION` or `TERMINATION` declares in advance what will be done to the respondent if the complaint is substantiated, and it defaults to `NONE`; a `sanction` other than `NONE` with no `respondent_volunteer_id` set is not permitted, since there would be nobody to sanction, and that omission is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_TARGET_REQUIRED` as well — reported last of the five target checks, after the category-driven ones. Each of the four pointers, when supplied, must name a real row, and the four are resolved before any target check runs, so a `VOLUNTEER_CONFLICT` complaint naming a nonexistent respondent answers `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_INCIDENT_TARGET_REQUIRED`. A new incident sits on `status` `OPEN` with a null `adjudicated_by_user_id` and a null `remediation_note`. Incidents are browsable by `program_id`, `episode_id`, `underwriting_spot_id`, `respondent_volunteer_id`, `category`, `severity` and `status`.

**Penalty points are priced at creation and then frozen.** The base price comes from the pair of `category` and `severity`. For `INDECENCY` the base is 2 points at `MINOR`, 5 at `MAJOR` and 9 at `CRITICAL`. For `POLITICAL_CONTENT` it is 1, 3 and 6. For `PUBLIC_FILE` it is 1, 3 and 5. For `SPONSOR_ID` it is 1, 2 and 4, and for `VOLUNTEER_CONFLICT` it is likewise 1, 2 and 4.

One multiplier applies. The `safe_harbor_violation` flag is true when — and only when — the `category` is `INDECENCY` and the station-local hour of `occurred_at` falls in the window from 06:00 up to but not including 22:00. That window is half-open at the top: conduct at 21:59 is inside the window and conduct at exactly 22:00 is outside it, and conduct at exactly 06:00 is inside it. When `safe_harbor_violation` is true the base points are **doubled**, because indecency broadcast during the hours when children may be listening is the station's gravest exposure. A `CRITICAL` indecency at 20:00 is therefore priced at 18 points, while the same conduct at 23:00 is priced at 9 and carries `safe_harbor_violation` false. No other category is ever a safe-harbour violation, no matter what hour it occurred at.

The resulting `penalty_points` are written to the incident at creation and never recalculated. Amending the incident is impossible, and a later change to the pricing tables would not disturb a point figure already recorded. Filing an incident charges nothing: an `OPEN` incident leaves the show's `compliance_penalty_points` and `compliance_standing` completely untouched.

**Adjudication** is where the points bite. The compliance officer rules with a `finding` of `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNFOUNDED`, and either way the officer's `X-User-ID` is written to `adjudicated_by_user_id`. Before either ruling is accepted, the station's segregation-of-duties rule is enforced: when the incident names an `underwriting_spot_id`, the adjudicating officer's `X-User-ID` may not equal that spot's `reviewed_by_user_id`, because the officer who cleared a piece of copy may not sit in judgement on a complaint about that same copy. A violation answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and §2.10 places that refusal **above** the state check — an officer who cleared the copy is told they are conflicted even when the incident has already been adjudicated and is no longer `OPEN`. The rule only bites where there is something to conflict with: a spot whose `reviewed_by_user_id` is still null, and an incident that names no `underwriting_spot_id` at all, both pass the check freely.

An `UNFOUNDED` finding moves the incident `OPEN → UNFOUNDED`, charges nothing at all and is terminal. A `SUBSTANTIATED` finding moves the incident `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED` and fires up to three consequences at once.

First, the incident's frozen `penalty_points` are added to the named show's `compliance_penalty_points`, and the show's `compliance_standing` is recomputed from the new running total: `CLEAR` at exactly 0 points, `WATCH` from 1 through 5 points, and `RESTRICTED` at 6 points or more. A `CLEAR` show can go straight to `RESTRICTED` when a single incident's points reach 6 on their own — a `CRITICAL` safe-harbour indecency does that with 18 points from a standing start. `WATCH` is a warning state only: a show on `WATCH` keeps airing, keeps its occupations and can still be given new airings, and the standing dimension is entirely independent of `programming_status`. `RESTRICTED` is different, and it fires the cascade already described: every occupation of that show in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `PREEMPTED` — with `preempted_by_program_id` left null, because no rival show displaced it — each preemption recomputes the owning grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct` and advances that grid's `version`, and every airing of that show whose `air_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`.

Second, when the incident names an `underwriting_spot_id` and that spot's `status` is `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_EDITS`, the spot moves to `SUSPENDED` and stops running.

Third, the declared `sanction` is applied to the respondent. `NONE` does nothing. `WARNING` raises the respondent's `strike_count` by 1 and, when that brings them to two strikes, moves them `CERTIFIED → PROBATION`. `SUSPENSION` moves a respondent whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` or `PROBATION` to `SUSPENDED`, which in turn takes every show they host whose `programming_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` to `UNDER_REVIEW` and strips the suspended volunteer off those shows' `SCHEDULED` airings. `TERMINATION` moves a respondent whose `status` is `CERTIFIED`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` to `RESIGNED`, which cancels every show they host that is not already `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `HOST_TERMINATED` and releases those shows' live occupations. Both of those cascades are set out in full in §3.2.

**Remediation** is the exact compensating move, available only from `SUBSTANTIATED`, and it requires a `remediation_note` describing the corrective action taken. It moves the incident `SUBSTANTIATED → REMEDIATED`, subtracts exactly the incident's `penalty_points` from the show's `compliance_penalty_points` and recomputes `compliance_standing` from the new total using the same thresholds — so a show walks `RESTRICTED → WATCH` when its remaining total is between 1 and 5, and `RESTRICTED → CLEAR` or `WATCH → CLEAR` when the total returns to exactly 0. Because the charge and the refund are the same frozen figure, charging and then remediating an incident always returns the show to precisely the total it had before, whatever else has happened in between. When the incident carried a `SUSPENSION`, remediation also moves the respondent `SUSPENDED → PROBATION` — but only when they are actually still `SUSPENDED`; a respondent who has since resigned, or who was never suspended because they were not `CERTIFIED` or `PROBATION` when the sanction landed, keeps the standing they have. Remediation reverses no other sanction: the strike a `WARNING` added stays on the respondent's `strike_count`, and a `TERMINATION` is never undone.

Two things remediation deliberately does **not** do. Occupations that were moved to `PREEMPTED` by the restriction stay preempted: recovering the air position requires the director to explicitly reinstate each one, and if the window has since been given to another show the reinstatement will be refused with `ERR_SLOT_CONFLICT`. And airings that were `CANCELLED` by the restriction stay cancelled; new airings must be scheduled. `REMEDIATED` and `UNFOUNDED` are both terminal.

### 3.8. Program Performance Review, Probation and Cancellation

The station reviews its shows on the numbers. A **performance review** is run by the program director and is permitted only while the show's `programming_status` is `ON_AIR_ACTIVE`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `PROBATION`; a show that is `APPROVED_UNSCHEDULED`, merely `SCHEDULED` or already `CANCELLED` cannot be reviewed and answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The request carries one field, `review_window`, selecting which of the two scoring variants to apply, and the resulting figure is written to `performance_score`, the variant used is recorded in `performance_score_method`, and `last_evaluated_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Those three are written on every accepted review whether or not the score moves the show anywhere, and because a hosted show's score has been rewritten the host's `seniority_score` is recomputed in the same request.

The **window** is what the two variants disagree about. `TRAILING_SIX` looks at the six most recent airings of the show whose `scheduled_start_utc` is at or before the current clock, taken in descending order of `scheduled_start_utc` and excluding any airing whose `air_status` is `CANCELLED`. Where two airings share the same `scheduled_start_utc` the one written later is treated as the more recent, so it is the one that survives the cut to six. `QUARTER_TO_DATE` looks at every airing of the show from the first instant of the calendar quarter containing the current clock through the clock itself, again excluding `CANCELLED` airings, with no limit on how many airings enter the window. Airings whose `scheduled_start_utc` is in the future are excluded from **both** variants, because a show is not judged on shows it has not yet had the chance to broadcast, and `CANCELLED` airings never enter either window — a cancellation is not a failure attributable to the show. The consequence is that the two variants can disagree sharply: an older `MISSED` airing inside the current quarter drags `QUARTER_TO_DATE` down while leaving a `TRAILING_SIX` window that no longer reaches back that far entirely untouched.

Within the chosen window three ratios are computed. **Reliability** compares airings that happened against airings that should have: the aired count is the number of airings whose `air_status` is `AIRED` or `SUBSTITUTE_AIRED` — a substitute counts as fully aired, because the station got its programme — and the missed count is the number whose `air_status` is `MISSED`. When those two counts sum to zero the reliability is taken as 1.0 rather than as an undefined division; otherwise it is the aired count divided by the sum of the aired and missed counts. **Log compliance** compares logs filed against logs owed: the logged count is the number of airings whose `log_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `CERTIFIED`, and the required count is the number whose `log_status` is `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED` or `DELINQUENT`. Airings whose `log_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` are in neither count, so a talk show that owes no logs at all has a required count of zero and its log compliance is taken as 1.0. Otherwise log compliance is the logged count divided by the required count. Note that a `DELINQUENT` log is in the denominator but not the numerator, so a missed log deadline is a straight loss. **Local share** compares local featured spins against all featured spins: the featured total is the sum of `featured_performance_count` across the window and the local total is the sum of `local_featured_count`. When the featured total is zero the local share is taken as 1.0 rather than as a division by zero; otherwise it is the local total divided by the featured total.

The three ratios are weighted and scaled to a hundred-point base: reliability at 0.55, log compliance at 0.30 and local share at 0.15, with the weighted sum multiplied by 100. The show's compliance record is then charged against that base at 5 points of score per point of `compliance_penalty_points`, and the result is clamped to the range 0 through 100 before being rounded half up to two decimal places. The clamp is what stops a heavily penalised show from producing a negative score: a show carrying 20 penalty points and no aired airings clamps at `0.00`, not at a negative figure. The rounding happens once, at the very end; the three ratios are carried at full precision throughout.

The score routes the show. A score below `60.00` — the station's underperformance threshold — moves an `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` show to `UNDER_REVIEW`. A score below `45.00` moves an `UNDER_REVIEW` show on to `PROBATION`. A score of at least `60.00` restores a show from `UNDER_REVIEW` or from `PROBATION` back to `ON_AIR_ACTIVE`, but only when the host is fit to be on air: the host's roster `status` must be `CERTIFIED` or `PROBATION`, so a show whose host is `SUSPENDED` or `RESIGNED` cannot be restored by a good score alone. That is the asymmetry §2.9 records — a suspended host's shows drop to `UNDER_REVIEW` automatically but never climb back automatically, even after the host is reinstated.

The daily sweep runs the same calculation unattended for every `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` show, always using the `TRAILING_SIX` variant, writing `performance_score`, `performance_score_method` and `last_evaluated_at`, recomputing each host's `seniority_score` just as an attended review does, and moving any show whose recomputed score is below `60.00` to `UNDER_REVIEW`. A show demoted this way is still named in the sweep's `reviewed_program_ids`. The sweep never moves a show to `PROBATION` and never restores one to `ON_AIR_ACTIVE`; those routes require a director's explicit review. One route deliberately does not touch the score at all: a show that drops to `UNDER_REVIEW` because its host was suspended keeps whatever `performance_score` it already had, because the host's misconduct is not evidence about the show's numbers.

**Cancellation** is the end of the line, available only from `UNDER_REVIEW` or `PROBATION` and never directly from `ON_AIR_ACTIVE` — a show must first be found wanting before it can be cancelled. The request carries `expected_program_version`, as §2.8 requires, together with a `cancellation_reason` recording why. A successful cancellation moves the show to `CANCELLED`, records the reason, releases every occupation of that show in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `PROGRAM_CANCELLED`, recomputes the owning grid's `total_assigned_minutes` and `public_affairs_minutes_pct` and advances that grid's `version`, and moves every airing of the show whose `air_status` is `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`. `CANCELLED` is terminal on the programming dimension: a cancelled show is never revived, and putting the same idea back on the air means a fresh proposal through the committee.

### 3.9. Membership Drives, Phone Banks and Premium Fulfilment

The station funds itself with **membership drives**. The development coordinator plans one with a public `name`, a `goal_cents` of at least 1, a `start_at` and an `end_at` strictly after it — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` — and a `phone_line_count` of at least 1 recording how many physical phone lines the station actually has. A new drive sits on `status` `PLANNED` with all five money aggregates at 0 and `attainment_pct` at `0.00`. Activation moves it `PLANNED → ACTIVE`, which is what makes phone-bank shifts and pledges writable: rostering a shift or taking a pledge against a drive that is `PLANNED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_DRIVE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Activation is reachable only from `PLANNED` and closure only from `ACTIVE`; either transition attempted from any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and for closure that state check is reported ahead of the outstanding-pledge check, so a second attempt to close an already-`CLOSED` drive answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_PLEDGES_PENDING`. Drives are browsable by `status`.

A **phone-bank shift** rosters one volunteer on the phones for one window, naming the `drive_id` being staffed, the `volunteer_id` working it, and a `start_at` and `end_at` strictly after it. Four checks bind the roster, in the order §2.10 sets out. A volunteer whose roster `status` is `SUSPENDED` or `RESIGNED` may not be rostered at all and answers `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The shift must sit inside the drive's own window: a `start_at` earlier than the drive's `start_at`, or an `end_at` later than the drive's `end_at`, answers `ERR_SHIFT_OUTSIDE_DRIVE_WINDOW`.

The remaining two checks use **deliberately different interval conventions**, and the difference is the crux of the phone-bank rules. A volunteer cannot be in two places at once, so a volunteer's own shifts are compared as **closed** intervals: a new shift conflicts with any existing shift of the same volunteer whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` when the two closed intervals from `start_at` through `end_at` inclusive intersect at all. That comparison is taken across **every** drive, not just the one being staffed — a volunteer already on the phones for one drive cannot be double-booked onto a concurrent shift for another — whereas the phone-line count below is a property of one drive's own switchboard and is counted only over that drive's shifts. Back-to-back shifts that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending at 14:00 and the next starting at 14:00 — **do** conflict, and the roster is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_CONFLICT`. Phone lines, by contrast, are handed over instantly, so line capacity is measured on **half-open** windows: a shift consumes a line for the interval from its `start_at` up to but not including its `end_at`, counted against shifts on the same drive whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. Two shifts that touch at an endpoint therefore do **not** consume the same line at the same time. When the number of shifts simultaneously holding a line would exceed the drive's `phone_line_count`, the roster is refused with `ERR_PHONE_LINE_CAPACITY`. Shifts whose `status` is `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` count for neither check: a no-show frees the line and stops blocking the volunteer's own calendar. Shifts are browsable by `drive_id`, `volunteer_id` and `status`.

A shift is closed with an `outcome` of `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`. Only a `SCHEDULED` shift can be closed out, and any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `COMPLETED` moves the shift `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and recomputes `pledges_taken_count` as the number of pledges whose `shift_id` is this shift, counted regardless of how those pledges later settled. `NO_SHOW` moves the shift `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`, leaves `pledges_taken_count` where it stood, and is a disciplinary matter: it raises the volunteer's `strike_count` by 1, which lowers their `seniority_score` by 15 points through the strike term of §3.2, and moves them `CERTIFIED → PROBATION` when the new count reaches 2. Closing the drive itself moves it `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, which requires that no pledge on the drive is still `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_PLEDGES_PENDING` — cancels every shift on the drive still `SCHEDULED`, and recomputes all five money aggregates and `attainment_pct` one final time. `CLOSED` is terminal for a drive; `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a shift.

**Premium items** are the thank-you gifts. Each is registered with a `sku` that must be non-empty and unique across premiums — a reused code answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` — a `name` announced on air, a `tier_threshold_cents` of at least 1 setting the minimum pledge that qualifies for it, a `fair_market_value_cents` of at least 0 that will be subtracted from a donor's deductible, and a `units_received_qty` of at least 0. At registration `on_hand_qty` is initialised to exactly `units_received_qty`, with `reserved_qty` and `shipped_qty` both 0. A premium's `status` is derived, never supplied: it is `AVAILABLE` while `on_hand_qty` minus `reserved_qty` is greater than 0, and `DEPLETED` otherwise — so a premium with ten units on hand and ten already reserved reads `DEPLETED` even though the units are physically in the room. A receipt from the vendor supplies a `received_qty` of at least 1 — a zero or negative delivery answers `ERR_VALIDATION` — and raises both `units_received_qty` and `on_hand_qty` by that quantity, which keeps the unit conservation invariant of §2.11 intact and may bring a `DEPLETED` premium back to `AVAILABLE`. A premium's `tier_threshold_cents`, `fair_market_value_cents` and `name` are fixed at registration; there is no amendment operation. Premiums are browsable by `status`, which filters on the derived value, and by `sku`.

A **pledge** records what a donor promised. It names the `drive_id`, the `donor_name`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, optionally the `shift_id` of the phone-bank shift that took the call, and optionally `wants_premium`, which defaults to true. A named shift must belong to the named drive, or the pledge is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_DRIVE_MISMATCH`. A new pledge sits on `payment_status` `PENDING` and `premium_status` as the allocation decides.

The **premium allocation waterfall** runs exactly once, at the moment the pledge is created, and its outcome is pinned for the life of the pledge. A donor who declined a gift short-circuits it entirely: `wants_premium` false leaves `premium_item_id` null, sets `premium_status` `WAIVED`, and makes `tax_deductible_cents` the full `amount_cents`, since nothing of value was given in return. Otherwise the candidate set is every premium whose `tier_threshold_cents` is at most the pledge's `amount_cents` — the tiers the pledge qualifies for. Those candidates are ranked by `tier_threshold_cents` descending, so the most generous tier the donor earned comes first; ties are broken by `fair_market_value_cents` ascending, so that among equally-priced tiers the station gives away the item that costs the donor the least deductible; and any remaining tie is broken by `sku` ascending, which makes the ranking total and the outcome deterministic.

The waterfall then walks that ranking **once**, from the top, and selects the first candidate with at least one unreserved unit on hand — meaning its `on_hand_qty` minus its `reserved_qty` is at least 1. When such a candidate is found, `premium_item_id` is set to it, `premium_status` becomes `RESERVED`, and that premium's `reserved_qty` rises by 1. When every candidate in the ranking is out of stock, the waterfall does not fall back to a lesser tier: it selects the **top-ranked** candidate, sets `premium_status` `BACKORDERED` and reserves **no** unit, because the station promises the donor the gift they earned and orders more stock. When no premium qualifies at all — the pledge is below every `tier_threshold_cents` — `premium_item_id` stays null and `premium_status` is `NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

The donor's `tax_deductible_cents` follows from the selection. When nothing was selected — `NOT_ELIGIBLE` or `WAIVED` — it is the full `amount_cents`. When a premium was selected, whether reserved or backordered, it is the `amount_cents` less that premium's `fair_market_value_cents`, floored at 0 so that a gift worth more than the pledge produces a deductible of zero rather than a negative figure. The selection is never re-run: later stock receipts, later premium registrations and later amendments to a premium's tier or value leave an existing pledge's `premium_item_id`, `premium_status` and `tax_deductible_cents` exactly as they were.

Settlement moves the payment dimension. An `outcome` of `PAID` moves the pledge `PENDING → PAID`. An `outcome` of `LAPSED` moves it `PENDING → LAPSED`, and a pledge whose `premium_status` was `RESERVED` releases its held unit: the `premium_status` becomes `RELEASED` and the premium's `reserved_qty` falls by 1, putting the unit back in play for the next donor.

Fulfilment moves the premium dimension, with an `action` of `SHIP` or `WAIVE`. `SHIP` requires the pledge's `premium_status` to be `RESERVED` — otherwise `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_RESERVED` — and the `payment_status` to be `PAID`, since the station does not ship against an unsettled promise, otherwise `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_PAID`; and the premium must still have a unit to send, since an `on_hand_qty` below 1 answers `ERR_PREMIUM_OUT_OF_STOCK`. A successful ship moves the pledge to `SHIPPED`, lowers the premium's `reserved_qty` by 1, lowers its `on_hand_qty` by 1 and raises its `shipped_qty` by 1 — the unit leaves the room and the conservation invariant still balances. `WAIVE` moves a `RESERVED` pledge to `WAIVED` and lowers `reserved_qty` by 1 without touching `on_hand_qty`, which is the path for a donor who changes their mind about the gift. `WAIVE` asks nothing of the money: it needs only the `RESERVED` earmark, so a still-`PENDING` pledge can be waived, and only `SHIP` is gated on `PAID`. Both actions therefore answer `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_RESERVED` first for a pledge on any other `premium_status` — `BACKORDERED`, `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `WAIVED`, `SHIPPED`, `RELEASED` or `RETURNED` alike — since fulfilment only ever acts on a held unit.

**Refund** is the compensating action for a payment, moving the pledge `PAID → REFUNDED`. It also unwinds whatever happened to the gift: a pledge whose `premium_status` is `SHIPPED` moves to `RETURNED`, lowering the premium's `shipped_qty` by 1 and raising its `on_hand_qty` by 1 as the item comes back to the fulfilment room; a pledge whose `premium_status` is `RESERVED` moves to `RELEASED`, lowering `reserved_qty` by 1. Any other `premium_status` is left exactly as it stands — a refunded pledge that was `WAIVED`, `BACKORDERED` or `NOT_ELIGIBLE` holds that value, because there is no unit to give back. Refund is available only from `PAID`, and any other `payment_status` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as does settling a pledge that is no longer `PENDING`. Because the amount moves out of `fulfilled_total_cents` and into `refunded_total_cents` rather than disappearing, and because the unit returns to `on_hand_qty` rather than vanishing, both conservation invariants of §2.11 hold after a refund exactly as they did before the payment. `REFUNDED` and `LAPSED` are terminal on the payment dimension; `SHIPPED`, `WAIVED`, `RELEASED`, `RETURNED` and `NOT_ELIGIBLE` are the resting states of the premium dimension.

The drive's five money aggregates are all recomputed from its pledges whenever any of them changes. `gross_pledged_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over **every** pledge on the drive regardless of its `payment_status` — the conserved total, which only ever rises. `pledged_total_cents` is the sum over pledges whose `payment_status` is `PENDING` or `PAID`, the money still expected or already in hand. `fulfilled_total_cents` is the sum over pledges that are `PAID`. `refunded_total_cents` is the sum over pledges that are `REFUNDED`, and `lapsed_total_cents` the sum over those that are `LAPSED`. Finally `attainment_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `pledged_total_cents`, divided by `goal_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places — and it is computed from `pledged_total_cents`, not from `gross_pledged_cents`, so a lapse or a refund pulls the station's reported attainment back down. Pledges are browsable by `drive_id`, `shift_id`, `premium_item_id`, `payment_status` and `premium_status`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is rooted at the API base with no version or namespace prefix: each collection is a peer at the URL root. Endpoints that create a new resource respond with HTTP 201; every other successful call responds with HTTP 200. All responses use the envelope of §2.2, and all requests carry the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers of §2.1 with the sole exception of the logical-clock endpoint. Path parameters are resolved left to right as described in §2.6, and all list endpoints accept the `limit` and `offset` parameters of §2.3 in addition to the filters named in their description.
