# Shelf Label & Price Accuracy Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retail price integrity is the discipline of making the number a customer reads on a shelf tag identical to the number the register charges. When those two numbers diverge, the consequences are not merely operational: a jurisdiction's weights-and-measures inspector arrives, cites the store, assesses a penalty per violation, and the chain owes refunds to every customer who was overcharged. The failure almost never begins at the register. It begins upstream, in a price change file that was distributed late, a shelf label that was printed but never placed, a unit-price figure rounded the wrong way, or a promotional sign left hanging three weeks after the promotion expired.

This document specifies the Price Integrity service, the system of record that closes that loop end to end. Corporate pricing publishes dated price change files; each store turns the affected lines into concrete labour — print the tag, hang the tag, have a second person verify the tag — and every one of those steps is timestamped and attributable to a named associate. Independently of the label workflow, price accuracy auditors walk aisles with a scanner, comparing shelf price to register price line by line, and the resulting error rate is measured against the jurisdiction's inclusive failure threshold. Audits that fail do not merely produce a report: they issue coaching, written warnings or suspensions against the associate accountable for the aisle, and a suspended associate's placed-but-unverified labels fall back into the store's backlog.

Two design commitments run through every requirement that follows. First, **a store's freedom to act is derived, never declared**: the `compliance_posture` field on a store is recomputed from live findings, failed audits, signage violations and label backlog after every relevant mutation, and it is that derived value — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — which decides whether the store may take on new label work or post new signage. Second, **money and percentages are rounded exactly once**, at a pinned point in each formula, because a per-step rounding in a penalty tier or a unit-price division produces a legally different number than a single rounding at the end. Implementers should treat both commitments as testable contracts rather than guidance.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every endpoint in this service is mounted under the `/price-integrity` prefix. The rules in this section apply uniformly across that surface unless a later section explicitly narrows them.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request carries `X-User-Role`, naming the role the caller is acting as, and — for the roles that are backed by a persisted employee record — `X-User-ID`, naming the acting entity. There are exactly six roles, spelled precisely as follows: `PricingAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `StoreManager`, `LabelAssociate`, `PriceAuditor`, and `CustomerServiceDesk`.

A missing or empty `X-User-Role` header is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same code and status are returned when `X-User-ID` is missing or empty on a request claiming one of the three entity-backed roles. When the header names a role that is not one of the six, or names a role that is not listed among the target endpoint's authorized roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Three roles are **entity-backed**, meaning the caller identity resolves to a persisted employee record:

- When `X-User-Role` is `StoreManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Associate`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Associate` compare the resource's associate-valued field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `LabelAssociate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Associate`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Associate` compare the resource's associate-valued field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PriceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Associate`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Associate` compare the resource's associate-valued field to `X-User-ID`.

For all three, the resolved associate record must exist — an `X-User-ID` naming no associate is `ERR_ASSOCIATE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — and its `role_code` must correspond to the claimed role. `LabelAssociate` requires `role_code` `LABEL_ASSOCIATE`, `PriceAuditor` requires `PRICE_AUDITOR`, and `StoreManager` requires `STORE_MANAGER`. A mismatch between the claimed role and the stored `role_code` is `ERR_ROLE_CODE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), and this check runs on every endpoint whose authorized-role list includes `LabelAssociate`, `PriceAuditor` or `StoreManager`.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**:

- When `X-User-Role` is `PricingAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example, as `issued_by_ref` on an `AccountabilityAction` the operator issues) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example, as `issued_by_ref` on an `AccountabilityAction` the operator issues) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerServiceDesk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

None of the three opaque roles is required to send `X-User-ID` at all, and any value they do send is ignored for identity purposes.

Role scope, in business terms: `PricingAdmin` is the corporate pricing administrator who owns jurisdictions, stores, price change files and their lines, and may drive the logical clock. `ComplianceOfficer` owns scanner audit governance, regulatory inspections, findings and exception reports, and may also drive the logical clock during audit replays. `StoreManager` staffs the store, creates label tasks, may perform any floor step, and answers inspections. `LabelAssociate` prints and places shelf labels and posts or removes promotional signage, but never verifies their own placement. `PriceAuditor` verifies label placement and runs scanner accuracy audits, and is deliberately barred from printing or placing labels so that audit evidence stays independent. `CustomerServiceDesk` files and settles overcharge refund claims and holds no authority over labels, audits or inspections. Appending a scan observation to an audit is reserved to `PriceAuditor` alone; setting or clearing an aisle's accountable associate is reserved to `StoreManager` alone.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because `ScanLine` rows are append-only evidence, this ordering also means a scan line list reproduces the exact order in which observations were recorded. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`; `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Filter parameters are documented alongside the workflows that produce the filtered data, but they follow one uniform rule: an unrecognised enum value supplied to a filter (for example a `status`, `classification`, `compliance_state`, `training_status`, `print_status`, `placement_status`, `line_status`, `outcome`, `audit_type`, `severity`, `priority`, `role_code`, `trigger_code`, `compliance_posture` or `overdue` filter) is a schema error and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, assigned at creation and never reused. Clients never supply `id` values for resources they create; they supply the business keys described in §3.

Fourteen of the fifteen resource types additionally carry a full audit triplet. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, in the same format, and is refreshed on every mutation including mutations applied as a side effect of an operation on another resource. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, again including cascade-driven mutations. The one exception is `ScanLine`: because a scan observation is immutable once appended, a `ScanLine` carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field should appear in its response payload.

All time-dependent behaviour in this service is measured against a **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any operator has advanced it, every deadline comparison, promotional window test, certification expiry check, verification grace period and thirty-day posture window reads that instant. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes it back. Throughout this document, "the current instant" means the logical clock's value at the moment the request is processed.

### 2.5. The Jurisdiction Rulebook

A **jurisdiction** is a configuration resource, not an operational one: it holds the regulatory ruleset that governs every store assigned to it, and it is the single place where display formatting, thresholds, refund policy, penalty rates and disclosure requirements are parameterised. A jurisdiction is identified to operators by its short `code` — globally unique, 2 to 16 characters, restricted to uppercase letters, digits and hyphens — and carries a human-readable `name`.

The unit-price display block is the most formatting-sensitive part of the rulebook. `unit_price_required` is a boolean defaulting to true; when true, a price change file cannot be distributed unless every one of its live lines can produce a unit price. `unit_price_uom_rule` selects the basis on which unit price is expressed and is one of `PER_UNIT_MEASURE_CANONICAL` (the default), `PER_100_COUNT`, or `PER_SINGLE_ITEM`. `unit_price_decimal_places` is the number of decimal places of the **dollar** figure printed on the shelf label and must be either 2 or 3, defaulting to 2. `unit_price_rounding` selects the rounding mode applied at the single rounding step of the unit-price computation and is either `ROUND_HALF_UP` (the default) or `ROUND_CEILING`.

Audit thresholds and variants live in the same rulebook. `scanner_error_rate_threshold_pct` is the inclusive failure threshold for an audit's error rate; it must be greater than 0, at most 100, carry at most two decimals, and defaults to 2.0. `default_rate_variant` names the error-rate variant applied when an audit is closed without an explicit variant, and is either `GROSS_MISMATCH` (the default) or `NET_OVERCHARGE`.

The refund block parameterises what a store owes a customer who was overcharged. `overcharge_refund_policy` selects the branch of the refund computation and is one of `REFUND_DIFFERENCE_ONLY` (the default), `REFUND_DIFFERENCE_PLUS_FIXED`, or `ITEM_FREE_UP_TO_CAP`. `refund_fixed_bonus_cents` is the fixed goodwill amount added under the plus-fixed policy; it must be zero or greater and defaults to 0. `refund_cap_cents` is the per-claim ceiling applied under every policy branch; it must be greater than 0 and defaults to 5000. `refund_budget_per_audit_cents` is the aggregate ceiling on a store's outstanding refund liability; it must be greater than 0 and defaults to 100000.

The penalty block parameterises regulatory exposure. `penalty_per_violation_cents` is the base per-violation unit multiplied into every finding's penalty component; it must be greater than 0 and defaults to 5000. `penalty_max_cents` is the hard ceiling on any single inspection's penalty exposure; it must be greater than 0 and defaults to 2000000.

Three remaining settings govern signage, deadlines and accountability. `signage_disclosure_codes` is an array of strings with no duplicates, defaulting to the empty array, naming the disclosure codes every promotional sign in the jurisdiction must carry — for example `UNIT_PRICE` and `END_DATE`. `grace_period_hours` is an integer of zero or greater, defaulting to 48, giving the number of hours after a price change file's effective date before an unexecuted label task becomes overdue. `accountability_variant` selects the associate scoring variant and is either `OCCURRENCE_WEIGHTED` (the default) or `TASK_WEIGHTED`.

Amending a jurisdiction is a live operation with cross-entity consequences, described in §3.1 and §3.6: because the required disclosure set is read from the jurisdiction at evaluation time rather than copied onto each sign, changing `signage_disclosure_codes` re-derives the missing-disclosure set and the compliance verdict of every non-removed sign in the jurisdiction and recomputes each affected store's posture.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and the Single-Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this service are integer cents. There are no fractional-cent fields and no floating-point currency. Percentages — audit error rates, category error rates, accountability scores and training scores — are decimal numbers carried to **2 decimal places, rounded half up**, and that rounding is applied exactly once per computation, after all arithmetic is complete.

Three formulas deviate from half-up rounding in a specific, deliberate way, and the deviations are normative rather than incidental. The unit-price display rounds using the jurisdiction's own `unit_price_rounding` mode, which may be half-up or ceiling. The penalty exposure applies a single **floor** to the scaled sum of finding components. The inspection settlement applies a **floor** independently to each of the two refund-credit candidates before taking the smaller of them. In every case the rounding step is a single step at a pinned position in the formula; rounding an intermediate value first and the result second produces a different, incorrect number, and the tests treat that difference as a defect rather than a tolerance.

Threshold comparisons are stated explicitly wherever they appear, because inclusivity is legally load-bearing. Unless a rule says otherwise, a threshold comparison in this service is inclusive: an audit whose error rate is exactly equal to the jurisdiction threshold fails, a refund whose gross amount is exactly equal to the cap is paid in full, and a label task whose due instant is exactly the current instant is already overdue. The accountability suspension floor is the one prominent strict comparison, and it is called out as such in §3.2.

### 2.7. Store Compliance Posture — the Derived Gate

A store is created inside a jurisdiction with an operator-assigned `store_number` (globally unique, 1 to 16 characters), a display `name`, and a `jurisdiction_id` naming the ruleset that governs it. Everything else on a store is derived: a store never accepts a direct write to any counter, and every counter is recomputed from the underlying rows rather than adjusted by a delta, so that forward operations and their compensating operations cannot drift apart.

`compliance_posture` is the gate. It takes exactly one of three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `FROZEN` — and a freshly opened store starts at `OPEN` with every derived counter at 0. The posture is recomputed after every finding transition, every audit close or reopen, every label-task placement-axis transition, every signage post, removal or sweep transition, every refund settlement, the price change file cascades into the executed and superseded states, and every amendment of the governing jurisdiction.

Recomputation reads five inputs, and the backlog input is always refreshed before the posture is evaluated:

- `open_finding_count` is the number of inspection findings belonging to this store's inspections whose status is either `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`.
- `escalated_finding_count` is the number of findings belonging to this store's inspections whose status is `ESCALATED`.
- `failed_audit_count_30d` is the number of this store's scanner audits that are `CLOSED`, carry outcome `FAIL`, and whose closing instant is at or after the instant thirty days before the current instant. The window is measured backwards from the logical clock and its lower edge is inclusive.
- `signage_violation_count` is the number of this store's promotional signs whose status is either `EXPIRED_PENDING_REMOVAL` or `NON_COMPLIANT`.
- `label_backlog_count` is the number of this store's label tasks whose placement axis is `UNPLACED` or `REJECTED` **and** whose underlying price change line is not `WITHDRAWN`. There is no time window on this count; it is a full recompute from scratch on every trigger, and its triggers are label-task creation, every placement-axis transition, the withdrawal cascade that follows a supersede, and the cascade that fires when a price change file becomes fully executed.

The verdict is evaluated in a pinned order — the frozen tests first, then the restricted tests, then the fallthrough. The posture becomes `FROZEN` when at least one escalated finding exists, **or** when the open finding count is at least 3, **or** when the thirty-day failed-audit count is at least 2. Otherwise the posture becomes `RESTRICTED` when the open finding count is at least 1, **or** when the thirty-day failed-audit count is exactly 1, **or** when the label backlog is at least 5, **or** when at least one signage violation exists. If none of those hold, the posture is `OPEN`. Note the asymmetry deliberately built into the audit input: two failed audits inside the window freeze the store, exactly one restricts it, and zero leaves it unconstrained.

Four further counters are carried on a store for reporting and settlement rather than for gating. `refund_liability_outstanding_cents` is the sum of the claimed amounts of this store's refund claims whose status is `PENDING` or `APPROVED`. `refund_paid_total_cents` is the sum of the claimed amounts of this store's claims whose status is `PAID`. `cumulative_penalty_exposure_cents` is the sum of the assessed penalties of this store's closed regulatory inspections. `executed_file_count` is the number of price change files that reached the executed state with this store in their scope.

Posture gating is enforced at three write points, and every refusal carries `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 422). A `FROZEN` store outright blocks label-task creation, label printing, and signage posting. A `RESTRICTED` store blocks signage posting unless the request sets `override_acknowledged` to true, and additionally requires that the sign being posted names a `price_change_line_id`; posting a floating promotional sign in a restricted store is refused with the same code. One escape hatch exists in the file-distribution direction: a price change file whose `priority` is `REGULATORY` bypasses the frozen posture gate for label tasking, on the theory that a legally mandated price change must reach the shelf regardless of the store's compliance standing. Files with `priority` `ROUTINE` or `COMPETITIVE` enjoy no such exemption.

A dedicated read capability exists specifically so that a client can reconcile every derived counter against the underlying rows: it recomputes the label backlog and the posture from live data before returning the store projection, and is therefore the authoritative way to observe posture drift after a sequence of cascades.

### 2.8. Version Pinning and Stale-Parent Rejection

Three write operations in this service target a child resource while depending on the state of a parent, and in all three cases the operation is legitimately repeatable — so no state precondition can distinguish a fresh request from one composed against an outdated view of the parent. Those three operations therefore require the client to pin the parent's `version`, and reject the write when the pinned value does not match.

A request creating a label task against a price change file must carry `expected_price_change_file_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `PriceChangeFile`. Tasking one line of a file is legitimately repeatable for other lines and other stores, so the request cannot be rejected on state grounds; the stale version is the only signal that the client's view of the file — its distribution status, its supersede pointer, and its line set — is out of date. If the supplied value does not equal the file's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRICE_CHANGE_FILE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A request submitting a store's remediation response against a regulatory inspection must carry `expected_regulatory_inspection_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `RegulatoryInspection`. Re-submission is legitimate by design, because the rejected-then-resubmitted cycle is a declared part of the inspection lifecycle, so a second submission cannot be refused on state grounds; the stale version is the only signal that the response the client composed answers a superseded finding set. Because issuing findings and every individual finding transition silently advance the inspection's `version`, this pin is the mechanism that forces a store manager to re-read the finding set before answering it. A mismatch with the inspection's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A request filing an overcharge refund claim must carry `expected_scanner_audit_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `ScannerAudit` that owns the scan line being claimed against. Multiple claims against different scan lines of the same audit are legitimate, so the request cannot be refused on state grounds; the stale version is the only signal that the audit was reopened and reclosed under a different rate variant after the client read it. A mismatch with the audit's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCANNER_AUDIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

These three fields are **required**, not optional, on their respective requests: omitting one is a schema failure yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. No other endpoint in this service accepts a version-pinning field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.9. The Validation Priority Ladder

When a single request would fail several checks, the response must report the failure the ladder reaches first. The ladder for this domain runs: authentication, then authorization, then schema, then existence, then the target's own state, then cross-entity state, then version pinning, then regulatory and segregation-of-duties rules, then temporal rules, then constraint and uniqueness rules, and finally fiscal rules — budgets, caps and quotas are always evaluated last, so a request that is both over budget and malformed reports the malformed body. The complete ordering, with the priority number each rule occupies, is:

1. **Priority 10 — authentication.** `X-User-Role` is present and non-empty, and `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty whenever the claimed role is entity-backed (`StoreManager`, `LabelAssociate`, `PriceAuditor`). The three opaque roles (`PricingAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `CustomerServiceDesk`) send no `X-User-ID` and any value they do send is ignored. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Priority 20 — authorization.** `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and is listed among the target endpoint's authorized roles. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Priority 22 — role/record agreement.** For entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing associate whose `role_code` matches the claimed role. Failure: `ERR_ROLE_CODE_MISMATCH` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorized roles include `LabelAssociate`, `PriceAuditor` or `StoreManager`.
4. **Priority 25 — schema.** The request body parses, every required input field is present, enum values are members of their declared sets, integer fields are integers within range, timestamps are RFC 3339, and array fields are arrays. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
5. **Priority 26 — pagination.** Pagination parameters are integers with `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
6. **Priority 30 — path existence.** Every resource named by a path parameter exists, validated left to right in path order. Failure: the per-entity not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint with path parameters.
7. **Priority 32 — body reference existence.** Every foreign-key value supplied in the request body references an existing row, validated in the field order the endpoint declares its inputs. Failure: the per-entity not-found code (404). Applies to every write endpoint carrying reference inputs.
8. **Priority 40 — own state.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Only open or reopened audits accept scan lines; only scanning or reopened audits close; only posted signage can be removed; only closed audits can back a refund claim or an issue-findings call. Because an open audit necessarily has zero scan lines, a close attempt against an open audit is reported by the empty-sample rule at priority 63 as `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_EMPTY` rather than by this rule. On the five endpoints listed at priority 41 this rule covers only resources whose state is neither the transition's source nor its target; a resource already sitting in the target state falls through to priority 41 instead. Failure: the entity's invalid-state code or the transition-specific code (409). Applies to every action and transition endpoint.
9. **Priority 41 — repeat transition.** The requested transition is not a repeat of one already performed — posting an already-posted sign, closing an already-closed audit, verifying an already-verified label task, or paying an already-paid refund claim. Failure: `ERR_TRANSITION_ALREADY_APPLIED` (409). Applies to exactly five endpoints: audit close, signage post, signage remove, label-task placement verification, and refund-claim settlement.
10. **Priority 42 — refund evidence state.** A refund claim may only be created against a scan line whose owning audit is `CLOSED`. Failure: `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_CLOSED` (409). Applies to refund-claim creation.
11. **Priority 43 — print gating on file state.** Label printing requires the owning price change file to be `DISTRIBUTED` or `PARTIALLY_EXECUTED`; a `DRAFT`, `EXECUTED` or `SUPERSEDED` file blocks the print. Failure: `ERR_FILE_NOT_DISTRIBUTED` (409). Applies to label-task printing.
12. **Priority 44 — inspection evidence state.** Issuing inspection findings requires the inspection's evidence audit to be `CLOSED`. Failure: `ERR_EVIDENCE_AUDIT_NOT_CLOSED` (409). Applies to issuing findings.
13. **Priority 45 — version pinning.** When the request carries a version-pinning field, it equals the referenced entity's current `version`. Failure: the pinning rule's own code — `ERR_STALE_PRICE_CHANGE_FILE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SCANNER_AUDIT_VERSION` (412). Applies to exactly three endpoints: label-task creation, inspection response submission, and refund-claim creation.
14. **Priority 50 — segregation of duties on verification.** The verifying associate named by `X-User-ID` must differ from the label task's `placed_by_associate_id`. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422). Applies to placement verification.
15. **Priority 50 — segregation of duties on remediation.** The `remediation_owner_associate_id` assigned to an exception report must not be an associate who closed any scanner audit counted in the report's period. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422). Applies to exception-report root-cause assignment. These two rules share priority 50 and never contend, because no endpoint is subject to both.
16. **Priority 51 — actor certification.** The acting associate's `training_status` is `CERTIFIED` and their `certification_expires_at` is strictly after the current instant. Failure: `ERR_ASSOCIATE_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422). Applies to four endpoints: placement verification, scanner-audit creation, scan-line posting, and audit close.
17. **Priority 52 — posture gate.** A `FROZEN` store blocks label-task creation, label printing and signage posting outright; a `RESTRICTED` store blocks signage posting unless the request sets `override_acknowledged` to true. Failure: `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (422). Applies to three endpoints: label-task creation, label printing, and signage posting.
18. **Priority 54 — undisplayable unit price.** A line whose unit price cannot be displayed cannot be tasked for labelling: this is the case when `net_content_qty` is not positive while the jurisdiction's `unit_price_uom_rule` is something other than `PER_SINGLE_ITEM`. This rule keys on `unit_price_uom_rule` alone. `unit_price_required` governs only the distribution precondition, so a jurisdiction with `unit_price_required` set to false distributes such a file happily and still refuses to task the line. Failure: `ERR_UNIT_PRICE_UNAVAILABLE` (422). Applies to label-task creation.
19. **Priority 56 — distribution deadline.** A price change file may only be distributed while the current instant is strictly before its effective date plus the jurisdiction's grace period. Failure: `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_PASSED` (422). Applies to file distribution.
20. **Priority 58 — well-formed intervals.** Declared intervals must have a start strictly before their end: `promo_start_at` before `promo_end_at`, `scan_window_start` before `scan_window_end`, `scheduled_start_at` before `scheduled_end_at`, and `period_start_at` before `period_end_at`. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). Applies to four endpoints: signage creation, scanner-audit creation, label-task creation, and exception-report creation.
21. **Priority 59 — verification grace period.** Placement verification must happen within 48 hours of the label task's `placed_at`. Failure: `ERR_VERIFICATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). Applies to placement verification.
22. **Priority 60 — aisle audit window exclusion.** Audit windows on one aisle must not overlap, and each existing window is extended by a 60-minute cooldown after its end. A conflict exists when the new window's start is earlier than an existing window's end plus 60 minutes **and** that existing window's start is earlier than the new window's end, considering only audits on the same aisle that are not `CLOSED`. Failure: `ERR_AISLE_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to scanner-audit creation.
23. **Priority 61 — associate labour exclusion.** Labour intervals for one assignee must not overlap, with closed-closed semantics: a new interval that merely touches an existing non-terminal label task window of the same assignee is a conflict. A conflict exists when the new start is at or before an existing end **and** that existing start is at or before the new end. Failure: `ERR_ASSOCIATE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to label-task creation.
24. **Priority 62 — required disclosures.** Posted promotional signage must carry every disclosure code the jurisdiction requires, meaning the missing-disclosure set must be empty. Failure: `ERR_MISSING_DISCLOSURE` (422). Applies to signage posting.
25. **Priority 63 — uniqueness.** One label task per line-and-store pair; one `sign_code` per store; one open exception report per store-and-period triple; one refund claim per scan-line-and-customer pair. Failure: `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409). Applies to four endpoints: label-task creation, signage creation, exception-report creation, and refund-claim creation.
26. **Priority 63 — empty candidate guard for reports.** An exception report needs at least one category with a sample of at least 2 scan lines drawn from closed audits inside the half-open period. Failure: `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_AUDIT_DATA` (422). Applies to exception-report creation.
27. **Priority 63 — empty sample and non-overcharge guards.** An audit cannot be closed with zero scan lines, since the first scan line is what moves it out of the open state; and a refund claim requires a scan line whose classification is `OVERCHARGE`, meaning its register price exceeds its shelf price. Failures: `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_EMPTY` (422) on the close path and `ERR_NOT_AN_OVERCHARGE` (422) on the claim path. Applies to audit close and refund-claim creation. These three rules share priority 63 and are disjoint by endpoint and by path within an endpoint.
28. **Priority 64 — remediation evidence.** A finding may only be waived with a non-empty `waiver_reason`, and may only be remediated once both `remediation_action_code` and `remediation_evidence_ref` are supplied. Failure: `ERR_REMEDIATION_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to finding transitions.
29. **Priority 66 — aisle/store agreement.** The aisle named by `aisle_id` must belong to the store named by `store_id`. Failure: `ERR_AISLE_STORE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to three endpoints: scanner-audit creation, signage creation, and label-task creation.
30. **Priority 67 — associate/store agreement.** The associate named by an assignment or actor field must belong to the store the operation targets. Failure: `ERR_ASSOCIATE_STORE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to four endpoints: label-task creation, scanner-audit creation, aisle associate assignment, and exception-report root-cause assignment.
31. **Priority 68 — file scope.** The store must be present in the price change file's `scope_store_ids` and must share the file's `jurisdiction_id`. Failure: `ERR_STORE_OUT_OF_FILE_SCOPE` (422). Applies to label-task creation.
32. **Priority 70 — daily labour cap.** The total scheduled minutes for one assignee on a single UTC calendar day may not exceed 480 minutes — eight hours — counting the new task plus all existing non-terminal tasks. Failure: `ERR_ASSOCIATE_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to label-task creation.
33. **Priority 72 — refund budget.** The store's outstanding refund liability plus this claim's computed amount may not exceed the jurisdiction's per-audit refund budget. Failure: `ERR_REFUND_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to refund-claim creation.
34. **Priority 74 — penalty override ceiling.** An `assessed_penalty_cents` override supplied on adjudication may not exceed the freshly recomputed penalty exposure. Failure: `ERR_PENALTY_OVERRIDE_TOO_HIGH` (422). Applies to inspection adjudication.
35. **Priority 76 — refund settlement guards.** A refund claim may only be settled to paid while its status is `APPROVED` and the store's outstanding liability covers it, and a denial requires a non-empty `denial_reason`. Failures: `ERR_REFUND_NOT_APPROVED` (409) on the pay path and `ERR_DENIAL_REASON_REQUIRED` (422) on the deny path. Applies to refund-claim settlement.

Label-task creation is the most heavily guarded endpoint in the service, and the ladder above fixes the order in which its guards fire: existence of the file and of every referenced row, then the file's own state, then the version pin, then the posture gate, then the undisplayable-unit-price refusal, then the well-formed-interval test, then the schedule-overlap test, then the duplicate-task test, then aisle/store agreement, then associate/store agreement, then file scope, and only then the daily minutes cap.

### 2.10. Nested Paths and Not-Found Semantics

Several collections are reachable through a parent segment: a store's aisles and associates, a file's lines, an audit's scan lines, an inspection's findings, and the aisles or accountability actions of an associate. Path parameters are always resolved strictly left to right. When a request names a store that does not exist and an aisle that does not exist, the response is the store's not-found code, never the aisle's; the second segment is not consulted until the first resolves.

Every entity has its own not-found code, all of them HTTP 404: `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AISLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSOCIATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABEL_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCANNER_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCAN_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROMO_SIGNAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGULATORY_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFUND_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_ACCOUNTABILITY_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`. The same codes serve for body-supplied references: a `jurisdiction_id` naming no jurisdiction, a `store_id` naming no store, an `aisle_id` naming no aisle, an associate id in any assignment field naming no associate, a `superseded_by_file_id` naming no file, a `price_change_line_id` naming no line, a `scanner_audit_id` naming no audit, and a `scan_line_id` naming no scan line each produce their entity's code.

Because a nested collection is a convenience view rather than an ownership boundary, no entity in this service owns another's lifecycle. Relationships are expressed as reference fields plus filtered list views, and the self-referential supersession pointer between two price change files is no exception — it is a plain reference field that also serves as a list filter.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Cascade Semantics

Two independent segregation-of-duties rules run through the domain, and both exist because the value of an attestation collapses when the attester is the actor. The associate who records a label placement may never verify it. The auditor who closed a scanner audit may never own the remediation of an exception report that counted that audit. Both refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Layered on top, the workforce is partitioned by `role_code`, so a floor associate physically cannot present the auditor role and vice versa.

Cascades in this service are **immediate and synchronous**: by the time a response is returned, every derived counter, every propagated state and every cascade-created record already reflects the operation. Cascade-driven mutations are indistinguishable from direct ones in their bookkeeping — a resource touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented exactly as if a client had written it. Records created by a cascade rather than by an operator are stamped with the literal string `system` in the field that records the issuing actor, which is how a client tells an automatically issued accountability action from a manually issued one.

Several cascades run several hops deep. The deepest is the audit-failure chain: closing a failing audit issues an accountability action, which re-derives the responsible associate's score, which can suspend them, which resets their placed-but-unverified label tasks, which changes each affected store's backlog and therefore its posture. The label-verification chain is equally deep in the opposite direction: verifying a placement advances the price change line, which may complete the file, which credits every scoped store's executed-file count and re-derives each store's backlog and posture. Every hop is described in §3 at the point where it fires.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

This section walks the domain in the order the business itself runs: first the estate is configured and staffed, then price changes are published and executed at shelf, then accuracy is measured, then regulators are answered, then customers are made whole, and finally the whole period is rolled up and root-caused.

### 3.1. Standing Up the Estate: Jurisdictions, Stores, Aisles and Associates

Nothing in this service can happen before a jurisdiction exists, because a jurisdiction is where every threshold lives. A pricing administrator registers one by supplying its `code` and `name`; every other rulebook setting described in §2.5 — `unit_price_required`, `unit_price_uom_rule`, `unit_price_decimal_places`, `unit_price_rounding`, `scanner_error_rate_threshold_pct`, `default_rate_variant`, `overcharge_refund_policy`, `refund_fixed_bonus_cents`, `refund_cap_cents`, `refund_budget_per_audit_cents`, `penalty_per_violation_cents`, `penalty_max_cents`, `signage_disclosure_codes`, `grace_period_hours` and `accountability_variant` — is optional at registration and falls back to its documented default when omitted. Jurisdictions may be listed and filtered by `code`.

Amendments are where a jurisdiction becomes interesting. Every rulebook setting except `code` and `name` may be amended after the fact, and an amendment takes effect immediately for evaluations that read the rulebook at evaluation time. The most consequential amendment is to `signage_disclosure_codes`: because a sign's missing-disclosure set is derived rather than copied, adding a required code re-derives `missing_disclosure_codes` and the compliance verdict for every non-removed sign in the jurisdiction and then recomputes the posture of every store affected. A sign that was compliant when it went up can therefore become deficient without anyone touching the sign.

A store is opened inside a jurisdiction with `store_number`, `name` and `jurisdiction_id`. Stores may be listed filtered by `jurisdiction_id` or by `compliance_posture`, which is the practical way for a compliance officer to find every frozen store in a region.

Aisles are the audit-scannable units of a store, and they are created against the store whose path they hang from — the `store_id` on an aisle is taken from that path segment rather than from the body. Each aisle carries an `aisle_code` (1 to 8 characters, unique within its store) such as A07, and a `category_code` (1 to 24 uppercase characters) such as DAIRY naming the merchandising category audited there. An aisle may optionally name its accountable label-maintenance owner as `assigned_associate_id` at creation, and a store manager may later set or clear that owner; the assignee must belong to the aisle's own store, which is where `ERR_ASSOCIATE_STORE_MISMATCH` is raised. The accountable owner matters far more than its modest appearance suggests: it is the single candidate the audit-failure cascade of §3.5 holds responsible, and an aisle with no owner absorbs an audit failure without anyone being coached. Aisles carry one system-maintained timestamp, `last_audit_closed_at`, holding the closing instant of the most recently closed audit on that aisle. Aisles may be listed by store and filtered by `category_code`, and may also be listed by the associate they are assigned to.

Associates are hired into a store, again taking their `store_id` from the path segment, with an `employee_number` unique within the store, a `full_name`, and a `role_code` of `LABEL_ASSOCIATE`, `PRICE_AUDITOR` or `STORE_MANAGER`. A newly hired associate starts `UNTRAINED` with an `accountability_score` of 100, an `issued_action_count` of 0 and a `verified_placement_count` of 0. Associate lists are scoped to a store and may be filtered by `role_code` and by `training_status`.

### 3.2. Training, Certification and the Accountability Ledger

Certification is the gate that separates an employee from a competent one, and this service treats it as a lifecycle rather than a flag. An associate's `training_status` moves among `UNTRAINED`, `IN_TRAINING`, `CERTIFIED` and `SUSPENDED`, and there is no terminal state — an associate can always be retrained.

A store manager records a training result by supplying the `module_code` completed and the `score_pct` achieved. The passing bar is 80 percent. A result of at least 80 taken while the associate is `UNTRAINED` or `IN_TRAINING` moves them to `CERTIFIED` and stamps `certification_expires_at` at the current instant plus 365 days. A result below 80 taken from either of those two states leaves the associate `IN_TRAINING`: from `UNTRAINED` this is the `UNTRAINED → IN_TRAINING` transition, and from `IN_TRAINING` it is a self-loop that may repeat without bound. A failing score for an `UNTRAINED` or `IN_TRAINING` associate is emphatically **not** an error — the attempt is recorded and the associate stays in training. Either way, `last_training_module_code` and `last_training_score_pct` are stored, overwriting whatever the previous attempt left; and on a failing attempt `certification_expires_at` stays null rather than being cleared or extended.

An already-`CERTIFIED` associate who passes again stays `CERTIFIED` and has `certification_expires_at` re-stamped at the current instant plus 365 days, so recertification extends the window rather than starting a second one. This is the only way an expiring certification is renewed; certifications do not renew themselves.

Suspension is never requested — it is derived. Whenever an accountability action against the associate is created, completed or voided, and whenever one of their label tasks reaches a verified placement, the associate's `accountability_score` is recomputed. The score starts conceptually from a clean 100 and is reduced by the weight of the accountability actions still outstanding against them. Only actions whose status is `ISSUED` count; `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED` actions never do. The weight assigned to an action by its `action_type` is 5 for `COACHING`, 10 for `RETRAINING_ASSIGNED`, 15 for `WRITTEN_WARNING` and 25 for `SUSPENSION`, and that weight is stored on the action itself as `score_weight`.

Which arithmetic then applies depends on the governing jurisdiction's `accountability_variant`, and the two variants can disagree about the same associate. Under `OCCURRENCE_WEIGHTED`, the score is 100 reduced by the sum of the weights of the outstanding actions, floored at zero — each issued action costs its flat weight regardless of how much correct work the associate has done. Under `TASK_WEIGHTED`, the outstanding weight sum is first multiplied by 40, that product is then divided by the associate's verified placement count plus 4, the quotient is subtracted from 100, the difference is rounded half up to two decimal places, and the result is floored at zero. The verified placement count used here is the number of label tasks whose `placed_by_associate_id` is this associate and whose placement axis reads `VERIFIED`. The practical consequence is that the same outstanding actions are amortised over demonstrated correct work: a low-volume associate lands far below the suspension floor under `TASK_WEIGHTED` while `OCCURRENCE_WEIGHTED` keeps them above it, and a high-volume associate is the reverse.

The suspension floor is 80, and the comparison is **strict**: a recomputed score of exactly 80 does not suspend, a score of 79.99 does. When a `CERTIFIED` associate's recomputed score falls strictly below 80, they transition `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED`, and that transition immediately reaches into their outstanding floor work. Every label task assigned to them whose placement axis reads `PLACED` is pushed back — its placement axis returns from `PLACED` to `UNPLACED`, its `placed_by_associate_id` and `placed_at` are both cleared to null, and its print axis is pushed from `PRINTED` back to `REPRINT_REQUIRED` with `printed_label_serial` cleared to null. Each store touched by those tasks then recomputes its label backlog and, on that basis, its posture — so one associate's suspension can restrict or freeze a store in the same request.

Reinstatement is deliberately narrow. A `SUSPENDED` associate is reinstated only by completing the remedial module `REMEDIAL_LABEL_101` with a score of at least 80. Recording any other module for a suspended associate is refused with `ERR_ASSOCIATE_INVALID_STATE`, and recording `REMEDIAL_LABEL_101` with a score below 80 is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. A qualifying remedial completion applies three compensating effects in order: first, every accountability action against this associate whose status is `ISSUED` and whose `action_type` is `SUSPENSION` or `RETRAINING_ASSIGNED` transitions `ISSUED → COMPLETED`; second, the `accountability_score` is recomputed from the actions that remain `ISSUED`; third, `certification_expires_at` is re-stamped at the current instant plus 365 days. Whether the associate actually returns to duty depends on the recomputed score: if it is at least 80 the associate transitions `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED`; if it is still strictly below 80 the associate remains `SUSPENDED`, the action completions still stand, and the response reports `training_status` `SUSPENDED` together with the improved score.

Accountability actions can also be issued directly. A store manager or compliance officer issues one against an associate by supplying the `trigger_code` explaining why — one of `AUDIT_FAIL`, `OVERDUE_LABEL_TASK`, `VERIFICATION_REJECTED`, `INSPECTION_FINDING` or `EXCEPTION_ROOT_CAUSE` — the `action_type` setting its severity, and optionally a free-text `source_ref` pointing at the triggering artifact such as an audit's id string. The `source_ref` is a pointer for humans, not a reference the server resolves. `issued_by_ref` records the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, or the literal string `system` when the action was created by a cascade rather than by a person. A newly issued action starts `ISSUED`, has its `score_weight` computed from its `action_type`, and immediately re-derives the target associate's `issued_action_count` and `accountability_score` — which can drive the `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` transition and its whole downstream chain inside the very same request. Issuing an action against an already-`SUSPENDED` associate is refused with `ERR_ASSOCIATE_INVALID_STATE`.

An issued action leaves the outstanding weight sum in one of two ways. It transitions `ISSUED → COMPLETED` as a system effect of the remedial-training path described above, which applies only to `SUSPENSION` and `RETRAINING_ASSIGNED` actions. Alternatively a store manager or compliance officer voids it, supplying `target_status` of `VOIDED` together with a `void_reason`; voiding is the compensating operation for the accountability-score invariant, and after it the score returns to **exactly** its pre-issue value. Voiding never changes `training_status`, so voiding the action that caused a suspension does not by itself reinstate the associate. A void with no `void_reason`, or an empty one, is refused with `ERR_VOID_REASON_REQUIRED`; attempting either transition on an action that is already `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_ACTION_INVALID_STATE`. Because cascade-created actions have no direct creation response, the per-associate action list — filterable by `status` and by `trigger_code` — is the only channel through which a client discovers them.

### 3.3. Composing, Distributing and Superseding a Price Change File

Corporate pricing publishes retails in dated batches. A pricing administrator drafts a batch by supplying a `file_number` (globally unique, 1 to 24 characters), the `jurisdiction_id` whose unit-price and grace rules will govern every line of the batch, the `effective_date` — the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the new retails become legally binding at shelf — and `scope_store_ids`, an array of 1 to 20 distinct store ids each of which must name an existing store. An optional `priority` of `ROUTINE` (the default), `COMPETITIVE` or `REGULATORY` records the distribution urgency, and as noted in §2.7 the `REGULATORY` value is what lets a legally mandated change reach the shelf of a frozen store. A new batch begins in `DRAFT` with `line_count` and `verified_line_count` both 0, and with `distributed_at` and `superseded_by_file_id` both null. Files may be listed filtered by `jurisdiction_id`, `status`, `priority`, and by `superseded_by_file_id` — the last of which is how a client walks the self-referential supersession chain from the replacement back to what it replaced.

Lines are appended to a draft batch one at a time, each taking its `price_change_file_id` from the path segment it hangs from. A line carries the `item_upc` being repriced (8 to 14 digits, unique within its file), the `item_description` that will be printed on the shelf label, the merchandising `category_code` (1 to 24 uppercase characters) which is later matched against an aisle's category for exception reporting, `old_retail_cents` — the retail in effect before this change, zero or greater — and `new_retail_cents`, the retail that becomes binding at the effective date, which must be strictly greater than zero. The unit-price denominator is described by `net_content_qty`, a quantity of zero or greater carrying at most three decimals, and `net_content_uom`, one of `EACH`, `OZ`, `FL_OZ`, `LB`, `GAL` or `CT`. A `net_content_qty` of exactly zero is accepted, not rejected: it is the honest description of a variable-weight or bulk item, and it simply leaves the line without a displayable unit price. An optional `promo_flag`, defaulting to false, marks a line that backs a promotion requiring signage. Adding a line to a batch that has left `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_FILE_INVALID_STATE`. Lines may be listed per file and filtered by `line_status` and `category_code`, and a line begins its own lifecycle in `PENDING`.

**Computing the printed unit price.** Three fields on every line — `unit_price_display_cents`, `unit_price_basis_qty` and `unit_price_basis_uom` — are derived from the line's net content and the jurisdiction's display rulebook, and they are computed the moment the line is created and recomputed for every live line when the batch is distributed. The computation has exactly one division and exactly one rounding, in that order, and the order is normative.

The first step selects the basis, and **nothing is rounded in this step**. Each unit of measure belongs to a family: `OZ` and `LB` are weight, `FL_OZ` and `GAL` are volume, `CT` is count, and `EACH` stands alone as a single-item measure. If the jurisdiction's rule is `PER_SINGLE_ITEM`, the basis quantity is 1 and the basis label is `EACH`, whatever the line's own unit of measure says. If the rule is `PER_100_COUNT` **and** the line's unit of measure is in the count family, the basis quantity is the net content quantity divided by 100 and the basis label is `100CT`. In every other case — that is, under `PER_UNIT_MEASURE_CANONICAL`, and also under `PER_100_COUNT` when the line is not a count item — the canonical basis for the line's family applies: for a weight line the basis label is `LB` and the basis quantity is the net content quantity divided by 16 when the unit of measure is `OZ` and the net content quantity unchanged when it is already `LB`; for a volume line the basis label is `GAL` and the basis quantity is the net content quantity divided by 128 when the unit of measure is `FL_OZ` and the net content quantity unchanged when it is already `GAL`; for a count line the basis label is `CT` and the basis quantity is the net content quantity unchanged; and for an `EACH` line the basis quantity is 1 with basis label `EACH`. The two conversion constants are the sixteen ounces in a pound and the one hundred twenty-eight fluid ounces in a gallon.

If the basis quantity works out to exactly zero — reachable only when the net content quantity is zero under a rule other than `PER_SINGLE_ITEM` — the computation stops there without attempting any division: all three of `unit_price_display_cents`, `unit_price_basis_qty` and `unit_price_basis_uom` are left null and the line is still created. Otherwise the second step performs the single division: the new retail in cents divided by the basis quantity, evaluated at full precision as an exact rational with no truncation. The third step performs the single rounding. The number of decimal places retained on the **cent** figure is the jurisdiction's `unit_price_decimal_places` reduced by two, and never less than zero — so a jurisdiction printing dollars to two places rounds the cent figure to a whole number, while one printing dollars to three places retains one decimal on the cent figure. The rounding mode is the jurisdiction's own `unit_price_rounding`, either half up or ceiling. The rounded result is stored as `unit_price_display_cents`; the basis quantity is stored exactly as computed, never rounded, to as many as six fractional digits, as `unit_price_basis_qty`; and the basis label is stored as `unit_price_basis_uom`.

The pinned order is worth spelling out because getting it wrong is silent. A 26-ounce item stays 1.625 pounds as its basis quantity — never 1.63 — and a retail of 499 cents divided by that exact 1.625 gives 307.0769 and change, which rounds to 307 cents per pound. Rounding the basis quantity to two decimals first and then dividing gives 306 instead. Only 307 is correct, and the same asymmetry applies to every fluid-ounce and every fractional-count conversion in the catalogue.

**Distribution.** Distributing a batch moves it `DRAFT → DISTRIBUTED`. It requires the batch to be in `DRAFT`, refusing a second attempt with `ERR_FILE_INVALID_STATE`; it requires at least one line, refusing an empty batch with `ERR_FILE_HAS_NO_LINES`; and when the jurisdiction's `unit_price_required` is true it requires that every line of the batch can resolve a unit-price basis, refusing otherwise with `ERR_UNIT_PRICE_UNAVAILABLE`. It also has a deadline: distribution is permitted only while the current instant is strictly before the batch's effective date plus the jurisdiction's grace period — with the default 48-hour grace period, a batch effective at noon on the first may still be distributed at 11:59 on the third but not at noon on the third, and a late attempt is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_PASSED`. On success, `distributed_at` is stamped with the current instant, the unit-price fields of every line that is not `WITHDRAWN` are recomputed and re-stored, and the batch's `version` advances by one.

**Line lifecycle.** A line's `line_status` walks `PENDING`, `TASKED`, `LABELED`, `VERIFIED`, `FAILED` and `WITHDRAWN`, and every one of those transitions is driven by the system rather than requested directly. Creating the store-level execution task for a line moves it `PENDING → TASKED`, and that same event may auto-advance its owning batch `DISTRIBUTED → PARTIALLY_EXECUTED` — the batch itself changes nothing but its status, `updated_at` and `version` on that hop. When the task's print axis reaches `PRINTED`, the line moves `TASKED → LABELED` with no further effect. When the task's placement is verified, the line moves `LABELED → VERIFIED`, which in turn re-derives the batch's verified-line count and may complete the batch. When a placement is rejected instead, the line moves `LABELED → FAILED`, again with no further effect; and when the placement is subsequently re-recorded, the line moves `FAILED → LABELED`. That `LABELED → FAILED → LABELED` revision loop may repeat without bound. `VERIFIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the two terminal line states.

Completion is the fourth hop of the verification chain. A batch in `PARTIALLY_EXECUTED` advances `PARTIALLY_EXECUTED → EXECUTED` the moment a line's move into `VERIFIED` makes the verified-line count equal the line count, provided the line count is at least 1. The line count itself counts every line of the batch whose status is not `WITHDRAWN`, and the verified-line count counts those whose status is `VERIFIED` — which is why withdrawing lines can complete a batch that looked incomplete. On completion, every store named in the batch's `scope_store_ids` has its `executed_file_count` raised by one, then its label backlog recomputed, then its posture recomputed, in that order.

**Supersession.** A pricing administrator supersedes a batch by naming the replacement in `superseded_by_file_id`. The batch being superseded must be `DISTRIBUTED` or `PARTIALLY_EXECUTED`; a `DRAFT`, already-`EXECUTED` or already-`SUPERSEDED` batch is refused with `ERR_FILE_INVALID_STATE`. The named replacement must be a **different** batch, must share this batch's `jurisdiction_id`, must itself be `DRAFT` or `DISTRIBUTED`, and must carry an effective date at or after this batch's effective date; naming the batch itself, naming a batch in another jurisdiction, or naming a batch that is already superseded is refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDING_FILE_INVALID`, and naming a batch that does not exist is `ERR_PRICE_CHANGE_FILE_NOT_FOUND`.

Supersession is the widest cascade in the price-file half of the domain, and it is identical whether it fires from `DISTRIBUTED → SUPERSEDED` or from `PARTIALLY_EXECUTED → SUPERSEDED`. The pointer is stored. Every line of the batch whose status is not `VERIFIED` — that is, every line still in `PENDING`, `TASKED`, `LABELED` or `FAILED` — transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, and a withdrawn line permanently leaves every store's label backlog. For each withdrawn line, every label task on it whose placement axis is not `VERIFIED` has its print axis forced to `REPRINT_REQUIRED` with its `printed_label_serial` cleared, because the tag in the printer's tray now shows a price nobody is allowed to charge. Each affected store then recomputes its label backlog and, on that basis, its posture. The batch's `version` advances by one. Both `EXECUTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal batch states.

### 3.4. Store-Level Label Execution: Print, Place, Verify

A price change becomes real only when someone walks the aisle with a tag. The **label task** is that unit of work, and it is the busiest resource in the system: it spans four other resources, carries two independent status axes that evolve without reference to each other, and is guarded by more preconditions than anything else in the domain.

Creating a label task is the operation described at length in §2.9, and it is initiated by a store manager or a pricing administrator against the batch that owns the line. The request names `store_id` (the store that will execute the work), `price_change_line_id` (the line whose shelf tag must be replaced), `aisle_id` (the aisle holding that tag), `assigned_associate_id` (the floor associate scheduled to do it), the labour interval as `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` with the end strictly after the start, and the pinned `expected_price_change_file_version`. The aisle must belong to the named store or the request fails with `ERR_AISLE_STORE_MISMATCH`; the assigned associate must belong to the named store or it fails with `ERR_ASSOCIATE_STORE_MISMATCH`; the store must appear in the batch's scope and share the batch's jurisdiction or it fails with `ERR_STORE_OUT_OF_FILE_SCOPE`; and only one task may exist for any given line-and-store pair, a second attempt yielding `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Tasking is refused outright when the batch is neither `DISTRIBUTED` nor `PARTIALLY_EXECUTED`, which surfaces as `ERR_FILE_INVALID_STATE`.

Two scheduling rules apply to the assignee rather than to the task. Labour intervals for one assignee may not touch or overlap: because the comparison uses closed-closed semantics, a new interval that begins at the exact instant an existing non-terminal task of the same assignee ends is already a conflict, refused with `ERR_ASSOCIATE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Separately, one assignee's total scheduled minutes on a single UTC calendar day — counting the new task together with all their existing non-terminal tasks — may not exceed eight hours; the four-hundred-and-eightieth minute is fine, the four-hundred-and-eighty-first is not, and the refusal is `ERR_ASSOCIATE_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. As the fiscal-style quota at the very bottom of the ladder, this cap is the last thing checked, so a request that is both over cap and schedule-conflicting reports the conflict.

A newly created task begins with its print axis at `NOT_PRINTED`, its placement axis at `UNPLACED`, `printed_label_serial`, `placed_by_associate_id`, `verified_by_associate_id`, `placed_at`, `verified_at` and `observed_shelf_price_cents` all null, and `rejection_count` at 0. Two derived fields are computed at creation. `due_at` is the effective date of the batch that owns the line, advanced by the jurisdiction's grace period in hours — with the default grace period that is two days after the price becomes binding. `overdue` is true when the current instant is at or after `due_at` **and** the placement axis reads either `UNPLACED` or `REJECTED`; it is re-derived on every read of the task and on every placement-axis transition, and instants are compared at millisecond resolution with the boundary counted as already overdue rather than still on time. A task that has been placed or verified is never overdue no matter how late it was, which is the point: the flag measures outstanding exposure, not historical punctuality.

The two axes are genuinely independent, and no transition on one implies a transition on the other. The **print axis** takes `NOT_PRINTED`, `PRINTED` and `REPRINT_REQUIRED` and has no terminal state — a tag can always need reprinting. The **placement axis** takes `UNPLACED`, `PLACED`, `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED`, and `VERIFIED` is its terminal value. What couples them is a guard rather than a cascade: the placement axis's legal moves depend on the current value of the print axis.

**Printing.** A label associate or store manager prints the tag, moving the print axis to `PRINTED` from either `NOT_PRINTED` or `REPRINT_REQUIRED`. Printing requires the owning batch to be `DISTRIBUTED` or `PARTIALLY_EXECUTED`, refusing a draft, executed or superseded batch with `ERR_FILE_NOT_DISTRIBUTED`; it requires the store not to be `FROZEN`, refusing with `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; and it requires that the task's assigned associate is not `SUSPENDED`, refusing with `ERR_LABEL_TASK_INVALID_STATE`. On success a `printed_label_serial` is minted as the literal prefix `LBL-` followed by a zero-padded print sequence number maintained per store, and the same per-store sequence supplies the re-minted serial on a reprint. Printing also advances the underlying line `TASKED → LABELED` when the line is in `TASKED`. The placement axis is untouched by printing.

**Placing.** A label associate records the physical placement, moving the placement axis to `PLACED` from either `UNPLACED` or `REJECTED`. The request carries `observed_shelf_price_cents`, the price actually readable on the tag once it is on the rail; that observation is retained on the task and is re-stamped as the verifier's reading when the placement is subsequently verified. Placement requires the print axis to read exactly `PRINTED` — a task that was rejected and consequently needs reprinting cannot simply be re-placed, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_LABEL_NOT_PRINTED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to an associate belonging to the task's own store, otherwise `ERR_ASSOCIATE_STORE_MISMATCH`. On success, `placed_by_associate_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID` and `placed_at` from the current instant, the store's label backlog is recomputed and then its posture recomputed, and when the underlying line sits in `FAILED` it moves `FAILED → LABELED`. The `PLACED → REJECTED → PLACED` revision loop on this axis may repeat without bound.

**Verifying.** A price auditor — or a store manager acting in that capacity — verifies the placement, supplying an `outcome` of either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED` and, on the rejection path, an optional `rejection_reason`. Both outcomes require the placement axis to read exactly `PLACED`, refusing an `UNPLACED` task with `ERR_LABEL_TASK_INVALID_STATE` and a second verification of an already-`VERIFIED` task with `ERR_TRANSITION_ALREADY_APPLIED`. Both outcomes require that the verifying `X-User-ID` differs from the task's `placed_by_associate_id`, refusing self-verification with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both outcomes require the verifier's `training_status` to be `CERTIFIED` **and** their `certification_expires_at` to be strictly after the current instant, refusing an uncertified or lapsed verifier with `ERR_ASSOCIATE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. And verification must happen within 48 hours of `placed_at`; a later attempt is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

The `PLACED → VERIFIED` branch is the head of the four-hop completion chain. It stamps `verified_by_associate_id` from `X-User-ID` and `verified_at` from the current instant, and stores the observed shelf price as the verifier's reading. It then advances the underlying line `LABELED → VERIFIED` (hop two), which re-derives the batch's verified-line count and may complete the batch (hop three), which credits `executed_file_count` on every scoped store and re-derives their backlogs and postures (hop four). Within the same request it also recomputes the **placer's** `verified_placement_count` — not the verifier's — because that counter feeds the task-weighted accountability variant, and it recomputes the store's label backlog and then its posture.

The `PLACED → REJECTED` branch is the head of a shorter but sharper chain. It increments `rejection_count` by one, pushes the print axis from `PRINTED` back to `REPRINT_REQUIRED`, and pushes the underlying line `LABELED → FAILED`. When `rejection_count` reaches 2, it additionally creates an accountability action against the associate named by `placed_by_associate_id` — with `trigger_code` `VERIFICATION_REJECTED`, `action_type` `COACHING`, and `issued_by_ref` set to the literal `system` — which immediately re-derives that associate's score and may suspend them and reset their other placed tasks, exactly as §3.2 describes. It then recomputes the store's label backlog and its posture. A single rejection is treated as an honest mistake; the second one is a pattern.

One further move on this resource is never requested by anybody. When an associate is suspended, every task assigned to them that sits at `PLACED` is pushed `PLACED → UNPLACED` with `placed_by_associate_id` and `placed_at` cleared, and any task whose print axis reads `PRINTED` while its placement axis is not `VERIFIED` is pushed `PRINTED → REPRINT_REQUIRED` with `printed_label_serial` cleared. The same print-axis reset fires when a supersede withdraws the underlying line. Tasks pushed back into `UNPLACED` re-enter the store's backlog, which is why a suspension can restrict a store that was open a moment earlier.

Label tasks are discoverable through a single filtered list that collapses all six of the task's associate- and resource-valued references into query parameters: `store_id`, `price_change_line_id`, `aisle_id`, `assigned_associate_id`, `placed_by_associate_id` and `verified_by_associate_id`, plus `print_status`, `placement_status` and `overdue`.

### 3.5. Scanner Accuracy Audits and the Failure Cascade

Label execution proves that the tag was hung; only an audit proves that the tag and the register agree. A price auditor or compliance officer schedules one by naming the `store_id` being audited, the `aisle_id` whose scan window will be occupied, the `auditor_associate_id` of the price auditor conducting it, the `audit_type` — `ROUTINE`, `FOLLOW_UP` or `REGULATORY` — and the half-open occupation window as `scan_window_start` (inclusive) and `scan_window_end` (exclusive, and strictly after the start). The named aisle must belong to the named store, the named auditor must belong to that store, and the acting associate must be currently certified, which is to say `CERTIFIED` with an expiry strictly in the future.

An aisle can only be walked by one auditor at a time, and it needs a breather afterwards. Each existing window on the same aisle is therefore extended by a 60-minute cooldown after its end, and a new window conflicts when its start falls before that extended end **while** the existing window's start falls before the new window's end — considering only audits on that aisle which are not yet `CLOSED`. A conflict is refused with `ERR_AISLE_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A `ROUTINE` audit is additionally blocked while the audited store's posture is `FROZEN`, refused with `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; `FOLLOW_UP` and `REGULATORY` audits proceed whatever the posture, because a frozen store is precisely the store a regulator wants re-walked.

A new audit starts in `OPEN` with `outcome` `PENDING`, with `sample_size`, `overcharge_count`, `undercharge_count` and `reopen_count` all 0, and with `gross_error_rate_pct`, `net_overcharge_rate_pct`, `error_rate_pct`, `rate_variant_applied` and `closed_at` all null. Audits may be listed filtered by `store_id`, `aisle_id`, `auditor_associate_id`, `status`, `outcome` and `audit_type`.

**Evidence.** Only a price auditor may append a scan observation, and each observation names the `item_upc` scanned (8 to 14 digits, unique within its audit — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`), the `shelf_price_cents` read from the shelf label, and the `register_price_cents` the register returned. Both prices must be zero or greater. Three fields are derived at insert and never change afterwards: `variance_cents` is the register price minus the shelf price; `classification` is `MATCH` when the variance is exactly zero, `OVERCHARGE` when it is greater than zero, and `UNDERCHARGE` when it is less than zero; and `category_code` is copied from the audited aisle's own merchandising category at scan time, which makes it the grouping key for the exception reporting of §3.9 even if the aisle is later recategorised. A scan line is append-only evidence: it takes its `scanner_audit_id` from the path segment it hangs from, has no update or delete path, and — as §2.4 notes — carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. The first observation appended to an `OPEN` audit moves the audit `OPEN → SCANNING`; an observation appended while the audit sits in `REOPENED` moves it `REOPENED → SCANNING`, closing the audit's re-scanning cycle. Appending to a `CLOSED` audit is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`. Scan lines may be listed per audit and filtered by `classification`.

**Closing, and the two error rates.** Closing moves the audit `SCANNING → CLOSED` or `REOPENED → CLOSED` — the two source states behave identically — and it is what stamps the verdict. The audit must be in one of those two states, and it must have at least one observation; a close attempt against an audit with an empty sample is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_EMPTY`, which is also what an operator sees when they try to close an audit that never left `OPEN`, because an `OPEN` audit necessarily has zero observations. Closing a second time is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ALREADY_APPLIED`. The acting associate must be currently certified.

Two error rates are always computed and always stored, regardless of which one is going to be used. The sample size is the count of every scan line belonging to the audit, with no status filter of any kind, because the evidence is immutable and therefore the set is exactly what was posted. The overcharge and undercharge counts are the counts of those lines classified `OVERCHARGE` and `UNDERCHARGE` respectively. The **gross mismatch** rate, stored as `gross_error_rate_pct`, is the overcharge count plus the undercharge count, divided by the sample size, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places. The **net overcharge** rate, stored as `net_overcharge_rate_pct`, is the undercharge count subtracted from the overcharge count with the difference floored at zero, divided by the sample size, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places.

Which of the two governs the verdict is the request's choice. An optional `rate_variant` of `GROSS_MISMATCH` or `NET_OVERCHARGE` may be supplied on the close request; when it is omitted, the jurisdiction's `default_rate_variant` decides. The chosen variant is recorded in `rate_variant_applied`, and its value is copied into `error_rate_pct`, which is the **only** rate ever compared against the jurisdiction's threshold. The `outcome` becomes `FAIL` when `error_rate_pct` is at or above `scanner_error_rate_threshold_pct`, and `PASS` otherwise — the comparison is inclusive, so a rate exactly equal to the threshold fails. The difference between the variants is not cosmetic: gross mismatch counts every discrepancy, so undercharges that cost the customer nothing still inflate the rate, while net overcharge nets undercharges against overcharges and floors at zero, meaning an identical set of observations can pass under one variant and fail under the other. Closing also stamps `closed_at` with the current instant and refreshes the audited aisle's `last_audit_closed_at` to the same instant.

**The failure cascade.** A passing audit ends there. A failing audit initiates the deepest chain in the service, and the first hop selects a single accountable person and a single consequence. The candidate is not searched for: it is exactly the audited aisle's `assigned_associate_id`, referenced explicitly. That candidate is eligible only if the reference is non-null and their `training_status` is one of `UNTRAINED`, `IN_TRAINING` or `CERTIFIED`. When the aisle has no assigned associate, or the referenced associate is already `SUSPENDED`, **no accountability action is created at all**: the audit still closes with `FAIL`, the store's failed-audit count still rises and its posture is still recomputed, and the failure is simply unattributed.

For an eligible candidate, the action's severity is chosen by a first-match waterfall. If the candidate already has at least one accountability action with status `ISSUED` and `action_type` `WRITTEN_WARNING`, the new action's `action_type` is `SUSPENSION`. Otherwise, if the audit's applied `error_rate_pct` is at or above twice the jurisdiction's threshold — a doubled-threshold test that is likewise inclusive, so exactly 4.00 percent against a 2.00 percent threshold qualifies — the new action's `action_type` is `WRITTEN_WARNING`. Otherwise it is `COACHING`. The action is created with its `associate_id` set to the accountable candidate just selected, its `trigger_code` set to `AUDIT_FAIL`, and its `issued_by_ref` set to the literal `system`.

The second hop recomputes the candidate's `issued_action_count` and `accountability_score`. The third hop applies the derived suspension: if the recomputed score is strictly below the floor of 80, the associate transitions `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED`. The fourth hop is that suspension's own consequence, described in §3.2 and §3.4 — the associate's placed-but-unverified tasks are pushed back to `UNPLACED` and their printed tags marked for reprint. Finally, independently of whether any action was created, the store's `failed_audit_count_30d` is recomputed and then its posture recomputed, in that order. The interaction worth internalising is that the second failing audit inside thirty days is what freezes a store, while the first merely restricts it.

**Reopening.** A compliance officer may reopen a `CLOSED` audit for additional scanning, supplying a `reopen_reason` for the record. Reopening is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE` when the audit is not `CLOSED`, and also when any regulatory inspection using this audit as its evidence of record has already reached `CLOSED` — evidence that has been adjudicated cannot be retroactively rewritten. On success the verdict is withdrawn: `outcome` returns to `PENDING`, and `error_rate_pct`, `rate_variant_applied`, `gross_error_rate_pct`, `net_overcharge_rate_pct` and `closed_at` all return to null. `reopen_count` rises by one. The store's `failed_audit_count_30d` is then recomputed and its posture recomputed — a reopened failure leaves the posture inputs, so reopening can relax a frozen store back to restricted or open until the audit closes again. The audit then walks `CLOSED → REOPENED → SCANNING → CLOSED`, and that cycle may repeat.

### 3.6. Promotional Signage: Disclosures, Expiry and the Sweep

A promotional sign is a second, parallel channel through which a store can misprice an item, and it fails in two characteristic ways: it omits a disclosure the jurisdiction requires, or it stays on the endcap after the promotion ends. Both failures feed the store's posture.

A store manager or pricing administrator drafts a sign by naming the `store_id` and `aisle_id` where it will hang, a `sign_code` identifying the sign template (1 to 24 characters, unique within the store — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`), the advertised window as `promo_start_at` (inclusive) and `promo_end_at` (exclusive, strictly after the start), and `disclosure_codes`, the array of distinct disclosure codes actually printed on the sign. A sign may optionally name the `price_change_line_id` whose promotional price it advertises; that reference is nullable, and a sign with no line is a legitimate generic sign in an unrestricted store. The named aisle must belong to the named store, failing otherwise with `ERR_AISLE_STORE_MISMATCH`.

Two derived fields accompany every sign. `missing_disclosure_codes` is the set difference between the jurisdiction's `signage_disclosure_codes` and the sign's own `disclosure_codes`, compared by exact string equality and **ordered as the jurisdiction declares them** rather than sorted — if the jurisdiction lists `UNIT_PRICE` before `END_DATE` and the sign carries neither, the missing list reads `UNIT_PRICE` then `END_DATE`. `compliance_state` is the sign's derived verdict and takes one of `COMPLIANT`, `MISSING_DISCLOSURE`, `EXPIRED_NOT_REMOVED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. It is a pure projection of the sign's `status`: `NOT_APPLICABLE` while the sign is `DRAFT` or `REMOVED`, `COMPLIANT` while it is `POSTED`, `EXPIRED_NOT_REMOVED` while it is `EXPIRED_PENDING_REMOVAL`, and `MISSING_DISCLOSURE` while it is `NON_COMPLIANT`. It is re-derived at creation, on posting, on removal, on every sweep, and on every amendment of the governing jurisdiction. A newly drafted sign therefore reads `DRAFT` with `compliance_state` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `posted_at` and `removed_at` null. Signs may be listed filtered by `store_id`, `aisle_id`, `price_change_line_id`, `status` and `compliance_state`.

**Posting.** A store manager or label associate posts a `DRAFT` sign to the floor, moving it `DRAFT → POSTED`. Four gates apply. The sign must be in `DRAFT` — posting a `NON_COMPLIANT` or `REMOVED` sign is refused with `ERR_SIGNAGE_INVALID_STATE`, and posting an already-`POSTED` sign is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ALREADY_APPLIED`. The current instant must be strictly before `promo_end_at`; posting a sign whose promotion has already ended is likewise `ERR_SIGNAGE_INVALID_STATE`. Every disclosure the jurisdiction requires must be present, meaning `missing_disclosure_codes` must be empty; otherwise the post is refused with `ERR_MISSING_DISCLOSURE`. And the store's posture must permit it: a `FROZEN` store refuses the post outright, and a `RESTRICTED` store refuses it unless the request sets `override_acknowledged` to true **and** the sign names a `price_change_line_id`, both refusals carrying `ERR_STORE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. On success `posted_at` is stamped with the current instant and `compliance_state` becomes `COMPLIANT`.

**The sweep.** Signs do not expire by themselves; an operator checkpoint re-evaluates them. A store manager or compliance officer runs the sweep against a store, and it walks every non-removed sign of that store against the current instant in a **pinned order**: the expiry test runs first, and only then the disclosure test. A `POSTED` sign whose `promo_end_at` is at or before the current instant transitions `POSTED → EXPIRED_PENDING_REMOVAL` and its `compliance_state` becomes `EXPIRED_NOT_REMOVED`. A `POSTED` sign whose promotion is still running but whose `missing_disclosure_codes` is non-empty transitions `POSTED → NON_COMPLIANT` and its `compliance_state` becomes `MISSING_DISCLOSURE`. Because the expiry test runs first, a sign that is both expired **and** missing a disclosure becomes `EXPIRED_PENDING_REMOVAL`, never `NON_COMPLIANT`. Each transition recomputes the store's `signage_violation_count` and then its posture. The sweep reports what it did: a `swept_count` of signs examined, an `expired_count` and a `non_compliant_count` of transitions applied, and the store's resulting `compliance_posture`.

The `NON_COMPLIANT` state deserves a note, because it is unreachable through the sign's own inputs. A sign cannot be posted while a disclosure is missing, so the only way a posted sign acquires a missing disclosure is for a pricing administrator to add a required code to the jurisdiction after the sign went up; the next sweep then finds it deficient. That is the intended interaction between the jurisdiction amendment path of §3.1 and this lifecycle.

**Removal.** A store manager or label associate takes a sign down, moving it to `REMOVED` from any of `POSTED`, `EXPIRED_PENDING_REMOVAL` or `NON_COMPLIANT` — the three source states behave identically. Removing a sign still in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_SIGNAGE_INVALID_STATE`, and a second removal is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ALREADY_APPLIED`. Removal stamps `removed_at` with the current instant, resets `compliance_state` to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and recomputes the store's `signage_violation_count` and then its posture; when the sign was swept into a violation state, this is exactly the compensating operation that returns the store's violation count to where it stood before. `REMOVED` is the sign's only terminal state.

### 3.7. Regulatory Inspections, Findings and Penalty Settlement

When an inspector arrives, the store's exposure is computed against a specific piece of evidence rather than against its general reputation. A compliance officer logs the visit by naming the inspected `store_id`, the `scanner_audit_id` that will serve as the evidence of record, the regulator's `inspector_ref` string, and the `inspection_date` of the visit. A new inspection starts `SCHEDULED` with `finding_count`, `penalty_exposure_cents`, `refund_liability_cents`, `refund_credit_cents` and `response_round` all 0, and with `assessed_penalty_cents`, `remediation_summary` and `closed_at` all null. Inspections may be listed filtered by `store_id`, `scanner_audit_id` and `status`.

**Issuing findings.** A compliance officer transcribes the inspector's citations in one call, moving the inspection `SCHEDULED → FINDINGS_ISSUED`, and the findings are created as a cascade of that single call rather than one at a time. Each element of the supplied `findings` array carries the `finding_code` cited — one of `SHELF_TAG_MISMATCH`, `UNIT_PRICE_FORMAT`, `SIGNAGE_DISCLOSURE`, `EXPIRED_SIGNAGE` or `SCANNER_ERROR_RATE` — a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, an `affected_item_count` of at least 1, and an optional `affected_aisle_code`, which is the free-text aisle label the regulator wrote down and is deliberately **not** resolved against any aisle record. An empty `findings` array is refused with `ERR_FINDINGS_EMPTY`; issuing findings against an inspection that has left `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`; and the evidence audit must be `CLOSED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_EVIDENCE_AUDIT_NOT_CLOSED`. Each created finding takes its `regulatory_inspection_id` from the path segment of the call that issued it rather than from the body, and starts `OPEN` with `penalty_component_cents` at 0 and `remediation_action_code`, `remediation_evidence_ref` and `waiver_reason` all null. Because the findings have no creation response of their own, the per-inspection finding list — filterable by `status` and `severity` — is the only channel through which a client discovers them. Issuing findings computes every non-waived finding's penalty component, computes the inspection's exposure, re-derives `finding_count`, and advances the inspection's `version` by one.

**Computing penalty exposure.** The exposure computation has a pinned evaluation order: count the considered findings, select the tier, compute the per-finding components, sum them, scale the sum once, then cap. The considered set is every finding of the inspection whose status is not `WAIVED`, and `finding_count` is the size of that set. The **tier multiplier** is 100 percentage points when the considered set holds at most 2 findings, 125 when it holds at most 5, and 150 beyond that. An **audit aggravator** of 25 percentage points is added when the referenced evidence audit's outcome is `FAIL`, and 0 percentage points otherwise. Each considered finding's `penalty_component_cents` is its severity weight — 1 for `MINOR`, 3 for `MAJOR`, 8 for `CRITICAL` — multiplied by the jurisdiction's `penalty_per_violation_cents`, multiplied by the smaller of the finding's `affected_item_count` and the per-finding item cap of 10. The raw total is the sum of those components, taken **before** any scaling. The scaled total is that raw total multiplied by the tier multiplier plus the aggravator, divided by 100, with a single **floor** applied to the result — one floor, after the sum, never per finding, because per-finding floors would lose up to one cent per finding. The exposure finally stored as `penalty_exposure_cents` is the smaller of the scaled total and the jurisdiction's `penalty_max_cents`.

**Finding lifecycle.** A finding's `status` branches at `OPEN` into three genuinely different futures, and the three side-effect sets differ. `REMEDIATED` and `WAIVED` are terminal.

A store manager plans remediation, moving a finding to `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` from either `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` by supplying `target_status` `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` together with a `remediation_action_code`; omitting the action code is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`, and a finding in any other state is refused with `ERR_FINDING_INVALID_STATE`. The action code is stored. From `OPEN`, the store's `open_finding_count` is recomputed and the posture recomputed — the count is unchanged, because both `OPEN` and `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` are counted as open exposure, so planning remediation buys the store nothing on the posture. From `ESCALATED`, the store's `escalated_finding_count` is recomputed and the posture recomputed, and here it matters: retiring the last escalated finding can relax a `FROZEN` store to `RESTRICTED`. The `OPEN → ESCALATED → REMEDIATION_PLANNED → ESCALATED` recovery loop may repeat across response rounds.

A store manager completes remediation, moving a finding `REMEDIATION_PLANNED → REMEDIATED` by supplying `target_status` `REMEDIATED` together with a `remediation_evidence_ref`; the finding must already be in `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`, and the evidence reference is mandatory — a transition to `REMEDIATED` missing either the action code or the evidence reference is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`. The evidence reference is stored, the store's `open_finding_count` is recomputed — it decreases by one — and the posture is recomputed.

A compliance officer waives a finding, moving it `OPEN → WAIVED` by supplying `target_status` `WAIVED` together with a non-empty `waiver_reason`; a missing or empty reason is `ERR_REMEDIATION_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`, a finding not in `OPEN` is `ERR_FINDING_INVALID_STATE`, and the owning inspection must not yet be `CLOSED`. Waiving is the branch with the widest financial reach: the reason is stored, the finding's `penalty_component_cents` is zeroed, the owning inspection's `finding_count` and `penalty_exposure_cents` are recomputed — and because the waived finding leaves the considered set, that recomputation can select a **lower** tier multiplier, so waiving the third finding of three moves the tier from 125 back to 100 and reduces the exposure by more than the waived finding's own component. The store's `open_finding_count` is then recomputed and its posture recomputed.

The fourth branch is never requested directly: when an inspection's response is rejected, every finding of that inspection sitting in `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED` transitions to `ESCALATED`, and any escalated finding forces the store's posture to `FROZEN`.

**Responding and adjudicating.** A store manager submits the store's answer by supplying a `remediation_summary` together with the pinned `expected_regulatory_inspection_version`, moving the inspection to `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` from either `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `RESPONSE_REJECTED`; any other state is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`. Each submission raises `response_round` by one, overwrites `remediation_summary` with the latest narrative, and advances the inspection's `version` by one. The `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_REJECTED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` negotiation loop may repeat without bound, which is exactly why the version pin exists.

A compliance officer adjudicates a submitted response by supplying a `decision`. The inspection must be in `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`. A `decision` of `REJECT`, optionally accompanied by a `rejection_note`, moves the inspection `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → RESPONSE_REJECTED`, escalates every finding still in `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_PLANNED`, recomputes the store's `escalated_finding_count` and `open_finding_count` and then its posture — which any escalated finding forces to `FROZEN` — and advances the inspection's `version` by one.

A `decision` of `ACCEPT` closes the inspection, and it is refused unless every finding has left `OPEN`: an inspection with even one untouched finding yields `ERR_FINDING_INVALID_STATE`. The request may optionally supply an `assessed_penalty_cents` override, which may not exceed the freshly recomputed exposure; a higher override is refused with `ERR_PENALTY_OVERRIDE_TOO_HIGH`.

**The five-step settlement.** On acceptance, the settlement runs exactly once, top to bottom, with no iteration and no feedback from a later step into an earlier one. The dependency *looks* circular — the penalty depends on the finding tier, the refund credit depends on the penalty, and the refund programme is itself what closes findings — so the order below is normative.

Step one recomputes `penalty_exposure_cents` from the inspection's **current** findings, using the tier and cap rules above. Step two computes `refund_liability_cents` as the sum of the claimed amounts of every refund claim whose scan line belongs to this inspection's evidence audit and whose status is `APPROVED` or `PAID`; claims that are `PENDING` or `DENIED` are excluded. Step three computes `refund_credit_cents` as the smaller of two independently floored candidates: seventy-five percent of the refund liability, floored; and fifty percent of the penalty exposure, floored. Each candidate is floored on its own before the smaller is chosen. Step four sets `assessed_penalty_cents`: if the adjudication supplied an override, that override is used verbatim; otherwise it is the refund credit subtracted from the penalty exposure, with the difference floored at zero. Step five raises the store's `cumulative_penalty_exposure_cents` by the assessed penalty. The credit computed in step three never re-enters step one's tier selection. The inspection then stamps `closed_at` with the current instant, advances its `version` by one, moves to `CLOSED` — its only terminal state — and the store's posture is recomputed.

### 3.8. Overcharge Refunds at the Service Desk

An overcharge is not settled by correcting the tag; it is settled by paying the customer. A customer service desk operator, or a store manager, files a claim against one immutable scan observation by supplying the `scan_line_id` that evidences the overcharge, a `customer_ref` identifying the customer at the desk, and the pinned `expected_scanner_audit_version`. The claim's `store_id` is never supplied by the client: it is resolved server-side from the scan line's owning audit at creation, so a claim can never be attributed to the wrong store.

Four gates guard creation, in the ladder order of §2.9. The scan line's owning audit must be `CLOSED`, refusing an in-flight audit with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_CLOSED`. The scan line's `classification` must be `OVERCHARGE` — that is, its `register_price_cents` must exceed its `shelf_price_cents` — refusing a matching or undercharged line with `ERR_NOT_AN_OVERCHARGE`. Only one claim may exist per scan-line-and-customer pair, a repeat yielding `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`; a second, different customer may legitimately claim against the same observation. And the store's outstanding refund liability plus this claim's computed amount may not exceed the jurisdiction's `refund_budget_per_audit_cents`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_REFUND_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Being a fiscal check, the budget test is evaluated last of the four.

**Computing what is owed.** The amount is derived from the scan line and the jurisdiction's `overcharge_refund_policy`, and the policy branch actually used is recorded on the claim as `policy_applied` so that a later audit of the audit can reproduce the arithmetic. The variance is the scan line's register price minus its shelf price — always a positive number here, since a non-positive variance was refused upstream. Under `REFUND_DIFFERENCE_ONLY` the gross amount is that variance alone. Under `REFUND_DIFFERENCE_PLUS_FIXED` the gross amount is the variance plus the jurisdiction's `refund_fixed_bonus_cents` goodwill payment. Under `ITEM_FREE_UP_TO_CAP` the customer keeps the item, so the gross amount is the full `register_price_cents` the customer was charged. In all three branches the amount finally stored as `claimed_amount_cents` is the smaller of the gross amount and the jurisdiction's `refund_cap_cents`; the cap is applied last and is inclusive, so a gross amount exactly equal to the cap is paid in full. A new claim starts `PENDING` with `denial_reason` and `paid_at` null, and claims may be listed filtered by `scan_line_id`, `store_id` and `status`.

**Settling.** Settlement is a single operation driven by a `target_status`, with an optional `denial_reason` on the denial path, and its three branches are separated by role. A store manager approves a `PENDING` claim, moving it `PENDING → APPROVED`; the store's outstanding liability is unchanged by approval, because `PENDING` and `APPROVED` are both outstanding. A store manager denies a claim, moving it to `DENIED` from either `PENDING` or `APPROVED` — the two source states behave identically — storing the `denial_reason` and recomputing the store's `refund_liability_outstanding_cents`, from which this claim's amount now departs. Denial is the compensating operation of this ledger, and a denial with no `denial_reason`, or an empty one, is refused with `ERR_DENIAL_REASON_REQUIRED`. A customer service desk operator pays an `APPROVED` claim, moving it `APPROVED → PAID`, stamping `paid_at` with the current instant, and recomputing both the store's `refund_liability_outstanding_cents` — from which the amount now leaves — and its `refund_paid_total_cents`, into which the amount now enters. Requesting payment on a claim whose status is anything other than `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_REFUND_NOT_APPROVED`; any other disallowed settlement transition, such as approving a denied claim or denying a paid one, is refused with `ERR_REFUND_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`; and repeating a settlement that has already been applied, such as paying an already-`PAID` claim, is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ALREADY_APPLIED`. `PAID` and `DENIED` are the two terminal states.

Approved and paid claims are also what earns a store credit against its regulatory penalty, through step two of the settlement in §3.7 — which is the commercial argument for approving quickly rather than litigating each claim.

### 3.9. Exception Reporting and Root-Cause Remediation

The last workflow closes the improvement loop: instead of asking which items were mispriced, it asks which **categories** keep going wrong, and who owns fixing them. A compliance officer or store manager generates a report by naming the `store_id` and a half-open reporting window as `period_start_at` (inclusive) and `period_end_at` (exclusive, strictly after the start). Only one report may exist per store-and-period triple, a repeat yielding `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Generation aggregates evidence rather than opinions. The qualifying audits are this store's audits whose status is `CLOSED` and whose closing instant falls at or after `period_start_at` and strictly before `period_end_at`; audits sitting in `OPEN`, `SCANNING` or `REOPENED` are excluded entirely, which means reopening an audit removes it from reports generated afterwards. `audit_count` is the number of qualifying audits and `scan_line_count` is the total number of scan lines belonging to them. Those scan lines are then grouped by their stored `category_code`, and within each group the `sample_size` is the number of lines, the `error_count` is the number of lines whose `classification` is anything other than `MATCH`, and the `error_rate_pct` is the error count divided by the sample size, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Only groups whose sample size is at least 2 are eligible for the ranking — a single scan line is anecdote, not evidence. The eligible groups are then ordered by error rate descending; ties are broken by error count descending; remaining ties are broken by `category_code` ascending in lexicographic byte order. Entity ids never participate in the ordering, so the ranking is fully deterministic. The ordered list is stored as `category_breakdown`, each element carrying `category_code`, `sample_size`, `error_count` and `error_rate_pct`. The first element supplies `top_category_code` and `top_category_error_rate_pct`. When no category is eligible — because the window caught no closed audits, or every category had a single line — the report is **not** created and the request fails with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_AUDIT_DATA`.

A new report starts `GENERATED` with `root_cause_code`, `remediation_owner_associate_id` and `closure_note` null. Reports may be listed filtered by `store_id`, `remediation_owner_associate_id` and `status`.

**Assigning the root cause.** A compliance officer or store manager moves the report `GENERATED → REMEDIATION_OPEN` by supplying the `root_cause_code` — one of `LABEL_NOT_PLACED`, `LABEL_PRINT_ERROR`, `FILE_LATE_DISTRIBUTION`, `SIGNAGE_NOT_REMOVED`, `REGISTER_FILE_MISMATCH` or `TRAINING_GAP` — together with the `remediation_owner_associate_id` who will own the fix. Assigning a root cause twice is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`. The named owner must belong to the report's own store, failing otherwise with `ERR_ASSOCIATE_STORE_MISMATCH`. And the second segregation-of-duties rule of this domain applies here: the owner must not be the auditor of **any** audit counted in the report's period, because the person who measured the problem may not also be the person who certifies it fixed; a violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

One root cause carries a cascade. When `root_cause_code` is `TRAINING_GAP`, the assignment additionally creates an accountability action against the owner with `trigger_code` `EXCEPTION_ROOT_CAUSE`, `action_type` `RETRAINING_ASSIGNED` and `issued_by_ref` set to the literal `system`; the owner's `accountability_score` is then recomputed, and if it falls strictly below the floor of 80 the owner transitions `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with all the label-task consequences of §3.2. The other five root causes store the assignment and nothing more.

**Closing.** A compliance officer or store manager closes a report whose remediation is complete by supplying a `closure_note`, moving it `REMEDIATION_OPEN → CLOSED`. Closing a report still in `GENERATED` is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`. When the root cause was `TRAINING_GAP`, closure carries one extra precondition: the accountability action created by the assignment cascade must have reached `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED`, so a training gap cannot be declared closed while the retraining it mandated is still outstanding; an action still `ISSUED` blocks the closure with `ERR_ACTION_INVALID_STATE`. `CLOSED` is the report's only terminal state.
