# Veterans Education Benefit Certification API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university's school certifying officials sit at the narrowest point of a very wide funnel. On one side is the Department of Veterans Affairs, which pays tuition and a monthly housing allowance only against enrollment that has been certified precisely — the right credit hours, the right term dates, the right benefit chapter, the right entitlement charge. On the other side is the veteran student, whose registration, account balance and academic standing must never be damaged because the institution reported something late or because VA's payment has not yet cleared. Between those two sides sits a state approving agency that can withdraw a program's approval mid-term, an annual tuition cap that spans several terms, a Yellow Ribbon pool that several students draw on at once, and a debt-reconciliation process that must decide whether an overpayment is the student's problem or the school's.

This document specifies the HTTP service that carries that workload. It covers the full arc of a certification: registering a veteran student and verifying their Certificate of Eligibility, keeping program catalogs compliant with the state approving agency, drafting and submitting an enrollment certification, pricing the covered tuition portion, certifying enrollment to VA with the resulting housing-allowance and entitlement arithmetic, matching an unmet tuition gap against a Yellow Ribbon agreement, and reporting a change in enrollment so that any overpayment is reconciled, attributed and either collected or waived. It also specifies the statutory guardrail that runs across all of it: while VA's payment for a certified term is outstanding, the institution is barred from penalising the student.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **money and time are exact**. Every monetary amount is an integer count of cents, every day count is a whole number of days, and every rounding step happens at a pinned point in a pinned order — the specification is explicit about which quantity is rounded and when, because the same inputs rounded in a different order produce a different answer and a different debt. Second, **authority is divided**. The official who prepares a certification may not be the official who certifies it, and the official who reconciles a change report may not be the official who waives the debt that reconciliation creates. The service enforces both separations at the request level rather than trusting the client to observe them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this service is mounted at the root path, so cross-entity links travel in request bodies rather than in nested URLs. The single nested path in the surface is the read-only audit sub-resource of an enrollment certification.

### 2.1. Officials, Agencies, and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the caller, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are:

- **`SchoolCertifyingOfficial`** — the front-line school certifying official. Registers veteran students, verifies certificates of eligibility, drafts, revises and submits enrollment certifications, files changes in enrollment, and sends debt notifications. This role never certifies.
- **`SupervisorySCO`** — the supervisory certifying official. Owns the approval side of the workflow: certifies, returns and voids certifications, reconciles amendments, waives debts, and maintains academic terms, programs, catalog versions and Yellow Ribbon agreements. A `SupervisorySCO` may also perform everything a `SchoolCertifyingOfficial` may perform, and may advance the logical clock.
- **`BursarOfficer`** — the student accounts officer. Prices the covered tuition portion, posts student account charges, sets and releases registration holds, and books repayments against benefit debts.
- **`StateApprovingAgent`** — the State Approving Agency reviewer. Decides catalog version approvals and suspends previously approved catalog versions.
- **`VaProcessingOffice`** — the VA regional processing office operator. Credentials certifying officials, expires certificates of eligibility, records what VA actually disbursed, escalates unnotified debts, and advances the logical clock.
- **`Student`** — the veteran student. May read their own student record and their own debts, and must acknowledge a debt notification.

Three of the six roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those the header ties directly to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolCertifyingOfficial`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CertifyingOfficial`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CertifyingOfficial` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `SupervisorySCO`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `CertifyingOfficial`, and the same comparison applies. When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `VeteranStudent`, and ownership checks compare the resource's student foreign key to `X-User-ID`.

The other three roles are not backed by any persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `BursarOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, as `actor_user_id` on a `CertificationAuditEntry` written by an action that role performs) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `StateApprovingAgent`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, recorded where an audit entry captures the actor but never resolved against an entity. When `X-User-Role` is `VaProcessingOffice`, the same holds: the identifier is opaque, may be recorded as `actor_user_id`, and is not matched against any `id`.

Because `SchoolCertifyingOfficial` and `SupervisorySCO` are entity-backed, three additional gates apply to every endpoint whose authorised roles include either of them. The identifier in `X-User-ID` must resolve to a `CertifyingOfficial` row, or the request fails with `ERR_OFFICIAL_NOT_REGISTERED` (HTTP 403). That official's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_OFFICIAL_INACTIVE` (HTTP 403). And the official's `official_level` must agree with the claimed tier — `SUPERVISORY` when `X-User-Role` is `SupervisorySCO`, and `SCO` when `X-User-Role` is `SchoolCertifyingOfficial` — or the request fails with `ERR_OFFICIAL_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

The `Student` role is confined to its own data. On the four operations where a student may act or read — reading one veteran student by path id, listing benefit debts, reading one benefit debt by path id, and acknowledging a debt notification — `X-User-ID` must equal the `VeteranStudent` identifier the target resource belongs to. For the single-student read that means the path id itself; for a single debt read and for acknowledgement it means the debt's `student_id`; and for the debt list it means that the `student_id` filter supplied on the query string must equal `X-User-ID`. Any deviation is rejected with `ERR_STUDENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0 when omitted. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, a negative `offset`, or either parameter carrying a value that is not a base-ten integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list responses are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp in ascending order, with rows sharing an identical `created_at` broken by the order in which the server wrote them. The one documented exception is the certification audit trail, which is returned in ascending `sequence_index` order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of items matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching item remains beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Collection Reads and Filter Vocabulary

Because the topology is flat, every foreign key is exposed as a list filter so that related rows are reachable from any parent identifier. Filters combine conjunctively, and each filter accepts exactly the value domain of the field it names: a filter naming an enum-typed field rejects any value outside that enum with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), while a filter naming a free-text or identifier field is matched by exact string equality and is not otherwise validated.

- Certifying officials may be filtered by `official_level` and by `status`.
- Veteran students may be filtered by `student_number`, `chapter`, `coe_status` and `account_hold_status`.
- Academic terms may be filtered by `academic_year`, `term_type` and `status`.
- Programs may be filtered by `approval_status`, `degree_level` and `approved_catalog_version_id`.
- Catalog versions may be filtered by `program_id`, `review_status` and `catalog_year`.
- Yellow Ribbon agreements may be filtered by `academic_year`, `degree_level` and `status`.
- Enrollment certifications may be filtered by `student_id`, `term_id`, `program_id`, `pinned_catalog_version_id`, `submitted_by_official_id`, `certified_by_official_id` and `status`.
- Tuition and fee reports may be filtered by `certification_id` and `status`.
- Yellow Ribbon awards may be filtered by `certification_id`, `agreement_id` and `status`.
- Enrollment change reports may be filtered by `certification_id`, `reconciled_by_official_id`, `change_type`, `reporting_status` and `status`.
- Benefit debts may be filtered by `student_id`, `certification_id`, `change_report_id`, `waived_by_official_id`, `debtor_type`, `collection_status` and `notification_status`.
- Student account charges may be filtered by `student_id`, `term_id` and `charge_type`.
- The certification audit trail may be filtered by `event_type`.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Certification Audit Trail

Every resource in this service carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix. Alongside it, resources carry `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

All resources except the audit trail additionally carry two system-managed fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits that is refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version` is an integer that is 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation. A mutation for this purpose includes the writing of derived fields: when a tuition and fee report rewrites a certification's covered amount, or when a reconciliation rewrites a certification's housing and tuition figures, or when an award creation debits an agreement's pool, the affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, even though the client did not address that row directly.

The `CertificationAuditEntry` is the exception. It is append-only and immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`: both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from an audit entry. Each entry records the `certification_id` of the certification it documents, an `event_type`, the `actor_role` and `actor_user_id` of whoever caused it, a human-readable `detail` summary, and a `sequence_index`. The `sequence_index` is a per-certification counter that starts at 1 and is assigned in ascending `created_at` order of the entries on that certification.

An entry's `event_type` is one of `CERTIFICATION_SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFICATION_RETURNED`, `CERTIFICATION_CERTIFIED`, `CERTIFICATION_AUTO_RETURNED`, `CERTIFICATION_VOIDED`, `AMENDMENT_OPENED`, `AMENDMENT_RECONCILED`, `DEBT_CREATED`, `DEBT_WAIVED`, `VA_PAYMENT_RECORDED` or `AWARD_REVERSED`. Its `actor_role` is one of the six role names — `SchoolCertifyingOfficial`, `SupervisorySCO`, `StateApprovingAgent`, `BursarOfficer`, `VaProcessingOffice`, `Student` — or the literal `system` when the entry was written by a cascade rather than by a direct request. In that cascaded case `actor_user_id` is likewise the literal string `system`. Note that `actor_user_id` is deliberately a raw string rather than a foreign key, precisely because the opaque roles present identifiers that match no persisted row.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Date Semantics

Every date-dependent rule in this service — the protection window, the certification deadline, change-report lateness, and the debt notification deadline — reads a single server-held logical UTC clock rather than real wall-clock time. At startup that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must not fall back to real time either before or after it is set.

The clock is advanced through the administrative test-driving capability described in §4, which takes a single `to_iso8601` instant. The clock never moves backwards: if the supplied instant is earlier than the clock's current value, the request is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422). A value equal to the current clock is accepted. Because advancing the clock can change whether a student is still inside a protection window, every successful advance recomputes `benefit_payment_protected` for every veteran student in the system; here alone a student row is stamped — `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented — only when that recomputation actually changes the stored snapshot, whereas the per-operation recomputations listed in §3.2 stamp the student row unconditionally. A successful advance responds `200 OK` with an empty object in `data` rather than with any resource.

Calendar dates supplied as inputs — `delimiting_date`, `coe_issued_on`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `drop_deadline`, `certification_deadline`, `effective_start`, `effective_end`, `term_start_certified`, `term_end_certified`, `new_term_end_certified`, `effective_date` and `posted_on` — are ISO 8601 dates at `00:00:00Z`, are echoed back on every read exactly as the client supplied them, and all date comparisons and day-count arithmetic in this document operate on whole calendar days. A calendar date the server itself computes — `coe_verified_on`, `protected_until`, `reported_on`, `notification_due_on`, `notified_on` and `acknowledged_on` — is rendered as a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` string.

### 2.7. Cents, Days, and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this service is an integer number of cents, both on input and on output, and never a decimal or a string. Whenever a monetary computation would produce a fraction of a cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent — a half-cent rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude.

The same discipline governs the non-monetary derived quantities. `rate_of_pursuit_pct` and `rop_tier_pct` are integer percentages produced by rounding half up. `certified_days`, `entitlement_days_charged` and `days_late` are whole day counts; `days_late` may legitimately be negative when a change is reported in advance of its effective date, and no rounding is involved in producing it.

Where a formula rounds more than once, the number of rounding steps and their position are part of the specification and not an implementation detail. Two places in particular are pinned: the term housing-allowance total is rounded once per calendar-month segment and only then summed, and the prorated entitlement charge is rounded once on each side of a change's effective date and only then summed. Collapsing either into a single rounding over the whole period produces a different answer, and the different answer is wrong.

### 2.8. The Order of Rejection

When more than one rule would reject a request, the outcome is determined by a single global ladder. Validations are evaluated in ascending priority order and the **first** violated rule decides the response; no later rule can mask an earlier one. The ladder is:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and the role is one of the six declared names, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** `X-User-Role` is listed among the target endpoint's authorised roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** The body parses, every required input field is present, every field carries its declared type, and every enum-typed field carries a declared member, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). An absent or empty body is read as an empty JSON object, a body that is not a JSON object is malformed, and a field explicitly set to `null` counts as absent — malformed when the field is required, and taking its declared default when it is optional. Applies to every endpoint, and also covers out-of-range pagination parameters. The single shape check that does **not** sit at this priority is the mark-overdue due-date check of §3.9, which is evaluated only after that endpoint's own notification-status gate.
4. **Official registered.** For every endpoint whose authorised roles include `SchoolCertifyingOfficial` or `SupervisorySCO`, `X-User-ID` resolves to a `CertifyingOfficial`, else `ERR_OFFICIAL_NOT_REGISTERED` (403).
5. **Official active.** On the same set of endpoints, that official's `status` is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_OFFICIAL_INACTIVE` (403).
6. **Official level.** On the same set of endpoints, that official's `official_level` matches the claimed tier, else `ERR_OFFICIAL_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (403).
7. **Path identifiers.** Every id embedded in the URL path resolves, validated left to right, else HTTP 404 carrying the code for the first unresolved id: `ERR_OFFICIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATALOG_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TUITION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANGE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEBT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`.
8. **Student scope.** On reading one veteran student, listing benefit debts, reading one benefit debt and acknowledging a debt notification, a `Student` caller is confined to their own data, else `ERR_STUDENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
9. **Body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, validated in the fixed order `student_id`, then `term_id`, then `program_id`, then `certification_id`, then `agreement_id`, else the matching HTTP 404 code. This applies to certification creation, catalog version creation, tuition and fee report creation, Yellow Ribbon award creation, change report creation and student account charge creation.
10. **Certification version check.** On tuition and fee report creation, `expected_certification_version` equals the referenced certification's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATION_VERSION` (412).
11. **Agreement version check.** On Yellow Ribbon award creation, `expected_agreement_version` equals the referenced agreement's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (412).
12. **State gates.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists. Cross-entity state gates belong to this same band and are evaluated in the order the transition lists them, always after the target resource's own status: on certify, the student's `coe_status` is checked immediately after the certification's own status; on submit, the term's `status` is checked; and on award creation, the certification's `status` is checked before the agreement's `status`. The codes in this band all carry HTTP 409 and are `ERR_OFFICIAL_ALREADY_INACTIVE`, `ERR_COE_ALREADY_VERIFIED`, `ERR_COE_NOT_VERIFIED`, `ERR_TERM_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_PENDING_SAA`, `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_SUSPENDABLE`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_EDITABLE`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TERMINAL`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_PROVISIONAL`, `ERR_AWARD_ALREADY_REVERSED`, `ERR_CHANGE_REPORT_ALREADY_RECONCILED`, `ERR_DEBT_ALREADY_SETTLED`, `ERR_DEBT_ALREADY_NOTIFIED`, `ERR_DEBT_NOT_NOTIFIED`, `ERR_TERM_CLOSED` and `ERR_AGREEMENT_CLOSED`.
13. **Certifying segregation of duties.** On certify, `X-User-ID` differs from the certification's `submitted_by_official_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422).
14. **Program approval.** On certify, the certification's program is in an approval posture that permits certification, else `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_APPROVED` (422).
15. **Student protection.** While the student's live protection predicate is true, no penalty charge may be posted, no registration hold may be set, and no change report may carry a punitive-grade intent, else `ERR_PROTECTED_STUDENT_PENALTY` (422). This rule guards exactly three operations: student account charge creation, setting or releasing a registration hold, and change report creation.
16. **Catalog compliance.** On submitting a catalog version to the state approving agency, all three catalog compliance attestations are true, else `ERR_CATALOG_COMPLIANCE_INCOMPLETE` (422).
17. **Waiver segregation of duties.** On waiving a debt, `X-User-ID` differs from the `reconciled_by_official_id` of the change report identified by the debt's `change_report_id`, else `ERR_WAIVER_SEGREGATION` (422).
18. **Certification deadline.** On certify, the logical clock date is on or before the term's `certification_deadline`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (422).
19. **Delimiting date.** On submit, `term_end_certified` is on or before the student's `delimiting_date`, else `ERR_DELIMITING_DATE_PASSED` (422).
20. **Certified dates inside the term.** On submit, `term_start_certified` is on or after the term's `start_date` and `term_end_certified` is on or before the term's `end_date`, else `ERR_CERTIFIED_DATES_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422).
21. **Change date inside the certified period.** On change report creation, `effective_date` falls within `term_start_certified` through `term_end_certified` inclusive, else `ERR_CHANGE_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422).
22. **Enrollment period overlap.** On submit, the certified period collides with no other live certification of the same student, else `ERR_OVERLAPPING_ENROLLMENT_PERIOD` (409).
23. **Catalog window overlap.** On catalog version creation, the new effective window collides with no other live catalog version of the same program, else `ERR_CATALOG_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
24. **Effective catalog version.** On submit, an approved catalog version of the program covers the certified start date, else `ERR_CATALOG_VERSION_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (422).
25. **Agreement scope.** On award creation, the agreement's `academic_year` equals the certified term's `academic_year` and the agreement's `degree_level` equals the certified program's `degree_level`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (422).
26. **Unique student number.** On veteran student creation, `student_number` is not already used by another veteran student, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_NUMBER` (409).
27. **Unique term code.** On academic term creation, `term_code` is not already used by another term, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERM_CODE` (409).
28. **Unique program code.** On program creation, `program_code` is not already used by another program, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE` (409).
29. **Unique official email.** On certifying official creation, `email` is not already used by another official, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_OFFICIAL_EMAIL` (409).
30. **Term date ordering.** On academic term creation, `start_date` is strictly before `end_date`, `drop_deadline` is on or after `start_date` and on or before `end_date`, and `certification_deadline` is on or after `start_date`, else `ERR_INVALID_TERM_DATES` (422).
31. **Credit split.** `credit_hours_online` does not exceed `credit_hours_total`, and on a change report `new_credit_hours_online` does not exceed `new_credit_hours`, else `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_SPLIT` (422). This rule guards exactly three operations: certification creation, certification revision and change report creation.
32. **Tuition report present.** On certify, a tuition and fee report for the certification is in force, else `ERR_TUITION_REPORT_MISSING` (422).
33. **Yellow Ribbon student eligibility.** On award creation, the certification's student is entitled under chapter `CH33` at a benefit level of 100 percent, else `ERR_YR_INELIGIBLE_STUDENT` (422).
34. **One award per certification.** On award creation, no live award already exists for the certification, else `ERR_AWARD_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409).
35. **VA payment recorded once.** On recording a VA payment, the certification's `tuition_payment_posted_on` is still null, else `ERR_VA_PAYMENT_ALREADY_RECORDED` (409).
36. **Clock monotonicity.** On advancing the clock, `to_iso8601` is on or after the current logical clock value, else `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (422).
37. **Yellow Ribbon participant limit.** On award creation, the agreement's `participant_count` is strictly less than its `max_participants`, else `ERR_YR_PARTICIPANT_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).
38. **Yellow Ribbon pool.** On award creation, the agreement's `pool_remaining_cents` is strictly greater than zero, else `ERR_YR_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (422).
39. **Entitlement sufficiency.** On certify, the entitlement days the certification would charge do not exceed the student's `entitlement_days_remaining`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ENTITLEMENT` (422).
40. **Repayment within balance.** On booking a repayment, `amount_cents` does not exceed the debt's `outstanding_cents`, else `ERR_REPAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (422).

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Ledgers

Two write operations in this service overwrite state that another writer may already have advanced, and both require the client to prove it is working from a current view. In each case the version field is a **required** input, not an optional hint, and its absence is a malformed request under priority 3.

Creating a tuition and fee report must carry `expected_certification_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `EnrollmentCertification`. If that value does not equal the certification's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new report legitimately supersedes the prior report in force and rewrites the certification's covered amount; a writer holding a stale view of the certification would silently discard a pricing revision that another writer had already had accepted.

Creating a Yellow Ribbon award must carry `expected_agreement_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `YellowRibbonAgreement`. If that value does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Award creation debits the agreement's remaining pool and increments its participant count, so a writer whose view of the agreement predates an already-accepted award would compute a school share from a pool balance that no longer exists.

No other endpoint in this service takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two approvals in this service are deliberately split between two people, and in both cases the comparison is made on `X-User-ID` at request time.

A certification is prepared and submitted by one official and certified by another. The submitting official's identifier is retained on the certification as `submitted_by_official_id`, and the certifying official's identifier is retained as `certified_by_official_id`. An attempt to certify a certification that the same official submitted is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A debt is created by reconciling a change report and may be waived only by a different official. The reconciling official's identifier is retained on the change report as `reconciled_by_official_id`, and the waiving official's identifier is retained on the debt as `waived_by_official_id`. An attempt to waive a debt by the same official who reconciled the change report identified by that debt's `change_report_id` is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_SEGREGATION`.

### 2.11. Cascades, Parallel State Dimensions, and Ledger Invariants

Some rows in this service carry two independent lifecycle dimensions on the same record, and the two dimensions never constrain one another. A `VeteranStudent` carries `coe_status`, which tracks entitlement verification, alongside `account_hold_status`, which tracks the bursar's registration hold; setting or releasing a hold leaves `coe_status` untouched, and verifying or expiring a certificate of eligibility leaves `account_hold_status` untouched. A `BenefitDebt` carries `collection_status` alongside `notification_status`; a debt may be fully satisfied while its notification is still pending, and a debt may be acknowledged while still wholly unpaid.

Two operations propagate consequences beyond the row the client addressed, and the propagation is part of the contract rather than a courtesy. Suspending a catalog version walks down through the affected program to that program's in-flight certifications and appends one audit entry per certification it returns. Reconciling a change report walks from the report to the certification, from the certification to the student's entitlement ledger, from the recomputed amounts to any overpayment debt, and from a vanished tuition gap to any provisional Yellow Ribbon award. Both chains are specified step by step in §3. Every row touched anywhere in a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, in the same way as a directly addressed row.

Two ledgers must balance at all times, and both are maintained by paired debits and credits rather than by recomputation from scratch. First, a student's `entitlement_days_remaining` starts equal to `entitlement_days_granted`, which is never mutated after creation; it is debited by the entitlement days a certification charges and credited back by exactly the difference when reconciliation revises that charge, so that the remaining figure always equals the granted figure minus the sum of entitlement currently charged. Second, an agreement's `pool_remaining_cents` starts equal to `annual_pool_cents`, which is never mutated after creation; it is debited by an award's school share when the award is created and credited by exactly that same school share when the award is reversed, while `participant_count` counts the agreement's awards that are provisional or final and moves by one in step with each of those events.

### 2.12. Reference Resolution Order

Identifier resolution follows a fixed order so that a request carrying several bad references always fails on the same one. Path identifiers are resolved first, strictly left to right, and the first path segment that does not resolve produces its own HTTP 404 code; a request against the audit trail of a non-existent certification therefore fails with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` before the audit collection is ever consulted. Only once every path identifier resolves does the server resolve the foreign keys carried in the request body, and those are resolved in the fixed order `student_id`, `term_id`, `program_id`, `certification_id`, `agreement_id`, regardless of the order in which the client happened to serialise them.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Credentialing Officials and Registering Veteran Students

Nothing in this service can happen until the VA regional processing office has credentialed at least one certifying official, which is why official creation is reserved to `VaProcessingOffice` — the officials table has to be bootstrapped before any official exists to bootstrap it. Credentialing supplies the official's `full_name`, the institutional `email` under which VA corresponds with them, and the authority tier `official_level`, which is either `SCO` for a front-line certifying official or `SUPERVISORY` for a supervisory one. An `email` already carried by another official is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OFFICIAL_EMAIL`. The new official's `status` is not a client input: it is set by the server to `ACTIVE`, and its only other possible value is `INACTIVE`.

Revoking credentials moves an official `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`. The operation is available only to `VaProcessingOffice`, and an official already sitting at `INACTIVE` cannot be deactivated again — that attempt is refused with `ERR_OFFICIAL_ALREADY_INACTIVE`. `INACTIVE` is terminal; there is no reinstatement path in this surface. The consequence is felt everywhere else: from that moment on, any request whose `X-User-ID` equals the deactivated official's `id` is refused with `ERR_OFFICIAL_INACTIVE`, and because that check sits at priority 5 it fires before any resource is located and before any business rule is consulted.

A veteran student is registered by either certifying-official role. The registration carries the institutional `student_number`, the student's `full_name`, the benefit `chapter` under which they are entitled — one of `CH30`, `CH31`, `CH33`, `CH35` or `CH1606` — and the `benefit_level_pct` printed on their Certificate of Eligibility, which VA issues only at 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 or 100 — any other value is a malformed request refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Entitlement itself arrives as `entitlement_days_granted`, which must be at least 1 and which is never mutated after creation; it is the fixed ceiling against which everything is later charged. The `delimiting_date` marks the date after which entitlement may no longer be used. A `student_number` already carried by another veteran student is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_NUMBER`.

One registration field is optional. The `coe_issued_on` date printed on the Certificate of Eligibility may be supplied at registration or omitted, in which case it is stored as null; it becomes mandatory later, at the moment the certificate is actually verified. Everything else on a fresh student record is server-initialised: `entitlement_days_remaining` opens equal to `entitlement_days_granted`, `coe_status` opens at `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `account_hold_status` opens at `NONE`, `benefit_payment_protected` opens false, `open_debt_principal_cents` opens at 0, and `coe_verified_on`, `verified_document_reference` and `protected_until` all open null.

The `open_debt_principal_cents` figure deserves an early word because it is a running total rather than a stored input. It is the sum of `principal_cents` across that student's benefit debts whose `debtor_type` is `STUDENT` and whose `collection_status` is either `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_REPAID`, and the service maintains it by increment and decrement as debts are raised, repaid and waived rather than by recomputing it on read.

### 3.2. Certificates of Eligibility and the Protection Window

Entitlement verification is the gate through which a student must pass before any enrollment can be certified to VA. A `SchoolCertifyingOfficial` or `SupervisorySCO` performs it, and it is permitted only when the student's `coe_status` is `NOT_SUBMITTED` or `EXPIRED`; a certificate already sitting at `VERIFIED` cannot be verified again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COE_ALREADY_VERIFIED`. Verification requires both the `coe_issued_on` date printed on the certificate and a `verified_document_reference` identifying the document the official actually inspected — the field that was optional at registration is not optional here.

A successful verification therefore arrives by one of two symmetric paths, `NOT_SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` for a first verification and `EXPIRED → VERIFIED` for a re-verification, and both paths carry identical conditions and identical effects. A successful verification moves `coe_status` to `VERIFIED`, records the supplied issue date and document reference, and stamps `coe_verified_on` with the current logical clock date. It then opens the statutory protection window by setting `protected_until` to 90 days after `coe_verified_on`, and finally recomputes the student's `benefit_payment_protected` snapshot. VA may later withdraw verification following an entitlement review: `VaProcessingOffice` moves the certificate `VERIFIED → EXPIRED`, which is permitted only from `VERIFIED` and otherwise refused with `ERR_COE_NOT_VERIFIED`. Expiry deliberately leaves `coe_verified_on`, `protected_until`, `coe_issued_on` and `verified_document_reference` exactly as they were — the historical record of when verification happened is not erased — and recomputes `benefit_payment_protected`, which necessarily becomes false because the predicate requires a verified certificate.

An expired certificate can be verified again, and the re-verification is the same operation with the same side effects, closing the cycle `VERIFIED → EXPIRED → VERIFIED`. The one thing that differs is that `protected_until` restarts from the new `coe_verified_on`: a re-verified student gets a fresh 90 days measured from the new verification date, not the remainder of the old window.

The protection itself derives from 38 USC 3679(e), which bars an institution from penalising a student because VA has not yet paid. The service reduces that statute to a three-part predicate that must all hold at once for a student to count as protected: their `coe_status` is `VERIFIED`; the current logical clock date is on or before their `protected_until`, with the boundary day itself still inside the window; and at least one enrollment certification exists for that student whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` and whose `tuition_payment_posted_on` is still null. The third conjunct is what makes the protection about an outstanding payment rather than about verification alone — once VA's money has posted for the only live certification, the student is no longer protected even though the certificate remains verified and the window remains open.

`benefit_payment_protected` on the student record is a snapshot of that live predicate, and the two must never be allowed to diverge. Accordingly, every operation capable of changing any of the three conjuncts rewrites the snapshot for the affected student or students: verifying a certificate, expiring a certificate, certifying an enrollment, recording VA's disbursement against a certification, and advancing the logical clock — the last of which rewrites it for every student in the system, since the clock's movement can push any of them past their `protected_until`.

While a student is protected, three things the institution might otherwise do are forbidden. It may not post a student account charge whose `charge_type` is `LATE_FEE` or `REINSTATEMENT_FEE`. It may not place the student's `account_hold_status` at `REGISTRATION_HOLD`. And it may not file a change in enrollment whose `punitive_grade_flag` is true. All three refusals carry `ERR_PROTECTED_STUDENT_PENALTY`, and all three are evaluated against the live predicate rather than against a possibly stale snapshot.

### 3.3. Terms, Programs, and State Approving Agency Review

An academic term is the calendar container for certification, and only a `SupervisorySCO` may create one. A term is defined by its registrar `term_code`, which must be unique across terms and is otherwise refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERM_CODE`, by an `academic_year` label such as AY2026-2027 that later serves as the scope key for both the annual tuition cap and Yellow Ribbon agreements, and by a `term_type` of `FALL`, `SPRING`, `SUMMER` or `WINTER`. Four dates complete it: `start_date` is the first instructional day, `end_date` is the last instructional day and is inclusive, `drop_deadline` is the last day a student may drop without a punitive grade, and `certification_deadline` is the last day an enrollment certification for the term may be certified. Those dates must be mutually consistent — `start_date` strictly earlier than `end_date`, `drop_deadline` on or after `start_date` and on or before `end_date`, and `certification_deadline` on or after `start_date` — and any violation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TERM_DATES`. A new term opens with `status` at `OPEN`.

Closing a term moves it `OPEN → CLOSED`, is reserved to `SupervisorySCO`, and cannot be repeated: a term already at `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_TERM_ALREADY_CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is terminal and there is no reopening. The cross-entity consequence is that submitting any enrollment certification for a closed term is thereafter refused with `ERR_TERM_CLOSED`; note that closure blocks submission specifically, and does not by itself disturb certifications that were already submitted or certified.

A program of study is likewise a `SupervisorySCO` creation. It carries a unique `program_code` — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE` — the program `name` as printed in the catalog, and a `degree_level` of `UNDERGRAD`, `GRADUATE` or `CERTIFICATE`. Two numeric attributes make the program the arithmetic basis of every certification against it: `full_time_credit_hours` is the credit-hour load the institution defines as full time for this program and must be at least 1, and `campus_bah_monthly_cents` is the monthly Basic Allowance for Housing rate for the campus ZIP code at the E-5-with-dependents level, expressed in cents and at least 0. A new program opens with `approval_status` at `DRAFT` and `approved_catalog_version_id` null. The `approval_status` dimension moves among `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SAA`, `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONAL` and `SUSPENDED` — but never in response to a request aimed at the program. A program has no direct action of its own; every one of its transitions is a downstream consequence of something that happened to one of its catalog versions.

Catalog versions are where compliance with the state approving agency actually lives. A `SupervisorySCO` creates one against a `program_id` with a `catalog_year` label such as 2026-2027 and a governing window given by `effective_start` and `effective_end`. That window is **half-open**: `effective_start` is the first day the version governs and is inclusive, while `effective_end` is the day the version stops governing and is exclusive; no ordering rule is enforced between the two endpoints, and the pair is stored exactly as supplied. Three boolean attestations form the compliance checklist — `has_published_tuition_schedule` for a published tuition and fee schedule, `has_refund_policy` for a pro-rata refund policy, and `has_program_length_disclosure` for a program length and credit-hour disclosure. A new version opens with `review_status` at `DRAFT` and with both `conditions_note` and `suspension_reason` null. Because the window semantics are half-open, so is the collision rule: a new version may not overlap any other catalog version of the same program whose `review_status` is `PENDING_SAA`, `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, and a collision is refused with `ERR_CATALOG_WINDOW_OVERLAP` — but two windows that merely touch, where the earlier version's `effective_end` equals the new version's `effective_start`, are perfectly legal and must be accepted.

Submitting a catalog version to the agency is a `SupervisorySCO` action available from `review_status` `DRAFT`, `REJECTED` or `SUSPENDED`, and from nowhere else; any other posture is refused with `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. All three compliance attestations must be true at that moment, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CATALOG_COMPLIANCE_INCOMPLETE`. The submission moves the version to `PENDING_SAA`, and when it comes from `SUSPENDED` it also clears the stored `suspension_reason`; the response carries the catalog version rather than the program the submission also moved. All three entry points are otherwise identical in their conditions and effects: `DRAFT → PENDING_SAA` is a first submission, while the two non-`DRAFT` entry points are the recovery cycles of this machine — `REJECTED → PENDING_SAA` is resubmission after a rejection, and `SUSPENDED → PENDING_SAA` is reinstatement after a suspension.

Submission also reaches sideways into the program, and this is the first of the service's cascades. Whatever the program's `approval_status` was — `DRAFT`, `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `SUSPENDED` — it is set to `PENDING_SAA`, because an open agency re-review suspends the program's certifiable posture regardless of where it stood before. When the program was previously `APPROVED`, its `approved_catalog_version_id` stays pinned to the version the agency approved earlier; re-review does not unpin history. The practical effect on the certification desk is immediate and often surprising: an already-approved program that re-enters review will refuse every certify attempt with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_APPROVED` until the agency decides, which makes `APPROVED → PENDING_SAA → APPROVED` a genuine round trip rather than a formality.

The agency's decision is recorded by a `StateApprovingAgent` and is permitted only while the version sits at `PENDING_SAA`; anything else is refused with `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_PENDING_SAA`. The `decision` supplied selects one of three branches, and each branch has its own distinct set of consequences rather than a shared one. Whichever branch is taken, the response carries the catalog version rather than the program, a `conditions_note` supplied on a branch that does not store it is silently discarded, and the version's stored `suspension_reason` is left untouched:

- A `decision` of `APPROVED` moves the version `PENDING_SAA → APPROVED`. The program is set to `approval_status` `APPROVED`, and the program's `approved_catalog_version_id` is pinned to this catalog version's `id`. This is the branch that unlocks certification.
- A `decision` of `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` moves the version `PENDING_SAA → APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` and stores the accompanying `conditions_note`; that note is required on this branch, and its absence is a malformed request refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The program is set to `approval_status` `CONDITIONAL` — a distinct posture from `APPROVED`, though one that still permits certification — and the program's `approved_catalog_version_id` is pinned to this catalog version's `id`.
- A `decision` of `REJECTED` moves the version `PENDING_SAA → REJECTED`. Here the program is set to `approval_status` `DRAFT` and its `approved_catalog_version_id` is cleared to null: rejection unpins the catalog rather than pinning it.

Suspension is the agency's enforcement tool, and it is the widest cascade in the service. A `StateApprovingAgent` supplies a `suspension_reason`, and the action is available only from `review_status` `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`; from any other posture it is refused with `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_SUSPENDABLE`. Both entry points behave identically, and the chain runs four levels deep. At the first level the catalog version itself moves to `SUSPENDED` and stores the supplied `suspension_reason`, and it is that version, not any program, that the response returns. At the second level, because that version was suspended, every program whose `approved_catalog_version_id` equals this catalog version's `id` is set to `approval_status` `SUSPENDED` — a program in that position may have been sitting at either `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, and both are pulled to `SUSPENDED`, while its `approved_catalog_version_id` stays pinned to the suspended version rather than being cleared. Those programs are walked in ascending `created_at` order. At the third level, because those programs became suspended, every enrollment certification whose program is one of them **and whose `status` is exactly `SUBMITTED`** is moved to `RETURNED` with `return_reason` set to `PROGRAM_SUSPENDED`; this level is deliberately conditional, and certifications sitting at `DRAFT`, `RETURNED`, `CERTIFIED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, `TERMINATED` or `VOID` are left entirely untouched. At the fourth level, because those certifications were returned, one audit entry is appended per auto-returned certification with `event_type` `CERTIFICATION_AUTO_RETURNED`, `actor_role` `system` and `actor_user_id` the literal `system`, and those entries are appended in ascending `created_at` order of the certifications they document.

### 3.4. Drafting, Revising, and Submitting an Enrollment Certification

An enrollment certification is drafted by either certifying-official role and starts life entirely private to the institution. Its creation payload names the three rows it binds together — the `student_id` of the veteran being certified, the `term_id` of the term, and the `program_id` of the program of enrollment — and states the enrollment itself: `credit_hours_total` must be strictly greater than 0 and permits halves — any number above 0 is accepted and no half-step granularity is enforced — `credit_hours_online` is the portion of that total delivered fully online and must be at least 0, and `term_start_certified` and `term_end_certified` bracket the student's certified enrollment period with the end date inclusive, no ordering rule being enforced between the two at draft time. An online portion exceeding the total is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_SPLIT`. The new certification opens with `status` at `DRAFT` and `amount_basis` null, with `pinned_catalog_version_id`, `submitted_by_official_id`, `certified_by_official_id`, `return_reason` and `tuition_payment_posted_on` all null, and with `mha_term_total_cents`, `net_covered_cents`, `ay_cap_remaining_before_cents`, `va_tuition_cents`, `cap_charged_cents`, `yr_gap_cents`, `entitlement_days_charged`, `tuition_paid_cents` and `mha_paid_cents` all 0; over its life its `status` moves among `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, `CERTIFIED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, `TERMINATED` and `VOID`, of which the last two are terminal.

Creation immediately derives the enrollment's shape, and the derivation is rerun identically on every revision. First, `certified_days` is the inclusive count of days in the certified period: the whole number of days from `term_start_certified` to `term_end_certified`, plus one, so that a period whose start and end fall on the same day counts as one day. Next, `rate_of_pursuit_pct` expresses the load as an integer percentage of the program's own definition of full time — take `credit_hours_total`, multiply by 100, divide by the program's `full_time_credit_hours`, and round the result half up to a whole percent. From that figure `rop_tier_pct` is derived by rounding the rate of pursuit half up to the nearest multiple of ten and then capping the outcome at 100, so a rate of pursuit of 115 percent still yields a tier of 100.

The `training_time` band is read off the **unrounded** rate of pursuit, not off the tier. It is `FULL_TIME` when `rate_of_pursuit_pct` is at least 100; otherwise `THREE_QUARTER` when it is at least 75; otherwise `HALF_TIME` when it is at least 50; otherwise `QUARTER_TIME` when it is at least 25; and otherwise `LESS_THAN_QUARTER`. The `modality` band is read off the credit split: it is `ONLINE_ONLY` when `credit_hours_online` equals `credit_hours_total`, otherwise `RESIDENTIAL` when `credit_hours_online` is exactly 0, and otherwise `MIXED`.

Housing-allowance eligibility is settled at the same moment, and it is narrow. `mha_eligible` is true only when the student's `chapter` is `CH33` **and** their `rate_of_pursuit_pct` is strictly greater than 50 — the comparison is strict, so a student pursuing at exactly 50 percent earns no housing allowance at all, whereas one at 51 percent does. The monthly rate follows from that. When `mha_eligible` is false the housing base is 0 cents. When it is true and the `modality` is `ONLINE_ONLY`, the housing base is the nationally set online rate of 99650 cents per month, which is $996.50 and takes no account of where the campus is. When it is true and the modality is anything else, the housing base is the program's own `campus_bah_monthly_cents`. From the base, `mha_monthly_rate_cents` is obtained by multiplying the housing base by the student's `benefit_level_pct` and by `rop_tier_pct`, dividing by 10000, and rounding half up — a single rounding applied after **both** percentage multiplications, in that pinned order. Rounding after the first multiplication and again after the second is a different computation and produces a different rate.

Revision is permitted while the certification's `status` is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED`, and from any other status it is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_EDITABLE`. A revision may supply any subset of `credit_hours_total`, `credit_hours_online`, `term_start_certified` and `term_end_certified`, overwrites whichever of them it supplies, and then recomputes the whole derived block above from the resulting values. A revision applied while the certification sits at `DRAFT` leaves it at `DRAFT`. A revision applied while it sits at `RETURNED` moves it `RETURNED → DRAFT` and clears the stored `return_reason`, which is how a rejected certification re-enters the drafting stage. The credit-split rule applies to revisions exactly as it does to creation.

Submission is the handoff from preparation to review, and it is where the certification is checked against the world outside it. Six conditions must hold, and they are evaluated in the §2.8 ladder order given here, which puts the delimiting-date check ahead of the inside-the-term check. The certification's own `status` must be `DRAFT` or `RETURNED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. The term's `status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_TERM_CLOSED`. The certified period must not run past the student's entitlement expiry: `term_end_certified` on or before the student's `delimiting_date`, else `ERR_DELIMITING_DATE_PASSED`. The certified period must sit inside the term: `term_start_certified` on or after the term's `start_date` and `term_end_certified` on or before the term's `end_date`, else `ERR_CERTIFIED_DATES_OUTSIDE_TERM`. The certified period must not collide with another live period for the same student, else `ERR_OVERLAPPING_ENROLLMENT_PERIOD`. And an approved catalog version must actually govern the start of the period, else `ERR_CATALOG_VERSION_NOT_EFFECTIVE`.

The two interval rules at submission use deliberately different semantics, and confusing them is the classic error. Enrollment-period collision is judged on a **closed** interval scoped to the student: no other enrollment certification of the same student whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` may share **any** day with the period running from `term_start_certified` through `term_end_certified` inclusive, and a merely touching endpoint counts as a collision — if another certification's `term_end_certified` falls on exactly this certification's `term_start_certified`, the submission is rejected. Catalog governance, by contrast, is judged on the **half-open** window established in §3.3: exactly one catalog version of the certification's program whose `review_status` is `APPROVED` or `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` must satisfy `effective_start` on or before `term_start_certified` and `effective_end` strictly after `term_start_certified`. That resolved version is captured on the certification as `pinned_catalog_version_id`, so the catalog the enrollment was certified under is a matter of record even if the catalog is later replaced or suspended.

A successful submission moves the certification `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`, records the acting official's `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_official_id`, pins the resolved catalog version, clears `return_reason` when the submission came from `RETURNED`, and appends an audit entry with `event_type` `CERTIFICATION_SUBMITTED`. The two entry paths are otherwise identical in both their conditions and their consequences.

Supervisory review can send a submitted certification back. Returning it is a `SupervisorySCO` action available only while the `status` is `SUBMITTED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED` — and it takes the `return_reason` the supervisor wants the front-line official to act on. It moves the certification `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, stores that reason, and appends an audit entry with `event_type` `CERTIFICATION_RETURNED`. Together with revision and resubmission this forms the service's revision loop: `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, repeatable as often as review requires.

A certification that is never going to be certified can instead be voided. Voiding is a `SupervisorySCO` action permitted from `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` and refused elsewhere with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_EDITABLE`. It moves the certification to `VOID`, which is terminal, and appends an audit entry with `event_type` `CERTIFICATION_VOIDED`. No compensating bookkeeping is needed on this path, because a certification that never reached `CERTIFIED` never wrote an entitlement charge or a monetary amount in the first place. Both terminal states are absolute: once a certification's `status` is `TERMINATED` or `VOID`, every state-changing operation against it — submitting, certifying, returning, voiding, recording a VA payment, or opening a further change in enrollment — is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TERMINAL`.

### 3.5. Pricing the Covered Portion

Pricing is the bursar's job, not the certifying official's, and it is recorded as a tuition and fee report against a certification. A `BursarOfficer` supplies the `certification_id` being priced together with four amounts, each of which must be at least 0: `gross_tuition_cents` is the gross tuition assessed for the certified period, `mandatory_fees_cents` is the fees charged to every student in the program, `tuition_specific_aid_cents` is aid restricted to tuition and fees, and `non_tuition_aid_cents` is aid that is not so restricted. The report also carries the mandatory `expected_certification_version` described in §2.9, which is consumed by the version check alone and is not itself stored on or echoed back from the report row. Pricing imposes no state gate of its own on the certification: a report may be created against a certification in any `status`, terminal ones included.

From those four figures the report derives its own `net_covered_cents`: add `gross_tuition_cents` and `mandatory_fees_cents`, subtract `tuition_specific_aid_cents`, and floor the result at 0 so that aid exceeding the assessment yields zero rather than a negative covered amount. The exclusion of `non_tuition_aid_cents` is deliberate and load-bearing: aid that the donor did not restrict to tuition and fees does not reduce the portion VA covers, and it must not be subtracted here even though it is collected and stored.

Only one report is ever in force for a certification. A report is created with `status` `REPORTED`, and in the same operation any earlier report for the same `certification_id` that was still `REPORTED` is moved `REPORTED → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal — so a certification always has exactly one report in force and an arbitrarily long trail of superseded ones behind it. Acceptance of a report also writes the derived covered amount onto the certification itself as the certification's `net_covered_cents`, and because that is a mutation of the certification row it increments the certification's `version` by one. That version bump is exactly what the concurrency guard protects: a second bursar working from a view of the certification taken before the first report landed will find their `expected_certification_version` no longer current and will be turned away with `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATION_VERSION` rather than silently discarding the pricing revision that was already accepted.

### 3.6. Certifying Enrollment to VA: Housing, Covered Tuition, and Entitlement

Certification is the moment the institution tells VA what to pay, and it belongs exclusively to the `SupervisorySCO`. Seven conditions are evaluated, and they are evaluated in this order so that the reason a certification cannot proceed is always the earliest reason. The certification's own `status` must be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`. Immediately after that, and within the same state-gate band, the student's `coe_status` must be `VERIFIED`, else `ERR_COE_NOT_VERIFIED`. The acting official's `X-User-ID` must differ from the certification's `submitted_by_official_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The program's `approval_status` must be either `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` — a program at `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SAA` or `SUSPENDED` cannot support certification — else `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_APPROVED`. The current logical clock date must be on or before the term's `certification_deadline`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A tuition and fee report with `status` `REPORTED` must exist for the certification, else `ERR_TUITION_REPORT_MISSING`. And the entitlement charge the certification is about to make must not exceed the student's `entitlement_days_remaining`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ENTITLEMENT`.

Once those conditions pass, the certification moves `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`, records the acting official's `X-User-ID` as `certified_by_official_id`, and stamps `amount_basis` with `AS_CERTIFIED` — that field, whose only values are `AS_CERTIFIED` and `AS_RECONCILED`, tells any later reader which variant of the arithmetic produced the amounts currently stored on the row. Three computations then run, the student's entitlement ledger is debited, the student's protection snapshot is refreshed, and an audit entry with `event_type` `CERTIFICATION_CERTIFIED` is appended.

**The housing allowance owed for the term.** `mha_term_total_cents` is not the monthly rate multiplied by a number of months; it is built by segmenting the certified period, pricing each segment, and summing. The period from `term_start_certified` through `term_end_certified` inclusive is cut at every calendar-month boundary that falls inside it, producing segments that are themselves inclusive of their own first and last day, and those segments are taken in ascending order of start date. Each segment is then assigned a billable-day count under a rule that treats a whole calendar month as a uniform 30 days: if a segment begins on the first calendar day of its month **and** ends on the last calendar day of that same month, its billable days are 30 regardless of whether the month actually has 28, 29, 30 or 31 days; otherwise its billable days are the whole number of days from its start to its end, plus one. Each segment's amount is then the monthly rate multiplied by that segment's billable days, divided by 30, and **rounded half up at the segment level**. Only after every segment has been rounded are the segment amounts summed into `mha_term_total_cents`. The order is pinned and matters: summing all billable days first and rounding once at the end is a different computation that differs by whole cents, and it is not what this service specifies. When `mha_eligible` is false, `mha_term_total_cents` is 0 and no segmentation is performed at all.

**The covered tuition VA will pay.** This is a waterfall of seven steps evaluated in a pinned order, because the dependency graph only looks circular: what VA pays is limited by the annual cap ledger, the ledger is charged by what VA pays, and the Yellow Ribbon gap is whatever the cap did not cover. The order resolves it. Step one takes the covered amount straight from the tuition and fee report in force — add its `gross_tuition_cents` and `mandatory_fees_cents`, subtract its `tuition_specific_aid_cents`, floor at 0 — and stores it as the certification's `net_covered_cents`. That figure is computed once here and is never afterwards reduced by any VA payment computed downstream; there is no second pass and no fixed point. Step two accumulates what this student has already charged against the annual cap: sum the `cap_charged_cents` of every **other** enrollment certification of the same student whose term carries the same `academic_year` as this term and whose `status` is `CERTIFIED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `TERMINATED`, accumulated in ascending `created_at` order. Note that a terminated certification still counts — withdrawing from a term does not release the cap it consumed. Step three sets `ay_cap_remaining_before_cents` to the statutory annual cap of 2790000 cents, that is $27,900.00 per academic year, minus that accumulated total, floored at 0. Step four takes the smaller of `net_covered_cents` and `ay_cap_remaining_before_cents` as the capped base. Step five sets `va_tuition_cents` to that capped base multiplied by the student's `benefit_level_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up. Step six sets `cap_charged_cents` equal to `va_tuition_cents` and to nothing else. Step seven sets `yr_gap_cents` to `net_covered_cents` minus `va_tuition_cents` — the unmet tuition gap that Yellow Ribbon may later match.

**The entitlement the term consumes.** At certification, `entitlement_days_charged` is a single rounding over the whole inclusive period at a single tier: multiply `certified_days` by `rop_tier_pct`, divide by 100, and round half up. That figure is what the seventh precondition above tests against `entitlement_days_remaining`, and once the certification succeeds the student's `entitlement_days_remaining` is debited by exactly it, leaving remaining entitlement equal to granted entitlement minus everything currently charged.

Certifying also flips the student's protection on. Because a freshly certified certification necessarily has a null `tuition_payment_posted_on`, the third conjunct of the protection predicate is satisfied, and provided the certificate is verified and the window is still open the recomputed `benefit_payment_protected` becomes true.

The protection is switched off again when VA's money actually arrives. `VaProcessingOffice` records the disbursement against a certified certification, supplying `tuition_amount_cents` and `mha_amount_cents` as the amounts VA actually paid and `posted_on` as the date the tuition payment posted. Those are written to the certification as `tuition_paid_cents`, `mha_paid_cents` and `tuition_payment_posted_on` respectively, the student's `benefit_payment_protected` is recomputed — losing its third conjunct for this certification — and an audit entry with `event_type` `VA_PAYMENT_RECORDED` is appended. A disbursement may be recorded only once per certification: a second attempt, recognisable because `tuition_payment_posted_on` is no longer null, is refused with `ERR_VA_PAYMENT_ALREADY_RECORDED`. Beyond the terminal-state guard of §3.4 the recording imposes no state gate of its own: the certification's `status` is not required to be `CERTIFIED`, and the only postures that block the recording are `TERMINATED` and `VOID`, which are refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TERMINAL`.

The complete history of a certification is readable as its append-only audit trail, returned in ascending `sequence_index` order and filterable by `event_type`, which lets a reviewer reconstruct who submitted, who certified, what was auto-returned by an agency suspension, and where every debt and reversal came from.

### 3.7. Yellow Ribbon Matching

A Yellow Ribbon agreement is the institution's standing commitment to split an unmet tuition gap with VA, and a `SupervisorySCO` creates one. It is scoped by the `academic_year` it covers and the `degree_level` it covers, and bounded three ways: `per_student_annual_cap_cents` is the most the agreement will contribute to any one student within that academic year, `annual_pool_cents` is the total school share available under the agreement for the year and is never mutated after creation, and `max_participants` is the greatest number of live awards the agreement may carry and must be at least 1. Both money figures must be at least 0. A new agreement opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `pool_remaining_cents` equal to `annual_pool_cents`, and `participant_count` at 0.

Closing an agreement moves it `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, and an agreement already at `CLOSED` cannot be closed again — that attempt is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is terminal, and thereafter any attempt to create a new award against the agreement is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_CLOSED`. Closure is forward-looking only: awards already `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` on a closed agreement are untouched and may still be finalized or reversed.

Creating an award is a `SupervisorySCO` action naming the `certification_id` whose gap is being matched, the `agreement_id` being drawn on, and the mandatory `expected_agreement_version`. The guards stack up in the ladder order of §2.8. The version check comes first. Then the state gates, in the order the transition lists them: the certification's `status` must be `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and only then is the agreement's `status` checked for `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_CLOSED`. Then scope: the agreement's `academic_year` must equal the `academic_year` of the certification's term and the agreement's `degree_level` must equal the `degree_level` of the certification's program, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Then student eligibility, which is deliberately narrow: Yellow Ribbon is available only to a student whose `chapter` is `CH33` and whose `benefit_level_pct` is exactly 100, and any other combination is refused with `ERR_YR_INELIGIBLE_STUDENT`. Then uniqueness: no award whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` may already exist for that certification, else `ERR_AWARD_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Then the two fiscal gates, in this order: the agreement's `participant_count` must be strictly less than its `max_participants`, else `ERR_YR_PARTICIPANT_LIMIT_REACHED`, and its `pool_remaining_cents` must be strictly greater than 0, else `ERR_YR_POOL_EXHAUSTED`.

The award's amounts are the eighth and final step of the covered-tuition waterfall, and they are all snapshots taken at creation time. `yr_gap_basis_cents` records the certification's `yr_gap_cents` as it stood when the award was created. `per_student_remaining_before_cents` is this student's headroom under this agreement: take the agreement's `per_student_annual_cap_cents`, subtract the sum of `school_share_cents` across every award on the same agreement belonging to the same student whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, accumulated in ascending `created_at` order, and floor the result at 0. `pool_remaining_before_cents` records the agreement's `pool_remaining_cents` immediately before this award. From those three, `school_share_cents` is the **smallest** of: half of `yr_gap_cents` rounded half up, the per-student headroom, and the pool headroom. VA then matches the school dollar for dollar, so `va_match_cents` is always exactly equal to `school_share_cents`. Whatever the two contributions together fail to cover falls to the student: `student_out_of_pocket_cents` is `yr_gap_cents` minus `school_share_cents` minus `va_match_cents`, floored at 0.

One consequence of that structure needs stating plainly, because it is easy to get wrong. The `va_match_cents` is VA money paid toward tuition, but it is deliberately **not** charged against the annual tuition cap and it never enters the accumulation of prior cap charges that governs later terms in the same academic year. Only `cap_charged_cents`, which equals `va_tuition_cents`, touches that ledger.

Award creation debits the agreement: `pool_remaining_cents` falls by `school_share_cents`, `participant_count` rises by one, and the agreement's `version` increments by one. The new award opens at `status` `PROVISIONAL`.

Once VA confirms the match, a `SupervisorySCO` finalizes the award, moving it `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`; an award not at `PROVISIONAL` is refused with `ERR_AWARD_NOT_PROVISIONAL`. Finalization moves no money: the agreement's `pool_remaining_cents` and `participant_count` are unchanged, because both `PROVISIONAL` and `FINAL` awards are already counted as committed.

Reversal is the release valve, and it is available from either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`; an award already `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_AWARD_ALREADY_REVERSED`. The acting `SupervisorySCO` supplies a `reversal_reason`. Reversal moves the award to `REVERSED`, which is terminal, stores the reason, credits the agreement's `pool_remaining_cents` back by exactly the award's `school_share_cents`, decrements `participant_count` by one, increments the agreement's `version`, and appends an audit entry with `event_type` `AWARD_REVERSED` against the certification the award was made for. Both entry paths, `PROVISIONAL → REVERSED` and `FINAL → REVERSED`, behave identically.

### 3.8. Changes in Enrollment, Reconciliation, and Overpayment Debt

Students drop courses and withdraw from terms, and VA must be told within a deadline. A change in enrollment is reported by either certifying-official role against the `certification_id` of a certification whose `status` is `CERTIFIED`; against anything else the report is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The report states its `change_type` — `CREDIT_HOUR_REDUCTION`, `COMPLETE_WITHDRAWAL` or `TERM_DATE_CHANGE` — and the `effective_date` on which the change takes effect, which must fall within `term_start_certified` through `term_end_certified` inclusive and is otherwise refused with `ERR_CHANGE_DATE_OUTSIDE_TERM`. It also carries the `mitigating_circumstances_flag`, set true when VA-acceptable mitigating circumstances are on file.

Which of the remaining inputs matter depends entirely on the `change_type`. For a `CREDIT_HOUR_REDUCTION` the report must supply `new_credit_hours` and `new_credit_hours_online`, the credit load and its online portion after the change; both are ignored for the other two change types. For a `TERM_DATE_CHANGE` the report must supply `new_term_end_certified`, the replacement inclusive end date; it is ignored for the other two change types. Ignored here means ignored by every computation rather than rejected: all three fields are stored on the report and echoed back exactly as supplied, and stand null when omitted. Where a new credit split is supplied, `new_credit_hours_online` may not exceed `new_credit_hours`, and a violation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_SPLIT`. Two further inputs are optional. `punitive_grade_flag` defaults to false and states whether the institution intends to assign a punitive grade for the withdrawal; when it is true the student's live protection predicate must be false, or the report is refused with `ERR_PROTECTED_STUDENT_PENALTY`. `disbursed_tuition_aid_cents` defaults to 0 and records the tuition aid already disbursed to the student at the time of the change; it is informational for liability review and does not enter any computation.

Timeliness and liability are settled at the moment of reporting and never revisited. `reported_on` is stamped with the current logical clock date. `days_late` is the whole number of days from `effective_date` to `reported_on`, which is negative when the change is reported in advance of taking effect. The `reporting_status` is `TIMELY` when `days_late` is at most 30 and `LATE` otherwise — the comparison is non-strict, so reporting on exactly the thirtieth day after the effective date is still timely, and the thirty-first day is the first late day. From that, `liability_party` is `STUDENT` when the report is `TIMELY` and `SCHOOL` when it is `LATE`: an institution that missed the reporting deadline owes the resulting overpayment itself rather than passing it to the veteran. Both fields flip together at the same boundary. The report opens at `status` `REPORTED`.

Filing the report also opens the amendment. The certification moves `CERTIFIED → AMENDMENT_PENDING`, and — importantly — its stored amounts stay on the `AS_CERTIFIED` basis for now; nothing is recomputed until a supervisor reconciles. An audit entry with `event_type` `AMENDMENT_OPENED` is appended.

Reconciliation is a `SupervisorySCO` action and the deepest cascade in the service. Two conditions guard it: the change report's `status` must be `REPORTED`, else `ERR_CHANGE_REPORT_ALREADY_RECONCILED`, and the certification's `status` must be `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The response carries the reconciled change report rather than the recomputed certification. The chain then runs as follows.

At the first level the change report itself moves `REPORTED → RECONCILED`, which is terminal, and the acting official's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `reconciled_by_official_id` — the identifier that later bars that same official from waiving the resulting debt.

At the second level, because the report was reconciled, the certification is recomputed on the `AS_RECONCILED` basis. The derived enrollment block is rebuilt first: `rate_of_pursuit_pct`, `rop_tier_pct`, `training_time`, `modality`, `mha_eligible` and `mha_monthly_rate_cents` are recomputed exactly as at drafting, but from the post-change credit load where the change supplied one. The certification's own stored inputs are left alone: `credit_hours_total`, `credit_hours_online`, `term_start_certified`, `term_end_certified` and the derived `certified_days` all keep their as-certified values — even for a `TERM_DATE_CHANGE`, whose replacement end date is read from the report and never copied onto the certification. Then the housing total, the covered tuition and the entitlement charge are recomputed under the reconciled variants described below, `amount_basis` is set to `AS_RECONCILED`, and the certification's `status` is set by `change_type`: a `CREDIT_HOUR_REDUCTION` or a `TERM_DATE_CHANGE` returns it `AMENDMENT_PENDING → CERTIFIED`, so the cycle `CERTIFIED → AMENDMENT_PENDING → CERTIFIED` may repeat for successive changes, while a `COMPLETE_WITHDRAWAL` moves it `AMENDMENT_PENDING → TERMINATED`, which is terminal.

The housing total is rebuilt under the reconciled variant of the same segmentation rule used at certification, with two differences. First, `effective_date` is added as a cut point alongside the calendar-month boundaries, so one term can carry two different monthly rates. Second, each segment is priced at the rate appropriate to its side of the change: a segment whose end falls before `effective_date` is priced at the **pre-change** `mha_monthly_rate_cents`, and a segment whose start falls on or after `effective_date` is priced at the **post-change** rate. Segments are still rounded individually before being summed, and a whole calendar month still bills as 30 days. Eligibility is applied per segment through those rates, which means that housing already earned before the change is never retroactively erased: if the recomputed post-change `mha_eligible` is false, so that the post-change rate is 0, the segments before `effective_date` still carry the pre-change rate. The period being segmented also depends on the change: for a `COMPLETE_WITHDRAWAL` it ends on the day before `effective_date`, for a `TERM_DATE_CHANGE` it ends on `new_term_end_certified`, and for a `CREDIT_HOUR_REDUCTION` it still ends on `term_end_certified`.

The covered tuition is rebuilt by replacing only the first step of the waterfall and rerunning the rest unchanged. For a `COMPLETE_WITHDRAWAL` with `mitigating_circumstances_flag` false, tuition is prorated to attendance: count the whole days from `term_start_certified` to `effective_date` with the effective date itself **excluded**, then set `net_covered_cents` to the as-certified covered amount multiplied by that attended-day count, divided by the as-certified `certified_days`, rounded half up. For a `COMPLETE_WITHDRAWAL` with `mitigating_circumstances_flag` true, `net_covered_cents` keeps the as-certified covered amount in full — VA pays the whole assessed tuition, which is precisely what mitigating circumstances buy the student. For a `CREDIT_HOUR_REDUCTION` or a `TERM_DATE_CHANGE`, `net_covered_cents` likewise keeps the as-certified covered amount, because the tuition was already assessed and the assessment did not change. Steps two through seven then rerun exactly as at certification, producing fresh `ay_cap_remaining_before_cents`, `va_tuition_cents`, `cap_charged_cents` and `yr_gap_cents`. Identical input data therefore yields a different `va_tuition_cents` under the two bases whenever the prorated withdrawal branch applies.

The entitlement charge is rebuilt under a **prorated** rule that splits the term at the change and rounds each side separately at its own tier. The days before the change are the whole days from `term_start_certified` to `effective_date`, with the effective date excluded. The end of the period after the change is the day before `effective_date` for a `COMPLETE_WITHDRAWAL`, `new_term_end_certified` for a `TERM_DATE_CHANGE`, and `term_end_certified` for a `CREDIT_HOUR_REDUCTION`; the days after the change are the whole days from `effective_date` to that end, plus one, floored at 0. The new `entitlement_days_charged` is then the days before multiplied by the **pre-change** `rop_tier_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up, **plus** the days after multiplied by the **post-change** `rop_tier_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up. The two roundings are separate and both are required. Re-running the single-rounding certify-time rule at the new tier would give a materially different answer — for a 110-day term reduced from tier 100 to tier 60 on day 53, the prorated rule charges 52 plus 35, that is 87 days, where a single rounding at the new tier would charge 66.

At the third level the consequences of that recomputation are booked. Because the certification's `entitlement_days_charged` changed, the student's `entitlement_days_remaining` is credited by the old charge minus the new charge — a credit when the charge fell and a further debit when it rose — so that remaining entitlement continues to equal granted entitlement minus everything currently charged. And because the recomputed `va_tuition_cents` and `mha_term_total_cents` may now sit below what VA already disbursed, an overpayment debt may be raised.

Debt derivation has its own pinned order, because the recomputed amounts and `amount_basis` depend on each other. First, snapshot the pre-recomputation figures: the certification's `tuition_paid_cents`, its `mha_paid_cents`, its `va_tuition_cents`, its `mha_term_total_cents` and its `entitlement_days_charged`. Second, run the reconciled housing, covered-tuition and entitlement computations described above **without yet writing** `amount_basis`. Third, derive the components: `tuition_component_cents` is the snapshotted `tuition_paid_cents` minus the new `va_tuition_cents`, floored at 0; `mha_component_cents` is the snapshotted `mha_paid_cents` minus the new `mha_term_total_cents`, floored at 0; `principal_cents` is their sum and is never mutated after creation; and `outstanding_cents` opens equal to `principal_cents`. Fourth, write the recomputed fields onto the certification together with `amount_basis` set to `AS_RECONCILED`. Fifth, and only if `principal_cents` is strictly greater than 0, create exactly one benefit debt. When `principal_cents` is 0 no debt row is created at all.

The debt records the certification's `student_id`, the `certification_id` whose amounts moved, and the `change_report_id` of the reconciliation that created it. Its obligor and its reason are chosen together from the change report's `liability_party`: a `liability_party` of `STUDENT` yields a `debtor_type` of `STUDENT` and a `source` of `AMENDMENT_OVERPAYMENT`, while a `liability_party` of `SCHOOL` yields a `debtor_type` of `SCHOOL` and a `source` of `LATE_REPORTING_LIABILITY`. The debt's `notification_due_on` is stamped 30 days after the current logical clock date. Everything else on a fresh debt is server-initialised: `repaid_cents`, `tuition_repaid_cents` and `mha_repaid_cents` open at 0, and `notified_on`, `acknowledged_on`, `notification_channel`, `waived_by_official_id` and `waiver_reason` all open null.

At the fourth level, because the debt was created, the student's `open_debt_principal_cents` is increased by `principal_cents` — but only when `debtor_type` is `STUDENT`, since a debt the school owes is not the student's open balance — and an audit entry with `event_type` `DEBT_CREATED` is appended. The audit ordering here is fixed: the `AMENDMENT_RECONCILED` entry is appended **before** the `DEBT_CREATED` entry, so the trail reads in the order the events logically occurred.

At the fifth level a conditional cascade fires. If the recomputed `yr_gap_cents` came out at 0, then any Yellow Ribbon award on this certification whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` is moved to `REVERSED` with `reversal_reason` set to `YR_GAP_ELIMINATED_BY_RECONCILIATION`, its agreement's `pool_remaining_cents` is credited back by the award's `school_share_cents`, its agreement's `participant_count` is decremented by one, and an audit entry with `event_type` `AWARD_REVERSED`, `actor_role` `system` and `actor_user_id` the literal `system` is appended after the entries written by the levels above it, so the trail reads `AMENDMENT_RECONCILED`, then `DEBT_CREATED`, then `AWARD_REVERSED`. This cascade is deliberately restricted to provisional awards: an award already at `FINAL` is **not** auto-reversed, because VA has already confirmed that match and unwinding it requires a deliberate reversal.

Once a certification has been terminated by a complete withdrawal, it is closed for good. Submitting it, certifying it, returning it, voiding it, recording a VA payment against it and filing any further change in enrollment are all refused.

### 3.9. Debt Notification, Collection, Waiver, and the Student's Account

A benefit debt carries two lifecycles at once, and they do not constrain one another. The `collection_status` dimension tracks the money and moves among `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_REPAID`, `SATISFIED` and `WAIVED`, opening at `OPEN`, with `SATISFIED` and `WAIVED` both terminal. The `notification_status` dimension tracks whether the debtor has been told and moves among `PENDING`, `SENT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `OVERDUE`, opening at `PENDING`, with `ACKNOWLEDGED` terminal. A debt may be fully satisfied while its notification is still `PENDING`, and equally may be `ACKNOWLEDGED` while nothing at all has been repaid.

Who owes the debt is a matter of `debtor_type`, and the same table serves two quite different obligors. When `debtor_type` is `STUDENT`, the obligor is the veteran student referenced by `student_id`, and the debt participates in that student's `open_debt_principal_cents` balance. When `debtor_type` is `SCHOOL`, the obligor is the institution itself, which has no row of its own anywhere in this service; `student_id` is still recorded, because the overpayment still relates to that student's enrollment, but the debt never touches the student's open balance in either direction.

Notification is sent by either certifying-official role and requires the debt's `notification_status` to be exactly `PENDING`; anything else is refused with `ERR_DEBT_ALREADY_NOTIFIED`. The sender states the `notification_channel` used, one of `EMAIL`, `POSTAL` or `PORTAL`. The debt moves `PENDING → SENT`, the channel is stored, and `notified_on` is stamped with the current logical clock date.

Acknowledgement is the one operation reserved to the `Student` role. It is permitted while the `notification_status` is `SENT` or `OVERDUE` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_DEBT_NOT_NOTIFIED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the debt's `student_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Acknowledgement moves the debt to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamps `acknowledged_on` with the current logical clock date. Both entry paths, `SENT → ACKNOWLEDGED` and `OVERDUE → ACKNOWLEDGED`, are identical in their conditions and their effects.

Escalation belongs to `VaProcessingOffice`. Marking a debt overdue is permitted while its `notification_status` is `PENDING` or `SENT` — any other posture is refused with `ERR_DEBT_ALREADY_NOTIFIED` — and additionally requires that the current logical clock date be **strictly after** the debt's `notification_due_on`; escalating on or before the due date is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two checks run in the order just written, so a debt at `ACKNOWLEDGED` whose due date has not yet passed is refused with `ERR_DEBT_ALREADY_NOTIFIED` rather than with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two entry paths differ only in where they come from: `SENT → OVERDUE` escalates a notified debt the debtor has ignored, while `PENDING → OVERDUE` records a debt that became overdue without any notification having been sent at all.

Repayments are booked by the `BursarOfficer` against a debt whose `collection_status` is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_REPAID`; a debt already `SATISFIED` or `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_DEBT_ALREADY_SETTLED`. The payment states a single `amount_cents`, which may not exceed the debt's `outstanding_cents` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_REPAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Allocation follows a fixed two-bucket waterfall in which the tuition bucket fills first: the amount applied to tuition is the smaller of `amount_cents` and the tuition bucket's unfilled room, which is `tuition_component_cents` minus `tuition_repaid_cents`; the amount applied to housing is then the smaller of whatever remains of `amount_cents` after the tuition allocation and the housing bucket's unfilled room, which is `mha_component_cents` minus `mha_repaid_cents`. If any of the payment is still unallocated after both buckets — that is, if `amount_cents` exceeds the two allocations together — the payment is rejected with `ERR_REPAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Otherwise the two allocations are added to `tuition_repaid_cents` and `mha_repaid_cents` respectively, `repaid_cents` becomes their new sum, and `outstanding_cents` becomes `principal_cents` minus `repaid_cents`. The debt's `collection_status` then becomes `SATISFIED` when `outstanding_cents` has reached 0 and `PARTIALLY_REPAID` otherwise, so a payment that exactly clears the balance settles the debt in one step whether it started at `OPEN` or at `PARTIALLY_REPAID`. When `debtor_type` is `STUDENT`, the student's `open_debt_principal_cents` falls by the paid `amount_cents`.

Waiving is the supervisory alternative to collecting, and it is available from `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_REPAID`, with a debt already `SATISFIED` or `WAIVED` refused as `ERR_DEBT_ALREADY_SETTLED`. The acting `SupervisorySCO` may not be the official recorded as `reconciled_by_official_id` on the change report identified by the debt's `change_report_id`; that attempt is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_SEGREGATION`. A waiver takes a `waiver_reason`, sets `collection_status` to `WAIVED`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `waived_by_official_id`, stores the reason, and drives `outstanding_cents` to 0 while leaving `principal_cents` untouched. When `debtor_type` is `STUDENT`, the student's `open_debt_principal_cents` falls by the debt's outstanding balance as it stood immediately before the waiver — not by the full principal, since any part already repaid has already been removed from that running total. An audit entry with `event_type` `DEBT_WAIVED` is appended. Both entry paths, `OPEN → WAIVED` and `PARTIALLY_REPAID → WAIVED`, are identical.

The student's own account is the other side of this ledger, and it is the `BursarOfficer` who writes to it. Posting a charge names the `student_id` charged and the `term_id` the charge belongs to, states a `charge_type` of `TUITION`, `MANDATORY_FEE`, `LATE_FEE` or `REINSTATEMENT_FEE`, and an `amount_cents` of at least 0. A posted charge opens with `status` `POSTED`, which is the only value that field ever takes: charges are never reversed in this surface. The statutory bar of §3.2 lands here — a `LATE_FEE` or a `REINSTATEMENT_FEE` may not be posted while the student's live protection predicate is true, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROTECTED_STUDENT_PENALTY`, while a `TUITION` or `MANDATORY_FEE` charge is unaffected and may always be posted.

Registration holds are the bursar's other lever, and they move the student's parallel `account_hold_status` dimension without disturbing `coe_status` at all. The bursar states the desired `hold_status`, either `NONE` or `REGISTRATION_HOLD`. Moving a student `NONE → REGISTRATION_HOLD` requires that the live protection predicate be false for that student; while VA's payment for a certified term is outstanding, placing the hold is exactly the penalty the statute forbids, and it is refused with `ERR_PROTECTED_STUDENT_PENALTY`. The gate keys off the requested `hold_status` rather than off any actual movement, so re-asserting `REGISTRATION_HOLD` on a student who already carries it is refused just the same while the predicate holds; and a request that leaves the value unchanged still counts as a mutation of the student row. Moving the student the other way, `REGISTRATION_HOLD → NONE`, carries no such precondition: releasing a hold is never blocked by the protection predicate, because releasing a hold can only ever help the student.
