# Pilgrimage Group Travel Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A faith-based group travel operator sells promises long before it spends money. A parish signs up for a ten-day pilgrimage eighteen months out; the operator must already have negotiated a seat block with a carrier, held Mass slots at shrines whose daily admission is rationed, contracted motorcoaches and guides in local currency, and quoted a price that depends on how many travelers the recruiting group leader eventually delivers. Every one of those commitments can move independently, and each movement re-prices the others. This document specifies the API that holds that web of commitments together.

Three properties make this domain unusually demanding, and the specification below is organised around them. First, **almost every number is provisional until it isn't**: group-size pricing is quoted forward-looking from deposited travelers while sales are open, then re-derived from fully paid confirmed travelers when the roster closes, and both figures must remain separately observable on the same departure. Second, **money and capacity are conserved quantities**: a traveler's applied cash must always reconcile exactly against installment ledgers and parked credit, and a sacred site's per-day pax slots must return to their prior value when a held service is released, with no drift of a single cent or a single seat. Third, **risk is exogenous**: a government advisory published against a destination country reaches across programs, re-derives the posture of every departure to that country, cancels services, expires seat blocks, reverts confirmed travelers, and opens an itinerary modification that a second officer must ratify before sales may close again.

The API is a flat, top-level collection topology — programs, departures, pilgrims, agreements, air blocks, sacred sites, service bookings, land resources, land assignments, payment plans, payments, travel protections and risk advisories are all addressed as peers, linked by explicit identifier fields carried in request payloads rather than by deep URL nesting. A small number of sub-resource routes exist purely for reverse reachability (a traveler's payment plans and payments hang off the traveler). Readers implementing this specification should treat §2 as a contract to be honoured literally and §3 as the business narrative from which the behaviour of each operation must be reconstructed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads, writes and the clock-control capability alike — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. When `X-User-Role` is absent, empty, or carries a string that is not one of the seven roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the role is recognised but is not one of the roles listed for the addressed operation in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before anything else in the request is inspected — before path identifiers are resolved, before the body is parsed, and before any business rule runs.

The seven roles are `ProgramManager`, `RiskOfficer`, `AirDeskAgent`, `LandContractor`, `FinanceController`, `ProtectionAdministrator` and `GroupLeader`.

- A `ProgramManager` is the commercial operator of the pilgrimage: they own products, dated departures, the traveler roster, rooming, itinerary proposals and sacred-site services.
- A `RiskOfficer` owns destination risk advisories and the itinerary consequences of a posture change.
- An `AirDeskAgent` negotiates seat blocks with carriers and administers ticketed names and deviation requests.
- A `LandContractor` contracts hotels, guides and motorcoaches, and manages their capacity pools and local-currency lines.
- A `FinanceController` owns deposit schedules, installment plans, cash receipts, reversals, delinquency sweeps and leader settlement.
- A `ProtectionAdministrator` quotes travel protection, records medical review, decides pre-existing condition waivers and binds policies.
- A `GroupLeader` is a recruited parish or ministry leader acting under exactly one agreement.

Identity semantics differ between the operator-side roles and the leader role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramManager`, `RiskOfficer`, `AirDeskAgent`, `LandContractor`, `FinanceController` or `ProtectionAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `pending_modification_proposed_by_user_id` on a departure when an itinerary modification is proposed, or as `medical_review_by_user_id` on a travel protection when a medical review is recorded) but never compares it to any row's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `GroupLeader`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `GroupLeaderAgreement`; ownership checks on any resource that references a group leader agreement compare the resource's agreement reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `GroupLeader` may countersign only the agreement whose `id` equals their `X-User-ID`, may read only that agreement, and may read or create only travelers whose `recruited_by_agreement_id` equals their `X-User-ID`. A `GroupLeader` who addresses another leader's agreement or another leader's traveler is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, exactly as if the role were not listed on the endpoint at all; the same code answers a `GroupLeader` whose `X-User-ID` does not identify any stored agreement.

Two operations additionally enforce **segregation of duties**, which is an authorization rule rather than a business rule and is therefore evaluated at the same early stage. The user who proposes an itinerary modification on a departure may not be the user who ratifies it, and the user who records a travel protection's medical review may not be the user who decides its pre-existing condition waiver. In both cases the offending request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Because operator identities are opaque, this comparison is a plain string comparison of `X-User-ID` against the stored proposer or reviewer identifier.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the result set; when omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, an `offset` below 0, or either parameter carrying a non-integer value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Besides pagination, most list endpoints accept domain filters, which are named in §3 alongside the workflow they serve. Departures may be filtered by `program_id`, `governing_advisory_id`, `status` and `risk_posture`; leader agreements by `departure_id`, `superseded_by_agreement_id` and `status`; travelers by `departure_id`, `recruited_by_agreement_id`, `roommate_pilgrim_id` and `booking_status`; installment plans by `pilgrim_id`, `superseded_by_plan_id` and `status`; cash receipts by `pilgrim_id` and `status`; seat blocks by `departure_id` and `status`; service bookings by `departure_id`, `sacred_site_id` and `status`; land lines by `land_resource_id`, `departure_id` and `status`; and travel protections by `pilgrim_id` and `status`. All filters are optional and combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

No business rule in this system reads the host wall clock. Every deadline, notice window, day count, age computation and due-date comparison reads a **logical clock** that the server owns and that clients may set explicitly. The only values taken from the host wall clock are the purely audit-side ones: the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` of §2.2 and the `created_at` and `updated_at` row timestamps of §2.5. Wherever else the server reads a clock to stamp an instant or a calendar date onto a stored row — a departure's `last_sweep_at`, a receipt's `reversed_at`, a receipt's `received_at` when the client omits it, a protection's `deposit_reference_date` — it reads the logical clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that fixed startup default must never be replaced by real wall-clock time. An operator-side role may move the clock forward or backward by supplying `current_utc`, an RFC3339 UTC instant; a value that is not a well-formed RFC3339 UTC instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Setting the clock changes no stored row and recomputes no derived field on its own — it only changes what subsequent requests observe as "now".

All day arithmetic in this specification is whole-day arithmetic on the UTC calendar, with no partial-day fractions. Where a rule speaks of "days between" two calendar dates, it means the signed difference in whole days between those two UTC calendar dates. Where a rule compares two instants (a deadline, for example), the comparison is an instant comparison against the logical clock, and the boundary inclusivity is stated at each such rule.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every stored row carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Twelve of the thirteen resource types — programs, departures, group leader agreements, pilgrims, payment plans, air blocks, sacred sites, service bookings, land resources, land assignments, travel protections and risk advisories — additionally carry an `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and a `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by one on every subsequent mutation of that row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade. A freshly created row's `updated_at` is written equal to its own `created_at`. Both of these audit timestamps are stamped from the host wall clock — real UTC time at the moment the write happens — and not from the logical clock of §2.4, so a clock-control call never changes what they read back and a row written while the logical clock still reads its `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` startup default does not carry that instant.

Cash receipts are the exception. A `Payment` row carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field. Reversal of a receipt is recorded on the receipt's own `status` and `reversed_at` fields rather than by bumping a version that does not exist.

Client-supplied identifiers are never accepted as `id` values, and derived fields are never accepted as input. Where a field is described in §3 as computed, written by a cascade, or frozen by a transition, supplying it in a request body has no effect on the stored value.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer count of minor currency units, and every field that holds one is named with a `_cents` suffix. There are no decimal money values anywhere in the API, and no monetary field is ever returned as a string. Local-currency amounts on land lines are likewise integer minor units of the operator's local currency, converted to USD cents through a per-contract rate.

Rates and shares are expressed in **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points is parity, or one hundred percent. A rate of 350 basis points is three and one half percent; a rate of 15,000 basis points is one hundred and fifty percent. Whenever an amount is multiplied by a basis-point rate, the product is divided by 10,000 and the result is rounded **half up** to a whole number of cents — a fractional half-cent always rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude.

Two rounding disciplines matter enough to state globally, because getting either wrong changes stored totals by cents:

- **Round at each stated step, not once at the end.** Where a calculation in §3 names two or more rounding points (the traveler invoice rounds its promotional grant and its service fee independently; a protection premium rounds its base, its medical uplift and its waiver surcharge independently), each rounding is applied where it is stated, and later steps consume the already-rounded figure.
- **Round per line, then sum.** Where a total aggregates several converted lines, each line is rounded to whole USD cents first and the rounded lines are then added. Summing unrounded local amounts and rounding once at the end is forbidden and produces a different total whenever two or more lines have fractional parts.

Where a cap is applied to a rounded amount, the cap is applied after the rounding, never before. Where a floor of zero is applied to a subtraction, the subtraction is performed first and the result is then floored at zero.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent record, because in both cases a repeated write against unchanged state would legitimately succeed and therefore cannot be filtered out by a state precondition alone. In both cases the version field is a **required** input, not an optional one, and an absent or non-integer value is a malformed request.

- Creating an installment plan for a traveler — `POST /pilgrims/{pilgrimId}/payment-plans` — requires `expected_pilgrim_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Pilgrim` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the traveler's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PILGRIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is what stops a client from rescheduling against a traveler ledger it no longer understands — for instance building a plan on an invoice total that a later air deviation or a group-size repricing has already replaced.
- Creating a group leader agreement — `POST /group-leader-agreements` — requires `expected_departure_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Departure` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the departure's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEPARTURE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a new agreement for the same departure and the same leader identifier legitimately supersedes the prior one, the stale-version check is the only deterministic guard against renegotiating terms on an outdated view of the departure.

No other operation accepts a version field, and no other operation performs a version comparison. Note that a successful agreement creation itself increments the parent departure's `version`, so a client that creates two agreements in sequence must re-read the departure between them.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not name a stored row terminates the request with that collection's own 404 code: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPARTURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SACRED_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAND_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAND_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTECTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ADVISORY_NOT_FOUND`. Which code is returned is decided by the collection segment that owns the failing path parameter, not by the resource the caller was ultimately after.

Identifiers supplied inside a request body are resolved the same way and answer with the same codes, and they are checked **in the order they appear in that operation's input list**. Creating a departure resolves `program_id`; creating a leader agreement resolves `departure_id`; enrolling a traveler resolves `departure_id` and then `recruited_by_agreement_id`; opening a seat block resolves `departure_id`; holding a service resolves `departure_id` and then `sacred_site_id`; drawing a land line resolves `land_resource_id` and then `departure_id`; quoting a protection resolves `pilgrim_id`; and assigning rooming resolves `roommate_pilgrim_id`. A body identifier that names no stored row yields the owning collection's 404 code — a missing recruiting agreement yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing rooming partner yields `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_FOUND`.

Body-level referential existence is checked before any state, safety, temporal or fiscal rule, but after authentication, authorization and request-shape validation.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by a fixed ladder. The ladder is stated here once in full; §3 repeats the inversions that most often surprise implementers.

1. **Authentication** — a missing, empty or unrecognised `X-User-Role` yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This applies to every endpoint without exception, including read endpoints and the clock-control capability.
2. **Authorization** — a recognised role that is not listed on the addressed operation, or a `GroupLeader` reaching outside their own agreement, yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This too applies to every endpoint.
3. **Segregation of duties** — ratifying one's own itinerary modification, or deciding a waiver on a medical review one recorded oneself, yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). These are the only two operations that carry this check.
4. **Path existence** — an unresolvable path identifier yields that collection's 404 code, per §2.8. This applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
5. **Request shape** — a required field absent or null, a field of the wrong JSON type, an enumerated field carrying a value outside its declared set, a negative money or count field, an inverted date range (a land line whose `date_to` precedes its `date_from`, a departure whose `return_date` precedes its `departure_date`, a departure whose `final_payment_due_date` precedes its `deposit_due_date`), a malformed `price_tiers` ladder (empty, overlapping ranges, a gap between consecutive ranges, or an item whose `min_pax` exceeds its `max_pax`), a malformed `cancellation_grid` (empty, or containing no item whose `min_days_before` is 0), an installment `plan_type` outside its declared set, or a `current_utc` that is not an RFC3339 UTC instant — all yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This applies to every endpoint with a request body.
6. **Pagination shape** — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400) on every list endpoint, per §2.3.
7. **Body foreign keys** — an unresolvable identifier inside the body yields the owning collection's 404 code, per §2.8.
8. **State** — the addressed row, or its parent departure, is not in a status that admits the request. The applicable code is the one belonging to the entity whose state blocks the request: `ERR_DEPARTURE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SERVICE_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_LAND_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PROTECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ADVISORY_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_REVERSED` or `ERR_NO_PENDING_MODIFICATION` — all HTTP 409. Within this band the departure's own state is checked before the addressed child's state, and the traveler's `booking_status` outranks every rule below it.
9. **Optimistic concurrency** — the two stale-version rejections of §2.7, HTTP 412.
10. **Business safety** — mobility, risk-posture and senior-protection rules, HTTP 422: `ERR_MOBILITY_UNSUPPORTED`, then `ERR_MOBILITY_ACCOMMODATION_UNMET`, then `ERR_DEPARTURE_SUSPENDED_NO_NEW_BOOKINGS`, then `ERR_RISK_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_DEPARTURE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_CONFIRM`, then `ERR_SENIOR_PROTECTION_REQUIRED`, in that order.
11. **Business temporal** — deadline and window rules, HTTP 422, in this order: `ERR_NAME_CHANGE_DEADLINE_PASSED`, then `ERR_DIETARY_LEAD_TIME_UNMET`, then `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_CLOSED`, then `ERR_ADVISORY_EFFECTIVE_IN_FUTURE`.
12. **Business constraint** — capacity, interval and threshold rules, in this order: `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` (409), then `ERR_SITE_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409), then `ERR_SITE_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), then `ERR_LAND_RESOURCE_OVERSUBSCRIBED` (409), then `ERR_ROOM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), then `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_TIER` (422), then `ERR_MIN_PAX_NOT_MET` (422), then `ERR_PENDING_MODIFICATION_UNRATIFIED` (422).
13. **Business referential** — cross-row consistency rules, all HTTP 422, in this order: `ERR_AGREEMENT_DEPARTURE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_ROOMMATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_NOT_CONFIRMED`, then `ERR_CLERGY_NOT_CONFIRMED`, then `ERR_PERMIT_REFERENCE_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, then `ERR_NO_EARNED_FREE_SEAT`.
14. **Business fiscal** — `ERR_OVERPAYMENT_NOT_ALLOWED` (422) is the lowest-ranked rule in the entire system. Every state, safety, temporal, constraint and referential rule above it wins when both are violated.

Three inversions deserve emphasis because they are counter-intuitive. A cash receipt against a cancelled traveler that would also overshoot the invoice answers `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE`, because traveler state sits far above the fiscal ceiling. A wheelchair traveler enrolled with another leader's agreement onto a departure with no accessible coach answers `ERR_MOBILITY_UNSUPPORTED`, because the mobility rule outranks the agreement-mismatch rule. And a late ticketed-name submission against a seat block that is already full answers `ERR_NAME_CHANGE_DEADLINE_PASSED`, because the deadline rule outranks the seat-exhaustion rule.

### 2.10. Two Conserved Quantities

The system maintains two invariants that must hold after every forward action **and** after every compensating action. They are not decorative: they are the reason reversal walks its audit trail backwards rather than recomputing from scratch.

**Traveler cash.** For any traveler, the sum of `amount_paid_cents` across the installments of all that traveler's plans whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED` or `DEFAULTED`, plus that traveler's `credit_balance_cents`, must equal that traveler's `cash_applied_cents`, which must in turn equal the sum of `amount_cents` over that traveler's receipts whose `status` is `APPLIED`. Plans in `SUPERSEDED` contribute zero to the installment sum, because their paid amounts are returned to `credit_balance_cents` at supersede time and then re-drained into the replacement plan. The tolerance for drift is zero cents: a single cent of discrepancy is a defect. This identity is asserted after every cash receipt, after every receipt reversal, and after every plan creation.

**Sacred-site capacity.** For a given sacred site and a given UTC calendar date, the occupied pax count is the sum of `pax_count` over that site's service bookings whose start date falls on that date and whose `status` is `SITE_HELD`, `CLERGY_CONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMED`; the free pax count is the site's `daily_capacity_pax` minus that occupied count. Holding a service decreases the free count by exactly its `pax_count`; cancelling that service increases it by exactly the same amount, so the free count after a hold followed by a cancellation equals the free count before the hold, for every date. A cancellation driven by a risk-suspension cascade restores capacity identically to one an operator requested. The tolerance for drift is zero pax. This identity is asserted after every service hold and after every service cancellation, whichever party initiated it.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this system mutate rows the caller never named. A published advisory can touch every departure to a country, the services on those departures, their seat blocks, their confirmed travelers and those travelers' recruiting agreements. A traveler cancellation can supersede a plan, void a protection, release a seat, unpair a roommate, re-price siblings and reduce a leader's earned free seats. Three rules govern all of it.

Cascades are **immediate and synchronous**: by the time a response is returned, every downstream row is already in its final state, and a read of any affected row reflects the completed cascade. There is no deferred reconciliation and no eventual consistency.

Cascades are **ordered and deterministic**. Where a cascade visits a set of rows, §3 states the ordering: re-pricing sweeps a departure's travelers by `created_at` ascending; free-seat revocation walks the granted free seats by `created_at` descending, newest first; the land cost rollup walks countersigned lines by `date_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Where a cascade has several hops, later hops observe the results of earlier hops.

Cascades are **audited like any other mutation**. Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and, where the row type carries one, its `version` incremented by one — the same as if a client had written the row directly.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Pilgrimage Products and the Departure Sales Lifecycle

A pilgrimage **Program** is the marketable product: "Holy Land, ten days, Catholic". A `ProgramManager` registers one with a marketing `name` of between 1 and 160 characters, a `destination_country` given as exactly two uppercase letters (the ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code of the country the itinerary operates in, and the key by which destination advisories later reach it), a `faith_tradition` drawn from `CATHOLIC`, `ORTHODOX`, `PROTESTANT`, `JEWISH`, `MUSLIM` and `INTERFAITH`, and a nominal `duration_days` of at least 2 and at most 40. Two ladders complete the product, and both are validated on the way in.

The first ladder is `price_tiers`, the group-size price ladder and the single source of truth for tiered pricing. Each item names a `tier_code`, a `min_pax` and `max_pax` bounding the group sizes it covers, a `per_pax_price_cents`, a `single_supplement_cents` and a `deposit_cents`. At least one item is required; every `tier_code` must be unique; each `min_pax` must be at least 1; each item's `max_pax` must be greater than or equal to its own `min_pax`; the items must be sorted by `min_pax` ascending; and consecutive items must be contiguous, meaning each item's `min_pax` equals the previous item's `max_pax` plus one, so the ladder has no gaps and no overlaps. The second ladder is `cancellation_grid`, the days-before-departure penalty ladder. Each item names a `min_days_before` and a `penalty_bp`. At least one item is required; every `min_days_before` must be at least 0 and unique across the ladder; the items must be sorted by `min_days_before` descending; every `penalty_bp` must lie between 0 and 10,000 inclusive; and the ladder must contain an item whose `min_days_before` is exactly 0, so that a very late cancellation always has a bucket to land in. Any breach of any of these ladder rules is a malformed request answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A program also exposes `departure_count`, a computed tally of the dated departures created against it in any status, starting at 0.

A **Departure** is one dated operation of a program. Creating one carries the `program_id` it instantiates, a `departure_date` and `return_date` as `YYYY-MM-DD` UTC calendar dates, a `min_pax` of at least 1, a `max_pax` greater than or equal to `min_pax`, a `deposit_due_date` and `final_payment_due_date` marking when each traveler's deposit and balance are expected, and `mobility_accessible_coach`, a flag recording whether the contracted motorcoach fleet for this departure carries a wheelchair lift, defaulting to false. The `departure_date` must be strictly after the logical clock's calendar date at the moment of creation; `return_date` must be strictly after `departure_date`; and `final_payment_due_date` must be strictly after `deposit_due_date` and strictly before `departure_date`. A new departure opens in `PLANNING`, increments its program's `departure_count`, and immediately derives its `risk_posture` and `governing_advisory_id` from whatever advisories already govern its program's destination country — so a departure created into an already-published `DO_NOT_TRAVEL` advisory starts life `SUSPENDED` rather than `OPEN`. The provisional group-size snapshot is evaluated against an empty roster at creation, which leaves `provisional_tier_code` unset and `provisional_per_pax_price_cents`, `provisional_single_supplement_cents` and `provisional_deposit_cents` all at 0; those fields acquire values from the first roster mutation onwards.

**The two group-size variants.** Group-size pricing is computed twice over the life of a departure, from the same price ladder but from two different populations, and both results are stored side by side so that they remain independently observable. In both variants the candidate count is the number of travelers on the departure whose `is_paying` is true; travelers occupying a leader's free seat never enter either count, and travelers whose `booking_status` is `CANCELLED` never enter either count either.

- The **provisional** variant counts travelers whose `booking_status` is `DEPOSITED` or `CONFIRMED` — it is forward-looking, treating a deposited-but-unconfirmed traveler as one who will travel. It then raises that count to at least 1 before selecting a tier, so an empty roster is still quoted at the smallest-group tier and `provisional_deposit_cents` is never left at 0 once the roster has been touched. It writes `provisional_tier_code`, `provisional_per_pax_price_cents`, `provisional_single_supplement_cents` and `provisional_deposit_cents` from the matched tier's `tier_code`, `per_pax_price_cents`, `single_supplement_cents` and `deposit_cents`. It runs whenever the paying set moves: on traveler enrolment, on the receipt that carries a traveler from `INQUIRY` to `DEPOSITED`, on traveler confirmation, on traveler cancellation, and on the opening of sales.
- The **final** variant counts only travelers whose `booking_status` is `CONFIRMED` **and** whose `payment_status` is `PAID_IN_FULL`, and it applies **no** floor of one. It writes `final_tier_code` and `final_per_pax_price_cents` and nothing else. It runs at exactly one moment: the closing of sales. Because its population is strictly narrower, the same roster routinely selects a smaller group and therefore a higher per-pax tier price than the provisional variant did.

Tier selection itself is the same in both variants: the single item of `price_tiers` whose range contains the effective count, where the range is a **closed interval** inclusive at both ends. Because the ladder is validated contiguous and non-overlapping, at most one item can match and no tie-break is needed. When no item matches — the final variant with a fully-paid confirmed count of zero, or any count above the largest tier's `max_pax` — the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_TIER` (HTTP 422). Tier prices are stored as integer cents and no rounding occurs in tier selection.

**Opening the roster.** A `ProgramManager` opens sales, moving the departure from `PLANNING` to `OPEN_FOR_SALE`. Three conditions must hold: the departure is currently in `PLANNING` (or in `SALES_CLOSED`, for the reopen case below); its `risk_posture` is `OPEN`, `WATCH` or `RESTRICTED`, with a `SUSPENDED` posture rejected as `ERR_DEPARTURE_SUSPENDED_NO_NEW_BOOKINGS`; and the departure already carries at least one service booking whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, since a pilgrimage with no scheduled service is not a product — an empty itinerary is rejected with `ERR_DEPARTURE_INVALID_STATE`. Opening sales clears `final_tier_code` back to unset and `final_per_pax_price_cents` back to 0, re-derives the provisional snapshot, re-reads the risk posture, and increments the departure's `version`.

**Closing the roster.** Closing sales moves `OPEN_FOR_SALE` to `SALES_CLOSED` and is the only writer of the final variant. Its guards are evaluated in a fixed order: first whether the final variant finds a tier covering the fully-paid confirmed paying count, including the case of a count of zero, failing which the answer is `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_PRICE_TIER`; then whether that same count reaches the departure's `min_pax`, failing which the answer is `ERR_MIN_PAX_NOT_MET`; then whether `pending_modification_required` is false, failing which the answer is `ERR_PENDING_MODIFICATION_UNRATIFIED`. Once those pass, closing sales writes `final_tier_code` and `final_per_pax_price_cents`, leaves the provisional fields untouched, rewrites every `CONFIRMED` traveler's `price_locked_cents` to the new `final_per_pax_price_cents` and recomputes each of their invoices, re-quotes each such traveler's still-`QUOTED` travel protection at the new invoice, recomputes the departure's `land_cost_total_cents`, sets `service_coverage_ok` to true only when every service booking on the departure has reached `CONFIRMED`, and increments the departure's `version`.

**The reopen loop.** `SALES_CLOSED` is not terminal. A `ProgramManager` may reopen a closed departure back to `OPEN_FOR_SALE` under the same three conditions as the first opening, and may then close it again; the `SALES_CLOSED → OPEN_FOR_SALE → SALES_CLOSED` loop may repeat without bound, and each closing re-derives the final variant from scratch rather than adjusting the previous result. Reopening always discards the previous final snapshot, so a departure sitting in `OPEN_FOR_SALE` after a reopen carries no final tier at all.

**Terminal states.** A departure reaches `OPERATED` when a `FinanceController` runs the payment sweep on a departure already in `SALES_CLOSED` at a moment when the logical clock's calendar date is at or after the `departure_date`; the sweep runs first and the status change follows, and the same call increments `sweep_run_count` and stamps `last_sweep_at` with the logical clock instant. A departure reaches `CANCELLED` from `PLANNING`, `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED`, with the full unwind described in §3.9. `OPERATED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal and admit no further transition.

**The departure's derived counters.** Alongside the two pricing snapshots, a departure carries a small set of derived figures that the operator watches. `paying_pax_count` is the number of travelers on the departure whose `is_paying` is true and whose `booking_status` is `DEPOSITED` or `CONFIRMED` — the same population the provisional variant prices from — and it opens at 0 and is refreshed on every roster movement. `service_coverage_ok` opens at true on a brand-new departure and is thereafter true only while the departure has at least one non-cancelled service booking, every non-cancelled booking has reached `CONFIRMED`, and none of them was force-cancelled by a risk suspension. `land_cost_total_cents` opens at 0 and holds the USD-cent land cost described in §3.3. `itinerary_cost_delta_cents` opens at 0 and accumulates the cost delta of every ratified itinerary modification, while `ratified_modification_count` opens at 0 and counts them. `sweep_run_count` opens at 0 and counts delinquency sweeps, with `last_sweep_at` holding the logical-clock instant of the most recent one and remaining unset until the first sweep runs. The four `pending_modification_required`, `pending_modification_reason`, `pending_modification_proposed_by_user_id` and `pending_modification_cost_delta_cents` fields open at false, unset, unset and 0 respectively, and are governed by §3.9.

**Where departure state gates other work.** The departure's own status is the most frequently consulted precondition in the system, and every failure of it answers `ERR_DEPARTURE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Opening sales requires `PLANNING` or `SALES_CLOSED`. Closing sales requires `OPEN_FOR_SALE`. Cancelling requires `PLANNING`, `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED`. The payment sweep requires `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED`. Proposing an itinerary modification and ratifying one both require `PLANNING`, `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED`, and proposing additionally requires that `pending_modification_required` be false, so a second proposal stacked on an unratified one is rejected with this same code. Enrolling a traveler, opening a seat block and holding a service all require the referenced departure to be in `PLANNING` or `OPEN_FOR_SALE`; drawing a land line is the one child-creating operation that additionally tolerates `SALES_CLOSED`. Beyond the roster gate, enrolment also fails with `ERR_DEPARTURE_INVALID_STATE` when the count of travelers on the departure whose `booking_status` is not `CANCELLED` has already reached `max_pax` — the hard ceiling on non-cancelled travelers.

### 3.2. Sacred Sites and the Service Schedule

Access to holy places is the scarce resource of this business, and the system models it as two independent scarcities on the same row. A **SacredSite** registered by a `ProgramManager` carries a `name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `country` as exactly two uppercase letters, an `access_regime` of `OPEN_ACCESS`, `PERMIT_REQUIRED` or `TICKETED_SLOT`, a `daily_capacity_pax` between 1 and 5,000 stating how many group travelers the site admits per calendar day, a `turnaround_buffer_minutes` between 0 and 240, and `requires_local_clergy`, defaulting to false, recording whether a service here needs a named local celebrant before it can be confirmed. The buffer is appended to the **end** of every booked interval at the site: it is the time the sacristy needs before the next group may begin.

A **ServiceBooking** is one scheduled act of worship or devotion on a departure's itinerary. Holding one carries the `departure_id` whose itinerary contains it, the `sacred_site_id` hosting it, a `service_type` of `MASS`, `LITURGY`, `PRAYER_SERVICE` or `GUIDED_VISIT`, a `scheduled_start_utc` instant, a `duration_minutes` between 10 and 480 covering the service itself and excluding the site's turnaround, a `pax_count` of at least 1 for the travelers attending, and optionally a `permit_reference` naming the site permit or slot reservation. The server computes `buffered_end_utc` as the `scheduled_start_utc` advanced by `duration_minutes` and then by the site's `turnaround_buffer_minutes`; that instant is the **exclusive** end of the interval the booking occupies. The row is written in `SITE_HELD`, with `cancelled_by_risk` false, and holding it sets the parent departure's `service_coverage_ok` to false, because a newly held service is not yet confirmed.

Four gates guard a hold, and the order in which they are evaluated is the order of the ladder in §2.9. The departure must be in `PLANNING` or `OPEN_FOR_SALE`. The site's `country` must equal the program's `destination_country`, since a Fátima slot cannot appear on an Israeli itinerary; a mismatch is `ERR_SERVICE_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. When the site's `access_regime` is anything other than `OPEN_ACCESS`, a non-empty `permit_reference` must be supplied at hold time, failing which the answer is `ERR_PERMIT_REFERENCE_REQUIRED`. Then the two scarcity checks run in sequence: first the interval check, then the daily aggregate.

**The interval check.** Two bookings at the same site conflict when their buffered intervals overlap, where each interval runs from its `scheduled_start_utc` up to but not including its `buffered_end_utc`. Concretely, the requested booking conflicts with an existing booking whose `status` is `SITE_HELD`, `CLERGY_CONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMED` when the requested start falls strictly before the existing booking's buffered end **and** the existing booking's start falls strictly before the requested booking's buffered end. Touching exactly at a buffered-end boundary is therefore **not** an overlap: a service that begins at the precise instant a previous group's buffer expires is accepted. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_SITE_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

**The daily aggregate check.** Independently of intervals, the site admits only so many people per day. The sum of `pax_count` over the site's bookings whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` and whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls on the same UTC calendar date, taken together with the requested `pax_count`, must not exceed the site's `daily_capacity_pax`; exceeding it is `ERR_SITE_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). This check fires even when every individual booking clears the interval check — three non-overlapping morning Masses of 60 travelers each will still breach a site whose daily admission is 150.

**Clergy and confirmation.** Where a site `requires_local_clergy`, a `ProgramManager` records the celebrant before confirming: this moves the booking from `SITE_HELD` to `CLERGY_CONFIRMED` and writes `clergy_contact_name` and `clergy_tradition`. Recording clergy against a site that does not require it is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. The recorded `clergy_tradition` — one of `CATHOLIC`, `ORTHODOX`, `PROTESTANT`, `JEWISH`, `MUSLIM` or `INTERFAITH` — must equal the program's `faith_tradition`, unless the program's tradition is `INTERFAITH`, which accepts any celebrant; a mismatch is `ERR_CLERGY_NOT_CONFIRMED`. Confirming the booking then moves it to `CONFIRMED`, and its own preconditions are asymmetric by design: when the site requires local clergy the booking must already be in `CLERGY_CONFIRMED`, and arriving at confirmation without that step is `ERR_CLERGY_NOT_CONFIRMED`; when the site does not require local clergy the booking must be in `SITE_HELD`, and any other state is `ERR_SERVICE_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. Confirmation additionally requires a `permit_reference` on the booking whenever the site's `access_regime` is `PERMIT_REQUIRED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PERMIT_REFERENCE_REQUIRED`, and it requires the departure's `risk_posture` to be `OPEN` or `WATCH`, with `RESTRICTED` and `SUSPENDED` both answered by `ERR_DEPARTURE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_CONFIRM`. A successful confirmation sets the departure's `service_coverage_ok` to true when no risk-cancelled booking remains on the departure and every non-cancelled booking has reached `CONFIRMED`.

**Release.** A `ProgramManager` or a `RiskOfficer` may cancel a booking in `SITE_HELD`, `CLERGY_CONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMED`, supplying a `cancellation_reason`; cancelling an already-`CANCELLED` booking is `ERR_SERVICE_BOOKING_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation vacates the buffered interval and returns exactly `pax_count` to the site's free capacity for that date, leaving `cancelled_by_risk` false and prompting a recomputation of the departure's `service_coverage_ok`. The same transition happens without an operator when a risk suspension cascades onto the departure, and in that case `cancelled_by_risk` is set to true and the departure's `service_coverage_ok` is forced to false. Either way the capacity restoration is exact, per the conservation identity in §2.10.

### 3.3. Land Operator Contracting and the Cost Rollup

Hotels, motorcoaches and guides are bought as pooled capacity from destination management companies. A `LandContractor` registers a **LandResource** with the `operator_name` holding it under contract (1 to 120 characters), the `country` of performance as exactly two uppercase letters, a `resource_type` of `HOTEL_ROOM_BLOCK`, `MOTORCOACH` or `GUIDE`, a `units_total` between 1 and 500 stating how many units the operator guarantees per calendar day, a `unit_rate_local_cents` of at least 1 in the local currency's minor units per unit per day, an `fx_rate_bp` between 1 and 1,000,000 pinning the local-to-USD conversion in basis points where 10,000 basis points is parity, and a `force_majeure_notice_days` between 0 and 180 giving the local-conditions notice window inside which termination is penalty-free.

A **LandAssignment** draws units of one pool for one departure over a date range. It carries the `land_resource_id` being drawn down, the `departure_id` consuming the capacity, a `date_from` and `date_to` as `YYYY-MM-DD` dates with `date_to` on or after `date_from`, and a `units_requested` of at least 1 drawn on **every** date in the range. The range is **closed and inclusive at both ends**, which is the single most important arithmetic fact about this resource: the server computes `days_inclusive` as the whole-day difference between `date_to` and `date_from` plus one, so a line whose `date_from` equals its `date_to` bills one day, not zero. From there the server computes `line_local_cents` as `units_requested` multiplied by `days_inclusive` multiplied by the resource's `unit_rate_local_cents`, and then `line_usd_cents` as `line_local_cents` multiplied by the resource's `fx_rate_bp`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Lines are written in `DRAFT`.

**Oversubscription.** Before a line is written, the server tests every single calendar date in the requested inclusive range. For each such date, it sums `units_requested` over the same resource's existing lines whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `COUNTERSIGNED` and whose own inclusive range contains that date, adds the requested `units_requested`, and compares the result against the resource's `units_total`. If any one date exceeds the pool, the whole line is rejected with `ERR_LAND_RESOURCE_OVERSUBSCRIBED` (HTTP 409). Note that unlike sacred-site intervals, which are half-open, land ranges are closed: two lines that share only a single endpoint date **do** overlap on that date and their draws **do** add up. Draft lines occupy units exactly as countersigned lines do, so an uncountersigned line still blocks the pool.

**The rollup.** A departure's `land_cost_total_cents` aggregates only those of its lines whose `status` is `COUNTERSIGNED`. Draft lines occupy units but contribute nothing to cost; terminated lines contribute nothing and release their units. The rollup walks the countersigned lines ordered by `date_from` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, recomputes each line's `days_inclusive`, `line_local_cents` and `line_usd_cents`, and sums the `line_usd_cents` figures. The rounding order is pinned: each line is rounded to whole USD cents **first**, and the already-rounded lines are then added. Summing local amounts across lines and converting once at the end is forbidden and yields an off-by-cents total whenever two or more lines convert to fractional cents. The rollup is recomputed when a line is countersigned, when a line is terminated, when an itinerary modification is ratified, and when sales close; creating a draft line computes that line's own figures but leaves the departure's total unchanged.

**Countersigning and termination.** Countersigning a line moves it from `DRAFT` to `COUNTERSIGNED`, requires that the line be in `DRAFT` and that the parent departure not be `CANCELLED`, recomputes the departure's rollup, and increments the line's `version`; countersigning anything other than a draft is `ERR_LAND_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Termination, supplied with a `termination_reason`, moves a line in `DRAFT` or `COUNTERSIGNED` to `TERMINATED`, and terminating an already-terminated line is again `ERR_LAND_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Termination releases `units_requested` on every date of the closed range, recomputes the rollup so the line's contribution disappears, and records `terminated_penalty_free`: it is set true when the whole-day difference between the line's `date_from` and the logical clock's calendar date is greater than or equal to the resource's `force_majeure_notice_days`, and false otherwise. A notice period of exactly the contracted number of days is therefore penalty-free; one day less is not. When a departure cancellation terminates a line as a cascade, `terminated_penalty_free` is decided by the same notice-window comparison.

Drawing a line has two referential guards beyond the departure-status gate: the resource's `country` must equal the program's `destination_country`, because local conditions and the pinned FX basis belong to the country of performance, and a mismatch is `ERR_LAND_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.4. Group Leader Recruitment, Free Seats and Settlement

Pilgrimages are sold through parishes, and the parish contact who fills the coach is compensated. A `ProgramManager` offers a **GroupLeaderAgreement** on a departure, supplying the `departure_id`, the recruiting leader's `leader_name` (1 to 120 characters), a `leader_user_id` of 1 to 64 characters that the operator assigns to that leader, a `free_travel_threshold` between 1 and 60 giving the number of recruited paying travelers that earns one free seat, a `commission_mode` of `PER_PAX_CASH`, `FREE_SEAT_CREDIT` or `HYBRID`, a `commission_rate_cents_per_pax` between 0 and 100,000 for the cash commission per recruited paying traveler, an `accrual_basis` of `RECRUITED` or `TRAVELED` defaulting to `RECRUITED`, and the `expected_departure_version` demanded by §2.7. The agreement is written in `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN`.

The pair of `departure_id` and `leader_user_id` is the supersede key. When a new agreement is offered for a departure and leader identifier that already have an agreement in `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` or `ACTIVE`, that prior agreement moves to `TERMINATED` and has its `superseded_by_agreement_id` set to the new agreement's identifier. Travelers already recruited under the superseded agreement keep their `recruited_by_agreement_id` pointing at the superseded row — the history of who recruited whom is not rewritten by a renegotiation. Offering an agreement also increments the parent departure's `version`, which is why a client offering two agreements in a row must re-read the departure between them.

Only the leader themself may accept. Countersigning moves `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` to `ACTIVE` and is performed under the `GroupLeader` role, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the agreement's own `id`; countersigning from any other state is `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Acceptance immediately evaluates the accrual snapshot, so an agreement countersigned after recruits already exist picks up their count at once rather than waiting for the next enrolment.

**The accrual calculation, in two variants.** Like group-size pricing, leader benefit is computed twice from two different populations, and both results are stored side by side. In both variants the recruited count is the number of travelers whose `recruited_by_agreement_id` equals this agreement's identifier and whose `is_paying` is true; travelers occupying a free seat under this very agreement are excluded from that count precisely because their `is_paying` is false, and are tallied separately in `free_seats_granted_count`. From the recruited count the calculation derives the earned free seats as the recruited count divided by the agreement's `free_travel_threshold` using **integer floor division** — two recruits against a threshold of three earns zero seats, not a fraction of one. It then values those seats at a per-pax price basis that depends on the variant, giving a free-seat value equal to the earned free seats multiplied by that basis, and computes a gross cash commission equal to the recruited count multiplied by `commission_rate_cents_per_pax`. The payable commission then depends on `commission_mode`:

- Under `PER_PAX_CASH` the payable commission is the gross cash commission.
- Under `FREE_SEAT_CREDIT` the payable commission is zero: the leader is paid entirely in seats.
- Under `HYBRID` the payable commission is the gross cash commission reduced by the value of the free seats already earned, and the result is floored at zero — the subtraction is performed first and the floor applied to its result, never the other way round.

The **snapshot** variant counts travelers whose `booking_status` is `DEPOSITED` or `CONFIRMED` and values seats at the departure's `provisional_per_pax_price_cents`; it writes `recruited_paying_count`, `earned_free_seats` and `commission_payable_cents`. It runs on traveler enrolment, on traveler confirmation, on traveler cancellation and on agreement countersignature. The **settlement** variant counts only travelers whose `booking_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `payment_status` is `PAID_IN_FULL`, and values seats at the departure's `final_per_pax_price_cents`; it writes `settled_recruited_count`, `settled_earned_free_seats` and `settled_commission_cents`, leaving the snapshot fields untouched so both figures stay observable. Because both the population **and** the price basis differ, the same roster reliably produces a different earned-seat count and a different commission figure under the two variants. The agreement's `accrual_basis` records the contractual basis for audit only; which variant runs is decided entirely by the operation being performed and never by that field.

Settlement is a `FinanceController` action that moves an `ACTIVE` agreement to `SETTLED`. It requires the agreement to be `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and it requires the parent departure's status to be `SALES_CLOSED` or `OPERATED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_DEPARTURE_INVALID_STATE` — there is no traveled basis to settle against until the roster has closed. `TERMINATED` and `SETTLED` are both terminal.

An agreement in `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` or `ACTIVE` also reaches `TERMINATED` without any request against it, either because a newer agreement superseded it or because the departure was cancelled. In the departure-cancellation case, `recruited_paying_count`, `earned_free_seats` and `commission_payable_cents` are all recomputed to 0, because nobody travelled and nothing is owed.

### 3.5. Enrolling a Traveler: Three Independent Dimensions

A **Pilgrim** is one traveler on one departure, and the row deliberately carries **three orthogonal state dimensions** that advance independently and must never be collapsed into one. `booking_status` tracks the booking itself through `INQUIRY`, `DEPOSITED`, `CONFIRMED` and `CANCELLED`. `payment_status` tracks money through `UNPAID`, `DEPOSIT_PAID`, `PLAN_ACTIVE`, `PAID_IN_FULL`, `IN_DEFAULT` and `REFUNDED`. `protection_status` tracks insurance through `NONE`, `QUOTED`, `MEDICAL_REVIEWED`, `WAIVER_APPROVED`, `WAIVER_DECLINED`, `PURCHASED` and `VOIDED`, mirroring the traveler's own protection record. A quoted protection may legitimately sit on an `INQUIRY` traveler whose `payment_status` is still `UNPAID`; no dimension gates another except where a rule below says so explicitly.

Enrolment is performed by a `ProgramManager` or by the recruiting `GroupLeader`, and carries the `departure_id` being booked, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters as it will appear on documents, a `date_of_birth` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form that must be a valid ISO date strictly before the departure's `departure_date`, a `room_type_requested` of `SINGLE`, `DOUBLE` or `TWIN`, optionally a `recruited_by_agreement_id` naming the recruiting leader agreement (absent for a direct booking), optionally a `mobility_code` of `NONE`, `CANE`, `WALKER` or `WHEELCHAIR` defaulting to `NONE`, optionally `is_free_seat_grant` defaulting to false to mark the booking as a leader's free-travel seat rather than a paying booking, and optionally `risk_acknowledged` defaulting to false to record that the traveler signed the elevated-risk acknowledgement.

Six business gates guard enrolment, in the ladder order of §2.9. The departure must be in `PLANNING` or `OPEN_FOR_SALE` and must not already hold `max_pax` non-cancelled travelers. The departure's `risk_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`; a suspended departure takes no new bookings at all and answers `ERR_DEPARTURE_SUSPENDED_NO_NEW_BOOKINGS` (HTTP 422), and no acknowledgement can unlock it. When the posture is `RESTRICTED`, `risk_acknowledged` must be true, failing which the answer is `ERR_RISK_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). When `mobility_code` is `WHEELCHAIR` or `WALKER`, the departure's `mobility_accessible_coach` must be true — a cane needs no lift, a walker does — failing which the answer is `ERR_MOBILITY_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422). When a `recruited_by_agreement_id` is supplied, that agreement's `departure_id` must equal the supplied `departure_id` and its `status` must be `PENDING_COUNTERSIGN` or `ACTIVE`, failing which the answer is `ERR_AGREEMENT_DEPARTURE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). When `is_free_seat_grant` is true, a `recruited_by_agreement_id` must be present and that agreement's `earned_free_seats` must be strictly greater than its `free_seats_granted_count` — there must be an unconsumed earned seat — failing which the answer is `ERR_NO_EARNED_FREE_SEAT` (HTTP 422).

A successful enrolment writes `booking_status` `INQUIRY`, `payment_status` `UNPAID` and `protection_status` `NONE`; sets `is_paying` to the logical negation of `is_free_seat_grant`; computes `age_at_departure` as the number of complete years between `date_of_birth` and the departure's `departure_date`, floored, so a traveler whose birthday falls the day after departure is one year younger for every purpose in this system; increments the recruiting agreement's `free_seats_granted_count` when the booking is a free seat; and recomputes both the departure's provisional tier snapshot and the recruiting agreement's snapshot accrual. The remaining computed fields open at their defaults: `roommate_pilgrim_id`, `room_type_assigned`, `room_label`, `ticketed_name` and `price_locked_cents` unset, `ground_floor` false, `dietary_codes` holding the single code `NONE`, `deviation_type` `NONE`, and `single_supplement_applied_cents`, `deviation_fee_cents`, `invoice_total_cents`, `protection_premium_cents`, `cash_applied_cents`, `credit_balance_cents`, `refund_cents` and `penalty_retained_cents` all 0.

**Rooming.** A `ProgramManager` assigns the room, supplying `room_type_assigned`, `room_label`, `ground_floor`, and optionally `roommate_pilgrim_id` and `dietary_codes`. The `dietary_codes` array holds 1 to 4 items with no duplicates, each one of `NONE`, `VEGETARIAN`, `GLUTEN_FREE`, `KOSHER`, `HALAL`, `DIABETIC` or `LOW_SODIUM`, and `NONE` may not be combined with any other code — a violation of the array's shape is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Four business rules then apply, in ladder order. A traveler whose `mobility_code` is `WHEELCHAIR` or `WALKER` may not be assigned a room with `ground_floor` false; that is `ERR_MOBILITY_ACCOMMODATION_UNMET` (HTTP 422), and it outranks every other rooming rule, including the pairing and capacity checks. Requesting `KOSHER` or `HALAL` catering requires lead time: when the whole-day difference between the departure's `departure_date` and the logical clock's date is strictly less than 21 days, any `dietary_codes` array containing `KOSHER` or `HALAL` is rejected with `ERR_DIETARY_LEAD_TIME_UNMET` (HTTP 422); exactly 21 days still qualifies. A supplied `roommate_pilgrim_id` must name a different traveler on the same departure whose `booking_status` is not `CANCELLED` and whose `room_type_requested` equals this traveler's `room_type_requested`; naming oneself, a traveler on another departure, a cancelled traveler or a mismatched preference is `ERR_ROOMMATE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). And the room must have space: the count of travelers already assigned to the same `room_label` on the same departure plus this one must not exceed the capacity of `room_type_assigned`, which is 1 for `SINGLE` and 2 for both `DOUBLE` and `TWIN`; exceeding it is `ERR_ROOM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A successful assignment writes `room_type_assigned`, `room_label`, `ground_floor` and `dietary_codes`, writes `roommate_pilgrim_id` on **both** travelers so the pairing is mutual, sets `single_supplement_applied_cents` to the departure's `provisional_single_supplement_cents` when `room_type_assigned` is `SINGLE` and clears it to 0 otherwise, recomputes the invoice, and increments the traveler's `version`.

**The invoice, in six pinned steps.** A traveler's `invoice_total_cents` is the land invoice and deliberately excludes the travel-protection premium, which is tracked separately on `protection_premium_cents`. It is recomputed on confirmation, on rooming, on an air deviation, and on every re-pricing cascade hop that rewrites `price_locked_cents`. The step order is pinned, and evaluating the grant tier on the post-fee total rather than on the gross produces a different answer whenever the service fee pushes the figure across a grant breakpoint.

1. Take the effective per-pax price: the traveler's `price_locked_cents` when it has been set, and otherwise the departure's `provisional_per_pax_price_cents`.
2. Form the gross by adding the effective per-pax price, the traveler's `single_supplement_applied_cents` and the traveler's `deviation_fee_cents`.
3. Select the parish-grant rate **from the gross, and only from the gross**: when the gross is greater than or equal to $5,000.00 (500,000 cents) the grant rate is 5% (500 basis points); otherwise when the gross is greater than or equal to $3,000.00 (300,000 cents) the grant rate is 3% (300 basis points); otherwise there is no grant and the rate is 0.
4. Compute the grant as the gross multiplied by the grant rate in basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents.
5. Form the net by subtracting the grant from the gross. Then compute the operator's service fee as the **net** multiplied by 350 basis points (3.5%), divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents — the fee is charged on the post-grant amount, never on the gross.
6. `invoice_total_cents` is the net plus the service fee. No rounding occurs at any step other than steps 4 and 5.

**Confirmation.** A `ProgramManager` confirms a traveler, moving `booking_status` from `DEPOSITED` to `CONFIRMED`; a free-seat traveler, who never pays a deposit, is confirmed directly from `INQUIRY` and is the only exception. Any other starting `booking_status` is `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The departure must be in `OPEN_FOR_SALE`. The departure's `risk_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_DEPARTURE_POSTURE_BLOCKS_CONFIRM` (HTTP 422); and when the posture is `RESTRICTED`, the traveler's `risk_acknowledged` must be true, failing which the answer is `ERR_RISK_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_REQUIRED`. A traveler whose `age_at_departure` is 70 or greater must already own a travel protection record in `PURCHASED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SENIOR_PROTECTION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) — this is the one place where the protection dimension gates the booking dimension. The traveler must already have a `room_type_assigned`, since an unroomed traveler cannot be manifested; that failure is `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the departure must not be carrying an unratified modification, failing which the answer is `ERR_PENDING_MODIFICATION_UNRATIFIED` (HTTP 422). Confirmation freezes `price_locked_cents` at the departure's current `provisional_per_pax_price_cents`, recomputes `invoice_total_cents`, recomputes the departure's `paying_pax_count` and provisional tier fields, and recomputes the recruiting agreement's snapshot accrual.

**Cancellation and the settlement grid.** A `ProgramManager` or `FinanceController` cancels a traveler with a `cancellation_reason`; the only precondition is that `booking_status` is not already `CANCELLED`, and violating it is `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The settlement first computes the days before departure as the whole-day difference between the departure's `departure_date` and the logical clock's calendar date. It then selects the bucket of the program's `cancellation_grid` with the **greatest** `min_days_before` that the days-before figure still reaches, comparing **inclusively**: a days-before figure exactly equal to a breakpoint sits in the more generous bucket, not the harsher one. A negative days-before figure — a cancellation recorded after the departure date has passed — falls into the bucket whose `min_days_before` is 0. Two overrides then apply in order: when the departure's `risk_posture` is `SUSPENDED` the penalty rate is forced to 0, an advisory waiver; and when the cancellation is a hop of a departure-wide cancellation the penalty rate is likewise forced to 0. Finally `penalty_retained_cents` is the lesser of the traveler's `cash_applied_cents` and the traveler's `invoice_total_cents` multiplied by the penalty rate in basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up — the rounding happens before the comparison, and the cap means a traveler can never be penalised more cash than they actually paid. `refund_cents` is then `cash_applied_cents` minus `penalty_retained_cents`, and `payment_status` becomes `REFUNDED`. A purchased protection premium is never refunded and never penalised: it sits outside `invoice_total_cents` and outside this arithmetic entirely.

Cancellation then unwinds the traveler's footprint in ordered hops. The traveler's `ticketed_name` is cleared, which decrements the seat count on the departure's seat block. The rooming fields `room_label`, `room_type_assigned` and `ground_floor` are cleared, and when a `roommate_pilgrim_id` was set, the partner's `roommate_pilgrim_id` is cleared too and that partner's `single_supplement_applied_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` are recomputed. The traveler's `ACTIVE` payment plan moves to `SUPERSEDED` with every installment in `SCHEDULED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAST_DUE` moved to `WAIVED`, and their travel protection, unless it is already `PURCHASED` or `VOIDED`, moves to `VOIDED` with `premium_cents`, `base_premium_cents`, `medical_uplift_cents` and `waiver_surcharge_cents` all zeroed and `protection_status` set to `VOIDED`. Because the traveler is no longer a paying recruit, the recruiting agreement's `recruited_paying_count` and `earned_free_seats` are recomputed; when the recomputed `earned_free_seats` falls below `free_seats_granted_count`, the free-seat travelers granted under that agreement are visited in `created_at` **descending** order — newest granted seat revoked first — and flipped to `is_paying` true one at a time until `free_seats_granted_count` is no longer greater than `earned_free_seats`, each flipped traveler receiving `price_locked_cents` equal to the departure's `provisional_per_pax_price_cents` and a recomputed `invoice_total_cents`. Finally, because the departure's `paying_pax_count` changed, the provisional tier fields are recomputed, and when `provisional_per_pax_price_cents` actually changed, every traveler on the departure whose `booking_status` is `DEPOSITED` or `CONFIRMED` **and** whose `payment_status` is not `PAID_IN_FULL` is re-priced in `created_at` **ascending** order: `price_locked_cents` re-frozen to the new provisional price, `single_supplement_applied_cents` re-read from `provisional_single_supplement_cents` when `room_type_assigned` is `SINGLE`, and `invoice_total_cents` recomputed. A traveler who has already paid in full is never re-priced.

### 3.6. Seat Blocks: A Cyclic Carrier Negotiation

An **AirBlock** is a negotiated group seat allocation. An `AirDeskAgent` opens one with the `departure_id` it serves, a `carrier_code` of exactly two uppercase alphanumeric characters, a `seats_held` between 1 and 400, a `name_change_deadline_utc` instant after which ticketed names may no longer be filed, and a `deposit_deadline_utc` instant by which the block deposit is due. The block opens in `DRAFT` with `negotiation_round` at 0, `offered_fare_cents` at 0, no `countered_fare_cents` and no `contracted_fare_cents` yet, and `seats_assigned` at 0.

**The negotiation cycle.** A negotiation round carries a `party` of `CARRIER_OFFER` or `OPERATOR_COUNTER`, a `fare_cents` per seat, and optionally a replacement `name_change_deadline_utc` and a replacement `requested_seats`. The `party` must match the standing state, and a mismatch is `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409):

- A `CARRIER_OFFER` is accepted from `DRAFT` or from `COUNTERED`, and moves the block to `OFFERED`. It overwrites `offered_fare_cents` and, when one is supplied, `name_change_deadline_utc`, and increments `negotiation_round` by 1.
- An `OPERATOR_COUNTER` is accepted from `OFFERED` only, and moves the block to `COUNTERED`. Its `fare_cents` must be **strictly less** than the standing `offered_fare_cents` — a counter must improve on the offer, and a counter at or above it is rejected with `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`. It writes `countered_fare_cents` and overwrites `seats_held` from `requested_seats` when one is supplied, and it does **not** touch `negotiation_round`, which counts carrier offers only.

The `OFFERED → COUNTERED → OFFERED` cycle may repeat without bound, so `negotiation_round` after three carrier offers separated by two counters is 3. `CONFIRMED`, `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` admit no negotiation round at all.

**Confirmation, expiry and release.** Confirming a block requires it to be in `OFFERED`, requires the logical clock to be at or before `deposit_deadline_utc`, and requires that no other block on the same departure is already `CONFIRMED` — a departure has at most one contracted block. Any of those failures is `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`. Confirmation freezes `contracted_fare_cents` at the standing `offered_fare_cents` and recomputes `seats_assigned`. Expiry is not an operator action: any write against a block in `DRAFT`, `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` at a moment when the logical clock has passed `deposit_deadline_utc` first moves the block to `EXPIRED` and then fails the original request with `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`, so the caller observes both the state change and the rejection. A `CONFIRMED` block moves to `RELEASED` when the departure is cancelled or suspended, which clears every `ticketed_name` on the departure and drives `seats_assigned` to 0. `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

**Seats and names.** `seats_assigned` is not an incrementing counter but a derived count: the number of travelers on the same departure who carry a non-null `ticketed_name` and whose `booking_status` is `DEPOSITED` or `CONFIRMED`. Filing a name is an `AirDeskAgent` action carrying the `ticketed_name`. It requires the departure to have a block in `CONFIRMED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_NOT_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 422); it requires the logical clock to be at or before that block's `name_change_deadline_utc`, failing which the answer is `ERR_NAME_CHANGE_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422); and it requires a free seat, meaning that when `seats_assigned` has already reached `seats_held` and this traveler does not already hold a seat, the answer is `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). The deadline outranks the seat-exhaustion check, so a request that is both late and over-capacity reports the deadline. Re-filing a name for a traveler who already holds a seat leaves the count unchanged.

**Deviation fees.** An `AirDeskAgent` records an air deviation carrying a `deviation_type` of `ORIGIN_CHANGE`, `DATE_DEVIATION` or `CLASS_UPGRADE`, and this too requires a `CONFIRMED` block on the departure, failing which the answer is `ERR_AIR_BLOCK_NOT_CONFIRMED`. The fee is built in two rounded stages. The base depends on the type: an `ORIGIN_CHANGE` costs a flat $125.00 (12,500 cents); a `DATE_DEVIATION` costs a flat $90.00 (9,000 cents); a `CLASS_UPGRADE` is priced off the contracted block fare, as the block's `contracted_fare_cents` multiplied by 4,500 basis points (45%), divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents. A lateness multiplier then applies: the whole-day difference between the departure's `departure_date` and the logical clock's date is compared against 30 days, and when it is 30 days or fewer the multiplier is 15,000 basis points (1.5 times), and otherwise it is 10,000 basis points (parity, leaving the base unchanged). `deviation_fee_cents` is the base multiplied by the multiplier in basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up — a second, independent rounding. The traveler's `deviation_type` is written, `invoice_total_cents` is recomputed (which can move the invoice across a grant breakpoint and change the grant rate), and any `QUOTED` travel protection on the traveler is re-quoted against the new invoice.

### 3.7. The Traveler's Ledger: Receipts, Plans, Waterfall and Sweep

**Receipts.** A `FinanceController` records cash with an `amount_cents` of at least 1, a `method` of `CARD`, `ACH`, `CHECK` or `PARISH_TRANSFER`, and optionally a `received_at` instant which defaults to the logical clock and may not be later than it. The traveler's `booking_status` must not be `CANCELLED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The receipt is also subject to a fiscal ceiling: `cash_applied_cents` plus the new `amount_cents` must not exceed `invoice_total_cents` plus `protection_premium_cents`. This is the one place in the system where the protection premium joins the land invoice, and exceeding the sum is `ERR_OVERPAYMENT_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422) — the lowest-ranked rule in the whole ladder, so a receipt against a cancelled traveler always reports `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE` rather than the ceiling breach.

**The waterfall.** The receipt is then allocated. The traveler has at most one payment plan in `ACTIVE`. When there is no such plan, the whole amount is parked as credit: `credit_balance_cents` increases by the full amount and the receipt's `allocation` array records a single entry with `target` `CREDIT`, a null `sequence_no` and the full `amount_cents`. With no plan the money dimension is judged directly against the invoice: `payment_status` becomes `PAID_IN_FULL` when `cash_applied_cents` has reached `invoice_total_cents`, otherwise `DEPOSIT_PAID` when it has reached the departure's `provisional_deposit_cents`, and otherwise `UNPAID`.

When an `ACTIVE` plan exists, the amount fills installments in order. Fundable installments are those whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAST_DUE`; a `WAIVED` installment is never funded. They are visited in `due_date` ascending order, with `sequence_no` ascending as the tie-break and the owning plan's `created_at` ascending as the final fallback; the row `id` is never a decision input. For each installment in turn, the shortfall is its `amount_due_cents` less its `amount_paid_cents`, the amount taken is the lesser of that shortfall and the cash still unallocated, `amount_paid_cents` increases by the amount taken, the remaining cash decreases by it, and the installment's `status` becomes `PAID` when `amount_paid_cents` now equals `amount_due_cents` and `PARTIALLY_PAID` otherwise. Note that this installment-level enum is distinct from the traveler's `payment_status`. Each such placement appends an entry to the receipt's `allocation` array with `target` `INSTALLMENT`, the installment's `sequence_no` and the amount taken. Allocation stops as soon as the unallocated cash reaches zero. Any cash left after every fundable installment is full is added to `credit_balance_cents` and appended as a final `CREDIT` entry — which is why credit entries always sit at the end of the array. The plan's `past_due_count` is then refreshed as the number of its installments still in `PAST_DUE`, and when every installment on the plan is `PAID` the plan moves to `COMPLETED` and the traveler's `payment_status` becomes `PAID_IN_FULL`.

A receipt that carries a paying traveler's `cash_applied_cents` up to the departure's `provisional_deposit_cents` also moves `booking_status` from `INQUIRY` to `DEPOSITED` and `payment_status` from `UNPAID` to `DEPOSIT_PAID`. That transition stamps `deposit_reference_date` with the logical clock's calendar date on any travel protection the traveler owns, anchoring the waiver window; recomputes the departure's `paying_pax_count` and provisional tier fields; recomputes the recruiting agreement's snapshot accrual; and recomputes `seats_assigned` on the departure's seat blocks, since the derived seat count is scoped to `DEPOSITED` and `CONFIRMED` travelers. Free-seat travelers never take this path — they are confirmed directly. A receipt itself never changes the paying set, so a payment that does not cross the deposit threshold performs no tier recomputation.

**Plans.** A **PaymentPlan** is the traveler's installment ledger, and a traveler has at most one of them in `ACTIVE` at any moment. A `FinanceController` creates one with a `plan_type` of `SINGLE_BALANCE`, `MONTHLY_3` or `MONTHLY_6`, a `first_due_date` that must be a valid ISO date on or after the logical clock's date and on or before the departure's `final_payment_due_date`, and the `expected_pilgrim_version` demanded by §2.7. The traveler's `booking_status` must be `DEPOSITED` or `CONFIRMED` and their `invoice_total_cents` must be strictly greater than zero. The plan derives `installment_count` as 1 for `SINGLE_BALANCE`, 3 for `MONTHLY_3` and 6 for `MONTHLY_6`, captures `total_scheduled_cents` from the traveler's current `invoice_total_cents`, and builds the `installments` array. The base amount is `total_scheduled_cents` divided by the installment count using integer floor division, and the remainder is `total_scheduled_cents` less the base amount multiplied by the count. The remainder cents load onto the **earliest** installments, one cent each: the installment at `sequence_no` 1 through the installment at `sequence_no` equal to the remainder each carry the base amount plus one cent, and the rest carry the base amount exactly. The due date of installment 1 is `first_due_date`, and each later installment falls one further calendar month on, keeping the same day of month but clamping to the last day of a shorter month — a plan whose `first_due_date` is the 31st bills the 28th or 29th in February. Every installment opens with `amount_paid_cents` 0 and `status` `SCHEDULED`, and by construction the `amount_due_cents` figures sum to `total_scheduled_cents` exactly.

Creating a plan supersedes any prior plan. The prior `ACTIVE` plan moves to `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_plan_id` is set to the new plan's identifier, every cent already recorded in its installments' `amount_paid_cents` is summed back into the traveler's `credit_balance_cents`, and its remaining installments move to `WAIVED`. The new plan's schedule is built from the traveler's **current** `invoice_total_cents` — the prior plan's payments do not reduce it, because that money has just been returned to credit — and then the whole of `credit_balance_cents` is drained into the new installments by the same waterfall, earliest due date first. The traveler's `payment_status` becomes `PLAN_ACTIVE`, except that when the drained credit already covers the whole schedule the plan completes and `payment_status` becomes `PAID_IN_FULL` in the same request. The cash conservation identity of §2.10 is asserted at the end, and the traveler's `version` is incremented.

**Reversal.** Reversing a receipt is the exact compensating action, carrying a `reversal_reason`. The receipt's `status` must be `APPLIED`, and reversing twice is `ERR_PAYMENT_ALREADY_REVERSED` (HTTP 409); the traveler must not be `CANCELLED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The reversal walks the receipt's `allocation` array in **reverse list order**, so the `CREDIT` entries appended last are removed from `credit_balance_cents` first, and the `INSTALLMENT` entries are then unwound in descending `sequence_no`, each subtracting its `amount_cents` from that installment's `amount_paid_cents` and re-deriving that installment's `status`. The receipt's `status` becomes `REVERSED` and `reversed_at` is stamped with the logical clock; the traveler's `cash_applied_cents` decreases by the receipt's `amount_cents`; a plan that had reached `COMPLETED` reverts to `ACTIVE`. The traveler's `payment_status` is then re-derived from scratch: `PAID_IN_FULL` only while every installment is `PAID`; otherwise `PLAN_ACTIVE` when an `ACTIVE` plan exists; otherwise `DEPOSIT_PAID` when `cash_applied_cents` still reaches the departure's `provisional_deposit_cents`; otherwise `UNPAID`. A traveler who falls back below the deposit threshold while still in `DEPOSITED` reverts to `INQUIRY`, which recomputes the departure's provisional tier fields and the recruiting agreement's snapshot accrual. Because every allocated cent was recorded, the reversal leaves no drift, and the conservation identity is asserted again.

**The delinquency sweep.** A `FinanceController` runs the sweep on a departure in `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED`, and it proceeds in three hops over every `ACTIVE` plan of every non-cancelled traveler of that departure. Hop one: every installment whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and whose `due_date` is strictly before the logical clock's date moves to `PAST_DUE`, and each plan's `past_due_count` is refreshed. Hop two: a plan whose refreshed `past_due_count` has reached 2 or more moves to `DEFAULTED` and its traveler's `payment_status` becomes `IN_DEFAULT` — one missed installment is tolerated, two is not. Hop three: a defaulted traveler has their `ticketed_name` cleared, which decrements `seats_assigned` on the departure's blocks, and a defaulted traveler in `CONFIRMED` reverts to `DEPOSITED`, which in turn re-runs the departure's provisional tier calculation and the recruiting agreement's snapshot accrual. The sweep always increments the departure's `sweep_run_count` and stamps `last_sweep_at` with the logical clock, even when it defaults nobody; and when the clock has reached the `departure_date`, the same call carries a `SALES_CLOSED` departure on to `OPERATED`.

### 3.8. Travel Protection: Quote, Review, Waiver, Bind

A **TravelProtection** is one traveler's policy record, and its four-step path exists so that a medical judgement and a commercial concession are made by two different people. A `ProtectionAdministrator` quotes one with the `pilgrim_id` being insured, a `plan_code` of `BASIC`, `STANDARD` or `PREMIER`, a `medical_limit_cents` of at least 100,000, and optionally `pre_existing_waiver_requested` defaulting to false. The traveler's `booking_status` must be `INQUIRY`, `DEPOSITED` or `CONFIRMED`, and no protection already in `QUOTED`, `MEDICAL_REVIEWED` or `PURCHASED` may exist for that traveler, failing which the answer is `ERR_PROTECTION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — a `WAIVER_DECLINED` or `VOIDED` record does not block a fresh quote. The record opens in `QUOTED` and the traveler's `protection_status` mirrors it.

**Pricing.** The premium is re-derived at quote, at medical review, at waiver decision and at purchase, and each time it begins by capturing `insured_trip_cost_cents` from the traveler's current `invoice_total_cents`. The `age_band` follows from the traveler's `age_at_departure`: band `D` at 75 or above priced at 1,280 basis points (12.8%), band `C` from 65 through 74 at 910 basis points (9.1%), band `B` from 40 through 64 at 620 basis points (6.2%), and band `A` at 39 or below at 400 basis points (4%). `base_premium_cents` is `insured_trip_cost_cents` multiplied by the band rate in basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up. The medical uplift then applies only when `medical_limit_cents` is **strictly greater** than $50,000.00 (5,000,000 cents): a limit exactly at the threshold attracts no uplift, and above it `medical_uplift_cents` is `base_premium_cents` multiplied by 1,500 basis points (15%), divided by 10,000, rounded half up; otherwise it is 0. The waiver surcharge applies only when `pre_existing_waiver_requested` is true **and** `waiver_eligible` is true **and** the traveler's `protection_status` is not `WAIVER_DECLINED`: then `waiver_surcharge_cents` is the lesser of $450.00 (45,000 cents) and `base_premium_cents` multiplied by 2,200 basis points (22%), divided by 10,000, rounded half up — the surcharge is rounded first and the cap applied to the rounded figure. Otherwise `waiver_surcharge_cents` is 0. `premium_cents` is the sum of `base_premium_cents`, `medical_uplift_cents` and `waiver_surcharge_cents`, with no further rounding.

**Eligibility.** `waiver_eligible` is derived at medical review and re-derived at the waiver decision. When `deposit_reference_date` is unset — the traveler never reached `DEPOSITED` — it is false outright. Otherwise it is true only when all three of the following hold: `questionnaire_complete` is true; `stable_period_days` is 60 or greater; and the whole-day difference between the logical clock's calendar date and `deposit_reference_date` is 21 days or fewer. Exactly 60 days of stability qualifies, and exactly 21 days after the deposit is still inside the window; 22 days is outside it.

**Review.** Recording the medical review moves the record from `QUOTED` to `MEDICAL_REVIEWED`; any other starting state is `ERR_PROTECTION_INVALID_STATE`, and a cancelled traveler makes it `ERR_PILGRIM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The call writes `questionnaire_complete` and `stable_period_days`, stamps `medical_review_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, re-derives `waiver_eligible` and re-quotes the premium.

**Decision.** The waiver decision carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`, requires the record to be in `MEDICAL_REVIEWED`, and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the stored `medical_review_by_user_id`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On the `APPROVE` branch two further gates apply, and their order matters: first the purchase window, where a whole-day difference between the logical clock's date and `deposit_reference_date` strictly greater than 21 days is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422); then general eligibility, where a `waiver_eligible` of false for any other reason — an incomplete questionnaire, or a `stable_period_days` below 60 — is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). An approval moves the record to `WAIVER_APPROVED`, recomputes `waiver_surcharge_cents` under its cap and refreshes `premium_cents`. A `DECLINE` moves the record to `WAIVER_DECLINED`, forces `waiver_surcharge_cents` to 0 and refreshes `premium_cents`; a decline is accepted even when the purchase window has closed, so `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_CLOSED` is reachable only on the approve branch.

**Binding.** Purchase requires the record to be in `QUOTED`, `WAIVER_APPROVED` or `WAIVER_DECLINED`, and requires a non-cancelled traveler. `MEDICAL_REVIEWED` is deliberately not purchasable: a reviewed waiver request must be decided one way or the other first. Further, when `pre_existing_waiver_requested` is true the record may **not** be purchased straight out of `QUOTED` — a requested waiver must have been through review and decision — and that attempt is `ERR_PROTECTION_INVALID_STATE`. Purchase moves the record to `PURCHASED`, mirrors `protection_status`, and copies `premium_cents` onto the traveler's `protection_premium_cents`, which raises that traveler's fiscal ceiling for receipts. `PURCHASED` and `VOIDED` are terminal. A non-terminal record is driven to `VOIDED` by a traveler cancellation or a departure cancellation, with `premium_cents`, `base_premium_cents`, `medical_uplift_cents` and `waiver_surcharge_cents` zeroed and the traveler's `protection_premium_cents` returned to 0. A `PURCHASED` policy is never voided by any cancellation: the premium is non-refundable, which is exactly why the cancellation settlement never touches protection money.

### 3.9. Destination Risk, Itinerary Modification and the Departure Unwind

A **RiskAdvisory** is a government or insurer notice about a country. A `RiskOfficer` drafts one with the `country` it covers as exactly two uppercase letters, a `level` of `NORMAL`, `EXERCISE_CAUTION`, `RECONSIDER` or `DO_NOT_TRAVEL`, an `effective_from` instant from which it governs, and a `source` of 1 to 120 characters naming the issuing authority. It opens in `DRAFT` with `affected_departure_count` at 0.

**Posture derivation.** A departure's `risk_posture` and `governing_advisory_id` are projections, recomputed on publication for every departure of the advisory's country and re-read on every departure write. The candidate set is every advisory whose `country` equals the departure's program's `destination_country`, whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, and whose `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock; drafts and not-yet-effective advisories are excluded. When the candidate set is empty, `risk_posture` is `OPEN` and `governing_advisory_id` is unset. Otherwise the governing advisory is the candidate that maximises, in lexicographic order, first its severity rank — where `NORMAL` ranks 0, `EXERCISE_CAUTION` ranks 1, `RECONSIDER` ranks 2 and `DO_NOT_TRAVEL` ranks 3 — then its `effective_from`, then its `created_at`; ties are therefore broken by the later effective date and then by the later creation. The governing advisory's identifier is written to `governing_advisory_id` and its level maps to the posture: `NORMAL` gives `OPEN`, `EXERCISE_CAUTION` gives `WATCH`, `RECONSIDER` gives `RESTRICTED` and `DO_NOT_TRAVEL` gives `SUSPENDED`. One special case: when the governing advisory's level is `NORMAL`, `governing_advisory_id` is cleared back to unset even though a candidate was found, because an all-clear notice does not govern anything.

**Publication and its cascade.** Publishing requires the advisory to be in `DRAFT`, and publishing a `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` advisory is `ERR_ADVISORY_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). It also requires `effective_from` to be at or before the logical clock, since an advisory may not be published before it takes effect; a future-dated advisory is rejected with `ERR_ADVISORY_EFFECTIVE_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). Publication first moves any other advisory for the same `country` that is currently `PUBLISHED` to `SUPERSEDED`, so at most one advisory per country governs at a time. It then cascades in four hops.

Hop one: every departure whose program's `destination_country` equals the advisory's `country` and whose `status` is `PLANNING`, `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED` has its `risk_posture` and `governing_advisory_id` recomputed, and `affected_departure_count` records how many such departures were visited — the count is of rows visited, not of rows whose posture actually changed. A departure whose recomputed posture is `SUSPENDED` and whose `status` was `OPEN_FOR_SALE` is additionally moved to `SALES_CLOSED`. Hop two: on each newly suspended departure, every service booking whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_by_risk` set to true, releasing the sacred site's daily pax capacity and buffered interval exactly as an operator cancellation would. Hop three: because services were risk-cancelled, that departure's `service_coverage_ok` flips to false, `pending_modification_required` flips to true, `pending_modification_reason` is set to the literal string `RISK_SUSPENSION`, `pending_modification_cost_delta_cents` is set to 0, and `pending_modification_proposed_by_user_id` is set to the publishing officer's `X-User-ID`, so that the officer who published the advisory cannot also ratify the change it forced. Hop four: because `pending_modification_required` is now true, that departure cannot close sales until a ratification clears the flag. Additionally, on each suspended departure every seat block in `DRAFT`, `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `EXPIRED` and a `CONFIRMED` block moves to `RELEASED`. Every affected departure's `version` is incremented.

Supersession is not a rollback. When a later advisory for the same country downgrades the level to `NORMAL`, the departures' `risk_posture` returns to `OPEN` and `governing_advisory_id` is cleared, but risk-cancelled service bookings are **not** resurrected and `pending_modification_required` stays true. The operator must rebuild the itinerary and have the modification ratified.

**Itinerary modification.** The modification flag is a small sub-machine on the departure with two states, none and proposed. A `ProgramManager` proposes a modification with a `modification_reason` and a `cost_delta_cents`; the departure must be in `PLANNING`, `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED` and `pending_modification_required` must currently be false, so a second proposal stacked on an unratified one is rejected with `ERR_DEPARTURE_INVALID_STATE`. Proposing sets `pending_modification_required` true, copies the reason into `pending_modification_reason`, stamps `pending_modification_proposed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, copies the delta into `pending_modification_cost_delta_cents`, and increments the departure's `version`. A `RiskOfficer` ratifies it. Ratification requires `pending_modification_required` to be true, failing which — nothing was proposed, or the previous proposal was already ratified — the answer is `ERR_NO_PENDING_MODIFICATION` (HTTP 409); and it requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `pending_modification_proposed_by_user_id`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Ratification adds `pending_modification_cost_delta_cents` to the departure's cumulative `itinerary_cost_delta_cents`, increments `ratified_modification_count` by 1, clears `pending_modification_required` to false with `pending_modification_reason` and `pending_modification_proposed_by_user_id` returned to unset and `pending_modification_cost_delta_cents` returned to 0, recomputes the departure's `land_cost_total_cents`, and increments the departure's `version`. The cycle may repeat, and `ratified_modification_count` accumulates across cycles.

**The departure unwind.** A `ProgramManager` cancels a departure with a `cancellation_reason`, from `PLANNING`, `OPEN_FOR_SALE` or `SALES_CLOSED`. The departure's `status` becomes `CANCELLED` and the unwind proceeds in ordered hops. Hop one: every traveler whose `booking_status` is not `CANCELLED` moves to `CANCELLED`, with the cancellation settlement run at a forced penalty rate of 0, so each traveler's `penalty_retained_cents` is 0 and `refund_cents` equals the whole of their `cash_applied_cents` — a full refund, because the operator, not the traveler, walked away. Hop two: each of those travelers' `ACTIVE` payment plans moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and each travel protection in `QUOTED`, `WAIVER_APPROVED` or `WAIVER_DECLINED` moves to `VOIDED` with `premium_cents` zeroed; purchased policies survive. Hop three: every service booking whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_by_risk` false and full site capacity restored; every land assignment in `DRAFT` or `COUNTERSIGNED` moves to `TERMINATED`, with `terminated_penalty_free` decided by the same notice-window comparison described in §3.3; every `CONFIRMED` seat block moves to `RELEASED`, clearing ticketed names and driving `seats_assigned` to 0; and every `ACTIVE` group leader agreement moves to `TERMINATED` with `recruited_paying_count`, `earned_free_seats` and `commission_payable_cents` all recomputed to 0. The departure's `land_cost_total_cents` is recomputed to 0, since no countersigned line survives.
