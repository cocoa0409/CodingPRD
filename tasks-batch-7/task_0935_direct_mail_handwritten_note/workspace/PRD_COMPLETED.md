# Direct-Mail & Handwritten-Note Fulfillment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Agencies that send physical mail on behalf of their clients live with an awkward split. One half of the work is data work: a spreadsheet of names arrives, addresses have to be standardized against postal records, households collapsed into single pieces, movers chased, and undeliverable rows quarantined before a single envelope is printed. The other half is plant work: a bank of robot-pen machines writes each note in a chosen handwriting style, drawing on ink lots that deplete, on machine-days that fill up, and on a calendar that refuses to let mail drop on federal holidays. Between those halves sits money — a USPS permit escrow that is drawn at commit, refunded when a job is voided, and nicked whenever a Marketing Mail piece comes back marked undeliverable. This document specifies the service that holds all three together.

The service is mounted under `/mail`, with a single administrative exception described below for the logical clock. It manages two structurally different mail paths through one production pipeline. **Addressed** jobs carry individually named recipients drawn from an imported mailing list, and everything recipient-level applies to them: address standardization, deduplication, move updates, return handling, reprints, and per-piece presort pricing. **EDDM** jobs — Every Door Direct Mail — carry no recipient names at all; they saturate whole carrier routes, are machine-printed rather than hand-written, and are priced from route coverage. A large amount of the specification below exists to say precisely which rules are structurally inapplicable to the EDDM path and which of those inapplicable rules are silently skipped versus loudly refused.

Three characteristics of this domain shape the API contract more than anything else. First, a campaign can be frozen from outside by a compliance officer or by a billing close, and that freeze pre-empts almost every other rule in the system — a request against a frozen campaign must report the freeze rather than whatever else happens to be wrong with it. Second, editing creative content is not a local operation: changing a template's body copy invalidates every outstanding approval built on it and unwinds the schedules, machine reservations, ink draws and escrow charges that were made in reliance on those approvals. Third, almost every commitment in the system is reversible, and every reversal is expected to restore the resource it consumed exactly — no drift in ink microlitres, no drift in machine slot-minutes, no drift in cents. Readers implementing this service should treat those three properties as the spine of the design; the rest is detail hung on them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Account Scope

Every request to this API — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-Role`, naming the role the caller is acting as, and `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller. Six roles exist and their spellings are exact: `PlatformAdmin`, `AccountOwner`, `CampaignManager`, `ListOperator`, `ProductionOperator`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is absent, or whose value is not one of those six strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request acting as `AccountOwner` with no `X-User-ID` header is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; for the other five roles the `X-User-ID` header is never verified at all, so a request from one of them that omits it entirely is authenticated normally and proceeds down the ladder. Once the caller is authenticated, the role must appear in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4; if it does not, the response is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles divide the work by segregation of duties, and the division is deliberate rather than cosmetic.

- `PlatformAdmin` is the operator superset: it may invoke every endpoint in §4, and it alone may drive the logical clock and register new mailer accounts.
- `AccountOwner` is the paying customer acting as their own account. They top up the permit escrow, create campaigns, templates, mailing lists, mail jobs and proofs, request provisional quotes, and read everything scoped to their own account. Approving and rejecting a proof are theirs exclusively — nobody else signs off on creative.
- `CampaignManager` is the agency operator. They build campaigns, templates, jobs, proofs and drip schedules, submit proofs for approval, drive the job pipeline through scheduling, commitment and voiding, select EDDM carrier routes, release drip steps, activate and pause campaigns, and maintain the blackout calendar.
- `ListOperator` is the data operator: importing recipient records, running address standardization and deduplication, locking a finished list, applying move updates, and recording returns.
- `ProductionOperator` owns the plant: registering handwriting styles, robot-pen machines and ink lots, quarantining and releasing lots, reserving and driving production runs, dropping finished jobs into the mail stream, and fulfilling or denying reprints.
- `ComplianceOfficer` owns the two global freeze axes — placing and lifting compliance holds and closing billing — and may read campaigns, jobs, proofs, ledger rows and reports. A `ComplianceOfficer` cannot build or price anything.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this determines what `X-User-ID` is compared against. When `X-User-Role` is `AccountOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Account` compare the resource's owning account to `X-User-ID`. The owning account is reached through `Campaign.account_id`, `MailingList.account_id`, `Template.account_id`, or — for anything hanging off a mail job — by walking from the job to its campaign and then to `account_id`. If the resolved owner differs from `X-User-ID`, the request is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). For the other five roles — `PlatformAdmin`, `CampaignManager`, `ListOperator`, `ProductionOperator` and `ComplianceOfficer` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on a `Proof` when a `CampaignManager` submits it for approval) but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no account-scope check applies to those roles. The ownership comparison is made only where a request resolves a single owning resource, and **no collection endpoint applies it**: an `AccountOwner` listing campaigns, templates, mailing lists, recipient records, mail jobs, proofs, ledger rows, return events, reprint requests, drip schedules or drip steps receives every row matching the supplied filters regardless of which account owns it. Nor is the ownership of two resources ever compared against one another: a mail job may name a `template_id` or a `mailing_list_id` belonging to a different account from its own campaign's, and no rung objects.

One rule crosses role boundaries and deserves early mention: a proof may never be approved by the same person who submitted it. The approving `X-User-ID` is compared against the `Proof.submitted_by_user_id` recorded at submission, and equality is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Because submission is a `CampaignManager` action and approval an `AccountOwner` action, the two identifiers normally differ by construction; the check exists because `PlatformAdmin` may perform both, and because an `X-User-ID` is just a string.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. Both are integers, and a value that is not an integer or that falls outside those bounds is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400) — neither `limit` nor `offset` is a member of rung 8's closed numeric list, so neither ever answers `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Collections are always returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first — regardless of any filter applied, and where several rows share a `created_at`, which the coarse logical clock makes common, the tie is broken by ascending `id`. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

Beyond pagination, individual list endpoints accept domain filters, and those filters are named in §4's endpoint table and discussed with the workflows they serve in §3. Filters combine conjunctively: supplying both `campaign_id` and `status` when listing mail jobs returns only jobs matching both. A filter value is matched by exact equality against the stored value and is never itself validated: a filter naming a value outside its field's declared enum simply matches no rows rather than raising `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, and an unparseable date filter likewise matches nothing.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`. Ids are minted from a single monotonically increasing sequence, so ordinary ascending string comparison of two ids recovers the order in which those two rows were created; that is what makes the `created_at` tie-break of §2.3 deterministic. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded at creation and never changed afterwards.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that resource. `version` is the concurrency token described in §2.7; it is not the same thing as a template's content revision counter, which is a separate field discussed in §3.2. Three resource types are append-only records of something that already happened, and they carry only `id` and `created_at` — no `updated_at` and no `version` at all: `PostageLedgerEntry`, `ReturnEvent` and `BlackoutDate`. Do not emit those two fields for those three types.

All temporal reasoning in this service reads a **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it to any instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value to the administrative clock endpoint, which answers with the resulting `current_utc`. Every subsequent evaluation — proof-approval expiry, in-home date validity, production lead time, blackout resolution — reads the logical clock's current instant, and every timestamp the server mints (`created_at`, `updated_at`, `approved_at`, `scheduled_at`, `expires_at`) is that instant rendered in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits. Implementations must not fall back to real time when the clock has never been set.

Where the domain deals in calendar dates rather than instants — `mail_date`, `target_in_home_date`, `run_date`, `blackout_date`, `anchor_date`, `computed_mail_date`, `computed_in_home_date` — the value is an ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component, and all arithmetic on those values is whole-day arithmetic. The "current date" against which such dates are compared is the calendar-date part of the logical clock's current instant.

### 2.5. The Campaign Write Gate

A campaign carries three status axes that evolve independently of one another. `lifecycle_status` tracks where the campaign is in its working life and takes one of `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `COMPLETED`. `compliance_status` is either `CLEAR` or `HOLD`. `billing_status` is either `OPEN` or `CLOSED`. From those last two the server derives a fourth, read-only field, `write_gate`, which reads `FROZEN` whenever `compliance_status` is `HOLD` or `billing_status` is `CLOSED`, and `OPEN` only when compliance is `CLEAR` and billing is `OPEN` at the same time.

The write gate is the single most pre-emptive rule in the system. Any write request that resolves to a campaign — whether directly by `campaign_id`, or by walking from a mail job to its campaign, from a drip schedule to its campaign, from a drip step through its job to its campaign, from a proof through its job to its campaign, from a production run through its job to its campaign, from a reprint request through its `campaign_id`, or from a list record through `last_mail_job_id` to its job and thence to its campaign — is evaluated against both freeze axes before any state, kind, temporal, capacity or fiscal rule of its own is considered. One write resolves to more than one campaign at once: editing a template resolves to every campaign behind a mail job that names it. Both axes are then evaluated over the whole set, with **every** resolved campaign's `compliance_status` examined before **any** campaign's `billing_status`, and a single frozen campaign anywhere in the set refuses the edit. A template that no mail job references resolves to no campaign at all and is not gated.

Two distinct rejections come out of that evaluation, and they are ordered. A campaign whose `compliance_status` is `HOLD` rejects the write with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409); exactly one write endpoint is exempt, namely lifting the hold itself. A campaign whose `billing_status` is `CLOSED` rejects the write with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BILLING_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), and here there is no exemption whatever — not even the hold-lifting endpoint, which means a campaign that is both held and closed can never be unfrozen. The compliance check is evaluated first, so a campaign that is simultaneously held and billing-closed reports the compliance hold.

Read endpoints are never gated. A frozen campaign remains fully readable, and its jobs, proofs, ledger rows and reports continue to answer normally; the gate governs writes only. Equally, a write that resolves to no campaign is never gated, and the set of such writes is larger than it first looks: registering accounts, handwriting styles, robot-pen machines, ink lots and blackout dates, funding the permit escrow, quarantining and releasing an ink lot, creating a campaign, creating a template, and **every mailing-list write** — importing a record, running standardization, running deduplication and locking the list — proceed regardless of any freeze anywhere, even when some of that list's records carry a `last_mail_job_id` pointing at a job under a frozen campaign. Exactly two writes walk the list-record path — applying a move update and recording a return — and each of them resolves to no campaign, and so goes ungated, whenever the record's `last_mail_job_id` is still null. A reprint request and its fulfilment or denial reach their campaign through the request's own `campaign_id` rather than through the record.

### 2.6. The Validation Ladder: One Total Order Over Forty-One Rungs

Requests to this API frequently violate more than one rule at once, and the response must be deterministic. Every check in the system therefore occupies a numbered rung on a single global ladder, and the rules are evaluated strictly in ascending rung order. **For any request, the first rung whose condition fails produces the response, and no later rung is evaluated.** A rung whose subject matter does not apply to the endpoint at hand is skipped without effect and evaluation continues down the ladder.

The complete order is as follows. Each rung's substance is developed in §3 alongside the workflow it guards; what this subsection fixes is the ordering.

1. `X-User-Role` is present and names one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-ID` is present whenever the caller acts as `AccountOwner`, the one identity-backed role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
4. Every referenced resource exists. Path parameters are resolved first, left to right along the URL, then body foreign keys in the order they are listed for that endpoint in §4's companion narrative. The rejection is `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` when an account reference dangles, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND` for a campaign reference, `ERR_MAIL_JOB_NOT_FOUND` for a mail-job reference, and `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for every other resource type.
5. An `AccountOwner`'s `X-User-ID` equals the account that owns the resolved resource — otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
6. Every field the endpoint marks required is present and of the declared JSON type, and every date or datetime string parses as ISO 8601 — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`.
7. Every enum-valued field carries a member of its declared set — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.
8. Every numeric field lies inside its declared range — otherwise `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.
9. **Global write gate A** — the campaign this request resolves to has `compliance_status` of `CLEAR`; otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_COMPLIANCE_HOLD`.
10. **Global write gate B** — the campaign this request resolves to has `billing_status` of `OPEN`; otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BILLING_CLOSED`.
11. The optimistic-concurrency field required by the endpoint matches its parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.
12. The target mailing list is not locked — otherwise `ERR_LIST_LOCKED`.
13. The operation is not a recipient-level operation aimed at an EDDM job — otherwise `ERR_EDDM_NOT_APPLICABLE`.
14. `job_kind` agrees with its dependent fields — otherwise `ERR_JOB_KIND_MISMATCH`.
15. The campaign's own status axes permit the operation — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_INVALID_STATE`.
16. The mailing list's `import_status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_LIST_INVALID_STATE`.
17. The mail job's `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_MAIL_JOB_INVALID_STATE`.
18. The proof's `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_PROOF_INVALID_STATE`.
19. The production run's `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`.
20. The remaining state gates — drip steps, drip schedules and reprint requests — permit the operation; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
21. The named ink lot is in a usable state — otherwise `ERR_INK_LOT_UNAVAILABLE`.
22. The named writing machine is online — otherwise `ERR_MACHINE_OFFLINE`.
23. **Segregation of duties** — the approver of a proof is not its submitter; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
24. The mail job has at least one proof that ever reached approval — otherwise `ERR_PROOF_NOT_APPROVED`.
25. That approval is still live — otherwise `ERR_PROOF_APPROVAL_STALE`.
26. Scoped-unique values do not collide — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.
27. The supplied anchor or target in-home date is not in the past — otherwise `ERR_IN_HOME_DATE_PAST`.
28. The backwards walk over blackout dates terminates within its cap — otherwise `ERR_BLACKOUT_UNRESOLVABLE`.
29. The derived mail date clears the production lead time — otherwise `ERR_MAIL_DATE_UNREACHABLE`.
30. A new drip step is far enough from its siblings — otherwise `ERR_DRIP_GAP_TOO_SMALL`.
31. The drip schedule has room for another step — otherwise `ERR_DRIP_STEP_LIMIT`.
32. The ink lot's formulation matches the handwriting style's — otherwise `ERR_INK_TYPE_MISMATCH`.
33. The EDDM rate variant is eligible for every selected route — otherwise `ERR_EDDM_VARIANT_INELIGIBLE`.
34. The EDDM job's total selected pieces lie within bounds — otherwise `ERR_EDDM_PIECE_BOUNDS`.
35. The job has at least one billable piece — otherwise `ERR_NO_MAILABLE_PIECES`.
36. The run's piece count fits inside the job's unreserved pieces — otherwise `ERR_RUN_PIECES_EXCEED_JOB`.
37. The cited return event carries a reprint entitlement — otherwise `ERR_REPRINT_NOT_ENTITLED`.
38. The record has not used up its reprint allowance — otherwise `ERR_REPRINT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
39. The machine-day has room for the run's slot-minutes — otherwise `ERR_MACHINE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
40. The ink lot has volume above its safety reserve after the draw — otherwise `ERR_INK_INSUFFICIENT`.
41. The account's permit escrow covers the amount due — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMIT_BALANCE`.

Rungs 5 through 8 deserve a note of their own, because they decide which of several 400-family answers a badly shaped request earns. Rung 5 is the account-scope check of §2.1 and answers `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Rung 6 covers request shape: a required body field that is absent, a field whose JSON type is wrong, or a date or datetime string that does not parse as ISO 8601, all answering `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). Four further shape rules belong to this rung. A request body that is not a JSON object at all — a bare array, string or number — is `ERR_VALIDATION`; an absent or unparseable body is read as the empty object, so the complaint becomes whichever required field it then lacks. An **optional** field supplied explicitly as `null` is treated exactly as though it had been omitted, and takes whatever default the narrative states for absence. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than refused. And the only string field anywhere in this API checked for emptiness is an `Account`'s `permit_number`, whose empty or whitespace-only value is `ERR_VALIDATION` at this rung; every other string field — a name, a `reason`, a `body_text` — accepts the empty string without complaint. Rung 7 covers enum membership and answers `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 422), and its scope is **exhaustive and closed**: the fields that route here are exactly `Account.status`; the campaign lifecycle, compliance and billing status values when supplied as input — a clause that is in fact vacuous, since no endpoint in §4 accepts a campaign status value as input at all; `pen_type` and `ink_type` on a handwriting style; `envelope_style` on a template; `dpv_confirmation_code` on a recipient record and `new_dpv_confirmation_code` on a move update; `job_kind`, `mail_class` and `eddm_rate_variant` on a mail job; the `mode` of a dedupe run; `capacity_variant` on a production run; `anchor_variant` on a drip schedule; `ink_type` on an ink lot; `status` on a writing machine; and `return_reason` on a return. **No other field routes to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`** — every other malformed value is reported one rung earlier as `ERR_VALIDATION`. Rung 8 covers numeric ranges and answers `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422); the bounded fields are a template's `character_count` between 1 and 2000 inclusive, a production run's `piece_count` of at least 1, a route selection's `total_deliveries` of at least 1 with `residential_piece_count` and `business_piece_count` each at least 0, a postage top-up's `amount_cents` of at least 1, a drip step's `sequence_no` of at least 1, a machine's `daily_capacity_minutes` of at least 1, an ink lot's `initial_volume_ul` of at least 1, and a handwriting style's `chars_per_minute` of at least 1 with `ink_ul_per_1000_chars` strictly greater than zero.

Three structural consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright, because they are the source of most implementation error. First, the two write-gate rungs sit **above** every per-endpoint state gate, so a frozen campaign pre-empts state, kind, temporal, capacity and fiscal complaints alike — a request that is both aimed at a held campaign and aimed at a job in the wrong state reports the hold. Second, the write-gate rungs sit **below** existence and request-shape rungs, so a malformed body or a dangling identifier is still reported as such even on a frozen campaign; the gate never masks a 400, 401, 403, 404 or 422 arising from rungs 1 through 8. Third, because placing a compliance hold is itself a write against the campaign and is not exempt from rung 9, attempting to hold a campaign that is already held is refused at rung 9 with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` — it never reaches rung 15's campaign-state check.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Four write endpoints attach a child record to a parent whose state the client must have observed first. Each of them requires a non-optional version field in the request body carrying the client's last-observed `version` of that parent. If the value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected at rung 11 with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and nothing is created or modified. The four rules are:

- **Reserving a production run.** `POST /mail/mail-jobs/{mail_job_id}/production-runs` requires `expected_mail_job_version` matching the target `MailJob`'s current `version`. A second run may legitimately be added to the same job — jobs are routinely split across machine-days — so no state precondition blocks the write; only a stale parent version distinguishes a client working from an out-of-date view of the job's committed piece budget. A mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Approving a proof.** `POST /mail/proofs/{proof_id}/approve` requires `expected_template_version` matching the current `version` of the `Template` the proof renders. Note carefully that this is the template's concurrency `version`, not its content revision counter `version_no`; the two are separate integers and both change over a template's life. Editing a template legitimately supersedes outstanding approvals, so an approval carrying a stale template version would bind the approver to content they never saw. A mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Requesting a reprint.** `POST /mail/reprint-requests` requires `expected_list_record_version` matching the cited `ListRecord`'s current `version`. A record can legitimately accumulate further return events and move updates that change its entitlement, so the create is not blocked by state alone. A mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Adding a drip step.** `POST /mail/drip-schedules/{drip_schedule_id}/steps` requires `expected_drip_schedule_version` matching the `DripSchedule`'s current `version`. New steps reshape the schedule's mail-date spacing, so a client holding a stale view of the schedule would compute the minimum-gap check against the wrong neighbours. A mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and none should be invented. Endpoints outside this list are ordered by state preconditions alone.

### 2.8. Money, Rounding and the Postage Escrow Ledger

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of **cents**. Postage rates are quoted in cents per piece with one decimal place — a rate of `23.9` means twenty-three and nine tenths of a cent for each piece — and rate arithmetic therefore produces fractional cents that must be resolved before any total is stored. The resolution rule is **round half up** to a whole number of cents, and the *point* at which rounding happens is pinned per calculation in §3; it is not a free choice. Two other rounding directions appear and are equally pinned: the handling percentage is rounded **up** to the next whole cent, and the volume credit is rounded **down** to the whole cent below. Where the specification says a percentage of an amount, the percentage is applied to that amount and nothing else.

An `Account` holds a USPS permit escrow in `permit_balance_cents`, and every movement of that balance is recorded as a `PostageLedgerEntry`. Each ledger row names the `account_id` whose balance it moves, an `entry_type` drawn from `TOPUP`, `DRAW`, `REFUND` and `FORFEIT`, an `amount_cents` that is always a positive magnitude of at least 1, a `signed_amount_cents` that renders the direction of the movement (positive for `TOPUP` and `REFUND`, negative for `DRAW` and `FORFEIT`), a `balance_after_cents` snapshot of the account balance immediately after the row was appended, and a free-text `reason` recording provenance such as a commit, a void refund, or a return-to-sender fee. The `reason` strings the server writes for itself are fixed literals: `commit mail job` on a commit's `DRAW`, `void refund` on a void's `REFUND` unless the void request supplied a `reason` of its own, `template edit refund` on the `REFUND` raised by hop 6 of §3.2's cascade, `return-to-sender fee` on a return's `FORFEIT`, and `postage top-up` on a `TOPUP` unless the top-up supplied one. Rows attributed to campaign activity also carry `campaign_id` and `mail_job_id`; escrow top-ups carry neither, leaving both null.

The balance is not an independently maintained number that happens to track the ledger — it is defined by the ledger. **Conservation invariant (postage money):** an account's `permit_balance_cents` equals the sum of its `TOPUP` amounts plus the sum of its `REFUND` amounts, minus the sum of its `DRAW` amounts and minus the sum of its `FORFEIT` amounts, over every ledger row belonging to that account. Four lifetime counters expose the same sums individually — `lifetime_topup_cents`, `lifetime_draw_cents`, `lifetime_refund_cents` and `lifetime_forfeit_cents` — each being the sum of `amount_cents` over that account's rows of the corresponding type. A commit that draws and a void that refunds the identical amount must leave the balance exactly where it started; a refund is a compensating movement, never a reversal that deletes history.

Campaign-level money mirrors the same source. `committed_postage_cents` on a campaign is the sum of `amount_cents` over that campaign's `DRAW` rows, `refunded_postage_cents` the same sum over its `REFUND` rows, and `forfeited_postage_cents` the same over its `FORFEIT` rows.

### 2.9. Two Mail Paths: Addressed Pieces and EDDM Saturation

A `MailJob` declares its `job_kind` at creation, and the choice is structural rather than cosmetic. An `ADDRESSED` job names a `mailing_list_id`, uses a `mail_class` of either `FIRST_CLASS` or `MARKETING_MAIL`, and leaves `eddm_rate_variant` null. An `EDDM` job leaves `mailing_list_id` null, uses a `mail_class` of exactly `EDDM`, and must name an `eddm_rate_variant` of either `SATURATION` or `HIGH_DENSITY_PLUS`. Any disagreement among those fields at job creation is `ERR_JOB_KIND_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) at rung 14, as is an attempt to attach a carrier route to a job whose `job_kind` is not `EDDM`.

Because an EDDM job owns no recipient records at all, a whole family of recipient-level rules has nothing to act on. The system distinguishes two responses to that fact, and the distinction is deliberate. Where a rule simply has no subject matter — address standardization, deduplication, per-record presort counting — the rule is **skipped** and the job proceeds; an EDDM job's `automation_piece_count`, `retail_piece_count` and `unmailable_piece_count` are all zero by construction, and its `presort_tier` is always `EDDM_ROUTE` rather than any of the addressed tiers. Where a caller **actively requests** a recipient-level operation through an EDDM job, the request is refused at rung 13 with `ERR_EDDM_NOT_APPLICABLE` (HTTP 422). Exactly four operations refuse in this way: reserving a robot-pen production run against the job, recording a return against a record whose last mail job is that job, applying a move update reached through such a record, and creating a reprint request reached through it. Only the first of the four names its job directly; the other three reach it through a record's `last_mail_job_id`, and because committing an EDDM job attaches no records at all, no record ever comes to point at one. Rung 13 is therefore in practice reached only by the production-run reservation, and the other three carry the check without ever firing it. Saturation pieces are machine-printed in bulk rather than written one by one, which is why an EDDM job never enters production and never owns a production run.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants and Compensating Reversals

Four quantities in this system are conserved, and every reversal path exists to keep them so. Implementations that treat reversals as approximate will drift, and drift is observable through the reporting endpoints.

- **Postage money conservation.** As stated in §2.8, `permit_balance_cents` is exactly the signed sum of the account's ledger rows. Voiding a committed job appends a `REFUND` row of precisely the amount that was drawn — no more, no less — and raises the balance by that same amount, restoring it to its pre-commit value when no other activity intervened.
- **Ink volume conservation.** An `InkLot`'s `remaining_ul` equals its `initial_volume_ul` minus the sum of `planned_ink_ul` over every production run drawing on that lot whose status is `RESERVED`, `RUNNING`, `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`. Runs in `VOIDED` status release their draw and are excluded from the sum. Reserving a run and then voiding it must return `remaining_ul` to exactly its pre-draw value.
- **Machine slot-minute conservation.** For every pairing of a machine and a `run_date`, the committed minutes are the sum of `planned_slot_minutes` over production runs on that machine and that date whose status is `RESERVED`, `RUNNING`, `COMPLETED` or `FAILED`; `VOIDED` runs are excluded. That sum must never exceed the machine's `daily_capacity_minutes`. The `run_date` is a discrete bucket, not an interval — two runs on the same date always contend for the same capacity regardless of any clock time — and voiding a run must restore the committed total to its exact pre-reservation value.
- **Deliverability partition.** Every recipient record belongs to exactly one of four reporting buckets: automation-tier mailable, retail-tier mailable, unmailable, or duplicate. In the campaign-level report the buckets are made mutually exclusive by evaluating the unmailable bucket **first** — a record that is both unmailable and a duplicate is counted only as unmailable — and the four counts therefore sum to the number of records considered. One qualification: a record still sitting at `UNASSIGNED` tier, because standardization has never run over its list, satisfies none of the four membership tests and falls into no bucket at all, so the partition identity holds only once a standardization pass has classified every record.

That last point extends to the per-list counters as well, and the precedence is the same one. The campaign-level deliverability report states its unmailable-first precedence explicitly; the per-list counters `automation_count`, `retail_count`, `unmailable_count` and `duplicate_count` **inherit exactly that precedence** rather than being read as literal independent counts. A record is tested against the four buckets in the order unmailable, duplicate, automation, retail, and lands in the first bucket it satisfies and no other: `unmailable_count` counts every record whose `deliverability_tier` is `UNMAILABLE`; `duplicate_count` counts every **remaining** record whose `dedupe_status` is `DUP_EXACT` or `DUP_HOUSEHOLD`, so a record that is both unmailable and a duplicate lands only in `unmailable_count` and is never counted twice; `automation_count` and `retail_count` then take the remaining records of the corresponding tier whose `dedupe_status` is `UNSCREENED`, `UNIQUE` or `HOUSEHOLD_HEAD`. The partition sum of the four counters therefore equals `record_count` unconditionally on any standardized list, which is what makes the declared invariant reachable rather than accidental, and `mailable_count` is `automation_count` plus `retail_count`.

### 2.11. Path Resolution, Existence and Not-Found Semantics

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. When a URL names a parent and a child, the parent is looked up first and a missing parent is reported before the child is even considered; only once every path segment resolves does the server look at body foreign keys, and those are resolved in the order they appear in the endpoint's input list. This ordering matters because different resource types produce different error identifiers at the same rung: an unresolvable account reference is `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, a campaign reference is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, a mail-job reference is `ERR_MAIL_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, and every other type — mailing list, list record, template, handwriting style, proof, production run, writing machine, ink lot, EDDM route selection, drip schedule, drip step, return event, reprint request, ledger row, blackout date — is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All four carry HTTP 404.

A child resource addressed through the wrong parent is treated as absent rather than as a mismatch: there is no separate "wrong parent" error in this catalogue. A request to a path this API does not define at all, or to a defined path with a method it does not support, is answered the same way — `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, never HTTP 405. A trailing slash on any path is insignificant.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Mailer Account and Its Permit Escrow

Everything in this system hangs off an `Account`, and only a `PlatformAdmin` may register one. Registration takes the customer's `name` and the USPS `permit_number` under which their mail will be entered, and optionally an initial `status` — one of `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE` when omitted. Permit numbers are globally unique across accounts and must be non-empty; a second registration reusing a permit number already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (HTTP 409). A freshly registered account starts with `permit_balance_cents` at 0 and all four lifetime counters — `lifetime_topup_cents`, `lifetime_draw_cents`, `lifetime_refund_cents`, `lifetime_forfeit_cents` — at 0 as well. `status` is recorded and returned but is consulted by no other rule in this specification: a `SUSPENDED` account may still be funded, may still own campaigns, templates and lists, and may still have jobs committed against its escrow. Nothing in the API ever changes an account's `status` after registration.

Funding the escrow is open to both a `PlatformAdmin` and the `AccountOwner` themselves. A top-up carries `amount_cents`, which must be at least 1 — a zero or negative amount is `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422) at rung 8 — and an optional `reason` string recorded on the resulting ledger row. The top-up appends a `TOPUP` ledger entry with both `campaign_id` and `mail_job_id` left null, raises `permit_balance_cents` and `lifetime_topup_cents` by `amount_cents`, and increments the account's `version` by one. The response body is the updated account — not the ledger row it appended — and answers **201 Created**, so `permit_balance_cents` and `balance_after_cents` on the new row agree by construction.

Ledger rows are readable individually and as a filtered collection, narrowed by `account_id`, `campaign_id`, `mail_job_id` or `entry_type`, and always returned oldest-first. Because ledger rows are historical facts they are never edited: they carry `id` and `created_at` and nothing else from the common property set. Reading an account returns its live escrow balance rather than a cached figure.

The blackout calendar is global rather than per-account, and both a `PlatformAdmin` and a `CampaignManager` may extend it. Each entry names a `blackout_date` on which no mail may drop and a `reason` explaining why — a federal holiday, a plant shutdown. Dates are globally unique on the calendar, so a repeat submission of a date already blacked out is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. The calendar is readable by `PlatformAdmin`, `CampaignManager` and `ProductionOperator`, optionally filtered to a specific `blackout_date`, and every subsequent mail-date derivation consults it. Like ledger rows, blackout entries are immutable records carrying only `id` and `created_at`.

### 3.2. Handwriting Styles, Templates, and What an Edit Destroys

A **handwriting style** describes what the robot arm physically does. Registering one — a `ProductionOperator` or `PlatformAdmin` task — takes a `name` such as "Casual Script", a `pen_type` of `GEL`, `FOUNTAIN` or `ROLLERBALL` naming the pen the arm must be fitted with, an `ink_type` of `INK_BLUE_A`, `INK_BLACK_B` or `INK_NAVY_C` naming the formulation the style binds to, a `chars_per_minute` throughput of at least 1 character per slot-minute, and an `ink_ul_per_1000_chars` consumption figure strictly greater than zero, expressed in microlitres per thousand characters. Those last two numbers are what turn a piece count into a machine-day footprint and an ink draw; §3.8 shows the arithmetic. `pen_type` is recorded for the operator's benefit and enters no calculation and no check anywhere in this API — of a style's five fields only `ink_type`, `chars_per_minute` and `ink_ul_per_1000_chars` are ever read downstream. A handwriting style is never edited or deleted once registered.

A **template** is the note itself. Creating one takes the owning `account_id`, the `handwriting_style_id` the robot will write it in, a `name`, the `body_text` copy, a `character_count` giving how many characters the pen actually lays down per piece, and optionally an `envelope_style` of `STANDARD`, `WINDOW` or `HAND_ADDRESSED`, defaulting to `STANDARD`. The character count is bounded at both ends: at least 1 character and at most 2000, both bounds inclusive, with anything outside that window rejected as `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Every new template starts with its content revision counter `version_no` at 1. `character_count` is taken from the client verbatim and is never derived from, nor reconciled against, `body_text`; the two may disagree freely, and it is `character_count` alone that the capacity plan of §3.8 reads. `envelope_style` is likewise recorded and returned but enters no pricing, capacity or eligibility calculation. Templates are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `account_id` or `handwriting_style_id`.

Editing a template is where this domain stops being simple. The edit accepts any subset of `body_text`, `character_count` and `envelope_style`, and it always increments the template's concurrency `version` by one. But whether it disturbs anything downstream depends entirely on *what* changed. **Only a change to `body_text` or to `character_count` bumps `version_no`.** An edit that touches nothing but `envelope_style` leaves `version_no` where it was, and the cascade below does not fire at all. The test is by **value** rather than by presence of the key: an edit that supplies a `body_text` or a `character_count` identical to the one already stored counts as no change, leaves `version_no` alone and fires nothing, while still incrementing the concurrency `version` like any other edit. A field supplied as `null`, and a field omitted entirely, are both ignored — there is no way to clear a template field through this endpoint — and an edit that supplies no recognised field at all is accepted, bumping `version` and nothing else.

When `version_no` does move, the following seven-hop cascade runs in order and must be completed atomically with the edit.

1. **Hop 1** — `version_no` increases by exactly one.
2. **Hop 2** — every `Proof` on this template whose status is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, and each such proof's `version` increments by one. Supersession applies to approved proofs regardless of what state their job is in.
3. **Hop 3** — every `MailJob` whose outstanding approval was superseded in hop 2 and whose `status` is `PROOFED`, `SCHEDULED` or `COMMITTED` reverts to `DRAFT` along `PROOFED → DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED → DRAFT` or `COMMITTED → DRAFT` respectively. A job in `DRAFT`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `MAILED` or `CLOSED` is not moved by this hop even though its proof was superseded. A job reverting from `PROOFED` clears its `pinned_template_version`; a job reverting from `SCHEDULED` additionally clears `mail_date`, `blackout_shifted_days` and `scheduled_at`. Each reverting job's `version` increments by one.
4. **Hop 4** — every `ProductionRun` on those reverting jobs whose status is `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → VOIDED`.
5. **Hop 5** — each run voided in hop 4 returns its `planned_ink_ul` to its ink lot's `remaining_ul` and releases its `planned_slot_minutes` on its machine-day bucket, per the conservation rules of §2.10.
6. **Hop 6** — each job that left `COMMITTED` appends a `REFUND` ledger entry for its `drawn_postage_cents` and raises the owning account's `permit_balance_cents` by that same amount.
7. **Hop 7** — every `DripStep` on those jobs whose status is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → PENDING`, clearing its `computed_mail_date`, `computed_in_home_date` and `shifted_days`.

Consider the practical effect. A `CampaignManager` who fixes a typo in body copy on a Friday afternoon has, by that single act, invalidated the customer's sign-off, pulled a committed job back to the drawing board, released the plant's reserved ink and machine time, refunded the postage draw, and unscheduled the drip steps that depended on it. That is the intended behaviour: content and commitment are coupled, and the coupling is enforced rather than advisory. It also means the edit endpoint is an ordinary write against the campaigns behind those jobs, and is therefore subject to the write gate of §2.5 like any other.

### 3.3. Importing, Standardizing and Screening the Recipient List

A **mailing list** is created empty under an `account_id` with a `name`, entering `import_status` `UPLOADED` with `record_count` and every derived count at 0. The import pipeline then advances through `UPLOADED → CASS_RUN → DEDUPED → LOCKED`, but only the last of those edges is one-way; the middle stages can be re-run and even walked backwards, which is where most of the subtlety lives.

**Adding records.** A `ListOperator` (or `PlatformAdmin`) imports recipients one at a time, and only while the list is still `UPLOADED` — once standardization has run, the record set is closed and a further import attempt is refused with `ERR_LIST_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Each record supplies `first_name`, `last_name`, `address_line1`, `city`, `state_code` (a two-letter state or province code) and `zip5` (a five-digit postal code) — both of those last two are checked only for being present and being strings, never for their length or their shape, so any string is accepted and stored verbatim — plus three optional values: `address_line2` for a unit or suite, defaulting to null when absent; `country_code`, defaulting to `US`; and `dpv_confirmation_code`, the delivery-point-validation outcome supplied with the record, defaulting to `U` and restricted to `Y`, `D`, `S`, `N` or `U`. On acceptance the record receives `insertion_seq`, a monotonically increasing 1-based position within its list, and two derived screening keys. `exact_key` is the record's given name, family name, primary street line and postal code joined in that order by a vertical-bar separator, with each of the three name and street components lower-cased and stripped of leading and trailing whitespace. `household_key` is formed the same way but omits the given name entirely, joining family name, primary street line and postal code. The list's `record_count` rises by one and its `version` increments by one. A list may hold at most 100000 records; that ceiling is documentary only — no endpoint counts against it, and no error in §5 is defined for crossing it. A newly imported record begins at `cass_status` `PENDING`, `deliverability_tier` `UNASSIGNED`, `dedupe_status` `UNSCREENED`, `mail_status` `NOT_MAILED`, `move_status` `NONE`, `reprint_count` 0 and `last_mail_job_id` null — five independent facts about the same row, four of which are parallel status axes that move on their own schedules.

**Standardization.** Running CASS/DPV standardization is permitted while the list is `UPLOADED`, `CASS_RUN` or `DEDUPED`, and it requires at least one record on the list — a pass over an empty list is refused with `ERR_LIST_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) at rung 16, the same error and the same rung as a wrong `import_status`. It visits **every** record regardless of that record's current screening state, and applies a deterministic mapping evaluated top to bottom, first match winning:

- If the record's `country_code` is anything other than `US`, its `cass_status` moves `PENDING → UNMAILABLE` and its `deliverability_tier` becomes `UNMAILABLE`. Foreign addresses are out of scope for this service's postal contracts, and this clause wins over the delivery-point code even when that code is favourable.
- Otherwise, if `dpv_confirmation_code` is `Y` — the delivery point was confirmed outright — `cass_status` moves `PENDING → STANDARDIZED` and `deliverability_tier` becomes `AUTOMATION`.
- Otherwise, if `dpv_confirmation_code` is `D` (primary address confirmed but the secondary unit is missing) or `S` (a secondary unit is present but invalid), `cass_status` moves `PENDING → STANDARDIZED` and `deliverability_tier` becomes `RETAIL`.
- Otherwise — that is, for `N` (not confirmed) or `U` (unconfirmed or never run) — `cass_status` moves `PENDING → UNMAILABLE` and `deliverability_tier` becomes `UNMAILABLE`.

Records that land at `UNMAILABLE` are excluded from every piece count and are never queued for mailing. Beyond the per-record mapping, a standardization pass has three list-level consequences: every record's `dedupe_status` is reset to `UNSCREENED` and the list's `dedupe_mode` is set back to null, discarding any prior screening entirely; `import_status` moves to `CASS_RUN` — the edge is `UPLOADED → CASS_RUN` on a first pass, the self-loop `CASS_RUN → CASS_RUN` on a repeat, and the backward edge `DEDUPED → CASS_RUN` when screening had already been done; and `automation_count`, `retail_count`, `unmailable_count`, `duplicate_count` and `mailable_count` are all recomputed. The list's `version` increments by one on every pass. The individual **records** are more discriminating: a record's own `version` and `updated_at` move only when the pass actually changes one of its three screening axes — `cass_status`, `deliverability_tier` or `dedupe_status`. A record the pass re-derives to precisely the state it already held is left entirely untouched, so a repeat standardization over an unchanged, already-standardized list bumps the list's `version` while leaving every record's `version` exactly where it stood. Re-running standardization on an already-deduped list is legitimate and its cost is exactly that the dedupe work must be redone.

**Screening for duplicates.** Deduplication requires the list to be in `CASS_RUN` or `DEDUPED` — attempting it on an `UPLOADED` list is `ERR_LIST_INVALID_STATE` — and takes a single input, `mode`, which must be `EXACT` or `HOUSEHOLD`. Every record on the list is first returned to `UNSCREENED`, then screened afresh; records whose tier is `UNMAILABLE` participate in screening like any other, they simply never count as mailable afterwards. Records are gathered into groups by the key the mode selects, and within each group they are ordered by `created_at` ascending; when two records in a group carry an identical `created_at`, the tie is broken by `insertion_seq` ascending. The record's `id` is never consulted for ordering. The first record in each ordered group survives and every later member is marked a duplicate.

The two modes differ only in which key groups the records and what the two outcome labels are called, but the business consequence is large.

- Under `EXACT`, records group on `exact_key`. The survivor's `dedupe_status` moves `UNSCREENED → UNIQUE` and later members move `UNSCREENED → DUP_EXACT`. A husband and wife at the same address have different given names, so they form two groups and remain two distinct pieces.
- Under `HOUSEHOLD`, records group on `household_key`. The survivor's `dedupe_status` moves `UNSCREENED → HOUSEHOLD_HEAD` and later members move `UNSCREENED → DUP_HOUSEHOLD`. That same husband and wife share a family name, street line and postal code, so they collapse into one piece. Whenever any two records on a list share a household key, `HOUSEHOLD` mode yields a strictly smaller `mailable_count` than `EXACT` mode over the identical data.

After screening, the list counters are recomputed: `automation_count` is the number of records whose `deliverability_tier` is `AUTOMATION` and whose `dedupe_status` is `UNSCREENED`, `UNIQUE` or `HOUSEHOLD_HEAD`; `retail_count` is the same count for `RETAIL` tier; `duplicate_count` is the number of records whose `dedupe_status` is `DUP_EXACT` or `DUP_HOUSEHOLD`; `unmailable_count` is the number whose tier is `UNMAILABLE`; and `mailable_count` is `automation_count` plus `retail_count`. The list's `dedupe_mode` records the mode just used, and `import_status` moves to `DEDUPED` — the edge is `CASS_RUN → DEDUPED` on a first screening and the self-loop `DEDUPED → DEDUPED` on a re-screen. A second run in a different mode is explicitly supported and can move counts in either direction: a record marked `DUP_HOUSEHOLD` under household screening travels `DUP_HOUSEHOLD → UNSCREENED` and then `UNSCREENED → UNIQUE` under an exact re-run, raising `mailable_count` again. The same reset edge back to `UNSCREENED` exists as `UNIQUE → UNSCREENED`, `HOUSEHOLD_HEAD → UNSCREENED` and `DUP_EXACT → UNSCREENED`, and a standardization pass applies it to every record on the list. The list's `version` increments on every run, and — exactly as with standardization — an individual record's `version` moves only if the screening actually changed one of its three screening axes.

**Locking.** Once the operator is satisfied, the list is locked. Locking requires `import_status` to be exactly `DEDUPED` and at least one mailable record; a list with `mailable_count` of zero cannot be locked, and the refusal is `ERR_LIST_INVALID_STATE`. Locking moves `import_status` `DEDUPED → LOCKED` and increments the list's `version`. `LOCKED` is terminal for the import pipeline, and it is enforced high on the ladder: at rung 12, every further list write — importing another record, re-running standardization, re-running deduplication, locking again, or applying a move update to any of its records — is refused with `ERR_LIST_LOCKED` (HTTP 409), which pre-empts the more specific `ERR_LIST_INVALID_STATE` those same operations would otherwise report.

**Move updates.** Between standardization and locking, a `ListOperator` may apply an NCOA move-update to a single record. The payload's `new_address_line1` key must be present, and its value is the fork in the road: a non-null forwarding street line means the recipient moved and left an address, while an explicit null means the recipient moved and left none. Three optional values accompany it — `new_city`, `new_state_code`, `new_zip5` and `new_dpv_confirmation_code`, that last one restricted to the same five-member set as the original delivery-point code.

The two branches have deliberately divergent effects on two of the record's four axes. A forwarding address moves `move_status` `NONE → MOVE_APPLIED` (or `MOVE_NO_FORWARD → MOVE_APPLIED`, if an earlier update had found no forwarding address), rewrites `address_line1` from `new_address_line1`, and rewrites `city`, `state_code`, `zip5` and `dpv_confirmation_code` from their accompanying values **only where those values are actually supplied and non-null** — an omitted `new_city` or `new_zip5` leaves that component of the address exactly as it was — and then re-derives `cass_status` and `deliverability_tier` through the same mapping standardization uses. That re-derivation can move the record's CASS axis in either direction: a record sitting at `UNMAILABLE` whose new delivery-point code is `Y`, `D` or `S` recovers along `UNMAILABLE → STANDARDIZED`, and a record sitting at `STANDARDIZED` whose new code is `N` or `U` falls along `STANDARDIZED → UNMAILABLE`. A null forwarding address, by contrast, moves `move_status` `NONE → MOVE_NO_FORWARD`, forces `dpv_confirmation_code` to `N`, and drives `deliverability_tier` to `UNMAILABLE`; it leaves `address_line1`, `city`, `state_code` and `zip5` exactly as they stood and ignores any `new_city`, `new_state_code`, `new_zip5` or `new_dpv_confirmation_code` that happens to accompany it, though those values are still type- and enum-checked at rungs 6 and 7 before being discarded. Either branch recomputes the record's `exact_key` and `household_key` and the owning list's partition counts, and increments the record's `version` by one unconditionally — a move update is the one record write that bumps `version` even when it changes nothing observable. Neither branch disturbs the record's `dedupe_status`, so a move that turns a survivor unmailable leaves it labelled a survivor.

The distinction between those two outcomes is not merely bookkeeping — it decides money later. A record whose `move_status` is `MOVE_APPLIED` when a piece comes back is entitled to a free reprint; a record at `NONE` or `MOVE_NO_FORWARD` is entitled instead to a refund settlement that may cost the account a fee. §3.9 works through both.

Recipient records are readable individually and as a filtered collection. The collection accepts `mailing_list_id`, `last_mail_job_id`, and any of the four parallel axes — `cass_status`, `dedupe_status`, `mail_status`, `move_status` — as filters, which is how an operator finds, say, every `RETURNED` record on a particular job.

### 3.4. Campaigns, Mail Jobs and the Provisional Quote

**Opening a campaign.** A `PlatformAdmin`, `AccountOwner` or `CampaignManager` opens a campaign under an `account_id` with a `name`. It begins at `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT`, `compliance_status` `CLEAR`, `billing_status` `OPEN` and therefore `write_gate` `OPEN`, with `hold_reason` null, `mailed_piece_count` and `returned_piece_count` at 0, and `committed_postage_cents`, `refunded_postage_cents` and `forfeited_postage_cents` at 0. Campaigns are readable individually — a read returns all three axes plus the derived gate — and as a collection filtered by `account_id` or `lifecycle_status`. `ComplianceOfficer`, `ListOperator` and `ProductionOperator` may read a campaign; only the first four roles may list them.

**Working the lifecycle axis.** Activation, driven by a `CampaignManager` or `PlatformAdmin`, moves the campaign `DRAFT → ACTIVE` on its first use and `PAUSED → ACTIVE` thereafter; both edges require the write gate to be `OPEN` and increment the campaign's `version` by one. Pausing moves `ACTIVE → PAUSED`, again requiring an open gate, and carries one side effect beyond the status change: every drip step belonging to this campaign's schedules whose status is `SCHEDULED` is unscheduled back to `PENDING`, clearing the derived dates it was holding. The lifecycle axis is genuinely cyclic — a campaign may be paused and reactivated as often as the agency likes — and terminates only at `COMPLETED`, which is reached exclusively through the billing close described in §3.9. Rung 15 polices this axis and the compliance axis together: activation demands `DRAFT` or `PAUSED`, pausing demands `ACTIVE`, closing the books demands `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, placing a hold demands `compliance_status` `CLEAR`, and lifting one demands `compliance_status` `HOLD`. Any mismatch is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Note that rung 15 sits below the two write-gate rungs, so the campaign-state complaint is only ever heard on a campaign whose gate is `OPEN`; a second hold placed on an already-held campaign is pre-empted at rung 9, and any of these five operations against a billing-closed campaign is pre-empted at rung 10. Outside those five transitions **no rule anywhere in this specification reads `lifecycle_status`**: a job under a campaign still sitting in `DRAFT` may be quoted, scheduled, committed, produced and dropped into the mail exactly as one under an `ACTIVE` campaign, and drip schedules and steps behave the same way. The lifecycle axis is reporting, not enforcement.

**Working the compliance axis.** A `ComplianceOfficer` (or `PlatformAdmin`) places a hold by supplying a `hold_reason`, which is stored on the campaign and survives the hold being lifted, so the most recent reason remains readable afterwards; before any hold has ever been placed the field is null. Placing a hold moves `compliance_status` `CLEAR → HOLD`, sets `write_gate` to `FROZEN`, and increments `version`. It requires `billing_status` to still be `OPEN` — a campaign whose books are closed cannot be newly held, and the refusal comes from rung 10 as `ERR_CAMPAIGN_BILLING_CLOSED`. Lifting a hold moves `HOLD → CLEAR` and restores `write_gate` to `OPEN` provided `billing_status` is still `OPEN`; if the books were closed in the meantime the gate stays `FROZEN`. Lifting is the single write in the entire API exempt from rung 9, which is what makes recovery from a hold possible at all; it is not exempt from rung 10. This axis evolves entirely independently of the lifecycle axis — a `DRAFT` campaign can be held, an `ACTIVE` one can be held, and holding never changes `lifecycle_status`.

**Creating a mail job.** A job is the unit of production and of pricing. Creating one names the owning `campaign_id`, the `template_id` supplying content and handwriting style, a `job_kind`, and a `mail_class`, plus — depending on the kind — `mailing_list_id`, `eddm_rate_variant` and an optional `target_in_home_date`. The kind agreement rules of §2.9 are checked at rung 14, before the mailing list's own state is examined at rung 16, so an `ADDRESSED` job naming a list that is still `UPLOADED` is rejected with `ERR_LIST_INVALID_STATE` only once its field combination is coherent: an addressed job may be created against a list in `CASS_RUN`, `DEDUPED` or `LOCKED`, but not one that has never been standardized. A new job starts at `status` `DRAFT`, with `pinned_template_version`, `provisional_postage_cents`, `final_postage_cents`, `handling_fee_cents`, `volume_credit_cents`, `total_due_cents`, `mail_date` and `scheduled_at` all null, `presort_tier` at `UNASSIGNED`, `blackout_shifted_days` at 0, and every piece count — `automation_piece_count`, `retail_piece_count`, `unmailable_piece_count`, `eddm_piece_count`, `billable_piece_count`, `returned_piece_count` — and `drawn_postage_cents` and `refunded_postage_cents` at 0. A `target_in_home_date` supplied at creation is parsed and stored without any temporal check whatever: rung 27 governs scheduling and drip-step creation only, so a job may perfectly well be created carrying an in-home date already in the past, and will hear `ERR_IN_HOME_DATE_PAST` only when someone tries to schedule it. Jobs are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `campaign_id`, `template_id`, `mailing_list_id`, `status` or `job_kind`.

**Selecting carrier routes.** An EDDM job's pieces come from carrier routes rather than from names, and a `CampaignManager` adds them one at a time while the job is still `DRAFT` — a later attempt is `ERR_MAIL_JOB_INVALID_STATE`, and an attempt against an `ADDRESSED` job is `ERR_JOB_KIND_MISMATCH` at the earlier rung 14. Each selection carries a `route_code` identifying the carrier route, a `total_deliveries` count of at least 1 giving every delivery point on that route, and the split of what the mailer actually wants to hit: `residential_piece_count` and `business_piece_count`, each at least 0. From those the server computes `selected_piece_count` as the sum of the residential and business counts, and `saturation_pct` as one hundred multiplied by `selected_piece_count`, divided by `total_deliveries`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Route codes are unique within a job, so re-submitting one already selected on that job is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. Each accepted selection rewrites the job's `eddm_piece_count` — and, alongside it, the job's `billable_piece_count`, since for an EDDM job the two are the same number — to the sum of `selected_piece_count` across every route now on the job, and raises the job's `version` by one. Selections are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `mail_job_id`. When a later quote or commitment refreshes a selection's `selected_piece_count` and `saturation_pct`, it rewrites those two fields in place **without** incrementing the selection's own `version` or `updated_at`; a route selection's `version` therefore never moves past 1.

**The provisional quote.** Before anything is committed, an `AccountOwner`, `CampaignManager` or `PlatformAdmin` may ask the system to price the job. The quote is available while the job is `DRAFT` or `PROOFED`; in any later state it is refused with `ERR_MAIL_JOB_INVALID_STATE`. Quoting writes `provisional_postage_cents`, refreshes `automation_piece_count`, `retail_piece_count`, `unmailable_piece_count`, `eddm_piece_count`, `billable_piece_count` and `presort_tier`, and increments the job's `version`.

The population the quote counts is what distinguishes it from the final price, and the difference is exactly one clause. **The provisional quote counts every record whose `deliverability_tier` is `AUTOMATION` or `RETAIL`, ignoring `dedupe_status` entirely** — it is the number a customer sees before the list has been screened, and it therefore includes records that later screening will discard. The final price at commit, described in §3.7, additionally requires each surviving record's `dedupe_status` to be `UNSCREENED`, `UNIQUE` or `HOUSEHOLD_HEAD`. Everything else about the two calculations is identical. Call the count of surviving automation-tier records **A** and the count of surviving retail-tier records **R**; for an addressed job `billable_piece_count` is A plus R, `unmailable_piece_count` is the count of the list's records at `UNMAILABLE` tier, and `eddm_piece_count` is 0. For an EDDM job all three recipient-level counts are 0 and `billable_piece_count` is `eddm_piece_count`.

**Presort tier selection.** For an addressed job the per-piece rate depends on how many automation-tier pieces the job carries, because the postal service discounts deeply sorted mail. Only A enters this decision; retail-tier and unmailable pieces never affect tier selection. An EDDM job's `presort_tier` is always `EDDM_ROUTE`. A `FIRST_CLASS` job takes `PRESORT_AADC` when A is at least 6 and `NO_PRESORT` otherwise. A `MARKETING_MAIL` job is evaluated against three thresholds highest-first, the first satisfied threshold winning: `PRESORT_5D` at 12 or more automation pieces, `PRESORT_AADC` at 9 or more, `PRESORT_MIXED_AADC` at 6 or more, and `NO_PRESORT` below that. Every threshold is inclusive — six automation pieces select `PRESORT_MIXED_AADC`, five select `NO_PRESORT`.

**Pricing an addressed job.** The rate card is quoted in cents per piece to one decimal place. For `MARKETING_MAIL`, the automation-tier rate follows the selected presort tier: 23.9 cents at `PRESORT_5D`, 28.1 cents at `PRESORT_AADC`, 31.8 cents at `PRESORT_MIXED_AADC`, and 51.0 cents when the job misses every presort tier. Retail-tier pieces on a marketing job always price at 51.6 cents each. For `FIRST_CLASS`, automation-tier pieces price at 58.0 cents each when A is at least 6, and at the retail rate of 73.0 cents each when A falls below that minimum; retail-tier pieces on a first-class job always price at 73.0 cents each.

**The rounding order is pinned and is not a matter of taste.** The postage total is built in three steps. First, the automation subtotal is A multiplied by the automation rate, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Second, the retail subtotal is R multiplied by the retail rate, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Third, the postage total is those two whole-cent subtotals added together. Summing the two unrounded products and rounding once at the end is wrong, and it is observably wrong: the two approaches differ by one cent whenever both tier fractions are at least one half and their sum is below one and a half. Six automation pieces at 31.8 cents give 190.8, which rounds to 191; one retail piece at 51.6 gives 51.6, which rounds to 52; the correct total is 243 cents, whereas rounding the combined 242.4 once yields 242.

**Pricing an EDDM job.** An EDDM job has a single tier and a single rounding. Every route selection on the job is first refreshed — `selected_piece_count` recomputed as residential plus business, `saturation_pct` recomputed to two decimal places, rounded half up — and the job's total pieces **P** is the sum of `selected_piece_count` across all of the job's routes. Eligibility for the declared `eddm_rate_variant` is then evaluated over **every** route on the job, and one failing route fails the whole job. The `SATURATION` variant demands complete coverage: each route's `selected_piece_count` must equal that route's `total_deliveries`. The `HIGH_DENSITY_PLUS` variant demands only that each route carry at least 3 residential pieces, which lets a mailer take partially covered routes. If the declared variant is not eligible for every route, the request fails with `ERR_EDDM_VARIANT_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). Otherwise the rate is 22.3 cents per piece under `SATURATION` and 25.6 cents per piece under `HIGH_DENSITY_PLUS`, and the postage total is P multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents in a single step. The variant choice is consequential on identical data: two fully covered routes of six deliveries each yield twelve pieces, priced at 268 cents under `SATURATION` and 307 cents under `HIGH_DENSITY_PLUS`.

**Nothing to price.** If a job's billable piece count comes out at zero — an addressed job whose survivors were all screened away or all found unmailable, or an EDDM job with no routes selected — the quote is refused with `ERR_NO_MAILABLE_PIECES` (HTTP 422). Because the eligibility check sits at rung 33 and the emptiness check at rung 35, an EDDM job whose routes are individually ineligible for its declared variant reports `ERR_EDDM_VARIANT_INELIGIBLE` rather than the emptiness complaint. An EDDM job with **no** routes at all passes rung 33 vacuously — eligibility is a claim about every route, and there are none — and is caught at rung 35. A quote refused at either rung writes nothing: the piece counts, the `presort_tier` and `provisional_postage_cents` are recomputed as part of a successful quote only, so a job whose quote was refused still carries whatever counts an earlier successful quote left behind, and the job's `version` does not move.

### 3.5. Proof, Approval, and the Thirty-Day Window

No job may be scheduled or committed until the customer has signed off on a rendering of the piece. That sign-off is a `Proof`, and its lifecycle is `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, with a revision loop back through `REJECTED` and two ways for a live approval to stop counting.

A proof is created against a `mail_job_id` and the `template_id` it renders, entering `status` `DRAFT` with `pinned_template_version`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `approved_at`, `expires_at` and `reject_reason` all null. `PlatformAdmin`, `AccountOwner` and `CampaignManager` may create one. The `template_id` a proof renders is **not** required to equal the `template_id` of the mail job it is created against; no rung compares the two, and every later evaluation of the approval gate reads the proof's own template rather than the job's. Proofs are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `template_id`, `mail_job_id` or `status`.

**Submission** is a `CampaignManager` (or `PlatformAdmin`) act. It requires the campaign's write gate to be `OPEN` and the proof to be in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`; anything else is `ERR_PROOF_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) at rung 18. Submitting moves the proof `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, snapshots `pinned_template_version` from the template's current content revision counter `version_no`, records `submitted_by_user_id` as the submitting caller's `X-User-ID`, and increments the proof's `version`. The `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` edge behaves identically and re-pins `pinned_template_version` to whatever `version_no` the template carries at that moment, which is the point of the revision loop: the manager fixes the copy, the template's revision counter advances, and the resubmitted proof binds to the corrected content. A `reject_reason` recorded by the earlier rejection is left standing on the proof rather than cleared by the resubmission, so a `SUBMITTED` proof may legitimately carry a non-null `reject_reason` from a previous round.

**Approval** belongs to the `AccountOwner` (and to `PlatformAdmin`). The request carries `expected_template_version` — the template's concurrency `version` as last observed, per §2.7 — and is evaluated in ladder order: the concurrency check at rung 11 fires `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before the proof's own status is examined at rung 18, and the segregation-of-duties check at rung 23 fires `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` only after the proof has been confirmed to be in `SUBMITTED`. An approval therefore reports a wrong-state proof before it reports a self-approval. On success the proof moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, `approved_by_user_id` is set to the approver's `X-User-ID`, `approved_at` is set to the logical clock's current instant, and `expires_at` is set to exactly thirty days after `approved_at`. The owning mail job moves `DRAFT → PROOFED` and takes the proof's `pinned_template_version` as its own, incrementing its own `version` as it does so. That step is taken **only from `DRAFT`**: approving a second proof on a job that has already reached `PROOFED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMMITTED` or beyond leaves the job's `status`, its `pinned_template_version` and its `version` all untouched, even though the new approval is now the one the gate at rungs 24 and 25 will consult. The template's concurrency `version` increments by one, and so does the proof's.

**Rejection** is likewise an `AccountOwner` act, requires an open write gate and a `SUBMITTED` proof, carries a mandatory `reject_reason` that is stored on the proof, moves it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, and increments its `version`. Crucially, rejection leaves the mail job exactly where it was, in `DRAFT` — approval is what advances a job, rejection merely declines to.

A live approval can stop being live in two different ways, and the system keeps them distinguishable. The first is **expiry**: once the logical clock reaches or passes `expires_at`, an `APPROVED` proof becomes `EXPIRED`, and the boundary is inclusive — an instant exactly equal to `expires_at` is already expired. Expiry is materialized rather than swept: reading a proof individually moves it `APPROVED → EXPIRED` if the window has elapsed, incrementing its `version`, and so does any evaluation of the approval gate. The owning job's own `status` is untouched by expiry; what changes is that the job can no longer be scheduled or committed. The second is **supersession**: editing the template's copy moves every `APPROVED` proof on it to `SUPERSEDED`, as described in hop 2 of §3.2's cascade. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal — a superseded or expired proof is never revived, and a new proof must be created and submitted afresh.

Two rungs read the approval state of a job, and the distinction between them is the whole reason they are two rungs rather than one. Rung 24 asks whether the job has **ever** had a proof reach `APPROVED`. If it has not, the answer is `ERR_PROOF_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). Rung 25 asks whether the job's most recent approval is **still live** — that its proof's status is `APPROVED` rather than `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED`, that its `pinned_template_version` still equals the template's current `version_no`, and that the logical clock is strictly before its `expires_at`. If an approval exists but fails any of those, the answer is `ERR_PROOF_APPROVAL_STALE` (HTTP 409). A `DRAFT` job that has never been proofed reports the former; a `DRAFT` job that reverted through a void or a content edit and whose approval has since gone stale reports the latter. Both rungs read the job's proofs by way of the same evaluation, and three details of it are fixed. A proof counts as having "ever reached approval" precisely when its `approved_at` is non-null, which survives both the `SUPERSEDED` and the `EXPIRED` transitions — so a job all of whose approvals have been superseded clears rung 24 and fails at rung 25, never the other way round. The job's **most recent** approval is the one with the greatest `approved_at`, ties broken by the greater `id`, that is by the later-created proof; only that one proof is examined at rung 25, and an older approval that is still live cannot rescue a newer one that is not. And the evaluation materialises expiry as it goes: every proof on the job is moved `APPROVED → EXPIRED` where the window has elapsed, incrementing that proof's `version`, before the two rungs are answered — so a failed schedule or commit can leave proof versions bumped behind it.

Two questions in this area need settling explicitly, because each has a plausible alternative reading that this specification rejects. **First**, approval carries a stated precondition that the proof's `pinned_template_version` still equals the template's `version_no` — but no rung in the ladder is scoped to approval for that condition, since rung 25 is declared to guard scheduling and committing. The approval therefore **proceeds** on the pinned version it already holds: the `expected_template_version` concurrency field checked at rung 11 is the only template guard the approval endpoint applies, and a proof whose `pinned_template_version` has fallen behind the template's `version_no` is approved normally, moving `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and stamping `approved_at` and `expires_at` like any other. Refusing it here would widen rung 25's declared scope; letting it stand is also what keeps the "an approval exists but is already stale" path to `ERR_PROOF_APPROVAL_STALE` reachable, since the very next schedule or commit against that job answers exactly that. **Second**, expiry is described as materializing on any read of a proof: reading **one** proof by identifier is such a read and does move it `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, incrementing its `version`, as does any evaluation of the approval gate. Enumerating proofs through the **collection** endpoint does **not**: the list endpoint carries no side effects whatever, and an elapsed proof continues to be reported there as `APPROVED` until it is read individually or a gate consults it. Every `EXPIRED`-related behaviour remains reachable through those two paths.

### 3.6. Drop Dates, the Blackout Walk, and Drip Sequences

Mail does not arrive on the day it is dropped, and it cannot be dropped on every day of the calendar. One derivation reconciles those two facts, and it is used in exactly two places: when a `CampaignManager` schedules a job directly, and when a step is added to a drip sequence.

**Transit assumptions.** Each mail class carries a fixed transit expectation in calendar days: 3 days for `FIRST_CLASS`, 10 days for `MARKETING_MAIL`, and 12 days for `EDDM`. A saturation mailing therefore needs nearly two weeks of runway where a first-class note needs less than half a week — the same anchor date produces very different drop dates depending on the class the job was created with.

**The derivation, step by step.** The current date is the calendar-date part of the logical clock.

1. If the supplied anchor date is strictly earlier than the current date, the request is refused with `ERR_IN_HOME_DATE_PAST` (HTTP 422) at rung 27. The comparison is strict, so an anchor equal to today passes this step.
2. A raw drop date is derived from the anchor according to the anchoring variant. Under `ANCHOR_IN_HOME`, the supplied date is read as the desired in-home date and the raw drop date is that date less the class's transit days. Under `ANCHOR_MAIL_DATE`, the supplied date **is** the drop date and is carried through unchanged.
3. The raw drop date is then walked backwards over the blackout calendar. While a blackout entry exists for the raw drop date, the date moves back by one calendar day and a shift counter rises by one. If the shift counter would exceed 5, the request is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_UNRESOLVABLE` (HTTP 422) at rung 28. The cap is inclusive: a walk that shifts exactly 5 days is accepted; one that would need 6 is not.
4. Finally, if the resolved drop date is earlier than the current date plus 3 days of production lead time, the request is refused with `ERR_MAIL_DATE_UNREACHABLE` (HTTP 422) at rung 29. This comparison is also strict, so a drop date landing exactly on the third day from today is accepted.

The outputs are the resolved drop date, the in-home date computed as the resolved drop date plus the class's transit days, and the shift count. Ordering across these steps is load-bearing. An anchor date in the past reports `ERR_IN_HOME_DATE_PAST` even when the drop date it would have produced is also unreachable — that is precisely how the two temporal errors stay distinguishable, and rung 27 sitting above rung 29 is what makes it happen. Worked through: an anchor of 2026-06-15 on a `MARKETING_MAIL` job under `ANCHOR_IN_HOME`, with no blackouts in the way, resolves to a drop date of 2026-06-05; the same anchor under `ANCHOR_MAIL_DATE` resolves to a drop date of 2026-06-15 and an in-home date of 2026-06-25 — the identical input, ten days apart.

**Scheduling a job directly.** A `CampaignManager` or `PlatformAdmin` schedules a job, moving it `PROOFED → SCHEDULED`. The write requires an open campaign write gate, a job in `DRAFT` or `PROOFED` at rung 17, and a live approval at rungs 24 and 25. Scheduling always uses the `ANCHOR_IN_HOME` reading and takes its anchor from the job's `target_in_home_date`; the request body may carry a `target_in_home_date` of its own. On success it writes `mail_date` and `blackout_shifted_days` from the derivation, sets `scheduled_at` to the logical clock's current instant, and increments the job's `version`.

A `DRAFT` job is deliberately admitted at rung 17 rather than refused there, and the reason is diagnostic: by letting a `DRAFT` job through the state gate, the ladder allows rungs 24 and 25 to explain *why* it cannot be scheduled instead of returning a bare state complaint. There are two ways a job comes to be `DRAFT` at this point. It may have reverted through a void or a content edit while an approval stayed live, in which case scheduling succeeds along the re-entry edge `DRAFT → SCHEDULED`, re-pins `pinned_template_version` from that live approval, and proceeds exactly as from `PROOFED`. Or it may never have been approved at all, in which case rung 24 answers `ERR_PROOF_NOT_APPROVED` — and this is the only path by which that error is reachable, since a job can enter `PROOFED` only through an approval in the first place.

A schedule request that supplies no `target_in_home_date` in its body **and** is aimed at a job that itself holds none — a job created without one and never given one — is treated as a **missing required input** and answers `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400) at **rung 6**, exactly as though `target_in_home_date` had been a declared required field of the request. The body value is otherwise an override, written onto the job's `target_in_home_date` when present; when absent the job's stored value is used and left alone. Resolving the absence at rung 6 rather than letting a null date fall through to a temporal rung matters twice over: no rung in the ladder is defined to compare a null date, and pinning it here leaves rungs 27, 28 and 29 reachable only with real dates. Note that the rung-6 complaint therefore pre-empts the write gate, the job's state gate and the approval gate alike.

**Drip sequences.** A campaign may run a sequence of drops rather than a single one. A `CampaignManager` creates a `DripSchedule` under a `campaign_id` with a `name` and a fixed `anchor_variant` of either `ANCHOR_IN_HOME` or `ANCHOR_MAIL_DATE`, which governs how every one of its steps reads its date. The schedule begins at `status` `DRAFT` with `step_count` 0, moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE` when its first step is accepted, and can end at `CANCELLED`. Schedules are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `campaign_id` or `status`.

Adding a step names the `mail_job_id` it drops, a `sequence_no` giving its ordering position within the schedule, an `anchor_date` interpreted per the schedule's variant, and `expected_drip_schedule_version` for the concurrency check of §2.7. Sequence numbers must be at least 1 and are unique within a schedule, so reusing one is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` at rung 26 — which sits above every temporal rung, so a colliding sequence number is reported even when the supplied date is also bad. A schedule that has been cancelled accepts no further steps and answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) at rung 20.

Three constraints then apply in ladder order. The derivation of §3.6 runs, producing the temporal refusals at rungs 27 through 29. Next, at rung 30, the new step's resolved drop date must differ from the resolved drop date of **every** existing non-cancelled sibling step on the same schedule by at least 2 calendar days; a closer spacing is `ERR_DRIP_GAP_TOO_SMALL` (HTTP 422). The comparison runs over every non-cancelled sibling, and a sibling that holds **no** resolved drop date is skipped as having nothing to conflict with — the two readings converge on that outcome, so the rule may be stated either way. What matters in practice is which siblings actually hold a date: a step created in `PENDING` because its job was not yet `PROOFED` still writes `computed_mail_date`, `computed_in_home_date` and `shifted_days` at creation and therefore **does** participate; the only siblings with a null `computed_mail_date` are those a campaign pause or hop 7 of §3.2's cascade unscheduled back to `PENDING`, and those alone are skipped. `RELEASED` and `MAILED` siblings participate like any other. Finally, at rung 31, the schedule must be holding fewer than 8 non-cancelled steps before this one is added; an attempt to add a ninth is `ERR_DRIP_STEP_LIMIT` (HTTP 409).

An accepted step writes `computed_mail_date`, `computed_in_home_date` and `shifted_days`, and takes its initial status from the job it drops: a step whose job is in `PROOFED` or `SCHEDULED` is admitted straight along `PENDING → SCHEDULED`, while a step whose job is in any other status rests at `PENDING`. That initial status is decided once, at creation, from the job's status at that instant; the job later reaching `PROOFED` or `SCHEDULED` does **not** retroactively promote a step that was admitted at `PENDING`, and no endpoint in §4 applies the `PENDING → SCHEDULED` edge afterwards. Either way the step writes its `computed_mail_date`, `computed_in_home_date` and `shifted_days` from the derivation. The schedule moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE` on the first accepted step, `step_count` is recomputed as the number of its non-cancelled steps, and the schedule's `version` increments by one — on every accepted step, including those after the first. A schedule that has itself reached `CANCELLED` reports `step_count` 0 regardless of how many non-cancelled steps still hang off it.

Steps then move through their own lifecycle. Releasing a step — a `CampaignManager` act requiring an open write gate, a step in `SCHEDULED`, and a job in `COMMITTED` — moves it `SCHEDULED → RELEASED` and increments its `version`. Both of those preconditions are evaluated together at **rung 20**, with the step's own status examined first: a step that is not `SCHEDULED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when its job is also in the wrong state, and only a `SCHEDULED` step whose job is anything other than `COMMITTED` answers `ERR_MAIL_JOB_INVALID_STATE` — which on this one endpoint is therefore reported from rung 20 rather than from rung 17. When the job is subsequently dropped into the mail stream, every `RELEASED` step on that job moves `RELEASED → MAILED`, incrementing its `version`. Two events unschedule a step, moving it `SCHEDULED → PENDING` and clearing `computed_mail_date`, `computed_in_home_date` and `shifted_days`: pausing the owning campaign, and the seventh hop of the content-edit cascade in §3.2. Both leave the step at `PENDING` holding no dates at all, and neither is undone by anything: reactivating the campaign, re-approving the proof or re-scheduling the job does not restore the step, because the `PENDING → SCHEDULED` edge is applied only when a step is first created. An unscheduled step stays `PENDING` — still counting toward its schedule's `step_count` and toward the rung-31 limit of eight, but skipped by the rung-30 gap comparison — until the books close and cancel it, as §3.9 describes. Steps are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `drip_schedule_id`, `mail_job_id` or `status`.

### 3.7. Commitment, Settlement, and the Compensating Void

Committing is the moment the job stops being a plan. A `CampaignManager` or `PlatformAdmin` commits a job in `SCHEDULED`, moving it `SCHEDULED → COMMITTED`, and the operation prices the job finally, draws money from the permit escrow, and queues the physical pieces. It requires an open campaign write gate at rungs 9 and 10, a job in `SCHEDULED` at rung 17, a live approval at rungs 24 and 25, and — for an addressed job — a mailing list in `import_status` `LOCKED` at rung 16, which is the whole point of locking: a job may be *created* against a list that is merely standardized, but it may not be *committed* until the list is frozen. For an EDDM job, the eligibility of its declared rate variant is re-checked at rung 33 and its total selected pieces must fall within 5 and 50 inclusive at rung 34 — fewer than 5 pieces is not worth entering, more than 50 exceeds what one job-day may carry — with a violation answering `ERR_EDDM_PIECE_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422). Either kind of job must carry at least one billable piece at rung 35.

**Final pricing.** Postage is recomputed with the `FINAL` reading of the population: a record survives only if its `deliverability_tier` is `AUTOMATION` or `RETAIL` **and** its `dedupe_status` is `UNSCREENED`, `UNIQUE` or `HOUSEHOLD_HEAD`. Duplicates found by screening are excluded. Everything downstream of that clause — presort tier selection from the automation count, the rate card, and the pinned round-then-sum order — is exactly as described for the provisional quote in §3.4, and the result is written to `final_postage_cents` along with a refreshed `presort_tier`.

A consequence that surprises customers and must not be smoothed over: because removing pieces can drop the automation count below a presort threshold, **the final per-piece rate can be higher than the provisional one, and the final total can exceed the provisional total.** A marketing job quoted with six automation pieces at the mixed-AADC rate and one retail piece comes to 243 cents provisionally; if household screening then collapses one of those six, the job falls to five automation pieces, misses every presort tier, and prices at 51.0 cents each — 255 cents for the automation tier plus 52 for the retail piece, or 307 cents in total. Fewer pieces, more money. That is correct behaviour, not a defect.

**Settlement.** With final postage in hand, the amount due is built in five pinned steps, and the order matters because the figures look circular — the volume credit is a percentage of a total that itself contains a handling fee derived from postage.

1. Postage is the `final_postage_cents` just computed.
2. The handling fee is 250 cents of base charge plus eight percent of postage, with that percentage component rounded **up** to the next whole cent. The result is written to `handling_fee_cents`.
3. The subtotal is postage plus the handling fee.
4. The volume credit is five percent of the subtotal, rounded **down** to the whole cent below, but only when the job carries at least 6 billable pieces; a job with 5 or fewer billable pieces earns no credit and the figure is 0. The result is written to `volume_credit_cents`.
5. The amount due is the subtotal less the volume credit, and is written to `total_due_cents`.

The credit is computed once, from the subtotal, and is never recomputed against the reduced amount due — there is no iteration to a fixed point here, only the value step 4 produces on its single pass.

**Drawing the escrow.** The account's `permit_balance_cents` must be at least `total_due_cents`; if it is not, the commit fails at rung 41 with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMIT_BALANCE` (HTTP 402), the lowest rung on the ladder and therefore the last complaint a request can earn. On success the server appends a `DRAW` ledger entry for `total_due_cents` attributed to this job and campaign, lowers `permit_balance_cents` by that amount, sets the job's `drawn_postage_cents` to the same figure, and recomputes the campaign's `committed_postage_cents`. For an addressed job, every surviving recipient record moves `NOT_MAILED → QUEUED` and takes `last_mail_job_id` pointing at this job, its own `version` incrementing as it does so; a surviving record already in any other `mail_status` — `QUEUED`, `MAILED`, `RETURNED` or `DELIVERED_ASSUMED` from an earlier job — is left entirely alone and does **not** take this job's `last_mail_job_id`, even though it was counted and priced as a billable piece. An EDDM job has no records to move. The job's `version` increments by one. Exactly one ledger row is written per commit.

**Voiding.** A committed job can be unwound in full, and the unwinding is a compensation rather than an erasure. A `CampaignManager` voids a job in `COMMITTED` — any other status is `ERR_MAIL_JOB_INVALID_STATE` — optionally supplying a `reason`, and the job moves `COMMITTED → DRAFT`. Five things happen together. A `REFUND` ledger entry is appended for exactly the job's `drawn_postage_cents` and the account's `permit_balance_cents` rises by precisely that amount, restoring the postage-money conservation invariant of §2.10. The job's `drawn_postage_cents` is set to 0, and its `refunded_postage_cents` is not a stored figure at all but is re-derived on every read as the sum of `amount_cents` over every `REFUND` ledger row attributed to this job — so it accumulates across repeated commit-and-void cycles rather than recording only the latest return, and it also picks up the refund raised by hop 6 of §3.2's cascade. Every production run on the job whose status is `RESERVED` or `FAILED` moves to `VOIDED`, returning its ink and its machine slot-minutes. Every `QUEUED` recipient record on the job returns to `NOT_MAILED` with `last_mail_job_id` cleared. And the job's derived figures are cleared: `final_postage_cents`, `handling_fee_cents`, `volume_credit_cents`, `total_due_cents`, `mail_date`, `scheduled_at` and `pinned_template_version` all return to null. That list is exhaustive, and the omissions are deliberate: `blackout_shifted_days`, `provisional_postage_cents`, `target_in_home_date`, `presort_tier` and all five piece counts survive the void with whatever values the last quote or commitment wrote into them. The job's `version` increments by one. A voided job is a `DRAFT` job again, and if its approval is still live it can be re-scheduled and re-committed without a fresh sign-off.

### 3.8. The Plant Floor: Machines, Ink, and Robot-Pen Runs

**Machines and lots.** A `ProductionOperator` registers a robot-pen machine with a globally unique `machine_code`, an optional `daily_capacity_minutes` of at least 1 slot-minute defaulting to 480 — eight hours of pen time per calendar day — and an optional `status` of `ONLINE` or `OFFLINE`, defaulting to `ONLINE`. Ink arrives as lots: a globally unique `lot_code`, an `ink_type` naming the formulation, and an `initial_volume_ul` of at least 1 microlitre. A new lot's `remaining_ul` is initialised to its `initial_volume_ul` and its `status` to `SEALED`. Reusing a machine code or a lot code is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. Both machines and lots are readable individually. No endpoint in §4 changes a machine's `status` or its `daily_capacity_minutes` after registration, so `ERR_MACHINE_OFFLINE` is reachable only against a machine that was registered `OFFLINE` in the first place, and a machine's `version` never moves past 1.

An ink lot's status tracks its working life rather than only its volume. It opens on first use, moving `SEALED → OPEN` when a run first draws on it. It ends at `DEPLETED`, along the edge `OPEN → DEPLETED`, when a draw brings `remaining_ul` down to exactly the 500-microlitre safety reserve that must always be left in the bottle — no further reservation may draw on a depleted lot. Independently of volume, an operator may quarantine a lot that is in `SEALED` or `OPEN`, moving it along `SEALED → QUARANTINED` or `OPEN → QUARANTINED`, and may release it along `QUARANTINED → OPEN`; each of those increments the lot's `version` by one, and the quarantine endpoint accepts an optional `reason` which is validated as a string and then discarded — it is stored nowhere and appears in no response. Release always returns the lot to `OPEN`, even for a lot that was still `SEALED` when it was quarantined, so a quarantine-and-release round trip is the one way a lot reaches `OPEN` without any ink having been drawn from it. Quarantining a `DEPLETED` or already-`QUARANTINED` lot, or releasing one that is not `QUARANTINED`, is `ERR_INK_LOT_UNAVAILABLE` at rung 21. Because voiding a run returns ink, a `DEPLETED` lot whose `remaining_ul` rises back to strictly more than the 500-microlitre reserve travels `DEPLETED → OPEN` and becomes reservable again. A first-ever draw against a `SEALED` lot that lands exactly on the safety reserve carries the lot **straight through to `DEPLETED`**: the opening and the depletion are two consecutive transitions applied by that one reservation, `SEALED → OPEN → DEPLETED`, and the lot is returned as `DEPLETED` with a single `version` increment covering both. Leaving a freshly opened lot at `OPEN` on exactly 500 microlitres would strand it there permanently — no further draw could ever succeed against it, and neither the `OPEN → DEPLETED` edge nor its compensating `DEPLETED → OPEN` return would be reachable for that lot. The depletion test is on the resulting volume alone: a lot whose `remaining_ul` after a draw is exactly 500 is `DEPLETED`, and one above 500 is `OPEN`, irrespective of which status the lot held before the draw.

**Reserving a run.** A production run is a slice of a committed job put on one machine for one calendar day. A `ProductionOperator` reserves it against a job in `COMMITTED`, naming the `machine_id`, the `ink_lot_id` the run will draw from, the `run_date` machine-day bucket, the `piece_count` this run writes — at least 1 — an optional `capacity_variant` of `SINGLE_PASS` or `SPLIT_RUN` defaulting to `SINGLE_PASS`, and `expected_mail_job_version` for the concurrency check of §2.7. Because an EDDM job is machine-printed in bulk rather than written note by note, reserving a run against one is refused at rung 13 with `ERR_EDDM_NOT_APPLICABLE`, which sits above the job's own state gate and therefore pre-empts it.

Several referential and capacity checks then apply, in ladder order. The named ink lot must be in `SEALED` or `OPEN` — a `QUARANTINED` or `DEPLETED` lot is `ERR_INK_LOT_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409) at rung 21. The named machine must be `ONLINE` — an offline machine is `ERR_MACHINE_OFFLINE` (HTTP 409) at rung 22. The lot's `ink_type` must equal the `ink_type` of the handwriting style behind the job's template, because the arm cannot write navy ink from a lot of blue; a mismatch is `ERR_INK_TYPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) at rung 32. The run's `piece_count` must not exceed the job's `billable_piece_count` less the pieces already reserved by that job's runs which are not `VOIDED`; asking for more is `ERR_RUN_PIECES_EXCEED_JOB` (HTTP 422) at rung 36.

**The capacity plan.** From the run's inputs the server derives three numbers. The character load is the run's `piece_count` multiplied by the template's `character_count`. `planned_write_minutes` is that character load divided by the handwriting style's `chars_per_minute`, rounded **up** to a whole minute. `planned_slot_minutes` adds setup time to that: each setup pass costs 12 slot-minutes, and a `SINGLE_PASS` run is charged one setup pass while a `SPLIT_RUN` run is charged two. `planned_ink_ul` is the character load multiplied by the style's `ink_ul_per_1000_chars` and divided by 1000, rounded **up** to a whole microlitre.

The variant choice is not cosmetic. A six-piece run of a 400-character template in a style that writes 4000 characters per slot-minute has a character load of 2400 and therefore one write-minute; as a `SINGLE_PASS` it occupies 13 slot-minutes of the machine-day, and as a `SPLIT_RUN` it occupies 25. The same pieces, the same ink, a different machine-day footprint — enough to flip the capacity check on a machine configured with only 20 minutes of daily capacity.

Two resource checks close the reservation, both at the bottom of the ladder. The committed minutes on the pairing of this `machine_id` and this `run_date` — the sum of `planned_slot_minutes` over runs on that bucket whose status is `RESERVED`, `RUNNING`, `COMPLETED` or `FAILED` — plus this run's own `planned_slot_minutes` must not exceed the machine's `daily_capacity_minutes`; exceeding it is `ERR_MACHINE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) at rung 39. The comparison rejects only on a strict excess, so a run that exactly fills the remaining minutes of a machine-day is accepted. Then the lot's `remaining_ul` less this run's `planned_ink_ul` must not fall below the 500-microlitre safety reserve; falling below it is `ERR_INK_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409) at rung 40. That comparison likewise rejects only on a strict shortfall, so a draw landing exactly on 500 microlitres is accepted — and, as noted above, is the draw that depletes the lot.

On acceptance the run is created at `status` `RESERVED` with `spoiled_piece_count` 0, `planned_ink_ul` is drawn from the lot's `remaining_ul`, `planned_slot_minutes` is committed against the machine-day bucket, and **both** the ink lot's and the owning job's `version` increment by one — the lot's because its `remaining_ul`, and possibly its `status`, have moved. The machine's own `version` does not move, because the committed minutes on a bucket are derived from the runs themselves rather than stored on the machine. Runs are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `mail_job_id`, `machine_id`, `ink_lot_id`, `run_date` or `status` — which is also how an operator observes machine-day utilization, since the committed minutes on any bucket are simply the sum over the non-voided runs it returns.

**Driving a run.** Starting a run moves it `RESERVED → RUNNING`, and it is also the retry edge `FAILED → RUNNING`. A start requires an open campaign write gate, a run in `RESERVED` or `FAILED` at rung 19, a lot that is specifically `OPEN` at that moment — `SEALED` is sufficient to reserve but not to start — and an `ONLINE` machine. The first start on a job also drives the job `COMMITTED → IN_PRODUCTION`; a start against a job already in `IN_PRODUCTION` leaves the job where it is. Retrying a failed run draws **no** additional slot-minutes and **no** additional ink: the original reservation was never released, which is exactly why a failed run still counts against its machine-day bucket and its lot. Completing a `RUNNING` run moves it `RUNNING → COMPLETED` and its minutes and ink stay committed permanently. Failing a `RUNNING` run moves it `RUNNING → FAILED`, stores the supplied `spoiled_piece_count`, and likewise leaves minutes and ink committed. `spoiled_piece_count` is a mandatory integer on that request and is recorded and read back on the run, but it is consulted by nothing afterwards: it never reduces the job's piece counts, never releases any part of the reservation, never re-opens the job's unreserved budget at rung 36, and never enters any pricing. Each of those transitions increments the run's `version` by one, and none of them touches the job's `version` except the first start, which moves the job itself. Voiding a run — permitted from `RESERVED` or `FAILED`, never from `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`, with an optional `reason` that is validated and then likewise discarded — moves it `RESERVED → VOIDED` or `FAILED → VOIDED`, returns its `planned_ink_ul` to the lot, releases its `planned_slot_minutes` on the machine-day bucket, restores a depleted lot to `OPEN` if the return lifts `remaining_ul` strictly above the safety reserve, and increments the run's `version` and the lot's. Any status outside those the operation permits is `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

**Dropping the mail.** A `ProductionOperator` drops a finished job into the mail stream, and the two mail paths reach that moment differently. An addressed job must be in `IN_PRODUCTION`, and every production run on it must have finished — each run's status is `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED`, with at least one `COMPLETED` — before the job may move `IN_PRODUCTION → MAILED`. An EDDM job never enters production at all and moves `COMMITTED → MAILED` directly. Rung 17 encodes both readings, so an addressed job still sitting in `COMMITTED` is refused with `ERR_MAIL_JOB_INVALID_STATE`. The requirement that every run be finished has no rung of its own in the ladder, and it is **folded into the job's own state gate at rung 17**, answering `ERR_MAIL_JOB_INVALID_STATE`. It is not reported as `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`: rung 19's declared scope covers only the four production-run endpoints, and reporting the drop's complaint there would widen a declared rung's scope. Three conditions are checked at that one rung, all answering the same error, after the job has been confirmed to be in `IN_PRODUCTION`: the job must own at least one run at all, no run on it may be in any status other than `COMPLETED` or `VOIDED`, and at least one run must be `COMPLETED` — a job all of whose runs were voided cannot be dropped.

Dropping the mail requires an open write gate, moves every recipient record on an addressed job that is both pointing at this job through `last_mail_job_id` and still in `QUEUED` along `QUEUED → MAILED`, incrementing each such record's `version`; moves every `RELEASED` drip step on the job to `MAILED`, incrementing each step's `version`; refreshes the campaign's `mailed_piece_count`; and increments the job's `version` by one. Records on the job in any other `mail_status`, and steps in any status other than `RELEASED`, are left untouched. The campaign's `mailed_piece_count` is defined as the sum of `billable_piece_count` over that campaign's jobs whose status is `MAILED` or `CLOSED`, and every narrative statement about the count rising is a statement about that sum being refreshed.

### 3.9. Returns, Reprints, Closing the Books, and Reporting

**Recording a return.** Some pieces come back. A `ListOperator` or `PlatformAdmin` records the return against the recipient record, supplying a `return_reason` drawn from `MOVED_NO_FORWARD`, `VACANT`, `REFUSED` or `INSUFFICIENT_ADDRESS` — the endorsement the carrier printed on the envelope. The record must currently be in `mail_status` `MAILED`, and its `last_mail_job_id` must name a job whose `job_kind` is `ADDRESSED`; a return reached through an EDDM job is refused at rung 13 with `ERR_EDDM_NOT_APPLICABLE`, since saturation mail has no named recipient to come back from.

Recording a return runs a four-hop cascade.

1. **Hop 1** — the recipient record moves `MAILED → RETURNED`, and its `version` increments by one.
2. **Hop 2** — a `ReturnEvent` is appended, naming the `list_record_id` whose piece came back, the `mail_job_id` that mailed it, the supplied `return_reason`, a derived `entitlement`, and a derived `return_fee_cents`. Like other historical records it carries only `id` and `created_at` beyond its own fields.
3. **Hop 3** — when the entitlement is a refund settlement and the class fee is non-zero, a `FORFEIT` ledger entry for `return_fee_cents` is appended and the account's `permit_balance_cents` falls by that amount.
4. **Hop 4** — the job's `returned_piece_count` rises, which in turn refreshes the campaign's `returned_piece_count` and `forfeited_postage_cents`.

**The settlement fork.** Entitlement is decided by one fact and one fact only: the record's `move_status` at the moment the return is recorded. A record whose `move_status` is `MOVE_APPLIED` — someone found a forwarding address for them — takes the `REPRINT` entitlement. A record at `NONE` or `MOVE_NO_FORWARD` takes the `REFUND` entitlement. The two branches behave quite differently.

Under a `REPRINT` entitlement, no ledger row is written at all and the account balance is untouched; what the mailer receives instead is the right to write and send the piece again, once, as described below. Under a `REFUND` entitlement, the fee is read from the mailing job's class: a `FIRST_CLASS` return costs nothing, because the postal service returns first-class mail free, while a `MARKETING_MAIL` return costs 89 cents per piece. When that fee is zero, **no `FORFEIT` row is written at all** — the class decides whether the forfeit exists as a record, not merely how large it is. When it is 89 cents, a `FORFEIT` row of that amount is appended and the balance drops. A forfeit that would drive `permit_balance_cents` below zero is **allowed**, and the balance goes negative: no rung guards the forfeit path, the escrow check at rung 41 is scoped to commitment alone, and the error catalogue of §5 contains no code that could refuse this movement. Refusing it would also make the refund-entitled return branch unreachable on a thin escrow. The ledger row is appended exactly as it would be on a healthy balance, its `balance_after_cents` faithfully carrying the negative figure, and the account's `permit_balance_cents` reads negative thereafter until a top-up or a refund lifts it back.

Return events are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `list_record_id`, `mail_job_id` or `entitlement`.

**Requesting a reprint.** A `CampaignManager` or `ListOperator` requests a reprint by naming the `list_record_id` to be re-sent, the `return_event_id` that entitles it, the `campaign_id` the reprint allowance is scoped to, and `expected_list_record_version` for the concurrency check of §2.7. The cited return event must carry the `REPRINT` entitlement; citing a refund-entitled return is `ERR_REPRINT_NOT_ENTITLED` (HTTP 409) at rung 37. A request reached through an EDDM job is refused at rung 13. An accepted request is created at `status` `REQUESTED` with `deny_reason` null, and the recipient record's `version` increments by one. Requests are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `list_record_id`, `return_event_id`, `campaign_id` or `status`.

**Fulfilling or denying.** A `ProductionOperator` resolves the request. Fulfilment requires an open write gate, a request in `REQUESTED` at rung 20, a return event still carrying the `REPRINT` entitlement at rung 37, and an unused allowance at rung 38: the record's `reprint_count` must be strictly below the cap of one reprint per record per campaign, so a record that has already been reprinted once is refused with `ERR_REPRINT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Fulfilment moves the request `REQUESTED → FULFILLED`, moves the recipient record `RETURNED → QUEUED` so it will be written and mailed again with the job, and raises the record's `reprint_count` by one — the count being derived from the record's `FULFILLED` requests rather than stored, it rises of its own accord with no separate write. The request's `version` always increments; the record's `version` moves only when the fulfilment actually re-queues it, that is only when the record was still in `RETURNED`, and a record already moved on by some other path is left untouched. Fulfilment changes neither the record's `last_mail_job_id` nor the job's status or piece counts, so the re-queued piece rides along with whatever job the record already points at. Denial is the sibling branch: it likewise requires an open write gate and a request in `REQUESTED` at rung 20, carries a mandatory `deny_reason`, moves the request `REQUESTED → DENIED`, leaves the record in `RETURNED`, leaves `reprint_count` untouched, and increments the request's `version`. Both branches are terminal for the request.

The scoping of the allowance is stated two ways in this domain, and the **per-campaign** reading governs. The cap is one reprint per record per campaign, a reprint request names the `campaign_id` it is scoped to, and rung 38 counts the record's `FULFILLED` reprint requests **against the campaign that the request being fulfilled names** — nothing else. A record mailed under two different campaigns may therefore be reprinted once in each, and a second fulfilment within one campaign is `ERR_REPRINT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. `reprint_count` as projected on the record is the same per-campaign figure rather than a running total: it is derived, at read time, as the number of that record's `FULFILLED` requests belonging to the campaign of the record's **most recent** mail job, so it reads 0 for a record whose `last_mail_job_id` is null — a record whose job was voided, for instance — even when fulfilled reprints exist against it, and it changes as `last_mail_job_id` moves the record between campaigns. The single-running-counter reading is rejected because it would make the cap error unreachable in any second campaign and would strand a legitimately re-mailable record.

**Closing the books.** A `ComplianceOfficer` (or `PlatformAdmin`) closes billing on a campaign that is in `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` with `billing_status` still `OPEN`. The close moves `billing_status` `OPEN → CLOSED` and moves `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` or `PAUSED → COMPLETED` according to where the campaign stood, and sets `write_gate` to `FROZEN` permanently — this is the one freeze that admits no exemption at all, and after it no write against the campaign or anything reachable from it will ever succeed again. A four-hop cascade follows.

1. **Hop 1** — the campaign's billing axis closes and its lifecycle axis completes, as just described; the campaign's `version` increments by one.
2. **Hop 2** — every mail job on the campaign whose `status` is `MAILED` moves `MAILED → CLOSED`, and each such job's `version` increments. Within each closing job, every recipient record still at `MAILED` with no return event against it moves `MAILED → DELIVERED_ASSUMED`; no ledger movement accompanies that assumption. Jobs in any other status are left alone.
3. **Hop 3** — every drip step whose `mail_job_id` is one of the jobs closed in hop 2, and whose status is `PENDING`, `SCHEDULED` or `RELEASED`, moves along `PENDING → CANCELLED`, `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` or `RELEASED → CANCELLED`, decrementing its schedule's `step_count`.
4. **Hop 4** — every drip schedule whose recomputed `step_count` is 0 after hop 3 moves `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` and has its `step_count` set to 0.

The campaign's `mailed_piece_count` is recomputed from the jobs closed in hop 2.

**Postage reconciliation.** The reconciliation report re-derives a campaign's money movement from the ledger rather than from any counter, and the ledger replay is authoritative. Over every `PostageLedgerEntry` attributed to the campaign, read in `created_at` ascending order, it reports `draw_cents` as the sum of `amount_cents` on `DRAW` rows, `refund_cents` as the same sum over `REFUND` rows, `forfeit_cents` as the same over `FORFEIT` rows, and `row_count` as the number of rows considered. `net_postage_cents` is the draws less the refunds plus the forfeits — money the campaign genuinely consumed. `account_delta_cents` is the mirror image from the account's point of view: the refunds less the draws and less the forfeits, which is exactly how far the campaign moved `permit_balance_cents`. Top-up rows carry a null `campaign_id` and are excluded from every one of those sums.

The tempting shortcut — summing `total_due_cents` over the campaign's jobs whose status is not `DRAFT` — is **not** what this report returns, and it gives a different answer. It omits forfeit rows entirely and misses refunds that have already been folded back into the jobs. A campaign with one committed job of 553 cents and one marketing-mail return reconciles to 642 cents under the ledger replay and 553 cents under the counter shortcut: an 89-cent gap that only the replay gets right.

**Deliverability reporting.** The partition report answers how a campaign's recipients divided up. For each `ADDRESSED` job on the campaign, over the records of that job's mailing list, it counts `automation_pieces` as records whose tier is `AUTOMATION` and whose `dedupe_status` is `UNSCREENED`, `UNIQUE` or `HOUSEHOLD_HEAD`; `retail_pieces` as the same count for `RETAIL` tier; `unmailable_pieces` as records whose tier is `UNMAILABLE`; and `duplicate_pieces` as records whose `dedupe_status` is `DUP_EXACT` or `DUP_HOUSEHOLD`. Those four buckets are mutually exclusive and, together, exhaust the list: the unmailable bucket is evaluated first, so a record that is both unmailable and a duplicate is counted **only** as unmailable, and the four counts sum to `record_total`. Alongside the recipient buckets, `eddm_pieces` is the sum of `eddm_piece_count` over the campaign's EDDM jobs — saturation mail contributes pieces but no recipient-level detail — and `returned_pieces` is the count of return events across all of the campaign's jobs. Two consequences of the per-job iteration are intended rather than accidental. Two `ADDRESSED` jobs drawing on the **same** mailing list contribute that list's records twice, once for each job, and `record_total` counts them twice with them; the report is a per-job roll-up, not a set union. And a record still at `UNASSIGNED` tier, on a list standardization has never touched, satisfies none of the four bucket tests while still counting toward `record_total`, so the four buckets sum to `record_total` only once every contributing list has been standardized. A campaign with no `ADDRESSED` jobs at all reports every recipient bucket and `record_total` as 0.

Every endpoint in this API is mounted under `/mail`, with the single exception of the administrative logical-clock endpoint. Creating a new resource answers **201 Created** and returns the created resource; every other successful operation — reads, state transitions, recomputations, and the two reports — answers **200 OK**. Requests that fail carry the HTTP status of the error identifier they report, per §5. ## 5. Error Code Catalogue

The following identifiers are the complete set this API may return for any specified condition. The condition that triggers each one is stated in the §2 or §3 narrative governing the rule it guards, and the relative order in which competing conditions are evaluated is fixed by the forty-one-rung ladder in §2.6. One identifier outside the table exists purely as a backstop: an unexpected server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500 in the same error envelope.
