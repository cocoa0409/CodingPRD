# Hazard Mitigation Buyout Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a river takes the same twenty houses for the third time in a decade, the cheapest permanent mitigation is often to stop rebuilding them. Federal hazard mitigation grant programs fund exactly that: a local government subrecipient buys the flood-prone or fire-prone property from a willing owner at pre-event value, clears the structure, and records a deed restriction that keeps the parcel as open space in perpetuity. The administrative burden is what makes these programs hard to run. A single parcel accumulates a benefit-cost justification, a pre-event fair market value determination, a staff appraisal and an independent review appraisal, a duplication-of-benefits reconciliation against every prior disaster payout the owner received, an offer built from all of the above, relocation entitlements for whoever was living there, a funded closing, a demolition with asbestos clearance and four utility disconnections, a debris diversion report, and then a monitoring obligation that outlives the grant itself.

This document specifies the API for a system that administers that pipeline end to end. Its central design tension is that almost nothing here is a local decision. A parcel's appraisal cannot be reviewed by the appraiser who wrote it; a project cannot be approved by the administrator who validated its benefit-cost analysis; a violation cannot be cured by the monitor who reported it. Money is conserved across the whole project — every federal obligation booked against a purchase offer must be released, to the cent, when that offer is withdrawn. And a single owner declining to sell does not merely remove one parcel: it severs the adjacency edges that ran through that parcel, shrinks the largest contiguous block of acquired land, and can render the entire neighborhood buyout non-viable at closeout.

The API is therefore written around **derived state that must always reconcile**. Counts, ratios, balances and acreage totals on the project are not client-supplied; they are recomputed by the server every time a subordinate record moves, and the specification below states, for every operation, exactly which totals move and which entities have their `version` advanced as a result. All collections are mounted at the service root — there is no versioned or domain prefix in front of them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, resource identity, the logical clock, arithmetic discipline, stale-read protection, cascade semantics, rejection precedence, and the segregation-of-duties bars.

### 2.1. Program Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If `X-User-ID` is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); the sole exception is the logical-clock endpoint described in §2.5, which is outside the business surface and requires no identity. If `X-User-Role` names a role that is not permitted to invoke the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); an absent, empty or unrecognised `X-User-Role` is treated identically and is likewise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than a structural failure.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramAdministrator`, `SubrecipientCoordinator`, `StaffAppraiser`, `ReviewAppraiser`, `RelocationSpecialist`, and `ComplianceMonitor`.

The `ProgramAdministrator` is the state or federal grant administrator and holds every approval, funding, expiry, suspension, termination and reversal authority. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque administrator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — as `validated_by_user_id` on a benefit-cost analysis and on the project it supports, and as `funded_by_user_id` on a closing record — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The `SubrecipientCoordinator` is the local government's buyout coordinator and is the only project-scoped role. When `X-User-Role` is `SubrecipientCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `MitigationProject`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `MitigationProject` compare the resource's project foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Because most acquisition artifacts hang off a parcel rather than off the project directly, the comparison follows the chain: a parcel's `project_id`, an appraisal's parcel and then that parcel's `project_id`, a purchase offer's parcel and then its project, a closing record's parcel and then its project, a demolition work order's parcel and then its project, a utility disconnection's work order and then that work order's parcel and project, a deed restriction's parcel and then its project, and a program income ledger entry's own `project_id`. A coordinator acting on anything that resolves to a different project id receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. The comparison is made once the path parent has been resolved, so a path identifier naming no row reports its not-found error ahead of the ownership failure; where the project id is read out of the request body instead — as it is on adjacency creation — the comparison is made ahead of every existence check. The bar guards only the write operations: every list and retrieve endpoint is unscoped, so a coordinator may read any project's rows and list results are never narrowed to the acting project.

The `StaffAppraiser` determines pre-event fair market value and authors `INITIAL` appraisals. When `X-User-Role` is `StaffAppraiser`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque appraiser identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `appraiser_user_id` on each appraisal that user creates and compares it only against that stored value, never against an entity `id`. The `ReviewAppraiser` authors `REVIEW` appraisals and renders review decisions; `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque identifier recorded as `appraiser_user_id` on the appraisals that user authors and read back by the segregation-of-duties bar in §2.10. For both appraiser roles, only the author of an appraisal may submit it — a submission attempt by anyone whose `X-User-ID` differs from that appraisal's `appraiser_user_id` is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Similarly, a `StaffAppraiser` attempting to create a `REVIEW` appraisal, or a `ReviewAppraiser` attempting to create an `INITIAL` appraisal, is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The `RelocationSpecialist` carries the federal relocation-law obligations: opening displacement cases, determining eligibility, submitting claims and lodging appeals. When `X-User-Role` is `RelocationSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque specialist identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server neither records nor compares it against any entity's `id`. A relocation specialist cannot pay a claim; only a `ProgramAdministrator` can.

The `ComplianceMonitor` performs long-term open-space monitoring, reports prohibited uses, records cures, and fails demolition clearance inspections. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceMonitor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque monitor identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `inspector_user_id` on each monitoring inspection and copies it onto the restriction as `violation_reported_by_user_id` when the inspection opens a violation, and compares it only against that stored value.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. An operation that mints a new row answers HTTP 201 — that covers every collection `POST` and also the reversal of a program income entry, which mints and returns the mirror; every other successful operation answers HTTP 200.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request body that is not valid JSON, or that is valid JSON but not a JSON object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), as is a request whose method is not allowed on an otherwise known path. A path matching no route at all answers HTTP 404 carrying the off-catalogue `error_id` `ERR_NOT_FOUND`. An entirely absent request body is read as an empty object, so an operation with no required fields succeeds without one.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a structural failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), as is either parameter carrying a value that is not a whole number. The filter query parameters named per endpoint in §4 behave as follows: a filter whose value is drawn from a declared enumeration is validated and an out-of-set value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a filter that takes an identifier or a free-text value is not validated at all and simply matches no record when nothing carries it; and a boolean filter accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` case-insensitively and rejects anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An unrecognised query parameter is ignored.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` block, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching record remains after the window.

The default sort on every list endpoint is `created_at` ascending. The one deliberate exception is the program income ledger listing, which is sorted `posted_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending so that the page order matches the order in which the running balance accumulates. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock described in §2.5, any number of records created without an intervening clock move carry one identical `created_at`; every ordering in this specification that keys on `created_at` — the default list sort, the ledger listing's secondary key, the cascade processing orders, the per-record sweeps of §3.5 and §3.9, and the component orderings of §3.3 — breaks such a tie by the order in which the server created the records, earliest created first.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits. Clients never supply either.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation that the server applies to it, including mutations applied as a cascade from another entity's operation. `MitigationProject`, `BenefitCostAnalysis`, `Parcel`, `Appraisal`, `DuplicationOfBenefitsRecord`, `PurchaseOffer`, `RelocationCase`, `ClosingRecord`, `DemolitionContractor`, `DemolitionWorkOrder`, `UtilityDisconnection` and `DeedRestriction` all behave this way.

Three record types are append-only audit artifacts and carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their stored form and from their responses. Those types are `ParcelAdjacency` (an adjacency edge, once drawn, is never edited), `MonitoringInspection` (a site-visit finding is a historical fact), and `ProgramIncomeLedgerEntry` (a posted ledger entry is never rewritten; its `status` flips to `REVERSED` only through the reversal operation, and even that flip does not introduce a `version` field).

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

Purchase offers expire, appraisal validity windows run twelve months, monitoring intervals run three years, cure windows run ninety days, and a period of performance eventually ends. None of those can be exercised against wall-clock time in a deterministic system, so the service reads a **logical clock** wherever the specification below says "the current instant" or "the current date". On a cold service the logical clock stands at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A single administrative capability moves it: a caller supplies a `current_utc` instant and the clock is set to that value. Moving the clock backwards is permitted. This capability is outside the business surface — any of the six roles may invoke it, it requires no `X-User-ID`, it creates and mutates nothing, it advances no `version`, and the only error it can return is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Its response body is a plain object holding the newly set `current_utc`, echoed back as the exact string the caller supplied rather than re-rendered into the canonical shape.

Every server-stamped instant is read from this clock: `pre_event_value_determined_at`, `presented_at` and the derived `expires_at`, `resolved_at`, `funded_at`, `recorded_at`, and the date part that becomes `recorded_on` — and equally the `created_at` and `updated_at` audit stamps of §2.4. Two rows written while the clock stands at one value therefore carry byte-identical stamps, and a record mutated without an intervening clock move has `created_at` and `updated_at` equal. The envelope's `api_request_timestamp` is the one instant that is **not** read from the logical clock: it is the real wall-clock time at which the request was served.

Every datetime field stored on a resource is rendered in the same shape as `created_at` — `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits. That covers `pre_event_value_determined_at`, `presented_at`, `expires_at`, `resolved_at`, `funded_at`, `recorded_at`, a work order's derived `buffered_start` and `buffered_end`, and the client-supplied `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, which are re-rendered into that shape when they are stored however the client wrote them.

Two calendar conventions apply throughout and never vary. **Month arithmetic** adds whole calendar months and, when the anchor day-of-month does not exist in the target month, clamps to the last day of the target month — so an anchor of 31 January plus 36 months lands on 31 January three years later, and an anchor of 30 November plus 36 months lands on 30 November three years later. **Day arithmetic** adds exact calendar days with no clamping. Dates are written `YYYY-MM-DD`; a date field that is not in that shape, or a datetime field that is not a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A date field is accepted only in the exact `YYYY-MM-DD` shape with no time part, and a client-supplied date that is stored verbatim on a resource — `period_of_performance_end_date`, an appraisal's `effective_date`, a parcel's `voluntary_acknowledgement_signed_on`, a closing's `scheduled_closing_date`, a ledger entry's `posted_on` and the rest — is echoed back exactly as the client wrote it. Datetime parsing is the more permissive of the two: a `Z` suffix, an explicit `±HH:MM` offset or no zone marker at all are all accepted, an offset is converted to UTC and a missing marker is read as UTC, seconds and fractional seconds are optional, and a space may stand in for the `T` separator — the value is then stored re-rendered to three fractional digits.

### 2.6. Money, Measurement and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer **cents**. Any field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer; a negative value in a `_cents` field is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Wherever a non-integer result must be reduced to a stored value, the rounding method is **round half up** — a value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero. For signed federal shares this matters: the rounding is applied to the positive magnitude before the sign is attached, so a magnitude of one-half cent on a debit becomes a whole cent of debit rather than being truncated toward zero.

The stored precision of each derived quantity is fixed: `pv_annuity_factor` carries six decimal places; `bcr` and the project's `validated_bcr` carry three; `neighborhood_viability_ratio` carries four; `protected_open_space_acres` and `diversion_pct` carry two. Client-supplied measurements are likewise bounded: `acreage`, `shared_boundary_feet`, `substantial_damage_pct`, `total_debris_tons` and `recycled_tons` all carry two decimal places, as do the fractions `cost_share_federal_pct` and `discount_rate_pct`. A client value carrying more decimal places than its field permits is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than silently rounded, and every numeric field must arrive as a JSON number — a numeric string, a boolean or a null in a required numeric field is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. All of these quantities, derived and client-supplied alike, are rendered back in responses as JSON numbers rather than strings.

One discipline governs every aggregate in this system and is the single most common source of off-by-one-cent divergence: **round each item, then sum the rounded items — never sum the raw products and round the total.** This applies to the per-record duplication-of-benefits deduction, to the per-entry federal share of program income, and to the per-item federal share of the obligation ledger. The one deliberate exception is protected open space acreage, where the inputs are already at two decimal places and the rounding is applied to the aggregate.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Parcel and Project Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because both snapshot a value off a parent whose derived fields move constantly.

Creating a purchase offer on a parcel requires the request to carry `expected_parcel_version`, a non-optional integer of at least 1 holding the `version` of that `Parcel` as the client last observed it. If it does not match the parcel's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard is load-bearing rather than decorative: a new offer legitimately supersedes any prior `DRAFT`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED` offer on the same parcel, so a create issued from a stale view would silently displace an offer the client never saw and would freeze a `base_value_cents` and `dob_deduction_cents` pair that no longer reflects the parcel.

Posting a program income ledger entry against a project requires the request to carry `expected_project_version`, a non-optional integer of at least 1 holding the `version` of that `MitigationProject` as the client last observed it. If it does not match the project's current `version`, the post is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every entry's federal share is computed against the project's live `cost_share_federal_pct` and validated against the running `program_income_balance_cents`, and both of those move whenever any parcel-level cascade touches the project, so an entry posted from a stale view would book a federal share against a superseded cost-share basis.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. In both cases the supplied guard value is retained on the row it created and echoed in that row's responses: a `PurchaseOffer` carries `expected_parcel_version` and a `ProgramIncomeLedgerEntry` carries `expected_project_version`, the latter null on a mirror entry the server minted itself during a reversal.

### 2.8. Path Resolution, Cascades and Derived-Total Reconciliation

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right. When a request addresses a sub-resource collection beneath a parent, the parent named in the path is looked up first and a miss there is reported against the parent, not the child; only once the parent resolves does the server consider the body and the remaining referenced entities. Body-referenced entities are checked for existence after path-referenced entities, and referenced entities are then checked for belonging to the right parent.

Every resource is addressed in a path by its own identifier, and each such path identifier is the `id` of the entity it names: `project_id` for a mitigation project, `bca_id` for a benefit-cost analysis, `parcel_id` for a parcel, `adjacency_id` for a parcel adjacency, `appraisal_id` for an appraisal, `dob_record_id` for a duplication-of-benefits record, `offer_id` for a purchase offer, `case_id` for a relocation case, `closing_id` for a closing record, `contractor_id` for a demolition contractor, `work_order_id` for a demolition work order, `disconnection_id` for a utility disconnection, `restriction_id` for a deed restriction, `inspection_id` for a monitoring inspection, and `entry_id` for a program income ledger entry. Where a list operation filters on a stored foreign key rather than on a path parent, the filter takes the stored field's own name — so monitoring inspections are filtered by `deed_restriction_id` even though the same restriction is addressed as `restriction_id` when it appears in a path. An identifier that is well formed but names no row produces the corresponding not-found error from §2.9 rather than a validation error.

Cascades in this system are **transitive and immediate**. Several operations start a chain three links deep — a project denial cancels parcels, each cancelled parcel withdraws its live offers, and each withdrawn offer releases its federal share back out of the project's obligation total. Every entity touched anywhere along such a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, including the entity that started the chain and the project at the far end of it. When a chain revisits the same entity more than once in a single request, that entity's `version` still advances once per applied mutation. The narratives in §3 state, for each operation, exactly which entities move and which project totals are recomputed.

The project carries four families of reconciled totals, and none of them is ever client-supplied: the federal obligation total `federal_obligated_cents`; the program income balance `program_income_balance_cents`; the protected acreage `protected_open_space_acres`; and the neighborhood-viability family `participating_parcel_count`, `holdout_parcel_count`, `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count`, `largest_component_parcel_ids`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and `viability_status`. Each is recomputed from scratch over its declared input set at every trigger listed in §3, so a compensating action always returns the total to exactly the value it held before the action it compensates.

### 2.9. Rejection Precedence

A write endpoint may have many applicable checks. They are evaluated in one **single global ascending order**, and the response reports the **first** violated check only. The order is:

1. Missing or empty `X-User-ID` — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. This applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock capability. A header present but holding only whitespace counts as empty.
2. The acting role may not invoke this operation — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Also every endpoint except the logical-clock capability, and an absent or unrecognised `X-User-Role` lands here too. Two related bars sit deliberately later than this step: the coordinator's project-scope comparison of §2.1, which needs the path parent resolved and so fires with the existence checks of step 5 (or, when the project id comes from the body, just ahead of them); and the appraisal-authorship match of §3.4, which needs `appraisal_type` read out of the body and so fires inside step 4. Both still report `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. A segregation-of-duties bar collides with the acting `X-User-ID` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This is evaluated after the path entity has been resolved, because the bar is read off that path entity, but before existence checks on entities named in the body. It guards exactly three operations: the appraisal review decision, project approval, and recording a violation cure.
4. Structural validation of the request body or query string — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This applies to every write endpoint and every list endpoint, and covers a missing required field, a wrong JSON type, an enum value outside its declared set, a negative `_cents` value, a negative `acreage`, `shared_boundary_feet`, `substantial_damage_pct`, `total_debris_tons` or `recycled_tons`, a `recycled_tons` greater than `total_debris_tons`, a `cost_share_federal_pct` outside the permitted band, a `discount_rate_pct` of zero or less when `analysis_method` is `FEMA_BCA_TOOLKIT`, a `discount_rate_pct` of 1.00 or greater under either method, a `project_useful_life_years` below 1 or above 100, a `max_concurrent_work_orders` outside 1 through 10, a `scheduled_end` at or before `scheduled_start`, a date not in `YYYY-MM-DD` form or a datetime that is not ISO 8601 UTC, a `prohibited_use_code` supplied alongside a `COMPLIANT` finding or omitted alongside a `PROHIBITED_USE_OBSERVED` finding, a missing `comparable_dwelling_price_cents` when `displacee_type` is `OWNER_OCCUPANT` and `rhp_method` is `COMPARABLE_DWELLING`, a missing `actual_purchase_price_cents` when `rhp_method` is `ACTUAL_REPLACEMENT`, a missing `disaster_declaration_number` when `grant_program` is `HMGP`, a missing `confirmation_reference` when a disconnection `outcome` is `CONFIRMED`, and an out-of-range `limit` or `offset`.
5. Existence of the entities named by the request, in this fixed order: the project (`ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`), the benefit-cost analysis (`ERR_BCA_NOT_FOUND`), the parcel (`ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`), the appraisal (`ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`), the duplication-of-benefits record (`ERR_DOB_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`), the purchase offer (`ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`), the relocation case (`ERR_RELOCATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`), the closing record (`ERR_CLOSING_NOT_FOUND`), the demolition contractor (`ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`), the demolition work order (`ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`), the utility disconnection (`ERR_DISCONNECTION_NOT_FOUND`), the deed restriction (`ERR_DEED_RESTRICTION_NOT_FOUND`), the program income ledger entry (`ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`), the parcel adjacency (`ERR_ADJACENCY_NOT_FOUND`), and the monitoring inspection (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`). All are HTTP 404.
6. Referential integrity: an adjacency naming the same parcel on both ends (`ERR_ADJACENCY_SELF_REFERENCE`), then a referenced entity belonging to the wrong parent (`ERR_PARCEL_PROJECT_MISMATCH`).
7. Optimistic concurrency: `ERR_STALE_PARCEL_VERSION` on offer creation, then `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` on ledger posting.
8. State checks, in this order: `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_INVALID_BCA_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_APPRAISAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLOSING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DISCONNECTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RESTRICTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACTOR_STATE`, `ERR_ALREADY_REVERSED`.
9. Life-safety and contractor-eligibility checks on demolition mobilization, in this order: `ERR_ASBESTOS_SURVEY_MISSING`, then `ERR_CONTRACTOR_SUSPENDED`, then `ERR_DEMOLITION_REWORK_LIMIT`.
10. Temporal checks, in this order: `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_DEMOLITION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_YET_EXPIRED`, `ERR_RELOCATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_FUTURE_DATED_FINDING`, `ERR_MONITORING_OVERDUE`, `ERR_PERIOD_OF_PERFORMANCE_EXPIRED`.
11. Business constraints, referential business rules and fiscal rules, interleaved in this order: `ERR_BCR_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, `ERR_UTILITY_DISCONNECT_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_NEIGHBORHOOD_NONVIABLE`, `ERR_PRE_EVENT_VALUE_NOT_DETERMINED`, `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_VOLUNTARY`, `ERR_REVIEW_APPRAISAL_REQUIRED`, `ERR_DEBRIS_DIVERSION_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_DOB_EXCEEDS_BASE_VALUE`, `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_PROGRAM_INCOME_OVERDRAWN`, `ERR_PROGRAM_INCOME_OUTSTANDING`, `ERR_RELOCATION_NOT_SETTLED`, `ERR_TENANT_INELIGIBLE`, `ERR_DEMOLITION_NOT_COMPLETE`, `ERR_OPEN_SPACE_RESTRICTION_ACTIVE`, `ERR_VALUATION_BASIS_MISMATCH`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_REVISION_LIMIT`, `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_TERMINATION_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_PROHIBITED_USE_UNRESOLVED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROJECT_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_APN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADJACENCY`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_UTILITY_TYPE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_HOUSEHOLD_REFERENCE`.

Three consequences of that single ordering are worth calling out, because each is a case where two checks can be violated by the same request and readers routinely guess the wrong winner.

**On presenting a purchase offer**, the offer-state check outranks the federal-share cap check: an offer that is not `DRAFT` and would also breach the cap reports `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`, not `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

**On mobilizing a demolition**, the asbestos gate outranks the utility-completeness gate: a work order sitting in `UTILITY_HOLD` whose `asbestos_survey_status` is also unusable reports `ERR_ASBESTOS_SURVEY_MISSING`, not `ERR_UTILITY_DISCONNECT_INCOMPLETE`. This is deliberate — a life-safety defect is surfaced ahead of a scheduling defect.

**On funding a closing**, the period-of-performance check outranks the federal-share cap check: a closing scheduled past `period_of_performance_end_date` that would also breach the cap reports `ERR_PERIOD_OF_PERFORMANCE_EXPIRED`, not `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three bars prevent a single individual from occupying both sides of a control. All three return `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) and all three are evaluated at the precedence position described in §2.9.

**SoD-1 — appraisal independence.** On an appraisal review decision, the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal that appraisal's `appraiser_user_id`. When the appraisal under decision has `appraisal_type` of `REVIEW`, the acting `X-User-ID` additionally may not equal the `appraiser_user_id` of the `INITIAL` appraisal named by `reviewed_appraisal_id`. A reviewer must therefore be a third party to both the review opinion and the original opinion it rests on.

**SoD-2 — approval independence.** On project approval, the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the project's `validated_by_user_id`, which was stamped there by whoever validated the benefit-cost analysis. The administrator who accepted the economics may not also be the administrator who awards the grant.

**SoD-3 — cure independence.** On recording the cure of a prohibited use, the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the restriction's `violation_reported_by_user_id`. The monitor who reported a violation may not be the monitor who certifies it fixed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Project: Application, Benefit-Cost Analysis and Award

A `MitigationProject` is the grant award and the fiscal container for everything beneath it. A `ProgramAdministrator` registers one by supplying a `project_code` that must be unique across every project in the system and between 4 and 32 characters long — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROJECT_CODE`; a `grant_program` of `HMGP`, `FMA` or `BRIC`; a `disaster_declaration_number`, which is mandatory when `grant_program` is `HMGP` and optional otherwise; a `subrecipient_name` naming the local government carrying out the buyout; a `cost_share_federal_pct` that defaults to 0.75 and must be at least 0.50 and at most 1.00 with two decimal places; a `total_federal_share_cap_cents` that must be greater than zero; and a `period_of_performance_end_date`.

The new project opens in `status` `DRAFT` at `version` 1 with every derived total at its identity value: `federal_obligated_cents` 0, `program_income_balance_cents` 0, `protected_open_space_acres` 0.00, `participating_parcel_count` 0, `holdout_parcel_count` 0, `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count` 0, `largest_component_parcel_ids` an empty array, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` 0.0000 and `viability_status` `NONVIABLE`. The three review-trail fields `validated_bcr`, `validated_bca_method` and `validated_by_user_id` are null.

A project's `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `CLOSEOUT_PENDING` and `CLOSED_OUT`, or diverts to `DENIED`. `CLOSED_OUT` and `DENIED` are terminal: no transition leaves either.

**The benefit-cost analysis.** Federal mitigation money is conditioned on the project's benefits exceeding its costs, so a `SubrecipientCoordinator` attaches one or more `BenefitCostAnalysis` records to the project while the project is in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`; attaching to a project in any other state is `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. Each analysis declares an `analysis_method` of `FEMA_BCA_TOOLKIT` or `PRE_CALCULATED_BENEFIT`; a `hazard_type` of `RIVERINE_FLOOD`, `COASTAL_SURGE`, `WILDFIRE` or `LANDSLIDE`; an `sfha_zone` of `AE`, `VE`, `A` or `X`; a `repetitive_loss_flag` that defaults to false; an `annualized_loss_avoided_cents` greater than zero; a `project_useful_life_years` between 1 and 100 inclusive; a `discount_rate_pct` that defaults to 0.07; and a `mitigation_cost_cents` greater than zero. Whatever the method, `discount_rate_pct` must be strictly less than 1.00 — a rate of 1.00 or above is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — while a rate of zero or below is admissible under `PRE_CALCULATED_BENEFIT` precisely because that method's arithmetic never reads it. It opens in `status` `SUBMITTED` at `version` 1 with `validated_by_user_id` null. Attaching an analysis moves nothing on the project: neither the project's `version` nor any of its derived totals advances.

The analysis computes `pv_annuity_factor`, `npv_benefits_cents` and `bcr` at creation, and **the arithmetic differs materially between the two methods — this is not one formula with a switch, it is two formulas.**

Under `FEMA_BCA_TOOLKIT`, the present-value annuity factor is one minus the quantity (one plus `discount_rate_pct`) raised to the power of the negative of `project_useful_life_years`, all divided by `discount_rate_pct`; that quotient is rounded half up to six decimal places and stored as `pv_annuity_factor`. The critical sequencing is that `npv_benefits_cents` is then `annualized_loss_avoided_cents` multiplied by the **already-rounded six-decimal factor** — not by the unrounded quotient — and that product is rounded half up to whole cents. Because `discount_rate_pct` appears in a denominator, a value of zero or below is a structural failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; there is no fallback to the undiscounted method.

Under `PRE_CALCULATED_BENEFIT`, no discounting occurs at all. `pv_annuity_factor` is stored as exactly 0.000000, `discount_rate_pct` is retained on the record but never read by the arithmetic, and `npv_benefits_cents` is `annualized_loss_avoided_cents` multiplied by `project_useful_life_years` as an exact integer product with no rounding step whatsoever.

Both methods then converge: `bcr` is `npv_benefits_cents` divided by `mitigation_cost_cents`, rounded half up to three decimal places.

An analysis's `status` runs `SUBMITTED` to either `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED`, both of which are terminal.

**Submitting the application.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` submits the project, moving `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and advancing the project's `version` by 1. Submission requires the project to be in `DRAFT`, to carry at least one `BenefitCostAnalysis` in `status` `SUBMITTED`, and to carry at least one `Parcel`; a project that fails any of these three conditions is not in a submittable posture and the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`.

**Returning the application.** A `ProgramAdministrator` may return a submitted project for revision with a `return_reason`, which is mandatory and may not be empty, moving `SUBMITTED` back to `DRAFT` and advancing the project's `version` by 1. The return is deliberately shallow: no benefit-cost analysis is disturbed by it, so analyses sitting in `SUBMITTED` remain in `SUBMITTED` and do not need re-attaching. A project may cycle between `DRAFT` and `SUBMITTED` any number of times.

**Validating the economics.** A `ProgramAdministrator` renders a `decision` of `VALIDATE` or `REJECT` on a specific analysis, optionally with a `review_note`. The analysis must be in `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BCA_STATE`, and the project must be in `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. A `VALIDATE` decision additionally requires that no other analysis on the same project already holds `status` `VALIDATED`; a project may carry only one validated economic justification, and an attempt to validate a second is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BCA_STATE`.

`VALIDATE` moves the analysis to `VALIDATED`, stamps its `validated_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and pushes three values up onto the project: `validated_bcr` takes the analysis's `bcr`, `validated_bca_method` takes its `analysis_method`, and the project's `validated_by_user_id` takes the same acting `X-User-ID` that SoD-2 will later read. Both the analysis's `version` and the project's `version` advance by 1. `REJECT` is the deliberately narrower sibling: it moves the analysis to `REJECTED`, leaves `validated_by_user_id` on the analysis null, leaves all three of the project's `validated_bcr`, `validated_bca_method` and `validated_by_user_id` untouched, and advances only the analysis's `version` by 1 — the project's `version` does not move.

**Awarding or denying.** A `ProgramAdministrator` renders a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` on a project in `SUBMITTED`, optionally with a `decision_note`. Either decision is subject to SoD-2, so the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the project's `validated_by_user_id`.

`APPROVE` requires that exactly one analysis on the project holds `status` `VALIDATED` and that the project's `validated_bcr` is at least 1.0. The threshold is inclusive: a `validated_bcr` of exactly 1.000 is approvable, and a `validated_bcr` of 0.999 is not. A `validated_bcr` below the threshold — and equally the case where no analysis ever reached `VALIDATED`, so `validated_bcr` is still null — is refused with `ERR_BCR_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. On approval the project moves to `APPROVED`, the whole neighborhood-viability family (`participating_parcel_count`, `holdout_parcel_count`, `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count`, `largest_component_parcel_ids`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio`, `viability_status`) is recomputed, and the project's `version` advances by 1.

`DENY` moves the project to `DENIED` and starts the first of this system's three-link cascades. Every `Parcel` belonging to the project whose `acquisition_status` is one of `NOT_STARTED`, `APPRAISAL_IN_PROGRESS`, `VALUE_ESTABLISHED`, `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` or `OFFER_ACCEPTED` moves to `acquisition_status` `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED`, and each such parcel's `version` advances by 1. For each parcel so cancelled, every `PurchaseOffer` on that parcel whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `PRESENTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each such offer's `version` advances by 1. Because a `WITHDRAWN` offer is no longer a member of the obligation ledger's first input group, the project's `federal_obligated_cents` is recomputed and drops by exactly the rounded federal share that each withdrawn offer had contributed. The project's own `version` advances by 1. Parcels already at `CLOSED`, `DEMOLISHED` or `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED` are untouched by a denial — money already spent and land already restricted are not unwound by an administrative decision.

Program income may be posted against a project only once it is in `APPROVED`, `CLOSEOUT_PENDING` or `CLOSED_OUT`; see §3.9.

### 3.2. Parcel Inventory, Adjacency Mapping and Pre-Event Value

**Adding parcels.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` registers each candidate property as a `Parcel` while the project is in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`; in any other project state this is `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. A parcel declares an `apn` — the assessor's parcel number, which must be unique within its project, with a collision reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_APN`, and which may legitimately repeat across two different projects; a `site_address`; an `acreage` greater than zero with two decimal places; an `occupancy_type` of `OWNER_OCCUPIED`, `TENANT_OCCUPIED` or `VACANT`; a `structure_type` of `SINGLE_FAMILY`, `MULTI_FAMILY`, `MANUFACTURED_HOME` or `NONE`; and a `substantial_damage_pct` that defaults to 0.00 and must be between 0 and 100 inclusive with two decimal places.

The new parcel opens at `version` 1 with `participation_status` `NOT_CONTACTED`, `acquisition_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `dob_deduction_total_cents` 0, and `pre_event_fmv_cents`, `pre_event_value_basis`, `pre_event_value_determined_at` and `voluntary_acknowledgement_signed_on` all null. Creation recomputes the project's `participating_parcel_count` and `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and advances the project's `version` by 1 — and those two only, so `viability_status` is deliberately left holding whatever value it had even where the enlarged denominator has just moved the ratio across a band boundary; it catches up at the next trigger that does refresh it.

**Two independent dimensions.** A parcel carries two orthogonal state dimensions that advance on separate schedules and must never be conflated. `participation_status` tracks the owner's willingness — federal buyouts must be voluntary — and takes the values `NOT_CONTACTED`, `INTERESTED`, `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED`, `DECLINED` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which `WITHDRAWN` is the intended end state — though, as §3.3 records, the participation operation itself carries no from-state gate. `acquisition_status` tracks the transaction and takes the values `NOT_STARTED`, `APPRAISAL_IN_PROGRESS`, `VALUE_ESTABLISHED`, `OFFER_OUTSTANDING`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, `CLOSED`, `DEMOLISHED`, `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED` and `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED`. `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED` is absolutely terminal. `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED` is terminal in the ordinary sense — no forward progress leaves it — with exactly one revival edge back to `NOT_STARTED`, described in §3.3, that fires when a holdout owner re-enters the program. A parcel at `DECLINED` on the participation dimension can still be at `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED` or back at `NOT_STARTED` on the acquisition dimension; a parcel at `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED` can be anywhere from `NOT_STARTED` to `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED`.

**Mapping adjacency.** Contiguity is what turns a scattering of purchases into a functioning floodplain park, so the coordinator draws the parcel-to-parcel adjacency graph explicitly as `ParcelAdjacency` edges. An edge declares a `project_id`, a `parcel_a_id`, a `parcel_b_id`, and a `shared_boundary_feet` greater than zero with two decimal places. Edges may be drawn only while the project is in `APPROVED`; any other project state is `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`.

Edges are **undirected**: the pair naming parcel P then parcel Q is the same edge as the pair naming Q then P, so an attempt to draw either direction when the unordered pair already exists is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADJACENCY`. An edge naming the same parcel at both ends is `ERR_ADJACENCY_SELF_REFERENCE`. Both `parcel_a_id` and `parcel_b_id` must name parcels whose `project_id` equals the `project_id` in the request body — an edge crossing two projects, or an edge whose body `project_id` disagrees with the parcels it names, is `ERR_PARCEL_PROJECT_MISMATCH`. An edge is an immutable audit artifact: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, it is never edited, and there is no operation that deletes one.

Drawing an edge recomputes the project's `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count`, `largest_component_parcel_ids`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and `viability_status`, and advances the project's `version` by 1. Note that the edge itself has no `version` to advance.

**Determining pre-event value.** Buyouts pay pre-event fair market value, not the depressed post-disaster value, so a `StaffAppraiser` records the pre-event basis on the parcel before any offer can be built. The determination supplies a `pre_event_fmv_cents` greater than zero and a `pre_event_value_basis` of `ASSESSOR_ROLL`, `PRE_EVENT_APPRAISAL` or `MARKET_STUDY`, and the server stamps `pre_event_value_determined_at` from the logical clock. The project must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`. The parcel's `version` advances by 1.

A determination may be redone, and re-determination is expressly permitted while the parcel's `participation_status` is `NOT_CONTACTED` or `INTERESTED`: the second determination overwrites all three fields, including re-stamping `pre_event_value_determined_at` from the clock at that moment. The determination carries no state check of its own beyond the project's `APPROVED` requirement — the failure modes declared for it are the structural body check and the project-approval check, and nothing else.

### 3.3. Voluntary Participation, Holdouts and Neighborhood Viability

Federal buyouts must be voluntary, which means the program cannot force contiguity. Every owner decision therefore moves both the parcel and the project's viability picture, and the `SubrecipientCoordinator` records those decisions by supplying a `participation_decision` of `INTERESTED`, `CONFIRM_VOLUNTARY`, `DECLINE` or `WITHDRAW`, optionally with a `voluntary_acknowledgement_signed_on` date and a `decision_note`.

This operation carries **no from-state admissibility check**: each of the four decisions is accepted whatever the parcel's current `participation_status` is, there is no error for an unlisted transition, and the landing status is fixed by the decision alone — `INTERESTED` lands `INTERESTED`, `CONFIRM_VOLUNTARY` lands `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED`, `DECLINE` lands `DECLINED` and `WITHDRAW` lands `WITHDRAWN`. The from-states named in the narratives below therefore identify where each decision's *additional* effects attach — the acquisition-dimension revival, the cascade, the recomputations and the project `version` bump — and not a gate on the decision itself. The only checks the operation performs are the ones each narrative states.

**Expressing interest.** From `NOT_CONTACTED` — and identically from any `participation_status` other than `DECLINED` — a decision of `INTERESTED` moves `participation_status` to `INTERESTED` and advances the parcel's `version` by 1, and nothing else moves at all: no acquisition-dimension change, no project total recomputed and no project `version` bump, even where a project count would arithmetically have shifted. The project must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`. Note carefully that the project-approval gate attaches to the `INTERESTED` decision specifically: `CONFIRM_VOLUNTARY`, `DECLINE` and `WITHDRAW` are not gated on the project's state, because an owner's decision to sign, refuse or back out is not something the program can suspend by leaving the grant unapproved.

**Confirming voluntary participation.** From `INTERESTED` — and identically from any other `participation_status` — a decision of `CONFIRM_VOLUNTARY` moves `participation_status` to `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED`. It requires the parcel's `pre_event_fmv_cents` to be non-null — an owner cannot sign a voluntary acknowledgement before knowing what the pre-event basis is — and a null `pre_event_fmv_cents` is refused with `ERR_PRE_EVENT_VALUE_NOT_DETERMINED`. It also requires the request to supply `voluntary_acknowledgement_signed_on`, and that date must be no later than the date part of the logical clock; an absent or future-dated signature date is a structural failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The supplied date is stored on the parcel as `voluntary_acknowledgement_signed_on` — exactly as the client wrote it — and the parcel's `version` advances by 1. Like a bare `INTERESTED`, this decision touches the project not at all: no total is recomputed and the project's `version` does not move, even when the from-state was `WITHDRAWN` or `DECLINED` and `participating_parcel_count` or `holdout_parcel_count` has therefore arithmetically shifted. Those stored values stay where they are until a later trigger refreshes them.

**Declining — the holdout cascade.** A decision of `DECLINE` has two shapes, deliberately asymmetric in how much wreckage they leave: the shallow one, which is what a decline from `NOT_CONTACTED` does and equally what a decline from any `participation_status` other than `INTERESTED` does, and the cascading one, reserved for a decline from `INTERESTED`.

In the shallow shape, nothing downstream is unwound, so `DECLINE` simply sets `participation_status` to `DECLINED`, recomputes the project's `holdout_parcel_count`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and `viability_status`, advances the parcel's `version` by 1 and the project's `version` by 1. No acquisition dimension change, no offer withdrawal and no obligation movement occurs — whatever `acquisition_status` the parcel happens to hold — and `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count` and `largest_component_parcel_ids` are not recomputed.

From `INTERESTED`, the same `DECLINED` landing triggers a three-link transitive cascade. Link one: the parcel's `acquisition_status` moves to `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED` if and only if it is currently one of `NOT_STARTED`, `APPRAISAL_IN_PROGRESS`, `VALUE_ESTABLISHED`, `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` or `OFFER_ACCEPTED`. Link two: a parcel that has just arrived at `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED` moves every `PurchaseOffer` on it whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `PRESENTED` to `WITHDRAWN`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each of those offers' `version` advancing by 1. Link three: each offer arriving at `WITHDRAWN` leaves the obligation ledger's offer group, so the project's `federal_obligated_cents` is recomputed and falls by exactly the rounded federal share that offer had contributed. The project's `holdout_parcel_count`, `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count`, `largest_component_parcel_ids`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and `viability_status` are all recomputed — the wider set, because a holdout severs adjacency edges — and the parcel's `version` and the project's `version` each advance by 1.

**Re-entering after declining.** A holdout owner may change their mind. From `DECLINED` a decision of `INTERESTED` moves `participation_status` back to `INTERESTED`, provided the project is in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`. This is the one edge that revives the acquisition dimension: if the parcel's `acquisition_status` is `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED` at that moment it returns to `NOT_STARTED`, so the pipeline can begin again from appraisal. Offers withdrawn by the earlier decline stay `WITHDRAWN` and are not resurrected, and the deobligation they caused is not reversed. The project's `holdout_parcel_count`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and `viability_status` are recomputed, and the parcel's `version` and the project's `version` each advance by 1.

**Withdrawing after confirming.** From `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED` — and identically from any other `participation_status` — a decision of `WITHDRAW` moves `participation_status` to `WITHDRAWN`, the intended end of the participation dimension. It is barred once title has passed: if the parcel's `acquisition_status` is `CLOSED`, `DEMOLISHED` or `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED`, the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_SPACE_RESTRICTION_ACTIVE`, because the encumbrance is perpetual and the seller has already been paid. Otherwise the withdrawal runs the same cascade as a decline from `INTERESTED` — `acquisition_status` to `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED` when it is in one of the five pre-closing states, all `DRAFT` and `PRESENTED` offers on the parcel to `WITHDRAWN`, each releasing its federal share out of `federal_obligated_cents` — and adds one more link that a decline does not have: every `RelocationCase` on the parcel whose `status` is `OPENED`, `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED`, `CLAIM_SUBMITTED`, `CLAIM_DENIED` or `APPEALED` moves to `CLAIM_DENIED_FINAL`, because there is no longer a displacement to compensate. A case already at `CLAIM_PAID` is untouched. The project's `federal_obligated_cents`, `participating_parcel_count`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and `viability_status` are recomputed, and the parcel's `version` and the project's `version` each advance by 1. `holdout_parcel_count`, `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count` and `largest_component_parcel_ids` are pointedly **not** recomputed here, so a withdrawal from `DECLINED` leaves `holdout_parcel_count` standing one higher than the `DECLINED` population until some later trigger refreshes it.

**The viability calculation.** The program's central judgement is whether what it has actually managed to acquire forms a usable block of open space. Four project fields answer that, and they are recomputed together as one unit.

Begin with the **node set**: every `Parcel` of this project whose `acquisition_status` is `CLOSED`, `DEMOLISHED` or `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED`. These are the acquired parcels; everything else — including a parcel with an accepted offer that has not funded — is outside the graph.

Then the **edge set**: every `ParcelAdjacency` of this project whose `parcel_a_id` and `parcel_b_id` are **both** members of the node set. An adjacency with exactly one acquired endpoint contributes no edge at all. This is precisely the holdout effect: the neighbour who refuses to sell severs the boundary that ran through their lot, and two acquired parcels that were only connected through that lot cease to be contiguous. Edges are undirected.

Partition the node set into connected components under that edge set. An acquired parcel with no surviving edge is its own component of size 1. Then `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count` is the size of the largest component, or 0 when the node set is empty. `largest_component_parcel_ids` holds the `id` values of that winning component's members ordered by their `created_at` ascending, or an empty array when the node set is empty. When two or more components tie for the maximum size, the winner is the component whose **earliest member's `created_at` is earliest**; a parcel's `id` is never used as a tie-break at any point.

The denominator is `participating_parcel_count`, the count of parcels of this project whose `participation_status` is `NOT_CONTACTED`, `INTERESTED`, `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED` or `DECLINED` — that is, every parcel except those that have withdrawn. `neighborhood_viability_ratio` is 0 when that denominator is 0, and otherwise `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count` divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places. `holdout_parcel_count` is separately the count of parcels of this project whose `participation_status` is exactly `DECLINED`.

Finally `viability_status` reads off the rounded ratio against two inclusive lower bounds: `VIABLE` when the ratio is at least 0.75; `AT_RISK` when the ratio is at least 0.50 and below 0.75; `NONVIABLE` otherwise. A ratio of exactly 0.5000 is `AT_RISK`, not `NONVIABLE`, and a ratio of exactly 0.7500 is `VIABLE`, not `AT_RISK`.

This whole family is recomputed on every change to a parcel's `participation_status`, on every change to a parcel's `acquisition_status` that enters or leaves the acquired set of `CLOSED`, `DEMOLISHED` and `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED`, on the creation of an adjacency edge, and at project approval — but that is a summary of the triggers, not a promise that all six move at each one. Each operation refreshes only the subset its own narrative names, and those narratives, together with §3.2 and §3.7, are authoritative wherever they name a narrower set. A member left unrefreshed simply keeps its stored value until some later trigger recomputes it, which is observable in the three cases each narrative flags: parcel creation leaving `viability_status` behind, `CONFIRM_VOLUNTARY` and a non-reviving `INTERESTED` refreshing nothing at all, and `WITHDRAW` leaving `holdout_parcel_count` behind.

### 3.4. Establishing Value: Appraisal and Independent Review

Federal acquisition requires an opinion of value from a qualified appraiser and an independent review of that opinion. The system models both as `Appraisal` rows on the parcel, distinguished by `appraisal_type`.

**Authoring an appraisal.** An appraisal declares an `appraisal_type` of `INITIAL` or `REVIEW`; a `valuation_basis` of `PRE_EVENT` or `POST_EVENT`; an `appraiser_license_no`; an `effective_date`, which is the inclusive start of the validity interval; an `indicated_value_cents` greater than zero; and, for review appraisals, a `reviewed_appraisal_id`. The server stamps `appraiser_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and the row opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_count` 0, `validity_end_date` null and `version` 1.

Authorship is role-bound. An `INITIAL` appraisal is authored by a `StaffAppraiser` and a `REVIEW` appraisal by a `ReviewAppraiser`; a `StaffAppraiser` attempting an `appraisal_type` of `REVIEW`, or a `ReviewAppraiser` attempting an `appraisal_type` of `INITIAL`, is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Because that determination has to read `appraisal_type` out of the body, it is the one authorization check in this API evaluated inside the structural stage: `appraisal_type` is validated first as an enum, the role match is then tested, and only afterwards are `valuation_basis`, `appraiser_license_no`, `effective_date`, `indicated_value_cents` and `reviewed_appraisal_id` structurally validated. The project must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`. A `REVIEW` appraisal must supply `reviewed_appraisal_id`, and it must name an appraisal whose `appraisal_type` is `INITIAL` — an omitted `reviewed_appraisal_id` on a review, or one naming another review, is `ERR_REVIEW_APPRAISAL_REQUIRED`. `reviewed_appraisal_id` must be null when `appraisal_type` is `INITIAL`. A `reviewed_appraisal_id` naming an appraisal that does not exist is `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`; one naming an appraisal that exists but sits on a different parcel is `ERR_PARCEL_PROJECT_MISMATCH`.

Creating an `INITIAL` appraisal also starts the parcel's acquisition dimension: if the parcel's `acquisition_status` is `NOT_STARTED` and its `participation_status` is `INTERESTED` or `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED`, `acquisition_status` moves to `APPRAISAL_IN_PROGRESS` and the parcel's `version` advances by 1. This is a conditional side effect, not a gate: if the parcel is already past `NOT_STARTED`, or if the owner has not yet expressed interest, the appraisal is still created and the parcel's acquisition dimension is simply left where it stands. Creating a `REVIEW` appraisal never moves the parcel's acquisition dimension.

**Submitting for review.** Only the author may submit — a caller whose `X-User-ID` differs from the appraisal's `appraiser_user_id` is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Submission is admitted from `DRAFT` and from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`; from any other state it is `ERR_INVALID_APPRAISAL_STATE`. The submission may optionally carry a revised `effective_date` and a revised `indicated_value_cents`, which overwrite the stored values before the validity window is computed.

Resubmission after a return is bounded. A submission from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` requires `revision_count` to be at most 2; a `revision_count` of exactly 2 still submits, and a `revision_count` of 3 is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_REVISION_LIMIT`. Because each return increments `revision_count` by 1, an appraisal survives being returned three times and dies on the fourth resubmission attempt.

**The validity window.** On submission the server computes `validity_end_date` as `effective_date` plus 12 calendar months, clamped to month end where the anchor day-of-month does not exist in the target month. The validity interval is **half-open**: it includes `effective_date` and excludes `validity_end_date`. Two `INITIAL` appraisals on the same parcel, both with `status` of `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`, overlap when the first's `effective_date` is strictly earlier than the second's `validity_end_date` **and** the second's `effective_date` is strictly earlier than the first's `validity_end_date`. Because both comparisons are strict, intervals that merely touch — where one appraisal's `validity_end_date` equals the next one's `effective_date` — do **not** overlap and both may coexist. An overlap is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

Two exclusions on that check matter. First, the peer set is restricted to appraisals in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`: appraisals sitting in `DRAFT`, `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` or `REJECTED` are invisible to it, so a rejected appraisal never blocks a fresh one over the same period. Second, `REVIEW` appraisals are exempt from the check entirely — both as the appraisal being submitted and as a peer — because a review opinion is expected to sit squarely inside the interval of the `INITIAL` appraisal it reviews. A submission from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` re-runs the overlap check against the possibly revised `effective_date`.

**The review decision.** A `ReviewAppraiser` renders a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `RETURN` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `review_note`. The decision is subject to SoD-1 in all three branches: the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the appraisal's own `appraiser_user_id`.

`RETURN` is admitted only from `SUBMITTED`; a return attempted against an appraisal already in `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` is `ERR_INVALID_APPRAISAL_STATE`. It moves `status` to `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, increments `revision_count` by 1, and advances `version` by 1. Nothing else moves — no parcel state, no project total.

`REJECT` is admitted from `SUBMITTED` and from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`. It moves `status` to `REJECTED`, which is terminal, and advances `version` by 1. `revision_count` is not incremented, and no parcel state or project total moves.

`ACCEPT` is admitted only from `SUBMITTED`; an accept attempted against an appraisal in `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` is `ERR_INVALID_APPRAISAL_STATE`. When the appraisal being accepted has `appraisal_type` of `REVIEW`, two extra conditions bite. Its `reviewed_appraisal_id` must name an `INITIAL` appraisal whose `status` is already `ACCEPTED` — a review cannot be accepted ahead of the original opinion it rests on, and a violation is `ERR_REVIEW_APPRAISAL_REQUIRED`. And the second limb of SoD-1 applies: the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the `appraiser_user_id` of that reviewed `INITIAL` appraisal either, so the reviewer is a genuine third party to both opinions. That second limb is in fact evaluated at the SoD position of §2.9, before the `decision` is even read, so it bars a `RETURN` and a `REJECT` on a `REVIEW` appraisal by the reviewed appraisal's author exactly as it bars an `ACCEPT`.

Accepting an `INITIAL` appraisal moves nothing but the appraisal itself to `ACCEPTED` with `version` advanced by 1. Accepting a `REVIEW` appraisal additionally moves the parcel's `acquisition_status` from `APPRAISAL_IN_PROGRESS` to `VALUE_ESTABLISHED` and advances the parcel's `version` by 1 — this is the moment the parcel's value is established and offers become possible. `ACCEPTED` is terminal.

### 3.5. Duplication of Benefits, Offer Development and the Federal Obligation

**Why deductions exist.** An owner who already collected a flood insurance claim, an increased-cost-of-compliance payment or an SBA loan for the same structure cannot be paid twice for the same loss. Federal law requires the prior benefit to be deducted from the acquisition price, except to the extent the owner can document that they actually spent it on eligible repairs.

**Recording a prior benefit.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` records each prior benefit as a `DuplicationOfBenefitsRecord` on the parcel, declaring a `benefit_source` of `NFIP_CLAIM`, `ICC`, `PRIVATE_INSURANCE`, `SBA_LOAN` or `CDBG_DR`; an `amount_cents` greater than zero; a `repair_documented_cents` that defaults to 0 and must be at least 0; and a `received_on` date. The project must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`. The record opens in `status` `ACTIVE` at `version` 1 with `reversal_reason` null and its own `deductible_cents` computed immediately.

**The deduction sweep.** The input set is every `DuplicationOfBenefitsRecord` on the parcel whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; `REVERSED` records are excluded entirely. The sweep processes them ordered by `received_on` ascending and then, for records sharing a `received_on`, by `created_at` ascending. A record's `id` is never used as a sort key.

For each record in that order: the net award is `amount_cents` minus `repair_documented_cents`, and if that difference is negative it is taken as zero — documented repairs can eliminate a deduction but can never produce a credit. That net award is multiplied by the factor for its `benefit_source`, which is 1.00 for `NFIP_CLAIM`, 1.00 for `ICC`, 1.00 for `PRIVATE_INSURANCE`, 0.50 for `SBA_LOAN` and 0.85 for `CDBG_DR`. The product is rounded half up to whole cents **at this point, for this record**, and stored as that record's `deductible_cents`.

The parcel's `dob_deduction_total_cents` is then the sum of those already-rounded per-record `deductible_cents` values, accumulated in the sweep order. Summing the unrounded products and rounding the total is a different number and is wrong: two `SBA_LOAN` records each landing on a half-cent both round up individually, whereas their raw sum contains no fraction at all. Recording a benefit recomputes `dob_deduction_total_cents` and advances the parcel's `version` by 1.

**Reversing a prior benefit.** A `ProgramAdministrator` reverses a record found not to duplicate, supplying a `reversal_reason`. The record must be `ACTIVE`; reversing one already at `REVERSED` is `ERR_ALREADY_REVERSED`. Its `status` becomes `REVERSED`, the `reversal_reason` is stored, and its `deductible_cents` is **frozen at its last `ACTIVE` value for audit** rather than zeroed, so the record still shows what it had been deducting. The record's `version` advances by 1, the parcel's `dob_deduction_total_cents` is recomputed without it, and the parcel's `version` advances by 1. Offers already created keep their snapshotted `dob_deduction_cents` — a reversal never rewrites an offer that has already been built.

**Building an offer.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` creates a `PurchaseOffer` on a parcel, declaring a `source_appraisal_id`, an `offer_basis` of `PRE_EVENT_FMV` or `POST_EVENT_FMV`, and the OCC guard `expected_parcel_version` described in §2.7.

The gate conditions are: the project must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED`; the parcel's `participation_status` must be `INTERESTED` or `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_VOLUNTARY` — note this is a cross-dimension check, and a parcel may perfectly well have reached `VALUE_ESTABLISHED` on the acquisition dimension while still only `INTERESTED` on the participation dimension; the appraisal named by `source_appraisal_id` must exist, otherwise `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`, and must sit on this same parcel, otherwise `ERR_PARCEL_PROJECT_MISMATCH`; that appraisal must have `appraisal_type` of `REVIEW` and `status` of `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_APPRAISAL_REQUIRED`; the `offer_basis` must correspond to the source appraisal's `valuation_basis`, meaning `PRE_EVENT_FMV` pairs with `PRE_EVENT` and `POST_EVENT_FMV` pairs with `POST_EVENT`, and any other pairing is `ERR_VALUATION_BASIS_MISMATCH`; and the parcel's `dob_deduction_total_cents` must not exceed the source appraisal's `indicated_value_cents`, otherwise `ERR_DOB_EXCEEDS_BASE_VALUE`, because prior benefits that swallow the entire value mean there is nothing left to buy. That list is written by topic, not by precedence: the checks fire in the single §2.9 order, which places `ERR_DOB_EXCEEDS_BASE_VALUE` ahead of `ERR_VALUATION_BASIS_MISMATCH`, and places the `expected_parcel_version` comparison ahead of the project-approval check.

**The offer arithmetic, in a load-bearing order.** `base_value_cents` is the `indicated_value_cents` of the appraisal named by `source_appraisal_id`. `dob_deduction_cents` is the parcel's `dob_deduction_total_cents` at that instant. `pre_incentive_net_cents` is `base_value_cents` minus `dob_deduction_cents`, taken as zero if that difference is negative.

The owner-occupant incentive rate is 0 whenever the parcel's `occupancy_type` is anything other than `OWNER_OCCUPIED`. For an `OWNER_OCCUPIED` parcel, the rate is read from the single half-open band containing `pre_incentive_net_cents`: 0.10 when `pre_incentive_net_cents` is below 10,000,000 cents; 0.07 when it is at least 10,000,000 and below 25,000,000; and 0.05 when it is 25,000,000 or more. The rate is **flat across the whole amount** — an average rate, never a marginal rate applied band by band.

`incentive_cents` is `pre_incentive_net_cents` multiplied by that rate, **rounded half up to whole cents first and only then capped** at 2,250,000 cents; rounding after the cap would give a different answer whenever the uncapped product sits just above the ceiling. `net_offer_cents` is `pre_incentive_net_cents` plus `incentive_cents`.

The critical property is that the tier is keyed on the **post-deduction** figure, not on `base_value_cents`, so the result is not monotone in the appraised value. Two parcels each carrying a 100,000-cent deduction illustrate it: a `base_value_cents` of 10,000,000 nets 9,900,000 pre-incentive, falls in the 0.10 band, earns 990,000 and yields a `net_offer_cents` of 10,890,000; a `base_value_cents` of 10,100,000 nets exactly 10,000,000 pre-incentive, crosses into the 0.07 band, earns only 700,000 and yields a smaller `net_offer_cents` of 10,700,000. A larger appraisal produces a smaller offer. Tiering on `base_value_cents` instead, or applying the cap before the tier lookup, produces different numbers and is wrong.

All five of `base_value_cents`, `dob_deduction_cents`, `pre_incentive_net_cents`, `incentive_cents` and `net_offer_cents` are frozen snapshots evaluated once at creation. Later duplication-of-benefits records, and later reversals, never move them.

**Supersession.** Creating an offer also retires the parcel's stale ones. Every existing `PurchaseOffer` on the same parcel whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_offer_id` set to the newly created offer's `id`, and each of those offers' `version` advances by 1. Offers at `ACCEPTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or an existing `SUPERSEDED` are untouched. The new offer opens in `status` `DRAFT` at `version` 1 with `presented_at`, `expires_at`, `withdrawal_reason` and `superseded_by_offer_id` all null, and the parcel's `version` advances by 1. This silent displacement of a prior offer is exactly why `expected_parcel_version` is required here.

**Presenting the offer.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` presents an offer that must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. The parcel's `participation_status` must now be fully `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED` — merely `INTERESTED` was enough to build the offer but is not enough to put it in front of the owner — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_VOLUNTARY`. The parcel's `acquisition_status` must be `VALUE_ESTABLISHED`, and a parcel elsewhere on the acquisition dimension is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. The appraisal named by `source_appraisal_id` must still be a `REVIEW` appraisal in `ACCEPTED`. And the obligation cap must hold: the project's current `federal_obligated_cents` plus this offer's prospective federal item must be at most `total_federal_share_cap_cents`, otherwise `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. As noted in §2.9, when both the offer-state check and the cap check are violated the response is `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`; for the same reason the acquisition-dimension check, which also reports `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`, outranks the participation check even though it is described second here.

Presentation moves `status` to `PRESENTED`, stamps `presented_at` from the logical clock, computes `expires_at` as `presented_at` plus 30 calendar days, moves the parcel's `acquisition_status` from `VALUE_ESTABLISHED` to `OFFER_OUTSTANDING`, recomputes the project's `federal_obligated_cents`, and advances the offer's `version`, the parcel's `version` and the project's `version` each by 1.

**The owner's response.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` records a `response` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, optionally with a `response_note`. The offer must be `PRESENTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`, and the logical clock must be at or before `expires_at` — the boundary is inclusive, so a response landing exactly on `expires_at` is timely, and anything strictly later is `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`.

The two branches are deliberately asymmetric. `ACCEPT` additionally requires the parcel's `participation_status` to be `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_VOLUNTARY`; it moves the offer to `ACCEPTED`, moves the parcel's `acquisition_status` from `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` to `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, leaves `federal_obligated_cents` **unchanged** because the obligation booked at presentation simply carries over from the offer group's `PRESENTED` membership to its `ACCEPTED` membership, and advances the offer's `version` and the parcel's `version` each by 1 — the project's `version` does not move.

`DECLINE` carries no participation check at all; a parcel whose owner has slipped out of `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED` may still have their refusal recorded. It moves the offer to `DECLINED`, moves the parcel's `acquisition_status` from `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` back to `VALUE_ESTABLISHED` so a superseding offer can be built without re-appraisal, recomputes `federal_obligated_cents` downward by exactly this offer's rounded federal item, and advances the offer's `version`, the parcel's `version` and the project's `version` each by 1. The parcel's `participation_status` stays `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED`.

**Expiry.** A `ProgramAdministrator` expires an offer that must be `PRESENTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`, and whose validity window must have genuinely lapsed: the logical clock must be **strictly after** `expires_at`, and an attempt at exactly `expires_at` or earlier is `ERR_OFFER_NOT_YET_EXPIRED`. Note how this dovetails with the owner-response window — at exactly `expires_at` the owner may still respond and the administrator may not yet expire, so there is no instant at which both or neither is available. Expiry moves the offer to `EXPIRED` and otherwise has exactly the same effects as a decline: parcel back to `VALUE_ESTABLISHED`, `federal_obligated_cents` recomputed downward, and the offer's, parcel's and project's `version` each advanced by 1.

**Withdrawal.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` withdraws an offer with a `withdrawal_reason`, from `DRAFT` or from `PRESENTED`; any other state is `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. There is no expiry check on withdrawal — a lapsed presented offer may still be withdrawn rather than expired. From `PRESENTED` the withdrawal is the exact compensating action for presentation: the offer moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` stored, the parcel's `acquisition_status` returns from `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` to `VALUE_ESTABLISHED`, `federal_obligated_cents` is recomputed and returns bit-for-bit to its pre-presentation value, and the offer's, parcel's and project's `version` each advance by 1. From `DRAFT` the withdrawal is far narrower: the offer moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` stored and its `version` advanced by 1, and nothing else moves at all — no parcel state, no `federal_obligated_cents` change (a `DRAFT` offer was never obligated) and no project `version` bump.

One condition guards the parcel's revert edge. `OFFER_OUTSTANDING` returns to `VALUE_ESTABLISHED` only when no **other** offer on that parcel is still `PRESENTED`; while another presented offer remains outstanding the parcel stays at `OFFER_OUTSTANDING`.

**The federal obligation ledger.** `federal_obligated_cents` is a conserved quantity recomputed from scratch over three input groups, taken in this group order, each group internally ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Group one is every `PurchaseOffer` on a parcel of this project whose `status` is `PRESENTED` or `ACCEPTED`, contributing `net_offer_cents` multiplied by the project's `cost_share_federal_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Group two is every `ClosingRecord` on a parcel of this project whose `status` is `FUNDED` or `RECORDED`, contributing `settlement_cost_cents` multiplied by `cost_share_federal_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Group three is every `RelocationCase` on a parcel of this project whose `status` is exactly `CLAIM_PAID`, contributing `total_relocation_benefit_cents` multiplied by `cost_share_federal_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents.

`federal_obligated_cents` is the sum of those per-item amounts, **each rounded individually before accumulation**, taken across group one then group two then group three. Everything outside those status sets contributes nothing: a `DRAFT` offer, a `SCHEDULED` closing, and a relocation case in any state other than `CLAIM_PAID` are all invisible to the ledger. An offer that leaves `PRESENTED` or `ACCEPTED` for `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED` drops out of group one entirely, and because the item was rounded on the way in and is simply absent on the way out, the deobligation is the exact amount that was added, never a re-derived approximation.

The cap check `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED` reads this total at three moments — presenting an offer, funding a closing and paying a relocation claim — and in each case tests the current `federal_obligated_cents` plus the prospective per-item federal share against `total_federal_share_cap_cents`, with the ceiling inclusive: landing exactly on the cap is permitted.

### 3.6. Relocation Assistance under Federal Relocation Law

Buying someone's house displaces whoever was living in it. Federal relocation law entitles the displaced person to a replacement housing payment, rental assistance and moving expenses, on a scale that depends sharply on which statutory class they fall into.

**Opening a case.** A `RelocationSpecialist` opens a `RelocationCase` on the parcel, declaring a `displacee_type` of `OWNER_OCCUPANT`, `TENANT_90_DAY`, `TENANT_UNDER_90_DAY` or `BUSINESS`; a `household_reference`; an `rhp_method` of `COMPARABLE_DWELLING` or `ACTUAL_REPLACEMENT`; a `rental_assistance_start_date`; and up to eight optional monetary inputs each defaulting to 0 — `current_rent_cents`, `current_utility_cents`, `comparable_rent_cents`, `comparable_utility_cents`, `monthly_gross_income_cents`, `comparable_dwelling_price_cents`, `actual_purchase_price_cents` and `moving_expense_cents`.

Two of those optional inputs become conditionally mandatory. `comparable_dwelling_price_cents` must be present when `displacee_type` is `OWNER_OCCUPANT` and `rhp_method` is `COMPARABLE_DWELLING`, and `actual_purchase_price_cents` must be present whenever `rhp_method` is `ACTUAL_REPLACEMENT`; an omission in either case is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

`household_reference` is unique **within a parcel only** — a collision on the same parcel is `ERR_DUPLICATE_HOUSEHOLD_REFERENCE`, but the same `household_reference` may legitimately appear on two different parcels of one project, which is exactly the situation the rental-assistance overlap check below is designed to catch. The case opens in `status` `OPENED` at `version` 1 with `appeal_count` 0, `denial_reason` and `appeal_basis` null, `rental_assistance_end_date` null, and every computed benefit field at 0. Opening a case consults neither the project's `status` nor either of the parcel's two state dimensions, and nothing outside the case moves — the parcel's `version` and every project total stand exactly where they were.

**Determining eligibility.** A `RelocationSpecialist` determines eligibility on a case that must be in `OPENED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. Exactly one `PurchaseOffer` on the case's parcel must have reached `ACCEPTED`; when no offer on the parcel has reached `ACCEPTED` the request is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, because there is no acquisition price to compute against; where more than one offer on the parcel has reached `ACCEPTED`, no error is raised and the earliest of them by `created_at` supplies the acquisition price. And no **other** case in the same project — reached through the case's parcel's `project_id` — carrying the same `household_reference` may have a rental-assistance occupation interval that intersects this case's. That interval is half-open: `[rental_assistance_start_date, rental_assistance_end_date)`. Two such intervals intersect when the first's `rental_assistance_start_date` is strictly earlier than the second's `rental_assistance_end_date` and the second's `rental_assistance_start_date` is strictly earlier than the first's `rental_assistance_end_date`, so intervals that merely abut do not intersect. An intersection is `ERR_RELOCATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, and a household cannot draw rental assistance for two dwellings over the same months.

Because `rental_assistance_end_date` is the exclusive upper bound and is null until eligibility is determined, the two sides of that comparison are read differently. A **peer** case whose `rental_assistance_end_date` is still null occupies no interval at all and is skipped by the check outright, whatever its `status` — only a peer that has already had its end date computed can collide. The **subject** case has no stored end date either, so its interval is taken prospectively, ending at `rental_assistance_start_date` plus the `rental_assistance_months` its `displacee_type` is about to draw — 42 months for `TENANT_90_DAY` and 0 for `OWNER_OCCUPANT`, `TENANT_UNDER_90_DAY` and `BUSINESS` — which is exactly the `rental_assistance_end_date` the determination will store. For every `displacee_type` but `TENANT_90_DAY` that makes the subject's interval empty, and the test above is still applied to it unchanged, so such a case collides only with a peer interval that strictly straddles its `rental_assistance_start_date`.

Determination snapshots `acquisition_price_cents` from that accepted offer's `net_offer_cents`, computes `rental_assistance_months`, `rental_assistance_end_date`, `replacement_housing_payment_cents`, `rental_assistance_cents` and `total_relocation_benefit_cents`, moves `status` to `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED`, and advances the case's `version` by 1. No project total moves — an eligibility determination is not yet an obligation.

**The replacement housing payment.** This is a dual-variant calculation selected by `rhp_method`, and the two variants read a different replacement-cost leg against the same `acquisition_price_cents`.

Under `COMPARABLE_DWELLING`, the gross gap is `comparable_dwelling_price_cents` minus `acquisition_price_cents`. Under `ACTUAL_REPLACEMENT`, the gross gap is `actual_purchase_price_cents` minus `acquisition_price_cents`; `comparable_dwelling_price_cents` remains stored on the record but is never read by this variant. In both variants the eligible gap is that gross gap taken as zero when it is negative — an owner who was paid more than the replacement dwelling costs receives no supplement rather than owing one.

`replacement_housing_payment_cents` is then, for a `displacee_type` of `OWNER_OCCUPANT`, the lesser of the eligible gap and 3,150,000 cents; and for a `displacee_type` of `TENANT_90_DAY`, `TENANT_UNDER_90_DAY` or `BUSINESS`, exactly 0. Every leg is integer cents, so no rounding is required anywhere in this calculation. The variants can diverge substantially on identical stored data: an `acquisition_price_cents` of 19,794,998 against a `comparable_dwelling_price_cents` of 22,000,000 and an `actual_purchase_price_cents` of 20,500,000, for an `OWNER_OCCUPANT`, yields 2,205,002 under `COMPARABLE_DWELLING` and only 705,002 under `ACTUAL_REPLACEMENT`. Note also that the shared cap can mask that divergence entirely when both legs sit far above 3,150,000.

**The rental assistance payment.** This is a chain of ten steps whose nested comparisons each silently discard a loser, so the order matters.

First, `rental_assistance_months` is 42 when `displacee_type` is `TENANT_90_DAY`, and exactly 0 when `displacee_type` is `OWNER_OCCUPANT`, `TENANT_UNDER_90_DAY` or `BUSINESS`. Only a tenant in lawful occupancy for at least ninety days draws the full statutory forty-two months; nobody else draws any.

Second, `rental_assistance_end_date` is `rental_assistance_start_date` plus `rental_assistance_months` calendar months, clamped to month end where the anchor day-of-month does not exist in the target month, and it is the **exclusive** upper bound of the occupation interval.

Third, the current housing cost is `current_rent_cents` plus `current_utility_cents`. Fourth, the ability to pay is `monthly_gross_income_cents` multiplied by 0.30, **rounded half up to whole cents before it is used** — that is, the rounding happens ahead of the comparison in the next step, not after it. Fifth, the base monthly amount is the **lesser** of the current housing cost and the ability to pay; the larger of the two is discarded and never resurfaces.

Sixth, the comparable housing cost is `comparable_rent_cents` plus `comparable_utility_cents`. Seventh, the monthly differential is the comparable housing cost minus the base monthly amount, taken as zero if that is negative — a displacee moving somewhere cheaper receives nothing rather than a negative subsidy. Eighth, the uncapped total is the monthly differential multiplied by `rental_assistance_months`, which is why a zero month count zeroes the whole leg regardless of how large the differential is. Ninth, `rental_assistance_cents` is the lesser of that uncapped total and 3,150,000 cents.

Tenth, `total_relocation_benefit_cents` is `replacement_housing_payment_cents` plus `rental_assistance_cents` plus `moving_expense_cents`. Every step after the ability-to-pay rounding is exact integer arithmetic.

**Submitting the claim.** A `RelocationSpecialist` submits the claim from `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` or from `APPEALED`; any other state is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. An optional `claim_note` may accompany it. A `displacee_type` of `TENANT_UNDER_90_DAY` is statutorily ineligible and is refused with `ERR_TENANT_INELIGIBLE`, on both the initial submission and any resubmission after appeal. Submission moves `status` to `CLAIM_SUBMITTED` and advances the case's `version` by 1.

**Resolving the claim.** A `ProgramAdministrator` renders a `decision` of `PAY` or `DENY`, optionally with a `denial_reason`. The case must be in `CLAIM_SUBMITTED` or `APPEALED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`.

`PAY` is admitted only from `CLAIM_SUBMITTED`, and requires the obligation cap to hold — the project's current `federal_obligated_cents` plus this case's `total_relocation_benefit_cents` multiplied by `cost_share_federal_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents must be at most `total_federal_share_cap_cents`, otherwise `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. It moves `status` to `CLAIM_PAID`, recomputes the project's `federal_obligated_cents` so that the case now enters the ledger's third group, and advances the case's `version` and the project's `version` each by 1. `CLAIM_PAID` is terminal.

`DENY` carries no cap check and moves the project's totals not at all, but its landing state depends on where it fired from — this is the branch asymmetry to watch. `DENY` from `CLAIM_SUBMITTED` moves `status` to `CLAIM_DENIED`, stores the `denial_reason`, and advances only the case's `version` by 1; the displacee retains the right to appeal. `DENY` from `APPEALED` moves `status` to `CLAIM_DENIED_FINAL`, stores the `denial_reason`, and advances only the case's `version` by 1; `CLAIM_DENIED_FINAL` is terminal and there is no further recourse.

**Appealing.** A `RelocationSpecialist` records the displacee's appeal with an `appeal_basis`. The case must be in `CLAIM_DENIED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`, and `appeal_count` must be **strictly less than** 2, otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. So an `appeal_count` of 0 or 1 may appeal and an `appeal_count` of 2 may not: the displacee gets two bites. The appeal moves `status` to `APPEALED`, stores the `appeal_basis`, increments `appeal_count` by 1, and advances the case's `version` by 1. From `APPEALED` the claim may be resubmitted, which is the cycle `CLAIM_SUBMITTED` to `CLAIM_DENIED` to `APPEALED` to `CLAIM_SUBMITTED`, bounded by that appeal count.

**The withdrawal cascade.** As described in §3.3, when a parcel's `participation_status` moves to `WITHDRAWN` — from `VOLUNTARY_CONFIRMED` or from any other from-state — every case on that parcel in `OPENED`, `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED`, `CLAIM_SUBMITTED`, `CLAIM_DENIED` or `APPEALED` — that is, every non-terminal state — moves directly to `CLAIM_DENIED_FINAL` with its `version` advanced by 1. A case already at `CLAIM_PAID` is untouched, and its federal share stays booked.

### 3.7. Closing, Demolition, Utilities and Debris

**Scheduling the closing.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` creates a `ClosingRecord` on the parcel, declaring an `accepted_offer_id`, a `scheduled_closing_date` and a `settlement_cost_cents` that must be at least 0. The offer named by `accepted_offer_id` must exist, otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`; must sit on the same parcel, otherwise `ERR_PARCEL_PROJECT_MISMATCH`; and must have `status` `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. The closing opens in `status` `SCHEDULED` at `version` 1, snapshots `purchase_price_cents` from that offer's `net_offer_cents`, and leaves `funded_at`, `funded_by_user_id`, `recording_reference` and `recorded_at` null. A `SCHEDULED` closing contributes nothing to the obligation ledger. No uniqueness rule guards the pairing, so more than one `SCHEDULED` closing may be created against the same accepted offer; and scheduling one moves nothing outside the closing itself — neither the parcel's `acquisition_status` nor its `version`, and no project total or project `version`.

**Funding the closing.** A `ProgramAdministrator` funds a closing that must be in `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CLOSING_STATE`, and whose parcel must be at `acquisition_status` `OFFER_ACCEPTED` — a parcel elsewhere on the acquisition dimension is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLOSING_STATE`.

Three further conditions apply. The `scheduled_closing_date` must be no later than the project's `period_of_performance_end_date`; a date strictly after it is `ERR_PERIOD_OF_PERFORMANCE_EXPIRED`, and a date falling exactly on the end date is still fundable. Every `RelocationCase` on the same parcel must have `status` of `CLAIM_PAID` or `CLAIM_DENIED_FINAL` — a case still sitting in `OPENED`, `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED`, `CLAIM_SUBMITTED`, `CLAIM_DENIED` or `APPEALED` blocks funding with `ERR_RELOCATION_NOT_SETTLED`, because the displacee's entitlement must be resolved before title passes. And the obligation cap must hold: `federal_obligated_cents` plus this closing's `settlement_cost_cents` multiplied by `cost_share_federal_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, must be at most `total_federal_share_cap_cents`, otherwise `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. As stated in §2.9, when both the period-of-performance check and the cap check are violated the response is `ERR_PERIOD_OF_PERFORMANCE_EXPIRED`. The three are evaluated in the §2.9 order rather than the order of presentation here — period of performance first, then the obligation cap, then relocation settlement — so a closing that would breach the cap and also has an unsettled relocation case reports `ERR_FEDERAL_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Funding moves the closing to `FUNDED`, stamps `funded_at` from the logical clock and `funded_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, moves the parcel's `acquisition_status` from `OFFER_ACCEPTED` to `CLOSED`, recomputes the project's `federal_obligated_cents` to include the settlement-cost item, recomputes `contiguous_open_space_parcel_count`, `largest_component_parcel_ids`, `neighborhood_viability_ratio` and `viability_status` — because the parcel has just entered the acquired node set — and advances the closing's, parcel's and project's `version` each by 1. Note that `purchase_price_cents` is not what enters the ledger; only `settlement_cost_cents` does, because the purchase price was already obligated when the offer was presented.

**Registering a contractor.** A `ProgramAdministrator` registers a `DemolitionContractor` with a `name` that must be unique across all contractors, a `license_no`, an `asbestos_certified` flag defaulting to false, and a `max_concurrent_work_orders` defaulting to 1 and required to be between 1 and 10 inclusive. It opens in `status` `ACTIVE` at `version` 1 with `active_work_order_count` 0. A duplicate `name`, or a `max_concurrent_work_orders` outside the permitted range, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A `ProgramAdministrator` may set the contractor's `status` to `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, optionally with a `status_reason`. Setting it to the value it already holds is `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACTOR_STATE`, so the toggle is strictly a change. Suspension is deliberately shallow: existing work orders are left exactly where they are and none of their fields change; the only effect is that no work order may thereafter be mobilized under that contractor. Reinstatement is the reverse edge and lifts that block. Either direction advances the contractor's `version` by 1.

`active_work_order_count` is the count of that contractor's work orders whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `UTILITY_HOLD`, `READY`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `FAILED_INSPECTION`. `COMPLETED` work orders drop out and release capacity.

**Issuing a work order.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` issues a `DemolitionWorkOrder` on the parcel, declaring a `contractor_id`, an optional `asbestos_survey_status` defaulting to `NOT_PERFORMED` and drawn from `NOT_PERFORMED`, `PERFORMED_POSITIVE`, `ABATED` and `CLEARED`, a `scheduled_start` and a `scheduled_end` that must be strictly after `scheduled_start` — a `scheduled_end` at or before `scheduled_start` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The parcel's `acquisition_status` is deliberately **not** gated here: a work order may be issued at any time, and it is only the completion cascade that looks at whether the parcel has actually reached `CLOSED` — and even there the parcel's state is a condition on a side effect, never a ground for refusal. The contractor named by `contractor_id` is resolved only after the body has passed structural validation, so a malformed window or a bad `asbestos_survey_status` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` ahead of `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, and a contractor in `SUSPENDED` may still be issued a work order.

The work order opens in `status` `ISSUED` at `version` 1 with `failed_inspection_count` 0, `required_utility_types` copied as the four values `WATER`, `ELECTRIC`, `GAS` and `SEWER`, `confirmed_utility_types` an empty array, `total_debris_tons` 0, `recycled_tons` 0, `diversion_pct` 0, and `disposal_manifest_no` and `disposal_facility_type` null. Issuing it recomputes the contractor's `active_work_order_count` and advances the contractor's `version` by 1.

**The buffered window and contractor capacity.** Demolition crews need mobilization and demobilization time either side of the scheduled work, so each work order occupies a wider slot than it nominally books. `buffered_start` is `scheduled_start` minus 24 hours and `buffered_end` is `scheduled_end` plus 24 hours, and the buffered occupation interval is **closed** — both endpoints are included. This is the opposite convention from the appraisal validity interval in §3.4, and the difference is intentional.

Two work orders with the same `contractor_id`, both with `status` in `ISSUED`, `UTILITY_HOLD`, `READY`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `FAILED_INSPECTION`, conflict when the first's `buffered_start` is at or before the second's `buffered_end` **and** the second's `buffered_start` is at or before the first's `buffered_end`. Because both comparisons are non-strict, buffered windows that merely touch — where one's `buffered_end` equals the other's `buffered_start` — **do** conflict.

The check is an aggregate capacity check rather than a pairwise ban. Issuing a work order is refused with `ERR_DEMOLITION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` when the number of existing conflicting work orders found for that contractor is at least `max_concurrent_work_orders`. So with a `max_concurrent_work_orders` of 2, two mutually conflicting work orders are perfectly legal and a third conflicting one is rejected: each row is individually permissible, and it is the collective that exceeds capacity. Work orders belonging to a different `contractor_id` never conflict with each other no matter how their windows overlap, and `COMPLETED` work orders are invisible to the check.

**Ordering utility disconnections.** A structure cannot be demolished with live services running to it, so a `SubrecipientCoordinator` orders a `UtilityDisconnection` for each utility, declaring a `utility_type` of `WATER`, `ELECTRIC`, `GAS` or `SEWER` and a `provider_name`. The parent work order must be in `ISSUED`, `UTILITY_HOLD` or `READY`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. A `utility_type` is unique within its work order, and a second disconnection for the same utility on the same work order is `ERR_DUPLICATE_UTILITY_TYPE` — the same `utility_type` may of course appear once on each of many work orders.

The disconnection opens in `status` `PENDING` at `version` 1 with `failed_attempt_count` 0 and `confirmation_reference` and `resolved_at` null. Creating the **first** child moves the parent work order from `ISSUED` to `UTILITY_HOLD` and advances the parent's `version` by 1; subsequent children do not move the parent's `status`, though the parent's derived lists are maintained as children resolve.

**Resolving a disconnection.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` records an `outcome` of `CONFIRMED` or `FAILED`, and a `CONFIRMED` outcome requires a non-empty `confirmation_reference` — omitting it, or supplying an empty one, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The gate here sits on the **parent**, not on the child: the parent work order must be in `UTILITY_HOLD` or `READY`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DISCONNECTION_STATE`. The child's own `status` is unconstrained, so a `PENDING`, a `CONFIRMED` and a `FAILED` disconnection may all be resolved again. Every resolve stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock, and every `FAILED` outcome increments `failed_attempt_count` by 1 — including a `FAILED` outcome recorded against an already-`FAILED` disconnection, which is the self-loop by which repeated provider failures accumulate.

The six admitted movements differ in what they do to the parent. A `CONFIRMED` outcome from `PENDING`, from `FAILED`, or from an already-`CONFIRMED` state — the last being the confirmation self-loop by which a reference is corrected — sets `status` to `CONFIRMED`, overwrites the `confirmation_reference` and `resolved_at`, recomputes the parent's `confirmed_utility_types` and, if the confirmed set has just become complete, moves the parent from `UTILITY_HOLD` to `READY`; the child's `version` and the parent's `version` each advance by 1. A `FAILED` outcome from `PENDING` sets `status` to `FAILED` and increments `failed_attempt_count`, and advances **only** the child's `version` by 1 — the parent is not touched, because a `PENDING` child was never counted as confirmed. A `FAILED` outcome from `FAILED` likewise advances only the child's `version` by 1 while incrementing `failed_attempt_count` and re-stamping `resolved_at`; `status` stays `FAILED` and the parent is not touched. A `FAILED` outcome from `CONFIRMED` is the bubble-up revocation: `status` becomes `FAILED`, `failed_attempt_count` increments, the parent recomputes `confirmed_utility_types` and, if the parent was `READY`, falls back to `UTILITY_HOLD`; both the child's `version` and the parent's `version` advance by 1.

**The compound parent.** A work order's `UTILITY_HOLD` and `READY` states are not set by any endpoint; they are derived from the children. `confirmed_utility_types` holds the `utility_type` values of children whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, ordered by those children's `created_at` ascending. The parent is `READY` exactly when every `utility_type` listed in `required_utility_types` has a child with that `utility_type` in `status` `CONFIRMED`, and it cycles back to `UTILITY_HOLD` the moment any of those confirmations is revoked.

**Mobilizing the demolition.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` starts a work order. The operation is admitted from `UTILITY_HOLD`, `READY` and `FAILED_INSPECTION`; from `ISSUED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` it is `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. Note that `UTILITY_HOLD` is admitted by the state check specifically so that the more informative utility error can be reported instead.

Four substantive gates then apply, and their relative order is fixed by §2.9. First the asbestos gate: `asbestos_survey_status` must be `CLEARED` or `ABATED`, and when it is `ABATED` the contractor named by `contractor_id` must have `asbestos_certified` true — only a NESHAP-certified contractor may work a site where asbestos was found and abated. A `NOT_PERFORMED` or `PERFORMED_POSITIVE` survey, or an `ABATED` survey under an uncertified contractor, is `ERR_ASBESTOS_SURVEY_MISSING`. Second, the contractor's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACTOR_SUSPENDED`. Third, when starting from `FAILED_INSPECTION`, `failed_inspection_count` must be at most 2; a count strictly greater than 2 is `ERR_DEMOLITION_REWORK_LIMIT`, so a work order survives three failed inspections and dies on the fourth restart attempt. Fourth, the work order must not still be in `UTILITY_HOLD` — if some `utility_type` in `required_utility_types` has no `CONFIRMED` child, the start is `ERR_UTILITY_DISCONNECT_INCOMPLETE`.

Because the asbestos gate sits ahead of the utility gate, a work order that is simultaneously in `UTILITY_HOLD` and carrying an unusable survey reports `ERR_ASBESTOS_SURVEY_MISSING`, as noted in §2.9. A successful start moves `status` to `IN_PROGRESS` and advances the work order's `version` by 1. Nothing else moves — the parcel's acquisition dimension is not checked and not changed at mobilization.

**Failing clearance.** A `ComplianceMonitor` fails an inspection with an `inspection_note`. The work order must be `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. It moves to `FAILED_INSPECTION`, `failed_inspection_count` increments by 1, and the work order's `version` advances by 1. No parcel or contractor field moves, and because `FAILED_INSPECTION` is one of the statuses counted by `active_work_order_count`, the contractor's capacity is not released.

**Completing the demolition.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` completes a work order that must be `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`, supplying a `total_debris_tons` of at least 0, a `recycled_tons` of at least 0 and no greater than `total_debris_tons`, a `disposal_manifest_no`, and a `disposal_facility_type` of `LANDFILL`, `RECYCLING` or `COMBINED`. A `recycled_tons` exceeding `total_debris_tons` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

`diversion_pct` is 0.00 when `total_debris_tons` is 0, and otherwise `recycled_tons` divided by `total_debris_tons`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. The gate is then: a `total_debris_tons` of 0 passes under the zero-quantity waiver, since a work order that generated no debris cannot be held to a diversion percentage; otherwise a `diversion_pct` of at least 25.0 passes; anything else is `ERR_DEBRIS_DIVERSION_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The comparison is made against the **rounded** `diversion_pct`, so a raw ratio of 24.9958 percent that rounds to 25.00 passes, and a `diversion_pct` of exactly 25.00 passes because the threshold is inclusive.

Completion stores the four reported fields and `diversion_pct`, moves `status` to `COMPLETED`, moves the parcel's `acquisition_status` from `CLOSED` to `DEMOLISHED`, recomputes the contractor's `active_work_order_count` — which now drops the completed row and releases its buffered window capacity — and advances the work order's, the parcel's and the contractor's `version` each by 1. That parcel movement is a conditional side effect rather than a gate: it fires only when the parcel is standing exactly at `CLOSED`, and a parcel anywhere else on the acquisition dimension is left untouched with its `version` unmoved while the completion itself still succeeds — so only the work order's and the contractor's `version` advance in that case. `COMPLETED` is terminal.

**Recording the deed.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` records the deed on a closing that must be in `FUNDED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CLOSING_STATE`, supplying a `recording_reference` and a `maintenance_responsible_party`. The parcel's `acquisition_status` must be `DEMOLISHED` — the perpetual restriction is recorded against cleared land, not against a standing house — and any other acquisition state is `ERR_DEMOLITION_NOT_COMPLETE`. Because the closing must be `FUNDED` and recording moves it to `RECORDED`, no closing can produce a second `DeedRestriction`.

Recording starts the third of the depth-three cascades. The closing moves to `RECORDED` with `recording_reference` stored and `recorded_at` stamped from the logical clock. Link one creates a `DeedRestriction`, described in §3.8. Link two: the creation of that restriction moves the parcel's `acquisition_status` from `DEMOLISHED` to `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED`. Link three: the parcel arriving at `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED` recomputes the project's `protected_open_space_acres`. The closing's, the parcel's and the project's `version` each advance by 1, and the restriction opens at `version` 1. `RECORDED` is terminal on the closing.

### 3.8. The Perpetual Open-Space Restriction and Its Monitoring

**What the restriction is.** Federal buyout land is bought once and held as open space forever, so every acquired and cleared parcel carries a `DeedRestriction`. There is no create endpoint for a restriction: rows come into existence only as the side effect of recording a deed, described in §3.7. The restriction records the `parcel_id` it encumbers, the `closing_record_id` whose recording produced it, a `restriction_type` that can only ever be `OPEN_SPACE_PERPETUAL`, a `prohibited_uses` array copied as the four codes `NEW_STRUCTURE`, `FILL_PLACEMENT`, `RESALE_FOR_DEVELOPMENT` and `PERMANENT_STORAGE`, a `recorded_on` taken as the date part of the logical clock at the recording call, a `monitoring_interval_months` of 36, and the `maintenance_responsible_party` supplied at recording. It opens with `status` `RECORDED` and with `corrective_deadline_date`, `open_violation_code`, `violation_reported_by_user_id`, `cured_on` and `termination_authorization_reference` all null.

**The monitoring schedule.** `next_monitoring_due_date` is always an anchor date plus 36 calendar months, and the anchor changes over the restriction's life. At creation the anchor is `recorded_on`. When an inspection sets the restriction to `IN_COMPLIANCE`, the anchor becomes that inspection's `inspection_date`. When a cure is recorded, the anchor becomes `cured_on`. Month arithmetic follows §2.5: when the anchor's day of month does not exist in the target month the result clamps to the last day of the target month, so a `recorded_on` of 2026-01-31 gives 2029-01-31 while a `recorded_on` of 2026-09-15 gives 2029-09-15.

`corrective_deadline_date` is set only when the restriction enters `VIOLATION_OPEN`, and it is the observing inspection's `inspection_date` plus 90 calendar **days** — day arithmetic, not month arithmetic, and the two units are deliberately different. A violation does **not** move `next_monitoring_due_date`; the routine monitoring clock keeps running on its own anchor while the violation is worked. The `corrective_deadline_date` is cleared to null on cure.

A restriction is **overdue** when its `status` is `RECORDED`, `IN_COMPLIANCE` or `CURED` and its `next_monitoring_due_date` is strictly earlier than the date part of the logical clock. A restriction whose `next_monitoring_due_date` falls exactly on the current logical date is due but not yet overdue. `VIOLATION_OPEN` and `TERMINATED` restrictions are never overdue, whatever their due date says: the first is already in enforcement and the second no longer exists as an obligation.

**Recording a monitoring inspection.** A `ComplianceMonitor` records a `MonitoringInspection` against a restriction, supplying an `inspection_date`, a `finding` of `COMPLIANT` or `PROHIBITED_USE_OBSERVED`, an optional `prohibited_use_code`, and a `narrative`. The `prohibited_use_code` is required when `finding` is `PROHIBITED_USE_OBSERVED` and must be null when `finding` is `COMPLIANT`; violating either half of that pairing is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. When supplied it must be one of `NEW_STRUCTURE`, `FILL_PLACEMENT`, `RESALE_FOR_DEVELOPMENT` or `PERMANENT_STORAGE`, and it must be a member of the restriction's `prohibited_uses` — a code outside that array is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, tested at the structural stage and therefore reported ahead of the restriction's own state check and ahead of the future-dating check.

The restriction's `status` must be `RECORDED`, `IN_COMPLIANCE`, `VIOLATION_OPEN` or `CURED` — inspections may be recorded in all four of those states, and only a `TERMINATED` restriction refuses them with `ERR_INVALID_RESTRICTION_STATE`. The `inspection_date` may not be later than the date part of the logical clock; a future-dated site visit is `ERR_FUTURE_DATED_FINDING`, and an `inspection_date` falling exactly on the current logical date is accepted.

While the restriction is already `VIOLATION_OPEN`, a `PROHIBITED_USE_OBSERVED` inspection whose `prohibited_use_code` differs from the restriction's `open_violation_code` is `ERR_PROHIBITED_USE_UNRESOLVED`: a second, different prohibited use may not be opened while the first is still uncured. Re-observing the **same** open code is permitted — it creates another inspection row, leaves the restriction's `status`, `open_violation_code`, `violation_reported_by_user_id`, `corrective_deadline_date` and `next_monitoring_due_date` all exactly as they were, and refreshes nothing. The restriction's `version` still advances by 1.

The inspection row itself is immutable: it stores `inspector_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and, as noted in §2.4, carries only `id` and `created_at` alongside its own fields — no `updated_at` and no `version`.

**What a finding does to the restriction.** A `COMPLIANT` finding recorded against a restriction in `RECORDED` or in `CURED` moves it to `IN_COMPLIANCE` and re-anchors `next_monitoring_due_date` to `inspection_date` plus 36 months. Those two from-states are exhaustive for that effect: a `COMPLIANT` finding recorded against a restriction already in `IN_COMPLIANCE`, or against one in `VIOLATION_OPEN`, changes no restriction field whatsoever — in particular `next_monitoring_due_date` is **not** re-anchored by a clean visit to a restriction already in compliance, and a clean visit does **not** close an open violation or clear its `corrective_deadline_date` — though the inspection row is still created and the restriction's `version` still advances by 1. A `PROHIBITED_USE_OBSERVED` finding recorded against a restriction in `RECORDED`, in `IN_COMPLIANCE` or in `CURED` moves it to `VIOLATION_OPEN`, sets `open_violation_code` to the inspection's `prohibited_use_code`, sets `violation_reported_by_user_id` to the inspection's `inspector_user_id`, and sets `corrective_deadline_date` to `inspection_date` plus 90 days while leaving `next_monitoring_due_date` untouched. When the violation is opened from `CURED`, the previous `cured_on` is retained rather than cleared, so the record of the earlier cure survives. Every one of these movements advances the restriction's `version` by 1 and none of them touch the parcel or the project.

The compliance lifecycle is therefore cyclic: a parcel may cycle `IN_COMPLIANCE` to `VIOLATION_OPEN` to `CURED` to `IN_COMPLIANCE` indefinitely, which is exactly what perpetual monitoring means.

**Curing a violation.** A `ComplianceMonitor` records the cure with a `cure_date` and a `cure_narrative`. The restriction must be in `VIOLATION_OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RESTRICTION_STATE`, and the `cure_date` may not be later than the date part of the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_FUTURE_DATED_FINDING`. Segregation of duties SoD-3 applies as described in §2.10: the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the restriction's `violation_reported_by_user_id`, so the monitor who reported the prohibited use may not be the monitor who signs off that it was fixed. A self-cure is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The cure moves `status` to `CURED`, sets `cured_on` from the request's `cure_date`, clears `open_violation_code` and `corrective_deadline_date` to null, re-anchors `next_monitoring_due_date` to `cure_date` plus 36 months, and advances the restriction's `version` by 1. Note that `violation_reported_by_user_id` is **not** cleared — it persists so that the next violation's SoD-3 check has a prior value to compare against until a new violation overwrites it.

Nothing in the system enforces the `corrective_deadline_date`: a cure recorded after that date is still accepted. The deadline is a reporting and closeout signal, not a bar on curing late.

**Terminating a restriction.** A perpetual restriction can be lifted only under explicit federal authorization. A `ProgramAdministrator` terminates it with a `termination_authorization_reference` that must begin with the literal prefix `FEMA-OSR-`; a reference that does not begin with that prefix is `ERR_TERMINATION_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, and an absent or empty reference is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The restriction must be in `RECORDED`, `IN_COMPLIANCE` or `CURED`, and all three of those from-states behave identically. A restriction in `VIOLATION_OPEN` may **not** be terminated — the violation must be cured first — and a restriction already `TERMINATED` may not be terminated again; both are `ERR_INVALID_RESTRICTION_STATE`.

Termination sets `status` to `TERMINATED`, stores the `termination_authorization_reference`, recomputes the project's `protected_open_space_acres` so that the parcel's acreage leaves the protected total, and advances the restriction's and the project's `version` each by 1. `TERMINATED` is terminal. Termination does **not** move the parcel's `acquisition_status`, which stays at `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED`; the parcel remains publicly owned, it is merely no longer encumbered.

**Protected open space.** `protected_open_space_acres` is the sum of `acreage` over every parcel of the project whose `acquisition_status` is `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED` and whose deed restriction has a `status` **other than** `TERMINATED` — that is, `RECORDED`, `IN_COMPLIANCE`, `VIOLATION_OPEN` or `CURED` all count. The sum is taken over those parcels ordered by their `created_at` ascending and, per the exception noted in §2.6, rounded half up to two decimal places **at the aggregate** rather than per parcel, since each `acreage` is already carried to two decimals.

A parcel whose restriction is `VIOLATION_OPEN` still counts: the encumbrance exists, it is merely being violated. A parcel sitting at `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED` that carries no restriction row at all contributes nothing, and a parcel carrying more than one restriction counts its `acreage` exactly once and counts it as soon as any one of those restrictions is not `TERMINATED`. Only termination removes acreage. Deed recording and restriction termination are therefore exact inverses of each other on this quantity: three parcels of 0.34, 0.41 and 1.25 acres all recorded give 2.00, and terminating the 0.41-acre parcel's restriction returns 1.59.

The quantity is recomputed at exactly three points: when a parcel's `acquisition_status` moves from `DEMOLISHED` to `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED`, when a restriction is terminated, and at closeout initiation.

**Monitoring survives closeout.** A restriction remains fully mutable through the monitoring, cure and terminate operations after its project has reached `CLOSED_OUT`. Long-term maintenance and monitoring obligations outlive the grant, so none of the three operations in this subsection consults the project's `status` at all.

### 3.9. Program Income and Project Closeout

**Why a program income ledger exists.** Open-space land generates money — agricultural leases, salvage from demolished structures, easement fees — and duplicated benefits are sometimes recovered after the fact. All of it is program income, the federal share of which must be accounted for and either spent on eligible costs or returned before the grant can be closed. The ledger is therefore a signed running balance that must be driven to exactly zero at closeout.

**Posting an entry.** A `SubrecipientCoordinator` posts a `ProgramIncomeLedgerEntry` against a project, supplying an `entry_type`, an `amount_cents`, a `posted_on`, an optional `applied_to_parcel_id`, and an `expected_project_version`. The `entry_type` is one of `LEASE_REVENUE`, `SALVAGE_PROCEEDS`, `EASEMENT_FEE`, `DOB_RECOVERY` or `EXPENDITURE`. `amount_cents` is the gross **positive** magnitude of the movement and must be strictly greater than 0 — a zero or negative `amount_cents` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and direction is expressed by the `entry_type`, never by the sign of the input.

The project's `status` must be `APPROVED`, `CLOSEOUT_PENDING` or `CLOSED_OUT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`; a `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `DENIED` project has no ledger. Posting is guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the project as described in §2.7: `expected_project_version` must equal the project's live `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`. The guard sits here because the federal share is computed against the project's live `cost_share_federal_pct` and because the running balance moves whenever any parcel-level cascade touches the project.

When `applied_to_parcel_id` is supplied it must name an existing parcel, otherwise `ERR_PARCEL_NOT_FOUND`, and that parcel must belong to the project in the path, otherwise `ERR_PARCEL_PROJECT_MISMATCH`. When `reverses_entry_id` is present on an entry it must name an existing ledger entry, otherwise `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, and that entry must belong to the same project, otherwise `ERR_PARCEL_PROJECT_MISMATCH`. A `null` `applied_to_parcel_id` is entirely legitimate and marks the entry as project-level.

The entry is created with `status` `ACTIVE` and, as noted in §2.4, carries only `id` and `created_at` alongside its own fields — no `updated_at` and no `version`, because a posted ledger entry is immutable.

**The signed federal share.** `federal_share_cents` is computed per entry, and the rounding happens on each entry before the balance accumulates it. The sign is −1 when `entry_type` is `EXPENDITURE` and +1 for every other `entry_type`, so `EXPENDITURE` is the only debit and `LEASE_REVENUE`, `SALVAGE_PROCEEDS`, `EASEMENT_FEE` and `DOB_RECOVERY` are all credits. The magnitude is `amount_cents` multiplied by the project's `cost_share_federal_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. `federal_share_cents` is then the sign multiplied by that magnitude. Because rounding half up is applied to the positive magnitude and the sign is applied afterwards, a debit magnitude of one half cent rounds **away** from zero to −1 rather than toward zero.

`program_income_balance_cents` is the sum of `federal_share_cents` over every entry of the project whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, accumulated in order of `posted_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. The entry `id` is never used as a sort key. Round-each-entry-then-sum matters: with a `cost_share_federal_pct` of 0.75, a `LEASE_REVENUE` of 100002 cents yields a federal share of 75002 (75001.5 rounded half up) and a `SALVAGE_PROCEEDS` of 300002 cents yields 225002, giving a balance of 300004. Summing the gross amounts first would give 300003, which is wrong. An `EXPENDITURE` of 100002 cents against that balance contributes −75002 and brings it to 225002.

**The balance may never go negative.** Posting an entry whose signed `federal_share_cents` would drive `program_income_balance_cents` below zero is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_INCOME_OVERDRAWN` and no entry is created. An expenditure that brings the balance to exactly 0 is accepted, since 0 is not negative — and that is in fact the state closeout requires. In practice this means a project cannot spend program income it has not earned.

A successful posting recomputes `program_income_balance_cents` and advances the project's `version` by 1. Because the posting endpoint's own OCC guard reads that `version`, two clients cannot interleave postings against a stale view of the balance.

**Reversing an entry.** A `ProgramAdministrator` reverses a posted entry, optionally with a `reversal_note`. The target must exist, otherwise `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, and must have `status` `ACTIVE`; a target already at `REVERSED` is `ERR_ALREADY_REVERSED`, so reversal is strictly once-only. The operation flips the target's `status` from `ACTIVE` to `REVERSED` and creates a mirror entry carrying `reverses_entry_id` set to the target's `id`, the **opposite** sign, the **same** magnitude, and `status` `REVERSED`. The mirror copies the target's `project_id`, `applied_to_parcel_id`, `entry_type`, `amount_cents` and `posted_on` verbatim — so the mirror's own `entry_type` is the target's, and it is `federal_share_cents` alone that carries the flipped sign — and its `expected_project_version` is null, since no client supplied one. The mirror's `federal_share_cents` is the exact arithmetic negation of the target's stored value rather than a fresh derivation from the project's current `cost_share_federal_pct`, which is what guarantees the balance returns bit-for-bit. The optional `reversal_note` is structurally validated but stored on neither row and echoed nowhere. The mirror is created already `REVERSED` rather than `ACTIVE`, which is the whole point: both the target and its mirror leave the `ACTIVE` input set, so the balance returns to exactly its pre-target value with no rounding residue left behind. Reversal recomputes `program_income_balance_cents` and advances the project's `version` by 1. Unlike posting, reversal carries no `expected_project_version` guard. The response body is the **reversing** entry, not the target, and because the operation mints that new row the response status is HTTP 201.

Because the mirror is never `ACTIVE`, reversing an entry can never itself trigger `ERR_PROGRAM_INCOME_OVERDRAWN`, even when the reversed entry was a large credit that expenditures had already drawn against. The balance simply drops by the target's contribution.

**Initiating closeout.** A `ProgramAdministrator` closes the project out in two steps, selected by a `decision` of `INITIATE` or `FINALIZE`; any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

`INITIATE` requires the project's `status` to be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. It then requires five substantive conditions. The project's `participating_parcel_count` must be at least 1, so a project that acquired nothing cannot be closed out; every parcel of the project must have `acquisition_status` of `OPEN_SPACE_RECORDED` or `ACQUISITION_CANCELLED`, so no parcel may be mid-acquisition; and no deed restriction on the project's parcels may have `status` `VIOLATION_OPEN`. All three of those are reported as `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, since the scaffold provides no more specific error for them. No deed restriction on the project's parcels may be overdue — that is, no restriction with `status` in `RECORDED`, `IN_COMPLIANCE` or `CURED` may have a `next_monitoring_due_date` strictly earlier than the date part of the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_MONITORING_OVERDUE`. And the project's `viability_status` must not be `NONVIABLE`, otherwise `ERR_NEIGHBORHOOD_NONVIABLE`; `AT_RISK` and `VIABLE` both pass, because the program tolerates a partially assembled block but not a failed one.

`INITIATE` moves the project's `status` to `CLOSEOUT_PENDING`, recomputes both `program_income_balance_cents` and `protected_open_space_acres` — a deliberate reconciliation sweep so that the two conserved quantities are freshly derived at the moment the books are closed — and advances the project's `version` by 1.

**Finalizing closeout.** `FINALIZE` requires the project's `status` to be `CLOSEOUT_PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, and requires `program_income_balance_cents` to be exactly 0, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_INCOME_OUTSTANDING`. A positive balance means unspent federal program income is still held and a negative balance is impossible by construction. `FINALIZE` moves `status` to `CLOSED_OUT` and advances the project's `version` by 1. `CLOSED_OUT` is terminal: the project may not be returned, re-approved or closed out again.

Note the ordering consequence. Between `INITIATE` and `FINALIZE` the project sits at `CLOSEOUT_PENDING`, and posting program income entries is still permitted in that state — which is precisely how a residual balance is spent down to zero before finalizing. Once `CLOSED_OUT`, posting entries is still permitted as well, so a late lease payment or a late duplication-of-benefits recovery can be booked against a closed grant even though no further closeout transition exists to consume it.
