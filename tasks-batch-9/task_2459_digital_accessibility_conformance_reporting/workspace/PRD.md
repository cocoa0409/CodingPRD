# Digital Accessibility Conformance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Software vendors that sell into government, education and enterprise procurement are asked, repeatedly and in writing, one deceptively simple question: *how accessible is your product, and where exactly does it fall short?* Answering that question honestly is an engineering programme, not a marketing exercise. It requires a chosen standard and a conformance level target, an inventory of the product's components with an explicit assessment scope, automated and manual testing across screen readers, keyboard-only operation and magnification, per-criterion support levels backed by evidence that a second pair of eyes has attested, a published roadmap of known issues with dates the company is prepared to be held to, and a procurement questionnaire whose answers stay accurate after the ink dries.

This service is the system of record for that programme. It tracks accessibility standards and their success criteria, products enrolled in the programme and the components in their assessment scope, assistive-technology test benches and the runs scheduled on them, per-criterion conformance authoring and independent attestation, conformance reports moving through review to customer publication, known issues flowing through triage and prioritization onto funded release trains, customer procurement questionnaire responses with an ongoing accuracy obligation, and complaint intake with both a remediation route and an accommodation route.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **nothing customer-facing is published on unverified evidence**: coverage percentages, attestation freshness, design-review sign-off and disability-category validation all gate publication, and the person who authors evidence may never be the person who signs it off. Second, **the product's exposure is continuously derived, not manually asserted**: a single compound `publication_posture` is recomputed from issue overdueness, questionnaire accuracy and the provisional conformance index after every relevant write, and it is that derived posture — never an operator's opinion — that decides whether a report may be published, a questionnaire may be attested, a design review may be recorded or a remediation budget may be allocated. Because the system exposes a settable logical clock for deterministic testing, every temporal rule in this document is evaluated against that clock rather than against wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called — as tabulated in §4 — the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `AccessibilityLead`, `AccessibilityTester`, `ConformanceReviewer`, `ProductOwner` and `CustomerLiaison`.

An `AccessibilityLead` owns the programme: standards and criteria configuration, product enrolment, the component inventory, bench registration, release trains and the remediation budget, issue triage and prioritization, complaint routing and closure, report publication and withdrawal, questionnaire accuracy attestation, and the logical clock. An `AccessibilityTester` performs the actual testing: scheduling, completing and voiding assessment runs, authoring per-criterion assessments, and verifying fixes. A `ConformanceReviewer` is the independent second pair of eyes: design-review decisions, attestation of assessments authored by someone else, and approval or return of reports submitted by someone else. A `ProductOwner` is accountable for exactly one product and submits that product's report for review. A `CustomerLiaison` faces the customer: drafting, submitting and revising procurement questionnaire responses, taking complaint and accommodation intake, and recording accommodation fulfilment. Logging a known issue is shared between `AccessibilityTester` and `AccessibilityLead`; every read endpoint is open to all five roles.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter. When `X-User-Role` is `ProductOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Product`; ownership checks on any resource that references a product compare that resource's product reference to `X-User-ID`, so a `ProductOwner` submitting a conformance report must present an `X-User-ID` equal to the `product_id` recorded on that report, and any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The same rule applies to every endpoint a `ProductOwner` is permitted to call, whether the product is identified by a path segment or by a body reference. When `X-User-Role` is `AccessibilityLead`, `AccessibilityTester`, `ConformanceReviewer` or `CustomerLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a transition demands provenance — as `authored_by_user_id` and `attested_by_user_id` on a criterion assessment, and as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a conformance report — but it never compares those values to any entity's `id`.

Two operations enforce **segregation of duties** by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against a recorded provenance value rather than against a role. A reviewer may not attest a criterion assessment whose `authored_by_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, and a reviewer may not approve a conformance report whose `submitted_by_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection below states a different ordering for a specific derived collection, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` as the total number of rows matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`. Filters offered by a list endpoint are combined conjunctively, and a filter value that is not a member of the field's enumeration is a request-shape failure.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Resources also carry `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, formatted identically to `created_at` and refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There is one deliberate exception: a success criterion is immutable configuration. Success criteria carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and there is no endpoint that modifies one.

Every temporal rule in this specification — evidence ageing, bench occupation windows, remediation target dates, accommodation dates, questionnaire update cadence, staleness buckets — is evaluated against a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. The clock begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and an `AccessibilityLead` may move it to any instant supplied as `to_iso8601`. Moving the clock is a pure control operation: it performs no writes of its own and recomputes nothing, so derived fields that depend on the current instant refresh only on the next mutating call that touches them, or when an `AccessibilityLead` explicitly forces a product-wide recomputation. When a rule compares a date-only value (such as a remediation target date or an accommodation date) against the clock, the comparison is made on the clock's UTC calendar date.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Resources

Two create operations are optimistically locked against a parent, because each of them supersedes something the client may not have seen.

Opening a conformance report requires a non-optional `expected_product_version` field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Product`. If that value does not equal the product's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is not a courtesy check: a report legitimately supersedes the product's previously published report at publication time, and a writer holding a stale view of the product — of its posture, its provisional index, its component counts, or of an already-open report — must re-read the product before authoring.

Authoring a criterion assessment requires a non-optional `expected_component_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProductComponent`. A mismatch with the component's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMPONENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the reason is supersession: a new assessment for the same component-and-criterion pair silently retires the previous one, so a writer whose view of the component's coverage, assessment state or evidence has been overtaken must re-read before authoring the superseding row.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint may be rejected on version grounds.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Referential Existence

Endpoints with path parameters validate their path segments from left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. A product identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`; a standard yields `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`; a component `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`; a test environment `ERR_ENVIRONMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an assessment run `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`; a criterion assessment `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a conformance report `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`; a release train `ERR_RELEASE_TRAIN_NOT_FOUND`; a known issue `ERR_KNOWN_ISSUE_NOT_FOUND`; a questionnaire response `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_FOUND`; and a complaint `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404.

The same catalogue answers unresolvable references supplied in a request body, evaluated after path resolution and before request-shape validation of business fields: a `standard_id`, `product_id`, `component_id`, `environment_id`, `criterion_id`, `release_train_id`, `known_issue_id`, `report_id`, `evidence_run_id` or `resolution_run_id` that names no existing row is a 404 using the corresponding code, with a criterion reference reported as `ERR_CRITERION_NOT_FOUND`.

Existence is a weaker question than consistency. A reference that resolves but points into the wrong aggregate is a 422-class failure described in §2.7 and in the workflow subsections: a criterion belonging to another standard is `ERR_CRITERION_STANDARD_MISMATCH`, a report opened against a standard other than the product's own is `ERR_REPORT_STANDARD_MISMATCH`, a run declaring an assistive-technology profile the bench does not provide is `ERR_ENVIRONMENT_PROFILE_MISMATCH`, and a component, report or issue drawn from a different product is `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCT_REFERENCE`.

### 2.7. Order of Validation

When several rules could reject the same request, the outcome must be deterministic. Checks are evaluated strictly in the following order, and the first failing check produces the response; later checks are never reached and never mask an earlier one.

1. Authentication headers present and the role recognised — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. The acting role is authorized for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. `ProductOwner` ownership: `X-User-ID` matches the product that owns the target resource — `ERR_PRODUCT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (403).
4. Path-segment existence, left to right — the 404 codes of §2.6.
5. Body reference existence — the 404 codes of §2.6.
6. Request-body shape: required fields present, types correct, enum members valid, numeric ranges respected, `limit` within 1 through 100, `offset` at least 0, arrays non-empty and free of duplicates, a run's `scheduled_end` strictly after its `scheduled_start`, and a train's `window_end` on or after its `window_start` — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
7. Optimistic concurrency: `expected_product_version` on report creation and `expected_component_version` on assessment authoring — `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_COMPONENT_VERSION` (412).
8. Assessment-run actionability — `ERR_RUN_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (409).
9. Criterion-assessment attestation state is `DRAFT` — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_DRAFT` (409).
10. Conformance-report from-state — `ERR_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_APPROVED` or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE` (409).
11. Known-issue from-state — `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_TRIAGED` or `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_RESOLVABLE` (409).
12. Questionnaire-response from-state — `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_SUBMITTED` or `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_REVISABLE` (409).
13. Complaint from-state — `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_NEW`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_TRIAGED` or `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_CLOSABLE` (409).
14. Release-train allocation state — `ERR_TRAIN_BUDGET_ALREADY_ALLOCATED` or `ERR_TRAIN_BUDGET_NOT_ALLOCATED` (409).
15. The product's posture is not `FROZEN`, for report publication, questionnaire accuracy attestation, component design review and budget allocation — `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409).
16. Segregation of duties on assessment attestation and report approval — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
17. The product's posture is exactly `OPEN`, for questionnaire accuracy attestation only — `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_NOT_OPEN` (409).
18. Disability-category validation coverage at publication — `ERR_DISABILITY_COVERAGE_GAP` (422).
19. The assessed component's assessment state is `COMPLETE`, at attestation — `ERR_COMPONENT_ASSESSMENT_INCOMPLETE` (409).
20. Design-review sign-off of every in-scope component at publication — `ERR_DESIGN_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE` (422).
21. Explanatory remarks required when the support level is not `SUPPORTS` — `ERR_REMARKS_REQUIRED` (422).
22. A run's exercised criteria do not exceed the component's declared applicable criteria — `ERR_EXERCISED_EXCEEDS_SCOPE` (422).
23. Assessment evidence has not expired — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_EVIDENCE_EXPIRED` (422).
24. Bench occupation windows do not conflict — `ERR_ENVIRONMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
25. Release-train windows neither overlap nor abut — `ERR_TRAIN_WINDOW_ADJACENT` (409).
26. A committed issue's target date lies inside the train's usable window — `ERR_TARGET_DATE_OUTSIDE_TRAIN_WINDOW` (422).
27. An accommodation's needed-by date is not in the past — `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_DATE_IN_PAST` (422).
28. The product has at least one in-scope component at publication — `ERR_NO_IN_SCOPE_COMPONENTS` (422).
29. Pinned assessments are unchanged at publication — `ERR_PINNED_ASSESSMENT_CHANGED` (409).
30. The cited report is published, at questionnaire attestation — `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
31. The complaint's route matches the requested branch action and closure outcome — `ERR_COMPLAINT_ROUTE_MISMATCH` (409).
32. The linked known issue has come to rest before complaint closure — `ERR_LINKED_ISSUE_UNRESOLVED` (409).
33. Cross-aggregate consistency of resolved references — `ERR_CRITERION_STANDARD_MISMATCH`, `ERR_REPORT_STANDARD_MISMATCH`, `ERR_ENVIRONMENT_PROFILE_MISMATCH` or `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCT_REFERENCE` (422).
34. Resolution evidence is a completed run on the issue's own component — `ERR_RESOLUTION_EVIDENCE_INVALID` (422).
35. Uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CRITERION_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPONENT_NAME`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENVIRONMENT_NAME` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAIN_NAME` (409).
36. At most one open report per product and standard — `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_OPEN` (409).
37. Conformance level and surface compatibility with the standard — `ERR_LEVEL_OR_SURFACE_UNSUPPORTED` (422).
38. Release-train effort capacity at commit — `ERR_TRAIN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
39. At least one fundable issue at allocation — `ERR_NO_FUNDABLE_ISSUES` (422).

Note the two consequences readers most often get wrong. Because the posture gate at step 15 precedes segregation of duties at step 16, a reviewer attempting to attest their own work on a frozen product sees `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_FROZEN`, not `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And because publication's disability-coverage check at step 18 precedes the design-review check at step 20, which in turn precedes the empty-inventory check at step 28 and the pinned-evidence check at step 29, a report that fails several publication preconditions at once reports them in exactly that order.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Ordering of Arithmetic

All monetary values are integer cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system. All computed ratios, indices, scores and debts are stored to **2 decimal places using round-half-up**. Budget allocation rounds to **whole cents, also half-up**.

Three rounding disciplines are normative and must not be reorganised for convenience. First, a component's testing coverage keeps every per-profile intermediate score at full precision and rounds exactly once, on the final weighted sum. Second, an issue's risk score never pre-rounds its base term: aging and complaint amplification multiply the unrounded base, and the single rounding happens on the final clamped product. Third, remediation budget allocation rounds each issue's ask to whole cents **before** the waterfall consumes the budget, never by summing raw asks and rounding the total — the two orders differ at the exhaustion boundary, and only the per-issue order is correct.

Where a calculation prescribes a comparison order or a tie-break, that order is part of the contract. Where it prescribes an evaluation sequence — most notably the questionnaire disclosure debt, whose tier is keyed on a partial figure rather than on the final one — the sequence must be followed exactly once and never iterated to a fixed point.

### 2.9. Derived Fields, Cascades and Version Semantics

A large fraction of this system's state is derived. Counters, coverage percentages, indices, postures, funding states and readiness flags are never supplied by clients and never accepted in a request body; they are recomputed by the server from their inputs whenever an operation changes one of those inputs. A client that reads a derived field is reading the value as of the last write that touched it, which is why an explicit product-wide recomputation capability exists for use after the logical clock moves.

Cascades run transitively within the request that triggered them, and they are deep. Completing an assessment run, for example, credits the component's evidence total, which forces recomputation of the component's coverage percentage, which may advance the component's assessment dimension, which in turn updates the owning product's completion counters, readiness and posture — four hops from one call. Voiding a completed run runs the mirror-image chain and can travel one hop further, because a component falling out of `COMPLETE` invalidates attested assessments on it. Two conservation invariants hold across these chains: a voided run withdraws from the component's evidence total exactly the number of criteria it had contributed, no more and no less; and a release train's remediation budget always equals its unallocated remainder plus the sum of funds granted to its issues.

Version bumps follow the cascade, and the specific bumps are part of the contract because clients hold them for optimistic concurrency. Adding a component, authoring a criterion assessment, attesting an assessment, opening a report, publishing a report, withdrawing a report, logging a known issue and forcing a product recomputation each increase the owning product's `version` by 1. Recording a design-review decision, completing a run and voiding a run each increase both the component's `version` and the owning product's `version` by 1. Every mutation also refreshes the mutated row's `updated_at`.

One reentrancy rule prevents infinite regress: writes made **by** a posture transition — specifically the questionnaire accuracy flips described in §3.8 — do not themselves re-trigger a posture recomputation inside the same request.

### 2.10. Uniqueness, Immutability and Enumerations

Names are the programme's stable handles, so several of them are unique. A standard's `code` is globally unique across standards; a criterion's `criterion_number` is unique within its owning standard; a component's `name` is unique within its owning product; a test environment's `name` and a release train's `name` are each globally unique. Violations surface as `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CRITERION_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPONENT_NAME`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENVIRONMENT_NAME` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAIN_NAME` respectively, all with HTTP 409.

Ownership references are immutable once written. A criterion's owning standard, a component's owning product, a run's component and bench, an assessment's component and criterion, a report's product and standard, an issue's product, component and criterion, a questionnaire response's product and cited report, and a complaint's product are all fixed at creation; no endpoint reassigns them.

The system's enumerations are closed sets, and the exact spellings below are the contract. Standard codes are `WCAG_2_1`, `WCAG_2_2`, `EN_301_549` and `SECTION_508_SW`. A standard's `surface_scope` and a product's `surface_type` are each one of `WEB`, `SOFTWARE` or `BOTH`. Conformance levels — a standard's `max_target_level`, a criterion's `level` and a product's `target_level` — are `A`, `AA` or `AAA`. Disability categories are `VISION`, `HEARING`, `MOTOR` and `COGNITIVE`. Component types are `WEB_UI`, `DESKTOP_APP`, `MOBILE_APP`, `AUTHORING_TOOL` and `DOCUMENTATION`; a component's `assessment_scope` is `FULL`, `SAMPLED` or `EXCLUDED`. Assistive-technology profiles are `SCREEN_READER`, `KEYBOARD` and `MAGNIFICATION`; bench platforms are `WINDOWS`, `MACOS`, `LINUX`, `ANDROID` and `IOS`. A run's `method` is `AUTOMATED` or `MANUAL`. Per-criterion support levels are `SUPPORTS`, `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTS`, `DOES_NOT_SUPPORT` and `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Issue severities are `BLOCKER`, `SERIOUS`, `MODERATE` and `MINOR`, and an issue's `publish_intent` is `INCLUDED` or `EXCLUDED`. A report's `publication_channel` is `CUSTOMER_TRUST_PORTAL`, `PROCUREMENT_PACKET` or `PUBLIC_WEBSITE`. Questionnaire flavours (`questionnaire_kind`) are `VPAT_ACR`, `SECTION_508_CHECKLIST`, `EN_301_549_ANNEX` and `CUSTOM_RFP`. Complaint intake channels are `EMAIL`, `PHONE`, `WEB_FORM` and `REGULATOR_REFERRAL`, and a complaint's `closure_outcome` is one of `REMEDIATION_SCHEDULED`, `ACCOMMODATION_PROVIDED`, `ACCOMMODATION_DENIED` or `NOT_A_DEFECT`. The lifecycle enumerations — component `assessment_state` and `design_review_state`, run `status`, assessment `attestation_state`, report `state`, issue `state` and `funding_state` and `risk_variant`, train `allocation_state`, questionnaire `review_status` and `accuracy_status` and `disclosure_tier`, complaint `state` and `route`, and product `assessment_readiness` and `publication_posture` — are enumerated where their workflows are described in §3.

### 2.11. Independence of Parallel State Dimensions

Three entities carry two state dimensions each, and their independence is a deliberate design decision rather than an accident of modelling. A component's `assessment_state` (testing progress) and its `design_review_state` (development-lifecycle sign-off) advance on entirely separate triggers and never gate each other: a component may reach `COMPLETE` testing while still `NOT_REVIEWED`, and it may be `REVIEWED_PASS` while still `PLANNED`. A questionnaire response's `review_status` (authoring progress) and `accuracy_status` (the standing accuracy obligation) likewise evolve independently, and every combination of the two is reachable — a `SUBMITTED` response may be `CURRENT`, `STALE` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`.

Recovery in these machines is asymmetric where the domain demands it. A component that falls out of `COMPLETE` can climb back; a questionnaire that has gone `STALE` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` is never healed by the product's posture improving on its own — only an explicit revision, or an attestation where one is permitted, returns it to `CURRENT`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standard Selection, Success Criteria and Product Enrolment

The programme begins with configuration that, once written, is never edited. An `AccessibilityLead` registers each accessibility standard the company works against by supplying its `code`, a human-readable `title` of 1 to 200 characters, the `surface_scope` describing which product surfaces the standard governs, and the `max_target_level` naming the highest conformance level the standard itself defines. Because a standard code identifies a published document, registering a second standard with an already-registered `code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_CODE`. A freshly registered standard reports a `criteria_count` of 0; that counter is derived, and it always equals the number of success criteria whose owning standard is this one.

Success criteria are then attached to a standard one at a time. Each carries a `criterion_number` written as dotted decimal groups — two or three groups of digits separated by single dots, as in "1.4.3" or "2.1" — a `title` of 1 to 200 characters, the `level` at which the criterion applies, a non-empty and duplicate-free list of `disability_categories` the criterion protects, and a non-empty and duplicate-free list of `applies_to_surfaces` drawn from `WEB` and `SOFTWARE`. The owning standard is recorded on the criterion as its `standard_id` and can never be changed. A `criterion_number` that already exists on that same standard is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CRITERION_NUMBER`, and a criterion whose `level` is stricter than the standard's `max_target_level` — for instance `AAA` on a standard that stops at `AA` — is rejected with `ERR_LEVEL_OR_SURFACE_UNSUPPORTED`. Criteria may be read individually or listed for a standard, and that listing accepts an optional `level` filter alongside `limit` and `offset`.

Enrolling a product is the moment the programme commits to a target. The `AccessibilityLead` supplies the product `name` (1 to 160 characters), the `version_label` of the release under assessment (1 to 40 characters), the `standard_id` of the selected standard, the `target_level` the product intends to claim, and the `surface_type` classifying the product. Two compatibility rules apply together and both report `ERR_LEVEL_OR_SURFACE_UNSUPPORTED`: the `target_level` must not be stricter than the chosen standard's `max_target_level`, and the surfaces must be compatible — when the standard's `surface_scope` is `BOTH` any `surface_type` is accepted, and otherwise the product's `surface_type` must equal the standard's `surface_scope`.

Everything else on a product is derived and starts empty. At enrolment `components_in_scope_count`, `components_complete_count`, `assessed_criteria_count`, `stale_questionnaire_count`, `correction_required_questionnaire_count`, `overdue_committed_blocker_count` and `accommodations_fulfilled_count` are all 0, `conformance_index_provisional` is 0.00, `assessment_readiness` is `NOT_READY`, and `publication_posture` is `OPEN`. The two component counters have precise definitions that matter downstream: `components_in_scope_count` is the number of the product's components whose in-scope flag is true, and `components_complete_count` is the number of those in-scope components whose assessment dimension has reached `COMPLETE`. From them, `assessment_readiness` is `READY` when the product has at least one in-scope component and every in-scope component is complete, and `NOT_READY` in every other case, including the case of an empty inventory. `stale_questionnaire_count` and `correction_required_questionnaire_count` are the numbers of the product's questionnaire responses whose accuracy dimension is `STALE` and `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` respectively; `accommodations_fulfilled_count` is the number of the product's complaints that carry a recorded accommodation-fulfilment instant; and `overdue_committed_blocker_count` is the number of the product's known issues that are simultaneously of `BLOCKER` severity, in state `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED`, and carrying a customer-published target date earlier than the logical clock's current date. Products may be read individually or listed with optional `standard_id`, `publication_posture` and `assessment_readiness` filters.

Because moving the logical clock writes nothing, a product's clock-sensitive derivations can drift out of date between calls. An `AccessibilityLead` may therefore force a full product-wide recomputation, supplying a `reason` for the audit trail. That recomputation rebuilds `components_in_scope_count`, `components_complete_count`, `assessment_readiness`, `assessed_criteria_count` and `conformance_index_provisional`; re-evaluates questionnaire accuracy, so that any response still marked `CURRENT` whose `update_due_at` deadline now lies before the clock becomes `STALE`; rebuilds `stale_questionnaire_count`, `correction_required_questionnaire_count`, `overdue_committed_blocker_count` and `accommodations_fulfilled_count`; and finally re-derives `publication_posture`. The product's `version` increases by 1 as a result.

### 3.2. The Component Inventory and Development-Lifecycle Design Review

A conformance report is only as honest as its component inventory, so each product's inventory is declared explicitly. Adding a component requires its `name` (1 to 160 characters, unique among that product's components — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPONENT_NAME`), its `component_type`, the boolean `in_scope` flag stating whether the component sits inside the report's assessment scope, the declared `assessment_scope` depth, the `applicable_criteria_count` between 1 and 500 that the tester declares to be in this component's scope, and the `user_reach_pct` between 0 and 100 describing what share of the product's users touch the component. Those last two numbers are not decoration: `applicable_criteria_count` is the denominator of the component's coverage arithmetic, and `user_reach_pct` is the risk-weighting input for every issue logged against the component.

A new component starts with `assessment_state` at `PLANNED`, `design_review_state` at `NOT_REVIEWED`, `coverage_pct` at 0.00, `evidence_units_total` at 0, an empty `validated_disability_categories` list and no `design_review_note`. Its creation immediately rebuilds the owning product's `components_in_scope_count`, `assessment_readiness`, `conformance_index_provisional` and `publication_posture`, and raises the product's `version` by 1. Components can be read individually — the read exposes `coverage_pct`, `validated_disability_categories` and both state dimensions — or listed for a product with optional `in_scope` and `assessment_state` filters.

The development-lifecycle side of a component is handled by a `ConformanceReviewer`, who records a design-review verdict together with the acceptance-criteria sign-off. The submission carries a `review_decision` of either `PASS` or `BLOCK` and a `review_note`. A `PASS` moves the design dimension to `REVIEWED_PASS`, whether the component was `NOT_REVIEWED` or `REVIEWED_BLOCKED`; a `BLOCK` moves it to `REVIEWED_BLOCKED`, whether the component was `NOT_REVIEWED` or `REVIEWED_PASS`. The dimension is therefore cyclic: `NOT_REVIEWED → REVIEWED_PASS → REVIEWED_BLOCKED → REVIEWED_PASS` is a legal history. In all four transitions the supplied `review_note` is written to `design_review_note`, replacing any earlier remark, a design-review instant is recorded from the logical clock, and both the component's `version` and the product's `version` increase by 1. Design review may not be recorded at all while the product's posture is `FROZEN`; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_FROZEN`. What a design review never does is touch the testing dimension: `assessment_state`, `coverage_pct` and the product's `assessment_readiness` are all left exactly as they were, because the two dimensions of a component are independent.

### 3.3. Assistive-Technology Benches and Run Scheduling

Testing happens on benches, and a bench is a concrete assistive-technology configuration rather than an abstraction. Registering one requires a globally unique `name` of 1 to 120 characters — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENVIRONMENT_NAME` — the `at_profile` the bench provides, the `at_product_name` naming the concrete assistive-technology product and version such as "NVDA 2026.1", the `os_platform` hosting it, and optionally the `reconfiguration_buffer_minutes` between 0 and 240 that models teardown and re-imaging after each booking. When the buffer is omitted it defaults to half an hour, that is 30 minutes. A bench also reports a derived `scheduled_run_count`, being the number of runs on it whose `status` is either `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`.

An `AccessibilityTester` books a bench by scheduling an assessment run against one component. The booking carries the `component_id` of the component under test, the `environment_id` of the bench, the `method` distinguishing `AUTOMATED` from `MANUAL` testing, the `at_profile` exercised, the occupation window as `scheduled_start` (inclusive) and `scheduled_end` (exclusive), the `criteria_exercised` count between 1 and 500 that this run will contribute as coverage numerator, and the non-empty duplicate-free `disability_categories_validated` list naming the disability categories validated with assistive technology during the run. The run is created with `status` at `SCHEDULED`, with no `defects_found`, no `void_reason` and no `completed_at`; nothing is credited to coverage until the run is completed.

Four scheduling rules guard the booking. The window must be well formed, with `scheduled_end` strictly later than `scheduled_start`, or the request fails as a malformed body. The declared `at_profile` must equal the profile the chosen bench provides, since a magnification bench cannot exercise a screen reader; a mismatch is `ERR_ENVIRONMENT_PROFILE_MISMATCH`. The `criteria_exercised` figure must not exceed the component's declared `applicable_criteria_count`, because a run cannot exercise more criteria than the component has in scope; exceeding it is `ERR_EXERCISED_EXCEEDS_SCOPE`. And the bench must be free: the new run's half-open window, from its `scheduled_start` up to but not including its `scheduled_end`, must not intersect the occupation window of any other run on the same bench whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, where each existing run's occupation window runs from its own `scheduled_start` up to but not including its `scheduled_end` extended forward by that bench's `reconfiguration_buffer_minutes`. Any intersection is `ERR_ENVIRONMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Voided runs release their booking and are ignored by this check entirely.

Scheduling the first run for a component is what starts its testing dimension: the component moves `PLANNED → IN_TESTING`, provided its declared `assessment_scope` is not `EXCLUDED` — a component declared out of assessment depth stays at `PLANNED` no matter how many runs are booked against it. Each newly scheduled run also raises the bench's `scheduled_run_count` by 1. Runs can be read individually or listed with optional `component_id`, `environment_id`, `status` and `method` filters.

### 3.4. Run Completion, Voiding and the Coverage Calculation

Closing out a run records its `defects_found` — a count from 0 to 500 when supplied — and is only possible while the run's `status` is `SCHEDULED`; attempting it on a run that is already `COMPLETED` or `VOID` is `ERR_RUN_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. On success the run moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, its `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the cascade begins: the component's `evidence_units_total` rises by exactly this run's `criteria_exercised` (that field is always the sum of `criteria_exercised` over the component's `COMPLETED` runs), the component's `coverage_pct` is recomputed, and the component's `validated_disability_categories` is rebuilt as the union of `disability_categories_validated` over the component's `COMPLETED` runs whose `method` is `MANUAL` — automated runs contribute nothing at all to validated categories, however many criteria they exercise. If the recomputed coverage has reached the attestation floor, the component advances `IN_TESTING → COMPLETE`. The product's `components_complete_count`, `assessment_readiness` and `publication_posture` are then rebuilt, and both the component's `version` and the product's `version` rise by 1.

**Coverage arithmetic.** A component's `coverage_pct` is a weighted score over the three assistive-technology profiles, computed as follows. Let the denominator be the component's declared `applicable_criteria_count`, which is at least 1 by construction. For each of the three profiles `SCREEN_READER`, `KEYBOARD` and `MAGNIFICATION`, take the automated evidence for that profile as the sum of `criteria_exercised` over the component's `COMPLETED` runs whose `method` is `AUTOMATED` and whose `at_profile` is that profile, and the manual evidence as the same sum restricted to `MANUAL` runs. Automated evidence earns four tenths of a criterion of credit per criterion exercised, while manual evidence earns full credit, so the profile's raw score is the sum of four tenths of the automated evidence plus the whole of the manual evidence, divided by the denominator, and then capped at 1. A second cap then applies conditionally: a profile with no manual evidence at all may not score above 0.60, whereas a profile with any manual evidence is capped only at 1.00. The profile's score is the smaller of its raw score and its applicable cap. The three profile scores are combined with fixed weights — 45 percent of the score for `SCREEN_READER`, 35 percent for `KEYBOARD` and 20 percent for `MAGNIFICATION` — and the weighted sum is multiplied by 100 to yield a percentage. Exactly one rounding happens in the whole calculation: the per-profile scores are kept at full precision and only the final percentage is rounded half-up to two decimal places, which becomes `coverage_pct`.

The attestation floor is 45.00 percent. A component whose recomputed `coverage_pct` is at least 45.00 qualifies as `COMPLETE`; a component whose recomputed `coverage_pct` falls below 45.00 does not. Note what the automated-only cap implies when combined with the weights: a component tested purely by automation can never exceed 60 percent of any profile's score, giving a ceiling of 60.00 percent overall, so a well-automated component can pass the floor without manual testing, but only if automation is spread across all three profiles.

Voiding a run is the compensating operation, and it exists because evidence sometimes turns out to be unusable. The tester supplies a `void_reason`; the run moves to `VOID`, which is terminal. A run may be voided from `SCHEDULED` or from `COMPLETED`, but never from `VOID` — a repeat attempt is `ERR_RUN_NOT_ACTIONABLE` — and a `COMPLETED` run that some known issue cites as its resolution evidence may not be voided at all, which is likewise reported as `ERR_RUN_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Voiding a merely `SCHEDULED` run has no coverage consequences; it simply records the `void_reason` and lowers the bench's `scheduled_run_count` by 1, freeing the window for other bookings.

Voiding a `COMPLETED` run runs the mirror-image cascade, and it is the deepest chain in the system. The component's `evidence_units_total` decreases by exactly the `criteria_exercised` that run had contributed — never by an approximation and never by a recomputed guess. `coverage_pct` is then recomputed from the remaining `COMPLETED` runs, and `validated_disability_categories` is rebuilt as the union over the component's remaining `COMPLETED` `MANUAL` runs, so voiding the only manual run for a profile can both drop the category union and reintroduce that profile's automated-only cap. If the recomputed `coverage_pct` has fallen below 45.00, the component reverts `COMPLETE → IN_TESTING`, and that reversion in turn flips every `ATTESTED` criterion assessment on that component to `NEEDS_REVALIDATION`. Finally the product's `components_complete_count`, `assessed_criteria_count`, `conformance_index_provisional`, `assessment_readiness` and `publication_posture` are rebuilt, and both the component's `version` and the product's `version` rise by 1. The bench's `scheduled_run_count` drops by 1 in this case as well.

### 3.5. Authoring, Superseding and Attesting Per-Criterion Assessments

The substance of a conformance report is a grid of per-criterion judgements, one row per component and criterion. An `AccessibilityTester` authors each row by naming the `component_id` being assessed, the `criterion_id` being judged, the `support_level` claimed, optionally the `evidence_run_id` of the run cited as evidence, optionally the explanatory `remarks`, and the mandatory `expected_component_version` that optimistically locks the row against the component as described in §2.5. The cited criterion must belong to the standard the product selected, or the request fails with `ERR_CRITERION_STANDARD_MISMATCH`.

Remarks are where accessibility reporting earns or loses its credibility, so they are mandatory whenever the claim is anything other than unqualified support. A `support_level` of `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTS`, `DOES_NOT_SUPPORT` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` requires `remarks` of at least 10 characters; omitting them, or supplying fewer than 10 characters, is rejected with `ERR_REMARKS_REQUIRED`. When `remarks` are present at all they must be between 10 and 2000 characters. Only a `support_level` of `SUPPORTS` may stand without explanation.

A newly authored row begins with `attestation_state` at `DRAFT`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as its `authored_by_user_id`, and carries no `attested_by_user_id`, no `attested_at`, no `attestation_note` and no `superseded_by_assessment_id`. Authoring also **supersedes history**: any earlier assessment for the same component-and-criterion pair whose `attestation_state` is `DRAFT`, `ATTESTED` or `NEEDS_REVALIDATION` moves to `SUPERSEDED` and has its `superseded_by_assessment_id` set to the new row's identifier. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, and a superseded row never counts as evidence again. Because retiring an `ATTESTED` row changes what the product can claim, authoring always rebuilds the product's `assessed_criteria_count`, `conformance_index_provisional` and `publication_posture`, and raises both the component's `version` and the product's `version` by 1.

Attestation is the independent sign-off, performed by a `ConformanceReviewer` who supplies an `attestation_note`. Four preconditions guard it, and they are evaluated in the order given in §2.7. The row must still be `DRAFT`, or the request is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. The product must not be `FROZEN`, which is checked before segregation of duties. The reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the row's `authored_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The owning component's `assessment_state` must be `COMPLETE`, or the request is `ERR_COMPONENT_ASSESSMENT_INCOMPLETE` — which is the coverage floor of §3.4 reaching into the attestation workflow. And the evidence must be fresh: attestation must happen no later than 90 days after the row's `created_at`, measured on the logical clock, or the request is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_EVIDENCE_EXPIRED`. An attestation exactly 90 days after authoring is still accepted; one day later is not.

A successful attestation moves the row `DRAFT → ATTESTED`, stamps `attested_at` from the logical clock, records the reviewer's `X-User-ID` as `attested_by_user_id`, stores the `attestation_note`, rebuilds the product's `conformance_index_provisional` and `assessed_criteria_count`, re-derives `publication_posture`, and raises the product's `version` by 1.

Attested evidence can go stale in two ways, and both drive the row `ATTESTED → NEEDS_REVALIDATION` without any client action: the owning component leaving `COMPLETE`, or the run the row cites as `evidence_run_id` being voided. In that flip `attested_by_user_id` and `attested_at` are deliberately retained for audit, but the row stops qualifying as evidence for either conformance-index variant, and the product's `conformance_index_provisional` and `publication_posture` are rebuilt. A row in `NEEDS_REVALIDATION` cannot be re-attested; the tester must author a fresh assessment, which supersedes it. Assessments can be read individually or listed with optional `component_id`, `criterion_id`, `evidence_run_id`, `superseded_by_assessment_id` and `attestation_state` filters.

### 3.6. The Conformance Index: an Optimistic Running View and a Pessimistic Certified One

The programme carries two numbers that answer the same question with different levels of charity, and the gap between them is the point. The **provisional** index, exposed on the product as `conformance_index_provisional`, is a running view that rises as soon as any tester writes an assessment; it is what the posture gate of §3.8 watches. The **certified** index, frozen once onto a report as `conformance_index_certified` at publication, accepts only independently attested evidence and charges the product for everything it has not assessed. On identical data the certified figure can be far lower than the provisional one, and a programme that publishes without noticing that difference will publish a number it cannot defend.

Both variants share the same five steps. **First**, determine the in-scope criteria: all success criteria belonging to the product's selected standard whose `level` is within the target, where a `target_level` of `A` admits only `A` criteria, a target of `AA` admits `A` and `AA` criteria, and a target of `AAA` admits `A`, `AA` and `AAA` criteria. Their presentation order is by `criterion_number` ascending, which affects display only and never the arithmetic. **Second**, determine the in-scope components: the product's components whose in-scope flag is true. **Third**, for every pairing of an in-scope criterion with an in-scope component, select at most one qualifying assessment — the row for that component and criterion whose `attestation_state` is one of the variant's qualifying states, and when more than one row qualifies, the one with the greatest `created_at`, ties broken in favour of the greatest `version`. The provisional variant treats both `DRAFT` and `ATTESTED` as qualifying; the certified variant treats only `ATTESTED` as qualifying, so drafts are invisible to it. Neither variant ever accepts a `SUPERSEDED` or `NEEDS_REVALIDATION` row.

**Fourth**, roll each criterion up across the in-scope components on a worst-of basis. Qualifying rows whose `support_level` is `NOT_APPLICABLE` are ignored and contribute nothing; every other qualifying row contributes a support weight of 1.0 for `SUPPORTS`, 0.5 for `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTS` and 0.0 for `DOES_NOT_SUPPORT`. When at least one in-scope component contributes such a row, the criterion's rolled weight is the smallest of the contributed weights — one component reporting `DOES_NOT_SUPPORT` drags the whole criterion to 0.0 however well the others do — and the criterion is included. When no in-scope component contributes such a row, the two variants diverge. The provisional variant excludes the criterion from both the numerator and the denominator, which is exactly why it flatters: unassessed criteria simply vanish. The certified variant excludes the criterion only when at least one in-scope component has a qualifying `NOT_APPLICABLE` row for it and no in-scope component has a qualifying row of any other kind; in every other case — most importantly when the criterion has no attested evidence anywhere — the certified variant includes the criterion with a rolled weight of 0.0. The certified variant carries one further penalty on criteria that do have evidence: if any single in-scope component lacks a qualifying attested row for the criterion, the criterion's rolled weight is forced to 0.0 and the criterion is included, so partial coverage of the component inventory is scored as no coverage at all.

**Fifth**, the denominator is the count of included criteria and the numerator is the sum of the rolled weights of those included criteria. When the denominator is 0 the index is 0.00; otherwise the index is 100 multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The product's `assessed_criteria_count` is the provisional variant's denominator — that is, the number of in-scope criteria that have at least one qualifying provisional assessment — and it is what tells the posture gate whether the index floor is meaningful yet.

At publication the certified rollup is also frozen into the report's `criterion_support_snapshot`, which holds one entry per in-scope criterion as classified by the certified rollup, each entry naming that criterion's `criterion_id`, the worst-of `rolled_support_level` that produced its weight, and the `weight` itself. That snapshot is the customer-facing per-criterion table, and it never changes afterwards.

### 3.7. Report Authoring, Review, Publication and Withdrawal

Opening a report is an `AccessibilityLead` action carrying the `product_id`, the `standard_id`, the `report_title` that customers will see (1 to 200 characters) and the `expected_product_version` that locks the create against the product. The report's standard must be the product's own selected standard; anything else is `ERR_REPORT_STANDARD_MISMATCH`. A product may have only one report per standard in flight: if another report for the same product and standard is currently `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, the create is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_OPEN`. A new report starts in `state` `DRAFT` with `pinned_assessment_ids` and `pinned_assessment_versions` both empty, an empty `criterion_support_snapshot`, no `conformance_index_certified`, a `published_issue_count` of 0, a `change_request_count` of 0, no `publication_channel`, no `published_at`, no `withdrawal_reason`, no `superseded_by_report_id`, and neither a `submitted_by_user_id` nor an `approved_by_user_id`. Opening it raises the product's `version` by 1. Reports can be read individually — the read exposes the pinned evidence, the per-criterion snapshot and the certified index — or listed with optional `product_id`, `standard_id`, `state` and `superseded_by_report_id` filters.

Submission is the one action reserved for the accountable `ProductOwner`, who supplies a `submission_note`. It is legal from `DRAFT` and from `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, both landing in `IN_REVIEW`; from any other state it is `ERR_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. The owner's `X-User-ID` must equal the report's `product_id`, or the request is `ERR_PRODUCT_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. On success the acting `X-User-ID` is written to `submitted_by_user_id`, overwriting any earlier submitter when the report is coming back around the review loop, and a submission instant is stamped from the logical clock. Submission deliberately leaves `pinned_assessment_ids` and `pinned_assessment_versions` untouched: evidence is pinned later, at approval, not at submission.

A `ConformanceReviewer` may then return the report or approve it, and both actions require the report to be `IN_REVIEW` — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_NOT_IN_REVIEW`. Returning it carries a `change_request_note`, moves the report `IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, raises `change_request_count` by 1, and clears `pinned_assessment_ids` and `pinned_assessment_versions` back to empty lists. From `CHANGES_REQUESTED` a fresh submission returns the report to `IN_REVIEW`, and the loop `IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → DRAFT → IN_REVIEW` may repeat without bound, with `change_request_count` recording how many times the report has been returned. Approval carries an `approval_note` and moves the report `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`; the approving reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the report's `submitted_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On approval the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `approved_by_user_id`, an approval instant is stamped from the logical clock, and the evidence is **pinned**: `pinned_assessment_ids` is frozen as the identifiers of every criterion assessment currently in `ATTESTED` state belonging to one of the product's in-scope components, ordered by the assessments' `created_at` ascending, and `pinned_assessment_versions` captures those same rows' `version` values in the same index-aligned order.

Publication is the customer-facing commitment, performed by an `AccessibilityLead` who names the `publication_channel`. It requires the report to be `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_NOT_APPROVED` — and then five substantive conditions, evaluated in the order set out in §2.7. The product's posture must not be `FROZEN`, that is it must be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, or the request is `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Every in-scope component's `validated_disability_categories` must contain every disability category appearing anywhere in the `disability_categories` of the in-scope criteria — a superset relationship, checked component by component — or the request is `ERR_DISABILITY_COVERAGE_GAP`; in practice this means a product whose in-scope criteria protect vision, hearing, motor and cognitive users cannot be certified until every in-scope component has manual assistive-technology validation for all four. Every in-scope component's `design_review_state` must be `REVIEWED_PASS`, or the request is `ERR_DESIGN_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE`. The product must have at least one in-scope component, since an empty inventory cannot be certified, or the request is `ERR_NO_IN_SCOPE_COMPONENTS`. And the pinned evidence must be exactly as it was at approval: for every position in the pinned lists, the assessment must still exist, its current `version` must still equal the captured `pinned_assessment_versions` entry at that position, and its `attestation_state` must still be `ATTESTED`; any divergence is `ERR_PINNED_ASSESSMENT_CHANGED`.

Publication then does a great deal at once, and every one of these effects is part of the contract. The report moves `APPROVED → PUBLISHED`, its `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `conformance_index_certified` and `criterion_support_snapshot` are frozen from the certified rollup of §3.6. Any report for the same product and standard that was previously `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `superseded_by_report_id` set to the newly published report. Every known issue of the same product that is in state `COMMITTED` with a `publish_intent` of `INCLUDED` moves `COMMITTED → PUBLISHED_COMMITTED`, has its `public_target_date` set by copying its internal `target_remediation_date`, and has its `published_report_id` set to this report; the number of issues moved this way is recorded on the report as `published_issue_count`. Issues whose `publish_intent` is `EXCLUDED` are left untouched, which is how internally tracked work stays internal. The product's `overdue_committed_blocker_count` is then rebuilt from the newly published commitments and its `publication_posture` re-derived, so a publication that exposes a blocker whose target date has already passed flips the very product it just certified to `FROZEN`. If that posture transition happens, the questionnaire cascade of §3.8 follows in the same request. Finally the product's `version` rises by 1.

Withdrawal reverses the commitment. An `AccessibilityLead` supplies a `withdrawal_reason`, and the action is legal from `PUBLISHED` and from `APPROVED`; from anywhere else it is `ERR_REPORT_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`. Both branches land in `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and both record the `withdrawal_reason` — but their side effects are deliberately asymmetric. Withdrawing an `APPROVED` report that was never published records the reason and nothing else: no known issue changes state, no questionnaire is touched, no counter moves, because nothing had been exposed to customers. Withdrawing a `PUBLISHED` report compensates the entire publication cascade: every known issue whose `published_report_id` is this report and whose state is `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED` moves `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED → COMMITTED` with both `public_target_date` and `published_report_id` cleared to nothing; every questionnaire response citing this report as its `report_id` whose accuracy dimension is `CURRENT` or `STALE` flips to `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` with `correction_due_at` set 10 days after the logical clock, because answers citing a report that is no longer public are by definition answers that need correcting; and the product's `overdue_committed_blocker_count`, `correction_required_questionnaire_count` and `publication_posture` are all rebuilt. The product's `version` rises by 1.

### 3.8. Publication Posture: the Compound Gate and its Questionnaire Cascade

`publication_posture` is the single derived signal that decides how much the programme is allowed to say in public, and it is recomputed by the server rather than set by anyone. It takes one of three values. `FROZEN` means the product has a public commitment it has already broken or an answer it has admitted is wrong. `RESTRICTED` means the product's own running conformance figure is below the bar, or its customer answers are drifting out of date. `OPEN` means neither problem is present.

The derivation is a strict cascade over three counters, each of which is itself derived. `overdue_committed_blocker_count` counts the product's known issues of `BLOCKER` severity in state `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED` whose `public_target_date` is earlier than the logical clock's date. `correction_required_questionnaire_count` counts the product's questionnaire responses whose accuracy dimension is `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`. `stale_questionnaire_count` counts those whose accuracy dimension is `STALE`. The posture is then determined as follows. If `overdue_committed_blocker_count` is at least 1, or `correction_required_questionnaire_count` is at least 1, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if the product has at least one assessed criterion and its `conformance_index_provisional` is strictly below 85.00, or its `stale_questionnaire_count` is at least 2, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Note the guard on the index test: a product whose `assessed_criteria_count` is 0 is never `RESTRICTED` on index grounds, because a provisional index of 0.00 over an empty denominator says nothing about conformance — the index floor of 85.00 only bites once at least one criterion has been assessed. Note too that one stale questionnaire is tolerated and two are not.

Recomputation is triggered by every write that could move one of those inputs: attesting, authoring or superseding a criterion assessment; a criterion assessment flipping to `NEEDS_REVALIDATION`; completing or voiding an assessment run; any known-issue state change, including intake, triage, board review, commitment, publication-driven commitment and resolution; any questionnaire accuracy change originating from an API action; publishing or withdrawing a report; adding a component; and the explicit product-wide recomputation an `AccessibilityLead` can force after moving the logical clock.

Posture **transitions** carry cascades onto the product's questionnaire responses, and the cascades are one-directional. A transition into `RESTRICTED` from `OPEN` flips every questionnaire response of that product whose accuracy dimension is `CURRENT` to `STALE`, setting each one's `update_due_at` to 30 days after the logical clock. A transition into `FROZEN`, whether from `OPEN` or from `RESTRICTED`, flips every questionnaire response of that product whose accuracy dimension is `CURRENT` or `STALE` to `CORRECTION_REQUIRED`, setting each one's `correction_due_at` to 10 days after the logical clock and clearing its `update_due_at`. Recovery transitions carry no cascade at all: `RESTRICTED → OPEN`, `FROZEN → RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN → OPEN` leave every questionnaire response exactly where it is, so a response that has gone `STALE` or `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` stays there until someone revises or re-attests it. The asymmetry is intentional — a posture improving on paper is not evidence that a customer's answers were corrected. And because these cascade writes are performed *by* a posture transition, they do not themselves trigger a further posture recomputation within the same request.

Four operations are gated on posture, and two of them use different thresholds. Report publication, component design review and release-train budget allocation require only that the posture is not `FROZEN`, so they proceed while the posture is `RESTRICTED`; a `FROZEN` product rejects all three with `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Questionnaire accuracy attestation is stricter: it requires the posture to be exactly `OPEN`. A `FROZEN` product rejects attestation with `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and a `RESTRICTED` product rejects it with `ERR_PRODUCT_POSTURE_NOT_OPEN` — two different codes for two different degrees of the same problem, distinguished because attesting to a customer that your answers are accurate is a stronger claim than publishing a report that discloses its own gaps.

### 3.9. Known Issues: Intake, Risk Scoring, Release Trains and the Remediation Budget

Known issues are the honest part of an accessibility report — the part customers actually read. Either an `AccessibilityTester` or an `AccessibilityLead` may log one, naming the `product_id` affected, the `component_id` where the defect lives, the `criterion_id` it violates, the `severity`, a customer-facing `summary` of 10 to 1000 characters, an `effort_points` estimate from 1 to 100, the internal `target_remediation_date` as a calendar date, and the `publish_intent` deciding whether this issue is disclosed to customers with the report. The component must belong to the named product and the criterion must belong to that product's standard, or the request is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCT_REFERENCE` and `ERR_CRITERION_STANDARD_MISMATCH` respectively. A new issue starts in state `OPEN`, with `release_train_id`, `public_target_date`, `published_report_id`, `resolution_run_id`, `triage_note`, `board_review_note` and `resolution_note` all empty, `funded_cents` at 0, `funding_state` at `UNFUNDED`, `linked_complaint_count` at 0, `risk_variant` at `INTAKE_ESTIMATE`, and a `risk_score` computed immediately under that variant. Logging an issue also rebuilds the product's `publication_posture` and the `disclosure_debt` of every `SUBMITTED` or `ATTESTED` questionnaire response of that product, and raises the product's `version` by 1. Issues can be read individually or listed with optional `product_id`, `component_id`, `criterion_id`, `release_train_id`, `resolution_run_id`, `published_report_id` and `state` filters.

**Risk scoring.** Every issue carries a `risk_score` produced by one of two variants, and `risk_variant` records which one produced the current value. Both share a base term built from three factors. The severity contributes fixed points: 40 for `BLOCKER`, 25 for `SERIOUS`, 12 for `MODERATE` and 5 for `MINOR`. The breadth factor counts how many distinct disability categories the violated criterion protects — a number from 1 to 4, read from that criterion's `disability_categories` — and equals 1 plus fifteen hundredths for each category beyond the first, so a criterion protecting all four categories carries a breadth factor of 1.45. The reach factor equals one half plus one half multiplied by the affected component's `user_reach_pct` divided by 100, so a component nobody touches still carries half weight and a component every user touches carries full weight. The base term is the severity points multiplied by the breadth factor multiplied by the reach factor.

Under the `INTAKE_ESTIMATE` variant — used when the issue is first logged and again at triage, before the prioritization board has looked at it — the `risk_score` is simply that base term rounded half-up to two decimal places, with no aging, no complaint amplification and no ceiling. Under the `BOARD_REVIEW` variant — used at prioritization board review, and again whenever a complaint is linked to an issue already carrying that variant — two multipliers apply. The aging multiplier is 1 plus two hundredths for each whole day by which the logical clock's date has passed the issue's `target_remediation_date`, counting no days at all when the target date is still in the future, and counting no more than 60 days however late the issue is; an issue two months past target therefore carries an aging multiplier of 2.2 at most. The complaint multiplier is 1 plus one tenth for each complaint linked to the issue, counted from `linked_complaint_count` and capped at 5 complaints, so the amplification never exceeds 1.5. The board-review score is the base term multiplied by the aging multiplier multiplied by the complaint multiplier, clamped so it can never exceed 100.00, and only then rounded half-up to two decimal places. The base term is never pre-rounded: exactly one rounding happens, on the final clamped product. An issue's state is irrelevant to the arithmetic, and issues that have come to rest as `DEFERRED` or `RESOLVED` simply keep whatever score was last computed for them.

**Triage and prioritization.** Triage is an `AccessibilityLead` action legal only from `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_OPEN` — carrying a mandatory `triage_note` and an optional `severity` re-grade. It moves the issue `OPEN → TRIAGED`, stamps a triage instant from the logical clock, recomputes `risk_score` under the `INTAKE_ESTIMATE` variant (picking up the re-graded severity when one was supplied), and rebuilds questionnaire disclosure debt and the product's `publication_posture`. Board review is legal only from `TRIAGED` — otherwise `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_TRIAGED` — and carries a `board_review_note` plus the boolean `defer`. Either way it recomputes `risk_score` under the `BOARD_REVIEW` variant, sets `risk_variant` to `BOARD_REVIEW`, stamps a board-review instant, and rebuilds questionnaire disclosure debt and the product's posture. When `defer` is true the issue moves `TRIAGED → DEFERRED`, which is terminal and only reachable while the issue is not committed to any release train; a deferred issue also leaves the disclosure-debt input set entirely, which is the one way a company can stop paying disclosure debt on work it has decided not to do. When `defer` is false the issue stays in `TRIAGED` — the transition is a self-loop that exists purely to switch the scoring variant — and remains committable.

**Release trains.** A release train is the remediation roadmap's unit of delivery, created by an `AccessibilityLead` with a globally unique `name` of 1 to 120 characters (a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRAIN_NAME`), a closed delivery window from `window_start` to `window_end` inclusive where `window_end` is on or after `window_start`, optional trailing `freeze_days` between 0 and 60 which must be fewer than the number of days in the window and default to 0, an `effort_capacity_points` figure from 1 to 1000, and a `remediation_budget_cents` figure from 0 to 100000000. Trains must be genuinely separated in time: a new train's closed window must neither overlap an existing train's closed window nor merely touch it — at least one full calendar day of gap is required on both sides — and a violation is `ERR_TRAIN_WINDOW_ADJACENT`. A new train reports `committed_effort_points` of 0, `unallocated_cents` equal to its whole `remediation_budget_cents`, and `allocation_state` of `UNALLOCATED`. Thereafter `committed_effort_points` always equals the sum of `effort_points` over the train's issues in states `COMMITTED` and `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED`, and `unallocated_cents` always equals `remediation_budget_cents` minus the sum of `funded_cents` over the train's issues.

Committing an issue to a train is legal only from `TRIAGED`, carries the `release_train_id` of the chosen train, and is guarded by two rules. The issue's `target_remediation_date` must land inside the train's **usable** window, which begins at `window_start` and ends at `window_end` minus the train's `freeze_days`, both endpoints included — a target date on the last usable day is accepted, a target date one day later falls inside the code freeze and is rejected with `ERR_TARGET_DATE_OUTSIDE_TRAIN_WINDOW`. And the train must have room: the train's current `committed_effort_points` plus this issue's `effort_points` must not exceed its `effort_capacity_points`, or the request is `ERR_TRAIN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; landing exactly on capacity is allowed. On success the issue moves `TRIAGED → COMMITTED`, its `release_train_id` is recorded, the train's `committed_effort_points` rises by the issue's `effort_points`, and the product's `publication_posture` is rebuilt.

**Budget allocation.** An `AccessibilityLead` allocates a train's remediation budget for one product at a time, naming the `product_id`. The train must not have allocated already — a second attempt is `ERR_TRAIN_BUDGET_ALREADY_ALLOCATED` — the product must not be `FROZEN`, and there must be at least one candidate issue or the request is `ERR_NO_FUNDABLE_ISSUES`. The candidate set is precisely the known issues whose `release_train_id` is this train, whose `product_id` is the requested product, and whose state is `COMMITTED` or `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED`; issues in `OPEN`, `TRIAGED`, `DEFERRED` or `RESOLVED` are excluded even if they still carry a `release_train_id`. Candidates are then ordered into a strict total order: by `risk_score` descending, then by `target_remediation_date` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending.

The allocation is a strict waterfall over that order, starting from a remaining balance equal to the train's full `remediation_budget_cents`. Each candidate's ask is its `effort_points` multiplied by the standard remediation rate of 187 dollars 50 cents per effort point — that is, 18750 cents per point — multiplied by the programme overhead factor of 1.0725, which is a 7.25 percent uplift; that product is rounded half-up to whole cents **for that issue** before anything is spent. The issue is then granted the smaller of the remaining balance and its rounded ask, and that grant becomes its `funded_cents`. Its `funding_state` becomes `FULLY_FUNDED` when the grant equals the rounded ask and is greater than zero, `PARTIALLY_FUNDED` when the grant is greater than zero but short of the ask, and `UNFUNDED` when the grant is zero. The remaining balance drops by the grant, and the next candidate is served from what is left. When the waterfall finishes, the train's `unallocated_cents` becomes the untouched remainder and its `allocation_state` becomes `ALLOCATED`. Rounding each ask before spending, rather than summing raw asks and rounding once, is normative: the two orders produce different splits for the issue that sits on the exhaustion boundary, and only the per-issue order is correct.

Reversing an allocation requires the train to be `ALLOCATED` — otherwise `ERR_TRAIN_BUDGET_NOT_ALLOCATED` — and carries the `product_id` whose allocation is being reversed together with a `reason`. Every affected issue's `funded_cents` returns to 0 and its `funding_state` returns to `UNFUNDED`, the train's `unallocated_cents` rises by exactly the reclaimed total, and `allocation_state` returns to `UNALLOCATED`. The invariant that the train's `remediation_budget_cents` equals its `unallocated_cents` plus the sum of its issues' `funded_cents` holds before, during and after both operations.

**Resolution.** An `AccessibilityTester` resolves an issue by supplying a `resolution_note` and the `resolution_run_id` of the run that verified the fix. Resolution is legal from `COMMITTED` and from `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED`, both landing in `RESOLVED`, which is terminal; from any other state it is `ERR_ISSUE_NOT_RESOLVABLE`. The cited run must have `status` `COMPLETED` and must belong to the same component as the issue, or the request is `ERR_RESOLUTION_EVIDENCE_INVALID` — a passing run on a neighbouring component proves nothing about this defect. On success the `resolution_note` and `resolution_run_id` are recorded, a resolution instant is stamped from the logical clock, and the compensating financial effects run: the train's `committed_effort_points` drops by the issue's `effort_points`, the train's `unallocated_cents` rises by the issue's `funded_cents`, and the issue's `funded_cents` returns to 0 with `funding_state` returning to `UNFUNDED`. Questionnaire disclosure debt, the product's `overdue_committed_blocker_count` and its `publication_posture` are then rebuilt; because the counter only looks at issues still in `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED`, resolving an overdue published blocker is one of the ways a `FROZEN` product recovers. The two resolution branches differ in exactly one respect: an issue resolved out of `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED` keeps its `public_target_date` as an audit trace of what was promised to customers, while an issue resolved out of `COMMITTED` never had one.

### 3.10. Procurement Questionnaires: Accuracy Obligations and Disclosure Debt

Customers do not read reports so much as they send questionnaires. A `CustomerLiaison` drafts a response by naming the `product_id` it covers, the `report_id` of the conformance report the answers cite, the `customer_name` of the requesting customer or procurement body (1 to 160 characters), the `questionnaire_kind` describing the questionnaire flavour, and the narrative `answers_summary` of 10 to 4000 characters submitted to the customer. The cited report must belong to the named product, or the request is `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCT_REFERENCE`. A new response starts with `review_status` at `DRAFT` and `accuracy_status` at `CURRENT`, a `disclosure_debt` of 0.00, a `disclosure_tier` of `STANDARD`, a `revision_count` of 0, and no `submitted_at`, `last_attested_at`, `update_due_at` or `correction_due_at`. Responses can be read individually or listed with optional `product_id`, `report_id`, `review_status` and `accuracy_status` filters.

Submission takes no payload and is legal only from `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_DRAFT`. It moves `review_status` `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and computes `disclosure_debt` and `disclosure_tier` for the first time. Accuracy attestation is the `AccessibilityLead`'s act of standing behind the answers, carrying an `accuracy_attestation` statement; it is legal only from `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_SUBMITTED` — and additionally requires that the cited report's `state` is `PUBLISHED`, or the request is `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PUBLISHED`, and that the product's posture is exactly `OPEN` as described in §3.8. On success `review_status` moves `SUBMITTED → ATTESTED`, `last_attested_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the accuracy dimension is set to `CURRENT`, any `correction_due_at` is cleared, and the update cadence restarts: `update_due_at` is set to 90 days after `last_attested_at`, which is the quarterly refresh obligation the company takes on when it attests. `disclosure_debt` and `disclosure_tier` are recomputed from the new reference instant, and the product's `stale_questionnaire_count`, `correction_required_questionnaire_count` and `publication_posture` are rebuilt.

Revision is the `CustomerLiaison`'s corrective path, carrying a replacement `answers_summary`. It is legal from `SUBMITTED` and from `ATTESTED`, both landing in `SUBMITTED` — a `DRAFT` response cannot be revised, only submitted, and the attempt is `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_REVISABLE`. A revision replaces `answers_summary`, raises `revision_count` by 1, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, returns the accuracy dimension to `CURRENT`, and clears both `update_due_at` and `correction_due_at`. Because clearing `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` lowers the product's `correction_required_questionnaire_count`, a revision is the path by which a `FROZEN` product can return to `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN`; `disclosure_debt`, `disclosure_tier` and the product's `publication_posture` are all rebuilt as part of it.

The accuracy dimension moves on four kinds of event. A response that is `CURRENT` becomes `STALE` when the product's posture transitions into `RESTRICTED`, and also when an `AccessibilityLead` forces a product recomputation and finds the response `CURRENT` with an `update_due_at` deadline that the logical clock has already passed — the update cadence has lapsed. In both cases `update_due_at` is reset to 30 days after the clock and the product's `stale_questionnaire_count` is rebuilt. A response that is `CURRENT` or `STALE` becomes `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` when the product's posture transitions into `FROZEN`, and when the report it cites is withdrawn; in both cases `correction_due_at` is set to 10 days after the clock, `update_due_at` is cleared, and the product's counters are rebuilt. Recovery runs only through explicit action: a `STALE` response returns to `CURRENT` on either a revision or an attestation, and a `CORRECTION_REQUIRED` response returns to `CURRENT` on a revision, with the relevant deadlines cleared and the product's counters and posture rebuilt.

**Disclosure debt.** `disclosure_debt` measures how much unresolved accessibility exposure the company is asking a customer to accept, and it is computed in four pinned steps that must be evaluated exactly once in this order. First, the base debt is a sum over the product's known issues whose `publish_intent` is `INCLUDED` and whose state is `OPEN`, `TRIAGED`, `COMMITTED` or `PUBLISHED_COMMITTED`; issues that are `DEFERRED` or `RESOLVED`, and issues marked `EXCLUDED`, contribute nothing whatsoever. Each contributing issue adds 8 points for `BLOCKER` severity, 5 for `SERIOUS`, 3 for `MODERATE` and 1 for `MINOR`, plus a further overdue surcharge of 2 points when that issue's `target_remediation_date` is earlier than the logical clock's date. Second, the tier is derived from that base debt **and from nothing else**: `STANDARD` when the base debt is 6 or less, `ELEVATED` when it is greater than 6 but 12 or less, and `CRITICAL` when it is greater than 12. Third, the staleness penalty is computed against a reference instant, which is `last_attested_at` when the response has ever been attested and the response's own `created_at` otherwise: count the whole days from that reference instant to the logical clock, divide by 30 discarding any remainder to obtain a bucket number, cap that bucket at 4, and multiply it by the tier's penalty rate — half a point per bucket at `STANDARD`, one point per bucket at `ELEVATED`, two points per bucket at `CRITICAL`. Fourth, `disclosure_debt` is the base debt plus that penalty, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and `disclosure_tier` is the tier from the second step. The apparent circularity — a penalty that depends on a tier that depends on a debt figure — is resolved by keying the tier on the base debt only; recomputing the tier from the final `disclosure_debt`, or iterating the two to a fixed point, is forbidden and produces wrong answers.

### 3.11. Complaint Intake, Routing, Accommodation and Closure

Complaints and accommodation requests arrive through the same door and then fork. A `CustomerLiaison` records intake with the `product_id` complained about, optionally the `component_id` and optionally the `criterion_id` the complaint maps to, the intake `channel`, the complainant's narrative `description` of 10 to 2000 characters, and the `disability_category` the complainant reports. Both optional references are checked when supplied: a named component must belong to the named product, or the intake is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCT_REFERENCE`, and a named criterion must belong to that product's selected standard, or the intake is rejected with `ERR_CRITERION_STANDARD_MISMATCH`. A new complaint is in state `NEW` with no `route` yet assigned, and no `linked_issue_id`, `requested_accommodation`, `needed_by_date`, `accommodation_fulfilled_at`, `closure_outcome` or `closed_at`. Complaints can be read individually or listed with optional `product_id`, `component_id`, `criterion_id`, `linked_issue_id`, `state` and `route` filters.

Triage is where the fork happens, and it belongs to the `AccessibilityLead`. It is legal only from `NEW` — otherwise `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_NEW` — and carries the chosen `route` of either `REMEDIATION` or `ACCOMMODATION`. On the `ACCOMMODATION` route two further fields become mandatory: the `requested_accommodation` describing what the complainant needs, between 10 and 1000 characters, and the `needed_by_date` naming the calendar date by which it is needed. That date must not be in the past — it must fall on or after the logical clock's current date — or the request is rejected with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_DATE_IN_PAST`. Successful triage moves the complaint `NEW → TRIAGED`, records the `route`, persists the accommodation fields on that branch, and stamps a triage instant from the logical clock.

The `REMEDIATION` branch continues by linking the complaint to a known issue, naming the `known_issue_id`. Linking requires the complaint to be `TRIAGED` — otherwise `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_TRIAGED` — requires its `route` to be `REMEDIATION`, since an accommodation request has no defect to link and the attempt is `ERR_COMPLAINT_ROUTE_MISMATCH`, and requires the named issue to belong to the same product as the complaint, or the request is `ERR_CROSS_PRODUCT_REFERENCE`. On success the complaint moves `TRIAGED → LINKED`, its `linked_issue_id` is recorded, the issue's `linked_complaint_count` rises by 1, and the issue's `risk_score` is recomputed under whichever `risk_variant` it currently carries — which is how customer pressure feeds back into prioritization, since the `BOARD_REVIEW` variant amplifies by the complaint count while the `INTAKE_ESTIMATE` variant ignores it. The product's `publication_posture` is rebuilt as well.

The `ACCOMMODATION` branch continues with fulfilment, recorded by a `CustomerLiaison` with no payload. It likewise requires the complaint to be `TRIAGED`, and requires its `route` to be `ACCOMMODATION` — attempting it on a remediation-routed complaint is `ERR_COMPLAINT_ROUTE_MISMATCH`. On success the complaint moves `TRIAGED → ACCOMMODATED`, `accommodation_fulfilled_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the product's `accommodations_fulfilled_count` is rebuilt.

Both branches join at closure, performed by an `AccessibilityLead` who supplies the `closure_outcome`. Closure is legal from `LINKED` and from `ACCOMMODATED`, both landing in `CLOSED`, which is terminal; from anywhere else it is `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_CLOSABLE`. The branches are symmetric in structure and different in substance. Closing from `LINKED` requires that the linked known issue has come to rest — its state must be `RESOLVED` or `DEFERRED`, or the request is `ERR_LINKED_ISSUE_UNRESOLVED` — and the `closure_outcome` must be either `REMEDIATION_SCHEDULED` or `NOT_A_DEFECT`; anything else is `ERR_COMPLAINT_ROUTE_MISMATCH`. Closing from `ACCOMMODATED` imposes no cross-entity condition at all, and its `closure_outcome` must be either `ACCOMMODATION_PROVIDED` or `ACCOMMODATION_DENIED`, with the same mismatch code guarding the branch. In both cases the `closure_outcome` and a `closed_at` instant from the logical clock are recorded; on the accommodation branch no known issue is touched, and on the remediation branch the linked issue keeps whatever state it had reached.
