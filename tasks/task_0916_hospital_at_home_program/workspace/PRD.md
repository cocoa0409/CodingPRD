# Hospital-At-Home Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Acute inpatient care delivered inside a patient's own home is now a reimbursable model of care, but it is also one of the most tightly audited. A hospital-at-home program takes a patient who would otherwise occupy a ward bed, admits them to a virtual ward, and then must prove — day by day, visit by visit, telemetry minute by telemetry minute — that the care actually delivered met the standard the payer bought. Everything that a physical ward gets for free (a nurse walking past the door, a monitor wired into the wall, a pharmacy two floors down, a bed that is simply *there* if the patient deteriorates) has to be manufactured logistically and then evidenced in a record that survives an audit years later.

This document specifies the backend service that runs such a program end to end. It covers the admission funnel (referral intake, the eligibility gauntlet, the in-person physician evaluation window), the operational machinery of the virtual ward (remote-monitoring device kits and their component-level chain of custody, nurse and paramedic in-person visits, physician telehealth rounding, telemetry gap accounting, medication and durable-medical-equipment delivery, meal provision), the clinical safety net (a four-tier escalation ladder with acknowledgement service-level agreements, a held inpatient bed, conversion of an escalation into a physical bed return, and readmission back into the home program), and the revenue cycle that closes the loop (per-service-date compliance scoring, claim construction from compliant days only, payer outcomes, and a capped appeal loop).

Two design commitments run through every section that follows. First, **the calendar day is the unit of truth**: each served UTC calendar date of an episode owns exactly one compliance record, that record is scored against the episode's billing track, and only days that score `COMPLIANT` ever become money. Second, **nothing is decided by wall-clock accident**: the service runs on an explicit logical clock that tests and operators drive, so a service-level agreement breach, a bed-hold expiry, and a date roll are all deterministic and reproducible. The remainder of this document defines the global contracts (§2), the business workflows that scatter the program's rules across the clinical and financial narrative (§3), the HTTP surface (§4), and the complete error vocabulary (§5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Care-Team Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other evaluation takes place. When both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint (see the Authorized Role(s) column of the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `AdmittingPhysician`, `FieldNurse`, `CommunityParamedic`, `PharmacyAuthorizer`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `BillingAnalyst`, and `Patient`. Their remits are as follows:

- **`ProgramAdmin`** is the operations lead. This role alone drives the logical clock, adds monitoring kits and their components to inventory, reads the program-wide kit census, and registers inpatient units. It shares patient registration and patient updates, telemetry-lapse entry, compliance-day finalization, escalation opening and escalation promotion with other roles, and it may perform any read in the system.
- **`AdmittingPhysician`** is the attending clinician. This role alone files referrals, marks a referral eligible, admits a patient to the program, signs medication reconciliation, begins and completes discharge, readmits a returned episode, holds an inpatient bed, and converts an escalation into a bed return. It shares patient registration and update, eligibility-assessment recording, visit scheduling and all four visit actions, delivery-order raising, compliance-day finalization, escalation acknowledgement and escalation resolution, and it may perform any read.
- **`FieldNurse`** is the registered nurse who works the home. This role alone deploys a device kit to an episode and reconciles a returned component. It shares eligibility-assessment recording, visit scheduling and all four visit actions, telemetry-lapse entry, escalation opening, acknowledgement, promotion and resolution, and reads over patients, referrals, episodes, visits, kits, components, lapses and escalations.
- **`CommunityParamedic`** performs in-person visits, completes or refuses delivery handoffs at the door, and answers escalations. It holds no exclusive operation; it shares visit scheduling and the four visit actions, delivery completion and refusal, escalation opening, acknowledgement and resolution, and reads over patients, episodes, visits, delivery orders and escalations.
- **`PharmacyAuthorizer`** exists to be the second pair of eyes on medication. This role alone authorizes a delivery order, and otherwise may only read a single episode and that episode's delivery orders.
- **`LogisticsCoordinator`** runs the supply chain. This role alone dispatches a delivery order and opens a meal plan. It shares delivery-order raising, delivery completion and refusal, and reads over patients, episodes, delivery orders and meal plans.
- **`BillingAnalyst`** owns the revenue cycle. This role alone builds a claim, submits it, records the payer outcome and files an appeal. It shares reads over the episode list, a single episode, the episode ledger, compliance days, visits, meal plans and billing claims.
- **`Patient`** is the admitted patient reading their own record, limited to their own patient record, their own referral list, an episode and that episode's visits.

Seven of the eight roles carry an **opaque** identity: when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `AdmittingPhysician`, `FieldNurse`, `CommunityParamedic`, `PharmacyAuthorizer`, `LogisticsCoordinator` or `BillingAnalyst`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for provenance — as `med_rec_signed_by_user_id` on an episode, as `attested_by_user_id` on a visit, as `authorized_by_user_id` on a delivery order, as `acknowledged_by_user_id` on an escalation, as `reconciled_by_user_id` on a device component, and as the `opened_by_user_id` of the escalation raised automatically when a delivery is abandoned — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. The eighth role is different: when `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient` record, and ownership checks on any resource that references a patient compare that resource's patient foreign key to `X-User-ID`.

One safety rule is expressed purely in terms of these opaque identifiers rather than roles, and it is described in full in §3.6: a controlled delivery order may never be authorized by the same `X-User-ID` that requested it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100; a supplied value below 1 or above 100 is a bound violation rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with negative values likewise rejected as `ERR_VALIDATION`. Both are echoed back in the `meta` object of the response alongside `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a specific list is documented otherwise, **every list endpoint sorts by `created_at` ascending**, and where two rows carry the same `created_at`, insertion order breaks the tie so that pagination is stable. Filter parameters supported by an individual list (for example `status`, `service_date`, `scheduling_status`, `reconciliation_status`, `patient_id`, `referral_id`, `episode_id` or `unit_id`) are applied before the sort and before the page window, so `total_count` always reflects the filtered set rather than the whole table.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Timestamps and the Program Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in canonical UUID form. Identifiers never carry a type prefix — an episode's `id` and a visit's `id` are indistinguishable in shape, and clients must never attempt to parse meaning out of one. Clients never supply an `id`; any client-supplied identifier field in a request body is a foreign key pointing at an already-existing row, never a request to choose a new primary key.

Fourteen of the fifteen resource types also carry `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. Both timestamps are rendered as ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional-second digits. `created_at` is stamped once, at creation, and never changes. `updated_at` equals `created_at` at creation and is refreshed to the current logical-clock value on every subsequent mutation of that row. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the caller did not directly request, such as a bed census shifting because a sibling reservation expired, or an episode's day-ledger counters being recomputed after a compliance day flips.

The single exception is the telemetry-gap record. A `MonitoringLapse` is append-only: once written it is never edited, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field, and neither should appear in its response payload.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right. In a nested read such as the compliance-day list of an episode, the episode identifier is resolved first; if it does not exist the response is `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND` and the rest of the path is never examined. The error identifier chosen at this step depends on the resource type of the segment that failed: a patient segment yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, a referral segment yields `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, an episode segment yields `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, and every other resource type — visit, compliance day, device kit, device component, delivery order, meal plan, escalation event, inpatient unit, bed reservation, billing claim — yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All four carry HTTP 404.

**The logical clock.** This service does not read the host's wall clock for any business decision. It maintains a single logical UTC instant which, before any operator has touched it, reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. A `ProgramAdmin` moves it by calling `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` with a body carrying `to_iso8601`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant the clock should now report; the response body returns `current_utc`, the clock value now in force, and the call answers HTTP 200. Every subsequent timestamp stamped by the system (`admitted_at`, `returned_at`, `discharged_at`, `deployed_at`, `finalized_at`, `held_at`, `occupied_at`, `tier_entered_at`, `updated_at`), every acknowledgement-deadline comparison, every bed-hold expiry evaluation and every "today's UTC date" derivation reads this logical clock and nothing else.

### 2.5. The Rejection Ladder: How Competing Failures Are Ranked

A single request into this system routinely violates more than one rule at once — a claim build can be simultaneously unauthorized, aimed at a missing episode, carrying a stale version and standing on an episode with no billable days. The response must nonetheless carry exactly one `error_id`, and which one it carries is not a matter of implementation convenience. The program defines **sixteen rungs**, evaluated strictly in the order below. Evaluation stops at the first rung that reports a violation, and the `error_id` of that rung's violated rule is what the client sees. Within a rung the listed rules are evaluated in the order given.

1. **Rung 1 — Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and `X-User-Role` names one of the eight declared roles. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint in the system.
2. **Rung 2 — Authorization.** The caller's `X-User-Role` appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list. Violation: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint in the system.
3. **Rung 3 — Path resource existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to a live row, checked left to right. Violation: `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` per §2.4, all at HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint that carries a path identifier.
4. **Rung 4 — Request body schema.** Three checks, in this order. (4a) Every field the endpoint declares required is present and not null — violation `ERR_VALIDATION` (400), applying to every POST, PUT and PATCH endpoint. (4b) Every enum-typed field carries a value inside its declared value set — violation `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (422), applying to every POST, PUT and PATCH endpoint that accepts an enum input. (4c) Numeric, format and ordering bounds hold — violation `ERR_VALIDATION` (400), applying to every endpoint that accepts the named fields. The bounds are: `home_distance_km` at least 0; `weight_kg` at least 0; `distance_km` at least 0; `replacement_cost_usd` at least 0; `total_beds` at least 1; `daily_delivery_count` at least 1 and at most 3, both bounds inclusive; `excursion_minutes` at least 0; `scheduled_end_at` strictly later than `scheduled_start_at`; `gap_end_at` strictly later than `gap_start_at`; `service_date` exactly equal to the UTC calendar-date portion of `scheduled_start_at`; `limit` at least 1 and at most 100 inclusive; `offset` at least 0; and every datetime supplied anywhere parses as an ISO 8601 UTC instant.
5. **Rung 5 — Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key supplied in a request body resolves: `patient_id` yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `referral_id` yields `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `episode_id` yields `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, and `unit_id` or `kit_id` yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; all at HTTP 404. This rung guards `POST /referrals`, `POST /eligibility-assessments`, `POST /episodes`, `POST /visits`, `POST /monitoring-lapses`, `POST /delivery-orders`, `POST /meal-plans`, `POST /escalation-events`, `POST /bed-reservations`, `POST /billing-claims` and `POST /device-kits/{id}/deploy`.
6. **Rung 6 — Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_kit_version`, `expected_unit_version` or `expected_episode_version` equals the current `version` of the named parent row. Violation: `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). This rung guards exactly three endpoints: `POST /device-kits/{id}/deploy`, `POST /bed-reservations` and `POST /billing-claims`.
7. **Rung 7 — Target state precondition.** The target row's current state is one of the states the requested transition may legally leave from. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every state-transition endpoint. Cap-guarded edges are deliberately *not* handled here: a redelivery attempt on an order that has already used both redeliveries, and an appeal on a claim that has already used both appeals, are legal `from:` states and are rejected further down at rung 14 instead.
8. **Rung 8 — Parent episode state gate.** For operations that write into a live episode, the parent episode's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Violation: `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). This rung guards exactly nine endpoints: `POST /visits`, `POST /visits/{id}/depart`, `POST /visits/{id}/complete`, `POST /device-kits/{id}/deploy`, `POST /delivery-orders`, `POST /delivery-orders/{id}/authorize`, `POST /delivery-orders/{id}/dispatch`, `POST /meal-plans` and `POST /monitoring-lapses`. No other endpoint carries an episode-must-be-active gate — in particular, holding a bed does not, because a bed is most often needed precisely when the episode has left `ACTIVE`.
9. **Rung 9 — Admission eligibility gauntlet.** Eight rules, evaluated in this exact sub-order, described in full in §3.1: referral origin (`ERR_REFERRAL_ORIGIN_INELIGIBLE`, 422), qualifying condition (`ERR_CONDITION_NOT_QUALIFYING`, 422), service radius (`ERR_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_RADIUS`, 422), physician-evaluation window (`ERR_EVAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, 422), home safety (`ERR_HOME_SAFETY_FAILED`, 422), connectivity (`ERR_CONNECTIVITY_INSUFFICIENT`, 422), caregiver presence (`ERR_CAREGIVER_REQUIRED`, 422) and consent (`ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`, 422). Seven of the eight — every one except the physician-evaluation window — guard `POST /referrals/{id}/mark-eligible`. Two of them guard `POST /episodes`: the service-radius rule, which is re-checked at admission, and the physician-evaluation window rule, which is checked *only* at admission and never at mark-eligible time. Because the service-radius rule sits above the evaluation-window rule in this sub-order, an admission attempt that violates both reports `ERR_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_RADIUS`.
10. **Rung 10 — Segregation of duties.** When a delivery order is flagged controlled, the authorizing caller's `X-User-ID` differs from the order's `requested_by_user_id`. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Guards `POST /delivery-orders/{id}/authorize`.
11. **Rung 11 — Clinical custody and safety.** Two rules, in this order. (11a) When the order is controlled, `witness_staff_id` is supplied and non-empty in the handoff body — violation `ERR_WITNESS_REQUIRED` (422). (11b) When the order requires cold chain, the supplied `excursion_minutes` is at or below 30 — violation `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_EXCURSION` (422). Both guard `POST /delivery-orders/{id}/deliver`, and because the witness rule is listed first, a controlled cold-chain handoff missing its witness *and* reporting a 45-minute excursion reports `ERR_WITNESS_REQUIRED`.
12. **Rung 12 — Temporal overlap.** The requested visit interval does not collide with another visit of the same `staff_user_id` once the turnaround buffer is applied. Violation: `ERR_VISIT_OVERLAP` (409). Guards `POST /visits`.
13. **Rung 13 — Referential compatibility.** A meal plan's `diet_code` is compatible with the patient's `diet_restriction`. Violation: `ERR_DIET_INCOMPATIBLE` (422). Guards `POST /meal-plans`.
14. **Rung 14 — Capacity and caps.** Four rules, in this order. (14a) The inpatient unit has at least one available bed after lazy expiry — violation `ERR_NO_BEDS_AVAILABLE` (409), guarding `POST /bed-reservations`. (14b) The target kit is `AVAILABLE` and the target episode has no other deployed kit — violation `ERR_KIT_UNAVAILABLE` (409), guarding `POST /device-kits/{id}/deploy`. (14c) A redelivery dispatch requires `redelivery_count` strictly below 2 — violation `ERR_REDELIVERY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409), guarding `POST /delivery-orders/{id}/dispatch`. (14d) An appeal requires `appeal_count` strictly below 2 — violation `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409), guarding `POST /billing-claims/{id}/appeal`.
15. **Rung 15 — Discharge reconciliation gate.** Three rules, in this order, all guarding `POST /episodes/{id}/discharge`. (15a) `med_rec_signed` is true — violation `ERR_MED_REC_UNSIGNED` (409). (15b) Every delivery order of the episode has reached a terminal status — violation `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_TERMINAL` (409). (15c) No component of the episode's most recently deployed kit is still outstanding — violation `ERR_RECONCILIATION_INCOMPLETE` (409). The ordering matters: an episode that is missing its medication reconciliation *and* has an in-flight delivery reports `ERR_MED_REC_UNSIGNED`, not `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_TERMINAL`.
16. **Rung 16 — Fiscal gate.** The episode's `billable_day_count` is at least 1. Violation: `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_DAYS` (422). Guards `POST /billing-claims`. Because this rung sits below the concurrency rung, a claim build carrying a stale `expected_episode_version` against an episode with zero billable days reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, not `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_DAYS`.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Inventory and Ledgers

Three write paths in this program contend for a resource whose state another actor may have moved underneath the caller, and all three are protected by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* row's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required**, non-optional element of the request body — omitting it is a rung-4 required-field violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` — and the check itself sits at rung 6 of the ladder, above every state, capacity and fiscal rule.

- **Deploying a kit.** A `POST /device-kits/{id}/deploy` request must carry `expected_kit_version` holding the `version` of the `DeviceKit` the client last observed. If the kit's current `version` differs — because another nurse deployed it, or because it was pulled into refurbishment — the deployment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. A kit legitimately serves at most one episode at a time, so refusing the stale write is what prevents the kit from being silently re-pointed at a different episode.
- **Holding a bed.** A `POST /bed-reservations` request must carry `expected_unit_version` holding the `version` of the target `InpatientUnit` the client last read. Every hold, expiry and release mutates that unit's bed census and therefore its `version`; a mismatch means the caller's picture of remaining capacity is out of date, and the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.
- **Building a claim.** A `POST /billing-claims` request must carry `expected_episode_version` holding the `version` of the target `Episode` the client last read. A newly built claim legitimately supersedes any prior claim still in `BUILT`, so the build has to be anchored to the exact day-ledger the client saw; if the episode's `version` has advanced — a compliance day flipped, a visit was attested, the episode was discharged — the build is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Callers must not send `expected_kit_version`, `expected_unit_version` or `expected_episode_version` anywhere else, and the server ignores them if they appear.

### 2.7. Money, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount this system stores or returns is a number carried to **two decimal places, rounded half up** — a value whose third decimal digit is exactly 5 rounds away from zero, upward in magnitude. That applies to `travel_payment_usd`, `travel_reimbursement_total_usd`, `handling_fee_usd`, `meal_credit_total_usd`, `reimbursement_amount_usd`, `gross_amount_usd`, `withhold_amount_usd`, `net_payable_usd` and `paid_amount_usd` alike.

Rounding **order**, however, is not uniform, and three places in this program pin it explicitly because two defensible orders give different answers:

- **Travel reimbursement rounds per visit and only then sums.** Each completed, attested visit's travel payment is rounded to two decimals on its own, and the episode total is the sum of those already-rounded per-visit amounts, rounded once more to two decimals. Summing the raw unrounded products and rounding a single time at the end is explicitly *not* the rule. Because every addend is already rounded, the order in which visits are summed cannot change the result.
- **The delivery handling fee rounds its weight component before the sum, and applies its floor before its cap.** The weight component is rounded to two decimals before it joins the other components; the raw total is then raised to the floor if it falls short, and only afterwards lowered to the cap if it overshoots; the final figure is rounded to two decimals. Floor-then-cap is fixed and not interchangeable with cap-then-floor.
- **The claim arithmetic keys its withhold rate on the gross amount, never on the net.** The four steps run in this pinned order and no other: first the gross amount, then the withhold rate selected by comparing that gross amount against the tier threshold, then the withhold amount, then the net payable. Each step is rounded to two decimals half up as it is produced.

Time is handled with equal care but different rules. Daily telemetry-gap totals are expressed in **whole minutes truncated downward**: a merged gap of 119 seconds contributes 1 minute, not 2. Escalation acknowledgement windows are expressed in **whole minutes rounded half up** after the acuity multiplier is applied, which is why a fifteen-minute tier window on a high-acuity episode becomes eight minutes rather than seven. Counts — service days, billable days, carved-out days, pending days, qualified visits, components, beds, redeliveries, appeals, promotions — are plain integers with no rounding involved at all.

### 2.8. Conservation Invariants Across the Program

Three quantities in this system must balance at all times, and every operation that moves one of them must move its counterpart in the same request so that the balance is never observably broken.

**The episode day ledger.** Every calendar service date of an episode owns exactly one compliance record, and every such record sits in exactly one of `PENDING`, `COMPLIANT` or `CARVED_OUT`. Therefore an episode's `service_day_count` always equals the sum of its `billable_day_count`, its `carved_out_day_count` and its `pending_day_count`. All four counters are recomputed together on every event that can move a record between those buckets, and are exposed on the episode resource and on the episode's dedicated ledger read alongside `reimbursement_amount_usd`, `meal_credit_total_usd` and `travel_reimbursement_total_usd`.

**The kit census.** Every monitoring kit ever created sits in exactly one of `AVAILABLE`, `DEPLOYED`, `IN_REFURB` or `RETIRED`. Kits are never deleted, so the program-wide census read returns `total_kits` equal to the sum of `available_count`, `deployed_count`, `in_refurb_count` and `retired_count`. Deployment, refurbishment and retirement move a kit between buckets; they never create or destroy one.

**The bed census.** An inpatient unit's `total_beds` is supplied at registration and is immutable thereafter; it always equals the sum of `available_bed_count`, `held_bed_count` and `occupied_bed_count`. Holding a bed moves one unit of capacity from available to held; occupying it moves that unit from held to occupied; releasing or expiring a hold moves it back to available. `held_bed_count` is the number of that unit's reservations in `HELD`, `occupied_bed_count` is the number in `OCCUPIED`, and `available_bed_count` is `total_beds` less those two counts.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Version Propagation

Several operations in this program are not single-row writes. They are cascades that reach across three, four or five entity types in one request, and the whole cascade either happens or does not — a client never observes a half-applied cascade. Four are large enough to be named here and are described in operational detail in §3:

- **The bed-return cascade.** Converting an escalation into a bed return moves the escalation to its terminal converted state, occupies the episode's earliest held bed, returns the episode to the facility, pulls the deployed monitoring kit into refurbishment, forces the current date's compliance record to carve out, and voids any claim still sitting in `BUILT`. Along the way it also suspends the episode's active meal plans and cancels its non-terminal delivery orders.
- **The attestation cascade.** Attesting a visit recomputes that service date's compliance record; a second attestation on the same visit amends it, which reopens an already-finalized compliance day back to `PENDING` and pulls the episode's day-ledger counters back with it.
- **The abandoned-delivery cascade.** Refusing an order that has exhausted both of its redeliveries fails the order permanently and opens a fresh tier-1 escalation on the same episode, which in turn raises the episode's open-escalation counter.
- **The discharge cascade.** Discharging an episode cancels every visit still open on the scheduling axis, finalizes every compliance day still pending, recomputes meal credits from that freshly finalized day set, and closes out the patient and referral records.

Whenever a cascade touches a row, that row's own `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock — this is true of the secondary rows the caller never named as well as the primary target. Version bumps are what make the concurrency rules in §2.6 bite: an episode whose day ledger shifted because a nurse attested a visit now carries a higher `version`, and a billing analyst holding the older number can no longer build a claim against it.

### 2.10. Controlled Vocabularies of the Program

Every enumerated value below is matched exactly, case-sensitively; anything outside the declared set is rejected at rung 4 with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

A patient's `primary_diagnosis_code` is one of `CHF_EXAC`, `COPD_EXAC`, `CAP_PNEUMONIA`, `CELLULITIS`, `UTI_COMPLICATED`, `COVID_MODERATE` or `OTHER`, and the same seven values are legal for a referral's `qualifying_condition_code`. A patient's `diet_restriction` and a meal plan's `diet_code` share the value set `NONE`, `LOW_SODIUM`, `RENAL`, `CARB_CONTROLLED`, `PUREED`. A patient's `status` is `REGISTERED`, `IN_PROGRAM` or `DISCHARGED`. A referral's `origin` is `ED`, `INPATIENT_WARD`, `CLINIC` or `SNF`; its `acuity`, and the episode `acuity` copied from it, is `STANDARD` or `HIGH`; and its `status` runs `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING`, `ELIGIBLE`, `ADMITTED`, `CLOSED`. An eligibility assessment's `home_safety_outcome` is `PASS`, `CONDITIONAL` or `FAIL`, while its `broadband_check` is only `PASS` or `FAIL`.

An episode's `program_track` is `CMS_WAIVER` or `FEE_FOR_SERVICE` and its `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `ESCALATED`, `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY`, `DISCHARGE_PENDING`, `DISCHARGED`. A visit's `visit_type` is `RN_IN_PERSON`, `PARAMEDIC_IN_PERSON`, `PHYSICIAN_IN_PERSON` or `PHYSICIAN_TELEHEALTH`; its `scheduling_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`; its `attestation_status` is `UNATTESTED`, `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED`. A compliance record's `status` is `PENDING`, `COMPLIANT` or `CARVED_OUT`, and when it is carved out its `carve_out_reason` is one of `INSUFFICIENT_IN_PERSON_VISITS`, `MISSING_PHYSICIAN_EVAL`, `MONITORING_LAPSE_EXCEEDED` or `KIT_NOT_DEPLOYED`.

A device kit's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, `DEPLOYED`, `IN_REFURB` or `RETIRED`. A component's `component_type` is `TABLET`, `BP_CUFF`, `PULSE_OX`, `SCALE`, `THERMOMETER` or `GATEWAY`, and its `reconciliation_status` is `IN_STOCK`, `DEPLOYED`, `PENDING_RECONCILIATION`, `RETURNED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. A telemetry gap's `cause` is `DEVICE_OFFLINE`, `CONNECTIVITY_LOSS`, `PATIENT_REMOVED` or `UNKNOWN`. A delivery order's `order_kind` is `MEDICATION` or `DME` and its `status` is one of `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, `AUTHORIZED`, `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`, `DELIVERED`, `REFUSED`, `FAILED`, `CANCELLED`. A meal plan's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. An escalation's `reason` is `VITALS_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `PATIENT_REQUEST`, `TELEMETRY_LOSS`, `MEDICATION_FAILURE` or `FALL`, and its `status` walks the ten-value ladder `TIER1_OPEN`, `TIER1_ACKED`, `TIER2_OPEN`, `TIER2_ACKED`, `TIER3_OPEN`, `TIER3_ACKED`, `TIER4_OPEN`, `TIER4_ACKED`, `RESOLVED`, `BED_RETURN_CONVERTED`. A bed reservation's `status` is `HELD`, `OCCUPIED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED`. A billing claim's `status` is `BUILT`, `SUBMITTED`, `PAID`, `DENIED`, `APPEALED`, `CLOSED` or `VOIDED`.

Three action endpoints take an `outcome` field whose accepted values are narrower than the target field's full vocabulary. Closing an unperformed visit accepts exactly `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`. Reconciling a component accepts exactly `RETURNED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. Recording a payer outcome accepts exactly `PAID`, `DENIED` or `CLOSED`. Any other string in any of the three is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. From Referral to Eligibility: the Admission Gauntlet

Before anyone can be admitted to a virtual ward, they must exist in the program's records. Registering a patient captures the medical record number `mrn`, which must be non-empty and unique across all patients, together with the patient's legal `full_name` and `home_distance_km` — the road distance in kilometres from the program hub to the front door, which must be at least 0 and is the number the service-radius rule later interrogates. The working `primary_diagnosis_code` is captured at the same time. Three further attributes are optional at registration and take documented defaults if omitted: `diet_restriction` defaults to `NONE`, `broadband_verified` defaults to `false`, and `caregiver_present` defaults to `false`. The new record's `status` is initialized by the system to `REGISTERED`; callers never supply it. Because home circumstances change between the first phone call and the eligibility visit, a `ProgramAdmin` or `AdmittingPhysician` may later revise `home_distance_km`, `diet_restriction`, `broadband_verified` and `caregiver_present` on an existing patient — each such revision advances that patient's `version` by 1 and refreshes `updated_at`. `mrn`, `full_name` and `primary_diagnosis_code` are not revisable through that path.

A referral is the physician's formal request to take that patient home. Filing one requires the `patient_id` of the registered patient, the `origin` describing where the patient is being pulled from, the `qualifying_condition_code` being claimed as the reason for home hospitalization, the clinical `acuity` band, and `referring_physician_user_id` recording the opaque identifier of the physician filing it. A newly filed referral opens in `SUBMITTED` with `first_failed_gauntlet_rung` null. Only an `AdmittingPhysician` may file one, and the patient's own referrals can be listed back — filterable by `status` — by a `ProgramAdmin`, an `AdmittingPhysician`, or the `Patient` themselves.

Recording an eligibility assessment is what starts screening. The assessment payload carries the `referral_id` it documents, `in_person_eval_at` (the UTC instant of the mandatory in-person physician evaluation), `home_safety_outcome` from the home-environment walkthrough, `broadband_check` from the connectivity test, the boolean `caregiver_present_confirmed` recording the assessor's own confirmation that a competent adult caregiver is in the home, the boolean `consent_signed`, and `assessor_user_id` naming the clinician who performed it. The **first** assessment recorded against a referral drives that referral from `SUBMITTED` to `SCREENING` and advances the referral's `version` by 1; the precondition for that transition is that the referral is still in `SUBMITTED`. Any subsequent assessment for the same referral is recorded normally — assessments are additive, never overwriting — but leaves the referral's `status` untouched, because the transition's precondition no longer holds. Assessments can be listed back per referral, and where several exist, **the latest-created one wins**: order the referral's assessments by `created_at` ascending and take the last, and it is that assessment alone whose facts the gauntlet reads.

Marking a referral eligible is the moment of truth, and it is available only to an `AdmittingPhysician`. The referral must currently be in `SCREENING` — a referral still in `SUBMITTED`, already `ELIGIBLE`, already `ADMITTED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and at least one assessment must exist for it. The system then runs seven eligibility rules against the latest-created assessment, **in this exact order**, stopping at the first failure:

1. The referral's `origin` must be either `ED` or `INPATIENT_WARD`. A referral arriving from `CLINIC` or from `SNF` is not eligible for home hospitalization and is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_ORIGIN_INELIGIBLE`.
2. The referral's `qualifying_condition_code` must be one of the six qualifying conditions: `CHF_EXAC`, `COPD_EXAC`, `CAP_PNEUMONIA`, `CELLULITIS`, `UTI_COMPLICATED` or `COVID_MODERATE`. The seventh legal enum value, `OTHER`, is accepted by the schema but never qualifies, and referrals claiming it are refused with `ERR_CONDITION_NOT_QUALIFYING`.
3. The patient's `home_distance_km` must lie within the program's forty-kilometre service radius. The bound is inclusive at the top: a home exactly 40.0 km from the hub passes, a home 40.1 km away is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_RADIUS`.
4. The latest assessment's `home_safety_outcome` must be `PASS`. Both `CONDITIONAL` and `FAIL` are rejections here — a conditional pass is not a pass — and both yield `ERR_HOME_SAFETY_FAILED`.
5. The latest assessment's `broadband_check` must be `PASS`; a `FAIL` yields `ERR_CONNECTIVITY_INSUFFICIENT`, because a virtual ward without connectivity is not a ward.
6. When the referral's `acuity` is `HIGH`, **both** the patient record's `caregiver_present` flag and the latest assessment's `caregiver_present_confirmed` flag must be true; if either is false the referral is refused with `ERR_CAREGIVER_REQUIRED`. A referral whose `acuity` is `STANDARD` skips this rule entirely — no caregiver is required and neither flag is consulted.
7. The latest assessment's `consent_signed` must be true; otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`.

When all seven pass, the referral moves `SCREENING → ELIGIBLE`, `first_failed_gauntlet_rung` is cleared back to null, and the referral's `version` advances by 1. When any rule fails, the response carries that rule's error identifier at HTTP 422, the referral **stays** in `SCREENING` so the physician can fix the underlying fact and retry, and the referral's `first_failed_gauntlet_rung` is written with the error identifier of the highest-priority rule that failed — the same string the caller just received — so that the rejection remains observable on the record afterward. That bookkeeping write is a mutation of the referral row like any other, so it advances the referral's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`.

### 3.2. Admission, the Day Ledger and the Episode Lifecycle

Admission consumes an eligible referral. An `AdmittingPhysician` supplies the `patient_id`, the `referral_id` of the referral being consumed, and the `program_track` that will govern this episode's compliance and reimbursement arithmetic for its entire life. The referral must be in `ELIGIBLE`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Two eligibility rules are then re-run at this later moment, in this order: first the service-radius rule, because a patient's `home_distance_km` may have been revised since screening (failure: `ERR_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_RADIUS`), and then the physician-evaluation window rule, which is checked **only here and nowhere else**. That window rule reads the latest-created assessment's `in_person_eval_at` and requires it to fall at or after the admission instant minus twenty-four hours *and* at or before the admission instant itself; both bounds are inclusive, so an evaluation performed exactly twenty-four hours before admission passes, an evaluation twenty-four hours and one minute before admission fails, and an evaluation dated after the admission instant also fails. Either failure yields `ERR_EVAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

A successful admission is a five-part write. The new episode opens in `ACTIVE`; `admitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock; `physician_eval_at` is copied from the latest-created assessment's `in_person_eval_at`; the episode's own `acuity` is copied verbatim from the referral's `acuity`, which is what later halves or does not halve the escalation deadlines; the referral advances `ELIGIBLE → ADMITTED` with its `version` incremented by 1; the patient's `status` moves `REGISTERED → IN_PROGRAM`; and exactly one compliance record is minted in `PENDING` for the UTC calendar date of `admitted_at`. Episodes are readable individually and as a filterable list narrowed by `patient_id`, `referral_id` and `status`.

**How service dates come into existence.** An episode's day ledger is not a date range computed on the fly; it is a set of concrete compliance records, one per served UTC calendar date, minted by four distinct mechanisms. The admission itself mints the record for the admission date. Scheduling a visit mints a record for that visit's `service_date` when none exists yet. Recording a telemetry gap mints a record for the UTC calendar date of the gap's `gap_start_at` when none exists yet. And three operations perform a **back-fill sweep**: beginning discharge, converting an escalation into a bed return, and readmitting a returned episode each walk the inclusive range from the UTC date of `admitted_at` to the UTC date of the current logical clock and mint a `PENDING` record for every date in that range that does not already have one. There is never more than one record per episode per `service_date`.

Four integer counters roll up that set and are recomputed together on every event that can move a record between buckets — every compliance-day finalization or reopening, every readmission, every begin-discharge and every discharge. `service_day_count` is the number of compliance records the episode owns. `billable_day_count` is the number whose `status` is `COMPLIANT`. `carved_out_day_count` is the number whose `status` is `CARVED_OUT`. `pending_day_count` is the number still in `PENDING`. Records are enumerated with no status filter and each belongs to exactly one bucket, so the first counter always equals the sum of the other three. Alongside them the episode carries `reimbursement_amount_usd`, `meal_credit_total_usd` and `travel_reimbursement_total_usd`; a dedicated computed read returns exactly those seven numbers — `service_day_count`, `billable_day_count`, `carved_out_day_count`, `pending_day_count`, `reimbursement_amount_usd`, `meal_credit_total_usd` and `travel_reimbursement_total_usd` — for a `ProgramAdmin`, `AdmittingPhysician` or `BillingAnalyst`.

An episode also tracks its own history: `returned_at` holds the logical-clock value at which it most recently entered `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY` and stays null if that never happened; `discharged_at` holds the instant it entered `DISCHARGED` and is null while the episode is open; `readmission_count` starts at 0 and counts how many times the episode has come back to `ACTIVE` from `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY`; and `open_escalation_count` is the live number of the episode's escalations that have not yet reached `RESOLVED` or `BED_RETURN_CONVERTED`.

Medication reconciliation is a standalone attestation an `AdmittingPhysician` performs on the episode, and it is a hard prerequisite of discharge. Signing it sets `med_rec_signed` to true, records the signing physician's opaque identifier in `med_rec_signed_by_user_id`, and advances the episode's `version` by 1. Both fields start out as false and null respectively on a freshly admitted episode. There is no episode-state gate on this operation — an episode may have its medication reconciliation signed while `ACTIVE`, while `ESCALATED`, while `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY` or while `DISCHARGE_PENDING`.

Taken as a whole, the episode walks a cycle rather than a line: `ACTIVE → ESCALATED` when a tier-3 escalation opens, `ESCALATED → ACTIVE` when the last open escalation resolves, `ESCALATED → RETURNED_TO_FACILITY` when an escalation is converted into a physical bed return, `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY → ACTIVE` on readmission, `ACTIVE → DISCHARGE_PENDING` when discharge begins, and `DISCHARGE_PENDING → DISCHARGED` when it completes. `DISCHARGED` is the only terminal state. The clinical machinery behind the first four edges is §3.8; the last two are §3.9.

### 3.3. Visiting the Patient: Two Independent Axes

A visit is a reserved slot in a clinician's day. Scheduling one requires the `episode_id` being served, the `visit_type` describing modality and discipline, the `staff_user_id` of the clinician who owns the slot, the `service_date` the visit counts toward as a `YYYY-MM-DD` UTC calendar date, the half-open interval `scheduled_start_at` (inclusive) to `scheduled_end_at` (exclusive), and `distance_km`, the one-way travel distance for the trip. `scheduled_end_at` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start_at`; `service_date` must equal the UTC calendar-date portion of `scheduled_start_at` exactly; `distance_km` must be at least 0 and is conventionally 0 for a `PHYSICIAN_TELEHEALTH` visit, which involves no travel. The episode must be `ACTIVE` at the moment of scheduling. When the visit's `service_date` has no compliance record yet, one is minted in `PENDING` as part of the same write.

**The turnaround buffer.** A clinician cannot be in two homes at once, and cannot teleport between them either. Before a slot is accepted the system examines every other visit carrying the same `staff_user_id` whose `scheduling_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE` or `COMPLETED` — visits that are `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` are ignored, as are visits belonging to any other clinician, and the check is program-wide rather than per-episode. Each such existing visit is treated as occupying its own half-open interval padded on its trailing edge by a fifteen-minute turnaround allowance. The requested slot is accepted only if its `scheduled_start_at` falls at or after the existing visit's `scheduled_end_at` plus fifteen minutes, or its `scheduled_end_at` falls at or before the existing visit's `scheduled_start_at`. Exactly fifteen minutes of separation after an existing visit is therefore accepted; fourteen minutes collides and is refused with `ERR_VISIT_OVERLAP`. Anything else — a slot that starts inside an existing one, one that swallows an existing one whole, or one that begins during the buffer — collides as well.

The two axes of a visit then evolve **independently on the same row**, and this independence is the point. The scheduling axis, held in `scheduling_status`, starts at `SCHEDULED`. Departing for the visit moves it `SCHEDULED → EN_ROUTE` and requires the parent episode to still be `ACTIVE`. Arriving and finishing moves it `EN_ROUTE → COMPLETED`, likewise requiring an `ACTIVE` episode; that transition computes the visit's own `travel_payment_usd`, recomputes the compliance record for the visit's episode and `service_date`, recomputes the episode's `travel_reimbursement_total_usd`, and advances the visit's `version` by 1. A visit that never happened is closed out instead, from either `SCHEDULED` or `EN_ROUTE`, with an `outcome` naming which of the two unperformed endings applies. When the `outcome` is `NO_SHOW` the visit's `scheduling_status` becomes `NO_SHOW`, the compliance record for its episode and `service_date` is recomputed, and the visit's `version` advances by 1. When the `outcome` is `CANCELLED` the visit's `scheduling_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, the compliance record for its episode and `service_date` is likewise recomputed, the visit's `version` advances by 1, and — in the specific case where this cancellation is being applied as part of a discharge, so that the episode has reached `DISCHARGED` — that recomputed record is also finalized straight out of `PENDING` rather than being left pending. `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal on the scheduling axis.

The attestation axis, held in `attestation_status`, starts at `UNATTESTED` and is what turns a visit from a calendar entry into clinical evidence. The first attestation moves it `UNATTESTED → ATTESTED`, writes the attesting clinician's opaque identifier into `attested_by_user_id`, recomputes the compliance record's `qualified_in_person_visit_count` and `physician_eval_present` for that episode and `service_date`, and advances the visit's `version` by 1. Its only precondition beyond the current axis value is that the visit's `scheduling_status` is not `CANCELLED`; a visit that is still `SCHEDULED` or still `EN_ROUTE` may perfectly well be attested — it simply contributes nothing to the day's requirement until the scheduling axis also reaches `COMPLETED`. A visit may be `ATTESTED` while `SCHEDULED`, and may be `COMPLETED` while `UNATTESTED`; only the combination of `COMPLETED` on one axis and `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED` on the other counts toward anything.

Attesting a second time is an **amendment**, and amendments are expensive. The axis moves `ATTESTED → AMENDED`, `attested_by_user_id` is overwritten with the amending clinician's identifier, the visit's `version` advances by 1, and the compliance record for that episode and `service_date` is reopened: if that record had already been finalized to `COMPLIANT` or to `CARVED_OUT`, and the owning episode is not yet `DISCHARGED`, it is driven back to `PENDING` before being recomputed. Reopening from `COMPLIANT` clears `finalized_at` back to null, decrements the episode's `billable_day_count` and increments its `pending_day_count`, and advances the record's `version` by 1. Reopening from `CARVED_OUT` clears `finalized_at` back to null, additionally clears `carve_out_reason` back to null, decrements the episode's `carved_out_day_count` and increments its `pending_day_count`, and advances the record's `version` by 1. `AMENDED` is terminal on the attestation axis: a third attestation attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**What a trip is worth.** Travel is reimbursed by an average-rate band, not by marginal allocation: one rate applies to the whole distance. A visit whose `distance_km` is at or below 10.0 kilometres is paid at 0.92 US dollars per kilometre. A visit strictly beyond 10.0 kilometres but at or below 25.0 kilometres is paid at 0.74 US dollars per kilometre. A visit strictly beyond 25.0 kilometres is paid at 0.61 US dollars per kilometre. The distance is multiplied by the single selected rate and the product is rounded half up to two decimals; if that rounded figure is below the eight-dollar minimum per-visit payment, the visit pays 8.00 instead. That eight-dollar floor is unconditional and applies to every qualifying visit regardless of `visit_type` — a `PHYSICIAN_TELEHEALTH` visit with `distance_km` of 0 still earns 8.00, and there is no telehealth exemption. The episode's `travel_reimbursement_total_usd` is then the sum of the already-rounded `travel_payment_usd` values of the episode's visits whose `scheduling_status` is `COMPLETED` **and** whose `attestation_status` is `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED`, rounded once more to two decimals. Visits that are `SCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED` or still `UNATTESTED` contribute 0.00 to that total.

An episode's visits are listable, filterable by `service_date` and by `scheduling_status`, and are readable by a `ProgramAdmin`, `AdmittingPhysician`, `FieldNurse`, `CommunityParamedic`, `BillingAnalyst` or the `Patient`.

### 3.4. Continuous Monitoring, Telemetry Gaps and the Daily Verdict

A virtual ward is only a ward while the telemetry is flowing. When it stops, a `ProgramAdmin` or `FieldNurse` records the gap against the episode: `episode_id`, the inclusive `gap_start_at`, the exclusive `gap_end_at` — which must be strictly later than the start — and an optional `cause`, which defaults to `UNKNOWN` when omitted. The parent episode must be `ACTIVE`. Gap records are append-only evidence: once written they are never edited or deleted, which is why they carry neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`. Writing one mints a `PENDING` compliance record for the UTC calendar date of `gap_start_at` if that date does not have one yet, and recomputes the affected date's `monitoring_lapse_minutes`.

Turning raw gaps into a daily minute count is an interval-merge, because two monitoring systems reporting the same outage must not be charged twice. For one episode and one UTC calendar date, the day window runs from that date at 00:00:00 UTC inclusive to the following date at 00:00:00 UTC exclusive. Every gap of that episode which starts before the end of the window and ends after the start of the window is taken and **clipped** to the window: its effective start is the later of its own `gap_start_at` and the window start, and its effective end is the earlier of its own `gap_end_at` and the window end. The clipped intervals are sorted by effective start ascending, ties broken by the gap record's `created_at` ascending. They are then merged left to right: an interval whose effective start is strictly later than the end of the interval accumulated so far begins a new accumulated interval, while an interval whose effective start is at or before that end is absorbed into it by extending the accumulated end to the later of the two ends. Adjacency with zero gap therefore merges — a gap ending at 10:30 and another starting at 10:30 become a single sixty-minute outage rather than two thirty-minute ones. The merged durations are summed in seconds and converted to whole minutes by dividing by sixty and **truncating downward**, so partial minutes are dropped.

**Scoring the day.** Each compliance record answers one question — did this episode receive the care its billing track promises on this date? — and it answers it with four clauses evaluated in a fixed order. Two of the four inputs are recomputed from the visit set: `qualified_in_person_visit_count` is the number of that episode's visits on that `service_date` whose `visit_type` is `RN_IN_PERSON` or `PARAMEDIC_IN_PERSON`, whose `scheduling_status` is `COMPLETED`, and whose `attestation_status` is `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED`; `physician_eval_present` is true when at least one visit on the same date has `visit_type` of `PHYSICIAN_IN_PERSON` or `PHYSICIAN_TELEHEALTH` and satisfies the same completion-and-attestation pair. Visits that are `SCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` are excluded no matter how they were attested. The third input is the day's merged `monitoring_lapse_minutes`; the fourth is `kit_deployed_all_day`, a flag that begins life as true and is set to false the moment the episode's monitoring kit stops being deployed on or before the end of that service date.

The thresholds those inputs are measured against fork on the episode's `program_track`, and the fork is stark enough that the very same visit set can score compliant on one track and be carved out on the other. On a `CMS_WAIVER` episode the day requires **two** qualified in-person visits, **one** physician evaluation, and continuous kit deployment. On a `FEE_FOR_SERVICE` episode the day requires **one** qualified in-person visit, requires **no** physician evaluation at all, and skips the kit-deployment clause entirely. The four clauses are then evaluated in this order, and the first one that fails both carves the day out and names the reason:

1. `qualified_in_person_visit_count` is at least the track's required in-person count — two under the waiver, one under fee-for-service. Failure names `INSUFFICIENT_IN_PERSON_VISITS`.
2. Either the track requires no physician evaluation (which is the fee-for-service case, where this clause is automatically satisfied) or `physician_eval_present` is true. Failure names `MISSING_PHYSICIAN_EVAL`.
3. `monitoring_lapse_minutes` is at or below 240 minutes — four hours of telemetry silence in a day is tolerated, four hours and one minute is not. Failure names `MONITORING_LAPSE_EXCEEDED`.
4. Either the track does not apply the kit clause (which is the fee-for-service case) or `kit_deployed_all_day` is true. Failure names `KIT_NOT_DEPLOYED`.

If all four clauses pass the day is `COMPLIANT`; otherwise it is `CARVED_OUT` with `carve_out_reason` set to the first failing clause's reason. Because the clauses are ordered, a waiver day that satisfied the first three clauses and lost only its kit is the sole way `KIT_NOT_DEPLOYED` is ever produced.

Finalization is the act of committing that verdict. A `ProgramAdmin` or `AdmittingPhysician` finalizes a `PENDING` record, and the same scoring also runs automatically as part of the kit-release cascade and as part of the discharge sweep. When the verdict is compliant, the record moves `PENDING → COMPLIANT`, `finalized_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the episode's `billable_day_count` is incremented and its `pending_day_count` decremented, the episode's `reimbursement_amount_usd` and `meal_credit_total_usd` are recomputed, and the record's `version` advances by 1. When the verdict is a carve-out, the record moves `PENDING → CARVED_OUT`, `carve_out_reason` is written with the first failing clause's reason, `finalized_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the episode's `carved_out_day_count` is incremented and its `pending_day_count` decremented, the episode's `reimbursement_amount_usd` and `meal_credit_total_usd` are recomputed — a carved-out day drops out of the meal-credit eligible set as well as out of the billable count — **every** billing claim of that episode still sitting in `BUILT` is driven to `VOIDED`, and the record's `version` advances by 1. Finalizing a record that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An episode's compliance records are listable and filterable by `status` and by `service_date`.

### 3.5. The Monitoring Kit: Deployment, Refurbishment and Chain of Custody

Kits are capital equipment, and the program accounts for them individually. A `ProgramAdmin` adds one to inventory with a single attribute, the non-empty and program-unique `asset_tag`; the new kit opens in `AVAILABLE` with `assigned_episode_id` null, `refurb_cycle_count` 0 and `deployed_at` null. Components are then attached to the kit one at a time — the kit they are attached to is identified by the path, and each component carries that kit's identifier as its own `kit_id` — each carrying a `component_type`, a manufacturer `serial_number` that must be non-empty and unique across all components in the program, and a `replacement_cost_usd` of at least 0 recording the book value that would be charged if the item never came back. Each component opens with `reconciliation_status` of `IN_STOCK`, and adding one refreshes the owning kit's `component_count`, which is simply the number of components attached to it. Its sibling counter, `unreconciled_component_count`, is the number of that kit's components currently sitting in `PENDING_RECONCILIATION`.

Deployment is the only operation in this section a `FieldNurse` performs against inventory, and it is guarded on four fronts at once. The request names the `episode_id` receiving the kit and carries `expected_kit_version`, the kit `version` the nurse last observed; a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. The target episode must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the capacity rule that follows requires both that the kit itself is currently `AVAILABLE` and that no *other* kit is already `DEPLOYED` to the same episode — one episode, one kit — with either failure reported as `ERR_KIT_UNAVAILABLE` at HTTP 409. A successful deployment moves the kit `AVAILABLE → DEPLOYED`, sets `assigned_episode_id` to the target episode, stamps `deployed_at` from the logical clock, moves every one of that kit's components from `IN_STOCK` to `DEPLOYED`, and advances the kit's `version` by 1. The kits currently assigned to an episode are listable, as are a kit's components, the latter filterable by `reconciliation_status`.

Kits come home in two circumstances, and in both the system — not a human — performs the release: when an episode is converted to a facility bed return, and when discharge begins on an episode. In either case the kit whose `assigned_episode_id` is that episode and whose `status` is `DEPLOYED` moves `DEPLOYED → IN_REFURB`, its `assigned_episode_id` is cleared to null, its `refurb_cycle_count` is incremented by 1, every one of its components sitting in `DEPLOYED` moves to `PENDING_RECONCILIATION`, and the kit's `version` advances by 1. The release also reaches sideways into the day ledger: the released episode's compliance record whose `service_date` equals the UTC calendar date of the logical clock has its `kit_deployed_all_day` set to false, and if that record is still `PENDING` it is immediately scored and finalized through the daily verdict described in §3.4. Whether it lands on `COMPLIANT` or on `CARVED_OUT` — and if carved out, which reason it carries — falls out of the ordered clause evaluation exactly as it would from any other finalization.

Reconciliation closes the custody chain component by component. A `FieldNurse` reconciles a component that is currently in `PENDING_RECONCILIATION` — a component still `IN_STOCK` or still `DEPLOYED`, or one already `RETURNED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — with an `outcome` of either `RETURNED`, meaning the item came back, or `WRITTEN_OFF`, meaning it did not and its `replacement_cost_usd` stands as the loss. The component's `reconciliation_status` is set to the supplied outcome and `reconciled_by_user_id` is stamped with the nurse's opaque identifier; the owning kit's `unreconciled_component_count` is then recomputed. When that count reaches 0 the kit leaves refurbishment, and **which state it leaves for depends on how many cycles it has already served**: a kit whose `refurb_cycle_count` is at or below five moves `IN_REFURB → AVAILABLE`, and as part of that move every component of the kit sitting in `RETURNED` is returned to `IN_STOCK` so the kit is whole again for its next deployment; a kit whose `refurb_cycle_count` is strictly greater than five moves `IN_REFURB → RETIRED` instead, which is terminal — it is never redeployed and its components are not restocked. Either transition advances the kit's `version` by 1. Components that were `WRITTEN_OFF` stay `WRITTEN_OFF` and are never restocked.

The program-wide census is a `ProgramAdmin`-only computed read reporting `total_kits`, `available_count`, `deployed_count`, `in_refurb_count` and `retired_count` — every kit ever created, counted exactly once, with the sum of the four buckets always equal to the total.

### 3.6. Medication and DME Delivery Under Chain-of-Custody Rules

Raising a delivery order is how medication and durable medical equipment reach the home. A `LogisticsCoordinator` or `AdmittingPhysician` supplies the `episode_id`, the `order_kind` distinguishing medication from equipment, the boolean `is_controlled` marking a controlled substance, the boolean `cold_chain_required` marking a payload that must stay refrigerated, the billable `weight_kg` (at least 0), and `requested_by_user_id` recording who asked for it. The episode must be `ACTIVE`. The order opens in `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` with `redelivery_count` 0, `authorized_by_user_id` null, `witness_staff_id` null and `last_excursion_minutes` null, and its `handling_fee_usd` is computed immediately.

**The handling fee.** Two of the fee's components fork on whether the order is controlled: a controlled order starts from a base of 45.00 US dollars and carries a witness fee of 12.50 US dollars, while a non-controlled order starts from a base of 12.00 US dollars and carries no witness fee at all — the same weight and cold-chain inputs therefore produce a raw figure 45.50 lower on a non-controlled order before any floor or cap applies. To the base and witness components the system adds a cold-chain surcharge of 22.00 US dollars when `cold_chain_required` is true and nothing when it is false; a weight component equal to `weight_kg` multiplied by 1.40 US dollars per kilogram, **rounded half up to two decimals before it enters the sum**; and a redelivery component equal to `redelivery_count` — read at the instant of the recompute — multiplied by 9.00 US dollars per redelivery. That raw total is then bounded in a pinned order: first it is raised to the fifteen-dollar floor if it falls below it, and only afterwards lowered to the one-hundred-and-twenty-dollar cap if it exceeds it. The bounded figure is rounded half up to two decimals and stored as `handling_fee_usd`. The fee is computed when the order is raised and recomputed on every redelivery dispatch and on delivery.

Authorization is the segregation-of-duties gate, and only a `PharmacyAuthorizer` may perform it. The order must be in `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` and the episode must be `ACTIVE`. When `is_controlled` is true, the authorizer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `requested_by_user_id`; an identical value means the same human requested and approved a controlled substance, and it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. A non-controlled order skips that comparison entirely. Success moves the order `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION → AUTHORIZED`, writes the authorizer's identifier into `authorized_by_user_id`, and advances the order's `version` by 1.

Dispatch is a `LogisticsCoordinator` operation and covers two distinct edges. From `AUTHORIZED` it simply moves the order to `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` and advances its `version` by 1, with the episode required to be `ACTIVE`. From `REFUSED` it is a **redelivery**: the episode must still be `ACTIVE`, `redelivery_count` must be strictly below 2, and success moves the order back to `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`, increments `redelivery_count` by 1, recomputes `handling_fee_usd` with the new redelivery component, and advances the order's `version` by 1. A redelivery attempt on an order whose `redelivery_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_REDELIVERY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 409 and the order stays in `REFUSED` — this cap is deliberately enforced below the state check rather than as part of it, because `REFUSED` remains a legal state to dispatch from until both redeliveries are spent.

Completing the handoff moves the order `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → DELIVERED` and accepts two optional body fields, `witness_staff_id` and `excursion_minutes`, which become mandatory in the two circumstances the program cares about. A controlled order must be handed off with a non-empty `witness_staff_id`; omitting it is `ERR_WITNESS_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422. A cold-chain order must report `excursion_minutes` at or below 30 — exactly thirty minutes out of temperature passes, thirty-one minutes spoils the payload and is refused with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_EXCURSION` at HTTP 422. Whichever of the two fields are supplied are recorded on the order, `witness_staff_id` as itself and `excursion_minutes` into `last_excursion_minutes`; `handling_fee_usd` is recomputed; and the order's `version` advances by 1. Either a `LogisticsCoordinator` or a `CommunityParamedic` may complete a handoff.

Refusal at the door is the branch point that produces both terminal outcomes. A refusal recorded against an order in `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` always succeeds, moving it to `REFUSED` and advancing its `version` by 1 — and it never touches `redelivery_count`, because only a dispatch increments that counter. A refusal recorded against an order **already** in `REFUSED` is the abandonment path: it is permitted only when `redelivery_count` is exactly 2, meaning both permitted redeliveries have been spent, and an already-refused order with fewer than two redeliveries behind it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` because a redelivery is still available to it. When abandonment does apply, the order moves `REFUSED → FAILED`, a brand-new escalation is opened on the same episode with a `reason` of `MEDICATION_FAILURE`, a `status` of `TIER1_OPEN` and an `opened_by_user_id` set to the refusing caller's identifier — that escalation is stamped exactly as a manually opened one would be, with `current_tier` 1, `tier_entered_at` taken from the logical clock, `promotion_count` 0, `acknowledged_by_user_id` null and `ack_deadline_at` computed from the tier-1 allowance under the episode's acuity — the episode's `open_escalation_count` is recomputed upward by one, and the order's `version` advances by 1. Separately, an order in any of `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, `AUTHORIZED`, `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` or `REFUSED` is driven to `CANCELLED` by the system when its episode is converted to a facility bed return, with the order's `version` advancing by 1. `DELIVERED`, `FAILED` and `CANCELLED` are the three terminal statuses, and reaching one of them for every order of an episode is a precondition of discharge. An episode's orders are listable and filterable by `status`.

### 3.7. Meals and Care Coordination

Feeding the patient is a `LogisticsCoordinator` responsibility. Opening a meal plan requires the `episode_id`, the `diet_code` the kitchen will cook to, and `daily_delivery_count`, the number of meal drops per service day, which must be at least 1 and at most 3 with both bounds inclusive. The episode must be `ACTIVE`. The diet compatibility rule is deliberately asymmetric: when the patient's `diet_restriction` is `NONE` any `diet_code` is accepted, because an unrestricted patient can eat anything; when the patient carries any other restriction the plan's `diet_code` must equal that restriction exactly, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_DIET_INCOMPATIBLE` at HTTP 422. A newly opened plan takes `status` of `ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_on_date` with the UTC calendar date of the logical clock at creation, and starts `suspension_day_count` at 0 before computing its credits.

**Meal credit accrual** runs in two steps, and the first is a day count rather than money. The eligible dates are the compliance records of the plan's episode whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMPLIANT` and whose `service_date` falls on or after the plan's `activated_on_date`, compared as calendar dates. Carved-out days never accrue a meal credit, which is precisely why a carve-out cascades into a meal-plan recompute. From the number of eligible dates the plan subtracts its own `suspension_day_count`, and the result is floored at 0 — never negative — to give `credited_day_count`. The money step then forks on the episode's `program_track`: on a `CMS_WAIVER` episode meals are bundled inside the per-diem, so `credited_day_count` is still tracked but `meal_credit_total_usd` is always 0.00; on a `FEE_FOR_SERVICE` episode meals are separately billable at 28.00 US dollars per credited day, so `meal_credit_total_usd` is `credited_day_count` multiplied by 28.00, rounded half up to two decimals, and that money later rolls into the claim. Identical day ledgers therefore produce different claim totals across the two tracks. The episode's own `meal_credit_total_usd` is the sum of the `meal_credit_total_usd` of its plans whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `COMPLETED`, rounded half up to two decimals; plans that are `CANCELLED` contribute 0.00 to it. Accrual is recomputed when a plan is opened, on every meal-plan status change, and on every compliance-day status change of the same episode.

Plans follow the episode through its own cycle, and every one of the four moves is performed by the system rather than by an operator. When the episode is converted to a facility bed return, each of its plans in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, `suspension_day_count` is incremented by exactly 1 — each suspension permanently forfeits one credited day — the plan's `credited_day_count` and `meal_credit_total_usd` are recomputed, the episode's `meal_credit_total_usd` is recomputed with them, and the plan's `version` advances by 1. When the episode is readmitted and has returned to `ACTIVE`, each plan in `SUSPENDED` moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, its `credited_day_count` and `meal_credit_total_usd` are recomputed, and its `version` advances by 1 — closing the suspend-and-resume cycle, though the forfeited day is not restored.

Discharge ends a plan, and which ending it gets depends on where the plan stood. A plan that is `ACTIVE` at the moment of discharge moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`; its `credited_day_count` and `meal_credit_total_usd` are recomputed from the freshly finalized set of compliance days, the episode's `meal_credit_total_usd` is recomputed with them, and the plan's `version` advances by 1. A plan that is still `SUSPENDED` at the moment of discharge — an episode that went back to the facility and never came home — moves `SUSPENDED → CANCELLED` instead; its `meal_credit_total_usd` is set to 0.00, and its `version` advances by 1. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal. An episode's meal plans are listable and filterable by `status`.

### 3.8. Escalation, the Held Bed, and Coming Back Home

When a patient in a virtual ward deteriorates, the program has minutes rather than hours, and the escalation ladder is what converts that clinical urgency into an auditable clock. A `FieldNurse`, `CommunityParamedic` or `ProgramAdmin` opens an escalation by naming the `episode_id`, the `reason` describing the clinical trigger, and `opened_by_user_id` recording who raised it. The new escalation opens in `TIER1_OPEN` with `current_tier` 1, `tier_entered_at` stamped from the logical clock, `promotion_count` 0, `acknowledged_by_user_id` null, and `ack_deadline_at` computed immediately. The owning episode's `open_escalation_count` is recomputed as part of the same write.

**The acknowledgement clock.** Each open tier carries its own base allowance measured from `tier_entered_at`: ten minutes at tier 1, twenty minutes at tier 2, thirty minutes at tier 3, and fifteen minutes at tier 4 — the ladder deliberately tightens again at the top, because a tier-4 escalation is already a physician-level emergency. Those base allowances apply as written on an episode whose `acuity` is `STANDARD`. On an episode whose `acuity` is `HIGH` every tier allowance is **halved and then rounded half up to a whole number of minutes**, giving five, ten, fifteen and eight minutes for tiers 1 through 4 respectively — note that halving fifteen yields seven and a half minutes, which rounds up to eight. `ack_deadline_at` is `tier_entered_at` plus the effective number of minutes for the current tier, and it is recomputed from scratch on every promotion. The consequence is that an identical advance of the logical clock can leave a standard-acuity tier-4 escalation comfortably inside its window while the same advance has already breached a high-acuity one.

Acknowledging an open tier moves it from `TIER1_OPEN` to `TIER1_ACKED`, from `TIER2_OPEN` to `TIER2_ACKED`, from `TIER3_OPEN` to `TIER3_ACKED` or from `TIER4_OPEN` to `TIER4_ACKED`, writes the responder's opaque identifier into `acknowledged_by_user_id`, and advances the escalation's `version` by 1. It is permitted only while the logical clock is **at or before** `ack_deadline_at`; an acknowledgement arriving when the clock has passed the deadline is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409, as is an acknowledgement aimed at an escalation that is not currently in an open tier. Any of `FieldNurse`, `AdmittingPhysician` and `CommunityParamedic` may acknowledge any tier — in practice a nurse answers tier 1, a physician tier 2, a paramedic tier 3 and a physician again tier 4, but the endpoint imposes no per-tier role restriction beyond its own authorized-role list.

Promotion is the ladder's other move, available to a `ProgramAdmin` or `FieldNurse`, and it works from either an open or an acknowledged tier. From an **open** tier it represents a breach: it is permitted only when the logical clock is **strictly greater than** `ack_deadline_at`, and a promotion attempted while the clock is still at or before the deadline is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. From an **acknowledged** tier it is always permitted, because a clinician who has seen the patient may escalate on judgement alone. Either way the escalation moves up one rung — `TIER1_OPEN` or `TIER1_ACKED` to `TIER2_OPEN`, `TIER2_OPEN` or `TIER2_ACKED` to `TIER3_OPEN`, `TIER3_OPEN` or `TIER3_ACKED` to `TIER4_OPEN` — and every promotion does the same six things: `current_tier` is set to the new number, `status` is set to the new open state, `tier_entered_at` is re-stamped from the logical clock, `ack_deadline_at` is recomputed from the new tier's allowance under the episode's acuity, `acknowledged_by_user_id` is reset to null so the new tier starts unanswered, and `promotion_count` is incremented by 1, with the escalation's `version` advancing by 1 alongside. Promotion **into `TIER3_OPEN` additionally moves the owning episode `ACTIVE → ESCALATED`**, recomputing `open_escalation_count` and advancing the episode's `version` by 1. There is no rung above tier 4, so a promotion attempt from `TIER4_OPEN` or `TIER4_ACKED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Resolution closes an escalation from any acknowledged tier — `TIER1_ACKED`, `TIER2_ACKED`, `TIER3_ACKED` or `TIER4_ACKED` — driving it to `RESOLVED` and advancing its `version` by 1; resolving from an unacknowledged tier is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The episode's `open_escalation_count` is recomputed, and when no escalation of that episode remains outside `RESOLVED` and `BED_RETURN_CONVERTED` **and** the episode is currently `ESCALATED`, the episode is driven `ESCALATED → ACTIVE` with its `open_escalation_count` landing at 0 and its `version` advancing by 1. An episode's escalations are listable and filterable by `status`.

**Holding a bed.** Escalation only has somewhere to go if a physical bed is waiting. A `ProgramAdmin` registers a receiving ward with a non-empty, program-unique `unit_code` and a `total_beds` count of at least 1; the unit opens with `held_bed_count` and `occupied_bed_count` at 0 and `available_bed_count` equal to `total_beds`, and `total_beds` is immutable thereafter. An `AdmittingPhysician` then holds a bed by supplying the `unit_id`, the `episode_id` the bed is for, and `expected_unit_version` carrying the unit `version` last read; a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Holding is permitted while the episode is `ACTIVE`, `ESCALATED`, `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY` or `DISCHARGE_PENDING` — there is deliberately **no** active-only gate here, because a bed is most often needed exactly when the episode has already left `ACTIVE` — while an episode that has reached `DISCHARGED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Before capacity is judged, the unit's stale holds are swept (see below); if the resulting `available_bed_count` is then below 1 the request is refused with `ERR_NO_BEDS_AVAILABLE` at HTTP 409. A successful hold opens the reservation in `HELD`, stamps `held_at` from the logical clock, sets `hold_expires_at` to `held_at` plus one hundred and twenty minutes, increments the unit's `held_bed_count` and decrements its `available_bed_count`, and advances the **unit's** `version` by 1. Reservations are readable individually and as a list filterable by `unit_id`, `episode_id` and `status`.

Hold expiry is **lazy**, never scheduled. A reservation still in `HELD` whose `hold_expires_at` the logical clock has passed — strictly greater, so a clock sitting exactly on `hold_expires_at` has not yet expired it — moves `HELD → EXPIRED` at the moment anyone looks: when that single reservation is read, when its inpatient unit is read, and when a new hold is attempted against the same `unit_id`. Expiring a reservation decrements the unit's `held_bed_count` and increments its `available_bed_count`, and advances the reservation's `version` by 1. `occupied_at` stays null on a reservation that expires without ever being occupied.

**Converting an escalation into a bed return** is the program's deepest single operation and belongs to the `AdmittingPhysician` alone. It requires an escalation in `TIER3_ACKED`, `TIER4_OPEN` or `TIER4_ACKED`, an episode currently in `ESCALATED`, and at least one reservation of that episode still in `HELD`; any of the three missing is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When it fires, the following all happen in one request: the escalation moves to `BED_RETURN_CONVERTED`, which is terminal; the episode's **earliest-created held reservation**, chosen by `held_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending breaking ties, moves `HELD → OCCUPIED` provided the logical clock is at or before its `hold_expires_at`, stamping `occupied_at` from the clock, decrementing the unit's `held_bed_count`, incrementing its `occupied_bed_count` and advancing the reservation's `version` by 1; the episode moves `ESCALATED → RETURNED_TO_FACILITY` with `returned_at` stamped from the clock; every UTC date still missing a compliance record in the inclusive range from the `admitted_at` date to the current clock date is back-filled as a `PENDING` record; the episode's monitoring kit, if `DEPLOYED`, is released into `IN_REFURB` with everything that release entails (§3.5), which in turn forces the current date's compliance record to be scored and, when it carves out, voids any claim of that episode still in `BUILT`; every meal plan of the episode in `ACTIVE` is suspended; every delivery order of the episode in `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, `AUTHORIZED`, `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` or `REFUSED` is cancelled; and the episode's `open_escalation_count` is recomputed. The escalation's and the episode's `version` values each advance by 1.

**Readmission** brings the patient home again and is likewise physician-only. The episode must be in `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY`, and at least one kit somewhere in inventory must be `AVAILABLE`, because a readmission that cannot redeploy monitoring hardware is not a readmission at all; failing either precondition is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Success moves the episode `RETURNED_TO_FACILITY → ACTIVE`, increments `readmission_count` by 1, drives every reservation of that episode in `HELD` or `OCCUPIED` to `RELEASED` — decrementing whichever of `held_bed_count` or `occupied_bed_count` that reservation was counted in, incrementing the unit's `available_bed_count`, and advancing each reservation's `version` by 1 — resumes every meal plan of the episode in `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, back-fills every still-missing UTC date in the inclusive range from the `admitted_at` date to the clock date as a `PENDING` compliance record, recomputes `service_day_count`, `billable_day_count`, `carved_out_day_count` and `pending_day_count`, and advances the episode's `version` by 1. This edge closes the episode's cycle: `ACTIVE → ESCALATED → RETURNED_TO_FACILITY → ACTIVE`, and an episode may travel it more than once.

### 3.9. Discharge, Claim Construction and Payer Settlement

Discharge is deliberately split into two acts so that the operational wind-down and the compliance sign-off cannot be confused with one another. **Beginning** discharge requires the episode to be `ACTIVE` and to have an `open_escalation_count` of exactly 0 — an episode with a live escalation cannot start winding down, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Success moves the episode `ACTIVE → DISCHARGE_PENDING`, back-fills every still-missing UTC date in the inclusive range from the `admitted_at` date to the current clock date as a `PENDING` compliance record, releases the episode's `DEPLOYED` monitoring kit into `IN_REFURB` with its components moving to `PENDING_RECONCILIATION` and the current date's compliance record scored as described in §3.5, and advances the episode's `version` by 1.

**Completing** discharge requires the episode to be in `DISCHARGE_PENDING` and then clears three reconciliation gates in a fixed order. First, `med_rec_signed` must be true, or the request is refused with `ERR_MED_REC_UNSIGNED`. Second, every delivery order belonging to the episode must have reached one of `DELIVERED`, `FAILED` or `CANCELLED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_TERMINAL`. Third, no component of the kit most recently deployed to the episode may still be sitting in `DEPLOYED` or in `PENDING_RECONCILIATION`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_INCOMPLETE`. All three carry HTTP 409, and because they are ordered, an episode failing more than one reports the first.

When those gates clear, discharge fires the program's closing cascade in this sequence: the episode moves `DISCHARGE_PENDING → DISCHARGED` and `discharged_at` is stamped from the logical clock; every visit of the episode whose `scheduling_status` is still `SCHEDULED` or `EN_ROUTE` is driven to `CANCELLED`; every compliance record of the episode still in `PENDING` is finalized to `COMPLIANT` or `CARVED_OUT` by the daily verdict of §3.4, with all the counter movements, `finalized_at` stamping, `carve_out_reason` writing and claim-voiding that finalization normally entails; the episode's meal plans are ended per §3.7 and their credits recomputed from that freshly finalized day set; `service_day_count`, `billable_day_count`, `carved_out_day_count`, `pending_day_count`, `reimbursement_amount_usd`, `meal_credit_total_usd` and `travel_reimbursement_total_usd` are all recomputed; the patient's `status` moves to `DISCHARGED`; the referral's `status` moves to `CLOSED` with its `version` advancing by 1; and the episode's `version` advances by 1.

**What the days are worth.** An episode's `reimbursement_amount_usd` is driven entirely by `billable_day_count` — carved-out and pending days contribute nothing — and the arithmetic forks on `program_track`. A `CMS_WAIVER` episode is paid a strictly linear per-diem of 1850.00 US dollars for each billable day, the product rounded half up to two decimals; six billable days are therefore worth 11100.00. A `FEE_FOR_SERVICE` episode is paid a diagnosis-related-group equivalent instead: zero billable days pay 0.00, and any positive number of billable days pays a flat base of 9600.00 US dollars plus 640.00 US dollars for each billable day **strictly beyond the sixth** — the outlier day count is the number of billable days minus six, floored at zero — with the total rounded half up to two decimals. Six billable days under fee-for-service therefore pay 9600.00 where the waiver would have paid 11100.00 on identical data. This figure is recomputed whenever `billable_day_count` changes and is snapshotted into the claim at build time.

Building a claim is a `BillingAnalyst` operation naming the `episode_id` and carrying `expected_episode_version`; a stale value is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412, and an episode whose `billable_day_count` is 0 is refused with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_DAYS` at HTTP 422 — at least one compliant day is required before a claim exists at all. The claim opens in `BUILT` with `billed_day_count` snapshotted from the episode's `billable_day_count` at that instant, `appeal_count` 0, `paid_amount_usd` 0.00 and `denial_reason` null, and building it advances the **episode's** `version` by 1. Its money is then computed in four pinned steps and in no other order: `gross_amount_usd` is the episode's `reimbursement_amount_usd` plus its `meal_credit_total_usd`, rounded half up to two decimals; `withhold_rate` is then selected by comparing **that gross figure** — never the net — against the 9600.00 threshold, taking 0.02 when the gross is at or below 9600.00 and 0.05 when it is strictly above; `withhold_amount_usd` is the gross multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals; and `net_payable_usd` is the gross less the withhold, rounded half up to two decimals. A claim built and left in `BUILT` is never re-added to anything once it has been voided, which is what stops a carve-out from double-counting. An episode's claims are listable and filterable by `status`.

Submission moves a claim `BUILT → SUBMITTED` and requires the episode to have reached `DISCHARGED`; a claim whose episode is still open is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as is a submission aimed at a claim not in `BUILT`. Recording a payer outcome accepts an `outcome` of `PAID`, `DENIED` or `CLOSED`, plus `denial_reason`, which is required when the outcome is `DENIED` and ignored otherwise. From `SUBMITTED` or `APPEALED`, an outcome of `PAID` sets `status` to `PAID` and sets `paid_amount_usd` according to where the claim came from: a claim paid straight out of `SUBMITTED` receives its full `net_payable_usd`, while a claim paid after an appeal receives `net_payable_usd` multiplied by 0.60 and rounded half up to two decimals, because appeal recovery is partial. From either of the same two states, an outcome of `DENIED` sets `status` to `DENIED`, writes the supplied `denial_reason`, and sets `paid_amount_usd` to 0.00. From `PAID` or `DENIED`, an outcome of `CLOSED` sets `status` to `CLOSED`, which is terminal, and leaves `paid_amount_usd` as it stood. Every one of these advances the claim's `version` by 1.

Appealing is the capped loop. A claim in `DENIED` whose `appeal_count` is strictly below 2 moves `DENIED → APPEALED` with `appeal_count` incremented by 1 and `version` advanced by 1; the payer's response then arrives as another recorded outcome, so the cycle `SUBMITTED → DENIED → APPEALED → DENIED` can run at most twice before the door shuts. An appeal attempted on a claim whose `appeal_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 409, leaving the claim in `DENIED`, from which the only remaining move is to close it. Finally, a claim still in `BUILT` is driven to `VOIDED` by the system whenever any compliance day of the same episode is carved out — the snapshot inside a voided claim is never re-added to any total, and a replacement claim must be built afresh from the surviving compliant days. `CLOSED` and `VOIDED` are the two terminal claim states.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are mounted at the server root — there is no additional API prefix. Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence answer **HTTP 201** on success; every other successful call, including every state transition, every update and every read, answers **HTTP 200**. The fourteen creating endpoints are patient registration, referral filing, eligibility-assessment recording, admission, visit scheduling, kit registration, component addition, telemetry-gap recording, delivery-order raising, meal-plan opening, escalation opening, inpatient-unit registration, bed reservation and claim building; all fourteen return the created resource in `data`. Failures follow the ladder of §2.5 and carry the status codes catalogued in §5.
