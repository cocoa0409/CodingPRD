# Library Programming & Summer Reading API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A public library system's programming department carries an unusual amount of operational weight for its size. In a single season it must publish a calendar of story times, teen workshops, adult lectures and outreach visits across several branches; contract and pay outside presenters; screen those presenters when children will be in the room; book rooms with real setup, teardown and accessibility requirements; run a summer reading challenge with an incentive ladder and a finite stock of prizes; buy and share materials across branches; and then hand a grant funder a defensible attendance and survey number for every program it ran. Today that work is spread across spreadsheets, a shared calendar and a filing cabinet of insurance certificates, which means nobody can answer the two questions that matter most: *can this program legally and physically happen?* and *did we stay inside the budget?*

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. The service is mounted under `/library` and is designed around three hard commitments. First, compliance is never implicit: a program that serves minors cannot be staffed by an unscreened presenter, and a room that lacks a hearing loop cannot host a program that promised an ASL interpreter. Second, money and inventory are conserved: every cent of a materials or prize budget is at all times either available, committed or spent, and every procured unit of stock is either available, reserved or issued — the service publishes those balances as first-class fields and never lets them drift. Third, time is explicit and testable: every window, expiry, notice period and date comparison reads a single server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so an operator (or an automated test) can drive a whole summer forward in seconds.

The reader of this document should expect an unusually rich set of derived state. Programs carry two independent status dimensions that advance on disjoint triggers, presenter screening cycles through validity and expiry, prize awards may be deferred when stock runs out and later promoted when it returns, and a single screening decision can ripple through contracts, program compliance, supply reservations and a branch-level budget counter in one request. Those ripples are specified in full below; they are the product, not an implementation detail.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-Id` (equivalently spelled `X-User-ID`; header names are matched case-insensitively) identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the four recognised roles, or carries a recognised role that is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The four roles are spelled exactly `ProgramManager`, `ProgramCoordinator`, `ScreeningOfficer` and `Reader`.

A `ProgramManager` is the programming department head. This role owns reference data (branches, rooms, procured inventory), the whole summer reading challenge lifecycle, program confirmation and every form of program closure, and it is the only role permitted to execute a presenter contract. A `ProgramCoordinator` is a branch programming coordinator: they build the calendar, book and confirm rooms, take registrations, register presenters, draft contracts and negotiate fees, enrol readers and keep the reading log on readers' behalf — but they may never execute a contract and may never confirm, close out, weather-close or cancel a program. A `ScreeningOfficer` is a human-resources screening officer and the sole owner of the background-screening workflow; outside of screening requests, screening results and adjudications this role may only read a presenter record, read a branch record and set the logical clock. A `Reader` is a registered summer-reading participant acting for themselves.

Identity semantics differ between the staff roles and the reader role, and the distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramManager`, `ProgramCoordinator` or `ScreeningOfficer`, `X-User-Id` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail (as `offered_by_user_id` and `executed_by_user_id` on a presenter contract, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on a reading log entry) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Reader`, by contrast, `X-User-Id` is the `id` of the acting `Participant` record: ownership checks compare the `{participant_id}` named in the request path against `X-User-Id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403. That ownership check guards exactly three operations: reading a participant record, posting a reading log entry, and posting a log correction. Corrections are in fact never available to a `Reader` at all — only staff may adjust a ledger — so in practice a reader meets the ownership rule when reading their own record and when self-logging.

Segregation of duties is enforced on the user id, not on the role. Because issuing a contract offer is shared between `ProgramCoordinator` and `ProgramManager`, it is entirely possible for a `ProgramManager` to have issued the standing offer on a contract; that same `X-User-Id` may then not execute it. A second `ProgramManager` user id must perform the execution, and an attempt by the offering user is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, and when it is supplied it must be an integer no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip before the first returned item; when omitted it defaults to 0, and when supplied it must be an integer no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the permitted range, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of items matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching item lies beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Append-Only Reading Ledger

Almost every resource in this service carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the resource was first persisted.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource — including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a money roll-up or a status dimension.
- `version` (integer): a monotone counter that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that resource.

Branches, rooms, programs, room reservations, presenters, presenter contracts, reading challenges, incentive tiers, participants, inventory lots and tier awards all carry the full set. `ReadingLogEntry` is the single deliberate exception. A reading log entry is an append-only ledger record: it carries `id` and `created_at` — `created_at` is both the chronological replay key for the ledger and the target that a correction points at — but it has **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version`, because an entry is never mutated. A mistake in the ledger is fixed by appending a correction entry that references the original, never by editing the original in place.

A derived-field refresh is a real mutation. When a contract execution moves money on the owning program, or a log entry pushes a participant's cumulative progress up, or a cascade reverts a confirmed program, the touched resource's `updated_at` moves and its `version` increments even though no client sent a body field for it. The specific version increments each write produces are called out with the workflows in section 3; the invariant to hold is that any resource whose stored fields changed in a request has advanced its `version` by 1 by the time the response is written.

### 2.5. The Logical Programming Clock

Every temporal rule in this service — registration windows, program end instants, screening validity, weather-closure notice, challenge logging windows, cancellation notice periods — reads a single server-side logical clock. The clock never falls back to real wall-clock time. At server startup it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it reports that instant until a caller moves it.

Any of the four roles may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the administrative clock-setting capability. Setting the clock mutates no entity and eagerly recomputes nothing: fields such as a presenter's `screening_status` are re-derived from the new clock the next time they are read or the next time a rule consults them. This is deliberate — it means a test can jump the clock a year forward and observe a screening lapse without any intervening write.

Two comparison granularities coexist and must not be conflated. Screening validity, challenge logging windows, log entry dating and library-cancellation notice are compared at **date** granularity on ISO calendar dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Program windows, registration cut-offs, close-out eligibility, weather-closure eligibility and weather-cancellation notice are compared at **instant** granularity against the full UTC timestamp.

### 2.6. Resolving Identifiers: Path Before Body

Identifiers named in the request **path** are resolved first, strictly left to right, and the response reports the leftmost segment that names nothing. A request addressed at a room inside a branch that does not exist fails with `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the room lookup. The path identifiers in play across the API resolve to these HTTP 404 codes:

- `{branch_id}` — `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND`
- `{room_id}` — `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`
- `{program_id}` — `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`
- `{reservation_id}` — `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`
- `{presenter_id}` — `ERR_PRESENTER_NOT_FOUND`
- `{contract_id}` — `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`
- `{challenge_id}` — `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_FOUND`
- `{tier_id}` — `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_FOUND` (there is no separate tier-level 404 code; an unknown incentive tier is reported with the challenge-family code)
- `{participant_id}` — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`
- `{entry_id}` — `ERR_LOG_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`
- `{lot_id}` — `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`
- `{award_id}` — `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`

Only once every path identifier has resolved does the server resolve foreign keys supplied in the request **body**, and it resolves them in the order the fields are declared on the owning resource. `branch_id` and `home_branch_id` resolve to `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_FOUND`; `challenge_id` to `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_FOUND`; `room_id` to `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`; `presenter_id` to `ERR_PRESENTER_NOT_FOUND`; `program_id` to `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; and `corrected_entry_id` to `ERR_LOG_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key resolution is in scope for exactly six write operations: `POST /library/programs`, `POST /library/contracts`, `POST /library/participants`, `POST /library/inventory-lots`, `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations` and `POST /library/participants/{participant_id}/log-corrections`. Path resources always outrank body resources: a request naming both a missing program in the path and a missing room in the body reports the program.

Request-shape validation covers a parseable JSON body, the presence of every required input field, correct types, membership in the declared enumeration for every enum field, well-formed ISO-8601 dates and instants, and satisfaction of every declared field constraint (the various "at least 1", "at least 0" and non-negative bounds named in section 3). Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. Unknown fields in a body are ignored rather than rejected. Where a creation path already fixes a foreign key — a room created under a branch, a reservation created under a program, an incentive tier created under a challenge, a log entry or correction created under a participant — the value is taken from the path and any same-named value in the body is ignored.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence: One Decisive Rejection

A request that violates several rules at once must report exactly one error, and which one is not a matter of taste. Rules are evaluated in strictly ascending priority order and the **first** violated rule decides the response; no later rule can mask an earlier one. The complete ladder follows. Trigger conditions are described in full alongside the workflows they guard in section 3; the purpose of this list is to fix the order and the scope.

1. Both authentication headers present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` — every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` is a recognised role and is authorised for this endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — every endpoint.
3. Request shape, required fields, types, enum members, ISO-8601 formats and declared field constraints — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — every write endpoint.
4. `limit` and `offset` within range — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` — every list endpoint.
5. Path identifiers exist, left to right — the appropriate `ERR_*_NOT_FOUND` — every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
6. Body foreign keys resolve, in field-declaration order — the appropriate `ERR_*_NOT_FOUND` — the six write operations named in section 2.6.
7. A `Reader`'s `X-User-Id` equals the `{participant_id}` in the path — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — `GET /library/participants/{participant_id}`, `POST /library/participants/{participant_id}/log-entries` and `POST /library/participants/{participant_id}/log-corrections`.
8. The executing user differs from `offered_by_user_id` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — `POST /library/contracts/{contract_id}/execute`.
9. `expected_program_version` matches the program's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations`.
10. `expected_participant_version` matches the participant's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` — `POST /library/participants/{participant_id}/log-corrections`.
11. `operational_status` admits the requested program transition — `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` — the schedule, reservation-create, registration, confirm, close-out, weather-close and cancel operations on `/library/programs/{program_id}`, and additionally the contract offer and contract execute operations on `/library/contracts/{contract_id}`, each of which requires the contract's owning program to still be in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`.
12. Reservation status admits the action — `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID` — `POST /library/reservations/{reservation_id}/confirm` and `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations`.
13. Contract status admits the action — `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID` — the offer, counter and execute operations on `/library/contracts/{contract_id}`.
14. Challenge status admits the action — `ERR_CHALLENGE_STATE_INVALID` — `POST /library/reading-challenges/{challenge_id}/incentive-tiers`, `.../open`, `.../reconcile`, `POST /library/participants`, `POST /library/participants/{participant_id}/log-entries` and `POST /library/participants/{participant_id}/log-corrections`.
15. The screening step is posted in order — `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID` — `POST /library/presenters/{presenter_id}/screening-requests`, `.../screening-results` and `.../adjudications`.
16. The presenter is not suspended — `ERR_PRESENTER_SUSPENDED` — `POST /library/contracts` and the offer, counter and execute operations on `/library/contracts/{contract_id}`.
17. Program compliance permits the action — `ERR_PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/confirm` and `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/registrations`.
18. The room is in service — `ERR_ROOM_OUT_OF_SERVICE` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations`.
19. Screening is active where the program serves minors — `ERR_PRESENTER_SCREENING_REQUIRED` — `POST /library/contracts/{contract_id}/execute`.
20. The presenter's certificate covers the program — `ERR_PRESENTER_INSURANCE_LAPSED` — `POST /library/contracts/{contract_id}/execute`.
21. The partner's certificate covers the program — `ERR_PARTNER_INSURANCE_LAPSED` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/confirm`, and also `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/schedule` for a partner-site program (see section 3.2).
22. The room supports the promised accommodations — `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_UNSUPPORTED` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations`.
23. Setup and teardown buffers meet their floors — `ERR_SETUP_BUFFER_TOO_SHORT` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations`.
24. The booking covers the public window — `ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_MISMATCH` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations`.
25. The room's occupancy interval is free — `ERR_ROOM_DOUBLE_BOOKED` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/reservations`.

27. The presenter's engagement window is free — `ERR_PRESENTER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` — `POST /library/contracts/{contract_id}/execute`.
28. The branch's overlapping-program load fits — `ERR_BRANCH_STAFFING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/confirm`.
29. The program window is ordered and not in the past — `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_WINDOW` — `POST /library/programs` and `POST /library/programs/{program_id}/schedule`.
30. Partner fields are coherent with the delivery mode — `ERR_PARTNER_FIELDS_REQUIRED` — `POST /library/programs`.

42. The challenge has at least one tier — `ERR_CHALLENGE_HAS_NO_TIERS` — `POST /library/reading-challenges/{challenge_id}/open`.
43. The logging window has closed — `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN` — `POST /library/reading-challenges/{challenge_id}/reconcile`.
44. Negotiation rounds remain — `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` — `POST /library/contracts/{contract_id}/offer`.
45. The payable total fits under the fee cap — `ERR_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED` — `POST /library/contracts/{contract_id}/execute`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. Program confirmation checks compliance (step 17) before it checks the confirmed room (step 26), the branch staffing load (step 28), supply stock (step 46) and the materials budget (step 47) — a program whose presenter contract was drafted but never executed therefore reports `ERR_PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED`, not a downstream money or inventory error. And a stale version echo (steps 9 and 10) is decided *before* any business validation of the payload: a log correction with a stale `expected_participant_version` reports `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` even if its `corrected_entry_id` is also invalid.

### 2.8. Money, Units and Rounding Discipline

All money in this service is carried as **integer cents** in fields whose names end in `_cents`, and every such field is emitted as a JSON number, never a string. Intermediate arithmetic is performed in integer cents wherever the inputs are integral; where a rate must be applied — a weekend premium, a kill-fee percentage, a cross-branch surcharge, a materials contingency — the product is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** (a value of exactly one half of a cent rounds away from zero).

Where a rounding happens is as important as how. Three rules are absolute and are restated in context in section 3: a presenter contract rounds exactly once, when its weekend premium is derived from the pre-premium subtotal; a supply draw rounds its cross-branch surcharge **once per draw line**, never once over the combined draw subtotal; and a prize award rounds its transfer surcharge **once per award**, never once over the challenge's committed total. Summing per-line or per-award rounded values and rounding a summed base give different answers, and the published roll-ups make the difference observable.

Percentages behave differently from money. The three outcome-measurement percentages — `attendance_attainment_funder_pct`, `attendance_attainment_internal_pct` and `survey_response_rate_pct` — carry exactly **one** decimal place, also rounded half up. Seat counts round the other way: a capacity reduction for a wheelchair layout is taken with a **floor**, never rounded up, because the seats lost to the layout are physically unavailable. Unit counts in the reading ledger and in inventory are integers throughout, and a participant's cumulative progress has a hard floor of zero.

Two conservation invariants must hold on every read, not merely after a settlement. For a program, `materials_budget_cents` equals `materials_available_cents` plus `materials_committed_cents` plus `materials_spent_cents`. For a reading challenge, `prize_budget_cents` equals `prize_available_cents` plus `prize_committed_cents` plus `prize_spent_cents`. For an inventory lot, `quantity_procured` equals `quantity_available` plus `quantity_reserved` plus `quantity_issued`, and `quantity_available` is never negative. Note that a budget *variance* may legitimately be negative: an overrun is recorded and surfaced, never rejected.

### 2.9. Version Echo and Stale-Write Rejection

Two write operations demand that the caller prove it is acting on data it has actually seen, by echoing the `version` of a parent resource. In both cases the echo field is a required, non-optional input; omitting it is a request-shape failure, and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is a stale-write rejection with HTTP 412. No other endpoint in this API takes a version echo field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

Creating a room reservation for a program requires `expected_program_version` in the body, carrying the `version` of that program that the caller last observed when it computed the requested window. A room booking is derived from the program's window and accessibility profile, so a caller that computed a window against an older reading of the program is working from a picture the server has already moved past — for example because the program was rescheduled, because a registration changed its effective capacity, or because a contract execution changed its committed fee. When the echoed value does not match the program's current `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Creating a *second* reservation for the same program while echoing the current `version` is entirely legitimate and is the intended re-booking path: the program's prior `HELD` reservation moves to `SUPERSEDED` and the new one becomes the program's `HELD` reservation. Only a `CONFIRMED` reservation blocks a replacement. Every accepted reservation create increments the program's `version` by 1, so a client that creates two reservations in a row must re-read the program between them.

Posting a log correction for a participant requires `expected_participant_version` in the body, carrying the `version` of that participant the caller last observed when it chose the target entry. A correction rewrites the arithmetic of a previously accepted entry and, under one of the two correction policies, can retract prize awards and release reserved stock; if a log entry was accepted for that participant after the caller's read, the echoed value is behind and the correction is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 — before the target entry is validated at all. A second correction against the same original entry, echoing the current `version`, is legitimate and supersedes the first correction's arithmetic; corrections chain through `corrected_entry_id` and are never edited in place. A participant's `version` increments by 1 on every accepted log entry and on every accepted correction alike, which is precisely why the echo is required: an interleaved self-service log entry invalidates a staff member's in-flight correction.

### 2.10. Cascades and Derived-Field Reconciliation

A single accepted write in this service routinely mutates several resources. Four named cascades recur throughout section 3, and each is specified link by link where it is triggered:

- **The weather-closure cascade.** Closing a program for weather cancels its live contracts at the weather kill-fee rate, re-derives the program's committed presenter fees from those kill fees, re-derives the budget variance and posture from that, and finally adjusts the owning branch's over-budget counter if and only if the posture flipped.
- **The suspension cascade.** A flagged screening result suspends the presenter, blocks each of that presenter's live contracts, blocks the compliance dimension of each of those contracts' programs, and reverts any of those programs that were already confirmed back to scheduled — returning their supply draw and un-confirming their room along the way.
- **The award cascade.** A reading log entry that crosses one or more tier thresholds creates a tier award per newly reached tier, each of which draws prize units from a lot, raises the challenge's committed prize money, and can deplete a lot and thereby flip the challenge's prize inventory posture.
- **The correction cascade.** A log correction that drops a participant below a held threshold can revoke awards, return their units to stock, reactivate a depleted lot, promote deferred awards into the freed units, and re-derive the challenge's prize money and posture.

Two rules govern every cascade. First, a cascade is **all or nothing with its trigger**: if the triggering operation is rejected by any validation, no cascade link is applied and no partial allocation is persisted — a supply draw that cannot be covered in full allocates nothing at all. Second, a branch's `programs_over_budget_count` is adjusted **only on a posture flip**, incrementing by 1 when a program moves from within budget to over budget and decrementing by 1 when it moves back; a program that recomputes to the same posture leaves the counter untouched, and a program that was already over budget and becomes more so does not increment it twice.

Note also that reverting a confirmed program is not the same as cancelling it. A revert returns supply units to `quantity_available` (reactivating any lot that had been depleted) and drops `materials_committed_cents` to zero, while the room reservation falls back from `CONFIRMED` to `HELD` — still occupying the room against other bookings, but no longer satisfying the confirmation gate.

### 2.11. Interval Semantics: Where Touching Counts

Three separate overlap rules exist in this domain and they deliberately do **not** share the same boundary convention. Getting one of them wrong produces answers that are correct almost everywhere and wrong exactly at the boundary, so the conventions are stated once here and repeated with each rule in section 3.

A **room occupancy interval** is **half-open**: it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. Back-to-back bookings of the same room are therefore legal — one reservation's occupancy ending at the exact instant another's begins is not a conflict.

A **presenter engagement window** is **closed** on both ends. Two engagements of the same presenter that merely touch at a single instant *do* conflict, because the presenter cannot be in both places.

A program's **public window** for branch staffing purposes is likewise **closed** on both ends, and no buffers are applied to it. Two confirmed programs whose windows share only their boundary instant both count toward the branch's overlapping-program load.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Branch Infrastructure, Rooms and Procured Stock

Everything in the system hangs off a **Branch**. A `ProgramManager` registers one by supplying a display `name` and a `branch_code` — a short code of between 2 and 8 uppercase characters that must be unique across all branches; a collision is rejected with `ERR_BRANCH_CODE_DUPLICATE`. Two operational ceilings are set at the same time and never move afterwards without an explicit edit: `overlapping_program_capacity`, an integer of at least 1 that caps how many of this branch's confirmed programs may have intersecting public windows (it is a staffing ceiling, not a room count), and `per_program_fee_cap_cents`, a non-negative ceiling on what any single program at this branch may pay one presenter. A newly created branch reports `programs_over_budget_count` as 0; that field is a roll-up the branch never accepts as input, and it is recomputed only when one of the branch's programs changes its budget posture.

Rooms belong to a branch and are created inside it, so a room's `branch_id` is taken from the creation path and any `branch_id` in the body is ignored. A room carries a `room_name` label, a `capacity_seats` nominal seated capacity of at least 1, and two non-negative buffer minimums that the physical space genuinely requires: `min_setup_minutes` before a program and `min_teardown_minutes` after it. Its `accessibility_features` array holds any subset — possibly empty — of `WHEELCHAIR_ACCESSIBLE`, `HEARING_LOOP`, `ADJUSTABLE_LIGHTING` and `QUIET_ZONE`. A room's `status` is either `AVAILABLE` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and defaults to `AVAILABLE` when the creating request omits it; a room that is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` cannot be booked, and an attempt to reserve it is rejected with `ERR_ROOM_OUT_OF_SERVICE`.

Physical stock is tracked in **inventory lots**, and a lot is the unit of procurement. A `ProgramManager` procures one by naming the `branch_id` that paid for and stores it, a `category` of either `PRIZE` or `SUPPLY`, the `sku` that identifies what is in the lot, a non-negative `unit_cost_cents`, a `quantity_procured` of at least 1, and a `reuse_pool` boolean that decides whether other branches may draw from this lot at all. An optional free-text `description` may accompany the lot and is null when omitted. The `sku` is the join key that matters: a `SUPPLY` lot is matched against a program's `supply_sku` and a `PRIZE` lot against an incentive tier's `prize_sku`.

Procurement fixes the lot's accounting. `quantity_procured` is never mutated for the life of the lot; instead the lot publishes a three-way split, starting with `quantity_available` equal to `quantity_procured`, `quantity_reserved` at 0 and `quantity_issued` at 0, and a `status` of `ACTIVE`. Units move from available to reserved when a program's supply draw or a prize award holds them, and from reserved to issued when that hold is settled permanently. The lot's `status` is derived strictly from availability: it becomes `DEPLETED` the moment `quantity_available` reaches 0 in a request, and returns to `ACTIVE` the moment a release or return takes `quantity_available` back above 0. Procuring a fresh lot is itself one of the events that can free capacity for waiting readers — a new lot immediately promotes any deferred prize award whose tier's `prize_sku` matches it and whose earning reader may draw from it, oldest deferred award first by `created_at`, which raises the owning challenge's committed prize money.

Reads over this reference data are how callers reach a branch's children, since nothing in this API is nested more than one action segment deep. Rooms may be listed filtered by `branch_id` and by `status`; inventory lots may be listed filtered by `branch_id`, `category`, `sku` and `status`, and that listing returns lots in `created_at` ascending order, which is exactly the order the allocation rules walk them in.

### 3.2. Drafting and Publishing a Program

A **Program** is a single scheduled offering, and it is drafted before it is published. Either a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ProgramManager` may draft one, naming the owning `branch_id`, a `title`, an `age_group` drawn from `EARLY_CHILDHOOD`, `SCHOOL_AGE`, `TEEN`, `ADULT` and `ALL_AGES`, a `delivery_mode` of either `IN_BRANCH` or `PARTNER_SITE`, a `registration_policy` of either `REGISTRATION` or `DROP_IN`, the public window as `start_utc` and `end_utc`, an `attendance_target` of at least 1, an `attendance_cap` that must be at least as large as the target, the `supply_sku` of the materials kit the program will draw at confirmation time, a non-negative `supply_units_per_attendee`, and the two budget ceilings `materials_budget_cents` and `presenter_fee_budget_cents`, both non-negative. A program that belongs to the summer reading season also carries `challenge_id`; a program outside the season leaves it null, which is its default.

The single most consequential derived field is set at draft time and never supplied by a client. `serves_minors` is true whenever `age_group` is `EARLY_CHILDHOOD`, `SCHOOL_AGE`, `TEEN` or `ALL_AGES`, and is false only for `ADULT`. An `ALL_AGES` story time serves minors exactly as much as a `SCHOOL_AGE` one does, and the entire background-screening regime keys off this flag.

Partner-site delivery brings its own required trio. When `delivery_mode` is `PARTNER_SITE`, the draft must carry a `partner_type` other than `NONE` — one of `SCHOOL`, `PARKS` or `NONPROFIT` — a non-empty `partner_org_name`, and a `partner_insurance_expires_on` calendar date recording when the partner's liability certificate lapses. When `delivery_mode` is `IN_BRANCH` the mirror image is required: `partner_type` must be `NONE` (which is also its default) and both `partner_org_name` and `partner_insurance_expires_on` must be null. Either form of incoherence is rejected with `ERR_PARTNER_FIELDS_REQUIRED`. Separately, the drafted window must satisfy `start_utc` strictly before `end_utc`, and both instants must lie at or after the logical clock at the time of drafting; a violation is `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_WINDOW`.

Accessibility promises are made at draft time too. `accessibility_accommodations` is an array holding any subset of `ASL_INTERPRETER`, `SENSORY_QUIET_HOUR`, `WHEELCHAIR_LAYOUT` and `LARGE_PRINT_HANDOUTS`, and it defaults to empty. These are commitments to the public, and section 3.3 shows how they constrain which room can host the program, how much setup time must be claimed, and how many seats actually exist.

A freshly drafted program reports `operational_status` of `DRAFT` and `compliance_status` of `UNVERIFIED` — the two dimensions that advance independently throughout its life. Its counters start clean: `registered_count` 0, `registrant_references` empty, `confirmed_room_capacity` 0, `supply_draw_lines` empty, `materials_committed_cents` 0, `materials_spent_cents` 0, `presenter_fee_committed_cents` 0, `presenter_fee_spent_cents` 0, `closure_reason` null, and the three close-out inputs `verified_registrant_attendance`, `drop_in_headcount` and `surveys_collected` all 0 alongside the derived `gross_attendance`, `no_show_count`, `attendance_attainment_funder_pct`, `attendance_attainment_internal_pct` and `survey_response_rate_pct`. Because nothing is yet encumbered, `materials_available_cents` equals `materials_budget_cents`, `budget_variance_cents` equals the sum of `materials_budget_cents` and `presenter_fee_budget_cents`, and `budget_posture` is `WITHIN_BUDGET`.

Publishing moves `DRAFT` to `SCHEDULED`, and either a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ProgramManager` may do it. The request may optionally supply a fresh `start_utc`, a fresh `end_utc`, or both, which reschedules the program in the same step; supplying neither publishes the drafted window unchanged. Whatever the window ends up being, it must still satisfy `start_utc` strictly before `end_utc` with both instants at or after the logical clock, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_WINDOW`, and `attendance_cap` must still be at least `attendance_target`. Two further gates apply, split by delivery mode: an `IN_BRANCH` program must already hold a room reservation in either `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status, or the publication is rejected with `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_ROOM`, whereas a `PARTNER_SITE` program never holds a room reservation at all — its space gate is instead that `partner_insurance_expires_on` falls on or after the calendar date of `end_utc`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_PARTNER_INSURANCE_LAPSED`.

Rescheduling has a deliberate cost. When the publishing request actually moves the window by supplying `start_utc` or `end_utc`, every reservation of this program still in `HELD` status is moved to `RELEASED`: a moved program must be re-booked, because the old booking no longer covers the new public window. Publishing an already-`SCHEDULED` program is accepted and is the ordinary way to reschedule. Every accepted publication increments the program's `version` by 1, and the operation answers 200 OK with the updated program. Attempting to publish a program whose `operational_status` is anything other than `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`.

Programs may be listed filtered by `branch_id`, by `challenge_id`, by `operational_status` and by `compliance_status`, which is how a coordinator finds the calendar for one branch or the season roster for one challenge.

### 3.3. Reserving Space: Accessibility, Buffers and Occupancy

Booking a room is a two-step affair — hold, then confirm — and both steps are open to a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ProgramManager`. Holding a room requires the program to be in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` (anything else is `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`), and the request names the `room_id` to be reserved, the booked window as `start_utc` and `end_utc`, the `setup_minutes` claimed before that window and the `teardown_minutes` claimed after it — both non-negative — together with the `expected_program_version` echo described in section 2.9. The reservation's `program_id` comes from the path, never from the body.

Only one live reservation per program is meaningful, so a hold that lands while the program already carries a `HELD` reservation moves that earlier one to `SUPERSEDED` and installs the newcomer as the program's `HELD` reservation. A program that already carries a `CONFIRMED` reservation is a different matter: the create is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`, because the confirmed booking is what the program's capacity is derived from and it must be released deliberately rather than displaced silently. The reserved room must itself be `AVAILABLE`; a room whose `status` is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` yields `ERR_ROOM_OUT_OF_SERVICE`.

Accessibility promises are checked against the room's physical fittings before anything else about the booking is examined. If the program's `accessibility_accommodations` contain `WHEELCHAIR_LAYOUT`, the room's `accessibility_features` must contain `WHEELCHAIR_ACCESSIBLE`. If they contain `ASL_INTERPRETER`, the room must offer `HEARING_LOOP`. If they contain `SENSORY_QUIET_HOUR`, the room must offer `QUIET_ZONE`. `LARGE_PRINT_HANDOUTS` imposes no room requirement, since it is a print decision rather than a fitting. Any unmet requirement is rejected with `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_UNSUPPORTED`.

Buffer floors come next, and they combine two independent minimums. The claimed `setup_minutes` must be at least the greater of the room's own `min_setup_minutes` and — whenever the program's `accessibility_accommodations` array is non-empty, whatever it contains — an accessible-setup floor of 30 minutes; the claimed `teardown_minutes` must be at least the room's `min_teardown_minutes`. A program carrying any accommodation at all in a room whose `min_setup_minutes` is 10 must therefore still claim at least 30 minutes of setup. Falling short on either buffer is `ERR_SETUP_BUFFER_TOO_SHORT`.

The booking must also cover the program: the reservation's `start_utc` must be at or before the program's `start_utc` and its `end_utc` at or after the program's `end_utc`, since the room has to be the library's for the whole advertised event. A booking that leaves either end of the public window uncovered is rejected with `ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_MISMATCH`.

Only then is the room's calendar consulted. The reservation's occupancy is derived and stored as two fields: `occupancy_start_utc` is `start_utc` moved earlier by `setup_minutes`, and `occupancy_end_utc` is `end_utc` moved later by `teardown_minutes` — the two buffers are independent and need not be symmetric. As stated in section 2.11, occupancy is a **half-open** interval that includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, and it is compared against the occupancy intervals of every other reservation of the *same* `room_id` whose status is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`. Reservations in `RELEASED` or `SUPERSEDED` status are invisible to this check. Because the interval is half-open, a reservation whose occupancy begins at exactly the instant another's occupancy ends is accepted; a reservation that begins one minute before that instant collides and is rejected with `ERR_ROOM_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

The reservation also freezes the seat count at the moment it is taken. Its `effective_capacity_seats` is the reserved room's `capacity_seats` when the program promises no wheelchair layout. When `WHEELCHAIR_LAYOUT` is among the program's accommodations, a fifteen-hundredths seat-loss rate applies: the effective figure is the room's `capacity_seats` multiplied by the remaining eighty-five hundredths, then taken down to the next whole seat by flooring — a 60-seat room yields 51 effective seats, and so does a 61-seat room. Because the figure is stored on the reservation, a later edit to the room's `capacity_seats` does not retroactively adjust a booking already taken.

An accepted hold answers 201 Created with the new reservation in `HELD` status and increments the owning program's `version` by 1. Confirming that hold is a separate call carrying no body: it requires the reservation to be `HELD` (a reservation in any other status yields `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`), the owning program to still be in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, the room to still be `AVAILABLE`, and the occupancy interval to still be free of any other `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` reservation of the same room — the same half-open comparison, re-run because the room's calendar may have moved since the hold was taken. Confirmation moves the reservation to `CONFIRMED`, copies its `effective_capacity_seats` onto the program as `confirmed_room_capacity`, increments the program's `version` by 1, and answers 200 OK. That copied number immediately tightens what registrations may accept.

A reservation's four statuses are `HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `RELEASED` and `SUPERSEDED`, and the last two are terminal. Beyond superseding, three system-driven movements exist. When the owning program becomes `WEATHER_CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, every reservation of that program in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status moves to `RELEASED` and the program's `confirmed_room_capacity` returns to 0. When a confirmed program is reverted to `SCHEDULED` by the suspension cascade, its `CONFIRMED` reservation falls back to `HELD` and `confirmed_room_capacity` returns to 0 — the room stays occupied against competing bookings, but the program no longer satisfies its confirmation gate. And when the program's window is moved by a reschedule, its `HELD` reservations are `RELEASED` as described in section 3.2. Reservations may be listed filtered by `program_id`, by `room_id` and by `status`.

### 3.4. Registration Intake and the Tighter of Two Ceilings

Registration is only meaningful for programs that promised it. When a `ProgramCoordinator` or `ProgramManager` registers a party, the program's `registration_policy` must be `REGISTRATION`; attempting to register against a `DROP_IN` program is rejected with `ERR_DROP_IN_NO_REGISTRATION`, because a drop-in program counts heads at the door instead. The program must be in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status — a `DRAFT` program is not yet public and a closed or cancelled one is past registering — and anything else is `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`.

Compliance interacts with registration more gently than it does with confirmation. A program whose `compliance_status` is `BLOCKED` stops accepting registrations and reports `ERR_PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED`; a program whose `compliance_status` is merely `PENDING_SCREENING` keeps accepting them, on the reasoning that a screening still in flight is not yet a reason to turn the public away.

The request carries a `registrant_reference` — the library-card or household reference that identifies the registering party — and an optional `seats` count that defaults to 1 when omitted. Registration closes at the moment the program starts: the logical clock must be strictly before the program's `start_utc`, and a request at or after that instant is rejected with `ERR_REGISTRATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The same party may not register twice, so a `registrant_reference` already present in the program's `registrant_references` array is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRANT`.

The seat ceiling is the tighter of two numbers, and this is where a wheelchair layout becomes visible to the public. When the program has a `CONFIRMED` room reservation — that is, when `confirmed_room_capacity` is greater than 0 — the ceiling is the smaller of `attendance_cap` and `confirmed_room_capacity`; when no room is confirmed, as is always the case for a `PARTNER_SITE` program, the ceiling is `attendance_cap` alone. The request is accepted only if `registered_count` plus the requested `seats` stays at or below that ceiling; otherwise it is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_AT_CAPACITY`. A program with an `attendance_cap` of 55 whose confirmed room yields 51 effective seats therefore refuses the registration that would take it to 52, even though its own cap has room to spare.

An accepted registration adds `seats` to `registered_count`, appends the `registrant_reference` to `registrant_references`, increments the program's `version` by 1, and answers 200 OK with the updated program so the caller can read the new count directly. `registered_count` is deliberately monotone: it is a running total of seats ever accepted and is never decremented, which is what makes the no-show arithmetic at close-out meaningful.

### 3.5. Presenter Contracting: Negotiation, Pricing and Execution

Presenters are registered before they are contracted, and either a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ProgramManager` may register one. A presenter record carries a `legal_name` exactly as it will appear on the contract, a `presenter_type` of either `INDIVIDUAL` or `AGENCY`, the calendar date `insurance_certificate_expires_on` on which their liability certificate lapses, and a non-negative `quoted_fee_cents` standing quote. That quote is a reference the coordinator opens negotiation from; it never enters the payable arithmetic. A new presenter reports `engagement_status` of `PROSPECT`, `screening_decision`, `screening_status` and `screening_tier` all `NONE`, `screening_decided_on` and `screening_valid_until` both null, and `executed_contract_count` of 0.

A **presenter contract** binds one presenter to one program. Either coordinating role may draft one by naming the `presenter_id`, the `program_id` being staffed, and the non-negative `round_trip_miles` that will be reimbursed. A suspended presenter cannot be contracted at all, so a draft against a presenter whose `engagement_status` is `SUSPENDED` is rejected with `ERR_PRESENTER_SUSPENDED`. The new contract reports `status` of `DRAFT`, `negotiation_round` of 0, `offered_fee_cents`, `countered_fee_cents` and `agreed_fee_cents` all null, and `travel_fee_cents`, `escort_fee_cents`, `insurance_rider_fee_cents`, `weekend_premium_cents`, `total_fee_cents` and `kill_fee_cents` all 0, with `offered_by_user_id` and `executed_by_user_id` null.

Drafting a contract is also the event that pulls the program's compliance dimension into motion, and it does so without touching the operational dimension at all — the two dimensions advance on disjoint triggers. If the program's `compliance_status` is `UNVERIFIED`, the program's `serves_minors` is true, and the contracted presenter's `screening_status` is not `ACTIVE`, then `compliance_status` becomes `PENDING_SCREENING`: the library has committed to putting an unscreened adult in a room with children and the program is not confirmable until that is resolved. If the program does not serve minors, or the presenter's screening is already `ACTIVE`, `compliance_status` stays `UNVERIFIED`.

Negotiation is a bounded loop. Issuing the library's offer requires the contract to be in `DRAFT` or `COUNTERED` status (anything else is `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`), requires the presenter not to be `SUSPENDED`, and requires the owning program to still be in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` — a program past scheduling cannot have its staffing renegotiated, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. The offer carries `offered_fee_cents`, which is stored, moves the contract to `OFFERED`, increases `negotiation_round` by 1, and stamps `offered_by_user_id` with the issuing `X-User-Id`. That stamp is overwritten on every offer, so it always names the author of the *standing* offer. A presenter's reply is recorded as a counter, which requires the contract to be `OFFERED`, requires the presenter not to be `SUSPENDED`, stores `countered_fee_cents`, and moves the contract to `COUNTERED` while leaving `negotiation_round` untouched. The library may then offer again, and the `OFFERED → COUNTERED → OFFERED` cycle is the negotiation. Its termination condition is a hard ceiling of three offers: the fourth offer on a contract that has already consumed three rounds is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Both offering and countering increment the contract's `version` by 1 and answer 200 OK.

Execution is where the contract becomes money, and it is reserved to a `ProgramManager`. The request carries no body. Beyond the segregation-of-duties rule of section 2.1 — the `X-User-Id` executing must differ from `offered_by_user_id`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the standing offer must be live (`status` must be `OFFERED`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`), the presenter must not be `SUSPENDED`, and the owning program must still be in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`. Then three substantive gates apply in this order. First, if the program's `serves_minors` is true, the presenter's `screening_status` must be exactly `ACTIVE` at the moment of execution; a presenter whose screening is absent, pending, awaiting adjudication, expired or blocked is refused with `ERR_PRESENTER_SCREENING_REQUIRED`. Second, the presenter's `insurance_certificate_expires_on` must fall on or after the calendar date of the program's `end_utc`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PRESENTER_INSURANCE_LAPSED`. Third, the presenter must not already be committed elsewhere.

That last gate uses the presenter's **engagement window**, which is the program's public window widened by a 90-minute travel buffer on each side: it runs from 90 minutes before the program's `start_utc` to 90 minutes after its `end_utc`, and as section 2.11 states it is **closed** at both ends. The candidate contract's window is compared against the engagement window of every other contract of the *same* presenter whose `status` is `EXECUTED` or `COMPLETED`; contracts in `DRAFT`, `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED`, `BLOCKED`, `CANCELLED_WEATHER` or `CANCELLED_LIBRARY` status hold no window and are ignored. Because the interval is closed, two engagement windows that share only their boundary instant conflict, and the execution is rejected with `ERR_PRESENTER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. A presenter finishing at 15:00 with a 90-minute buffer is therefore unavailable for anything whose own buffered window reaches back to 16:30 or earlier.

Pricing then runs, in a strictly ordered sequence whose order is externally observable. The base is the standing `offered_fee_cents` — never `countered_fee_cents`, which is a record of what the presenter asked for and never a payable amount. Next, `travel_fee_cents` is a two-band mileage reimbursement over `round_trip_miles`: the first 20 miles of the round trip are not reimbursed at all; mileage above 20 and up to 60 forms the first billable band and pays 65 cents per mile; mileage above 60 forms the second band and pays 45 cents per mile. Precisely, the first band's billable mileage is the round-trip mileage capped at 60 and then reduced by 20, treated as zero if that would be negative; the second band's billable mileage is the round-trip mileage reduced by 60, likewise treated as zero if negative; and `travel_fee_cents` is the first band's mileage multiplied by 65 plus the second band's mileage multiplied by 45. A 20-mile round trip reimburses nothing, 21 miles reimburses 65, 60 miles reimburses 2600, and 61 miles reimburses 2645.

Three surcharges follow. `escort_fee_cents` is 4000 when the program's `serves_minors` is true and 0 otherwise, covering the staff escort a minors program requires. `insurance_rider_fee_cents` is 2500 when the presenter's `insurance_certificate_expires_on` falls strictly earlier than the calendar date 30 days after the program's end date — that is, when the certificate will lapse inside a 30-day window following the program — and 0 otherwise. Note that this is a *narrower* test than the outright lapse check above: a certificate that comfortably covers the program but expires three weeks after it still attracts the rider. The three components are then added to the base to give a pre-premium subtotal: base plus `travel_fee_cents` plus `escort_fee_cents` plus `insurance_rider_fee_cents`.

The weekend premium sits on that whole subtotal, not on the base. When the program's `start_utc` falls on a Saturday or a Sunday evaluated in UTC, `weekend_premium_cents` is one tenth of the pre-premium subtotal, rounded half up to a whole number of cents; on any other weekday it is 0. Finally `total_fee_cents` is the pre-premium subtotal plus `weekend_premium_cents`. Applying the premium to the base alone instead of to the subtotal produces a visibly different total, and the published component fields make the difference detectable.

The payable total is never silently trimmed to fit. `total_fee_cents` is compared against a ceiling that is the smaller of a system-wide cap of 250000 cents and the owning branch's `per_program_fee_cap_cents`, and a total above that ceiling is rejected outright with `ERR_FEE_CAP_EXCEEDED` — the contract stays `OFFERED` and nothing is stored.

An accepted execution does a great deal at once. `agreed_fee_cents` is set to the `offered_fee_cents` that was standing; `travel_fee_cents`, `escort_fee_cents`, `insurance_rider_fee_cents`, `weekend_premium_cents` and `total_fee_cents` are all stored; the contract moves to `EXECUTED` and `executed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-Id`. On the presenter, `executed_contract_count` increases by 1 and `engagement_status` becomes `ACTIVE` — this is the `PROSPECT → ACTIVE` transition, and it is entirely independent of the screening dimension, so a presenter who has never been screened at all may sit at `ACTIVE` for as long as none of their programs serves minors. On the program, `compliance_status` becomes `CLEARED` (from either `UNVERIFIED` or `PENDING_SCREENING`), `presenter_fee_committed_cents` increases by `total_fee_cents`, and `budget_variance_cents` and `budget_posture` are re-derived as described in section 3.7; if that re-derivation flips the posture from `WITHIN_BUDGET` to `OVER_BUDGET`, the owning branch's `programs_over_budget_count` increases by 1. The contract's `version` and the program's `version` each increment by 1, and the answer is 200 OK with the contract.

A contract's full status set is `DRAFT`, `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED`, `EXECUTED`, `BLOCKED`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED_WEATHER` and `CANCELLED_LIBRARY`, of which the last three are terminal. An `EXECUTED` contract becomes `COMPLETED` when its program closes out. The `BLOCKED` status and the routes out of it belong to screening, and are covered next. Contracts may be listed filtered by `presenter_id`, by `program_id` and by `status`, and that listing is the only channel through which a caller can observe contracts that a cascade — rather than the caller — has mutated.

### 3.6. Background Screening, Adjudication and the Compliance Dimension

Background screening is the exclusive province of a `ScreeningOfficer`, and it is a cycle rather than a one-way street. A presenter's screening state is carried in four fields that move together — `screening_tier`, `screening_decision`, `screening_decided_on` and `screening_valid_until` — plus a fifth, `screening_status`, that is derived from them and the logical clock and is what every other rule in the system actually consults.

Opening a screening request records the `screening_tier` being run: `NAME_CHECK` for a database name check or `FINGERPRINT` for a fingerprint-based check. A request is permitted only when the presenter's current `screening_status` is `NONE`, `EXPIRED` or `BLOCKED`; posting one while a screening is already `PENDING`, awaiting adjudication or `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID`. The request sets `screening_decision` to `PENDING`, stores the supplied tier, clears `screening_decided_on` and `screening_valid_until` back to null, leaves `screening_status` at `PENDING`, increments the presenter's `version` by 1 and answers 200 OK with the presenter. Because `EXPIRED` and `BLOCKED` are both admissible starting points, this same operation is the renewal path after a lapse and the re-screen path after a denial.

Recording a result requires `screening_decision` to be exactly `PENDING`, and carries a `result` of either `CLEAR` or `FLAGGED` together with the `decided_on` calendar date the outcome was reached. A `CLEAR` result sets `screening_decision` to `CLEAR`, stores `decided_on` into `screening_decided_on`, and derives `screening_valid_until` as described below; `screening_status` then reads `ACTIVE` for as long as the clock's date has not passed that expiry.

Validity is a plain calendar-day addition whose length depends on the tier: a `NAME_CHECK` decision is good for 365 days and a `FINGERPRINT` decision for 730 days, counted forward from `screening_decided_on` as whole calendar days, and the result is stored as the ISO date `screening_valid_until`. A name check decided on 2026-06-01 is therefore valid until 2027-06-01; a fingerprint check decided the same day is valid until 2028-05-31, because the 730-day span crosses the 2028 leap day. Comparison is done at date granularity, not instant granularity: with the clock anywhere inside 2027-06-01 the name check above is still `ACTIVE`, and it becomes `EXPIRED` the moment the clock's date rolls to 2027-06-02.

`screening_status` is derived, never stored as an independent input, and it takes exactly one of six values. It is `NONE` when `screening_decision` is `NONE`; `PENDING` when the decision is `PENDING`; `PENDING_ADJUDICATION` when the decision is `FLAGGED`; `BLOCKED` when the decision is `DENIED`; `ACTIVE` when the decision is `CLEAR` or `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS` and the clock's date is at or before `screening_valid_until`; and `EXPIRED` when the decision is `CLEAR` or `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS` and the clock's date is past `screening_valid_until`. There is no screening history entity: a re-screen overwrites the same decision fields, so the presenter always carries exactly one screening story.

A `FLAGGED` result is the trigger for the system's widest cascade. It sets `screening_decision` to `FLAGGED`, stores `decided_on`, clears `screening_valid_until` to null, and leaves `screening_status` reading `PENDING_ADJUDICATION`. Its first cascade link sets the presenter's `engagement_status` to `SUSPENDED` — the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` transition when the presenter had already worked, and the `PROSPECT → SUSPENDED` transition when no contract of theirs has ever been executed. The second link, caused by the first, moves every contract of this presenter whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `EXECUTED` to `BLOCKED`; where such a contract was `EXECUTED`, its `total_fee_cents` is subtracted from the owning program's `presenter_fee_committed_cents`, because a blocked fee is no longer committed money. The third link, caused by the second, sets each affected contract's program `compliance_status` to `BLOCKED`, from either `CLEARED` or `PENDING_SCREENING`. The fourth link, caused by the third, reverts each such program whose `operational_status` was `CONFIRMED` back to `SCHEDULED`: for every entry in that program's `supply_draw_lines` the drawn units are returned from the lot's `quantity_reserved` to its `quantity_available`, any lot that had gone `DEPLETED` returns to `ACTIVE` now that it has stock again, `supply_draw_lines` is emptied and `materials_committed_cents` drops to 0 with `materials_available_cents` re-derived, the program's `CONFIRMED` reservation falls back to `HELD` and `confirmed_room_capacity` returns to 0, and the program's `budget_variance_cents` and `budget_posture` are re-derived with the branch's `programs_over_budget_count` adjusted only if the posture actually flipped. The presenter's `version` increments by 1 and the answer is 200 OK with the presenter — every other mutation in the cascade is observable only through the contract, program and lot listings.

Adjudication resolves a flag and requires `screening_decision` to be exactly `FLAGGED`; anything else is `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID`. It carries a `decision` of either `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS` or `DENIED`, plus the `decided_on` date. A `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS` adjudication sets `screening_decision` to `CLEARED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, stores `decided_on`, derives `screening_valid_until` by the same tier-dependent calendar-day rule using the tier of the most recent request, and leaves `screening_status` reading `ACTIVE`. It restores `engagement_status` to `ACTIVE` when the presenter's `executed_contract_count` is greater than 0, and to `PROSPECT` when that count is 0 — the two sibling transitions out of `SUSPENDED`. Every contract of this presenter still in `BLOCKED` status is restored to `OFFERED`, with `negotiation_round` and `offered_by_user_id` both preserved exactly as they were and `total_fee_cents` left at 0; a restored contract must be executed again, and until it is, it commits no money. Each such contract's program moves its `compliance_status` to `PENDING_SCREENING` — never straight back to `CLEARED` — so the program must be re-executed and then re-confirmed before it can run.

A `DENIED` adjudication is the opposite settlement. It sets `screening_decision` to `DENIED`, clears `screening_valid_until` to null, leaves `screening_status` reading `BLOCKED`, keeps `engagement_status` at `SUSPENDED`, and leaves every contract that the flag had already blocked in `BLOCKED` status. Nothing is restored and no money moves. The only route forward is a fresh screening request, which `BLOCKED` admits — closing the cycle `CLEAR` to `EXPIRED` to `PENDING`, and `DENIED` to `PENDING`. Both adjudication outcomes increment the presenter's `version` by 1 and answer 200 OK.

The compliance dimension of a program therefore has exactly four values — `UNVERIFIED`, `PENDING_SCREENING`, `CLEARED` and `BLOCKED` — and no terminal state among them. It starts `UNVERIFIED`, moves to `PENDING_SCREENING` when an unscreened presenter is contracted for a minors program, moves to `CLEARED` from either of those two on a successful contract execution, moves to `BLOCKED` from either `CLEARED` or `PENDING_SCREENING` when one of the program's contracts is blocked by a suspension, and moves from `BLOCKED` back to `PENDING_SCREENING` when a conditional clearance restores that contract.

### 3.7. Confirming a Program: Staffing Load, the Supply Draw and the Materials Commitment

Confirmation is the moment a scheduled program becomes an operational commitment: it consumes branch staffing, it draws physical stock out of inventory, and it encumbers money. Only a `ProgramManager` may confirm, and the request carries no body — everything it needs is already on the program. Seven gates stand in front of it, and they are evaluated in the precedence order of section 2.7 so that a program failing several of them always reports the same single error.

The program must be in `SCHEDULED` status — confirmation is reachable only from a published program, so a `DRAFT`, already-`CONFIRMED`, closed or cancelled program is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. Next the compliance dimension is consulted: `compliance_status` must be neither `PENDING_SCREENING` nor `BLOCKED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PROGRAM_COMPLIANCE_BLOCKED`. Note what this admits and what it refuses. `UNVERIFIED` passes — a program that never needed a presenter at all is perfectly confirmable. `CLEARED` passes. But a program that drafted, offered or countered a contract for a minors program and never executed it is sitting at `PENDING_SCREENING`, and it cannot be confirmed: the library will not commit a room and stock to a session whose presenter has not cleared. This is stricter than the registration gate, which as section 3.4 notes refuses only `BLOCKED`.

The space gate then splits on `delivery_mode`, and the two branches are mutually exclusive. A `PARTNER_SITE` program's space is the partner's, so its gate is the paperwork: `partner_insurance_expires_on` must fall on or after the calendar date of `end_utc`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PARTNER_INSURANCE_LAPSED`. An `IN_BRANCH` program skips that rule entirely and must instead have exactly one reservation of its own in `CONFIRMED` status; a program holding only a `HELD` reservation, or one whose reservation was released or superseded, is rejected with `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_ROOM`. A `PARTNER_SITE` program skips the room rule entirely, and never holds a reservation at all.

**Branch staffing load.** A branch can only run so many programs at once, and `overlapping_program_capacity` is that ceiling. The load is computed over the program's **public window**, which runs from `start_utc` to `end_utc` with no setup or teardown buffers at all and, as section 2.11 records, is **closed** at both ends. The peers that count are the programs of the same `branch_id` whose `operational_status` is exactly `CONFIRMED` and whose own public window intersects the candidate's. Programs in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `CLOSED_OUT`, `CANCELLED` or `WEATHER_CLOSED` status consume no staff and are not peers. `PARTNER_SITE` programs *are* counted — outreach still draws on branch staff to run it. The load is the number of such peers plus one, because the candidate counts itself; a load above `overlapping_program_capacity` is rejected with `ERR_BRANCH_STAFFING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Two properties of this rule deserve stating outright. It is an aggregate ceiling, not a pairwise check: with a capacity of 2, a program from 09:00 to 11:00 confirms at load 1, one from 10:00 to 12:00 confirms at load 2, and one from 10:30 to 11:30 computes load 3 and is refused even though it individually overlaps each peer only once. And because the interval is closed, a program starting at exactly 11:00 conflicts with a peer ending at exactly 11:00 — the opposite of the room rule, where an 11:00 start slots cleanly against an 11:00 occupancy end.

**The supply draw.** The units the program needs are its `supply_units_per_attendee` multiplied by its `attendance_target` — the target, not the cap, and not the number actually registered. Those units are drawn from `SUPPLY` lots whose `sku` equals the program's `supply_sku` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, considered in a strict two-bucket order: first the lots belonging to the program's own `branch_id`, oldest `created_at` first; then the lots of *other* branches whose `reuse_pool` is true, again oldest `created_at` first. A lot at another branch whose `reuse_pool` is false is never a candidate no matter how much stock it holds. Lots that are `DEPLETED` are excluded.

The draw is a waterfall. Walking the candidates in that order while units are still needed, each lot contributes the smaller of the units still needed and its own `quantity_available`. For each contributing lot one entry is appended to the program's `supply_draw_lines`, recording the `lot_id`, the `units` taken, the lot's `unit_cost_cents`, a `surcharge_cents`, and a `line_total_cents`. The line's base cost is the units taken multiplied by the lot's unit cost. `surcharge_cents` is 8 percent of that base cost, rounded half up to whole cents, when the lot belongs to a *different* branch than the program, and exactly 0 when the lot is the program's own; `line_total_cents` is the base plus the surcharge. **The surcharge is rounded once per line and never once over the combined base** — that distinction is observable in the stored line totals and in the committed total that follows.

If the candidates run out before the required units are covered, the confirmation is refused with `ERR_SUPPLY_INVENTORY_INSUFFICIENT` and **nothing is allocated**: no partial draw is ever persisted, no lot is touched, and `supply_draw_lines` stays empty. The draw subtotal, used by the next step, is the sum of every line's `line_total_cents`. As an illustration, a program needing 49 units whose own branch holds a lot with 30 available at 200 cents each draws a line of 6000 with no surcharge, then takes the remaining 19 from a cross-branch reuse lot at 150 cents each: base 2850, surcharge 8 percent of 2850 rounded half up which is 228, line total 3078 — a draw subtotal of 9078.

**The materials commitment.** Immediately after the draw, a contingency is added on top of the post-surcharge draw subtotal. The rate depends on the size of the draw, and the relationship is inverted from what one might expect: a draw subtotal *below* 20000 cents carries a 10 percent contingency, while a subtotal of 20000 cents or more carries only 5 percent, because small kits are the ones whose costs move unpredictably. The contingency is that rate applied to the draw subtotal and rounded half up to whole cents — one rounding, on the subtotal, never per line — and `materials_committed_cents` is the draw subtotal plus that contingency. Continuing the illustration, a subtotal of 9078 is below the threshold, so the contingency is 10 percent of 9078 rounded half up, which is 908, and `materials_committed_cents` becomes 9986; a subtotal of exactly 20000 takes the 5 percent rate for a contingency of 1000 and commits 21000. Computing the contingency on the pre-surcharge base instead of on the post-surcharge subtotal gives a visibly different commitment.

If `materials_committed_cents` would exceed the program's `materials_budget_cents`, the confirmation is refused with `ERR_MATERIALS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` and, once again, nothing is allocated — the lots are left exactly as they were. Otherwise `materials_available_cents` is set to `materials_budget_cents` less `materials_committed_cents` less `materials_spent_cents`, which is the form the conservation invariant of section 2.8 takes for materials: the budget always equals available plus committed plus spent.

**Budget posture.** Every write that moves a program's committed or spent money re-derives the program's fiscal position, and confirmation is one such write. The program's total budget is `materials_budget_cents` plus `presenter_fee_budget_cents`. Its total encumbrance is `materials_committed_cents` plus `materials_spent_cents` plus `presenter_fee_committed_cents` plus `presenter_fee_spent_cents`. `budget_variance_cents` is the budget less the encumbrance, and it may go negative — an overrun is recorded, never rejected. `budget_posture` is `OVER_BUDGET` when the variance is strictly negative and `WITHIN_BUDGET` otherwise, so a variance of exactly 0 is `WITHIN_BUDGET`. The owning branch's `programs_over_budget_count` is a roll-up of that posture and is adjusted **only when the posture actually flips**: it increases by 1 on a move from `WITHIN_BUDGET` to `OVER_BUDGET`, decreases by 1 on the reverse move, and is left completely alone when a write changes the variance without changing the posture. A program that is already `OVER_BUDGET` and slides further into deficit must not increment the counter a second time. Cancelled and weather-closed programs still count toward the roll-up while they hold kill fees, because a kill fee is real money.

An accepted confirmation stores `supply_draw_lines`, moves each drawn lot's units from `quantity_available` to `quantity_reserved` (re-deriving `quantity_available` and setting the lot's `status` to `DEPLETED` when it reaches 0), stores `materials_committed_cents` and `materials_available_cents`, sets `confirmed_room_capacity` to the `effective_capacity_seats` of the `CONFIRMED` reservation — or leaves it at 0 for a `PARTNER_SITE` program, which has no room — moves `operational_status` to `CONFIRMED`, re-derives `budget_variance_cents` and `budget_posture` with the branch roll-up adjusted only on a flip, increments the program's `version` by 1, and answers 200 OK with the program.

### 3.8. The Summer Reading Challenge: Ladder, Logging, Corrections and Prize Awards

The summer reading challenge runs alongside the program calendar and shares the same inventory pool, but it has its own lifecycle, its own money and its own participants. A `ProgramManager` creates one by supplying its `name`, the `logging_method` that governs how progress is measured — `MINUTES`, `BOOKS` or `ACTIVITIES` — a `unit_label` that names one unit in human terms, the inclusive logging window `start_on` and `end_on` (the end date may equal but never precede the start date), the `correction_policy` of either `HIGH_WATER` or `LEDGER_TRUTH`, and a non-negative `prize_budget_cents`. A new challenge reports `status` of `PLANNING`, `prize_committed_cents` and `prize_spent_cents` of 0, `prize_available_cents` equal to the full `prize_budget_cents`, `prize_inventory_posture` of `AMPLE`, and `participant_count` and `deferred_award_count` of 0.

The **incentive ladder** is a sequence of tiers appended one at a time, and it may only be extended while the challenge is in `PLANNING`; once the challenge opens, the ladder is locked and a further append is rejected with `ERR_CHALLENGE_STATE_INVALID`. Each tier carries a `tier_index`, a `threshold_units`, a `prize_sku` naming the stock it draws from, and a `prize_units_per_participant` of at least 1. The tier's `challenge_id` is taken from the creation path and any value in the body is ignored. Two ordering constraints hold jointly: `tier_index` must be exactly one greater than the challenge's current highest tier index — so the first tier must be 1, the second 2, and no gap or repeat is ever admitted — and `threshold_units` must be strictly greater than the previous tier's `threshold_units`. Either violation is reported as `ERR_TIER_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. A new tier reports `award_count` and `deferred_count` of 0, and appending one re-derives the challenge's `prize_inventory_posture` against the stock available for the new `prize_sku`.

`prize_inventory_posture` is a standing read on whether the challenge can actually pay out. It is `CONSTRAINED` when at least one of the challenge's tiers currently has no drawable units — that is, when no `ACTIVE` `PRIZE` lot exists that the tier could draw its `prize_sku` from — and `AMPLE` when every tier again has at least one such lot. It is re-derived whenever a prize lot's availability changes: when a lot's `quantity_available` reaches 0 and the lot becomes `DEPLETED`, and again when a released or newly procured unit takes a lot's availability back above 0 and it returns to `ACTIVE`. The posture is a warning surface, never a gate: a `CONSTRAINED` challenge still accepts logging and still grants awards, it simply grants some of them as deferrals.

Opening the challenge is a `ProgramManager` action with no body. It requires `status` of `PLANNING` — reopening or re-opening a reconciled challenge is `ERR_CHALLENGE_STATE_INVALID` — and it requires the ladder to exist: a challenge with no tiers at all is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_HAS_NO_TIERS`, and the tiers that exist must form the gap-free ascending ladder starting at index 1 that the append rule already guarantees. The accepted transition sets `status` to `OPEN`, locks the ladder, increments the challenge's `version` by 1 and answers 200 OK.

**Enrolment.** Either a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ProgramManager` enrols a reader by naming the `challenge_id`, the reader's `home_branch_id`, a `display_name` and an `age_band` of `UNDER_5`, `AGE_5_11`, `AGE_12_17` or `ADULT`. Enrolment is permitted while the challenge is in `PLANNING` or `OPEN` — readers may be signed up before the window opens — and is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_STATE_INVALID` once the challenge is `RECONCILED`. The `home_branch_id` is not decorative: it selects the first bucket in the prize allocation order and therefore decides whether a reader's prizes attract a cross-branch surcharge. A new participant reports `cumulative_units`, `awarded_tier_index`, `eligible_tier_index` and `entry_count` all 0 and `last_entry_on` null, and the challenge's `participant_count` increases by 1. The resulting participant `id` is the value a `Reader` presents as `X-User-Id` when self-logging, which is what makes the ownership rule of section 2.1 enforceable.

Participants may be listed filtered by `challenge_id` and by `home_branch_id`. Incentive tiers may be listed filtered by `challenge_id`, and that listing is returned in ascending `tier_index` order.

**The progress ledger.** A participant's reading history is an append-only ledger of `ReadingLogEntry` records, and as section 2.4 states an entry is never edited: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and the only way to change its effect is to append a `CORRECTION` entry pointing at it. A participant's `cumulative_units` is derived by replaying that ledger in ascending `created_at` order: it is the sum of `units` over every entry whose `entry_type` is `LOG`, plus the signed `units_delta` of every entry whose `entry_type` is `CORRECTION`, with a hard floor of 0 — the total never reads negative even if the corrections outweigh the logs. `eligible_tier_index` is then the highest `tier_index` among the challenge's tiers whose `threshold_units` is less than or equal to `cumulative_units`, and 0 when no tier is met; because the comparison is "less than or equal", a participant sitting at exactly a tier's threshold holds that tier, and one unit short holds nothing. Only the participant's own entries contribute — another reader's entries in the same challenge never do — and the thresholds come from the participant's own challenge's ladder.

**Posting a log entry.** A `Reader`, a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ProgramManager` may post one, carrying the `units` read (which must be greater than 0), the `logged_on` date the reading happened, and a `source` of `SELF_SERVICE`, `STAFF_DESK` or `OUTREACH_SITE`. The entry's `participant_id` comes from the path, its `entry_type` is set by the server to `LOG`, and `recorded_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-Id`. The challenge must be `OPEN` — logging against a `PLANNING` or `RECONCILED` challenge is `ERR_CHALLENGE_STATE_INVALID` — and a `Reader` must be logging against their own participant record.

Four business rules then apply in the order fixed by section 2.7. A `Reader` may only claim `SELF_SERVICE` provenance; a reader posting `STAFF_DESK` or `OUTREACH_SITE` is refused with `ERR_LOG_SOURCE_NOT_PERMITTED`, because those two sources assert that a staff member witnessed the entry. Next, `logged_on` must lie inside the challenge's inclusive window from `start_on` to `end_on`; a date outside it is `ERR_LOG_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`, and both endpoints are admissible. Next, `logged_on` must not be after the logical clock's date; a future-dated entry is `ERR_LOG_DATE_IN_FUTURE`, while an entry dated exactly today is fine. Finally the per-day cap applies: the participant's net `LOG` units for that single `logged_on` date, including the entry being posted and after accounting for any corrections already recorded against that date, may not exceed the cap implied by the challenge's `logging_method` — 300 for `MINUTES`, 10 for `BOOKS` and 5 for `ACTIVITIES`. Exceeding it is `ERR_DAILY_LOG_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The cap is per date, not per entry and not per challenge: three separate 100-minute entries on one date are fine, a fourth is not, and the same three entries spread across three dates are never in tension.

An accepted log entry re-derives the participant's `cumulative_units`, `eligible_tier_index`, `entry_count` (which counts entries of both types) and `last_entry_on` (the greatest `logged_on` across the participant's `LOG` entries only), increments the participant's `version` by 1, and answers 201 Created with the log entry. It may also, in the same request, grant prizes.

**Granting awards.** Whenever a log entry raises `eligible_tier_index` above the participant's `awarded_tier_index`, one `TierAward` is created for *every* tier index in between — strictly above the previously awarded index and up to and including the new eligible index. A single large entry that vaults a reader past three thresholds at once creates three awards, one per tier, in ascending tier order; it never creates only the topmost. `awarded_tier_index` is the highest `tier_index` for which the participant holds an award in `RESERVED`, `DEFERRED` or `ISSUED` status, and 0 when they hold none — so a deferred award still counts as held and is never re-granted.

Each award is priced independently. The units it needs are the tier's `prize_units_per_participant`. Candidate lots are the `PRIZE` lots whose `sku` matches the tier's `prize_sku`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `quantity_available` is at least the units needed — a lot holding fewer units than one award requires is not a candidate at all, because a single award is never split across two lots. The candidates are ordered in two buckets exactly as the supply draw is: first the lots belonging to the participant's `home_branch_id`, oldest `created_at` first, then the lots of other branches whose `reuse_pool` is true, oldest `created_at` first. Other branches' non-reuse lots are never candidates.

If no candidate exists, the award is still created, with `status` of `DEFERRED`, `lot_id` null, and `unit_cost_cents`, `line_cost_cents`, `transfer_surcharge_cents` and `award_total_cents` all 0. A deferred award is a promise the library has recorded and not yet met; it commits no money and holds no stock, and the challenge's `deferred_award_count` and the tier's `deferred_count` reflect it. Otherwise the first candidate is taken: `lot_id` names it, `units` captures the tier's `prize_units_per_participant`, `unit_cost_cents` captures that lot's `unit_cost_cents` at award time, `line_cost_cents` is the units multiplied by that unit cost, `transfer_surcharge_cents` is 8 percent of `line_cost_cents` rounded half up when the lot belongs to a branch other than the participant's `home_branch_id` and exactly 0 when it is the home branch's own lot, and `award_total_cents` is the line cost plus the surcharge. **That surcharge is rounded once per award and never once over the challenge's committed total**: two readers each earning a one-unit prize from a cross-branch lot costing 469 cents each attract 38 cents of surcharge apiece for 507 each and 1014 committed in total, where rounding 8 percent over the combined base of 938 would have given 1013 — and `prize_committed_cents` makes the difference visible.

The award's inventory and money effects follow immediately: the lot moves the award's `units` from `quantity_available` to `quantity_reserved`, and the challenge's `prize_committed_cents` increases by `award_total_cents` with `prize_available_cents` re-derived as `prize_budget_cents` less `prize_committed_cents` less `prize_spent_cents`. A lot whose `quantity_available` reaches exactly 0 becomes `DEPLETED`, and that depletion may in turn flip the challenge's `prize_inventory_posture` to `CONSTRAINED`. Awards are not readable through any creation response — the log entry is what comes back — so the award listing, filterable by `participant_id`, `tier_id`, `lot_id` and `status` and returned in ascending `created_at` order, is the only channel through which cascade-created awards can be observed.

**Deferral and promotion.** A deferred award is not forgotten. Whenever stock for its tier's `prize_sku` becomes available again — because a new lot was procured, or because a revoked or forfeited award released units back into an existing lot — the challenge's `DEFERRED` awards are promoted to `RESERVED` in ascending `created_at` order, and exactly as many are promoted as there are freed units to fill. Promotion is a fresh pricing, not a replay of the original: the promoted award's `lot_id` becomes the lot it drew from, `unit_cost_cents` captures *that* lot's cost, and `line_cost_cents`, `transfer_surcharge_cents` and `award_total_cents` are recomputed by exactly the rule above against the participant's `home_branch_id` — so an award originally deferred when only a cross-branch lot might have served can be promoted at a cheaper local price, or the reverse. The promoting lot moves the units from `quantity_available` to `quantity_reserved` and becomes `DEPLETED` if that empties it, and the challenge's `prize_committed_cents` rises by the recomputed `award_total_cents`. Promotion order is `created_at` ascending over the challenge's deferred awards, never identifier order: the reader who waited longest is served first.

Procuring a new prize lot is therefore a write with a cascade attached. When a `ProgramManager` procures an `InventoryLot` — supplying its `branch_id`, `category` of `PRIZE` or `SUPPLY`, `sku`, `unit_cost_cents`, `quantity_procured` of at least 1, `reuse_pool` flag and optional `description` — the lot reports `quantity_available` equal to `quantity_procured`, `quantity_reserved` and `quantity_issued` of 0, and `status` of `ACTIVE`, and `quantity_procured` is never mutated thereafter. If the new lot is a `PRIZE` lot, every `DEFERRED` award whose tier's `prize_sku` matches it and whose participant may legitimately draw from it — the participant's own branch, or any branch when `reuse_pool` is true — is promoted in ascending `created_at` order up to the number of units the lot can fill, which raises `prize_committed_cents` and may return the challenge's posture to `AMPLE`.

**Corrections.** A correction is the only way to change a ledger's arithmetic, and it is available to staff alone: a `ProgramCoordinator` or a `ProgramManager`, never a `Reader`. It carries the `corrected_entry_id` of the `LOG` entry being adjusted, a non-zero signed `units_delta`, the `expected_participant_version` echo required by section 2.9, and an optional free-text `note` recording the justification. The server sets `entry_type` to `CORRECTION`, copies `logged_on` from the target entry so the correction lands on the same calendar date as the reading it adjusts, and records `recorded_by_user_id`. **The target entry itself is never mutated.**

The stale-version check runs before any payload validation, so a correction whose `expected_participant_version` is behind reports `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` with HTTP 412 even when its `corrected_entry_id` is also wrong. If the echo is current, `corrected_entry_id` must name an entry whose `entry_type` is `LOG`, which belongs to this same participant, and which no earlier `CORRECTION` has already superseded; naming a correction entry, another participant's entry, or an already-corrected entry is `ERR_CORRECTION_TARGET_INVALID`. Then `units_delta` must be non-zero and must leave the target entry's own post-correction units at or above 0 — a correction may reduce an entry to nothing but may not drive a single entry negative — failing which the answer is `ERR_CORRECTION_WOULD_GO_NEGATIVE`. A second correction against the same original entry, posted with the current version, is legitimate and supersedes the first correction's arithmetic; corrections chain through `corrected_entry_id` and are never edited in place.

An accepted correction re-derives `cumulative_units` and `eligible_tier_index`, increments the participant's `version` by 1, and answers 201 Created with the correction entry. What happens to awards next depends entirely on the challenge's `correction_policy`, and the two policies are exact opposites on identical data.

Under `LEDGER_TRUTH`, the ledger is the truth and an award that is no longer earned is taken back. Every `TierAward` of this participant for a tier whose `threshold_units` now exceeds the recomputed `cumulative_units` becomes `REVOKED`, and `awarded_tier_index` falls back to the highest tier still met. A revoked award that had been `RESERVED` returns its `units` from its lot's `quantity_reserved` to `quantity_available` — which returns a `DEPLETED` lot to `ACTIVE` and immediately triggers the deferred-award promotion described above — and decreases the challenge's `prize_committed_cents` by that award's `award_total_cents`, with `prize_available_cents` and `prize_inventory_posture` re-derived. A revoked award that had been `DEFERRED` moves no inventory and no money at all, because a deferred award never held a reserved unit and never committed a cent; only the counts change, with the tier's `deferred_count` and the challenge's `deferred_award_count` each falling by 1. The tier's `award_count`, which counts its `RESERVED` and `ISSUED` awards, falls by 1 for each revoked reserved award.

Under `HIGH_WATER`, an award once granted is never retracted. `cumulative_units` still falls and `eligible_tier_index` still falls with it, but `awarded_tier_index` is monotone non-decreasing and stays where it was, every `TierAward` keeps its existing status, no inventory unit is released, no prize budget is released, and no deferred award is promoted. The divergence is stark and deliberate: with tiers at 60 and 150 units, a reader who logs 100 and then 80 units reaches 180 `cumulative_units`, an `eligible_tier_index` of 2 and awards for tiers 1 and 2; a subsequent correction of minus 50 leaves `cumulative_units` at 130 and `eligible_tier_index` at 1 under both policies, but under `LEDGER_TRUTH` the tier-2 award becomes `REVOKED` and `awarded_tier_index` becomes 1, while under `HIGH_WATER` the tier-2 award stays `RESERVED` and `awarded_tier_index` stays 2 — identical requests, divergent published state.

Log entries may be listed in ascending `created_at` order filtered by `participant_id`, by `entry_type` and by `corrected_entry_id`; that last filter is how a caller finds the correction that superseded a given entry. Inventory lots may be listed in ascending `created_at` order filtered by `branch_id`, `category`, `sku` and `status`, which is how a caller observes the allocation order that both the supply draw and the prize award depend on.

### 3.9. Endings: Close-Out, Weather Closure, Cancellation and Season Reconciliation

Every program ends in exactly one of three ways, and every ending settles money. All three are reserved to a `ProgramManager`.

**Close-out** is the successful ending: the program ran, and its outcomes are being recorded for the grant funder. It requires `operational_status` of `CONFIRMED` — a program that was never confirmed has nothing to settle and reports `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` — and it requires the logical clock to be at or after the program's `end_utc`; a clock still inside the program's window reports `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_YET_ENDED`, and a clock exactly at `end_utc` is admissible. The request carries three counts: `verified_registrant_attendance`, the registrants confirmed present at the door; `drop_in_headcount`, the unregistered walk-in and outreach-site heads counted; and `surveys_collected`, the completed outcome survey instruments returned. Two coherence rules guard them. `verified_registrant_attendance` may not exceed the program's `registered_count`, failing which the answer is `ERR_ATTENDANCE_EXCEEDS_REGISTRATIONS` — you cannot verify more registrants than registered. And `surveys_collected` may not exceed the sum of `verified_registrant_attendance` and `drop_in_headcount`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SURVEY_COUNT_EXCEEDS_ATTENDANCE` — you cannot collect more surveys than there were people.

Five outcome figures are then derived from those three inputs and the program's own targets. `gross_attendance` is `verified_registrant_attendance` plus `drop_in_headcount`. `no_show_count` is `registered_count` less `verified_registrant_attendance`, with a floor of 0 — and because `registered_count` is the monotone running total of seats ever accepted, this is a meaningful figure rather than a rounding artefact. `survey_response_rate_pct` is 0.0 when `gross_attendance` is 0, and otherwise 100 multiplied by `surveys_collected` divided by `gross_attendance`, rounded half up to one decimal place.

The two attainment figures differ **only** in their denominator, and that difference is the whole point of publishing both. `attendance_attainment_funder_pct` divides `gross_attendance` by `attendance_target` — the number promised in the grant application — because that is what the funder was told to expect. `attendance_attainment_internal_pct` divides `gross_attendance` by the seats that actually existed: `confirmed_room_capacity` when that value is greater than 0, and `attendance_target` when it is 0. Both are expressed as 100 multiplied by that quotient, rounded half up to one decimal place. A `PARTNER_SITE` program has no room and therefore a `confirmed_room_capacity` of 0, so its two figures are always identical; an in-branch program in a room tighter than its target reports a higher internal figure than funder figure, which is exactly the space-utilisation signal the branch wants. With 39 registered, 33 verified present, no drop-ins, 12 surveys, a target of 55 and a confirmed room capacity of 51, the program reports `gross_attendance` 33, `no_show_count` 6, `survey_response_rate_pct` 36.4, `attendance_attainment_funder_pct` 60.0 and `attendance_attainment_internal_pct` 64.7. A close-out with `gross_attendance` of 0 reports 0.0 for all three percentages and never a division error.

Close-out then settles everything the confirmation had encumbered. `materials_committed_cents` moves wholesale into `materials_spent_cents` and drops to 0, with `materials_available_cents` re-derived. Each entry in `supply_draw_lines` moves its `units` from its lot's `quantity_reserved` to that lot's `quantity_issued` — the stock was consumed, so `quantity_available` does not change and the lot's `status` does not change either, which means a lot that went `DEPLETED` at confirmation stays `DEPLETED`. Every contract of this program whose `status` is `EXECUTED` becomes `COMPLETED`, `presenter_fee_committed_cents` moves wholesale into `presenter_fee_spent_cents` and drops to 0, and every reservation of the program still in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status becomes `RELEASED`. `operational_status` becomes `CLOSED_OUT`, `budget_variance_cents` and `budget_posture` are re-derived with the branch's `programs_over_budget_count` adjusted only on a flip, the program's `version` increments by 1, and the answer is 200 OK with the program. Note that close-out moves money between buckets without changing the total encumbrance, so the posture normally does not flip here.

**Weather closure** is the involuntary ending. It requires `operational_status` of either `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` — both are admissible, so a program can be weather-closed before it was ever confirmed — and it requires the logical clock to be strictly before the program's `end_utc`: a closure declared after the program has already ended is refused with `ERR_WEATHER_WINDOW_PASSED`, because by then the event either happened or did not. The request carries a `closure_reason`, which is stored.

Weather closure fires a four-link cascade over the program's contracts and money. First, every contract of this program whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `EXECUTED` becomes `CANCELLED_WEATHER` and has its `kill_fee_cents` computed by the weather rule below; contracts still in `DRAFT`, or already blocked or cancelled, are skipped entirely. Second, caused by the first, the program's `presenter_fee_committed_cents` is replaced by — not increased by — the sum of the `kill_fee_cents` of the contracts just cancelled: the fee the library owes for a cancelled program is the kill liability and nothing else. Third, caused by the second, `budget_variance_cents` and `budget_posture` are re-derived. Fourth, caused by the third, the branch's `programs_over_budget_count` is decremented when that re-derivation flipped the posture from `OVER_BUDGET` back to `WITHIN_BUDGET`. Independently of that money cascade, every reservation of the program in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` status becomes `RELEASED` and `confirmed_room_capacity` returns to 0, and the supply draw is returned to its lots: each entry in `supply_draw_lines` gives its `units` back from `quantity_reserved` to `quantity_available`, a lot whose availability rises above 0 returns from `DEPLETED` to `ACTIVE`, `supply_draw_lines` is emptied, `materials_committed_cents` drops to 0 and `materials_available_cents` is re-derived. `operational_status` becomes `WEATHER_CLOSED`, the program's `version` increments by 1, and the answer is 200 OK.

**Cancellation** is the voluntary ending — the library changed its mind. It is admissible from `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` status, it has no timing gate at all (a program may be cancelled at any point in its life, before or after its window), and it carries a `closure_reason`. Its cascade mirrors the weather cascade with one significant difference in scope: every contract whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `EXECUTED` becomes `CANCELLED_LIBRARY` — `DRAFT` contracts are included here, where weather closure skips them, because a voluntary cancellation terminates even the paperwork that had not yet been offered. Each such contract's `kill_fee_cents` is computed by the library rule below, `presenter_fee_committed_cents` is replaced by the sum of those kill fees, `budget_variance_cents`, `budget_posture` and the branch's `programs_over_budget_count` are re-derived, every `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` reservation becomes `RELEASED` with `confirmed_room_capacity` returning to 0, and the supply draw is returned to its lots in exactly the same way as under a weather closure. `operational_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, `closure_reason` is stored, the `version` increments by 1, and the answer is 200 OK.

**Kill fees.** A cancelled contract's `kill_fee_cents` depends on how much notice the presenter got and on who caused the cancellation, and the two causes are priced very differently. Before anything else, a contract whose `total_fee_cents` is 0 — one that was offered or countered but never executed, and therefore never priced — carries a `kill_fee_cents` of 0 under either cause. There is nothing to compensate.

For a **weather** cancellation, the notice is measured in hours: the number of hours from the logical clock to the program's `start_utc`, computed as the difference between those two instants divided by 3600 seconds and *not* rounded to whole hours. When that notice is 48 hours or less, `kill_fee_cents` is one half of `total_fee_cents` rounded half up to whole cents; when it is more than 48 hours, `kill_fee_cents` is 0. The boundary is inclusive: exactly 48.0 hours of notice still charges the half fee. A contract with a `total_fee_cents` of 109753 weather-closed 12 hours out therefore owes half of 109753, which is 54876.5 rounded half up to 54877; the same contract weather-closed 72 hours out owes nothing.

For a **library** cancellation, the notice is measured in whole calendar days between the clock's date and the date of the program's `start_utc` — a date-level subtraction, not an hours calculation. When that notice is fewer than 14 days, the library owes the presenter the **entire** `total_fee_cents`; when it is 14 days or more, it owes one quarter of `total_fee_cents` rounded half up to whole cents. The boundary is strict in the other direction from the weather rule: exactly 14 days of notice takes the lighter quarter rate. A contract with a `total_fee_cents` of 99775 cancelled 3 days out owes the full 99775; the same contract cancelled 30 days out owes a quarter of 99775, which is 24943.75 rounded half up to 24944. Voluntary late cancellation is deliberately the most expensive ending in the system.

**Season reconciliation** closes the reading challenge, and it is the mirror of program close-out for prize money. A `ProgramManager` reconciles a challenge whose `status` is `OPEN` — anything else is `ERR_CHALLENGE_STATE_INVALID` — and only after the logging window has actually shut: the logical clock's date must be strictly after the challenge's `end_on`, and a reconciliation attempted on or before that date is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN`. The request carries no body.

Reconciliation settles every outstanding award. Each `TierAward` whose `status` is `RESERVED` becomes `ISSUED`: its lot moves the award's `units` from `quantity_reserved` to `quantity_issued`, the challenge's `prize_committed_cents` decreases by that award's `award_total_cents` and its `prize_spent_cents` increases by the same amount — the prize was handed over, and committed money becomes spent money. Each `TierAward` whose `status` is `DEFERRED` becomes `FORFEITED`, with no inventory movement and no money movement whatsoever, because a deferred award never held a unit or committed a cent; the prize simply never materialised, and the challenge's `deferred_award_count` falls accordingly. Finally `participant_count` and `deferred_award_count` are refreshed from the challenge's participants and awards, `prize_available_cents` is re-derived so that the challenge's conservation invariant still holds, `status` becomes `RECONCILED`, the challenge's `version` increments by 1, and the answer is 200 OK. `RECONCILED` is terminal: no further tier may be appended, no reader may be enrolled, and no entry or correction may be logged against the challenge afterwards.
