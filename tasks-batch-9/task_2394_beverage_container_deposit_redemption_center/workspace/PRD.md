# Beverage Container Deposit Redemption API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A container deposit programme only works if a single cent of deposit can be traced from the moment a bottler puts a labelled container into commerce to the moment a member of the public hands it back and is paid for it. Today that trace is spread across spreadsheets held by distributors, paper tally sheets at redemption centres, reverse-vending-machine printouts, plant count slips from the central sorting facility, and quarterly reconciliation workbooks kept by the cooperative's finance staff. Nobody can answer, on demand, how much deposit liability is currently outstanding, how many containers a given account has already been paid for today, or whether a store that stopped accepting empties is actually entitled to the nearby-centre exemption it claims.

This document specifies the API that replaces that patchwork. The service owns the covered-container determination for every registered label, the deposit-initiation ledger on the distributor side, the counting and payout workflow at redemption centres and at obligated retailers, the reverse-vending machine fleet with its jam handling and cool-down discipline, the bag-drop account programme with its deferred plant counting and provisional credit, the commodity sorting/baling/sale pipeline that turns accepted containers into material revenue, and the quarterly settlement that decides how much of the unredeemed deposit pool is consumed by programme funding.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **money and containers are conserved**: every movement of deposit value is an append-only journal line, and every derived total on a centre, an account or a reporting quarter is a projection over those lines rather than an independently maintained number. Second, **fraud controls are per-visit and per-day, not per-request**: per-visit container ceilings, per-account daily allowances, out-of-state marking ratios and bag-count variance are all evaluated at the moment counting closes, and they either reduce what is paid or withhold the payout entirely. Implementers should read §2 as a set of hard contracts and §3 as the operating narrative that those contracts serve.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries two mandatory headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which it acts. The only exception is the logical-clock endpoint described in §2.4, which is reachable without headers for test-harness bootstrapping. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not spell one of the six recognised roles exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `DistributorAgent`, `CenterOperator`, `PlantCounter`, `AccountHolder` and `RetailClerk`. When the headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles listed for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The roles are peers, not a hierarchy — each owns a slice of the graph.

- `ProgramAdmin` is cooperative programme staff. It registers distributors, redemption centres, retailers and reporting quarters; approves, rejects, suspends and reinstates labels; flags, unflags and closes redeemer accounts; releases payout holds and reverses payments; voids bag drops; revokes retailer exemptions; reconciles, reopens and closes reporting quarters; and sets the logical clock.
- `DistributorAgent` is staff of one beverage distributor. It registers that distributor's own labels, drives those labels through submission and withdrawal, and initiates deposits for them.
- `CenterOperator` is staff of one redemption centre. It installs and operates reverse vending machines, opens and closes redemption visits, approves and pays redemptions, opens redeemer accounts, posts credit journal lines, accepts drop bags, credits settled bags, draws commodity batches and sells baled batches.
- `PlantCounter` is central plant counting and sorting staff, not tied to any single centre. It submits plant counts for drop bags and sorts and bales commodity batches, and it may draw a commodity batch.
- `AccountHolder` is a registered redeemer. It reads its own account, credit journal, visits and bag drops, and it disputes its own bag-drop counts.
- `RetailClerk` is staff of one obligated retail store. It runs hand-count redemptions at that store and claims the nearby-centre exemption for it.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as the `source_ref_id` of a journal line whose `source_kind` is `PROGRAM_ACTION`) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantCounter`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, recorded where a journal line demands a `PROGRAM_ACTION` source but never matched against an entity `id`. The remaining four roles are entity-backed:

- When `X-User-Role` is `DistributorAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor`; ownership checks compare it to the addressed distributor's own `id` and to the `distributor_id` of any addressed label.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CenterOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RedemptionCenter`; ownership checks compare it to the addressed centre's own `id` and to the `center_id` of any addressed machine, visit, bag drop or commodity batch.
- When `X-User-Role` is `AccountHolder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RedeemerAccount`; ownership checks compare it to the addressed account's own `id` and to the `account_id` of any addressed bag drop.
- When `X-User-Role` is `RetailClerk`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Retailer`; ownership checks compare it to the addressed store's own `id` and to the `retailer_id` of any addressed visit.

An entity-backed role that reaches for a resource owned by a different instance of its backing entity is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE` (HTTP 403). This check applies to every endpoint whose path or body names a `Distributor`, `RedemptionCenter`, `RedeemerAccount`, `Retailer`, `RedemptionVisit`, `ReverseVendingMachine`, `BagDrop` or `CommodityBatch`.

**Action-level narrowing on shared endpoints.** Several endpoints are shared by more than one role but expose different `action` values to each. The `RELEASE_HOLD` and `REVERSE` payout actions, the `REJECT`, `APPROVE`, `SUSPEND` and `REINSTATE` registration actions, the `VOID` settlement action, the `FLAG`, `UNFLAG` and `CLOSE` account actions, the `ACTIVATE`, `REINSTATE` and `CLOSE` centre actions, the `REVOKE_EXEMPTION` exemption action and the `RETIRE` machine event are reachable only by `ProgramAdmin`, even on an endpoint that other roles may otherwise call. Symmetrically, `CenterOperator` may drive the centre state machine only with the `SUSPEND` action, `AccountHolder` may drive the bag-drop state machine only with the `DISPUTE` action, and `RetailClerk` may drive the retailer state machine only with the `CLAIM_EXEMPTION` action. A role reaching for an action outside its narrowing is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1 and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 to 100, a non-integer `limit` or `offset`, or a negative `offset` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The container-lot listing for a visit, the credit journal listing for an account and the deposit-ledger listing for a reporting quarter all follow this same ascending creation order, which matters because two of the calculations in §3 replay those collections in exactly that order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation — including mutations that only rewrite derived counters. Recomputing a centre's container counters, restating a reporting quarter's settlement figures, or rolling an account's daily bucket forward are all mutations in this sense.

Two collections are append-only journals and deliberately omit both of those fields. An `AccountCreditEntry` carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no endpoint that modifies one once written. A `DepositLedgerEntry` is the same — `id` and `created_at` only, no `updated_at`, no `version`. Corrections to either journal are made by appending a compensating line, never by editing a line in place.

All time-dependent behaviour in this system is evaluated against a **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time afterwards. A `ProgramAdmin` moves it by supplying `to_iso8601`, the instant the clock should read from that point on. Every timestamp the server stamps, every `visit_date`, the machine cool-down window and the bag-drop dispute window are all evaluated against the logical instant in force when the request arrives. Date-typed fields such as `visit_date`, `jam_day`, `redemption_day`, `start_date` and `end_date` are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form derived from that same clock.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, lowest number first; the first check that fails produces the response and no later check runs. The ordering principle is: authenticate, then authorise, then resolve identifiers, then inspect the request shape, then compare versions, then test state, then apply regulatory screening, then temporal windows, then uniqueness, then referential capacity, and only last the fiscal questions about caps, drawers and balances. Money questions are answered last on purpose — a request that is malformed or aimed at a suspended centre must not be reported as a cash problem.

1. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the six roles in §2.1 → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. The acting role appears in the requested operation's authorised-role list → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint.
4. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` matches the owning entity of the addressed resource as spelled out in §2.1 → otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE`. Applies to every endpoint whose path or body names a `Distributor`, `RedemptionCenter`, `RedeemerAccount`, `Retailer`, `RedemptionVisit`, `ReverseVendingMachine`, `BagDrop` or `CommodityBatch`.
5. Every identifier in the path resolves to a stored row, validated left to right → otherwise the 404 that names the first unresolved segment: `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MACHINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BAG_DROP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row → otherwise the corresponding 404 from that same set (`ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MACHINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`). Applies to label creation, deposit initiation, visit creation, lot addition, machine events, bag-drop creation, commodity-batch creation and the batch sale.
7. Request shape: every required body field is present, every enum value is a declared member, every integer lies inside its declared range, every state code is exactly two uppercase letters, dates are well formed, and the site/channel pairing rules hold — `center_id` required and `retailer_id` absent when `site_type` is `CENTER`; `retailer_id` required and `center_id` absent when `site_type` is `RETAILER`; `machine_id` required when `channel` is `MACHINE_COUNT` and absent when `channel` is `HAND_COUNT`; and `channel` forced to `HAND_COUNT` whenever `site_type` is `RETAILER`. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Applies to every write endpoint.
8. `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Applies to every list endpoint.
9. On a container lot, `out_of_state_marked_quantity` plus `contaminated_quantity` is at most `presented_quantity` → otherwise `ERR_LOT_QUANTITY_INCONSISTENT` (HTTP 422). Applies to lot addition.
10. The supplied expected-version field equals the parent's current `version`, per §2.6 → otherwise `ERR_STALE_VISIT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_BAG_DROP_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Applies to lot addition, bag-drop count submission and account credit actions.
11. The `RedemptionCenter` owning the addressed resource has `status` `OPERATIONAL` → otherwise `ERR_CENTER_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (HTTP 409). Applies to six operations: opening a `CENTER` visit, adding a lot, closing the count, opening a machine counting session, accepting a drop bag, submitting a bag-drop plant count, and drawing a commodity batch.
12. The store named by `retailer_id` has `obligation_status` `OBLIGATED` → otherwise `ERR_RETAILER_EXEMPT` (HTTP 409). Applies to opening a `RETAILER` visit.
13. The visit's `count_status` matches the requested transition's originating state → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COUNT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Applies to lot addition and close-count.
14. The visit's `payout_status` matches the requested payout action's originating state, and for `APPROVE` the `count_status` is `COUNTED` → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAYOUT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Once those hold, `APPROVE` additionally requires `requires_manual_recount` to be false → otherwise `ERR_MANUAL_RECOUNT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). The ordering inside this rung matters: a visit that is both in the wrong payout state and flagged for manual recount reports `ERR_INVALID_PAYOUT_STATE`.
15. The label's `registration_state` matches the requested registration action's originating state → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE` (HTTP 409).
16. The machine's `status` matches the requested event's originating state and the event's own counter precondition holds — a `BIN_FULL` event requires `bin_filled_count` to be at least `bin_capacity_containers` → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MACHINE_STATE` (HTTP 409).
17. The bag drop's `status` matches the requested count submission's or settlement action's originating state → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BAG_DROP_STATE` (HTTP 409).
18. The commodity batch's `status` matches the requested processing action's or sale's originating state → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE` (HTTP 409).
19. The reporting quarter's `status` matches the requested period action's originating state, and is `OPEN` for every endpoint that appends a deposit-ledger line other than the reconcile and reopen actions themselves → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE` (HTTP 409). Applies to period actions, deposit initiation, the batch sale and payout actions.
20. The centre's `status`, the account's `status` and the store's `obligation_status` each match the originating state of the requested status change, account action or exemption action → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CENTER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ACCOUNT_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_OBLIGATION_STATE` (all HTTP 409).
21. Account standing permits the operation: a `CLOSED` account accepts no operation at all (`ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`, HTTP 409), and a `FLAGGED` account accepts no new visit, no new bag drop and no credit action (`ERR_ACCOUNT_FLAGGED`, HTTP 409). Applies to visit creation, bag-drop creation, bag-drop settlement actions, credit actions and account status changes.

A reporting quarter with `status` `OPEN` exists whose closed interval from `start_date` to `end_date` contains the relevant date — the `visit_date` for a payment or reversal, the calendar date of `settled_at` for a bag-drop credit → otherwise `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). ### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Additive Writes

Three write operations in this system are *additive* — nothing about the target's business state forbids a second one, so a state precondition cannot protect a client that is working from an outdated read. For those three, and only those three, the request body carries a non-optional expected-version field, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is refused before any business rule runs (rung 10 of §2.5). Endpoints not named here take no version field at all.

- Adding a container lot to a redemption visit requires `expected_visit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RedemptionVisit`. A second lot for the same label is perfectly legitimate product behaviour, so the only defence against a writer whose view of `presented_total`, `out_of_state_marked_total` and `raw_accepted_count` has been superseded is this comparison. If the visit's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the addition is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VISIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Submitting a plant count for a drop bag requires `expected_bag_drop_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `BagDrop`. Because a recount fully replaces the previous count result, a submission made against a superseded view of the bag would silently overwrite a newer count; instead it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BAG_DROP_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Posting a credit journal line against a redeemer account requires `expected_account_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `RedeemerAccount`. Credit movements are additive journal lines that no state field forbids, so an operator acting on a superseded `credit_balance_cents` would post an amount derived from a balance the account no longer holds; that request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.7. Container Counts, Cents and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are **integer cents**. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field or a response body, and no monetary field is ever represented as a string. All container counts are non-negative integers.

Where a computation produces a fractional intermediate, the rounding rule is fixed per computation and never left to the implementation:

- **Half-up rounding** is the only rounding mode used. A value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero.
- **Ratios** — a visit's `out_of_state_ratio` and a bag drop's `variance_pct` — are rounded to 4 decimal places before any comparison is made against a threshold.
- **Distances** — a store's `nearest_center_distance_miles` — are rounded to 2 decimal places before the radius comparison, not after.
- **Weights** — a batch's `gross_weight_kg` — are rounded to 2 decimal places. Individual entries of `bale_weights_kg` are supplied with 2 decimal places and each must be greater than 0.
- **The hand-count shrink deduction rounds up**, not half-up: any fractional container becomes a whole container of deduction.
- **Per-item rounding precedes summation** in the material-sale revenue computation: each bale's cent figure is rounded first and the rounded figures are then added. This can differ by a cent from rounding a single summed product, and the per-bale order is the required behaviour.

### 2.8. The Two Journals and the Deposit Liability Projection

The programme keeps two append-only journals. The **credit journal** (`AccountCreditEntry`) records every movement of a redeemer's account credit; an account's `credit_balance_cents` is by definition the signed sum of the `amount_cents` of its journal lines, and each line records `balance_after_cents`, the account balance immediately after that line was posted. The **deposit ledger** (`DepositLedgerEntry`) records every movement of programme deposit liability inside a reporting quarter; each line records `liability_after_cents`, the quarter's outstanding liability immediately after that line was posted.

Both journals use a polymorphic pair of fields instead of a declared foreign key. `source_kind` names the kind of record that produced the line and `source_ref_id` identifies it, to be interpreted against `source_kind`. On a credit journal line, `source_kind` is one of `BAG_DROP`, `REDEMPTION_VISIT` or `PROGRAM_ACTION`, and `source_ref_id` is respectively a bag drop's `id`, a visit's `id`, or the acting operator's `X-User-ID`. On a deposit ledger line, `source_kind` is one of `DISTRIBUTOR`, `REDEMPTION_VISIT`, `BAG_DROP`, `COMMODITY_BATCH` or `PROGRAM_ACTION`, and `source_ref_id` is respectively a distributor's `id`, a visit's `id`, a bag drop's `id`, a batch's `id`, or the acting operator's `X-User-ID`. A credit line may also carry a free-text `memo` kept for audit.

**Liability is a projection, never an accumulator.** Whenever a deposit ledger line is appended to a quarter — by deposit initiation, by paying or reversing a redemption, by crediting a settled drop bag, by selling a baled batch, or by reconciling or reopening the quarter — the entire ledger of that quarter is replayed in `created_at` ascending order (never in `id` order) and the running liability is rebuilt from zero. Each line contributes its `amount_cents` to the running total, except lines whose `entry_type` is `HANDLING_FEE` or `MATERIAL_REVENUE`, which contribute nothing: those are pure money movements against programme funding and do not change what the programme owes to redeemers. As the replay walks the ledger it rewrites each line's `liability_after_cents`, and the final running value becomes the quarter's `outstanding_liability_cents`. A container mass balance holds after every replay: a quarter's `containers_initiated_count` is always at least its `containers_redeemed_count`.

### 2.9. Derived Counters and Cascading Consistency

No aggregate in this system is maintained by hand. A centre's `machine_count` is the number of its machines whose `status` is anything other than `RETIRED`, and `out_of_service_machine_count` is the number whose `status` is exactly `OUT_OF_SERVICE`; both are recomputed whenever a machine event changes a machine's status. A centre's `accepted_container_count` is the running sum of `accepted_quantity` over the container lots of its visits whose `count_status` is `COUNTED`; its `batched_container_count` is the running sum of `container_count` over its commodity batches whose `status` is `ACCUMULATING`, `SORTED`, `BALED` or `SOLD`; and `unbatched_container_count` is `accepted_container_count` minus `batched_container_count` — the pool a new batch may draw from. A centre's `withheld_visit_count` is the number of its visits whose `payout_status` is `WITHHELD`, and its `cash_drawer_balance_cents` is `opening_cash_cents`, less the sum of `payout_cents` over its visits whose `payout_status` is `PAID`, plus the sum of `payout_cents` over its visits whose `payout_status` is `REVERSED`.

Cascades propagate in both directions across the hierarchy. A change to a machine can suspend its centre; closing a centre retires its machines and returns its exempt retailers to obligation; closing a visit's count rewrites its lots, its centre's container counters, its account's daily bucket and possibly its account's standing. Whenever a cascade touches a resource, that resource is genuinely mutated: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1, exactly as if a client had written to it directly. Cascaded transitions are performed by the server without any client request naming the cascaded resource; the sections below state each one explicitly, because an implementation that performs only the primary effect will diverge from the specified behaviour.

Compensating operations are the mirror image. Reversing a payment, voiding a bag drop and reopening a reporting quarter each restore the affected balances and counters to their pre-operation values by appending compensating journal lines and re-running the liability projection — never by deleting the original lines.

### 2.10. Nested Path Resolution

Several collections hang off a parent resource — the machines of a centre, the container lots of a visit, the credit journal of an account, the deposit ledger of a reporting quarter, the container lots presented for a label, the deposit initiations of a distributor. For any request whose path contains more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not name a stored row determines the 404 returned. A request for the lots of a non-existent visit therefore reports `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`; the server does not look at anything to the right of the failed segment. Path resolution always precedes request-shape validation, state checks and fiscal checks, so a lot addition aimed at a visit that does not exist reports the 404 rather than complaining about a missing body field.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Distributors, Label Registration and the Covered-Container Test

Nothing else in the programme can happen until a label exists and has been judged a covered container. A `ProgramAdmin` opens that door by registering a **Distributor** — the bottler or wholesaler that puts containers into commerce. Registration takes the `legal_name` under which the business is licensed, a `state_code` giving the two-letter state of licensure (exactly two uppercase letters), and the `permit_number` from its beverage distribution permit. The permit number is the distributor's natural key; a second registration reusing one already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER`. An optional `status` may be supplied and defaults to `ACTIVE`; the only other accepted value is `SUSPENDED`, and a suspended distributor may neither submit labels nor initiate deposits. Both lifetime counters — `deposits_initiated_cents`, the running sum of deposit value this distributor has put into commerce, and `containers_initiated_count`, the running sum of containers behind that value — start at 0.

A `DistributorAgent` (or a `ProgramAdmin` acting on its behalf) then creates a **BeverageProduct**, which is the programme's word for one registered label. The payload names the owning distributor with `distributor_id` and describes the label: `brand_name` as printed, `beverage_type`, `container_material`, `fill_volume_ml` as the labelled fill volume in millilitres (an integer of at least 1), the twelve-digit `upc`, and `label_state_code`, the two-letter state code printed on the redemption legend — `OR` marking a label redeemable in the programme state. A duplicate `upc` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UPC`. A newly created label sits in `registration_state` `DRAFT` with `deposit_cents` at 0 and no coverage determination yet made; `coverage_status` and `coverage_reason` are only written when the label is first submitted.

`beverage_type` is one of `WATER`, `FLAVORED_WATER`, `BEER`, `MALT_BEVERAGE`, `CARBONATED_SOFT_DRINK`, `HARD_CIDER`, `KOMBUCHA`, `ENERGY_DRINK`, `RTD_COFFEE_TEA`, `FRUIT_JUICE`, `WINE`, `DISTILLED_SPIRITS`, `MILK`, `INFANT_FORMULA`, `MEAL_REPLACEMENT` or `VEGETABLE_JUICE`. `container_material` is one of `ALUMINUM`, `GLASS`, `PET_PLASTIC`, `HDPE_PLASTIC`, `POUCH`, `ASEPTIC_BOX` or `PAPER_CARTON`.

**The covered-container test.** Whenever a label is submitted for registration — from `DRAFT` on its first attempt, or from `REJECTED` on any later attempt — the server re-derives coverage from scratch and rewrites `coverage_status`, `coverage_reason` and `deposit_cents`. The test has three clauses evaluated in a pinned order, and the *first* clause that fails is the one recorded in `coverage_reason`:

1. The beverage category must be one of the ten covered types: `WATER`, `FLAVORED_WATER`, `BEER`, `MALT_BEVERAGE`, `CARBONATED_SOFT_DRINK`, `HARD_CIDER`, `KOMBUCHA`, `ENERGY_DRINK`, `RTD_COFFEE_TEA` or `FRUIT_JUICE`. Anything else — `WINE`, `DISTILLED_SPIRITS`, `MILK`, `INFANT_FORMULA`, `MEAL_REPLACEMENT` or `VEGETABLE_JUICE` — yields `coverage_status` `NOT_COVERED` with `coverage_reason` `EXCLUDED_BEVERAGE_TYPE`, and the remaining clauses are not consulted.
2. Otherwise, the container material must be one of the four covered materials: `ALUMINUM`, `GLASS`, `PET_PLASTIC` or `HDPE_PLASTIC`. A `POUCH`, `ASEPTIC_BOX` or `PAPER_CARTON` yields `NOT_COVERED` with `coverage_reason` `EXCLUDED_MATERIAL`.
3. Otherwise, the labelled fill volume must fall between 118 millilitres and 3000 millilitres, **inclusive at both ends**. A volume below 118 or above 3000 yields `NOT_COVERED` with `coverage_reason` `VOLUME_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Exactly 118 and exactly 3000 both pass.

A label that clears all three clauses is `COVERED` with `coverage_reason` `COVERED_STANDARD`. `deposit_cents` is then set to the standard per-container deposit of 10 cents; a `NOT_COVERED` label has `deposit_cents` 0.

**Driving the registration lifecycle.** A label moves through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REGISTERED`, `REJECTED`, `SUSPENDED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `WITHDRAWN` terminal. Each move is requested by naming an `action`, optionally accompanied by a `reason`, and every accepted move increments the label's `version` by 1.

- Submitting a `DRAFT` label advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. This is a `DistributorAgent` action and requires the owning distributor's `status` to be `ACTIVE`; a suspended owner blocks it with `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED`. The covered-container test runs as part of the transition.
- Approval by a `ProgramAdmin` advances the label `SUBMITTED → REGISTERED`. Approval is only available when the coverage determination came out `COVERED`; a `NOT_COVERED` label is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_COVERED`. On success the server stamps `registered_at` with the current logical instant and clears `rejection_reason` to null.
- Rejection by a `ProgramAdmin` moves the label `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and records the supplied `reason` into `rejection_reason`.
- Resubmitting a rejected label moves it `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` and re-runs the covered-container test, so a corrected volume or material genuinely changes the outcome. This cycle may be repeated any number of times.
- Suspension by a `ProgramAdmin` moves a live label `REGISTERED → SUSPENDED` and records the supplied `reason` into `rejection_reason`. Reinstatement moves it `SUSPENDED → REGISTERED`, re-stamps `registered_at` with the current logical instant and clears `rejection_reason` to null.
- Withdrawal is the `DistributorAgent`'s own exit and is available from two states: `REGISTERED → WITHDRAWN` and `SUSPENDED → WITHDRAWN`. Both branches behave identically — `deposit_cents` is set to 0, so containers of a withdrawn label carry no deposit value from that point on.

Requesting an action that is not declared from the label's current state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`. Labels may be listed and filtered by `distributor_id`, `upc`, `registration_state` and `coverage_status`, and the container lots ever presented for a given label may be listed as well.

**Initiating deposits.** With a `REGISTERED` label in hand, a `DistributorAgent` initiates deposits by declaring how many containers of that label entered commerce: the payload names `product_id`, a `container_count`, and the `period_id` of the reporting quarter the movement belongs in. The label must be `REGISTERED` (otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_REGISTERED`), the distributor must be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_SUSPENDED`), and the named quarter must be `OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`). The initiation appends a deposit ledger line whose `entry_type` is `DEPOSIT_INITIATED`, whose `amount_cents` is the declared container count multiplied by the label's `deposit_cents` — a positive amount, because initiating deposits increases what the programme owes — whose `container_count` repeats the declared count, whose `source_kind` is `DISTRIBUTOR` and whose `source_ref_id` is the distributor's `id`. The distributor's own `deposits_initiated_cents` and `containers_initiated_count` grow by the same two amounts, and the liability projection of §2.8 re-runs so that the quarter's `deposits_initiated_cents`, `containers_initiated_count` and `outstanding_liability_cents` all move together. A distributor's own initiations can be listed back for reconciliation.

### 3.2. The Redemption Network: Centres, Machines and Retailer Obligation

A **RedemptionCenter** is created by a `ProgramAdmin` with a public `site_name`, a location given as `x_miles` and `y_miles` on the programme's planar grid, and the list of `accepted_materials` it will take (each element one of `ALUMINUM`, `GLASS`, `PET_PLASTIC` or `HDPE_PLASTIC`). Three optional inputs shape its economics: `accepts_all_materials`, which defaults to true and marks a centre that takes every covered material — only such a centre can satisfy a retailer exemption; `daily_account_cap_containers`, the per-account daily payable container allowance the centre enforces, defaulting to 350; and `opening_cash_cents`, the cash loaded into the payout drawer, defaulting to 0. A new centre opens in `status` `PROVISIONAL` with `cash_drawer_balance_cents` at 0 and every derived counter at 0. Centres may be listed and filtered by `status`.

Before it can take a single container the centre must be activated, and activation is only granted once the fleet exists. A centre's lifecycle is driven by naming an `action` of `ACTIVATE`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `CLOSE`, with an optional `reason`. Activation with `ACTIVATE` by a `ProgramAdmin` moves the centre `PROVISIONAL → OPERATIONAL` and requires `machine_count` to be at least 1; a centre with no machine installed is refused with `ERR_CENTER_HAS_NO_MACHINE`. Activation copies `opening_cash_cents` into `cash_drawer_balance_cents` and clears `suspension_reason` to null.

Suspension with `SUSPEND` moves the centre `OPERATIONAL → SUSPENDED` and always records why in `suspension_reason`, which is one of `MACHINE_FLEET_DOWN`, `OPERATOR_REQUEST` or `PROGRAM_ACTION` and is null in every other `status`. The reason depends on who asked: when a `CenterOperator` suspends its own site the reason is `OPERATOR_REQUEST`, and when programme staff suspend it the reason is `PROGRAM_ACTION`. A third path is not requested by anyone — when a machine event leaves every non-`RETIRED` machine of the centre in `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, that is, when `out_of_service_machine_count` equals `machine_count` and `machine_count` is at least 1, the server itself moves the centre `OPERATIONAL → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `MACHINE_FLEET_DOWN`.

Reinstatement with `REINSTATE`, a `ProgramAdmin` prerogative, moves the centre `SUSPENDED → OPERATIONAL` and clears `suspension_reason` to null, but only while `out_of_service_machine_count` is strictly less than `machine_count`; asking to reinstate a site whose whole fleet is still down is refused with `ERR_MACHINE_FLEET_DOWN`.

Closure with `CLOSE` is terminal and available from two states, `OPERATIONAL → CLOSED` and `SUSPENDED → CLOSED`. Both branches carry the identical precondition and the identical pair of cascades. The precondition is that no visit of this centre still has `count_status` `OPEN`, `COUNTING` or `JAM_HOLD`; unfinished counting blocks closure with `ERR_CENTER_HAS_OPEN_VISITS`. The cascades are, first, that every non-`RETIRED` machine of the centre moves to `RETIRED`; and second, that every store whose `nearest_center_id` names this centre moves `EXEMPT → OBLIGATED` and has `nearest_center_id`, `nearest_center_distance_miles` and `exemption_granted_at` cleared to null. Each cascaded machine and each cascaded store is mutated in its own right, with `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented. Requesting a centre action that is not declared from the centre's current `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CENTER_STATE`.

**Installing and operating the fleet.** A **ReverseVendingMachine** is installed at a centre with an `asset_tag` (its natural key — a reused tag is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_TAG`), the single `accepted_material` it counts, and an optional `bin_capacity_containers` defaulting to 2000 containers. Installation records the machine's `center_id`, opens it in `status` `IDLE` with `bin_filled_count` at 0 and `jam_count_today` at 0, recomputes the hosting centre's `machine_count`, and increments the centre's `version`. A centre's installed machines may be listed, and any single machine may be read directly.

Machine behaviour is driven by events, each naming an `event_type`, optionally a `visit_id` identifying the redemption visit the machine is serving, and optionally a free-text `note`. The accepted event types are `SESSION_OPEN`, `SESSION_CLOSE`, `JAM_REPORTED`, `JAM_CLEARED`, `BIN_FULL`, `EMPTY_BIN`, `TAKE_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, `RETURN_TO_SERVICE` and `RETIRE`, and the machine's `status` runs over `IDLE`, `COUNTING`, `JAMMED`, `BIN_FULL`, `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and `RETIRED`, with `RETIRED` terminal. Every accepted event increments the machine's `version` by 1. An event whose originating state does not match the machine's current `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MACHINE_STATE`.

Opening a counting session moves the machine `IDLE → COUNTING`. It requires the hosting centre to be `OPERATIONAL`, and it requires the supplied `visit_id` to name a visit whose `machine_id` is this very machine and whose `count_status` is `OPEN` or `COUNTING`; a visit belonging to another machine, or a machine belonging to another centre than the visit, is refused with `ERR_MACHINE_CENTER_MISMATCH`. It also requires the **cool-down window** to have elapsed. That window is anchored on `last_session_closed_at`, the instant at which the machine's most recent counting session closed. If `last_session_closed_at` is null the machine has never held a session and there is no cool-down. Otherwise the window runs from `last_session_closed_at` up to five minutes later, closed at its start and open at its end: an attempt at any instant from `last_session_closed_at` up to but not including `last_session_closed_at` plus five minutes is refused with `ERR_MACHINE_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`, while an attempt at exactly five minutes after closing is accepted. The logical clock resolves to the second for this comparison. A successful open stamps `session_started_at` with the current logical instant and sets `open_session_count` to 1.

Closing the session moves the machine `COUNTING → IDLE`, stamps `last_session_closed_at` with the current logical instant, clears `session_started_at` to null and returns `open_session_count` to 0. The `open_session_count` field is the fleet's occupancy signal: it reads 1 while the machine is `COUNTING`, and stays 1 while the machine is `JAMMED` until the jam is cleared; it is 0 otherwise. Which visit is occupying a machine is discovered by listing visits filtered by that machine's identifier.

Jams are the fleet's most consequential events, and they are capped per calendar day of the logical clock. Two counters track them: `jam_hold_count`, the machine's lifetime number of accepted jam reports, and `jam_count_today`, the number of jams reported on `jam_day` — the date `jam_count_today` refers to, null before the first jam ever. Reporting a jam while `jam_count_today` for the current logical date is strictly below 3 moves the machine `COUNTING → JAMMED`. That event increments `jam_hold_count` by 1; sets `jam_day` to the current logical date and increments `jam_count_today` by 1, resetting the counter to 1 first when `jam_day` had been an earlier date; and cascades onto the visit named by `visit_id`, moving its `count_status` `COUNTING → JAM_HOLD`, setting its `requires_manual_recount` to true and incrementing its `jam_events_count` by 1. Clearing the jam moves the machine `JAMMED → IDLE`, stamps `last_session_closed_at` with the current logical instant, clears `session_started_at` to null, returns `open_session_count` to 0, and cascades the visit's `count_status` back `JAM_HOLD → COUNTING`. The loop `IDLE → COUNTING → JAMMED → IDLE → COUNTING` is legal and may repeat until the day's third jam.

The fourth jam of a logical date behaves differently. Reporting a jam while `jam_count_today` for the current date already equals 3 moves the machine `COUNTING → OUT_OF_SERVICE` instead of to `JAMMED`. That event still increments `jam_hold_count` by 1 and still increments `jam_count_today` by 1, still clears `session_started_at` to null and sets `open_session_count` to 0, and still cascades the visit `COUNTING → JAM_HOLD` with `requires_manual_recount` set true and `jam_events_count` incremented by 1. It additionally recomputes the hosting centre's `out_of_service_machine_count`, and when that count has reached `machine_count` it cascades the centre `OPERATIONAL → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `MACHINE_FLEET_DOWN`.

Three more events round out the fleet's vocabulary. Taking an idle machine out of service moves it `IDLE → OUT_OF_SERVICE` and carries the same centre cascade — recompute `out_of_service_machine_count`, and suspend the centre with `suspension_reason` `MACHINE_FLEET_DOWN` when that count has reached `machine_count`. Returning it to service moves it `OUT_OF_SERVICE → IDLE`, resets `jam_count_today` to 0 and `jam_day` to null so the machine starts the day's jam allowance afresh, and recomputes the centre's `out_of_service_machine_count`. Retiring a machine — a `ProgramAdmin` action, available from two states, `IDLE → RETIRED` and `OUT_OF_SERVICE → RETIRED` — recomputes both the centre's `machine_count` and its `out_of_service_machine_count` in either branch.

Bin capacity is handled by its own pair of events. Declaring a bin full moves the machine `IDLE → BIN_FULL` and is only accepted when `bin_filled_count` has reached or passed `bin_capacity_containers`; a premature declaration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MACHINE_STATE`. Emptying the bin moves the machine `BIN_FULL → IDLE` and resets `bin_filled_count` to 0. The bin fills up as a by-product of counting, described in §3.5.

**Retailer obligation and the nearby-centre exemption.** Grocery and convenience stores carry a redemption obligation of their own. A `ProgramAdmin` registers a **Retailer** with a `store_name`, its location as `x_miles` and `y_miles` on the same planar grid, and `store_area_sqft`, the interior retail area in square feet (an integer of at least 1). Store size sets the store's per-visit hand-count ceiling: `daily_limit_containers` is derived as 50 containers for a store of 5000 square feet or larger, and 24 containers for any smaller store. A new store's `obligation_status` is `OBLIGATED`, with `nearest_center_id`, `nearest_center_distance_miles` and `exemption_granted_at` all null. Stores may be listed and filtered by `obligation_status` and by `nearest_center_id`.

A `RetailClerk` may claim relief from the obligation on the grounds that a full-service redemption centre is close by, moving the store `OBLIGATED → EXEMPT`. Three things must hold. First, the store must have no visit still sitting in `count_status` `OPEN`, `COUNTING` or `JAM_HOLD`; unfinished counting blocks the claim with `ERR_RETAILER_HAS_OPEN_VISITS`. Second, at least one candidate centre must exist — candidates are exactly those centres whose `status` is `OPERATIONAL` and whose `accepts_all_materials` is true. If that candidate set is empty the claim is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CENTER`. Third, the selected centre must actually be near. For each candidate the server computes the straight-line distance on the planar grid — the square root of the sum of the squared difference in the miles-east coordinates and the squared difference in the miles-north coordinates — and rounds that distance to 2 decimal places *before* any comparison. The candidates are then ranked by rounded distance ascending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by identifier, and the first candidate is selected. If the selected centre's rounded distance is greater than 3.50 miles the claim is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_DISTANCE_EXCEEDED`; the radius test is non-strict, so a centre at exactly 3.50 miles grants the exemption. On success the server writes `nearest_center_id` and `nearest_center_distance_miles` from the selected centre and stamps `exemption_granted_at` with the current logical instant.

The exemption can end in two ways, and both clear the same three fields. A `ProgramAdmin` may revoke it explicitly, moving the store `EXEMPT → OBLIGATED` and clearing `nearest_center_id`, `nearest_center_distance_miles` and `exemption_granted_at` to null. Alternatively the server revokes it without being asked when the centre named by `nearest_center_id` is closed, moving that store `EXEMPT → OBLIGATED` and clearing the same three fields. After either revocation the store is free to claim a fresh exemption against another qualifying centre. Requesting an exemption action not declared from the store's current `obligation_status` — claiming while already `EXEMPT`, or revoking while already `OBLIGATED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OBLIGATION_STATE`.

### 3.3. Redeemer Accounts, Standing and the Credit Journal

A **RedeemerAccount** identifies a member of the public who redeems containers under their own name rather than anonymously. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or a `CenterOperator` opens one with the `holder_name` of the holder and an `account_code`, the printed code used on drop-bag tags; a reused code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCOUNT_CODE`. An optional `bag_drop_enrolled` flag, defaulting to false, records whether the holder is entitled to leave drop bags for deferred counting. A new account opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `credit_balance_cents` at 0 and `fraud_strikes` at 0.

An account tracks four numbers that the rest of the system writes to. `credit_balance_cents` is, by definition, the signed sum of `amount_cents` over the account's credit journal lines. `redemption_day` names the calendar date that `containers_redeemed_today` refers to, and is null before the account's first payable visit; `containers_redeemed_today` is the sum of `payable_count` over the account's visits whose `visit_date` equals `redemption_day` and whose `count_status` is `COUNTED`. `cap_reached_today` is true exactly when `containers_redeemed_today` has reached or passed the applicable daily container cap of the centre where the visit took place. `fraud_strikes` is the account's lifetime count of fraud triggers, and `last_strike_reason` records the most recent one — one of `OUT_OF_STATE_RATIO`, `DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` or `BAG_DROP_VARIANCE`, null before the first strike.

**Standing.** An account's `status` runs over `ACTIVE`, `FLAGGED` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal. Standing is changed by naming an `action` of `FLAG`, `UNFLAG` or `CLOSE` with an optional `reason`. A `ProgramAdmin` may flag an account outright with `FLAG`, moving it `ACTIVE → FLAGGED`. The server also flags accounts on its own: when a strike recorded by the request in flight brings `fraud_strikes` to 2, the account moves `ACTIVE → FLAGGED`, `last_strike_reason` is set to the reason of the strike that reached the limit, and — when the strike came from closing a visit's count — the triggering visit is cascaded `PENDING → WITHHELD` on its payout dimension with `withhold_reason` `ACCOUNT_FLAGGED`. Unflagging with `UNFLAG`, a `ProgramAdmin` prerogative, moves the account `FLAGGED → ACTIVE` and wipes the slate: `fraud_strikes` returns to 0 and `last_strike_reason` to null.

Closing an account with `CLOSE` is available from two states, `ACTIVE → CLOSED` and `FLAGGED → CLOSED`, and both branches carry the identical pair of preconditions and the identical side effect. The account's `credit_balance_cents` must equal 0, and none of the account's bag drops may still sit in `DROPPED`, `COUNTED` or `DISPUTED`; a non-zero balance or an unsettled bag blocks the close with `ERR_ACCOUNT_BALANCE_NONZERO`. On success `bag_drop_enrolled` is set to false in both branches. Requesting a status change that is not declared from the account's current `status` — closing a `CLOSED` account, or flagging a `FLAGGED` one — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ACCOUNT_STATE`. Beyond the state machine, standing gates operations elsewhere: a `CLOSED` account accepts no operation at all, refusing with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`, while a `FLAGGED` account may not be given a new visit, a new bag drop or a new credit action, refusing with `ERR_ACCOUNT_FLAGGED`.

**The credit journal.** Credit movements are posted as journal lines rather than balance edits. An operator posts one by naming an `action` — `WITHDRAW`, `MANUAL_GRANT` or `CLAWBACK` — together with the `amount_cents` of the movement, the `expected_account_version` demanded by §2.6, and an optional `memo`. The posted line's `entry_type` is `PAYOUT_WITHDRAWAL` for a `WITHDRAW`, `MANUAL_GRANT` for a `MANUAL_GRANT` and `CLAWBACK` for a `CLAWBACK`; its `source_kind` is `PROGRAM_ACTION` and its `source_ref_id` is the acting operator's `X-User-ID`. Amounts are signed, and both `PAYOUT_WITHDRAWAL` and `CLAWBACK` lines carry negative amounts, because both remove value from the holder. Before a `WITHDRAW` or a `CLAWBACK` is accepted the account's `credit_balance_cents` must be at least the requested `amount_cents`; an over-draw is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT_BALANCE`. On success the account's `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed, `balance_after_cents` is written onto the new line, and the account's `version` increments by 1. The journal may be listed for an account in `created_at` ascending order, filterable by `entry_type`; the entry types that can appear are `PROVISIONAL`, `ADJUSTMENT`, `PAYOUT_WITHDRAWAL`, `CLAWBACK` and `MANUAL_GRANT`.

### 3.4. Presenting Containers: Opening a Visit and Screening Lots

A **RedemptionVisit** is one person's trip to one counting point. Opening a visit takes a `site_type` of either `CENTER` or `RETAILER`, a `channel` of either `MACHINE_COUNT` or `HAND_COUNT`, and then the identifiers implied by those two choices: `center_id` when the site type is `CENTER` and `retailer_id` when it is `RETAILER`, plus `machine_id` when the channel is `MACHINE_COUNT`. The pairing rules are strict rather than permissive — the identifier that does not belong must be absent, not merely ignored, and a retailer visit is always hand-counted. An `account_id` may be supplied to attribute the visit to a named holder; omitting it makes the visit an anonymous walk-in, which has consequences for the per-visit ceiling in §3.5. The server stamps `visit_date` from the logical clock as the calendar date on which the visit opened, and opens the visit with `count_status` `OPEN` and `payout_status` `PENDING`.

A visit carries two independent state dimensions, and it is worth being explicit about that: `count_status` moves over `OPEN`, `COUNTING`, `JAM_HOLD` and `COUNTED`, terminating at `COUNTED`, while `payout_status` moves over `PENDING`, `WITHHELD`, `APPROVED`, `PAID` and `REVERSED`, terminating at `REVERSED`. Neither dimension constrains the other except where §3.5 and §3.6 say so.

Opening a visit is gated by the site's own posture. A `CENTER` visit requires the named centre to be `OPERATIONAL`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_CENTER_NOT_OPERATIONAL`; a `RETAILER` visit requires the named store to be `OBLIGATED`, refusing an exempt store with `ERR_RETAILER_EXEMPT`. When a machine is named, that machine's `center_id` must equal the visit's `center_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MACHINE_CENTER_MISMATCH`. An account, if named, must not be `CLOSED` or `FLAGGED`. Visits may be listed and filtered by `center_id`, `retailer_id`, `account_id`, `machine_id`, `count_status`, `payout_status` and `visit_date`.

**Adding container lots.** Containers are handed over in lots, one lot per label. A lot names the `product_id` of the label, the `presented_quantity` handed over (an integer of at least 1), and the `expected_visit_version` demanded by §2.6. Two optional quantities declare what should not be paid for: `out_of_state_marked_quantity`, the subset of the presented containers whose redemption legend does not name the programme state, and `contaminated_quantity`, the subset rejected for residue, crushing or non-container waste. Both default to 0, both must be at least 0 and at most `presented_quantity`, and their sum may not exceed `presented_quantity` — a lot claiming more disqualified containers than were presented is refused with `ERR_LOT_QUANTITY_INCONSISTENT`.

Several conditions guard a lot. The named label must be in `registration_state` `REGISTERED`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_REGISTERED`. On a `CENTER` visit the label's `container_material` must appear in the centre's `accepted_materials`, and on a `MACHINE_COUNT` visit it must additionally equal the serving machine's `accepted_material`; either mismatch is refused with `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_ACCEPTED`. A `CENTER` visit requires its centre to still be `OPERATIONAL`, and a `RETAILER` visit requires its store to still be `OBLIGATED`. If the visit is attributed to an account that has already exhausted its daily allowance for this `visit_date` — that is, whose `cap_reached_today` is true while `redemption_day` equals the visit's `visit_date` — no further lot may be added and the request is refused with `ERR_DAILY_CAP_EXHAUSTED`.

The first accepted lot moves the visit `OPEN → COUNTING`. Later lots are accepted as a self-transition `COUNTING → COUNTING`, and any number of them may be added while counting is open; both branches run the same screening and the same recomputation. Adding a lot to a visit whose `count_status` is neither `OPEN` nor `COUNTING` — a `JAM_HOLD` visit awaiting a jam clear, or an already `COUNTED` visit — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COUNT_STATE`. Every accepted lot increments the visit's `version` by 1.

**Lot screening.** As each lot is created the server screens it. First it snapshots the label's current per-container deposit into the lot's own `deposit_cents_per_container`, so that a later withdrawal of the label cannot retroactively change what this lot was worth. Then coverage decides the shape of the outcome. If the label's `coverage_status` is `NOT_COVERED`, the lot's `accepted_quantity` is 0 and its `reject_reason` is `NOT_COVERED`. Otherwise `accepted_quantity` is the presented quantity less the out-of-state marked quantity less the contaminated quantity, and `reject_reason` names the dominant cause of whatever was refused: `MIXED` when both the out-of-state marked quantity and the contaminated quantity are greater than 0, `OUT_OF_STATE` when only the out-of-state marked quantity is greater than 0, `CONTAMINATED` when only the contaminated quantity is greater than 0, and null when neither is — in which case nothing was rejected at all. In every case `rejected_quantity` is the presented quantity less the accepted quantity, and `deposit_value_cents` is the accepted quantity multiplied by the snapshotted per-container deposit. A freshly screened lot has not been paid for anything yet, so `paid_quantity` starts at 0 and `reconciliation_flag` starts at `UNSETTLED`; the other three values that flag can take — `PAID_FULL`, `PAID_PARTIAL` and `UNPAID` — are only written when counting closes. All of these are whole containers and whole cents; no rounding occurs anywhere in screening.

Each accepted lot also refreshes four visit-level roll-ups. `presented_total` is the sum of `presented_quantity` across the visit's lots; `out_of_state_marked_total` is the sum of `out_of_state_marked_quantity`; `raw_accepted_count` is the sum of `accepted_quantity`; and `out_of_state_ratio` is `out_of_state_marked_total` divided by `presented_total`, rounded to 4 decimal places, and defined as 0 when `presented_total` is 0. The lots of a visit may be listed back in `created_at` ascending order, which is exactly the order the payout waterfall will walk them in.

### 3.5. Close-Count Valuation

Closing the count is the single most consequential operation in the system: it freezes what was counted, decides what will be paid, updates the day's allowance, and sets the fraud posture. It takes no request body at all — everything it needs is already on the visit and its lots. The normal path advances the visit `COUNTING → COUNTED`; a `CENTER` visit additionally requires its centre to still be `OPERATIONAL`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_CENTER_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. A visit sitting in `JAM_HOLD` cannot be closed and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COUNT_STATE` — the jam must be cleared first, which returns it to `COUNTING`. Closing stamps `counted_at` with the current logical instant and increments the visit's `version` by 1.

Valuation runs in five stages, and the order is not negotiable because each stage consumes the previous one's output.

**Stage one — the per-visit ceiling.** `visit_limit_applied` starts from a base ceiling chosen by where and how the containers were counted. A machine-counted visit at a redemption centre has a base ceiling of 240 containers. A hand-counted visit at a redemption centre has a base ceiling of 50 containers. A visit at a retailer — always hand-counted — takes the store's own `daily_limit_containers` as its base ceiling, which §3.2 derived from the store's floor area. One further narrowing applies to anonymity: if the visit carries no `account_id`, the ceiling actually applied is the lesser of that base ceiling and 144 containers, so an unattributed walk-in can never be paid for more than twelve dozen containers in a single visit. When the visit does name an account, `visit_limit_applied` is the base ceiling unchanged.

**Stage two — the remaining daily allowance.** For an anonymous visit there is no daily allowance to speak of, and the allowance is treated as equal to `raw_accepted_count`, leaving it non-binding. For an attributed visit the server first picks the applicable cap: at a redemption centre it is that centre's `daily_account_cap_containers`, and at a retailer it is the programme's default daily cap of 350 containers. It then works out how much of that cap the account has already used *on this visit's date*: the account's `containers_redeemed_today` if the account's `redemption_day` equals the visit's `visit_date`, and 0 otherwise, because a bucket belonging to an earlier date carries no weight. The remaining allowance is the cap less the amount already used, floored at 0 so it can never go negative.

**Stage three — the three-way minimum and the shrink deduction.** `capped_count` is the smallest of three numbers: `raw_accepted_count`, `visit_limit_applied` and the remaining daily allowance. From that, `shrink_adjustment_count` is derived by channel. A machine-counted visit has a `shrink_adjustment_count` of 0 and therefore a `payable_count` equal to its `capped_count`. A hand-counted visit is charged a two-per-cent shrink allowance: `shrink_adjustment_count` is two per cent of `capped_count` rounded **up** to the next whole container, which means any hand-counted visit with a `capped_count` of at least 1 has a `payable_count` strictly below its `capped_count`. In both channels `payable_count` is `capped_count` less `shrink_adjustment_count`, and `refused_over_limit_count` is `raw_accepted_count` less `capped_count` — containers the site accepted for recycling but will not pay for.

**Stage four — the payout waterfall.** `payable_count` is then distributed across the visit's lots in `created_at` ascending order. Walking the lots in that order, each lot's `paid_quantity` is the lesser of its own `accepted_quantity` and whatever remains undistributed, and the remainder shrinks by that amount before the next lot is considered. Each lot's `reconciliation_flag` records the result: `PAID_FULL` when its `paid_quantity` equals its `accepted_quantity` *and* that accepted quantity is greater than 0, `UNPAID` when its `paid_quantity` is 0, and `PAID_PARTIAL` in every remaining case. Note the consequence of that ordering of tests: a lot that accepted nothing at all is `UNPAID`, not `PAID_FULL`. Finally the visit's `payout_cents` is the sum, across its lots, of each lot's `paid_quantity` multiplied by that lot's own snapshotted `deposit_cents_per_container`.

**Stage five — the fraud posture.** Two independent triggers can put the visit on hold, and either one sets `fraud_hold` to true and records a `withhold_reason`. The first is out-of-state marking: if the visit's `out_of_state_ratio` — computed as in §3.4, rounded to 4 decimal places — is **strictly greater** than one tenth, `fraud_hold` becomes true, `withhold_reason` becomes `OUT_OF_STATE_RATIO`, and the account's `fraud_strikes` increments by 1 with `last_strike_reason` `OUT_OF_STATE_RATIO`. A ratio of exactly 0.1000 does not trigger. The second is the daily cap: if the visit names an account and `raw_accepted_count` exceeds the remaining daily allowance computed in stage two, `fraud_hold` becomes true, `withhold_reason` becomes `DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and the account's `fraud_strikes` increments by 1 with `last_strike_reason` `DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Whenever `fraud_hold` ends up true, the visit's payout dimension moves `PENDING → WITHHELD` and the centre's `withheld_visit_count` is recomputed. And if the strikes recorded in this request bring the account's `fraud_strikes` to 2, the account moves `ACTIVE → FLAGGED` as described in §3.3 and the visit's `withhold_reason` is overwritten with `ACCOUNT_FLAGGED`. The four values `withhold_reason` can carry are `OUT_OF_STATE_RATIO`, `DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ACCOUNT_FLAGGED` and `MANUAL_REVIEW`; it is null in every `payout_status` other than `WITHHELD`.

**The daily bucket.** Closing the count also rolls the account's daily bucket forward, and this step is skipped entirely for an anonymous visit. If the account's `redemption_day` differs from the visit's `visit_date`, `redemption_day` is set to the visit's date and `containers_redeemed_today` is first reset to 0 — the bucket belongs to one date only. The visit's `payable_count` is then added to `containers_redeemed_today`. Finally `cap_reached_today` is set by comparing the updated `containers_redeemed_today` against the applicable cap — the visit centre's `daily_account_cap_containers` for a `CENTER` visit, or the programme default of 350 containers for a `RETAILER` visit — using a non-strict comparison, so reaching the cap exactly sets the flag.

**Two more effects, then the boundary case.** On a machine-counted visit the whole `raw_accepted_count` is added to the serving machine's `bin_filled_count`, which is what eventually makes a bin-full declaration legitimate. In all cases the hosting centre's `accepted_container_count` and `unbatched_container_count` are recomputed, releasing the newly accepted containers into the pool that commodity batches draw from. And a visit may legitimately be closed with no lots at all: closing from `OPEN → COUNTED` runs the same valuation over an empty lot set, yielding `capped_count` 0, `shrink_adjustment_count` 0, `payable_count` 0, `refused_over_limit_count` 0 and `payout_cents` 0, and the daily bucket step leaves `containers_redeemed_today` unchanged. No fraud strike is recorded in that case, because a zero `presented_total` gives an `out_of_state_ratio` of 0 and a `raw_accepted_count` of 0 cannot exceed any allowance.

### 3.6. The Payout Dimension: Withholding, Approval, Payment and Reversal

Once counting has closed, money moves on the visit's second state dimension. Each move is requested by naming an `action` — `APPROVE`, `PAY`, `REVERSE`, `RELEASE_HOLD` or `WITHHOLD` — with an optional `reason`, and every accepted move increments the visit's `version` by 1. A payout action whose originating state does not match the visit's current `payout_status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAYOUT_STATE`.

An operator may put a pending payout on hold deliberately, moving the visit `PENDING → WITHHELD`. On this explicit path `withhold_reason` is recorded as `MANUAL_REVIEW`, in contrast to the automatic path of §3.5 where it is `OUT_OF_STATE_RATIO`, `DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` or `ACCOUNT_FLAGGED`. Either way the hosting centre's `withheld_visit_count` is recomputed. Releasing a hold is a `ProgramAdmin` prerogative and moves the visit `WITHHELD → PENDING`; it is permitted only when the visit's account is `ACTIVE`, or when the visit names no account at all. Releasing sets `fraud_hold` back to false and `withhold_reason` back to null, and recomputes the centre's `withheld_visit_count`. A released visit can be withheld again later, so the `PENDING → WITHHELD → PENDING` loop is legal.

Approval moves the visit `PENDING → APPROVED` and is the point at which three conditions must all hold: the count must be finished, so `count_status` is `COUNTED`; `fraud_hold` must be false; and `requires_manual_recount` must be false. The first two, together with the payout state itself, are reported as `ERR_INVALID_PAYOUT_STATE`. The manual-recount condition is checked strictly after them and has its own code: a visit whose `payout_status` is `PENDING` and whose `count_status` is `COUNTED` but which was jammed at least once during counting is refused with `ERR_MANUAL_RECOUNT_REQUIRED`. Since `requires_manual_recount` stays true once a jam has set it — clearing the jam returns the count dimension to `COUNTING` but does not clear the flag — a jammed visit can only be paid after a `ProgramAdmin` intervenes.

Payment moves the visit `APPROVED → PAID`. Two fiscal conditions guard it. At a redemption centre the drawer must actually hold the money: `cash_drawer_balance_cents` must be at least the visit's `payout_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CASH_DRAWER`. And a reporting quarter with `status` `OPEN` must exist whose closed date interval contains the visit's `visit_date`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD`; a quarter that has moved to `RECONCILING` or `CLOSED` blocks payment with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`. On success the server stamps `paid_at` with the current logical instant, decreases the centre's `cash_drawer_balance_cents` by `payout_cents`, and appends a deposit ledger line into that quarter whose `entry_type` is `REDEMPTION_PAID`, whose `amount_cents` is the negative of `payout_cents` — paying a redemption discharges liability — whose `container_count` is `payable_count`, whose `source_kind` is `REDEMPTION_VISIT` and whose `source_ref_id` is this visit's `id`. The liability projection of §2.8 then re-runs, refreshing the quarter's `outstanding_liability_cents`, `redemptions_paid_cents` and `containers_redeemed_count`.

Reversal is the compensating operation and is reserved to a `ProgramAdmin`. It moves the visit `PAID → REVERSED`, which is terminal, and is permitted only while the reporting quarter containing the visit's `visit_date` is still `OPEN`. Every effect of payment is undone by adding a counter-effect rather than by erasing anything. The centre's `cash_drawer_balance_cents` increases by `payout_cents`. A deposit ledger line is appended whose `entry_type` is `REDEMPTION_REVERSED`, whose `amount_cents` is the positive `payout_cents`, whose `container_count` is `payable_count`, whose `source_kind` is `REDEMPTION_VISIT` and whose `source_ref_id` is this visit's `id`. The liability projection re-runs so that the quarter's `outstanding_liability_cents`, `redemptions_paid_cents` and `containers_redeemed_count` all return to their pre-payment values. Every container lot of the visit is reset to `paid_quantity` 0 with `reconciliation_flag` `UNPAID`. And the account's daily bucket is rolled back: `payable_count` is subtracted from `containers_redeemed_today` and `cap_reached_today` is recomputed from the reduced figure, which can free the holder to redeem again the same day.

### 3.7. The Bag-Drop Programme: Deferred Counting and Account Credit

Enrolled holders may skip the counting queue entirely by leaving a tagged bag and being paid later. A `CenterOperator` accepts a **BagDrop** by naming the `account_id` of the enrolled holder, the `center_id` of the receiving centre, the `tag_code` printed on the bag tag, and the `declared_count` the holder claims is inside (an integer of at least 1). The tag code is the bag's natural key, and a reused one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAG_CODE`. The holder must actually be enrolled — an account whose `bag_drop_enrolled` is false is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ENROLLED` — and the receiving centre must be `OPERATIONAL`. Acceptance stamps `dropped_at` with the current logical instant and opens the bag in `status` `DROPPED`. A bag's `status` runs over `DROPPED`, `COUNTED`, `DISPUTED`, `CREDITED` and `VOIDED`, with both `CREDITED` and `VOIDED` terminal. Bags may be listed and filtered by `account_id`, `center_id` and `status`.

**The provisional credit.** Because the holder walks away before anything has been counted, the programme pays a conservative advance immediately, and it is deliberately worth less per container than a real redemption. `provisional_units` is the lesser of `declared_count` and 250 units, and `provisional_credit_cents` is `provisional_units` multiplied by 8 cents. Two consequences follow directly from those two constants: the advance never exceeds 2000 cents however large the declared count, and at eight cents against a ten-cent deposit the holder is always advanced less than the containers are worth. The advance is posted as a credit journal line with `entry_type` `PROVISIONAL`, `source_kind` `BAG_DROP` and `source_ref_id` equal to the new bag's `id`, after which the account's `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed.

**The plant count.** Later a `PlantCounter` submits the real count. The submission carries `counted_quantities`, the array of per-material counted line quantities, and the `expected_bag_drop_version` demanded by §2.6. Submitting against a bag in `DROPPED` moves it `DROPPED → COUNTED` and requires the receiving centre to still be `OPERATIONAL`; submitting against a bag in `DISPUTED` moves it `DISPUTED → COUNTED` as a recount. Either way the final valuation runs and every one of its outputs is *replaced*, not merged — a recount fully supersedes the previous count result rather than adding to it. A submission against a bag in any other `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BAG_DROP_STATE`, and every accepted submission increments the bag's `version` by 1.

The final valuation works as follows. `counted_lines` is the number of entries in the submitted array, and `counted_total` is their sum. `gross_final_cents` is `counted_total` multiplied by the standard per-container deposit of 10 cents. From that gross the programme deducts a flat bag service fee of 50 cents, exactly once per count: `final_credit_cents` is `gross_final_cents` less 50 cents, floored at 0, so a bag holding fewer than five containers yields no credit rather than a debt. `adjustment_cents` is `final_credit_cents` less `provisional_credit_cents`, and it is signed — negative whenever the holder's advance turned out to be generous relative to what was actually in the bag. Finally the count is measured against the holder's declaration: `variance_pct` is `counted_total` less `declared_count`, divided by `declared_count`, rounded to 4 decimal places, and `variance_flag` is true exactly when the **absolute value** of `variance_pct` is strictly greater than one tenth. Over-declaring and under-declaring by more than ten per cent are treated alike. A true `variance_flag` increments the account's `fraud_strikes` by 1 with `last_strike_reason` `BAG_DROP_VARIANCE`, and if that brings the account's `fraud_strikes` to 2 the account moves `ACTIVE → FLAGGED` as in §3.3.

**Settling the bag.** Three settlement actions close a bag out, each requested by naming an `action` with an optional `reason`, and each incrementing the bag's `version` by 1. A settlement action not declared from the bag's current `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BAG_DROP_STATE`.

An `AccountHolder` who disagrees with the count may dispute it, moving the bag `COUNTED → DISPUTED` and incrementing `recount_count` by 1. Disputes are time-boxed: the current logical instant must be strictly earlier than `dropped_at` plus 72 hours, and a dispute raised at or after that three-day deadline is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The loop `DROPPED → COUNTED → DISPUTED → COUNTED` may run more than once, with `recount_count` recording how often.

A `CenterOperator` credits a counted bag, moving it `COUNTED → CREDITED`, which requires the holder's account to be `ACTIVE`. Crediting stamps `settled_at` with the current logical instant and does four things. It appends a credit journal line with `entry_type` `ADJUSTMENT`, `amount_cents` equal to the bag's `adjustment_cents`, `source_kind` `BAG_DROP` and `source_ref_id` equal to this bag's `id` — the signed true-up between what was advanced and what the count justified — and recomputes the account's `credit_balance_cents`. It appends a deposit ledger line with `entry_type` `BAG_DROP_CREDIT`, `amount_cents` equal to the negative of `final_credit_cents`, `container_count` equal to `counted_total`, `source_kind` `BAG_DROP` and `source_ref_id` equal to this bag's `id`, booked into the reporting quarter whose closed date interval contains the calendar date of `settled_at`; if no `OPEN` quarter contains that date the credit is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD`. And it re-runs the liability projection, refreshing the quarter's `outstanding_liability_cents`, `redemptions_paid_cents` and `containers_redeemed_count`.

A `ProgramAdmin` may instead void a bag, and voiding is available from two states, `DROPPED → VOIDED` and `DISPUTED → VOIDED`. Both branches behave identically: a compensating credit journal line is appended with `entry_type` `CLAWBACK`, `amount_cents` equal to the negative of `provisional_credit_cents`, `source_kind` `BAG_DROP` and `source_ref_id` equal to this bag's `id`, clawing back exactly the advance that was paid at drop time, and the account's `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed. Nothing is booked to the deposit ledger, because a voided bag never became a redemption.

### 3.8. Commodity Batches, Baling and Material Revenue

Accepted containers do not vanish once they are paid for; they become a commodity the cooperative sells. A **CommodityBatch** is drawn from a centre's pool by naming the `center_id`, the `material` stream (one of `ALUMINUM`, `GLASS`, `PET_PLASTIC` or `HDPE_PLASTIC`), a `batch_code` as its natural key, and the `container_count` to draw (an integer of at least 1). A reused batch code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BATCH_CODE`, and the centre must be `OPERATIONAL`. The draw cannot exceed what the centre actually holds: `container_count` must be at most the centre's `unbatched_container_count`, and an over-draw is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UNBATCHED_CONTAINERS`. On success the batch opens in `status` `ACCUMULATING`, the centre's `batched_container_count` grows by the drawn count and its `unbatched_container_count` falls by the same amount. A batch's `status` runs `ACCUMULATING`, `SORTED`, `BALED`, `SOLD`, with `SOLD` terminal, and batches may be listed and filtered by `center_id`, `material` and `status`.

Plant processing drives the batch forward through two named actions, `SORT` and `BALE`, each requested by naming an `action` and each incrementing the batch's `version` by 1. A `PlantCounter` sorts the batch with `SORT`, moving it `ACCUMULATING → SORTED`; the request supplies `contamination_pct`, the fraction of the batch mass rejected at sorting, which must be at least 0 and at most 1 inclusive and is stored on the batch. The same role then bales it with `BALE`, moving it `SORTED → BALED`; the request supplies `bale_weights_kg`, the array of per-bale scale weights in kilograms, which must contain at least one element with each element greater than 0 and carrying 2 decimal places. Baling is only permitted on a clean enough batch: `contamination_pct` must be at most 0.05, and a batch above five per cent contamination is refused with `ERR_CONTAMINATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Baling stores the weights, sets `bale_count` to the number of weights supplied, and sets `gross_weight_kg` to the sum of those weights rounded to 2 decimal places. A processing action not declared from the batch's current `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`.

**Selling to the mill.** A `CenterOperator` sells a baled batch, moving it `BALED → SOLD` and stamping `sold_at` with the current logical instant. The sale names the `pricing_mode` — `SPOT` for the price the mill quoted, `INDEX` for the published index price — the `quoted_price_cents_per_tonne` in cents per metric tonne, and the `period_id` of the reporting quarter that will carry the revenue. That quarter must be `OPEN`, or the sale is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`.

Revenue is computed in four pinned steps. First, `effective_price_cents_per_tonne`: under `SPOT` pricing it equals `quoted_price_cents_per_tonne` unchanged, while under `INDEX` pricing the quoted price is reduced by 250 basis points — that is, multiplied by 0.9750 — and rounded to the nearest cent, which lowers every per-bale figure and therefore the batch's gross revenue. Second, `gross_revenue_cents` is built bale by bale, walking `bale_weights_kg` in the order the baling action supplied them. Each bale contributes its weight in kilograms divided by 1000 to convert to tonnes, multiplied by `effective_price_cents_per_tonne`, multiplied by one minus `contamination_pct` — because the contaminated fraction earns nothing — and **that per-bale figure is rounded to the nearest cent before it is added in**. The rounded per-bale figures are then summed. Rounding each bale first and summing afterwards can differ by a cent from rounding a single summed product, and the per-bale order is the required behaviour. Third, `freight_cents` is `gross_weight_kg` divided by 1000 to convert to tonnes, multiplied by a flat freight rate of 4500 cents per tonne, rounded to the nearest cent. Fourth, `net_revenue_cents` is `gross_revenue_cents` less `freight_cents`, floored at 0 — a batch whose freight exceeds its gross earns nothing rather than costing the programme money on this line.

The sale then appends a deposit ledger line whose `entry_type` is `MATERIAL_REVENUE`, whose `amount_cents` is `net_revenue_cents`, whose `container_count` is 0 because this is a pure money line rather than a container movement, whose `source_kind` is `COMMODITY_BATCH` and whose `source_ref_id` is this batch's `id`, booked into the named quarter. The liability projection then re-runs, refreshing the quarter's `material_revenue_cents` and `outstanding_liability_cents` — the latter unchanged in value by this line, since material revenue contributes nothing to deposit liability as §2.8 explains.

### 3.9. Reporting Quarters, Unredeemed Deposits and Funding Settlement

A **ProgramPeriod** is the accounting container for everything above. A `ProgramAdmin` opens one with a `quarter_code` such as `2026Q3` — four digits, the letter Q, then a digit from 1 to 4 — plus a `start_date` and an `end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, where the end date must fall strictly after the start date. An optional `operating_cost_cents` records the administrative cost of running the programme during the quarter and defaults to 0. The quarter code is the period's natural key, refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUARTER_CODE` if reused, and the date interval must be exclusive of every other quarter: if the requested closed interval from `start_date` to `end_date` shares even a single calendar date with any existing quarter — whatever that quarter's `status`, `OPEN`, `RECONCILING` or `CLOSED` — the request is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A new quarter opens in `status` `OPEN` with `funding_status` `UNSETTLED` and every money field at 0. Quarters may be listed and filtered by `status`, and each quarter's deposit ledger may be listed in `created_at` ascending order, filterable by `entry_type` over the seven kinds of line the ledger carries: `DEPOSIT_INITIATED`, `REDEMPTION_PAID`, `REDEMPTION_REVERSED`, `BAG_DROP_CREDIT`, `UNREDEEMED_SWEEP`, `HANDLING_FEE` and `MATERIAL_REVENUE`.

Three period actions drive the quarter, each requested by a `ProgramAdmin` naming an `action` of `RECONCILE`, `REOPEN` or `CLOSE` with an optional `reason`, and each incrementing the quarter's `version` by 1. Reconciliation — `RECONCILE` — moves the quarter `OPEN → RECONCILING` and requires the field work to be finished: no visit whose `visit_date` lies inside the closed interval from `start_date` to `end_date` may still have `count_status` `OPEN`, `COUNTING` or `JAM_HOLD`, and unfinished counting blocks the action with `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_OPEN_VISITS`. Reconciliation stamps `reconciled_at` with the current logical instant and runs the funding settlement over every deposit ledger line of the quarter in `created_at` ascending order, together with the quarter's own `operating_cost_cents`. The settlement runs in ten steps and each step consumes the outputs of the earlier ones:

1. `deposits_initiated_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the quarter's `DEPOSIT_INITIATED` lines, and `containers_initiated_count` is the sum of `container_count` over those same lines.
2. `redemptions_paid_cents` is the negation of the summed `amount_cents` over the quarter's `REDEMPTION_PAID`, `BAG_DROP_CREDIT` and `REDEMPTION_REVERSED` lines taken together — since paid lines are negative and reversal lines positive, the negation yields a positive figure net of reversals. `containers_redeemed_count` is the summed `container_count` over the `REDEMPTION_PAID` and `BAG_DROP_CREDIT` lines, less the summed `container_count` over the `REDEMPTION_REVERSED` lines.
3. `material_revenue_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the quarter's `MATERIAL_REVENUE` lines.
4. `handling_fee_cents` is the tiered reimbursement owed to redemption sites, walked **marginally** over `containers_redeemed_count` rather than as a single average rate. The first 50,000 redeemed containers earn 3 cents each. Containers from the 50,001st through the 150,000th earn 2 cents each. Every container beyond 150,000 earns 1 cent each. Concretely, the fee is 3 cents times the lesser of the redeemed count and 50,000; plus 2 cents times the lesser of the redeemed count and 150,000 reduced by 50,000, floored at 0; plus 1 cent times the redeemed count reduced by 150,000, floored at 0.
5. `gross_unredeemed_cents` is `deposits_initiated_cents` less `redemptions_paid_cents`, floored at 0 — the deposit money that was taken in but never claimed back.
6. `admin_shortfall_cents` is `operating_cost_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents` less `material_revenue_cents`, floored at 0 — what the programme still needs after material sales have paid what they can.
7. `sweep_to_funding_cents` is the lesser of `gross_unredeemed_cents` and `admin_shortfall_cents` — the programme may consume unredeemed deposits only up to the size of its shortfall, and only up to the size of the unredeemed pool.
8. `unredeemed_deposit_cents` is `gross_unredeemed_cents` less `sweep_to_funding_cents` — the residual unredeemed deposits the cooperative retains.
9. `funding_balance_cents` is `material_revenue_cents` plus `sweep_to_funding_cents`, less `operating_cost_cents`, less `handling_fee_cents`. A negative result means the quarter could not fund itself.
10. `funding_status` becomes `SURPLUS` when `funding_balance_cents` is greater than or equal to 0 — the comparison is non-strict, so exactly zero is a surplus — and `SHORTFALL` otherwise.

Reconciliation then books two ledger lines, both with `container_count` 0 and `source_kind` `PROGRAM_ACTION` because neither represents containers moving: one with `entry_type` `HANDLING_FEE` and `amount_cents` equal to `handling_fee_cents`, and one with `entry_type` `UNREDEEMED_SWEEP` and `amount_cents` equal to the negative of `sweep_to_funding_cents`. The liability projection then re-runs to refresh `outstanding_liability_cents`.

Reopening — `REOPEN` — moves the quarter `RECONCILING → OPEN` so late corrections can be absorbed, and it is a true compensation rather than an erasure. Two compensating lines are appended, again with `container_count` 0 and `source_kind` `PROGRAM_ACTION`: a `HANDLING_FEE` line whose `amount_cents` is the negative of `handling_fee_cents`, and an `UNREDEEMED_SWEEP` line whose `amount_cents` is the positive `sweep_to_funding_cents`. Then `handling_fee_cents`, `gross_unredeemed_cents`, `admin_shortfall_cents`, `sweep_to_funding_cents`, `unredeemed_deposit_cents` and `funding_balance_cents` are all reset to 0, `funding_status` returns to `UNSETTLED` and `reconciled_at` returns to null. The liability projection re-runs once more. A reopened quarter may be reconciled again, and the `OPEN → RECONCILING → OPEN` loop may repeat.

Closing — `CLOSE` — moves the quarter `RECONCILING → CLOSED`, which is terminal and settles the books for good; it has no effect beyond the transition itself and the `version` increment every period action carries. A period action not declared from the quarter's current `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`, as is any attempt to append a deposit ledger line — a deposit initiation, a payment, a reversal, a bag-drop credit or a batch sale — to a quarter that is not `OPEN`.

The Authorized Role(s) column lists the roles whose `X-User-Role` header is accepted at the endpoint at all; where a role is additionally restricted to a subset of the actions or the records reachable through an endpoint, that narrowing is stated in section 2.1 and in the relevant subsection of section 3, and it is enforced in addition to the table below.
