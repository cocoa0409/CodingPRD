# Airline Station Ground Handling API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A ground handling company that holds a station contract at an airport lives or dies on the turnaround. Every arriving aircraft must be met by the right number of qualified agents and the right pieces of ground support equipment, within a scheduling window that the carrier published months earlier, and it must leave the gate on time. When it does not, the contract carries money: on-time-performance penalties, damage penalties, volume rebates, and a service-level invoice that an independent auditor must approve before it can be issued. This API is the system of record for that contract at a single station and across a portfolio of stations.

The problems this system solves are the ones a station manager cannot solve on a spreadsheet. Staffing plans must be sized against a published bank of flights whose composition keeps changing; equipment must be allocated without double-booking a tug that still needs twenty minutes to reposition across the apron; training endorsements expire silently and, when they do, an agent must stop working immediately and every occupation already booked against that agent must unwind cleanly; a critical ramp incident must quarantine the implicated equipment within the same request that reported it. Each of those effects reaches across several records at once, and the system is expected to apply all of them immediately and consistently rather than leaving a background job to catch up.

Two further characteristics shape the design. First, the system is **temporally driven**: certification currency, incident age decay, and maintenance holds are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than against wall-clock time, so that a station's state can be reasoned about deterministically. Second, the system is **fiscally two-faced**: the same turnaround is priced one way while an invoice is still open and provisional, and a different way once the invoice is finalized, and the transition between the two must be perfectly reversible if the auditor sends the invoice back.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere in the API. Where a rule below conflicts with a narrative convenience in Section 3, this section governs.

### 2.1. User, Role and Identity Model

Every request — without exception, including reads and including the test-driving clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity that user is acting in. Six roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `StationManager`, `DutyOfficer`, `SafetyOfficer`, `BillingController`, `ContractAuditor`, and `SubcontractorAdmin`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries any string other than those six, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If the headers are well-formed but the role is not one of the roles listed against the requested endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403.

Several endpoints accept more than one action on the same path, and on those endpoints the role check is evaluated against the specific action requested, not merely against the endpoint. Staffing `SUBMIT` and `REVISE` are open to `DutyOfficer` only; staffing `APPROVE` and `REJECT` are open to `StationManager` only. Bank `PUBLISH` is open to `DutyOfficer` only; bank `LOCK` and `ARCHIVE` are open to `StationManager` only. Invoice `FINALIZE` is open to `BillingController` only; invoice `APPROVE` and `REJECT` are open to `ContractAuditor` only. A caller whose role is listed on the endpoint but not on the requested action receives the same `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` with HTTP 403.

Five of the six roles carry an **opaque identity**. When `X-User-Role` is `StationManager`, `DutyOfficer`, `SafetyOfficer`, `BillingController`, or `ContractAuditor`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition asks it to — as `last_staffing_decision_by_user_id` on a flight bank, as `reported_by_user_id`, `investigator_user_id`, `findings_issued_by_user_id` or `closed_by_user_id` on an incident, and as `finalized_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a billing period — but it never compares that value to any record's `id`.

The sixth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `SubcontractorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Subcontractor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Subcontractor` compare that resource's subcontractor foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `SubcontractorAdmin` registering an agent must supply a `subcontractor_id` equal to `X-User-ID`, and a `SubcontractorAdmin` issuing an endorsement must name an agent whose `subcontractor_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A violation of either is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403.

Independently of role, three operations enforce **segregation of duties** on the acting `X-User-ID` value rather than on the role name: the user who reported an incident may not be the user who takes it into investigation; the user who issued findings on an incident may not be the user who closes it; and the user who finalized a billing period may not be the user who approves it. Any of these three is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. Note that this is a check on identity, not on capability — a second `SafetyOfficer` may perfectly well investigate a report filed by the first.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Sorting and List Filters

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` sets how many records are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts the records that match the filters before pagination is applied.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Most list endpoints also accept domain filters, and where a filter is supplied it narrows the result set by exact match on that attribute. Stations may be filtered by `status` and `season_code`. Subcontractors may be filtered by `station_id`, `parent_subcontractor_id` and `status`. Flight banks accept `station_id`, `season_code`, `bank_status` and `staffing_status`. Turnarounds accept `station_id`, `flight_bank_id`, `status` and `otp_result`. Agents accept `station_id`, `subcontractor_id` and `status`. Endorsements accept `agent_id`, `station_id`, `cert_type` and `status`. Equipment accepts `station_id`, `gse_type` and `status`. Resource occupations accept `turn_id`, `agent_id`, `gse_unit_id`, `status` and `resource_kind`. Incidents accept `station_id`, `turn_id`, `gse_unit_id`, `agent_id`, `subcontractor_id`, `status` and `severity`. Billing periods accept `station_id` and `status`. Billing lines accept `billing_period_id`, `turn_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties and Left-to-Right Addressing

All eleven record types in this system — stations, subcontractors, flight banks, turnarounds, agents, certifications, GSE units, turn resources, incidents, billing periods and billing lines — carry the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the record was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record.

A "mutation" here includes the recomputation of a derived attribute. When a resource occupation is created and the owning turnaround's `assigned_agent_count` moves as a result, the turnaround has been mutated: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments. The same is true of every record touched by a cascade described in Section 2.7.

Every endpoint whose URL carries record identifiers resolves them strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve produces its own 404 immediately, before any later identifier or any body field is examined. The eleven not-found codes are `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLIGHT_BANK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GSE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURN_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BILLING_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. The same eleven codes are reused when a foreign key supplied inside a request body fails to resolve — an unknown `station_id` on a subcontractor, flight bank, turnaround, agent, certification, GSE unit, incident or billing period creation yields `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `parent_subcontractor_id` or `subcontractor_id` yields `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `flight_bank_id` yields `ERR_FLIGHT_BANK_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `turn_id` yields `ERR_TURN_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `agent_id` yields `ERR_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `gse_unit_id` yields `ERR_GSE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `billing_period_id` yields `ERR_BILLING_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`.

Every record in this system also belongs to exactly one station, directly or transitively, and the system refuses to knit records together across a station boundary. A turnaround's flight bank must sit at the turnaround's station; an agent assigned to a turnaround must be homed at that turnaround's station; a GSE unit committed to a turnaround must be based at that turnaround's station; an endorsement must be issued at its agent's station; a subcontractor must be registered at its agent's station; a tier-2 subcontractor's sponsor must be registered at the same station as the tier-2 firm itself; the turnaround a billing line charges must belong to the same station as the billing period the line accrues into; and an incident's referenced turnaround, GSE unit and agent must all belong to the incident's own station. Any of these mismatches is rejected with `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 409.

### 2.5. The Logical Station Clock

Everything time-sensitive in this system is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**, never against the host machine's wall clock. Before any client has moved it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to real time under any circumstance. An administrative capability accepts a `current_utc` instant, sets the clock to it, and reports the resulting `current_utc` back to the caller; the clock may be moved forward or backward to any instant, and all six roles may drive it because it exists to make the system's temporal behaviour testable.

Setting the clock changes no record's status. What it does change is every value that is *derived* from the current instant: an endorsement's `currency_state`, an agent's `certification_posture`, and both subcontractor compliance figures are all recomputed against the new instant the next time they are read. Records that require a status change — an agent suspension, an equipment quarantine — only move when the corresponding business event is submitted.

Timestamps stamped by the server, such as a station's `opened_at` and `closed_at`, an endorsement's `revoked_at`, and a GSE unit's `last_service_at`, are taken from the logical clock at the moment of the transition.

### 2.6. Order of Evaluation

When a single request could fail several different ways, the system evaluates the checks in a fixed order and returns the first failure it finds. This ordering is normative: a request that violates checks at positions 4 and 12 must report the position-4 error, never the position-12 one.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The role is listed on the endpoint and, on multi-action endpoints, is permitted for the requested action, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every record named in the URL exists, validated left to right, otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint that carries a path identifier.
4. **Request shape.** Every required body field is present; every enum field carries a declared member; every numeric field sits inside its declared range; `window_end` is strictly after `window_start`; an endorsement's `expires_at` is strictly after its `issued_at`; an incident's `occurred_at` is not after the current logical clock; an agent occupation carries `agent_id` and `role_on_turn` and carries neither `gse_unit_id` nor `gse_type`; an equipment occupation carries exactly one of `gse_unit_id` or `gse_type` and carries neither `agent_id` nor `role_on_turn`. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
5. **Body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing record, otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to the creation endpoints for subcontractors, flight banks, turnarounds, agents, certifications, GSE units, turn resources, incidents, billing periods and billing lines.
6. **Station-scope coherence.** The cross-record station checks listed in Section 2.4, otherwise `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to the creation endpoints for subcontractors, flight banks, turnarounds, agents, certifications, turn resources, incidents and billing lines.
7. **Stale-view protection.** The supplied expected-version field equals the live parent version, otherwise `ERR_STALE_TURN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (412). Applies to turn-resource creation and billing-line creation.
8. **Acting-entity ownership.** A `SubcontractorAdmin` writes only records belonging to the subcontractor whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`, otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to agent creation and certification creation.
9. **Segregation of duties.** The reporter may not investigate, the findings issuer may not close, the finalizer may not approve, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to the incident transition endpoint and the billing-period transition endpoint.
10. **Own-state admissibility.** The target record's own status admits the requested transition, including terminal-state lockdown, the incident reopen cap, the rule that billing lines may only be written against an `OPEN` period, and the rule that resource occupations may only be written against a `SCHEDULED` or `RESOURCED` turnaround. The codes are `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TURN_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED` and `ERR_TURN_NOT_BILLABLE`, all HTTP 409 except `ERR_TURN_NOT_BILLABLE`, which is 422. Applies to all action endpoints plus turn-resource creation and billing-line creation.
11. **Cross-entity state gates.** The station must be `OPEN` for flight-bank creation, turnaround creation, turn-resource creation and turnaround `START`; the owning bank's `bank_status` must be `PUBLISHED` for a staffing `SUBMIT` and must be either `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED` for a turnaround `START`; the owning bank's `staffing_status` must be `APPROVED` for a turnaround `START`; an assigned agent's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; that agent's employing subcontractor must be in `APPROVED` or `PROBATION`; an explicitly named GSE unit's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`. The codes are `ERR_STATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_BANK_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_STAFFING_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_APPROVED` and `ERR_GSE_NOT_SERVICEABLE`, all HTTP 409. Applies to flight-bank creation, turnaround creation, turn-resource creation, the turnaround lifecycle endpoint and the staffing-transition endpoint.
12. **Safety gates.** The station's `oversight_posture` must permit the operation; the assigned agent must hold every endorsement its ramp role demands, current at the occupation's start; and a billing period may not be finalized while its station carries an unclosed critical incident. The codes are `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (409), `ERR_OPEN_CRITICAL_INCIDENT` (409) and `ERR_CERT_NOT_CURRENT` (422). Applies to turnaround creation, flight-bank creation, turn-resource creation and the billing-period transition endpoint.
13. **Interval geometry.** A turnaround's scheduled window must sit inside its bank's window; an equipment occupation must not collide with another occupation on the same unit under the buffered half-open test, nor with that unit's closed service-hold window; an agent occupation must not collide with another occupation for the same agent under the bare closed-interval test. The codes are `ERR_TURN_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_BANK`, `ERR_GSE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_GSE_SERVICE_HOLD_CONFLICT` and `ERR_AGENT_DUTY_OVERLAP`, all HTTP 409. Applies to turnaround creation and turn-resource creation.
14. **Aggregates and uniqueness.** An agent's projected same-day duty must not exceed the daily cap; a staffing plan may only be approved at full coverage; `iata_code` is unique across all stations while `bank_code`, `employee_code`, `asset_tag` and `period_code` are each unique within their station; the equipment selector's candidate pool must not be empty. The codes are `ERR_AGENT_DUTY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422), `ERR_STAFFING_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (422), `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409) and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_GSE` (409). Applies to station creation, flight-bank creation, agent creation, GSE-unit creation, billing-period creation, turn-resource creation and the staffing-transition endpoint.
15. **Fiscal gates.** A turnaround may carry at most one non-void billing line across all periods; a billing period may only be finalized when its combined penalties stay within the station's cap; a station may not be closed while it still holds live turnarounds or an unfinished billing period. The codes are `ERR_DUPLICATE_BILLING_LINE` (409), `ERR_PENALTY_CAP_BREACHED` (422) and `ERR_STATION_HAS_ACTIVE_WORK` (409). Applies to billing-line creation, the billing-period transition endpoint and the station lifecycle endpoint.
16. **Season coherence.** A flight bank's `season_code` must equal its station's `season_code` at creation, and a seasonal rollover must name a season different from the one already in force. The code is `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to flight-bank creation and the season-rollover endpoint.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline Across the Station Hierarchy

Records in this system are peers linked by foreign keys rather than nested URLs, but they are not independent. A single request routinely moves several records, and when it does, every touched record is mutated in full: its status changes if the cascade says so, its `updated_at` is refreshed, and its `version` increments. The cascades are applied immediately, inside the request that triggered them — there is no deferred reconciliation.

Cascades chain. Suspending a station locks its published banks; because those banks became locked, their approved staffing plans fall back to revision; because those plans fell back, the live turnarounds inside them are disrupted; and because those turnarounds were disrupted, every reserved or committed occupation on them is cancelled. Each hop fires because the previous hop happened, and a hop whose precondition is not met simply does not fire, without stopping the hops that are met.

Whenever an occupation leaves the reserved-or-committed population, the two minute ledgers it fed must be reduced by exactly that occupation's minutes. The system guarantees three conserved quantities: creating an equipment occupation of a given number of minutes and then releasing or cancelling it returns that unit's `committed_occupation_minutes` to the value it had before the create; the same holds for an agent's `assigned_duty_minutes` on agent occupations; and creating a billing line and then voiding it returns the owning period's `accrued_cents` and `line_count` to their pre-create values. Compensating movements are exact, not approximate.

### 2.8. Stale-View Protection on Turnaround and Period Writes

Two write operations require the client to prove it has seen the current state of the record it is accruing against.

Creating a resource occupation requires the non-optional field `expected_turn_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Turn`. If that value does not equal the turnaround's live `version` at the moment the write is processed, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TURN_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Adding a second, third or fourth occupation to the same turnaround is a perfectly legitimate repeatable operation — a client that re-reads the turnaround and retries with the fresh `version` succeeds — so this is not a state precondition in disguise. It exists because the previous write already moved that turnaround's coverage counters and possibly its `status`, and a client holding the older view would be committing against a picture that no longer exists.

Creating a billing line requires the non-optional field `expected_billing_period_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `BillingPeriod`. A mismatch with the period's live `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Accruing further lines into an open period is likewise legitimate and repeatable, but every accrual moves `gross_total_cents` and can move `rebate_tier`, so a writer with a stale view of the period would be accruing against a superseded rebate basis.

No other endpoint takes an expected-version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.9. Cents, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional currency values anywhere in a request or a response.

Wherever a monetary computation produces a fractional result, it is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Rounding happens at pinned points and nowhere else, and the arithmetic that leads up to a rounding point must be carried out in exact decimal arithmetic. Binary floating-point arithmetic is not acceptable: a raw charge of eighteen thousand plus twenty-three thousand three hundred cents, multiplied by one and by zero point nine six five, is exactly thirty-nine thousand eight hundred fifty-four and one half cents, and must round up to thirty-nine thousand eight hundred fifty-five.

The rounding *order* is pinned as tightly as the rounding *method*. Where an amount is derived per billing line and then aggregated into a period, each line's amount is rounded first and the already-rounded line amounts are then added together. Rounding the aggregate of unrounded line amounts is forbidden and produces different answers.

Percentages — a station's `otp_target_pct` and `otp_rolling_pct`, a bank's `staffing_coverage_pct`, a subcontractor's `compliance_score` and `audit_compliance_score`, and a station's `damage_penalty_multiplier` — are carried to exactly two decimal places and rounded half up. Durations are whole numbers of minutes; wherever a duration is derived from two instants, the elapsed seconds are divided by sixty and the result is taken down to the next whole minute.

### 2.10. Station Oversight Posture

Each station continuously carries an `oversight_posture`, one of `NORMAL`, `ENHANCED` or `RESTRICTED`. It is derived, never supplied, and it is recomputed on every incident status change, on every turnaround completion, and when a suspended station resumes.

The derivation reads three inputs. A station's `open_critical_incident_count` is the number of incidents at that station whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`; its `open_major_incident_count` is the number whose `severity` is `MAJOR` and whose `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`. In both counts, `REPORTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` and `REOPENED` all count as open, and only `CLOSED` does not. The posture is then `RESTRICTED` if `open_critical_incident_count` is at least 1. Otherwise it is `ENHANCED` if either `open_major_incident_count` is at least 1, or the station has completed at least 3 turnarounds and its `otp_rolling_pct` is strictly below its `otp_target_pct`. Otherwise it is `NORMAL`.

Posture is a gate, not a label. While a station is `RESTRICTED`, no new turnaround and no new flight bank may be created there; both are refused with `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` and HTTP 409. While a station is `ENHANCED`, an occupation may not be created for an agent whose `employment_type` is `SUBCONTRACTED`; that too is refused with `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. A station at `NORMAL` posture carries no additional gate. Note that a `RESTRICTED` station still gates only those two creations — it does not, by itself, block resource commitment, and the subcontracted-agent gate is specific to `ENHANCED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Station and Rolling Its Season

A `StationManager` opens the contract by registering the station itself. The registration carries the `iata_code` — exactly three characters, all uppercase letters A through Z, and unique across every station the system knows about — together with a human-readable `name` and the `handler_code` of the ground handling company operating the contract. An `iata_code` that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

The registration must also name the `season_code` in force at the station, either `SUMMER` or `WINTER`; a station record that has never had one set reads `SUMMER`. The remaining commercial parameters of the contract may each be omitted in favour of a default. `season_start_date` and `season_end_date` describe that season's window and both default to null; they are informational only and are never validated against the dates of any turnaround. `otp_target_pct` is the contractual on-time-performance target, defaulting to 85.00, carried to two decimal places, and constrained to the range from 0.00 to 100.00 inclusive. `otp_tolerance_minutes` is the departure delay, in whole minutes, that still counts as on time — the boundary is inclusive — and it defaults to 15 and may not be negative. `gse_reposition_buffer_minutes` is the repositioning time appended to the end of every equipment occupation before that unit may be occupied again; it defaults to 20 and may not be negative. `penalty_cap_cents` is the largest combined penalty an invoice may carry before finalization is refused, defaulting to 5000000 cents, and may not be negative. `damage_penalty_multiplier` scales the severity-based damage penalty table for this station, defaults to 1.00, is carried to two decimal places, and must be strictly greater than zero.

A freshly registered station lands in `PLANNED` with `oversight_posture` at `NORMAL`, `otp_rolling_pct` at 100.00, and `completed_turn_count`, `on_time_turn_count`, `open_critical_incident_count` and `open_major_incident_count` all at zero. Both `opened_at` and `closed_at` are null.

The station's own lifecycle is driven by a single manager-only capability that takes an `action` — one of `OPEN`, `SUSPEND`, `RESUME` or `CLOSE` — and an optional `reason`. Note that the action names and the status names are not the same vocabulary: the `CLOSE` action produces the `CLOSED` status. Opening a station with `OPEN` moves it `PLANNED → OPEN`, stamps `opened_at` from the logical clock, and recomputes `oversight_posture`, which on a fresh station evaluates to `NORMAL`. Suspending one with `SUSPEND` moves it `OPEN → SUSPENDED` and is the single most destructive operation in the system — see below. Resuming with `RESUME` moves it `SUSPENDED → OPEN`, but only while `open_critical_incident_count` is exactly zero; on success the posture is recomputed. Resumption deliberately does **not** restore anything the suspension broke: turnarounds that went to `DISRUPTED` stay `DISRUPTED`, and occupations that were cancelled stay cancelled. Closing with `CLOSE` moves the station `OPEN → CLOSED` or `SUSPENDED → CLOSED`. Any action that is not an edge out of the station's present status — opening a station that is already `OPEN`, resuming one that was never suspended, or anything at all attempted on a `CLOSED` station, which is terminal — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Closing carries two guards of its own. The station must hold no turnaround in `SCHEDULED`, `RESOURCED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and no billing period in `OPEN` or `PENDING_APPROVAL`; either condition refuses the close with `ERR_STATION_HAS_ACTIVE_WORK`. When the close succeeds, `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, every flight bank at the station whose `bank_status` is not already `ARCHIVED` moves to `ARCHIVED`, and every GSE unit at the station whose `status` is not already `RETIRED` moves to `RETIRED`.

Suspension is where the cascade discipline of Section 2.7 earns its keep, and it runs four hops deep. First, every flight bank at the station whose `bank_status` is `PUBLISHED` moves to `LOCKED`. Second, because those banks became locked, each of them whose `staffing_status` is `APPROVED` moves to `REVISION_REQUIRED`. Third, because those staffing plans fell back, every turnaround inside those banks whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RESOURCED` moves to `DISRUPTED`. Fourth, because those turnarounds were disrupted, every occupation on them whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `cancel_reason` recorded as `STATION_SUSPENDED`, and each such cancellation reduces the affected unit's `committed_occupation_minutes` or the affected agent's `assigned_duty_minutes` by that occupation's `occupied_minutes`.

Rolling the season is a separate manager-only capability whose entire value is its cascade: the station's `status` stays `OPEN` throughout. The request names `to_season_code` together with a replacement `season_start_date` and `season_end_date`, all three required. The station must be `OPEN`, and `to_season_code` must differ from the `season_code` already in force — naming the season the station is already running is refused with `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`. On success the station's `season_code`, `season_start_date` and `season_end_date` are replaced, and three hops follow. Every flight bank at the station whose `season_code` equals the *outgoing* season and whose `bank_status` is `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED` moves to `ARCHIVED`. Because those banks were archived, every turnaround inside them whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RESOURCED` moves to `CANCELLED`. Because those turnarounds were cancelled, every occupation on them whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `SEASON_ROLLOVER`, decrementing the corresponding `committed_occupation_minutes` and `assigned_duty_minutes` ledgers. Archiving driven by a station close or by a seasonal rollover is exempt from the ordinary live-turnaround guard that a manual archive must satisfy.

### 3.2. Subcontractor Oversight and the Two Compliance Scores

Ground handling at a large station is rarely done entirely in-house, and the contract makes the handler answerable for the firms it subcontracts to. A `StationManager` registers a subcontracting firm against a `station_id` with its `legal_name` and, when the firm is a second-tier subcontractor working under another firm, a `parent_subcontractor_id` naming its tier-1 sponsor. The `tier` attribute is never supplied: it reads `TIER_2` when `parent_subcontractor_id` is non-null and `TIER_1` otherwise. A newly registered firm sits in `PENDING_VETTING` with `compliance_score` and `audit_compliance_score` both at 100.00 and `oversight_flag` false.

Oversight decisions are recorded through a manager-only capability that carries a `decision` — one of `APPROVE`, `PLACE_ON_PROBATION`, `REINSTATE`, `SUSPEND` or `TERMINATE` — and an optional `note`. Approving a firm with `APPROVE` moves it `PENDING_VETTING → APPROVED`; when the firm is `TIER_2`, its sponsor must itself be `APPROVED` at that moment, and a sponsor in any other status refuses the approval with `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_APPROVED`. Placing a firm on probation with `PLACE_ON_PROBATION` moves it `APPROVED → PROBATION`. Reinstating one with `REINSTATE` moves it `PROBATION → APPROVED`, but only while its `audit_compliance_score` is at least 40.00. Suspending a firm with `SUSPEND` moves it `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` or `PROBATION → SUSPENDED` — the decision is admissible from either of those two statuses — and once suspended, no new occupation may be created for any agent that firm employs. Terminating a firm with `TERMINATE` moves it `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`, which is terminal. Approval, probation and reinstatement each recompute both compliance figures. Any decision that is not an edge out of the firm's present status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Termination cascades. Every agent whose `subcontractor_id` names the terminated firm and whose `status` is not already `TERMINATED` moves to `TERMINATED`; because those agents were terminated, every occupation of theirs whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `SUBCONTRACTOR_TERMINATED`, and each cancellation reduces that agent's `assigned_duty_minutes` by the occupation's `occupied_minutes`.

Two counters and two scores describe a firm's record. Its `total_incident_count` is the number of incidents whose `subcontractor_id` points at it, regardless of investigation status or of who was found liable. Its `closed_liable_incident_count` is the number of those incidents whose `status` is `CLOSED` **and** whose `liability_party` is `SUBCONTRACTOR`. Both scores start from a clean sheet of 100 and are reduced by severity weights of 5 points for a `MINOR` incident, 15 for a `MAJOR` one and 40 for a `CRITICAL` one, with the final figure floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimal places. They differ in which incidents they read and in whether age matters.

The **rolling** figure, published as `compliance_score`, reads only those incidents that are both `CLOSED` and attributed to `liability_party` `SUBCONTRACTOR`, and it decays each one by age. For every such incident, the age in whole days is the elapsed time from its `occurred_at` to the current logical clock, divided into whole days and taken down to the next whole day. An incident aged 90 days or fewer contributes its full severity weight; one aged more than 90 days but no more than 180 days contributes half its severity weight; one aged more than 180 days contributes nothing at all. The score is 100 minus the sum of those decayed contributions, floored at 0.00. A firm whose `compliance_score` falls strictly below 60.00 has its `oversight_flag` set to true; at exactly 60.00 the flag stays false.

The **audit** figure, published as `audit_compliance_score`, is deliberately harsher. It reads every incident whose `subcontractor_id` points at the firm — any `status`, any `liability_party`, including incidents still under investigation and incidents where a carrier or third party was ultimately blamed — and applies no decay whatsoever. It is 100 minus the plain sum of the severity weights of all of those incidents, floored at 0.00. This is the figure that gates reinstatement, and it is also the figure that drives the automatic drop to probation described in Section 3.8. Both figures are recomputed when a firm is registered, whenever an incident enters or leaves `CLOSED`, and on every read of the firm.

### 3.3. Flight Banks and Their Two Independent Status Axes

A **flight bank** is a published window of connecting flights that the station staffs as a unit. Either a `DutyOfficer` or a `StationManager` creates one against a `station_id` with a `bank_code` that must be unique among the banks at that station, a `season_code`, and the `window_start` and `window_end` instants that bound the bank. The `season_code` supplied must equal the station's own `season_code` at the moment of creation; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`. The optional `planned_headcount` is the agent headcount the station intends to roster for the bank; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Bank creation requires the station to be `OPEN`, and it is refused at a `RESTRICTED` station.

What makes a bank unusual is that it carries **two status axes on one record**, and they move independently of one another. The `bank_status` axis tracks schedule publication and runs `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `LOCKED`, `ARCHIVED`. The `staffing_status` axis tracks approval of the staffing plan and runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `REVISION_REQUIRED`. A new bank starts at `DRAFT` on both axes, with `required_headcount` and `turn_count` at 0 and `staffing_coverage_pct` at 100.00. Nothing that moves one axis touches the other.

The publication axis is driven by a bank lifecycle capability taking an `action` of `PUBLISH`, `LOCK` or `ARCHIVE`. A `DutyOfficer` publishes the bank with `PUBLISH`, moving `bank_status` `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`; publication requires that the bank still be `DRAFT` on that axis, that the owning station be `OPEN`, and that `turn_count` be at least 1 — an empty bank cannot be published. Publication recomputes `required_headcount` and `staffing_coverage_pct`. A `StationManager` locks the bank with `LOCK`, moving `bank_status` `PUBLISHED → LOCKED`, which is admissible only when `staffing_status` is `APPROVED`; once locked, no further turnaround may be created inside the bank. A `StationManager` archives the bank with `ARCHIVE`, moving `bank_status` `PUBLISHED → ARCHIVED` or `LOCKED → ARCHIVED`; a manual archive is admissible from `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED`, and requires that the bank hold no turnaround in `SCHEDULED`, `RESOURCED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. That live-turnaround requirement is waived when the archive arrives as a cascade from a station closure or a seasonal rollover. An archived bank is read-only and terminal.

The staffing axis is driven by a separate staffing capability taking an `action` of `SUBMIT`, `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `REVISE`, plus an optional `planned_headcount`. A `DutyOfficer` submits the plan with `SUBMIT`, moving `staffing_status` `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`; submission requires the staffing axis to be `DRAFT` and requires `bank_status` to be exactly `PUBLISHED`, and a submission attempted while the bank is not published is refused with `ERR_BANK_NOT_PUBLISHED`. A `StationManager` approves the plan with `APPROVE`, moving `staffing_status` `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`; approval requires the station to be `OPEN` and requires `staffing_coverage_pct` to be at least 100.00, with a shortfall refused as `ERR_STAFFING_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. A `StationManager` rejects it instead with `REJECT`, moving `staffing_status` `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUIRED`. Both approval and rejection stamp `last_staffing_decision_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. A `DutyOfficer` then revises with `REVISE`, moving `staffing_status` `REVISION_REQUIRED → DRAFT`; revision replaces `planned_headcount` when the field is supplied, leaves it untouched when it is omitted, and recomputes `staffing_coverage_pct` either way. `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUIRED → DRAFT` is the staffing revision cycle, and it may be walked as many times as the station needs.

There is also an edge on the staffing axis that no client requests. When a turnaround inside the bank demotes from `RESOURCED` back to `SCHEDULED`, when a newly created turnaround drops `staffing_coverage_pct` below 100.00, or when the owning station is suspended, a bank whose `staffing_status` is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → REVISION_REQUIRED` and recomputes `required_headcount` and `staffing_coverage_pct`. The station has, in effect, lost the plan it signed off on.

Coverage itself is a simple ratio with one convention worth stating plainly. The bank's `required_headcount` is the sum, over every turnaround in the bank whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `RESOURCED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, of the agent requirement of that turnaround's service level; turnarounds in `CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED` are excluded from the sum entirely. The bank's `turn_count` is the number of turnarounds pointing at the bank whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`. When `required_headcount` is zero, `staffing_coverage_pct` is 100.00 by convention — an empty bank is fully covered. Otherwise `staffing_coverage_pct` is 100 multiplied by `planned_headcount`, divided by `required_headcount`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Coverage is recomputed when the bank is created, when a turnaround is created or cancelled, on staffing submission and revision, and immediately before every approval decision.

### 3.4. Scheduling and Running a Turnaround

The **turnaround** is the unit of work. A `DutyOfficer` schedules one inside a bank by supplying the `station_id`, the `flight_bank_id`, the carrier's `flight_number`, the `turn_type`, and the scheduled on-block and off-block instants `scheduled_in` and `scheduled_out`. All six are required. The station must be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STATION_NOT_OPEN`; the station must not be at `RESTRICTED` posture; and the bank must still accept new work — a bank that has been locked or archived takes no further turnarounds, and an attempt to add one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The scheduled window must sit inside the bank's own window: `scheduled_in` earlier than the bank's `window_start`, or `scheduled_out` later than the bank's `window_end`, is refused with `ERR_TURN_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_BANK`. Both bank boundaries are inclusive.

The `turn_type` is the single most consequential field on the record, because it drives four different tables at once. It is one of `TOWING_ONLY`, `NARROWBODY_QUICK`, `NARROWBODY_STANDARD`, `FREIGHTER` or `WIDEBODY`. A towing-only movement needs 1 agent and 1 piece of equipment, bills at 18000 cents, and has a standard ground time of 20 minutes. A quick narrowbody turn needs 2 agents and 1 unit, bills at 42000 cents, and is standard at 30 minutes. A standard narrowbody turn needs 3 agents and 1 unit, bills at 58000 cents, and is standard at 45 minutes. A freighter needs 3 agents and 2 units, bills at 110000 cents, and is standard at 120 minutes. A widebody needs 4 agents and 2 units, bills at 145000 cents, and is standard at 90 minutes. The agent and equipment figures are published on the turnaround as `required_agent_count` and `required_gse_count`; the rate and the standard ground time surface later, in billing.

On creation the turnaround lands in `SCHEDULED` with `otp_result` at `PENDING`, `version` at 1, `assigned_agent_count` and `assigned_gse_count` at zero, and `billed` false. Its `scheduled_ground_minutes` is the elapsed time from `scheduled_in` to `scheduled_out` expressed in whole minutes. `actual_in`, `actual_out`, `actual_ground_minutes`, `delay_minutes` and `delay_code` are all null until the turnaround runs. The owning bank immediately recomputes `required_headcount`, `turn_count` and `staffing_coverage_pct`, and if the new turnaround drags coverage below 100.00 on a bank whose `staffing_status` was `APPROVED`, that plan drops to `REVISION_REQUIRED`.

Two of the turnaround's status changes are never requested by a client. When an occupation is added and the turnaround's `assigned_agent_count` reaches or exceeds `required_agent_count` **and** its `assigned_gse_count` reaches or exceeds `required_gse_count`, the turnaround promotes `SCHEDULED → RESOURCED`; no ledger moves on this hop, only the turnaround's own `status` and `version`. When an occupation is released or cancelled and either count falls back below its requirement, the turnaround demotes `RESOURCED → SCHEDULED`, and because it demoted, an owning bank sitting at `APPROVED` on the staffing axis drops to `REVISION_REQUIRED`. Separately, suspending the station moves a turnaround `SCHEDULED → DISRUPTED` or `RESOURCED → DISRUPTED`, cancelling every reserved or committed occupation on it with `cancel_reason` `STATION_SUSPENDED` and the matching ledger decrements.

The turnaround's own lifecycle capability belongs to the `DutyOfficer` and takes an `action` of `START`, `COMPLETE` or `CANCEL`, with the optional fields `actual_in`, `actual_out`, `delay_code` and `reason`. Starting the turnaround with `START` moves it `RESOURCED → IN_PROGRESS`; it must be `RESOURCED`, the station must be `OPEN`, the owning bank's `bank_status` must be `PUBLISHED` or `LOCKED` (otherwise `ERR_BANK_NOT_PUBLISHED`), and its `staffing_status` must be `APPROVED` (otherwise `ERR_STAFFING_NOT_APPROVED`). On success `actual_in` takes the supplied value, or the current logical clock when the field is omitted, and every occupation on the turnaround sitting at `RESERVED` moves to `COMMITTED`. That hop moves no ledger, because reserved and committed occupations both count toward the minute ledgers.

Completing the turnaround with `COMPLETE` moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and does the most work of any single request in the system. The supplied `actual_out` must not be earlier than the recorded `actual_in`. On success `actual_out` is recorded; `actual_ground_minutes` becomes the elapsed time from `actual_in` to `actual_out` in whole minutes, rounded down; `delay_minutes` becomes the elapsed time from `scheduled_out` to `actual_out` in whole minutes rounded down, floored at zero so that an early departure records zero rather than a negative number; and `delay_code` records the supplied attribution, one of `GH_STAFFING`, `GH_EQUIPMENT`, `GH_LOADING`, `CARRIER`, `ATC`, `WEATHER` or `NONE`. Every occupation on the turnaround sitting at `COMMITTED` moves to `RELEASED`, reducing the relevant unit's `committed_occupation_minutes` or agent's `assigned_duty_minutes` by that occupation's `occupied_minutes`. Each equipment occupation released this way adds 1 to its unit's `cycles_since_service`, and any unit whose `cycles_since_service` has thereby reached or passed its `service_interval_cycles` moves `AVAILABLE → IN_MAINTENANCE` and receives a four-hour service hold running from the current logical clock. Finally the station's `completed_turn_count`, `on_time_turn_count`, `otp_rolling_pct` and `oversight_posture` are all recomputed.

On-time classification is decided at that same moment, and the order of its three tests matters. First, if `delay_minutes` is less than or equal to the station's `otp_tolerance_minutes`, the `otp_result` is `ON_TIME` — the boundary is inclusive, and because this test runs first, a turnaround inside tolerance is `ON_TIME` even when its `delay_code` is `WEATHER`. Otherwise, if `delay_code` is `CARRIER`, `ATC` or `WEATHER`, the `otp_result` is `EXCLUDED`: the delay was not the handler's to prevent. Otherwise the `otp_result` is `DELAYED`, which in practice means the attribution was `GH_STAFFING`, `GH_EQUIPMENT`, `GH_LOADING` or `NONE`. The station's `completed_turn_count` then counts its turnarounds whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `otp_result` is `ON_TIME` or `DELAYED`; its `on_time_turn_count` counts those whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `otp_result` is `ON_TIME`. `EXCLUDED` and `PENDING` turnarounds appear in neither the numerator nor the denominator. The station's `otp_rolling_pct` is 100.00 when `completed_turn_count` is zero, and otherwise is 100 multiplied by `on_time_turn_count`, divided by `completed_turn_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Cancelling a turnaround with `CANCEL` moves it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, `RESOURCED → CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED → CANCELLED` — those three are the admissible origins — and is refused with `ERR_TURN_NOT_BILLABLE` while the turnaround's `billed` flag is true, because a charge has already been accrued against it. Cancellation moves every occupation on the turnaround sitting at `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` to `CANCELLED` with the corresponding decrements to `committed_occupation_minutes` and `assigned_duty_minutes`, and the owning bank recomputes `required_headcount` and `staffing_coverage_pct`. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal; any action attempted from either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.5. The Ramp Workforce: Agents and Endorsement Currency

An agent is registered either by a `StationManager` or by a `SubcontractorAdmin` acting for its own firm. The registration names the `station_id` that is the agent's home station, an `employee_code` that must be unique among the agents at that station, and the agent's `full_name`. Supplying a `subcontractor_id` makes the agent an employee of that firm; omitting it leaves the agent directly employed. The derived `employment_type` reads `SUBCONTRACTED` when `subcontractor_id` is non-null and `DIRECT` otherwise, and it is never supplied by the client. A new agent lands `ACTIVE` with `assigned_duty_minutes` at zero and `certification_posture` at `LAPSED`, because an agent who holds no ramp-safety endorsement yet is not current for anything.

Endorsements are issued as **certifications**, again by a `StationManager` or by a `SubcontractorAdmin` acting for its own firm. Each names the `agent_id` it belongs to, the `station_id` it is valid at, the `cert_type`, and the effective window `issued_at` to `expires_at`, where `expires_at` must be strictly later than `issued_at`. The `cert_type` is one of `RAMP_SAFETY`, `PUSHBACK`, `BELT_LOADER`, `GPU`, `DEICING` or `LOADMASTER`. Endorsements are emphatically station-scoped and do not travel: an endorsement issued at one station never satisfies a requirement at another, and the `station_id` on the endorsement must match the agent's own `station_id`. A newly issued endorsement lands at `VALID` with `revoked_at` and `revoked_reason` null. Issuing one makes the owning agent recompute `certification_posture`, and when that agent was `SUSPENDED` and the new endorsement is a currently-effective `RAMP_SAFETY` at the agent's own station, the agent moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`.

Whether an endorsement is *effective at a given instant* is the primitive that everything else is built on: the endorsement's `status` must be `VALID`, its `issued_at` must be at or before that instant, and its `expires_at` must be strictly after it. The upper bound is exclusive, so an endorsement whose `expires_at` falls exactly on the instant being tested is not effective. The published `currency_state` applies that primitive at the current logical clock: it reads `REVOKED` when the endorsement's `status` is `REVOKED`; otherwise `LAPSED` when the endorsement is not effective at the clock; otherwise `EXPIRING` when the time remaining from the clock to `expires_at` is 30 days or less; otherwise `CURRENT`. An agent's `certification_posture` summarises the one endorsement the ramp cannot do without: it reads `LAPSED` when the agent holds no `RAMP_SAFETY` endorsement at their own station that is effective at the clock, `EXPIRING` when they hold one but it expires within 30 days, and `CURRENT` otherwise.

Assignment-time currency is anchored differently, and this distinction is deliberate. When an agent is committed to a turnaround, each required endorsement is tested for effectiveness **at the occupation's own `window_start`**, not at the current logical clock. Which endorsements are required depends on the ramp role the agent will perform. A `LEAD` needs `RAMP_SAFETY` and `LOADMASTER`. A `RAMP` needs `RAMP_SAFETY` alone. A `LOADER` needs `RAMP_SAFETY` and `BELT_LOADER`. A `PUSHBACK_OPERATOR` needs `RAMP_SAFETY` and `PUSHBACK`. A `DEICER` needs `RAMP_SAFETY` and `DEICING`. Every one of the required endorsements must be held by the agent at the turnaround's station and be effective at the occupation's start; if even one is missing, expired, not yet issued or revoked, the commitment is refused with `ERR_CERT_NOT_CURRENT` and HTTP 422.

Revocation is the sharp end of this design. A `SafetyOfficer` or a `StationManager` revokes an endorsement with a mandatory `reason`, moving it `VALID → REVOKED`; the endorsement must still be `VALID`, and revoking an already-revoked one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On revocation the endorsement's `revoked_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the supplied text is stored as `revoked_reason`, and `currency_state` becomes `REVOKED`. The cascade then runs four hops. The owning agent recomputes `certification_posture`. If the revoked endorsement was that agent's only effective `RAMP_SAFETY` at their own station, the agent's posture becomes `LAPSED` and the agent moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. Because the agent was suspended, every occupation of theirs sitting at `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `AGENT_SUSPENDED`, and `assigned_duty_minutes` is reduced to its new ledger value. Because those occupations were cancelled, any turnaround that thereby loses coverage demotes `RESOURCED → SCHEDULED`, and because it demoted, an owning bank at `APPROVED` on the staffing axis drops to `REVISION_REQUIRED`.

The reverse edge exists too: `ACTIVE ↔ SUSPENDED` is a genuine cycle driven entirely by certification currency, and a suspended agent returns to `ACTIVE` the moment a fresh, currently-effective `RAMP_SAFETY` is issued to them at their own station. Termination, by contrast, is terminal and comes only from outside: when the employing firm moves `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`, every one of its agents whose `status` is not already `TERMINATED` moves there — `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED` alike — an agent leaving `ACTIVE` drags its reserved and committed occupations to `CANCELLED` with the corresponding duty-minute decrements, and a terminated agent can never be assigned again. An attempt to commit an agent whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; an attempt to commit an agent whose employing firm is in any status other than `APPROVED` or `PROBATION` is refused with `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_APPROVED`.

### 3.6. Ground Support Equipment: Serviceability, Holds and Selection

A `StationManager` registers each piece of equipment against a `station_id` with an `asset_tag` unique among that station's units and a `gse_type` drawn from `PUSHBACK_TUG`, `BELT_LOADER`, `GPU`, `DEICER` and `STAIRS`. Three optional engineering fields complete the record: `condition_score`, an integer rating from 0 to 100 inclusive that defaults to 100; `service_interval_cycles`, the number of turn cycles the unit may run between scheduled services, defaulting to 25 and required to be at least 1; and `cycles_since_service`, the cycles already accumulated, defaulting to 0 and never negative. A new unit lands `AVAILABLE` with `committed_occupation_minutes` at zero, `last_service_at` null and both `service_hold_start` and `service_hold_end` cleared.

Maintenance is recorded through a service-event capability open to a `StationManager` or a `SafetyOfficer`, taking an `event_type` of either `HOLD_SCHEDULED` or `SERVICE_COMPLETED`, with the optional window fields `hold_start` and `hold_end`. Scheduling a hold with `HOLD_SCHEDULED` whose `hold_start` is at or before the current logical clock takes the unit `AVAILABLE → IN_MAINTENANCE` and records the supplied window into `service_hold_start` and `service_hold_end`; the unit stops being eligible for selection. Scheduling a hold whose `hold_start` is strictly after the current logical clock is the interesting case: the window is recorded exactly the same way, but the unit's `status` stays `AVAILABLE`, because the hold is merely booked and the unit can still work turnarounds before that window opens. Recording a completed service with `SERVICE_COMPLETED` returns the unit to `AVAILABLE` from either `IN_MAINTENANCE` or `QUARANTINED` — both `IN_MAINTENANCE → AVAILABLE` and `QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE` are admissible — resets `cycles_since_service` to 0, stamps `last_service_at` from the logical clock, and clears both hold boundaries. `AVAILABLE ↔ IN_MAINTENANCE` is therefore a cycle the fleet walks repeatedly.

The system also puts units into maintenance without being asked. As described in Section 3.4, a unit whose `cycles_since_service` reaches or passes `service_interval_cycles` when a turnaround completes moves `AVAILABLE → IN_MAINTENANCE` with a four-hour hold running from the current logical clock. Quarantine works differently and is driven by safety: reporting a `CRITICAL` incident that names a `gse_unit_id` takes that unit `AVAILABLE → QUARANTINED` or `IN_MAINTENANCE → QUARANTINED`, and because the unit was quarantined, every occupation of it sitting at `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `GSE_QUARANTINED` and `committed_occupation_minutes` reduced to its new ledger value. A quarantined unit is released only when the incident that quarantined it moves `FINDINGS_ISSUED → CLOSED`, at which point it moves `QUARANTINED → IN_MAINTENANCE` with a four-hour hold from the current logical clock — it goes to the workshop, not straight back to the apron. Closing the station moves every unit that is not already `RETIRED` to `RETIRED`, so `AVAILABLE → RETIRED` fires for the serviceable fleet and the same edge fires from `IN_MAINTENANCE` and `QUARANTINED`; `RETIRED` is terminal: a retired unit is permanently ineligible for assignment.

Two different interval tests protect the fleet, and they use deliberately different arithmetic. The **occupation test** treats every equipment occupation as running from its `window_start` up to but not including its `window_end`, and then extends it at the end by the station's `gse_reposition_buffer_minutes` of repositioning time. A proposed occupation conflicts with an existing occupation on the same unit — considering only rows whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`, since released and cancelled rows never conflict — when the proposed start is strictly earlier than the existing end plus the buffer **and** the existing start is strictly earlier than the proposed end plus the buffer. Both comparisons are strict, which means an occupation that begins exactly the buffer's worth of minutes after another one ends is admissible. A conflict is refused with `ERR_GSE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The **service-hold test** is a closed interval with no buffer at all: when the unit carries a non-null `service_hold_start`, a proposed occupation conflicts if its `window_start` is at or before `service_hold_end` **and** `service_hold_start` is at or before its `window_end`. Touching endpoints do conflict here. That collision is refused with `ERR_GSE_SERVICE_HOLD_CONFLICT`, and it is reachable on a unit that is still `AVAILABLE`, precisely because a hold booked for a future start does not change the unit's status.

When a request names no specific unit, the server picks one, and the selection rule is fully determined. The candidate pool is every unit based at the turnaround's station whose `gse_type` matches the requested type, whose `status` is exactly `AVAILABLE`, which reports no conflict under the buffered occupation test, and which reports no conflict under the closed service-hold test; units in `IN_MAINTENANCE`, `QUARANTINED` or `RETIRED` are never candidates. Candidates are then ranked by remaining service life — that is, by `service_interval_cycles` minus `cycles_since_service`, largest first — then by `condition_score`, largest first, and finally by `created_at`, earliest first. The first candidate under that ranking is the one selected. A unit's `id` plays no part in eligibility, in ranking, or in the tie-break; `created_at` is the only fallback. If the candidate pool comes back empty, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_GSE` and HTTP 409. When a request does name a specific unit, that unit's `status` must be exactly `AVAILABLE`; a unit that is `IN_MAINTENANCE`, `QUARANTINED` or `RETIRED` is refused with `ERR_GSE_NOT_SERVICEABLE`.

### 3.7. Committing Agents and Equipment to a Turnaround

An **occupation** is the record that binds one resource to one turnaround for one interval, and creating one is a `DutyOfficer` privilege. Every occupation names the `turn_id` it serves, a `resource_kind` of either `AGENT` or `GSE`, the interval bounds `window_start` and `window_end` where the end must be strictly after the start, and the `expected_turn_version` described in Section 2.8. Beyond that, the payload's shape depends entirely on the resource family. An agent occupation carries `agent_id` and `role_on_turn` — the ramp role, one of `LEAD`, `RAMP`, `LOADER`, `PUSHBACK_OPERATOR` or `DEICER` — and must carry neither `gse_unit_id` nor `gse_type`. An equipment occupation carries exactly one of `gse_unit_id`, naming a specific unit, or `gse_type`, asking the server to choose one by the selection rule in Section 3.6, and must carry neither `agent_id` nor `role_on_turn`. Supplying both or neither of the equipment fields, or mixing fields across families, is a shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The turnaround must be receptive. Its `status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `RESOURCED`; a turnaround that is `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED` is refused with `ERR_TURN_NOT_ASSIGNABLE`. The owning station must be `OPEN`, and a station that is `PLANNED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` refuses the commitment with `ERR_STATION_NOT_OPEN`. The agent or the unit being committed must belong to the same station as the turnaround, on pain of `ERR_STATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. And while the station's `oversight_posture` is `ENHANCED`, an agent whose `employment_type` is `SUBCONTRACTED` may not be committed at all, as described in Section 2.10; that refusal carries `ERR_STATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`.

An accepted occupation lands at `RESERVED`. Its `occupied_minutes` is the elapsed time from `window_start` to `window_end` in whole minutes, rounded down. That figure is immediately added to the relevant ledger — a unit's `committed_occupation_minutes` for an equipment occupation, an agent's `assigned_duty_minutes` for an agent occupation — and both ledgers are, by definition, the sum of `occupied_minutes` over that resource's occupations whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`; released and cancelled occupations contribute nothing. The turnaround then recomputes `assigned_agent_count` and `assigned_gse_count`, which count that turnaround's occupations by family among rows at `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`, and a fully covered `SCHEDULED` turnaround promotes to `RESOURCED`. The turnaround's `version` increments by 1 on every such write, which is what makes the stale-view check in Section 2.8 meaningful.

Agent occupations carry two constraints that equipment occupations do not, and both are checked before the occupation is written. The **duty-overlap test** treats an agent's committed interval as a bare closed interval with no buffer of any kind: a proposed occupation conflicts with an existing occupation for the same agent — again considering only rows at `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` — when the proposed `window_start` is at or before the existing `window_end` **and** the existing `window_start` is at or before the proposed `window_end`. Touching endpoints do conflict, and the collision is refused with `ERR_AGENT_DUTY_OVERLAP`. This is deliberately different arithmetic from the equipment test in Section 3.6, which is half-open and buffered. The **daily duty cap** then looks at the UTC calendar date of the proposed `window_start`, adds up `occupied_minutes` across all of that agent's occupations at `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` whose own `window_start` falls on that same UTC date, adds the proposed occupation's `occupied_minutes` to that sum, and refuses the request with `ERR_AGENT_DUTY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422 if the projected total is strictly greater than ten hours of duty in the day. A projection landing on exactly 600 minutes is admissible; only a projection above it is refused. Equipment carries no aggregate cap at all.

Occupations are unwound in two ways. A `DutyOfficer` may **release** one ahead of the turnaround, optionally supplying a `reason`; release is admissible from `RESERVED` or from `COMMITTED` — that is, `RESERVED → RELEASED` and `COMMITTED → RELEASED` are both edges — and moves the occupation to `RELEASED`, reducing the relevant unit's `committed_occupation_minutes` or agent's `assigned_duty_minutes` by that occupation's `occupied_minutes`. The owning turnaround then recomputes its coverage counters and may demote `RESOURCED → SCHEDULED`, which in turn drops an `APPROVED` bank staffing plan to `REVISION_REQUIRED`. Release also happens automatically: completing a turnaround moves every `COMMITTED` occupation on it to `RELEASED` with the same decrements, and that path additionally advances the equipment's `cycles_since_service` as described in Section 3.4. Alternatively, an occupation may be **cancelled** by the system, moving `RESERVED → CANCELLED` or `COMMITTED → CANCELLED` with the same ledger decrement and with the machine-readable `cancel_reason` recorded. The reasons are `GSE_QUARANTINED` when a critical incident quarantines the unit, `AGENT_SUSPENDED` when a revoked endorsement suspends the agent, `SUBCONTRACTOR_TERMINATED` when the employing firm is terminated, `STATION_SUSPENDED` when the station is suspended and the turnaround is disrupted, and `SEASON_ROLLOVER` when the seasonal rollover cancels the turnaround; a turnaround cancelled by its own lifecycle action likewise cancels its occupations. `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal for an occupation. The one hop that moves no ledger is `RESERVED → COMMITTED`, fired when the turnaround starts, because both of those statuses already count toward the ledgers.

### 3.8. Reporting and Investigating Incidents

A `DutyOfficer`, a `SafetyOfficer` or a `StationManager` may file an incident report. The report names the `station_id` where the event happened, an `incident_type` of `AIRCRAFT_DAMAGE`, `GSE_DAMAGE`, `PERSONAL_INJURY` or `NEAR_MISS`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, and the `occurred_at` instant, which must not be later than the current logical clock — a report of the future is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It may optionally name the `turn_id`, `gse_unit_id` and `agent_id` involved and carry a free-text `description`. Each of those three, when supplied, must belong to the incident's own station.

Filing lands the incident at `REPORTED` with `reopen_count` at 0 and `reported_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`. If an `agent_id` was supplied, the incident takes a snapshot of that agent's `subcontractor_id` into its own `subcontractor_id`; the snapshot is null when the agent is directly employed or when no agent was named, and it is a snapshot, so later changes to the agent's employment do not move it. The station's `open_critical_incident_count`, `open_major_incident_count` and `oversight_posture` are recomputed at once. A `CRITICAL` report that names a `gse_unit_id` additionally fires the four-hop equipment cascade: the unit is quarantined, its reserved and committed occupations are cancelled with ledger decrements, turnarounds that lose coverage demote `RESOURCED → SCHEDULED`, and their `APPROVED` bank staffing plans drop to `REVISION_REQUIRED`.

Investigation is exclusively a `SafetyOfficer` matter and runs through one capability taking an `action` of `INVESTIGATE`, `ISSUE_FINDINGS`, `CLOSE` or `REOPEN`, plus the optional fields `root_cause_code`, `liability_party`, `assessed_damage_cents` and `note`. Taking the file into investigation with `INVESTIGATE` moves it `REPORTED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `REOPENED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and stamps `investigator_user_id`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `reported_by_user_id`. Issuing findings with `ISSUE_FINDINGS` moves it `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → FINDINGS_ISSUED` and requires both a `root_cause_code` and a `liability_party`, the latter being one of `HANDLER`, `SUBCONTRACTOR`, `CARRIER` or `THIRD_PARTY`; when the `incident_type` is `AIRCRAFT_DAMAGE` or `GSE_DAMAGE`, an `assessed_damage_cents` valuation is required as well and may not be negative. All three values are recorded on the incident and `findings_issued_by_user_id` is stamped.

Closing the file with `CLOSE` moves it `FINDINGS_ISSUED → CLOSED`, stamps `closed_by_user_id`, and requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from `findings_issued_by_user_id`. Closure does four things beyond the status change. The station's two open-incident counters and its `oversight_posture` are recomputed, and because a `CLOSED` incident no longer counts as open, this is typically the moment a `RESTRICTED` station comes back to `ENHANCED` or `NORMAL`. If the incident named a `gse_unit_id` and that unit is currently `QUARANTINED`, the unit moves `QUARANTINED → IN_MAINTENANCE`. If the incident carries a non-null `subcontractor_id`, that firm recomputes both `compliance_score` and `audit_compliance_score`, and if the recomputed `audit_compliance_score` is strictly below 40.00 while the firm's `status` is `APPROVED`, the firm is automatically moved `APPROVED → PROBATION` without anyone requesting it.

Reopening with `REOPEN` moves the file `CLOSED → REOPENED` and is admissible only while `reopen_count` is strictly less than 2; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. Reopening adds 1 to `reopen_count`, recomputes the station's counters and posture — the incident counts as open again, so a reopened `CRITICAL` file pushes the station straight back to `RESTRICTED` — and recomputes both compliance figures on any linked firm. `CLOSED → REOPENED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` is a genuine cycle, and `CLOSED` becomes effectively terminal only once `reopen_count` has reached 2.

One asymmetry is worth stating explicitly, because it separates two things that feel similar. A `PERSONAL_INJURY` or `NEAR_MISS` incident is exactly as reportable as a damage incident, counts identically toward the station's open-incident counters and `oversight_posture`, and carries exactly the same severity weight in both subcontractor compliance figures. What it never does is contribute a damage penalty to an invoice, however severe it is; only `AIRCRAFT_DAMAGE` and `GSE_DAMAGE` do that.

### 3.9. Service-Level Billing: Accrual, Finalization and Audit

A `BillingController` opens a billing period for a station by naming the `station_id`, a `period_code` unique among that station's periods, and the `window_start` and `window_end` of the billed window. The period lands at `OPEN` with `line_count` at 0, `accrued_cents`, `gross_total_cents`, `otp_penalty_cents`, `damage_penalty_cents`, `rebate_cents` and `net_total_cents` all at 0, `rebate_tier` at `NONE`, `reject_count` at 0, and both `finalized_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null.

Charges are accrued one turnaround at a time. A `BillingController` creates a **billing line** naming the `billing_period_id`, the `turn_id` being charged, and the `expected_billing_period_version` required by Section 2.8. The period must be `OPEN`, and a line written against a period in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. The turnaround must be billable: one whose `status` is `CANCELLED` or `DISRUPTED` is refused with `ERR_TURN_NOT_BILLABLE` and HTTP 422. And a turnaround may carry at most one live charge anywhere in the system — if any line in any period already references that turnaround with `status` `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, the new line is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BILLING_LINE`. On success the line lands at `PROVISIONAL`, copies the turnaround's `turn_type` into its own `turn_type_snapshot` — every rate lookup thereafter reads the snapshot, never the live turnaround — sets the turnaround's `billed` flag to true, and increments the period's `version` by 1. The period then recomputes `line_count`, which counts its lines at `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, along with `gross_total_cents`, `accrued_cents` and `net_total_cents`. `rebate_tier` stays `NONE` for as long as any line in the period is still `PROVISIONAL`.

Voiding is the compensating action: a `BillingController` voids a line with a mandatory `reason`, which is stored as the line's `void_reason` and moves the line to `VOID`. Voiding, like accrual, is only admissible while the owning period is `OPEN`; a void attempted against a period in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. The period recomputes `line_count`, `gross_total_cents`, `accrued_cents`, `rebate_tier`, `rebate_cents` and `net_total_cents` without that line, and the turnaround's `billed` flag returns to false. Void lines are excluded from every subsequent computation, including the rebate basis.

**How a line is priced.** The charge starts from the base rate for the line's `turn_type_snapshot`, published on the line as `base_rate_cents`, and from that same service level's standard ground time. Which ground time enters the charge depends on the line's own `status`, and this is the pivot the whole billing model turns on. While the line is `PROVISIONAL`, `charged_ground_minutes` is the turnaround's `scheduled_ground_minutes`. Once the line is `FINAL`, `charged_ground_minutes` is the turnaround's `actual_ground_minutes` when that turnaround is `COMPLETED`, and falls back to `scheduled_ground_minutes` when the turnaround is `CANCELLED`, since a cancelled turnaround has no actuals.

From there, `excess_minutes` is `charged_ground_minutes` minus the standard ground time for the snapshot service level, floored at zero. The excess is charged at three marginal rates: 900 cents for each of the first 15 excess minutes, 1400 cents for each of the next 30 excess minutes — that is, minutes 16 through 45 — and 2100 cents for every excess minute beyond the 45th. The rates are marginal, not flat: a line with 50 excess minutes pays 15 minutes at 900, 30 minutes at 1400, and 5 minutes at 2100. The line also publishes a `ground_time_tier` describing where it landed: `TIER_1` when `excess_minutes` is 15 or fewer, `TIER_2` when it is more than 15 but no more than 45, and `TIER_3` when it is more than 45.

Two multipliers then apply to the sum of the base rate and the excess charge. The seasonal multiplier reflects winter-operations and de-icing cost across the whole station: it is 1.15 when the station's `season_code` is `WINTER` and 1.00 when it is `SUMMER`. The subcontract-dilution factor is 0.965 when at least one occupation on the charged turnaround has `resource_kind` `AGENT`, has `status` `RESERVED`, `COMMITTED` or `RELEASED`, and names an agent whose `employment_type` is `SUBCONTRACTED`; otherwise it is 1.00. Cancelled occupations are ignored by that test, so a subcontracted agent whose assignment was cancelled does not dilute the charge. The base rate plus the excess charge, multiplied by the seasonal multiplier and then by the subcontract factor, is rounded half up to a whole cent exactly once — after both multiplications, in exact decimal arithmetic. That single rounded figure is then clamped: it is raised to the base rate if it fell below it, and lowered to three times the base rate if it exceeded that. The result is the line's `gross_amount_cents`.

**How penalties attach.** While a line is `PROVISIONAL`, both `otp_penalty_cents` and `damage_penalty_cents` are exactly 0, and its `rebate_alloc_cents` is 0 as well. Once a line is `FINAL`, its `otp_penalty_cents` is 12 percent of its own `gross_amount_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent, but only when the charged turnaround's `otp_result` is `DELAYED` **and** its `delay_code` is one of the handler-attributable codes `GH_STAFFING`, `GH_EQUIPMENT` or `GH_LOADING`; in every other case it is 0. Its `damage_penalty_cents` is the sum, over every incident whose `turn_id` is that turnaround, whose `status` is `CLOSED`, whose `liability_party` is `HANDLER` and whose `incident_type` is `AIRCRAFT_DAMAGE` or `GSE_DAMAGE`, of the severity's damage figure — 25000 cents for `MINOR`, 250000 cents for `MAJOR`, 900000 cents for `CRITICAL` — multiplied by the station's `damage_penalty_multiplier` and rounded half up to a whole cent for each contributing incident before the incidents are added together. Incidents still `REPORTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `REOPENED` contribute nothing, and neither do incidents blamed on the subcontractor, the carrier or a third party.

The rounding order here is pinned and is not a matter of taste. The 12 percent on-time penalty is rounded per line, and the already-rounded per-line figures are then added into the period's `otp_penalty_cents`. Adding the gross amounts together first and taking 12 percent of that sum is forbidden and gives a different answer: two delayed lines of 39855 and 18239 cents produce 4783 plus 2189, that is 6972 cents, under the pinned order, where 12 percent of their combined 58094 cents rounds to 6971.

**How the period rolls up.** Whenever the period is finalized, whenever a rejected period reverts, and on every line creation and void, the period recomputes itself in eight steps that must run in exactly this sequence. Step one recomputes every non-void line's `gross_amount_cents` under that line's current variant, rounding each to whole cents once, at that point. Step two sets `gross_total_cents` to the sum of those already-rounded line grosses — round per line, then add; never add first and round after. Step three selects `rebate_tier` from that total: `TIER_B` when `gross_total_cents` is at least 150000, `TIER_A` when it is at least 60000 but below that, and `NONE` otherwise; because the rebate exists only in the final variant, the tier is `NONE` while any line in the period is still `PROVISIONAL`, so a period that has never been finalized reports `NONE` and a `rebate_cents` of 0 no matter how large it is. Step four sets `rebate_cents` to `gross_total_cents` multiplied by the tier's rebate rate — 0.00 for `NONE`, 0.03 for `TIER_A`, 0.06 for `TIER_B` — rounded half up to a whole cent.

Step five distributes that rebate across the non-void final lines by the **largest-remainder method**. Each line's exact share is `rebate_cents` multiplied by that line's `gross_amount_cents` and divided by `gross_total_cents`, held as an exact rational value and deliberately not rounded. Each line provisionally takes the whole-number part of its exact share, and the fractional part is kept as that line's remainder. The leftover is `rebate_cents` minus the sum of all the whole-number parts. Lines are then ranked by remainder, largest first; ties are broken by `gross_amount_cents`, largest first; any remaining tie is broken by `created_at`, earliest first. One extra cent is given to each of the first however-many lines the leftover covers, and each line's `rebate_alloc_cents` is its whole-number part plus that extra cent where it won one. Rounding each line's share independently is forbidden, because it can make the allocations add up to one cent more or less than `rebate_cents` and break the ledger identity.

Step six takes each line's `otp_penalty_cents` and `damage_penalty_cents` from the penalty rules above, and sets the period's `otp_penalty_cents` and `damage_penalty_cents` to the sums of those per-line figures. Step seven sets each line's `net_amount_cents` to its `gross_amount_cents`, less its `otp_penalty_cents`, less its `damage_penalty_cents`, plus its `rebate_alloc_cents`. Step eight sets the period's `accrued_cents` to the sum of `net_amount_cents` over its lines at `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, and its `net_total_cents` to `gross_total_cents` less `otp_penalty_cents`, less `damage_penalty_cents`, plus `rebate_cents`. Those two figures must always be equal, and it is precisely the largest-remainder allocation in step five that guarantees they are.

**Finalization and audit.** A `BillingController` finalizes the period with the `FINALIZE` action, moving it `OPEN → PENDING_APPROVAL`. Four preconditions guard the move: the period must hold at least one line; every turnaround referenced by a non-void line must have `status` `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`; the station must hold no incident whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CRITICAL_INCIDENT`; and the combined `otp_penalty_cents` plus `damage_penalty_cents` that finalization would produce must not exceed the station's `penalty_cap_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PENALTY_CAP_BREACHED` and HTTP 422. On success every non-void line moves `PROVISIONAL → FINAL` and is recomputed under the final variant of both the charge and the penalty rules, the eight-step rollup runs, and `finalized_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`.

A `ContractAuditor` then disposes of the finalized period through the same capability, supplying an `action` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `note`. Approving with `APPROVE` moves it `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_user_id`, and makes the period read-only; `APPROVED` is terminal. The auditor's `X-User-ID` must differ from `finalized_by_user_id`. Rejecting with `REJECT` moves it `PENDING_APPROVAL → OPEN` and is a full compensating recomputation rather than a mere status change: every `FINAL` line returns to `PROVISIONAL` and is recomputed under the provisional variant, so each line's `otp_penalty_cents`, `damage_penalty_cents` and `rebate_alloc_cents` all return to 0 and each line's `charged_ground_minutes` reverts to the turnaround's scheduled figure. The period's `gross_total_cents`, `otp_penalty_cents`, `damage_penalty_cents`, `rebate_tier`, `rebate_cents`, `accrued_cents` and `net_total_cents` are recomputed from the reverted lines, `finalized_by_user_id` is cleared back to null, and `reject_count` is increased by 1. `OPEN → PENDING_APPROVAL → OPEN` is the approval revision cycle, and the station may walk it as often as the auditor insists.
