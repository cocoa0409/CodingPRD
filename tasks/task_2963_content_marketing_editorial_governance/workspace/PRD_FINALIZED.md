# Content Marketing Editorial Governance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Brand content teams do not fail because they cannot write. They fail because a quarter's worth of editorial ambition, a finite bench of subject matter experts, four independent review dimensions, a legal department that must vouch for every performance claim, and a stock-and-talent asset library with hard licence expiry dates all move on separate clocks — and nobody can see all five at once. A managing editor commits nine pieces to a single week because the capacity ledger lived in a spreadsheet; a comparison claim ships with two-year-old internal research behind it; a talent photograph runs in a paid social burst eleven days after its licence lapsed. Each of these is individually small and collectively reputational.

This document specifies the Content Marketing Editorial Governance service, an HTTP API mounted under `/editorial` that makes the whole editorial supply chain a single governed ledger. Campaigns carry weekly capacity slots measured in effort points. Briefs are scored for effort from their search intent and target length, and that score is converted into reserved hours on a named expert's week. Content items consume slot capacity at a surcharge that rises with how full the week already is, fork into a set of review gates with per-dimension turnaround service levels, and may only publish once every gate has cleared, every claim on the piece is substantiated by evidence that is still valid, and every attached asset is still inside its licensed window on the channel being used.

Governance is not a report produced after the fact; it is a live posture on the campaign that closes doors. A campaign accumulating breached review deadlines becomes `RESTRICTED` and loses the right to waive gates. A campaign carrying an unsubstantiated claim on a published piece, or two lapsed asset rights, becomes `FROZEN` and loses the right to create, publish or refresh anything at all. An ordered nightly sweep re-derives licence status, rights suppression, claim expiry, scheduled activation, content decay, service-level breaches and posture — in that order, each step observing the one before it. The reader of this document is the engineer who must make all of that deterministic.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role they are acting in. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is a string outside the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before anything else in the request pipeline, in that order, so a malformed role string never produces a 403 and an unauthorised role never produces a 404 for a resource it was not permitted to look at.

The six roles are `ManagingEditor`, `SubjectMatterExpertUser`, `LegalReviewer`, `BrandReviewer`, `AccessibilityReviewer` and `AnalyticsService`. A `ManagingEditor` owns the editorial calendar, the brief pipeline, the content spine, publication, the asset library, the logical clock and the governance sweep; it is the only role that creates resources and the only role that may waive a review gate, and it is deliberately never the role that substantiates a claim or approves a subject-matter gate. A `SubjectMatterExpertUser` is the assigned expert acting on their own work: approving or sending back a brief, filing evidence, and deciding the `SME` review dimension. A `LegalReviewer` substantiates or rejects claims and decides the `LEGAL` review dimension. A `BrandReviewer` decides the `BRAND` dimension and an `AccessibilityReviewer` decides the `ACCESSIBILITY` dimension; the two are peers with no authority over each other's dimension. An `AnalyticsService` has exactly one write — the immutable measurement period — plus read access to the content spine and its measurement history.

Identity semantics differ between the roles, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `SubjectMatterExpertUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SubjectMatterExpert` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `SubjectMatterExpert` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, which in practice means a brief's `sme_expert_id` is compared directly against the caller's `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ManagingEditor`, `LegalReviewer`, `BrandReviewer`, `AccessibilityReviewer` or `AnalyticsService`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a transition writes it — as `created_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a content item, as `decided_by_user_id` on a review gate or a claim, as `submitted_by_user_id` on an evidence row — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Because those recorded identifiers are what the two segregation-of-duties rules compare, an opaque identity is still fully accountable.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request. All four pagination fields — `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` — appear in the `meta` object of every list response, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the supplied filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, oldest row first, with `id` ascending as a stable tie-breaker. Five collections override that default and one inverts it. The campaign collection is ordered by `created_at` descending, newest campaign first. Calendar slots — both the per-campaign collection and the cross-campaign collection — are ordered by `week_start_date` ascending and then by `funnel_stage` ascending; the expert collection is ordered by `full_name` ascending; the asset collection is ordered by `license_end` ascending; an asset's usages and a content item's usages are ordered by `attached_at` ascending; a claim's evidence rows are ordered by `collected_at` ascending; and an item's measurement periods are ordered by `period_start` ascending. Two collections use a fixed enumeration order rather than a timestamp: an item's review gates are returned in the order `SME`, `LEGAL`, `BRAND`, `ACCESSIBILITY`, and an item's distributions are returned in the order `OWNED`, `EMAIL`, `SOCIAL`.

Filters are additive and case-sensitive against the stored enumeration strings. The campaign collection filters on `status`, `governance_posture`, `persona` and `quarter_code`. The per-campaign slot collection filters on `funnel_stage` and `status`; the cross-campaign slot collection additionally filters on `campaign_id`. The expert collection filters on `status` and on `expertise_tag`, which matches when the supplied tag is a member of the expert's `expertise_tags`. The brief collection filters on `campaign_id`, `sme_expert_id`, `state` and `funnel_stage`. The content-item collection filters on `campaign_id`, `brief_id`, `slot_id` and `workflow_state`. An item's gates filter on `gate_type` and `state`; its claims on `claim_type` and `substantiation_state`; its distributions on `channel` and `state`; its usages on `usage_state` and `channel`; its measurement periods on `touch_position`. An asset's usages filter on `usage_state`, the asset collection filters on `asset_type` and `status`, and a claim's evidence rows filter on `source_type`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Auditing Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered with exactly three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format, rewritten on every mutation that changes the row — and `version`, a positive integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations the row receives as a cascade from some other request rather than as the direct target of the call. Two entities are exceptions and are deliberately immutable once written: an evidence row and a measurement period carry only `id` and `created_at`, and for both of them `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response entirely. Nothing in the system rewrites an evidence row or a measurement period; corrections are made by filing another one.

All temporal reasoning in this service — service-level deadlines, licence windows, evidence recency, decay windows, expiry sweeps, publication stamps — reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it must never fall back to the host's real time. An operator advances it by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the response echoes the new value as `{ "current_utc": ... }`. The clock never rewinds: a requested value strictly earlier than the clock's present value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422), while a value equal to the present value is accepted and is a no-op. Advancing the clock changes no entity row; it only changes what the next governance sweep, licence evaluation or decay recompute will conclude.

Whenever this document says an instant is "stamped", it means the logical clock's value at the moment the request is processed. That applies to `opened_at` and `decided_at` on a review gate, `decided_at` on a claim, `published_at` and `retracted_at` and `last_audit_at` on a content item, `attached_at` and `released_at` on an asset usage, and `went_live_at` and `suppressed_at` on a distribution row.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and the Ordered Validation Ladder

Path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that names a missing row terminates the request. A campaign is resolved before its calendar slots; a content item is resolved before its claims, its asset usages, its distributions, its review gates or its measurement periods; a claim is resolved before its evidence. The eight existence errors are `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BRIEF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTENT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. The same eight identifiers are reused when a foreign key carried in a request **body** fails to resolve, but body-key resolution happens later in the ladder than path resolution, as set out below.

Because many operations in this service can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is itself part of the contract. A request that breaks two rules must return the error belonging to the **lower-numbered** rule. The ladder is:

1. Authentication — both headers present and non-empty and `X-User-Role` naming one of the six roles; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Authorisation — the acting role appears in the endpoint's role list; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Path existence — every resource named by a path segment exists, left to right; otherwise the matching 404 from the eight identifiers above. Applies to every endpoint that has path parameters.
4. Request shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) on any write whose body is malformed: a required field missing, an enum value outside its declared set, a negative or out-of-range number, `target_word_count` outside 100 through 9000, `capacity_points` outside 1 through 200, `weekly_capacity_hours` outside 1 through 60, `max_licensed_uses` outside 1 through 50, `promotion_window_days` outside 1 through 7, a timestamp that is not a parseable ISO 8601 UTC instant, a `week_start_date` that is not a Monday, a `license_end` at or before `license_start`, a `valid_until` at or before `collected_at`, a `review_window_end` at or before `review_window_start`, a `review_window_start` and `review_window_end` that do not fall in the same ISO week, or a review window whose duration is not a whole number of hours at least as large as the brief's computed `sme_reserved_hours`.
5. Measurement period sanity — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/performance-snapshots`: `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, `period_end` at or before the logical clock, and the half-open interval from `period_start` to `period_end` not overlapping the interior of another measurement period on the same item; otherwise `ERR_SNAPSHOT_PERIOD_INVALID` (400).
6. Clock monotonicity — on `POST /editorial/_clock/set_current_utc`: the requested value is at or after the current clock; otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (422).
7. Uniqueness — `code` is unique across all campaigns (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_CODE_DUPLICATE`), the combination of `campaign_id`, `week_start_date` and `funnel_stage` is unique across calendar slots (`ERR_SLOT_DUPLICATE_WEEK`), and `url_slug` is unique across content items not in `PRUNED` (`ERR_URL_SLUG_DUPLICATE`) — all HTTP 409. These apply to `POST /editorial/campaigns`, `POST /editorial/campaigns/{id}/calendar-slots` and `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/publish` respectively.
8. One item per brief — on `POST /editorial/content-items`, no other content item may already exist on the same `brief_id` in any `workflow_state` other than `PRUNED`; otherwise `ERR_BRIEF_ALREADY_HAS_ITEM` (409).
9. Body foreign keys — every foreign key carried in the request body (`campaign_id`, `brief_id`, `slot_id`, `sme_expert_id`, `asset_id`) resolves to an existing row, producing the matching 404. Applies to `POST /editorial/content-items`, `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/assign-sme` and `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/asset-usages`.
10. Expert ownership — when the acting role is `SubjectMatterExpertUser`, `X-User-ID` equals the `sme_expert_id` of the brief being acted on, or of the brief behind the `SME` gate being decided; otherwise `ERR_SME_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_BRIEF` (403). Applies to `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/approve`, `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/request-changes` and `POST /editorial/review-gates/{id}/decide`.
11. Gate-role match — on `POST /editorial/review-gates/{id}/decide`, the acting role is the single reviewer role authorised for the gate's `gate_type`: `SME` for `SubjectMatterExpertUser`, `LEGAL` for `LegalReviewer`, `BRAND` for `BrandReviewer`, `ACCESSIBILITY` for `AccessibilityReviewer`. The one exception is a `ManagingEditor` submitting a waiver on a waivable gate. Otherwise `ERR_GATE_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403).
12. Legal segregation of duties — on `POST /editorial/review-gates/{id}/decide`, the `X-User-ID` deciding a `LEGAL` gate differs from the `decided_by_user_id` of every claim on the same content item; otherwise `ERR_SOD_LEGAL_CONFLICT` (409).
13. Publication segregation of duties — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/publish`, `X-User-ID` differs from the item's `created_by_user_id`; otherwise `ERR_SOD_PUBLISH_CONFLICT` (409).
14. Optimistic concurrency — `expected_campaign_version` equals the campaign's current `version` on `POST /editorial/content-items` (`ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION`), and `expected_expert_version` equals the expert's current `version` on `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/assign-sme` (`ERR_STALE_EXPERT_VERSION`) — both HTTP 412.
15. Declared transition — the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, yielding `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BRIEF_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_GATE_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID` as appropriate — all HTTP 409. Applies to every transition endpoint.
16. Slot not locked — on `POST /editorial/content-items`, the targeted calendar slot is not `LOCKED`; otherwise `ERR_SLOT_LOCKED` (409).
17. Campaign has no open items — on `POST /editorial/campaigns/{id}/close`, no content item of the campaign is in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `READY`; otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_HAS_OPEN_ITEMS` (409).
18. Refresh eligibility — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/refresh`, the item is in `NEEDS_REFRESH`, or in `PUBLISHED` with `refresh_required` true; otherwise `ERR_REFRESH_NOT_REQUIRED` (409).
19. Prune eligibility — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/prune`, the item's `last_audit_verdict` is `PRUNE`; otherwise `ERR_PRUNE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409).
20. Brief approval dependency — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/submit-for-review`, the item's brief is in `APPROVED`; otherwise `ERR_BRIEF_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
21. Slot and brief alignment — on `POST /editorial/content-items`, the slot's `campaign_id` equals the brief's `campaign_id` and the slot's `funnel_stage` equals the brief's `funnel_stage`; otherwise `ERR_SLOT_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH` (422).
22. Frozen posture — a campaign whose `governance_posture` is `FROZEN` refuses content-item creation, publication, refresh and gate waiver with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409). Applies to `POST /editorial/content-items`, `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/publish`, `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/refresh` and `POST /editorial/review-gates/{id}/decide`.
23. Restricted posture — a campaign whose `governance_posture` is `RESTRICTED` still permits publication but refuses gate waivers with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (409). Applies to `POST /editorial/review-gates/{id}/decide`.
24. Claim substantiation at publish — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/publish`, every claim on the item is in `SUBSTANTIATED` with `evidence_valid_until` strictly after the logical clock; otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` (422).
25. Regulated-claim scope — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/claims`, a claim whose `claim_type` is `REGULATED` may only be attached to an item whose brief has `requires_regulated_review` true; otherwise `ERR_REGULATED_REVIEW_REQUIRED` (422).
26. Waiver scope — on `POST /editorial/review-gates/{id}/decide`, only a `BRAND` or `ACCESSIBILITY` gate may be waived; a waiver aimed at an `SME` or `LEGAL` gate is `ERR_GATE_NOT_WAIVABLE` (422).
27. Accessibility metadata — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/publish`, `meta_description` is at least 50 characters long and `tags` carries at least 2 entries; otherwise `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_METADATA_MISSING` (422).
28. Expert availability — on `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/assign-sme`, the target expert's `status` is `AVAILABLE`; otherwise `ERR_EXPERT_UNAVAILABLE` (409).
29. Expertise match — on `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/assign-sme`, the brief's `required_expertise_tag` is a member of the expert's `expertise_tags`; otherwise `ERR_EXPERTISE_TAG_MISMATCH` (422).
30. Review-window exclusivity — on `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/assign-sme`, the brief's reserved window does not interior-overlap the window of any other brief already held by the same expert in `SME_ASSIGNED` or `APPROVED`; otherwise `ERR_EXPERT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
31. Asset usage window exclusivity — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/asset-usages`, for `STOCK` and `ORIGINAL` assets, this item's promotion window does not overlap the promotion window of another item already holding an `ACTIVE` usage of the same asset on the same channel; otherwise `ERR_ASSET_USAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
32. Talent exclusivity buffer — on the same endpoint, for an asset whose `asset_type` is `TALENT`, the two promotion windows are additionally separated by at least 7 whole days; otherwise `ERR_TALENT_EXCLUSIVITY_BUFFER` (409).
33. Usage rights validity — attaching an asset requires the asset's `license_end` strictly after the logical clock, and publication requires the same of every asset behind an `ACTIVE` usage of the item; otherwise `ERR_ASSET_RIGHTS_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/asset-usages` and `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/publish`.
34. Renewal extends — on `POST /editorial/assets/{id}/renew`, `new_license_end` is strictly after the logical clock and strictly after the asset's current `license_end`; a failure of either leg is `ERR_ASSET_RENEWAL_NOT_EXTENDING` (422).
35. Publish window — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/publish`, `scheduled_at` falls inside the slot week of the item's calendar slot; otherwise `ERR_PUBLISH_OUTSIDE_SLOT_WEEK` (422).
36. Evidence freshness at decision — on `POST /editorial/claims/{id}/decide`, at least one weight-contributing evidence row has `valid_until` strictly after the logical clock; otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_EXPIRED` (422).
37. Channel licensing — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/asset-usages`, the requested `channel` is a member of the asset's `licensed_channels`; otherwise `ERR_ASSET_CHANNEL_NOT_LICENSED` (422).
38. Evidence sufficiency — on `POST /editorial/claims/{id}/decide`, the claim's `evidence_score` is at least its `required_score` under the claim's `scoring_variant`; otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
39. Asset use budget — on `POST /editorial/content-items/{id}/asset-usages`, the asset's `remaining_licensed_uses` is at least 1 before the new usage is recorded; otherwise `ERR_ASSET_USES_EXHAUSTED` (409).
40. Expert weekly capacity — on `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/assign-sme`, the expert's already-committed hours for the brief's ISO week plus this brief's `sme_reserved_hours` do not exceed the expert's `weekly_capacity_hours`; otherwise `ERR_EXPERT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
41. Calendar-slot capacity — on `POST /editorial/content-items`, the slot's `committed_points` plus the new item's surcharged `commit_points` do not exceed the slot's `capacity_points`; otherwise `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are the ones implementations most often get backwards. Expert availability at position 28 is checked before weekly capacity at position 40, so assigning an over-capacity brief to an expert who is also `ON_LEAVE` returns `ERR_EXPERT_UNAVAILABLE`, not `ERR_EXPERT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Claim substantiation at position 24 is checked before asset rights validity at position 33, so publishing an item that both carries an unsubstantiated claim and holds a usage of a lapsed asset returns `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`, not `ERR_ASSET_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Capacity-Consuming Writes

Two writes in this service consume a shared, finite ledger that the caller cannot see atomically, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency rather than by a state precondition. The distinction matters: a state precondition says "this operation is not allowed right now", whereas these two operations are perfectly legitimate — the caller is simply working from a view of the ledger that has since moved.

A `POST /editorial/content-items` request must carry `expected_campaign_version` as a required body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the campaign the brief belongs to. If that value does not equal the campaign's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Creating an item consumes calendar-slot capacity and advances the campaign's `committed_points_total`, so the caller must prove it observed the campaign's current commitment ledger; another item commit, or a posture recompute, may already have advanced the campaign since the client read it. There is no state precondition that could substitute for this check, because a second create against the same campaign is a legitimate operation whenever capacity remains.

A `POST /editorial/briefs/{id}/assign-sme` request must likewise carry `expected_expert_version` as a required body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `SubjectMatterExpert` being assigned. A value that does not equal the expert's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EXPERT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the reserved hours the client believed were free may already have been changed by another assignment or by a leave transition. Assigning a second, different brief to the same expert is a legitimate operation whenever weekly capacity remains, so again only the version check can express the rule.

No other endpoint in the service accepts a version field. In particular, deciding a review gate, deciding a claim, filing evidence, publishing, retracting, refreshing, pruning, attaching an asset usage, renewing an asset and recording a measurement period all take no expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Order-Pinned Arithmetic

Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a tie rounds away from zero — and the target precision is stated with the rule. Percentages and scores that carry decimals (`utilization_pct`, `decay_pct`, `evidence_score`, `required_score`, `turnaround_hours`) are rounded to exactly 2 decimal places and serialised with 2 decimal places. Points, hours, counts and cent amounts (`effort_points`, `commit_points`, `sme_reserved_hours`, `committed_points`, `capacity_points`, `sla_hours`, `committed_hours_total`, `weekly_capacity_hours`, `engaged_sessions_total`, `conversions_total`, `last_touch_pipeline_cents`, `assisted_pipeline_cents`, `influenced_opportunity_amount_cents`) are integers; monetary quantities are held in whole cents and never in a fractional currency unit.

Three arithmetic rules have a pinned evaluation order, and in each case a plausible alternative order produces a different observable answer. First, when a service-level deadline is derived, the base turnaround hours and every applicable multiplier are multiplied together in full and the product is rounded to a whole number of hours exactly once; the multipliers are never applied and rounded one at a time. Second, when a content item's capacity commitment is computed, the capacity tier is selected from the slot's utilization *before* the new item's points are added, and the surcharge that tier produces is never permitted to re-select the tier. Third, when assisted pipeline attribution is computed, each measurement period's weighted share is rounded to whole cents individually and only then are the rounded shares summed; summing unrounded shares and rounding the total once is the wrong order and differs by whole cents on adversarial inputs. Sections 3.4, 3.5 and 3.9 state each of these in full.

### 2.8. The Two Campaign Axes and Governance Posture

A campaign carries two independent state axes. The first is `status`, driven only by an operator: it begins at `PLANNING`, moves to `ACTIVE`, and ends at `CLOSED`. The second is `governance_posture`, which is never written directly by any request and always derived: it takes the values `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN`, begins at `OPEN`, and is recomputed from three stored counters at the end of every write that changes a review-gate decision, a claim's substantiation state, an asset usage's state, or a content item's workflow state anywhere within the campaign — as well as by both campaign transitions and by the governance sweep.

The three counters are stored on the campaign and reconciled on every recompute, which means the posture walks back down as readily as it walks up: a cascade that clears the last offending row must also relax the posture. `breached_gate_count` is the number of review gates across the campaign's content items whose `sla_breached` flag is true. `expired_usage_count` is the number of asset usages across the campaign's content items whose `usage_state` is `RIGHTS_LAPSED`. `unsubstantiated_published_claim_count` is the number of claims across the campaign's content items where the owning item's `workflow_state` is `PUBLISHED` or `NEEDS_REFRESH` and the claim's `substantiation_state` is `DRAFT`, `EVIDENCE_PENDING`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED`. All three counters are computed over review gates, claims and asset usages reachable from the campaign through its content items, **excluding** items whose `workflow_state` is `PRUNED` — pruning a piece therefore stops its claims and usages from counting against the campaign.

The posture itself is a two-tier threshold evaluated with the stronger tier first. The posture is `FROZEN` when `unsubstantiated_published_claim_count` is at least 1 or `expired_usage_count` is at least 2. Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` when `breached_gate_count` is at least 2 or `expired_usage_count` is at least 1. Otherwise it is `OPEN`. Because the freeze test runs first, a campaign that satisfies both tiers reads `FROZEN`, never `RESTRICTED`. Notice also that a single lapsed usage is enough for `RESTRICTED` while two lapsed usages tip the same campaign into `FROZEN`, and that a single unsubstantiated claim on a published piece freezes the campaign outright.

A `FROZEN` posture refuses four operations — creating a content item, publishing, refreshing and waiving a gate — with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. A `RESTRICTED` posture refuses only gate waivers, with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`; a `RESTRICTED` campaign may still publish. Every other operation in the service is indifferent to the posture. The `status` axis gates a different set of things: briefs and content items may only be created against an `ACTIVE` campaign, and a refresh requires the campaign to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID` otherwise).

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this service touch more than one row. Where a cascade is described as a numbered sequence of steps, those steps run in the stated order within the same request, each step observing the results of the previous ones, and every row a step actually changes has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Rows a step visits but does not change — because they are already in the target state, or fall outside the step's filter — are not touched and their `version` does not move. This matters for every step described as a no-op below: it is a genuine no-op, not a silent re-write.

Cascades are also deliberately **partial and asymmetric**, and the asymmetries are specified rather than accidental. Closing a campaign archives its `DRAFT` and `SME_ASSIGNED` briefs but leaves its `APPROVED` briefs alone. An expert going on leave detaches their briefs but returning from leave never re-attaches them. Retracting a published piece releases its asset usages but does **not** return its calendar points. Renewing an expired asset restores the usages and distributions the expiry knocked down but does **not** move content items that had already been flagged for refresh back to `PUBLISHED`. When a rule in section 3 says a cascade stops short of something, that boundary is part of the specification.

### 2.10. Windows, ISO Weeks and Interval Arithmetic

Every interval in this system is **half-open**: it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. A slot week is the interval that begins at `week_start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z` and ends 7 days later at `00:00:00.000Z`; an instant exactly at the next Monday midnight is outside the week. A brief's reserved review window runs from `review_window_start` up to but excluding `review_window_end`. A content item's promotion window begins at `planned_publish_at` and runs for `promotion_window_days` days. A measurement period runs from `period_start` up to but excluding `period_end`. An asset's licence runs from `license_start` up to but excluding `license_end`.

Two half-open intervals **overlap** when each one's start is strictly before the other's end. Two intervals that merely touch — one ends at exactly the instant the other begins — do not overlap, and every overlap rule in this document permits touching endpoints unless it says otherwise. The only rule that goes further is the talent buffer, which demands positive separation rather than mere non-overlap.

An **ISO week** is anchored on Monday: the ISO week containing an instant is identified by the Monday `00:00:00.000Z` at or before it. Two instants are in the same ISO week when they resolve to the same Monday anchor. The expert weekly capacity ledger, the review-window same-week constraint, and the requirement that `week_start_date` be a Monday all rest on this single definition.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Planning a Campaign and Its Editorial Calendar

Planning begins with a `ManagingEditor` registering a campaign. The payload names a human-readable `code` of 1 to 32 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, which must be unique across all campaigns under a case-sensitive comparison — a second campaign reusing an existing `code` is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CODE_DUPLICATE`. The campaign also declares the `persona` it targets, one of `IT_BUYER`, `PRACTITIONER`, `EXECUTIVE` or `DEVELOPER`; a `primary_funnel_stage` of `AWARENESS`, `CONSIDERATION`, `DECISION` or `RETENTION`, which is carried for reporting only and never gates anything; and a `quarter_code` planning label matching a four-digit year beginning with 20 followed by the letter Q and a digit from 1 to 4, as in `2026Q3`. A newly registered campaign opens in `status` `PLANNING` with `governance_posture` `OPEN`, with `breached_gate_count`, `expired_usage_count`, `unsubstantiated_published_claim_count` and `committed_points_total` all at 0.

Activation moves the campaign `PLANNING → ACTIVE`, which is the state in which brief and content-item work is permitted; the transition is available only from `PLANNING`, recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture`, and increments the campaign's `version` by 1. Closure moves it `ACTIVE → CLOSED` and is available only from `ACTIVE`. Before the campaign may close, it must hold no content item in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `READY`; a single unfinished piece blocks the close with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_HAS_OPEN_ITEMS`. `CLOSED` is terminal — no transition leaves it, and attempting to activate a closed campaign is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`.

Closure then runs an ordered cascade. Step 1 sets the campaign's `status` to `CLOSED`. Step 2, caused by step 1, locks the calendar: every calendar slot of this campaign whose `status` is `OPEN` or `FULL` moves to `LOCKED`. Step 3, also caused by step 1, archives the unfinished planning work: every brief of this campaign whose `state` is `DRAFT` or `SME_ASSIGNED` moves to `ARCHIVED` with its `sme_expert_id` cleared to null — and briefs already in `APPROVED` are deliberately left untouched, because the cascade is conditional on the child's state. Step 4, caused by step 3, recomputes `committed_hours_total` on every expert who lost a brief in step 3. Step 5 recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture`. The campaign's `version` increments by 1.

A campaign's editorial capacity lives in weekly slots. Each slot is created against a campaign and takes its `campaign_id` from the path; it names a `week_start_date` as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date that must be a Monday, a `funnel_stage` from the same four-value set that the slot will accept, and a `capacity_points` budget between 1 and 200 effort points inclusive. The triple of owning campaign, `week_start_date` and `funnel_stage` is unique, so a campaign may hold one awareness slot and one decision slot in the same week but not two awareness slots; the duplicate is `ERR_SLOT_DUPLICATE_WEEK`. Creating a calendar slot is deliberately not gated on the owning campaign's `status`: the endpoint carries no campaign-state precondition, so a slot may be created against a campaign still in `PLANNING`, against an `ACTIVE` campaign, and against a campaign already in `CLOSED` alike, and none of those cases yields `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. A fresh slot opens with `committed_points` 0, `utilization_pct` 0.00 and `status` `OPEN`.

Three things move a slot's `status`. The system moves it `OPEN → FULL` at the end of a content-item creation once the slot's `committed_points`, after the new item's points have been added, have reached or passed `capacity_points`; the same recompute refreshes `utilization_pct` and increments the slot's `version` by 1. The system moves it back `FULL → OPEN` at the end of a prune once the released points bring `committed_points` back below `capacity_points`, again refreshing `utilization_pct` and incrementing `version`. And a `ManagingEditor` may lock a week directly, moving the slot `OPEN → LOCKED` or `FULL → LOCKED` so that no further pieces may be committed to it without closing the whole campaign; the campaign-close cascade produces the same two edges. Locking leaves `committed_points` and `utilization_pct` exactly as they were and increments the slot's `version` by 1. `LOCKED` is terminal for a slot: a second lock attempt on an already-locked slot is `ERR_SLOT_STATE_INVALID`, and a prune that releases points from a locked slot recomputes the numbers but leaves the slot `LOCKED`. `utilization_pct` itself is always the slot's `committed_points` multiplied by 100, divided by its `capacity_points`, and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. A campaign's `committed_points_total` is the sum of `committed_points` over every calendar slot belonging to that campaign.

### 3.2. The Expert Bench and the Weekly Reservation Ledger

A subject matter expert is registered by a `ManagingEditor` with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `expertise_tags` list of 1 to 8 topic tags — each 1 to 40 lowercase characters, either a single word or hyphen-separated words — and a `weekly_capacity_hours` figure between 1 and 60 giving the reviewable hours the expert can hold inside a single ISO week. Registration produces an expert with `status` `AVAILABLE` and `committed_hours_total` 0.

`committed_hours_total` is the expert's denormalised all-weeks ledger: it is the sum of `sme_reserved_hours` over every brief whose `sme_expert_id` is this expert and whose `state` is `SME_ASSIGNED` or `APPROVED`, across every week. Briefs in `DRAFT` and briefs in `ARCHIVED` are excluded, which is why a detachment reduces the figure and an archival does too. The per-week figure that actually gates assignment is a different number computed on demand: the sum of `sme_reserved_hours` over the briefs of one expert in `SME_ASSIGNED` or `APPROVED` whose `review_window_start` resolves to the same Monday anchor as the window being considered. Both figures are recomputed whenever a brief is assigned, approved or sent back, and whenever an expert's availability changes.

Availability is an axis with two values, and the operator sets it explicitly by supplying a `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `ON_LEAVE`. Moving `AVAILABLE → ON_LEAVE` is where the deepest cascade in the service lives, and it runs five levels deep, each hop caused by the hop before it rather than by the original request. Step 1 sets the expert's `status` to `ON_LEAVE`. Step 2, caused by step 1, releases the expert's workload: every brief whose `sme_expert_id` is this expert and whose `state` is `SME_ASSIGNED` or `APPROVED` moves to `DRAFT` with `sme_expert_id` cleared to null. Step 3, caused by step 2, recomputes the expert's `committed_hours_total` from the surviving briefs — which, since every counted brief has just been detached, settles at 0. Step 4, also caused by step 2, disturbs the content behind those briefs: every content item on one of the reverted briefs whose `workflow_state` is `IN_REVIEW` or `READY` moves to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Step 5, caused by step 4, voids the in-flight review: every review gate of those items whose `state` is `PENDING` moves to `VOIDED` with `decided_at` reset to null and `sla_breached` reset to false. Step 6, caused by step 5, recomputes `governance_posture` for every campaign that owned an item touched in step 4. The expert's `version` increments by 1.

The return leg is deliberately shallow. Moving `ON_LEAVE → AVAILABLE` sets the expert's `status` back to `AVAILABLE` and increments the expert's `version` by 1, and does nothing else: the briefs released on the way out are never re-attached, and the items pushed into `CHANGES_REQUESTED` are never pulled back. Each transition is available only from the opposite state, so setting `ON_LEAVE` on an expert already on leave is refused.

### 3.3. Brief Development, Effort Scoring and Assignment

A brief is the planning document for one piece of content, and it is created by a `ManagingEditor` against a campaign that is already `ACTIVE`; a brief aimed at a campaign still in `PLANNING`, or already `CLOSED`, is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. The payload carries the `campaign_id` of the campaign it plans against, the `funnel_stage` of the planned asset, the `search_intent` it targets — one of `INFORMATIONAL`, `COMMERCIAL`, `TRANSACTIONAL` or `NAVIGATIONAL` — a `primary_keyword` of 1 to 80 lowercase characters, a `target_word_count` between 100 and 9000, the `required_expertise_tag` of 1 to 40 lowercase characters that the eventual expert must be able to sign off on, and the two ends of the review window the brief reserves, `review_window_start` and `review_window_end`. Two fields are optional: `secondary_keywords`, a list of 0 to 10 further keyword targets each 1 to 80 lowercase characters and subject to no uniqueness rule at all, which defaults to an empty list; and `requires_regulated_review`, a boolean that defaults to false and marks the brief as carrying regulated statements. The `primary_keyword` is intended to be unique within its campaign across briefs whose `state` is not `ARCHIVED`; the validation ladder in section 2.5 defines no dedicated conflict code for it, so this expectation is a planning convention rather than an enforced rejection. A new brief lands in `state` `DRAFT` with `sme_expert_id` null.

Two figures are computed at creation and frozen there, and any client-supplied value for either is ignored. The first is `effort_points`. Start from a base determined by `search_intent`: 4 points for `INFORMATIONAL`, 6 for `COMMERCIAL`, 8 for `TRANSACTIONAL` and 3 for `NAVIGATIONAL`. Add 2 points for each 500-word block of `target_word_count`, where the number of blocks is `target_word_count` divided by 500 and rounded **up** to a whole number — so a 1,000-word brief buys two blocks and a 1,001-word brief buys three. Add a funnel uplift: nothing for `AWARENESS`, 2 points for `CONSIDERATION`, 4 points for `DECISION` and 1 point for `RETENTION`. Add a further 5 points when `requires_regulated_review` is true, and nothing when it is false. Finally clamp the sum into the closed range 6 through 40: any total below 6 becomes 6, any total above 40 becomes 40, and `effort_points` is the clamped integer.

The second figure is `sme_reserved_hours`, computed immediately after `effort_points` from `effort_points` alone: it is half of `effort_points`, rounded **up** to the next whole hour. Nine effort points therefore reserve five hours, and ten reserve five. This reserved figure is what the review window must accommodate: the duration from `review_window_start` to `review_window_end` must be a whole number of hours no smaller than `sme_reserved_hours`, `review_window_end` must be strictly after `review_window_start`, and both instants must resolve to the same Monday-anchored ISO week. Both are ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits. A window that fails any of these is a malformed request.

Assignment is where the expert's ledger is spent. A `ManagingEditor` names an `sme_expert_id` and supplies `expected_expert_version`, moving the brief `DRAFT → SME_ASSIGNED`. The brief must be in `DRAFT`; the owning campaign must be `ACTIVE` and must not be `FROZEN`; the expert must be `AVAILABLE`, or the call is refused with `ERR_EXPERT_UNAVAILABLE`; the brief's `required_expertise_tag` must be one of the expert's `expertise_tags`, or the call is refused with `ERR_EXPERTISE_TAG_MISMATCH`; the brief's reserved window must not overlap, in its interior, the window of any other brief already held by this expert in `SME_ASSIGNED` or `APPROVED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_EXPERT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` — two windows that merely touch, where one ends at exactly the instant the other begins, are permitted; and the expert's already-committed hours for the Monday-anchored week of this brief's `review_window_start`, plus this brief's `sme_reserved_hours`, must not exceed the expert's `weekly_capacity_hours`, or the call is refused with `ERR_EXPERT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the brief's `sme_expert_id` is set from the payload, the expert's `committed_hours_total` is recomputed, and both the expert's `version` and the brief's `version` increment by 1.

The assigned expert then decides. Approval moves the brief `SME_ASSIGNED → APPROVED`, which is the state a brief must reach before content behind it may enter review; the brief must be in `SME_ASSIGNED`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the brief's `sme_expert_id`, and the expert must still be `AVAILABLE`. The brief's `version` increments by 1. Requesting changes runs the opposite way and is available from both `SME_ASSIGNED` and `APPROVED`, closing the revision cycle `DRAFT → SME_ASSIGNED → APPROVED → DRAFT`; the caller may attach free-text `notes`, and must again be the assigned expert. Both edges — `SME_ASSIGNED → DRAFT` and `APPROVED → DRAFT` — run the identical five-step cascade. Step 1 sets the brief's `state` to `DRAFT` and clears `sme_expert_id` to null. Step 2, caused by step 1, recomputes `committed_hours_total` for the detached expert. Step 3, also caused by step 1, moves every content item on this brief whose `workflow_state` is `IN_REVIEW` or `READY` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Step 4, caused by step 3, moves every review gate of those items whose `state` is `PENDING` to `VOIDED`. Step 5, caused by step 4, recomputes the owning campaign's `governance_posture`. The brief's `version` increments by 1. Approving a brief that is not `SME_ASSIGNED`, or requesting changes on a brief that is in neither `SME_ASSIGNED` nor `APPROVED`, is `ERR_BRIEF_STATE_INVALID`.

Two further edges are system-driven, both produced by step 3 of the campaign-close cascade and both requiring that the owning campaign move to `CLOSED` in the same request. `DRAFT → ARCHIVED` sets the brief's `state` to `ARCHIVED`, clears `sme_expert_id` to null and increments the brief's `version` by 1. `SME_ASSIGNED → ARCHIVED` does the same and additionally recomputes the released expert's `committed_hours_total`, since an assigned brief was consuming that ledger and a draft one was not. `ARCHIVED` is terminal.

### 3.4. Committing a Content Item to the Calendar

A content item is the piece of content itself, and creating one is the moment editorial ambition becomes a booked commitment. A `ManagingEditor` supplies the `brief_id` of the brief the item realizes, the `slot_id` of the calendar week whose capacity it will consume, a working `title` of 1 to 160 characters, the `planned_publish_at` instant that opens its promotion window as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and the `expected_campaign_version` described in section 2.6. Optionally it may supply `promotion_window_days`, an integer from 1 to 7 giving the length in days of the promotion window that starts at `planned_publish_at`, which defaults to 3. The three publication-metadata fields `meta_description`, `url_slug` and `tags` may also be supplied this early, though they are only tested at publication time; when they are not supplied, `meta_description` and `url_slug` are null and `tags` is an empty list.

The gauntlet a create must pass reads at least four sibling aggregates. The referenced brief must be in `state` `APPROVED`, or the create is refused with `ERR_BRIEF_NOT_APPROVED`. The campaign behind that brief must be `ACTIVE` and its `governance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`. The named slot must belong to the same campaign as the brief and its `funnel_stage` must equal the brief's `funnel_stage`; either mismatch is `ERR_SLOT_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH`. The slot must be `OPEN` — a `LOCKED` slot is refused with `ERR_SLOT_LOCKED` and a slot already at `FULL` is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. No other content item may exist on the same `brief_id` in any `workflow_state` other than `PRUNED`, or the create is `ERR_BRIEF_ALREADY_HAS_ITEM`. And `planned_publish_at` must fall inside the slot week — the half-open interval from the slot's `week_start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z` to seven days later — or the create is `ERR_PUBLISH_OUTSIDE_SLOT_WEEK`. Only `planned_publish_at` itself is constrained this way; the promotion window it opens is free to extend past the end of the slot week.

The capacity commitment is then computed in a pinned seven-step order, and the order is observable through the response, so it matters. **Step 1** computes the slot's pre-commit utilization: the slot's current `committed_points` multiplied by 100, divided by its `capacity_points`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. **Step 2** selects a capacity tier from that pre-commit figure alone: the tier is `HIGH` when the pre-commit utilization is at least 80.00 percent, otherwise `MEDIUM` when it is at least 50.00 percent, otherwise `LOW`. **Step 3** reads the surcharge that tier carries: 0 points for `LOW`, 1 point for `MEDIUM`, 3 points for `HIGH` — a crowded week costs more to book into. **Step 4** sets the item's `commit_points` to the brief's `effort_points` plus that surcharge, and the item's `effort_points` to a straight copy of the brief's `effort_points`. **Step 5** projects the slot forward: the slot's current `committed_points` plus this item's `commit_points`. **Step 6** rejects the create with `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that projected total is greater than the slot's `capacity_points`. **Step 7** writes the outcome: the slot's `committed_points` becomes the projected total, its `utilization_pct` becomes the projected total multiplied by 100, divided by `capacity_points`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, its `status` becomes `FULL` when the projected total equals `capacity_points` and stays `OPEN` otherwise — it can never exceed capacity, because step 6 already rejected that case — and the item's `capacity_tier_at_commit` is frozen to the tier chosen in step 2, not to any re-derivation from the post-commit figure.

That last clause is the whole point of pinning the order. Recomputing the tier from the post-commit utilization changes the surcharge, which changes `commit_points`, which changes whether step 6 rejects at all. Both orders are arithmetically plausible; only the one above is correct. A slot sitting at exactly 80.00 percent selects `HIGH` and pays 3 extra points, where the same slot at 75.00 percent selects `MEDIUM` and pays 1 — and that three-point difference is frequently the difference between a create that fits and a create that is refused.

On success the item is created with `workflow_state` `DRAFT` and `revision_number` 1, with `created_by_user_id` set to the caller's `X-User-ID` — the value that will later forbid this same operator from publishing the piece — and with `campaign_id` set to a denormalised copy of the brief's `campaign_id` that is never rewritten afterwards, not even by a refresh. The slot's `committed_points`, `utilization_pct` and `status` are advanced as described, the campaign's `committed_points_total` advances by the item's `commit_points`, the campaign's `governance_posture` is recomputed, and the campaign's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.5. The Review Fork, Turnaround Service Levels and the Join Back

Submitting a piece for review forks it into a set of parallel review gates, one per review dimension. A `ManagingEditor` submits an item that is in `DRAFT`, advancing it `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`; the item's brief must be in `APPROVED` at the moment of submission, or the submission is refused with `ERR_BRIEF_NOT_APPROVED`, and the campaign's `governance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or it is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. The fan-out is conditional: gate rows are opened in the fixed order `SME`, `LEGAL`, `BRAND`, `ACCESSIBILITY`, but the `LEGAL` gate is omitted entirely when the item carries no claims. Three gates without claims, four with at least one claim. Every gate row records the `content_item_id` of the item under review, its `gate_type`, a `state` that begins at `PENDING`, and an `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock. The item's `version` increments by 1 and the campaign's `governance_posture` is recomputed.

Each gate's turnaround service level is computed once per opening. Start from the base turnaround for the dimension: 24 hours for `SME`, 48 hours for `LEGAL`, 24 hours for `BRAND` and 36 hours for `ACCESSIBILITY`. Then assemble a single multiplier, beginning at 1.0: multiply it by 1.25 when the item's brief has `funnel_stage` `DECISION`, and multiply it by 1.15 when the campaign's `persona` is `EXECUTIVE`. Multiply the base by that assembled multiplier and round the product half up to a whole number of hours — **exactly once**, over the full product. A brand gate on a decision-stage piece for an executive persona is 24 multiplied by 1.25 multiplied by 1.15, which is 34.5, which rounds to 35 hours; it is never 24 rounded to 30 and then to 34.5 and then to 35 by successive rounding. Only for a `LEGAL` gate is anything added afterwards: count the claims on the item whose `claim_type` is `REGULATED` and whose `substantiation_state` is anything other than `SUBSTANTIATED`, and add 12 hours for each one. The result is stored as the gate's `sla_hours`, and the gate's `due_at` is its `opened_at` advanced by that many hours.

A gate is decided by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `WAIVED`, optionally with a `decision_notes` free-text note of up to 500 characters that is recorded with the decision. Any decision requires the gate to be in `PENDING` — a gate already `APPROVED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_GATE_ALREADY_DECIDED` — and requires the owning item to be in `IN_REVIEW`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. The acting role must be the single reviewer role for the gate's `gate_type`, as set out in section 2.5, or the decision is refused with `ERR_GATE_ROLE_MISMATCH`. Deciding an `SME` gate additionally requires that the caller's `X-User-ID` equal the `sme_expert_id` of the item's brief, or the decision is refused with `ERR_SME_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_BRIEF`. Deciding a `LEGAL` gate additionally requires that the caller's `X-User-ID` differ from the `decided_by_user_id` of **every** claim on the item — the operator who vouched for a claim may not also clear the legal gate on the piece that carries it — or the decision is refused with `ERR_SOD_LEGAL_CONFLICT`.

Whichever way the decision goes, the gate records `decided_at` from the logical clock, `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and the supplied `decision_notes`; it computes `turnaround_hours` as the elapsed seconds from `opened_at` to `decided_at` divided by 3600 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and it sets `sla_breached` to true exactly when `decided_at` is strictly later than `due_at`. The gate's `version` increments by 1 and the campaign's `governance_posture` is recomputed, since a decided gate no longer sits pending against its deadline.

Approval moves the gate `PENDING → APPROVED`. When that approval makes every gate of the item either `APPROVED` or `WAIVED`, the fork joins and the item advances `IN_REVIEW → READY`, recomputing the campaign's `governance_posture` and incrementing the item's `version` by 1; the join is evaluated over exactly the three or four gates that were opened at fan-out. A request for changes moves the gate `PENDING → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and drives the item `IN_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` — and sibling gates keep whatever state they had reached, because a request for changes on one dimension does not void the work of the others. A waiver moves the gate `PENDING → WAIVED` and is the only decision a `ManagingEditor` may make: it requires the gate's `gate_type` to be `BRAND` or `ACCESSIBILITY`, since `SME` and `LEGAL` gates must be decided by their own reviewer (`ERR_GATE_NOT_WAIVABLE` otherwise), and it requires the campaign's `governance_posture` to be exactly `OPEN` — a `RESTRICTED` campaign refuses the waiver with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` and a `FROZEN` one with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. A waiver that completes the set also triggers the same join to `READY`.

Gates are also voided by cascades rather than decided. A gate moves `PENDING → VOIDED` with `decided_at` reset to null and `sla_breached` reset to false when an expert goes on leave, when the brief behind the item is sent back to draft, or when the item is refreshed; each such gate's `version` increments by 1. The refresh cascade goes further than the other two: it voids every gate of the item regardless of state, so `APPROVED → VOIDED` and `WAIVED → VOIDED` are declared edges that occur only when the owning item returns to `DRAFT` through a refresh. Outside a refresh, `APPROVED` and `WAIVED` are stable.

Resubmission closes the revision cycle. A `ManagingEditor` submits an item that is in `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, moving it `CHANGES_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW` under the same two preconditions as a first submission — the brief must be `APPROVED` and the campaign must not be `FROZEN`. Re-review is scoped to the gates that actually objected: every gate whose `state` is `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `VOIDED` reopens to `PENDING` — the two declared reopen edges are `CHANGES_REQUESTED → PENDING` and `VOIDED → PENDING`, and they behave identically — with a fresh `opened_at` from the clock, `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` cleared to null, `turnaround_hours` cleared to null, `sla_breached` reset to false, and `sla_hours` and `due_at` recomputed against the item's state at resubmit time — which means a claim added since the first pass can lengthen the legal deadline. Gates already in `APPROVED` or `WAIVED` are left completely untouched. A `LEGAL` gate is opened now, as a new row, when the item carries at least one claim and no `LEGAL` gate exists yet. The item's `revision_number` is **not** advanced by a resubmission — it counts refreshes only, as described in section 3.10 — and the campaign's `governance_posture` is recomputed. One further edge exists into `CHANGES_REQUESTED`: an item that has already reached `READY` is pushed `READY → CHANGES_REQUESTED` when the brief behind it leaves `APPROVED` or `SME_ASSIGNED` in the same request, whether through a request for changes on the brief or through an expert going on leave, and that push voids the item's `PENDING` gates and recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture` as well.

### 3.6. Claim Substantiation and the Evidence Dossier

Any statement in a piece that asserts a measurable fact needs a paper trail. A `ManagingEditor` attaches a claim to a content item, taking the `content_item_id` from the path and supplying the `claim_text` of the statement — 10 to 400 characters — together with a `claim_type` of `PERFORMANCE`, `COMPARISON` or `REGULATED`. A `REGULATED` claim may only be attached to a piece whose brief has `requires_regulated_review` true, or the attachment is refused with `ERR_REGULATED_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. The claim is created in `substantiation_state` `DRAFT` with `evidence_score` 0.00, `evidence_valid_until` null, `decided_by_user_id` null and `decided_at` null. Two derived fields are fixed at creation from `claim_type`: `scoring_variant` becomes `STRICT_REGULATED` when the `claim_type` is `REGULATED` and `STANDARD` for the other two, and `required_score` becomes 2.00 for `PERFORMANCE`, 3.00 for `COMPARISON` and 4.00 for `REGULATED`. Attaching a claim to a piece that is already `PUBLISHED` or `NEEDS_REFRESH` recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture` — and since a fresh claim is by definition not yet substantiated, that recompute will freeze the campaign.

Evidence is filed against a claim by either a `ManagingEditor` or a `SubjectMatterExpertUser`, taking the `claim_id` from the path. Each evidence row names a `source_type` of `INTERNAL_STUDY`, `THIRD_PARTY_TEST`, `CUSTOMER_DATA` or `REGULATORY_FILING`; a human-readable `citation` of 5 to 300 characters for audit purposes; a `collected_at` instant, which must be at or before the logical clock; and a `valid_until` instant strictly after `collected_at`, after which the evidence no longer supports the claim. Both are ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits. The row's `weight` is frozen at ingestion from its `source_type` — 1.00 for `INTERNAL_STUDY`, 2.00 for `THIRD_PARTY_TEST`, 0.50 for `CUSTOMER_DATA` and 2.50 for `REGULATORY_FILING` — and its `submitted_by_user_id` records the filer's `X-User-ID`. Evidence rows are immutable: they carry `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Filing evidence is accepted from four claim states and refused from exactly one. A claim in `DRAFT`, `EVIDENCE_PENDING`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` accepts the row and lands in `EVIDENCE_PENDING`; only a claim already in `SUBSTANTIATED` refuses new evidence, with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. The owning content item must not be `PRUNED`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. Four edges therefore exist into `EVIDENCE_PENDING` and each does the same core work: the evidence row is appended, the claim's `substantiation_state` becomes `EVIDENCE_PENDING` with `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` reset to null, the claim's `evidence_score` is recomputed under its `scoring_variant` over every evidence row of the claim, and the claim's `version` increments by 1. `DRAFT → EVIDENCE_PENDING` and `REJECTED → EVIDENCE_PENDING` do exactly that. `EVIDENCE_PENDING → EVIDENCE_PENDING` is a self-loop that lets a dossier be built up one row at a time, and does exactly that too. `EXPIRED → EVIDENCE_PENDING` does that and two more things: the claim's `evidence_valid_until` is cleared to null until the claim is substantiated again, and the campaign's `governance_posture` is recomputed, because an `EXPIRED` claim sitting on a published piece had been freezing the campaign and re-entering evidence review does not by itself unfreeze it.

Scoring an evidence dossier is a weighted sum with a recency discount, and the two variants diverge sharply. For each evidence row, first compute its age in whole days: the elapsed seconds from `collected_at` to the logical clock, divided by 86400, rounded **down**. Then determine the row's contribution. A row whose `source_type` is not admissible under the claim's `scoring_variant` contributes exactly 0. An admissible row contributes its `weight` multiplied by a recency factor. Under the `STANDARD` variant all four source types are admissible and the recency factor is 1.0 when the row's age is at most 730 days and 0.5 beyond that — stale evidence is discounted, never discarded. Under the `STRICT_REGULATED` variant only `THIRD_PARTY_TEST` and `REGULATORY_FILING` are admissible, so `INTERNAL_STUDY` and `CUSTOMER_DATA` rows contribute 0 no matter how fresh they are, and the recency factor is 1.0 when the row's age is at most 365 days and 0.0 beyond that — stale evidence is discarded outright. The claim's `evidence_score` is the sum of every row's contribution, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, with the rounding applied once to the summed contributions. Identical evidence therefore scores strictly lower under the strict variant, and because regulated claims also carry the highest `required_score`, the two effects compound. Note that no state filter applies to the sum: a row whose `valid_until` has already passed still contributes its weight to the score.

A `LegalReviewer` then decides the claim by supplying a `decision` of `SUBSTANTIATED` or `REJECTED`. Either decision requires the claim to be in `EVIDENCE_PENDING`, or it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Substantiation additionally requires that the claim's `evidence_score` be at least its `required_score` — a shortfall is `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` — and that at least one weight-contributing evidence row have a `valid_until` strictly after the logical clock, which is where the score's indifference to expiry is corrected: a dossier that is arithmetically sufficient but entirely out of date is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_EXPIRED`. On `EVIDENCE_PENDING → SUBSTANTIATED` the claim records `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the clock, sets `evidence_valid_until` to the earliest `valid_until` among the evidence rows that contributed a non-zero weight at that moment, recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture`, and increments the claim's `version` by 1. On `EVIDENCE_PENDING → REJECTED` the claim records the same two decision fields, recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture`, and increments its `version` by 1 — and remains open to further evidence, since a rejection is not terminal.

The one remaining edge is system-driven. During the governance sweep, a claim in `SUBSTANTIATED` whose `evidence_valid_until` is at or before the logical clock moves `SUBSTANTIATED → EXPIRED` in an ordered cascade. Step 1 sets `substantiation_state` to `EXPIRED`. Step 2, caused by step 1, suppresses the piece's reach: when the owning item's `workflow_state` is `PUBLISHED` or `NEEDS_REFRESH`, every distribution row of that item whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` moves to `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `CLAIM_SUBSTANTIATION_EXPIRED`. Step 3, also caused by step 1, recomputes the campaign's `unsubstantiated_published_claim_count` and its `governance_posture`, which drives the campaign to `FROZEN`. The claim's `version` increments by 1. Because `EXPIRED` accepts new evidence, the recovery path is to file a fresh row and take the claim through legal review again.

### 3.7. The Asset Library, Usage Rights and Exclusivity

The asset library holds every licensed visual or audio element the content team may use, and it is the other half of the many-to-many graph: one piece of content may use many assets, and one asset may be used by many pieces on many channels. A `ManagingEditor` registers an asset with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `asset_type` of `STOCK`, `TALENT` or `ORIGINAL`, a licence window given as `license_start` and a strictly later `license_end`, a `licensed_channels` list of 1 to 3 distinct entries drawn from `OWNED`, `EMAIL` and `SOCIAL`, and a `max_licensed_uses` integer between 1 and 50 giving how many concurrent active usages the licence permits. Registration derives the asset's rights posture immediately: `active_use_count` is 0, `remaining_licensed_uses` equals `max_licensed_uses`, and `status` is evaluated against the logical clock.

That rights posture has three values and a fixed evaluation order. The `status` is `EXPIRED` when the asset's `license_end` is at or before the logical clock — the test is inclusive, so an asset whose `license_end` falls exactly on the clock is already expired. Otherwise the `status` is `EXPIRING` when the remaining time from the clock to `license_end` is at most 30 days. Otherwise it is `LICENSED`. Alongside it, `active_use_count` is the number of usage rows on this asset whose `usage_state` is `ACTIVE`, and `remaining_licensed_uses` is `max_licensed_uses` less `active_use_count`, floored at 0 so it never reads negative. Usages in `RIGHTS_LAPSED` and `RELEASED` do not consume a licensed use. All three branches are re-derived at registration, at each new attachment, whenever a retraction, refresh or prune releases usages, and at the start of the governance sweep.

Attaching an asset to a piece creates a usage row that records the `content_item_id` from the path, the `asset_id` of the asset, and the `channel` the usage is claimed for. Five checks guard the attachment, in the ladder order of section 2.5. The requested `channel` must appear in the asset's `licensed_channels`, or the attachment is refused with `ERR_ASSET_CHANNEL_NOT_LICENSED`. The asset's `license_end` must be strictly after the logical clock, or the attachment is refused with `ERR_ASSET_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`. For a `STOCK` or `ORIGINAL` asset, this item's promotion window — the half-open interval that begins at the item's `planned_publish_at` and runs for `promotion_window_days` days — must not overlap the promotion window of any other item that already holds an `ACTIVE` usage of the same asset on the same channel, or the attachment is refused with `ERR_ASSET_USAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; two windows that merely touch are accepted. For a `TALENT` asset the bar is higher: the two promotion windows must be separated by at least 7 whole days, so the zero-gap touching case a stock photograph would accept is refused here with `ERR_TALENT_EXCLUSIVITY_BUFFER`. And the asset's `remaining_licensed_uses` must be at least 1 before the new usage is recorded, or the attachment is refused with `ERR_ASSET_USES_EXHAUSTED`. On success the usage row is created in `usage_state` `ACTIVE` with `attached_at` stamped from the clock and `released_at` null, and the asset's `active_use_count` and `remaining_licensed_uses` are recomputed.

A usage row has three states and never returns to `ACTIVE` except through renewal. It goes to `RIGHTS_LAPSED` when the asset behind it expires during a sweep. It goes to `RELEASED`, with `released_at` stamped from the clock, when the owning piece is retracted, refreshed or pruned. `RELEASED` is where a usage row rests.

Expiry is a four-level cascade run by the sweep, and it reaches all the way to the campaign's posture. An asset in `LICENSED` moves `LICENSED → EXPIRING` when its `license_end` is still after the clock but the gap is at most 30 days, incrementing the asset's `version` by 1 and doing nothing else. An asset in `LICENSED` or `EXPIRING` whose `license_end` is at or before the clock moves to `EXPIRED` — the edges `EXPIRING → EXPIRED` and `LICENSED → EXPIRED` are both declared, the second covering the case where no sweep ever observed the warning window — and both run the identical cascade. Step 1 sets the asset's `status` to `EXPIRED`. Step 2, caused by step 1, flips every usage row on this asset whose `usage_state` is `ACTIVE` to `RIGHTS_LAPSED` and recomputes the asset's `active_use_count` and `remaining_licensed_uses`. Step 3, caused by step 2, silences the channels that lost their rights: for each lapsed usage, every distribution row of the same content item on the same `channel` whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` moves to `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `ASSET_RIGHTS_LAPSED`. Step 4, caused by step 3, flags the content: a `PUBLISHED` item whose every distribution row is now `SUPPRESSED` moves to `NEEDS_REFRESH`. Step 5, caused by steps 2 through 4, recomputes the campaign's `expired_usage_count` and `governance_posture` — one lapsed usage restricts the campaign, two freeze it. The asset's `version` increments by 1.

Renewal reverses most of that, but not all of it. A `ManagingEditor` supplies a `new_license_end` that must be strictly after both the logical clock and the asset's current `license_end`; a failure of either leg reports `ERR_ASSET_RENEWAL_NOT_EXTENDING`. Renewal is declared only from `EXPIRING` and from `EXPIRED`, so renewing an asset whose `status` already reads `LICENSED` is refused with `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`. Both `EXPIRED → LICENSED` and `EXPIRING → LICENSED` run the identical restoration. Step 1 sets the asset's `license_end` to `new_license_end` and stamps its `status` to `LICENSED` unconditionally. Step 2, caused by step 1, revives the usages: every usage row on this asset whose `usage_state` is `RIGHTS_LAPSED` returns to `ACTIVE` with `released_at` cleared to null, and `active_use_count` and `remaining_licensed_uses` are recomputed. Step 3, caused by step 2, restores the reach: every distribution row suppressed with `suppression_reason` `ASSET_RIGHTS_LAPSED` on the same item and channel returns to `LIVE` when its `scheduled_at` is at or before the clock and to `SCHEDULED` otherwise, with `suppression_reason` and `suppressed_at` cleared to null. Step 4 recomputes the campaign's `expired_usage_count` and `governance_posture`. The asset's `version` increments by 1. Steps 2 and 3 are genuine no-ops when nothing had lapsed, as on the renewal of a merely `EXPIRING` asset.

Two boundaries of that restoration are deliberate. Content items already moved to `NEEDS_REFRESH` by step 4 of the expiry cascade are **not** moved back to `PUBLISHED` — the compensation is intentionally partial, because an editor should look at a piece that lost its imagery before it goes back out. And because step 1 stamps `LICENSED` unconditionally rather than re-deriving the three branches, an asset renewed to an end date that falls inside the 30-day warning window reads `LICENSED` until the next sweep re-derives `EXPIRING` for it; a second renewal attempted in that interval is therefore an undeclared transition and is refused with `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.8. Publication, Metadata and Channel Distribution

Publication is the narrowest gate in the service, and it is the one a `ManagingEditor` cannot walk through alone. The payload carries a `scheduled_at` instant for the distribution fan-out, a `channels` list naming the channels the piece will go out on, and the three publication-metadata fields: a `meta_description` of up to 200 characters, a `url_slug` of 1 to 80 lowercase kebab-case characters, and a `tags` list of taxonomy tags each 1 to 40 lowercase characters.

The checks run in a fixed order, and because several of them can fail together on a realistic piece, the order is the contract. The `url_slug` must not already be held by another content item that is not `PRUNED`, or the publication is refused with `ERR_URL_SLUG_DUPLICATE`. The caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the item's `created_by_user_id` — the editor who created a piece may not be the one who ships it — or the publication is refused with `ERR_SOD_PUBLISH_CONFLICT`. The item must be in `READY`, advancing `READY → PUBLISHED`; anything else is `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`. The campaign's `governance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the publication is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`; a `RESTRICTED` campaign may still publish. Every claim on the item must be in `substantiation_state` `SUBSTANTIATED` with `evidence_valid_until` strictly after the logical clock, or the publication is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED`. The accessibility floor must be met: `meta_description` at least 50 characters long and `tags` carrying at least 2 entries, with `url_slug` present, or the publication is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBILITY_METADATA_MISSING`. Every asset behind an `ACTIVE` usage of this item must still have `license_end` strictly after the clock, or the publication is refused with `ERR_ASSET_RIGHTS_EXPIRED`. The supplied `scheduled_at` must fall inside the slot week of the item's calendar slot — the half-open interval from the slot's `week_start_date` at `00:00:00.000Z` to seven days later — or the publication is refused with `ERR_PUBLISH_OUTSIDE_SLOT_WEEK`. Finally, for every requested channel, every asset behind an `ACTIVE` usage of the item must license that channel in its `licensed_channels`, or the publication is refused with `ERR_ASSET_CHANNEL_NOT_LICENSED`.

On success the item's `workflow_state` becomes `PUBLISHED`, `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `published_by_user_id` records the publisher's `X-User-ID`, and `meta_description`, `url_slug` and `tags` are stored on the item. The fan-out then creates one distribution row per requested channel, in the fixed order `OWNED`, `EMAIL`, `SOCIAL` restricted to the channels actually requested, each recording the item's `content_item_id`, its own `channel`, and a `scheduled_at` copied from the request. A row is created directly in `state` `LIVE` with `went_live_at` stamped from the clock when `scheduled_at` is at or before the clock, and directly in `state` `SCHEDULED` with `went_live_at` null when `scheduled_at` is later than the clock. The campaign's `governance_posture` is recomputed and the item's `version` increments by 1.

A distribution row's later life is entirely system-driven; no endpoint addresses one directly. A row still `SCHEDULED` whose `scheduled_at` has been reached moves `SCHEDULED → LIVE` during the sweep, stamping `went_live_at` from the clock and incrementing the row's `version` by 1. A row in `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` moves to `SUPPRESSED` when the rights behind it fail — either because an asset expired or because a claim's substantiation lapsed — stamping `suppressed_at` from the clock and setting `suppression_reason` to `ASSET_RIGHTS_LAPSED` or `CLAIM_SUBSTANTIATION_EXPIRED` respectively, and incrementing the row's `version` by 1; both `LIVE → SUPPRESSED` and `SCHEDULED → SUPPRESSED` are declared and behave identically. A row suppressed specifically for lapsed asset rights is restored by that asset's renewal, moving `SUPPRESSED → LIVE` when its `scheduled_at` is at or before the clock and `SUPPRESSED → SCHEDULED` when it is later, clearing `suppression_reason` and `suppressed_at` to null and, on the live branch, stamping `went_live_at` from the clock. Rows suppressed for claim expiry are not restored by any renewal. And any row in `SCHEDULED`, `LIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` moves to `RETRACTED` when the owning piece is retracted, refreshed or pruned — the three declared edges `SCHEDULED → RETRACTED`, `LIVE → RETRACTED` and `SUPPRESSED → RETRACTED` are identical in effect, each incrementing the row's `version` by 1. `RETRACTED` is terminal for a distribution row.

### 3.9. Measurement, Attribution and Decay

Measurement arrives from the `AnalyticsService` as immutable periods. Each one records the `content_item_id` from the path, a `period_start` and a strictly later `period_end` bounding the half-open interval measured, an `engaged_sessions` count, a `conversions` count, an `influenced_opportunity_amount_cents` figure giving the pipeline amount influenced during the period in whole cents, and a `touch_position` of `FIRST`, `MIDDLE` or `LAST` describing where the piece sat in the influenced journey during that period. All three numeric fields must be at or above 0. The `period_end` may not report the future — it must be at or before the logical clock — and the interval must not overlap the interior of another period already recorded on the same item; either failure is `ERR_SNAPSHOT_PERIOD_INVALID`. Periods are recorded regardless of the item's `workflow_state`, they carry only `id` and `created_at` with no `updated_at` and no `version`, and nothing in the service ever rewrites one.

Every recorded period triggers a recompute of four denormalised figures on the item, folded from scratch over every period of that item. `engaged_sessions_total` is the sum of `engaged_sessions` across all of them and `conversions_total` the sum of `conversions`. The two pipeline figures are the service's dual-variant attribution, maintained side by side and deliberately disagreeing. The last-touch variant, surfaced as `last_touch_pipeline_cents`, credits the full `influenced_opportunity_amount_cents` of every period whose `touch_position` is `LAST` and credits nothing at all to `FIRST` and `MIDDLE` periods; it is a plain sum over the qualifying subset. The assisted-fractional variant, surfaced as `assisted_pipeline_cents`, credits every period a fraction of its amount according to its `touch_position` — 0.40 for `FIRST`, 0.20 for `MIDDLE` and 0.40 for `LAST` — and the rounding order here is pinned: each period's weighted share is rounded half up to whole cents **individually**, and only then are the rounded shares summed. Summing the unrounded shares and rounding the total once is the plausible-but-wrong order, and it differs by whole cents on adversarial inputs. For the same set of periods the two variants disagree both in total and in which periods contribute anything at all. The item's `version` increments by 1 on each recorded period.

Decay measures whether a published piece is still earning its place. The window length is 28 days. The baseline window is the half-open interval that begins at the item's `published_at` and runs 28 days; the trailing window is the half-open interval that ends at the logical clock and begins 28 days before it. A measurement period counts toward a window only when it falls **entirely** inside it — its `period_start` at or after the window's start and its `period_end` at or before the window's end — so a period straddling a window edge is excluded from that window rather than partially credited. Sum `engaged_sessions` over the periods inside the baseline window and over the periods inside the trailing window. When the baseline sum is 0, `decay_pct` is 0.00 and no division is attempted. Otherwise `decay_pct` is one minus the trailing sum divided by the baseline sum, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and floored at 0.00 — a piece that grew reads 0.00 rather than a negative figure. The item's `refresh_required` flag is then true when `decay_pct` has reached the threshold for the funnel stage of the item's brief — 55.00 percent for `AWARENESS`, 50.00 percent for `CONSIDERATION`, 45.00 percent for `DECISION` and 60.00 percent for `RETENTION` — or when the item's `accuracy_status` is `INACCURATE`, and false otherwise.

Auditing is how an editor records a judgement about a live piece. The call names an `audit_type` of `DECAY` or `ACCURACY` and, for an accuracy audit, an `accuracy_finding`. It stamps `last_audit_type` with the requested type and `last_audit_at` from the logical clock. A decay audit runs the decay computation above, storing the resulting `decay_pct` and `refresh_required`, and then sets `last_audit_verdict` to `PRUNE` when `decay_pct` has reached 85.00 percent, otherwise to `REFRESH` when `refresh_required` is true, otherwise to `KEEP`. An accuracy audit instead writes the item's `accuracy_status` from the supplied `accuracy_finding` — one of `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFIED` or `INACCURATE` — and sets `last_audit_verdict` to `REFRESH` when the finding is `INACCURATE` and to `KEEP` otherwise. Either way, a `PUBLISHED` item whose verdict came out `REFRESH` or `PRUNE` is moved to `NEEDS_REFRESH`, and the item's `version` increments by 1. A brand-new item begins with `accuracy_status` `UNVERIFIED`, `decay_pct` 0.00, `refresh_required` false, and `last_audit_type`, `last_audit_verdict` and `last_audit_at` all null, alongside `engaged_sessions_total`, `conversions_total`, `last_touch_pipeline_cents` and `assisted_pipeline_cents` at 0 and `published_at`, `published_by_user_id` and `retracted_at` all null.

### 3.10. Retraction, Refresh and Pruning

Three compensating actions exist for a piece that has already gone out, and they differ precisely in what they give back.

Retraction pulls a piece off the channels. It is available from both `PUBLISHED` and `NEEDS_REFRESH`, and a `ManagingEditor` may attach a free-text `reason`. Both edges — `PUBLISHED → RETRACTED` and `NEEDS_REFRESH → RETRACTED` — run the identical five-step cascade. Step 1 sets the item's `workflow_state` to `RETRACTED` and stamps `retracted_at` from the logical clock. Step 2, caused by step 1, moves every distribution row of the item whose `state` is `SCHEDULED`, `LIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` to `RETRACTED`. Step 3, also caused by step 1, moves every asset usage of the item whose `usage_state` is `ACTIVE` or `RIGHTS_LAPSED` to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped from the clock. Step 4, caused by step 3, recomputes `active_use_count` and `remaining_licensed_uses` on every asset touched in step 3. Step 5 recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture`. The item's `version` increments by 1. What retraction pointedly does **not** do is return the calendar commitment: the slot's `committed_points` are untouched, because the piece still occupies its week.

Refresh sends a piece back to the top of the pipeline for a new revision. It is available from `PUBLISHED` and from `NEEDS_REFRESH`, but eligibility is narrower than availability: the item must either be in `NEEDS_REFRESH` already, or be `PUBLISHED` with `refresh_required` true, or the refresh is refused with `ERR_REFRESH_NOT_REQUIRED`. The owning campaign must additionally be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID` — and its `governance_posture` must not be `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Both `NEEDS_REFRESH → DRAFT` and `PUBLISHED → DRAFT` run the identical five-step cascade. Step 1 sets the item's `workflow_state` to `DRAFT`, increments `revision_number` by 1 — this is the only operation in the service that advances that counter — resets `published_at`, `published_by_user_id` and `retracted_at` to null, resets `accuracy_status` to `UNVERIFIED`, resets `last_audit_verdict` to null, and resets `refresh_required` to false. Step 2, caused by step 1, moves every review gate of the item whose `state` is anything other than `VOIDED` to `VOIDED`, which is what makes the second pass a full re-review rather than a scoped one. Step 3, also caused by step 1, moves every distribution row of the item whose `state` is `SCHEDULED`, `LIVE` or `SUPPRESSED` to `RETRACTED`. Step 4, also caused by step 1, moves every asset usage of the item whose `usage_state` is `ACTIVE` or `RIGHTS_LAPSED` to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped from the clock, and recomputes `active_use_count` and `remaining_licensed_uses` on each touched asset. Step 5 recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture`. The item's `version` increments by 1. Like retraction, refresh keeps the calendar commitment: `commit_points` are not returned, and the slot's numbers do not move.

Pruning is the one action that gives the capacity back, and it is the compensating action for the creation commit. It is available from `PUBLISHED`, `NEEDS_REFRESH` and `RETRACTED`, and it additionally requires that the item's `last_audit_verdict` be `PRUNE` — a piece may only be pruned after an audit that recommended it, and anything else is `ERR_PRUNE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. All three edges — `NEEDS_REFRESH → PRUNED`, `PUBLISHED → PRUNED` and `RETRACTED → PRUNED` — run the identical five-step cascade. Step 1 sets the item's `workflow_state` to `PRUNED`. Step 2, caused by step 1, returns the capacity: the slot's `committed_points` are reduced by this item's `commit_points`, its `utilization_pct` is recomputed, and its `status` moves `FULL → OPEN` when the slot is no longer at capacity — a slot that is `LOCKED` stays `LOCKED`. Step 3, caused by step 2, recomputes the campaign's `committed_points_total`. Step 4, caused by step 1, moves every distribution row of the item that is not already `RETRACTED` to `RETRACTED` and every asset usage whose `usage_state` is `ACTIVE` or `RIGHTS_LAPSED` to `RELEASED`, recomputing `active_use_count` and `remaining_licensed_uses` on each touched asset. Step 5 recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture`, and because the counters ignore pruned items, a prune can be the event that unfreezes a campaign. The item's `version` increments by 1. When the item was already `RETRACTED`, steps 3 and 4 find nothing left to change and are genuine no-ops for rows already `RETRACTED` or `RELEASED`. `PRUNED` is terminal: no transition leaves it, and pruning a `DRAFT` item is `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`.

One further edge into `NEEDS_REFRESH` is system-driven and belongs to the sweep. A `PUBLISHED` item moves `PUBLISHED → NEEDS_REFRESH` when either every distribution row of the item has been suppressed, or the item's `refresh_required` reads true after the sweep's decay recompute; the transition recomputes the campaign's `governance_posture` and increments the item's `version` by 1.

### 3.11. The Ordered Governance Sweep

Nothing in this service expires on its own. Licence lapse, claim expiry, scheduled activation, decay and service-level breach are all discovered by an operator-triggered sweep, evaluated against the logical clock described in section 2.4. The sweep is a `ManagingEditor` capability that takes no input, and it runs eight steps in a fixed order where each step observes the results of the steps before it. Within a step, rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order. The step order is observable in the outcome — a claim that expires in step 4 suppresses distribution rows that step 5 then declines to activate, and the decay recompute in step 6 sees items that step 3 has already flagged — so the order is part of the contract, not an implementation detail.

**Step 1 — asset licences.** Every asset is re-evaluated against the clock: `LICENSED` becomes `EXPIRING` inside the 30-day warning window, and `LICENSED` or `EXPIRING` becomes `EXPIRED` once `license_end` is at or before the clock. Each newly expired asset flips its `ACTIVE` usages to `RIGHTS_LAPSED`. **Step 2 — rights suppression.** Every distribution row on the same content item and the same channel as a usage lapsed in step 1, whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE`, becomes `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `ASSET_RIGHTS_LAPSED`. **Step 3 — flagging from rights loss.** A `PUBLISHED` item whose every distribution row is now `SUPPRESSED` or `RETRACTED` becomes `NEEDS_REFRESH`. **Step 4 — claim expiry.** Claims in `SUBSTANTIATED` whose `evidence_valid_until` is at or before the clock become `EXPIRED`, and the distribution rows of their `PUBLISHED` or `NEEDS_REFRESH` items become `SUPPRESSED` with `suppression_reason` `CLAIM_SUBSTANTIATION_EXPIRED`. **Step 5 — scheduled activation.** Distribution rows still `SCHEDULED` whose `scheduled_at` is at or before the clock become `LIVE` with `went_live_at` stamped from the clock. **Step 6 — decay.** The decay computation of section 3.9 is re-run for every `PUBLISHED` item, and those whose `refresh_required` comes out true become `NEEDS_REFRESH`. **Step 7 — service-level breach.** Review gates still in `PENDING` whose `due_at` is at or before the clock and whose `sla_breached` is still false have `sla_breached` set to true. **Step 8 — posture.** The campaign posture of section 2.8 is recomputed for every campaign touched by steps 1 through 7, with the freeze tier evaluated before the restrict tier so the stronger posture always wins.

The sweep returns a tally rather than an entity, and three of its nine counters have counting rules that make a replayed sweep informative rather than merely repetitive. `assets_expired` counts the assets that reached `EXPIRED`, `usages_lapsed` the usages that reached `RIGHTS_LAPSED`, `distributions_activated` the rows that reached `LIVE` in step 5, and `claims_expired` the claims that reached `EXPIRED` in step 4. `distributions_suppressed` is the total over steps 2 and 4 with each row counted once, even if both causes would have applied to it. `items_flagged_refresh` is likewise the total over steps 3 and 6 with each item counted once. `gates_breached` counts only the gates newly flagged in this run, so re-running the sweep without advancing the clock reports 0. And `campaigns_restricted` and `campaigns_frozen` count only campaigns whose posture actually **changed** to `RESTRICTED` or to `FROZEN` during this run, so a replay at an unmoved clock reports 0 for both.
