# School Capacity Portable Allocation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

District facilities planning offices are asked, every spring, to answer one deceptively simple question: does a campus have enough classroom seats for the students it is projected to enrol next year, and if not, is moving a portable classroom onto the site the cheapest defensible response? Today that question is answered in spreadsheets. Room inventories drift out of date, class-size loading factors are applied inconsistently between the state's loading standard and the district's own board policy, the portable inventory is tracked on a whiteboard in the maintenance yard, and the cost of relocating a unit is compared against a boundary change or a permanent addition only anecdotally. The result is a district that quietly accumulates decades-long reliance on "temporary" buildings without ever recording that it has done so.

This document specifies the School Capacity Portable Allocation API, the system of record for that entire chain of reasoning. It governs the building program capacity calculation, class-size policy and special-program set-asides, utilization measurement against grade-level enrolment projections, the portable classroom inventory with its condition history, placement-site feasibility covering pad geometry, fire access, utilities and the accessible path of travel, multi-discipline permitting, installation scheduling inside the summer construction window, relocation cost modelling against the two capital alternatives, and finally decommissioning, disposal and long-term reliance reporting. All resources are mounted under the `/facilities` prefix.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the planner who authors a capacity study may never certify it, the coordinator who files a permit may never decide it, and a crew lead may only touch the installation windows booked to that lead's own crew. The second is **fiscal conservation**: every cent of a board appropriation is, at every instant, in exactly one of three buckets — uncommitted, encumbered, or expended — and no cascade anywhere in the system is permitted to break that partition.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; a header that is present but empty or whitespace-only counts as absent, and surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both header values before they are used. If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not listed as authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

The eight roles are `DistrictAdmin`, `FacilitiesPlanner`, `CapacityCertifier`, `PermitCoordinator`, `BuildingOfficial`, `OperationsScheduler`, `AssetManager`, and `CrewLead`.

- A `DistrictAdmin` is the district facilities director. This role owns configuration — campuses, class-size policies, rooms, program set-asides, candidate pads, appropriations and crews — and is the only role permitted to sign an off-summer work waiver or to move the server's logical clock.
- A `FacilitiesPlanner` authors capacity studies and placement requests, assesses candidate pads, and withdraws requests. This role may never certify or return a study.
- A `CapacityCertifier` is the independent reviewer who certifies or returns submitted studies, and does nothing else that mutates state.
- A `PermitCoordinator` files permit applications and refiles them after corrections, and may never decide a permit.
- A `BuildingOfficial` is the building-department reviewer who approves a permit, requests corrections on it, or denies it, and may never file or refile one.
- An `OperationsScheduler` books installation windows against crews and pads, and is the only role that may cancel a booked window.
- An `AssetManager` owns the portable inventory: condition inspections, legacy placement records, decommissioning and disposal.
- A `CrewLead` runs an installation crew and may start or complete only the windows booked to that crew.

Identity semantics differ between the two kinds of role. When `X-User-Role` is any of `DistrictAdmin`, `FacilitiesPlanner`, `CapacityCertifier`, `PermitCoordinator`, `BuildingOfficial`, `OperationsScheduler`, or `AssetManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says so — it is stamped into `authored_by_user_id` when a `FacilitiesPlanner` opens a capacity study and into `certified_by_user_id` when a `CapacityCertifier` certifies one — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CrewLead`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `InstallationCrew`; ownership checks on an installation schedule compare the schedule's `installation_crew_id` against `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CREW_NOT_ASSIGNED`.

Two identity-derived rules deserve emphasis here because they are enforced independently of the role list. Certifying or returning a capacity study is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` is equal to the study's `authored_by_user_id` — a certifier who happens to share an operator identity with the author is blocked even though the role check passed. And supplying `off_summer_waiver` set to true when booking an installation window is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_ROLE_REQUIRED` unless the acting role is `DistrictAdmin`, even though `OperationsScheduler` is otherwise authorized to book windows.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned on the page is still less than `total_count`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three transport-level failures outside the catalogue of section 5 use the same envelope: a request body that is not a JSON object raises `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with 422, a path matching no declared route raises `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with 404, and a method not declared on an existing path raises `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with 405.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted the server uses 10, the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins; when it is omitted the server uses 0, and the smallest accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer inside the range from 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer of at least 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`. Both parameters are read as plain decimal integer literals: surrounding whitespace is tolerated, but any other spelling — a decimal point, a leading plus sign, a leading zero, or any non-numeric text — is rejected with the same error.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and rows sharing one `created_at` instant — which the logical clock makes common — keep their insertion order so the page is stable. Every optional list filter is a query parameter named exactly after the row field it matches, its value is compared against that field as a string (`true` or `false` for a boolean field), a parameter naming a value no row carries simply yields an empty page, and any query parameter an endpoint does not declare is ignored. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before the page window is applied.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Most resources also carry an `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical-clock instant on every mutation of the row, and a `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only change a derived field.

Three record types are append-only ledgers rather than living rows, and they carry only `id` and `created_at`: `ClassSizePolicy`, `EnrollmentProjection`, and `DisposalRecord` all have no `updated_at` and no `version` at all. A published class-size policy is never edited; a projection row is never edited, only displaced by a newer row for the same campus, year and grade; and a disposal record is the final entry in a unit's history.

All date-derived behaviour in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `DistrictAdmin` may move it by supplying `to_iso8601`, a string of 1 to 64 characters holding an ISO 8601 instant with either a trailing `Z` or an explicit offset — an instant carrying no zone is read as UTC, and one that cannot be parsed at all is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the response `data` reports the new value as `current_utc` and carries nothing else. Permit validity windows, the summer-window test, salvage age, certification stamps, and the `submitted_on`, `approved_on`, `certified_on` and `disposed_on` instants are all derived from this clock. Every instant the server renders from this clock — `created_at` and `updated_at`, `submitted_on`, `approved_on`, `certified_on`, `disposed_on`, the `current_utc` the clock endpoint reports and the `api_request_timestamp` of every response envelope — is written in one and the same form: an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.

### 2.5. Flat Addressing and Identifier Resolution Order

The domain is deliberately flat: every collection sits directly beneath `/facilities`, and every relationship between resources is expressed as a foreign-key field on the row plus a filtered list query, never as a nested URL. A room's campus, for example, is reached through the room's `school_id`, and the rooms of a campus are reached by filtering the room list on `school_id`.

Identifier resolution nevertheless happens in a fixed order, and the order is observable. Identifiers appearing in the request path are resolved first, left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error: `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SET_ASIDE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DISPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying 404. Only after every path identifier resolves does the server resolve foreign keys carried in the request body, and those are resolved in the order in which the fields are declared on the owning entity — so a placement-request creation resolves `capacity_study_id` before `placement_site_id`, `placement_site_id` before `portable_unit_id`, and `portable_unit_id` before `capital_budget_id`. Body-level failures raise `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. A body foreign key that is present but is not a string cannot resolve and raises the same 404 as an unknown identifier; one that is absent or null is passed over here and left to the rule-6 body-shape check.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

Many endpoints carry several checks that a single malformed request could trip at once. The evaluation order below is normative: the server reports the failure of the earliest applicable rule and stops. Where a rule names its scope, that scope is exhaustive.

1. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` is one of the eight declared roles — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401, on every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized role list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403, on every endpoint.
3. When the acting role is `CrewLead`, `X-User-ID` is equal to the target schedule's `installation_crew_id` — `ERR_CREW_NOT_ASSIGNED`, 403; because the comparison needs the row, this check necessarily runs after the schedule identifier in the path has resolved, so an unknown schedule id still reports `ERR_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND` first. It applies on exactly two endpoints: `POST /facilities/installation-schedules/{id}/start` and `POST /facilities/installation-schedules/{id}/complete`.
4. Every identifier in the request path resolves, left to right — the 404 family, on every path-parameterised endpoint.
5. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, in entity-declaration order — the 404 family, on every endpoint that carries body foreign keys.
6. Required body fields are present, types match, enum members are legal, numeric ranges hold, dates parse in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and every free-text note field (`correction_note`, `correction_response`, and the withdrawal, cancellation and decommission `reason`) holds 1 to 2000 characters whenever it is supplied — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422, on every endpoint.
7. `limit` is an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, 422, on every list endpoint.
8. `expected_school_version` is equal to the campus's `version` at the moment of the write — `ERR_STALE_SCHOOL_VERSION`, 412, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies`.
9. `expected_study_version` is equal to the study's `version` at the moment of the write — `ERR_STALE_STUDY_VERSION`, 412, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests`.
10. The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — `ERR_STUDY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PERMIT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SCHEDULE_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`, 409, on every action endpoint.
11. The capacity study referenced by the request is `CERTIFIED` — `ERR_STUDY_NOT_CERTIFIED`, 409, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/assess-site`.
12. The placement request named by the booking is `PERMITTED` and no sibling schedule of that request is `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` — `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_PERMITTED`, 409, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
13. The portable unit is `IN_STORAGE`, its `condition_status` is `UNSAFE` or its `condition_grade` is `D`, and no disposal record already references it — `ERR_UNIT_NOT_DISPOSABLE`, 409, on `POST /facilities/disposal-records`.
14. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the study's `authored_by_user_id` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/certify` and `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/return`.
15. The referenced portable unit's `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE` — `ERR_UNIT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`, 422, on exactly four endpoints: `POST /facilities/placement-requests`, `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/auto-assign-unit`, `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/assess-site`, and `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/submit-permits`.
16. The referenced portable unit's `condition_grade` is not `D` — `ERR_UNIT_GRADE_NOT_PLACEABLE`, 422, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests`.
17. The pad's `distance_to_fire_lane_ft` is at most 150 and the campus's `fire_lane_clear_width_ft` is at least 20 — `ERR_FIRE_ACCESS_NONCOMPLIANT`, 422, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/assess-site`. This rule is deliberately evaluated ahead of rule 18 so that a pad already recorded as infeasible for fire access reports the specific fire-access failure rather than the generic feasibility failure.
18. The referenced pad's `feasibility_status` is either `FEASIBLE` or `FEASIBLE_WITH_MITIGATION`, and its `assessed_for_size_class` is equal to the assigned unit's `size_class` — `ERR_SITE_NOT_FEASIBLE`, 422, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/assess-site`.
19. The referenced unit's `ada_ramp_installed` is true and the referenced pad's `ada_path_of_travel_compliant` is true — `ERR_ADA_NONCOMPLIANT`, 422, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules/{id}/complete`.
20. The pad's `school_id` is equal to the `school_id` of the study named by `capacity_study_id` — `ERR_SITE_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`, 422, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests`.
21. The pinned policy's `grade_band` is the primary band of the campus's `grade_configuration` — `ERR_POLICY_BAND_MISMATCH`, 422, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies` and `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/submit`.
22. The pinned policy's `effective_school_year` is equal to the study's `school_year` — `ERR_POLICY_YEAR_MISMATCH`, 422, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies` and `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/submit`.
23. A set-aside consumes only rooms of its own campus, and the pad named for a legacy placement belongs to the `school_id` supplied with that legacy placement — `ERR_SITE_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`, 422, on `POST /facilities/portable-units/{id}/record-existing-placement`.
24. The permit's owning placement request is `PERMIT_PENDING` — `ERR_PERMIT_REQUEST_NOT_PENDING`, 409, on `POST /facilities/permits/{id}/decision` and `POST /facilities/permits/{id}/resubmit`.
25. Every grade in the campus's grade set has at least one enrolment projection surviving the study method's source filter — `ERR_PROJECTION_COVERAGE_GAP`, 422, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/submit`.
26. The program capacity computed under the study's method is greater than 0 seats — `ERR_ZERO_PROGRAM_CAPACITY`, 422, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/submit`.
27. No selected projection has `confidence_tier` equal to `LOW` when the study's `method` is `DISTRICT_POLICY` — `ERR_LOW_CONFIDENCE_PROJECTION`, 422, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/certify`.
28. The study's `revision_round` is strictly less than 3 — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, 409, on `POST /facilities/capacity-studies/{id}/reopen`.
29. The unit's `lifetime_relocation_count` is strictly less than 4 — `ERR_UNIT_RELOCATION_LIMIT`, 422, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests` and `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/auto-assign-unit`.
30. Pad geometry admits the unit: `pad_length_ft` is at least the unit's chassis length plus 10 feet and `pad_width_ft` is at least the unit's chassis width plus 10 feet — `ERR_PAD_TOO_SMALL`, 422, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests`.
31. The target pad's `occupied_by_unit_id` is null — `ERR_SITE_OCCUPIED`, 409, on exactly three endpoints: `POST /facilities/placement-requests`, `POST /facilities/placement-sites/{id}/assess`, and `POST /facilities/portable-units/{id}/record-existing-placement`.
32. Uniqueness: a class-size policy is unique on the pair `grade_band` and `effective_school_year` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY`); a room is unique on the pair `school_id` and `room_number` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM`); a portable unit is unique on `asset_tag` across the whole inventory (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSET_TAG`); a pad is unique on the pair `school_id` and `pad_code` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PAD_CODE`). All four carry 409, on `POST /facilities/class-size-policies`, `POST /facilities/rooms`, `POST /facilities/portable-units` and `POST /facilities/placement-sites` respectively.
33. The crew's `certified_work_types` contains the requested `work_type` — `ERR_CREW_NOT_CERTIFIED`, 422, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
34. The closing date of a window is not earlier than its opening date, applied both to the requested window on a placement request and to the booked window on a schedule — `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, 422, on `POST /facilities/placement-requests` and `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
35. The booked window lies entirely inside June 15 through August 15 of a single calendar year, unless `off_summer_waiver` is true — `ERR_OUTSIDE_SUMMER_WINDOW`, 422, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
36. `off_summer_waiver` may be set true only by a `DistrictAdmin` — `ERR_WAIVER_ROLE_REQUIRED`, 403, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
37. The booked window's opening date is not later than 180 calendar days after `approved_on` for every `APPROVED` permit of the request — `ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED`, 409, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
38. The pad turnaround buffer holds — `ERR_PAD_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
39. The crew's concurrency ceiling holds — `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409, on `POST /facilities/installation-schedules`.
40. The appropriation's `uncommitted_cents` is at least the amount being committed — the request's `estimated_relocation_cost_cents` when the final permit approval commits the encumbrance, and the size-class removal charge when a decommission completes — `ERR_BUDGET_INSUFFICIENT`, 409, on `POST /facilities/permits/{id}/decision` and `POST /facilities/placement-requests/{id}/complete-decommission`.
41. Disposal proceeds satisfy the channel's recovery rule — `ERR_DISPOSAL_UNDERVALUED`, 422, on `POST /facilities/disposal-records`.

Two further conditions are not ordered checks but outcomes of a computation that finds nothing to work with: auto-assignment that finds no eligible unit fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNIT` (409), and a per-campus reliance report requested for a campus with no certified study fails with `ERR_NO_CERTIFIED_STUDY` (409).

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Study and Request Creation

Two creation endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row, because both of them legitimately overwrite planning work that the caller may not have observed.

`POST /facilities/capacity-studies` requires `expected_school_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the `version` of the campus that the client last read. If it does not match the campus's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCHOOL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard is necessary because a newly created study supersedes any existing `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` study for the same campus and school year; a create issued from a stale view of the campus would silently discard planning work the client never saw, so the planner is forced to re-read the campus first.

`POST /facilities/placement-requests` requires `expected_study_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the `version` of the justifying capacity study that the client last read. If it does not match that study's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STUDY_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Several placement requests against one study are entirely legitimate, so no state precondition blocks the second create; the stale-version check is the only thing that stops a request from being sized against a `seat_deficit` the study no longer reports.

Both tokens must be integers of at least 0; a non-integer or negative token is a body-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the 412 comparison is ever reached. No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints outside these two are processed without any concurrency token.

### 2.8. Seats, Percentages and Money: Precision Rules

Seat counts are integers throughout. Wherever a seat count is obtained by dividing one quantity by another — area by area-per-seat, deficit by seats-per-portable — the rule states explicitly whether the quotient is taken downward to the next whole number or upward to the next whole number; no seat division ever rounds to nearest.

Percentages are carried to one decimal place and rounded half up. This applies to a study's `utilization_pct`, to a campus's portable seat share, and, separately and again, to the district-wide average of those shares.

Money is carried in integer cents everywhere, in fields whose names end in `_cents`. Cost calculations name their rounding points explicitly and have no others: the relocation estimate rounds half up exactly twice, once when the off-summer escalation is applied to the mobilisation subtotal and once when the contingency percentage is taken, in that order; the alternatives comparison rounds half up exactly once, when the soft-cost percentage is applied to a direct cost; and the salvage valuation rounds half up when the straight-line figure is taken and again when a condition multiplier is applied to that already-rounded figure.

### 2.9. The Capital Budget Partition

A `CapitalBudget` records one board appropriation for a `fiscal_year`, with `authorized_cents` as the conserved total. The appropriation is always partitioned into exactly three buckets: `encumbered_cents`, holding cents committed to placement requests that are `PERMITTED` or `SCHEDULED` but not yet spent; `expended_cents`, holding cents actually spent on completed installations and completed decommissions; and `uncommitted_cents`, which is `authorized_cents` reduced by the encumbered amount and reduced again by the expended amount. After every mutation of either of the first two buckets, the invariant must hold that `authorized_cents` is equal to the sum of `uncommitted_cents`, `encumbered_cents` and `expended_cents`.

The encumbered bucket is defined by aggregation, not by drift: it is the sum of `encumbered_cost_cents` over every placement request charged to that appropriation whose status is `PERMITTED` or `SCHEDULED`. The expended bucket is a monotone accumulator that never decreases; it grows by the request's `encumbered_cost_cents` when an installation completes, and by the size-class removal charge when a decommission completes. Any event that changes either bucket recomputes `uncommitted_cents` and increments the appropriation's `version`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Deterministic Ordering

Many operations in this system mutate more than one row. Every such secondary mutation is a real mutation: it refreshes the affected row's `updated_at` and increments that row's `version` by 1, exactly as a client-initiated write would. A cascade that touches a placement request, its appropriation, its unit, its pad and its campus increments five separate version counters.

When a cascade fans out over a set of sibling rows — the studies superseded by a newer study, the placement requests withdrawn because a unit was declared unsafe — the rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, with rows sharing one instant taken in insertion order, so the resulting version sequence is reproducible. Where a selection rule must pick exactly one row from several candidates, the rule always ends in a tie-break over persisted field values rather than over `id`, and each such rule is stated in full where the workflow that uses it is described.

### 2.11. Enumerations of Record

The vocabularies below are closed. Any value outside them is a body-shape failure and raises `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A campus's `grade_configuration` is one of `ELEMENTARY_K5`, `MIDDLE_68`, `HIGH_912` or `K8_COMBINED`. A class-size policy's `grade_band` is one of `K_3`, `G4_6`, `G7_8` or `G9_12`. A room's `room_type` is one of `STANDARD_CLASSROOM`, `SCIENCE_LAB`, `SPED_RESOURCE`, `ART_MUSIC`, `GYM`, `LIBRARY` or `ADMIN`, and a set-aside's `reserved_room_type` draws from the same seven values. A room's `status` is one of `IN_SERVICE`, `OUT_OF_SERVICE_RENOVATION` or `DECOMMISSIONED`. A set-aside's `program_type` is one of `SPED_SELF_CONTAINED`, `DUAL_LANGUAGE`, `TITLE_I_INTERVENTION` or `CTE_LAB`. A projection's `source` is `COHORT_SURVIVAL` or `DEVELOPER_YIELD`, and its `confidence_tier` is `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` or `LOW`.

A study's `method` is `STATE_LOADING_STANDARD` or `DISTRICT_POLICY`, and its `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED`, `CERTIFIED` or `SUPERSEDED`. A portable unit's `size_class` is `SINGLE_24x40`, `DOUBLE_28x64` or `TRIPLE_36x64`; its `condition_grade` is `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`; its `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE`, `NEEDS_REHAB` or `UNSAFE`; and its `allocation_status` is one of `IN_STORAGE`, `RESERVED`, `SCHEDULED`, `INSTALLED`, `PENDING_REMOVAL` or `DISPOSED`. A pad's `feasibility_status` is `UNASSESSED`, `FEASIBLE`, `FEASIBLE_WITH_MITIGATION` or `INFEASIBLE`, its `infeasible_reason` is `PAD_TOO_SMALL`, `FIRE_ACCESS` or null, and each entry of its `mitigation_flags` array is `ELECTRICAL_UPGRADE`, `SEWER_HOOKUP` or `ADA_PATH_UPGRADE`.

A placement request's `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `SITE_ASSESSED`, `PERMIT_PENDING`, `PERMITTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `INSTALLED`, `DECOMMISSION_REQUESTED`, `DECOMMISSIONED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and its `cost_recommendation` is `RELOCATE`, `BOUNDARY_CHANGE`, `PERMANENT_ADDITION` or null. A permit's `permit_type` is one of `DSA_STRUCTURAL`, `FIRE_MARSHAL`, `ELECTRICAL`, `PLUMBING` or `ADA_ACCESS`, and its `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED`, `APPROVED` or `DENIED`. An installation schedule's `work_type` is `SET` or `RELOCATE`, each element of a crew's `certified_work_types` is drawn from the same two values, and a schedule's `status` is `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`. A disposal record's `method` is `SALE`, `SCRAP` or `TRANSFER_TO_COUNTY`, and its `valuation_basis` is `STRAIGHT_LINE` or `CONDITION_ADJUSTED`. A campus's `reliance_flag` is `NONE`, `MONITOR` or `LONG_TERM_RELIANCE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Building Program

Everything begins with a campus. A `DistrictAdmin` onboards one by supplying its `name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters; its `grade_configuration`; its usable `site_acreage`, which must be at least 0.5; the narrowest clear width of its fire-access loop as `fire_lane_clear_width_ft`, which may be 0 or more; the campus-level spare electrical service as `electrical_spare_capacity_kw`, also 0 or more; and the count of unused sanitary sewer connection stubs as `sewer_spare_connections`, an integer of 0 or more. A campus may optionally arrive carrying history: `consecutive_reliance_years` is the number of consecutive prior school years the campus has already leaned on portable classrooms, supplied at onboarding from the prior reporting system and defaulting to 0. Three campus fields are never supplied by a client — `hosted_portable_seats` begins at 0, `reliance_flag` begins at `NONE`, and `certified_study_id` begins as null.

**Class-size policy** is published district-wide rather than per campus. A policy names the `grade_band` it governs and the `effective_school_year` it applies to, written in the form `2027-28` — validated as four digits, a hyphen and two digits, with the two halves never cross-checked for consecutiveness. It fixes `max_class_size`, the contractual maximum pupils per teaching station, between 1 and 40; `min_area_sqft_per_seat`, the minimum assignable square feet per seat for a standard classroom, which must be greater than 0; and `lab_area_premium_sqft`, the extra square feet per seat that a science lab demands on top of the standard figure, which may be 0 or more. Each campus configuration maps to exactly one **primary band**: an `ELEMENTARY_K5` campus is governed by the `K_3` band, a `MIDDLE_68` campus by `G7_8`, a `HIGH_912` campus by `G9_12`, and a `K8_COMBINED` campus by `G4_6`. A study whose pinned policy is published for any other band is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_BAND_MISMATCH`, and one whose policy is published for a different year than the study measures is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_YEAR_MISMATCH`.

Rooms are the raw material of capacity. A room is registered against a campus by `school_id`, labelled with a `room_number` of 1 to 20 characters that must be unique on that campus, typed by `room_type`, and sized by `area_sqft`, which must be greater than 0. Its `status` is not a client input at creation and begins at `IN_SERVICE`. A `DistrictAdmin` may later revise a room's `status` or its `area_sqft`, both of which are optional on that revision; such a revision increments the room's `version` — even one that supplies neither field, which still counts as a mutation — and — importantly — never retroactively rewrites the capacity already frozen onto a submitted study. Only `IN_SERVICE` rooms count toward program capacity; a room in `OUT_OF_SERVICE_RENOVATION` or `DECOMMISSIONED` contributes nothing.

Special programs consume rooms without producing general-education seats. A **program set-aside** attaches to a campus by `school_id`, names the `program_type` holding the reservation, the `reserved_room_type` it consumes, the `reserved_room_count` of such rooms it holds (at least 1), and `seats_excluded_per_room`, the number of seats removed from program capacity for each reserved room (0 or more).

Enrolment projections are append-only. A row names its campus by `school_id`, its `school_year`, its `grade_level` as an integer from 0 through 12 where 0 means kindergarten, its `projected_students` headcount of 0 or more, its methodology in `source`, and the analyst's `confidence_tier`. Rows are never edited; a newer row simply displaces an older one for the same campus, year and grade.

Finally, the operational scaffolding. An appropriation is recorded with a `fiscal_year`, written in the same four-digit, hyphen, two-digit form as a school year, and `authorized_cents` greater than 0, opening with both `encumbered_cents` and `expended_cents` at 0 and `uncommitted_cents` equal to the authorized figure. An installation crew is registered with a `name` of 1 to 80 characters, a `max_concurrent_jobs` ceiling from 1 through 6 counting how many installation windows the crew may have overlapping at once, and a non-empty `certified_work_types` array. The portable inventory itself is onboarded unit by unit with an `asset_tag` of 1 to 24 characters unique across the district, a `size_class`, an `acquisition_cost_cents` greater than 0, an `in_service_year` between 1970 and 2100, an optional `condition_grade` defaulting to `B`, an optional `ada_ramp_installed` flag defaulting to false, an optional `replaced_unit_id` naming the unit this one was bought to replace (null when it is not a replacement), and an optional `prior_relocation_count` from 0 through 4 recording moves the unit made before this system existed, defaulting to 0. On creation the server derives `rated_seats` from `size_class` — a `SINGLE_24x40` seats 24, a `DOUBLE_28x64` seats 48, a `TRIPLE_36x64` seats 60 — sets `condition_status` to `SERVICEABLE` and `allocation_status` to `IN_STORAGE`, seeds `lifetime_relocation_count` from `prior_relocation_count`, leaves `current_school_id` null, and opens `accumulated_relocation_cost_cents` at 0.

### 3.2. Measuring Program Capacity: Two Methods, One Campus

A **capacity study** is the district's formal statement of how many seats a campus actually has and how hard those seats are being worked. A `FacilitiesPlanner` opens one against a campus by `school_id`, pins the policy it will use through `class_size_policy_id`, names the `school_year` it measures, and chooses a `method`. The pinned policy is resolved once, at creation, and is never re-resolved afterwards — republishing policy later cannot silently change a study's arithmetic. A new study opens in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, with `authored_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, with `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_on` null, and with all seven computed capacity and utilization fields at their defaults.

The choice of `method` selects one of two loading variants, and the divergence between them is the analytical heart of the system.

Under the **`DISTRICT_POLICY`** variant, seats are area-driven. For each `IN_SERVICE` room of the campus, take the room's `area_sqft`, divide it by the policy's `min_area_sqft_per_seat`, take the quotient downward to a whole number, and then take the smaller of that figure and the policy's `max_class_size` — that is the seat count for a `STANDARD_CLASSROOM`. A `SCIENCE_LAB` is treated identically except that the divisor is the policy's `min_area_sqft_per_seat` increased by its `lab_area_premium_sqft`, so a lab needs more floor area per pupil. A `SPED_RESOURCE` room contributes a fixed 12 seats, regardless of area. An `ART_MUSIC` room loads at half strength: compute the standard-classroom figure for that room exactly as described above, multiply it by 0.50, and take the product downward to a whole number. A `GYM`, a `LIBRARY` and an `ADMIN` room each contribute 0 seats.

Under the **`STATE_LOADING_STANDARD`** variant, area is ignored entirely and each room contributes a fixed statewide loading factor determined solely by its `room_type`: 25 seats for a `STANDARD_CLASSROOM`, 24 for a `SCIENCE_LAB`, 12 for a `SPED_RESOURCE`, 25 for an `ART_MUSIC` room, and 0 each for `GYM`, `LIBRARY` and `ADMIN`.

Whichever variant applies, summing the per-room seat counts over every `IN_SERVICE` room of the campus gives `gross_room_seats`.

Set-asides then deduct. Under `DISTRICT_POLICY` every program set-aside of the campus participates; under `STATE_LOADING_STANDARD` only set-asides whose `program_type` is `SPED_SELF_CONTAINED` participate, because the state method honours the special-education reservation and nothing else. For each participating set-aside, count how many `IN_SERVICE` rooms of the campus have the set-aside's `reserved_room_type`, take the smaller of that count and the set-aside's `reserved_room_count`, and multiply by `seats_excluded_per_room`. Summing that product over the participating set-asides gives `set_aside_deduction_seats`. A set-aside can therefore never deduct for rooms the campus does not have.

`program_capacity_seats` is `gross_room_seats` reduced by `set_aside_deduction_seats`, and is clamped at 0 from below so that an over-reserved campus reports zero rather than a negative capacity. A study whose program capacity computes to 0 seats cannot be submitted; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ZERO_PROGRAM_CAPACITY`.

A read-only **capacity breakdown** view exists precisely so that the divergence between the two methods is directly observable without changing anything. It recomputes both variants live against the campus's current rooms and set-asides and reports, side by side, the study's own `method`, the `gross_room_seats`, `set_aside_deduction_seats` and `program_capacity_seats` under that method, and the net program capacity under each variant separately as `state_standard_seats` and `district_policy_seats`. It also reports the utilization side of the picture: `projected_enrollment_total` under the study's own method, the same total computed under each variant's source filter as `state_projected_total` and `district_projected_total`, plus `utilization_pct`, `portable_trigger_met`, `seat_deficit` and `required_portable_count`. Both variants are recomputed against the study's own pinned policy. When the recomputed program capacity under the study's method is 0, the view reports `utilization_pct` as 0.0 with `portable_trigger_met` false rather than dividing by zero. Because this view recomputes rather than reads, it will disagree with an already-submitted study whose figures were frozen before a room was renovated — and that disagreement is the intended signal.

### 3.3. Utilization, the Portable Trigger, and the Study Review Cycle

Utilization compares the frozen capacity against projected demand for exactly the grades the campus serves. An `ELEMENTARY_K5` campus counts grades 0 through 5; a `MIDDLE_68` campus counts grades 6, 7 and 8; a `HIGH_912` campus counts grades 9 through 12; a `K8_COMBINED` campus counts grades 0 through 8.

For each grade in that set, the server gathers the projection rows for the study's campus and `school_year` at that `grade_level`. Under the `DISTRICT_POLICY` variant both methodologies compete, so a later `DEVELOPER_YIELD` row can displace an earlier `COHORT_SURVIVAL` row for that grade; under the `STATE_LOADING_STANDARD` variant `DEVELOPER_YIELD` rows are dropped before selection, so the grade falls back to the newest `COHORT_SURVIVAL` row. Among the surviving candidates for a grade, exactly one row is selected: order the candidates by `created_at` descending, break ties on `projected_students` descending, and take the first; a pair still tied on both keys is resolved in favour of the row inserted first. Summing `projected_students` over the one selected row per grade gives `projected_enrollment_total`. If any grade in the campus's grade set has no surviving candidate at all, submission is rejected with `ERR_PROJECTION_COVERAGE_GAP`.

`utilization_pct` is the projected enrolment total multiplied by 100, divided by `program_capacity_seats`, and rounded half up to one decimal place. The portable trigger fires when `utilization_pct` reaches 95.0 or more — the boundary is inclusive, so a campus computing exactly 95.0 percent has `portable_trigger_met` true. Separately, the district's target loading is also 95 percent of program capacity: multiply `program_capacity_seats` by 95, divide by 100, and take the quotient downward to a whole number to obtain the campus's effective capacity at target loading. When the trigger has fired, `seat_deficit` is the projected enrolment total reduced by that effective capacity, clamped at 0 from below; when the trigger has not fired, `seat_deficit` is 0. `required_portable_count` is the seat deficit divided by 24 — the seats a standard single-wide portable provides — with the quotient taken upward to the next whole number.

**Submission** is the moment all of this is frozen. A `FacilitiesPlanner` submits a study that is in `DRAFT`, and the transition `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` is permitted only when the pinned policy's `grade_band` is the primary band of the campus configuration, the policy's `effective_school_year` matches the study's `school_year`, every grade in the campus grade set has a surviving projection, and the program capacity computes greater than 0. On success the server runs both computations and freezes `gross_room_seats`, `set_aside_deduction_seats`, `program_capacity_seats`, `projected_enrollment_total`, `utilization_pct`, `portable_trigger_met`, `seat_deficit` and `required_portable_count` onto the study, and increments the study's `version` by 1.

**Certification** is performed by a `CapacityCertifier` on a study in `SUBMITTED`, moving it `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`. The certifier's `X-User-ID` must differ from the study's `authored_by_user_id`, and if the study's `method` is `DISTRICT_POLICY`, no selected projection may carry `confidence_tier` `LOW` — a low-confidence developer-yield row is acceptable input to a district-policy draft but not to a certified one, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_LOW_CONFIDENCE_PROJECTION`. A `STATE_LOADING_STANDARD` study is not subject to that confidence gate. Certification then cascades in three linked steps. It stamps `certified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `certified_on` from the logical clock. Because the campus now has a new governing study, every other study for the same campus and `school_year` whose status is `CERTIFIED` moves `CERTIFIED → SUPERSEDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order and each incrementing its own `version` by 1. And because the governing study has changed, the campus's `certified_study_id` is repointed to the newly certified study and its `reliance_flag` is recomputed. The newly certified study's `version` increases by 1 and the campus's `version` increases by 1.

**Return** is the other outcome of review. A `CapacityCertifier` returns a `SUBMITTED` study — again only when the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `authored_by_user_id` — supplying a `correction_note` that explains what must change. That note is a required body field, so a missing or empty one is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the study's state is examined, and it is read for validation only rather than persisted onto the study. The transition `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` increments `revision_round` by 1, clears the frozen capacity and utilization fields back to their defaults so no stale figure can be mistaken for a live one, and increments the study's `version` by 1.

**Reopening** closes the cycle. A `FacilitiesPlanner` reopens a study in `RETURNED`, moving it `RETURNED → DRAFT` and incrementing its `version` by 1, but only while `revision_round` is strictly less than 3. The fourth reopen attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, which is what caps the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → DRAFT` loop at three revision rounds.

One more path into `SUPERSEDED` exists, and it is the reason the campus stale-version guard matters. Opening a new study for a campus and `school_year` that already has a study in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` moves each such study to `SUPERSEDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order and each incrementing its own `version` by 1; the campus row itself is not mutated by the create, so the campus's `version` is unchanged by it. This is legitimate behaviour, not an error — which is exactly why nothing but `expected_school_version` stands between a stale client and the silent loss of another planner's draft. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: a superseded study never transitions again.

### 3.4. Judging a Pad: Geometry, Fire Access and Utilities

Candidate **placement sites** — pads — are registered by a `DistrictAdmin` against a campus by `school_id`, labelled with a `pad_code` of 1 to 20 characters unique on that campus, and described by `pad_length_ft` and `pad_width_ft` (both greater than 0), `distance_to_fire_lane_ft` giving the hose-lay distance from the pad to the nearest fire-access lane (0 or more), `electrical_drop_available_kw` giving the electrical service available at the pad (0 or more), an optional `sewer_connection_available` flag defaulting to false, and an optional `ada_path_of_travel_compliant` flag defaulting to false. A newly registered pad has `feasibility_status` `UNASSESSED`, a null `occupied_by_unit_id`, an empty `mitigation_flags`, a null `infeasible_reason` and a null `assessed_for_size_class`.

A `FacilitiesPlanner` assesses a pad against a specific chassis. The assessment optionally accepts `candidate_size_class`; when it is omitted the pad is assessed against `SINGLE_24x40`. Three chassis geometries exist: a `SINGLE_24x40` measures 40 feet by 24 feet, draws 12 kW of service, needs no sewer connection and seats 24; a `DOUBLE_28x64` measures 64 feet by 28 feet, draws 20 kW, needs a sewer connection and seats 48; a `TRIPLE_36x64` measures 64 feet by 36 feet, draws 28 kW, needs a sewer connection and seats 60. Every chassis requires 5 feet of clearance on each side, so the pad must offer the chassis length plus 10 feet and the chassis width plus 10 feet. A pad that is currently occupied cannot be assessed at all: if `occupied_by_unit_id` is set, the assessment is rejected with `ERR_SITE_OCCUPIED`.

The assessment runs in three ordered steps and stops at the first hard failure.

The first step is geometry. If `pad_length_ft` is less than the chassis length plus 10 feet, or `pad_width_ft` is less than the chassis width plus 10 feet, the pad is judged `INFEASIBLE` with `infeasible_reason` `PAD_TOO_SMALL` and an empty `mitigation_flags`, and nothing further is evaluated.

The second step is fire access. If `distance_to_fire_lane_ft` is greater than 150 feet, or the campus's `fire_lane_clear_width_ft` is less than 20 feet, the pad is judged `INFEASIBLE` with `infeasible_reason` `FIRE_ACCESS` and an empty `mitigation_flags`, and nothing further is evaluated. Both boundaries are inclusive on the passing side: a hose-lay distance of exactly 150 feet passes, 150.1 feet fails, and a campus fire lane of exactly 20 feet of clear width passes.

The third step collects mitigations, appended in one fixed order. If the pad's `electrical_drop_available_kw` is less than the chassis's service draw, `ELECTRICAL_UPGRADE` is appended. If the chassis needs a sewer connection and `sewer_connection_available` is false, `SEWER_HOOKUP` is appended. If `ada_path_of_travel_compliant` is false, `ADA_PATH_UPGRADE` is appended. A pad that collects no flags is judged `FEASIBLE`; a pad that collects at least one is judged `FEASIBLE_WITH_MITIGATION`. In both cases `infeasible_reason` is set to null and `mitigation_flags` holds the collected list in that fixed order.

Every assessment outcome stores `assessed_for_size_class` as the chassis the assessment was run against and increments the pad's `version` by 1. Assessment is re-enterable: a pad already carrying any verdict — `UNASSESSED`, `FEASIBLE`, `FEASIBLE_WITH_MITIGATION` or `INFEASIBLE` — may be re-assessed against a different chassis at any time while it is unoccupied, and the new verdict wholly replaces the old one. That is why the downstream gate checks not only that the verdict is favourable but that `assessed_for_size_class` matches the chassis actually assigned; a pad cleared for a `SINGLE_24x40` is not cleared for a `TRIPLE_36x64`.

### 3.5. Opening a Placement Request and Choosing a Unit

A **placement request** is the work order that carries one portable from wherever it is to one pad. A `FacilitiesPlanner` opens one by naming the justifying study through `capacity_study_id`, the destination pad through `placement_site_id`, and the appropriation through `capital_budget_id`; by declaring the intended installation window with `requested_window_start` and `requested_window_end`, both written as `YYYY-MM-DD` with the closing date not earlier than the opening date; by stating `haul_miles`, the one-way haul distance from the unit's present location to the pad, as an integer from 0 through 500; and by presenting `expected_study_version`. A specific unit may be named up front through `portable_unit_id`, or that field may be omitted and left for the server to fill later. The request's own `school_id` is not a client input: it is copied from the study at creation and never re-resolved, so the request permanently records the campus the study measured. A new request opens in `DRAFT` with `required_permit_count` at 0, `encumbered_cost_cents` at 0, and `rejection_reason` null.

Creation is where most of the placement preconditions bite. The pad must belong to the same campus as the study, or the create fails with `ERR_SITE_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`. The pad must be unoccupied, or it fails with `ERR_SITE_OCCUPIED`. When a unit is named, that unit's `condition_status` must be `SERVICEABLE` (`ERR_UNIT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`), its `condition_grade` must not be `D` (`ERR_UNIT_GRADE_NOT_PLACEABLE`), its `lifetime_relocation_count` must be strictly less than 4 (`ERR_UNIT_RELOCATION_LIMIT`), and the pad must physically admit that chassis with its 5 feet of clearance on every side (`ERR_PAD_TOO_SMALL`). On success the create increments the study's `version` by 1, reserves the named unit by moving its `allocation_status` from `IN_STORAGE` to `RESERVED` and incrementing the unit's `version` by 1, and runs both cost computations to store `estimated_relocation_cost_cents` and `cost_recommendation`.

Reserving a unit is itself gated on the unit's own state. The `IN_STORAGE → RESERVED` move requires that the unit be `IN_STORAGE`, that its `condition_status` be `SERVICEABLE`, that its `condition_grade` not be `D`, and that its `lifetime_relocation_count` be strictly less than 4. A unit that is not `IN_STORAGE` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` and 409, and that check is evaluated ahead of the condition, grade, campus-match, relocation-count and pad-geometry checks. A reservation is released back to `IN_STORAGE` whenever the owning request leaves for `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and advances to `SCHEDULED` when the owning request reaches `PERMITTED`.

When no unit is named at creation, the planner can ask the server to choose one, and the selection rule is fully determined. The candidate pool is every portable in the district whose `allocation_status` is `IN_STORAGE`, whose `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE`, whose `condition_grade` is `A`, `B` or `C`, whose `lifetime_relocation_count` is strictly less than 4, whose `rated_seats` is at least 24, and whose chassis fits the request's pad — that is, the chassis length plus 10 feet is at most `pad_length_ft` and the chassis width plus 10 feet is at most `pad_width_ft`. Candidates are then ranked by `accumulated_relocation_cost_cents` ascending, so the least-moved-around asset in the fleet is preferred; ties are broken by condition grade in the order `A` before `B` before `C`; remaining ties are broken by `rated_seats` descending; and the final tie-break is `created_at` ascending. The first candidate wins. If the pool is empty the operation fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNIT`. On success the selected unit is reserved by the same `IN_STORAGE → RESERVED` move, `estimated_relocation_cost_cents` and `cost_recommendation` are recomputed against the newly known chassis, and the request's `version` is incremented by 1. Auto-assignment may equally be run on a request that already names a unit: that unit is never in the candidate pool, because it is `RESERVED` rather than `IN_STORAGE`, and when a different unit wins the previously reserved one is released back to `IN_STORAGE` with its own `version` incremented by 1.

Confirming the site closes the `DRAFT → SITE_ASSESSED` gap and is the point at which the certification requirement lands. A `FacilitiesPlanner` may confirm only a request in `DRAFT`, only when the study named by `capacity_study_id` has reached `CERTIFIED` (otherwise `ERR_STUDY_NOT_CERTIFIED`), only when `portable_unit_id` is no longer null (a request still carrying no unit is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`), only when that unit's `condition_status` is `SERVICEABLE`, only when the pad's `feasibility_status` is `FEASIBLE` or `FEASIBLE_WITH_MITIGATION` with `assessed_for_size_class` equal to the unit's `size_class`, and only when the pad's `distance_to_fire_lane_ft` is at most 150 feet and the campus's `fire_lane_clear_width_ft` is at least 20 feet. The fire-access condition is evaluated ahead of the feasibility condition, so a pad recorded `INFEASIBLE` for fire access reports `ERR_FIRE_ACCESS_NONCOMPLIANT` rather than the broader `ERR_SITE_NOT_FEASIBLE`. Confirmation increments the request's `version` by 1 and changes nothing else.

A planner may abandon a request at any point before it is installed. Withdrawal is permitted from `DRAFT`, `SITE_ASSESSED`, `PERMIT_PENDING`, `PERMITTED` or `SCHEDULED`, takes a required free-text `reason` which is recorded in `rejection_reason`, and unwinds everything the request had claimed: `encumbered_cost_cents` is released back out of the appropriation's `encumbered_cents` and then zeroed, `uncommitted_cents` is recomputed, the unit returns to `IN_STORAGE`, the pad's `occupied_by_unit_id` is cleared, and every installation schedule of the request whose status is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `REQUEST_WITHDRAWN`. The request's `version` and the appropriation's `version` each increase by 1 — the appropriation's unconditionally, even when the released amount was already 0 — as does the `version` of the unit whose reservation was actually released, of the pad that was in fact holding that unit, and of every schedule the withdrawal cancelled. `WITHDRAWN`, like `REJECTED` and `DECOMMISSIONED`, is terminal.

### 3.6. Costing the Move and Weighing the Alternatives

Every placement request carries a cost estimate, recomputed on creation and again whenever the server assigns a unit, and readable live through a cost-model view.

The estimate is built in seven steps, in this order. **Mobilisation** starts from the chassis move base — 4,500,000 cents for a `SINGLE_24x40`, 7,200,000 cents for a `DOUBLE_28x64`, 9,800,000 cents for a `TRIPLE_36x64` — and adds 38,000 cents for each of the request's `haul_miles`. **The off-summer escalation** is then applied to the mobilisation subtotal alone, before any other component joins it. The requested window counts as inside the summer construction window when `requested_window_start` falls on or after June 15 of its own calendar year, `requested_window_end` falls on or before August 15 of its own calendar year, and both dates fall in the same calendar year. If the window is not inside the summer window, the mobilisation subtotal is multiplied by 1.35 and rounded half up to whole cents right there; the cost-model view reports whether this happened as `off_summer_applied`. **Site work** is then added, conditional on the actual unit-and-pad pair: 1,850,000 cents when the pad's `electrical_drop_available_kw` is less than the chassis's service draw, 2,400,000 cents when the chassis needs a sewer connection and the pad's `sewer_connection_available` is false, and 3,150,000 cents when either the unit's `ada_ramp_installed` is false or the pad's `ada_path_of_travel_compliant` is false. **Rehab** is keyed on the unit's `condition_grade`: nothing for grade `A`, 1,250,000 cents for grade `B`, and 4,600,000 cents for grade `C`. Grade `D` units are never placeable, so no rehab figure exists for them. **Permit fees** are 210,000 cents for each discipline in the required permit set, and the set is sized as three baseline disciplines plus one more when the chassis needs a sewer connection and the pad has none, plus one more when either the unit lacks a compliant ramp or the pad path is non-compliant. **Direct cost** is the sum of the escalated mobilisation, the site work, the rehab and the permit fees. **Contingency** is 12 percent of that direct cost, rounded half up, then capped at 1,500,000 cents from above and lifted to 700,000 cents from below — the floor applies after the cap, so an estimate whose 12 percent computes below the floor pays exactly the floor and one whose 12 percent computes above the cap pays exactly the cap. `estimated_relocation_cost_cents` is the direct cost plus that clamped contingency. When `portable_unit_id` is still null, every component and the estimate itself are 0, while `off_summer_applied` still reports the window test truthfully because it depends on the window alone.

The cost-model view exposes each of those figures separately as `mobilisation_cents`, `site_work_cents`, `rehab_cents`, `permit_fee_cents`, `direct_cost_cents`, `contingency_cents` and `estimated_relocation_cost_cents`, alongside the `basis` in force.

The second computation asks a harder question: is relocating a portable actually the cheapest way to close this campus's `seat_deficit`? It reads the deficit and the `required_portable_count` frozen on the referenced study, plus the campus's `consecutive_reliance_years`, and prices three options.

**Relocation** multiplies the required portable count by a per-unit figure. Under the default 20-year lifecycle basis, the per-unit figure is this request's relocation estimate, plus 1,150,000 cents of annual operations and maintenance for each of 20 years, plus the size-class removal cost — 1,600,000 cents for a `SINGLE_24x40`, 2,300,000 cents for a `DOUBLE_28x64`, 2,900,000 cents for a `TRIPLE_36x64`, and 0 when the request names no unit yet and so has no size class. Under the immediate-capital basis the per-unit figure is the relocation estimate alone, with no operations, maintenance or removal carried. **A boundary change** costs a fixed 4,200,000 cents of study work plus 96,000 cents of transport for each deficit seat for each of 5 years. **A permanent addition** costs 3,850,000 cents for each deficit seat.

Each option's direct cost is then grossed up by a soft-cost percentage, and the evaluation order here is pinned because reading it the other way is circular. The tier is always selected from the **direct** cost, never from the total: a direct cost below 50,000,000 cents attracts 9 percent, a direct cost below 150,000,000 cents attracts 13 percent, and any larger direct cost attracts 17 percent. The soft cost is that percentage of the direct cost, rounded half up. The total program cost is the direct cost plus the soft cost — computed last, and never used to pick the tier.

The recommendation is the surviving option with the smallest total program cost. One eligibility filter applies first: when the campus's `consecutive_reliance_years` has reached 5 or more, `RELOCATE` is struck from the candidate set entirely, because a campus that has leaned on portables for five consecutive years may not answer growth with another portable. The cost-model view reports whether that filter fired as `relocate_excluded_for_reliance`. Two options always survive, so the candidate set is never empty. Ties are broken by the fixed option order `RELOCATE`, then `BOUNDARY_CHANGE`, then `PERMANENT_ADDITION`. The winning option is written to `cost_recommendation`, and the value stored on the request is always the one computed under the 20-year lifecycle basis — the basis that carries two decades of operations and the eventual removal, which frequently pushes relocation into a higher soft-cost tier and can flip the recommendation away from `RELOCATE`. The cost-model view accepts `basis` as a query parameter whose only legal values are `LIFECYCLE_20YR` and `IMMEDIATE_CAPITAL` — any other value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — defaults to `LIFECYCLE_20YR` when it is absent or empty, echoes the basis in force back as `basis`, and reports `relocation_direct_cents`, `relocation_soft_cost_cents`, `relocation_total_program_cost_cents`, `boundary_change_direct_cents`, `boundary_change_total_program_cost_cents`, `permanent_addition_direct_cents`, `permanent_addition_soft_cost_cents`, `permanent_addition_total_program_cost_cents` and the resulting `cost_recommendation`. The same request can recommend `RELOCATE` on the immediate-capital basis while recommending `BOUNDARY_CHANGE` on the lifecycle basis; both answers are correct for their basis.

### 3.7. Permitting: One Fork, Two Joins

Once a request is `SITE_ASSESSED`, a `PermitCoordinator` files its permit set with the building department, moving the request `SITE_ASSESSED → PERMIT_PENDING`. Filing requires the unit's `condition_status` still to be `SERVICEABLE`, since a unit condemned between site confirmation and filing must not be permitted; a request that still names no unit at all is rejected with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. The filing forks: the server creates one `Permit` per member of the required discipline set, each attached to the request by `placement_request_id`, each with its `permit_type` set by the server rather than the client, each opening in `SUBMITTED` with `review_cycle_count` at 0, a null `approved_on`, a null `correction_note` and `submitted_on` stamped from the logical clock. The required set is always `DSA_STRUCTURAL`, `FIRE_MARSHAL` and `ELECTRICAL`; `PLUMBING` joins it when the chassis needs a sewer connection and the pad has none; `ADA_ACCESS` joins it when either the unit lacks a compliant ramp or the pad path is non-compliant. The permits are minted in that fixed discipline order — `DSA_STRUCTURAL`, `FIRE_MARSHAL`, `ELECTRICAL`, then `PLUMBING`, then `ADA_ACCESS` — so a list of the request's permits comes back in it. The size of the resulting set is stored on the request as `required_permit_count`, and the request's `version` increases by 1.

A `BuildingOfficial` decides each permit by supplying `decision` — one of `APPROVED`, `CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED` or `DENIED` — optionally accompanied by a `correction_note`, which is stored only on a `CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED` decision: a note supplied alongside `APPROVED` or `DENIED` is accepted and discarded. Any decision requires the permit to be in `SUBMITTED` and the owning request still to be `PERMIT_PENDING`; a decision attempted while the request has moved on is rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_REQUEST_NOT_PENDING`.

A decision of `CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED` moves the permit `SUBMITTED → CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED`, requires that `correction_note` be supplied, stores that note, and increments the permit's `version` by 1. The `PermitCoordinator` then refiles, supplying a `correction_response`; refiling requires the permit to be in `CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED` and the owning request still to be `PERMIT_PENDING`, moves the permit `CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED → SUBMITTED`, increments `review_cycle_count` by 1, refreshes `submitted_on` from the logical clock, and increments the permit's `version` by 1. The `correction_response` is a required body field but is read for validation only rather than persisted, and the stored `correction_note` is left in place across the refiling. This review loop is unbounded — a permit may cycle between `SUBMITTED` and `CORRECTIONS_REQUIRED` as many times as the building official requires.

A decision of `APPROVED` moves the permit `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_on` from the logical clock, and increments the permit's `version` by 1. Approval validity runs 180 calendar days from that instant. When, and only when, every permit of the request now stands `APPROVED`, the request joins on the favourable arm: it moves `PERMIT_PENDING → PERMITTED`, and three linked effects follow. First, the join is gated on money — the appropriation's `uncommitted_cents` must be at least the request's `estimated_relocation_cost_cents`, and a shortfall is reported as `ERR_BUDGET_INSUFFICIENT`; that test is made before anything at all is written, so a shortfall leaves even the deciding permit itself in `SUBMITTED` with no row mutated. Second, the estimate is copied into the request's `encumbered_cost_cents`, added to the appropriation's `encumbered_cents`, and `uncommitted_cents` is recomputed. Third, the reserved unit moves `RESERVED → SCHEDULED`, and because the unit is now scheduled onto that pad, the pad's `occupied_by_unit_id` is set to the unit. The request's `version` and the appropriation's `version` each increase by 1, as do the unit's and the pad's.

A decision of `DENIED` moves the permit `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and takes the request down the other join arm, whose side effects are wholly disjoint from the approval arm's. The request moves `PERMIT_PENDING → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` recorded as `PERMIT_DENIED`; the reserved unit returns `RESERVED → IN_STORAGE`; no budget encumbrance is created at all; and the pad's `occupied_by_unit_id` remains null. Sibling permits of a denied request keep whatever status they already hold — they are not cancelled or rewritten — and no further decision on any of them is accepted, because the owning request is no longer `PERMIT_PENDING`. The permit's `version` and the request's `version` each increase by 1. Both `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are terminal for a permit.

### 3.8. Booking and Executing the Installation Window

An `OperationsScheduler` or a `DistrictAdmin` books an installation window against a `PERMITTED` request by naming the request through `placement_request_id`, the crew through `installation_crew_id`, the `work_type` — `SET` moves a unit out of storage, `RELOCATE` moves it from another campus — and the window's `window_start` and `window_end`, both `YYYY-MM-DD` with the closing date not earlier than the opening date. An optional `off_summer_waiver` flag, defaulting to false, records a board waiver permitting work outside the summer window. Booking moves the request `PERMITTED → SCHEDULED`, increments the request's `version` by 1, and opens the schedule in `PLANNED` with a null `cancellation_reason`.

Five constraints guard the booking, and each has its own code. The request must be `PERMITTED` and must have no sibling schedule in `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, so a request may hold at most one live window at a time (`ERR_REQUEST_NOT_PERMITTED`). The crew's `certified_work_types` must contain the requested `work_type` (`ERR_CREW_NOT_CERTIFIED`). The window must lie entirely inside June 15 through August 15 of a single calendar year unless `off_summer_waiver` is true (`ERR_OUTSIDE_SUMMER_WINDOW`), and only a `DistrictAdmin` may set that flag true (`ERR_WAIVER_ROLE_REQUIRED`). Every `APPROVED` permit of the request must still be current: `window_start` must not fall later than 180 calendar days after that permit's `approved_on` (`ERR_PERMIT_EXPIRED`).

Two interval constraints round out the booking, and they use deliberately different interval conventions. **Pad occupation** treats windows as closed intervals with a 7 calendar day turnaround buffer on each side: the new window conflicts with any schedule belonging to a different request on the same pad whose status is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` unless the new opening date falls more than 7 days after that schedule's closing date, or the new closing date falls more than 7 days before that schedule's opening date. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_PAD_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. **Crew capacity** treats windows as half-open intervals, so back-to-back bookings are permitted: count the crew's schedules whose status is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` for which the new opening date falls strictly before the existing closing date and the existing opening date falls strictly before the new closing date; that count must be strictly less than the crew's `max_concurrent_jobs`, and reaching the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Executing the window is a two-step affair, and either an `OperationsScheduler` or the crew's own `CrewLead` may drive it. Marking the crew on site moves the schedule `PLANNED → IN_PROGRESS`, requires the owning request to be `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, 409), and — when the actor is a `CrewLead` — requires `X-User-ID` to equal the schedule's `installation_crew_id`. Completing the work moves the schedule `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, requires the owning request to be `SCHEDULED` under the same code, and carries the accessibility and code-compliance gate for the relocated unit: the unit's `ada_ramp_installed` must be true and the pad's `ada_path_of_travel_compliant` must be true, and a failure here is reported as `ERR_ADA_NONCOMPLIANT`. A `CrewLead` completing a window is again restricted to its own crew's schedule.

Completion is the system's largest cascade. The owning request moves `SCHEDULED → INSTALLED`, which moves the request's `encumbered_cost_cents` out of the appropriation's `encumbered_cents` and into its `expended_cents` and recomputes `uncommitted_cents`. The request's own `encumbered_cost_cents` field is left standing at the figure it recorded rather than zeroed; it drops out of the encumbered aggregation purely because the request is no longer `PERMITTED` or `SCHEDULED`. The unit moves `SCHEDULED → INSTALLED`, has its `current_school_id` set to the request's `school_id`, has its `lifetime_relocation_count` incremented by 1, and has the request's `encumbered_cost_cents` added to its `accumulated_relocation_cost_cents` — which is why a heavily moved unit sinks to the bottom of the auto-assignment ranking. The campus has the unit's `rated_seats` added to its `hosted_portable_seats` and its `reliance_flag` recomputed. The schedule's own `version` increases by 1, as do the request's, the appropriation's, the unit's and the campus's. Both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a schedule.

Cancelling an open window is reserved to an `OperationsScheduler` and is possible from `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. It takes a required free-text `reason`, stores it in `cancellation_reason`, and returns the owning request `SCHEDULED → PERMITTED` so that another window may be booked — stepping the request back only when it is in fact `SCHEDULED`, and leaving it and its `version` alone when it is not. The encumbrance is deliberately left untouched: the money stays committed because the job is still going to happen. The schedule's `version` and the request's `version` each increase by 1.

### 3.9. Condition, Decommissioning, Disposal and the Reliance Record

A portable unit carries two independent state dimensions on the same row, and they move for entirely different reasons. `condition_status` records serviceability and moves only when an `AssetManager` records an inspection. `allocation_status` records where the unit stands in the allocation lifecycle and moves only as a consequence of placement, scheduling and disposal activity. The combinations are genuinely independent: a unit may be `UNSAFE` while still `INSTALLED` on a campus. The allocation dimension is cyclic — `IN_STORAGE → RESERVED → SCHEDULED → INSTALLED → PENDING_REMOVAL → IN_STORAGE` — and only `DISPOSED` is terminal.

An inspection supplies both `condition_status` and `condition_grade`, both required, and may be recorded on any unit whose `allocation_status` is not `DISPOSED`; attempting to inspect a disposed unit is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID`. Any `condition_status` may be recorded on any unit that is not `DISPOSED`, including the recovery of an `UNSAFE` unit straight back to `SERVICEABLE`, and the condition dimension is never gated on the allocation dimension beyond that one exclusion. Recording `SERVICEABLE → NEEDS_REHAB` sets `condition_grade` to the supplied grade and increments the unit's `version` by 1. Recording the return leg `NEEDS_REHAB → SERVICEABLE` is permitted only when the supplied `condition_grade` is `A`, `B` or `C` — a unit cannot be declared serviceable at grade `D`, and the pairing of `SERVICEABLE` with grade `D` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` and 409 whatever condition the unit currently holds — and likewise increments the unit's `version` by 1. The condition dimension cycles freely between those two values as rehab work is done and re-inspected.

Declaring a unit `UNSAFE`, which is reachable from either `SERVICEABLE` or `NEEDS_REHAB` and equally from `UNSAFE` itself, fires the withdrawal cascade in three linked steps every time `UNSAFE` is recorded. First, because the unit is now unsafe, every placement request referencing it whose status is `DRAFT`, `SITE_ASSESSED`, `PERMIT_PENDING`, `PERMITTED` or `SCHEDULED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `rejection_reason` recorded as `UNIT_UNSAFE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Second, because each of those requests became `WITHDRAWN`, its `encumbered_cost_cents` is released back out of the appropriation's `encumbered_cents` and zeroed, `uncommitted_cents` is recomputed, and every installation schedule of that request whose status is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `UNIT_UNSAFE`. Third, because each of those schedules was cancelled, the pad of the request releases its hold by clearing `occupied_by_unit_id`, and the unit's `allocation_status` returns to `IN_STORAGE` with `current_school_id` cleared — unless the unit's `allocation_status` was `INSTALLED`, in which case an already-installed unit is left standing where it is and its allocation dimension is not disturbed. The unit's own `version` increases by 1, as does the `version` of every request, appropriation, schedule and pad the cascade touched.

Legacy inventory needs a way onto the books without the permit and schedule pipeline. An `AssetManager` or `DistrictAdmin` records an existing placement by supplying the `school_id` of the hosting campus, the `placement_site_id` of the pad the unit stands on, and `installed_on`, the date the unit went in, which is validated as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date but is not persisted onto any row. The unit must be `IN_STORAGE` and its `condition_status` must not be `UNSAFE` — both failures report `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` and 409, and both are evaluated ahead of the pad checks — and the named pad must belong to the supplied campus and have a null `occupied_by_unit_id`; a pad belonging elsewhere is rejected with `ERR_SITE_SCHOOL_MISMATCH` and an occupied pad with `ERR_SITE_OCCUPIED`. On success the unit jumps `IN_STORAGE → INSTALLED`, its `current_school_id` is set, the pad's `occupied_by_unit_id` is set, `lifetime_relocation_count` is incremented by 1, the unit's `rated_seats` are added to the campus's `hosted_portable_seats`, and the campus's `reliance_flag` is recomputed. The unit's `version`, the pad's `version` and the campus's `version` each increase by 1.

Decommissioning runs in two stages, both driven by an `AssetManager`. Opening decommissioning on a request that is `INSTALLED` moves it `INSTALLED → DECOMMISSION_REQUESTED`, optionally records a free-text `reason`, drives the unit `INSTALLED → PENDING_REMOVAL`, and increments the request's `version` by 1; a unit that is not `INSTALLED` is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_STATE_INVALID` and 409, and the same code guards completion against a unit that is not `PENDING_REMOVAL`. Completing decommissioning moves the request `DECOMMISSION_REQUESTED → DECOMMISSIONED` and is gated on money: the appropriation's `uncommitted_cents` must be at least the size-class removal charge — 1,600,000 cents for a `SINGLE_24x40`, 2,300,000 cents for a `DOUBLE_28x64`, 2,900,000 cents for a `TRIPLE_36x64` — and a shortfall is reported as `ERR_BUDGET_INSUFFICIENT`. On success that removal charge is added to the appropriation's `expended_cents` and `uncommitted_cents` is recomputed; the unit moves `PENDING_REMOVAL → IN_STORAGE`, which subtracts the unit's `rated_seats` from the campus's `hosted_portable_seats` and clamps that figure at 0 from below, clears the unit's `current_school_id`, clears the pad's `occupied_by_unit_id` and recomputes the campus's `reliance_flag`. The request's `version`, the appropriation's `version`, the unit's `version`, the pad's `version` and the campus's `version` each increase by 1.

Disposal ends a unit's life. An `AssetManager` files a disposal record naming the unit through `portable_unit_id`, the channel through `method`, the variant selector through `valuation_basis`, and `proceeds_cents`, the cents actually received, which must be 0 or more. Disposal requires the unit to be `IN_STORAGE`, requires either its `condition_status` to be `UNSAFE` or its `condition_grade` to be `D`, and requires that no disposal record already reference that unit; any of these failing yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_DISPOSABLE`. The unit moves `IN_STORAGE → DISPOSED` and its `version` increases by 1.

Salvage is valued from the logical clock and the unit's own history. Age in years is the current logical-clock year reduced by the unit's `in_service_year`. The straight-line factor is 1 reduced by that age divided by a 20-year useful life, lifted to a floor of 5 percent when the subtraction would fall below it. The straight-line figure is the unit's `acquisition_cost_cents` multiplied by that factor and rounded half up. Under the `STRAIGHT_LINE` basis, `salvage_value_cents` is exactly that straight-line figure — a pure age-based book value with a 5 percent floor, ignoring condition entirely. Under the `CONDITION_ADJUSTED` basis, the already-rounded straight-line figure is multiplied by the grade multiplier — 1.00 for grade `A`, 0.80 for grade `B`, 0.55 for grade `C`, 0.25 for grade `D` — the product is rounded half up, and the result is then lifted to a floor equal to 5 percent of `acquisition_cost_cents` rounded half up. The straight-line figure is always rounded before the condition multiplier is applied, never after. `net_disposal_result_cents` is `proceeds_cents` reduced by `salvage_value_cents`, and a negative result is a recorded book loss. `disposed_on` is stamped from the logical clock.

The channel constrains what the district may accept. A `SALE` requires `proceeds_cents` to be at least 70 percent of the computed `salvage_value_cents`, rounded half up; a `SCRAP` or `TRANSFER_TO_COUNTY` requires `proceeds_cents` to be exactly 0. Either failure is reported as `ERR_DISPOSAL_UNDERVALUED`. Because the two valuation bases can produce materially different salvage figures for the same unit, the same unit and the same proceeds may pass the recovery test on one basis and fail it on the other.

**Long-term reliance reporting** is the accountability layer over everything above. For one campus, the governing study is the latest one whose status is `CERTIFIED`, selected by `school_year` descending and then by `created_at` descending, with a remaining tie resolved in favour of the row inserted first; a campus with no certified study cannot be reported on and the request fails with `ERR_NO_CERTIFIED_STUDY`. That selection is made live over the study table and is what the report returns as `certified_study_id`, which need not equal the campus's stored `certified_study_id` when studies for different school years were certified out of order. The campus's permanent seats are that study's `program_capacity_seats`, reported as `permanent_seats`, and its portable seats are its current `hosted_portable_seats`. `portable_seat_share_pct` is the hosted portable seats multiplied by 100, divided by the sum of the permanent seats and the hosted portable seats, rounded half up to one decimal place; when that sum is 0 the share is reported as 0.0 rather than dividing by zero. The campus's `reliance_flag` is `LONG_TERM_RELIANCE` when that share reaches 15.0 percent or more, or when `consecutive_reliance_years` reaches 5 or more; failing that, it is `MONITOR` when the share reaches 5.0 percent or more; otherwise it is `NONE`. A narrative to the board is required exactly when the flag is `LONG_TERM_RELIANCE`, reported as `narrative_required`. The per-campus report returns the campus's `school_id` and `certified_study_id` alongside those figures and the campus's `consecutive_reliance_years`. This flag is recomputed on study certification, on a legacy placement record, on installation completion and on decommission completion, and computed live whenever a report is read.

The district-wide roll-up is where the rounding order is load-bearing. Qualifying campuses are those whose `certified_study_id` is not null, counted as `qualifying_school_count`. Each qualifying campus's share is rounded to one decimal place first, exactly as its own report would show it; only then are those already-rounded values averaged, and the average is rounded half up to one decimal place again to give `district_average_portable_share_pct`. Pooling district-wide seats and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and yields a different number; the roll-up states the convention it used in `rounding_basis`, always the literal `PER_SCHOOL_THEN_AVERAGE`. When no campus qualifies, the district average is reported as 0.0. Finally, `long_term_reliance_school_count` counts the qualifying campuses whose flag is `LONG_TERM_RELIANCE` — the number the board is ultimately accountable for.
