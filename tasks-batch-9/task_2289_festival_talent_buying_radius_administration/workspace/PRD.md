# Festival Talent Buying API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Booking a multi-stage music festival is an exercise in spending a fixed pot of money against a moving target. A talent buyer commits guarantees months before a single ticket is scanned, negotiates each act through several rounds of offer and counter-offer, and all the while polices radius clauses — the contractual promise that an act will not play a competing show within a given mileage of the festival market during a given stretch of the calendar. Today that work lives in spreadsheets, agency emails and the buyer's memory. A tier closes without anyone noticing that four other offers were still on the table against it; a headliner plays a club two hundred miles away three weeks before the gates open and nobody withholds the balance; a rider quietly doubles in cost between the agent's ask and the production manager's sign-off.

This document specifies the Festival Talent Buying & Radius Administration service, an HTTP API mounted under the `/festival-ops` prefix. The system is the single ledger of record for four intertwined concerns: **budget** (a talent pot split into lineup tiers by a waterfall, then debited booking by booking), **stage time** (finite minutes per stage per day, bounded by changeover buffers and municipal curfews), **paper** (offers, radius clauses, riders, technical advances and countersignatures) and **money** (deposits, day-of-show balances, waiver fees and post-cancellation clawbacks). Every one of those concerns is derived from the others, so the service's central design commitment is that derived figures are always reconciled inside the request that disturbed them — never by a nightly job, never by the client.

Five kinds of user share the system, and the service deliberately keeps their authority apart. A promoter-side buyer may write offers but may not accept them; an agent may accept but may not price a waiver; a production manager who submits a technical advance may not be the person who approves it. That separation, together with an optimistic-concurrency discipline on the three records most likely to be edited from two screens at once, is what makes the ledger trustworthy enough to pay against.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role Permissions

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the authority the caller is acting under. If either header is missing or empty, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the body is even parsed, so a syntactically broken payload sent without headers still reports `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rather than a shape complaint.

`X-User-Role` must be exactly one of five strings: `TalentBuyer`, `ArtistRep`, `BusinessAffairs`, `ProductionManager`, `FinanceController`. An unrecognised role string is treated as an authentication failure — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) — not as an authorization failure. A recognised role that does not appear in the authorized-role list for the endpoint being called (see §4) is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). That authorization decision is made against the static endpoint matrix before any path resource is loaded, which means a forbidden role addressing an identifier that does not exist receives 403, never 404.

The five roles divide as follows. A `TalentBuyer` books the festival: registering the edition, adding budget tiers, running the allocation waterfall, onboarding roster acts, drafting and issuing and revising offers, amending executed guarantees, and driving announcement waves and ticket-sales phases. An `ArtistRep` acts on behalf of exactly one act: countering, accepting or declining offers, filing rider lines, opening and submitting the technical advance, and asking to be let out of a radius clause. `BusinessAffairs` owns the paper: countersigning contracts, adjudicating competing-performance reports and deciding radius waivers. A `ProductionManager` runs the stages and the technical advance: creating stages, deciding rider lines, returning or approving advance packets. A `FinanceController` moves the money: releasing installments, recording payments and executing clawbacks. Sweeping expired offers, reporting a competing performance, cancelling a booking and locking an announcement wave are each shared between `TalentBuyer` and `BusinessAffairs`; opening and submitting an advance packet are shared between `ArtistRep` and `ProductionManager`; setting the logical clock is available to all five.

Identity binds to data differently for each role. When `X-User-Role` is `ArtistRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Artist row; ownership checks on any resource that references an Artist compare the resource's artist foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `ArtistRep` request must address a row whose `artist_id` equals `X-User-ID` — the `artist_id` of the offer, booking, radius clause or competing performance itself, or the `artist_id` of the parent booking when the addressed row is a rider item, an advance packet or a payment installment. A mismatch is `ERR_ARTIST_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), and because it is a row-level test it can only be evaluated after the row has been found: an `ArtistRep` reaching for another act's booking gets 403 even when the operation itself is open to the role. On list reads, the same binding is applied implicitly — an `ArtistRep` listing offers or bookings sees only rows belonging to their own act. `ArtistRep` is never authorized on festival, budget-tier or stage reads at all; those come back as `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

When `X-User-Role` is `TalentBuyer`, `BusinessAffairs`, `ProductionManager` or `FinanceController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `issued_by_user_id` on an offer when a buyer issues or revises it, as `reported_by_user_id` on a competing-performance report, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on an advance packet — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`. The one place an opaque identifier is compared to anything is the segregation-of-duties rule on the technical advance: the `X-User-ID` that returns or approves an advance packet must differ from the `X-User-ID` recorded as that packet's `submitted_by_user_id`, and a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) even though the caller genuinely holds `ProductionManager`. A second production manager with a different `X-User-ID` may adjudicate the packet.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection reads accept two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, must be an integer, and must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` defaults to 0, must be an integer, and must be at least 0. A `limit` of 0 and a `limit` of 101 are both rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); a `limit` of 1 and a `limit` of 100 are both accepted, as is any non-negative `offset`. The pagination guard applies to every paginated read in the system: the offer list, the booking list, the radius-clause list, the competing-performance list, the announcement-wave list, the per-festival stage list, the per-festival budget-tier list, the per-booking rider-item list, the per-booking advance-packet list and the per-booking payment-installment list. Each of those responses reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond it.

Unless a specific ordering is documented below, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where an ordering is documented, `created_at` ascending is always the final tie-break, and `id` is never a sort key. The documented orderings are: budget tiers by `tier_rank` ascending; stages by `is_flagship` descending, then `name` ascending; offers by `slot_start_utc` ascending; radius clauses by `radius_miles` descending, then `window_start_date` ascending — deliberately the same ranking the radius conflict detector uses to pick a winning clause; competing performances by `performance_date` ascending; bookings by `slot_start_utc` ascending; rider items by `category` ascending; advance packets by `created_at` ascending alone; payment installments by `due_date` ascending, which is exactly the order the chronological recovery replay uses; and announcement waves by `wave_number` ascending. The tier list returned by the allocation waterfall follows the same `tier_rank` ascending order.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every stored resource in this service — festivals, budget tiers, stages, artists, offers, radius clauses, competing performances, bookings, rider items, advance packets, payment installments and announcement waves — carries the same four housekeeping properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation was requested directly or produced as a side effect of an operation against some other resource. Any write that changes a row also refreshes that row's `updated_at`. There are no exceptions: no entity in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Enumerated values are exact, case-sensitive strings. Timestamps supplied by clients — `slot_start_utc`, `offer_expires_utc`, `scheduled_reveal_utc`, `current_utc` — are ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits. Calendar-date fields — `event_start_date`, `event_end_date`, `window_start_date`, `window_end_date`, `performance_date`, `due_date` — are ISO 8601 dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and are interpreted in UTC.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this service reads wall-clock time. Every temporal comparison — offer expiry, the payment release window, whether an announcement reveal is due, the deposit tiering at booking time, the days remaining until show time at cancellation — reads a logical clock that clients set explicitly by supplying a `current_utc` instant. Until that capability has been exercised for the first time, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Setting the clock is inert on its own: it does not sweep expired offers, does not release installments and does not publish waves. Those remain explicit operations, and the clock merely changes the answers they compute.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations in the system re-derive an entire dependent structure from a parent record, and each of them therefore requires the client to prove it was looking at the parent's current state. In each case the version field is a required input, not an optional one, and the value must equal the parent row's current `version`.

Re-running the budget waterfall requires `expected_festival_version` to match the target festival's `version`. Re-allocation legitimately supersedes the previous per-tier figures, so the operation is repeatable by design; the version check is what stops a client from overwriting an allocation it never observed — for example one written after another budget tier was added, which changed the denominator the percentages are read against. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FESTIVAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Amending an executed guarantee requires `expected_booking_version` to match the target booking's `version`. An amendment supersedes the booking's whole scheduled installment pair, so a second amendment is legitimate on its own terms; the version check rejects an amendment computed against a stale view of the booking — a view that predates an earlier amendment, a rider decision that moved the settlement figure, or a deposit payment. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Assigning a booking into an announcement wave requires `expected_announcement_wave_version` to match the target wave's `version`. Each assignment mutates the wave's assignment and headliner counters, so a client that chose a reveal position from a stale snapshot would be sequencing a lineup it never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WAVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field, and none may be made to require one.

### 2.7. Cents, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

All money in this API is expressed as an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents at rest and no floating-point money in any response. Where a monetary figure is the product of a rate and a base, the rounding method and the point at which rounding happens are both part of the specification, because the two orderings give different answers:

- **Round half up** is the only rounding mode used for money. A value exactly halfway between two whole cents rounds away from zero.
- The contingency reserve withheld from a talent budget is rounded half up exactly once, off the top, before any tier sees a cent.
- The waterfall that splits the allocatable budget across tiers uses **floor** per tier, never round half up, and hands the entire floor remainder to a single tier rather than spreading it.
- Rider lines are rounded half up **per line**, and only then summed. Summing raw products and rounding the total once at the end is the wrong reading and produces a different figure.
- The settlement calculation applies exactly one round-half-up step, to the promoter's reimbursed share of the rider.
- The contractual recovery figure applies two independent round-half-up steps — the deposit share and the liquidated-damages share are each rounded on their own and only then added. Rounding their sum is the wrong reading.
- The deposit portion of a payment schedule is rounded half up once; the balance is then the exact integer complement, never independently rounded, so the two installments always sum back to the contracted guarantee.
- The artist risk score is an integer, with round half up applied only to the draw penalty component, and the total clamped to a ceiling of 100.
- Great-circle distances are rounded half up to two decimal places before any comparison against a clause mileage.
- Ratios that exist only to be compared against a threshold — the committed-budget ratio behind lineup posture, the per-genre committed share — are compared as exact rational values and are never rounded or stored.
- Stage time is integer minutes throughout.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

Many operations in this system can fail for several reasons at once, and the answer the client receives is fully determined: the checks form one strictly ordered ladder, the lowest-ranked failing check is the response, and every check ranked below it is never evaluated at all — so its side effects never happen and its error is never seen. Implementers must treat the ladder below as the evaluation order, not as an unordered set of independent guards.

1. Both identity headers present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the five recognised roles — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The role is authorized for this endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. Request shape: required body fields present, JSON types correct, enum values members of their declared set, integers non-negative where declared so, dates and instants well-formed ISO 8601 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This is pure shape validation with no database reads.
5. Pagination bounds on list reads — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
6. Every path parameter resolves to a stored row, resolved left to right — the not-found error keyed to the entity of the segment that failed: `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_FOUND` for a festival edition, `ERR_BUDGET_TIER_NOT_FOUND` for a budget tier, `ERR_STAGE_NOT_FOUND` for a stage, `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_FOUND` for a roster act, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` for an offer, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` for a booking, `ERR_RADIUS_CLAUSE_NOT_FOUND` for a radius clause, `ERR_COMPETING_PERFORMANCE_NOT_FOUND` for a competing-performance report, `ERR_RIDER_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` for a rider line, `ERR_ADVANCE_PACKET_NOT_FOUND` for an advance packet, `ERR_PAYMENT_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a payment installment and `ERR_ANNOUNCEMENT_WAVE_NOT_FOUND` for an announcement wave.
7. Every identifier-bearing body field resolves to a stored row, resolved in the order the fields are declared for that operation — again the entity-specific not-found error from the list above, so an unresolvable `festival_id` is `ERR_FESTIVAL_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `budget_tier_id` is `ERR_BUDGET_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `stage_id` is `ERR_STAGE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `artist_id` is `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `booking_id` or `replaces_booking_id` is `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` and an unresolvable `competing_performance_id` is `ERR_COMPETING_PERFORMANCE_NOT_FOUND`. A body reference that resolves but belongs to the wrong festival is a rank 8 concern, not a rank 7 one.
8. Body references that must belong to the same festival do: an offer's budget tier, stage and festival must agree, and a wave assignment's booking must belong to the wave's festival — `ERR_FESTIVAL_MISMATCH`.
9. Row-level artist ownership for `ArtistRep` callers — `ERR_ARTIST_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
10. Optimistic-concurrency version match on the three operations that require it — the corresponding stale-version error. This sits above every state, safety and fiscal check, so a stale version on an otherwise-invalid request still reports 412.
11. Tier code and tier rank uniqueness within a festival — `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE`.
12. Stage name uniqueness within a festival — `ERR_DUPLICATE_STAGE_NAME`.
13. Global uniqueness of an act's performing name — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ARTIST_NAME`.
14. Wave number uniqueness within a festival — `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAVE_NUMBER`.
15. The addressed row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — the state error belonging to that entity. This band covers terminal-state lockdown and the second call of a non-idempotent action.
16. The festival's lineup posture is not `FROZEN` — `ERR_LINEUP_FROZEN`.
17. The festival's tiers have been allocated — `ERR_TIERS_NOT_ALLOCATED`.
18. A wave's phase matches the festival's current announcement phase — `ERR_WAVE_PHASE_MISMATCH`.
19. No radius clause of the addressed booking is in `BREACHED` — `ERR_RADIUS_BREACH_ACTIVE`.
20. The act's adjudicated risk score is below the blocking threshold — `ERR_ARTIST_RISK_BLOCKED`.
21. Segregation of duties on the technical advance — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
22. The booking has an approved advance packet — `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_APPROVED`.
23. Every booking assigned to the wave is countersigned — `ERR_WAVE_BOOKING_NOT_COUNTERSIGNED`.
24. The adjudication referenced by a waiver decision is the right one and is in the right state; and a violation finding is only available on a report that actually detected a conflict — `ERR_ADJUDICATION_MISMATCH`.
25. A replacement offer points at a cancelled booking of the same festival — `ERR_REPLACEMENT_TARGET_NOT_CANCELLED`.
26. The offer has not expired against the logical clock — `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`.
27. No buffered stage-interval collision — `ERR_STAGE_SLOT_CONFLICT`.
28. The set ends no later than the stage's curfew minute — `ERR_CURFEW_EXCEEDED`.
29. The stage's programmed minutes for the day stay within its daily cap — `ERR_STAGE_DAY_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
30. The act is not already committed elsewhere at this festival on that UTC date — `ERR_ARTIST_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.
31. The slot falls inside the festival's event window — `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_EVENT_WINDOW`.
32. The radius window is coherent and near enough to the event — `ERR_RADIUS_WINDOW_INVALID`.
33. A wave's reveal instant precedes the first show date — `ERR_REVEAL_AFTER_EVENT_START`.
34. A wave's reveal instant has arrived — `ERR_REVEAL_NOT_DUE`.
35. An installment is inside its release window — `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_RELEASABLE`.
36. Phase progression prerequisites are met — `ERR_PHASE_PREREQ_UNMET`.
37. The billing position is free on that stage for that date — `ERR_BILLING_POSITION_TAKEN`.
38. The budget tier is open and has a free slot — `ERR_TIER_CLOSED`.
39. A top-billed offer targets a flagship stage — `ERR_HEADLINER_STAGE_REQUIRED`.
40. The wave being locked is not empty — `ERR_EMPTY_WAVE`.
41. The booking being assigned is not already in a wave — `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`.
42. The advance packet has submissions left — `ERR_ADVANCE_SUBMISSIONS_EXHAUSTED`.
43. The offer has negotiation rounds left — `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.
44. The stage-day input-channel hard maximum is respected — `ERR_STAGE_CHANNEL_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
45. Tier allocation percentages close to one — `ERR_ALLOCATION_PCT_INVALID`.
46. No money is yet committed against the festival being re-allocated — `ERR_ALLOCATION_LOCKED`.
47. The projected genre share stays within the genre cap — `ERR_GENRE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
48. The offered guarantee plus production fee stays within the stamped ceiling — `ERR_GUARANTEE_CEILING_EXCEEDED`.
49. The tier's committed total plus this commitment stays within its allocation — `ERR_TIER_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`.
50. A granted waiver is not priced below the agent's own offer — `ERR_WAIVER_FEE_BELOW_OFFER`.
51. A clawback does not push recovery past the contractual ceiling — `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_CONTRACTUAL`.

Three consequences of that ordering are worth stating outright, because they are the cases where an intuitive reading gets the wrong answer. First, an offer that has already been accepted and whose revision body would also breach the guarantee ceiling reports `ERR_OFFER_NOT_NEGOTIABLE` — the state check at rank 15 fires and the fiscal check at rank 48 is never reached. Second, a booking sitting in `RIDER_SETTLED` that has both a `BREACHED` clause and no approved advance packet reports `ERR_RADIUS_BREACH_ACTIVE` on countersignature, because the safety check at rank 19 outranks the referential check at rank 22. Third, an issue request that both overlaps a live slot and reuses a taken billing position reports `ERR_STAGE_SLOT_CONFLICT`, because the temporal check at rank 27 outranks the constraint check at rank 37.

Within the heaviest gate in the system — sending an offer to the agent — the concrete order is therefore: lineup posture, tier allocation done, artist risk, stage interval collision, curfew, stage day load, artist double-booking, billing position, tier open, flagship requirement for top billing, genre cap, guarantee ceiling.

### 2.9. Conservation of Budget and Stage Time

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and every operation that disturbs them must leave them reconciled inside the same request.

The first is committed money. A festival's `committed_budget_cents` must always equal the sum of `committed_cents` across that festival's budget tiers, and must equally always equal the sum of `guarantee_cents` plus `production_fee_cents` over that festival's bookings whose `contract_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`. All three expressions are compared as exact integers with no tolerance. A cancelled booking contributes zero to all three. Accepting an offer debits the guarantee plus the production fee into the tier; cancelling the resulting booking credits exactly the same amount back. Running an acceptance and then a cancellation on the same offer must return all three expressions to their pre-acceptance values with zero drift, including in the case where the tier flipped to `CLOSED` and back to `OPEN` in between.

The second is stage time. A stage's `booked_minutes` must always equal the sum of `set_length_minutes` over that stage's bookings whose `contract_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, again as exact integer equality. Acceptance adds the set length; cancellation subtracts exactly the same minutes. A replacement booking written after a cancellation re-adds its own set length, so a cancel-and-replace at equal length leaves `booked_minutes` where it started even though the stage's `version` has advanced twice.

A third conservation law governs the installment pair: the sum of `amount_cents` over a booking's installments whose status is anything other than `VOIDED` always equals that booking's `guarantee_cents`. It holds immediately after acceptance, after any guarantee amendment, and after any hold or release.

The computed budget-summary read exists to make the first law auditable. It reports `allocatable_budget_cents`, `committed_budget_cents`, `tier_committed_sum_cents`, `booking_committed_sum_cents`, `committed_ratio`, `lineup_posture`, `open_tier_count`, `closed_tier_count` and `headliner_anchor_count`, and asserts that the three committed figures agree.

### 2.10. Cascades and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Consistency in this service is immediate. When one operation changes a fact that another row's derived field depends on, that dependent field is recomputed before the response is written — never later, and never by the client. Four multi-step cascades run in this system, each four hops deep, and each hop is caused by the hop above it rather than by the originating call:

- **The commitment cascade**, fired when an agent accepts an offer: the booking is created and the clause activates, the tier and stage ledgers are debited, the tier's status and the festival's committed total and posture are re-derived, and a tier that just closed supersedes its siblings' outstanding offers.
- **The compensating cascade**, fired when a booking is cancelled: the booking closes out, the tier and stage ledgers are credited back and the installments are voided or flagged, the tier's status and festival's posture are re-derived and the settlement dimension moves into recovery, and the booking's departure from its announcement wave can push an empty wave back to planning.
- **The enforcement cascade**, fired when a competing performance is adjudicated a violation: the report becomes a violation and the act's risk scores move, the conflicting clause breaches, the booking's scheduled installments are held, and the booking's settlement dimension is withheld.
- **The waiver cascade**, fired when business affairs grants a waiver: the clause is waived, the underlying report is waived and the act's risk scores fall, the held installments return to scheduled, and the settlement figure is recomputed with the waiver fee folded in.

Whenever a cascade touches a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments — including rows the caller never named. Derived counters are always defined as aggregates over their inputs rather than as running deltas, so a re-derivation from scratch and an incremental adjustment must agree; where the two would differ, the aggregate definition wins.

### 2.11. Nested Reads and Path Resolution

Every collection in this service sits directly under the `/festival-ops` namespace, so each entity has a canonical single-resource read of its own. Relationships are expressed by identifier fields in request bodies, not by nested creation paths. A handful of reads are nested for convenience — a festival's stages and budget tiers, a booking's rider items, advance packets and payment installments, a booking's rider estimate and settlement breakdown, an act's radius risk. For all of those, path parameters are resolved strictly left to right: the parent must be found before the child collection is filtered, and if the parent identifier does not resolve, the response is the parent's own not-found error. A syntactically valid identifier that matches no stored row is always a 404, never a 422.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Edition: Market, Budget and the Tier Waterfall

Everything in this system hangs off a festival edition. A `TalentBuyer` registers one by supplying a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, an `edition_year` between 2020 and 2100, the market's centre point as a decimal-degree `market_latitude` between -90.0 and 90.0 and a `market_longitude` between -180.0 and 180.0, the event window as `event_start_date` and `event_end_date`, and a gross `talent_budget_cents` of at least 100000 (one thousand dollars). The event window must be coherent: `event_start_date` may not fall after `event_end_date`. The market coordinates are not decoration — they are the centre point every radius clause measures from, so an edition registered at the wrong coordinates will silently mis-police its exclusivity.

A promoter never spends the whole pot on talent. `contingency_reserve_pct` is an optional fraction between 0.00 and 0.25 which defaults to 0.05 — five percent — and it is withheld from the tiers before any tier is funded. At registration the edition's `allocatable_budget_cents` is set to `talent_budget_cents` less the reserve, where the reserve is the `talent_budget_cents` multiplied by `contingency_reserve_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents, taken off the top exactly once. The new edition also starts with `committed_budget_cents` at 0, `tiers_allocated` false, `announcement_phase` at `PRESALE`, `lineup_posture` at `OPEN`, and `headliner_anchor_count` at 0.

The lineup is budgeted in tiers rather than act by act. Each tier row names its parent edition through a `festival_id`, a `tier_code` drawn from `HEADLINER`, `DIRECT_SUPPORT`, `MID_CARD` and `EMERGING`, a `tier_rank` between 1 and 4 where 1 is the highest precedence, an `allocation_pct` between 0.01 and 1.00, and a `max_slots` between 1 and 40 capping how many non-cancelled bookings the tier may carry. A `genre_focus` may be supplied from `ELECTRONIC`, `HIP_HOP`, `ROCK`, `POP`, `LATIN`, `JAZZ_SOUL`, `COUNTRY` and `MIXED`, defaulting to `MIXED`; it documents what the tier is curated around and is never used as a selector by any rule in this specification. Both `tier_code` and `tier_rank` must be unique within a festival, and a collision on either surfaces the same `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE`. A freshly added tier carries `allocation_cents` 0, `committed_cents` 0, `slots_committed` 0 and `status` `OPEN`.

Running the waterfall is what turns percentages into money, and it is a deliberate, repeatable act rather than a consequence of adding tiers. The buyer supplies `expected_festival_version` and the system proceeds as follows. First it collects every budget tier of the edition, regardless of `status`, skipping none, and orders them by `tier_rank` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — because `tier_rank` is unique per festival, that tie-break can never actually fire. If the set is empty, or if the sum of `allocation_pct` across the set differs from 1.00 by more than one billionth, the run is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_PCT_INVALID`. It also refuses when the edition's `committed_budget_cents` is anything other than 0, reporting `ERR_ALLOCATION_LOCKED`, since re-splitting a budget that already carries commitments would strand them. Then each tier's `allocation_cents` is set to the edition's `allocatable_budget_cents` multiplied by that tier's `allocation_pct` and **floored** to whole cents — floored per tier, never rounded half up. Flooring leaves a remainder: the difference between `allocatable_budget_cents` and the sum of the floored per-tier figures is added, whole and un-floored, to the single tier with the lowest `tier_rank`. It is never spread across tiers and never re-floored. Finally `tiers_allocated` becomes true and the edition's `version` advances. The response is the tier set in `tier_rank` order.

A tier's `status` is derived, not set. It reads `CLOSED` once its `slots_committed` has reached its `max_slots`, or once its `committed_cents` has reached or passed its `allocation_cents`; otherwise it reads `OPEN`. `committed_cents` is defined as the sum of `guarantee_cents` plus `production_fee_cents` over that tier's bookings whose `contract_status` is not `CANCELLED`, and `slots_committed` as the count of those same bookings.

Above the tiers, the edition carries a posture that gates whether the buyer may keep spending at all. `lineup_posture` is derived from the committed ratio — `committed_budget_cents` divided by `allocatable_budget_cents`, compared as an exact rational value and never rounded or stored. The posture reads `FROZEN` when the edition's `announcement_phase` has reached `GA_ONSALE`, or when the committed ratio has reached or passed 1.00, or when the edition has at least one budget tier and every one of them reads `CLOSED`. It reads `RESTRICTED` when the committed ratio has reached 0.85 but is still below 1.00 — so a ratio of exactly 0.85 is `RESTRICTED` and a ratio of exactly 1.00 is `FROZEN`. Otherwise it reads `OPEN`. A `FROZEN` edition rejects offer issuance, offer revision and offer acceptance alike with `ERR_LINEUP_FROZEN`. `RESTRICTED` permits all three: it is an advisory posture whose only real effect is to narrow the guarantee ceiling through the shrinking festival headroom. The posture is re-derived whenever `committed_budget_cents` moves — on acceptance, on cancellation, on a guarantee amendment — whenever a tier's `status` flips, and whenever the edition's phase advances.

`headliner_anchor_count` counts the edition's anchors: the number of its bookings drawn from the tier whose `tier_code` is `HEADLINER` whose `contract_status` is not `CANCELLED`. Accepting such an offer raises it and cancelling such a booking lowers it.

Ticket-sales phases move in one direction and one step at a time. Advancing the edition's phase requires a `target_phase`, and the phase ladder is `PRESALE` then `TIER1_ONSALE` then `TIER2_ONSALE` then `GA_ONSALE`. The request is refused with `ERR_PHASE_PREREQ_UNMET` unless `target_phase` is the immediate successor of the current `announcement_phase`, at least one announcement wave of this edition is tied to the phase being left, and every wave tied to that phase has reached `PUBLISHED`. Progression is thus gated on the announcements belonging to the phase being vacated, not on the phase being entered. Each advance re-derives `lineup_posture`, and reaching `GA_ONSALE` forces it to `FROZEN` — after which no further offer may be issued, revised or accepted for this edition.

### 3.2. The Stage Estate, Curfews and the Programming Day

Stages are the production manager's domain. A `ProductionManager` adds one to an edition with a `festival_id`, a `name` of 1 to 80 characters that must be unique within the edition — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_STAGE_NAME`, because the stage name is the human key production works from on the day sheet — and a standing-field `capacity` of at least 500. Three operational numbers come with it. `changeover_buffer_minutes` is the gap that must separate two set intervals on the stage; it defaults to 20 minutes and may be anywhere from 0 to 90. `daily_curfew_minute` is the municipal hard stop expressed as minutes elapsed from midnight UTC of the set's start date, so 1500 means one o'clock in the morning UTC on the following day; it must be between 600 and 1560. `daily_program_minutes_cap` bounds the total programmed time — set minutes plus changeover buffer — that may be scheduled on the stage on one UTC calendar date, and must be between 60 and 1200. An `is_flagship` flag marks the main stage and defaults to false. A new stage starts with `booked_minutes` at 0.

Three separate stage rules police an offer, and they fail in a fixed order. The **curfew** rule is evaluated on issuance and on revision: the set's end, measured as minutes from midnight UTC of the date on which the set *starts*, must be no later than the stage's `daily_curfew_minute`. A set that runs past midnight is measured as 1440 plus its minutes past midnight, which is why a curfew of 1500 permits a set ending at one in the morning. Equality with the curfew minute passes. Exceeding it is `ERR_CURFEW_EXCEEDED`.

The **interval collision** rule is evaluated on issuance, on revision and again at acceptance. The occupants of a stage are every other offer on that stage whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` — the offer under evaluation is excluded from its own occupant set — plus every booking on that stage whose `contract_status` is not `CANCELLED`. Each interval runs from `slot_start_utc` up to but not including `slot_end_utc`, and every interval is inflated symmetrically by the stage's `changeover_buffer_minutes`. A collision exists when, for some occupant, the evaluated set's start is strictly earlier than that occupant's end advanced by the buffer *and* that occupant's start is strictly earlier than the evaluated set's end advanced by the buffer. Both comparisons are strict. The practical consequence is worth stating: two sets that abut exactly, where one ends at the very instant the other begins, collide when the stage carries any changeover buffer at all and do not collide when the buffer is 0. A collision is `ERR_STAGE_SLOT_CONFLICT`.

The **day load** rule is evaluated on issuance and on revision, and it is an aggregate rather than a pairwise test — every individual set can fit and no pair can overlap while the day as a whole still overflows. Take the UTC calendar date on which the evaluated set starts. Sum, over every other offer on that stage in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` whose set starts on that date, the offer's `set_length_minutes` plus the stage's `changeover_buffer_minutes`; add the same quantity for every booking on that stage that is not `CANCELLED` and starts on that date; then add the evaluated offer's own `set_length_minutes` plus one changeover buffer, counted exactly once. If that total exceeds `daily_program_minutes_cap` the request is refused with `ERR_STAGE_DAY_CAP_EXCEEDED`; a total exactly equal to the cap is allowed.

Two further stage-shaped constraints govern billing. An offer whose `billing_position` is 1 — the top-billing ordinal — must target a stage whose `is_flagship` is true, whatever tier the guarantee is drawn from; a top-billed offer aimed at a side stage is refused with `ERR_HEADLINER_STAGE_REQUIRED` on issuance and on revision, and the rule is silent for every other ordinal. And a `billing_position`, an integer slot ordinal between 1 and 200 where 1 is the headliner, 2 the sub-headliner and so on, must be unheld on the target stage for the target UTC date: no other offer with `status` `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`, and no booking whose `contract_status` is not `CANCELLED`, may already hold that ordinal on that stage-day. This uniqueness is *not* enforced when the offer is first drafted; it is enforced at issuance, at revision and again at acceptance, and a clash is `ERR_BILLING_POSITION_TAKEN`. Stage reads are open to every role except the agent side, and a stage's `booked_minutes` is the ledger production checks when it wants to know how much of the estate is already sold.

### 3.3. The Roster, the Genre Balance and the Guarantee Ceiling

A roster act is created by a `TalentBuyer` with a `legal_name` and a `performing_name`, both 1 to 120 characters, a `primary_genre` drawn from `ELECTRONIC`, `HIP_HOP`, `ROCK`, `POP`, `LATIN`, `JAZZ_SOUL` and `COUNTRY`, the `agency` of record, and a `draw_score` between 0 and 100 representing the promoter's own estimate of the act's ticket-driving power. `performing_name` is globally unique across the roster — two rows for the same act would silently defeat radius enforcement, so a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ARTIST_NAME`. An optional `default_radius_miles` between 0 and 500, defaulting to 75, records the mileage the act's agency normally demands and is used as the radius for a new offer's clause when the offer does not name one explicitly. A new act starts with `reported_conflict_count`, `radius_breach_count`, `strict_risk_score` and `adjudicated_risk_score` all at 0.

`draw_score` is the input to the ceiling on what a buyer may promise. Before any offer is sent, the system computes the **guarantee ceiling** from three independent headrooms and stamps it on the offer. Step one is tier headroom: the tier's `allocation_cents` less its `committed_cents`. Step two is festival headroom: the edition's `allocatable_budget_cents` less its `committed_budget_cents`. Step three is the genre balance factor. Step four converts draw into money: the draw component is the greater of a floor of 500000 cents — five thousand dollars, the least the system will ever consider an act worth — and the act's `draw_score` multiplied by 25000 cents, meaning two hundred and fifty dollars of headroom for every point of draw. Step five scales that draw component by the genre balance factor and **floors** the product to whole cents; this is the only rounding step in the whole computation, applied once at the end rather than per input. Step six sets `guarantee_ceiling_cents` to the smallest of the tier headroom, the festival headroom and the scaled draw component. An offer whose `guarantee_cents` plus `production_fee_cents` exceeds that ceiling is refused with `ERR_GUARANTEE_CEILING_EXCEEDED`; equality with the ceiling passes.

The genre balance factor exists to stop one genre from eating the lineup. For a given genre, the committed share is a numerator over a denominator. The numerator adds the `guarantee_cents` of every booking of this edition whose act's `primary_genre` is that genre and whose `contract_status` is not `CANCELLED`, plus the `guarantee_cents` of every *other* offer of this edition whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` and whose act shares that genre. Offers in `DRAFT`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded — an accepted offer is already represented by its booking, and the rest are not live money. The denominator is the edition's `allocatable_budget_cents`, which is never zero. The share is an exact rational value, never rounded and never stored.

That share is consulted twice per issuance and twice per revision, with different membership each time, and conflating the two readings is the classic error. Computed **excluding** the offer under evaluation, the share picks the factor: `genre_balance_factor` is stamped at 1.00 when the share is at most 0.30, at 0.85 when the share is above 0.30 but at most 0.40, and at 0.60 when the share is above 0.40. Computed **including** the offer under evaluation, the share is tested against the hard genre cap of 0.40: a projected share of exactly 0.40 passes and one cent of numerator more fails, with the rejection reported as `ERR_GENRE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

An act's radius risk is scored from its competing-performance history and its draw. The count of still-unadjudicated conflicts, `reported_conflict_count`, is the number of that act's competing-performance rows whose `status` is `REPORTED` and whose `conflict_assessment` is `CONFLICT`. The count of adjudicated breaches, `radius_breach_count`, is the number whose `status` is `VIOLATION`. Rows that have been `CLEARED` or `WAIVED` feed neither count. The draw penalty is 0.20 multiplied by the difference between 100 and the act's `draw_score`, rounded half up to a whole number of points — so a low-draw act carries a structural penalty of up to twenty points on its own. `strict_risk_score` is the smaller of 100 and the sum of twelve points per unadjudicated conflict, twenty-five points per adjudicated breach, and the draw penalty. `adjudicated_risk_score` is the smaller of 100 and the sum of twenty-five points per adjudicated breach and the draw penalty — it ignores unadjudicated reports entirely, so an act with three open reports and no adjudicated violation is not blocked. Only the adjudicated variant gates business: an act whose `adjudicated_risk_score` has reached 60 cannot have an offer issued or revised, and the refusal is `ERR_ARTIST_RISK_BLOCKED`. Both scores are recomputed when a competing performance is reported, when one is adjudicated, and when a waiver is granted.

A computed risk read exposes the same arithmetic on demand. It accepts a `variant` of `STRICT`, which is the default, or `ADJUDICATED`; any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It returns the requested `variant`, the act's `reported_conflict_count` and `radius_breach_count`, the `draw_penalty` in points, the resulting `risk_score`, and a `blocked` flag reflecting whether that variant's score has reached the blocking threshold of 60.

### 3.4. Offer Negotiation: Drafting, Issuing, Countering and Expiry

An offer starts life as private paperwork. A `TalentBuyer` drafts one against a `festival_id`, an `artist_id`, a `budget_tier_id` and a `stage_id`, with `guarantee_cents` of at least 0, a `billing_position`, a `set_length_minutes` between 20 and 180, a `slot_start_utc`, and an `offer_expires_utc` after which the offer may be swept. An optional `production_fee_cents`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, records the promoter-borne production cost that will be charged against the tier alongside the guarantee. The same payload carries the terms of the radius clause the offer will impose — `radius_miles`, `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`, plus an optional `exclusivity_scope` and an optional `waiver_fee_offer_cents` — and, on a replacement offer only, a `replaces_booking_id`. The tier and the stage must both belong to the named edition; when they do not, the draft is refused with `ERR_FESTIVAL_MISMATCH` even though each row exists. The dates of both `slot_start_utc` and the derived slot end must lie inside the inclusive window from `event_start_date` to `event_end_date` — a set starting exactly on `event_end_date` is inside, and anything outside is `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_EVENT_WINDOW`.

Drafting stamps `slot_end_utc` as `slot_start_utc` advanced by `set_length_minutes`, sets `status` to `DRAFT` and `round_number` to 0, and creates exactly one radius clause carrying the offer's identifier and the act's `artist_id`, with `enforcement_status` `PROVISIONAL`, `waiver_status` `NONE`, no `booking_id` yet, `waiver_fee_cents` 0 and `breach_count` 0. A draft commits no budget and reserves no stage time. The clause created alongside the draft can be found through the clause list, filtered by `offer_id`.

Sending the offer to the agent is the heaviest gate in the system, and the order in which it fails is specified in §2.8. Issuance requires the offer to be in `DRAFT`, the edition's `tiers_allocated` to be true — issuing before the waterfall has run would compare a guarantee against a zero allocation, and the refusal is `ERR_TIERS_NOT_ALLOCATED` — the edition's `lineup_posture` to be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, the tier's `status` to be `OPEN` with `slots_committed` still below `max_slots`, the act's `adjudicated_risk_score` to be below 60, and the offer's `round_number` to be below the negotiation cap of three. It also re-checks that the set ends within the stage curfew, that no buffered interval collision exists, that the stage day load stays within the cap, that the `billing_position` is unheld on the stage-day, that a top-billed offer sits on a flagship stage, that the act holds no other non-cancelled booking at this edition starting on the same UTC date, that the projected genre share stays within the cap, and that `guarantee_cents` plus `production_fee_cents` stays within the freshly computed ceiling. The event-window check is deliberately not among them: it is enforced when the offer is drafted and again when it is revised, but not at issuance, because issuance cannot move the slot. When all of that passes, `status` moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`, `round_number` increases by one, `genre_balance_factor` and `guarantee_ceiling_cents` are stamped from the ceiling computation, `issued_by_user_id` records the acting buyer's `X-User-ID`, `slot_end_utc` is recomputed from the current `slot_start_utc` and `set_length_minutes`, and the offer's radius clause moves `PROVISIONAL → ACTIVE`. That last effect matters more than it looks: the clause begins policing competing performances from the moment of issuance, long before any booking exists.

An agent replies with a counter, which records an ask without moving the promoter's terms at all. Countering requires the offer to be `ISSUED`, requires the caller's `X-User-ID` to be the offer's `artist_id`, and requires the logical clock to be strictly before `offer_expires_utc`. The payload carries a `counter_guarantee_cents` and optionally a `counter_billing_position`, a `counter_set_length_minutes` and a free-text `counter_note`; each is recorded on the offer, and any of the three optional fields that the counter does not move stays null. `status` moves `ISSUED → COUNTERED`. Critically, `round_number` does not change — only promoter-side issuances consume rounds — and `guarantee_cents`, `billing_position`, `set_length_minutes` and the slot itself are untouched.

The promoter answers a counter by revising, which is the cycle edge: the offer returns from `COUNTERED` to `ISSUED` and one round is consumed. A revision supplies `guarantee_cents` and may also supply `production_fee_cents`, `billing_position`, `set_length_minutes`, `slot_start_utc` and `offer_expires_utc`; fields the revision omits keep their current values. `round_number` must still be below three before the revision runs, and the fourth promoter-side issuance is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` — the cap is what terminates the `ISSUED → COUNTERED → ISSUED` loop. Every gate from the issuance ladder is re-evaluated against the revised terms rather than the original ones: ceiling, genre factor and cap, interval collision, day load, curfew, event window, billing position and flagship requirement. On success `round_number` increases by one, `guarantee_ceiling_cents` and `genre_balance_factor` are recomputed and re-stamped, `counter_guarantee_cents`, `counter_billing_position`, `counter_set_length_minutes` and `counter_note` are all reset to null, and `issued_by_user_id` is overwritten with the reviser's `X-User-ID`.

An agent may walk away from either side of the loop. Declining is available from `ISSUED` and from `COUNTERED`, requires the caller to be the offer's act, accepts an optional `decline_reason`, and moves `status` to `DECLINED`. The offer's radius clause goes to `RELEASED` — from `PROVISIONAL` when the offer was never issued, from `ACTIVE` when it was — and no budget or stage ledger is touched, because none was ever committed.

Offers also go cold on their own. Expiry is clock-derived rather than stored: an offer still reads `ISSUED` until a sweep runs, which is why countering, revising and accepting each independently check the clock against `offer_expires_utc` and refuse with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` when the clock has reached it. A clock exactly equal to `offer_expires_utc` is expired, since those operations require the clock to be strictly earlier. The ops-run sweep is what actually rewrites state: every offer whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` and whose `offer_expires_utc` is at or before the logical clock becomes `EXPIRED`, and each such offer's clause becomes `RELEASED` so that conflict detection stops seeing it. A `DRAFT` offer is never swept, because no transition from `DRAFT` to `EXPIRED` exists. The sweep accepts an optional `festival_id` to narrow its scope and reports `expired_count`, `expired_offer_ids` ordered by `offer_expires_utc` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and `released_clause_count`. A second sweep with nothing newly expired reports `expired_count` 0 and is not an error.

The fourth way an offer dies is being overtaken. When a sibling acceptance closes the tier an offer draws from, every other offer of that same `budget_tier_id` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` and each superseded offer's clause is released. `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal: a further negotiation call against any of them is `ERR_OFFER_NOT_NEGOTIABLE`, which is also what a repeat call on an already-transitioned offer returns. Offers can be listed and filtered by `festival_id`, `artist_id`, `budget_tier_id`, `stage_id`, `replaces_booking_id` and `status`, and an agent listing offers sees only their own act's.

### 3.5. Acceptance, the Commitment Cascade and the Payment Schedule

Acceptance is the moment money becomes real, and it is the agent's call. It requires the offer to be `ISSUED`, the caller's `X-User-ID` to equal the offer's `artist_id`, the logical clock to be strictly before `offer_expires_utc`, the edition's `lineup_posture` to be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, and the tier's `status` to be `OPEN`. Three checks that already passed at issuance are re-run against the world as it now stands, because a sibling acceptance may have overtaken them: the tier's `committed_cents` plus this offer's `guarantee_cents` plus its `production_fee_cents` must still fit inside the tier's `allocation_cents`, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_TIER_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`; no buffered interval collision may have appeared on the stage; and the `billing_position` must still be unheld on that stage-day. The act must still hold no other live offer or non-cancelled booking at this edition starting on the same UTC date, or the answer is `ERR_ARTIST_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. And when the offer carries a `replaces_booking_id`, the booking it points at must be `CANCELLED`.

What follows is a four-hop cascade in which each hop is caused by the hop above it rather than by the acceptance call itself, and every hop must land inside the same request.

**Hop one** flips the offer's `status` to `ACCEPTED` and writes a booking. The new booking copies `festival_id`, `artist_id`, `budget_tier_id`, `stage_id`, `guarantee_cents`, `production_fee_cents`, `billing_position`, `set_length_minutes`, `slot_start_utc` and `slot_end_utc` from the offer, records the accepted offer in `offer_id` — which is unique across bookings, since one offer yields at most one booking — and carries the offer's `replaces_booking_id` forward, leaving it null on an original booking. It opens with `contract_status` `PENDING_RIDER` and `settlement_status` `UNFUNDED`, with `announcement_wave_id` and `reveal_order` both null, `rider_proposed_cents` and `rider_estimate_cents` and `settlement_total_cents` and `deposit_paid_cents` and `contractual_recovery_cents` and `actual_recovered_cents` all 0, and `cancelled_at`, `cancellation_reason` and `cancelling_party` all null. In the same hop the offer's radius clause has its `booking_id` stamped with the new booking's identifier, and its `enforcement_status` moves `PROVISIONAL → ACTIVE` if the clause was not already activated at issuance.

**Hop two** debits the ledgers. The tier's `committed_cents` increases by the guarantee plus the production fee and its `slots_committed` increases by one; the stage's `booked_minutes` increases by the booking's `set_length_minutes`.

**Hop three** re-derives everything that depended on those ledgers. The tier's `status` is recomputed and flips `OPEN → CLOSED` when `slots_committed` has reached `max_slots` or `committed_cents` has reached `allocation_cents`. The same debit re-derives the edition's `committed_budget_cents` and `headliner_anchor_count`, which in turn re-derive `lineup_posture` — so a single large acceptance can move an edition from `OPEN` straight to `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`.

**Hop four** clears the table. A tier that reached `CLOSED` in hop three drives every other offer of that same tier whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `COUNTERED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and each of those offers' clauses moves from `PROVISIONAL` or `ACTIVE` to `RELEASED`.

Alongside the cascade, acceptance builds the money schedule and computes the booking's opening settlement figure. The schedule is always exactly two rows. First the system takes the whole number of days from the logical clock's date to the edition's `event_start_date`; that count may be negative when a booking is written after the gates open. The deposit percentage is 0.50 when there are at least 120 days to go — exactly 120 days still earns 0.50 — 0.40 when there are at least 60 but fewer than 120, and 0.25 when there are fewer than 60. The deposit's `amount_cents` is the booking's `guarantee_cents` multiplied by that percentage and rounded half up once; the balance's `amount_cents` is the guarantee less the deposit, taken as an exact integer complement and never independently rounded, so the pair always sums back to the guarantee. The deposit's `due_date` is the later of today's date and the earlier of two candidates: today plus 30 days, and the edition's `event_start_date` less 14 days. The balance's `due_date` is the calendar date of the booking's `slot_start_utc`. The deposit row carries `installment_type` `DEPOSIT` and `sequence_index` 1; the balance row carries `installment_type` `BALANCE` and `sequence_index` 2. Both open with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `released_at` and `paid_at` null, and `paid_amount_cents` and `recovered_cents` at 0. No other booking's installments participate in this computation.

A booking's `contract_status` and its `settlement_status` are two independent dimensions of the same row. The contracting dimension walks `PENDING_RIDER`, `RIDER_SETTLED`, `COUNTERSIGNED` and terminates in `CLOSED_OUT` or `CANCELLED`; once it reaches `CLOSED_OUT` no further contract transition is accepted. The money dimension walks `UNFUNDED`, `DEPOSIT_PAID`, `BALANCE_DUE`, `SETTLED`, and has the additional states `WITHHELD`, `RECOVERY_PENDING` and `RECOVERED`. The money dimension has no terminal state of its own: `SETTLED` and `RECOVERED` are absorbing in practice, but the row remains writable through its contracting dimension. Neither dimension waits on the other, and a transition illegal on either dimension is `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. Bookings can be listed and filtered by `festival_id`, `artist_id`, `offer_id`, `budget_tier_id`, `stage_id`, `replaces_booking_id`, `announcement_wave_id`, `contract_status` and `settlement_status`, with agents again seeing only their own act's rows.

### 3.6. Radius Enforcement: Clauses, Detection, Breach and Waiver

A radius clause is the promise that the act will not play a competing show too close to the festival market, too near in time. It is created only as a by-product of drafting an offer and it carries the offer's terms: a `radius_miles` between 0 and 500, measuring great-circle mileage from the festival market; a closed restricted window from `window_start_date` to `window_end_date` inclusive, where both endpoint dates are themselves restricted; and an `exclusivity_scope` of `FULL`, `TICKETED_ONLY` or `FESTIVAL_ONLY`, defaulting to `FULL`, determining which competing performances the clause reaches. The window must be coherent and near enough to the event: `window_end_date` may not precede `window_start_date`, and neither endpoint may lie more than 365 days from the edition's `event_start_date`, or the offer draft is refused with `ERR_RADIUS_WINDOW_INVALID`. A clause is **polymorphic in its attachment**: while the offer is unaccepted it carries only an `offer_id` and a null `booking_id`, and once the offer is accepted the `booking_id` is stamped so that enforcement can reach the money.

The clause's own `enforcement_status` walks `PROVISIONAL` at creation, `ACTIVE` from the moment the offer is issued or accepted, `BREACHED` when an adjudicated violation lands against it, `WAIVED` when business affairs grants relief, and `RELEASED`, which is terminal. A clause is released when its offer reaches `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` — whether it was `PROVISIONAL` or already `ACTIVE` — and also when its booking is cancelled, in which case `ACTIVE`, `BREACHED` and `WAIVED` all collapse to `RELEASED`. A released clause stops participating in conflict detection entirely, and any further clause operation against it is `ERR_INVALID_CLAUSE_STATE`.

Detection runs the moment a competing show is reported. A `TalentBuyer` or `BusinessAffairs` files the report with the act's `artist_id`, a `venue_city` label of 1 to 80 characters, the venue's `venue_latitude` and `venue_longitude` in decimal degrees, the `performance_date`, and the flags `is_ticketed` and `is_festival`; an optional `announced_capacity` of at least 0 is recorded for information only and is never used by any rule. The report opens with `status` `REPORTED` and stamps `reported_by_user_id` with the reporter's `X-User-ID`.

At creation the system selects, from all clauses, the candidate set: clauses belonging to the same act, whose `enforcement_status` is `ACTIVE` — `PROVISIONAL`, `BREACHED`, `WAIVED` and `RELEASED` clauses never enter the candidate set at all — whose closed window contains the `performance_date` inclusive of both endpoints, whose scope reaches this performance, and whose mileage covers the venue. Scope reaches the performance when `exclusivity_scope` is `FULL`, or when it is `TICKETED_ONLY` and the report's `is_ticketed` is true, or when it is `FESTIVAL_ONLY` and the report's `is_festival` is true. Mileage covers the venue when the great-circle distance from the clause's festival market to the venue, rounded half up to two decimal places, is at or below the clause's `radius_miles` — the comparison is inclusive, so a venue at exactly the clause mileage is inside. That distance is the haversine great-circle distance on a sphere of radius 3958.7613 miles: taking the differences in latitude and in longitude between the market's `market_latitude` and `market_longitude` and the venue's `venue_latitude` and `venue_longitude`, all converted to radians, form the sum of the square of the sine of half the latitude difference and the product of the cosine of the market latitude, the cosine of the venue latitude and the square of the sine of half the longitude difference; the distance is twice the sphere radius multiplied by the arcsine of the square root of that sum.

If the candidate set is empty, the report's `conflict_assessment` is `NO_CONFLICT`, its `conflicting_radius_clause_id` is null and its `distance_to_market_miles` is null. Otherwise the candidates are ranked by `radius_miles` descending, then `window_start_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending, and the first is the winner: `conflict_assessment` becomes `CONFLICT`, `conflicting_radius_clause_id` names the winning clause, and `distance_to_market_miles` records the distance to that winner's market, rounded half up to two decimals. Exactly one clause is selected however many candidates qualify. A report that lands as a `CONFLICT` raises the act's `reported_conflict_count` and re-derives its `strict_risk_score`; the act's `adjudicated_risk_score` is deliberately untouched, so a fresh report alone can never block issuance. Reports can be listed and filtered by `artist_id`, `conflicting_radius_clause_id`, `status` and `conflict_assessment`.

Adjudication is `BusinessAffairs`'s call, takes a `finding` and an optional `finding_note`, and requires the report to still be `REPORTED` — a second adjudication is `ERR_PERFORMANCE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. The fork has two branches with deliberately disjoint consequences.

A `finding` of `CLEARED` is available whether the report's `conflict_assessment` is `CONFLICT` or `NO_CONFLICT`, because clearing is precisely how business affairs rules that a detected conflict is not a breach. It moves `status` `REPORTED → CLEARED`. Because the row leaves the reported set, the act's `reported_conflict_count` is re-derived and both `strict_risk_score` and `adjudicated_risk_score` are recomputed. Nothing else moves: no clause, no booking and no installment is touched by a clearing.

A `finding` of `VIOLATION` requires the report's `conflict_assessment` to be `CONFLICT` with a non-null `conflicting_radius_clause_id`; a violation finding on a report that detected nothing is refused with `ERR_ADJUDICATION_MISMATCH`. When it is accepted it fires a four-hop enforcement cascade. Hop one moves `status` `REPORTED → VIOLATION`, re-derives the act's `radius_breach_count` and `reported_conflict_count`, and recomputes both risk-score variants — which is how a single violation can push an act's `adjudicated_risk_score` to 60 and lock it out of further offers. Hop two drives the conflicting clause `ACTIVE → BREACHED` and increases that clause's `breach_count` by one. Hop three moves every payment installment of the clause's booking whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` to `HELD`. Hop four sets that booking's `settlement_status` to `WITHHELD` from whichever of `UNFUNDED`, `DEPOSIT_PAID` or `BALANCE_DUE` it was in. While a booking is `WITHHELD`, no money may leave: an attempt to release an installment is refused with `ERR_RADIUS_BREACH_ACTIVE`, and so is a countersignature attempt or an attempt to record payment.

The way out of a breach is a waiver, and the two sides of it are separate operations. The agent asks: a waiver request names a `competing_performance_id`, offers a `waiver_fee_offer_cents` of at least 0, and may carry a `request_note`. It moves the clause's `waiver_status` `NONE → REQUESTED` and records the named performance in `waiver_competing_performance_id`. The clause's `enforcement_status` is deliberately unchanged by the request — an `ACTIVE` clause stays `ACTIVE` and a `BREACHED` clause stays `BREACHED` while the ask is pending — and a second request against a clause already in `REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAUSE_STATE`.

Business affairs decides, supplying a `decision` together with an optional `waiver_fee_cents` and an optional `decision_note`. Either decision requires the clause's `enforcement_status` to be `BREACHED` and its `waiver_status` to be `REQUESTED`, and requires the performance the decision names to be the same row recorded as the clause's `waiver_competing_performance_id` and to be in `VIOLATION`; naming an unrelated report is `ERR_ADJUDICATION_MISMATCH`. A `GRANTED` decision additionally requires the `waiver_fee_cents` it prices to be at least the `waiver_fee_offer_cents` the agent offered — a granted waiver may not be cheaper than the agent's own ask, and undercutting it is `ERR_WAIVER_FEE_BELOW_OFFER`.

Granting fires the compensating cascade for the enforcement cascade, four hops deep. The clause's `waiver_status` becomes `GRANTED` and its `enforcement_status` moves `BREACHED → WAIVED`, with the decided fee stored in `waiver_fee_cents`. The named competing performance moves `VIOLATION → WAIVED`, which re-derives the act's `radius_breach_count` and recomputes both risk-score variants, so the act's `adjudicated_risk_score` falls. Every installment of the booking whose `status` is `HELD` returns to `SCHEDULED`. And the booking's `settlement_status` leaves `WITHHELD` — but the state it returns to is **re-derived from the installments, never remembered**: it becomes `SETTLED` when both installments are `PAID`, otherwise `BALANCE_DUE` when the balance installment is `RELEASED`, otherwise `DEPOSIT_PAID` when the deposit installment is `PAID`, and otherwise `UNFUNDED`. Finally the booking's `settlement_total_cents` is recomputed with the granted `waiver_fee_cents` now folded into it, which can move the promoter's share bracket.

Denying is the declared self-edge: the clause's `waiver_status` becomes `DENIED`, `waiver_fee_cents` stays 0, `enforcement_status` remains `BREACHED`, and consequently the money stays held. Clauses can be listed and filtered by `offer_id`, `booking_id`, `artist_id`, `waiver_competing_performance_id`, `enforcement_status` and `waiver_status`.

### 3.7. Riders, the Technical Advance and Countersignature

Once a booking exists, the agent files its rider line by line. Each line names a `category` of `TECHNICAL`, `HOSPITALITY` or `TRAVEL`, carries a `description` of 1 to 200 characters as written in the rider, a `quantity` between 1 and 500, and a `unit_cost_cents` of at least 0 representing the promoter's estimated cost per unit. An `is_promoter_provided` flag, defaulting to false, marks lines the promoter supplies in kind. A new line opens with `approval_status` `PROPOSED` and an immediately computed `line_cost_cents`, and filing it re-derives the booking's `rider_proposed_cents` while leaving its `rider_estimate_cents` alone, since a still-proposed line is outside contracted scope. Rider lines may only be added while the booking's `contract_status` is `PENDING_RIDER`; once it has left that state, filing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`.

Each line's own cost is computed the same way in both scopes and is rounded **per line, before any summation** — summing raw products and rounding the total once at the end is the wrong reading and yields a different figure. The effective unit price is the line's `substitute_unit_cost_cents` when its `approval_status` is `SUBSTITUTED` and its `unit_cost_cents` otherwise. Travel lines carry a twelve percent escalation to cover fare drift; lines in the other two categories carry none. `line_cost_cents` is then the `quantity` multiplied by the effective unit price multiplied by one plus the escalation, rounded half up to whole cents.

From those line costs the system maintains two different rider totals on the booking, and the difference between them is the whole point. `rider_proposed_cents` is the **proposed scope**: it sums `line_cost_cents` over every line whose `approval_status` is `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `SUBSTITUTED`, charges promoter-provided lines at their full cost, and applies no per-line cap. It is the figure production negotiates against. `rider_estimate_cents` is the **contracted scope**: it sums only lines whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED` or `SUBSTITUTED` — dropping both undecided and rejected lines — contributes 0 for any line whose `is_promoter_provided` is true, contributes the lesser of the line's `line_cost_cents` and 150000 cents (fifteen hundred dollars) for any `HOSPITALITY` line, and contributes the full `line_cost_cents` otherwise. Contracted scope is the only rider figure that ever enters a settlement. A computed rider read exposes both side by side; it accepts a `variant` of `PROPOSED_SCOPE`, `CONTRACTED_SCOPE` or `BOTH`, defaults to `BOTH`, and returns both totals regardless, as `proposed_scope_cents` and `contracted_scope_cents`, together with `variant_delta_cents` — the proposed total less the contracted total — and the counts `priced_line_count`, `capped_line_count` and `zeroed_line_count`.

A `ProductionManager` decides each line with a `decision` of `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `SUBSTITUTED`, an optional `decision_note`, and, for a substitution only, a `substitute_unit_cost_cents` which must be present and at least 0. The line must still be `PROPOSED` — a second decision is `ERR_RIDER_ITEM_ALREADY_DECIDED` — and the parent booking's `contract_status` must be `PENDING_RIDER` or `RIDER_SETTLED`. All three branches carry the same weight of consequence. An approval recomputes the line's `line_cost_cents` and both booking-level rider totals and the booking's `settlement_total_cents`. A rejection likewise recomputes `line_cost_cents` — a rejected line still carries its own line cost for the record, it simply leaves both variant input sets — and recomputes both totals and the settlement figure. A substitution records `substitute_unit_cost_cents`, re-prices `line_cost_cents` off the substitute rather than the original `unit_cost_cents`, and recomputes both totals and the settlement figure. In all three branches, when the decision leaves no line of that booking still `PROPOSED` and the booking has at least one rider line, the booking's `contract_status` moves `PENDING_RIDER → RIDER_SETTLED`. Rider lines can be listed per booking and filtered by `category` and `approval_status`.

The technical advance runs in parallel with the rider and has its own revision loop. Either an `ArtistRep` or a `ProductionManager` opens a packet against a `booking_id`, with a `stage_plot_ref` of 1 to 120 characters pointing at the stage plot document, an `input_channel_count` between 1 and 96, a `monitor_mix_count` between 0 and 24, a `travel_party_size` between 1 and 60, and an optional `load_in_offset_minutes` between 30 and 480 recording how many minutes before the set load-in must begin. A new packet opens with `status` `DRAFT`, `submission_count` 0 and `channel_overage` 0.

Submitting the packet is available from `DRAFT` and from `RETURNED`, requires the parent booking's `contract_status` to be `PENDING_RIDER` or `RIDER_SETTLED`, and requires `submission_count` to be below the cap of three — the fourth attempt is `ERR_ADVANCE_SUBMISSIONS_EXHAUSTED`, and that cap is what terminates the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` loop. Submission also faces an aggregate production constraint across every packet sharing a stage-day: sum the `input_channel_count` of every advance packet whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` and whose booking sits on this same stage and the same UTC slot date, add this packet's own `input_channel_count`, and the total may not exceed the console's hard maximum of 112 channels. Exceeding it is `ERR_STAGE_CHANNEL_CAP_EXCEEDED` — each packet fits the console on its own while the day's total does not. On success `status` moves to `SUBMITTED`, `submission_count` increases by one, `submitted_by_user_id` is overwritten with the submitter's `X-User-ID` on every submission, and `channel_overage` is re-stamped as the amount by which that same stage-day channel total including this packet exceeds the advisory comfort level of 96 channels, or 0 when it does not exceed it. The advisory figure and the hard maximum are separate gates: a packet can be accepted while carrying a positive `channel_overage`, because 96 is advice and 112 is law.

Production returns a packet for revision with a `return_reason`, which is recorded on the packet and moves `status` `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`. `submission_count` is not reset, which is what keeps the loop bounded. Production approves a packet from `SUBMITTED`, which requires the parent booking's `contract_status` to be `PENDING_RIDER` or `RIDER_SETTLED` and requires the load-in to fit inside the slot's own calendar day: the `slot_start_utc` moved back by `load_in_offset_minutes` may not fall before midnight UTC of the date on which the set starts. Approval writes `approved_by_user_id` and moves `status` to `APPROVED`, which is terminal. Approval deliberately leaves `channel_overage` exactly as the last submission stamped it and never recomputes it. Both returning and approving are subject to segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, and a match is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even for a caller who legitimately holds `ProductionManager`. Any other advance transition — submitting an `APPROVED` packet, approving a `DRAFT` one — is `ERR_INVALID_ADVANCE_STATE`. Packets can be listed per booking and filtered by `status`.

Countersignature is where the contract dimension is executed, and it is `BusinessAffairs`'s exclusive act. It requires the booking's `contract_status` to be `RIDER_SETTLED`, requires the booking's radius clause to be in `ACTIVE` or `WAIVED` — a `BREACHED` clause is refused with `ERR_RADIUS_BREACH_ACTIVE` — and requires the booking to have at least one advance packet in `APPROVED`, without which the answer is `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_APPROVED`. When it succeeds, `contract_status` moves `RIDER_SETTLED → COUNTERSIGNED` and `settlement_total_cents` is recomputed and re-stamped. Countersignature is the gate that unlocks the balance installment.

### 3.8. Money Movement: Release, Payment, Amendment and Settlement

A `FinanceController` owns every movement of cash. Releasing an installment requires its `status` to be `SCHEDULED`, requires the booking's `settlement_status` not to be `WITHHELD`, requires no clause of that booking to be in `BREACHED`, and — for a balance installment only — requires the booking's `contract_status` to be `COUNTERSIGNED`, so that no day-of-show money can move before the paper is executed. It also requires the release window to be open: the logical clock's date must have reached the installment's `due_date` less 7 days. Money cannot leave more than a week ahead of its due date, and the boundary date itself is releasable; earlier than that is `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_RELEASABLE`. On success `status` moves `SCHEDULED → RELEASED` and `released_at` records the logical clock. Releasing the balance installment additionally moves the booking's `settlement_status` `DEPOSIT_PAID → BALANCE_DUE`.

Recording the wire moves a `RELEASED` installment to `PAID`, with `paid_at` stamped from the logical clock and the supplied `paid_amount_cents` stored — it equals the installment's `amount_cents`. A deposit payment recomputes the booking's `deposit_paid_cents`, defined as the sum of `paid_amount_cents` over that booking's deposit installments whose `status` is `PAID`, `CLAWBACK_PENDING` or `RECOVERED`, and moves the booking's `settlement_status` `UNFUNDED → DEPOSIT_PAID`. A balance payment moves `settlement_status` `BALANCE_DUE → SETTLED`, and that in turn bubbles the contract dimension `COUNTERSIGNED → CLOSED_OUT` when the booking's `contract_status` is `COUNTERSIGNED` at that moment. Either payment recomputes `settlement_total_cents`. Any installment transition that is not declared — releasing a `PAID` row, paying a `SCHEDULED` one, touching a `VOIDED` or `RECOVERED` row — is `ERR_INVALID_INSTALLMENT_STATE`. Installments can be listed per booking and filtered by `status` and `installment_type`, and their `sequence_index` gives a stable order inside a booking.

The settlement figure is recomputed at every event that could move it — a rider decision, countersignature, a deposit payment, a balance payment, a granted waiver — and its evaluation order is **pinned**, because a naive reading of it is circular: the promoter's share depends on a gross that contains the rider figure, and the rider figure that lands in the total has already been scaled by that share. The order is: take the booking's `rider_estimate_cents`, which is the contracted-scope rider total; take the `waiver_fee_cents` of the booking's clause, or 0 when the booking has no clause row; form the pre-share gross as `guarantee_cents` plus that rider figure plus that waiver fee, using the **unadjusted** rider on purpose; select the promoter share from that pre-share gross — 0.60 when the gross has reached 5000000 cents (fifty thousand dollars), 0.75 when it has reached 2000000 cents (twenty thousand dollars) but is below the higher bracket, and 0.90 when it is below 2000000 cents; compute the reimbursed rider as the rider figure multiplied by that share, rounded half up once; and finally set `settlement_total_cents` to `guarantee_cents` plus the reimbursed rider plus the waiver fee, less `deposit_paid_cents`. Selecting the bracket from a share-adjusted rider — the circular reading — changes the answer whenever the gross sits near a bracket edge, and is wrong. A computed settlement read exposes every pinned step for audit: `guarantee_cents`, `rider_estimate_cents`, `waiver_fee_cents`, `gross_pre_share_cents` reported with the unadjusted rider so the bracket choice is checkable, `promoter_share_pct`, `reimbursed_rider_cents`, `deposit_paid_cents` and `settlement_total_cents`.

Guarantees do get renegotiated after execution, and amending one is a `TalentBuyer` act that ripples through every ledger at once. The amendment supplies a new `guarantee_cents`, the required `expected_booking_version`, and optionally a new `production_fee_cents` and an `amendment_reason`. The amended commercial terms are re-tested against the stamped ceiling, so an amendment that overshoots is `ERR_GUARANTEE_CEILING_EXCEEDED`, and against the tier's allocation, so one that overcommits the tier is `ERR_TIER_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`. The delta between the new guarantee-plus-production-fee and the old one is applied to the tier's `committed_cents` and to the edition's `committed_budget_cents`, and both the tier's `status` and the edition's `lineup_posture` are re-derived — which means an amendment can close a tier or freeze an edition just as an acceptance can. Every installment of the booking whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` is voided and replaced by a rebuilt scheduled row, using the same two-row deposit-and-balance construction; installments already `PAID` or `RELEASED` are never rewritten, and the rebuilt scheduled rows absorb the whole difference so that the sum of `amount_cents` over the booking's non-voided installments again equals its `guarantee_cents`. Finally `settlement_total_cents` is recomputed and the booking's `version` advances.

### 3.9. Announcement Sequencing, Cancellation and Recovery

Announcements are staged deliberately against ticket sales. A `TalentBuyer` plans a wave with a `festival_id`, a `wave_number` between 1 and 20 that must be unique within the edition — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAVE_NUMBER`, since wave numbers drive announcement ordering — a `phase` drawn from `PRESALE`, `TIER1_ONSALE`, `TIER2_ONSALE` and `GA_ONSALE` tying the wave to a ticket-sales phase, and a `scheduled_reveal_utc` giving the earliest instant the wave may go out. That instant must be strictly before midnight UTC of the edition's `event_start_date`: an announcement that lands after the gates open is not an announcement, and the refusal is `ERR_REVEAL_AFTER_EVENT_START`, checked both when the wave is planned and again when it is locked. A new wave opens with `status` `PLANNED`, `assignment_count` 0, `headliner_count` 0 and `published_at` null. Waves can be listed and filtered by `festival_id`, `phase` and `status`.

Bookings are attached to waves one at a time, under the wave's own concurrency guard. The assignment supplies a `booking_id` and the required `expected_announcement_wave_version`. The booking must belong to the same edition as the wave, or the answer is `ERR_FESTIVAL_MISMATCH`, and it must not already carry an `announcement_wave_id`, or the answer is `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` — a booking belongs to at most one wave, and re-assigning it requires the first wave to have fallen back to `PLANNED`. The wave's own `status` must admit an assignment; a `LOCKED` wave accepts no further members and a `PUBLISHED` wave is terminal, both reported as `ERR_INVALID_WAVE_STATE`. On success the booking's `announcement_wave_id` is set and its `reveal_order` becomes the wave's `assignment_count` after the increment, so the first booking assigned reveals first. The wave's `assignment_count` increases by one, its `headliner_count` increases by one when the booking's `billing_position` is 1, and its `version` advances. Both counters are aggregates: `assignment_count` is the count of bookings carrying this `announcement_wave_id` whose `contract_status` is not `CANCELLED`, and `headliner_count` is the count of those whose `billing_position` is 1.

Locking freezes the roster ahead of the reveal and is shared between `TalentBuyer` and `BusinessAffairs`. It requires the wave to be `PLANNED`, requires `assignment_count` to be at least 1 — a wave with nothing in it is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_WAVE` rather than locking vacuously, and a single assignment is enough — requires every booking assigned to the wave whose `contract_status` is not `CANCELLED` to have reached `COUNTERSIGNED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_WAVE_BOOKING_NOT_COUNTERSIGNED`, requires the wave's `phase` to equal the edition's current `announcement_phase`, and requires the reveal instant to still precede the event start. A wave planned for `TIER2_ONSALE` cannot lock while the edition is still in `PRESALE`, and that mismatch is `ERR_WAVE_PHASE_MISMATCH`. Once `LOCKED`, the wave's members can no longer be moved to another wave.

Publishing takes the wave `LOCKED → PUBLISHED` and is the buyer's act alone. It requires the logical clock to have reached `scheduled_reveal_utc` — publishing exactly at the reveal instant is allowed, and publishing early is `ERR_REVEAL_NOT_DUE` — and it re-checks phase parity with the edition. It stamps `published_at` from the logical clock. `PUBLISHED` is terminal, and publishing the last outstanding wave of the current phase is what unblocks the edition's phase advance.

Cancellation is the system's principal compensating operation, available to a `TalentBuyer` or `BusinessAffairs` from `PENDING_RIDER`, `RIDER_SETTLED` and `COUNTERSIGNED` alike, with identical consequences from each. It supplies a `cancelling_party` of `ARTIST`, `PROMOTER` or `FORCE_MAJEURE` and a free-text `cancellation_reason`, and it too runs four hops.

Hop one sets `contract_status` to `CANCELLED`, stamps `cancelled_at` from the logical clock, records `cancellation_reason` and `cancelling_party`, and computes the contractual recovery figure. Hop two unwinds everything the acceptance did: the tier's `committed_cents` is reduced by the booking's `guarantee_cents` plus `production_fee_cents` and its `slots_committed` by one, the stage's `booked_minutes` is reduced by the booking's `set_length_minutes`, every installment whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `HELD` or `RELEASED` becomes `VOIDED` — none of those ever moved cash — every installment whose `status` is `PAID` becomes `CLAWBACK_PENDING`, and the booking's radius clause is released. Hop three re-derives what those ledgers feed: the tier's `status` flips `CLOSED → OPEN` when its `slots_committed` has fallen below `max_slots` and its `committed_cents` below its `allocation_cents`, the edition's `committed_budget_cents` and `headliner_anchor_count` are credited back and `lineup_posture` is re-derived — so a `FROZEN` edition can revert to `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN`. Independently in the same hop, the installment flags decide the money dimension: `settlement_status` becomes `RECOVERY_PENDING` when at least one installment is now `CLAWBACK_PENDING`, and otherwise becomes `RECOVERED` with `actual_recovered_cents` at 0. Hop four removes the booking from its announcement wave, decrementing that wave's `assignment_count` and, for a top-billed booking, its `headliner_count`; a wave whose `assignment_count` reaches 0 while it is `LOCKED` reverts `LOCKED → PLANNED`, with its `headliner_count` re-derived to 0. That revert is the only edge back to `PLANNED`, and it is what makes a booking re-assignable to a different wave.

What the promoter may claw back is fixed at cancellation, and it is computed forward-looking in a single shot. Take the whole number of days from the date in `cancelled_at` to the edition's `event_start_date`. The party percentage is 1.00 when `cancelling_party` is `ARTIST`, 0.50 when it is `FORCE_MAJEURE`, and 0.00 when it is `PROMOTER` — a promoter who walks away recovers no deposit. The liquidated-damages percentage is 0.10 when the act cancelled with fewer than 30 days to go, 0.05 when the act cancelled with 30 or more days to go, and 0.00 in every other case. `contractual_recovery_cents` is then the booking's `deposit_paid_cents` multiplied by the party percentage and rounded half up, **plus separately** the booking's `guarantee_cents` multiplied by the liquidated-damages percentage and rounded half up. The two products are rounded independently and only then added; rounding their sum is the wrong reading. Because liquidated damages are computed off the whole guarantee — an amount no installment ever carried — the contractual figure can exceed everything the promoter actually paid out.

What was actually recovered is a separate, replayed figure. Each clawback is a `FinanceController` act against an installment in `CLAWBACK_PENDING`, supplying `recovered_cents`. The amount may not exceed that installment's own `paid_amount_cents`, and it may not push the booking's recovered total past `contractual_recovery_cents`; either overreach is `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_CONTRACTUAL`. On success the installment's `recovered_cents` is stored and its `status` moves `CLAWBACK_PENDING → RECOVERED`. The booking's `actual_recovered_cents` is then re-derived by replaying the booking's installments in `due_date` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, accumulating the `recovered_cents` of every row whose `status` is `RECOVERED`, and clamping the running total at `contractual_recovery_cents`. The two recovery figures therefore diverge exactly when liquidated damages are owed but have not been collected. When no installment of the booking remains `CLAWBACK_PENDING`, the booking's `settlement_status` moves `RECOVERY_PENDING → RECOVERED`.

Replacing a cancelled act closes the loop. A replacement offer is drafted with `replaces_booking_id` naming the booking it stands in for; that booking must belong to the same edition and must already be `CANCELLED`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_TARGET_NOT_CANCELLED`. The link is carried forward onto the replacement booking when the offer is accepted, giving an auditable replacement chain, and because the cancellation credited the stage back its minutes, a replacement of equal `set_length_minutes` leaves the stage's `booked_minutes` exactly where it started.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is rooted at the `/festival-ops` prefix. An endpoint that brings a new resource into existence answers with HTTP 201; every other successful call answers with HTTP 200, and returns the resource it acted on unless §3 describes a computed or aggregate response shape. Path parameters are always the `id` of the named resource. The Authorized Role(s) column lists the roles whose `X-User-Role` header value is accepted; any other role receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and an `ArtistRep` is additionally confined to their own act as described in §2.1.
