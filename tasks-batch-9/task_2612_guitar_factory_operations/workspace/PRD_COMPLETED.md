# Guitar Factory Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A guitar is a wooden object that has to be manufactured to instrument tolerances while its raw material is still alive to the weather. That single tension drives almost everything in this specification. A billet of Sitka spruce booked into the dry store at twelve percent moisture will move dimensionally for weeks; a nitrocellulose finish sprayed into a humid booth will still be soft a fortnight after the schedule says it should be buffed; and a fingerboard blank of Brazilian rosewood cannot legally leave the country at all unless a paper trail reaching back to the original import permit can be reconstructed on demand. The factory currently reconstructs that trail from spreadsheets, whiteboards and the memory of two long-serving compliance staff, and it has begun to lose orders because a promised date cannot be defended and an export file cannot be closed.

This document specifies the API for a system that carries the whole plant: tonewood procurement and protected-species documentation, moisture-controlled storage, computer-controlled routing and hand-shaping stations, humidity-controlled build rooms, finishing across two incompatible chemistries with their own cure clocks, fretwork and playability setup, custom-shop option configuration with artist-relations pricing, serial number minting, warranty registration through an authorized repair network, and the export documentation that lets a protected-species instrument cross a border. Every one of those areas has an existing manual control that the system must reproduce exactly rather than approximate — the inclusive six-to-eight-percent moisture band, the segregation of duties between the luthier who sets a guitar up and the inspector who signs it off, the rule that wildlife law is checked before money.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating before the detail begins. First, quantities are conserved: board feet, deposit cents and station minutes each live in a set of buckets whose sum is fixed by an earlier event, and every operation that moves wood, money or machine time moves it between buckets rather than creating or destroying it. Second, the order of rejection is part of the contract: when a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one it is has been decided deliberately in every case. An implementation that gets the arithmetic right but reports the wrong error for a doubly-invalid request has not implemented this specification.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Shop Floor Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API, with the single exception of the logical-clock endpoint described in §2.5, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, if `X-User-ID` is empty or consists only of whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the ten role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles that the target endpoint permits, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` with HTTP 403.

The ten roles are `PlantAdmin`, `WoodBuyer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Machinist`, `Luthier`, `QualityInspector`, `FinishTech`, `CustomShopCoordinator`, `ServiceNetworkManager` and `AuthorizedShop`. Their remits are deliberately narrow and deliberately non-overlapping at the points where the plant needs an independent check. A `PlantAdmin` owns the physical plant — rooms, stations and engineering reference data — and cannot buy wood, cannot touch an order's commercial dimension and cannot sign off playability. A `WoodBuyer` owns the tonewood lifecycle up to the point where wood becomes cuttable but has no authority over CITES paperwork. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns protected-species documentation end to end — permit attestation, lot rejection, compliance-hold clearance and the whole export file — and deliberately cannot buy or release wood. A `Machinist` saws billets, opens parts and runs the routers, but cannot glue up, inspect or price. A `Luthier` does the hand work — shaping, assembly, fretwork, setup, and the opening and measuring of inspection rounds — and crucially cannot sign an inspection off. A `QualityInspector` is the independent gate: it signs inspection rounds, accepts or holds components, condemns finishes and certifies recalibrated rooms, and never performs the work it judges. A `FinishTech` owns the booths and the cure clock but cannot condemn its own finish. A `CustomShopCoordinator` owns the commercial dimension — quoting, option configuration, deposits, serialization, cancellation — and performs no physical work at all. A `ServiceNetworkManager` runs the authorized repair network. An `AuthorizedShop` is an external repair shop acting on its own behalf.

Nine of the ten roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `WoodBuyer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Machinist`, `Luthier`, `QualityInspector`, `FinishTech`, `CustomShopCoordinator` or `ServiceNetworkManager`, the `X-User-ID` is an arbitrary staff identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `attested_by_user_id` on a tonewood lot, as `created_by_user_id` on an option selection, as `performed_by_user_id` and `inspector_user_id` on an inspection round, as `released_by_user_id` on an export shipment — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded values exist solely so that the segregation-of-duties comparisons in §2.11 can be made.

The tenth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `AuthorizedShop`, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `RepairShop` row, and ownership checks compare the resource's shop reference to `X-User-ID` directly. An `AuthorizedShop` caller may register a warranty naming itself, read its own shop row, and list or read only its own registrations. When it names some other shop — supplying a `registering_shop_id` that is not its own `X-User-ID`, or reading a shop row whose path identifier is not its own `X-User-ID`, or listing registrations without filtering to its own `registering_shop_id` — the request is refused with `ERR_SHOP_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403. Everything else on the factory floor is closed to it: storage rooms, tonewood lots, billets, component specs, components, stations, station reservations, the option catalog, build orders, option selections, finish runs, setup inspections and export shipments all return `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, as does the unfiltered repair-shop network list and any attempt by a shop to authorize itself.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the server's real wall clock rather than from the logical shop clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422 — the values are never silently clamped. A `limit` or `offset` supplied with an empty value is treated as absent and takes its default, while a value that is not a whole number — `abc`, `1.5` — is that same schema violation; surrounding whitespace and a leading `+` are tolerated. `offset` has no upper bound, so an `offset` past the end of the result set is a successful empty page rather than an error. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. The default ordering for a collection is `created_at` ascending. Several collections have a domain-meaningful ordering instead, and where they do, the ordering is fixed and forms part of the contract: storage rooms are ordered by `room_code` ascending; tonewood lots by `lot_code` ascending; billets by `billet_code` ascending; component specs by `spec_code` ascending; stations by `station_code` ascending; catalog options by `option_code` ascending; repair shops by `shop_code` ascending; station reservations by `starts_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; build orders by `priority_score` descending then `created_at` ascending; option selections by the referenced option's `option_code` ascending then `created_at` ascending — which is exactly the iteration order the quote calculation of §3.6 uses; finish runs by `booth_hold_from` ascending then `created_at` ascending; and setup inspection rounds by `round_number` ascending. Components, warranty registrations and export shipments use the default `created_at` ascending order. Code orderings compare the raw string values, and wherever an ordering key ties — two rows minted inside the same millisecond, or two orders sharing a `priority_score` and a `created_at` — the server falls back to the sequence in which the rows were inserted; a row's `id` is never consulted.

Each collection also accepts the filters named in its row of §4. Filters are conjunctive: supplying several narrows the result set to rows matching all of them. Reverse-reachability filters — `storage_room_id` on lots, `lot_id` and `parent_billet_id` on billets, `spec_id` and `billet_id` and `build_order_id` on components, `room_id` on stations, `station_id` and `build_order_id` and `component_id` on reservations, `build_order_id` and `option_id` on selections, `build_order_id` and `booth_id` on finish runs, `build_order_id` on inspections and on export shipments, `build_order_id` and `registering_shop_id` on warranty registrations — let a caller walk the wood graph backwards from any node. A filter whose value is empty is treated as absent; a filter value outside its declared enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); the two boolean filters — `is_custom_shop` on build orders and `cites_permit_required` on export shipments — accept `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and reject any other value with that same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and an identifier filter is matched as an opaque exact string, so naming a row that does not exist yields an empty page rather than a 404.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and Immutable Reference Data

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional digits, minted from the server's real wall clock rather than from the logical shop clock of §2.5. Client-supplied identifiers are never accepted as `id`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits that is refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation. This applies to storage rooms, tonewood lots, billets, components, stations, station reservations, build orders, option selections, finish runs, setup inspections, repair shops, warranty registrations and export shipments. When a cascade described later in this document mutates a row the caller did not name — a finish run suspended because its booth drifted, a billet quarantined because its lot was rejected — that row's `updated_at` and `version` move too, exactly as if it had been written directly.

Two resources are pure reference data and are treated differently. A `ComponentSpec` and an `OptionCatalog` row are published once and never modified: they carry `id` and `created_at` only. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations, there is no update endpoint for either, and neither has a state machine. Any downstream calculation that needs a stable price or a stable dimension therefore pins the value onto a mutable child row rather than relying on the reference row staying put — see `locked_upcharge_cents` in §3.6 and `moisture_content_pct` on a billet in §3.3.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and always before any foreign key in the body — but after the body's own schema rung, which is step 4 of the ladder in §2.6. For a nested path such as `/storage-rooms/{room_id}/climate-readings`, a body missing `rh_pct` is therefore reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when `{room_id}` names no room, while a well-formed body aimed at a nonexistent room is `ERR_STORAGE_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`; for `/build-orders/{build_order_id}/option-selections`, the order must exist before the body's `option_id` is looked up. Path existence is always checked before body existence, so a request carrying both a nonexistent path identifier and a nonexistent body foreign key returns the 404 belonging to the path entity, never the one belonging to the body reference.

### 2.5. The Logical Shop Clock

Time in this system is explicit rather than ambient. The server holds a logical clock, readable and writable through a test-driving endpoint that accepts a `current_utc` instant and sets the clock to it, returning HTTP 200 and a `data` object carrying the single field `current_utc` set to the new value — not a resource row. Before the first such call the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Every domain instant this API accepts or returns — the clock itself, `starts_at` and `ends_at`, `booth_hold_from` and `booth_hold_until`, `promised_date`, `out_of_spec_since`, `last_reading_at`, `cure_ready_at`, `coverage_ends_at` — is rendered to whole seconds with a trailing `Z` and carries no fractional digits, and fractional seconds supplied on input are accepted and then discarded rather than retained. Only the audit columns of §2.4 and `api_request_timestamp` carry three fractional digits. Every cure window, reservation window, quarantine timer, warranty coverage window and priority score is evaluated against this clock and not against wall-clock time.

The clock endpoint is the one place where the authentication requirement of §2.1 does not apply, and it is open to all ten roles — it inspects neither header, so a call carrying no identity headers at all, or one naming a role this document does not define, is accepted. Setting the clock is purely mechanical: it triggers no state machine, fires no cascade and recomputes no stored score. Scores and postures are refreshed lazily — a priority score moves when the order is written or when the work selector of §3.4 reads it, not because time passed.

### 2.6. The Validation Ladder

Requests are validated in a fixed order, and the **first** rule whose condition is violated short-circuits the request: its error is returned, no later rule is evaluated, and no state whatsoever is mutated. A request that violates five rules therefore produces exactly one predictable error. The ladder, from first-checked to last-checked, is as follows.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` present and one of the ten declared roles, `X-User-ID` present and non-empty — on every endpoint except the logical-clock endpoint. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The caller's role appears in the endpoint's permitted-role set, on every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Shop identity.** For the entity-backed `AuthorizedShop` role, `X-User-ID` must equal the repair shop named by the request — `registering_shop_id` when registering a warranty, the path shop identifier when reading a shop row, and the `registering_shop_id` filter when listing registrations. Violation: `ERR_SHOP_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (403). Reading a single registration is the one case that cannot be decided at this height, because the comparison is against the fetched row's own `registering_shop_id`: the row is resolved first, so a shop asking for a registration that does not exist receives `ERR_WARRANTY_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` rather than the identity error, while a shop asking for another shop's existing registration still receives `ERR_SHOP_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`.
4. **Schema.** Required fields present, types correct, enum values members of their declared sets, integers inside their declared bounds, `limit` between 1 and 100, `offset` at least 0, timestamps in RFC 3339 form with a trailing `Z`, decimal fields inside their declared scale. This step also carries the intra-body checks that need no database read: `board_feet` greater than zero, `coat_count` at least 1, `quantity` at least 1, a positive `duration_minutes`. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Four conventions fix what "present" and "well formed" mean at this rung: an absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so its required fields simply report as missing, and a body that parses to something other than a JSON object — or that is not JSON at all — is this same violation rather than a transport-level 400; a field present but explicitly `null` counts as absent, so an optional field sent as `null` takes its documented default while a required field sent as `null` is missing; body fields an endpoint does not declare are ignored; and a body foreign key that is not a non-empty string is caught here rather than at rung 7, so it reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and not a 404.
5. **Window ordering.** On a station-reservation booking, `starts_at` must be strictly earlier than `ends_at`, and any explicit booth window must have `booth_hold_from` strictly earlier than `booth_hold_until`. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). A window that fails this rung necessarily also fails rung 4's positive-`duration_minutes` check, and this rung wins: `starts_at` equal to or later than `ends_at` is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, so the positive-`duration_minutes` violation only ever surfaces on a correctly ordered window shorter than one whole minute.
6. **Path referential integrity.** Every entity identifier in the URL path resolves to an existing row. Violation: the 404 belonging to that entity — `ERR_STORAGE_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TONEWOOD_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILLET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPTION_CATALOG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUILD_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPTION_SELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINISH_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETUP_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPAIR_SHOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WARRANTY_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_EXPORT_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
7. **Body referential integrity.** Every foreign key in the request body resolves to an existing row — `lot_id` and `parent_billet_id` when sawing a billet; `spec_id`, `billet_id` and `build_order_id` when opening a component; `room_id` when commissioning a station; `station_id`, `build_order_id` and `component_id` when booking a reservation; `option_id` when configuring an option; `build_order_id` and `booth_id` when booking a finish run; `build_order_id` and `registering_shop_id` when registering a warranty; `build_order_id` when opening an export shipment; `storage_room_id` when receiving a tonewood lot. Violation: the same family of 404 codes.
8. **Natural-key uniqueness.** `room_code`, `lot_code`, `billet_code`, `spec_code`, `station_code`, `option_code`, `order_code` and `shop_code` are each globally unique across their own resource, and each is a string of 1 to 64 characters — `room_code` alone is capped at 32, and the `model_code` of §3.6, which is not a natural key, at 16 — a length the schema rung above enforces; a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409). At most one non-terminal export shipment may exist for a build order at a time, also `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`. At most one `ACTIVE` warranty registration may exist per build order, and because that conflict is on a relationship rather than on a code it carries its own token, `ERR_WARRANTY_ALREADY_REGISTERED` (409).
9. **State.** The target entity's state field is among the from-states the requested transition permits. Violation: the state error belonging to that dimension — `ERR_ROOM_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BILLET_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_COMPONENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`, `ERR_COMMERCIAL_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_OPTION_SELECTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_FINISH_RUN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SHOP_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_WARRANTY_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`, all at HTTP 409.
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** The stale-version checks of §2.7. Violation: `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
11. **Climate posture.** The governing room must be `IN_SPEC`. Violation: `ERR_ROOM_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_SPEC` (409), or `ERR_ROOM_QUARANTINED` (409) when the room's posture is the stricter `QUARANTINED`.
12. **Protected-species compliance.** No operation that moves an instrument or its wood toward export may proceed while a contributing lot's permit is unverified, and no order parked on a compliance hold may advance. Violations: `ERR_CITES_PERMIT_UNVERIFIED`, `ERR_CITES_PERMIT_MISSING`, `ERR_PROTECTED_SPECIES_QUARANTINED` and `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_ACTIVE`, all at HTTP 409.
13. **Segregation of duties.** The §2.11 comparisons. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
14. **Referential compatibility.** The two entities being joined must be compatible: `ERR_GRADE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422), `ERR_SPECIES_NOT_PERMITTED` (422), `ERR_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422), `ERR_RESERVATION_COMPONENT_MISMATCH` (422), `ERR_OPTION_FINISH_MISMATCH` (422), `ERR_OPTION_INCOMPATIBLE` (409) and `ERR_BOOTH_ROOM_TYPE_INVALID` (422). This step guards component creation, reservation booking, routing, hand shaping, option configuration and finish-run booking.
15. **Cross-entity state dependency.** The other entity the operation leans on must itself be in an acceptable state: `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SIGNED`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`, `ERR_COMPONENTS_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_SHOP_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID` and `ERR_OPTIONS_NOT_SELECTED`, all at HTTP 409.
16. **Interval conflict.** The two interval semantics of §2.9. Violation on a station booking: `ERR_STATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). A booth overlap is not itself an error at this step; it feeds the two booth checks below.
17. **Booth chemistry.** Every non-terminal finish run whose booth hold touches the new run's hold must use the same finish system. Violation: `ERR_BOOTH_FINISH_INCOMPATIBLE` (409).
18. **Booth capacity.** The count of overlapping non-terminal booth holds, plus the new one, must not exceed the booth's `capacity_slots`. Violation: `ERR_BOOTH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
19. **Physical bands.** A lot's `moisture_content_pct` must sit inside the inclusive band before it may be conditioned or released — violation `ERR_MOISTURE_OUT_OF_BAND` (409) — and a component accepted at QC must sit inside its spec's thickness tolerance, violation `ERR_THICKNESS_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (409).
20. **Board-feet availability.** A billet's own `board_feet` plus its computed kerf loss must not exceed the lot's `board_feet_available`. Violation: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BOARD_FEET` (409).
21. **Rework ceiling.** An order's `rework_round_count` must be below the three-round ceiling before a further inspection round may be opened. Violation: `ERR_MAX_REWORK_EXCEEDED` (409).
22. **Fiscal thresholds.** The deposit minimums of §3.6 and §3.9. Violation: `ERR_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` (402). Money is the last thing this system checks.
23. **Single-assignment guards.** An order that already carries a `serial_number` cannot be serialized again — `ERR_SERIAL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (409) — and the work selector with an empty eligible pool returns `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BUILD_ORDER` (409) rather than an empty payload.

Four rungs of this ladder are close enough together that their relative order is worth stating twice, because each is a request that plausibly violates both rules at once.

- **State outranks climate.** A finish-run booking whose order is in the wrong build stage *and* whose booth is `OUT_OF_SPEC` returns `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`, never `ERR_ROOM_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_SPEC`.
- **Compliance outranks money.** An export release whose contributing lot still has an unverified permit *and* whose applied deposit falls short of the settlement threshold returns `ERR_CITES_PERMIT_UNVERIFIED`, never `ERR_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT`. Wildlife law outranks money.
- **Window conflict outranks capacity.** A station booking is tested for overlap only: a station carries no minutes-per-day or slot ceiling of its own, so `ERR_STATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` is the whole of the station-side capacity story. The only capacity ceiling in the system is a booth's `capacity_slots`, and it is weighed after the booth-chemistry rung above.
- **Compliance outranks compatibility.** Opening a component whose billet comes from a rejected, quarantined protected-species lot *and* whose grade is below the spec minimum returns `ERR_PROTECTED_SPECIES_QUARANTINED`, not `ERR_GRADE_BELOW_MINIMUM`.

Segregation of duties is deliberately checked *after* compliance, so an out-of-compliance shipment fails for the compliance reason even when the officer releasing it is the same officer who attested the permit.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Child Creation

Three create operations attach a child row to a parent whose state the client must have read first. Each of them requires the client to echo back the `version` it last observed on that parent, and each rejects the write when the echoed value does not match the parent's current `version`. The rejection carries `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412 in all three cases. These fields are not optional and there is no default: a create that omits them is a schema violation. Any integer is accepted at the schema rung, however — an echoed version carries no positivity bound — so a zero or a negative value is not a schema violation but simply a value the parent's `version` cannot equal, and it is reported as `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

- Booking a finish run requires `expected_build_order_version` to equal the current `version` of the build order named by `build_order_id`. A replacement finish run legitimately supersedes a condemned one under the refinish loop of §3.7, so a second booking against the same order is a normal product operation rather than a state violation; the stale-version guard is what stops a technician from committing a booth window on the strength of an obsolete view of the order's finish system, stage and priority.
- Configuring an option on an order requires `expected_build_order_version` to equal that order's current `version`. Several selections on one order are entirely legitimate, so nothing in the order's state machine refuses the second one; the guard is what stops a coordinator from stacking an option on top of a provisional quote it never saw, which would silently move the deposit threshold that confirmation later enforces.
- Sawing a billet requires `expected_tonewood_lot_version` to equal the current `version` of the lot named by `lot_id`. Cutting several billets from one released lot is ordinary practice, so the lot's state machine does not refuse the second cut; the guard is what stops a machinist from sawing against an obsolete `board_feet_available` figure and overdrawing the lot.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular the many action endpoints that advance a state machine — routing, hand shaping, QC, dispositioning, confirming, locking, assembling, spraying, buffing, signing, serializing, attaching documents, releasing — carry no expected-version input at all, and supplying one does not make them concurrency-guarded.

### 2.8. Money, Board Feet, Hours and Rounding

All money in this system is expressed in integer cents. All multipliers, discounts and percentage-like rates are expressed in integer basis points, where one basis point is one ten-thousandth: a rate of 1200 basis points means twelve percent, and applying it means multiplying by 1200 and dividing by 10000. No calculation in this specification requires binary floating point; every intermediate is an integer numerator over an integer denominator, and rounding is applied once at each point the calculation names. On the wire, money and basis-point fields appear as JSON integers and the decimal-valued fields — board feet, moisture percentages, humidity and temperature, thickness and its tolerance, the two scores — appear as plain JSON numbers rather than quoted strings, so a two-decimal figure whose hundredths are zero may serialise as `55.0`; the rounding is what the contract fixes, not the count of characters printed.

Rounding is always **half up**, meaning round to the nearest representable value with ties resolved away from zero. Money rounds half up to whole cents. Board feet round half up to two decimal places. The playability deviation score of §3.8 and the build priority score of §3.4 round half up to two decimal places. Cure durations are whole hours throughout. Station durations are whole minutes throughout. Where a calculation rounds more than once — the quote total of §3.6 rounds each option line and then rounds the surcharge and the discount separately, and the export declared value of §3.9 rounds the documentation fee and then the insurance — the roundings are performed at exactly the named steps and never collapsed into a single rounding at the end. That distinction changes answers by a cent, and a cent is a test failure.

### 2.9. Two Interval Semantics: Station Windows and Booth Holds

The plant books two kinds of time, and they do not share a definition of overlap. Unifying them is the single most common way to get scheduling wrong.

A **station window** is half-open: `starts_at` is inclusive and `ends_at` is exclusive. It is also widened symmetrically on both sides by the station's `changeover_minutes`, because a router has to be re-tooled between jobs. A proposed window conflicts with an existing reservation on the same station whose status is `HELD` or `CONSUMED` when the proposed `starts_at` falls strictly before that reservation's `ends_at` advanced by the changeover minutes, and at the same time the proposed `ends_at` advanced by the changeover minutes falls strictly after that reservation's `starts_at`. A window that begins exactly at an existing reservation's `ends_at` plus the changeover minutes does **not** conflict and is accepted. Released reservations never conflict with anything.

A **booth hold** is closed at both ends: both `booth_hold_from` and `booth_hold_until` are inclusive, and no buffer is added on either side. A proposed hold conflicts with an existing non-terminal finish run in the same booth when the proposed `booth_hold_from` is at or before that run's `booth_hold_until` and the proposed `booth_hold_until` is at or after that run's `booth_hold_from`. A hold that begins exactly at an existing hold's `booth_hold_until` therefore **does** collide — the opposite of the station rule.

Changeover minutes widen the conflict window only. They are never added to a reservation's `duration_minutes` and are never billed as machine time.

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants: Wood, Money and Machine Time

Three quantities in this system are conserved, and each identity must hold after every single write with no tolerance whatsoever.

**Wood.** A lot's `board_feet_received` always equals the sum of `board_feet_available`, `board_feet_unassigned`, `board_feet_reserved`, `board_feet_consumed`, `board_feet_scrapped` and `board_feet_kerf_loss`. Each bucket has one meaning and only one: `board_feet_available` is lumber still standing uncut in the lot; `board_feet_unassigned` is wood already sawn into billets that sit in `AVAILABLE` status, cut but not yet promised to any part; `board_feet_reserved` is billets in `RESERVED` status, promised to a part that is not yet installed; `board_feet_consumed` is billets in `CONSUMED` status, whose part went into an instrument; `board_feet_scrapped` is billets in `SCRAPPED` or `QUARANTINED` status, wood destroyed or impounded; and `board_feet_kerf_loss` is sawdust, the running sum of every billet's `kerf_loss_board_feet`. A seventh figure, `board_feet_cut`, is a convenience mirror equal to unassigned plus reserved plus consumed plus scrapped — it is reported but is **not** an independent bucket and must never be added into the identity a second time.

**Money.** A build order's `deposit_paid_cents` always equals the sum of `deposit_applied_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents`, `deposit_forfeited_cents` and `deposit_held_cents`. Cash enters only at confirmation and leaves the held bucket only at serialization or cancellation.

**Machine time.** A station's `total_scheduled_minutes` always equals the sum of `held_minutes`, `consumed_minutes` and `released_minutes`. `total_scheduled_minutes` is monotonically non-decreasing — it records every minute ever booked on the station and is never reduced.

Because each identity is exact, every operation described in §3 that touches wood, money or machine time is described as a movement between buckets. Some movements are forward and some are compensating, and two of the compensating ones are deliberately asymmetric in a way that is easy to get wrong: cancelling an order **recovers** wood, returning a reserved billet to `AVAILABLE` so its board feet go back from the reserved bucket into the unassigned bucket, whereas scrapping a part at QC **destroys** wood, moving the same board feet from the reserved bucket into the scrapped bucket permanently. Confusing those two is the fastest route to breaking the wood identity.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Independent Sign-Off

Three review steps in this factory must be performed by a different person than the step being reviewed, and in all three the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

Signing a playability inspection round requires the signing inspector's `X-User-ID` to differ from the `performed_by_user_id` recorded when the round was opened. Releasing an export shipment requires the releasing officer's `X-User-ID` to differ from the `attested_by_user_id` of every contributing tonewood lot — one match anywhere in the contributing set is enough to refuse the release. Approving an option selection requires the approving coordinator's `X-User-ID` to differ from the `created_by_user_id` stamped on the selection when it was configured.

A related but distinct rule guards ownership rather than independence: only the luthier who opened an inspection round may submit that round's measurements, and a different luthier attempting it is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_OWNER_MISMATCH` (403). That is an owner check, not a segregation check, and the two codes are never interchangeable.

### 2.12. Cascades and Hierarchical Consistency

Several operations in this factory reach far beyond the row the caller named. Two of them reach four entities deep, and both are described fully in §3, but the general principle belongs here: a cascade is atomic, it is ordered, and it never errors merely because a downstream set turned out to be empty.

Atomic means that if any link of a cascade would violate a rule, the whole request is rejected and nothing is written. Ordered means that each link fires because the previous link fired, so the reader can trace the chain: a humidity excursion suspends curing finish runs, the suspended runs park their orders, and the parked orders give back their future station bookings. Empty-tolerant means that a rejection of a protected-species lot that has not yet been sawn into anything is a perfectly successful request whose second, third and fourth links resolve over empty sets and write nothing — an aggregate over an empty collection yields its identity value, never an error and never null.

Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, which is precisely why the stale-version guards of §2.7 exist: a client that read a build order before a humidity excursion holds a version that the cascade has already invalidated.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Climate-Controlled Rooms and the Humidity Cascade

Everything physical in this factory happens inside a room, and the room's climate decides whether the work is allowed to happen at all. A `PlantAdmin` registers each climate-controlled space with a `room_code` that is unique across the plant and between 1 and 32 characters long, a `room_type` drawn from `DRY_STORE`, `KILN`, `BUILD_ROOM` and `FINISH_BOOTH`, a `target_rh_pct` between 20.0 and 80.0 inclusive, a `target_temp_c` between 10.0 and 35.0 inclusive, and a `capacity_slots` count of at least one. The optional `rh_tolerance_pct` sets how far the room may drift from its target before it is considered out of specification; it defaults to 3.0 and must lie between 0.5 and 15.0 inclusive. `capacity_slots` is only meaningful for a room whose `room_type` is `FINISH_BOOTH`, where it caps how many simultaneous finish-run occupancies of the same chemistry the booth can hold. A newly registered room starts with `climate_status` of `IN_SPEC`, `occupied_slots` of zero, `out_of_spec_since` null, and `latest_rh_pct` and `latest_temp_c` reporting the room's own `target_rh_pct` and `target_temp_c` until the first hygrometer sample is logged.

A `PlantAdmin` or a `FinishTech` logs a hygrometer sample as a pair of readings, `rh_pct` and `temp_c`. Both are required numbers and neither is bounded — an instrument may report any value at all, however implausible, and no range check is applied to a sample. Every sample overwrites `latest_rh_pct` and `latest_temp_c` and stamps `last_reading_at`, which is null until the first sample arrives, with the current logical clock instant, and the humidity figure alone drives the room's posture: `temp_c` is recorded for the cure arithmetic of §3.7 but never changes `climate_status`. From `IN_SPEC`, a sample whose absolute difference from `target_rh_pct` exceeds `rh_tolerance_pct` moves the room `IN_SPEC → OUT_OF_SPEC` and stamps `out_of_spec_since` with the current logical clock instant. From `OUT_OF_SPEC`, a sample whose absolute difference from `target_rh_pct` is less than or equal to `rh_tolerance_pct` returns the room `OUT_OF_SPEC → IN_SPEC` and clears `out_of_spec_since` to null — the boundary is inclusive, so a reading exactly the tolerance away from target counts as back in specification. From `OUT_OF_SPEC`, a sample that is *still* outside the band, taken when a full day or more has elapsed since `out_of_spec_since`, escalates the room `OUT_OF_SPEC → QUARANTINED`; the quarantine timer is twenty-four hours measured on the logical clock, and the comparison is inclusive at exactly twenty-four hours. That escalation carries `out_of_spec_since` over untouched — it goes on reporting the instant the excursion began, and only the certification described below clears it. Samples logged while the room is `QUARANTINED` still refresh `latest_rh_pct` and `latest_temp_c` but never change `climate_status`.

Quarantining a room voids conditioning. Every tonewood lot stored in that room whose status is `CONDITIONED` falls back `CONDITIONED → RECEIVED`, because acclimatisation achieved in a room that has since been condemned cannot be trusted; the lot must be re-measured and re-conditioned from scratch. Escaping quarantine requires a deliberate human act rather than a passing good reading: a `PlantAdmin` or a `QualityInspector` certifies the recalibrated room, which moves it `QUARANTINED → IN_SPEC` and clears `out_of_spec_since`. Certification is only accepted while the room is actually `QUARANTINED` — otherwise `ERR_ROOM_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the room's own `latest_rh_pct` sits within `rh_tolerance_pct` of `target_rh_pct`, otherwise `ERR_ROOM_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_SPEC`. The full cycle is therefore `IN_SPEC → OUT_OF_SPEC → QUARANTINED → IN_SPEC`.

Consider a booth that drifts humid while three guitars are curing in it. The `IN_SPEC → OUT_OF_SPEC` transition is the root of a four-deep cascade that runs in a single request. First, `latest_rh_pct` and `latest_temp_c` take the new sample and `out_of_spec_since` is stamped. Second, because the room left `IN_SPEC`, every finish run occupying that booth whose status is `CURING` moves `CURING → CURE_SUSPENDED` and has `cure_suspend_started_at` set to the current logical clock instant. Third, because those runs suspended, each of their build orders sitting at build stage `IN_FINISH` moves `IN_FINISH → FINISH_HELD`. Fourth, because those orders were held, every station reservation belonging to them whose status is `HELD` and whose `starts_at` is strictly in the future moves `HELD → RELEASED`, and each affected station has that reservation's `duration_minutes` subtracted from `held_minutes` and added to `released_minutes`. Reservations already under way or already begun are left alone.

Recovery is deliberately not automatic. When the booth comes back to `IN_SPEC`, the suspended runs stay suspended until a `FinishTech` resumes each of them explicitly, and the station reservations released by the fourth cascade link are **not** restored — the shop must re-book them. That asymmetry is intentional: giving a window back is cheap, and silently reinstating a booking whose slot has since been taken by someone else is not.

Climate posture then gates work across the plant. Routing a part, hand-shaping a part, booking a finish run, closing a spray, resuming a cure, moving a body to the buffing wheel and declaring a lot conditioned all require the governing room to be `IN_SPEC`, and each is refused with `ERR_ROOM_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_SPEC` when it is not. `QUARANTINED` is the stricter posture: it produces `ERR_ROOM_QUARANTINED` instead, and it additionally blocks commissioning a new station in that room and booking any reservation on a station that sits in it. Receiving a tonewood lot into a `QUARANTINED` room is likewise refused. For a machining operation the governing room is the room containing the station named by the cited reservation; for a finish operation it is the booth; for conditioning it is the room the lot is stored in.

One reported figure on a room deserves its own sentence because it is easy to misread as a constraint. `occupied_slots` counts the finish runs in that booth whose status is one of `SPRAYING`, `CURING`, `CURE_SUSPENDED` or `BUFFING` **and** whose closed booth-hold window contains the current logical clock instant. It is a read-only mirror evaluated at the present moment, not the check that admits a new booking: a non-terminal run whose projected hold has already elapsed occupies nothing and blocks nothing.

### 3.2. Receiving Tonewood, Protected-Species Paperwork and Conditioning

A `WoodBuyer` books a delivery in as a tonewood lot, supplying a `lot_code` unique across all lots, a `species_code` drawn from `SITKA_SPRUCE`, `HONDURAN_MAHOGANY`, `HARD_MAPLE`, `EAST_INDIAN_ROSEWOOD`, `BRAZILIAN_ROSEWOOD` and `EBONY`, the `cites_appendix` under which the species is declared at receipt — `NONE`, `APPENDIX_II` or `APPENDIX_I` — a positive `board_feet_received` figure carried to two decimal places, an initial `moisture_content_pct` between 2.0 and 30.0 inclusive, and the `storage_room_id` of the room the wood physically goes into. That room must have a `room_type` of either `DRY_STORE` or `KILN` — naming a room of any other type is a schema violation, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422), and it is caught before the `lot_code` uniqueness check — and as noted in §3.1 it must not be `QUARANTINED`, a refusal that comes after that uniqueness check. A `permit_number` of between 1 and 40 characters may be supplied at receipt but is not required there.

Receipt forks on the declared appendix, and the two branches differ in more than their landing state. A lot declared `NONE` is created directly in status `RECEIVED` with `permit_status` set to `NOT_REQUIRED`. A lot declared `APPENDIX_II` or `APPENDIX_I` is created in status `PERMIT_HOLD` with `permit_status` set to `PENDING` — protected species are frozen before any measurement, conditioning or cutting can touch them. Both branches then do the same three things: they stamp `moisture_at_receipt_pct` from the `moisture_content_pct` supplied at creation and never move that stamped figure again; they initialise the wood ledger with `board_feet_cut` and `board_feet_kerf_loss` at zero, `board_feet_available` equal to `board_feet_received`, and `board_feet_unassigned`, `board_feet_reserved`, `board_feet_consumed` and `board_feet_scrapped` all at zero; and they compute both yield projections described below. A `permit_number` supplied at receipt is stored verbatim on either branch and is null when omitted, and it verifies nothing by itself: an `APPENDIX_I` lot that arrives with a number still opens on `PERMIT_HOLD` with `permit_status` of `PENDING`. Both yield figures and the `board_feet_cut` mirror are derived on every read of the lot rather than kept as stored columns, which is why no endpoint has to remember to refresh them.

A lot on permit hold is freed only by a `ComplianceOfficer` attesting the permit, which requires a non-empty `permit_number` in the request. Attestation moves the lot `PERMIT_HOLD → RECEIVED`, writes the supplied `permit_number` onto the lot, sets `permit_status` to `VERIFIED`, and — importantly for §3.9 — records the attesting officer's `X-User-ID` in `attested_by_user_id`. Attestation from any state other than `PERMIT_HOLD` is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`.

The same officer may also reject a lot outright on documentation grounds, optionally supplying a `reason`; when present the reason must be a non-empty string, and it is validated and then discarded rather than written onto the lot. Rejection is legal from every non-terminal lot state — `RECEIVED`, `PERMIT_HOLD`, `KILN_DRYING`, `CONDITIONED` and `RELEASED` — and is refused with `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID` only when the lot is already `DEPLETED` or `REJECTED`. It always sets `permit_status` to `INVALID` and moves the lot to `REJECTED`, and it is the root of the second four-deep cascade in this system. Because the lot became `REJECTED`, every billet cut from it whose status is `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED` moves to `QUARANTINED`, with the lot's `board_feet_unassigned` or `board_feet_reserved` bucket decreasing by that billet's `board_feet` and `board_feet_scrapped` increasing by the same amount; any wood still standing uncut in `board_feet_available` also moves into `board_feet_scrapped`. Because those billets were quarantined, every component machined from one of them whose status is not `INSTALLED` or `SCRAPPED` moves to `QC_HELD` with `qc_hold_reason` set to `PROTECTED_SPECIES` — and that applies from every non-terminal component status, meaning `ROUGH_SAWN`, `CNC_ROUTED`, `HAND_SHAPED`, `QC_HELD`, `REWORK` and `ACCEPTED` alike. Because those components were held, every build order that owns at least one of them and whose build stage is not `SERIALIZED`, `SCRAPPED` or already `COMPLIANCE_HOLD` records its current build stage in `stage_before_compliance_hold` and moves that stage to `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`; this applies from every other non-terminal build stage, while an order already parked on a compliance hold is passed over untouched so that the stage snapshot taken the first time survives and the order's `version` does not move. When a lot is rejected while still on permit hold, no billets, components or orders exist yet, all three downstream links resolve over empty sets, and the request succeeds having changed nothing but the lot itself.

Drying is tracked by moisture-meter readings, which a `WoodBuyer` or a `PlantAdmin` may log with a fresh `moisture_content_pct` — bounded exactly as the figure supplied at receipt is, between 2.0 and 30.0 inclusive. A reading on a `RECEIVED` lot moves it `RECEIVED → KILN_DRYING`; a reading on a lot already `KILN_DRYING` leaves it there, so readings may be logged repeatedly through the kiln schedule. Either way the lot's `moisture_content_pct` is overwritten and the realised yield figure is recomputed. Logging a reading on a lot that is `PERMIT_HOLD`, `CONDITIONED`, `RELEASED`, `DEPLETED` or `REJECTED` is `ERR_LOT_STATE_INVALID`.

When the wood has settled, the buyer declares the lot acclimatised. Conditioning is accepted from `RECEIVED` or from `KILN_DRYING` and lands the lot in `CONDITIONED`. It requires the lot's current `moisture_content_pct` to sit inside the band from 6.0 to 8.0 percent with **both bounds inclusive** — 6.0 passes, 8.0 passes, 5.9 and 8.1 are refused with `ERR_MOISTURE_OUT_OF_BAND` — and it requires the lot's storage room to be `IN_SPEC` at that moment. Conditioning refreshes the forward-looking yield projection. The last step before wood becomes cuttable is release, accepted only from `CONDITIONED` and requiring `permit_status` to be either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `VERIFIED`; a lot whose permit is still `PENDING` or has gone `INVALID` cannot be released. Release is also gated on the same inclusive moisture band, so a lot that drifted since conditioning is refused with `ERR_MOISTURE_OUT_OF_BAND`. Only a lot in status `RELEASED` may be sawn.

Two yield figures are maintained on every lot, and they are genuinely different calculations rather than one calculation with a filter. Both lean on the same shrinkage helper: a lot's shrinkage allowance in basis points is the amount by which its moisture reading exceeds the 8.0 percent target, clamped at zero so that wood drier than target is never credited with negative loss, multiplied by 45 basis points per percentage point of excess. Wood at exactly 8.0 percent carries a shrinkage allowance of zero.

`nominal_yield_board_feet` is the receipt-time projection. It takes `board_feet_received`, reduces it by the shrinkage allowance computed from `moisture_at_receipt_pct` and by a single whole-lot sawing allowance of 30 basis points, and rounds half up to two decimal places: multiply `board_feet_received` by the result of subtracting both the shrinkage allowance and the 30-basis-point sawing allowance from 10000, then divide by 10000. It is computed when the lot is received and refreshed when the lot is conditioned.

`actual_yield_board_feet` is the realised figure and is a chronological replay. Every billet cut from the lot whose status is not `QUARANTINED` is replayed in `created_at` ascending order with `billet_code` ascending as the tie-break, and their `board_feet` are summed into a cut total. The realised yield is that cut total reduced by the shrinkage allowance computed from the lot's **current** `moisture_content_pct` — multiply the cut total by ten thousand less the shrinkage allowance, divide by 10000 — and rounded half up to two decimal places. It is recomputed whenever a billet is sawn, whenever a moisture reading is logged, and whenever a lot rejection removes quarantined billets from the replay set. The two variants therefore disagree on three axes at once: which moisture figure is authoritative, how sawing loss is charged, and what the input set is.

To make the difference concrete, take a lot received at 100.00 board feet with `moisture_at_receipt_pct` of 12.0 that has since been dried to a `moisture_content_pct` of 7.0, and from which two billets of 30.00 and 25.00 board feet have been cut. The nominal projection uses a shrinkage allowance of four percentage points above target times 45, or 180 basis points, giving 100.00 multiplied by 9790 divided by 10000, which is 97.90. The realised figure uses a shrinkage allowance of zero because the clamp fires at 7.0 percent, so it is simply the 55.00 board feet actually cut.

### 3.3. Sawing Billets and the Wood Ledger

A `Machinist` saws a billet from a released lot, supplying a `billet_code` unique across all billets, the `lot_id` of the source lot, a positive `board_feet` figure to two decimal places, a `grade` drawn from `AAA`, `AA`, `A` and `UTILITY`, and the `expected_tonewood_lot_version` demanded by §2.7. A `parent_billet_id` may be supplied when the new billet is a resaw of a thicker billet rather than a primary cut off the lot; it is omitted for a primary cut. The parent is only required to exist — it is never checked against the named lot and its own status is never examined — and naming one changes no arithmetic: a resaw draws its `board_feet` and its kerf from the lot's `board_feet_available` exactly as a primary cut does, and the parent billet's own `board_feet` and `status` are left untouched. Sawing a lot that is not in status `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`.

Two figures are derived at the cut and never re-synced afterwards. `kerf_loss_board_feet` is the sawdust charged against the lot for this particular cut: multiply the billet's `board_feet` by 300 basis points, divide by 10000, and round half up to two decimal places — a three-percent per-cut allowance, an order of magnitude larger than the 30-basis-point whole-lot allowance the nominal projection assumes, which is exactly why the two yield figures diverge in practice. `moisture_content_pct` on the billet is a snapshot of the parent lot's `moisture_content_pct` taken at the instant of the cut; later readings on the lot never propagate to billets already sawn.

Availability is checked before anything is written. The billet's `board_feet` plus its computed `kerf_loss_board_feet` must be less than or equal to the lot's current `board_feet_available`, and a cut that would overdraw the lot is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BOARD_FEET`. When the cut is booked, the lot's `board_feet_available` falls by the billet's `board_feet` and again by the kerf figure, `board_feet_unassigned` rises by the `board_feet`, `board_feet_kerf_loss` rises by the kerf figure, and `board_feet_cut` moves with the mirror definition of §2.10. A billet begins life in status `AVAILABLE`. If the cut leaves the lot with less than two board feet still standing in `board_feet_available`, the lot moves `RELEASED → DEPLETED` in the same request and no further billets may be sawn from it.

Following the same 100.00-board-foot lot through two cuts of 30.00 and 25.00 board feet makes the ledger legible: the kerf charges are 0.90 and 0.75, so `board_feet_kerf_loss` reaches 1.65, `board_feet_unassigned` reaches 55.00, and `board_feet_available` falls to 43.35. Those three plus the four zero buckets sum to exactly 100.00, as the wood identity of §2.10 requires.

A billet's `status` then follows the wood. Promising it to a part moves it `AVAILABLE → RESERVED`, shifting its board feet from the unassigned bucket to the reserved bucket. Installing that part in an instrument moves the billet `RESERVED → CONSUMED`, shifting the board feet from reserved to consumed. Cancelling the instrument's order moves it `RESERVED → AVAILABLE`, shifting the board feet back from reserved to unassigned, because a cancelled order returns unharmed wood to the rack. Scrapping the part at QC moves the billet `RESERVED → SCRAPPED`, shifting the board feet from reserved to scrapped, because a scrapped part destroys its wood. A lot rejection moves the billet from `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED` to `QUARANTINED`, shifting the board feet from whichever of those two buckets held them into the scrapped bucket. `SCRAPPED` and `QUARANTINED` are terminal; wood in either of them never comes back.

### 3.4. Engineering Specs, Station Time and Choosing the Next Job

Before any wood is machined, a `PlantAdmin` publishes the engineering specifications the parts are built to. A spec carries a `spec_code` unique across all specs, a `component_type` drawn from `BODY_TOP`, `BODY_BACK`, `NECK`, `FINGERBOARD`, `BRACE` and `BINDING`, a non-empty `required_species_codes` array listing which tonewood species the spec permits, a `min_grade` giving the lowest acceptable billet grade, a positive `nominal_board_feet` figure for the wood the spec nominally consumes, a positive `target_thickness_mm` to two decimal places, and the nominal machine times `cnc_minutes` and `hand_shape_minutes`, each at least one minute. Two optional fields carry defaults: `thickness_tolerance_mm`, the permitted absolute deviation from `target_thickness_mm` at quality control, defaults to 0.15 millimetres and must be positive; and `required_room_type`, the type of room the machining stations for this spec must sit in, defaults to `BUILD_ROOM` and may otherwise be `DRY_STORE`. Grade comparisons use a fixed ranking in which `UTILITY` is the lowest, then `A`, then `AA`, then `AAA` is the highest. Specs are reference data in the sense of §2.4: published once, never edited, no `version`, no state machine.

Stations are commissioned the same way. A station carries a `station_code` unique across the plant, a `station_type` drawn from `CNC_ROUTER`, `HAND_SHAPING`, `BRACING`, `BINDING`, `ASSEMBLY`, `FRETWORK` and `SETUP_BENCH`, and the `room_id` of the room it physically stands in — whose `climate_status` then governs every operation consuming that station, and whose `room_type` is not constrained at all, so a station may be commissioned in a room of any type. The optional `changeover_minutes` sets the symmetric buffer that must separate two bookings on the station and defaults to thirty minutes; it may be zero but never negative. A new station opens with `total_scheduled_minutes`, `held_minutes`, `consumed_minutes` and `released_minutes` all at zero.

Every station has a capacity of exactly one job at a time. A `Machinist` or a `Luthier` books a window by naming the `station_id`, a `starts_at` instant and an `ends_at` instant, optionally the `build_order_id` the booked work belongs to and optionally the `component_id` the work is for — the latter is omitted for whole-instrument bench work. Both timestamps must be RFC 3339 with a trailing `Z`; fractional seconds are permitted in the input and ignored. `starts_at` must be strictly earlier than `ends_at`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. When both a `build_order_id` and a `component_id` are given, the component must belong to that order, or the booking is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_COMPONENT_MISMATCH`. The window is then tested against the half-open, changeover-widened conflict rule of §2.9, and a collision is `ERR_STATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A station standing in a `QUARANTINED` room cannot be booked at all: `ERR_ROOM_QUARANTINED`.

An accepted booking computes `duration_minutes` as the whole number of minutes between `starts_at` and `ends_at`, discarding any fractional minute — that is, take the elapsed seconds, divide by sixty, and drop the remainder. It also snapshots `priority_score` from the owning build order at booking time — the score exactly as it currently stands on the order, without recomputing it first, and null when the booking names no `build_order_id` — and that snapshot is never re-synced when the order's live score later moves. The reservation opens in status `HELD`, and the station's `total_scheduled_minutes` and `held_minutes` each rise by `duration_minutes`. Changeover minutes are never included in `duration_minutes`.

From `HELD` a reservation has exactly two exits, and both preserve the station-minute identity. Routing or hand-shaping a part against the reservation moves it `HELD → CONSUMED`, subtracting `duration_minutes` from `held_minutes` and adding it to `consumed_minutes`. Giving the window back — available to a `Machinist`, a `Luthier` or a `PlantAdmin` — moves it `HELD → RELEASED`, subtracting `duration_minutes` from `held_minutes` and adding it to `released_minutes`. The climate cascade of §3.1 and the cancellation of §3.6 both perform that same compensating movement on the shop's behalf, the former only for reservations whose `starts_at` is still in the future. Citing a reservation that is no longer `HELD` — for routing, for hand-shaping or for release — is `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`. `CONSUMED` and `RELEASED` are both terminal.

Which job a station should take next is a scheduling question, and the system answers it rather than leaving it to the whiteboard. Every build order carries a live `priority_score` built from four terms added together. The artist-relations term contributes nothing for an `artist_tier` of `NONE`, twenty points for `ENDORSER` and forty points for `SIGNATURE`. The custom-shop term contributes fifteen points when `is_custom_shop` is true and nothing otherwise. The distress term contributes twenty-five points when `commercial_status` is `AT_RISK` and nothing otherwise. The urgency term looks ninety days ahead: take the whole number of days from the date part of the current logical clock instant to the order's `promised_date`, subtract that number of days from ninety, clamp the result at zero from below, and multiply by one half of a point per day. Because the per-day weight is exactly one half, every score lands on a `.00` or a `.50` boundary; scores are carried to two decimal places, rounded half up. An order whose `promised_date` has already passed has a negative day count, which makes the ninety-day difference larger than ninety and drives the urgency term above forty-five points accordingly. The score is refreshed when the order is created, when it is confirmed, when its options are locked and on every failed inspection round that fires the escalation block of §3.8 — whether or not that particular failure actually moves `commercial_status` to `AT_RISK`; moving the logical clock is explicitly **not** a trigger, and a stale score is simply refreshed the next time the order is written or the selector reads it. When the selector performs that refresh it writes the new score in place on the orders it considered, without moving their `updated_at` or `version` — a read never counts as a mutation of the row.

The station work selector is a read available to a `Machinist`, a `Luthier` or a `PlantAdmin`. An order is eligible for a given station only if it satisfies all three of the following: it holds at least one reservation on that station whose status is `HELD`, whether that window lies in the past or in the future; its `build_stage` is not `SERIALIZED`, `SCRAPPED` or `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`; and its `commercial_status` is not `QUOTED`, `INVOICED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`. Eligible orders are ranked by `priority_score` descending. Ties are broken first by the earliest `starts_at` among that order's `HELD` reservations on this station, ascending, and then by the order's own `created_at`, ascending. An order's `id` is never a tie-break — identifiers are opaque and must not decide a business outcome, so `created_at` is the only sanctioned final fallback. The reply names the winning `build_order_id`, its `order_code`, its `priority_score`, the `starts_at` that decided the tie-break, and an `eligible_count` giving the size of the pool the winner was drawn from. When the pool is empty the read returns `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BUILD_ORDER` rather than a null payload.

As an illustration, suppose the clock reads the first of August 2026. One order is a `SIGNATURE` custom-shop build sitting at `OPTIONS_LOCKED` with a `promised_date` sixty-one days out: forty points plus fifteen plus nothing plus twenty-nine days inside the horizon at half a point each, giving 69.50. Another is a plain non-custom order, also `OPTIONS_LOCKED`, promised nineteen days out: nothing, nothing, nothing, and seventy-one days inside the horizon at half a point each, giving 35.50. If both hold `HELD` reservations on the same router, the selector returns the first.

### 3.5. Parts: Routing, Hand Shaping, Quality Control and the Rework Loop

A part begins when a `Machinist` opens it against an engineering spec and a piece of wood, naming the `spec_id` and the `billet_id`. A `build_order_id` may be supplied once that order has been released to the machine shop, and is omitted entirely for shelf stock; the order is only required to exist at this point and its own `build_stage` is never examined here — the release gate is enforced on the first routing pass instead. The billet must be in status `AVAILABLE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BILLET_STATE_INVALID`. Two compatibility checks then join the wood to the spec: the species of the billet's lot must appear in the spec's `required_species_codes`, otherwise `ERR_SPECIES_NOT_PERMITTED`; and the billet's `grade` must rank at or above the spec's `min_grade` on the `UTILITY`-to-`AAA` ranking, otherwise `ERR_GRADE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Only after both of those is the lot itself weighed: it must still be `RELEASED`, or `ERR_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`. That is the ladder order of §2.6 — compatibility outranks cross-entity state — so a billet standing in a `DEPLETED` lot whose grade is also below the spec minimum reports the grade, not the lot. A billet impounded by a lot rejection is refused earlier and with a different code — `ERR_PROTECTED_SPECIES_QUARANTINED` — and as §2.6 records, that refusal outranks both compatibility checks, so a quarantined billet whose grade is also too low reports the compliance failure.

An accepted part opens in status `ROUGH_SAWN` with `qc_hold_reason` of `NONE`, `rework_count` at zero and `measured_thickness_mm` null until the first machining call. Opening it also moves the billet `AVAILABLE → RESERVED`, which is the forward half of the wood movement described in §3.3: the lot's `board_feet_unassigned` falls by the billet's `board_feet` and its `board_feet_reserved` rises by the same amount.

Machining happens against a booked window, never freehand. A `Machinist` routes the part on a computer-controlled router by naming a `station_reservation_id` and the `measured_thickness_mm` the router left behind; a `Luthier` then hand-shapes it the same way, again naming a `station_reservation_id` and the resulting `measured_thickness_mm`. On both calls `measured_thickness_mm` is a required number strictly greater than zero, with no upper bound and no fixed number of decimal places. Both calls require the cited reservation to be `HELD` and to name this very part in its `component_id`, otherwise `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_RESERVATION_COMPONENT_MISMATCH`. Both require the room housing the station to be `IN_SPEC`, and both are refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_HOLD_ACTIVE` when the part carries a `build_order_id` whose order is parked at build stage `COMPLIANCE_HOLD` — a refusal weighed after the reservation's own `HELD` status and after the climate posture, but before the `component_id` and station-type comparisons, exactly as §2.6 orders those rungs. They differ in the station they demand: routing requires a `station_type` of `CNC_ROUTER` and hand-shaping requires `HAND_SHAPING`, and the wrong kind of bench is `ERR_STATION_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Routing moves the part `ROUGH_SAWN → CNC_ROUTED`; when the part carries a `build_order_id`, that order's `build_stage` must be `COMPONENTS_RELEASED` for the first pass to be accepted, a check that is simply skipped for shelf stock. Hand-shaping moves the part `CNC_ROUTED → HAND_SHAPED`. Both store the supplied `measured_thickness_mm`, move the cited reservation `HELD → CONSUMED`, and shift that reservation's `duration_minutes` out of the station's `held_minutes` and into its `consumed_minutes`. Routing a part that is neither `ROUGH_SAWN` nor `REWORK`, or hand-shaping one that is not `CNC_ROUTED`, is `ERR_COMPONENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Dimensional quality control belongs to a `QualityInspector` and takes a `verdict` of either `ACCEPT` or `HOLD`, with an optional `hold_reason`. It is accepted only from `HAND_SHAPED`. An `ACCEPT` verdict requires the part's `measured_thickness_mm` to sit within the spec's `thickness_tolerance_mm` of its `target_thickness_mm`, measured as an absolute difference with the bound **inclusive**; a part exactly at the tolerance limit passes, and a part beyond it is refused with `ERR_THICKNESS_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`. An accepted part moves `HAND_SHAPED → ACCEPTED` and has its `qc_hold_reason` set to `NONE`. A `HOLD` verdict moves the part `HAND_SHAPED → QC_HELD` and records why, taking `qc_hold_reason` from the supplied `hold_reason` and defaulting it to `DIMENSION` when the field is omitted; the permitted reasons are `NONE`, `DIMENSION`, `GRAIN_RUNOUT`, `PROTECTED_SPECIES` and `CLIMATE_EXCURSION`.

A held part must then be dispositioned, and the two dispositions are split across two roles on purpose. That split is decided from the body's `disposition` alone and is decided early: a `QualityInspector` asking for `REWORK`, or a `Luthier` asking for `SCRAP`, is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` before the part is so much as looked up, so this role refusal outranks both the part's existence and its status. A `Luthier` may choose a `disposition` of `REWORK`, sending the part back around the machining loop: the part moves `QC_HELD → REWORK`, its `rework_count` rises by one and its `qc_hold_reason` returns to `NONE`. Rework is refused in two circumstances. Wood impounded for protected-species reasons may only be destroyed, so a part whose `qc_hold_reason` is `PROTECTED_SPECIES` is refused with `ERR_REWORK_NOT_PERMITTED`. And a part may go around the loop at most twice: once `rework_count` has reached two, a further rework attempt is refused with `ERR_MAX_REWORK_EXCEEDED`. That per-part ceiling of two is a different constant from the three-round ceiling on inspection rounds described in §3.8 and the two must not be conflated. A part in `REWORK` re-enters machining by being routed again, which closes the cycle `ROUGH_SAWN → CNC_ROUTED → HAND_SHAPED → QC_HELD → REWORK → CNC_ROUTED`; the second pass through routing does not re-check the owning order's build stage.

A `QualityInspector` may instead choose a `disposition` of `SCRAP`, which moves the part `QC_HELD → SCRAPPED` and clears `qc_hold_reason` to `NONE` — and clearing it matters, because it is what eventually allows an order parked on a compliance hold to be released. Scrapping destroys the wood: the part's billet moves `RESERVED → SCRAPPED` and its board feet move out of the lot's `board_feet_reserved` and into `board_feet_scrapped`. That destruction is skipped when the billet has already been driven to `QUARANTINED` by a lot rejection — such a billet stays `QUARANTINED` and its board feet, already counted in `board_feet_scrapped`, are not scrapped a second time. Dispositioning a part that is not `QC_HELD` is `ERR_COMPONENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Two further transitions reach parts from outside. Assembling an instrument moves each of its `ACCEPTED` parts to `INSTALLED` and each of their billets `RESERVED → CONSUMED`. Cancelling an order moves every part of that order that is neither `INSTALLED` nor `SCRAPPED` to `SCRAPPED` while returning each billet `RESERVED → AVAILABLE`; the part's `build_order_id` is deliberately left in place for audit, and so is its `qc_hold_reason` — unlike the QC scrap above, cancellation never clears that reason. Note the contrast with a QC scrap: cancellation recovers the wood, QC destroys it. `INSTALLED` and `SCRAPPED` are the terminal part states.

Every time a part enters or leaves the accepted population, the owning order's protected-species position is recomputed — that is, on quality control, on dispositioning and on assembly. On each of those three occasions the owning order's row is written, so its `updated_at` and `version` move even when the recomputed posture turns out identical to the one already stored; a part carrying no `build_order_id` writes no order at all. The computation walks three hops backwards through the wood graph: from the order to every part whose status is `ACCEPTED` or `INSTALLED`, from each part to its billet, and from each billet to its lot. The set of lots reached that way is the order's contributing set. `strictest_cites_appendix` is the strictest appendix present in that set on a total ranking where `NONE` is least strict, then `APPENDIX_III`, then `APPENDIX_II`, and `APPENDIX_I` is strictest — so a single `APPENDIX_I` fingerboard blank outranks every other component in the instrument. `cites_permit_required` is true exactly when `strictest_cites_appendix` is anything other than `NONE`. When the contributing set is empty, or when every lot in it is `NONE`, the aggregate takes its identity value: `strictest_cites_appendix` is `NONE` and `cites_permit_required` is false. That is not an error and not null.

### 3.6. The Commercial Dimension: Quoting, Options, Deposits and Cancellation

A build order carries two state dimensions on one row, and they evolve independently of one another. `commercial_status` is the sales dimension and runs through `QUOTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `OPTIONS_LOCKED`, `AT_RISK`, `INVOICED`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, with `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. `build_stage` is the shop-floor dimension and runs through `DRAFT`, `COMPONENTS_RELEASED`, `IN_ASSEMBLY`, `IN_FINISH`, `FINISH_HELD`, `FINISH_CURED`, `FRETWORK`, `SETUP`, `INSPECTION`, `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`, `SERIALIZED` and `SCRAPPED`, with `SERIALIZED` and `SCRAPPED` terminal. The commercial dimension can advance all the way to `OPTIONS_LOCKED` while the build stage is still `DRAFT`; the two interlock at exactly one point, described at the end of this subsection.

A `CustomShopCoordinator` opens an order with an `order_code` unique across all orders, a `model_code` of 1 to 16 characters made of uppercase alphanumerics and dashes that becomes the first segment of the eventual serial number, a positive `base_price_cents` list price, and a `promised_date` in RFC 3339 form with a trailing `Z`. Three optional flags shape the rest of the order's life: `is_custom_shop` defaults to false and decides whether the order runs through the custom shop; `artist_tier` defaults to `NONE` and may be `ENDORSER` or `SIGNATURE`; and `finish_system` defaults to `NITRO` and may be `POLY`. A new order opens with `build_stage` of `DRAFT`, `commercial_status` of `QUOTED`, `serial_number` null, `binding_quote_total_cents` null, `rework_round_count` and `refinish_count` at zero, `cites_permit_required` false, `strictest_cites_appendix` of `NONE`, `stage_before_compliance_hold` null, and all five deposit figures — `deposit_paid_cents`, `deposit_held_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents`, `deposit_refunded_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` — at zero. Its `provisional_quote_total_cents` and its `priority_score` are computed immediately, over an option set that is still empty.

Custom-shop options themselves are reference data published by a `PlantAdmin` or a `CustomShopCoordinator`. A catalog row carries an `option_code` unique across the catalog, an `option_group` drawn from `TOP_WOOD`, `INLAY`, `HARDWARE`, `ELECTRONICS` and `NECK_PROFILE`, a non-negative `upcharge_cents` list price before any complexity multiplier, a `complexity_class` of `STANDARD`, `ARTISAN` or `MASTER` describing the labour band, and a non-negative `lead_time_days` giving the extra calendar days the option adds to the build. Two optional fields carry defaults: `incompatible_option_codes` defaults to an empty array and lists option codes that may not coexist on one order — each entry need only be a non-empty string, and the entries are stored verbatim and never resolved against the catalog, so naming a code no catalog row carries is accepted rather than a 404, and `requires_finish_system` defaults to `ANY` and may instead pin the option to `NITRO` or `POLY`. Like engineering specs, catalog rows are never edited.

Configuring an option on an order creates a selection naming the `option_id`, optionally a `quantity` that defaults to 1 and must be between 1 and 4 inclusive, and the `expected_build_order_version` demanded by §2.7; the `build_order_id` may also be echoed in the body, where it is validated as a non-empty string and then ignored — it is never compared with the path identifier and never looked up, so echoing some other order's identifier changes nothing. Configuration carries no commercial gate of its own: a selection may be configured while the order stands at any `commercial_status`, an already locked, invoiced or cancelled one included, and only approval and rejection are restricted to an order still open. The coordinator's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `created_by_user_id`, and the selection opens in status `PENDING_APPROVAL`. Two compatibility rules apply at this point. If the catalog row's `requires_finish_system` is `NITRO` or `POLY` and differs from the order's `finish_system`, the request is refused with `ERR_OPTION_FINISH_MISMATCH`; a `requires_finish_system` of `ANY` never conflicts. And if the requested option's `option_code` appears in the `incompatible_option_codes` of an option already on the order in status `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `SELECTED` or `LOCKED` — or if one of those options' codes appears in the requested option's own `incompatible_option_codes`, since the check runs in both directions — the request is refused with `ERR_OPTION_INCOMPATIBLE`.

A selection then follows a short lifecycle from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to either `SELECTED`, `REJECTED` or, eventually, `LOCKED`. A `CustomShopCoordinator` approves a pending selection, moving it `PENDING_APPROVAL → SELECTED`, but only while the owning order's `commercial_status` is `QUOTED` or `CONFIRMED`, and only when the approver's `X-User-ID` differs from the `created_by_user_id` stamped on the row — the coordinator who configured an option may not be the one who approves it, and an attempt is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A coordinator may also reject a selection, optionally supplying a `reason` that must be a non-empty string when present and is validated and then discarded rather than written onto the row; rejection is accepted from `PENDING_APPROVAL` or from `SELECTED`, again only while the order is `QUOTED` or `CONFIRMED`, and moves the row to `REJECTED` while leaving its `line_total_cents` in place for audit. Approving or rejecting a row that is already `LOCKED` or `REJECTED`, or approving one that is already `SELECTED`, is `ERR_OPTION_SELECTION_STATE_INVALID`. On both calls the selection's own status is weighed before the owning order's `commercial_status`, so a `LOCKED` row on an `OPTIONS_LOCKED` order reports `ERR_OPTION_SELECTION_STATE_INVALID` and never `ERR_COMMERCIAL_STATUS_INVALID`. `LOCKED` and `REJECTED` are terminal.

Every order carries two quote totals, computed by the same six-step arithmetic over two different input sets. **`provisional_quote_total_cents`** is forward-looking: its input set is every selection on the order whose status is `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `SELECTED`, and it reads each option's unit upcharge live from the catalog row at evaluation time. It is refreshed when the order is created, when a selection is configured, when a selection is approved and when a selection is rejected. Because both `PENDING_APPROVAL` and `SELECTED` sit inside the input set, approval does not move the provisional total at all — it only makes the row eligible for locking; rejection, by contrast, drops the row out of the set and the total falls. Pricing an unapproved option in is deliberate: the deposit threshold at confirmation must cover the worst case the customer has actually asked for. **`binding_quote_total_cents`** is backward-looking: its input set is every selection whose status is `LOCKED`, and it reads each option's unit upcharge from that selection's own `locked_upcharge_cents` rather than from the catalog, which makes it immune to any later catalog price change. It is null until the configuration is locked, computed exactly once at that moment, and immutable afterwards.

The six steps are these. **First**, price each included selection on its own line: multiply that option's unit upcharge by the selection's `quantity`, multiply by the complexity multiplier for the catalog row's `complexity_class` — 10000 basis points for `STANDARD`, 13500 for `ARTISAN` and 17500 for `MASTER` — divide by 10000, and round half up to whole cents. The included rows are iterated by the referenced catalog row's `option_code` ascending, with the selection's own `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. The result is persisted on the selection as `line_total_cents`. **Second**, sum those per-line amounts into an options subtotal. **Third**, add the order's `base_price_cents` to the options subtotal to obtain a pre-discount amount. **Fourth**, compute the custom-shop surcharge: when `is_custom_shop` is true, multiply the pre-discount amount by 1200 basis points, divide by 10000 and round half up — twelve percent of the pre-discount amount; when `is_custom_shop` is false the surcharge is zero. **Fifth**, compute the artist discount: multiply the **options subtotal** by the discount rate for the order's `artist_tier` — zero basis points for `NONE`, 1000 for `ENDORSER`, 2500 for `SIGNATURE` — divide by 10000 and round half up. The endorsement discount is granted against the custom option content only; it is never applied to the base instrument price and never to the custom-shop surcharge. **Sixth**, the total is the pre-discount amount plus the surcharge minus the artist discount.

The rounding in step one is per line, and it happens before the summation in step two. Rounding each line and rounding the sum of raw upcharges are not interchangeable operations, and this specification pins the first reading: round, then sum. Summing raw unit upcharges across lines and applying the complexity multiplier once to that sum is forbidden. To see why it matters, take an order with a `base_price_cents` of 300000, `is_custom_shop` false and `artist_tier` of `NONE`, carrying three `ARTISAN` selections of quantity one at upcharges of 12004, 22004 and 32004 cents. Rounding each line gives 16205, 29705 and 43205, an options subtotal of 89115 and a total of 389115. Summing first would give 66012, then 89116 after the multiplier, and a total of 389116 — one cent higher, and wrong.

A fuller example shows the two variants diverging without any catalog price ever changing. An order with a `base_price_cents` of 480000, `is_custom_shop` true and `artist_tier` of `ENDORSER` carries two selections: an `ARTISAN` inlay at 130000 cents, quantity one, already `SELECTED`; and a `MASTER` top wood at 95000 cents, quantity one, still `PENDING_APPROVAL`. The provisional lines are 175500 and 166250, a subtotal of 341750, a pre-discount amount of 821750, a surcharge of 98610, an artist discount of 34175, and a `provisional_quote_total_cents` of 886185. Once the configuration is locked, the inlay becomes `LOCKED` with a `locked_upcharge_cents` of 130000 while the top wood is force-rejected, so the binding computation sees one line of 175500, a pre-discount amount of 655500, a surcharge of 78660, a discount of 17550, and a `binding_quote_total_cents` of 716610.

Confirmation is where cash arrives. A `CustomShopCoordinator` confirms the order with a `deposit_cents` amount — a required integer that may be zero but never negative — moving `commercial_status` `QUOTED → CONFIRMED` while `build_stage` stays exactly where it is. On an order whose `is_custom_shop` is true, at least one selection must already be in status `SELECTED`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_OPTIONS_NOT_SELECTED` — and because that check sits above the fiscal rung of the ladder, an under-funded custom order with no approved option reports the missing option rather than the thin deposit. The deposit itself must be at least ten percent of the provisional total: multiply `provisional_quote_total_cents` by 1000 basis points, divide by 10000, round half up, and require `deposit_cents` to be greater than or equal to that figure, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` with HTTP 402. A successful confirmation adds `deposit_cents` to both `deposit_paid_cents` and `deposit_held_cents` and refreshes `priority_score`. Confirming an order that is not `QUOTED` is `ERR_COMMERCIAL_STATUS_INVALID`.

Locking the configuration moves `commercial_status` `CONFIRMED → OPTIONS_LOCKED` and does four things at once. Every selection in status `SELECTED` moves `SELECTED → LOCKED` and has its `locked_upcharge_cents` stamped from the catalog row's `upcharge_cents` read at that instant. Every selection still in status `PENDING_APPROVAL` is force-rejected, moving `PENDING_APPROVAL → REJECTED` with its `locked_upcharge_cents` left null so that it never enters the binding input set at all — which is precisely how the two quote variants come to differ. `binding_quote_total_cents` is then computed. Finally the schedule is pushed out: take the date part of the current logical clock instant, add sixty days of base lead time plus the largest `lead_time_days` among the locked selections — or plus nothing, when no selection locked — as whole days, and set `promised_date` to the later of its existing value and that computed instant, so the promise never moves earlier. `priority_score` is recomputed afterwards. Locking an order that is not `CONFIRMED` is `ERR_COMMERCIAL_STATUS_INVALID`.

The two dimensions interlock exactly once. A `Machinist` releases the order to the machine shop, moving `build_stage` `DRAFT → COMPONENTS_RELEASED`, and that transition requires `commercial_status` to be `OPTIONS_LOCKED` — a price that is still negotiable may not consume wood. Attempting it while the commercial dimension is behind is `ERR_COMMERCIAL_STATUS_INVALID`; attempting it when the build stage is past `DRAFT` is `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`. The build stage is tested first, so an order that has already left `DRAFT` reports `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID` whatever its commercial status happens to be. From that point parts may cite this order.

Cancellation is the compensating half of both conservation invariants. A `CustomShopCoordinator` cancels the order, optionally supplying a `reason` that must be a non-empty string when present and is validated and then discarded rather than written onto the order. It is permitted from every build stage except `SERIALIZED` and from any `commercial_status` other than `INVOICED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` — so from `QUOTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `OPTIONS_LOCKED` or `AT_RISK` — and is otherwise refused with `ERR_COMMERCIAL_STATUS_INVALID`. Cancellation drives `commercial_status` to `CANCELLED` and `build_stage` to `SCRAPPED`; scraps every part of the order that is not already `INSTALLED` or `SCRAPPED` while returning each of their billets `RESERVED → AVAILABLE` and moving the board feet from the reserved bucket back to the unassigned bucket; releases every station reservation on the order still in status `HELD`, moving each one's `duration_minutes` from the station's `held_minutes` to its `released_minutes`; and settles the deposit.

Settlement works as a small waterfall. The basis is `binding_quote_total_cents` when the order's `commercial_status` at the moment of cancellation is `OPTIONS_LOCKED` or `AT_RISK`, and `provisional_quote_total_cents` otherwise. The forfeit is twenty-five percent of that basis — multiply by 2500 basis points, divide by 10000, round half up — but capped at whatever is actually still in `deposit_held_cents`, so the mill can never forfeit more than the customer left on deposit. The refund is whatever remains of `deposit_held_cents` after the forfeit. `deposit_forfeited_cents` rises by the forfeit, `deposit_refunded_cents` rises by the refund, and `deposit_held_cents` becomes zero. For an order that had 200000 cents on deposit, was `OPTIONS_LOCKED`, and carried a `binding_quote_total_cents` of 716610, the uncapped forfeit works out at 179153 cents, which is below the held balance, so 179153 is forfeited and 20847 is refunded — and applied plus refunded plus forfeited plus held then sums to the 200000 originally paid, exactly as §2.10 requires. A thin deposit on an expensive order refunds nothing and forfeits precisely what was held.

### 3.7. Assembly, Finish Booths and the Cure Clock

A `Luthier` starts assembly, moving `build_stage` `COMPONENTS_RELEASED → IN_ASSEMBLY`. Two counts must hold at that moment. At least one part on the order must be in status `ACCEPTED`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_COMPONENTS_NOT_ACCEPTED`; and no part on the order may still be in `ROUGH_SAWN`, `CNC_ROUTED`, `HAND_SHAPED`, `QC_HELD` or `REWORK`, otherwise it is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_IN_FLIGHT`. A part that has already been scrapped is neither accepted nor in flight, so it blocks nothing. Starting assembly then installs the accepted work: every part on the order in status `ACCEPTED` moves `ACCEPTED → INSTALLED`, each such part's billet moves `RESERVED → CONSUMED` with its lot's board feet moving from the reserved bucket into `board_feet_consumed`, and the order's `cites_permit_required` and `strictest_cites_appendix` are recomputed over the parts that are now installed.

Spraying begins when a `FinishTech` books a booth. The request names the `build_order_id`, the `booth_id`, the `finish_system` to be sprayed, a `coat_count` between 1 and 24 inclusive, and the `expected_build_order_version` demanded by §2.7 — the guard exists precisely because a rejected run legitimately gets superseded by a replacement, so two techs racing to rebook the same body must not both win. The booth must be a room whose `room_type` is `FINISH_BOOTH`, otherwise `ERR_BOOTH_ROOM_TYPE_INVALID`; a build room cannot be sprayed in. The `finish_system` on the request must equal the order's own `finish_system`, and a mismatched body field is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; that comparison needs the order row but not its stage, and it is weighed once both body foreign keys have resolved but ahead of every rule below, so a booking that names the wrong chemistry *and* cites an order outside `IN_ASSEMBLY` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The guards that remain then fire in ladder order rather than in the order this paragraph introduces them: the order's `build_stage`, then the echoed `expected_build_order_version`, then the booth's climate posture, and only then the booth's `room_type`. A booking that is both stale and stage-wrong therefore reports `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`, and one that names a room which is both `OUT_OF_SPEC` and not a finish booth reports `ERR_ROOM_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_SPEC` rather than `ERR_BOOTH_ROOM_TYPE_INVALID`.

The booth hold is projected from the coat schedule alone, before any climate sample has been consulted. `spray_started_at` and `booth_hold_from` are both set to the current clock instant. The extra coats are the amount by which `coat_count` exceeds the base coat count for the system — eight for `NITRO`, three for `POLY` — clamped at zero. `booth_hold_until` is `booth_hold_from` advanced by four spray hours plus the base cure hours for the system (336 for `NITRO`, 72 for `POLY`) plus the extra coats multiplied by the per-extra-coat hours for the system (18 for `NITRO`, 6 for `POLY`). A `NITRO` run of eight coats created at 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z therefore holds 340 hours, giving a `booth_hold_until` of 2026-08-15T04:00:00Z. This interval is closed at both ends, per §2.9.

Two booth rules are then evaluated in a fixed order against the non-terminal runs already holding intervals in that booth — every run whose status is not `COMPLETED` and not `REJECTED`. First, chemistry: every non-terminal run whose closed hold interval touches the new run's closed interval must be spraying the same `finish_system`, because nitrocellulose overspray poisons a polyurethane cure. A conflict is `ERR_BOOTH_FINISH_INCOMPATIBLE`. Second, capacity: the count of non-terminal runs whose closed hold interval overlaps the new hold, plus one for the new run, must not exceed the booth's `capacity_slots`. Exceeding it is `ERR_BOOTH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because chemistry is checked before capacity, a booth that is simultaneously chemically wrong and full reports `ERR_BOOTH_FINISH_INCOMPATIBLE` — never the capacity error. A successful booking opens the run in status `SPRAYING`, with `spray_completed_at`, `cure_hours` and `cure_ready_at` all null, `cure_suspended_minutes` at zero and `cure_suspend_started_at` null, and moves the order `IN_ASSEMBLY → IN_FINISH`. It writes nothing to the booth row itself: the new run raises the booth's reported `occupied_slots` only because that figure is the read-time mirror described next, so the room's own `updated_at` and `version` do not move.

`occupied_slots` deserves a note of its own. It is a read-only mirror of the same overlap count evaluated at the current clock instant rather than against a proposed hold, and it is neither an independent constraint nor a stored column that any operation increments or decrements. A non-terminal run whose projected hold has already elapsed occupies nothing at the present instant and cannot block a new booking whose window is disjoint from it; conversely, a booking is admitted or refused by the interval-overlap count above, not by comparing `occupied_slots` to `capacity_slots`.

Closing the booth door moves the run `SPRAYING → CURING` and requires the booth's `climate_status` to be `IN_SPEC`. It stamps `spray_completed_at` with the current clock instant and computes `cure_hours` and `cure_ready_at`, sampling the booth's `latest_rh_pct` and `latest_temp_c` **at that instant**. Those two readings are consumed once and pinned: a booth that drifts afterwards never re-derives `cure_hours`.

The cure arithmetic combines four independent dimensions — the finish system, the coat count, the booth humidity and the booth temperature — and each contributes its own term. Start from the coat schedule: the extra coats are the excess of `coat_count` over the base coat count for the system, clamped at zero, and the coat hours are the base cure hours for the system plus the extra coats multiplied by the per-extra-coat hours for the system. Humidity adds a penalty: take the amount by which `latest_rh_pct` exceeds 45.0, clamped at zero, divide by 5.0, and **round that quotient up to the next whole number** to get the humidity step count; the penalty is that step count multiplied by twelve hours. Temperature grants relief: take the amount by which `latest_temp_c` exceeds 21.0, clamped at zero, divide by 2.0, and **round that quotient down to a whole number** to get the temperature step count; the relief is the smaller of forty-eight hours and that step count multiplied by eight hours. The two rounding rules are deliberately asymmetric — humidity always rounds toward more penalty, temperature always rounds toward less relief — and they are not interchangeable. Finally, `cure_hours` is the larger of the per-system minimum (240 hours for `NITRO`, 48 for `POLY`) and the coat hours plus the humidity penalty minus the temperature relief. The floor is applied after the relief, so a hot dry booth can never drive a nitrocellulose cure below 240 hours no matter how much relief it earns. `cure_ready_at` is `spray_completed_at` advanced by `cure_hours` hours and then by `cure_suspended_minutes` minutes. All hour counts are whole numbers throughout.

Two examples pin the mechanics. A `NITRO` run of twelve coats whose booth reads a `latest_rh_pct` of 52.0 and a `latest_temp_c` of 27.0, sprayed to completion at 2026-08-05T09:00:00Z with `cure_suspended_minutes` at zero, has four extra coats and 408 coat hours; the humidity excess of 7.0 divided by 5.0 is 1.4, which rounds **up** to two steps and 24 penalty hours; the temperature excess of 6.0 divided by 2.0 is exactly three steps, giving 24 relief hours capped at nothing; `cure_hours` is the larger of 240 and 408, or 408, and `cure_ready_at` is 2026-08-22T09:00:00Z. A `POLY` run of three coats in a booth reading 40.0 and 29.0, sprayed to completion at 2026-08-10T12:00:00Z, has no extra coats and 72 coat hours; the humidity reading is below nominal so the clamp fires and the penalty is zero; the temperature excess of 8.0 divided by 2.0 gives four steps and 32 relief hours; the unfloored result of 40 hours is below the polyurethane minimum, so the floor fires and `cure_hours` is 48, giving a `cure_ready_at` of 2026-08-12T12:00:00Z.

Because the booth hold was projected without the climate terms, a humid booth can push the real `cure_ready_at` past `booth_hold_until`. That divergence is informational. It is never an error, it never shortens or extends the interval that capacity was checked against, and no operation compares the two.

When a booth goes out of specification, §3.1's cascade suspends the cure: every run in that booth whose status is `CURING` moves `CURING → CURE_SUSPENDED` with `cure_suspend_started_at` stamped at the current instant, and its order moves `IN_FINISH → FINISH_HELD`, which in turn releases the order's future station holds. A `FinishTech` resumes once the booth is `IN_SPEC` again, moving the run `CURE_SUSPENDED → CURING`. Resumption banks the lost time: the whole minutes elapsed since `cure_suspend_started_at` — complete minutes only, truncated downward rather than rounded half up, so a ninety-second suspension banks one minute and a fifty-second one banks none — are added to `cure_suspended_minutes`, `cure_suspend_started_at` is cleared to null, and `cure_ready_at` is recomputed as `spray_completed_at` advanced by `cure_hours` hours and then by the new `cure_suspended_minutes`. `cure_hours` itself is **not** recomputed — the booth sample stays pinned to the moment the spray closed, even though the booth has since misbehaved and been corrected. The order moves `FINISH_HELD → IN_FINISH`, and the station reservations that the cascade released are not restored; the shop must re-book them.

Once the cure has run, the tech moves the body to the buffing wheel, `CURING → BUFFING`. This requires the current clock instant to be at or after `cure_ready_at`, with the bound inclusive: an instant exactly equal to `cure_ready_at` is permitted, and anything earlier is `ERR_CURE_NOT_COMPLETE`. The booth must also be `IN_SPEC`, and that climate rung is weighed before the clock: a run whose cure has not run out in a booth that has meanwhile drifted reports `ERR_ROOM_CLIMATE_OUT_OF_SPEC`, never `ERR_CURE_NOT_COMPLETE`. The booth slot remains occupied while buffing. Completing the run moves it `BUFFING → COMPLETED` and moves the order `IN_FINISH → FINISH_CURED`; a `COMPLETED` run leaves the occupancy mirror, so the booth reports one slot fewer at the next read without its row being written. Completion itself consults no climate posture at all — a `BUFFING` run may be completed in a booth that has since gone `OUT_OF_SPEC` or been `QUARANTINED`.

A `QualityInspector` may instead condemn the finish, optionally supplying a `reason`; when present the reason must be a non-empty string, and it is validated and then discarded rather than written onto the run. Rejection consults no climate posture either. It is accepted from `CURING`, `CURE_SUSPENDED` or `BUFFING`, moves the run to `REJECTED` — which likewise takes it out of the booth's occupancy mirror without writing the booth row — and sends the order from `IN_FINISH` or `FINISH_HELD` back to `IN_ASSEMBLY` while incrementing its `refinish_count`. That closes the refinish loop — `IN_ASSEMBLY → IN_FINISH → IN_ASSEMBLY` — and a replacement run may then be booked. `COMPLETED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal, and any of these five operations attempted from a status the transition does not accept is `ERR_FINISH_RUN_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.8. Fretwork, Setup, Playability Inspection and Serialization

A cured body returns to the bench. A `Luthier` records fret levelling, crowning and polishing as complete, moving `build_stage` `FINISH_CURED → FRETWORK`, and then records the setup work — nut, saddle, truss rod, string height — moving `FRETWORK → SETUP`. The order is then eligible for inspection.

An inspection round is opened by a `Luthier` against the order. The order's `build_stage` may be `SETUP` or already `INSPECTION`, and in the latter case the transition is a self-loop that leaves the stage at `INSPECTION`; re-inspecting an instrument whose previous round passed is legitimate. Because `INSPECTION` is an accepted from-state, an order already under inspection clears the state rung of the ladder, and a second attempt therefore surfaces the business rule rather than a stage error: no round on the order may currently be in status `OPEN` or `MEASURED`, and an attempt to open a second concurrent round is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_ROUND_OPEN`. The order's `rework_round_count` must also be strictly below three, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_MAX_REWORK_EXCEEDED`. A successful call creates a round with status `OPEN`, `verdict` of `PENDING`, `deviation_score` null, `inspector_user_id` null, a `round_number` equal to the count of rounds that already exist for that order plus one, and `performed_by_user_id` set to the calling luthier's `X-User-ID`. The round with the highest `round_number` is the authoritative one.

Measurements are submitted against the open round, and only by the luthier who opened it: a request whose `X-User-ID` differs from the round's `performed_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_OWNER_MISMATCH`. This one call reads its body only after those two checks have been cleared, which is a deliberate departure from the schema-first rung of §2.6: a round that is not `OPEN` reports `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, and a foreign luthier `ERR_MEASUREMENT_OWNER_MISMATCH`, even when the measurements in the body are themselves missing, mistyped or out of bounds. Four figures are recorded. `action_mm_treble` is the string action at the treble E at the twelfth fret, between 0.50 and 5.00 inclusive with two decimal places. `action_mm_bass` is the action at the bass E at the same fret, between 0.50 and 6.00 inclusive with two decimal places. `relief_mm` is the neck relief at the seventh fret, between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive with two decimal places. `intonation_cents_max` is the worst-case intonation error across all strings at the twelfth fret, between 0.0 and 50.0 inclusive with one decimal place. Submitting them moves the round `OPEN → MEASURED` and computes its `deviation_score`. The measurement call writes no verdict and names no inspector, however the score comes out: the round's `verdict` stays `PENDING` and its `inspector_user_id` stays null at this step.

The score is a one-sided weighted penalty. Each measurement is first converted to whole sub-units so that no binary floating point enters the sum: the three millimetre figures are multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to hundredths of a millimetre, and the intonation figure is multiplied by 10 and rounded half up to tenths of a cent. The specification limits are 1.60 millimetres of treble action, 2.20 millimetres of bass action, 0.25 millimetres of relief and 4.0 cents of intonation error, which expressed in those same sub-units are 160, 220, 25 and 40. For each dimension, take the amount by which the measurement exceeds its limit, clamped at zero, and multiply by that dimension's weight — 600 for treble action, 500 for bass action, 400 for relief and 500 for intonation. Sum the four products, divide by 1000, and round half up to two decimal places to obtain `deviation_score`. Because each clamp is one-sided, a measurement exactly at its specification limit contributes exactly zero, and a guitar set up tighter than specification on one dimension earns no credit against another dimension that is out of specification.

The verdict follows from the score against a threshold of 10.00, with the bound inclusive: a `deviation_score` of 10.00 is a `PASS` and 10.01 is a `FAIL`. An inspection reading 1.70, 2.10, 0.20 and 4.8 converts to 170, 210, 20 and 48 sub-units; the treble action is ten hundredths over its limit and contributes 6000, the bass action and the relief are both inside specification and clamp to zero, and the intonation is eight tenths of a cent over and contributes 4000, for a total of 10000, a `deviation_score` of exactly 10.00 and a `PASS`. Raising the treble action alone to 1.75 makes that dimension fifteen hundredths over, contributing 9000 for a total of 13000, a `deviation_score` of 13.00 and a `FAIL`.

Signing is performed by a `QualityInspector` on a round in status `MEASURED`, and the signer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the round's `performed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The signature does not choose the outcome — the score does. A round whose `deviation_score` is at or below 10.00 moves `MEASURED → SIGNED` with `verdict` set to `PASS` and `inspector_user_id` stamped with the signer, and the order's `build_stage` stays at `INSPECTION`, now eligible for serialization. A round whose score is above 10.00 moves `MEASURED → FAILED` with `verdict` set to `FAIL` and its `inspector_user_id` left null — the stamp of the signer belongs to the passing branch alone — and that failure is the root of a three-link cascade: the order's `build_stage` is assigned `SETUP` — an unconditional assignment rather than a guarded `INSPECTION → SETUP` transition, so whatever stage the order then holds becomes `SETUP` — its `rework_round_count` increases by one, and if that count has reached two or more, the commercial dimension escalates. The escalation runs in a fixed internal order: `commercial_status` moves `CONFIRMED → AT_RISK` or `OPTIONS_LOCKED → AT_RISK` first, then `promised_date` advances by fourteen days, and only then is `priority_score` recomputed — so the refreshed score already carries both the twenty-five distress points of the new `AT_RISK` status and the pushed-out promise. That escalation block is guarded by the round count alone: the fourteen-day slip and the score recomputation fire on every failure taken while the count stands at two or more, whatever the order's `commercial_status` happens to be at that moment, whereas the status move is a transition that can already be spent — an order that is already `AT_RISK` keeps that status and still has its promise pushed out and its score refreshed. `AT_RISK` carrying its own scheduling points is what lifts the order in the station queue. `SIGNED` and `FAILED` are both terminal for a round, and any of these operations attempted against a round in the wrong status is `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`. The rework loop `INSPECTION → SETUP → INSPECTION` may run at most three times before `ERR_MAX_REWORK_EXCEEDED` stops it.

Serialization is the last shop-floor act. A `CustomShopCoordinator` assigns the serial number to an order whose `build_stage` is `INSPECTION` and whose `serial_number` is still null — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_SERIAL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`. That single-assignment guard is weighed before the build-stage check rather than after it, so the second attempt, made against an order the first attempt drove to `SERIALIZED`, reports `ERR_SERIAL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` and never `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`. The round with the highest `round_number` on that order must be in status `SIGNED` with `verdict` of `PASS`, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_SIGNED`. Only the latest round counts: an earlier passing round does not qualify, so a pass followed by a freshly re-opened round blocks serialization until that new round is itself signed.

The serial is minted as the order's `model_code`, then the four-digit UTC year of the current clock instant, then a sequence number zero-padded to five digits, joined by hyphens. The sequence is one plus the count of orders that already carry a non-null `serial_number` whose `model_code` matches this order's and whose serial's year segment matches this year — per model and per year, so two different models both start at 00001 in the same year. For a `model_code` of `OM45` at 2026-08-22T09:00:00Z with no `OM45` serials issued in 2026, the serial is `OM45-2026-00001`; the next `OM45` serialized in 2026 is `OM45-2026-00002`, while the first `D28` of that year is `D28-2026-00001`. Serials are never reused and never reassigned.

Serialization also closes the fiscal loop. `build_stage` becomes `SERIALIZED`, terminal. `commercial_status` moves `OPTIONS_LOCKED → INVOICED` or `AT_RISK → INVOICED`. And the deposit is applied against the binding price: `deposit_applied_cents` rises by the smaller of `deposit_held_cents` and `binding_quote_total_cents`, and `deposit_held_cents` falls by that same amount. Taking the smaller of the two keeps the deposit ledger of §2.10 balanced even when a customer has over-deposited relative to the final binding total.

One interruption can strike at any point on this path. When a tonewood lot is condemned, §3.2's cascade forces the affected parts to `QC_HELD` with a `qc_hold_reason` of `PROTECTED_SPECIES` and drives the owning order — from whatever non-terminal `build_stage` it happened to occupy — into `COMPLIANCE_HOLD`, recording the interrupted stage in `stage_before_compliance_hold`. A `ComplianceOfficer` clears the hold once no part on the order still carries a `qc_hold_reason` of `PROTECTED_SPECIES`; the order's `build_stage` is then restored to whatever `stage_before_compliance_hold` recorded and that field is set back to null. The target of that restoration is therefore data-dependent: an order held out of `IN_ASSEMBLY` returns to `IN_ASSEMBLY`, and one held out of `SETUP` returns to `SETUP`. Clearing a hold on an order that is not in `COMPLIANCE_HOLD` is `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. After the Factory: The Warranty Network and Export Clearance

A serialized instrument leaves the plant through two independent channels: the authorized repair network, which registers it to an owner, and export clearance, which documents it out of the country.

A `ServiceNetworkManager` enrols a repair shop with a `shop_code` unique across all shops, a `shop_name` of 1 to 80 characters and a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters. The shop opens in status `APPLIED` with an `authorization_level` of `NONE` and a `warranty_claim_count` of zero. Authorization is granted from `APPLIED` or from `SUSPENDED`, moving the shop to `AUTHORIZED` and setting `authorization_level` from the request body, which may be `WARRANTY_ONLY`, `FULL_SERVICE` or `CUSTOM_SHOP_CERTIFIED` but must not be `NONE` — a grant that grants nothing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because that is a schema-rung refusal decided from the body alone, it is reported even when the `shop_id` in the path resolves to nothing at all. Suspension may optionally supply a `reason`, which must be a non-empty string when present and is validated and then discarded rather than written onto the shop; it moves an `AUTHORIZED` shop to `SUSPENDED` and resets `authorization_level` to `NONE`; registrations the shop already holds are left `ACTIVE`, but new registrations from it are refused. Because authorization is reachable again from `SUSPENDED`, the `AUTHORIZED` and `SUSPENDED` states form a cycle and no shop state is terminal. Authorizing a shop that is already `AUTHORIZED`, or suspending one that is not `AUTHORIZED`, is `ERR_SHOP_STATE_INVALID`.

Registration is the one place where an outside party writes to the system. A `ServiceNetworkManager` or an `AuthorizedShop` — the entity-backed role of §2.1, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the `registering_shop_id` it names — creates a registration citing the `build_order_id`, the `registering_shop_id`, the `serial_number` of 1 to 32 characters, an `owner_email` containing exactly one `@` character, and a `purchase_date`. For a shop caller that identity comparison is made against the raw body before any other field is validated, so a shop whose body omits `registering_shop_id`, or sends it as something other than a string, is refused with `ERR_SHOP_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` rather than with the schema error a `ServiceNetworkManager` would receive for the same body. `purchase_date` is any well-formed instant and is never compared with the shop clock: a purchase dated in the future is accepted. The registering shop's status must be `AUTHORIZED`, otherwise `ERR_SHOP_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; a shop that is still `APPLIED` or has been `SUSPENDED` cannot register anything. The submitted `serial_number` must equal the `serial_number` minted on the named order, otherwise `ERR_SERIAL_MISMATCH` — which also means an order that was never serialized can never be registered, since a null serial matches nothing. At most one registration per order may be `ACTIVE` at a time, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_ALREADY_REGISTERED`. That uniqueness rung outranks both of the other two rules despite being stated last here: a second registration made through a shop that has since been suspended, or naming the wrong serial, still reports `ERR_WARRANTY_ALREADY_REGISTERED`. Between the remaining two, authorization is weighed before the serial, so an unauthorized shop citing a mismatched serial reports `ERR_SHOP_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.

The coverage window is set from the instrument, not from the shop. `coverage_months` is sixty when the order's `is_custom_shop` is true and twenty-four otherwise. `coverage_ends_at` is the `purchase_date` advanced by that many whole calendar months, expressed at `00:00:00Z` — calendar months, never thirty-day approximations — and clamped to the last valid day of the target month when the source day does not exist there. A custom-shop instrument purchased on 2026-08-22 is covered to 2031-08-22T00:00:00Z; a standard instrument bought the same day is covered to 2028-08-22T00:00:00Z; a purchase on 2026-08-31 with sixty months of coverage ends 2031-08-31, and one on 2026-01-31 with twenty-four months ends 2028-01-31. The registration opens in status `ACTIVE`, and the registering shop's `warranty_claim_count` reflects the count of its `ACTIVE` registrations — despite its name it counts registrations rather than repair claims, and it is derived at read time from the registration table rather than stored on the shop row and stepped up and down, so creating a registration leaves the shop's own `updated_at` and `version` untouched.

A `ServiceNetworkManager` may void a registration, optionally supplying a `reason`; when present the reason must be a non-empty string, and it is validated and then discarded rather than written onto the registration. Voiding moves it `ACTIVE → VOID`, which is terminal, and the registering shop consequently reports one fewer in `warranty_claim_count` at the next read — that count being the read-time figure just described, the shop's own row is not written. Voiding a registration that is already `VOID` is `ERR_WARRANTY_STATE_INVALID`.

Export runs in parallel. A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a shipment against the order with a `destination_country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters. The order may be at any `build_stage` when the file is opened — the serialization requirement bites at release and not at creation, so an export file may legitimately be opened against an instrument still on the bench. Creation snapshots the compliance position of the order at that instant: `cites_permit_required` and `required_cites_appendix` are copied from the order's `cites_permit_required` and `strictest_cites_appendix` and thereafter belong to the shipment. The shipment opens in status `DOCS_PENDING` with `cites_permit_number` and `released_by_user_id` null.

Creation also prices the customs declaration, in exactly this order. **Step one** sets `goods_value_cents` to the order's `binding_quote_total_cents`, read as zero when the order carries no binding quote yet — a file opened before the options were ever locked therefore prices at the first-tier floor, with `goods_value_cents` 0, `doc_fee_cents` 3000, `insurance_cents` 27 and `declared_value_cents` 3027. **Step two** selects the fee tier from `goods_value_cents` alone: at or below 500000 cents the shipment is in the first tier, at or below 2000000 cents the second, and otherwise the third, with both ceilings inclusive, so a goods value of exactly 500000 is first-tier and 500001 is second-tier. **Step three** sets `doc_fee_cents` to the larger of the tier-rate fee — `goods_value_cents` multiplied by the tier's rate in basis points (150 for the first tier, 110 for the second, 80 for the third), divided by 10000 and rounded half up — and a floor of 3000 cents, and then adds 12500 cents when `cites_permit_required` is true. **Step four** sets `insurance_cents` to `goods_value_cents` plus `doc_fee_cents`, multiplied by 90 basis points, divided by 10000 and rounded half up; the insurance base includes the documentation fee and excludes insurance itself. **Step five** sets `declared_value_cents` to `goods_value_cents` plus `doc_fee_cents` plus `insurance_cents`. Rounding happens at step three and again at step four, never once at the end. The same five steps are re-evaluated when documents are attached, and that re-run reads `goods_value_cents` afresh from the order's current `binding_quote_total_cents` while taking the 12500-cent endorsement question from the shipment's own snapshot rather than from the order's present posture — a binding total that has moved in the meantime re-prices the declaration, whereas a change in the order's `cites_permit_required` does not.

The tier is keyed on the goods value and never on the declared value that the fee and the insurance later inflate; re-deriving the tier from `declared_value_cents` is a circular reading that this specification forbids. The difference is observable near the boundary. A shipment with a `goods_value_cents` of 498000 and `cites_permit_required` false sits in the first tier: the fee is the larger of 7470 and 3000, so 7470; insurance is 90 basis points of 505470, or 4549; and `declared_value_cents` is 510019. The forbidden reading would notice that 510019 exceeds 500000, drop to the second tier, and produce a fee of 5478, insurance of 4531 and a declared value of 508009. The correct answer is 510019. With CITES paperwork the shape is the same: a `goods_value_cents` of 716610 with `cites_permit_required` true is second-tier, giving a tier fee of 7883, a `doc_fee_cents` of 20383 once the 12500-cent endorsement is added, `insurance_cents` of 6633 on a base of 736993, and a `declared_value_cents` of 743626.

Documents are attached from `DOCS_PENDING` or from `DOCS_REJECTED`, moving the shipment to `DOCS_ATTACHED`. When `cites_permit_required` is true the request must carry a non-empty `cites_permit_number` of 1 to 40 characters, otherwise `ERR_CITES_PERMIT_MISSING`; when the contributing lots include no CITES-listed wood at all, `cites_permit_required` is false and the permit number may be omitted entirely. Attaching stores the number, overwriting any value from a previous attempt; an attach that omits it on a shipment that requires no permit leaves whatever number was stored earlier in place rather than clearing it back to null. The issuing authority's objections come back through rejection, which demands a non-empty `reason` in the body — the one `reason` in this specification that is mandatory rather than optional, though it too is validated and then discarded rather than written onto the shipment — and which moves the shipment `DOCS_ATTACHED → DOCS_REJECTED` and retains the `cites_permit_number` for audit while no longer treating it as valid; documents may then be re-attached, closing the negotiation cycle `DOCS_ATTACHED → DOCS_REJECTED → DOCS_ATTACHED`.

Release is the strictest gate in the system, and its four preconditions are evaluated in ladder order. The order's `build_stage` must be `SERIALIZED`, otherwise `ERR_BUILD_STAGE_INVALID` — an unfinished instrument cannot be exported. Every contributing tonewood lot whose `cites_appendix` is not `NONE` must have `permit_status` of `VERIFIED` and `status` of `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_CITES_PERMIT_UNVERIFIED`. That contributing set is the §3.5 walk recomputed live at the moment of release — order, then its `ACCEPTED` and `INSTALLED` parts, then their billets, then their lots — and not the compliance snapshot the shipment took when its file was opened: the shipment's own `cites_permit_required`, `required_cites_appendix` and `cites_permit_number` play no part in this gate, so a lot that became protected after the file was opened still blocks the release, and a shipment that required no permit number is held to exactly the same lot-level rule. The permit walk and the segregation walk below are two separate passes over that set, so an unverified permit on any lot outranks a segregation clash on any other. The releasing officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `attested_by_user_id` of every contributing lot, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the officer who attested the incoming permits may not also sign the wood out of the country. And only then is money considered: the order's `deposit_applied_cents` must be at least twenty percent of `binding_quote_total_cents` — multiply by 2000 basis points, divide by 10000, round half up — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_INSUFFICIENT` with HTTP 402. Because the compliance rungs sit above the fiscal rung, a shipment that is both short of paperwork and short of settlement reports the paperwork, never the money. A successful release moves the shipment `DOCS_ATTACHED → RELEASED`, stamps `released_by_user_id` with the releasing officer, and closes the order commercially by moving `commercial_status` `INVOICED → CLOSED`.

Departure moves the shipment `RELEASED → EXPORTED`. The instrument has physically left the country and no further mutation is possible; `EXPORTED` is terminal. Any of these four operations attempted from a status the transition does not accept is `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID`.
