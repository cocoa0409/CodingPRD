# Household Hazardous Waste Collection Event API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every county solid-waste division runs into the same weekend problem. A parking lot is striped into unload lanes, a licensed disposal contractor sets up lab-pack drums under a canopy, and several hundred residents queue up in their cars with paint cans, pool acid, aerosols and half-empty solvent jugs in the trunk. Some of those residents live in the next county over. Some of them are running a small auto-body shop out of a garage and are legally a business generator, not a household. Some of them are bringing forty kilograms of latex paint for the fourth time this year. And underneath the whole operation sits a hard fiscal reality: the disposal contractor bills by the kilogram against a fiscal-year budget the board has already appropriated, and the board has also adopted a ceiling on what the program may spend per participant. Run the event badly and the county either turns away eligible residents or blows through its appropriation.

This document specifies the backend service that runs that program end to end. The service is mounted under `/hhw` and covers site selection and the environmental containment screen that qualifies a parking lot as a legal unload pad; event scheduling with mobilisation buffers and monthly site caps; resident registration and jurisdiction verification; appointment booking, waitlisting and per-resident quantity quotas; traffic-lane staffing against HAZWOPER certification expiry; the event-day screening ladder that decides whether a weighed material line is accepted, rejected as prohibited, rejected as over-limit, or referred out to a small-quantity-generator program; lab-packing into compatibility-segregated containers; uniform hazardous waste manifesting to a receiving TSDF; and the participation, tonnage, budget and cost-per-participant reporting the board reads afterwards.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is that an event carries **two independent state axes on one row** — a lifecycle axis that tracks the event from planning through reconciliation, and a containment axis that tracks whether the unload pad is legally usable. Neither axis waits on the other, but the containment axis can and does reach across into the lifecycle side and strip reservations off confirmed appointments the moment a pad fails inspection. The second is that money moves through the program in three named buckets — encumbered when a container is sealed, in transit when a manifest ships, spent when the TSDF signs for it — and the program's operating **posture** is derived from those buckets on every single write. When utilisation climbs or containment deficiencies pile up, the program tightens itself: registrations close, appointments stop confirming, and containers stop sealing. The API must make that self-tightening deterministic and auditable rather than a matter of operator judgement.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — without exception, including anonymous public self-registration — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). This check runs before anything else in the request, ahead of body parsing, path resolution and every business rule. Once the headers are accepted, the role is matched against the endpoint's authorised role list; a role that is not listed for the endpoint is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `EventCoordinator`, `ContainmentInspector`, `EligibilityClerk`, `Resident`, `UnloadTech`, `PackingContractor` and `Anonymous`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the county solid-waste division administrator. They own program setup, the published material list, administrative posture overrides, and the signing, receiving and rejection of manifests. An `EventCoordinator` is program staff: they nominate sites, schedule events, drive the event lifecycle axis, and roster lane staffing. A `ContainmentInspector` is an environmental health inspector who screens candidate sites and files event-day containment inspections. An `EligibilityClerk` staffs the front desk, verifying resident jurisdiction, reinstating suspended residents, and booking or managing appointments on a resident's behalf. A `Resident` is a verified county resident acting on their own registration and their own appointments. An `UnloadTech` works the unload lane: checking vehicles in, screening and weighing each material line, referring business generators out, and marking no-shows. A `PackingContractor` is the licensed disposal contractor who lab-packs containers, prepares manifests, and ships them to the receiving facility. `Anonymous` is the unauthenticated public self-registration channel.

Role authorisation is not always decided by the endpoint alone. Every state-driving endpoint in this system takes an `action` field in its payload naming which transition the caller intends, and for two families of endpoint the permitted role depends on that value rather than on the endpoint. Manifest actions are split between two roles that must remain distinct: signing, receiving and rejecting a manifest require `ProgramAdmin`, while shipping and reopening a manifest require `PackingContractor`. A request that carries a permitted role for the manifest-action endpoint but names an action reserved for the other role is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`. The same split-by-action pattern governs site lifecycle actions: approval, suspension and reinstatement belong to `ContainmentInspector`, while retirement belongs to `EventCoordinator`.

**Identity semantics.** Seven of the eight roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `EventCoordinator`, `ContainmentInspector`, `EligibilityClerk`, `UnloadTech`, `PackingContractor` or `Anonymous`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator or session identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server does record those identifiers where a transition writes them — a site's `approved_by_user_id`, a dropoff line's `screened_by_user_id`, a container's `sealed_by_user_id`, a manifest's `prepared_by_user_id` and `signed_by_user_id`, and the `inspector_user_id` stamped into an event's containment findings — but recording an opaque identifier is not the same as resolving it to an entity, and no lookup is ever performed against it.

The `Resident` role is the single exception. When `X-User-Role` is `Resident`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Resident` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a resident compare that resource's `resident_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Resident` may book an appointment only for their own `resident_id`, and may act on an appointment only when that appointment's `resident_id` equals their `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership test is scoped to appointment booking and appointment transitions; it does not restrict read endpoints, which every authenticated role may call.

One further identity rule cuts across the inspection workflow and exists purely as a segregation-of-duties control. The `ContainmentInspector` who drove a site to approval — recorded on that site as `approved_by_user_id` — may not personally file an event-day containment inspection for any event held at that site. When the acting inspector's `X-User-ID` equals the site's `approved_by_user_id`, the inspection is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT` (HTTP 403). The county's position is that the person who certified the pad on paper should not be the person who certifies it on the day.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. Where two rows share a `created_at` value, the tie-break is described alongside the workflow that produces it. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any rows remain beyond the current page.

Filters on list endpoints are the sole reverse-navigation mechanism in this API: because resources are addressed flatly, the way to find an event's appointments is to filter the appointment list by `event_id`, and the way to find a container's packed lines is to filter the dropoff-line list by `container_id`. Sites may be filtered by `program_id`, by `replaces_site_id`, and by `status`. Material categories may be filtered by `program_id`, by `acceptance_policy` and by `compatibility_class`. Residents may be filtered by `program_id` and by `eligibility_status`. Events may be filtered by `program_id`, by `site_id`, and — independently — by `lifecycle_status` or by `containment_status`. Staffers may be filtered by `program_id`, by `status` and by `role_code`. Lane assignments may be filtered by `event_id`, by `staffer_id` and by `status`. Appointments may be filtered by `event_id`, by `resident_id`, by `status` and by `waitlist_reason`. Dropoff lines may be filtered by `appointment_id`, by `material_category_id`, by `container_id` and by `disposition`. Containers may be filtered by `event_id`, by `manifest_id` and by `status`. Manifests may be filtered by `event_id`, by `status` and by `superseded_by_manifest_id`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client — a client-provided `id` in a creation payload is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation with three fractional digits.

Every entity in this system additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There are no version-free or timestamp-free entities here: programs, sites, material categories, residents, collection events, staffers, lane assignments, appointments, dropoff lines, packed containers and manifests all carry the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

The `version` counter is load-bearing in two directions. It is the value clients quote back to the server under the stale-version guards described in §2.7, and it is the visible evidence that a cascade actually touched a row. When a write to one entity produces a side effect on another — an appointment cancellation that decrements a resident's reserved quota, a manifest rejection that returns containers to sealed state — every row the cascade genuinely mutates has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. Rows a cascade merely reads are untouched. A rejected request writes nothing at all: no state change, no counter movement, no `version` increment, no `updated_at` refresh. This last point matters for the site containment screen in particular, where a failed approval attempt must leave `last_inspection_result` unwritten and the site's `version` exactly where it was.

Two endpoints are explicitly read-only despite being addressed by a verb-like path. The computed cost report on a program is a pure projection and does not increment the program's `version`. The logical-clock endpoint mutates no entity and increments no `version` on any row.

### 2.5. Request Validation Order

Validation is a strict ladder, not a set of independent checks. The rules below are evaluated in ascending priority order and the **first** rule that fails decides the response; no later rule is consulted, and no partial write occurs. Two implementations that disagree about which error a doubly-invalid request should return are not equivalent, so the full ordering is pinned here.

1. Context headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the eight declared roles — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_CONTEXT_HEADER`.
2. The role is authorised for the endpoint and, on the manifest-action endpoint, for the specific action named — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`.
3. Request shape: every required body field present and of the declared type, every enum value a declared member, every required array non-empty, every numeric range satisfied, and — for event scheduling — `window_end_utc` strictly after `window_start_utc` and falling on the same UTC calendar date. Violations return `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The numeric ranges policed here are: `declared_total_kg` greater than zero, `net_weight_kg` greater than zero, `lane_count` at least 1, `vehicle_count` either 1 or 2, `capacity_kg` greater than zero, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, and `offset` at least zero. This rule guards every mutating endpoint and every list endpoint.
4. Path identifier resolution, evaluated left to right along the URL: each identifier in the path must resolve to a stored row before the next is considered. The failure code names the missing entity — `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATERIAL_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LANE_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTAINER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_FOUND` — each at HTTP 404. This rule guards every endpoint that takes a path identifier.
5. Foreign-key resolution inside the request body, evaluated in this declared order: `program_id`, then `site_id`, then `replaces_site_id`, then `event_id`, then `resident_id`, then `staffer_id`, then `appointment_id`, then `material_category_id`, then each element of `container_ids`, then `dropoff_line_id`. The same not-found codes apply, at HTTP 404. A missing `dropoff_line_id` reports `ERR_CONTAINER_NOT_FOUND`, and a `declared_category_ids` element that names no stored category reports `ERR_MATERIAL_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`. This rule guards every mutating endpoint that carries foreign keys.
6. Resident ownership, on appointment booking and appointment transitions — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
7. Inspector independence, on event containment inspections — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT`.
8. Stale-version guards on appointment booking and manifest preparation — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
9. The requested action is a declared transition out of the target row's current state on the relevant axis — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). This includes any action attempted against a terminal state. The rule guards site transitions, event lifecycle transitions, appointment transitions, container transitions, manifest transitions, resident eligibility actions, lane-assignment release, container line-packing, and dropoff-line creation.
10. The target event is open for registration, when booking, confirming, waitlisting or promoting — otherwise `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ACCEPTING_REGISTRATIONS` (HTTP 409). This guards appointment creation and the confirm, waitlist and promote actions.
11. The event's site is approved, when scheduling an event or confirming its site — otherwise `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409).
12. The event's containment axis is neither deficient nor pending inspection, when confirming or promoting an appointment, checking a vehicle in, starting the event, or opening registration — otherwise `ERR_CONTAINMENT_DEFICIENT` (HTTP 409).
13. Every container named for a new manifest is sealed and unattached — otherwise `ERR_CONTAINER_NOT_SEALED` (HTTP 409).
14. The program is not frozen — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` (HTTP 409).
15. The program is fully open, for the narrower set of operations that a restricted posture also blocks — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409).
16. The site containment screen — otherwise `ERR_CONTAINMENT_REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422).
17. Staffer certification and roster status — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422).
18. No prohibited material declared at booking — otherwise `ERR_PROHIBITED_MATERIAL_DECLARED` (HTTP 422).
19. Household origin, when confirming, waitlisting or promoting — otherwise `ERR_BUSINESS_WASTE_EXCLUDED` (HTTP 422).
20. Compatibility class match when packing a line — otherwise `ERR_INCOMPATIBLE_MATERIAL_CLASS` (HTTP 422).
21. Same-event referential integrity for packing and manifesting — otherwise `ERR_CONTAINER_EVENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).
22. The line is unpacked and accepted — otherwise `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_PACKED` (HTTP 409).
23. Staffer interval geometry — otherwise `ERR_STAFFER_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
24. Site occupancy geometry — otherwise `ERR_SITE_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
25. Site monthly event cap — otherwise `ERR_SITE_MONTHLY_EVENT_CAP` (HTTP 409).
26. The arrival slot lies inside the event window — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_EVENT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
27. The arrival slot is aligned to a quarter-hour boundary — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_NOT_ALIGNED` (HTTP 422).
28. The resident is eligible, when confirming, waitlisting or promoting — otherwise `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 403).
29. Container capacity — otherwise `ERR_CONTAINER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).
30. Event slot capacity, when confirming or promoting — otherwise `ERR_EVENT_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
31. Program budget authority, when sealing a container — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

Several consequences of this ordering are worth naming explicitly, because they are counter-intuitive. A booking request that names a nonexistent event **and** quotes a stale resident version reports the missing event, because foreign-key resolution sits at rung 5 and the stale-version guard at rung 8. A confirm request against a business-origin appointment held by an ineligible resident reports `ERR_BUSINESS_WASTE_EXCLUDED`, not `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, because household origin is checked at rung 19 and resident eligibility at rung 28. A confirm request on a frozen program whose event is also over capacity reports `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`, because the posture gate at rung 14 precedes the capacity gate at rung 30. And an attempt to seal a container that would breach both the compatibility rules of its contents and the program budget never reaches the budget check, which is the last rung on the ladder.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision, Rounding and Unit Conventions

Money in this system is always expressed in whole cents as an integer. There is no fractional-cent representation anywhere in the API surface; every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer.

Mass is expressed in kilograms as a decimal number rounded to three decimal places, with ties rounded half up. This applies to every accumulated mass figure: `annual_quantity_reserved_kg`, `annual_quantity_settled_kg`, `settled_kg`, `accepted_tonnage_kg`, `rejected_tonnage_kg`, `tonnage_reported_kg`, `tonnage_ytd_kg`, and a container's or manifest's aggregate `net_weight_kg`.

Durations are expressed in hours as a decimal number rounded to two decimal places, ties half up — this covers a lane assignment's `duration_hours`, a staffer's `assigned_hours`, an event's `certified_staff_hours`, and an event's `window_duration_hours`.

One rate value carries finer precision: a manifest's `blended_rate_cents_per_kg` is rounded to four decimal places, ties half up, and is zero whenever the manifest's aggregate mass is zero.

Where a calculation calls for truncation rather than rounding, this document says so explicitly, and truncation always means discarding the fractional part toward zero. The capacity legs of the slot-capacity calculation, the volume rebate, and cost-per-participant all truncate; the contingency reserve and all mass and duration aggregations round half up.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Booking and Manifest Preparation

Two write operations in this system quietly destroy a sibling record as a legitimate part of their normal behaviour, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against a parent row's `version`.

Booking an appointment supersedes the resident's existing non-terminal appointment at the same event: the prior appointment is cancelled with a `cancel_reason` of `SUPERSEDED` and its reservation released. A client working from a stale picture of the resident would therefore discard a booking it had never seen. To prevent that, an appointment-creation request must carry `expected_resident_version` as a required, non-optional field whose value is the `version` of the target `Resident` row as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the resident's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and nothing is written.

Preparing a manifest supersedes any draft manifest already open on the same event: the prior draft is voided and its containers returned to sealed state. The same exposure applies, and the same remedy: a manifest-creation request must carry `expected_event_version` as a required, non-optional field whose value is the `version` of the target `CollectionEvent` row as the client last observed it. A value that does not equal the event's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412, and no manifest is created, no draft is voided, and no container changes state.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Confirming an appointment, sealing a container, filing an inspection and every other write proceed without a version quote, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Program Posture: the Fiscal and Containment Gate

A program's `posture` is a derived value — never written directly by a client — that summarises whether the program may keep taking on commitments. It takes one of three values: `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is recomputed at the end of every request that moves `budget_encumbered_cents`, `budget_in_transit_cents` or `budget_spent_cents`, that changes `open_containment_deficiency_count`, or that changes any event's `projected_cost_per_participant_cents`.

The derivation begins with a committed total: the sum of `budget_encumbered_cents`, `budget_in_transit_cents` and `budget_spent_cents`. The raw posture is then determined by two tiers, in order. The program is `FROZEN` when the committed total is greater than or equal to `budget_cents`, or when `open_containment_deficiency_count` has reached two or more. Failing that, the program is `RESTRICTED` when the committed total has reached or passed eighty-five percent of `budget_cents`, or when at least one collection event of the program is currently open for registration and that event's `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` is strictly greater than the program's `cost_per_participant_ceiling_cents`. Otherwise the raw posture is `OPEN`. The utilisation comparison is performed by cross-multiplication — the committed total multiplied by one hundred against `budget_cents` multiplied by eighty-five — so that no floating-point division enters the decision. Only events currently open for registration participate in the projected-cost clause; events that are planned, site-confirmed, in progress, closed, reconciled or cancelled are excluded from it entirely.

An administrative override sits on top of the raw posture. When `posture_override` holds the value `OPEN` and the logical clock has not yet reached `posture_override_expires_utc`, the effective `posture` is reported as `OPEN` — **but only when the raw posture is not `FROZEN`**. An override can lift a restriction; it can never lift a freeze. When `posture_override` holds its default value of `NONE`, or when the override has expired, the effective posture is simply the raw posture. `posture_override_expires_utc` is null whenever `posture_override` is `NONE`.

Each time the effective posture moves from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED`, from `OPEN` to `FROZEN`, or from `RESTRICTED` to `FROZEN` within a single request, `budget_alert_count` is incremented by one. That counter is monotonic: it never decrements when the posture relaxes again.

The two tiers gate different sets of operations, and the difference is deliberate. A `FROZEN` posture blocks event creation, site confirmation, opening registration, appointment confirmation and promotion, container sealing, and manifest creation — those requests are rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`. It also blocks site approval and site reinstatement. A `RESTRICTED` posture blocks a strictly narrower set: opening registration, and appointment confirmation and promotion — those requests are rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`. Sealing a container, preparing a manifest, and scheduling an event all remain permitted while merely restricted. Because the freeze gate is evaluated before the restriction gate, a frozen program always reports the freeze code.

### 2.9. The Logical Clock

Every temporal comparison in this specification — whether a registration window has opened, whether an event window has closed, whether a posture override has expired, what instant to stamp on a check-in — reads a server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any client moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdmin` advances it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity and increments no row's `version`; it is a test-driving affordance, not a business operation.

### 2.10. Cascade Ordering and Atomicity

Many operations in this system fan out into secondary and tertiary effects — an inspection failure that waitlists appointments, that releases reservations, that moves a projected cost, that tightens a posture. Three rules govern all of them.

First, cascades are **deterministic in order**. Whenever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, those rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order. This applies to appointments swept by an event closure or cancellation, appointments cancelled because their resident was suspended, lane assignments released by an event cancellation, containers returned by a manifest rejection, and containers re-attached by a manifest reopening. Where the waitlist promotion sweep needs a tie-break beyond `created_at`, it uses the appointment's `queue_seq` ascending; a resource's `id` is never consulted as an ordering key anywhere in this system.

Second, cascades are **atomic with their trigger**. A request either applies its primary transition together with every downstream effect, or applies nothing at all. There is no intermediate state in which an event has become deficient but its confirmed appointments are still holding reservations.

Third, cascades **complete before the response is rendered**. A response body reflects the fully settled world, including every recomputed rollup and the final posture.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program and Publishing the Material List

A county's participation in this system begins when a `ProgramAdmin` establishes the program of record for a fiscal year. The program is created with a `name`, the `fiscal_year` it covers (a four-digit year between 2020 and 2100 inclusive), the `budget_cents` of combined disposal and staffing authority the board has appropriated, the `cost_per_participant_ceiling_cents` the board has adopted as its efficiency target, the `staff_hourly_rate_cents` at which loaded event staffing is charged back, and `eligible_jurisdiction_codes` — a non-empty array of the jurisdiction codes whose residents the program serves, typically written in a hyphenated state-and-city form such as `CO-DEN` or `CO-ARV`. Budget authority may legitimately be zero, but neither the per-participant ceiling nor the hourly rate may be; both must be strictly greater than zero.

A newly created program starts fully open. Its `posture` is `OPEN`, `posture_override` is `NONE` with a null `posture_override_expires_utc`, and every derived counter starts at zero: `budget_encumbered_cents`, `budget_in_transit_cents`, `budget_spent_cents`, `open_containment_deficiency_count`, `budget_alert_count`, `participant_count_ytd` and `tonnage_ytd_kg`. Those counters are never written directly. `budget_encumbered_cents` is at all times the sum of `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` across the program's containers that are sealed or manifested; `budget_in_transit_cents` is the same sum across containers that are shipped; `budget_spent_cents` is the sum of `invoiced_disposal_cost_cents` across the program's manifests that have been received; `open_containment_deficiency_count` is the number of the program's events whose containment axis currently reads deficient; `participant_count_ytd` is the number of the program's appointments that have completed; and `tonnage_ytd_kg` is the sum of `net_weight_kg` across the program's received manifests, rounded to three decimal places.

An administrator may record a posture override at any time by supplying `posture_override` and `posture_override_expires_utc`. The override is written to the program, the posture is immediately recomputed under the rules of §2.8, and the program's `version` advances by one. This is the only path by which either field changes.

The published material list is the other half of program setup, and it is what every screening decision on event day ultimately reads. Each material category is created against a `program_id` with a `code` — an uppercase snake-case token such as `LATEX_PAINT`, `SOLVENT`, `POOL_ACID` or `EXPLOSIVE`, unique within its program — and a `display_name` that appears on the public accepted-materials flyer. The `acceptance_policy` is the fork in the road: `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMIT` means the material is taken up to the stated quantity ceilings, while `REJECTED_ALWAYS` means it is never taken at an HHW event under any circumstances. Every category also declares a `compatibility_class` drawn from `FLAMMABLE`, `CORROSIVE_ACID`, `CORROSIVE_BASE`, `OXIDIZER`, `TOXIC`, `AEROSOL` and `UNIVERSAL_WASTE`, which is the lab-pack segregation class that governs which drum the material may go into; a `dot_hazard_class` string printed on the manifest line, such as `3`, `8`, `6.1` or `NA`; and a `business_referral_program_code` naming the small-quantity-generator program a business is pointed toward for that material, which defaults to `CESQG-DROPOFF` when the payload omits it.

Two quantity ceilings and one rate complete the category. `max_kg_per_resident_per_event` caps how much of that material a single resident may have accepted at one event, `max_kg_per_resident_per_year` caps how much may be accepted across the whole fiscal year, and `disposal_rate_cents_per_kg` is what the contractor charges to dispose of it. Both ceilings must be strictly greater than zero; the disposal rate may be zero, which is how a program handles a category the contractor takes at no charge.

### 3.2. Site Selection and the Containment Screen

Site selection is where environmental liability is decided, and the county treats it as an inspection, not a booking. An `EventCoordinator` nominates a candidate location by registering it against a `program_id` with a `name` such as "Arvada Public Works Yard", an `address`, and the three physical attributes that decide whether it is legally usable as an unload pad: `impervious_surface`, a boolean that is true only when the unload pad is fully asphalt or concrete; `secondary_containment_liters`, the rated containment volume of the pad; and `storm_drain_distance_m`, the distance in metres from the pad edge to the nearest storm-drain inlet. The coordinator also states `lane_count`, the number of physical unload lanes the site can stripe, which must be at least one. When the new site is intended to take over from a location the county is retiring, the payload names that predecessor as `replaces_site_id`; the field is null for a first-generation site.

Every new site lands in `CANDIDATE` status with a null `approved_by_user_id` and no `last_inspection_result`. Only a `ContainmentInspector` can move it forward. Approval — the transition `CANDIDATE → APPROVED` — is granted only when all three containment thresholds hold at once: the surface must be impervious, `secondary_containment_liters` must be at least 2000, and `storm_drain_distance_m` must be at least 15.0 metres. All three are inclusive floors: a pad rated at exactly 2000 litres passes, and a drain inlet at exactly 15.0 metres passes. If any one of the three fails, the request is rejected with `ERR_CONTAINMENT_REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET` and — this is the part implementations most often get wrong — **nothing is written**. The site stays in `CANDIDATE`, `last_inspection_result` is left exactly as it was, and the site's `version` does not move. A failed screen is not a recorded failure; it is a non-event. Approval also requires that the owning program is not frozen.

A successful approval writes three things: `status` becomes `APPROVED`, `approved_by_user_id` is stamped with the `X-User-ID` of the acting inspector, and `last_inspection_result` becomes `PASS`. An optional `finding_code` may accompany any site lifecycle action for the inspector's own record. That stamped `approved_by_user_id` is what later powers the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.1: the same person may not both approve the pad and certify it on event day.

Suspension runs `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` and carries no preconditions at all — an inspector who finds a cracked pad on a Tuesday can suspend it immediately. The consequences, however, reach well beyond the site row. The site's `last_inspection_result` becomes `FAIL`, and every collection event at that site whose lifecycle axis currently reads `SITE_CONFIRMED` or `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION` has its containment axis forced to `DEFICIENT`, with an entry recording an origin of `SITE` and a result of `FAIL` appended to that event's `containment_findings`. Because those events have just become deficient, the full containment cascade described in §3.6 fires for each of them — every one of their confirmed appointments drops to the waitlist and gives back its reservation.

Reinstatement runs `SUSPENDED → APPROVED` and re-runs the same three-threshold containment screen, rejecting with `ERR_CONTAINMENT_REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET` when the pad still does not qualify, and requires that the program is not frozen. On success the site returns to `APPROVED`, `approved_by_user_id` is re-stamped from the reinstating inspector's `X-User-ID`, and `last_inspection_result` returns to `PASS`. What reinstatement does **not** do is restore any event's containment axis. Events that were forced deficient by the suspension stay deficient until a fresh event-day inspection is filed against each of them individually. The site being sound again is not evidence that any particular event's pad has been re-checked.

Retirement is the one site action that belongs to the `EventCoordinator` rather than the inspector, and it can be reached from either `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`. It is permitted only when the site has no live commitments: no collection event at that site may currently sit in `PLANNED`, `SITE_CONFIRMED`, `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION` or `IN_PROGRESS`. `RETIRED` is terminal — no further site action is accepted, and any attempt returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.3. Scheduling the Collection Event

A collection event is scheduled by an `EventCoordinator` against a `program_id` and a `site_id`, with a public collection window bounded by `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`. The window is inclusive at both ends for the purposes of describing it to the public; `window_end_utc` must be strictly later than `window_start_utc`, and both instants must fall on the same UTC calendar date, since these are one-day events. A window that spans midnight, or that ends at or before it starts, is rejected as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The coordinator may also state `min_viable_registrations`, the board-set floor of confirmed registrations below which the event is considered undersubscribed; it defaults to 20 when omitted and may not be negative.

Two values are computed at creation and never supplied by the client. `event_date` is the UTC calendar date of `window_start_utc` written as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` string — it is the key on which staffing certification and monthly caps are judged. `window_duration_hours` is the elapsed time between the two window bounds expressed in hours and rounded half up to two decimal places; an eight-in-the-morning to eight-in-the-evening window is `12.00`.

Three gates guard the creation itself. The site must currently be `APPROVED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPROVED`. The program must not be frozen, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN`. And the site must actually be free.

Site availability is judged on a **closed** interval widened symmetrically on both sides. Every non-cancelled event already at that site projects an occupancy band running from two full days before its own `window_start_utc` to two full days after its own `window_end_utc` — that is, a mobilisation-and-teardown buffer of forty-eight hours on each side. The new event's own closed window, from `window_start_utc` through `window_end_utc` inclusive, is tested against every such band, and if it intersects any of them at even a single instant the request is rejected with `ERR_SITE_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`. Because both intervals are closed, touching a buffered endpoint exactly is a conflict, not a clean fit: a new event beginning at precisely the instant two days after an existing event's window ended is refused. Cancelled events project no band at all and never block a rescheduling.

A second, coarser constraint sits alongside the buffer. No site may host more than two non-cancelled events whose `event_date` falls in the same UTC calendar month, expressed as year-and-month. When a site already hosts two such events, a third is rejected with `ERR_SITE_MONTHLY_EVENT_CAP`. Consider a site with events on the third and the twenty-first of September: a fourth-of-October event is fine, but a thirtieth-of-September event is refused on the cap even if it clears the forty-eight-hour buffer comfortably.

A newly scheduled event opens with `lifecycle_status` of `PLANNED` and `containment_status` of `PENDING_INSPECTION`. Its `slot_capacity` computes to zero, because no lanes are staffed and no pad has been certified.

The lifecycle axis then advances under the `EventCoordinator`'s hand. Confirming the site moves `PLANNED → SITE_CONFIRMED` and requires that the site is still `APPROVED` and the program is not frozen. Opening registration moves `SITE_CONFIRMED → OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION` and is the most heavily gated transition on the axis: the event's containment axis must read exactly `COMPLIANT`, the logical clock must still be strictly before `window_start_utc`, and the program's posture must be fully `OPEN` — a merely restricted program cannot open registrations, and that rejection carries `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED`, while a frozen one carries `ERR_PROGRAM_FROZEN` and a non-compliant pad carries `ERR_CONTAINMENT_DEFICIENT`. On success, `slot_capacity` and `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` are recomputed and the program's posture is recomputed.

Starting the event moves `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION → IN_PROGRESS` and requires three things: the logical clock must have reached or passed `window_start_utc`, the containment axis must read either `COMPLIANT` or `REMEDIATED`, and `open_lane_count` must be at least one. A remediated pad is good enough to open the gates on the day, even though it was not good enough to open registrations weeks earlier.

Closing moves `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED` and requires only that the logical clock has reached or passed `window_end_utc`. Closing sweeps the day's loose ends in two passes, each over the event's appointments in `created_at` ascending order. First, every appointment still sitting `WAITLISTED` transitions to `EXPIRED`. Second, every appointment still sitting `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` transitions to `NO_SHOW`, releasing whatever reservation it held. Because those appointments have entered `NO_SHOW`, each affected resident's `no_show_strike_count` is recomputed and the automatic suspension rule of §3.5 is evaluated for each of them in turn. An event resting in `CLOSED` accepts no further registrations, check-ins or unload activity; the only action left to it is reconciliation.

Reconciliation moves `CLOSED → RECONCILED` and is the accountant's gate. It is permitted only when every manifest of the event has reached `RECEIVED` or `VOIDED`; when every packed container of the event is either `DISPOSED`, or `OPEN` with a `line_count` of zero; and when the event's `discrepancy_container_count` is exactly zero. An event holding a container the receiving facility flagged cannot be reconciled until that discrepancy is worked through. On success, `tonnage_reported_kg` is frozen at its current value and the event enters a terminal state.

Cancellation is available from `PLANNED`, from `SITE_CONFIRMED` and from `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION`, always with the supplied `cancel_reason` recorded on the event, and it has no preconditions. What it cascades depends on where it starts, and the three branches differ. Cancelling from `PLANNED` cascades nothing — no lanes are rostered and no one has booked. Cancelling from `SITE_CONFIRMED` releases staffing: every lane assignment of the event still `ACTIVE` transitions to `RELEASED` with a `released_reason` of `EVENT_CANCELLED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and because those assignments were released each affected staffer's `assigned_hours` is recomputed while the event's own `certified_staff_hours` and `staffing_cost_cents` fall to zero. Cancelling from `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION` does both halves: every appointment of the event sitting `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_reason` of `EVENT_CANCELLED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and because those appointments were cancelled each affected resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` is recomputed and the event's `reserved_slot_count` falls to zero; then, exactly as in the `SITE_CONFIRMED` branch, every `ACTIVE` lane assignment is released with a `released_reason` of `EVENT_CANCELLED` in `created_at` ascending order. Both `RECONCILED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal on the lifecycle axis.

### 3.4. Rostering Lanes, Certified Hours and Vehicle Slot Capacity

Staffing an HHW event is a certification problem before it is a scheduling problem. An `EventCoordinator` registers each person on the roster against a `program_id` with a `full_name`, a `role_code` drawn from `TRAFFIC`, `UNLOAD`, `CHEMIST` and `SUPERVISOR`, and a `hazwoper_expires_on` date — the expiry of their HAZWOPER refresher training, written as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` string. A staffer also carries a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`; an inactive staffer remains on the roster for the record but may not be put on a lane. Their `assigned_hours` starts at zero.

Rostering someone onto a lane creates a lane assignment naming an `event_id`, a `staffer_id`, a `lane_number` of at least one, and the interval `start_utc` through `end_utc`. The interval must lie inside the event window and `end_utc` must be strictly later than `start_utc`. From those two instants the system computes `duration_hours` — the elapsed time in hours rounded half up to two decimal places — and a single shift is capped at twelve hours: `duration_hours` must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than `12.0`. The assignment is created `ACTIVE` with a null `released_reason`.

Two safety rules guard the roster. The first is certification: the staffer's `hazwoper_expires_on` must be no earlier than the event's `event_date`, compared as plain date strings so that an expiry falling exactly on the event date passes, and the staffer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. A lapsed or inactive staffer is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`.

The second is interval geometry, and it is deliberately asymmetric. A staffer's existing commitment occupies a **half-open** interval running from its own `start_utc` up to but not including its own `end_utc`, and to that interval the system appends — and only appends, at the trailing end — a thirty-minute decontamination buffer. A proposed assignment is rejected with `ERR_STAFFER_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` when its own half-open interval overlaps that buffered band for any `ACTIVE` assignment held by the same staffer, and the comparison is made across every event in the system, not just the event being staffed — a person cannot be at two collection sites at once. Because the buffer hangs off the tail only, a new assignment ending exactly when an existing one begins is accepted, while a new assignment beginning exactly thirty minutes after an existing one ends is also accepted; beginning twenty-nine minutes after it ends is not. Released assignments impose no band whatsoever.

Every assignment write refreshes both the hours ledger and everything downstream of it: creating an assignment recomputes the event's `open_lane_count`, `certified_staff_hours`, `staffing_cost_cents`, `slot_capacity` and `projected_cost_per_participant_cents`, and then recomputes the program's posture, since a change in projected cost per participant can by itself push the program into `RESTRICTED`. A staffer's `assigned_hours` is the sum of `duration_hours` across their `ACTIVE` assignments, rounded half up to two decimal places; released assignments are retained as history and contribute nothing. An event's `certified_staff_hours` is the sum of `duration_hours` across that event's `ACTIVE` assignments, but counting only assignments whose staffer is currently `ACTIVE` and whose `hazwoper_expires_on` is no earlier than the event's `event_date`, again rounded half up to two decimal places. An event's `staffing_cost_cents` is `certified_staff_hours` multiplied by the program's `staff_hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. And an event's `open_lane_count` is the number of **distinct** `lane_number` values across its `ACTIVE` assignments — three people rotating through lane 2 across the day contribute one open lane, not three.

Releasing an assignment is the compensating action. An `EventCoordinator` releases it with an optional `released_reason`, the assignment's `status` becomes `RELEASED`, the staffer's `assigned_hours` drops by that assignment's `duration_hours`, and the event's `open_lane_count`, `certified_staff_hours`, `staffing_cost_cents`, `slot_capacity` and `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` are all recomputed. The assignment's `version` increments by one.

**Vehicle slot capacity.** How many vehicles the event can physically process — its `slot_capacity` — is recomputed on every lane-assignment creation, on every release, on every containment-axis transition, and when registration opens. Three independent legs are each evaluated in full, and the binding constraint is the smallest of the three.

The first leg is lane throughput. A striped lane processes twelve vehicles an hour, so the lane leg is the number of open lanes multiplied by twelve and again by `window_duration_hours`, with any fractional vehicle discarded.

The second leg is staffing. Each vehicle consumes a quarter of a staff-hour — fifteen minutes of certified labour per car — so the staffing leg is `certified_staff_hours` divided by 0.25, with any fractional vehicle discarded.

The third leg is containment, and it is the one that reads the pad. The site's `secondary_containment_liters` supports forty litres of rated containment per vehicle, so the base containment figure is `secondary_containment_liters` divided by 40 with the fraction discarded. That base is then modulated by the event's containment axis: when the axis reads `COMPLIANT` the base stands unmodified; when it reads `REMEDIATED` the base is halved and the fraction discarded, because a remediated pad is run at half throughput as a precaution; and when it reads `PENDING_INSPECTION` or `DEFICIENT` the containment leg is zero, which drives the whole capacity to zero regardless of how many lanes are open.

Worked through: a single open lane on a twelve-hour window with one twelve-hour certified assignment at a site rated 2000 litres yields a lane leg of one times twelve times 12.00, or 144 vehicles; a staffing leg of 12.00 divided by 0.25, or 48 vehicles; and a base containment figure of 2000 divided by 40, or 50 vehicles. On a compliant pad the containment leg is 50 and `slot_capacity` settles at 48, the staffing leg binding. On a remediated pad the containment leg halves to 25 and `slot_capacity` settles at 25 instead. The site's declared `lane_count` bounds what the coordinator can physically stripe but does not itself enter this arithmetic; the capacity follows the lanes actually staffed.

### 3.5. Residents: Registration, Verification, Booking and the Quota Ledger

Residents reach the program through two doors that lead to the same room. A member of the public may self-register anonymously, or an `EligibilityClerk` may register them at the front desk; either way the payload carries a `program_id`, the resident's `full_name`, their `street_address` as the basis for jurisdiction proof, and the `claimed_jurisdiction_code` they self-declare. Both doors produce a resident in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with a `version` of 1, a null `verified_jurisdiction_code`, and zeroed ledgers: `annual_quantity_reserved_kg`, `annual_quantity_settled_kg`, `no_show_strike_count` and `completed_visit_count` all start at zero. The last of those is simply the number of the resident's appointments sitting in `COMPLETED`, and like the strike count it is derived rather than set. A self-declared jurisdiction is a claim, not a determination.

Verification is a clerk's action and always supplies a `verified_jurisdiction_code`, which is recorded on the resident whichever way the determination falls. When the verified code is a member of the program's `eligible_jurisdiction_codes`, the resident becomes `ELIGIBLE`; when it is not, the resident becomes `INELIGIBLE_OUT_OF_JURISDICTION`. Both outcomes are ordinary successful transitions out of `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, not errors — a neighbouring county's resident is a legitimate record the program keeps and simply may not serve. A resident sitting in `INELIGIBLE_OUT_OF_JURISDICTION` may be verified again later, and a corrected code that is in the eligible list moves them to `ELIGIBLE`; this is how a resident who moved across a boundary line, or whose address was mis-keyed, is corrected without creating a duplicate record.

The other eligibility action a clerk may take is reinstatement, which moves a resident from `SUSPENDED` back to `ELIGIBLE` and is permitted only from `SUSPENDED`. Reinstatement notably does **not** reset `no_show_strike_count`. That counter is not a settable penalty box; it is at all times the count of the resident's appointments sitting in `NO_SHOW`, and it keeps that value after reinstatement. The practical consequence is that a reinstated resident whose strike count already stands at or above the threshold will be suspended again by the very next no-show, so the county's expectation is that the clerk who reinstates has resolved the underlying cause first. Suspension itself is never a clerk action: it is applied by the system, described in §3.7.

**Booking.** An appointment is booked either by the `Resident` themselves — and only for their own `resident_id` — or by an `EligibilityClerk` on their behalf. The payload names the `event_id` and `resident_id`, the `slot_start_utc` of the fifteen-minute arrival slot, the `waste_origin` self-declaration of `HOUSEHOLD` or `BUSINESS`, the `declared_total_kg` the resident promises to bring, the `declared_category_ids` array naming which material categories they intend to present, and `expected_resident_version` as described in §2.7. The optional `vehicle_count` states how many vehicles will arrive on the booking; it defaults to 1 and may be no more than 2. `declared_total_kg` must be strictly greater than zero and `declared_category_ids` may not be empty.

Booking is gated on the event and on the slot geometry. The event's `lifecycle_status` must read `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ACCEPTING_REGISTRATIONS`. The `slot_start_utc` must lie inside the **half-open** event window — at or after `window_start_utc` and strictly before `window_end_utc`, so a slot booked at the closing instant is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_EVENT_WINDOW`. And the slot must be aligned to a fifteen-minute boundary measured **from `window_start_utc`**, not from the top of the hour: the offset in minutes from the window's start must be an exact multiple of fifteen, with zero seconds and zero milliseconds, or the request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_NOT_ALIGNED`. A window opening at ten minutes past eight therefore admits slots at ten past, twenty-five past, forty past and so on.

One further screen runs at booking time: no identifier in `declared_category_ids` may name a material category whose `acceptance_policy` is `REJECTED_ALWAYS`. A resident who tells the front desk they are bringing explosives is turned away at the desk rather than at the gate, with `ERR_PROHIBITED_MATERIAL_DECLARED`.

A successful booking creates the appointment in `REQUESTED` status with a null `waitlist_reason` and assigns it the next `queue_seq` for that event — a monotonic per-event sequence number starting at 1 and issued in creation order. It also **supersedes**: any other appointment this resident already holds at this same event with a status of `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_reason` of `SUPERSEDED`, releasing whatever reservation it held. The resident's `version` increments by one as part of the booking, which is precisely why the stale-version guard of §2.7 is there. Note that a `BUSINESS` declaration does not block the booking itself — a business generator may legitimately hold a `REQUESTED` appointment and be dealt with at the desk — it blocks everything that comes after.

**Confirmation and the waitlist.** Confirming an appointment moves `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED` and may be performed by the `EligibilityClerk` or by the owning `Resident`. It carries the longest precondition list in the system. The event must be `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION`; the event's containment axis must read `COMPLIANT` or `REMEDIATED`; the resident must be `ELIGIBLE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; the appointment's `waste_origin` must be `HOUSEHOLD`, or it is refused with `ERR_BUSINESS_WASTE_EXCLUDED`; the program's posture must be fully `OPEN`; and there must be room — the event's `reserved_slot_count` plus one must be no greater than its `slot_capacity`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EVENT_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the appointment's `reserved_kg` is set to its `declared_total_kg`, the resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` rises by `declared_total_kg`, the event's `reserved_slot_count` rises by one and its `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` is recomputed, and the program's posture is recomputed.

Waitlisting moves `REQUESTED → WAITLISTED` and is a clerk-only action for the case where the event is full. It requires only that the event is `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION` and the resident is `ELIGIBLE` — but the business-origin exclusion applies here too, so a `BUSINESS` appointment cannot be parked on the waitlist either. The appointment's `waitlist_reason` is set to `CAPACITY`, and crucially **no reservation is taken**: the resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` is untouched and the event's `reserved_slot_count` does not move. A waitlisted resident holds a place in line, not a slot.

Promotion moves `WAITLISTED → CONFIRMED` and may be driven either by a clerk's explicit action or by the automatic sweep of §3.6. Its preconditions mirror confirmation: the event `OPEN_FOR_REGISTRATION`, the containment axis `COMPLIANT` or `REMEDIATED`, the resident `ELIGIBLE`, the appointment's origin `HOUSEHOLD`, the program's posture `OPEN`, and `reserved_slot_count` plus one no greater than `slot_capacity`. On success `waitlist_reason` is cleared to null, `reserved_kg` becomes `declared_total_kg`, the resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` rises by `declared_total_kg`, and the event's `reserved_slot_count` rises by one with `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` recomputed.

Cancellation is available from `REQUESTED`, from `CONFIRMED` and from `WAITLISTED`, always by the owning `Resident` or an `EligibilityClerk`, and always without preconditions. The supplied `cancel_reason` is recorded and defaults to `RESIDENT_REQUEST`; the permitted values are `RESIDENT_REQUEST`, `SUPERSEDED`, `RESIDENT_SUSPENDED` and `EVENT_CANCELLED`. What differs is what has to be given back. Cancelling from `REQUESTED` releases nothing, because nothing was reserved. Cancelling from `WAITLISTED` likewise releases nothing. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` is the compensating action for confirmation and must undo it in full: `reserved_kg` falls to zero, the resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` falls by `declared_total_kg`, the event's `reserved_slot_count` falls by one with `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` recomputed, and the program's posture is recomputed.

**The two-ledger quota model.** A resident's consumption of the program's capacity is tracked twice over, on purpose, and the two figures are not expected to agree. `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` is the **declared** ledger: it is the sum of `declared_total_kg` across the resident's appointments whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `UNLOADING`, rounded half up to three decimal places. Appointments in `REQUESTED` or `WAITLISTED` reserve nothing, and every terminal status — `COMPLETED`, `REFERRED_OUT`, `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` — reserves nothing. This ledger never inspects a weighed line; it holds capacity against an operator-entered promise. Mirroring it at the appointment level, `reserved_kg` equals `declared_total_kg` while the appointment sits in `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `UNLOADING`, and zero otherwise.

`annual_quantity_settled_kg` is the **weighed** ledger: it is the sum of `net_weight_kg` across every dropoff line belonging to any of the resident's appointments whose `disposition` resolved to `ACCEPTED`, rounded half up to three decimal places. Lines that were rejected or referred out contribute zero kilograms to it, even though they were physically weighed and are still counted in the event's `rejected_tonnage_kg`. The appointment-level mirror is `settled_kg`, the sum of `net_weight_kg` over that appointment's accepted lines, again to three decimal places. A resident who declared 18.5 kg and had 4.5 kg accepted therefore reserved 18.5 and settled 4.5, and completing the appointment moves mass out of the reserved ledger without moving the same quantity into the settled one. That gap is intentional and must not be reconciled away.

### 3.6. Event Day: the Containment Axis and the Waitlist Sweep

The containment axis is the second, wholly independent state axis carried on the collection event row. It moves under a `ContainmentInspector`'s hand alone, it never waits on the lifecycle axis, and the lifecycle axis never waits on it — an event can be closed while its pad is deficient, and a pad can be certified compliant while the event is still merely planned. The only coupling is through preconditions: certain lifecycle transitions read the containment axis before they will proceed, as §3.3 sets out.

Filing an event-day inspection supplies a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, an optional `finding_code` naming what was observed, and an optional `remediation_note` describing what was done about it. Two preconditions guard every inspection regardless of result: the event's lifecycle axis must not have reached `RECONCILED` or `CANCELLED`, and the acting inspector must be independent of the site's approving inspector as §2.1 requires, which is enforced with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT`. Every inspection that is accepted appends an entry to the event's `containment_findings`, an append-only log in which each element records an `origin` of either `SITE` or `EVENT`, a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, the `finding_code`, the `inspector_user_id` of whoever filed it, and the `recorded_at` instant. Entries written by an event-day inspection carry an origin of `EVENT`; entries written by the site-suspension cascade of §3.2 carry an origin of `SITE`.

From `PENDING_INSPECTION`, a passing inspection moves the axis to `COMPLIANT` and recomputes `slot_capacity` — which is what finally lifts the containment leg of §3.4 off zero. A failing inspection from the same starting point moves the axis to `DEFICIENT`, recomputes the program's `open_containment_deficiency_count`, and recomputes the program's posture; there are no confirmed appointments to disturb yet, because registrations cannot have opened on a pad that was never certified.

The consequential transition is a pad that fails after it had passed — `COMPLIANT → DEFICIENT` — and it cascades four levels deep. First, the event's containment axis becomes `DEFICIENT` and the finding is appended. Second, because the event has become deficient, every appointment of that event currently `CONFIRMED` transitions to `WAITLISTED` with a `waitlist_reason` of `CONTAINMENT_DEFICIENCY`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; each such appointment's `reserved_kg` falls to zero and its resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` is reduced by that appointment's `declared_total_kg`. Third, because those reservations were released, the event's `reserved_slot_count` falls and its `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` is recomputed as `staffing_cost_cents` divided by the greater of `reserved_slot_count` and one, with the fraction discarded — which is why a deficiency makes the event look **more** expensive per head, not less: the staffing bill is already committed and there are now fewer participants to spread it over. Fourth, because that projected figure can now exceed the program's `cost_per_participant_ceiling_cents`, the program's posture is recomputed and may land on `RESTRICTED`, in which case `budget_alert_count` increments by one. The program's `open_containment_deficiency_count` is recomputed throughout, and two concurrently deficient events in one program are enough to freeze it outright.

Recovery is deliberately staged and cannot be short-circuited. A passing inspection on a deficient pad moves the axis to `REMEDIATED` — **never straight to `COMPLIANT`**. A second passing inspection then moves `REMEDIATED → COMPLIANT`. An attempt to jump from `DEFICIENT` directly to a compliant state is not a declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A remediated pad that fails again moves `REMEDIATED → DEFICIENT` and runs exactly the same four-level cascade as `COMPLIANT → DEFICIENT`: appointments to the waitlist with a `CONTAINMENT_DEFICIENCY` reason in `created_at` order, reservations released, `reserved_slot_count` and `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` recomputed, and the posture and `budget_alert_count` re-evaluated.

The difference between the two recovery steps is capacity. Entering `REMEDIATED` recomputes `slot_capacity` **with** the half derate of §3.4; entering `COMPLIANT` recomputes it **without** the derate, restoring full containment throughput. Both recovery transitions also recompute the program's `open_containment_deficiency_count` and posture.

**The waitlist promotion sweep.** Both recovery transitions — into `REMEDIATED` and into `COMPLIANT` — run an automatic sweep that tries to put back the people the deficiency displaced. The candidate set is precisely the appointments of this event whose status is `WAITLISTED` **and** whose `waitlist_reason` is `CONTAINMENT_DEFICIENCY`. Appointments waitlisted for `CAPACITY` are never swept; they are promoted only by an explicit clerk action, because they were never displaced by the pad in the first place.

Candidates are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by `queue_seq` ascending; the appointment's `id` is never consulted. Each candidate in turn is promoted to `CONFIRMED` — clearing `waitlist_reason` and re-reserving its `declared_total_kg` on the resident — but only when three conditions all hold at that moment: the event's `reserved_slot_count` plus one is no greater than its `slot_capacity`, the resident is `ELIGIBLE`, and the program's posture is `OPEN`. Each successful promotion raises `reserved_slot_count` by one, and capacity is re-read before the next candidate is considered, so the sweep stops exactly at the capacity line rather than overshooting it.

A candidate that fails any of the three conditions is **left waitlisted and the sweep continues to the next candidate** — it does not halt. This matters when a suspended resident sits early in the queue: their appointment is skipped, and the residents behind them are still promoted. Because capacity is re-read after every promotion, however, a sweep that has already filled the event will fail the capacity test for every remaining candidate and leave them all in place.

### 3.7. The Unload Lane: Check-in, Screening, Referral and No-Shows

Once the lifecycle axis reads `IN_PROGRESS`, the `UnloadTech` working the gate takes over the appointment. Checking a vehicle in moves `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN`, requires that the event is `IN_PROGRESS` and its containment axis reads `COMPLIANT` or `REMEDIATED`, and stamps `check_in_utc` with the current logical-clock instant. Starting the unload moves `CHECKED_IN → UNLOADING` and requires that the event is `IN_PROGRESS` and `open_lane_count` is at least one — there must be a staffed lane to pull into.

**Screening and the disposition ladder.** With the vehicle at the lane, the technician records one dropoff line per material presented, naming the `appointment_id`, the `material_category_id` the material was screened into, the `declared_kg` the resident had claimed for that category, and the `net_weight_kg` actually weighed on the scale. `net_weight_kg` must be strictly greater than zero; `declared_kg` may be zero, which is how a technician records something the resident never mentioned. The technician's `X-User-ID` is stamped onto the line as `screened_by_user_id`.

The line's `disposition` is resolved once, at creation, and frozen on the row thereafter. Resolution walks a four-rung ladder strictly from the top, and **the first rung that matches wins outright** — no lower rung is consulted even if it would also have matched.

Rung one asks whether the material is prohibited outright: if the named category's `acceptance_policy` is `REJECTED_ALWAYS`, the `disposition` is `REJECTED_PROHIBITED` and the `rejection_reason_code` is `PROHIBITED_MATERIAL`.

Rung two asks whether the resident has exhausted their year. The system totals the `net_weight_kg` of every previously accepted line for the **same** material category belonging to any appointment of the **same** resident, across every event of the program, excluding the line now being recorded; if that running total plus the new `net_weight_kg` is strictly greater than the category's `max_kg_per_resident_per_year`, the `disposition` is `REJECTED_OVER_ANNUAL_LIMIT` and the `rejection_reason_code` is `ANNUAL_LIMIT`.

Rung three asks the same question about the day. The system totals the `net_weight_kg` of every previously accepted line for the same material category belonging to the **same appointment**, excluding the line being recorded; if that total plus the new `net_weight_kg` is strictly greater than the category's `max_kg_per_resident_per_event`, the `disposition` is `REJECTED_OVER_EVENT_LIMIT` and the `rejection_reason_code` is `EVENT_LIMIT`.

Rung four is the fall-through: the `disposition` is `ACCEPTED` and the `rejection_reason_code` is null.

Three properties of this ladder are load-bearing. Both limit comparisons breach only on a **strict** excess, so a cumulative total that lands exactly on a ceiling is accepted, not rejected. Only previously **accepted** lines consume quota — a line rejected last spring never eats into this spring's allowance. And the annual rung sits above the event rung on purpose: a line that breaches both ceilings at once reports `REJECTED_OVER_ANNUAL_LIMIT`, never `REJECTED_OVER_EVENT_LIMIT`. That ordering is the difference between telling a resident "come back next year" and telling them "come back next month", and the county wants the more restrictive message to survive.

A rejected line is still a real, recorded line: the request succeeds with HTTP 201 and returns the resolved `disposition`, because rejected material was physically weighed at the gate and must be counted in the event's `rejected_tonnage_kg`. The ladder never raises an HTTP error of its own. A line's `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` is its `net_weight_kg` multiplied by the category's `disposal_rate_cents_per_kg`, rounded half up to whole cents, when the disposition is `ACCEPTED`, and zero in every other case. Its `container_id` is null until it is lab-packed. Recording a line refreshes the appointment's `settled_kg`, the resident's `annual_quantity_settled_kg`, and the event's `accepted_tonnage_kg` and `rejected_tonnage_kg` — where `accepted_tonnage_kg` sums `net_weight_kg` over the event's accepted lines and `rejected_tonnage_kg` sums it over the lines dispositioned `REJECTED_PROHIBITED`, `REJECTED_OVER_ANNUAL_LIMIT`, `REJECTED_OVER_EVENT_LIMIT` or `REFERRED_BUSINESS`, each to three decimal places.

**Completing the visit.** Completing the unload moves `UNLOADING → COMPLETED`, requires the event to be `IN_PROGRESS`, and requires the appointment to carry at least one dropoff line — a vehicle that presented nothing cannot be recorded as a participant. On completion `unload_complete_utc` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, `reserved_kg` falls to zero, the resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` falls by `declared_total_kg` while their `annual_quantity_settled_kg` and `completed_visit_count` are recomputed, the event's `reserved_slot_count` falls by one while its `participant_count` — the number of that event's appointments sitting in `COMPLETED` — rises by one and its `accepted_tonnage_kg` and `rejected_tonnage_kg` are recomputed, and the program's `participant_count_ytd` is recomputed.

**Referring a business generator out.** The parallel branch out of the same part of the flow handles the visitor who turns out to be a business. Referral is available from `UNLOADING` and equally from `CHECKED_IN`; both require the event to be `IN_PROGRESS` and both require a `referral_program_code` to be supplied in the payload. The appointment's status becomes `REFERRED_OUT`, the supplied `referral_program_code` is recorded, and a `referral_packet_code` is minted deterministically by joining three parts with hyphens: the `referral_program_code`, then the event's `event_date`, then the appointment's `queue_seq` — so a referral to `CESQG-DROPOFF` at an event dated 2026-09-12 for the seventh booking of that event reads `CESQG-DROPOFF-2026-09-12-7`. The appointment's `reserved_kg` falls to zero, the resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` falls by `declared_total_kg`, and the event's `reserved_slot_count` falls by one.

Referral also rewrites history on the lines already taken. Every dropoff line of that appointment has its `disposition` rewritten to `REFERRED_BUSINESS`, its `rejection_reason_code` set to `BUSINESS_GENERATOR`, and its `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` set to zero — and because those lines have left `ACCEPTED`, the resident's `annual_quantity_settled_kg` and the event's `accepted_tonnage_kg` and `rejected_tonnage_kg` are all recomputed. `REFERRED_BUSINESS` is the one disposition the screening ladder can never produce; it is written only here. When the referral is taken from `CHECKED_IN` the appointment has no lines yet, so only the reservation release and the `referral_packet_code` minting apply — the branch is otherwise identical. The one thing referral never does is raise `participant_count`: a visitor sent to another program is not a participant of this one, and the distinction propagates all the way into the cost-per-participant figure of §3.9.

**No-shows and automatic suspension.** When the slot passes with nobody in it, the technician marks the appointment `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW`, which requires only that the event is `IN_PROGRESS` and may carry an optional `no_show_note`. The cascade runs four levels. First, the appointment's status becomes `NO_SHOW`, its `reserved_kg` falls to zero, the resident's `annual_quantity_reserved_kg` falls by `declared_total_kg`, and the event's `reserved_slot_count` falls by one. Second, because the appointment has entered `NO_SHOW`, the resident's `no_show_strike_count` is recomputed; when it reaches two or more, the resident transitions `ELIGIBLE → SUSPENDED` automatically. Third, because the resident has become suspended, every **other** appointment they hold with a status of `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_reason` of `RESIDENT_SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order and releasing each reservation as it goes. Fourth, because those appointments were cancelled, each affected event's `reserved_slot_count` is decremented and its `projected_cost_per_participant_cents` recomputed, and the program's posture is recomputed. The same automatic suspension rule fires from the event-closure sweep of §3.3, where a batch of appointments enters `NO_SHOW` at once.

`COMPLETED`, `REFERRED_OUT`, `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal for an appointment; any further action against one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.8. Lab-Packing, Manifesting and Disposal Settlement

Behind the unload lane the `PackingContractor` consolidates loose material into shippable drums. A container is opened against an `event_id` with a `container_code` such as `DRUM-A1` that must be unique within that event, a `compatibility_class` declaring which segregation class the drum accepts, and a `capacity_kg` stating the maximum net mass it may hold, which must be strictly greater than zero. It is created `OPEN`, with `line_count` and `net_weight_kg` at zero, a null `manifest_id`, a null `sealed_by_user_id`, and `discrepancy_flag` false.

Packing a dropoff line into a container names the `dropoff_line_id` and is guarded by four checks. The line's material category must carry the same `compatibility_class` as the container, or the request is refused with `ERR_INCOMPATIBLE_MATERIAL_CLASS` — acid never goes in the flammables drum. The appointment behind the line must belong to the same event as the container, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTAINER_EVENT_MISMATCH`. The line must be unpacked and accepted — its `container_id` still null and its `disposition` exactly `ACCEPTED` — or the request is refused with `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_PACKED`, which is therefore also the code a rejected or referred-out line earns when someone tries to drum it. And the container must have room: its current `net_weight_kg` plus the line's `net_weight_kg` must be no greater than its `capacity_kg`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTAINER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A line that lands exactly on capacity is accepted. On success the line's `container_id` is set and the container's `line_count`, `net_weight_kg` and `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` are recomputed, with the container's `version` incrementing by one.

**The container estimate: round per line, then sum.** A container's `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` is built by valuing each packed line on its own and adding up whole cents. For every dropoff line packed into the container whose `disposition` is `ACCEPTED`, the line's cost is its `net_weight_kg` multiplied by its material category's `disposal_rate_cents_per_kg`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents; the container's estimate is the sum of those already-rounded line costs. The container's `net_weight_kg` is the sum of the same lines' `net_weight_kg`, rounded half up to three decimal places. Only accepted lines with a container participate; rejected and referred lines are never packable and contribute nothing to either figure.

Sealing a container moves `OPEN → SEALED` and is the moment the money is committed. It requires that the container holds at least one line, that the program is not frozen, and that the program can afford it — the sum of `budget_encumbered_cents`, `budget_in_transit_cents`, `budget_spent_cents` and this container's `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` must be no greater than the program's `budget_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 402. On success `sealed_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting contractor's `X-User-ID`, the container's `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` is added to the program's `budget_encumbered_cents`, and the program's posture is recomputed.

Unsealing moves `SEALED → OPEN` and is its compensating action. It is permitted only while the container's `manifest_id` is null — a drum already listed on a manifest cannot be reopened. On success `sealed_by_user_id` is cleared to null and the container's `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` is subtracted from `budget_encumbered_cents`, with the posture recomputed.

**Preparing the manifest.** The contractor prepares a uniform hazardous waste manifest over sealed containers of a single event, naming the `event_id`, a `manifest_tracking_number` that must be unique across every manifest in the system, the `tsdf_epa_id` of the receiving treatment-storage-disposal facility, the `container_ids` being shipped, and `expected_event_version` as §2.7 requires. Every container named must currently be `SEALED` with a null `manifest_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTAINER_NOT_SEALED`, and every container's event must match the manifest's event, or it is refused with `ERR_CONTAINER_EVENT_MISMATCH`. The program must not be frozen.

A successful preparation creates the manifest in `DRAFT` with `prepared_by_user_id` stamped from the contractor's `X-User-ID`, and moves each listed container `SEALED → MANIFESTED` with its `manifest_id` set and its `discrepancy_flag` cleared. It also supersedes: any other manifest of the same event still sitting in `DRAFT` transitions to `VOIDED`, with `superseded_by_manifest_id` set to the new manifest's `id`, and every container that voided manifest was carrying returns `MANIFESTED → SEALED` with its `manifest_id` cleared. `VOIDED` is terminal. The event's `version` increments by one as part of the preparation, which is exactly what the stale-version guard protects. A manifest's `container_ids` is at all times the identifiers of the containers whose `manifest_id` points at it, ordered by container `created_at` ascending; its `net_weight_kg` is the sum of those containers' `net_weight_kg` rounded half up to three decimal places; and its `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` is the sum of those containers' `estimated_disposal_cost_cents`, carrying the round-per-line-then-sum lineage forward unchanged.

Signing moves `DRAFT → SIGNED` and belongs to the `ProgramAdmin` — the county, not the contractor, signs as generator. It requires that `container_ids` is non-empty and that every container on it currently reads `MANIFESTED`. On success `signed_by_user_id` is stamped from the administrator's `X-User-ID` and `signed_at_utc` records the current logical-clock instant.

Shipping moves `SIGNED → SHIPPED` and returns to the contractor, who physically takes the load. It has no preconditions of its own. Every container of the manifest moves `MANIFESTED → SHIPPED`, and each container's `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` moves out of the program's `budget_encumbered_cents` and into its `budget_in_transit_cents` — the same money, a different bucket. The program's posture is recomputed.

**Receiving and the invoice.** Receiving moves `SHIPPED → RECEIVED` under the `ProgramAdmin` and has no preconditions; an optional `invoice_reference` may be recorded with it. This is where the manifest's own money figures are minted, and they are computed on a **different rounding discipline** from the container estimates, on purpose.

The manifest's `net_weight_kg` is the sum of its containers' `net_weight_kg`, rounded half up to three decimal places. A weighted total is then formed by taking every accepted line in every container of the manifest, multiplying each line's `net_weight_kg` by its material category's `disposal_rate_cents_per_kg`, and summing those products **exactly, with no intermediate rounding at all**. The `blended_rate_cents_per_kg` is that exact weighted total divided by the manifest's `net_weight_kg`, rounded half up to four decimal places — or zero when the manifest's `net_weight_kg` is zero. Finally, `invoiced_disposal_cost_cents` is the manifest's `net_weight_kg` multiplied by its `blended_rate_cents_per_kg`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, exactly once.

The two disciplines do not agree, and reconciling them is not the goal. Take one drum holding three accepted latex-paint lines at 155 cents per kilogram weighing 1.5, 2.5 and 0.5 kilograms. The container estimate rounds each line first — 232.5 becomes 233, 387.5 becomes 388, 77.5 becomes 78 — and sums to 699 cents. The manifest invoice sums the exact products to 697.5 against 4.5 kilograms, derives a blended rate of 155.0000, multiplies back to 697.5, and rounds once to 698 cents. Identical inputs, one cent apart: 699 was encumbered when the drum was sealed, 698 is spent when the load is received. That one-cent delta is the program's rounding variance, and it is **never** written back to the container.

The rest of receiving is bookkeeping. Every container of the manifest moves `SHIPPED → DISPOSED`, draining its `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` out of `budget_in_transit_cents`; the manifest's `invoiced_disposal_cost_cents` is added to the program's `budget_spent_cents`; the event's `tonnage_reported_kg` — the sum of `net_weight_kg` across that event's received manifests, rounded half up to three decimal places — and the program's `tonnage_ytd_kg` are recomputed; and the posture is recomputed. `RECEIVED` is terminal. `DISPOSED` is likewise terminal for a container.

**Discrepancy and the revision cycle.** When the receiving facility disputes the load, the `ProgramAdmin` rejects it instead, moving `SHIPPED → DISCREPANCY` with a `discrepancy_code` such as `WEIGHT_VARIANCE`, which the payload must supply. The cascade runs three levels. First, the manifest's status becomes `DISCREPANCY` and the code is recorded. Second, because the manifest has entered discrepancy, every container carrying that `manifest_id` moves `SHIPPED → SEALED`, clears its `manifest_id` and sets its `discrepancy_flag` to true, processed in container `created_at` ascending order; each such move returns that container's `estimated_disposal_cost_cents` from `budget_in_transit_cents` back into `budget_encumbered_cents`, undoing the shipping transfer exactly. Third, because those containers now carry a raised `discrepancy_flag`, the event's `discrepancy_container_count` rises above zero — and that is what blocks the event from reconciling. The posture is recomputed.

Reopening moves `DISCREPANCY → DRAFT` and belongs to the `PackingContractor`. It is permitted only when at least one container of that manifest's event currently carries a raised `discrepancy_flag` with a status of `SEALED` and a null `manifest_id` — there must be something to re-attach. On success the manifest returns to `DRAFT`, its `discrepancy_code` is cleared, and both `signed_by_user_id` and `signed_at_utc` are cleared so the county must sign afresh. Every container of that manifest's event carrying a raised `discrepancy_flag` with a status of `SEALED` and a null `manifest_id` is re-attached: `SEALED → MANIFESTED`, `manifest_id` set to this manifest, `discrepancy_flag` cleared, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Because those flags cleared, the event's `discrepancy_container_count` falls back to zero and reconciliation is unblocked. Throughout the whole revision cycle the re-attached containers stay encumbered in `budget_encumbered_cents` — the money never leaves the program's commitments while the load is being re-papered — and the manifest can then be signed, shipped and received again.

### 3.9. Reporting: Participation, Tonnage and Cost per Participant

The board's question at the end of a season is simple and the arithmetic behind it is not: what did the program cost, and what did it cost per resident served. The cost report is a read-only projection computed on demand and available to `ProgramAdmin` and `EventCoordinator` alike. It reports `event_count`, `participant_count`, `accepted_tonnage_kg`, `rejected_tonnage_kg`, `received_tonnage_kg`, `disposal_subtotal_cents`, `mobilization_fee_cents`, `volume_rebate_cents`, `staffing_cost_cents`, `pre_contingency_cents`, `contingency_cents`, `total_program_cost_cents` and `cost_per_participant_cents`. Reading it mutates nothing.

The steps execute in a fixed order and no step may read a later step's output. First, the set of the program's manifests that have reached `RECEIVED` is gathered; manifests still in `DRAFT`, `SIGNED`, `SHIPPED`, `DISCREPANCY` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing to any figure below. Second, `disposal_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `invoiced_disposal_cost_cents` across those received manifests. Third, `received_tonnage_kg` is the sum of their `net_weight_kg`, rounded half up to three decimal places.

Fourth, the contractor's volume tier is selected from `received_tonnage_kg`. Below two thousand kilograms the mobilisation fee is 250000 cents and there is no volume rebate. From two thousand kilograms up to but not including eight thousand, the mobilisation fee falls to 180000 cents and a three percent rebate applies. At eight thousand kilograms and above, the mobilisation fee falls again to 120000 cents and the rebate rises to six percent. The tier boundaries are inclusive at their lower edge. One override sits on top of the tier table: when no manifest has been received at all, `mobilization_fee_cents` is zero rather than the tier-one figure — the contractor does not bill mobilisation for a season that shipped nothing.

Fifth, `volume_rebate_cents` is `disposal_subtotal_cents` multiplied by the tier's rebate percentage, with the fraction discarded. Sixth, `staffing_cost_cents` is the sum of `staffing_cost_cents` across the program's events whose `lifecycle_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`. Seventh, `pre_contingency_cents` is `disposal_subtotal_cents` plus `mobilization_fee_cents` plus `staffing_cost_cents`, less `volume_rebate_cents`.

Eighth — and this is the step most likely to be misread — `contingency_cents` is eight percent of `pre_contingency_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The contingency reserve is described in the county's own budget documents as a percentage of total program cost, but the evaluation order pins the meaning: it is eight percent of the **pre-contingency** subtotal, never eight percent of a total that already includes it. Ninth, `total_program_cost_cents` is `pre_contingency_cents` plus `contingency_cents`.

Tenth, `participant_count` is the number of appointments in `COMPLETED` status across all of the program's events. Appointments that ended in `REFERRED_OUT`, `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` are not participants. Eleventh, `cost_per_participant_cents` is `total_program_cost_cents` divided by `participant_count` with the fraction discarded, and it is zero when `participant_count` is zero — the division is never attempted against an empty denominator.

Working a small season through: one received manifest invoiced at 698 cents carrying 4.5 kilograms, one event with 12.00 certified staff hours at 4200 cents an hour, and one completed appointment. The disposal subtotal is 698 and the received tonnage of 4.5 kilograms lands in the first tier, so mobilisation is 250000 and the rebate percentage is zero, making the rebate 0. Staffing is 50400. Pre-contingency is 698 plus 250000 plus 50400 less 0, or 301098. Contingency is eight percent of 301098, which is 24087.84 rounding half up to 24088. The total is 325186, and with one participant the cost per participant is 325186. A program with no completed appointments and no received manifests collapses the whole chain to a mobilisation of 0, a total of 0 and a cost per participant of 0.

The report's remaining figures come straight off the events. `event_count` is the number of collection events belonging to the program, and `accepted_tonnage_kg` and `rejected_tonnage_kg` are the program-wide roll-ups of the per-event tonnage described in §3.7. Alongside the report, a program's own `participant_count_ytd` and `tonnage_ytd_kg` carry the same participation and received-tonnage story on the program row itself, refreshed as each appointment completes and each manifest is received.
