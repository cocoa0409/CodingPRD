# Deposit Insurance Resolution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a federally insured depository institution fails, a resolution team has one weekend to answer a question that is simultaneously legal, actuarial and operational: for every depositor at that institution, how many cents are insured, how many are not, and who ends up holding the shortfall. Getting that answer wrong in either direction is unacceptable — under-insuring a depositor breaks a statutory guarantee, over-insuring one spends the insurance fund's money without authority. This API is the system of record for that determination and for everything that follows from it: the pre-failure data collection that makes the determination possible, the holds and receivership claims that carry the uninsured remainder, the competitive bidding that decides whether the franchise is sold or the deposits are simply paid off, and the multi-year receivership that liquidates what is left and pays dividends against the claims.

The product replaces a fragile chain of spreadsheets with a single ledger-grade service. Its central design commitment is **conservation**: every dollar booked into the institution's deposit file must be traceable, at any instant, through attribution, insurance determination, hold placement, claim issuance and dividend payment, and the sum must never drift. A second commitment is **segregation of duties** — the operator who computes a depositor's insured position may never be the operator who certifies it, and the analyst who qualifies an acquiring institution may never act as that acquirer. A third is **determinism under replay**: because resolution work is reconstructed after the fact by auditors and by courts, every threshold, every rounding step and every evaluation order in this specification is fixed, and no operation is permitted to produce a different answer on a second run against the same data.

Time in this system is a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that a resolution weekend can be simulated, replayed and tested end to end. The clock starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the service starts and can only be advanced forward. Every timestamp the server stamps — `failure_datetime`, `prepared_at`, `certified_at`, `notice_sent_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `qualified_at`, `issued_at`, `reopened_at`, `settled_at` and `voided_at` — is read from that clock, and every temporal rule in this document (bid deadlines, data-room separation, branch reopening windows) is evaluated against it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract that every endpoint in the API shares: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and audit fields, the arithmetic discipline, the cascade and concurrency model, and the order in which a request's checks are applied.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. A request missing either header, presenting either header as an empty string, or presenting a role string that is not one of the seven declared roles, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. The role string is matched case-sensitively against the seven declared names. A request that authenticates successfully but whose role is not listed for the target operation in §4 is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The seven roles are `ClosingManager`, `ClaimsAgent`, `ClaimsCertifier`, `ResolutionAnalyst`, `ReceivershipTrustee`, `Bidder` and `Depositor`. Their responsibilities partition the resolution by function rather than by seniority. A `ClosingManager` runs pre-failure readiness collection, the closing itself and branch reopening logistics. A `ClaimsAgent` loads depositor and account data and prepares insurance determinations, and may never certify one. A `ClaimsCertifier` independently certifies determinations and releases uninsured holds. A `ResolutionAnalyst` markets the franchise, qualifies bidders and runs the least-cost test and the award. A `ReceivershipTrustee` books asset-disposition proceeds and administers the dividend waterfall. All seven roles may perform every read operation in §4 and may advance the logical clock.

Five of the roles are **operator roles** whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ClosingManager`, `ClaimsAgent`, `ClaimsCertifier`, `ResolutionAnalyst` or `ReceivershipTrustee`, the `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — a `ClaimsAgent`'s identifier is stored as `prepared_by_user_id` on the determination it prepares, and a `ClaimsCertifier`'s identifier is stored as `certified_by_user_id` on the determination it certifies — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Bidder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Bidder` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Bidder` compare that resource's `bidder_id` to `X-User-ID`, so a bidder may submit a bid only in its own name and may withdraw only its own bid. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_BIDDER_MISMATCH`. When `X-User-Role` is `Depositor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DepositorRecord`; a depositor may acknowledge only the notice on its own record, and a mismatch between `X-User-ID` and the depositor record named in the path is rejected with `ERR_DEPOSITOR_MISMATCH`. Authentication itself does not check that an entity-backed `X-User-ID` resolves to a persisted row: a `Bidder` or `Depositor` header carrying an identifier that matches no row authenticates and authorizes normally and then simply fails the ownership comparison at stage 6 of §2.11, so it yields `ERR_BIDDER_MISMATCH` or `ERR_DEPOSITOR_MISMATCH` rather than a not-found code.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, freshly generated for every response.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Alone among the timestamps this API emits it is read from the host's wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §1, so it advances between two calls even when the logical clock has not moved and bears no relation to the `created_at` and `updated_at` values in the same response.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. It is diagnostic prose only: clients key off `error_id` and the HTTP status, never off the wording of `message`.
- The same envelope is also used for failures that never reach an endpoint at all. A request to a path or an HTTP method that §4 does not declare answers with the framework's own status — 404 for an unrouted path, 405 for an unrouted method on a declared path — carrying `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and any unexpected server-side failure answers 500 with the same `error_id`. A trailing slash is not such a case: see §2.5.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of resources returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of matching resources to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0; it has no declared upper bound.

Both bounds are enforced by **rejection, not clamping**: a `limit` of 0 or of 101, a negative `offset`, or either parameter carrying a value that is not a decimal integer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400 rather than being silently coerced to the nearest legal value. A parameter that is omitted, or supplied as an empty string, takes its default. Surrounding whitespace is tolerated. An `offset` beyond the end of the matching set is legal and yields an empty `data` array while `total_count` still reports the full number of matches and `has_more` reads false.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and because the logical clock is stationary unless an operator advances it, rows created in the same clock instant routinely share a `created_at`; such ties are broken by ascending order of creation, so the row that was created first is listed first. The ordering is therefore total and stable across repeated reads. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every resource matching the request's filters — not merely those on the returned page — and `has_more` indicates whether any matching resource remains beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Resources in this API are addressed by a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix — a random version-4 UUID rendered in the canonical 36-character lowercase hyphenated form — assigned at creation and never reused. Alongside it, most resources carry a `created_at` timestamp, an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. Both timestamps are ISO 8601 instants in UTC rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`), and both are read from the logical clock rather than from wall-clock time, so a resource created and then mutated without any intervening clock advance carries identical `created_at` and `updated_at` values. The `version` of a resource starts at 1 and increments by one on every server-side mutation of that resource, including mutations the client did not directly request — a cascade that flips a deposit account's hold status, a recomputation that changes an institution's aggregate counters, or a compensation that reverses a dividend all bump the affected resource's `version` and refresh its `updated_at` exactly as a direct write would. The increment is nonetheless **per request, not per field write**: a single request that writes several fields of the same row, or that reaches the same row twice through different levels of one cascade, advances that row's `version` by exactly one and refreshes its `updated_at`, never by two. Conversely a request that leaves a row's fields entirely alone does not touch its `version` at all.

Every field this document declares on a resource is present in that resource's `data` object on every read and every write response, whether or not it has a value; a field that has not been set yet is rendered as JSON `null` rather than omitted from the object, and a field described here as "an empty map" is rendered as `{}`. A timestamp a client supplies for storage on a resource — `opened_at`, `bid_deadline_at`, the data-room and reopening window bounds — is normalized before it is stored and is therefore echoed back in the same canonical three-fractional-digit UTC rendering, not in the exact spelling the client sent. Normalization accepts a trailing `Z` or an explicit numeric UTC offset and converts to UTC, reads a value carrying no offset at all as already being UTC, truncates any precision finer than a millisecond, and accepts a date-only value as midnight UTC on that date; anything that is not a parseable ISO 8601 value, including a numeric epoch, is a shape failure under §2.11.

`Institution`, `ReadinessSubmission`, `DepositorRecord`, `DepositAccount`, `InsuranceDetermination`, `ReceivershipCertificate`, `Bidder`, `Bid`, `BranchReopening` and `DividendDistribution` all carry the full set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. The one exception is `OwnershipInterest`. An ownership interest is an immutable historical assertion about who held what share of an account, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no operation that modifies one after it is attached.

State is process-local and starts empty. On every start of the service every collection in the API is empty and the logical clock sits at its epoch: nothing is seeded, preloaded or hardcoded, so every institution, submission, depositor, account, ownership interest, determination, certificate, bidder, bid, branch reopening and dividend round exists only because a client created it through one of the operations in §4, and none of it survives a restart.

Time is supplied by the logical clock described in §1. An operator of any role may advance it by supplying `to_iso8601`, which is a required field; the response's `data` holds exactly one field, `current_utc`, carrying the resulting clock value in the canonical rendering above, and no audit or version fields — the clock is not a resource. The clock is strictly monotonic: a request whose `to_iso8601` is earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONICITY`, while a request that sets the clock to exactly its current value is accepted and leaves it unchanged.

### 2.5. Reading the Estate: Collection Filters and Path Resolution

The URL topology is flat: every entity is a peer collection mounted at the root, and parenthood is expressed through foreign-key fields in request bodies plus a small number of convenience sub-resource routes hung off the parent. No resource is nested more than one segment deep. Paths are matched without regard to a trailing slash, so `/institutions` and `/institutions/` address the same collection and neither redirects.

When a path carries an identifier, that identifier is resolved before any body field is inspected, and where a path carries more than one identifier they are resolved strictly left to right — the first identifier that does not resolve determines the 404 that comes back. Each entity has its own not-found code: `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_FOUND` when an institution id in a path or in an `institution_id` body field does not resolve, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND` for a readiness submission, `ERR_DEPOSITOR_NOT_FOUND` when a depositor id in a path or in a `depositor_record_id` or `primary_depositor_id` body field does not resolve, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` for a deposit account, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND` for an insurance determination, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND` for a receivership certificate, `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_FOUND` when a bidder id in a path or in a `bidder_id` body field does not resolve, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND` for a bid, `ERR_BRANCH_REOPENING_NOT_FOUND` for a branch reopening, and `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND` for a dividend round. A body foreign key is resolved only when the key is actually present with a non-null value; a foreign key that is absent, or explicitly `null`, is not an existence failure but the missing-field shape failure of §2.11 stage 4. A foreign key that is present but is not a string at all — a number, a boolean, an object — is resolved like any other identifier and, resolving to nothing, yields that entity's not-found code and HTTP 404 rather than a 400, because existence is evaluated before shape. Identifiers are matched by exact string equality, so a well-formed UUID that names no row and a value that is not a UUID at all are indistinguishable: both are simply not found.

The read surface is deliberately complete: every relationship declared anywhere in this document can be traversed in reverse. Institutions can be listed and filtered by `status`, `charter_state` and `least_cost_status`. Readiness submissions are listed under their institution and filtered by `status`. Depositor records are listed with optional `institution_id`, `notice_status` and `depositor_type` filters. Deposit accounts are listed with optional `institution_id`, `primary_depositor_id`, `ownership_category`, `insurance_status`, `hold_status`, `transition_status` and `recordkeeping_status` filters. Ownership interests are readable both from the account side and from the depositor side, each with an optional `interest_role` filter. Insurance determinations are listed with optional `depositor_record_id`, `status` and `method_variant` filters. Receivership certificates are listed with optional `insurance_determination_id`, `depositor_record_id`, `institution_id` and `status` filters. Bidders are listed with an optional `qualification_status` filter. Bids are listed with optional `institution_id`, `bidder_id`, `status` and `structure` filters, and this is the only way to discover which bid won. Branch reopenings are listed under their institution with optional `status` and `branch_code` filters. Dividend rounds are listed with optional `institution_id`, `status` and `priority_class` filters. Every one of these list endpoints honours the pagination contract of §2.3.

Every filter named above behaves the same way, and the behaviour is deliberately permissive. A filter that is absent from the query string imposes no restriction. A filter that is present is applied as exact, case-sensitive string equality against the stored field, so a filter value that is not a member of the field's enum — or that differs only in case, or that is supplied as an empty string — is not a request-shape failure: it simply matches nothing and yields an empty page with `total_count` zero. Where several filters are supplied on one call they are conjunctive, and where a filter names a field whose stored value is null the row cannot match any filter value. Query parameters other than the filters declared for the endpoint and the `limit` and `offset` of §2.3 are ignored rather than rejected. On a sub-resource list route the parent identifier in the path is still resolved first, so listing under a parent that does not exist yields that parent's not-found code before any filter is considered.

### 2.6. Cents, Basis Points and the Rounding Discipline

Money never appears as a decimal in this API. Every monetary quantity is an integer count of cents, and every rate, share or ratio is an integer count of basis points, where 10000 basis points is the whole — so 5000 basis points is half, 3000 basis points is thirty percent, and 25 basis points is a quarter of one percent. Because the entire system is integer arithmetic, the only place error can enter is the point at which a division is resolved into a whole number, and this specification therefore pins that point for every computation individually. Two disciplines are used, and they are not interchangeable.

**Round half up** means the exact rational result is rounded to the nearest whole cent, with a value exactly halfway between two cents going to the larger. It is used wherever a share of money is being priced: the per-holder share of a jointly held account, the per-beneficiary share of a trust account, the per-participant share of a brokered omnibus balance, the subrogated recovery inside the liquidation baseline, every priced step of a bid evaluation, and the per-certificate gross dividend.

**Floor**, or truncation toward zero, is used wherever rounding up would let a figure claim more than the underlying data supports: both basis-point figures derived during readiness standardization, the insured-share ratio inside the liquidation baseline, the retention cap that screens a bid, and — critically — each account's share of an uninsured hold, where truncation plus an explicit remainder pass is the only way to keep the allocated total exactly equal to the uninsured amount.

Two further rules apply everywhere. First, **rounding is never chained**: when a computation has several priced steps, each step is rounded on its own from its own exact rational value, and the rounded results are then combined; multiplying several factors together and rounding once at the end is a different answer and is wrong. Second, **rounding order is pinned**: where a total is the sum of many rounded parts, each part is rounded first and the rounded parts are then summed. Summing the unrounded parts and rounding the total once is explicitly forbidden, because for a two-certificate dividend round with faces of 4000001 cents and 2000001 cents declared at 3333 basis points, rounding per certificate first yields 1333200 plus 666600, a total of 1999800 cents, whereas summing first and rounding once would yield 1999801 cents.

### 2.7. Independent Status Dimensions and Cascading Reconciliation

Several resources in this system do not have "a" status. An `Institution` carries three orthogonal dimensions on one row: `status` tracks the resolution lifecycle, `least_cost_status` tracks the bid-evaluation lifecycle, and `franchise_marketing_status` tracks the data room. A `DepositAccount` likewise carries three: `insurance_status` for the determination dimension, `hold_status` for the hold dimension, and `transition_status` for the customer-transition dimension. Each dimension advances under its own triggers and none of them gates another dimension's transition table; where one dimension's value matters to another dimension's transition, this document says so explicitly.

Operations in this system are **cascading**. A single request routinely mutates a chain of resources: the closing of an institution reaches down into its deposit accounts, the certification of a determination reaches through the depositor record into accounts, a new receivership certificate and a queued notice, and the award of a resolution reaches through the losing bids into accounts and branch reopenings. Cascades are described level by level in §3, and the rule for all of them is the same: every resource a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, and the cascade is applied in full within the request or not at all. There is no eventual consistency and no background settlement — a read issued immediately after a mutation observes every consequence of that mutation.

An institution's derived aggregates are **recomputed from scratch**, never adjusted incrementally, on every mutation that could change them: any write to a deposit account balance, any determination reaching or leaving certified status, any change to a receivership certificate's status, any posting of disposition proceeds, and any dividend settlement or void. `total_deposit_balance_cents` is the sum of `balance_cents` plus `accrued_interest_cents` across every deposit account of the institution with no status filter of any kind. `depositor_record_count` is the count of depositor records at the institution. `aggregate_insured_cents` is the sum of `insured_amount_cents` over the institution's determinations that are currently certified, and only those. `aggregate_certificate_face_cents` is the sum of `face_amount_cents` over the institution's receivership certificates whose status is `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID`, excluding `CANCELLED` ones, and `uninsured_claim_count` is the count of that same certificate set. `gross_disposition_proceeds_cents` is the running total of every gross proceeds figure posted against the estate, `disposition_expense_total_cents` the running total of every disposition expense posted, and `receivership_cash_cents` is the gross proceeds total minus the expense total minus the sum of `total_distributed_cents` over the institution's settled dividend rounds. `dividend_round_count` is the count of the institution's dividend rounds whose status is `DECLARED` or `SETTLED`, with voided rounds excluded, and that same count is the source of the next round's `distribution_round`. Recomputing from scratch is what allows a compensation — a superseded determination, a voided dividend round — to return every aggregate exactly to its pre-operation value with no accumulated drift.

### 2.8. Conservation Identities and the Insurance Summary

Two identities must hold at every observable instant, and the API exposes both as signed deltas through a computed read on the institution so that a client, an auditor or a test can assert them directly. The read recomputes everything from the underlying rows rather than echoing stored counters, and reports `institution_id`, `total_deposit_balance_cents`, `attributed_total_cents` (the sum of `attributed_amount_cents` across the institution's deposit accounts), `unattributed_balance_cents` (the institution's total deposit balance minus that attributed total), `conservation_delta_cents`, `aggregate_insured_cents`, `aggregate_certificate_face_cents`, `uninsured_claim_count`, `held_amount_total_cents` (the sum of `held_amount_cents` across the institution's deposit accounts), `receivership_cash_cents`, `gross_disposition_proceeds_cents`, `disposition_expense_total_cents`, `dividends_paid_cents` (the sum of `total_distributed_cents` over the institution's settled rounds) and `cash_conservation_delta_cents`.

The **deposit-attribution identity** states that certified insurance coverage plus outstanding uninsured claims exactly account for what was certified to depositors. Its delta, `conservation_delta_cents`, is computed by summing `certified_insured_cents` plus `certified_uninsured_cents` over every depositor of the institution that currently holds a certified determination, then subtracting the sum of `aggregate_insured_cents` and `aggregate_certificate_face_cents`. This delta must be zero.

The **cash identity** states that every cent the receivership realized is either still in the estate or has been paid out and recorded on the receiving claim. Its delta, `cash_conservation_delta_cents`, is computed by taking `gross_disposition_proceeds_cents` minus `disposition_expense_total_cents`, then subtracting the sum of three quantities: `receivership_cash_cents`, the total of `cumulative_dividends_cents` over the institution's certificates that are not cancelled, and the total of `total_distributed_cents` over the institution's settled rounds whose `priority_class` is `INSURED_SUBROGATION`. This delta must be zero as well. The third term exists because a subrogation round pays the insurance fund rather than a certificate holder, so its money leaves the estate without landing on any certificate.

### 2.9. Stale-View Protection on Determination and Dividend Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent resource's `version`, and only those two. In both cases the version field is a required input, not an optional hint: omitting it is a request-shape failure, and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is a precondition failure. Beyond having to be a JSON integer the field carries no declared range, so a zero or negative value is perfectly well shaped, cannot match any live `version`, and therefore fails at stage 7 with the 412 rather than at stage 4 with a 400.

Opening an insurance determination — `POST /insurance-determinations` — must carry `expected_depositor_record_version` holding the `version` of the target `DepositorRecord` as the client last observed it. If the depositor record's `version` has advanced since that observation, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEPOSITOR_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard exists because another writer may already have opened a determination that legitimately supersedes the depositor's certified position, in which case the current writer's view of `certified_insured_cents` and `certified_uninsured_cents` is stale and the determination it is about to open would be built on a picture that no longer exists. A successful create bumps the depositor record's `version` by one, so a second create replaying the same `expected_depositor_record_version` is rejected.

Declaring a dividend round — `POST /institutions/{id}/dividend-distributions` — must carry `expected_institution_version` holding the `version` of the target `Institution` as the client last observed it. If the institution's `version` has advanced, the declaration is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSTITUTION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Declaring a further round is a perfectly legitimate operation on its own, so this guard is aimed purely at the stale cash-and-round picture the declaring trustee would otherwise be working from: `receivership_cash_cents` and `dividend_round_count` both move under the trustee's feet as proceeds are booked and earlier rounds settle. A successful declaration bumps the institution's `version`, so the next round must be declared against the new value.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Resolution work is subject to a four-eyes rule that the API enforces rather than merely documents. Preparation and certification of an insurance determination are separate acts performed by separate roles, and they must also be performed by separate people: a certification whose `X-User-ID` equals the `prepared_by_user_id` recorded on the determination is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403, even though the caller holds the correct `ClaimsCertifier` role. Consequently `certified_by_user_id` can never equal `prepared_by_user_id` on any certified determination. The same principle separates the resolution desk from the market: a `ResolutionAnalyst` qualifies acquirers and runs the least-cost test but cannot submit a bid, while a `Bidder` can submit and withdraw its own bids but cannot influence its own qualification or the evaluation.

### 2.11. Order of Evaluation for a Request

When a request would fail more than one check, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the following ordered pipeline. Implementations must evaluate in this order so that a masked check never surfaces in place of the one that should have fired.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and the role recognized, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization** — the acting role is listed for the operation in §4, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Existence** of every resource the request names, whether in the path or in a body foreign key. Where a request names several, they are resolved in this entity order: institution, readiness submission, depositor record, deposit account, insurance determination, receivership certificate, bidder, bid, branch reopening, dividend round. Each yields its own code from the list in §2.5.
4. **Request shape** — every required field present, every type correct, every enum member recognized, and every integer inside its declared range, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. The declared ranges are: `designated_share_bps` from 1 to 10000; `camels_composite` from 1 to 5; `tier1_capital_ratio_bps` at least 0 with no upper bound, so a ratio above 10000 basis points is well shaped; `declared_rate_bps` from 1 to 10000; `staffing_headcount` at least 1; `record_count` at least 1; `malformed_record_count` at least 0 and no greater than `record_count`; `deposit_premium_bps`, `asset_discount_bps` and `loss_share_pct_bps` from 0 to 10000; and every field naming an amount in cents at least 0. `reopen_window_end` must be strictly after `reopen_window_start`, and `data_room_window_end` strictly after `data_room_window_start`. `broker_name` and `recordkeeping_status` must both be present exactly when `ownership_category` is `BROKERED_PASS_THROUGH`, and must both be absent otherwise. The rules that make this stage decidable are these. The body must be a JSON object: an array, a string, a number or unparseable JSON is refused at once. A request that carries no body at all, or an empty body, is read as the empty object `{}` — legal in itself, so an operation that requires no fields succeeds without a body and an operation that requires fields then fails on the first missing one. The `Content-Type` header is not inspected, so a well-formed JSON object is accepted however it is labelled. Fields the operation does not declare are **ignored**, never rejected and never stored, which also means a client cannot set a server-controlled field such as `status`, `version` or a derived counter by naming it in a create body. An explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as an omitted key: on a required field it produces the missing-field failure, and on an optional field it selects that field's documented default. No type is coerced — an integer field rejects a numeric string, rejects a JSON float even when the float is integral, and rejects a boolean; a string field rejects a number; a datetime field rejects anything but a parseable ISO 8601 string; and an enum field rejects any value that is not one of its declared members. A string field must be non-empty whenever it is present, whether it is required or optional. Every one of these failures is the single code `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400, and the accompanying `message` is diagnostic only.
5. **Clock monotonicity** on the clock-advance capability, else `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONICITY`.
6. **Caller identity** for the two entity-backed roles — `ERR_DEPOSITOR_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_BIDDER_MISMATCH`.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** on the two guarded writes of §2.9 — `ERR_STALE_DEPOSITOR_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_INSTITUTION_VERSION`.
8. **Cross-entity referential integrity**, in this order: `ERR_DEPOSITOR_INSTITUTION_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_BID_INSTITUTION_INELIGIBLE`, then uniqueness as `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.
9. **Lifecycle and business guards**, in the order in which §3 introduces them.
10. **Fiscal guards last** — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RECEIVERSHIP_CASH`, then `ERR_DISPOSITION_EXPENSE_EXCEEDS_PROCEEDS`.

Because several operations carry three or four guards at stage 9, the resolution order within that stage is pinned per operation, and each is stated where the operation is described in §3. For quick reference, the pinned orders are: on preparation, non-preparable state, then the institution-not-closed gate, then unbalanced joint shares; on certification, not-under-review state, then the institution-not-closed gate, then segregation of duties, then the non-certifiable variant; on opening a determination, the stale-version guard, then the already-active-determination guard, then the already-paid-certificate guard; on hold release, the no-hold guard, then the unacknowledged-notice guard; on bid submission, bidder-identity mismatch, then institution eligibility, then the marketing-window state, then the bid deadline, then the data-room separation, then bidder qualification; on branch rescheduling, the non-reschedulable state, then window overlap; on branch reopening, the not-ready state, then the reopening window; on dividend declaration, the stale-version guard, then priority-class ordering; on settlement, the not-declared state, then receivership cash sufficiency; on voiding, the not-settled state, then the latest-round guard.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The resolution runs as one long chronological arc: a troubled institution is registered and its deposit file is standardized; the charter is closed and the deposit base frozen; each depositor's insured position is computed, reviewed and certified, which places holds and issues claims; the franchise is marketed and bid on; a least-cost test either awards the franchise or pays the deposits off; branches reopen; and the receivership liquidates what is left and pays dividends until it can be terminated. The subsections below follow that arc.

### 3.1. Pre-Failure Readiness Collection and Deposit File Standardization

Long before a charter closes, a `ClosingManager` registers the troubled institution by supplying its `cert_number` — a four-to-six-digit certificate number that must be globally unique across institutions — together with its chartered `legal_name` of one to 160 characters, a `charter_state` given as exactly two uppercase letters, the `book_asset_value_cents` recorded for its whole asset book at intake, and the `estimated_liquidation_recovery_cents` that appraisers expect that book to realize under a straight liquidation. A second institution presenting a `cert_number` already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

A newly registered institution opens at `status` `PRE_FAILURE_MONITORING`, with `readiness_standardized` false, `franchise_marketing_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `least_cost_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and no `failure_datetime`, `bid_deadline_at`, `least_cost_test_variant`, `liquidation_baseline_cents` or `resolution_method` yet recorded. Every derived counter starts at zero: `total_deposit_balance_cents`, `depositor_record_count`, `aggregate_insured_cents`, `aggregate_certificate_face_cents`, `uninsured_claim_count`, `receivership_cash_cents`, `gross_disposition_proceeds_cents`, `disposition_expense_total_cents`, `dividend_round_count` and `conforming_bid_count`. Over the whole resolution the institution's `status` walks the sequence `PRE_FAILURE_MONITORING`, `READINESS_COLLECTION`, `RESOLUTION_READY`, `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP`, `RESOLUTION_COMPLETE` and finally the terminal `RECEIVERSHIP_TERMINATED`.

Readiness work begins when the institution delivers a deposit-file package. The `ClosingManager` files it against the institution with the `file_format` the institution used — `FDIC_STANDARD_V2`, `LEGACY_FIXED_WIDTH` or `VENDOR_CSV` — the `record_count` of deposit records in the package, the `malformed_record_count` the institution itself flagged as unparseable, and the `reported_deposit_total_cents` the institution asserts the package sums to. The package lands at `status` `RECEIVED` with `standardization_score_bps`, `deposit_total_variance_bps` and `rejection_reason` all unset. Receiving the very first package for an institution advances the institution from `PRE_FAILURE_MONITORING` to `READINESS_COLLECTION` and bumps its `version`; a further package arriving while the institution is already in `READINESS_COLLECTION` leaves the institution's `status` untouched, and because nothing on the institution row changed, its `version` is not bumped either. Filing a package carries no institution-status precondition of its own: a package may be filed while the institution is at any `status`, and when that status is `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` or later neither of the moves above applies, so the package is created and the institution row is left entirely untouched with its `version` unmoved.

A package that arrives after readiness was already achieved is treated as a revert: an institution at `RESOLUTION_READY` drops back to `READINESS_COLLECTION` and `readiness_standardized` is lowered to false, on the principle that readiness is only as good as the newest delivered file. The standing attestation is void until the new package is itself standardized, so `RESOLUTION_READY` must be re-earned. Read the other way, `readiness_standardized` reads true once a package for the institution has reached `STANDARDIZED`, and is lowered again the moment a further package is received.

**Scoring a package** is a two-part computation followed by two gates, and it is the only way a package leaves `RECEIVED`. The first part is the clean-record score: subtract `malformed_record_count` from `record_count`, multiply the difference by 10000, divide by `record_count`, and truncate the quotient downward to a whole number of basis points — that figure is `standardization_score_bps`. The second part reconciles what the institution asserted against what has actually been booked into the API: sum `balance_cents` plus `accrued_interest_cents` over every deposit account of that institution, with no filter on any of the account's three status dimensions, to obtain the booked total. When the booked total is zero, `deposit_total_variance_bps` is zero if `reported_deposit_total_cents` is also zero and 10000 otherwise. When the booked total is non-zero, take the absolute difference between `reported_deposit_total_cents` and the booked total, multiply by 10000, divide by the booked total, and truncate downward — that figure is `deposit_total_variance_bps`.

Both figures are written to the package before either gate is applied, so a rejected package still carries its diagnostics. The gates are then evaluated in a fixed order that makes `rejection_reason` deterministic even when both would fail. **First** the score floor: a `standardization_score_bps` below 9800 — that is, fewer than 98 percent of the delivered records parsing cleanly — rejects the package with `rejection_reason` `SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`. **Then** the variance ceiling: a `deposit_total_variance_bps` above 25 — a discrepancy larger than a quarter of one percent of the booked total — rejects the package with `rejection_reason` `TOTAL_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`. A score of exactly 9800 clears the floor, and a variance of exactly 25 clears the ceiling. Only a package clearing both gates reaches `STANDARDIZED`.

The two branches have deliberately different reach. A `STANDARDIZED` package raises `readiness_standardized` to true, advances the institution from `READINESS_COLLECTION` to `RESOLUTION_READY`, and triggers a recompute of `total_deposit_balance_cents` and `depositor_record_count`, bumping the institution's `version` — and that bump happens on every package that reaches `STANDARDIZED`, including a second package standardized while `readiness_standardized` is already true and `status` is already `RESOLUTION_READY`, where the institution's `version` and `updated_at` move even though none of its stored fields change. A `REJECTED` package writes its `rejection_reason` and bumps only its own `version` — the institution row is left entirely untouched and its `version` is **not** incremented. Either way the package's own `version` is bumped, and both `STANDARDIZED` and `REJECTED` are terminal for the package. Scoring a package that is no longer at `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_PENDING`, and that is the only guard scoring carries: scoring is *meaningful* only while the institution is in `READINESS_COLLECTION`, since that is the phase the score is supposed to adjudicate, but nothing enforces it — scoring a `RECEIVED` package while the institution sits at `PRE_FAILURE_MONITORING`, `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` or any later status succeeds, raises `readiness_standardized` to true and bumps the institution's `version` exactly as usual, and simply leaves `status` alone, because only an institution actually at `READINESS_COLLECTION` is advanced to `RESOLUTION_READY`.

### 3.2. The Depositor and Account Register

With readiness under way, a `ClaimsAgent` builds the depositor side of the file. Each depositor of the institution is registered with a `tin_hash` — a salted hash of the taxpayer identification number, one to 64 characters, unique within the institution — a `legal_name` of one to 160 characters as it will be printed on communications, a `depositor_type` of `NATURAL_PERSON`, `BUSINESS`, `GOVERNMENT` or `BROKER`, and a `contact_email` that must contain an at-sign. Depositor records open with `notice_status` `PENDING`, no `notice_template_code`, no `notice_sent_at` and no `acknowledged_at`, and with `attributed_total_cents`, `certified_insured_cents` and `certified_uninsured_cents` all at zero. Creating one increments the institution's `depositor_record_count` and bumps the institution's `version`. A repeated `tin_hash` within the same institution is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`; the same hash at a different institution is perfectly acceptable.

Accounts are then loaded one by one. An account names its `institution_id` and its `primary_depositor_id` — the titled owner, or for a brokered placement the broker of record — together with the `account_number` from the deposit file, which must be one to 32 characters and unique within the institution, the `ownership_category` that governs which insurance limit will apply, the `balance_cents` of principal as of the failure date, and the `opened_at` date. Two further inputs are optional in general: `accrued_interest_cents`, the interest accrued and unposted as of the failure date, which defaults to 0 when omitted, and the brokered pair `broker_name` and `recordkeeping_status`, which describe the deposit broker of record and the quality of that broker's beneficiary recordkeeping as `COMPLETE`, `DEFICIENT` or `PENDING`. The brokered pair is required exactly when `ownership_category` is `BROKERED_PASS_THROUGH` and must be omitted otherwise. The `ownership_category` is one of `SINGLE`, `JOINT`, `REVOCABLE_TRUST`, `IRREVOCABLE_TRUST`, `RETIREMENT` or `BROKERED_PASS_THROUGH`. Note that `opened_at` is captured for the record only and is never an input to any decision, threshold or calculation in this system.

A new account opens with all three of its dimensions at their starting values — `insurance_status` `PENDING`, `hold_status` `NONE`, `transition_status` `PENDING` — and with `held_amount_cents` and `attributed_amount_cents` at zero. The account's principal and accrued interest are immediately added to the institution's `total_deposit_balance_cents`, bumping the institution's `version`. Should the named `primary_depositor_id` belong to a different institution than the account, the load is refused with `ERR_DEPOSITOR_INSTITUTION_MISMATCH`; a repeated `account_number` within the institution is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`, and the mismatch is detected before the duplicate. None of the three register writes — the depositor record, the account load, or the ownership-interest attach of the next paragraph — carries any institution-status precondition: all three succeed at every institution `status`, so an account loaded after the charter has already closed simply opens at `insurance_status` `PENDING`, the freezing cascade of §3.3 having already run and not running again, and therefore never participates in any determination.

Co-ownership, trust beneficiaries and pass-through participants all ride on a single junction resource attached to an account. Each such **ownership interest** records the `deposit_account_id` of the account it sits on — taken from the account addressed in the request rather than from the body — together with a `depositor_record_id`, an `interest_role` of `CO_OWNER`, `BENEFICIARY` or `PARTICIPANT`, and a `designated_share_bps` between 1 and 10000 recording that holder's share of the account in basis points. The `interest_role` is what reinterprets the same junction row for three different purposes — a co-owner on a joint account, a named beneficiary on a trust account, or a participant behind a brokered omnibus placement — and each role is consumed by a different arm of the ownership-category analysis in §3.5. The depositor named on an interest must belong to the same institution as the account, again on pain of `ERR_DEPOSITOR_INSTITUTION_MISMATCH`. An ownership interest is immutable once attached: as noted in §2.4 it carries no `updated_at` and no `version`, there is no operation that edits or removes it, and attaching one bumps the `version` of the account it sits on rather than its own.

### 3.3. Closing the Charter and Freezing the Deposit Base

Closing is the hinge of the whole resolution and belongs exclusively to the `ClosingManager`. The institution must be at `status` `RESOLUTION_READY` with `readiness_standardized` true; an institution that is still at `PRE_FAILURE_MONITORING` or `READINESS_COLLECTION`, or whose standing readiness attestation has been lowered by a later package, is refused with `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_READY`, while an institution already at `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP`, `RESOLUTION_COMPLETE` or `RECEIVERSHIP_TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INSTITUTION_ALREADY_CLOSED` — the guard that makes a repeated closing safe. The two guards are evaluated in the order the closed-or-later partition demands rather than in the order this sentence names them: that set is screened **first**, so an institution at or past `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` always yields `ERR_INSTITUTION_ALREADY_CLOSED` and never `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_READY`, even though its `status` is not `RESOLUTION_READY` either.

A successful closing moves `status` from `RESOLUTION_READY` to `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` and stamps `failure_datetime` with the logical clock's current instant, marking the moment the charter was closed and the receivership began. It recomputes `total_deposit_balance_cents` and `depositor_record_count` so that the determination phase starts from a reconciled picture. Because the institution closed, the closing then cascades into the deposit file: every deposit account of that institution whose `insurance_status` is `PENDING` flips to `FROZEN_FOR_DETERMINATION`, each such account having its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` bumped, and the institution's `version` is bumped once for the closing itself. Freezing is what makes an account eligible to participate in a determination — the analysis in §3.5 reads accounts at `FROZEN_FOR_DETERMINATION` or `DETERMINED` and never at `PENDING`, which is why no insured position can be computed before the charter closes.

### 3.4. Determination Openings, Revision Rounds and Method Variants

An **insurance determination** is the reviewable document that states one depositor's insured and uninsured position. A `ClaimsAgent` opens one by naming the `depositor_record_id` it concerns and the `expected_depositor_record_version` required by §2.9, optionally supplying a `revision_reason` to record why the depositor's position is being reopened. The determination begins at `status` `DRAFT` with `method_variant`, `prepared_by_user_id`, `certified_by_user_id`, `prepared_at` and `certified_at` unset, with `revision_reason` holding whatever the opening supplied and null when the opening omitted it, `insured_amount_cents`, `uninsured_amount_cents` and `attributed_total_cents` at zero, and `category_breakdown` an empty map. Its `revision_round` is 0 for a depositor's first determination; when the opening supersedes a depositor's already-certified determination, `revision_round` starts at one more than the superseded determination's `revision_round`. Every successful opening bumps the parent depositor record's `version`, which is what makes the concurrency guard of §2.9 self-invalidating.

Only one live determination per depositor is permitted. An opening attempted while that depositor already has a determination at `DRAFT`, `PREPARED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_DETERMINATION_EXISTS`. An opening that would supersede a `CERTIFIED` determination is permitted only while no dividend has yet been paid against the receivership certificate that determination produced: if that certificate's `cumulative_dividends_cents` is greater than zero, the opening is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_ALREADY_PAID`, because money has already moved against the claim and the position can no longer be quietly rewritten. Of the two guards, the stale-version check runs first, then the active-determination check, then the already-paid check.

Preparation is the act that fills the document in. The preparing `ClaimsAgent` supplies the `method_variant` the run should use, and the server records that agent's `X-User-ID` as `prepared_by_user_id`, stamps `prepared_at` from the logical clock, writes `insured_amount_cents`, `uninsured_amount_cents`, `attributed_total_cents` and `category_breakdown` from the analysis in §3.5, writes `attributed_amount_cents` onto every deposit account that contributed to the depositor's total, copies the depositor's overall figure into `attributed_total_cents` on the depositor record, and moves the determination from `DRAFT` to `PREPARED`. The two write-backs differ in how they interact with the version rule of §2.4: a contributing account's `updated_at` and `version` move only when the recomputed `attributed_amount_cents` actually differs from the value already stored, so a re-prepare that reproduces the identical share leaves that account untouched, whereas the depositor record is always rewritten and therefore always has its `version` bumped even when `attributed_total_cents` comes out unchanged — which means preparing a determination invalidates any `expected_depositor_record_version` a client was holding under §2.9. Preparation deliberately does **not** touch any account's `insurance_status`: accounts stay at `FROZEN_FOR_DETERMINATION` and only reach `DETERMINED` at certification. Three guards apply, in this order: the determination must be at `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, else `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_PREPARABLE`; the depositor's institution must be at `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP`, else `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_CLOSED`; and every joint account contributing to this depositor must carry co-owner interests whose `designated_share_bps` sum to exactly 10000, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_SHARES_UNBALANCED`.

Review is a two-step handoff. A `ClaimsAgent` submits a prepared determination for independent review, which requires the determination to be at `PREPARED` — otherwise `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_PREPARED` — and requires `prepared_by_user_id` to have been recorded — a condition folded into that same `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_PREPARED` rather than carrying a code of its own — moving it `PREPARED` → `UNDER_REVIEW`. From there a `ClaimsCertifier` either certifies it, as described in §3.6, or sends it back. Sending it back takes a `revision_reason` that is a **required** non-empty string, so omitting it is the stage-4 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` of §2.11 and is reported before any state guard; it requires the determination to be at `UNDER_REVIEW`, else `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`; it stores that supplied `revision_reason`, increments `revision_round` by one, and moves the determination `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REVISION_REQUESTED`.

That send-back closes a cycle rather than ending the story. A determination at `REVISION_REQUESTED` can be prepared again by a `ClaimsAgent`, which recomputes every amount from scratch and overwrites the prior figures — and the `method_variant` may legitimately change on the re-prepare — moving it back to `PREPARED`. The document may therefore loop `PREPARED` → `UNDER_REVIEW` → `REVISION_REQUESTED` → `PREPARED` any number of times, with `revision_round` counting the completed loops. The full status set is `DRAFT`, `PREPARED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CERTIFIED` and the terminal `SUPERSEDED`.

Two **method variants** exist, and the difference between them is where the insurance limit bites. Under `AGGREGATE_BY_CATEGORY`, the statutory method, attributed dollars are pooled per ownership category first and the limit is applied once to the pooled bucket. Under `PER_ACCOUNT_PROVISIONAL`, the provisional read a claims agent produces before the deposit file has been fully reconciled, the same limit is applied independently to each account's own attributed share, so the insured figure is the sum over accounts of the smaller of that account's share and the category limit, and the uninsured figure is the sum of whatever each account's share exceeds the limit by. Consider a depositor holding two single-ownership accounts of 20000000 cents and 9000001 cents. Aggregated by category the pooled bucket is 29000001 cents against a limit of 25000000, so the determination reads 25000000 insured and 4000001 uninsured. Taken per account, neither account exceeds the limit on its own, so the same data reads 29000001 insured and 0 uninsured. The provisional figure is useful for triage but has no legal force, and certification of a determination whose `method_variant` is `PER_ACCOUNT_PROVISIONAL` is refused with `ERR_PROVISIONAL_VARIANT_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`.

### 3.5. Ownership Category Analysis and Pass-Through Attribution

The insurance analysis works in two movements: first every dollar the depositor can claim is attributed into one of five ownership-category buckets, then each bucket's limit is applied. The **standard maximum deposit insurance amount** is 25000000 cents — a quarter of a million dollars — and it is the unit out of which every limit in this section is built.

Throughout, an account's contribution is its `balance_cents` plus its `accrued_interest_cents`, and only accounts of the depositor's own institution participate. Accounts are read at `insurance_status` `FROZEN_FOR_DETERMINATION` — the normal post-closure state — or at `DETERMINED`, which is how a re-prepare after a revision loop still sees the accounts it already determined.

The **single-ownership bucket** collects three streams. It takes the full contribution of every account whose `ownership_category` is `SINGLE` and whose `primary_depositor_id` is this depositor. It takes every pass-through participant share credited to this depositor by the brokered analysis below. And it takes the full contribution of every `BROKERED_PASS_THROUGH` account whose `primary_depositor_id` is this depositor and whose `recordkeeping_status` is anything other than `COMPLETE` — pass-through having been denied, the omnibus balance belongs to the broker.

The **joint bucket** is share-based. For every account whose `ownership_category` is `JOINT` that carries an ownership interest naming this depositor with `interest_role` `CO_OWNER`, the bucket receives the account's contribution multiplied by that interest's `designated_share_bps`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole cent. Rounding happens on each share, before any limit is considered. Such a `CO_OWNER` interest is also what makes a joint account count as *contributing to this depositor*, both here and for the balanced-shares guard of §3.4: a `JOINT` account that names the depositor only as `primary_depositor_id` and carries no `CO_OWNER` interest for it contributes nothing to the bucket and is not screened by that guard, while an account that does carry one is screened by summing the `designated_share_bps` of **every** `CO_OWNER` interest on that account rather than only this depositor's. Only accounts read at the two insurance statuses named above are considered, so an account still at `PENDING` neither contributes nor is screened.

The **revocable-trust bucket** treats the depositor as the grantor: every account whose `ownership_category` is `REVOCABLE_TRUST` and whose `primary_depositor_id` is this depositor contributes its full amount. What varies is the limit, which is the standard maximum multiplied by the smaller of five and the number of distinct depositors named with `interest_role` `BENEFICIARY` on those accounts. The distinct beneficiaries are pooled across all of the depositor's revocable-trust accounts and counted once each however many of those accounts name them, and the floor of one is applied to that single pooled count rather than account by account — so a depositor holding one revocable-trust account naming two beneficiaries and a second naming none has a pooled count of two, not three, and the bucket's limit is two standard maximums. A revocable trust account carrying no beneficiary interests at all counts as one beneficiary, so its limit is one standard maximum; a trust naming eight beneficiaries is capped at five, so its limit is five standard maximums.

The **irrevocable-trust bucket** is the one bucket that does not use a bucket-wide limit at all. For every account whose `ownership_category` is `IRREVOCABLE_TRUST` with this depositor as grantor, the attributed figure is the sum of those accounts' full contributions, while the insured figure is built beneficiary by beneficiary: for each `BENEFICIARY` interest on such an account, take the account's contribution multiplied by that interest's `designated_share_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up, then take the smaller of that share and one standard maximum; the insured figure is the sum of those per-beneficiary results. An irrevocable trust account with no beneficiary interests is insured at the smaller of its attributed amount and one standard maximum. That per-beneficiary sum is finally clamped so that it never exceeds the bucket's own attributed total — which is what keeps the bucket coherent when the `BENEFICIARY` shares on one account sum to more than 10000 basis points — and the bucket's uninsured figure is then the attributed total minus that clamped insured figure, so the two always add back to the attributed total.

The **retirement bucket** takes the full contribution of every account whose `ownership_category` is `RETIREMENT` and whose `primary_depositor_id` is this depositor.

**Brokered pass-through attribution** runs as part of preparation and decides, for each `BROKERED_PASS_THROUGH` account at the institution, whose determination the omnibus dollars land in. When the account's `recordkeeping_status` is `COMPLETE`, pass-through is granted: each ownership interest on that account with `interest_role` `PARTICIPANT` credits the account's contribution multiplied by that interest's `designated_share_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up, into the single-ownership bucket of the depositor that interest names — where it aggregates with any single-ownership accounts that participant already holds — and the broker named as `primary_depositor_id` is credited nothing. When `recordkeeping_status` is `DEFICIENT` or `PENDING`, pass-through is denied: the entire omnibus contribution lands in the broker's own single-ownership bucket and the participants are credited nothing. The same dollars therefore reach entirely different depositors' determinations on the strength of one enum value, and because each participant's credited share then aggregates with that participant's own single-ownership balance before the standard maximum is applied, the insured outcome moves non-linearly in all three of the omnibus amount, the participant's share and the recordkeeping quality.

**Applying the limits** closes the analysis. The single-ownership, joint and retirement buckets each carry a limit of one standard maximum. The revocable-trust bucket carries the standard maximum multiplied by its capped beneficiary count. The irrevocable-trust bucket carries no bucket limit, its insured figure being the per-beneficiary sum already computed. Then `insured_amount_cents` is the sum over buckets of the smaller of that bucket's attributed amount and its limit; `uninsured_amount_cents` is the sum over buckets of the amount by which the attributed amount exceeds the limit, counting zero for any bucket that is at or below its limit; and `attributed_total_cents` is the plain sum of every bucket's attributed amount. Those first two sums describe `AGGREGATE_BY_CATEGORY`; under `PER_ACCOUNT_PROVISIONAL` the very same bucket limits are used, but the comparison is made share by share inside each bucket — the bucket's limit is applied separately to every per-account share the bucket holds, and the bucket's insured and uninsured figures are the sums of those per-account results. A pass-through participant's credited omnibus share is therefore screened against the standard maximum independently of that participant's own single-ownership accounts rather than aggregating with them, and the revocable-trust bucket's beneficiary-scaled limit is applied to each of its accounts on its own. The irrevocable-trust bucket is the one exception: having no bucket limit to apply, its per-beneficiary insured figure and its clamp come out identically under both method variants, and `attributed_total_cents` and every bucket's reported `limit_cents` are likewise variant-independent. Finally `category_breakdown` records, for each ownership category with a non-zero attribution and only those, an entry holding that category's `attributed_cents`, `limit_cents`, `insured_cents` and `uninsured_cents`. Its keys are drawn only from the five bucket names above: `BROKERED_PASS_THROUGH` is never a key, because the pass-through decision has already folded every omnibus dollar into some depositor's single-ownership bucket. For the irrevocable-trust bucket, which has no bucket limit, the reported `limit_cents` is that bucket's own clamped insured figure, so `attributed_cents` minus `limit_cents` still equals `uninsured_cents` there as it does in every other bucket. Each contributing account's own share of the total is written back to it as `attributed_amount_cents`, which is what the hold allocation in §3.6 later divides the uninsured remainder across. A brokered omnibus account with pass-through granted is a contributing account of *every* participant credited from it, so each participant's preparation overwrites that account's `attributed_amount_cents` with its own credited share rather than accumulating the shares, and the field ends up holding whatever the most recent preparation to reach the account computed. Nothing downstream depends on the stored field: the certify cascade of §3.6 and the hold allocation both read the per-account shares the determination's own most recent preparation recorded, not the values currently sitting on the accounts.

### 3.6. Certification: Holds, Receivership Certificates and Depositor Notices

Certification is the single most consequential operation in the system: it is where a computed opinion becomes a legal position and where money starts to be restrained. It is performed by a `ClaimsCertifier` on a determination at `UNDER_REVIEW`, and four guards apply in this pinned order. The determination must be at `UNDER_REVIEW`, else `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`. The depositor's institution must be at `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP`, else `ERR_INSTITUTION_NOT_CLOSED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `prepared_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`. And the `method_variant` must be `AGGREGATE_BY_CATEGORY`, else `ERR_PROVISIONAL_VARIANT_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`.

A certification that clears all four fires a five-level cascade. **First**, the determination moves `UNDER_REVIEW` → `CERTIFIED`, `certified_by_user_id` is set from the acting header and `certified_at` from the logical clock, the depositor record's `certified_insured_cents` and `certified_uninsured_cents` are written from the determination's `insured_amount_cents` and `uninsured_amount_cents`, and the institution's `aggregate_insured_cents` is recomputed. **Second**, because that determination became certified, every deposit account carrying an `attributed_amount_cents` greater than zero for this depositor flips `insurance_status` from `FROZEN_FOR_DETERMINATION` to `DETERMINED`. **Third**, because those accounts are now determined and the determination shows an `uninsured_amount_cents` greater than zero, the uninsured hold allocation described below writes each account's `held_amount_cents` and flips `hold_status` from `NONE` to `HELD` on every account whose allocated hold is greater than zero; an account allocated exactly zero cents stays at `hold_status` `NONE` with `held_amount_cents` zero. **Fourth**, because at least one account is now held, a receivership certificate is issued with `face_amount_cents` equal to the determination's `uninsured_amount_cents`, and the institution's `aggregate_certificate_face_cents` and `uninsured_claim_count` are recomputed. **Fifth**, because the certificate was issued, the depositor record's `notice_status` flips `PENDING` → `SENT` with `notice_template_code` `PARTIALLY_UNINSURED` and `notice_sent_at` stamped from the logical clock.

The fully insured depositor takes a shortened path. When the determination's `uninsured_amount_cents` is exactly zero, the third and fourth levels are skipped entirely — no account is touched, every `hold_status` stays `NONE`, and no receivership certificate is created at all — while the fifth level still runs, sending the notice with `notice_template_code` `FULLY_INSURED` instead. Both the determination and every resource the cascade touched have their `version` bumped, including the institution.

**Allocating the uninsured hold** must restrain exactly the uninsured amount and not one cent more or less. The eligible accounts are those carrying an `attributed_amount_cents` greater than zero for the certified depositor, processed in `created_at` ascending order with ties broken by `account_number` in ascending lexicographic order. Let the uninsured amount be the quantity to allocate and let the divisor be the sum of `attributed_amount_cents` over the eligible accounts. Each account's exact share is the uninsured amount multiplied by that account's `attributed_amount_cents` and divided by the divisor, kept as an exact rational with no rounding; each account's provisional hold is that exact share truncated downward to a whole cent. Truncating every share leaves a remainder — the uninsured amount minus the sum of the truncated holds, which is always at least zero and always smaller than the number of eligible accounts — and that remainder is distributed one cent at a time to the eligible accounts in the same `created_at` ascending, `account_number` ascending order until it is exhausted. The resulting `held_amount_cents` figures therefore sum to exactly the determination's `uninsured_amount_cents`. Rounding each share half up instead would over-allocate and is wrong.

The issued **receivership certificate** is the depositor's claim against the estate. It records the `insurance_determination_id` that produced it, the `depositor_record_id` holding the claim and the `institution_id` of the estate it is filed against, opens at `status` `ISSUED` with `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, and starts with `cumulative_dividends_cents`, `last_dividend_amount_cents` and `last_dividend_round` all at zero. Its lifecycle runs `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` and the terminal `PAID`, with the terminal `CANCELLED` reserved for the compensation described at the end of this subsection.

Once the notice has gone out, the **depositor** closes the loop. A depositor acting on its own record acknowledges the notice, which requires `notice_status` to be `SENT` — an attempt while it is still `PENDING` or already `ACKNOWLEDGED` is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_SENT` — and moves `notice_status` `SENT` → `ACKNOWLEDGED` with `acknowledged_at` stamped from the logical clock.

Acknowledgement is the key that unlocks hold release. A `ClaimsCertifier` releases the hold on a single account, and two guards apply in order: the account's `hold_status` must be `HELD`, else `ERR_HOLD_NOT_PLACED`, and the account's primary depositor must be at `notice_status` `ACKNOWLEDGED`, else `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. The release additionally presupposes that the depositor has a determination at `CERTIFIED` — the hold could not exist otherwise. Release moves `hold_status` `HELD` → `RELEASED` and sets `held_amount_cents` back to zero. Critically, the receivership certificate is left completely untouched: releasing an operational hold does not extinguish the uninsured claim, which continues to sit against the estate and continues to be paid dividends. Releasing a hold is also what makes an account eligible for the customer transition in §3.8, since that cascade skips accounts still at `HELD`.

**Supersession** is the compensating counterpart to the certify cascade, and it fires when a `ClaimsAgent` opens a new determination for a depositor who already holds a certified one. Four levels unwind in mirror image. **First**, the prior determination moves `CERTIFIED` → `SUPERSEDED`, the depositor record's `certified_insured_cents` and `certified_uninsured_cents` are reset to zero, and the institution's `aggregate_insured_cents` is recomputed. **Second**, because that determination is now superseded, the receivership certificate it issued moves `ISSUED` → `CANCELLED` — permitted only because its `cumulative_dividends_cents` is zero, as the guard in §3.4 established — and the institution's `aggregate_certificate_face_cents` and `uninsured_claim_count` are recomputed with that certificate excluded. **Third**, because the certificate was cancelled, every deposit account whose `held_amount_cents` was allocated by the superseded determination moves `hold_status` `HELD` → `RELEASED` with `held_amount_cents` reset to zero. **Fourth**, because those holds are gone, the depositor's `notice_status` resets to `PENDING` and `notice_template_code`, `notice_sent_at` and `acknowledged_at` all reset to unset. The superseded determination's own `version` is bumped along with every other row the compensation touched. The point of the exercise is arithmetical: because the institution's aggregates are recomputed from scratch rather than adjusted, `total_deposit_balance_cents` is conserved and `aggregate_insured_cents` and `aggregate_certificate_face_cents` both return exactly to their pre-certification values, leaving the conservation deltas of §2.8 at zero.

### 3.7. Franchise Marketing, Bidder Qualification and the Bid Book

While the claims side computes insured positions, the `ResolutionAnalyst` runs a parallel track: finding somebody to buy the franchise. The marketing dimension is opened and closed by a single capability that takes a `marketing_action` of either `OPEN` or `CLOSE`. Opening requires `franchise_marketing_status` to be `NOT_STARTED` and the institution's `status` to be `RESOLUTION_READY` or `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP`, and requires a `bid_deadline_at` in the payload, which is stored as the last logical-clock instant at which a bid may be submitted. Closing requires `franchise_marketing_status` to be `OPEN`. Either action taken out of turn — opening an already-open or already-closed franchise, or closing one that was never opened — is refused with `ERR_MARKETING_ACTION_INVALID`, and an `OPEN` attempted while the institution's `status` is outside `RESOLUTION_READY` and `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` is refused with that same code rather than one of its own. `bid_deadline_at` is required only for `OPEN`: a `CLOSE` may omit it, and one supplied on a `CLOSE` is still parsed as an ISO 8601 instant under §2.4 — an unparseable value is a shape failure either way — but is then ignored and never stored. The dimension therefore walks `NOT_STARTED` → `OPEN` → `CLOSED` and each successful action bumps the institution's `version`. Note that this dimension is independent of the resolution lifecycle: marketing may be opened while the institution is still `RESOLUTION_READY`, before the charter has closed.

Prospective acquirers are registered separately from any one institution. A `ResolutionAnalyst` registers a bidder with its `legal_name` of one to 160 characters, its own `cert_number` of four to six digits which must be unique across bidders, its `tier1_capital_ratio_bps` leverage ratio in basis points, and its `camels_composite` composite supervisory rating from 1 for the best to 5 for the worst. A bidder opens at `qualification_status` `PENDING` with no `qualified_at` and no `disqualification_reason`; a duplicate `cert_number` across bidders is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

Qualification is a supervisory judgement backed by two hard floors. The analyst submits a `decision` of `QUALIFY` or `DISQUALIFY`, optionally naming a `disqualification_reason`. A `QUALIFY` decision requires the bidder's `tier1_capital_ratio_bps` to be at least 1000 — a tier-1 leverage ratio of at least ten percent — and its `camels_composite` to be no worse than 2; a bidder failing either floor cannot be qualified and the decision is refused with `ERR_BIDDER_INELIGIBLE`. A successful `QUALIFY` moves `qualification_status` `PENDING` → `QUALIFIED` and stamps `qualified_at` from the logical clock. A `DISQUALIFY` decision moves `qualification_status` `PENDING` → `DISQUALIFIED` and stores the supplied `disqualification_reason`, which is one of `CAPITAL_BELOW_FLOOR`, `CAMELS_ABOVE_CEILING` or `MANUAL` and defaults to `MANUAL` when the analyst omits it. Either decision bumps the bidder's `version`. Qualification carries no state precondition at all: a decision may be re-issued against a bidder that is already `QUALIFIED` or `DISQUALIFIED`, which is exactly how the lapsed standing that §3.8 screens for comes about. Each decision writes only its own fields — a later `QUALIFY` stamps `qualified_at` and leaves any `disqualification_reason` an earlier `DISQUALIFY` stored in place, a `QUALIFY` whose payload names a `disqualification_reason` validates it as an enum member and then ignores it, and a `DISQUALIFY` leaves any `qualified_at` already stamped untouched.

A qualified acquirer then submits bids in its own name. A bid names the `institution_id` whose franchise is being bid on, the `bidder_id` of the acquirer, the `structure` offered — `WHOLE_BANK_PA`, `INSURED_DEPOSITS_ONLY_PA` or `LOSS_SHARE_PA` — the `deposit_premium_bps` premium offered on the assumed deposit base, the `asset_discount_bps` discount demanded on the transferred asset pool, and the `data_room_window_start` and `data_room_window_end` of the due-diligence reservation the bid claims. Two inputs are optional and default to 0 when omitted: `loss_share_pct_bps`, the share of covered-asset losses the insurer would reimburse, and `asset_retention_pool_cents`, the book value of assets the bidder intends to leave behind with the receivership. A bid opens at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `non_conformance_reason` unset, with `assumed_deposit_base_cents` snapshotted from the institution's `total_deposit_balance_cents` at submission time, and with `pre_fee_cost_cents`, `loss_share_fee_cents`, `expected_loss_share_reimbursement_cents` and `evaluated_cost_cents` all left unset until the least-cost test runs. The bid lifecycle spans `SUBMITTED`, `CONFORMING`, `NON_CONFORMING` and the terminal `WINNER`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN`.

Six guards screen a submission, in this pinned order. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `bidder_id` in the payload, else `ERR_BIDDER_MISMATCH`. The institution must be at `status` `RESOLUTION_READY` or `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP`, else `ERR_BID_INSTITUTION_INELIGIBLE`. The institution's `franchise_marketing_status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_MARKETING_NOT_OPEN`. The logical clock must be at or before the institution's `bid_deadline_at`, else `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The claimed data-room interval must clear the separation rule below, else `ERR_DATA_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And the referenced bidder must be at `qualification_status` `QUALIFIED`, so a `PENDING` or `DISQUALIFIED` acquirer is refused with `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_QUALIFIED`.

The **data-room separation rule** exists because the physical data room holds one acquirer at a time and needs an hour between visits to reset. A reservation is a **closed** interval — both endpoints belong to it — and a mandatory buffer of 60 minutes must separate a new reservation from every live reservation across **all** bidders on the same institution. Concretely, a new bid is rejected when its start instant is at or before an existing reservation's end plus 60 minutes **and** that existing reservation's start is at or before the new bid's end plus 60 minutes. Reservations count as live while the owning bid is at `SUBMITTED`, `CONFORMING`, `NON_CONFORMING`, `WINNER` or `REJECTED`; a `WITHDRAWN` bid's reservation stops blocking anything. Because the intervals are closed and the buffer is mandatory, exact adjacency is rejected here — a deliberate contrast with the branch-window rule in §3.8, where adjacency is fine.

A bidder may withdraw its own bid while it is still at `SUBMITTED`, which moves it `SUBMITTED` → `WITHDRAWN`, bumps its `version`, and frees the data-room interval so later reservations on the institution stop colliding with it. Withdrawal of a bid that has already been screened or awarded is refused with `ERR_BID_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`, and a bidder attempting to withdraw somebody else's bid is refused with `ERR_BIDDER_MISMATCH`.

### 3.8. The Least-Cost Test, the Award and Branch Reopening

The statutory question at the heart of a resolution is whether selling the franchise costs the insurance fund less than simply liquidating the institution and paying the insured deposits out. The `ResolutionAnalyst` answers it in two moves — an evaluation that prices everything, then an award that picks a winner or declares that none is cheap enough.

**Evaluation** requires the institution to be at `status` `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP`, its `franchise_marketing_status` to be either `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, and its `least_cost_status` to be `NOT_STARTED`; any other combination is refused with `ERR_LEAST_COST_TEST_NOT_OPEN`. The analyst supplies a `test_variant` of `EXPECTED_LOSS_SHARE` or `RETENTION_ADJUSTED`, which is pinned onto the institution as `least_cost_test_variant` for the remainder of the resolution and governs how every bid is priced.

The evaluation first computes the **liquidation baseline**, the benchmark every bid must beat, and stores it as `liquidation_baseline_cents`. Take the institution's `aggregate_insured_cents`, multiply by 10000, divide by its `total_deposit_balance_cents` and truncate downward to obtain the insured share in basis points — that share is 0 when `total_deposit_balance_cents` is zero. Multiply the institution's `estimated_liquidation_recovery_cents` by that insured share, divide by 10000 and round half up to obtain the subrogated recovery, the portion of liquidation proceeds that would come back to the insurer as subrogee. The baseline is then `aggregate_insured_cents`, plus an administrative cost of 150000 cents — 1500 dollars — for each depositor counted in `depositor_record_count`, minus that subrogated recovery. The truncation of the insured share happens before the subrogated recovery is rounded, in that order. Both `aggregate_insured_cents` and `total_deposit_balance_cents` are taken from a fresh recompute at the instant of evaluation, and the resulting figure is stored exactly as the arithmetic yields it with no clamping, so an estate whose subrogated recovery exceeds `aggregate_insured_cents` plus the administrative cost carries a negative `liquidation_baseline_cents`.

Each bid still at `SUBMITTED` is then screened, and the screens are applied in one fixed order so that `non_conformance_reason` is deterministic when a bid fails more than one. **First**, the bid's `asset_retention_pool_cents` must be no greater than the retention cap, which is the institution's `book_asset_value_cents` multiplied by 5000, divided by 10000 and truncated downward — in effect half the book asset value; a bid above the cap is screened out with `non_conformance_reason` `RETENTION_POOL_ABOVE_CAP`. **Second**, the bid's bidder must be at `qualification_status` `QUALIFIED` at the moment of evaluation, and a bidder whose standing has lapsed since submission yields `BIDDER_NOT_QUALIFIED_AT_EVALUATION`. **Third**, a bid whose `structure` is `INSURED_DEPOSITS_ONLY_PA` requires the institution's `aggregate_insured_cents` to be greater than zero, since there are no insured deposits to assume otherwise; failing that yields `INSURED_ONLY_WITHOUT_CERTIFIED_DETERMINATIONS`.

The two arms of the screen have deliberately unequal consequences. A bid clearing all three screens moves `SUBMITTED` → `CONFORMING`, receives its full set of cost fields — `assumed_deposit_base_cents`, `pre_fee_cost_cents`, `loss_share_fee_cents`, `expected_loss_share_reimbursement_cents` and `evaluated_cost_cents` — from the cost computation below, and increments the institution's `conforming_bid_count` by one. A bid failing any screen moves `SUBMITTED` → `NON_CONFORMING`, stores its `non_conformance_reason`, has **no** cost calculation run at all — so `pre_fee_cost_cents`, `loss_share_fee_cents`, `expected_loss_share_reimbursement_cents` and `evaluated_cost_cents` all stay unset and `assumed_deposit_base_cents` keeps the snapshot taken at submission rather than being overwritten — and does not increment `conforming_bid_count`. Bids already at `WITHDRAWN` are not touched by the evaluation. When every bid has been screened, `conforming_bid_count` holds the count of bids now at `CONFORMING`, `least_cost_status` moves `NOT_STARTED` → `EVALUATED`, and the institution's `version` is bumped.

**Pricing a conforming bid** proceeds in nine pinned steps. The fee tier and the total look mutually dependent — the loss-share administration fee is selected by tier from the cost, and the cost includes the fee — and the circularity is resolved by keying the tier on the pre-fee cost of step five and never on the final total.

1. The **assumed deposit base** is the institution's `aggregate_insured_cents` when the bid's `structure` is `INSURED_DEPOSITS_ONLY_PA`, and the institution's `total_deposit_balance_cents` for every other structure. It is stored as `assumed_deposit_base_cents`, overwriting the snapshot taken at submission.
2. The **deposit premium** the acquirer pays the receivership is the assumed deposit base multiplied by the bid's `deposit_premium_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up.
3. The **transferred asset value** is the institution's `book_asset_value_cents` minus the bid's `asset_retention_pool_cents`. The **asset bid value** is that transferred asset value multiplied by the quantity 10000 minus the bid's `asset_discount_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up.
4. The **retained shortfall** is the loss the insurer expects to eat on the pool the bidder leaves behind: the bid's `asset_retention_pool_cents` multiplied by a shortfall rate, divided by 10000, rounded half up. The shortfall rate is 3000 basis points — thirty percent — under the `EXPECTED_LOSS_SHARE` variant, and 4200 basis points — forty-two percent — under the `RETENTION_ADJUSTED` variant.
5. The **pre-fee cost** is the assumed deposit base, minus the asset bid value, minus the deposit premium, plus the retained shortfall. It is stored as `pre_fee_cost_cents` and it alone keys the fee tier.
6. The **fee tier** is selected from the pre-fee cost: a pre-fee cost of 5000000 cents or less takes 25 basis points; a pre-fee cost above that but no greater than 20000000 cents takes 40 basis points; anything above 20000000 cents takes 60 basis points.
7. The **loss-share administration fee**, stored as `loss_share_fee_cents`, is the pre-fee cost multiplied by the selected tier rate, divided by 10000, rounded half up — but only when the bid's `structure` is `LOSS_SHARE_PA`. For every other structure the fee is 0.
8. The **expected loss-share reimbursement**, stored as `expected_loss_share_reimbursement_cents`, is booked only when the pinned variant is `EXPECTED_LOSS_SHARE` **and** the bid's `structure` is `LOSS_SHARE_PA`; otherwise it is 0. When it is booked, it is the transferred asset value from step three multiplied by an expected loss rate of 1800 basis points and again by the bid's `loss_share_pct_bps`, divided by 100000000 — the product of the two basis-point scales — and rounded half up.
9. The **evaluated cost**, stored as `evaluated_cost_cents`, is the pre-fee cost plus the loss-share administration fee plus the expected loss-share reimbursement.

Steps two, three, four, seven and eight each round half up independently from their own exact rational value; the products are never chained together and rounded once. The two variants therefore diverge in two distinct ways. Under `EXPECTED_LOSS_SHARE` the retained pool is marked at thirty percent and the insurer books a probability-weighted reimbursement on the transferred pool, which makes loss-share bids look expensive. Under `RETENTION_ADJUSTED` no reimbursement is booked at all and the retained pool is instead stressed at forty-two percent, which raises the pre-fee cost and can push it across a fee-tier boundary. The same set of bids can therefore produce a different winner under the two variants, which is precisely why the variant is pinned at evaluation time and recorded.

**The award** requires the institution's `least_cost_status` to be `EVALUATED` — otherwise `ERR_LEAST_COST_TEST_NOT_EVALUATED` — and that is its only precondition: the institution's `status` is necessarily `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` whenever `least_cost_status` reads `EVALUATED`, so the award makes no separate check on it. A bid is eligible to win only if it is at `CONFORMING` **and** its `evaluated_cost_cents` is strictly less than the institution's `liquidation_baseline_cents`; a bid whose evaluated cost exactly equals the baseline is not least-costly and is not eligible. Among the eligible bids the winner is the one with the lowest `evaluated_cost_cents`, ties broken by the highest `deposit_premium_bps`, and any remaining tie broken by the earliest `created_at`.

When an eligible bid exists, four levels cascade. **First**, the selected bid moves `CONFORMING` → `WINNER`, the institution's `least_cost_status` moves `EVALUATED` → `AWARDED`, its `resolution_method` is set from the winning bid's `structure`, and its `status` moves `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` → `RESOLUTION_COMPLETE`. **Second**, because a winner now exists, every other bid still at `CONFORMING` or `NON_CONFORMING` moves to `REJECTED`. **Third**, because `resolution_method` is now pinned to a purchase-and-assumption structure, every deposit account of the institution whose `insurance_status` is `DETERMINED` and whose `hold_status` is either `NONE` or `RELEASED` moves `transition_status` `PENDING` → `TRANSFERRED`; accounts still at `hold_status` `HELD` are skipped, and accounts never certified stay behind as well. **Fourth**, and **only** when the third level actually moved at least one account, every branch reopening of the institution still at `PLANNED` moves to `READY`; an award over an institution with no eligible account at all leaves its `PLANNED` reopenings at `PLANNED` even though the award itself succeeded, and because `READY` is conferred nowhere else those branches can then never be reopened.

When no eligible bid exists — whether because `conforming_bid_count` is zero or because no conforming bid beat the baseline — the same capability takes the divergent branch, and the differences matter. The institution's `least_cost_status` moves `EVALUATED` → `FAILED_ALL_BIDS`, its `resolution_method` is set to `DEPOSIT_PAYOFF` rather than to any purchase-and-assumption structure, and its `status` still moves `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` → `RESOLUTION_COMPLETE`. No bid becomes `WINNER`, while every bid at `CONFORMING` or `NON_CONFORMING` still moves to `REJECTED`. Eligible accounts — again those at `insurance_status` `DETERMINED` with `hold_status` `NONE` or `RELEASED` — move `transition_status` `PENDING` → `PAID_OUT` instead of `TRANSFERRED`, because their deposits are being paid out rather than assumed by an acquirer. And the branch cascade still fires under exactly the same condition, moving every `PLANNED` reopening to `READY` only when at least one account actually moved to `PAID_OUT`. Both `AWARDED` and `FAILED_ALL_BIDS` are terminal for the least-cost dimension, so the award can only ever run once. Every resource the cascade touches has its `version` bumped.

**Branch reopenings** are planned in advance by the `ClosingManager`, who files them against the institution with a `branch_code` of one to 16 characters identifying the branch internally, a `reopen_window_start` and `reopen_window_end` bounding the window, and a `staffing_headcount` of at least one resolution staffer assigned to it. A reopening opens at `status` `PLANNED` with `reschedule_count` zero and no `reopened_at`; `READY` is reachable only through the award cascade, never by direct request. Windows are **half-open** — the start instant belongs to the window and the end instant does not — and unlike data-room reservations they carry **no** buffer. A new or rescheduled window on the same institution and the same `branch_code` is rejected with `ERR_BRANCH_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when the new start falls strictly before an existing window's end **and** that existing window's start falls strictly before the new end, considering only existing reopenings at `PLANNED` or `READY`. A new window beginning at exactly the instant an existing one ends is therefore allowed.

Rescheduling moves a window that has not yet been used. It requires the reopening to be at `PLANNED` or `READY`, else `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_RESCHEDULABLE`, and its proposed `reopen_window_start` and `reopen_window_end` face exactly the same half-open overlap screen as creation, except that the reopening being rescheduled is excluded from that screen — so a window may be moved onto ground its own current window already occupies, while every *other* `PLANNED` or `READY` reopening of the same institution and `branch_code` still blocks. It overwrites both endpoints, increments `reschedule_count` by one, bumps the `version`, and returns the reopening to `status` `PLANNED` — including from `READY`, which is the cycle `PLANNED` → `READY` → `PLANNED`. Because `READY` is only ever conferred by the award cascade and that cascade cannot fire twice, rescheduling a branch after the award strands it at `PLANNED` permanently; this is intended, and it is why staffing and window planning are expected to be settled before the award.

Reopening the branch for customer transition requires three things: the reopening must be at `status` `READY`, else `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_READY`; the institution's `least_cost_status` must be `AWARDED` or `FAILED_ALL_BIDS`, since a branch cannot open its doors before the resolution structure is known — a condition that shares `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_READY` rather than carrying a code of its own, and that any reopening at `READY` already satisfies, since only the award cascade confers `READY`; and the logical clock must fall inside the half-open window, at or after `reopen_window_start` and strictly before `reopen_window_end`, else `ERR_OUTSIDE_REOPEN_WINDOW`. A successful reopening moves `READY` → the terminal `REOPENED`, stamps `reopened_at` from the logical clock, and bumps the `version`.

### 3.9. Receivership Disposition, the Dividend Waterfall and Termination

Whatever the acquirer did not take stays with the receivership, and the `ReceivershipTrustee` spends the following years selling it and distributing the proceeds. Each disposition is booked against the institution with an `asset_pool_code` identifying the pool that was sold, the `gross_proceeds_cents` realized and the `disposition_expense_cents` incurred in realizing them. Expenses may not exceed proceeds on a single posting: a posting whose `disposition_expense_cents` is greater than its `gross_proceeds_cents` is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_EXPENSE_EXCEEDS_PROCEEDS`. A successful posting adds the gross figure to the institution's `gross_disposition_proceeds_cents`, adds the expense figure to `disposition_expense_total_cents`, increases `receivership_cash_cents` by the difference between them, and bumps the institution's `version`. No disposition resource comes into existence: `asset_pool_code` is a required non-empty string that is validated and then discarded rather than stored anywhere, and because the posting creates nothing it answers HTTP 200 with the updated institution in `data` rather than 201.

Dividends are declared per creditor class. A declaration names the `priority_class` being paid — `INSURED_SUBROGATION`, which reimburses the insurance fund for the insured deposits it made good, or `GENERAL_UNSECURED`, which pays the depositors' own receivership certificates — the `declared_rate_bps` between 1 and 10000 at which the class's allowed claims are being paid, and the `expected_institution_version` required by §2.9. The round's `distribution_round` is set to one more than the institution's `dividend_round_count` at declaration time, and the round opens at `status` `DECLARED` with `total_distributed_cents` and `certificate_count` at zero and neither `settled_at` nor `voided_at` stamped. Declaring bumps both the institution's `dividend_round_count` and its `version`. Priority classes may not be paid out of order: once any `GENERAL_UNSECURED` round for the institution has reached `SETTLED`, an attempt to declare an `INSURED_SUBROGATION` round is refused with `ERR_PRIORITY_CLASS_OUT_OF_ORDER`. The stale-version guard is evaluated before that ordering guard.

**Settlement** turns a declared round into money moved. The round must be at `status` `DECLARED`, else `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_DECLARED`, and that state together with the cash guard below are the only checks settlement makes: the institution is at `status` `CLOSED_IN_RECEIVERSHIP` or `RESOLUTION_COMPLETE` in the ordinary course, but its `status` is never inspected, so a round declared after the receivership was already terminated settles just the same. The payout is then computed, and the two priority classes compute it in completely different ways.

For an `INSURED_SUBROGATION` round, the payout total is the institution's `aggregate_insured_cents` — recomputed at the instant of settlement, not as it stood when the round was declared — multiplied by the round's `declared_rate_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. No receivership certificate is touched at all and the round's `certificate_count` is 0 — the money goes to the insurance fund as subrogee, not to a claimant.

For a `GENERAL_UNSECURED` round, the eligible claims are the institution's receivership certificates at `status` `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, with `CANCELLED` and `PAID` certificates excluded, processed in `created_at` ascending order with ties broken by `face_amount_cents` descending. For each claim, the gross entitlement is that certificate's `face_amount_cents` multiplied by `declared_rate_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up; the payable amount is the smaller of that gross entitlement and the certificate's remaining face, which is `face_amount_cents` minus `cumulative_dividends_cents`. The round's payout total is the sum of the payable amounts **after** each has been rounded individually — as §2.6 insists, summing the entitlements and rounding once is a different and incorrect answer — and `certificate_count` is the number of claims whose payable amount came out greater than zero.

Whichever class is being paid, the computed payout total is checked against the institution's `receivership_cash_cents` before anything is written: a total exceeding available cash is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RECEIVERSHIP_CASH` and HTTP 402, and a total exactly equal to available cash is allowed. On success, each participating certificate has its payable amount added to `cumulative_dividends_cents`, records that amount as `last_dividend_amount_cents` and the round's `distribution_round` as `last_dividend_round`, and recomputes its own `status`: it becomes `PAID` when `cumulative_dividends_cents` has reached `face_amount_cents` exactly, and `PARTIALLY_PAID` when a positive payment leaves the cumulative figure short of the face. A certificate allocated exactly zero cents keeps its status unchanged. The round then stores its `total_distributed_cents` and `certificate_count`, stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock, moves `status` `DECLARED` → `SETTLED`, and the institution's `receivership_cash_cents` is recomputed and falls by the distributed total. Both the round and every certificate touched have their `version` bumped, as does the institution.

**Voiding** a round reverses it exactly. Only a settled round may be voided — otherwise `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_SETTLED` — and only the round bearing the highest `distribution_round` among the institution's settled rounds, so an attempt on an earlier round is refused with `ERR_NOT_LATEST_DIVIDEND_ROUND`; this restriction is what makes exact reversal possible, since each certificate remembers only its most recent payment. Every certificate whose `last_dividend_round` equals the voided round's `distribution_round` has its `last_dividend_amount_cents` subtracted from `cumulative_dividends_cents`, then resets `last_dividend_amount_cents` and `last_dividend_round` to zero, and recomputes its `status` from the reduced balance: a certificate left with a `cumulative_dividends_cents` of exactly zero returns to `ISSUED`, and one left with a positive balance returns to `PARTIALLY_PAID` — which is how a fully `PAID` certificate reopens as `PARTIALLY_PAID` when an earlier partial payment survives the reversal. The round itself moves `status` `SETTLED` → the terminal `VOIDED`, stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock, and **keeps** its `total_distributed_cents` for audit rather than zeroing it. On the institution, `receivership_cash_cents` is recomputed and returns to its pre-settlement value, and `dividend_round_count` is recomputed with voided rounds excluded — so the next declared round reuses the `distribution_round` the voided one had held. Every touched row's `version` is bumped.

**Termination** closes the estate, and it is deliberately hard to reach. The institution must be at `status` `RESOLUTION_COMPLETE`, no dividend round for the institution may still be sitting at `DECLARED`, and no deposit account of the institution may still be at `hold_status` `HELD`; any of these unmet yields `ERR_RECEIVERSHIP_NOT_TERMINABLE`. Termination moves the institution's `status` `RESOLUTION_COMPLETE` → the terminal `RECEIVERSHIP_TERMINATED` and bumps its `version`. Receivership certificates still at `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` keep exactly the status they had — the unpaid remainder of a claim is not cancelled or written off by termination; only the estate closes.
