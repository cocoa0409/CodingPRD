# Electric Motor Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An electric motor plant lives and dies by the gap between what its engineers predict on paper and what its dynamometers measure on the bench. A design office trades lamination grade against copper mass against rotor construction to reach an efficiency class; the shop floor then winds, impregnates, casts, machines, balances and assembles that design into physical hardware; and only at the end of the line does a regulatory efficiency minimum decide whether the motor may carry a nameplate at all. Today those three worlds are stitched together by spreadsheets and travellers, which is why a design revision can reach the shop floor after the parts are already in the oven, why a distributor's stocking allowance can be over-committed twice in the same afternoon, and why a warranty claim for a burnt winding is argued rather than adjudicated.

This document specifies a single HTTP API that carries a motor from design intent to warranty settlement without losing the audit trail. Every commercially meaningful decision the plant makes — releasing a design against a target efficiency class, promising a ship date, booking a curing oven, applying a surge voltage, stamping a nameplate against a jurisdiction's minimum, granting a warranty credit — becomes a recorded state transition with named preconditions, computed outputs, and a deterministic rejection code when the preconditions do not hold. Cost, capacity, allowance and credit are not free-text notes but ledgers that the server recomputes from first principles after every mutation, so that a cancelled order or a disputed claim leaves the books exactly where they started.

Two design commitments shape the whole specification. First, **separation of duties is enforced by the server, not by policy**: the engineer who authors a design cannot release it, the technician who winds a stator cannot surge-test it, and the engineer who runs a performance test cannot certify the nameplate that cites it. Second, **every number is reproducible**: loss models, lead times, overhead bands, balance limits, misuse indices and warranty waterfalls are specified down to the rounding step and the inclusivity of every threshold, so that two independent implementations of this document produce byte-identical arithmetic.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must identify its caller with two headers: `X-User-ID`, carrying the acting user's identifier, and `X-User-Role`, carrying exactly one role name. If either header is absent, empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. When the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the target operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Nine roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- **`DesignEngineer`** — authors motor designs, submits them for review, and reopens released designs for revision.
- **`DesignReviewer`** — adjudicates designs under review: releases them, returns them for changes, or retires them.
- **`ProductionPlanner`** — owns the commercial order book and the work-order schedule: confirms and cancels orders, opens work orders, and ships finished motors.
- **`BuildTechnician`** — runs the shop floor: winds stators, books varnish impregnation, forms and machines rotors, and assembles motors.
- **`TestEngineer`** — runs surge tests, dispositions subassemblies, schedules dynamometer tests and records efficiency measurements.
- **`QualityCertifier`** — issues and revokes nameplate certificates against the governing regulatory standard.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — adjudicates warranty claims, distinguishing application misuse from manufacturing defect, and closes them.
- **`Distributor`** — a channel partner: places orders under its stocking program, files warranty claims for its customers, and disputes adjudications it disagrees with.
- **`PlantAdmin`** — configures reference data (regulatory standards, distributors, curing ovens, dynamometer test cells) and drives the plant's logical clock. `PlantAdmin` holds no business-transition authority whatsoever: it cannot release a design, confirm or cancel an order, run a build step, certify a nameplate, ship a work order, or adjudicate a claim.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Distributor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Distributor row; ownership checks on any resource that references a distributor compare that resource's `distributor_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Distributor` caller may therefore only create, read or act on a customer order or a warranty claim whose `distributor_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and any attempt to reach another distributor's order or claim — including supplying a foreign `distributor_id` in a creation payload — is rejected with `ERR_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

For the other eight roles — `DesignEngineer`, `DesignReviewer`, `ProductionPlanner`, `BuildTechnician`, `TestEngineer`, `QualityCertifier`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `PlantAdmin` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any row's `id`. The server does, however, record it wherever the workflow narrative below says so: a design's `created_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id`, a stator's `winder_user_id` and `surge_tester_user_id`, a rotor's `rotor_tech_user_id`, an assembly's `assembler_user_id`, a performance test's `test_engineer_user_id`, and a nameplate's `certifier_user_id` are all captured from the acting caller's `X-User-ID`. Two of those recorded values are compared to a later caller's `X-User-ID` for ownership rather than separation: only the authoring engineer, identified by `created_by_user_id`, may submit their own design for review or reopen it for revision, and a different `DesignEngineer` attempting either is rejected with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. The three genuine separations of duty, which compare a recorded identifier to a *different* caller's `X-User-ID` and reject with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), are described in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a collection's own narrative says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first — so that pagination is stable while rows are being added. Because the logical clock of §2.5 stands still between advances, rows routinely share an identical `created_at`; wherever this document orders rows by `created_at` ascending — list results, cascade hops and the certification selector's tiebreak alike — rows carrying the same instant are kept in the order the server accepted them, the earlier-accepted row first, and never reordered by `id`. The one documented exception is the regulatory-standards collection, which is presented with the most recently effective rows first: `effective_from` descending, with rows sharing an `effective_from` broken by `created_at` ascending.

Every list response carries the four pagination fields inside `meta`: the `limit` and `offset` actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` indicating whether any further rows follow the returned page. Single-resource reads carry neither pagination fields nor a page envelope.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Reference-Data Immutability

Every resource the API mints carries a server-generated `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z` (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Like every other instant the server itself writes onto a row, `created_at` is read from the logical plant clock of §2.5 and never from the host's wall clock. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied identifier in a creation payload is ignored.

Mutable resources — motor designs, distributors, customer orders, work orders, stator builds, rotor builds, assembly records, performance tests, nameplates, warranty claims, curing ovens and dynamometer test cells — additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC instant in the same format as `created_at`, refreshed from that same logical clock on every mutation of the row — so a row is created with `updated_at` equal to its `created_at`, and a row mutated with no intervening clock advance still reports the two instants as equal; only an advance of the clock between a create and a later mutation makes `updated_at` later than `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations the row suffers as a *consequence* of some other resource's transition. When a cascade sets a flag on a work order, recomputes an oven's booked minutes, or rewrites a distributor's ledger, that row's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed exactly as if it had been written directly.

Regulatory standards are the deliberate exception. A registered standard is immutable reference data: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and it has neither an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field. Because standards are never edited or withdrawn, the candidate pool the certification selector draws from only ever grows.

### 2.5. The Logical Plant Clock

Deadlines, booking windows, effective dates and service ages in this system are all read from a server-held **logical UTC clock**, never from the host's wall clock. The clock starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and remains at that value until a `PlantAdmin` advances it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; before any such call the clock reads the default and must not fall back to real time. Advancing the clock changes no stored row: promised ship dates, issue instants and shipment instants already written keep their recorded values, and only future computations see the new time.

Everything time-dependent reads this clock: the lead-time computation that turns a confirmation into a `promised_ship_date`, the regulatory-standard selector's effective-date filter, the buffered oven-window conflict check, the dynamometer capacity check, the warranty pro-rata factor, and every system-written instant field — `released_at`, `confirmed_at`, `shipped_at`, `recorded_at`, `issued_at`, `revoked_at` and `reported_at`.

### 2.6. Flat Collections and Left-to-Right Reference Resolution

The thirteen resource families in this system are peer collections: no resource is nested inside another resource's URL path. A child is always created by posting to its own flat collection with its parents' identifiers in the request body — a stator build carries `work_order_id`, a warranty claim carries both `work_order_id` and `distributor_id` — and a child is always discovered by filtering its own collection.

Identifier resolution is strictly ordered. When a request carries a path identifier, that resource's existence is checked first, left to right through the path, before any body field is examined. Foreign keys in the request body are then resolved in the order the workflow narrative introduces them: `design_id`, `order_id`, `distributor_id`, `work_order_id`, `stator_build_id`, `rotor_build_id`, `oven_id`, `test_cell_id`, `performance_test_id` and `supersedes_design_id`. The first unresolvable identifier decides the response, and the error identifies the *kind* of resource that was missing: `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBASSEMBLY_NOT_FOUND` (for a stator or rotor build), `ERR_ASSEMBLY_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERFORMANCE_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NAMEPLATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OVEN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_CELL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404.

### 2.7. Metrology, Rounding and Boundary Discipline

Every rounding operation in this specification uses the **round half up** rule: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. There is no banker's rounding anywhere in the system.

The decimal width of each quantity is fixed by the physical or financial convention it belongs to. Individual loss components — predicted and measured alike — are carried to one decimal place in watts. Efficiencies, surge voltages, insulation margins and regulatory minima are carried to two decimal places in percent or kilovolts. Air gaps, bore diameters, shaft diameters and their deviations are carried to three decimal places in millimetres. Shaft-fit deviation is a whole number of micrometres. Unbalance figures and permissible unbalance limits are carried to one decimal place in gram-millimetres. Cage conductivity ratio is carried to two decimal places, slip penalty to one. The warranty misuse index is carried to four decimal places. All money in the system is an integer number of cents; no monetary field is ever a fraction of a cent or a string. Lead times are whole calendar days and oven occupancy is whole minutes.

Where a component-wise sum feeds a further calculation, the **order of rounding is part of the specification, not an implementation detail**. Both loss models in this system round each individual loss component to one decimal place *first* and only then add the rounded components together; summing raw products and rounding the total once at the end produces a different and incorrect answer.

Threshold comparisons are inclusive unless the prose explicitly says otherwise. A measured value exactly equal to a limit, a class floor, a capacity ceiling or an allowance remainder satisfies the check; the smallest representable step beyond it fails. The three deliberate exceptions — strictly greater-than comparisons — are called out where they occur: a stock-replenishment quantity strictly above the distributor's buffer stock incurs a shortfall penalty; a booking window whose start falls strictly before another booking's buffered end conflicts; and round counters must be strictly below their maxima.

### 2.8. Design Pinning and Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system read a parent resource, act on the strength of what they read, and then mutate that parent. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent's `version`, and in both cases the version field is a **required** input, not an optional refinement.

Opening a work order requires `expected_design_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the motor design being pinned. Because creating a work order pins the design's revision and advances the design's own `version`, a request whose `expected_design_version` does not equal the design's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DESIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The design may still be `RELEASED` and the request otherwise entirely legitimate; the rejection means only that the caller's view of the design predates another writer's successful pin.

Scheduling a performance test requires `expected_work_order_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target work order. Because scheduling supersedes any test already scheduled on that work order — a legitimate re-test — and advances the work order's `version`, a request whose `expected_work_order_version` does not equal the work order's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WORK_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412), on the grounds that the caller has not observed the schedule it would be discarding.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Design review decisions, order confirmations and cancellations, impregnation bookings, surge tests, dispositions, assembly creation, result recording, nameplate issue and revocation, and every warranty operation are all applied without an expected-version input.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three separations are enforced on `X-User-ID`, each rejecting with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) when violated:

1. **Design approval.** The `DesignReviewer` who records a decision on a design under review must not be the `DesignEngineer` who authored it — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the design's `created_by_user_id`. This applies to all three decisions, including the one that merely returns the design for changes and the one that retires it.
2. **Surge testing.** The `TestEngineer` who applies a surge test to an impregnated stator must not be the `BuildTechnician` who wound it — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that stator's `winder_user_id`.
3. **Nameplate certification.** The `QualityCertifier` who issues a nameplate must not be the `TestEngineer` who recorded the performance test the nameplate cites — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that test's `test_engineer_user_id`.

Nothing else in the system is separated: the same operator may wind a stator, machine a rotor and assemble the motor, and the same compliance officer may analyse and close the same claim.

### 2.10. Cascades and Compensating Ledgers

Several transitions in this system reach beyond the row they name. Cascades are applied **breadth-first, hop by hop**, and within each hop the affected rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order; where a hop touches more than one collection, the collections are processed in the order the narrative names them. Every row a cascade touches has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, and the originating row's own `version` is incremented as well. The three multi-hop chains are the design-revision chain (design → work orders → scheduled performance tests), the order-cancellation chain (order → work orders → stator, rotor and assembly rows → curing ovens), and the certification-revocation chain (nameplate → work order → warranty claims → distributors).

Four ledgers are maintained by full recomputation rather than by incremental arithmetic, which is what makes them **compensating**: after any sequence of operations the ledger equals the sum defined for it, with no accumulated drift.

- A curing oven's `booked_minutes` is always the sum of `impregnation_minutes` over the stator builds booked into that oven whose status is `IMPREGNATED`, `SURGE_TESTED` or `ACCEPTED`. A build that leaves that set — by being rejected or scrapped — releases exactly the minutes it consumed.
- A dynamometer cell's `peak_reserved_kw` is always the largest instantaneous sum of reserved draw across the tests occupying that cell, so a superseded test releases its draw in full.
- A distributor's `units_outstanding` and `stock_allowance_remaining_units` always satisfy the identity that outstanding units plus remaining allowance equals `committed_annual_units`. Confirming an order debits the allowance; cancelling or fulfilling the same order credits back exactly the same quantity.
- A distributor's `warranty_credit_balance_cents` is always the sum of `warranty_credit_cents` over that distributor's claims whose `credit_posted` flag is true, and `open_presumed_defect_count` is always the count of that distributor's claims that carry a defect presumption and are still open. A dispute clears the flag and zeroes the amount, so the balance drops by exactly what the prior adjudication posted — never a running total of every adjudication ever made.

### 2.11. Rejection Order

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is decided by the **first** rule violated in the following order, and none of the request's side effects are applied. Authentication comes first (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then endpoint role authorisation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), then row ownership (`ERR_NOT_OWNER`), then segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Only then is the payload itself examined for shape: required fields present, correct types, enum values inside their declared sets, numeric values inside their declared ranges and decimal widths, ISO 8601 instants with three fractional digits and a `Z` suffix, calendar dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`, every interval pair satisfying end strictly after start (both `impregnation_window_end` after `impregnation_window_start` and `window_end` after `window_start`), and pagination inside its bounds — all failures reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Existence checks follow: path identifiers first, then body foreign keys, as described in §2.6. After existence come uniqueness and equipment availability (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MODEL_CODE`, then `ERR_RESOURCE_IN_MAINTENANCE`), then the state-machine preconditions of the target resource in the order design, order, work order, subassembly, performance test, nameplate, claim (`ERR_INVALID_DESIGN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SUBASSEMBLY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_TEST_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_NAMEPLATE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`), and then the requalification block (`ERR_REQUALIFICATION_REQUIRED`).

Cross-entity referential rules are next, in this order: design release status (`ERR_DESIGN_NOT_RELEASED`), stocking enrolment (`ERR_STOCKING_PROGRAM_NOT_ENROLLED`), rotor construction match (`ERR_ROTOR_CONSTRUCTION_MISMATCH`), insulation-system match (`ERR_INSULATION_SYSTEM_MISMATCH`), rotor bar-count match (`ERR_ROTOR_BAR_COUNT_MISMATCH`) and jurisdiction match (`ERR_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH`). The two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.8 are evaluated immediately after those referential rules and before the remaining business rules: `ERR_STALE_DESIGN_VERSION` on work-order creation, then `ERR_STALE_WORK_ORDER_VERSION` on test scheduling. Subassembly acceptance and duplication rules follow (`ERR_SUBASSEMBLY_NOT_ACCEPTED`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SUBASSEMBLY`), then teardown-evidence consistency (`ERR_TEARDOWN_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`), then the test-window start check (`ERR_TEST_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED`).

The safety, metrology and compliance limits are evaluated after all of the above, in this order: `ERR_SLOT_FILL_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_CURE_TEMP_EXCEEDS_OVEN_LIMIT`, `ERR_SURGE_OVERSTRESS`, `ERR_SURGE_MARGIN_NEGATIVE`, `ERR_AIR_GAP_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, `ERR_UNBALANCE_EXCEEDS_GRADE_LIMIT`, `ERR_SHAFT_FIT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, `ERR_LUBRICATION_FILL_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_DESIGN_CLASS_SHORTFALL`, `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_STANDARD`, `ERR_EFFICIENCY_BELOW_REGULATORY_MINIMUM` and `ERR_NAMEPLATE_NOT_CERTIFIED`. Capacity, quota and temporal ceilings come last, in the order `ERR_OVEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_TEST_CELL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_SHIP_DATE_UNACHIEVABLE` and finally `ERR_STOCK_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright. A surge test applied above the winding's proven capability is reported as an overstress before the margin check is ever reached, and a design whose guaranteed class falls short of its target is reported as a class shortfall only after the review-round ceiling has been confirmed to be intact — the round-limit rejection is evaluated after the class check, so a design at the round ceiling *and* short of its class reports the class shortfall.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Plant Reference Data and the Logical Clock

Before a single motor can be designed, a `PlantAdmin` populates the four reference collections the rest of the system leans on.

**Regulatory standards** encode the efficiency minima that jurisdictions impose. Registering one supplies a `standard_code` of 1 to 40 characters (for example `IEC_60034_30_1`), the `jurisdiction` it governs — one of `EU`, `US` or `CN` — the `efficiency_class` the minimum belongs to (`IE2`, `IE3`, `IE4` or `IE5`), the `poles` count it applies to (`2`, `4`, `6` or `8`), the output band it covers as `rated_output_kw_min` and `rated_output_kw_max` (each to one decimal place, the minimum greater than zero and the maximum at least equal to the minimum), the `minimum_efficiency_pct` a motor in that band must reach, given to two decimal places between 50.00 and 99.99 inclusive, and an `effective_from` calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form from which the row is in force. An optional `tolerance_pct`, defaulting to 0.00, records the tolerance band published alongside the standard; it is stored for reference and is **never** subtracted from `minimum_efficiency_pct` when a nameplate is gated. Standards are immutable once registered, and the collection can be filtered by `jurisdiction`, by `poles` and by `efficiency_class`.

**Distributors** are the channel partners who order motors and file claims. Registering one supplies a `name` of 1 to 80 characters, a stocking-program `tier` of `PLATINUM`, `GOLD` or `SILVER`, and the distributor's primary `jurisdiction` (`EU`, `US` or `CN`). Three optional commercial terms follow: `stocking_program_enrolled`, a boolean defaulting to false that records whether the partner holds a stocking agreement; `committed_annual_units`, an integer between 0 and 100000 inclusive defaulting to 0 that sets the size of the allowance pool; and `stock_buffer_units`, an integer defaulting to 0 recording the buffer stock the partner holds. A freshly registered distributor starts with `units_outstanding` at 0, `stock_allowance_remaining_units` equal to its `committed_annual_units`, `warranty_credit_balance_cents` at 0 and `open_presumed_defect_count` at 0. The collection may be filtered by `tier`, by `jurisdiction` and by `stocking_program_enrolled`.

**Curing ovens** are the varnish impregnation equipment. Each is registered with an `oven_code` of 1 to 24 characters, unique across all ovens; a `max_temp_c` ceiling between 60.0 and 300.0 degrees Celsius inclusive, to one decimal place; and a `cooldown_buffer_minutes` value between 0 and 240 inclusive, which is the mandatory cooldown that extends every booked window's trailing edge for conflict purposes. An optional `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE` defaults to `AVAILABLE`; only an `AVAILABLE` oven may be booked, and a booking aimed at an oven in `MAINTENANCE` is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_IN_MAINTENANCE`. A new oven's `booked_minutes` ledger starts at 0. Ovens may be listed and filtered by `status`.

**Dynamometer test cells** are where efficiency is verified. Each carries a `cell_code` of 1 to 24 characters, unique across all cells, and a `max_concurrent_kw` figure between 1.0 and 2000.0 inclusive to one decimal place, giving the aggregate electrical draw the cell can supply to tests scheduled at the same time. As with ovens, an optional `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE` defaults to `AVAILABLE`, a cell in `MAINTENANCE` cannot take a reservation and yields `ERR_RESOURCE_IN_MAINTENANCE`, the cells collection may be filtered by `status`, and a new cell's `peak_reserved_kw` ledger starts at 0.0.

Finally, an admin-only test-driving capability moves the plant's logical clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, as described in §2.5. It returns an empty data object rather than a resource.

### 3.2. Motor Design: Geometry, Materials and the Predicted Loss Model

A `DesignEngineer` opens the lifecycle by authoring a **motor design**, which lands in `DRAFT` with `revision_index` at 0, `review_round` at 0, and `created_by_user_id` captured from the acting engineer's `X-User-ID`.

The payload is a full engineering specification. Identity and rating come first: a `model_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must not already be used by any other design — the comparison is case-sensitive and includes designs that have already been retired, and a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MODEL_CODE` (HTTP 409) — a `frame_size` IEC designation of 1 to 16 characters such as `132M`, a `poles` count of `2`, `4`, `6` or `8` which fixes synchronous speed, a `rated_output_kw` mechanical rating between 4.0 and 15.0 kW inclusive to one decimal place, a `rated_voltage_v` line voltage of `230`, `400`, `460` or `690`, and a `target_efficiency_class` of `IE2`, `IE3`, `IE4` or `IE5` naming the class the design is intended to be released against.

Magnetic and winding choices follow: a `lamination_grade` electrical-steel grade of `M400_50A`, `M330_35A`, `M270_35A` or `M235_35A`; a `lamination_stack_length_mm` axial stack length between 120 and 260 mm inclusive as a whole number; a `winding_copper_kg` magnet-wire mass between 6.0 and 30.0 kg inclusive to one decimal place; a `winding_type` layout of `CONCENTRIC` or `LAP`; and an `insulation_class` thermal class of `B`, `F` or `H`. Air-gap geometry is given as a `stator_bore_id_mm` finished bore between 80.000 and 260.000 mm inclusive, a `design_air_gap_mm` nominal radial gap between 0.200 and 1.500 mm inclusive, and an `air_gap_tolerance_mm` permitted absolute deviation between 0.010 and 0.200 mm inclusive — all three to three decimal places. The rotor is specified by a `rotor_construction` route of `DIE_CAST_ALUMINUM` or `FABRICATED_COPPER` and a `rotor_bar_count` between 24 and 48 inclusive. The mechanical package is specified by a `shaft_diameter_mm` nominal drive-end journal diameter between 20.000 and 90.000 mm inclusive to three decimal places, a `shaft_tolerance_class` ISO fit class of `k6`, `m6` or `j6`, a `bearing_spec_de` drive-end bearing designation and a `bearing_spec_nde` non-drive-end designation (each 1 to 24 characters), and a `lubrication_spec` regime of `GREASE_LIFETIME`, `GREASE_REGREASABLE` or `OIL_MIST`. Application context closes the payload: a `duty_type` IEC duty classification of `S1`, `S2` or `S3` — which also fixes the starts-per-hour reference used much later in warranty scoring — an `enclosure` ingress rating of `IP55`, `IP65` or `IP66`, and a `mounting` arrangement of `B3`, `B5`, `B35` or `V1`.

A design may optionally declare `supersedes_design_id`, a self-reference to the design it replaces; it defaults to absent. When supplied, the referenced design must itself be in `RELEASED`, `REVISION_OPEN` or `OBSOLETE` status — a design that is still `DRAFT` or `IN_REVIEW` has never been in production and cannot be superseded. The designs collection may be filtered by `status`, by `target_efficiency_class`, by `predicted_class` and by `supersedes_design_id`, the last of which is how the successors of a superseded design are found.

**The predicted loss model.** Creation runs a five-component loss model against the submitted geometry and materials, and re-running that model is the first act of every subsequent submission for review. The model reads nothing but the design row itself; no other row participates.

Begin by converting the rating to watts: the output power in watts is `rated_output_kw` multiplied by 1000. From it derive a base loss figure by multiplying the output power in watts by the pole-dependent base loss fraction — 0.070 for a 2-pole design, 0.060 for 4 poles, 0.075 for 6 poles and 0.090 for 8 poles. The five components are then each derived from that base loss figure and each rounded to one decimal place:

- **Core loss** is the base loss multiplied by a core share of 0.30, multiplied by the lamination factor for the chosen grade (1.30 for `M400_50A`, 1.12 for `M330_35A`, 1.00 for `M270_35A`, 0.92 for `M235_35A`), and multiplied by the ratio of the 180 mm reference stack length to the design's own `lamination_stack_length_mm`. A longer stack than the reference therefore lowers core loss, and a shorter one raises it.
- **Copper loss** is the base loss multiplied by a copper share of 0.35, multiplied by the winding factor for the chosen layout (1.06 for `CONCENTRIC`, 1.00 for `LAP`), and multiplied by the ratio of the 12.0 kg reference copper mass to the design's own `winding_copper_kg`.
- **Rotor loss** is the base loss multiplied by a rotor share of 0.20 and by the rotor factor for the construction route (1.15 for `DIE_CAST_ALUMINUM`, 0.88 for `FABRICATED_COPPER`).
- **Friction and windage loss** is the base loss multiplied by a friction-and-windage share of 0.10.
- **Stray loss** is the base loss multiplied by a stray share of 0.05.

`total_predicted_loss_w` is then the sum of those five *already rounded* components, in that order. The rounding happens before the summation, never after; adding the raw products and rounding the total once produces a different, wrong figure. The five components are intermediate figures of the model: only `total_predicted_loss_w` is stored on the design row and returned by the motor-design endpoints, and the design resource carries no per-component loss field of its own. (The separately named per-component fields of the *measured* loss model in §3.8 are fields of a performance test, not of a design.)

From the loss total the design's two efficiency figures follow. `predicted_efficiency_pct` is 100 multiplied by the output power in watts, divided by the sum of the output power in watts and `total_predicted_loss_w`, rounded to two decimal places. `guaranteed_efficiency_pct` applies the IEC manufacturing-tolerance derate: take the design's loss share as 100 minus `predicted_efficiency_pct`, inflate that shortfall by a tolerance coefficient of 0.15 — that is, multiply it by 1.15 — subtract the inflated shortfall from 100, and round to two decimal places. The guaranteed figure is therefore always at or below the predicted figure.

**Two class verdicts, one gate.** The design reports two efficiency-class verdicts drawn from the same floor table, and they can legitimately disagree. `design_estimate_class` is the highest class among `IE2`, `IE3`, `IE4` and `IE5` whose floor for the design's pole count is less than or equal to `predicted_efficiency_pct`; `predicted_class` is the highest such class whose floor is less than or equal to `guaranteed_efficiency_pct`. When even the `IE2` floor is not reached, the verdict is `BELOW_IE2`. The floors, in percent, are 89.60 / 90.40 / 89.80 / 88.60 for `IE2` at 2, 4, 6 and 8 poles respectively; 91.40 / 92.10 / 91.60 / 90.50 for `IE3`; 93.00 / 93.60 / 93.10 / 92.20 for `IE4`; and 94.30 / 94.80 / 94.40 / 93.60 for `IE5`. A value exactly equal to a floor attains that class. Because the derate shifts the classified figure downward, identical inputs can land the estimate verdict a full class above the guaranteed verdict — a design that estimates `IE4` may only guarantee `IE3` — and it is the **guaranteed** verdict, `predicted_class`, that gates release. Nothing about the loss total is determined by a single factor: pole count, lamination grade, winding layout, rotor route, stack length and copper mass all move it, which is why the class verdict must always be read from the computed figures rather than assumed from the material choices.

### 3.3. Design Review, Release and the Revision Cycle

A design's `status` cycles through five values — `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `RELEASED`, `REVISION_OPEN` and the terminal `OBSOLETE` — and the cycle is genuinely a cycle: a rejected design returns to `DRAFT` and comes back for another round, and a released design can be reopened and released again.

**Submitting for review** advances the design `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, or `REVISION_OPEN → IN_REVIEW` when the engineer is closing out a reopened revision. The submitting `X-User-ID` must equal the design's `created_by_user_id`; anybody else is refused with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. A design in any other status yields `ERR_INVALID_DESIGN_STATE`. Submission re-runs every output of the loss model against the design's *current* field set — `total_predicted_loss_w`, `predicted_efficiency_pct`, `guaranteed_efficiency_pct`, `design_estimate_class` and `predicted_class` are all recomputed — which is what makes the revision loop meaningful: an engineer who thickens the lamination stack after a rejection sees the new numbers on re-submission. The design's `version` advances by 1.

**Recording a decision** is the `DesignReviewer`'s act, and it is the only place the design-approval separation of §2.9 applies. The reviewer supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_CHANGES` or `OBSOLETE`, and optionally `review_notes`. The design must be in `IN_REVIEW`, or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_DESIGN_STATE`. Every decision, without exception, increments `review_round` by 1 and advances the design's `version`; and every decision is barred once `review_round` has already reached 3, since the round counter must be strictly below the maximum of 3 review rounds — an attempt at or above that ceiling is refused with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The three branches then diverge:

- `APPROVE` moves the design `IN_REVIEW → RELEASED`. Release is gated on the guaranteed verdict: `predicted_class` must rank at or above `target_efficiency_class` on the ordering where `BELOW_IE2` is lowest, followed by `IE2`, `IE3`, `IE4` and `IE5` highest. A design whose guaranteed class ranks below its declared target is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_CLASS_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 422) — the estimate verdict is irrelevant to this gate. On success the server sets `released_at` to the current logical-clock instant and `released_by_user_id` to the reviewer's `X-User-ID`.
- `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the design `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT` and, in addition to the round counter, increments `revision_index` by 1. No release fields are written and no class gate is applied.
- `OBSOLETE` moves the design `IN_REVIEW → OBSOLETE`, which is terminal. An obsolete design can never again back a customer order or a work order; it survives only as history and as a legal target for another design's `supersedes_design_id`.

**Reopening a released design for revision** is the engineer's escape hatch and the most far-reaching cascade in the design domain. The authoring engineer — again matched on `created_by_user_id`, with `ERR_NOT_OWNER` otherwise — supplies a `revision_reason` and the design moves `RELEASED → REVISION_OPEN` with `revision_index` incremented by 1. The reopening then propagates in two hops. In the first hop, every work order pinned to this design whose `build_status` is `RELEASED`, `SUBASSEMBLIES_DONE`, `ASSEMBLED` or `TESTED` has `design_pin_stale` set to true and its own `version` incremented, processed in `created_at` ascending order; work orders already `SHIPPED` or `CANCELLED` are untouched, since their hardware has left the plant or been written off. In the second hop, caused by the first, every performance test still in `SCHEDULED` status on each of those newly stale work orders has `requires_requalification` set to true and its own `version` incremented, again in `created_at` ascending order. A test carrying that flag can no longer be recorded; it must be replaced by a freshly scheduled test. The design's own `version` advances by 1 last.

### 3.4. Distributor Programs, Lead Times and the Commercial Order Book

A **customer order** is opened by a `ProductionPlanner` or by the `Distributor` itself, and it starts in `DRAFT`. Its payload names the ordering `distributor_id` — which, when the caller is a `Distributor`, must equal that caller's own `X-User-ID` — the `design_id` of the motor ordered, an `order_type` of `STOCK_REPLENISH`, `MADE_TO_ORDER` or `ENGINEERED_SPECIAL`, a `quantity` between 1 and 5000 inclusive, a `requested_ship_date` calendar date the distributor asks to ship by, and the destination `jurisdiction` of `EU`, `US` or `CN`. An optional `special_requirements` object, defaulting to empty, carries engineered-motor flags; the four recognised keys are `non_standard_mounting`, `hazardous_environment`, `non_standard_duty` and `marine_certification`, each a boolean. An unrecognised key contributes nothing at all, and a recognised key that is simply absent is treated as false. Order creation computes a *provisional* lead time immediately; the orders collection can be filtered by `distributor_id`, by `design_id`, by `status` and by `order_type`.

**How a lead time is built.** The promised lead time is assembled from four additive contributions and one subtraction, then clamped. Start with the channel base: 5 days for `STOCK_REPLENISH`, 28 days for `MADE_TO_ORDER`, 56 days for `ENGINEERED_SPECIAL`. Add the special-requirement adders for every recognised flag that is true, summed in the fixed key order `non_standard_mounting` (7 days), `hazardous_environment` (14 days), `non_standard_duty` (5 days), `marine_certification` (21 days) — a fully engineered special with all four flags therefore carries 47 additional days. Add a stocking shortfall penalty of 11 days, but only for a `STOCK_REPLENISH` order whose `quantity` is **strictly greater** than the distributor's `stock_buffer_units`; a quantity exactly equal to the buffer draws no penalty, and no other order type ever draws it. Add the destination certification adder: 3 days for `EU`, 5 days for `US`, 9 days for `CN`. From that running total subtract the tier reduction the distributor has earned — 4 days for `PLATINUM`, 2 days for `GOLD`, none for `SILVER`. Finally, apply a floor: `promised_lead_time_days` is the larger of 3 days and the figure just computed, so no combination of tier reductions can promise a motor in under three days. The floor is applied **last**, after every adder and after the tier reduction. `promised_ship_date` is then the date portion of the current logical clock advanced by `promised_lead_time_days` calendar days, counted straight through weekends and holidays with no skipping. The design contributes nothing to lead time; only the order row and its distributor row participate.

**Confirming an order** is a `ProductionPlanner`'s act, moving it `DRAFT → CONFIRMED`, and it is where the promise becomes binding. Five conditions must hold. The order must be in `DRAFT` (`ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`). The design it names must be in `RELEASED` at that moment — a design still under review, reopened for revision, or retired is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_RELEASED` (HTTP 409). A `STOCK_REPLENISH` order additionally requires the distributor's `stocking_program_enrolled` flag to be true, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_STOCKING_PROGRAM_NOT_ENROLLED` (HTTP 409); other order types are indifferent to enrolment. The lead time is then recomputed from the *current* logical clock date, and the resulting `promised_ship_date` must fall on or before `requested_ship_date`; a date that slips past the request is refused with `ERR_SHIP_DATE_UNACHIEVABLE` (HTTP 422). Lastly, `quantity` must be no greater than the distributor's `stock_allowance_remaining_units` — a quantity exactly equal to the remainder is allowed, one unit more is refused with `ERR_STOCK_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

On success the recomputed `promised_lead_time_days` and `promised_ship_date` are stored as the binding promise, `confirmed_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant, and the distributor's allowance ledger is debited: `units_outstanding` rises by the order's `quantity` and `stock_allowance_remaining_units` falls by the same amount, with the distributor's `version` advancing by 1. The order's `version` advances by 1 as well.

**The order's remaining states** are driven by production and by cancellation. When the first work order is opened against a `CONFIRMED` order, the order moves `CONFIRMED → IN_PRODUCTION` as a system event and its `version` advances. When that work order ships, the order moves `IN_PRODUCTION → FULFILLED`, and the allowance ledger is credited back by exactly the quantity it was debited — `units_outstanding` falls by `quantity`, `stock_allowance_remaining_units` rises by `quantity`, and both the distributor's and the order's `version` advance.

**Cancellation** is available to a `ProductionPlanner` from `DRAFT`, from `CONFIRMED` and from `IN_PRODUCTION`, always with a `cancellation_reason`, and always advancing the order's `version`. From any other status — that is, from `FULFILLED` or from `CANCELLED` — it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. What cancellation *does* depends on how far the order had travelled:

- From `DRAFT`, cancellation is a bookkeeping no-op beyond the status change: no allowance was ever debited from a draft order, so nothing is credited back.
- From `CONFIRMED`, the allowance is credited back exactly as on fulfilment — `units_outstanding` down by `quantity`, `stock_allowance_remaining_units` up by `quantity`, distributor `version` incremented — and no production work exists to unwind.
- From `IN_PRODUCTION`, the same allowance credit-back occurs *and* a three-hop cascade unwinds the shop floor. In the first hop, every work order of this order whose `build_status` is neither `SHIPPED` nor `CANCELLED` moves to `CANCELLED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each incrementing its own `version`. In the second hop, caused by the first, each of those newly cancelled work orders has its subassemblies written off: every stator build on it whose status is neither `REJECTED` nor `SCRAPPED` moves to `SCRAPPED`, then every rotor build on it whose status is neither `REJECTED` nor `SCRAPPED` moves to `SCRAPPED`, then every assembly record on it in `BUILT` status moves to `SCRAPPED` — the three collections processed in that order, each collection internally in `created_at` ascending order, each row incrementing its own `version`. In the third hop, caused by the second, every curing oven that was holding one of those now-scrapped stator builds has its `booked_minutes` recomputed, because a scrapped build has left the counted status set, and that oven's `version` is incremented. Scrapping is explicitly **not** a rework event: it never increments a work order's `rework_reject_count` and never adds a rework surcharge.

### 3.5. Work Orders: Pinning a Revision and Costing the Build

A **work order** is the production instruction, and it is deliberately the only place in the system where two independent state dimensions live on one row. `build_status` tracks the physical build through `RELEASED`, `SUBASSEMBLIES_DONE`, `ASSEMBLED`, `TESTED`, `SHIPPED` and `CANCELLED`, driven by shop-floor events. `certification_status` tracks regulatory standing through `PENDING`, `CERTIFIED` and `SUSPENDED`, driven entirely by nameplate issue and revocation. The two axes move independently: a certification can cycle `PENDING → CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` without `build_status` changing at all, and `build_status` can advance from `RELEASED` all the way to `TESTED` while `certification_status` sits at `PENDING`.

A `ProductionPlanner` opens a work order against a customer order that is `CONFIRMED` or already `IN_PRODUCTION`; against an order in any other status the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The payload names the `order_id`, the `design_id` — which must be the very design the customer order named, and which must be in `RELEASED` status at that moment or the request is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_RELEASED` — a `unit_count` between 1 and 5000 inclusive giving the units built under this order, and the mandatory `expected_design_version` of §2.8. On success the row starts with `build_status` at `RELEASED`, `certification_status` at `PENDING`, `design_pin_stale` false and `rework_reject_count` at 0, and it captures `pinned_design_revision_index` from the design's current `revision_index`. The design's own `version` advances by 1, which is precisely why the concurrency guard exists. If the customer order was still `CONFIRMED` it moves to `IN_PRODUCTION` and its `version` advances. Work orders can be filtered by `order_id`, by `design_id`, by `build_status`, by `certification_status` and by `design_pin_stale`.

**Costing the build.** Cost is computed at creation and recomputed whenever `rework_reject_count` changes. All design inputs are read from the *pinned* design and are never re-read after a later revision, so a reopened design does not silently re-price work already in flight.

Per unit, four amounts are added. The copper amount is `winding_copper_kg` multiplied by 1150 cents per kilogram, rounded to whole cents. The lamination amount is `lamination_stack_length_mm` multiplied by the per-millimetre rate for the grade — 420 cents for `M400_50A`, 510 for `M330_35A`, 640 for `M270_35A`, 780 for `M235_35A`. The rotor amount is the fixed process cost of the construction route: 18000 cents for `DIE_CAST_ALUMINUM`, 47500 cents for `FABRICATED_COPPER`. To those three add a flat labour amount of 26000 cents per unit. That sum is the per-unit direct cost, exposed on the cost read-out as `per_unit_direct_cost_cents`; multiplying it by `unit_count` gives the work order's `direct_cost_cents`.

The `overhead_band` is then selected from `direct_cost_cents` **alone** — overhead itself is never part of the band input, even though overhead is added to reach the standard cost, so the apparent circularity is not real. A direct cost of at least 4000000 cents lands in `ENGINEERED`; otherwise a direct cost of at least 1500000 cents lands in `COMPLEX`; otherwise the band is `STANDARD`. Both thresholds are inclusive lower bounds: a direct cost of exactly 1500000 cents is `COMPLEX`, while 1499999 is `STANDARD`. `overhead_cents` is `direct_cost_cents` multiplied by the band's rate — 0.22 for `STANDARD`, 0.31 for `COMPLEX`, 0.44 for `ENGINEERED` — rounded to whole cents. `standard_cost_cents` is `direct_cost_cents` plus `overhead_cents`.

Rework is priced on top. `actual_cost_cents` is `standard_cost_cents` plus 62500 cents for each rejected subassembly counted in `rework_reject_count`, and `cost_variance_cents` is `actual_cost_cents` minus `standard_cost_cents` — which is to say, exactly the accumulated rework surcharge, and exactly zero on a work order that has never had a rejection. Only stator and rotor builds that reached `REJECTED` are counted; rows that reached `SCRAPPED` are excluded.

A derived cost read-out is available for a single work order, returning `work_order_id`, `unit_count`, `per_unit_direct_cost_cents`, `direct_cost_cents`, `overhead_band`, `overhead_cents`, `standard_cost_cents`, `actual_cost_cents`, `cost_variance_cents`, `rework_reject_count` and `unit_standard_cost_cents`. That last figure — `standard_cost_cents` divided by `unit_count`, rounded to whole cents — is computed on read rather than stored on the work order, and it is the same quantity that gets pinned onto a warranty claim at analysis time.

**Shipping** closes the build dimension. A `ProductionPlanner` ships a work order whose `build_status` is `TESTED`, moving it `TESTED → SHIPPED`; from any other `build_status` the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. The transition is guarded *across* the two dimensions: `certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED`, so a tested motor whose nameplate was never issued (`PENDING`) or has been revoked (`SUSPENDED`) is refused with `ERR_NAMEPLATE_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409) and must not leave the plant. On success `shipped_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant, the work order's `version` advances, and the owning customer order moves `IN_PRODUCTION → FULFILLED` with the allowance credit-back and version increments described in §3.4.

### 3.6. Stator Winding, Varnish Impregnation and Surge Qualification

A **stator build** travels a linear path — `WOUND`, `IMPREGNATED`, `SURGE_TESTED`, and then one of the terminal states `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `SCRAPPED` — and it is the only subassembly that consumes shared plant equipment along the way.

A `BuildTechnician` records a wound stator against a work order whose `build_status` is `RELEASED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. The payload names the `work_order_id`, the `insulation_system` actually applied — `CLASS_B_POLYESTER`, `CLASS_F_POLYESTERIMIDE` or `CLASS_H_POLYAMIDEIMIDE` — and the `slot_fill_pct`, the copper slot fill as a percentage of usable slot area to two decimal places. Slot fill must lie between 78.00 and 86.00 percent inclusive; outside that band the record is refused with `ERR_SLOT_FILL_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). The insulation system must also agree with the pinned design: `CLASS_B_POLYESTER` maps to thermal class `B`, `CLASS_F_POLYESTERIMIDE` to `F` and `CLASS_H_POLYAMIDEIMIDE` to `H`, and that mapped class must equal the design's `insulation_class` or the record is refused with `ERR_INSULATION_SYSTEM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). A work order may hold at most one live stator build at a time: if it already has a stator build that is neither `REJECTED` nor `SCRAPPED`, a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SUBASSEMBLY` (HTTP 409) — a rejected or scrapped predecessor, by contrast, may freely be replaced. On success the build lands in `WOUND` with `winder_user_id` captured from the technician's `X-User-ID`, the work order's `version` advances by 1, and the surge envelope's first output is fixed.

**The surge envelope.** `required_surge_kv` is the level the winding must withstand and is fixed at creation: take twice the design's `rated_voltage_v`, add a 1000 V base, multiply by the insulation-class factor (1.00 for class `B`, 1.15 for class `F`, 1.30 for class `H`), divide by 1000 to express the result in kilovolts, and round to two decimal places. `achieved_capability_kv` is the dielectric capability the winding actually has after impregnation, and it is `required_surge_kv` multiplied by the varnish-process factor and then by one plus the cycle bonus, rounded to two decimal places. The process factors are 1.00 for `DIP_BAKE`, 1.05 for `TRICKLE` and 1.20 for `VPI`. The cycle bonus is 0.04 for each impregnation cycle beyond the first, capped at 0.12 — so two cycles give 0.04, three give 0.08, four give 0.12, and five or six give no more than four does. Before impregnation, `achieved_capability_kv` is absent. `slot_fill_pct` plays no part in the surge arithmetic at all; it is validated at creation and then simply recorded.

**Booking impregnation** moves the stator `WOUND → IMPREGNATED` and is the `BuildTechnician`'s act. The payload names the `oven_id` of the curing oven, the occupancy window as `impregnation_window_start` and `impregnation_window_end`, the `varnish_process` of `DIP_BAKE`, `VPI` or `TRICKLE`, the `varnish_cycles` count between 1 and 6 inclusive, and the `cure_temp_c` requested for the window. Four conditions guard the booking. The stator must be in `WOUND` (`ERR_INVALID_SUBASSEMBLY_STATE`). The oven must be `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_RESOURCE_IN_MAINTENANCE`). The `cure_temp_c` must not exceed the oven's `max_temp_c`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_CURE_TEMP_EXCEEDS_OVEN_LIMIT` (HTTP 422); a requested temperature exactly equal to the ceiling is acceptable. And the window must not collide with any live booking on the same oven.

The collision rule is deliberately asymmetric, because an oven needs to cool between loads. For each existing stator build on the same oven whose status is `IMPREGNATED`, `SURGE_TESTED` or `ACCEPTED`, extend that booking's `impregnation_window_end` forward by the oven's `cooldown_buffer_minutes` to obtain its effective end. The requested window collides with that booking when the requested start falls strictly before the existing booking's effective end **and** the existing booking's `impregnation_window_start` falls strictly before the requested end. Any collision is refused with `ERR_OVEN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). A new window whose start coincides exactly with a previous window's end *plus the full cooldown buffer* does not collide, while a start one minute earlier does; and whenever the buffer is greater than zero, a window butting directly against a previous window's raw end always collides. Builds in `WOUND` have never been booked and are ignored, as are `REJECTED` and `SCRAPPED` builds.

On a successful booking the server stores `oven_id`, both window bounds, `varnish_process`, `varnish_cycles` and `cure_temp_c`; derives `impregnation_minutes` as the whole number of minutes between the two window bounds; computes `achieved_capability_kv`; recomputes the oven's `booked_minutes` ledger and advances that oven's `version` by 1; and advances the stator's own `version` by 1.

**Surge testing** moves the stator `IMPREGNATED → SURGE_TESTED` and belongs to a `TestEngineer` who is not the winder — the separation of §2.9, enforced against `winder_user_id`. The single payload field is `surge_test_kv`, the voltage actually applied. Two safety gates apply in a fixed order. First, the applied level must not exceed `achieved_capability_kv`; a level beyond the impregnated capability would destroy the winding and is refused with `ERR_SURGE_OVERSTRESS` (HTTP 422). Second, the applied level must be at least `required_surge_kv`, since a level below the required envelope does not qualify the insulation system; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_SURGE_MARGIN_NEGATIVE` (HTTP 422). Both gates are inclusive at the boundary: an applied level exactly equal to the capability passes, and a margin of exactly 0.00 kV passes. On success the server records `surge_test_kv` and `surge_tester_user_id`, computes `insulation_margin_kv` as `surge_test_kv` minus `required_surge_kv` rounded to two decimal places, sets `surge_test_result` to `PASS`, and advances the stator's `version`. Because both failure modes are rejected before any state change is applied, a stored stator build never carries a `surge_test_result` of `FAIL`.

**Dispositioning** is also the `TestEngineer`'s act, taking a `disposition` of `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` plus optional `disposition_notes`. Acceptance requires the stator to be in `SURGE_TESTED` with a `surge_test_result` of `PASS`, and moves it `SURGE_TESTED → ACCEPTED`. Rejection is available from `WOUND`, `IMPREGNATED` or `SURGE_TESTED` alike; from any other status either disposition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBASSEMBLY_STATE`. A rejection has two compensating effects beyond the status change: if the build was holding an oven booking, that oven's `booked_minutes` is recomputed — the rejected build has left the counted set, so its minutes are released in full — and the oven's `version` advances; and the owning work order's `rework_reject_count` rises by 1, with `actual_cost_cents` and `cost_variance_cents` recomputed accordingly. Either disposition advances both the work order's `version` and the stator's `version` by 1. Acceptance additionally checks the fork-join described in §3.7. Stator builds can be filtered by `work_order_id`, by `oven_id`, by `status` and by `varnish_process`.

### 3.7. Rotor Cage Routes, Air Gap and Balance

A **rotor build** forks. Its `status` starts at `PLANNED` and then takes exactly one of two mutually exclusive cage routes — `CAST_POURED` or `BARS_BRAZED` — before both routes rejoin at `MACHINED` and proceed to `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `SCRAPPED`. Which fork is legal is not the technician's choice: it is dictated by the design.

A `BuildTechnician` opens a rotor build against a work order whose `build_status` is `RELEASED`, supplying only the `work_order_id`. The server sets `status` to `PLANNED`, captures `rotor_tech_user_id` from the technician's `X-User-ID`, copies `construction` from the pinned design's `rotor_construction` — the value is fixed at creation and immutable thereafter — and advances the work order's `version` by 1. As with stators, a work order may hold at most one live rotor build: an existing rotor build that is neither `REJECTED` nor `SCRAPPED` makes a second one an `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_SUBASSEMBLY` refusal.

**Forming the cage** takes a `cage_method` of `DIE_CAST` or `BRAZED_BAR`, and the two branches carry different payloads, write different fields and perform different checks. The build must be in `PLANNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBASSEMBLY_STATE`.

- On the **`DIE_CAST`** branch, the build's `construction` must be `DIE_CAST_ALUMINUM`, the payload must carry both `melt_temp_c` (the aluminium melt temperature) and `die_preheat_c` (the die preheat temperature), and it must carry neither `braze_alloy` nor `bar_count`. A method that contradicts the construction, or a payload missing its own fork's fields or carrying the other fork's fields, is refused with `ERR_ROTOR_CONSTRUCTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). On success `melt_temp_c` and `die_preheat_c` are stored, the cage property triple takes its aluminium values — `cage_conductivity_ratio` 0.62, `slip_penalty_pct` 1.8, `rotor_process_cost_cents` 18000 — and the build moves `PLANNED → CAST_POURED`.
- On the **`BRAZED_BAR`** branch, the build's `construction` must be `FABRICATED_COPPER`, the payload must carry both `braze_alloy` (the filler alloy) and `bar_count` (the number of copper bars brazed), and it must carry neither `melt_temp_c` nor `die_preheat_c`; the same `ERR_ROTOR_CONSTRUCTION_MISMATCH` applies to every violation of that shape. This branch performs one extra cross-check the die-cast branch does not: `bar_count` must equal the pinned design's `rotor_bar_count`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_ROTOR_BAR_COUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). On success `braze_alloy` and `bar_count` are stored, the cage property triple takes its copper values — `cage_conductivity_ratio` 1.00, `slip_penalty_pct` 0.6, `rotor_process_cost_cents` 47500 — and the build moves `PLANNED → BARS_BRAZED`.

Either branch advances the rotor build's `version` by 1. The three cage properties are pure table lookups keyed on `construction`, not derived arithmetic: the conductivity ratio is expressed to two decimal places relative to copper, the slip penalty to one decimal place in percent, and the process cost in whole cents.

**Machining** is the join point: both `CAST_POURED` and `BARS_BRAZED` converge on it with identical downstream behaviour, and from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBASSEMBLY_STATE`. The technician supplies `measured_rotor_od_mm`, the finished rotor outer diameter, and `rotor_mass_kg`, the finished mass. From the outer diameter the server derives the air gap: `measured_air_gap_mm` is the pinned design's `stator_bore_id_mm` minus `measured_rotor_od_mm`, halved, rounded to three decimal places — the gap is radial, which is why the diametral difference is halved. `air_gap_deviation_mm` is then `measured_air_gap_mm` minus the design's `design_air_gap_mm`, again rounded to three decimal places, and it may be negative when the rotor came out oversize. The absolute value of that deviation must not exceed the design's `air_gap_tolerance_mm`; a deviation whose magnitude exactly equals the tolerance is accepted, while one thousandth of a millimetre beyond it is refused with `ERR_AIR_GAP_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (HTTP 422). On success both derived figures and both measurements are stored, the build moves to `MACHINED`, and its `version` advances by 1.

**Balancing and disposition** belong to a `TestEngineer` and require the build to be in `MACHINED`. The payload carries a `disposition` of `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, the `balance_grade` the rotor was balanced to (`G1_0`, `G2_5` or `G6_3`), the `initial_unbalance_g_mm` measured before correction and the `residual_unbalance_g_mm` remaining after it. Whatever the disposition, the server records all four values and computes `permissible_unbalance_g_mm`: multiply the ISO balance constant 9549 by the grade's velocity figure in millimetres per second (1.0 for `G1_0`, 2.5 for `G2_5`, 6.3 for `G6_3`), multiply by `rotor_mass_kg` as captured at machining, divide by the synchronous speed in revolutions per minute implied by the design's pole count (3000 rpm at 2 poles, 1500 at 4, 1000 at 6, 750 at 8), and round to one decimal place. An `ACCEPTED` disposition additionally requires `residual_unbalance_g_mm` to be no greater than that permissible limit — a residual exactly equal to the limit is accepted, anything above it is refused with `ERR_UNBALANCE_EXCEEDS_GRADE_LIMIT` (HTTP 422) — and moves the build `MACHINED → ACCEPTED`. A `REJECTED` disposition applies no unbalance gate at all, moves the build `MACHINED → REJECTED`, and raises the owning work order's `rework_reject_count` by 1 with `actual_cost_cents` and `cost_variance_cents` recomputed. Either disposition advances both the work order's `version` and the rotor build's `version` by 1.

**The fork-join to subassemblies-done.** A work order's `build_status` moves `RELEASED → SUBASSEMBLIES_DONE` as a system event the moment both branches have landed: at least one stator build on the work order is `ACCEPTED` and at least one rotor build on it is `ACCEPTED`. Whichever acceptance completes the pair triggers the move, and the order in which the two branches finish is immaterial. The transition increments the work order's `version` by 1.

Both subassembly families are also written off by the order-cancellation cascade of §3.4, moving to `SCRAPPED` from any non-terminal status and incrementing their own `version` — a stator from `WOUND`, `IMPREGNATED` or `SURGE_TESTED`, a rotor from `PLANNED`, `CAST_POURED`, `BARS_BRAZED` or `MACHINED`. A scrapped stator releases its oven minutes; a scrapped rotor has no equipment to release. Rotor builds may be filtered by `work_order_id`, by `construction`, by `status` and by `balance_grade`.

### 3.8. Assembly, Dynamometer Scheduling and Efficiency Verification

**Assembly** joins an accepted stator to an accepted rotor and is the `BuildTechnician`'s last act. It requires the work order's `build_status` to be `SUBASSEMBLIES_DONE`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. The payload names the `work_order_id`, the `stator_build_id` and `rotor_build_id` being consumed, the `de_bearing_lot` and `nde_bearing_lot` lot identifiers as installed (each 1 to 32 characters), the `bearing_install_method` of `INDUCTION_HEAT`, `PRESS` or `OIL_BATH`, the `shaft_measured_diameter_mm` measured at the drive-end journal to three decimal places, and the `grease_fill_pct` as a percentage of free bearing volume to two decimal places. Both cited subassemblies must belong to *this* work order and must both be in `ACCEPTED` status; a build belonging to another work order or in any other status is refused with `ERR_SUBASSEMBLY_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409).

Two metrology gates then apply. `shaft_fit_deviation_um` is the measured journal diameter minus the design's `shaft_diameter_mm`, converted to micrometres by multiplying by 1000 and rounded to a whole number, and it may be negative when the journal came out undersize. It must fall inside the ISO band for the design's `shaft_tolerance_class`, both bounds inclusive: 2 to 18 micrometres for `k6`, 7 to 25 for `m6`, and -5 to 11 for `j6`. Outside the band the assembly is refused with `ERR_SHAFT_FIT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (HTTP 422). Separately, `lubrication_spec_applied` is copied from the design's `lubrication_spec` at creation and determines the acceptable grease fill, compared to one decimal place with both bounds inclusive: 30 to 40 percent for `GREASE_LIFETIME`, 40 to 60 percent for `GREASE_REGREASABLE`, and exactly 0 for `OIL_MIST` — an oil-mist machine has no grease in it at all and must report a fill of 0.0. A fill outside its regime's range is refused with `ERR_LUBRICATION_FILL_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422).

On success the assembly record lands in `BUILT` with `assembler_user_id` captured from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and the work order moves `build_status` from `SUBASSEMBLIES_DONE` to `ASSEMBLED` with its `version` incremented by 1. An assembly record's only other state is `SCRAPPED`, reached solely through the order-cancellation cascade from `BUILT`. Assembly records may be filtered by `work_order_id`, by `stator_build_id`, by `rotor_build_id` and by `status`.

**Scheduling a dynamometer test** is a `TestEngineer`'s act against a work order whose `build_status` is `ASSEMBLED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`. The payload names the `work_order_id`, the `test_cell_id` reserved, the half-open window as `window_start` (inclusive) and `window_end` (exclusive), the `dyno_draw_kw` electrical draw the test reserves between 0.1 and 500.0 kW inclusive to one decimal place, the `test_method` — `IEC_60034_2_1_SUMMATION` or `IEEE_112_B_DYNAMOMETER` — and the mandatory `expected_work_order_version` of §2.8. The cell must be `AVAILABLE` or the request is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_IN_MAINTENANCE`.

Cell capacity is enforced as an aggregate, not a headcount. Collect every performance test on that cell whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` and whose window overlaps the requested one, where overlap means the existing window starts strictly before the requested end and the requested start falls strictly before the existing window's end — half-open intervals, so a window starting exactly when another ends does not overlap. Sum those tests' `dyno_draw_kw`, add the requested draw, and round the total to one decimal place. That aggregate must not exceed the cell's `max_concurrent_kw`: an aggregate exactly equal to the ceiling is allowed, and 0.1 kW beyond it is refused with `ERR_TEST_CELL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Tests in `SUPERSEDED` status release their draw entirely and never count. The cell's `peak_reserved_kw` is maintained as the largest such aggregate over every instant covered by that cell's `SCHEDULED` and `COMPLETED` tests, rounded to one decimal place, and it reads 0.0 when the cell holds no such tests.

Scheduling also supersedes: any performance test already `SCHEDULED` on the same work order moves `SCHEDULED → SUPERSEDED`, incrementing its own `version`, and the cell's `peak_reserved_kw` is recomputed with that test's draw released and the cell's `version` incremented. This is a legitimate re-test — and it is precisely why the operation carries the work-order concurrency guard, since a caller who has not observed the existing schedule would otherwise silently discard it. The new test lands in `SCHEDULED` with `requires_requalification` false, and the work order's `version` advances by 1.

**Recording results** moves the test `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and is the same `TestEngineer` role's act. The test must be in `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_TEST_STATE`), must not carry `requires_requalification` (`ERR_REQUALIFICATION_REQUIRED`, HTTP 409 — a test flagged by a design revision must be superseded by a freshly scheduled test rather than recorded), and the logical clock must have reached or passed `window_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED` (HTTP 409). The payload carries the bench measurements: `input_power_kw` electrical input, `output_power_kw` mechanical output, the raw `stator_loss_w`, `rotor_loss_w`, `core_loss_w` and `friction_windage_w` loss figures, and the `ambient_temp_c` at test.

The efficiency determination starts with two derived losses. Express input power in watts by multiplying `input_power_kw` by 1000. `stator_loss_corrected_w` applies a one-sided temperature correction: multiply `stator_loss_w` by one plus 0.0035 for each degree Celsius by which `ambient_temp_c` exceeds the 25.0 °C reference, take no correction at all when the ambient is at or below the reference — the correction is never a credit — and round to one decimal place. `stray_load_loss_w` is the input power in watts multiplied by 0.005, rounded to one decimal place. `total_loss_w` is then the sum of the five components `stator_loss_corrected_w`, `rotor_loss_w`, `core_loss_w`, `friction_windage_w` and `stray_load_loss_w`, each already rounded to one decimal place before the addition.

`measured_efficiency_pct` is then determined by the variant the test's own `test_method` names, and the two variants genuinely diverge on identical bench data:

- Under **`IEC_60034_2_1_SUMMATION`**, efficiency is derived from the itemised loss budget: 100 multiplied by the input power in watts less `total_loss_w`, divided by the input power in watts, rounded to two decimal places. Every loss field, the ambient temperature and the stray-load fraction all move the answer.
- Under **`IEEE_112_B_DYNAMOMETER`**, efficiency is the direct input/output measurement: 100 multiplied by `output_power_kw` divided by `input_power_kw`, rounded to two decimal places. Every loss field, the ambient temperature and the stray-load fraction are ignored entirely.

Because the summation variant carries the temperature correction and the direct variant does not, the divergence between them grows with ambient temperature, and the same bench numbers can land the two variants on different classes and on opposite sides of a regulatory minimum. `achieved_class` is read from the same class-floor table given in §3.2, applied with no tolerance derate: the highest class among `IE2`, `IE3`, `IE4` and `IE5` whose floor for the design's pole count is at or below `measured_efficiency_pct`, and `BELOW_IE2` when even the `IE2` floor is missed.

On success the server stores the measurements, `test_engineer_user_id` and `recorded_at`, writes all five computed figures, sets the status to `COMPLETED`, recomputes the cell's `peak_reserved_kw` and increments the cell's `version` by 1, and moves the work order's `build_status` from `ASSEMBLED` to `TESTED` with the work order's `version` incremented by 1. A `SUPERSEDED` test never contributes to any nameplate. Performance tests can be filtered by `work_order_id`, by `test_cell_id`, by `status`, by `test_method` and by `requires_requalification`.

### 3.9. Nameplate Certification, Revocation and the Regulatory Standard Selector

A nameplate is the plant's legal declaration that a specific motor meets a specific published minimum, and it is the only thing standing between a tested motor and the loading dock. Issuing one is a `QualityCertifier`'s exclusive act and requires the work order's `build_status` to be `TESTED` (`ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`). The payload is deliberately thin: the `work_order_id` being certified, the `performance_test_id` whose result is being stamped, and the `jurisdiction` the certificate is issued for — `EU`, `US` or `CN`. Notably `standard_id` is never supplied by the caller; it is always resolved by the server.

Four gates apply before anything is written. The certifier's `X-User-ID` must differ from the cited test's `test_engineer_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `jurisdiction` on the request must equal the `jurisdiction` of the `CustomerOrder` that owns the work order — a motor built for a European order cannot be certified to a Chinese standard — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The cited performance test must be in `COMPLETED` status; a `SCHEDULED` test has no result to stamp and a `SUPERSEDED` one has been abandoned, and either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERFORMANCE_TEST_STATE`.

The fourth gate is the standard itself. **Selecting the governing standard** proceeds as follows. Gather every regulatory standard whose `jurisdiction` equals the nameplate's `jurisdiction`, whose `poles` equals the pinned design's `poles`, whose output band contains the design's `rated_output_kw` — with `rated_output_kw_min` and `rated_output_kw_max` both inclusive bounds — and whose `effective_from` calendar date is on or before the date portion of the logical clock's current instant. Rank the survivors by `effective_from` descending, because the most recently effective standard governs; where two rows share an effective date, the earliest-registered row wins, comparing `created_at` ascending. The row's `id` is never consulted as a tiebreaker. If the eligible set is empty, no fallback standard is invented and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_STANDARD` (HTTP 422). Because regulatory standards are immutable, the candidate pool only ever grows, and a standard registered today with a past `effective_from` immediately becomes governing for every future certification.

The selected row's `id` becomes `standard_id`, and its `minimum_efficiency_pct` is copied onto the nameplate as `minimum_efficiency_pct_applied` — frozen at issue time, so a later standard cannot retroactively invalidate the certificate. The cited test's `measured_efficiency_pct` must be greater than or equal to that minimum; exactly equal passes, and anything below is refused with `ERR_EFFICIENCY_BELOW_REGULATORY_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). The standard's `tolerance_pct` is recorded on the standard row for information only and is never subtracted from the minimum before this comparison — the plant does not spend its regulator's tolerance band.

On success the nameplate copies `stamped_efficiency_pct` from the test's `measured_efficiency_pct` and `stamped_class` from its `achieved_class`, captures `certifier_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` and `issued_at` from the logical clock — the instant that later serves as the pro-rata origin for warranty credit — and lands in `ISSUED` status with `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` both unset. The work order's `certification_status` moves to `CERTIFIED`, legitimately from either `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED`, and its `version` advances by 1. Certification is thus re-attainable: a motor whose certificate was revoked can be re-certified by issuing a fresh nameplate, which is what makes the shipping gate of §3.5 a live check rather than a one-time milestone. Nameplates may be filtered by `work_order_id`, by `performance_test_id`, by `standard_id`, by `status` and by `jurisdiction`.

**Revoking** a certificate is the same `QualityCertifier` role's act, permitted only from `ISSUED` (`ERR_INVALID_NAMEPLATE_STATE`), and takes a mandatory `revocation_reason`. The nameplate moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` set from the logical clock and its `version` incremented by 1. Revocation then propagates in three hops, breadth-first: the owning work order's `certification_status` moves `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with its `version` incremented; then every warranty claim on that work order whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ANALYSIS` has `defect_presumption_applied` set to true; then `open_presumed_defect_count` is recomputed for every distributor owning such a claim, with each affected distributor's `version` incremented by 1. A revoked nameplate is terminal — the certificate itself is never reinstated, only replaced.

### 3.10. Warranty Claims: Adjudication, the Credit Waterfall and the Dispute Cycle

A warranty claim is filed against a motor that has left the plant, and the plant's job is to decide whether the failure was its own defect or the customer's abuse. Both a `Distributor` and a `ComplianceOfficer` may file one. The payload names the `work_order_id` the failed motor came from and the claiming `distributor_id`, which must equal the `distributor_id` on the work order's customer order; when the caller's role is `Distributor`, it must also equal the caller's own `X-User-ID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. The reported particulars are the `failure_mode` — one of `BEARING_FAILURE`, `WINDING_SHORT`, `ROTOR_BAR_CRACK`, `SHAFT_FRACTURE` or `INSULATION_BREAKDOWN` — the `operating_hours` at failure between 0 and 200000 inclusive, and the reported application conditions: `measured_load_pct` as a percentage of rating, `measured_ambient_c` in degrees Celsius, `measured_voltage_unbalance_pct` as a percentage, each to one decimal place, and `measured_starts_per_hour` between 0 and 500 inclusive. The money claimed is itemised as `claimed_parts_cents`, `claimed_labor_cents` and `claimed_freight_cents`, each a non-negative whole number of cents. The claim lands in `SUBMITTED` with `reported_at` set from the logical clock — the pro-rata endpoint for credit — `dispute_round` at 0, `warranty_credit_cents` at 0, and `credit_posted` and `defect_presumption_applied` both false. Claims may be filtered by `work_order_id`, by `distributor_id`, by `status`, by `failure_mode` and by `defect_presumption_applied`; a `Distributor` sees only their own rows.

**Adjudication** is a `ComplianceOfficer`'s exclusive act, permitted from `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ANALYSIS` and refused from any other status with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The caller chooses an `analysis_basis` of `FIELD_DATA` or `TEARDOWN`, and that choice governs both the payload shape and the arithmetic. `TEARDOWN` requires `teardown_defect_evidence_pct` to be present and within 0.0 to 100.0 inclusive; `FIELD_DATA` requires it to be absent; and a claim whose `defect_presumption_applied` is true may only be analysed on the `TEARDOWN` basis, because once a certification has been revoked, field telemetry alone can no longer rebut the presumption. Every violation of those three conditions is refused with `ERR_TEARDOWN_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

The **misuse index** is built from four application sub-scores, each of which measures how far past a benign reference the installation was run and each of which is clamped into the interval from 0 to 1 — negative excursions score 0, and excursions beyond the span score 1. The overload sub-score is the reported load less a 100.0 percent reference, divided by a 40-point span. The thermal sub-score is the reported ambient less a 40.0 °C reference, divided by a 25-degree span. The voltage sub-score is the reported unbalance less a 1.0 percent reference, divided by a 3-point span. The starts sub-score is the reported starts per hour less the reference for the design's `duty_type` — 6 for `S1`, 12 for `S2`, 30 for `S3` — divided by that same reference, so that a continuous-duty motor started twice as often as its reference already scores its full penalty. Sub-scores are carried at full precision; only the final index is rounded, to four decimal places.

The two bases then combine them differently:

- On the **`FIELD_DATA`** basis the index is the weighted sum of the four application sub-scores alone, with weights 0.34 on overload, 0.26 on thermal, 0.22 on voltage and 0.18 on starts — a total of 1.00. There is no physical-evidence term, so a hard-run application has nothing to offset it and tends to be adjudicated as misuse.
- On the **`TEARDOWN`** basis the same four sub-scores are carried at reduced weights — 0.20 on overload, 0.18 on thermal, 0.14 on voltage and 0.10 on starts, a total of 0.62 — and a fifth term at weight 0.38 is added, equal to one minus `teardown_defect_evidence_pct` divided by 100. That term *rewards* physical evidence of a manufacturing defect: strong evidence drives it toward zero and pulls the index down. Identical application data can therefore adjudicate the opposite way once a teardown has been performed.

`misuse_index` is the rounded result, and `adjudication` follows from it against a misuse threshold of 0.50: an index greater than or equal to the threshold — exactly 0.5000 included — yields `MISUSE_DETERMINED`, and anything below yields `DEFECT_CONFIRMED`. The claim's `status` is set to the adjudication value, and `analysis_basis`, `teardown_defect_evidence_pct` and `misuse_index` are all stored.

**When defect is confirmed, credit is computed and posted** by a strict waterfall. First the plant pins what the motor actually cost to build: `claim_unit_standard_cost_cents` is the work order's `standard_cost_cents` divided by its `unit_count`, rounded to whole cents, and copied onto the claim so a later cost recomputation cannot move a settled claim. Next the service age is measured as the number of complete calendar months from the date of the work order's `shipped_at` to the logical clock's date at analysis time — the count of whole months m for which the shipped date advanced by m months still falls on or before the current date. A work order that has not shipped yields a service age of 0. The pro-rata factor is the pro-rata window of 60 months less the service age capped at that window, divided by 60, floored at 0. The credit ceiling is `claim_unit_standard_cost_cents` multiplied by a ceiling fraction of 0.85 and by the pro-rata factor, rounded to whole cents.

The three buckets are then filled in a fixed order — parts, then labour, then freight — each capped independently *and* by whatever remains of the ceiling. The parts grant is the least of `claimed_parts_cents`, 0.60 of `claim_unit_standard_cost_cents` rounded to whole cents, and the remaining ceiling. The labour grant is the least of `claimed_labor_cents`, a flat 45000 cents, and what is left after parts. The freight grant is the least of `claimed_freight_cents`, a flat 18000 cents, and what is left after labour. `warranty_credit_cents` is the sum of the three grants; any ceiling left over is forfeited and never redistributed backwards to a bucket that was cut by its own cap. A claim at or beyond 60 months of service therefore settles at exactly 0. On a confirmed defect `credit_posted` is set true; when misuse is determined instead, `warranty_credit_cents` is set to 0 and `credit_posted` to false, and the waterfall is not run at all.

Either outcome recomputes the claiming distributor's `warranty_credit_balance_cents` as the sum of `warranty_credit_cents` over that distributor's claims whose `credit_posted` is true, increments the distributor's `version` by 1, and increments the claim's `version` by 1. The balance is always that sum — never a running total of every adjudication ever made.

**Disputing** closes the cycle. Only the owning `Distributor` may dispute, and only from `DEFECT_CONFIRMED` or `MISUSE_DETERMINED`; the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the claim's `distributor_id`. The payload carries a mandatory `dispute_reason`. `dispute_round` must be below the limit of 2 before the increment, and a third dispute is refused with `ERR_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A dispute raises `dispute_round` by 1 and performs the compensating half of the credit invariant: `credit_posted` is cleared, `warranty_credit_cents` is set to 0, and `adjudication` and `misuse_index` are both cleared back to unset. The distributor's `warranty_credit_balance_cents` is recomputed and their `version` incremented, so the balance drops by exactly the amount the prior analysis posted. The claim returns to `UNDER_ANALYSIS`, where it can be analysed again — possibly on the other basis — and the freshly computed amount reposted. No drift accumulates across any analyse/dispute/analyse sequence.

**Closing** is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s final act, permitted from `DEFECT_CONFIRMED` or `MISUSE_DETERMINED` and taking an optional `closure_notes`. `credit_posted` and `warranty_credit_cents` are frozen at whatever values they currently hold, the claim moves to `CLOSED`, the distributor's `open_presumed_defect_count` is recomputed — a closed claim no longer counts as an open presumed defect — with the distributor's `version` incremented, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal: no further analysis or dispute is possible.
