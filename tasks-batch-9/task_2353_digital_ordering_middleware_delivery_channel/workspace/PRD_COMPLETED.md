# Digital Ordering Middleware API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A restaurant brand that sells through a third-party marketplace, its own mobile app and an aggregator storefront does not run three menus, three price books and three kitchens — it runs one of each, and the difference between the channels is configuration. This document specifies the middleware that makes that true: a single-source menu is authored once per brand, published to a store, and materialized into per-channel listings whose prices, fees, suppression rules and availability semantics are derived from each channel's own configuration. Orders arrive from the channels, are admitted against the store's real kitchen capacity and operating hours, are injected into the point-of-sale (with a tablet fallback when the register refuses them), are handed to a courier or marketplace delivery network under an explicit handoff SLA, and eventually settle back to the originating channel as a remittance statement with refunds and fee credits routed to the same partner that collected the money.

The pain the product removes is the drift that appears when each of those concerns is handled independently. A cook takes the last portion of an item out of the walk-in; if that 86 does not reach every honoring channel within the same request, guests keep ordering an item that no longer exists and the store absorbs the refunds. A channel raises its commission mid-month; if listings silently reprice, the money already collected no longer reconciles. Two settlement analysts touch the same statement; without an explicit ordering rule and a segregation-of-duties gate, the brand cannot prove who approved what. Every rule in this document exists to close one of those gaps, and each one is expressed as a deterministic, observable outcome rather than a best effort.

Two structural decisions shape the whole specification. First, all money is integer cents and all rates are basis points, so that no monetary value ever passes through a binary floating-point representation. Second, every time-dependent behaviour — store hours, capacity buckets, quote times, handoff deadlines, courier occupancy, remittance periods — reads a **logical clock** that the platform controls explicitly, never the host machine's wall clock. Both decisions make the system's behaviour reproducible, which is the precondition for reconciling money with a channel partner at all.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint: identity, envelope shape, pagination, the logical clock, common resource properties, monetary precision, optimistic concurrency, the order in which requests are rejected, reference resolution, cascade semantics, and segregation of duties.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries an identity. Two headers are always required: `X-User-ID`, which names the acting human or system operator and is the value the platform records for segregation-of-duties checks, and `X-User-Role`, which names the operational capacity the caller is acting in. The role header may equivalently be sent as `X-Role`; the two spellings carry the same value and the server treats them interchangeably. A request that omits `X-User-ID`, omits the role header, or supplies a role string that is not one of the five recognized roles is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before any other processing occurs; a header that is present but empty or whitespace-only counts as omitted. A request whose role is recognized but is not permitted to reach the addressed endpoint is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The five roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `StoreOperator`, `ChannelPartner`, `CourierNetworkAgent`, and `SettlementAnalyst`.

Three of those roles act on behalf of a specific persisted entity, and each carries a dedicated scope header:

- When the role is `StoreOperator`, the caller sends `X-Store-ID`, whose value is the `id` of the acting `Store`. Ownership checks compare the addressed resource's store reference — the store in the path, or an order's `store_id` — against that value. A store operator that reaches for another store, or for an order, listing or delivery assignment belonging to another store, is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`.
- When the role is `ChannelPartner`, the caller sends `X-Channel-ID`, whose value is the `id` of the acting `Channel`. Ownership checks compare the addressed resource's channel reference — a channel in the path, a listing's `channel_id`, an order's `channel_id`, an adjustment's `channel_id`, a statement's `channel_id` — against that value, and a mismatch is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`.
- When the role is `CourierNetworkAgent`, the caller sends `X-Courier-Network-ID`, whose value is the `id` of the acting `CourierNetwork`. The agent may only act on delivery assignments whose `courier_network_id` equals that value; anything else is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`.

If an entity-backed role omits its scope header, or sends it blank, the server falls back to interpreting `X-User-ID` as the acting entity's `id` and applies exactly the same ownership comparisons. Ownership is only ever compared where a request addresses a specific resource. A top-level collection endpoint — `GET /stores`, `GET /channel-listings`, `GET /availability-events`, `GET /orders`, `GET /delivery-assignments`, `GET /adjustments`, `GET /remittance-statements` — applies no implicit scope filter and never returns `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`: it returns every record matching the supplied query filters whatever the caller's scope. A collection nested under a path resource — `GET /stores/{id}/hour-windows`, `GET /orders/{id}/lines`, `GET /orders/{id}/injection-attempts` — does apply the ownership comparison, to that parent.

The remaining two roles are not entity-backed. When the role is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`; the platform administrator owns the brand catalogue, the channel registry, the courier registry and the logical clock, and cannot place, inject, dispatch or settle orders. When the role is `SettlementAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, but the server does record it: it is stored as `proposed_by_user_id` on an `Adjustment` the analyst creates and as `reconciled_by_user_id` on a `RemittanceStatement` the analyst reconciles, and those two recorded values are later compared against the acting `X-User-ID` to enforce segregation of duties (see §2.11). Neither recorded value is ever compared to an entity `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

Every collection endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-10 integer. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is legal and simply returns an empty page.

Unless a subsection below states otherwise, list endpoints return their records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest record appears first; because the logical clock stands still within a request, records sharing a `created_at` are ordered by insertion order, the earlier-created record first. The availability event log follows the same rule, which is what makes "newest last" the natural reading of that collection.

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current page.

Filters, where an endpoint offers them, combine conjunctively: a request that supplies both `store_id` and `availability_state` returns only the listings matching both. Filter values are validated the same way as body fields, so an unrecognized `throttle_state`, `availability_state`, `event_type`, `settlement_status`, `injection_status`, `fulfillment_status`, `adjustment_type` or `state` value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than silently returning an empty page. The filters offered are, per collection: `brand_code`, `active_menu_version_id` and `throttle_state` on `GET /stores`; `day_of_week` on `GET /stores/{id}/hour-windows`; `sku` on `GET /menu-versions/{id}/items`; `store_id`, `channel_id`, `menu_item_id`, `sku` and `availability_state` on `GET /channel-listings`; `store_id`, `menu_item_id`, `actor_channel_id` and `event_type` on `GET /availability-events`; `store_id`, `channel_id`, `menu_version_id`, `remittance_statement_id`, `settlement_status`, `injection_status` and `fulfillment_status` on `GET /orders`; `order_id`, `courier_network_id` and `state` on `GET /delivery-assignments`; `order_id`, `channel_id`, `remittance_statement_id`, `state` and `adjustment_type` on `GET /adjustments`; and `channel_id` and `state` on `GET /remittance-statements`. `GET /orders/{id}/lines` and `GET /orders/{id}/injection-attempts` offer no filter beyond their path parent, and any query parameter an endpoint does not recognize is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock, with the single exception of the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`, which is transport metadata rather than a domain value. The platform maintains a single **logical clock**, and every rule that depends on time — whether a store is open, which 15-minute capacity bucket an order lands in, what ready time may be promised, when a courier's handoff deadline expires, how long an assignment occupies network capacity, and which orders fall inside a remittance period — reads that clock. Its value at server start is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A `PlatformAdmin` may set the clock at any time by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response echoes the new `current_utc`. Setting the clock may move it forward or backward. It is deliberately inert: no entity is recomputed, no state machine advances, and no derived field is refreshed as a side effect of moving the clock. Time-dependent consequences surface only when a subsequent request is evaluated against the new instant — for example, an assignment whose occupancy interval has expired stops counting towards its network's occupancy the next time a dispatch or acceptance is evaluated.

All timestamps that the API emits — `created_at`, `updated_at`, and every domain instant such as `published_at`, `superseded_at`, `last_published_at`, `effective_at`, `attempted_at`, `promised_ready_at`, `offered_at`, `accepted_at`, `handoff_deadline_at`, `occupancy_until`, `handoff_at`, `delivered_at`, `accepted_at` and `reversed_at` on an adjustment, and `settled_at` on a statement — are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and each is taken from the logical clock at the moment the write occurred.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Version Semantics

Ten resource types are first-class, independently addressable records: `Store`, `Channel`, `MenuVersion`, `MenuItem`, `ChannelListing`, `Order`, `CourierNetwork`, `DeliveryAssignment`, `Adjustment`, and `RemittanceStatement`. Each of them carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.
- `created_at` (string): the logical-clock instant at which the record was created, as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- `updated_at` (string): the logical-clock instant of the most recent modification, in the same format. It is refreshed on every mutation, including mutations that only touch derived fields — when an accepted refund moves an order's `refunded_total_cents`, that order's `updated_at` moves too.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, again including mutations that only touch derived fields. A single request moves a given record's `version` by at most 1 however many of that record's fields it changes, and stamps one and the same `updated_at` on all of them, so a cascade that touches a store's counters twice still leaves `version` one higher than it found it.

Four record types are append-only journal entries rather than mutable resources: `StoreHourWindow`, `AvailabilityEvent`, `OrderLine`, and `PosInjectionAttempt`. Each of these carries only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all — those two keys are absent from their representations, not present-and-null.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field holding one ends in `_cents`. There are no fractional currency values anywhere in a request body, a stored record, or a response.

Every rate is expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — and every basis-point field ends in `_bps`. Converting a basis-point rate into money always divides by a denominator of 10,000: a `commission_bps` of 2500 is a commission of twenty-five percent, and a `tax_bps` of 875 is a tax of eight and three-quarters percent.

Whenever a basis-point multiplication produces a fractional number of cents, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent: a computed 286.5 cents becomes 287, and a computed 286.4 cents becomes 286. The multiplication itself is performed on the exact rational value, so an implementation must not truncate to an intermediate representation before rounding. Two rules constrain *where* rounding happens, and both matter for reconciliation: order tax is rounded once per order line and the rounded line taxes are then summed, never recomputed from the order subtotal; and statement commission and statement rebate are each rounded independently from the same adjusted volume figure.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints overwrite state that another writer may legitimately have replaced, and each of them therefore requires the client to declare which version of the parent record it last observed. In all three cases the version field is a required input, not an optional hint, and omitting it is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

- `POST /stores/{id}/publish-menu` requires `expected_store_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Store`. If it does not equal the store's current `version`, the publish is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A publish supersedes the store's previous menu and rebuilds every one of its channel listings, so a writer holding a stale view of the store would silently discard listing state — suppression flags and 86 flags — that another writer has already replaced.
- `POST /adjustments` requires `expected_order_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Order` named by `order_id`. If it does not equal the order's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The refund ceiling is derived from the order's `refunded_total_cents` and `channel_collected_cents`, both of which move whenever any adjustment on that order is accepted or reversed, so a stale view of the order would let a refund be proposed against money that is no longer available.
- `PATCH /channels/{id}` requires `expected_channel_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Channel`. If it does not equal the channel's current `version`, the update is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CHANNEL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A configuration write overwrites the previous fee, markup, 86 and settlement configuration wholesale, so a writer holding a stale view would blindly revert values another writer has already changed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. In particular, marking an order ready, injecting it, cancelling it, dispatching it, accepting or declining a delivery offer, submitting or accepting an adjustment, and every remittance-statement transition take no expected-version input and perform no concurrency check.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

A single request can violate several rules at once, and the response must be predictable. The platform evaluates checks in the fixed order below and returns the **first** violation it finds; later checks are never reached. The ranking is normative and is the reason, for example, that a request against a store with no published menu reports the menu error rather than the closed-store error even when both are true.

1. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) — the role header is absent or names an unrecognized role, or `X-User-ID` is absent. Applies to every endpoint.
2. `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) — the role is recognized but is not listed for the addressed endpoint. Applies to every endpoint, independently of which resource is addressed.
3. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) — body and query shape: a required input is missing, a value has the wrong type, an enum value is not recognized, an integer falls outside its declared range, the `lines` array is empty, `day_of_week` is outside 0 through 6, `closes_at_minute` is not strictly greater than `opens_at_minute`, or pagination falls outside `limit` 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0. Applies to every endpoint.
4. The 404 family — every entity referenced by a path segment or by a body foreign key must resolve to an existing row. The concrete identifiers are `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AVAILABILITY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURIER_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint.
5. `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE` (403) — an entity-backed role's scope header does not identify the resource being addressed. Applies to every endpoint reachable by `StoreOperator`, `ChannelPartner` or `CourierNetworkAgent`. Because it ranks below existence, a wrong-scope request aimed at an identifier that does not exist still returns 404.
6. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — the acting `X-User-ID` equals the user recorded for the preceding step. Applies to `POST /adjustments/{id}/reverse` and `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/settle`.
7. The 412 concurrency family — `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_CHANNEL_VERSION` as described in §2.7. Applies to `POST /stores/{id}/publish-menu`, `POST /adjustments` and `PATCH /channels/{id}`. It is checked before any state or business rule, so a stale writer never observes a downstream error.
8. The 409 uniqueness family — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_NETWORK_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VERSION_LABEL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF`. Applies to `POST /channels`, `POST /courier-networks`, `POST /menu-versions`, `POST /menu-versions/{id}/items` and `POST /orders`.
9. `ERR_MENU_VERSION_LOCKED` (409) — the menu version is not `DRAFT`. Applies to `POST /menu-versions/{id}/items` and `POST /menu-versions/{id}/finalize`.
10. `ERR_MENU_VERSION_EMPTY` (409) — the version holds no items at finalize. Applies to `POST /menu-versions/{id}/finalize`.
11. `ERR_MENU_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409) — the version being pushed to a store is not `PUBLISHED`. Applies to `POST /stores/{id}/publish-menu`.
12. `ERR_BRAND_MISMATCH` (422) — the version's `brand_code` differs from the store's `brand_code`. Applies to `POST /stores/{id}/publish-menu`.
13. `ERR_STORE_HOURS_MISSING` (409) — the store has no operating-hours window. Applies to `POST /stores/{id}/publish-menu`.
14. `ERR_HOUR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) — a new window overlaps an existing window on the same local day. Applies to `POST /stores/{id}/hour-windows`.
15. `ERR_STORE_MENU_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409) — the store has no active menu version. Applies to `POST /orders`, `GET /stores/{id}/quote` and `POST /availability-events`.
16. `ERR_STORE_CLOSED` (422) — the logical clock falls outside every operating-hours window for the store's local day. Applies to `POST /orders` and `GET /stores/{id}/quote`.
17. `ERR_STORE_PAUSED` (409) — the store's `throttle_state` is `PAUSED`. Applies to `POST /orders` and `POST /orders/{id}/resume-injection`.
18. `ERR_CHANNEL_DELIVERY_UNSUPPORTED` (422) — a `DELIVERY` order on a channel whose `accepts_delivery` is false. Applies to `POST /orders`.
19. `ERR_SKU_NOT_LISTED` (422) — a line's `sku` has no listing for the order's store and channel. Applies to `POST /orders`.
20. `ERR_ITEM_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409) — a resolved listing's `availability_state` is not `AVAILABLE`. Applies to `POST /orders`.
21. `ERR_ITEM_NOT_DELIVERY_ELIGIBLE` (422) — a `DELIVERY` order contains an item whose `is_delivery_eligible` is false. Applies to `POST /orders`.
22. `ERR_CAPACITY_BUCKET_FULL` (409) — the target capacity bucket is full. Applies to `POST /orders` and `POST /orders/{id}/resume-injection`.
23. `ERR_QUOTE_WINDOW_EXCEEDED` (422) — the computed quote exceeds the maximum promise window. Applies to `POST /orders` and `GET /stores/{id}/quote`.
24. `ERR_INVALID_INJECTION_STATE` (409) — the order's `injection_status` does not permit the requested injection operation. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/inject` and `POST /orders/{id}/resume-injection`.
25. `ERR_ITEM_STILL_UNAVAILABLE` (409) — a held order still has a line whose listing is not `AVAILABLE`. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/resume-injection`.
26. `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` (409) — the order's `fulfillment_status` does not permit the requested operation, or, on cancel only, its `settlement_status` is no longer `SETTLEABLE`. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/ready`, `POST /orders/{id}/cancel` and `POST /orders/{id}/dispatch`.
27. `ERR_ORDER_NOT_INJECTED` (409) — the order's `injection_status` is neither `ACCEPTED` nor `TABLET_FALLBACK`. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/ready`, `POST /orders/{id}/dispatch` and `POST /delivery-assignments/{id}/accept`.
28. `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DELIVERY` (422) — dispatch of a `PICKUP` order. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/dispatch`.
29. `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_OPEN` (409) — the order already has an assignment in `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/dispatch`.
30. `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COURIER_NETWORK` (409) — the candidate network set is empty. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/dispatch`.
31. `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE` (409) — the assignment's `state` does not permit the requested courier operation, or, on acceptance only, the winning network's `is_active` has since been read as false. Applies to `POST /delivery-assignments/{id}/accept`, `POST /delivery-assignments/{id}/decline`, `POST /delivery-assignments/{id}/handoff` and `POST /delivery-assignments/{id}/deliver`.
32. `ERR_NETWORK_AT_CAPACITY` (409) — the network's occupancy no longer admits the assignment at acceptance time. Applies to `POST /delivery-assignments/{id}/accept`.
33. `ERR_ORDER_NOT_READY` (409) — handoff attempted while the order's `fulfillment_status` is not `READY`. Applies to `POST /delivery-assignments/{id}/handoff`.
34. `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_SETTLED` (409) — the order's `settlement_status` is `SETTLED`. Applies to `POST /adjustments`, `POST /adjustments/{id}/accept` and `POST /adjustments/{id}/reverse`. It ranks **above** the refund-ceiling check.
35. `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_COLLECTED` (422) — a refund would push the order's accepted refunds past what the channel collected. Applies to `POST /adjustments` and `POST /adjustments/{id}/revise`. It ranks **below** the already-settled check.
36. `ERR_INVALID_ADJUSTMENT_STATE` (409) — the adjustment's `state` does not permit the requested operation. Applies to `POST /adjustments/{id}/submit`, `POST /adjustments/{id}/request-revision`, `POST /adjustments/{id}/revise`, `POST /adjustments/{id}/accept` and `POST /adjustments/{id}/reverse`.
37. `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) — the adjustment has already used its revision allowance. Applies to `POST /adjustments/{id}/request-revision`.
38. `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD` (422) — the statement period is inverted or ends in the future. Applies to `POST /remittance-statements`.
39. `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` (409) — the statement's `state` does not permit the requested operation. Applies to `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/assign-orders`, `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/reconcile`, `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/dispute` and `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/settle`.
40. `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422) — a listed order's channel, period membership or `fulfillment_status` disqualifies it. Applies to `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/assign-orders`.
41. `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (409) — a listed order's `settlement_status` is not `SETTLEABLE`. Applies to `POST /remittance-statements/{id}/assign-orders`. 44. `ERR_ORDER_HAS_ACCEPTED_ADJUSTMENTS` (409) — cancel while the order carries accepted refunds. Applies to `POST /orders/{id}/cancel`.

### 2.9. Reference Resolution and Nested Paths

When a path carries more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve determines the response. A request for the items of a menu version that does not exist reports `ERR_MENU_VERSION_NOT_FOUND` without ever looking for an item; a request for the injection attempts of an order that does not exist reports `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.

Body foreign keys are resolved the same way, and with one deliberate consequence: a foreign key that names a row which exists but belongs to a different parent is treated as **not found**, never as a permission failure. If a channel partner submits an availability event naming a `menu_item_id` that belongs to a menu version the store has never published, the answer is `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, not `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`. The same applies to `store_id`, `channel_id`, `actor_channel_id`, `order_id`, `menu_version_id` and every entry of an `order_ids` array.

### 2.10. Cascades, Derived Fields and Reconciliation

Many operations in this system mutate more than one record. A single request may move a state machine on the addressed resource, adjust counters on its store, re-derive availability on dozens of listings, withdraw a courier's open offer, and recompute a settlement statement's money figures. Every one of those secondary writes is part of the same request: the system is immediately consistent, and a client that reads any touched record after a 2xx response sees the fully reconciled value. There is no background job, no eventual convergence, and no partial application — if a request is rejected, nothing it would have written is written.

Derived counters are maintained, not recomputed on read. `Store.open_order_count` always equals the number of that store's orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `PREPARING` or `READY` — orders that have reached `HANDED_OFF`, `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` no longer count. `Store.reserved_prep_slots` always equals the number of that store's orders holding a kitchen slot, that is, those whose `injection_status` is `PENDING`, `ACCEPTED` or `TABLET_FALLBACK` and which have not yet been released by readiness, cancellation or an injection hold. `Store.tablet_backlog_count` always equals the number of that store's orders whose `injection_status` is `TABLET_FALLBACK`. `Channel.listing_count` always equals the number of listings currently published to that channel. `MenuVersion.item_count` and `MenuVersion.published_store_count` follow the same pattern, as do `Order.injection_attempt_count`, `CourierNetwork.open_assignment_count`, `CourierNetwork.penalty_accrued_cents`, and every aggregate on a `RemittanceStatement`.

Because a derived-field write is a mutation like any other, it refreshes the owning record's `updated_at` and increments its `version` — but only when the recomputation actually moves a value: a cascade that re-derives a counter, a listing's `availability_state` or a store's `throttle_state` and finds it already correct writes nothing and leaves `updated_at` and `version` untouched. That is why a client that wants to publish a menu, or to raise a refund, must re-read the parent record first: a cascade triggered by somebody else's request will have moved the version it needs to declare.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties on the Money Chain

Two steps in the money chain must be performed by two different people, and the platform enforces that with recorded identities rather than with roles.

When a `SettlementAnalyst` proposes an adjustment, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored on it as `proposed_by_user_id`. Reversing that adjustment is also a `SettlementAnalyst` action, but the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `proposed_by_user_id`; if they are equal, the reversal is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Symmetrically, reconciling a statement stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `reconciled_by_user_id`, and settling that statement requires an acting `X-User-ID` different from the stored `reconciled_by_user_id`, on pain of the same error. When a disputed statement is reconciled again, `reconciled_by_user_id` is overwritten with the analyst who performed the newer reconciliation, so it is always the most recent reconciler who is barred from settling.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Authoring the Single-Source Menu

Everything a guest can buy originates in a **menu version** — a brand-scoped, immutable-once-frozen catalogue that the platform administrator authors centrally and that stores later adopt. Creating one takes a `brand_code`, naming the brand whose stores may publish it, and a `version_label` such as a quarter identifier. Labels are unique within a brand, so a second version created under the same `brand_code` with a label that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VERSION_LABEL`; the same label under a different brand is perfectly legal. A fresh version begins in `DRAFT` with `item_count` at 0, `published_store_count` at 0, and both `published_at` and `superseded_at` null.

Items are added to a draft one at a time. Each item carries a `sku`, a display `name`, a `base_price_cents` value that must be strictly greater than zero, and a `tax_bps` rate somewhere between 0 and 2000 basis points inclusive. An optional `is_delivery_eligible` flag marks whether the item may leave the building; when it is omitted the item is delivery-eligible. Each item records the `menu_version_id` of the version that owns it, and its `sku` must be unique inside that version — a repeat within the same version is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, while the same `sku` in a different version is fine because a version is the unit of pricing truth. Every accepted item increments the version's `item_count`.

Freezing a draft moves it from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED` and stamps `published_at` with the logical clock. A version with no items cannot be frozen: an attempt while `item_count` is zero is refused with `ERR_MENU_VERSION_EMPTY`, and at least one item is therefore a precondition of the transition. Once a version is `PUBLISHED`, its items are immutable — any further attempt to add an item is refused with `ERR_MENU_VERSION_LOCKED`, and so is any attempt to freeze a version that is not currently `DRAFT`.

The remaining two version states are reached indirectly and are described where they are triggered: `PUBLISHED` and `EXHAUSTED` versions bubble back and forth as items are taken out of and returned to stock (§3.4), and a version drops to `SUPERSEDED` — stamping `superseded_at` from the clock — at the moment the last store that was running it publishes a different version, that is, exactly when its `published_store_count` reaches 0 as a result of that publish. Both `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and `EXHAUSTED → SUPERSEDED` occur under that same condition. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: a superseded version can never be pushed to a store again, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_MENU_VERSION_NOT_PUBLISHED` — as is an attempt to push a version still in `DRAFT`.

### 3.2. Bringing a Store Online

A store is registered by the platform administrator with the `brand_code` it trades under, a human-readable `name`, and five operational numbers: `timezone_offset_minutes`, the store's fixed offset from UTC in minutes, anywhere from -720 through 840 inclusive, from which the local minute-of-day and the local day of week are derived; `base_prep_minutes`, the unloaded kitchen preparation time, from 1 through 60 inclusive; `prep_capacity_per_bucket`, the number of open orders a single 15-minute capacity bucket may admit, which must be at least 1; `pos_retry_limit`, the number of point-of-sale injection attempts allowed before tablet fallback is considered, from 1 through 5 inclusive; and `tablet_backlog_threshold`, the number of tablet-fallback orders the store tolerates before further register failures become terminal, which must be at least 1. A new store starts with `throttle_state` at `PAUSED`, `active_menu_version_id` null, `last_published_at` null, and `open_order_count`, `reserved_prep_slots` and `tablet_backlog_count` all at 0 — it is registered but not yet sellable.

Four of those numbers stay mutable: `base_prep_minutes`, `prep_capacity_per_bucket`, `pos_retry_limit` and `tablet_backlog_threshold` may each be revised later, and any such revision increments the store's `version` by 1. All four are optional on the update, which no field is required for, so a request that carries none of them is still accepted and still increments `version` by 1. Revising capacity deliberately does **not** re-derive `throttle_state`; the posture only changes when the store's throttle is explicitly recomputed (§3.5), which keeps a configuration edit from silently pausing a store mid-service.

Operating hours are declared one local day at a time. A window names its `day_of_week` — 0 for Monday through 6 for Sunday — an `opens_at_minute` from 0 through 1439 which is included in the window, and a `closes_at_minute` from 1 through 1440 which is excluded from it and which must be strictly greater than `opens_at_minute`; a window that fails either range or the ordering rule is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The stored window also exposes `window_minutes`, the number of minutes it spans, obtained by subtracting `opens_at_minute` from `closes_at_minute`. Windows on the same local day may not overlap, and because each window is half-open the overlap test is precise: a proposed window collides with an existing window on that day when the new window's `opens_at_minute` is strictly less than the existing window's `closes_at_minute` **and** the existing window's `opens_at_minute` is strictly less than the new window's `closes_at_minute`. Exact adjacency — a new window opening at the very minute an existing window closes — is not a collision and is accepted. A collision is refused with `ERR_HOUR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Windows on different days never collide with each other. Each window records the `store_id` of the store it belongs to.

Publishing turns a registered store into a selling store. A store operator names the `menu_version_id` to adopt, together with the `expected_store_version` described in §2.7. Four preconditions must hold: the named version's `state` is `PUBLISHED` or `EXHAUSTED`, its `brand_code` equals the store's `brand_code` (otherwise `ERR_BRAND_MISMATCH`), the declared store version matches, and the store has at least one operating-hours window (otherwise `ERR_STORE_HOURS_MISSING`, because a store with no hours has no defined ordering window and no defined quote). A successful publish does all of the following in one request: it sets the store's `active_menu_version_id` to the adopted version; it stamps `last_published_at` with the logical clock; it materializes one **channel listing** for every combination of an item in that version and a registered channel, snapshotting the item's `sku` into the listing's `sku` and the item's `base_price_cents` into the listing's `base_price_cents`, computing the listing's `price_cents` from the channel pricing rule below, and initialising both `suppressed` and `eighty_sixed` to false and then deriving `availability_state` through the precedence ladder of §3.3 — which yields `AVAILABLE` in the ordinary case, but yields `THROTTLE_SUPPRESSED` on a `SUPPRESS_ON_THROTTLE` channel when the publishing store is already `THROTTLED`; it deletes every listing the store currently holds, whichever version built it, and recomputes each affected channel's `listing_count`; it increments the adopted version's `published_store_count`; it decrements the previously active version's `published_store_count`, moving that version to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_at` stamped from the clock if the count reaches 0 — both counter movements being skipped entirely when the store republishes the version it is already running, so a same-version republish never supersedes anything; and it increments the store's own `version`. Each listing records the `store_id`, `channel_id` and `menu_item_id` it was built from, and the listings are built item by item in the adopted version's item order, each item's row spanning the channels in channel-registration order, which is therefore the order `GET /channel-listings` reports them in.

Any successful publish by a store whose posture is `PAUSED` lifts it to `NORMAL` — this is the only way a store leaves its initial paused posture, and also the way it recovers if a later throttle recompute paused it again. Republishing is legal and common: a store already in `NORMAL` that publishes again stays in `NORMAL`, a store in `THROTTLED` stays `THROTTLED`, and every side effect above applies again. One consequence of a republish deserves emphasis, because it is the intended way to clear a bad availability picture: 86 flags are **not** carried across a version change. Every rebuilt listing has `eighty_sixed` reset to false, and the availability event log is replayed only from `last_published_at` forward, so events recorded before the newest publish no longer influence any listing.

**Channel pricing.** A listing's `price_cents` is derived from exactly three inputs — the item's `base_price_cents`, the channel's `markup_bps`, and the channel's `rounding_mode` — in a fixed two-step ladder. First, the base price is multiplied by the markup: take the sum of 10,000 and `markup_bps`, multiply the `base_price_cents` by that sum, and divide by 10,000, keeping the exact rational result rather than an intermediate rounded one. That exact value is then rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Second, the rounded cent figure is lifted onto the channel's price grid: on a `CENT` channel it is already the final `price_cents`; on a `NICKEL` channel the final `price_cents` is the smallest multiple of 5 cents that is greater than or equal to the rounded cent figure, so a figure already on a 5-cent boundary is left alone and any other figure is raised to the next boundary. The order of the two steps is normative — the nickel grid is applied **after** the half-up rounding, never before. For a base price of 249 cents on a `NICKEL` channel with a markup of 1500 basis points, the exact marked-up value is 286.35 cents, which rounds half up to 286 cents, which is then lifted to a final `price_cents` of 290.

### 3.3. Channel Configuration and Per-Channel Suppression

A **channel** is the configuration object that makes one storefront differ from another. It is registered with a globally unique `code` — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_CODE` — a human-readable `name`, and a `channel_type` of `MARKETPLACE` for a third-party storefront, `DIRECT` for a brand-owned app or website, or `AGGREGATOR`. Its commercial terms are a `markup_bps` from 0 through 5000 inclusive, a `rounding_mode` of either `CENT` or `NICKEL`, a flat per-order `service_fee_cents` of at least 0, and a `commission_bps` from 0 through 5000 inclusive that the channel charges on the sum of the order subtotal and the service fee.

Four policy switches decide how the channel behaves when the operational world changes underneath it, and each one is worth reading as a business decision rather than a flag. `settlement_mode` is either `GROSS_MODE`, where the channel remits the full adjusted volume and the brand is invoiced for commission separately, or `NET_MODE`, where the channel withholds its commission before remitting. `eighty_six_policy` is either `HONOR_86`, where out-of-stock reports propagate to this channel's listings, or `IGNORE_86`, where the listings stay available regardless. `throttle_policy` is either `SUPPRESS_ON_THROTTLE`, where this channel's listings are hidden while the store is throttled or paused, or `IGNORE_THROTTLE`, where they are not. `adjustment_review_mode` is either `CHANNEL_REVIEW`, where a submitted refund waits for the channel partner to rule on it, or `AUTO_ACCEPT`, where submission accepts it immediately. A final optional `accepts_delivery` flag decides whether delivery orders may be placed on the channel at all; when omitted it is true. A newly registered channel starts with `listing_count` at 0.

Commercial terms change, and both the platform administrator and the channel partner itself may revise them: `markup_bps`, `rounding_mode`, `service_fee_cents`, `commission_bps`, `settlement_mode`, `eighty_six_policy`, `throttle_policy`, `adjustment_review_mode` and `accepts_delivery` are all individually revisable, and every accepted revision increments the channel's `version` by 1 under the stale-version rule of §2.7. Each of them is optional on the request — `expected_channel_version` is the only required input — so a request carrying nothing but that declared version still succeeds and still increments `version` by 1. Revising a channel does **not** reprice anything that already exists. Listings keep the `price_cents` they were built with; a new markup or rounding mode reaches the shelf only at the next publish of a menu to a store. Orders already placed keep the `service_fee_cents` and commission they were computed with, for the same reason.

Separately from platform-wide availability, a channel partner may hide a single item on its own channel and nowhere else by setting a listing's `suppressed` flag. Suppression is a channel-partner prerogative and applies strictly to the one listing addressed; the same item on a sibling channel is untouched. The `suppressed` flag is a required boolean on that request, and each such write increments the listing's `version` and immediately re-derives its `availability_state` — including a write that merely restates the flag the listing already carried.

**Availability precedence.** A listing's `availability_state` is never set directly. It is always derived, and the four candidate values are resolved by a first-match-wins ladder: if the listing's `suppressed` flag is true the state is `SUPPRESSED`; otherwise, if its `eighty_sixed` flag is true the state is `EIGHTY_SIXED`; otherwise, if the owning channel's `throttle_policy` is `SUPPRESS_ON_THROTTLE` and the store's `throttle_state` is either `THROTTLED` or `PAUSED` the state is `THROTTLE_SUPPRESSED`; otherwise the state is `AVAILABLE`. Suppression therefore outranks an 86, and an 86 outranks throttle suppression — a partner-suppressed item that is also out of stock still reads `SUPPRESSED`, which is what lets the partner keep the item hidden after the kitchen restores it. The ladder is re-evaluated whenever an availability event is recorded, whenever a listing's suppression changes, whenever a store's throttle posture is recomputed, and whenever a menu is published to a store.

### 3.4. Eighty-Sixing and Restoring Items Across Channels

When a cook runs out of an item, the store operator records an **availability event**, and so may a channel partner that notices the shortage first. The event names the `store_id`, the `menu_item_id` that ran out, and an `event_type` of either `EIGHTY_SIX` for out-of-stock or `RESTORE` for back-in-stock. An optional `actor_channel_id` records which channel reported it; when the store operator reports it, that field is null. The event stamps `effective_at` from the logical clock. A store with no `active_menu_version_id` cannot 86 anything and is refused with `ERR_STORE_MENU_NOT_PUBLISHED`. The `menu_item_id` must belong to that store's *active* version: an item of any other version is reported as `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, and that cross-parent check is evaluated after the unpublished-store gate, so a store with no active version reports `ERR_STORE_MENU_NOT_PUBLISHED` whichever item was named. `actor_channel_id`, when supplied, must resolve to a registered channel (`ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`) and must be the acting channel partner's own; it is recorded exactly as supplied and is never inferred from the caller, so a channel partner that omits it records null just as a store operator does. An event is scoped by its `store_id` alone — its `actor_channel_id` is not a scope reference — so any channel partner may read any event.

Propagation is the point of the operation. Every listing for that store-and-item pair is examined. Listings on channels whose `eighty_six_policy` is `HONOR_86` adopt the new flag: their `eighty_sixed` becomes true for an `EIGHTY_SIX` and false for a `RESTORE`, and each such listing re-derives `availability_state` through the precedence ladder of §3.3. The event reports how many listings actually changed their flag as `propagated_listing_count`. Listings on channels whose `eighty_six_policy` is `IGNORE_86` are left completely untouched — no flag change, no availability re-derivation — and the event reports how many it skipped as `skipped_listing_count`. A `HONOR_86` listing that already carries the flag the event would set falls into neither figure: it is counted as neither propagated nor skipped. Both counters are recorded on the event and are the audit record of what the near-real-time fan-out actually did.

The flag itself is defined as a replay rather than as a toggle, which matters when events arrive out of the order a human would expect. A listing's `eighty_sixed` is true exactly when the most recent honored event for its store-and-item pair — walking the event log for that pair in ascending `created_at` order, with ties broken by insertion order, and skipping the pair entirely on `IGNORE_86` channels — was an `EIGHTY_SIX`. A `RESTORE` therefore clears the flag, a second `EIGHTY_SIX` after an `EIGHTY_SIX` leaves it true, and the replay window begins at the store's `last_published_at`, so a publish wipes the slate as described in §3.2.

An 86 also reaches into live orders, and this cascade is the serialization point between the kitchen and the channels. Every order at that store whose `injection_status` is `PENDING`, whose channel's `eighty_six_policy` is `HONOR_86`, and which has a line whose `sku` matches the 86-ed item moves from `PENDING` to `INJECTION_HELD`. Each held order releases one of the store's reserved prep slots, decrementing `reserved_prep_slots`, and any delivery assignment for that order still sitting in `OFFERED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN` with its `occupancy_until` pulled back to the current clock so the courier network's capacity is freed immediately. The event records how many orders it held as `held_order_count`. Orders that the register has already accepted, orders already in tablet fallback, and orders on `IGNORE_86` channels are never held — the hold exists to stop a ticket before it reaches the kitchen, not to claw one back.

Menu versions bubble in response to the same propagation. If, after propagation completes, every listing referencing any item of the store's active version has `eighty_sixed` true, and that version's `published_store_count` is at least 1, the version itself moves from `PUBLISHED` to `EXHAUSTED` — a bubble-up that changes no listing by itself and simply reports that the whole catalogue is dark. The listings inspected are every listing built from any item of that version, at every store running it and not only at the store that reported the event; a version with no items, or whose items carry no listings at all, never bubbles in either direction. Symmetrically, if after propagation at least one listing of an `EXHAUSTED` version has `eighty_sixed` false, the version returns from `EXHAUSTED` to `PUBLISHED`, with no further side effects. An `EXHAUSTED` version is still a publishable version: it is only `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` versions that a store may not adopt.

Releasing a held order back into the queue is a separate store-operator action that moves `injection_status` from `INJECTION_HELD` back to `PENDING` and increments the store's `reserved_prep_slots` by 1. It is guarded by four conditions, and their relative order follows the ladder of §2.8 rather than the order a reader might guess: the store's `throttle_state` must not be `PAUSED`, or the request is `ERR_STORE_PAUSED`; then the order's capacity bucket must still admit it, or the request is `ERR_CAPACITY_BUCKET_FULL`; then the order must currently be in `INJECTION_HELD`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_INJECTION_STATE`; and only then is every line checked, so that an order still carrying an unavailable item is refused with `ERR_ITEM_STILL_UNAVAILABLE`. A line is satisfied only when it maps to a listing whose `availability_state` is `AVAILABLE`. A held order therefore cannot jump the queue: it re-enters the same bucket it was admitted into and competes for the same capacity as a fresh order.

### 3.5. Capacity Posture, Quote Times and Order Intake

A store's `throttle_state` is the middleware's answer to the question "can this kitchen take more work right now?", and it takes one of three values: `NORMAL`, `THROTTLED`, or `PAUSED`. It is never set by hand. A store operator asks the platform to re-derive it, and the derivation is a first-match-wins ladder over the store's own numbers: if the store has no `active_menu_version_id` the target is `PAUSED`; otherwise, if `reserved_prep_slots` is greater than or equal to twice `prep_capacity_per_bucket` the target is `PAUSED`; otherwise, if `reserved_prep_slots` is greater than or equal to `prep_capacity_per_bucket` the target is `THROTTLED`; otherwise the target is `NORMAL`. Both thresholds are inclusive, so a store whose reserved slots exactly equal its per-bucket capacity is `THROTTLED`, and one whose reserved slots exactly equal double that capacity is `PAUSED`. The recompute assigns whichever target the ladder produces, so a store in `NORMAL` may fall to `THROTTLED` or straight to `PAUSED`, a store in `THROTTLED` may recover to `NORMAL` or deteriorate to `PAUSED`, and a store in `PAUSED` may climb back to `THROTTLED` — the last of these only ever being reachable once the store has an active menu version, since a store without one always computes `PAUSED`.

Recomputing the posture cascades to the shelf. Every listing of that store re-derives its `availability_state` through the precedence ladder of §3.3, which in practice means that listings on channels configured with `SUPPRESS_ON_THROTTLE` become `THROTTLE_SUPPRESSED` when the store lands in `THROTTLED` or `PAUSED`, unless they are already `SUPPRESSED` or `EIGHTY_SIXED` and therefore outrank the throttle; and those same listings return to `AVAILABLE` when the store recovers to `NORMAL`, again unless suppression or an 86 still holds them down. Listings on `IGNORE_THROTTLE` channels are unaffected in either direction — that is precisely what the policy buys the channel.

**Capacity buckets.** Admission is enforced against 15-minute buckets measured in the store's own local time. The store's local minute of day is obtained by adding `timezone_offset_minutes` to the current minute of day on the logical clock in UTC and reducing the result modulo 1440, so it always lands between 0 and 1439. The `bucket_index` is that local minute of day divided by 15 and rounded down to a whole number, giving 96 buckets per local day. The `bucket_load` of a bucket is the number of orders at the store that carry the same `bucket_index` and are simultaneously open in both senses: their `injection_status` is `PENDING`, `ACCEPTED` or `TABLET_FALLBACK`, and their `fulfillment_status` is `PREPARING`. Held, failed, ready, handed-off, delivered and cancelled orders do not count. Admission fails when `bucket_load` is greater than or equal to `prep_capacity_per_bucket` — the comparison is inclusive, so a bucket already holding exactly its capacity is full — and the request is refused with `ERR_CAPACITY_BUCKET_FULL`. As a worked illustration, with the clock at 16:00 UTC and a store offset of -300 minutes, the local minute of day is 660 and the `bucket_index` is 44.

**Quote times.** A quote is built from the store's preparation load and, for delivery, a fixed travel allowance. The preparation figure `prep_minutes` starts at the store's `base_prep_minutes`, adds 2 minutes for each order counted in the load figure `load_count`, and adds a further 8 minutes when the store's posture is `THROTTLED` — and only when it is `THROTTLED`; a `NORMAL` or `PAUSED` posture adds no throttle pad. The travel figure `travel_minutes` is 11 minutes when the `fulfillment_mode` is `DELIVERY` and 0 when it is `PICKUP`. `total_minutes` is the sum of the two figures. If `total_minutes` exceeds 90 the quote is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_WINDOW_EXCEEDED` — a store so loaded that it would have to promise more than an hour and a half must stop quoting rather than promise a time it cannot keep. Otherwise the promised instant is the logical clock advanced by `total_minutes` whole minutes, carrying no additional seconds offset.

The same arithmetic serves two different questions and therefore has two variants that legitimately disagree. The **`PROVISIONAL`** variant answers "what may this channel promise a guest right now?" A store operator or a channel partner asks for a quote by supplying a `channel_id` and a `fulfillment_mode` as query parameters; both are required and both are validated before anything is looked up, so a missing or unrecognized value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, the named channel must then exist or the answer is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, and it must be the acting channel partner's own just as the store must be the acting store operator's own. The response echoes `store_id` and `channel_id`, names the `variant` it used, and reports `load_count`, `prep_minutes`, `travel_minutes`, `total_minutes`, the `bucket_index` and `bucket_load` the order would land in, and the resulting `quoted_ready_at`. In this variant `load_count` is the number of orders at the store, evaluated at request time, whose `injection_status` is `PENDING`, `INJECTION_HELD`, `ACCEPTED` or `TABLET_FALLBACK` and whose `fulfillment_status` is `PREPARING` — note that held orders **do** count towards a provisional quote even though they do not hold a capacity slot, because the kitchen will still have to make them. The store's live `throttle_state` supplies the throttle pad. Nothing is persisted by a quote, and quoting is gated by the same store checks as ordering: an unpublished store reports `ERR_STORE_MENU_NOT_PUBLISHED` and a closed store reports `ERR_STORE_CLOSED`.

The **`CONFIRMED`** variant answers "what shall we promise on this particular order?", and its answer is pinned onto the order as `promised_ready_at` at the moment of creation. Here `load_count` is a chronological replay rather than a live snapshot: it counts only those qualifying orders whose `created_at` is **at or before** the new order's own `created_at`, and it uses the store's `throttle_state` as of that same instant for the throttle pad. The new order is not yet stored when its own quote is computed, so every order the replay can see bearing that same instant was in fact created before it and still counts; the comparison only excludes orders stamped strictly later than the new one, which is possible only when the clock has been moved backwards. Once pinned, `promised_ready_at` is never recomputed — a later throttle change, a later order, or a later clock movement does not move it, which is what makes the promise auditable against the channel.

**Store gates on ordering.** Before a single line is examined, a new order must clear the store: the store must have an `active_menu_version_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STORE_MENU_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the logical clock, converted to the store's local minute of day, must fall inside one of the store's operating-hours windows whose `day_of_week` matches the store's local day, meaning the local minute of day is greater than or equal to that window's `opens_at_minute` and strictly less than its `closes_at_minute`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STORE_CLOSED`; and the store's `throttle_state` must not be `PAUSED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STORE_PAUSED`. The relative order of those three is normative: a store that is both unpublished and closed reports the menu error, and a store that is both closed and paused reports the closed error.

**Placing an order.** A channel partner submits an order naming the `store_id` it is for, the `channel_id` it originated on, a channel-side `external_ref` that must be unique within that channel — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF` — a `fulfillment_mode` of `PICKUP` or `DELIVERY`, and a non-empty `lines` array. Each entry of `lines` carries a `sku` and a `quantity` between 1 and 50 inclusive. An optional `tip_cents` of at least 0 records money the channel collected on the brand's behalf; when omitted it is 0. An empty `lines` array, a `quantity` outside its range or a negative tip are all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Line resolution goes through the shelf, not the raw menu. Each entry's `sku` must resolve to a listing for exactly that store and that channel, or the order is refused with `ERR_SKU_NOT_LISTED`. Every resolved listing must have `availability_state` equal to `AVAILABLE`, or the order is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_AVAILABLE` — suppressed, 86-ed and throttle-suppressed items are all unorderable, and this is the check that makes the fan-out of §3.4 commercially meaningful. A `DELIVERY` order additionally requires that the channel's `accepts_delivery` be true, or it is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_DELIVERY_UNSUPPORTED`, and that every line's underlying item have `is_delivery_eligible` true, or it is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_DELIVERY_ELIGIBLE`. Those checks run in the order just given, which is also the order of §2.8: the delivery-support check precedes listing resolution, listing resolution precedes availability, and availability precedes delivery eligibility.

A successful order snapshots everything it will later be reconciled against. Its `menu_version_id` is copied from the store's `active_menu_version_id`. One order line is stored per entry in `lines`, in the order the array gives — which is therefore the order `GET /orders/{id}/lines` reports them in — each recording the `order_id` it belongs to, its `sku`, its `quantity`, a `base_price_cents` copied from the listing's `base_price_cents`, a `unit_price_cents` copied from the listing's `price_cents`, and a `tax_bps` copied from the underlying item's `tax_bps`. The order's `service_fee_cents` is copied from the channel's `service_fee_cents`. `bucket_index` records the bucket the order was admitted into and `promised_ready_at` records the confirmed quote. The three status dimensions start at `injection_status` of `PENDING`, `fulfillment_status` of `PREPARING`, and `settlement_status` of `SETTLEABLE`, with `refunded_total_cents` at 0, `injection_attempt_count` at 0, and `remittance_statement_id` null. Finally the store's `open_order_count` and `reserved_prep_slots` are each incremented by 1.

**Order money.** The money breakdown is computed once, at creation, in a fixed order. Per line, `line_subtotal_cents` is `unit_price_cents` multiplied by `quantity`, and `line_tax_cents` is that line subtotal multiplied by the line's `tax_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to a whole cent — rounded once, per line. The order's `subtotal_cents` is the sum of the line subtotals and its `tax_cents` is the sum of the already-rounded line taxes; the order tax is never recomputed from the order subtotal, and the two figures can genuinely differ. Consider two lines, two units at 1265 cents and one unit at 460 cents, both taxed at 875 basis points: the line taxes round to 221 and 40, giving `tax_cents` of 261, whereas taxing the combined 2990-cent subtotal in one step would have produced 262. The 261 is correct. `markup_cents` is the order subtotal minus the sum, across lines, of each line's `base_price_cents` multiplied by its `quantity` — that is, the channel markup the brand's single-source price picked up. `channel_collected_cents`, the amount the channel charged the guest, is `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_cents` plus `service_fee_cents` plus `tip_cents`. `commission_cents` is the sum of `subtotal_cents` and `service_fee_cents`, multiplied by the channel's `commission_bps`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. `brand_net_cents`, the amount owed to the brand, is `subtotal_cents` plus `tax_cents` plus `tip_cents` minus `commission_cents`.

Those five figures satisfy one identity that holds for the entire life of the order, including after refunds: `channel_collected_cents` always equals the sum of `brand_net_cents`, `commission_cents`, `service_fee_cents` and `refunded_total_cents`. Every later money movement described in §3.8 is defined so as to preserve it, and it is the single best invariant to test an implementation against.

### 3.6. Point-of-Sale Injection, Tablet Fallback and Kitchen Readiness

An order that the middleware has accepted is not yet a kitchen ticket. It has to reach the register, and registers fail. A store operator reports each attempt by supplying the adapter's `pos_result_code`, which is `OK` on success or one of `TIMEOUT`, `PRINTER_DOWN` or `MENU_MISMATCH` on failure. Every reported attempt — successful or not — creates a **point-of-sale injection attempt** record carrying the `order_id`, a 1-based `attempt_number` within that order, the reported `pos_result_code` verbatim, an `attempted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED` when the `pos_result_code` was `OK` and `REJECTED` otherwise. The order's `injection_attempt_count` is incremented on every attempt.

Where the order lands depends on the result and on how many attempts it has already burned. On `OK` the order moves from `PENDING` to `ACCEPTED`, and an order already in `TABLET_FALLBACK` that finally gets an `OK` also moves to `ACCEPTED`, additionally decrementing the store's `tablet_backlog_count` because the ticket has left the tablet queue. On a non-`OK` result the decision is a threshold: if the order's `injection_attempt_count` after this attempt is still strictly less than the store's `pos_retry_limit`, the order simply stays in `PENDING` and may be retried. If that count has reached or passed the store's `pos_retry_limit`, retrying is over and the order falls out of the register path in one of two ways. When the store's `tablet_backlog_count` is strictly less than its `tablet_backlog_threshold`, the order moves from `PENDING` to `TABLET_FALLBACK` and the store's `tablet_backlog_count` is incremented — the ticket will be worked from the tablet. When the store's `tablet_backlog_count` has already reached or passed its `tablet_backlog_threshold`, the tablet queue is saturated and the order moves from `PENDING` to the terminal `FAILED`: its `fulfillment_status` is set to `CANCELLED`, the store's `reserved_prep_slots` and `open_order_count` are each decremented, and any delivery assignment for that order in `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN`. Both branches still record the `REJECTED` attempt. That whole threshold ladder applies only to an order that was in `PENDING`: a non-`OK` result reported against an order already in `TABLET_FALLBACK` records the `REJECTED` attempt and increments `injection_attempt_count`, and does nothing else — the order stays in `TABLET_FALLBACK` however often the register refuses it, and neither the retry limit nor the backlog threshold can drive it to `FAILED` from there.

Injection is legal only from `PENDING` or `TABLET_FALLBACK`. An attempt reported against an order in `INJECTION_HELD`, in `ACCEPTED`, or in the terminal `FAILED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INJECTION_STATE`.

Marking an order ready is the kitchen's signal that the food is made. It moves `fulfillment_status` from `PREPARING` to `READY` and decrements the store's `reserved_prep_slots` by 1 — the slot is released because the kitchen work is done — while leaving `open_order_count` untouched, since the order is still the store's responsibility until it leaves the building. Readiness requires an order that the register or the tablet has actually taken: the `injection_status` must be `ACCEPTED` or `TABLET_FALLBACK`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_INJECTED`. It also requires a `fulfillment_status` of `PREPARING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.

Cancellation is available to the store operator and to the originating channel partner, and takes a required `reason_code`. It is permitted only from `PREPARING` or `READY` — any other `fulfillment_status`, including the terminal `HANDED_OFF`-and-beyond path and an already-cancelled order, is `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Two money conditions guard it: the order's `settlement_status` must still be `SETTLEABLE`, or the cancel is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` — the same error a wrong `fulfillment_status` earns — and its `refunded_total_cents` must be exactly 0. An order carrying accepted refunds cannot be cancelled and is refused with `ERR_ORDER_HAS_ACCEPTED_ADJUSTMENTS`; the refunds must be reversed first so that the money identity of §3.5 stays intact. A successful cancellation sets `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED`, decrements the store's `open_order_count`, decrements `reserved_prep_slots` unless that slot was already released — which it was if the order had reached `READY` or had been put into `INJECTION_HELD` — decrements the store's `tablet_backlog_count` if the order's `injection_status` was `TABLET_FALLBACK`, and moves every delivery assignment for the order in `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` to `WITHDRAWN` with `occupancy_until` pulled back to the current clock.

### 3.7. Delivery Dispatch, Courier Selection and Handoff SLAs

Delivery capacity is registered as a set of **courier networks**. Each has a globally unique `code` — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_NETWORK_CODE` — a human-readable `name`, and a `network_type` of either `MARKETPLACE`, meaning it may only carry orders that originated on a channel whose `channel_type` is `MARKETPLACE`, or `COURIER`, meaning it may carry orders from any channel. Its selection and service terms are a `priority_rank` from 1 through 99 inclusive where lower ranks are preferred, a `per_delivery_fee_cents` of at least 0, a `handoff_sla_seconds` from 60 through 7200 inclusive within which the courier must take physical handoff after accepting, a `dispatch_buffer_seconds` from 0 through 3600 inclusive of extra time during which an assignment still occupies the network's capacity after its handoff deadline, and a `max_concurrent_assignments` from 1 through 20 inclusive. An optional `is_active` flag, true when omitted, controls whether the network participates in selection at all. A new network starts with `open_assignment_count` and `penalty_accrued_cents` at 0. `open_assignment_count` is a maintained counter that always equals the number of that network's assignments whose `state` is `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`, counted without reference to their occupancy intervals or to the current logical clock; it is therefore not the same figure as the clock-dependent occupancy count defined below.

Dispatch is a store-operator action on a specific order and creates a **delivery assignment**. Four gates precede selection, in this order: the order's `fulfillment_status` must be `PREPARING` or `READY`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; its `injection_status` must be `ACCEPTED` or `TABLET_FALLBACK`, or the request is `ERR_ORDER_NOT_INJECTED`; its `fulfillment_mode` must be `DELIVERY`, since a pickup order is never dispatched, or the request is `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DELIVERY`; and the order must not already have an assignment sitting in `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED`, or the request is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_ALREADY_OPEN`. Re-dispatch is therefore legal only after the previous offer was declined or withdrawn.

**Network selection.** Occupancy is the pivotal concept. An assignment occupies its network from its `offered_at` through its `occupancy_until`, and that interval is **closed at both ends** — an instant exactly equal to either endpoint counts as inside it. A network's occupancy count at a given instant is the number of its assignments whose `state` is `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` and whose occupancy interval contains that instant. A network is eligible for a given order when all three of the following hold: its `is_active` is true; either its `network_type` is `COURIER` or the order's channel has a `channel_type` of `MARKETPLACE`; and its occupancy count at the current logical clock is strictly less than its `max_concurrent_assignments`. If no network is eligible, dispatch is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COURIER_NETWORK` — an empty candidate set is always an error and never a null assignment. Otherwise the eligible networks are ordered by `priority_rank` ascending; ties are broken by `per_delivery_fee_cents` ascending, then by `handoff_sla_seconds` ascending, and finally by the network's `created_at` ascending, with registration order settling the residual case of two networks minted at the very same logical instant, which guarantees a single deterministic winner. The record identifier is never used as a tie-break. The first network in that ordering receives the offer.

A new assignment records the `order_id` it serves and the `courier_network_id` that won selection, starts in `OFFERED` with `offered_at` stamped from the clock, and sets `occupancy_until` to `offered_at` advanced by the network's `handoff_sla_seconds` and then by its `dispatch_buffer_seconds`. `accepted_at`, `handoff_deadline_at`, `handoff_at`, `delivered_at` and `handoff_lateness_seconds` are all null at this point, `handoff_penalty_cents` and `delivery_fee_cents` are 0, and `sla_breached` is false. The winning network's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed.

Acceptance is the courier agent's action and is legal only from `OFFERED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`. The network must still be active and the order must still be one the register or tablet has taken — its `injection_status` must be `ACCEPTED` or `TABLET_FALLBACK`. Those two run in that order, immediately after the state check and ahead of the occupancy re-check: an inactive network is reported as `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, and an order that is neither `ACCEPTED` nor in `TABLET_FALLBACK` as `ERR_ORDER_NOT_INJECTED`. Occupancy is re-checked at this moment, excluding the assignment being accepted from the count, because the clock may have advanced since the offer was made; if the network's remaining occupancy count has already reached its `max_concurrent_assignments`, the acceptance is refused with `ERR_NETWORK_AT_CAPACITY`. On success the assignment moves `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at` from the clock, sets `handoff_deadline_at` to `accepted_at` advanced by the network's `handoff_sla_seconds`, resets `occupancy_until` to that deadline advanced by the network's `dispatch_buffer_seconds`, and snapshots `delivery_fee_cents` from the network's `per_delivery_fee_cents` so a later fee change cannot rewrite history.

Declining is the mirror image and is likewise legal only from `OFFERED`. It takes an optional `reason_code`, has no preconditions beyond the state itself, moves the assignment `OFFERED → DECLINED`, and pulls `occupancy_until` back to the current logical clock so the network's capacity is released immediately rather than lingering for the buffer. The order becomes re-dispatchable, and the network's `open_assignment_count` is recomputed. `DECLINED` is terminal for that assignment.

Handoff is where the SLA is judged. It is legal only from `ACCEPTED`, and it additionally requires the order's `fulfillment_status` to be `READY` — a courier cannot take food the kitchen has not finished, and an attempt otherwise is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_READY`. On success the assignment moves `ACCEPTED → HANDED_OFF` with `handoff_at` stamped from the clock, and the penalty ladder runs. `handoff_lateness_seconds` is the number of whole seconds from `handoff_deadline_at` to `handoff_at`, floored at zero, so an early or on-time handoff is exactly 0. `sla_breached` is true when `handoff_lateness_seconds` is **strictly greater** than 300 seconds — five minutes of grace are forgiven, and lateness of exactly 300 seconds is not a breach. `handoff_penalty_cents` follows a four-band ladder in which every band is closed at its upper bound: lateness of at most 300 seconds carries no penalty; lateness above 300 seconds and at most 900 seconds carries 150 cents; lateness above 900 seconds and at most 1800 seconds carries 400 cents; lateness above 1800 seconds carries 900 cents. The penalty is added to the network's running `penalty_accrued_cents`. The same request also advances the order: its `fulfillment_status` moves `READY → HANDED_OFF` and the store's `open_order_count` is decremented, because the food has left the building.

Delivery confirmation closes the loop. It is legal only from `HANDED_OFF`, carries no further precondition, stamps `delivered_at` from the clock, moves the assignment `HANDED_OFF → DELIVERED`, and moves the order's `fulfillment_status` `HANDED_OFF → DELIVERED`. `DELIVERED` is terminal for the assignment, as is `WITHDRAWN` — the state an open offer is moved to whenever the order it serves stops being deliverable, whether because the order was cancelled, because a terminal register failure cancelled it, or because an 86 put it on hold. A withdrawal always pulls `occupancy_until` back to the current logical clock; a withdrawal driven by an 86 hold applies only to assignments still in `OFFERED`, whereas a withdrawal driven by cancellation or terminal register failure applies to assignments in `OFFERED` and in `ACCEPTED` alike.

### 3.8. Refunds, Fee Credits and the Channel Review Loop

When something goes wrong with an order, the money has to move back through the channel that collected it. That is what an **adjustment** is for, and it comes in two flavours: an `adjustment_type` of `REFUND` returns money to the guest, while a `FEE_CREDIT` credits a channel fee back to the brand. A settlement analyst proposes one by naming the `order_id`, the `adjustment_type`, an `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, a `reason_code` such as a missing-item or late-delivery code, and the `expected_order_version` required by §2.7.

Proposal is guarded by two money checks in a fixed order. The order's `settlement_status` must not be `SETTLED` — money on a settled order is closed, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Only then is the refund ceiling evaluated, and only for a `REFUND`: the sum of the order's current `refunded_total_cents` and the proposed `amount_cents` must be less than or equal to the order's `channel_collected_cents`, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_COLLECTED`. A refund may never exceed what the channel actually collected, counting refunds already accepted, and a refund for exactly the remaining balance is allowed. Because the already-settled check ranks above the ceiling check, an over-large refund against a settled order reports the settled error.

A new adjustment starts in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `accepted_at` and `reversed_at` null, and `remittance_statement_id` null. Its `channel_id` is copied from the order, which is the mechanism by which the adjustment routes back to the originating channel rather than to whichever partner happens to be looking at it. Its `proposed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. Its `commission_relief_cents` — the share of the refund the channel gives back out of its own commission — is the `amount_cents` multiplied by the channel's `commission_bps`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up for a `REFUND`, and exactly 0 for a `FEE_CREDIT`. Creating the adjustment increments the order's `version` by 1, and if the order already belongs to a statement, that statement's `open_adjustment_count` is recomputed on the spot.

Submitting the draft routes it according to the channel's own configuration, and the two branches are genuinely different outcomes rather than variations of one. On a channel whose `adjustment_review_mode` is `CHANNEL_REVIEW`, submission moves the adjustment `DRAFT → CHANNEL_REVIEW`, and any statement holding the order recomputes its `open_adjustment_count`; the money does not move yet. On a channel whose `adjustment_review_mode` is `AUTO_ACCEPT`, submission moves it `DRAFT → ACCEPTED` immediately and applies the full acceptance money cascade described below, exactly as if the partner had accepted it by hand. Submission is legal only from `DRAFT`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_ADJUSTMENT_STATE`. Submission itself repeats neither the already-settled check nor the refund-ceiling check, so an `AUTO_ACCEPT` submission moves the money on the strength of the checks already made when the adjustment was proposed.

An adjustment in `CHANNEL_REVIEW` sits with the channel partner, who may bounce it back or accept it. Bouncing it back moves it `CHANNEL_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and may carry an explanatory `note`, which the platform accepts as an optional string but neither stores on the adjustment nor echoes back. The channel's patience is bounded: an adjustment may be bounced back at most twice, so the transition requires `revision_round` to be strictly less than 2, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — after that the partner must either accept the adjustment or leave it sitting in review. Revising is the analyst's answer and moves it `REVISION_REQUESTED → CHANNEL_REVIEW`, incrementing `revision_round` by 1. Both `amount_cents` and `reason_code` are optional on a revision; when a new `amount_cents` is supplied it replaces the old one, `commission_relief_cents` is recomputed from it by the same basis-point rule, and the refund ceiling is re-checked against the order, so a revision that would breach it is refused with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_COLLECTED`. When no amount is supplied the existing figures stand.

Acceptance is the channel partner's decision and is the moment money actually moves. It is legal only from `CHANNEL_REVIEW`, and it requires the order's `settlement_status` to still not be `SETTLED`, or it is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_SETTLED`. On success the adjustment moves to `ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and three figures on the order move together: `refunded_total_cents` increases by the adjustment's `amount_cents`; `commission_cents` decreases by its `commission_relief_cents`; and `brand_net_cents` decreases by the `amount_cents` less the `commission_relief_cents`. Those three movements are chosen so that the money identity of §3.5 is preserved exactly — the channel and the brand each bear their agreed share of the refund, and nothing leaks. If the order already belongs to a statement, the adjustment inherits the order's `remittance_statement_id` and that statement's figures are recomputed in the normative order given in §3.9: `tier_base_cents` first, then `tier`, then `commission_cents`, then `rebate_cents`, and finally `net_payable_cents`.

Reversal exists because acceptance is irreversible for the guest but not for the ledger. It is legal only from `ACCEPTED`, takes a mandatory `reason_code`, and is performed by a settlement analyst — but not by the analyst who proposed it: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `proposed_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the order's `settlement_status` must still not be `SETTLED`, or it is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_SETTLED`. A reversal moves the adjustment to the terminal `REVERSED`, stamps `reversed_at` from the clock, and undoes every money effect of acceptance exactly: the order's `refunded_total_cents`, `commission_cents` and `brand_net_cents` all return to the values they held before that acceptance, and any statement holding the order recomputes its figures through the same pipeline. The reversed adjustment keeps whatever `remittance_statement_id` it had inherited — reversal does not detach it from the statement — but it stops feeding `adjustment_total_cents`, which counts accepted adjustments only. `REVERSED` is terminal — a reversed adjustment can never be resubmitted, revised or re-accepted, and every such attempt is `ERR_INVALID_ADJUSTMENT_STATE`.

Three of the five adjustment states are considered **open** for the purpose of blocking reconciliation: `DRAFT`, `CHANNEL_REVIEW` and `REVISION_REQUESTED`. `ACCEPTED` and `REVERSED` are settled outcomes and never block anything.

### 3.9. Remittance Statements: Assignment, Reconciliation, Dispute and Settlement

Money finally comes to rest in a **remittance statement** — one channel's account for one closed period. A settlement analyst opens a statement by naming the `channel_id` and a period bounded by an inclusive `period_start` and an exclusive `period_end`. The period must be real and finished: `period_start` must be strictly earlier than `period_end`, and `period_end` must not be later than the current logical clock, so an inverted period or a period reaching into the future is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD`. A new statement starts in `OPEN` with `order_count`, `gross_sales_cents`, `adjustment_total_cents`, `tier_base_cents`, `commission_cents`, `rebate_cents`, `net_payable_cents` and `open_adjustment_count` all at 0, `tier` at `TIER_1`, and both `reconciled_by_user_id` and `settled_at` null.

Orders are attached in batches by supplying an `order_ids` array — a required, non-empty array of identifier strings, an empty one being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the statement must be in `OPEN` or `DISPUTED` to accept them; any other statement state is `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`. Eligibility is all-or-nothing for the whole request: if a single listed order fails, no order in the batch is assigned. The three eligibility tests below are applied order by order in the array's own order, so it is the first listed order that fails any of them that determines the response. Each listed order must belong to the statement's channel, must have been created within the period — its `created_at` at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end` — and must have a `fulfillment_status` of `READY`, `HANDED_OFF` or `DELIVERED`, meaning the food was at least made. A failure of any of those three is `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. That eligibility test deliberately says nothing about settlement status; that is a separate, lower-ranked check, so an order that already sits on some statement — whose `settlement_status` is therefore `STATEMENT_ASSIGNED` or `SETTLED` rather than `SETTLEABLE` — surfaces as `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` and not as an eligibility failure. An order may belong to at most one statement at a time.

A successful assignment moves each listed order's `settlement_status` from `SETTLEABLE` to `STATEMENT_ASSIGNED` and sets its `remittance_statement_id`. Every already-`ACCEPTED` adjustment on those orders is pulled onto the same statement, so a refund never lands on a different account from the sale it belongs to. The statement then recomputes its aggregates, and its `open_adjustment_count` is recomputed as the number of adjustments on assigned orders that are still in one of the three open states.

**The settlement pipeline.** Statement figures are derived in a strictly ordered pipeline, because the volume tier, the commission and the rebate all read the same adjusted figure and would otherwise disagree. Every run of the pipeline also restates `order_count` as the number of orders currently assigned to the statement. First, `gross_sales_cents` is the sum, across assigned orders, of each order's `subtotal_cents` plus its `service_fee_cents`; and `adjustment_total_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` across the accepted adjustments assigned to the statement. Second, `tier_base_cents` is `gross_sales_cents` minus `adjustment_total_cents`, floored at zero so that a period whose refunds exceed its sales settles at zero rather than negative. Third, the `tier` is selected from that adjusted figure: `TIER_1` when `tier_base_cents` is strictly less than 50,000 cents; `TIER_2` when it is at or above 50,000 cents and strictly below 200,000 cents; and `TIER_3` at or above 200,000 cents. Fourth, an effective commission rate is derived by subtracting the tier's volume discount from the channel's `commission_bps` and flooring the result at zero, where the discount is 0 basis points at `TIER_1`, 50 basis points at `TIER_2`, and 125 basis points at `TIER_3`. Fifth, `commission_cents` is `tier_base_cents` multiplied by that effective rate, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up. Sixth, `rebate_cents` is `tier_base_cents` multiplied by the tier's rebate rate — 0 basis points at `TIER_1`, 25 basis points at `TIER_2`, and 75 basis points at `TIER_3` — divided by 10,000 and rounded half up independently of the commission. Seventh and last, `net_payable_cents` is derived from the channel's `settlement_mode`.

The two settlement modes differ in exactly one place, and both are computed the same way up to that point. On a `GROSS_MODE` channel the partner remits the adjusted volume and the brand is invoiced for commission separately, so `net_payable_cents` is `tier_base_cents` plus `rebate_cents`; `commission_cents` is still computed and still reported, but it is not withheld. On a `NET_MODE` channel the partner withholds its commission before remitting, so `net_payable_cents` is `tier_base_cents` minus `commission_cents` plus `rebate_cents`. As a worked illustration, a statement with `gross_sales_cents` of 6578, `adjustment_total_cents` of 1000 and a channel `commission_bps` of 2500 has a `tier_base_cents` of 5578, which selects `TIER_1`, leaving the effective rate at 2500 basis points, producing `commission_cents` of 1395 and `rebate_cents` of 0 — a `GROSS_MODE` channel would then show `net_payable_cents` of 5578 and a `NET_MODE` channel 4183.

The pipeline runs whenever the statement's inputs change: when orders are assigned, when the statement is reconciled, whenever an adjustment on an assigned order is accepted or reversed, and on demand for a preview. The preview is a read-only projection available to the platform administrator, the channel partner and the analyst alike: it reports the `statement_id`, the channel's `settlement_mode`, and the figures `gross_sales_cents`, `adjustment_total_cents`, `tier_base_cents`, `tier`, `effective_commission_bps`, `commission_cents`, `rebate_cents` and `net_payable_cents`, and it additionally shows both mode outcomes side by side as `gross_mode_net_payable_cents` and `net_mode_net_payable_cents` so a partner can see what the other mode would have paid. A preview persists nothing.

Reconciliation freezes the account. It is available from `OPEN` and from `DISPUTED`, moving the statement to `RECONCILED`, and it requires two things: the statement must hold at least one order, or it is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_EMPTY`, and its `open_adjustment_count` must be exactly 0, or it is refused with `ERR_OPEN_ADJUSTMENTS_PRESENT` — reconciling while a refund is still in review would let the tier base move afterwards, which is precisely what a frozen statement must not do. Reconciliation recomputes the aggregates through the pipeline and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `reconciled_by_user_id`, overwriting whatever was there before when a disputed statement is reconciled a second time.

Disputing is the channel partner's right and applies only to a `RECONCILED` statement. It takes a required `reason_code`, moves the statement `RECONCILED → DISPUTED`, and unwinds the account completely: every assigned order returns to `SETTLEABLE` with its `remittance_statement_id` cleared, every adjustment that had been pulled onto the statement is unassigned from it, `order_count` and every money aggregate — `gross_sales_cents`, `adjustment_total_cents`, `tier_base_cents`, `commission_cents`, `rebate_cents` and `net_payable_cents` — are reset to 0, `open_adjustment_count` is reset to 0 with them, and `tier` is reset to `TIER_1`. A disputed statement is not dead: orders may be assigned to it again and it may be reconciled again, which is why both of those operations accept `DISPUTED` as well as `OPEN`.

Settlement is the last act. It is available only from `RECONCILED`, and the acting analyst must not be the one recorded in `reconciled_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. It stamps `settled_at` from the logical clock, moves the statement to the terminal `SETTLED`, and moves every assigned order's `settlement_status` from `STATEMENT_ASSIGNED` to the terminal `SETTLED`. From that point the order's money is closed: no adjustment may be proposed against it, accepted on it, or reversed on it, and the statement itself admits no further assignment, reconciliation or dispute — every such attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Endpoints are mounted at the root of the service; there is no shared path prefix. Operations that bring a new, independently addressable resource into existence — a store, an operating-hours window, a channel, a menu version, a menu item, an availability event, an order, a courier network, a delivery assignment, an adjustment, a statement — return `201 Created`. Every other successful operation, including every state transition, every configuration update, every read and every list, returns `200 OK`.
