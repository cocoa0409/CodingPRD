# Film Sales Presale API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An international film sales agency lives or dies on the gap between what a picture costs and what its territories will pay before a single frame is publicly screened. Our agency represents feature films to distributors around the world, values each territory against comparable licensing history, negotiates minimum guarantees market by market, and then hands the resulting contracts to a bank as collateral so the production can be financed. Today that entire chain — market attendance planning, territory valuation, offer-and-counter negotiation, technical and legal delivery, cash collection, bank discounting and waterfall distribution — is run on spreadsheets that no two departments reconcile the same way. The consequence is not merely administrative untidiness: a valuation floor that nobody can prove, a buyer exposure figure computed on stale data, or a commission tier applied in the wrong order translates directly into money the agency cannot recover.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. Every resource is mounted under the `/film-sales` prefix and every state change is an explicit, auditable operation. The system tracks sixteen resource types across eleven independent state machines, and its defining characteristic is that money and rights are conserved: a minimum guarantee committed to a picture must appear in that picture's financing gap; cash booked into a collection account must either sit in the balance or have been distributed through a waterfall tier; a territory licensed to a buyer must be unavailable to every other buyer until the licence lapses, is reverted, or fails final delivery. Where an operation can be undone — a mis-posted receipt, a lapsed contract, a reverted licence — the compensating operation restores every quantity the forward operation touched, to the cent.

Two structural principles run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **segregation of duties**: the person who sets a territory's floor price may not be the person who signs the contract priced against it, and the lender officer who approves a facility may not be the officer who draws it. Second, **pinned evaluation order**: several calculations in this domain look circular or ambiguous — a commission rate that depends on a running total that this very receipt is about to change, a waterfall that rounds cents tier by tier — and for each one this document fixes exactly one evaluation order and names the alternative that is forbidden. Implementations that choose the other order will produce figures that are off by cents, and cents that do not reconcile are, in a collection account audit, indistinguishable from fraud.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. If either header is absent — or present but empty, or holding nothing but whitespace, which counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` does not spell one of the seven recognised roles exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the targeted operation (see the authorisation column of the endpoint table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `SalesAgent`, `HeadOfSales`, `Buyer`, `DeliveryManager`, `CollectionAgent`, `Lender` and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `SalesAgent` is the front-line seller. Agents value territories, book screening rooms and meeting tables at markets, open pre-sale offers, answer buyer counters with revised offers, build payment-milestone schedules and log recoupable sales expenses. Revising an offer and adding a payment milestone are the two operations that no role other than `SalesAgent` and `HeadOfSales` may perform.

A `HeadOfSales` carries the agency's signature. This role drafts and executes the sales agency agreement, publishes territory valuations, countersigns accepted contracts, lapses contracts that will not close, and reverts rights once a licence term has run. `HeadOfSales` also holds every `SalesAgent` capability, and shares control of the film's own sales lifecycle with `PlatformAdmin`.

A `Buyer` is a territory distributor on the other side of the table. Buyers counter and accept the offers made to them and record quality-control verdicts on the deliverable packages sent to them.

A `DeliveryManager` assembles and submits the technical and legal materials a contract obliges the agency to deliver, and resubmits them after a rejection.

A `CollectionAgent` runs the money. This role opens the collection account for a picture, defines the tiers of its distribution waterfall, posts and reverses buyer receipts, and executes distribution runs.

A `Lender` is the discounting bank. Lenders propose a banking facility against a picture's pre-sales, approve it, post the completion bond, draw it, and declare it in default.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the operational superuser for reference data — films, territories, comparable titles, buyers and markets — and owns the logical clock. `PlatformAdmin` holds no deal-making authority: it cannot publish a valuation, execute a contract, post a receipt or draw a facility.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `Buyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Buyer` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Buyer` compare the resource's buyer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Buyer` may counter or accept only a contract whose `buyer_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and may record a quality-control verdict only on a deliverable package whose owning contract has that same `buyer_id`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_NOT_CONTRACT_BUYER` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `SalesAgent`, `HeadOfSales`, `DeliveryManager`, `CollectionAgent`, `Lender` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a state transition asks for provenance — as `executed_by_user_id` when a `HeadOfSales` executes an agency agreement or a pre-sale contract, as `published_by_user_id` when a `HeadOfSales` publishes a valuation, as `offered_by_user_id` when a `SalesAgent` or `HeadOfSales` opens an offer, as `submitted_by_user_id` when a `DeliveryManager` submits a package, as `received_by_user_id` when a `CollectionAgent` books a receipt, as `run_by_user_id` when a `CollectionAgent` executes a distribution run, and as `approved_by_user_id` when a `Lender` approves a facility — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

**Segregation of duties on top of the role grid.** Two operations impose an additional identity comparison beyond the role check. Countersigning a pre-sale contract requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the `published_by_user_id` of the `PUBLISHED` territory valuation covering the contract's film-and-territory pair. Drawing a banking facility requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the facility's `approved_by_user_id`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Both checks compare user identifiers only; the role check has already passed by the time they run.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 24 randomly drawn lowercase-alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside these bounds — or one that is not a whole number at all — is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every other query parameter a list endpoint accepts is a filter, and filters are matched for exact equality against the stored value compared as text: a value matching nothing, including one that is not a member of the field's enumerated set, yields an empty page rather than an error; a recognised filter supplied with an empty value is treated as though it had not been supplied at all and narrows nothing; and a query parameter the endpoint does not recognise is ignored. A list endpoint recognises exactly the filters its §4 row names, so an endpoint whose row names none at all — the reverse lookups nested under a parent resource among them, including a picture's banking facilities and collection accounts and an account's waterfall tiers and distribution runs — narrows by that parent alone and ignores every query parameter but `limit` and `offset`.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 nominates a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Several collections do nominate a domain ordering, and where they do it is authoritative: the film slate is returned newest first by `created_at` descending with `id` ascending as the tie-break; territories are ordered by `code` ascending; markets by `start_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; screening slots and buyer meetings by `start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; a contract's payment milestones by `due_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; a contract's deliverable packages by `revision_round` ascending then `created_at` ascending; an account's waterfall tiers by `tier_order` ascending; and an account's distribution runs by `run_index` ascending.

Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock of §2.4, every row inserted between two clock moves carries exactly the same `created_at`, so ties on that column are the normal case rather than an edge case. Wherever `created_at` is the last ordering key an endpoint names — the plain `created_at` ascending default included, and likewise the `start_date`, `start_at`, `due_date` and `revision_round` orderings listed above once their leading key ties — rows sharing it are returned in **insertion order**, that is, in the order the server created them. The film slate is the one collection that nominates a tie-break of its own, and there `id` ascending governs instead. Where the leading ordering key is itself unique within its scope — a territory's `code`, a tier's `tier_order`, a run's `run_index` — no tie can arise.

For list responses the `meta` object carries four pagination fields: `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any row remains beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Logical Clock

Every resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no domain meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; supplying one has no effect. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded at insert time.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each successful mutation of that row. Three resource types are the exception and deliberately carry only `id` and `created_at`, with neither `updated_at` nor `version` present in their payloads at all: territories, comparable titles, and distribution runs. A territory and a comparable title are reference data that the sales operation reads but does not revise in place; a distribution run is an immutable allocation record, written once and never touched again.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. The server holds a single `current_utc` instant which reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` from startup and never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability described in §4 — a move that may carry the reading backwards as freely as forwards, since no ordering constraint is imposed on the supplied instant, and whose own response carries an empty `data` object rather than any representation of the clock; from that point every comparison the domain makes against the present — whether a holdback has elapsed, whether a licence term has expired, whether a cure window is still open, whether an agency agreement's term window contains the present instant, and how many days of discount tenor a lender may claim — uses the new value. Fields that record "when this happened" are stamped from `current_utc`, and the audit columns are no exception: `created_at` on every resource, and `updated_at` wherever `updated_at` is present, both read the logical present instant, exactly as `published_at` on a valuation, `license_start_date` on an executed contract and `submitted_at` on a deliverable package do. There is no second, real-clock time source behind any resource field. The one exception is `received_at` on a payment milestone, which the posting `CollectionAgent` supplies explicitly in the receipt payload. The response envelope's `api_request_timestamp` of §2.2 is not a resource field at all — it is metadata about the call, generated from the server's real clock, and it neither reads nor is affected by `current_utc`. Instants that a client supplies in a request body or to the clock-setting capability are read as ISO 8601 with some latitude: the fractional-second part may be omitted or carry any number of digits, the zone may be written as a trailing `Z` or as a numeric UTC offset, and a value carrying no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC. Whatever form arrives, the instant is normalised to UTC before it is stored and is echoed back in the three-fractional-digit form described above.

### 2.5. Money, Precision and Rounding

Every monetary field in the system is a USD number rounded to 2 decimal places using round-half-up. This applies uniformly to budgets, minimum guarantees, valuation estimates, ask and floor prices, milestone amounts, commission figures, expense totals, collateral and advance figures, tier allocations, account balances and distribution residuals. Territories carry a `currency_code` describing the local settlement currency, but it is informational only: no field in the system is ever denominated in anything but USD. A monetary — or, more generally, any numeric — input for which §3 declares no explicit range is checked at request-shape time only for being a finite number of the right kind, so a zero or negative figure passes that gate and is caught, if at all, by the business rule that consumes it — a non-positive logged expense, for instance, is refused by the expense-limit rule of §3.1 rather than as a malformed request.

Two quantities are held at wider precision because rounding them to cents would destroy the arithmetic that consumes them. A commission **rate** is held to 6 decimal places, round-half-up. A lender's **discount factor** is likewise held to 6 decimal places, round-half-up. Both are then multiplied against a USD figure, and only that product is rounded to 2 decimal places. That 6-decimal quantisation is a property of the rate the accrual step of §3.7 *derives*, not of the stored inputs: a ratio or rate field that §3 bounds but never describes as rounded — a territory's `base_value_index`, an agency agreement's `commission_pct`, a banking facility's `discount_rate_pct` and `advance_rate_pct`, and a waterfall tier's `pct_of_available` among them — is stored and echoed back exactly as the client supplied it, at whatever precision that was.

Two ratio fields are held to 4 decimal places: a contract's `milestone_pct_total`, which must reach exactly `1.0000` before the contract may be countersigned, and the individual `pct_of_mg` shares that sum into it. `pct_of_mg` is the one ratio field in the system that is quantised on the way in: the share the client supplies is rounded half-up to 4 decimal places before it is stored, and it is that stored 4-decimal share — not the figure as it was typed — from which the milestone's `amount_usd` and the contract's running total are computed. The total itself is the sum of those stored shares, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places.

Where a formula in §3 states that a figure is rounded before being used in a subsequent step, that order is binding. Rounding later — carrying full precision through a loop and rounding only at the end — produces different cents and is not an acceptable implementation of the same rule. The two places where this bites hardest are the waterfall allocation loop and the per-contract discounting of pre-sale collateral, and each is spelled out in the subsection that governs it.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Slate and Buyer Records

Two write operations in this system rewrite a shared denormalised counter on a parent record, and both are protected by an optimistic-concurrency guard that compares a client-supplied version against the parent's current `version`.

Publishing a territory valuation requires the request to carry `expected_film_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the parent film as the client last observed it. Publication legitimately retires the previously `PUBLISHED` valuation for the same film-and-territory pair and rewrites the film's `published_territory_count`, so a client working from a stale view of the film would silently retire a valuation it never saw. If `expected_film_version` does not equal the film's current `version`, the publication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FILM_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Opening a pre-sale offer requires the request to carry `expected_buyer_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target buyer as the client last observed it. A new offer for the same film, buyer and territory legitimately supersedes any earlier `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` contract for that triple and rewrites the buyer's `outstanding_exposure_usd`, so pricing an offer against a stale exposure figure would breach the buyer's credit limit without the client noticing. If `expected_buyer_version` does not equal the buyer's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BUYER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. In particular, countering, accepting, executing, lapsing and reverting a contract, posting and reversing receipts, drawing facilities and running distributions all proceed without any expected-version input.

### 2.7. Rejection Ordering

When a request would fail several checks at once, exactly one error is returned: the highest-precedence failing check. All lower-precedence checks are skipped, and their conditions are never evaluated. The precedence order is fixed system-wide, and it is the following ladder, from first to last.

**First, authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.

**Second, authorisation.** The acting role listed for the target endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

**Third, existence — resolved strictly left to right along the URL path, and only then against foreign keys carried in the body.** A film referenced by path or by a body `film_id` must exist, else `ERR_FILM_NOT_FOUND`; then a territory referenced by path or by a body `territory_id`, else `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_FOUND`; then a market referenced by path or by a body `market_id`, else `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`; then a buyer referenced by path or by a body `buyer_id`, else `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`; then a pre-sale contract referenced by path, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; then a territory valuation, else `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND`; then a sales agency agreement, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; then a payment milestone, else `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`; then a deliverable package, else `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`; then a banking facility, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`; then a collection account, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`; and finally a screening slot or buyer meeting referenced by path, either of which when unresolvable yields `ERR_MARKET_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve of these carry HTTP 404. A body foreign key that is *present* but does not resolve to a row — an unknown id, an empty string, or a value of some other JSON type entirely — fails on this rung with the relevant not-found code rather than on the shape rung below; only a foreign key that is absent altogether (or explicitly null) falls through to the shape rung and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

**Fourth, request shape.** Every required body field present, every enum value a member of its declared set, every number inside its declared range, and every end-or-close instant strictly after its start counterpart, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A field present but explicitly null is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: where the field is required the request is malformed, and where it is optional it takes the default §3 gives it. A string field is trimmed of its surrounding whitespace before it is validated and before it is stored, and must still be non-empty after that trim. A numeric field accepts either a JSON number or a string holding a decimal literal, and never a boolean; a field declared as an integer additionally accepts a number whose fractional part is zero and rejects any other fractional value. An absent request body, or a body that is not a JSON object at all, is read as an empty object — so it fails only on whatever fields that endpoint actually requires, and an endpoint requiring no field at all accepts it. This check sits *after* existence, so a request that both names a non-existent film and omits a required field reports the missing film.

**Fifth, entity-backed ownership.** When the acting role is `Buyer`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `buyer_id` of the targeted contract, or of the contract owning the targeted deliverable package. This applies to exactly three operations — countering an offer, accepting an offer, and recording a quality-control verdict — and its failure code is `ERR_NOT_CONTRACT_BUYER` (HTTP 403).

**Sixth, the two optimistic-concurrency guards.** `expected_film_version` is checked on valuation publication (`ERR_STALE_FILM_VERSION`) before `expected_buyer_version` is checked on offer creation (`ERR_STALE_BUYER_VERSION`). Both precede every state and business check that follows, so a stale-version request never reaches the domain rules at all.

**Seventh, the target row's own state.** Whether the row's own current status permits the requested transition, including a repeated trigger on a row already in the destination state or already in a terminal state, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). This guard covers every state-driving operation in the system: the state endpoints on films, agency agreements, markets, screening slots, buyer meetings, banking facilities and collection accounts, plus countering, revising an offer, accepting, executing, lapsing, reverting, publishing, recording a quality-control verdict, posting a receipt, reversing a receipt, and posting a completion bond.

**Eighth, the state of a *related* row**, in this order: the film's `sales_status` being in the window the operation needs, else `ERR_FILM_NOT_IN_MARKET` (409); then the referenced market being `OPEN`, else `ERR_MARKET_NOT_OPEN` (409); then the film's collection account existing and being `OPEN`, else `ERR_COLLECTION_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN` (409).

**Ninth, business safety** — segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403), then buyer blocking (`ERR_BUYER_BLOCKED`, 422), then the completion-bond requirement (`ERR_COMPLETION_BOND_REQUIRED`, 422).

**Tenth, business referential integrity** — the agency agreement being in force (`ERR_AGENCY_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_FORCE`, 422), then a published valuation existing (`ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_VALUATION`, 422), then eligible collateral existing (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COLLATERAL`, 422).

**Eleventh, temporal checks** in this order: screening-room conflict (`ERR_SCREENING_ROOM_CONFLICT`, 409), meeting conflict (`ERR_MEETING_SLOT_CONFLICT`, 409), containment in the market window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_MARKET_WINDOW`, 422), holdback elapsed (`ERR_HOLDBACK_NOT_ELAPSED`, 422), licence term expired (`ERR_LICENSE_TERM_NOT_EXPIRED`, 422), cure window still open (`ERR_CURE_WINDOW_ELAPSED`, 422).

**Twelfth, business constraints** in this order: duplicate territory offer (`ERR_DUPLICATE_TERRITORY_OFFER`, 409), no comparable titles (`ERR_NO_COMPARABLE_TITLES`, 422), insufficient comparable sample (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COMP_SAMPLE`, 422), invalid milestone schedule (`ERR_MILESTONE_SCHEDULE_INVALID`, 422), negotiation round limit (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`, 422), meeting minutes cap (`ERR_MEETING_MINUTES_CAP_EXCEEDED`, 422), incomplete deliverables (`ERR_DELIVERABLES_INCOMPLETE`, 422), tier order taken (`ERR_TIER_ORDER_TAKEN`, 409), territory code taken (`ERR_TERRITORY_CODE_TAKEN`, 409).

**Last, fiscal checks** in this order: gross buyer exposure (`ERR_BUYER_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED`, 422), net buyer exposure (`ERR_BUYER_NET_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED`, 422), minimum guarantee below floor (`ERR_MG_BELOW_FLOOR`, 422), receipt amount mismatch (`ERR_RECEIPT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, 422), insufficient account balance (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ACCOUNT_BALANCE`, 422), receipt already distributed (`ERR_RECEIPT_ALREADY_DISTRIBUTED`, 422), expense log limit (`ERR_EXPENSE_LOG_LIMIT`, 422).

The practical consequences of this ladder are worth naming. Because state precedes fiscal, an attempt to execute a contract that is not `ACCEPTED` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even if its minimum guarantee is also below the floor. Because segregation of duties precedes referential integrity, an operator who published the valuation and then tries to execute the contract gets `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when the agency agreement has meanwhile expired. And because the ownership check precedes the state check, a `Buyer` countering somebody else's `ACCEPTED` contract gets `ERR_NOT_CONTRACT_BUYER`, not `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 2.8. Cascade Propagation and Version Semantics

This domain's operations are rarely local. A single request routinely mutates several rows, and the specification treats every one of those secondary mutations as part of the contract rather than as an implementation detail. Whenever a row is mutated — whether the client targeted it directly or a cascade reached it — that row's `updated_at` is refreshed, and its `version` is incremented by 1 unless it is one of the three version-free resource types named in §2.4.

Seven resource types — films, sales agency agreements, markets, screening slots, buyer meetings, banking facilities and collection accounts — expose their lifecycle through a single generic state-change operation rather than through one endpoint per edge. Such a request carries a `to_status` field naming the destination state, and the service resolves which transition is being requested by pairing the row's current status with that destination. A destination that no transition out of the current status permits is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as is a destination equal to the current status. Where a particular edge needs extra input — recording a buyer meeting's `outcome`, for instance — that field travels in the same payload and is required only for the edge that consumes it. Every other lifecycle move in the system (countering, revising, accepting, countersigning, lapsing, reverting, publishing, recording a quality-control verdict, posting or reversing a receipt, posting a completion bond) has its own dedicated operation and takes no `to_status`.

Cascades are **immediate and synchronous**: by the time a response is returned, every derived counter the operation touches has already been rewritten, and a subsequent read observes the new figures. There is no deferred recomputation and no eventual consistency anywhere in this system.

Two cascade patterns recur and are worth naming once. A **conditional cascade** touches only children that are still in a non-terminal state: closing a market cancels its held screening slots and marks its still-scheduled buyer meetings as no-shows, while confirmed slots and already-completed meetings are left exactly as they are. A **derived-state cascade** recomputes a counter and then re-evaluates a state machine against the new counter, so that a picture's own sales status can change without any operator having asked for it — the mechanism by which a lapsed or reverted territory reopens a sold-out picture for sale.

The deepest cascade in the system runs from a deliverable package through its contract, that contract's payment milestones, every banking facility of the film, and finally the film itself. It is specified end to end in §3.6.

### 2.9. Enumerated Vocabularies

The system's enumerated values are fixed strings and are matched exactly, case included. A film's `genre` is one of `ACTION`, `DRAMA`, `COMEDY`, `THRILLER` or `DOCUMENTARY`, and a comparable title's `genre` draws from the same set. A film's `completion_status` is one of `SCRIPT`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `POST` or `DELIVERED`, defaulting to `SCRIPT`. A film's `sales_status` is one of `DEVELOPMENT`, `PACKAGING`, `IN_MARKET`, `SOLD_OUT` or `WRAPPED`.

A territory's `region` is one of `EMEA`, `APAC`, `LATAM` or `NORTH_AMERICA`. A valuation's `method` is either `COMPARABLE_WEIGHTED` or `CAST_ADJUSTED`, and its `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `LICENSED` or `STALE`. An agency agreement's `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `EXECUTED`, `TERMINATED` or `EXPIRED`.

A market's `status` is one of `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `CLOSED`. A screening slot's `slot_type` is one of `BUYER_SCREENING`, `PRESS` or `MARKET_PREMIERE`, and its `status` is one of `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `CANCELLED`. A buyer meeting's `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`, and its `outcome`, when set, is one of `PASS`, `INTERESTED` or `OFFER_REQUESTED`.

A buyer's `credit_rating` is one of `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BBB` or `SPECULATIVE`, and its trading `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `WATCHLIST` or `BLOCKED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`.

A pre-sale contract carries three independent status axes. Its `negotiation_status` is one of `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `EXECUTED`, `LAPSED` or `REVERTED`. Its `delivery_status` is one of `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `CURED`. Its `collection_status` is one of `UNPAID`, `PARTIALLY_PAID`, `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. The three axes advance independently and none constrains the enumerated values of the others.

A payment milestone's `label` is one of `ON_SIGNATURE`, `ON_DELIVERY`, `ON_RELEASE` or `ON_TERM_END`, and its `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `INVOICED`, `RECEIVED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. A deliverable package's `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `REJECTED_FINAL`; its `qc_result` is one of `PENDING`, `PASS` or `FAIL`, defaulting to `PENDING`; and its `rejection_reason`, present only on a rejected package, is one of `TECHNICAL_INCOMPLETE`, `LEGAL_INCOMPLETE`, `QC_FAIL` or `LATE_DELIVERY`.

A banking facility's `status` is one of `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED`, `DRAWN`, `MARGIN_CALL`, `REPAID` or `DEFAULTED`. A collection account's `status` is one of `OPEN`, `LOCKED` or `CLOSED`. A waterfall tier's `tier_type` is one of `COLLECTION_FEE`, `SALES_COMMISSION`, `SALES_EXPENSES`, `BANK_DEBT`, `EQUITY_RECOUPMENT`, `DEFERMENT` or `PARTICIPATION`, and its derived `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `SATISFIED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Picture onto the Slate and Engaging the Agency

A picture enters the system when a `PlatformAdmin` registers it. Registration supplies the `title`, the `genre` that will govern which comparable titles may be sampled when its territories are valued, the `production_budget_usd` representing the all-in negative cost, and a `principal_cast_score` — a cast value index between 0 and 100 inclusive that drives the cast tier multiplier in one of the two valuation methods. Two dates are supplied at the same time: `estimated_delivery_date`, the contractual instant at which technical materials become available, and `theatrical_release_date`, the domestic release date that serves as the baseline for every buyer's holdback window. A `completion_status` may optionally be supplied to record physical production progress; when omitted it defaults to `SCRIPT`.

Registration sets the picture's `sales_status` to `DEVELOPMENT` and initialises the derived slate counters that the rest of the system will maintain: `total_mg_committed_usd` and `total_mg_collected_usd` both start at 0.00, `published_territory_count` and `licensed_territory_count` both start at 0, and `financing_gap_usd` starts equal to the full `production_budget_usd`, because at this point nothing at all has been pre-sold.

Those five counters are never client-settable and each has an exact definition. `total_mg_committed_usd` is the sum of `minimum_guarantee_usd` across every pre-sale contract of this picture whose `negotiation_status` is either `ACCEPTED` or `EXECUTED`. `total_mg_collected_usd` is the sum of `received_amount_usd` across every payment milestone of every contract of this picture whose milestone `status` is `RECEIVED`. `financing_gap_usd` is the production budget less the committed minimum guarantees, rounded to two decimal places, and then floored at zero — a picture whose pre-sales exceed its budget reports a gap of exactly 0.00 rather than a negative figure. `published_territory_count` is the number of territory valuations of this picture whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `LICENSED`; note that a licensed territory still counts as published. `licensed_territory_count` is the number whose `status` is exactly `LICENSED`. All five are derived afresh from the picture's current contract, milestone and valuation rows every time the picture is serialised, exactly as the buyer's two exposure figures and the contract's schedule figures are, so no recomputation step can leave any of them stale. Wherever a later paragraph says one of the five is recomputed at some event, it names the figure the next read will observe rather than a counter that event writes; whether the same event also refreshes the picture's own `updated_at` and `version` is a separate question, answered at the event that raises it.

Production facts may be amended after registration, but only four of them: `completion_status`, `principal_cast_score`, `theatrical_release_date` and `estimated_delivery_date`. Each amendment is available to `PlatformAdmin` alone, increments the picture's `version` by 1, and refreshes `updated_at`; every one of the four fields is optional, and the version moves on every accepted call — including one that resubmits the values already stored, and one that supplies no mutable field whatsoever. The `sales_status` axis is deliberately not settable through amendment — it moves only through the sales lifecycle operation described below, or through a system cascade. One amendment carries a destructive side effect: if the submitted `principal_cast_score` differs from the stored value, every `PUBLISHED` territory valuation of the picture is moved to `STALE`, because the cast tier multiplier already baked into each published estimate is no longer defensible. `published_territory_count` is recomputed accordingly, which in most cases drops it.

Before any of that matters commercially, the agency must be formally engaged. A `HeadOfSales` drafts a **sales agency agreement** against the picture, supplying `commission_pct` — the base commission rate on gross receipts, which must be strictly greater than 0 and at most 0.25 — together with `expense_cap_usd`, the hard ceiling on recoupable sales expenses, `term_months`, the length of the term of representation in whole months which must be at least 6, and `term_start_date`, the first instant of that term. The agreement's `film_id` is taken from the creation path rather than the body. The service computes `term_end_date` by adding `term_months` calendar months to `term_start_date`: calendar-month addition keeps the same day of month, and when the target month is shorter it clamps to that month's last day. A freshly drafted agreement has `status` `DRAFT`, an `executed_by_user_id` of null, and every money counter — `expenses_incurred_usd`, `expenses_recouped_usd`, `commission_earned_usd` and `gross_collected_usd` — at 0.00.

Moving the agreement `DRAFT → EXECUTED` is reserved to `HeadOfSales` and is guarded by a single precondition: no other agreement for the same picture may already be in `EXECUTED` status. A picture has at most one live representation agreement, by construction. Execution records `executed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and recomputes `term_end_date` from `term_start_date` and `term_months`.

An agreement leaves `EXECUTED` in one of two directions. A `HeadOfSales` may terminate it — `EXECUTED → TERMINATED` — but only while no pre-sale contract of the same picture sits in `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`; live negotiations must be closed out first. Termination cascades into the picture: if the picture's `sales_status` was `IN_MARKET` or `SOLD_OUT` at the time, it moves to `WRAPPED`. Crucially, termination resets no money counter — `expenses_recouped_usd` and `commission_earned_usd` retain their values so the waterfall remains reconcilable after the relationship ends. The other exit is `EXECUTED → EXPIRED`, which no operator triggers directly; it fires as a system cascade whenever the picture reaches `sales_status` `WRAPPED`. `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal; an agreement is never revived from either.

An agreement is **in force** when its `status` is `EXECUTED` and the logical present instant is at or after `term_start_date` and strictly before `term_end_date`. Four operations require an in-force agreement for the relevant picture and reject with `ERR_AGENCY_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_FORCE` when there is none: publishing a territory valuation, opening a pre-sale offer, countersigning an accepted contract, and logging a sales expense. Note the asymmetry — countering, accepting, revising, lapsing and reverting do not consult the agreement's term window at all.

Sales expenses are logged against the agreement by a `SalesAgent` or a `HeadOfSales`, one at a time, each with an `amount_usd` and a free-text `memo` that is itself mandatory and must be a non-empty string. Neither field is retained as a resource of its own — a logged expense produces no row a client can read back, only the updated agreement, which the operation returns. The posted `amount_usd` must be strictly greater than zero, and the running `expenses_incurred_usd` plus this amount must not exceed twice the `expense_cap_usd` — the tolerance factor is exactly 2.00, so an agency with a 500,000.00 cap may log up to 1,000,000.00 of expenses in total. Anything beyond that, or a non-positive amount, is rejected with `ERR_EXPENSE_LOG_LIMIT`. The deliberate looseness here reflects reality: agencies overspend and must be able to record it. What they cannot do is *recoup* it — only the cap is ever recoverable through the waterfall, which is why logging an expense also resynchronises the `SALES_EXPENSES` waterfall tier's ceiling on every open collection account of the picture to the lesser of `expenses_incurred_usd` and `expense_cap_usd`, rounded to two decimal places.

The picture's own sales lifecycle runs `DEVELOPMENT → PACKAGING → IN_MARKET`, with `SOLD_OUT` as a cyclic side-state and `WRAPPED` as the single terminal state. Both `HeadOfSales` and `PlatformAdmin` may drive every operator-reachable edge of this machine — the two roles share the picture's sales lifecycle outright, and no individual edge is reserved to one of them. `DEVELOPMENT → PACKAGING` requires that at least one sales agency agreement for the picture already be `EXECUTED` — the agency must be formally engaged before the picture may be packaged, and it is bare `EXECUTED` status that is asked for here, not the in-force term window. `PACKAGING → IN_MARKET` requires two things: the picture's `published_territory_count` must be at least 1, and the picture's `EXECUTED` agreement must satisfy the in-force window described above. Both transitions increment the picture's `version` by 1.

Going to market is a one-way door in the sense that the picture never returns to `PACKAGING`, but `IN_MARKET` and `SOLD_OUT` form a genuine cycle. `IN_MARKET → SOLD_OUT` is never requested by an operator: the system evaluates it at the end of every successful contract countersigning, and fires it when `published_territory_count` is at least 1 and `licensed_territory_count` equals `published_territory_count` — that is, when every territory the agency has published a valuation for has been licensed. The transition writes `sales_status` to `SOLD_OUT` and increments `version`. The reverse edge, `SOLD_OUT → IN_MARKET`, is likewise system-driven and fires whenever `licensed_territory_count` falls strictly below `published_territory_count`. Exactly two operations re-evaluate that edge: reverting an executed contract after its licence term, and the final-rejection cascade that follows a failed quality-control verdict at the revision limit, which drives an executed contract to `LAPSED`. The `lapse` operation of §3.9 is not a third — it is reachable only from `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`, where the contract holds no licensed territory, and it does not re-evaluate this edge at all, so a picture whose licensed count already sits below its published count for some unrelated reason (a valuation published for a further territory, for instance) stays `SOLD_OUT` until a reversion or a final rejection re-evaluates it. In each case the picture reopens for sale with no operator action at all, and `version` increments. Both this edge and `IN_MARKET → SOLD_OUT` are evaluated only after the request that triggered them has already refreshed the picture's row for its recomputed counters, so a request that fires either one moves the picture's `version` by two — once for the counters and once for the `sales_status` move.

Wrapping the picture is the terminal move, available to `HeadOfSales` and `PlatformAdmin` alike from either `IN_MARKET` or `SOLD_OUT`. Both variants of the transition carry identical preconditions and identical side effects. The preconditions: no pre-sale contract of the picture may sit in `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`, and every collection account of the picture must have a `balance_usd` of exactly 0 — undistributed cash cannot be abandoned. The side effects: every collection account of the picture that is still `OPEN` moves to `CLOSED`, the picture's `EXECUTED` sales agency agreement moves to `EXPIRED`, and the picture's `version` increments by 1. Separately, the wrap operation lapses every `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` contract of the picture as it goes; the precondition above means an operator will normally have cleared them already.

### 3.2. Market Attendance: Screening Rooms and Meeting Tables

Film markets are where pictures are sold, and the system models each one as a bookable venue with fixed inventory. A `PlatformAdmin` creates a market with its `name` (for instance a season identifier such as AFM 2026), its host `city`, a `start_date` and `end_date` bounding the market window where `end_date` must be strictly after `start_date`, and two inventory counts: `screening_room_count`, the number of bookable screening rooms, and `meeting_table_count`, the number of bookable meeting tables. Each must be at least 1, and rooms and tables are numbered consecutively from 1 up to their respective counts. A new market's `status` is `PLANNED`.

A market's lifecycle is linear: `PLANNED → OPEN → CLOSED`, both edges reserved to `PlatformAdmin`. Opening requires only that `end_date` be strictly after `start_date`. Closing has no precondition of its own but carries a conditional cascade: every screening slot of this market still in `HELD` moves to `CANCELLED`, and every buyer meeting still in `SCHEDULED` moves to `NO_SHOW`. Slots already `CONFIRMED` and meetings already `COMPLETED` are left untouched — the cascade reaches only children that had not yet resolved. Both market transitions increment the market's `version`.

Booking a screening room creates a screening slot, an operation open to `SalesAgent` and `HeadOfSales`. The payload names the `market_id` hosting the screening, the `film_id` being screened, the `room_number` within that market — an integer from 1 up to the market's `screening_room_count` — a `start_at` instant that is inclusive, an `end_at` instant that is exclusive and must be strictly after `start_at`, a `slot_type` of `BUYER_SCREENING`, `PRESS` or `MARKET_PREMIERE`, and a `capacity` of at least 1 seat. A new slot's `status` is `HELD`.

Three gates apply before a slot is created. The picture's `sales_status` must be either `PACKAGING` or `IN_MARKET` — a picture still in `DEVELOPMENT`, already `SOLD_OUT`, or `WRAPPED` cannot be screened — and failure yields `ERR_FILM_NOT_IN_MARKET`. The market must be `OPEN`, and failure yields `ERR_MARKET_NOT_OPEN`. And the requested window must lie entirely inside the market window: `start_at` at or after the market's `start_date`, and `end_at` at or before the market's `end_date`, with failure yielding `ERR_OUTSIDE_MARKET_WINDOW`.

The interesting rule is room occupancy, and it is the first of two interval rules in this system with deliberately different semantics. The occupied resource is the pair of market and room number. Occupancy considers every existing slot of that same market and room number whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`; a `CANCELLED` slot releases its room immediately and its window becomes re-bookable at once. Intervals are treated as half-open — a slot occupies its `start_at` but not its `end_at` — and, on top of that, a mandatory turnaround buffer of 30 minutes is applied on **both** sides of every existing occupation, because a room must be cleared and reset between screenings.

Concretely, the requested window conflicts with an existing occupation when the requested `start_at` falls strictly before that occupation's `end_at` extended forward by 30 minutes, **and** the occupation's `start_at` falls strictly before the requested `end_at` extended forward by 30 minutes. Any conflict is rejected with `ERR_SCREENING_ROOM_CONFLICT`. Because both comparisons are strict, a gap of exactly 30 minutes between two screenings is accepted, a gap of 29 minutes is rejected, and back-to-back screenings with no gap at all are rejected. Instants are compared at whole-second resolution and the buffer is added in whole minutes.

A held slot may be confirmed or cancelled by a `SalesAgent` or `HeadOfSales`. `HELD → CONFIRMED` requires the slot's market to be `OPEN`; if it is not, the confirmation is rejected with `ERR_MARKET_NOT_OPEN`. Confirmation increments the slot's `version`. Cancellation is reachable from either of the slot's two live states: both `HELD → CANCELLED` and `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` are legal, neither carries any precondition, and both have the same effect: the market-and-room interval is released so that a later slot may occupy it. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

Buyer meetings occupy the second bookable resource, and their geometry is deliberately different. A `SalesAgent` or `HeadOfSales` books a meeting by naming the `market_id`, the `buyer_id` being met, the `film_id` being pitched, the `table_number` within the market — an integer from 1 up to the market's `meeting_table_count` — and the inclusive `start_at` and exclusive `end_at` instants, again with `end_at` strictly after `start_at`. The service computes `duration_minutes` as the whole minutes between the two instants, flooring any partial minute. A new meeting's `status` is `SCHEDULED` and its `outcome` is null.

Meeting creation shares three gates with screening slots — the picture must be `PACKAGING` or `IN_MARKET` (`ERR_FILM_NOT_IN_MARKET`), the market must be `OPEN` (`ERR_MARKET_NOT_OPEN`), and the window must be contained in the market window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_MARKET_WINDOW`) — and adds one of its own: a buyer whose trading `status` is `BLOCKED` cannot be met, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_BUYER_BLOCKED`.

Table occupancy uses half-open intervals with **no buffer whatsoever**, so touching intervals are perfectly legal: a meeting ending at 10:00 and another starting at 10:00 coexist. The set considered live comprises every meeting of the same market whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`; only `CANCELLED` releases a table. Two overlap tests run against that set. The requested window must not overlap any live meeting at the same `table_number`, and it must not overlap any live meeting of the same `buyer_id` at this market regardless of which table that meeting occupies — a distributor cannot be in two rooms at once. Overlap means the requested `start_at` falls strictly before the existing `end_at` and the existing `start_at` falls strictly before the requested `end_at`. Either failure returns `ERR_MEETING_SLOT_CONFLICT`.

Beyond geometry, each buyer has an aggregate minutes budget at each market — a non-geometric dimension the room rule has no analogue for. The agency will give any single distributor at most four hours of table time per market. The booked total is the sum of `duration_minutes` across every meeting of the same market and the same buyer whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`. If that total plus the new meeting's duration exceeds 240 minutes the booking is rejected with `ERR_MEETING_MINUTES_CAP_EXCEEDED`; a total landing exactly on 240 minutes is accepted.

A scheduled meeting resolves in one of three directions, all driven by a `SalesAgent` or `HeadOfSales`, and all three are terminal. `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` requires an `outcome` in the payload, and that value must be one of `PASS`, `INTERESTED` or `OFFER_REQUESTED`; the supplied value is written to the meeting's `outcome` field, and the meeting's `duration_minutes` continues to count against the buyer's minutes budget. `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW` has no precondition, may also arrive as the market-closing cascade, and likewise keeps consuming the buyer's minutes budget — a distributor who fails to appear does not get those minutes back. `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` has no precondition either, but it is the only one of the three that releases anything: it frees the market-and-table interval **and** returns the meeting's `duration_minutes` to the buyer's budget. That asymmetry between `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` is intentional and is the practical lever the sales desk uses to reshuffle a market schedule. `outcome` is consumed by the `COMPLETED` edge alone: supplied alongside a `NO_SHOW` or a `CANCELLED` destination it is ignored, and the meeting's `outcome` stays null.

### 3.3. Territory Valuation: Comparables, Cast Tiers and Publication

The sellable unit in international sales is the territory. A `PlatformAdmin` creates each one with a `code` — a 2-to-3-letter uppercase identifier that must be unique across every territory in the system, with a duplicate rejected as `ERR_TERRITORY_CODE_TAKEN` — a human-readable `name`, a `region` grouping, a `market_tier` between 1 and 3 inclusive where 1 denotes a major territory, a `currency_code` naming the local settlement currency for information only — any non-empty string is accepted there, since no currency-code list is consulted — and a `base_value_index` between 0.10 and 2.00 inclusive. That index is the multiplier the valuation engine applies to the comparable median, and it encodes how much a given territory habitually pays relative to the genre norm. Territories are reference data: they carry `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Against each territory the agency accumulates **comparable titles** — datapoints recording what similar films actually earned there. A `PlatformAdmin` or `SalesAgent` records one with the comparable's `name`, its `genre`, its `cast_score` (0 to 100 inclusive), the `gross_license_fee_usd` actually paid for it in this territory, and its `release_year`. A `verified` flag may be supplied and defaults to false; it is set true only once the datapoint has been confirmed against a signed contract. The comparable's `territory_id` is taken from the creation path rather than the body. Like territories, comparable titles carry only `id` and `created_at`.

Verification matters because **only verified comparables enter a valuation**. When a `SalesAgent` or `HeadOfSales` values one territory for one film, supplying just three inputs — the `film_id`, the `territory_id` and the `method` — the service assembles the eligible comparable set by taking every comparable title whose `territory_id` equals the territory being valued, whose `genre` equals the film's `genre`, whose `verified` flag is true, and whose `release_year` is at or after the calendar year of the logical present instant minus seven. The seven-year lookback means that at the default clock reading of 1 August 2026 a comparable released in 2019 is eligible and one released in 2018 is not. Unverified comparables, comparables from another genre, and comparables from another territory are excluded even when they are the only datapoints available at all.

The size of that set is stored as `comp_sample_size`, and two rejections follow from it. An empty set is rejected with `ERR_NO_COMPARABLE_TITLES`. A non-empty set of fewer than three rows is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COMP_SAMPLE`. Three rows exactly is sufficient. The distinction between the two codes exists so that a sales agent can tell "we have no history here" apart from "we have thin history here", which are different commercial problems.

`comp_median_usd` is the median `gross_license_fee_usd` of the eligible set, computed on a sort by `gross_license_fee_usd` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. When the sample size is odd, the median is the fee of the single middle row of that sorted sequence. When it is even, the median is the average of the two middle rows' fees — their sum divided by two — rounded half-up to two decimal places.

The **cast tier multiplier** is a step function of the film's `principal_cast_score`, with each band inclusive on its lower bound: a score of 85 or above yields 1.40; a score from 70 up to but not including 85 yields 1.20; a score from 50 up to but not including 70 yields 1.00; and a score below 50 yields 0.80. The multiplier is an exact two-decimal constant, not a rounded computation.

Here the two valuation methods diverge, and they diverge only here. Under `COMPARABLE_WEIGHTED` — the pure market view the agency uses to argue a territory's floor to financiers — `cast_multiplier` is forced to exactly 1.00, the film's `principal_cast_score` is ignored entirely, and `estimate_usd` is the comparable median multiplied by the territory's `base_value_index`, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Under `CAST_ADJUSTED` — the packaging view used to argue the ask to buyers — `cast_multiplier` takes the value the cast tier function returns for the film's `principal_cast_score`, and `estimate_usd` is the comparable median multiplied by the territory's `base_value_index` and then by that cast multiplier, rounded half-up to two decimal places. On identical comparables and a `principal_cast_score` of 85 or more, the `CAST_ADJUSTED` estimate is exactly 1.40 times the `COMPARABLE_WEIGHTED` estimate.

Two prices are then derived from whichever estimate the chosen method produced. `ask_price_usd` is the estimate multiplied by an ask premium of 1.25, rounded half-up to two decimal places — the opening number the sales agent carries into negotiation. `floor_price_usd` is the estimate multiplied by a floor discount of 0.70, rounded half-up to two decimal places — the minimum guarantee floor that will be enforced when a contract for this territory is countersigned. All four money figures on the row are two-decimal, round-half-up quantities, and all of them are frozen onto the row at creation time. A valuation never re-samples its comparables: if the comparable set changes, the agency creates a new valuation.

A new valuation's `status` is `DRAFT`, its `published_by_user_id` is null and its `published_at` is null. `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` is reserved to `HeadOfSales` and is the moment a valuation becomes commercially binding. Three things are checked. The valuation's own `comp_sample_size` must still be at least three. The picture's sales agency agreement must be in force, else `ERR_AGENCY_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_FORCE`. And the request must carry `expected_film_version` matching the picture's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_FILM_VERSION` as described in §2.6.

Publication does four things. It sets `status` to `PUBLISHED`, writes `published_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and stamps `published_at` with the logical present instant. It moves any *other* valuation for the same film-and-territory pair that was `PUBLISHED` to `STALE` — publication supersedes, so at most one valuation per pair is ever `PUBLISHED`. It recomputes the picture's `published_territory_count`, which for a first publication in a territory increments it by one. And it increments the picture's `version` by 1 — which is precisely why the operation needs the stale-version guard, since two agents publishing for two different territories in sequence must each observe the other's version bump.

A published valuation has two further edges, neither operator-driven. `PUBLISHED → LICENSED` fires as a cascade when a pre-sale contract for the same film-and-territory pair reaches `negotiation_status` `EXECUTED`; the picture's `licensed_territory_count` is recomputed as a consequence. `LICENSED → PUBLISHED` is the rights-conservation edge: it fires when the contract for that pair leaves `EXECUTED`, whether by reversion after the licence term, by lapse, or by the final-rejection cascade following a failed quality-control verdict. On that edge the territory returns to the sellable pool, so `published_territory_count` is unchanged while `licensed_territory_count` drops by exactly one — which is what can push the picture back from `SOLD_OUT` to `IN_MARKET`.

`PUBLISHED → STALE` fires from either of two causes, and `STALE` is terminal — a stale valuation is never revived. The first cause is supersession: a newer valuation for the same film-and-territory pair reached `PUBLISHED`. The second is a cast-score amendment: the `principal_cast_score` submitted in an amendment to the picture differs from the stored value, which invalidates every cast multiplier already baked into a published estimate. Either way the picture's `published_territory_count` is recomputed. To value that territory again the agency creates a fresh valuation, which re-samples the comparable set from scratch, and publishes that.

### 3.4. Buyers, Credit Limits and Pre-Sale Negotiation

A buyer is a distributor licensing rights in exactly one territory, and a `PlatformAdmin` onboards it against that territory: the buyer's `territory_id` is taken from the creation path. The payload supplies the distributor's `company_name`, its `credit_rating` — a lender-facing grade of `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BBB` or `SPECULATIVE` that gates whether the buyer's contracts can be discounted by a bank — and an `exposure_limit_usd`, the maximum gross minimum-guarantee exposure the agency is willing to carry for this distributor. A trading `status` may optionally be supplied and defaults to `ACTIVE`. Both denormalised exposure figures, `outstanding_exposure_usd` and `net_unpaid_exposure_usd`, start at 0.00.

A buyer's `status`, `credit_rating` and `exposure_limit_usd` may each be amended later by a `PlatformAdmin`, all three being optional in the payload. Every successful amendment increments the buyer's `version` by 1 — which is exactly what the offer-creation stale-version guard in §2.6 observes, and which happens on every accepted call, a call resubmitting the stored values or supplying no mutable field at all included — and additionally recomputes `eligible_collateral_usd` and `advance_amount_usd` on every `APPROVED` or `DRAWN` banking facility whose picture has an executed contract with this buyer, since a downgrade or a suspension can remove that buyer's paper from a lender's collateral pool outright.

Buyer exposure is measured two different ways, and the difference is not cosmetic. The **gross pipeline** figure, stored as `outstanding_exposure_usd`, is the sum of `minimum_guarantee_usd` across every contract of this buyer whose `negotiation_status` is `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `EXECUTED`, rounded half-up to two decimal places once at the end rather than per contract. It counts the *full* minimum guarantee of every live contract, including offers the buyer has not yet accepted, and it ignores cash already received. The **net unpaid** figure, stored as `net_unpaid_exposure_usd`, is the sum of `amount_usd` across every payment milestone belonging to a contract of this buyer whose `negotiation_status` is exactly `EXECUTED` and whose own milestone `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `INVOICED`, again rounded once at the end to two decimal places. It counts only unbilled and unpaid milestone amounts of executed contracts, so received milestones and every non-executed contract drop out of it. On identical data the two figures differ whenever the buyer holds a live offer or has paid any milestone at all. Both are derived afresh from the buyer's current contract and milestone rows every time the buyer is serialised, so no separate recomputation step can leave them stale: an offer created, revised, accepted, executed, lapsed or reverted, a receipt posted or reversed, a milestone written off inside the final-rejection cascade of §3.6 — every one of those is visible in the next read of the buyer, and the figures are never client-settable.

Opening an offer is available to `SalesAgent` and `HeadOfSales`. The payload carries the `film_id`, the `buyer_id`, the `territory_id`, the proposed `minimum_guarantee_usd`, a `license_term_months` of at least 12 whole months, a `holdback_days` value between 0 and 720 inclusive, and the `expected_buyer_version` guard. The picture's `sales_status` must be `IN_MARKET` or `SOLD_OUT`, else `ERR_FILM_NOT_IN_MARKET` — note this is a different window from the one market bookings require, and note in particular that a `SOLD_OUT` picture still accepts new offers, since a territory may reopen. The picture's agency agreement must be in force. The buyer must not be `BLOCKED`, else `ERR_BUYER_BLOCKED`.

Two further gates guard offer creation. The territory-exclusivity gate rejects with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERRITORY_OFFER` when either of two conditions holds: some contract for the same film-and-territory pair already has `negotiation_status` `ACCEPTED` or `EXECUTED`, or some contract for that pair with `negotiation_status` `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` belongs to a *different* buyer. A live offer to the *same* buyer for the same pair is permitted, and is in fact the supersede case described below. The credit gate rejects with `ERR_BUYER_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED` when the buyer's gross pipeline exposure plus the payload's `minimum_guarantee_usd` would exceed the buyer's `exposure_limit_usd`; landing exactly on the limit passes. Both gates read the data as it stands *before* the supersede cascade of the next paragraph runs, so when a live offer to this same buyer for this same pair is about to be superseded, that earlier offer's own `minimum_guarantee_usd` is still inside the gross pipeline figure the credit gate measures — it is lapsed only once every check has passed, never in order to make room for the new offer.

A newly created contract sits at `negotiation_status` `OFFERED`, `delivery_status` `PENDING` and `collection_status` `UNPAID`, with `negotiation_round` at 1, `milestone_pct_total` at 0.0000, `counter_minimum_guarantee_usd` null and `offered_by_user_id` recorded from `X-User-ID`. Its `license_start_date`, `license_end_date` and `holdback_release_date` are all left null; those are stamped only at countersigning. Creation increments the buyer's `version` by 1 and recomputes the buyer's gross pipeline exposure. It also lapses any earlier contract for the same film, buyer and territory that was sitting in `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` — the supersede cascade, which is why the buyer's version moves and why the stale-version guard on this operation exists.

Three of the figures a contract reports are not stored columns at all. `milestone_pct_total`, `unpaid_amount_usd` and `received_amount_usd` are derived afresh from the contract's current payment-milestone rows every time the contract is serialised, exactly as the buyer's two exposure figures are: `milestone_pct_total` as the sum of every milestone's stored `pct_of_mg` rounded to four decimal places, `unpaid_amount_usd` as the sum of `amount_usd` over the milestones whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `INVOICED` rounded to two, and `received_amount_usd` as the sum of `received_amount_usd` over those whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, likewise rounded to two. A contract carrying no milestones therefore reports 0.0000, 0.00 and 0.00, and a contract that has never been countersigned already reports a non-zero `unpaid_amount_usd` the moment its schedule is built. Wherever a later paragraph says one of the three is written or recomputed at some event, it is naming the figure the next read will observe rather than a separate counter that event maintains, so no recomputation step can leave any of them stale and none of them is ever client-settable.

The three status axes on a contract are genuinely independent. `negotiation_status` tracks the commercial deal, `delivery_status` tracks the materials, and `collection_status` tracks the cash. A contract may be `EXECUTED` and `REJECTED` and `PARTIALLY_PAID` all at once; no axis constrains another's enumerated values, and each advances on its own triggers.

Negotiation proceeds as an alternation. `OFFERED → COUNTERED` is the buyer's move and is reserved to the `Buyer` role acting as the contract's own buyer. The payload must carry a `counter_minimum_guarantee_usd`, and may optionally carry a `counter_license_term_months` and a `counter_holdback_days`. The countered figure is stored in `counter_minimum_guarantee_usd` and the contract's own `minimum_guarantee_usd` is deliberately left untouched — the agency's number on the table does not change just because the buyer named a different one. `negotiation_round` increments by 1. The two optional counter figures are bounded exactly as their non-counter twins are — at least 12 whole months, and 0 to 720 days inclusive — but neither appears anywhere in the contract's response representation and neither is ever read again: the revised offer of the next paragraph takes its own `license_term_months` and `holdback_days` from its own payload, so `counter_minimum_guarantee_usd` is the only counter figure a client can read back. Countering touches the contract alone; it moves neither the buyer's nor the picture's `version`.

`COUNTERED → OFFERED` is the agency's answer, available to `SalesAgent` and `HeadOfSales`. The payload carries a new `minimum_guarantee_usd`, and optionally a new `license_term_months` and `holdback_days`. This one is a real overwrite: `minimum_guarantee_usd` is replaced, the two optional fields are replaced when supplied, `counter_minimum_guarantee_usd` is cleared back to null, and every payment milestone already attached to the contract has its `amount_usd` recomputed as its own `pct_of_mg` times the new minimum guarantee, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The contract's `unpaid_amount_usd` is recomputed from those refreshed milestones, the buyer's gross pipeline exposure is recomputed and the buyer's `version` increments, and `negotiation_round` increments by 1. The revised offer is also subject to the same credit gate as a fresh offer: it is rejected with `ERR_BUYER_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED` when the buyer's gross pipeline exposure plus the new `minimum_guarantee_usd` would exceed the buyer's `exposure_limit_usd`, equality passing. That exposure is read before the overwrite lands, so this very contract's standing `minimum_guarantee_usd` is still inside the sum: a revision is measured against the old number plus the new one, not against the difference between them. Unlike a fresh offer, a revision does not consult the buyer's trading `status` at all — a `WATCHLIST` or even a `BLOCKED` buyer may still be answered with a revised offer. And the milestone refresh writes only where the figure actually moves: a milestone whose recomputed `amount_usd` equals what was already stored keeps its `updated_at` and its `version` exactly as they were.

The alternation may repeat, but not indefinitely. Both countering and revising require that `negotiation_round` be strictly below 4 before they run; a fourth round is where the cycle stops, and an attempt to go past it is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`.

`OFFERED → ACCEPTED` is the buyer's other move, reserved to the `Buyer` role acting as the contract's own buyer, and takes no payload at all. The buyer's trading `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `WATCHLIST`; a `BLOCKED` buyer is rejected with `ERR_BUYER_BLOCKED`. Acceptance recomputes the picture's `total_mg_committed_usd` and `financing_gap_usd` and increments the picture's `version`, and recomputes the buyer's gross pipeline exposure and increments the buyer's `version` — because an accepted contract is a commitment, and the financing gap the production is trying to close must reflect it immediately.

Before a contract can be countersigned its payment schedule must be complete. A `SalesAgent` or `HeadOfSales` adds milestones one at a time, each carrying a `label` of `ON_SIGNATURE`, `ON_DELIVERY`, `ON_RELEASE` or `ON_TERM_END`, a `pct_of_mg` share strictly greater than 0 and at most 1, and a contractual `due_date`. The milestone's `contract_id` comes from the creation path. The service computes `amount_usd` as `pct_of_mg` times the contract's current `minimum_guarantee_usd`, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and sets `status` to `SCHEDULED`, `received_amount_usd` to 0.00, `accrued_commission_usd` to 0.00, and both `received_at` and `received_by_user_id` to null. The supplied `pct_of_mg` is added to the contract's `milestone_pct_total`, which is maintained to four decimal places. Adding a milestone is not gated on the contract's `negotiation_status`: the two schedule rules below are its only obstacles, so a schedule may equally be built against an `OFFERED`, a `COUNTERED`, an `ACCEPTED`, a `LAPSED` or a `REVERTED` contract, and only an `EXECUTED` one is closed to further additions — not by a state check but because its schedule necessarily already totals exactly 1.0000, leaving no room. Each accepted addition also refreshes the parent contract's `updated_at` and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

Two rules govern the schedule, both reporting `ERR_MILESTONE_SCHEDULE_INVALID`. While the schedule is being built, the contract's running `milestone_pct_total` plus the new `pct_of_mg` must not exceed 1.0000 — a partial schedule is perfectly acceptable mid-build — and no two milestones of the same contract may share the same `label`. At countersigning time the rule tightens: `milestone_pct_total` must equal exactly 1.0000, so a schedule that stops at 0.9000 blocks execution just as surely as one that would have overshot. No individual label is mandatory, though: any subset of the four whose shares reach exactly 1.0000 is a complete schedule, and a lifecycle step that would invoice or re-date a label the schedule happens to omit — the `ON_SIGNATURE` invoicing at countersigning, the `ON_DELIVERY` invoicing on a passing quality-control verdict, the `ON_RELEASE` re-dating on a cured delivery — simply does nothing, and the operation carrying it still succeeds.

### 3.5. Countersigning: Where Every Guard Converges

Countersigning is the single most heavily guarded operation in the system, and it is reserved to `HeadOfSales`. It takes no request body at all: everything it needs is already on the contract, the valuation, the buyer and the agreement. It advances `negotiation_status` from `ACCEPTED` to `EXECUTED`.

Six conditions are checked, and because they sit at different points on the precedence ladder of §2.7 their relative order is fixed. First the contract's own axis: `negotiation_status` must be exactly `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Second, segregation of duties: `X-User-ID` must differ from the `published_by_user_id` of the `PUBLISHED` valuation for this contract's film-and-territory pair, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Third, the agency agreement must be in force, else `ERR_AGENCY_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_FORCE`. Fourth, a valuation with `status` `PUBLISHED` must exist for that film-and-territory pair at all, else `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_VALUATION`. Fifth, the contract's `milestone_pct_total` must equal exactly 1.0000, else `ERR_MILESTONE_SCHEDULE_INVALID`. Sixth, the net credit gate: the buyer's net unpaid exposure plus this contract's `unpaid_amount_usd` must not exceed the buyer's `exposure_limit_usd` multiplied by a net execution headroom of 1.50, that product rounded half-up to two decimal places, else `ERR_BUYER_NET_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED`; landing exactly on the headroom passes. And last, the floor: the contract's `minimum_guarantee_usd` must be at or above the `floor_price_usd` of that `PUBLISHED` valuation, else `ERR_MG_BELOW_FLOOR`; a minimum guarantee equal to the floor is acceptable, one a single cent below it is not.

Note the deliberate asymmetry between the two credit gates. Opening an offer is measured on the gross pipeline against the raw `exposure_limit_usd`. Countersigning is measured on net unpaid milestones against 1.50 times that limit. The looser headroom at execution reflects that by then the deal is real and the buyer's paper is bankable; the tighter gate at offer time stops the desk from papering a distributor with speculative offers it could never all honour.

Execution's side effects are extensive, and every one of them is part of the contract. The contract's `executed_by_user_id` is recorded from `X-User-ID`. `license_start_date` is stamped with the logical present instant, and `license_end_date` is that instant plus `license_term_months` calendar months, using the same same-day-of-month rule with clamping to a shorter target month's last day. `holdback_release_date` is computed as the picture's `theatrical_release_date` — as it stands at execution time, not as it stood when the offer was made — plus `holdback_days` whole days. The contract's `unpaid_amount_usd` is computed as the sum of `amount_usd` across its `SCHEDULED` and `INVOICED` payment milestones, and `received_amount_usd` as the sum of `received_amount_usd` across its `RECEIVED` milestones.

The milestone carrying the `ON_SIGNATURE` label moves from `SCHEDULED` to `INVOICED`, freezing its `amount_usd` against the executing contract's `minimum_guarantee_usd` — from this point the milestone amounts no longer track a changing minimum guarantee, because the minimum guarantee no longer changes.

The `PUBLISHED` valuation for the film-and-territory pair moves to `LICENSED`, the picture's `licensed_territory_count` is recomputed from that change, and the picture may therefore auto-transition from `IN_MARKET` to `SOLD_OUT` when the licensed count reaches the published count and the published count is above zero. The picture's `total_mg_committed_usd` grows by this contract's `minimum_guarantee_usd`, its `financing_gap_usd` shrinks by the same amount, and its `version` increments. The buyer's `net_unpaid_exposure_usd` is recomputed and the buyer's `version` increments. And finally every banking facility of the picture whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED`, `DRAWN` or `MARGIN_CALL` has its `eligible_collateral_usd` and `advance_amount_usd` recomputed, because a newly executed contract is new collateral — a recomputation that can pull a facility back from `MARGIN_CALL` to `DRAWN`.

### 3.6. Delivery of Materials and the Final-Rejection Cascade

Delivering the picture is the agency's side of the bargain, and it is gated on time. A `DeliveryManager` submits a deliverable package against an executed contract, and three conditions must hold. The contract's `negotiation_status` must be `EXECUTED`. The logical present instant must be at or after the contract's `holdback_release_date` — the exact instant of holdback release is deliverable, and anything earlier is rejected with `ERR_HOLDBACK_NOT_ELAPSED`. And the package must be complete.

Completeness is a pure threshold on two counts the payload supplies. `technical_items_count` — masters, music-and-effects tracks, subtitle files, quality-control reports and the rest — must be at least 14. `legal_items_count` — chain of title, errors-and-omissions insurance, music cue sheets and the rest — must be at least 6. Exactly 14 technical and exactly 6 legal items is complete; one fewer of either is not, and the submission is rejected with `ERR_DELIVERABLES_INCOMPLETE`. Both counts must be non-negative integers.

A submitted package has `status` `SUBMITTED`, `qc_result` `PENDING`, `rejection_reason` null, `cure_deadline` null, `submitted_at` stamped with the logical present instant and `submitted_by_user_id` recorded from `X-User-ID`. Its `contract_id` comes from the creation path. Its `revision_round` is 1 when no prior package exists for this contract, and otherwise one more than the `revision_round` of the contract's latest package, selected as the last row by `created_at` ascending. Submission also moves the contract's `delivery_status` from `PENDING` to `SUBMITTED`.

The buyer then records a verdict. This operation is reserved to the `Buyer` role acting as the contract's own buyer, requires the package to be in `SUBMITTED` — the package's own status is the whole of the state gate here, and the owning contract's `negotiation_status` is not re-checked — and carries a `result` of either pass or fail. A rejection additionally requires a `rejection_reason`, drawn from `TECHNICAL_INCOMPLETE`, `LEGAL_INCOMPLETE`, `QC_FAIL` or `LATE_DELIVERY`.

**A passing verdict** sets the package's `qc_result` to `PASS` and moves it `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal. What it does to the contract's `delivery_status` depends on how many attempts it took. A package at `revision_round` 1 drives the contract's `delivery_status` to `ACCEPTED`. A package at `revision_round` 2 or above drives it to `CURED` instead. Both branches move the milestone labelled `ON_DELIVERY` from `SCHEDULED` to `INVOICED`. The `CURED` branch carries one extra side effect the `ACCEPTED` branch does not: the milestone labelled `ON_RELEASE` has its `due_date` pushed out by 30 days. That is the buyer's compensation for a botched first delivery, and it has a downstream consequence worth flagging — a later `ON_RELEASE` due date lengthens the discount tenor a lender must apply to that milestone, which reduces the advance the picture can raise against it.

**A failing verdict below the revision limit** sets `qc_result` to `FAIL`, writes the supplied `rejection_reason`, moves the package `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stamps its `cure_deadline` as 30 days after the package's `submitted_at`, and drives the contract's `delivery_status` to `REJECTED`.

A rejected package may be cured. A `DeliveryManager` resubmits, which requires that the contract's latest package be in `REJECTED` status and that the logical present instant be at or before that package's `cure_deadline`; a resubmission arriving after the cure window has closed is rejected with `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_ELAPSED`. Resubmission creates a *new* package at one higher `revision_round`, moving the contract's delivery axis `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` again. The completeness threshold and the holdback gate both apply again to the resubmission.

**A failing verdict at the revision limit is final.** The revision limit is 2, so a package at `revision_round` 2 that receives a failing verdict is written directly to `REJECTED_FINAL` rather than `REJECTED`, and `REJECTED_FINAL` is terminal. Everything the below-limit branch writes onto the package it writes here too — `qc_result` `FAIL`, the supplied `rejection_reason`, and a `cure_deadline` 30 days after the package's `submitted_at` — even though a `REJECTED_FINAL` package can never be cured; the deadline is stamped before the branch is chosen and is simply inert. This triggers the deepest cascade in the system, and every step of it is part of the specification.

The contract's `delivery_status` becomes `REJECTED`, and its `negotiation_status` moves `EXECUTED → LAPSED` — the deal is dead. Every payment milestone of that contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `INVOICED` moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`, and the contract's `unpaid_amount_usd` is recomputed from what remains; already-`RECEIVED` milestones are untouched, because cash actually collected is not clawed back. The contract's `collection_status` is set to `WRITTEN_OFF`. The `LICENSED` valuation for the film-and-territory pair returns to `PUBLISHED`, freeing the territory for resale. Because milestones were written off, every banking facility of the picture recomputes its `eligible_collateral_usd` and `advance_amount_usd`; any facility in `DRAWN` whose recomputed `advance_amount_usd` has fallen below its `drawn_amount_usd` moves `DRAWN → MARGIN_CALL`, and because a facility in `MARGIN_CALL` still counts as live, the picture's `BANK_DEBT` waterfall tier remains `ACTIVE` and keeps recouping. Finally the picture's `total_mg_committed_usd`, `licensed_territory_count` and `financing_gap_usd` are all recomputed and its `version` increments, and the picture may auto-transition `SOLD_OUT → IN_MARKET` because its licensed count now sits below its published count.

The chain, stated once as a whole: package to contract, contract to milestones, milestones to facility, facility to picture. All of it happens inside the single request that recorded the failing verdict.

### 3.7. Collecting Cash and Accruing Tiered Commission

Every dollar a buyer pays for a picture flows through one place: the picture's **collection account**. A `CollectionAgent` opens it against the picture — the account's `film_id` comes from the creation path — supplying the `bank_name` of the collection account manager and an external `account_reference` string. A new account has `status` `OPEN`, `balance_usd` 0.00, `total_receipts_usd` 0.00, `total_distributed_usd` 0.00 and `run_count` 0. Opening one is gated on nothing beyond the picture existing — the picture's `sales_status` is never consulted, so an account may be opened against a picture still in `DEVELOPMENT` or one already `WRAPPED`.

Those counters have exact meanings and together they carry the system's money-conservation invariant. `total_receipts_usd` is the cumulative gross cash booked in through payment-milestone receipts, net of any reversals. `total_distributed_usd` is the cumulative cash paid out through waterfall distribution runs. `balance_usd` is the first minus the second, rounded to two decimal places — the undistributed cash actually sitting in the account. `run_count` counts the distribution runs created against the account and supplies each run's `run_index`. At every observable moment, `total_receipts_usd` minus `total_distributed_usd` minus `balance_usd` is exactly zero.

A `CollectionAgent` may lock and unlock the account at will: `OPEN → LOCKED` and `LOCKED → OPEN` carry no preconditions. While the account is `LOCKED`, no receipt, no reversal and no distribution may touch it. The third edge, `OPEN → CLOSED`, is not an operator move at all — it fires as the cascade from wrapping the picture, requires the account's `balance_usd` to be exactly 0, and is terminal; every waterfall tier of a closed account retains its `recouped_usd` for reporting. Three operations require a collection account that exists and is `OPEN` for the relevant picture and report `ERR_COLLECTION_ACCOUNT_NOT_OPEN` when there is none: posting a receipt, reversing a receipt, and running a distribution. Nothing stops a `CollectionAgent` opening more than one account against the same picture, and where an operation needs *the* picture's account rather than the one its path names — posting or reversing a receipt, and the tier-ceiling resynchronisations of §3.1, §3.7 and §3.8 — it looks only at the picture's accounts that are still `OPEN`, taking the earliest-created of them where exactly one account has to carry the cash.

Posting a receipt is reserved to `CollectionAgent` and supplies two fields: the `amount_usd` being booked and the `received_at` instant at which it was booked. Four conditions apply. The milestone's `status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `INVOICED` — a milestone already `RECEIVED` or already `WRITTEN_OFF` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The owning contract's `negotiation_status` must be `EXECUTED`. The picture's collection account must be `OPEN`. And the posted `amount_usd` must equal the milestone's own `amount_usd` **exactly**: no partial payment, no overpayment, no rounding slack. Any discrepancy is rejected with `ERR_RECEIPT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

A successful receipt sets the milestone's `status` to `RECEIVED`, writes `received_amount_usd` to the posted amount, writes `received_at` from the payload and `received_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. The account's `total_receipts_usd` grows by the amount and `balance_usd` is recomputed. The picture's `total_mg_collected_usd` grows by the amount. The contract's `received_amount_usd` and `unpaid_amount_usd` are recomputed and its `collection_status` advances, and the buyer's exposure figures are recomputed. Four rows are refreshed outright by the request — the milestone, the account, the agreement carrying the accrual and the contract — so `updated_at` and `version` move on all four whether or not the contract's `collection_status` actually changes; the picture's own row and the buyer's own row are not touched at all, so neither `version` moves on a receipt. A reversal refreshes the same four rows on the same terms.

The contract's cash axis advances by arithmetic, not by declaration. `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` fires when, after the receipt, the contract's `received_amount_usd` is greater than zero **and** its `unpaid_amount_usd` is still greater than zero. `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` fires when, after the receipt, the contract's `unpaid_amount_usd` has reached exactly zero — that is, once every milestone of the contract has been received. The compensating direction is that same arithmetic run backwards: on a reversal the axis returns to `UNPAID` when the contract's `received_amount_usd` returns to exactly zero, and to `PARTIALLY_PAID` when cash remains received while some milestone remains unpaid. Because the axis is re-derived from the contract's current milestones after every receipt and every reversal rather than walked edge by edge — `WRITTEN_OFF` first and terminally, then `UNPAID` when nothing is received, then `PAID` when nothing is unpaid, otherwise `PARTIALLY_PAID` — a contract whose whole schedule is a single milestone moves `UNPAID → PAID` on one receipt and `PAID → UNPAID` on its reversal, never passing through `PARTIALLY_PAID` at all.

**Commission accrual is the calculation whose evaluation order matters most in this system**, because the commission rate depends on a running total that this very receipt is about to change. The agreement in play is the picture's sales agency agreement with `status` `EXECUTED`, of which there is exactly one by construction; its term window is not consulted here, and in the corner where the picture holds no `EXECUTED` agreement at the moment the cash arrives, the earliest-created agreement of the picture stands in for it and carries the accrual. The resolution is fixed in four steps and there is no other correct reading.

Step one: read the agreement's `gross_collected_usd` as it stands *before* this receipt is applied. Call that the prior total. It equals the sum of every previously received milestone amount across **all** contracts of the picture, net of any reversals.

Step two: pick exactly one rate from the prior total, and charge the *whole* receipt at that rate. When the prior total is below one million dollars, the rate is the agreement's `commission_pct` unchanged. When the prior total is at or above one million and below three million, the rate is `commission_pct` multiplied by 0.75, that product rounded half-up to 6 decimal places. When the prior total is at or above three million, the rate is `commission_pct` multiplied by 0.50, likewise rounded half-up to 6 decimal places. The volume discount rewards a picture that sells well: the agency's marginal take falls as gross receipts climb.

Step three: the commission this receipt accrues is the posted `amount_usd` multiplied by that single rate, rounded half-up to two decimal places. That figure is stored on the milestone as `accrued_commission_usd`, precisely so a later reversal can subtract the same number rather than re-deriving it.

Step four, and only now: the agreement's `gross_collected_usd` becomes the prior total plus the posted amount, rounded to two decimal places, and its `commission_earned_usd` becomes its previous value plus the accrued commission, rounded to two decimal places.

The alternative reading is explicitly forbidden. A receipt that straddles a breakpoint is **not** split across two rates. A receipt of 400,000.00 posted when the prior total is 900,000.00 is charged entirely at the first-band rate; it is not treated as 100,000.00 at the first band plus 300,000.00 at the second. Nor is the rate picked from the total *after* the receipt. Because the rate is a function of request arrival order and receipts are applied in the order they arrive, the band a given receipt lands in depends on the sequence of requests, not on milestone `due_date` — there is no batch re-sort anywhere.

Every receipt also resynchronises two waterfall tier ceilings inside the same request, on every collection account of the picture that is still `OPEN` and on every tier of the named type such an account holds. The `SALES_COMMISSION` tier's `cap_usd` is set to the agreement's new `commission_earned_usd`. The `SALES_EXPENSES` tier's `cap_usd` is set to the lesser of the agreement's `expenses_incurred_usd` and its `expense_cap_usd`, rounded to two decimal places — which is the mechanism that makes overspend beyond the cap unrecoupable no matter how much was logged. A tier already carrying the target figure is left exactly as it stands, `updated_at` and `version` included; only a tier whose ceiling actually moves is rewritten.

Reversing a receipt is the compensating action, again reserved to `CollectionAgent`, and it carries a single `reason` field. Two conditions apply: the milestone's `status` must be `RECEIVED`, and the account's `balance_usd` must be at least the milestone's `received_amount_usd`. The second condition exists because a reversal must not drive the balance negative — if the cash has already been distributed away through the waterfall, the reversal is rejected with `ERR_RECEIPT_ALREADY_DISTRIBUTED`.

Reversal restores every quantity the forward receipt touched, exactly. The milestone moves `RECEIVED → INVOICED`, its `received_amount_usd` returns to 0.00, and its `received_at` and `received_by_user_id` are cleared to null. The account's `total_receipts_usd` falls by the reversed amount and `balance_usd` is recomputed. The agreement's `gross_collected_usd` falls by the reversed amount, and its `commission_earned_usd` falls by exactly the milestone's stored `accrued_commission_usd` — not by a freshly recomputed figure, because the rate band in force at the time of the original receipt may no longer be the band a recomputation would pick. The milestone's `accrued_commission_usd` is then cleared to 0.00. The `SALES_COMMISSION` tier's `cap_usd` is resynchronised down to the restored `commission_earned_usd`. The picture's `total_mg_collected_usd` falls by the reversed amount. The contract's `received_amount_usd` and `unpaid_amount_usd` are recomputed and its `collection_status` is re-derived, moving `PARTIALLY_PAID → UNPAID` when no milestone of the contract remains `RECEIVED`. When all of that has run, `total_receipts_usd` minus `total_distributed_usd` minus `balance_usd` is still exactly zero and `commission_earned_usd` has returned to its pre-receipt value.

Two milestone states are reached only by cascade rather than by any operator action. `SCHEDULED → WRITTEN_OFF` and `INVOICED → WRITTEN_OFF` both fire when the owning contract leaves `EXECUTED` through the final-rejection cascade of §3.6, and each recomputes the contract's `unpaid_amount_usd`. `WRITTEN_OFF` is terminal: a written-off milestone is never received, never reversed and never revived.

### 3.8. Bank Financing: Discounting Pre-Sales, Bonds and Margin Calls

Pre-sale contracts are collateral, and a bank will lend against them at a discount. A `Lender` proposes a facility against a picture — the facility's `film_id` comes from the creation path — supplying the `lender_name` of the discounting bank, a `discount_rate_pct` strictly above 0 and at most 0.30 representing the annual discount rate applied over each contract's tenor, an `advance_rate_pct` strictly above 0 and at most 1 giving the share of each eligible unpaid milestone the bank will advance, and a `facility_cap_usd` that is a hard ceiling on the advance. A `completion_bond_required` flag may be supplied and defaults to true. A new facility has `status` `PROPOSED`, `completion_bond_posted` false, `bond_provider` and `bond_amount_usd` null, and `drawn_amount_usd` and `outstanding_debt_usd` both 0.00. Its `eligible_collateral_usd` and `advance_amount_usd` are not zeroed: the creation runs the discounting rule below as its last step, so a facility proposed against a picture that already carries eligible executed paper is created with both figures already non-zero, and they read 0.00 only when there is nothing eligible to discount. Because those two figures are written onto the row being minted rather than onto an existing one, a newly created facility's `version` is 1 like any other new row.

**Which paper is discountable** is a strict filter. A contract of the facility's picture is eligible only when its `negotiation_status` is exactly `EXECUTED`, its `delivery_status` is anything other than `REJECTED`, and its buyer is investment-grade and trading: `credit_rating` of `AAA`, `AA` or `A`, and buyer `status` exactly `ACTIVE`. A buyer rated `BBB` or `SPECULATIVE` contributes 0.00 even when its milestones are fully current, and so does a buyer on `WATCHLIST` or `BLOCKED`. Within an eligible contract, only milestones whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `INVOICED` count; `RECEIVED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` milestones drop out, since the first has already been paid and the second never will be.

**The discounting itself proceeds contract by contract**, in order of `created_at` ascending, and its rounding order is pinned. For each eligible contract, gather its unpaid milestones; if there are none, that contract's value is 0.00 and it contributes nothing. Otherwise sum those milestones' `amount_usd` and round to two decimal places. The tenor is the **latest** `due_date` among those unpaid milestones — not the earliest, and not a weighted average. The days to payment is the whole days between the logical present instant and that tenor date, floored at zero, so a milestone already past due contributes no discount rather than a negative one. The discount factor is one minus the product of the facility's `discount_rate_pct` and the days to payment divided by 365, that whole expression rounded half-up to 6 decimal places. The contract's value is then the rounded unpaid sum multiplied by the facility's `advance_rate_pct` and by the discount factor, rounded half-up to two decimal places.

Only after each contract's value has been rounded to cents are the values summed, and that sum — rounded once more to two decimal places — becomes `eligible_collateral_usd`. Summing at full precision and rounding once at the end is forbidden, because it shifts cents.

`advance_amount_usd` follows in two steps. First take the lesser of the facility's `facility_cap_usd` and its `eligible_collateral_usd`. Then, if `completion_bond_required` is true and `completion_bond_posted` is still false, halve that figure — the unbonded haircut is exactly 0.50 — and round to two decimal places. A bank that demands a completion guarantee and has not received one will advance only half of what the collateral would otherwise support. Separately, `outstanding_debt_usd` is not derived from the waterfall at all but maintained incrementally: it stays 0.00 until the facility is drawn, is set to the drawn amount at the draw, and is thereafter only ever reduced — never recomputed from scratch — by each distribution run's `BANK_DEBT` allocation as §3.9 describes, each reduction rounded to two decimal places and floored at zero. No recompute of the two collateral figures above touches it.

These two figures are recomputed on a fixed set of events: when the facility is created, when it is approved and when it is drawn — the `MARGIN_CALL → DEFAULTED` move recomputes nothing — when a completion bond is posted, when a pre-sale contract of the picture is countersigned, when the `lapse` operation of §3.9 is invoked against one, when one is reverted, inside the final-rejection cascade of §3.6, and when a buyer the picture holds executed paper with is amended as §3.4 describes. The other two ways a contract reaches `LAPSED` are deliberately not on the list: neither the supersede cascade that lapses an earlier offer when a new one is opened for the same film, buyer and territory, nor the wrap cascade that lapses the picture's live offers, recomputes any facility figure. Nothing else triggers a recompute either: posting or reversing a receipt, running a distribution and moving the logical clock all change what a recompute *would* produce while leaving the stored figures exactly as they were until one of the listed events arrives. And a recompute that leaves both figures where they already stood writes nothing at all — the facility's `updated_at` and `version` stay put unless one of the figures or the status actually moves — though the facility's own approval, drawing and bond-posting operations refresh the row in any case.

Posting the completion bond is a `Lender` operation carrying a `bond_provider` name and a `bond_amount_usd` face amount. It is legal on any facility that has not yet reached a terminal state — `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED`, `DRAWN` and `MARGIN_CALL` all accept it, while `REPAID` and `DEFAULTED` refuse it with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and a facility that already carries a bond simply has both bond fields overwritten by the later posting. It sets `completion_bond_posted` to true, writes both supplied fields, and recomputes `advance_amount_usd` without the unbonded haircut — typically doubling it. If the facility was sitting in `MARGIN_CALL` and the recomputed `advance_amount_usd` is now at least its `drawn_amount_usd`, the facility moves `MARGIN_CALL → DRAWN`, cured by the bond alone.

The facility lifecycle runs `PROPOSED → APPROVED → DRAWN`, with `MARGIN_CALL` as a reversible detour, and `REPAID` and `DEFAULTED` as the two terminal states. `PROPOSED → APPROVED` is a `Lender` move requiring two things: at least one contract of the picture must be `EXECUTED` with a buyer whose `credit_rating` is `AAA`, `AA` or `A` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and the facility's `eligible_collateral_usd` must be strictly greater than zero. Failure on either count is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COLLATERAL`, and both figures are recomputed *before* either condition is tested, so approval reads freshly discounted collateral rather than whatever the row happened to be carrying. Approval records `approved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, keeps those recomputed figures, and re-derives the picture's `BANK_DEBT` waterfall tier from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` — until a bank is actually on the hook, that tier absorbs nothing.

`APPROVED → DRAWN` is also a `Lender` move and carries three conditions. `X-User-ID` must differ from the facility's `approved_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the officer who approved may not draw. Either `completion_bond_required` must be false or `completion_bond_posted` must be true, else `ERR_COMPLETION_BOND_REQUIRED`. And `advance_amount_usd`, recomputed once the first two conditions have passed, must be strictly greater than zero, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COLLATERAL`. Drawing sets `drawn_amount_usd` to that recomputed `advance_amount_usd`, sets `outstanding_debt_usd` to the same figure, and sets the `cap_usd` of every `BANK_DEBT` tier on every still-`OPEN` collection account of the picture to the drawn amount, leaving any such tier that already carries that figure untouched. It writes nothing to the picture: `financing_gap_usd` measures committed minimum guarantees against the budget, so no facility event moves it and no facility event refreshes the picture's row.

`DRAWN → MARGIN_CALL` is system-driven, never requested. It fires from the final-rejection cascade of §3.6, from a contract lapse or from a contract reversion, whenever the recomputed `advance_amount_usd` has fallen strictly below `drawn_amount_usd` — the bank is now over-advanced against shrunken collateral. The picture may auto-transition `SOLD_OUT → IN_MARKET` in the same cascade, but the margin call itself moves no figure of the picture's. The reverse edge `MARGIN_CALL → DRAWN` is likewise system-driven, firing from a countersigning or a completion-bond posting whenever the recomputed `advance_amount_usd` is at least `drawn_amount_usd` again. The two directions are not evaluated by the same events: a countersigning or a bond posting can only cure a margin call, never cause one, while a lapse, a reversion and the final-rejection cascade can only cause one, never cure it — and the buyer amendment of §3.4 does neither, moving the two figures without ever moving the status.

`DRAWN → REPAID` is system-driven and fires from a distribution run at the moment that run's `BANK_DEBT` allocation brings the facility's `outstanding_debt_usd` to zero or below. It sets `outstanding_debt_usd` to exactly 0.00 and, like every other facility event, leaves the picture's own figures alone. `MARGIN_CALL → DEFAULTED` is the only terminal move a `Lender` requests directly, and it requires the facility to be in `MARGIN_CALL`; it recomputes nothing and leaves `outstanding_debt_usd` at the value it already held. Default deliberately leaves the `BANK_DEBT` tier's `pct_of_available` and its `cap_usd` untouched, and a `DEFAULTED` facility's `outstanding_debt_usd` still absorbs whatever that tier is allocated — but a defaulted facility is not one of the live statuses the tier's own `SUSPENDED` test of §3.9 looks for, so unless some other facility of the picture is still `APPROVED`, `DRAWN` or `MARGIN_CALL` the tier derives `SUSPENDED` and is allocated nothing at all.

### 3.9. The Distribution Waterfall, Reversion and Wrap

Cash sitting in a collection account belongs to nobody until the waterfall says so. A `CollectionAgent` defines the waterfall one tier at a time against the account — each tier's `account_id` comes from the creation path — supplying a `tier_order` of at least 1 giving the tier's position in the cascade, a `tier_type` naming the economic bucket, a `recipient_name` used as the payee label in reporting, a `pct_of_available` strictly above 0 and at most 1 giving the share of the still-unallocated cash this tier takes when it is reached, and optionally a `cap_usd` setting a lifetime ceiling on how much the tier may ever recoup. A tier created without a `cap_usd` is **uncapped**, and an uncapped tier is the sweep destination described below. A new tier has `recouped_usd` 0.00 and a `status` that is derived by the rule below rather than fixed at creation: `ACTIVE` in the ordinary case, but `SATISFIED` from the outset when the supplied `cap_usd` is 0.00 or less, and `SUSPENDED` from the outset for a `BANK_DEBT` tier while the picture has no facility in `APPROVED`, `DRAWN` or `MARGIN_CALL`. Tier creation is gated on the account's own status no more than on anything else: a `LOCKED` or even a `CLOSED` account still accepts new tiers.

Two uniqueness rules guard tier creation, both reporting `ERR_TIER_ORDER_TAKEN`: `tier_order` must be unique within the account, and an account may hold at most one uncapped tier, so a second tier submitted without a `cap_usd` is refused.

A tier's `status` is never client-set; it is derived on every read and after every allocation, and the three cases are evaluated in this order. A tier is `SATISFIED` when its `cap_usd` is not null and its `recouped_usd` has reached or passed that cap. A tier is `SUSPENDED` when its `tier_type` is `BANK_DEBT` and no banking facility of the account's picture is in `APPROVED`, `DRAWN` or `MARGIN_CALL` — there is no bank to pay, so the tier absorbs nothing and the cash flows past it to equity. Otherwise the tier is `ACTIVE`.

Two of the seven tier types have their ceilings written for them rather than by the operator. The `SALES_COMMISSION` tier's `cap_usd` is continuously resynchronised to the agency agreement's `commission_earned_usd`, and the `SALES_EXPENSES` tier's `cap_usd` to the lesser of `expenses_incurred_usd` and `expense_cap_usd`, as described in §3.1 and §3.7. The `BANK_DEBT` tier's `cap_usd` is set to the facility's `drawn_amount_usd` when the facility is drawn. The remaining four types — `COLLECTION_FEE`, `EQUITY_RECOUPMENT`, `DEFERMENT` and `PARTICIPATION` — keep whatever `cap_usd` the operator supplied, or stay uncapped.

**Running a distribution** is a `CollectionAgent` operation taking a single field, `amount_in_usd`, the cash offered to the waterfall on this run. The account must be `OPEN`, and the offered amount must not exceed the account's `balance_usd`; landing exactly on the balance passes, and exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ACCOUNT_BALANCE`. The offered amount carries no lower bound of its own: a run of exactly 0.00 — or even a negative figure, which no rule refuses — clears the balance test, allocates 0.00 to every tier and is recorded as a run whose `residual_usd` is the offered figure itself. The run produces an immutable `DistributionRun` record — one of the three resource types carrying only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` — whose `run_index` is the account's `run_count` plus one at creation time, making run indices 1-based and monotonic per account, and whose `run_by_user_id` is recorded from `X-User-ID`.

The allocation loop walks **every** tier of the account in `tier_order` ascending, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, carrying a running remaining figure that starts at the offered `amount_in_usd`. A tier whose derived `status` is not `ACTIVE` — that is, `SUSPENDED` or `SATISFIED` — is allocated exactly 0.00 and skipped, but it still appears in the run's `allocations` array. That is a contract, not an optimisation: the array length always equals the account's tier count, and every entry carries that tier's `tier_id`, its `tier_type`, its `recipient_name` and the `amount_usd` allocated to it, ordered by `tier_order` ascending.

For an `ACTIVE` tier the rounding order is pinned. First compute the tier's target as the current remaining figure multiplied by the tier's `pct_of_available`, **rounded half-up to two decimal places immediately**. If the tier has a `cap_usd`, compute its remaining room as `cap_usd` less `recouped_usd`, rounded to two decimal places, and reduce the target to the lesser of the two. Then reduce the target to the lesser of itself and the running remaining figure, so a tier can never take more cash than is left. Add the target to the tier's `recouped_usd`, rounded to two decimal places; subtract the target from the running remaining figure, rounded to two decimal places; and append the tier and its target to the allocations array. The forbidden alternative is to carry full precision through the loop and round only the final allocations, which shifts cents to later tiers and produces a different answer. A tier that ends the run with nothing allocated to it is not rewritten at all — its `updated_at` and `version` stay where they were — and a tier that takes cash both in the loop and again in the residual sweep below is refreshed exactly once for the run.

**The residual sweep** runs once, after the loop. If any cash remains, gather the account's tiers whose derived `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `cap_usd` is null. If that candidate set is non-empty, the sweep destination is the candidate with the highest `tier_order`, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; the entire remaining amount is added to that tier's `recouped_usd` and to its entry in the allocations array, and the remaining figure drops to 0.00. If no uncapped `ACTIVE` tier exists at all, the cash simply stays in the account.

The run's own figures follow. `amount_in_usd` is what was offered. `amount_allocated_usd` is the sum of every allocation's `amount_usd`, rounded to two decimal places — the cash that actually left the account. `residual_usd` is `amount_in_usd` less `amount_allocated_usd`, rounded to two decimal places — cash that stayed behind because no uncapped `ACTIVE` tier could absorb it. The account's `total_distributed_usd` grows by `amount_allocated_usd`, its `balance_usd` is recomputed as `total_receipts_usd` less `total_distributed_usd` rounded to two decimal places, and its `run_count` increments by one. The invariant holds throughout: the run's `amount_allocated_usd` equals the sum of its allocations, and the account's receipts less distributions less balance is exactly zero.

Two cross-entity effects fall out of a run, each driven by the total this run allocated to tiers of the type concerned and each skipped outright when that total is 0.00. Whatever was allocated to `SALES_EXPENSES` tiers is added to the `expenses_recouped_usd` of the agreement §3.7 names as carrying the picture's commission and expense figures, which with a single such tier is simply that tier's `recouped_usd` viewed from the agreement's side. Whatever was allocated to `BANK_DEBT` tiers is subtracted from the `outstanding_debt_usd` of **every** banking facility of the picture whose `status` is `DRAWN`, `MARGIN_CALL` or `DEFAULTED` — the same figure comes off each of them, and each is floored at 0.00 rather than allowed to go negative — and a facility that was `DRAWN` and whose debt has reached 0.00 moves `DRAWN → REPAID` with `outstanding_debt_usd` set to exactly 0.00, while a `MARGIN_CALL` or `DEFAULTED` facility keeps its status however far its debt has fallen. The picture's `financing_gap_usd` is untouched by either effect.

**Lapsing a contract** is a `HeadOfSales` operation carrying a single `reason` field — mandatory, and non-empty once trimmed, but retained nowhere: no field of the contract or of any other row records it — and it abandons a deal. From `OFFERED` or from `COUNTERED` it simply ends the negotiation; the buyer's `outstanding_exposure_usd` and `net_unpaid_exposure_usd` are recomputed and the buyer's `version` increments. From `ACCEPTED` it additionally recomputes the picture's `total_mg_committed_usd` and `financing_gap_usd` and increments the picture's `version`, because an accepted commitment is being withdrawn. Every lapse, from whichever of the three statuses, also recomputes the picture's live facilities as §3.8 describes and may therefore drive one of them `DRAWN → MARGIN_CALL`; and where the contract had previously been executed the matching `LICENSED` valuation returns to `PUBLISHED` — a case only the final-rejection cascade reaches, since the `lapse` operation itself refuses an `EXECUTED` contract. The same lapse also arrives as a cascade in two other situations: the supersede cascade when a later offer is opened for the same film, buyer and territory, and the final-rejection cascade of §3.6, which drives an `EXECUTED` contract to `LAPSED` outright.

**Reversion** is what happens when a licence simply runs out, and it is the clean ending the agency wants. A `HeadOfSales` reverts an executed contract with no payload at all, moving `negotiation_status` from `EXECUTED` to `REVERTED`. Three conditions apply. The contract's `negotiation_status` must be exactly `EXECUTED`. The logical present instant must be at or after the contract's `license_end_date` — the exact instant of expiry is reversible, and anything earlier is rejected with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_NOT_EXPIRED`. And the contract's `collection_status` must be `PAID`: the agency does not hand rights back to itself while the buyer still owes money, and anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The three are evaluated in exactly the order written, which makes this the one place in the system where an `ERR_INVALID_STATE` is reported *after* rather than before a temporal check — an executed contract that is both still inside its licence term and not yet `PAID` reports `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_NOT_EXPIRED`.

Reversion returns the matching `LICENSED` valuation to `PUBLISHED`, recomputes the picture's `licensed_territory_count`, moves the picture `SOLD_OUT → IN_MARKET` when the licensed count now sits below the published count, increments the picture's `version`, and recomputes the buyer's `net_unpaid_exposure_usd` along with every affected facility's `advance_amount_usd`. Residual rights return to the agency and the territory becomes sellable again — but collected cash is explicitly **not** clawed back. `REVERTED` and `LAPSED` are both terminal on the negotiation axis.

Wrapping the picture closes the circle. Once no contract of the picture remains in `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`, and once every collection account of the picture has a `balance_usd` of exactly zero — every dollar received having been distributed through the waterfall — a `HeadOfSales` or a `PlatformAdmin` moves the picture to `WRAPPED` from either `IN_MARKET` or `SOLD_OUT`. Each still-`OPEN` collection account moves to `CLOSED` with its tiers' `recouped_usd` figures preserved for reporting, the picture's `EXECUTED` sales agency agreement moves to `EXPIRED`, and the picture's `version` increments a final time. Nothing further can be sold, delivered, collected or distributed against that title.

The table below lists every error identifier the domain rules can return together with its HTTP status code.
