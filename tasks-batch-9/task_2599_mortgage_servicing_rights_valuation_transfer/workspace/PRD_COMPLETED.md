# Mortgage Servicing Rights Transfer API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A mortgage servicing rights (MSR) transaction desk sits between two balance sheets and one regulator. On one side a **seller servicer** wants to convert a book of servicing cash flows into cash; on the other an **acquiring servicer** wants to buy those cash flows at a price it can defend to its own auditors; between them stand agency and investor consent requirements, statutory borrower-notice deadlines, custodial funds that belong to neither party, and a rate environment that can revalue the whole asset overnight. Today that choreography lives in spreadsheets, e-mail attachments and a "bid tape" workbook that is stale the moment it is sent. The consequences are unpleasant and familiar: a package is awarded against a valuation that a rate move has already invalidated, escrow balances are swept twice because nobody recorded the first sweep, a borrower is noticed fourteen days before the transfer date instead of fifteen, and a holdback is released to the seller while complaint volume is still climbing.

This document specifies the API for a system that owns that choreography end to end. It models the valuation model itself (prepayment speed, discount rate, cost to service and escrow float, run either as a single deterministic projection or as a probability-weighted set of rate shocks), the stratified loan-level tape and the bid package built on top of it, the negotiation of price and holdback, the agency and investor approval prerequisites that gate transfer of servicing, the purchase agreement with its holdback and representation periods, the operational transfer itself across three independently advancing work streams, the post-transfer error-resolution pipeline, and the hedge the desk carries against the servicing asset's negative duration.

Two design commitments run through every section that follows. First, **market state propagates**: publishing a new rate curve is not a bookkeeping act — it stales the valuation runs bound to the superseded curve, suspends the auctions those runs anchor, and lapses the live bids in those auctions, in that order. Second, **money is conserved**: custodial escrow and suspense funds move between loans and transfers without ever changing in total, and a holdback is at every instant exactly the sum of what is still retained, what has been released to the seller and what has been consumed by borrower remediation. Implementations are judged on both, and on the discipline with which they refuse operations that would break either.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every business resource in this specification is mounted under the `/msr` prefix. One non-business endpoint, the logical-clock control described in §2.4, sits outside that prefix.

### 2.1. Desk Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request except the logical-clock control must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. A request with a missing or empty `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header holding nothing but whitespace counts as empty. A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent, is blank, is not one of the eight roles enumerated below, or is a valid role that is simply not authorized for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Role names are matched exactly and are case-sensitive, so `sellerservicer` is not a known role.

The eight roles are `ValuationAnalyst`, `TransactionManager`, `SellerServicer`, `BuyerServicer`, `AgencyApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `HedgeDesk` and `PlatformAdmin`. Their write surfaces are deliberately disjoint: no role may perform another role's exclusive operations, and `PlatformAdmin` is emphatically **not** a hierarchical override — it onboards and suspends counterparties and drives the logical clock, and it may read anything, but it cannot value a pool, publish a package, bid, award, decide an approval, attest notices or release a holdback.

- A `ValuationAnalyst` owns the model: assumption sets, valuation runs, cutting the loan-level tape, and per-line inclusion decisions.
- A `TransactionManager` runs the trade: publishing packages, countering bids, awarding, raising approval prerequisites, submitting for approval, settling, and completing the transfer of servicing.
- A `SellerServicer` builds the pool, adds loans, locks the tape and issues transferor borrower notices.
- A `BuyerServicer` bids, revises countered bids, executes the data / escrow / pipeline work streams and works post-transfer error cases through acknowledgement and resolution.
- An `AgencyApprover` records agency or investor consent decisions and any attached conditions.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` attests statutory notice compliance, intakes borrower notices of error, reopens resolved cases, and takes holdback release and clawback decisions.
- A `HedgeDesk` publishes rate curve snapshots and opens and closes hedge positions.
- A `PlatformAdmin` registers and suspends counterparties, may open a portfolio jointly with a `SellerServicer`, and drives the logical clock.

**How `X-User-ID` binds to data.** `SellerServicer` and `BuyerServicer` are entity-backed roles: when `X-User-Role` is `SellerServicer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Counterparty`, and ownership checks compare it to the `seller_id` of the `ServicingPortfolio` the operation touches. When `X-User-Role` is `BuyerServicer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Counterparty`, and ownership checks compare it to the `bidder_id` of the `Bid` being revised or to the `buyer_id` of the `PurchaseAgreement` behind the transfer or error case being worked, whichever the operation implies. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The remaining six roles — `ValuationAnalyst`, `TransactionManager`, `AgencyApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `HedgeDesk` and `PlatformAdmin` — carry an opaque identity: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to an entity's `id`. It is, however, recorded where the workflow needs an audit trail or a segregation-of-duties key: `run_by_user_id` on a valuation run, `drafted_by_user_id` on a purchase agreement, `decided_by_user_id` on an approval request, `notice_attested_by_user_id` on a servicing transfer, and `published_by_user_id` on a rate curve snapshot.

Three segregation-of-duties rules are enforced against those recorded identifiers; §2.9 states them precisely.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Like every other timestamp the server mints, it reads the logical clock of §2.4 rather than the host wall clock, so it does not advance between requests unless the clock is moved.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated with two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A value outside those bounds is a request-shape violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and so is a value that is not a base-ten integer at all — `limit=abc` and `limit=2.5` are both shape violations, while surrounding whitespace is tolerated. List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a section below states otherwise; because the logical clock only moves when it is driven, many rows share an identical `created_at`, and ties are broken by a single server-wide monotonic creation sequence, which is insertion order. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`).

Collection endpoints additionally accept optional filters, each named exactly after the field it filters and matched for equality against the field's serialised string value; unrecognised query parameters are ignored. A recognised filter carrying a value outside the field's enumeration is *not* a shape violation — it is simply matched for equality and returns an empty page. The one filter that does validate its value is the boolean `included` on the two tape-line collections, which accepts `true` or `false` in any letter case and refuses anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The counterparty collection filters on `role_type` and `status`. The portfolio collection filters on `seller_id`, `status`, `agency_type` and `valuation_freshness`. A portfolio's loan collection filters on `status` and `delinquency_bucket`. The valuation-run collection filters on `portfolio_id`, `assumption_set_id`, `rate_curve_snapshot_id`, `method` and `status`. The bid-package collection filters on `portfolio_id`, `valuation_run_id` and `status`. Tape-line collections — whether reached through a package or through a loan — filter on `included`, and the package-scoped listing also filters on `stratum_label`. The bid collection filters on `bid_package_id`, `bidder_id` and `status`. The purchase-agreement collection filters on `bid_package_id`, `winning_bid_id`, `buyer_id` and `status`. An agreement's approval-request collection filters on `status` and `approver_type`. The servicing-transfer collection filters on `purchase_agreement_id`, `status`, `notice_status`, `data_status`, `pipeline_status` and `readiness`. Borrower-notice collections, whether scoped to a transfer or to a loan, filter on `notice_type` and `compliance_status`, and the transfer-scoped listing also filters on `loan_id`. The error-case collection filters on `servicing_transfer_id`, `loan_id`, `status` and `category`. Hedge-position collections filter on `status` and `instrument`. Rate curve snapshots are listed newest first — ordered by `as_of_date` descending, then `created_at` descending — and filter on `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versions and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, generated by the server and never accepted from the client. Every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits. That form applies to every timestamp the *server* mints; the three instants a client supplies in a request body — a package's `marketing_start_at` and `bid_deadline_at` and a counter's `counter_expires_at` — are accepted in any ISO 8601 form the server can parse (a trailing `Z`, an explicit offset, or no zone at all, which is read as UTC) and are then stored and echoed back verbatim in exactly the spelling the client sent, never renormalised. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` (same format, refreshed on every mutation) and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on each accepted mutation of that row. Two entities are exceptions and are deliberately immutable once written: an `AssumptionSet` and a `BorrowerNotice` carry only `id` and `created_at` — for those two entities both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the representation entirely.

Rejected requests never mutate anything. If a validation refuses an operation, no field changes, no child row is created, no `version` increments and no `updated_at` moves — including for the entities the operation would have cascaded into.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never wall-clock time. The clock starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the server boots, and the server must not fall back to the host clock under any circumstance. `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` accepts a single field `to_iso8601` and moves the clock to that instant, answering HTTP 200 with the new value as `current_utc` — the only field in its `data`. `to_iso8601` may also be given as a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, which moves the clock to midnight at the start of that date. The clock is free to move backwards as well as forwards; no monotonicity is enforced. It is the one endpoint outside the access-control regime altogether: it performs neither the `X-User-ID` check nor the role check, so it succeeds with no headers at all and equally under any of the eight roles — `PlatformAdmin` is the intended caller by convention rather than by enforcement. It changes no entity state. Every deadline, statutory window, marketing window, expiry, eligibility date and `*_at` stamp described anywhere in this document is evaluated against this logical clock. Where a rule compares calendar dates rather than instants, only the date part of the instant participates and the time of day is discarded.

### 2.5. Money, Rates and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the API. Rates and percentages are decimal numbers: note rates, prepayment speeds, discount rates and float earnings rates are carried to at most 4 decimal places, and holdback, hedge-ratio and reserve-floor percentages to at most 2. Supplying more decimal places than a field permits is a shape violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a rule says otherwise, rounding is **half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero, so a magnitude of 0.5 becomes 1 and a magnitude of −0.5 becomes −1. Three deliberate exceptions to the default exist and each is normative:

- **Rounding order in the valuation model.** Intermediate projection arithmetic is carried in exact decimal. Each loan's value is rounded to whole cents first, and only then are the loans summed. Rounding the pool total instead of each loan yields a different number for a heterogeneous pool and is wrong.
- **Rounding order in the purchase price.** The purchase price is rounded to whole cents **per stratum**, and the strata are then summed in ascending order of their labels. Summing the strata and rounding once is a different number and is wrong. This is deliberately distinct from the way a bid's own value is computed — a single rounding over the whole tape — so the two figures can legitimately differ by up to one cent less than the number of strata.
- **The remediation cap is floored.** When a borrower-remediation charge is capped at a fraction of a release, the cap is truncated downward to whole cents, never rounded up, so the cap can never drift above its stated fraction.

Ratios and derived percentages have their own precisions: a complaint concentration ratio is rounded half up to 2 decimals, a portfolio's UPB-weighted note rate to 4 decimals, and a valuation expressed in basis points of unpaid balance to 2 decimals.

### 2.6. Version Guards on Pricing-Sensitive Writes

Three write operations create a child row whose economics are only meaningful against a specific observed state of its parent. Each of the three requires the caller to echo back the parent's `version` as a mandatory, non-optional request field — an integer of zero or more, a negative value being a shape violation rather than a 412 — and each rejects a mismatch with a 412. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all, and supplying one to them is a shape violation.

- Creating a valuation run for a portfolio requires `expected_portfolio_version`, carrying the `version` of the `ServicingPortfolio` as the client last observed it (from the tape-lock response or from a read of the portfolio). If it does not equal the portfolio's current `version`, the request fails with `ERR_STALE_PORTFOLIO_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no valuation run row is created. A successful run increments the portfolio's `version` by 1, which invalidates the token the caller just spent — a second run must first re-read the portfolio. The guard exists because a valuation prices a frozen tape: a request carrying a version captured before the tape composition or a prior run moved the pricing basis would silently value a different pool.
- Submitting a bid on a package requires `expected_bid_package_version`, carrying the `version` of the `BidPackage` as the client last observed it (from the publish response or from a read of the package). A mismatch fails with `ERR_STALE_BID_PACKAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no bid row is created. A successful submission increments the package's `version` by 1. A bid is priced against the published tape and reserve, so any intervening change to the package — a re-stratification, a suspension, an award, another bid — must force the bidder to re-read before it can be scored.
- Releasing holdback on an agreement requires `expected_purchase_agreement_version`, carrying the `version` of the `PurchaseAgreement` as the client last observed it (from the settle response, from a previous release, or from a read of the agreement). A mismatch fails with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no holdback field changes. A successful release increments the agreement's `version` by 1. Because releases are partial and repeatable, an identical release payload would otherwise be indistinguishable from a duplicate submission; the version guard is what makes each release a distinct decision taken against a known retained balance.

### 2.7. Nested Path Resolution

Paths are hierarchical, and path parameters are validated strictly left to right. When a request addresses a child through its parent, the parent segment is resolved first: if the parent id does not resolve to a live row, the response is that parent's not-found error, and the child id is not examined at all. Only once every ancestor resolves is the leaf id checked, and only once every id resolves does the request proceed to ownership, state and business checks. Each entity has its own not-found code — `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORTFOLIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSUMPTION_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_CURVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VALUATION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAPE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROVAL_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICING_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ERROR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_HEDGE_POSITION_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. The same catalogue applies to identifiers carried in a request body rather than the path: an unresolvable `seller_id`, `bidder_id`, `loan_id`, `winning_bid_id`, `assumption_set_id`, `valuation_run_id` or `portfolio_id` produces the matching not-found error for the entity it should have named.

### 2.8. Market-State Propagation: Curve Supersession and the Staleness Chain

The desk keeps exactly one rate curve snapshot in `ACTIVE` status at a time. When the hedge desk publishes a new snapshot, the incumbent is superseded, and that single act propagates four levels deep — this chain is the system's most consequential cross-aggregate behaviour and every link is mandatory.

The first link retires the incumbent: the previously `ACTIVE` snapshot moves to `SUPERSEDED`, `superseded_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, and its `version` increments by 1. The second link stales the valuations that were priced off it: every `ValuationRun` whose bound snapshot is the one just superseded and whose status is `COMPLETED` moves `COMPLETED → STALE`, has `staled_at` stamped, and has its `version` incremented by 1; where such a run is the one a portfolio currently resolves to, that portfolio's `valuation_freshness` recomputes to `STALE`. The third link suspends the auctions those valuations anchor: every `BidPackage` whose bound valuation run just became `STALE` and whose status is `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → BIDDING_SUSPENDED`, has `suspended_reason` set to `VALUATION_STALE`, and has its `version` incremented by 1. The fourth link kills the live offers in those auctions: every `Bid` on such a package whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED` or `REVISED` moves to `LAPSED` with its `version` incremented by 1; bids already `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `LAPSED` are untouched.

Alongside — but explicitly *not* part of that chain — publishing a snapshot re-marks the hedge book: every `HedgePosition` whose status is `OPEN` has `mark_to_market_cents` recomputed against the new `ACTIVE` snapshot. Positions already `CLOSED` keep their frozen realised result and are never re-marked by a later curve.

Every entity a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1 — a cascade is not a silent write. Cascades are also idempotent with respect to rows that are already in the target state: a row that does not match the stated source status is skipped, never re-stamped.

### 2.9. Completion and Termination Propagation

Two further cascades run in the opposite direction — outward from a decision on a child to the parent aggregates.

**Completion.** Completing a servicing transfer stamps `completed_at` and sets `transferred_loan_count`, then flips every loan carried on the awarded tape from `ACTIVE` to `TRANSFERRED` with `transferred_at` stamped. Once no loan on the owning portfolio remains `ACTIVE`, the portfolio moves `UNDER_CONTRACT → TRANSFERRED`; at that point its `loan_count` and `total_upb_cents` recompute to 0 and its `weighted_avg_note_rate_pct` recomputes to 0.0000, because all three aggregates only count loans that are still `ACTIVE`. Reaching `TRANSFERRED` in turn closes the hedge: every `HedgePosition` on that portfolio still `OPEN` moves to `CLOSED` with `closed_at`, `close_base_rate_bps` and `realized_pnl_cents` stamped exactly as a manual close would stamp them. Unlike the manual close, this one carries no active-curve precondition — the transfer completes regardless — so where no snapshot is `ACTIVE` the cascade closes each position against the position's own `entry_base_rate_bps`, which drives `close_base_rate_bps` to that entry rate and both `mark_to_market_cents` and `realized_pnl_cents` to 0. This chain is **conditional at its second link**: a loan that was excluded from the tape stays `ACTIVE`, and while any loan on the portfolio is `ACTIVE` the portfolio does not advance and the hedge positions are not closed. Tape exclusion is a fact about the tape line, never a loan state — a loan has exactly two states.

**Termination.** A denied consent decision terminates the trade and unwinds the marketing. The agreement moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `APPROVAL_DENIED`; the awarded bid package moves to `CANCELLED`; the bid that had been `ACCEPTED` on that package moves to `REJECTED`, as does any bid on it still sitting in `SUBMITTED`; and the owning portfolio moves `UNDER_CONTRACT → TAPE_LOCKED` provided no agreement anywhere on that portfolio — that is, on any of its bid packages — is left in a status other than `TERMINATED` once this one has been terminated, and provided the portfolio is in fact still `UNDER_CONTRACT`, putting the pool back on the shelf where it can be re-marketed under a fresh package. A package is only cancelled this way while it is still the marketing vehicle — if any agreement on it has reached `SETTLED`, `HOLDBACK_RELEASED` or `CLOSED`, the package is left alone. A package sitting in `PUBLISHED` or `BIDDING_SUSPENDED` when the termination lands is cancelled on the same terms.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three pairs of steps may never be performed by the same operator identity, and each violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

1. **Consent decisions on one agreement.** When an `AgencyApprover` records a decision, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `decided_by_user_id` of *every* approval request on the same agreement that has already been decided. One approver cannot supply two of the consents an agreement needs.
2. **Attestation versus release.** When a `ComplianceOfficer` releases holdback, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `notice_attested_by_user_id` recorded on the bound servicing transfer. The officer who certified that borrowers were noticed on time cannot be the officer who signs the money back to the seller.
3. **Valuation versus award.** When a `TransactionManager` awards a package, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `run_by_user_id` of the valuation run the package is bound to. Nobody awards a trade against their own mark.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities

Two arithmetic identities must hold at every observable instant, and any implementation that lets either drift is incorrect regardless of which endpoints appear to work.

**Custodial funds.** Borrower escrow and unapplied suspense funds belong to borrowers, not to either counterparty, so moving them between a loan and a transfer must never change their total. A portfolio's `custodial_funds_total_cents` is defined as the sum of `escrow_balance_cents` plus `suspense_balance_cents` over the loans in that portfolio — *every* loan, whatever its `status`, which is what distinguishes this aggregate from the three that count only `ACTIVE` loans — plus the sum of `escrow_transferred_cents` plus `suspense_transferred_cents` over the servicing transfers reachable from that portfolio through its bid packages and their purchase agreements. It is derived afresh on every read of the portfolio rather than stored, which is what makes the identity hold at every observable instant. Sweeping custodial funds into a transfer and rolling that sweep back must both leave this figure exactly where it was.

**The holdback identity.** From the moment an agreement is drafted, `holdback_cents` equals `holdback_retained_cents` plus `holdback_released_cents` plus `holdback_applied_cents` — always. Every release, every remediation charge and every clawback is a redistribution among those three buckets, never a creation or destruction of value. When the holdback is re-sized upward on the conditional-approval branch, `holdback_cents` and `holdback_retained_cents` move together so the identity survives the re-sizing.

### 2.12. Refusal Ordering and Discriminated Errors

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is reported, and which one is not a matter of taste. Checks are evaluated in a fixed order and the first violated check wins:

1. `X-User-ID` present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` present, a known role, and authorized for this endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Request shape: body parses, required fields present, every value satisfying its declared type, enum, range and decimal-place constraints, and no attempt to write a system-maintained or computed field — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Concretely: an absent or whitespace-only body, and a body that is literally `null`, are all read as the empty object `{}`; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is a shape violation; every request key not in the operation's own accepted set is rejected, so an unrecognised body field is refused even though an unrecognised *query* parameter is ignored; a field supplied as explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, meaning `null` on a required field is the missing-field violation and `null` on an optional field takes that field's documented default; and integer-typed fields reject any JSON number carrying a fractional part, so `100.0` is refused where `100` is accepted. Where a field caps decimal places, the count is taken from the number's shortest decimal rendering, so trailing zeros never breach the cap. Endpoints that accept no body at all reject a non-empty one.
4. Every id in the URL path resolves to a live row — the matching not-found error.
5. Every id in the request body resolves to a live row — the matching not-found error.
6. Ownership for the entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
7. Counterparty side-of-trade integrity — `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`.
8. through 17. **State checks**, in this relative order: portfolio still `DRAFT` for adding loans (`ERR_TAPE_LOCKED`); tape locked for valuation, package creation and stratification (`ERR_TAPE_NOT_LOCKED`); bid package in the required state (`ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE`); package accepting bids (`ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACCEPTING_BIDS`); bid in an actionable state (`ERR_BID_INVALID_STATE`); agreement in the required state (`ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`); transfer in the required state and its dimension steps in order (`ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`); error case in the required state (`ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`); approval request not already decided (`ERR_APPROVAL_ALREADY_DECIDED`); hedge position still open (`ERR_HEDGE_ALREADY_CLOSED`).
18. The parent version guard of §2.6 — the matching 412.
19. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
20. onwards. **Business checks**: valuation freshness for publication, bidder eligibility, bidder risk, pending approvals, condition waiver, transfer-date lead time, notice sender role, notice effective date, tape membership, notice completeness, notice lateness, escrow-mapping prerequisite, transfer readiness, marketing-window collision, onboarding-window collision, duplicate approval request, duplicate loan number, curve date advancement, active-curve availability, bid deadline, negotiation-round ceiling, reopen window, reopen ceiling, holdback eligibility, bid-versus-reserve, empty tape, complaint tier, holdback exhaustion, and clawback bounds — each with the error identified in §3 where the rule is described. Within this band the relative order is as listed: in particular bidder eligibility outranks the bid deadline, so an ineligible bidder is told about its eligibility even when the deadline has also passed.

Two families of exception to the band ordering above are themselves normative. The first concerns the two negotiation actions: countering and revising a bid both test the *bid's* own state before the package's, so countering an `ACCEPTED` bid on a `CANCELLED` package reports `ERR_BID_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACCEPTING_BIDS`, inverting bands 11 and 12 for those two endpoints only. The second concerns two cross-field shape rules whose evaluation needs an entity the request has not yet resolved at band 3, and which therefore report their `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` from inside the business band instead: an approval request's `agency_type` matching the selling portfolio's, and settlement's rule that a waiver reference be absent unless the agreement is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, are both checked after the agreement's own state check.

Three consequences of this ordering are load-bearing. First, **state always beats economics**: a bid filed on a package that is not `PUBLISHED` reports `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACCEPTING_BIDS` even when the bid also sits below the reserve, so a refusal never leaks pricing information about a package that is not open. Second, **the version guard sits between state and business**: a stale version supplied on an action that would have been refused for state reasons anyway yields the state error, not the 412. Third, three state checks are deliberately permissive so that a more specific reason can be reported by a later, more informative check — settling an agreement that is still `PENDING_APPROVALS` passes the agreement state check and is refused with `ERR_AGENCY_APPROVAL_PENDING`; reconciling before the custodial sweep passes the transfer state check and is refused with `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_MAPPED`; and reopening an already `ESCALATED` case passes the case state check and is refused with `ERR_MAX_REOPENS_EXCEEDED`. Outside those three carve-outs the state checks refuse as stated: `DRAFTED`, `SETTLED`, `HOLDBACK_RELEASED`, `CLOSED` and `TERMINATED` agreements are refused for settle with `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and acknowledging or resolving an `ESCALATED` case is refused with `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows a trade from counterparty onboarding through to holdback closure, with the hedge book treated last because it runs alongside the whole timeline rather than inside it.

### 3.1. Onboarding Counterparties and Reading Transfer Risk

Before anything can be bought or sold, both institutions must exist on the desk. A `PlatformAdmin` registers each one with its `legal_name` (a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters), the side of the trade it is allowed to act on as `role_type` — either `SELLER` or `BUYER`, never both — its audited tangible net worth as `net_worth_cents` (zero or more), and its agency servicer rating as `servicer_rating`, one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`. The optional `agency_approvals` field lists the agency seller/servicer approvals the institution currently holds, drawn without repetition from `FNMA`, `FHLMC` and `GNMA`; it defaults to an empty list. Registration puts the counterparty in `ACTIVE` `status` with `suspended_at` null and `open_transfer_count` at 0. That last figure is derived, not stored input: it counts the servicing transfers whose purchase agreement names this counterparty as its buyer and whose overall status is `IN_PROGRESS`.

A counterparty that fails its own covenants can be suspended by a `PlatformAdmin`, moving `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, stamping `suspended_at` from the logical clock and incrementing `version` by 1. The operation carries no state precondition of its own: suspending a counterparty that is already `SUSPENDED` succeeds again, re-stamping `suspended_at` from the current clock and incrementing `version` once more. Suspension is forward-looking: bids already on the table are left exactly as they are, so nothing is retroactively voided. What suspension does is close two gates — a `SUSPENDED` counterparty may no longer be named as a bidder, and it may no longer be carried through to award. Both refusals are `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422), and the same code covers the second half of the eligibility test: a bidder must hold the agency approval matching the `agency_type` of the portfolio being sold, so a bidder with only `FNMA` approval cannot bid on a `GNMA` pool.

**Transfer risk scoring.** The desk scores each counterparty out of 100 on a computed read that takes an optional `variant` query parameter — one of exactly `PRE_AWARD` and `POST_TRANSFER`, any other value being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` as a shape violation before the counterparty id in the path is even resolved — and answers with `variant`, `score`, `tier` and the five penalty components `rating_penalty`, `capital_penalty`, `approval_penalty`, `complaint_penalty` and `sla_penalty`. Scoring starts from 100 and subtracts three balance-sheet penalties. The rating penalty is 0 for `TIER_1`, 10 for `TIER_2` and 25 for `TIER_3`. The capital penalty is 0 when tangible net worth is at least ten million dollars (1,000,000,000 cents), 15 when it is at least five million dollars (500,000,000 cents) but below ten million, and 30 below five million. The approval penalty charges 8 points for each agency approval the institution is short of two: it is the number of approvals it holds subtracted from two, floored at zero so holding three approvals earns no credit, multiplied by 8. The base score is 100 less those three penalties, clamped into the range 0 to 100 inclusive.

Under the `PRE_AWARD` variant — which is what the read answers when `variant` is omitted, and what the bid gate always uses — the score *is* the base score, and both `complaint_penalty` and `sla_penalty` report 0, because no post-transfer performance history exists at bid time. Under the `POST_TRANSFER` variant, two service-quality penalties are added on top. The first looks at complaint concentration: take the highest complaint concentration ratio (computed exactly as in §3.8, step one) across every servicing transfer of this counterparty whose status is `COMPLETED`, using 0.00 when the counterparty has none, multiply it by 3, round half up to a whole number, and cap the result at 30. The second looks at statutory service levels: count the error resolution cases on those transfers whose `ack_breached` is true and the cases whose `resolution_breached` is true, charge 5 points per acknowledgement breach and 10 points per resolution breach, and cap the sum at 20. The `POST_TRANSFER` score is the base score less both penalties, again clamped into 0 to 100. Because the two variants read different entity sets — one only the counterparty row, the other also its completed transfers and their error cases — the same institution scores differently the day after it closes a troubled transfer.

The reported `tier` is a band on the score: at least 80 is `LOW`, at least 60 is `MEDIUM`, at least 40 is `ELEVATED`, and anything below 40 is `HIGH`. That last band is also a hard gate: a bid whose named bidder scores below 40 under `PRE_AWARD` is refused with `ERR_BIDDER_RISK_TIER_TOO_HIGH` (HTTP 422).

### 3.2. Assembling the Pool and Freezing the Tape

A `SellerServicer` (or a `PlatformAdmin` acting for one) opens a `ServicingPortfolio` by naming the selling institution through `seller_id`, giving the pool a desk label as `portfolio_name` (non-blank, 1 to 120 characters) and declaring the `agency_type` whose transfer-of-servicing consent will govern the sale — `FNMA`, `FHLMC` or `GNMA`. The named counterparty must have `role_type` `SELLER`; naming a buyer as a seller, or a seller as a bidder, is refused with `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The ownership check that binds `X-User-ID` to `seller_id` applies only when the caller acts as `SellerServicer`; a `PlatformAdmin`, carrying an opaque identity, may open a portfolio naming any `SELLER`. A new portfolio starts in `DRAFT` with `loan_count`, `total_upb_cents` and `custodial_funds_total_cents` at 0, `weighted_avg_note_rate_pct` at 0.0000, `valuation_freshness` at `NONE` and `tape_locked_at` null. Its lifecycle runs `DRAFT` → `TAPE_LOCKED` → `UNDER_CONTRACT` → `TRANSFERRED`, with a revert edge from `UNDER_CONTRACT` back to `TAPE_LOCKED` when a trade dies.

Loans are added one at a time by the owning seller, and only while the portfolio is still `DRAFT`; an attempt on a portfolio that has moved on is refused with `ERR_TAPE_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). Each loan carries a `loan_number` (non-blank, 1 to 32 characters, unique within its portfolio — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOAN_NUMBER`, HTTP 409), an unpaid principal balance `upb_cents` of at least 1, a `note_rate_pct` greater than 0 and at most 25 expressed in percent with at most 4 decimals, and a contractual `servicing_fee_bps` strip between 1 and 100 basis points of unpaid balance per annum. Custodial balances are optional and default to 0: `escrow_balance_cents` holds the borrower's tax-and-insurance escrow and `suspense_balance_cents` holds unapplied funds, both zero or more. Two stratum-bearing attributes are also optional: `delinquency_bucket` defaults to `CURRENT` and may instead be `DQ_30_59`, `DQ_60_89`, `DQ_90_PLUS` or `FORECLOSURE`, and `loss_mit_status` defaults to `NONE` and may instead be `TRIAL_MOD`, `PERMANENT_MOD` or `FORBEARANCE`. The `state_code` of the mortgaged property is required and is exactly two uppercase letters. The loan's own `status` starts at `ACTIVE`; it has exactly one other value, `TRANSFERRED`, reached only by the completion cascade, which also stamps `transferred_at`. Two further loan fields stay null until the custodial sweep of §3.7 fills them: `escrow_balance_at_mapping_cents` and `suspense_balance_at_mapping_cents`.

Adding a loan recomputes four portfolio aggregates and increments the portfolio's `version` by 1. `loan_count` is the number of loans in the portfolio whose status is `ACTIVE`; `total_upb_cents` is the sum of their `upb_cents`; `custodial_funds_total_cents` follows the definition in §2.11; and `weighted_avg_note_rate_pct` is the unpaid-balance-weighted note rate — for the `ACTIVE` loans, the sum of each loan's `upb_cents` multiplied by its `note_rate_pct`, divided by the sum of their `upb_cents`, rounded half up to 4 decimals, and reported as 0.0000 whenever `loan_count` is 0.

When the pool is complete the seller freezes it. Locking the tape requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the portfolio's `seller_id`, requires the portfolio to still be `DRAFT` — a second lock is refused with `ERR_TAPE_LOCKED` — and requires the pool to hold at least one `ACTIVE` loan, an empty pool being refused with `ERR_TAPE_EMPTY` (HTTP 422), the same code publication uses for an emptied tape; it moves the portfolio `DRAFT → TAPE_LOCKED`, stamps `tape_locked_at` from the logical clock, recomputes all four aggregates and captures two of them as **the pricing basis** for everything downstream, and increments `version` by 1. The freeze is internal and not a second set of readable fields: the four aggregates on the portfolio representation stay derived live from the loan rows on every read, and what lock captures privately is the total unpaid balance that later serves as the reserve-price denominator (§3.4) and the custodial total that the reconciliation check of §3.7 compares against. Because the pool composition can no longer change, those captured figures normally equal the live ones; they diverge only where a later cascade moves the live loan rows, as completion does. The response to a lock is also the natural source of the version token the valuation step of §3.3 must echo back. From here on the composition of the pool cannot change, which is what allows a reserve price stamped days later to still mean something.

### 3.3. Assumptions, Rate Curves and the Fair-Value Model

Valuation has three inputs: an assumption vector, a market curve, and the frozen tape.

A `ValuationAnalyst` writes the assumption vector once and never edits it. An `AssumptionSet` carries `assumption_name` (non-blank, 1 to 80 characters, for example "Desk Base Case Aug-2026"), the annual constant prepayment rate `cpr_pct` in percent (greater than 0 and strictly less than 100, at most 4 decimals), the nominal annual `discount_rate_pct` in percent (greater than 0 and at most 40, at most 4 decimals), the base direct annual cost of servicing one performing loan as `cost_to_service_annual_cents` (zero or more), the annual `float_earnings_rate_pct` credited on escrow float (zero or more and at most 40, at most 4 decimals), and `projection_years`, the number of whole projection years in the cash-flow model, at least 1 and at most 10, which the server records as 3 when the caller does not supply it. An assumption set is immutable: it exposes `id` and `created_at` and nothing else in the way of bookkeeping — no `updated_at`, no `version`.

The `HedgeDesk` owns market state. Publishing a `RateCurveSnapshot` supplies the market date `as_of_date` as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the benchmark par mortgage rate `base_rate_bps` (at least 100 and at most 8000 basis points), and `primary_secondary_spread_bps` (0 to 200, recorded as 25 when not supplied), which exists purely for disclosure and enters no formula anywhere in this system. The new snapshot must advance the market clock: its `as_of_date` must be strictly greater than the `as_of_date` of the snapshot currently `ACTIVE`, and a date equal to or earlier than the incumbent's is refused with `ERR_RATE_CURVE_DATE_NOT_ADVANCING` (HTTP 422). On acceptance the snapshot is created `ACTIVE` with `superseded_at` null and `published_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and the four-link propagation of §2.8 fires against the outgoing incumbent.

Any operation that needs a market rate resolves it the same way: among snapshots whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, rank by `as_of_date` descending and break ties by `created_at` descending, and take the single highest-ranked row. Row identifiers are never a tie-break; where `created_at` is itself tied — as it is whenever the logical clock has not moved between two publications — the later-inserted row ranks higher, exactly as the general tie-break of §2.3 does, read in reverse. Because publishing always supersedes the incumbent, at most one snapshot is `ACTIVE` at any instant and the tie-break is a safety net rather than a routine path. When no snapshot is `ACTIVE` at all, the operations that need one — running a valuation, opening a hedge, and closing a hedge — fail with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATE_CURVE` (HTTP 409).

**Running a valuation.** A `ValuationAnalyst` runs the model against a locked tape, naming the `assumption_set_id` to evaluate and the `method` to use — either `DETERMINISTIC_STATIC` or `OAS_SHOCK_WEIGHTED` — and echoing `expected_portfolio_version` as §2.6 requires. The portfolio must be `TAPE_LOCKED`: a run against a portfolio still in `DRAFT`, or one already `UNDER_CONTRACT` or `TRANSFERRED`, is refused with `ERR_TAPE_NOT_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). The run resolves and records the currently active snapshot as its `rate_curve_snapshot_id`, takes `sequence_no` as the next integer in a monotonic per-portfolio counter that starts at 1, records the acting operator as `run_by_user_id`, and is created directly in `COMPLETED` status — the model runs synchronously — with `staled_at` null. Its input set is every loan in the target portfolio whose status is `ACTIVE`; loans already `TRANSFERRED` are excluded, and the size of that input set is reported as `included_loan_count`.

The model first shifts the assumption vector onto the curve. The rate delta, in percentage points, is the bound snapshot's `base_rate_bps` less the model reference rate of 4,000 basis points, divided by 100. The effective discount rate in percent is the assumption set's `discount_rate_pct` plus that rate delta; the effective float earnings rate in percent is `float_earnings_rate_pct` plus the same rate delta; and the prepayment speed used in the projection is `cpr_pct` divided by 100, that is, expressed as a fraction of the balance retired each year. The run records the effective discount rate as `effective_discount_rate_pct`, rounded half up to 4 decimals.

Then, for each loan in the input set, and for each projection year counted from 1 up to and including `projection_years`:

- the beginning balance for that year is the loan's `upb_cents` multiplied by the survival factor — one minus the prepayment speed — raised to the power of the year number less one;
- the ending balance is the same `upb_cents` multiplied by that survival factor raised to the power of the year number;
- the average balance for the year is the beginning balance plus the ending balance, divided by two;
- fee income for the year is the average balance multiplied by the loan's `servicing_fee_bps` and divided by 10,000;
- float income for the year is the loan's `escrow_balance_cents` multiplied by the effective float earnings rate and divided by 100 — the escrow balance is not decayed by prepayment;
- the cost of servicing for the year is the assumption set's `cost_to_service_annual_cents` multiplied by the delinquency cost multiplier for the loan's `delinquency_bucket`, plus a flat additive surcharge for the loan's `loss_mit_status`. The delinquency multiplier is 1.0 for `CURRENT`, 1.5 for `DQ_30_59`, 2.5 for `DQ_60_89`, 4.0 for `DQ_90_PLUS` and 6.0 for `FORECLOSURE`. The loss-mitigation surcharge is nothing for `NONE`, 20 dollars a year (2,000 cents) for `TRIAL_MOD`, 10 dollars a year (1,000 cents) for `PERMANENT_MOD` and 15 dollars a year (1,500 cents) for `FORBEARANCE` — a trial modification costs more to service than a permanent one because it is still being re-underwritten every month;
- net cash flow for the year is fee income plus float income less the cost of servicing;
- the discount factor for the year is one divided by the quantity one plus the effective discount rate divided by 100, that quantity raised to the power of the year number.

Each loan's value is the sum across its projection years of net cash flow multiplied by that year's discount factor, rounded half up to whole cents, and then floored at zero — a loan whose projected net cash flow is negative in every year contributes exactly nothing and never subsidises the pool. The run's `value_cents` is the sum of those per-loan figures. The rounding order is normative, as §2.5 states: round each loan, then sum. Alongside it the run reports `value_bps_of_upb`, which is `value_cents` multiplied by 10,000, divided by the portfolio's `total_upb_cents`, rounded half up to 2 decimals, and reported as 0.00 when `total_upb_cents` is 0.

Under `DETERMINISTIC_STATIC` that projection is evaluated exactly once, at the assumption set's own prepayment speed and the curve-adjusted discount rate, and `scenario_values` is null. Under `OAS_SHOCK_WEIGHTED` the same projection is evaluated three times, once per rate scenario. The scenarios are: `DOWN_50`, which multiplies the prepayment speed by 1.50 and shifts both the effective discount rate and the effective float earnings rate down by 0.50 percentage points, weighted 0.25; `BASE`, which leaves the prepayment speed and both rates unshifted, weighted 0.50; and `UP_50`, which multiplies the prepayment speed by 0.60 and shifts both rates up by 0.50 percentage points, weighted 0.25. In each scenario the prepayment speed used is `cpr_pct` multiplied by that scenario's multiplier and then divided by 100. Each scenario's value is itself computed by rounding per loan and then summing, and is stored in `scenario_values` under the scenario's own label as a whole-cent integer, so the map has exactly the three keys `DOWN_50`, `BASE` and `UP_50`, serialised in that scenario order rather than alphabetically. The run's `value_cents` is then the sum across the three scenarios of each weight multiplied by that scenario's value, rounded half up to whole cents. Under this method `effective_discount_rate_pct` records the `BASE` scenario's effective discount rate — the unshifted one. Because faster prepayment in the down-rate scenario shortens the fee strip, the shock-weighted value comes out strictly below the static value for any pool with a positive fee strip.

**Which run counts.** Creating a newer run for the same portfolio retires older ones: a run whose status is `COMPLETED` or `STALE` moves to `SUPERSEDED` (a terminal status) with its `version` incremented by 1 — *unless* a bid package whose status is `PUBLISHED`, `BIDDING_SUSPENDED` or `AWARDED` still points at it, in which case the older run keeps whatever status it had, so a published reserve price never loses its anchor. A portfolio's effective valuation is chosen from the runs on that portfolio whose status is `COMPLETED` or `STALE`, ranked by `sequence_no` descending, taking the single highest — because the counter strictly increases per portfolio the ranking is total and needs no tie-break. `SUPERSEDED` runs are excluded from that choice, so a superseded run can never anchor a reserve price or a hedge-effectiveness read. The portfolio's `valuation_freshness` follows directly: `NONE` when there is no eligible run at all, `CURRENT` when the chosen run is `COMPLETED`, and `STALE` when the chosen run is `STALE`. A successful run increments the portfolio's `version` by 1 in addition to recomputing its `valuation_freshness`.

### 3.4. Bid Package Preparation: Stratification, Reserve and Publication

A `BidPackage` is the marketing vehicle. Either a `ValuationAnalyst` or a `TransactionManager` creates one, naming the pool through `portfolio_id` and the run that will anchor its economics through `valuation_run_id`, choosing how the loan-level tape will be bucketed with `stratification_scheme` — `BY_STATE`, `BY_NOTE_RATE` or `BY_DELINQUENCY` — declaring the `reserve_price_floor_pct` the desk is willing to accept as a percent of the anchored valuation (at least 50, at most 150, at most 2 decimals — 95 means "95% of the mark"), and bounding the marketing window with `marketing_start_at` and `bid_deadline_at`. The window's start is inclusive and its end is exclusive: bids are refused at or after the deadline instant, and `bid_deadline_at` must be strictly greater than `marketing_start_at` — a deadline at or before the start is a shape violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in band 3, before either named id is resolved. Package creation requires the owning portfolio to be `TAPE_LOCKED`, failing otherwise with `ERR_TAPE_NOT_LOCKED`. The named `valuation_run_id` is resolved for existence only: neither creation nor stratification requires the bound run to belong to the named portfolio, so a package may legitimately be built on a run cut against a different pool, and it is the portfolio named on the package that supplies the tape. A new package sits in `DRAFT` with `tape_upb_cents`, `tape_loan_count`, `stratum_count` and `bid_count` at 0, and with `reserve_price_cents`, `published_at` and `suspended_reason` all null. Its lifecycle is `DRAFT` → `STRATIFIED` → `PUBLISHED`, from which it either reaches `AWARDED`, or is diverted to `BIDDING_SUSPENDED` by the staleness chain, or ends `CANCELLED`.

**Cutting the tape.** A `ValuationAnalyst` stratifies a `DRAFT` package whose portfolio is `TAPE_LOCKED`; the tape-lock check is reported ahead of the package-state check, so stratifying a `DRAFT` package on a portfolio that has since moved to `UNDER_CONTRACT` yields `ERR_TAPE_NOT_LOCKED`. Stratification takes no request body and answers with the transitioned package rather than the lines it created. It creates one `TapeLine` per loan of the portfolio whose status is `ACTIVE`, each line recording its `bid_package_id`, its `loan_id`, `included` set to true, `exclusion_reason` null, and `upb_snapshot_cents` copied from the loan's `upb_cents` at that moment. That snapshot is the pricing basis for the rest of the trade: a later change to the loan's own balance does not move the tape and does not reprice a live package. Each line's `stratum_label` is assigned from the package's scheme — under `BY_STATE` it is the loan's `state_code`; under `BY_DELINQUENCY` it is the loan's `delinquency_bucket`; and under `BY_NOTE_RATE` it is a rate band, namely `LT_600` when the loan's `note_rate_pct` is below 6.00, `600_699` when the note rate is at least 6.00 and below 7.00, and `700_PLUS` when the note rate is 7.00 or above, with the band comparison made against the stored 4-decimal note rate and no rounding first. The package's `tape_loan_count` is then the number of included lines, `tape_upb_cents` the sum of `upb_snapshot_cents` over included lines, and `stratum_count` the number of distinct `stratum_label` values among included lines; the package moves `DRAFT → STRATIFIED` and its `version` increments by 1. The tape is cut exactly once: stratifying a package that is no longer `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

**Scrubbing the tape.** While the package is `STRATIFIED`, a `ValuationAnalyst` may pull or restore individual lines by setting `included`, supplying `exclusion_reason` — `DOCUMENT_DEFECT`, `LITIGATION` or `INVESTOR_RESTRICTION` — which is required whenever `included` is set to false and must be absent whenever `included` is true. Each edit increments the tape line's own `version`, recomputes the package's `tape_upb_cents`, `tape_loan_count` and `stratum_count`, and increments the package's `version` by 1; it answers with the edited tape line, not the package. There is no idempotency shortcut — re-setting `included` to the value it already holds is accepted and still increments both versions. An edit attempted on a package that is not `STRATIFIED` is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE`. Note that the package's three aggregates — along with `bid_count`, which simply counts every bid ever filed on the package in whatever status — are derived on every read rather than stored, and that the three tape aggregates count only lines that are both included *and* whose loan is still `ACTIVE`. That second condition has a visible consequence at the far end of the trade: once the completion cascade of §2.9 flips the tape's loans to `TRANSFERRED`, a read of the awarded package reports `tape_loan_count`, `tape_upb_cents` and `stratum_count` as 0, while the already-stamped `reserve_price_cents`, the winning bid's `bid_value_cents` and the agreement's `purchase_price_cents` keep the figures they were frozen at.

**Publishing and the reserve.** A `TransactionManager` publishes a `STRATIFIED` package, which stamps `published_at` from the logical clock, moves the package `STRATIFIED → PUBLISHED`, increments its `version` by 1, and stamps the reserve price. The reserve is the bound run's `value_cents`, multiplied by the package's `reserve_price_floor_pct` and divided by 100, multiplied by the package's `tape_upb_cents` and divided by the portfolio's `total_upb_cents` as frozen at tape lock, with a single half-up rounding to whole cents applied only at the very end and no intermediate rounding. Should that frozen denominator be 0, the reserve is stamped as 0 rather than the division being attempted. Excluding a tape line therefore shrinks `tape_upb_cents` while leaving the portfolio total and the valuation untouched, so the reserve scales down pro rata by unpaid balance.

A package in any status other than `STRATIFIED` is turned away on state grounds with `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE` before any of the gates below is consulted. Publication then has three gates, and when more than one is violated they are reported in this order. First, the bound valuation run must be `COMPLETED`; a `STALE` or `SUPERSEDED` run is not a publishable basis and the attempt is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_STALE` (HTTP 409). Second, the marketing window must be free: the half-open interval from `marketing_start_at` up to `bid_deadline_at` must not overlap the window of any other package on the same portfolio whose status is `STRATIFIED`, `PUBLISHED` or `BIDDING_SUSPENDED` — capacity is exactly one live window per portfolio, and because the interval is half-open, back-to-back windows that merely touch at an instant do not collide. A collision is `ERR_MARKETING_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Third, the tape must not be empty: a package whose `tape_loan_count` is exactly 0 because every line was excluded cannot be published, and is refused with `ERR_TAPE_EMPTY` (HTTP 422).

### 3.5. Bidding and Negotiation

Bids arrive from a `BuyerServicer`, or from a `TransactionManager` entering one on a bidder's behalf. A bid names the bidding institution through `bidder_id`, offers a price in basis points of tape unpaid balance as `price_bps` (at least 1, at most 1000), declares the `holdback_pct` the buyer will withhold pending the representation period (at least 0, at most 25, at most 2 decimals), and echoes `expected_bid_package_version` as §2.6 requires. A new bid is created in `SUBMITTED` status with `round_number` at 1 and `counter_price_bps` and `counter_expires_at` null; the package's `bid_count` increments and the package's `version` increments by 1. Submission is the one buyer-side operation that is *not* ownership-checked: `bidder_id` is taken from the body and never compared to `X-User-ID`, which is what lets a `TransactionManager` enter a bid on a bidder's behalf and equally lets one `BuyerServicer` file naming another institution.

The bid's own economics are computed as `bid_value_cents`: the package's `tape_upb_cents` multiplied by `price_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents — **one** rounding over the whole tape. This is deliberately a different arithmetic from the purchase price of §3.6, and it is this figure, not the purchase price, that is compared against the reserve.

Five business gates apply to a submission, and the order in which they are reported matters. The named bidder must have `role_type` `BUYER` (`ERR_COUNTERPARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`), and this side-of-trade check comes first of the five, in band 7 of §2.12, ahead even of the package's state. The package must then be `PUBLISHED`; a `DRAFT`, `STRATIFIED`, `BIDDING_SUSPENDED`, `AWARDED` or `CANCELLED` package accepts nothing and refuses with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACCEPTING_BIDS` (HTTP 409). Because that state check outranks every economic check, a bid filed on a package that is not open never learns whether its price would have cleared. The bidder must be eligible — `ACTIVE` and holding the agency approval matching the portfolio's `agency_type` — else `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; and its `PRE_AWARD` risk score must be at least 40, else `ERR_BIDDER_RISK_TIER_TOO_HIGH`. The logical clock must fall inside the marketing window, at or after `marketing_start_at` and strictly before `bid_deadline_at`, else `ERR_BID_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422). And `bid_value_cents` must be at least the package's `reserve_price_cents`, else `ERR_BID_BELOW_RESERVE` (HTTP 422). Eligibility deliberately outranks the deadline, so an ineligible bidder is told about its eligibility even when the window has also closed; and the reserve test, being the last of the five, is only ever reported when everything else passes.

**The negotiation cycle.** A `TransactionManager` may counter a bid that is `SUBMITTED` or `REVISED` while the package is still `PUBLISHED` and the bid's `round_number` is at most 3, supplying `counter_price_bps` — an integer of at least 1, deliberately uncapped, unlike the 1000 basis-point ceiling on a bid's own `price_bps` — and `counter_expires_at`. Countering moves the bid to `COUNTERED`, stamps both fields from the request, leaves `round_number` where it was, and increments the bid's `version` by 1; the `round_number` condition is vacuous in practice, since the revision ceiling below already prevents any bid from passing 3. On both countering and revising, the requirement that the package still be `PUBLISHED` is enforced with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACCEPTING_BIDS`, and it is checked *after* the bid's own state, per the carve-out in §2.12. The buyer answers by revising: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the bid's `bidder_id`, the bid must be `COUNTERED`, the package must still be `PUBLISHED`, the logical clock must be strictly before `counter_expires_at` (otherwise `ERR_BID_DEADLINE_PASSED`), and `round_number` must be strictly less than 3 — a revision filed from round 3 exceeds the three-round negotiation ceiling and is refused with `ERR_MAX_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS` (HTTP 409). The revised offer must itself clear the reserve, so a recomputed `bid_value_cents` below `reserve_price_cents` is refused with `ERR_BID_BELOW_RESERVE`. An accepted revision replaces `price_bps` and `holdback_pct` from the request, recomputes `bid_value_cents` on the same single-rounding basis, increments `round_number` by 1, clears `counter_price_bps` and `counter_expires_at` back to null, moves the bid `COUNTERED → REVISED`, and increments its `version` by 1. The desk may counter again from `REVISED`, so `COUNTERED` and `REVISED` alternate until the round ceiling stops the cycle.

Attempting an action from a state that does not allow it is `ERR_BID_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409): countering a bid that is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `REVISED`, revising a bid that is not `COUNTERED`, or nominating for award a bid that is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `REVISED` — including a bid that has `LAPSED`.

Three terminal outcomes exist and none of them is reachable by a direct call from a bidder. A bid becomes `ACCEPTED` when it is the nomination on an award, moving there from either `SUBMITTED` or `REVISED`. It becomes `REJECTED` when a sibling bid on the same package is awarded — this applies to every bid on the package sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED` or `REVISED` — and also when an `ACCEPTED` bid's agreement is later terminated. It becomes `LAPSED` when the package is suspended by the staleness chain, again from any of `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTERED` or `REVISED`. Every one of those transitions increments the bid's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Award, Consent Prerequisites and Settlement

**Awarding.** A `TransactionManager` closes the auction by nominating a `winning_bid_id`, optionally setting the length of the representation-and-warranty period as `rep_warrant_days` (at least 30, at most 730, recorded as 180 when not supplied) and the length of the indemnification period as `indemnification_days` (at least 1, at most 1825, and never shorter than `rep_warrant_days` — that last comparison being a band-3 shape rule refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — recorded as 365 when not supplied). Both periods are measured in calendar days from the eventual transfer date. The package must be `PUBLISHED`, failing with `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE`; the nominated bid must belong to that package and be `SUBMITTED` or `REVISED`, a bid that resolves but hangs off some other package being refused with the same `ERR_BID_INVALID_STATE` as one in the wrong state; the nominated bid's institution must still be eligible, so a bidder suspended since it bid is refused here with `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` rather than being retroactively voided; and the acting operator must not be the one who produced the mark, as §2.10 requires.

An award creates a `PurchaseAgreement` in `DRAFTED` status. It records `bid_package_id`, `winning_bid_id`, a `buyer_id` copied from the winning bid's `bidder_id`, a `holdback_pct` copied from the winning bid, and `drafted_by_user_id` set to the acting operator; `sale_date`, `transfer_date`, `holdback_release_eligible_at`, `condition_waiver_reference` and `termination_reason` are all null, and `approval_pending_count` and `condition_count` are 0.

The purchase price is computed per stratum. For each distinct `stratum_label` on the awarded tape, visited in ascending lexicographic order of the label by code point — so a digit-leading band such as `600_699` sorts ahead of a letter-leading one such as `LT_600` — the stratum's unpaid balance is the sum of `upb_snapshot_cents` over that stratum's included lines, taking every line still marked `included` without regard to its loan's `status`, and the stratum's price is that balance multiplied by the winning bid's `price_bps`, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to whole cents. Those figures are recorded in `stratum_price_breakdown` as a map from `stratum_label` to an object carrying that stratum's `upb_cents` and its `price_cents`, in the same ascending label order, as the audit trail for the pinned rounding sequence. `purchase_price_cents` is the sum of the per-stratum prices. Because each stratum is rounded before the sum, this figure can legitimately differ from the winning bid's own `bid_value_cents` by up to one cent less than the number of strata — the per-stratum figure is the contractual one, because each stratum is separately priced and separately wired. It also means the chosen `stratification_scheme` changes the purchase price even when the tape and the offered `price_bps` are identical.

The holdback is then sized: `holdback_cents` is `purchase_price_cents` multiplied by `holdback_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. `holdback_retained_cents` is initialised to that figure, `holdback_released_cents` and `holdback_applied_cents` to 0, and `settlement_wire_cents` to `purchase_price_cents` less `holdback_cents`. Awarding also settles the auction around it: the nominated bid becomes `ACCEPTED`, every other live bid on the package becomes `REJECTED`, the package moves `PUBLISHED → AWARDED` with its `version` incremented, and the owning portfolio moves `TAPE_LOCKED → UNDER_CONTRACT` with its `version` incremented.

**Consent prerequisites.** Transfer of servicing needs consent, and the desk models each consent as an `ApprovalRequest` raised by the `TransactionManager` while the agreement is still `DRAFTED`. Each request declares `approver_type` — `AGENCY` for a transfer-of-servicing approval or `INVESTOR` for investor consent — and, when and only when the type is `AGENCY`, an `agency_type` that must equal the selling portfolio's own `agency_type`; for an `INVESTOR` request `agency_type` must be omitted. All three of those conditions — the field being required for `AGENCY`, forbidden for `INVESTOR`, and equal to the portfolio's own agency when present — are refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, the first two in band 3 and the third, which needs the resolved portfolio, only after the agreement's `DRAFTED` state has been confirmed. Raising a request against an agreement that is not `DRAFTED` is `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. At most one request may exist per combination of agreement, `approver_type` and `agency_type` — counted over every request on the agreement whatever its decision status — and a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPROVAL_REQUEST` (HTTP 409). A request is born `PENDING` with `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `condition_text` null.

Once the prerequisites are on file the `TransactionManager` submits the agreement for approval, which requires the agreement to be `DRAFTED`, requires at least one approval request to exist on it, and requires every one of those requests to still be `PENDING` — all three failures reporting `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Submission moves the agreement `DRAFTED → PENDING_APPROVALS`, recomputes `approval_pending_count` as the number of `PENDING` requests, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1.

**Deciding.** An `AgencyApprover` records exactly one decision per request, supplying `decision` as `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` or `DENIED`, with `condition_text` — a non-blank string of unbounded length — required when the decision is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` and forbidden otherwise. The operation answers with the decided `ApprovalRequest`, not with the agreement that joined on it, so a caller that needs the new agreement status must re-read the agreement. The agreement must be `PENDING_APPROVALS`, the request must still be `PENDING` — a second decision on the same request is `ERR_APPROVAL_ALREADY_DECIDED` (HTTP 409) — and the acting operator must not already have decided another request on the same agreement. Every decision stamps `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, moves the request to its terminal decision state, and increments the request's `version` by 1. `approval_pending_count` is recomputed as the number of requests still `PENDING`, and `condition_count` as the number whose status is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`. Because those two counters live on the agreement, *every* accepted decision increments the agreement's `version` by 1, including one that leaves the agreement in `PENDING_APPROVALS` because siblings are still undecided.

The agreement then joins on those two counters, and the three branches are genuinely different, not three names for one outcome:

- When `approval_pending_count` reaches 0 and `condition_count` is 0, the agreement moves `PENDING_APPROVALS → APPROVED` and its `version` increments by 1. Nothing else changes — in particular the holdback is **not** re-sized on this branch.
- When `approval_pending_count` reaches 0 and `condition_count` is at least 1, the agreement moves `PENDING_APPROVALS → CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` and the holdback is re-sized upward to a floor: the new `holdback_cents` is the greater of the existing `holdback_cents` and the figure obtained by multiplying `purchase_price_cents` by 15, dividing by 100 and rounding half up to whole cents. The floor is one-directional — a bid that already offered more than a fifteen percent holdback is left exactly as it was. `holdback_retained_cents` is reset to the new `holdback_cents`, `settlement_wire_cents` is recomputed as `purchase_price_cents` less the new `holdback_cents`, and the agreement's `version` increments by 1. Two identical bids therefore settle for different amounts of cash depending on which approval branch the agreement took.
- A single `DENIED` decision short-circuits the join. The agreement moves `PENDING_APPROVALS → TERMINATED` immediately, with `termination_reason` set to `APPROVAL_DENIED`, without waiting for any sibling request. Those siblings are left in `PENDING` and become permanently undecidable: a later decision on them is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), because the agreement they belong to is no longer `PENDING_APPROVALS`. The rest of the termination unwind — package `CANCELLED`, `ACCEPTED` bid `REJECTED`, portfolio back to `TAPE_LOCKED` — is exactly as §2.9 describes.

**Settling.** A `TransactionManager` settles an agreement that is `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, supplying the economic `sale_date` and the servicing `transfer_date`, both as `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates, plus `condition_waiver_reference` — the external waiver document reference — which is required when the agreement is `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` and must be omitted otherwise. Four refusals guard settlement, in this order. An agreement still in `PENDING_APPROVALS` reaches this operation rather than being turned away on state grounds, precisely so that the informative answer is given: while at least one request on it is `PENDING`, that is, while `approval_pending_count` is greater than 0, settlement fails with `ERR_AGENCY_APPROVAL_PENDING` (HTTP 409). A conditionally approved agreement settled with no waiver reference at all fails with `ERR_CONDITION_WAIVER_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); a reference supplied but blank never reaches that check, because a blank string is already a band-3 shape violation, and a reference supplied on an agreement that is *not* `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, reported here rather than in band 3 as §2.12 notes. The transfer date must be at least 15 days after the current logical clock date, and the sale date must not be after the transfer date; either violation is `ERR_TRANSFER_DATE_TOO_SOON` (HTTP 422). Finally, the buyer's onboarding capacity is exactly one transfer at a time: the closed window running from 30 calendar days before the transfer date to 30 calendar days after it must not intersect the equivalent window of any existing servicing transfer for the same buyer whose status is `IN_PROGRESS`. Because the interval is closed, two windows that merely touch at an endpoint **do** collide, and the refusal is `ERR_ONBOARDING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Agreements in `DRAFTED`, `SETTLED`, `HOLDBACK_RELEASED`, `CLOSED` or `TERMINATED` are turned away on state grounds with `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

Settlement stamps `sale_date` and `transfer_date` on the agreement, computes `holdback_release_eligible_at` as the transfer date advanced by `rep_warrant_days` calendar days (the expiry of the representations and warranties), sets `holdback_retained_cents` to `holdback_cents` with `holdback_released_cents` and `holdback_applied_cents` at 0, moves the agreement to `SETTLED`, and increments its `version` by 1. The indemnification period, which matters later for closure, expires on the transfer date advanced by `indemnification_days` calendar days; being at least as long as the representation period, it always expires on or after the holdback becomes eligible. Settlement also opens the operational work: exactly one `ServicingTransfer` is created for the agreement, recording its `purchase_agreement_id` and a `transfer_date` copied from the agreement, in overall status `IN_PROGRESS`, with `notice_status`, `data_status` and `pipeline_status` all `NOT_STARTED`, `readiness` `NOT_READY`, `escrow_transferred_cents` and `suspense_transferred_cents` at 0, `notice_count`, `late_notice_count`, `loss_mit_pipeline_count` and `transferred_loan_count` at 0, `mapping_scheme` null, and its onboarding blackout materialised as `onboarding_window_start` (the transfer date less 30 calendar days, inclusive) and `onboarding_window_end` (the transfer date plus 30 calendar days, inclusive). A transfer is never created directly, and settlement answers with the settled agreement rather than with the transfer; listing transfers, filtered by their `purchase_agreement_id`, is how a client discovers the one settlement produced.

### 3.7. Executing the Transfer: Three Independent Work Streams

A servicing transfer is not a single queue. It advances along three dimensions that progress independently of one another — borrower notices, data and custodial funds, and the loss-mitigation pipeline — plus an overall `status` that is `IN_PROGRESS` until the transfer `COMPLETED`. A derived `readiness` reads `READY` if and only if `notice_status` is `NOTICES_ATTESTED` **and** `data_status` is `RECONCILED` **and** `pipeline_status` is `PIPELINE_TRANSFERRED`; in every other combination it reads `NOT_READY`. It is re-derived on every dimension change, including changes that move backwards: rolling back the custodial sweep re-derives `readiness` and leaves it `NOT_READY`. That rollback is the only backward move any of the three dimensions has, and because it is admitted only from `data_status` `ESCROW_MAPPED` (see dimension two below), a transfer that has actually reached `READY` is never in a position to take it. Every step below requires the transfer's overall status to be `IN_PROGRESS`, and a step attempted on a transfer that has already `COMPLETED` — including a second completion — is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The same code, and only that code, answers every attempt to repeat a dimension step that has already been taken: a second `map-data`, a second `map-escrow`, a rollback with nothing swept, a second `reconcile`, a second `transfer-pipeline` and a second `attest-notices` are all `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`. That also means the mapping template cannot be re-chosen once recorded, since a rollback returns `data_status` to `MAPPED` rather than to `NOT_STARTED`.

**Dimension one: statutory borrower notices.** `notice_status` runs `NOT_STARTED` → `NOTICES_IN_PROGRESS` → `NOTICES_ATTESTED`. Each notice names the borrower's `loan_id`, the `notice_type` and the `effective_date` disclosed to the borrower. Three notice types exist: `TRANSFEROR_15DAY` and `COMBINED` are the seller's disclosures sent *before* the transfer, and `TRANSFEREE_15DAY` is the buyer's disclosure sent *after* it. The sender must match the type: `TRANSFEROR_15DAY` and `COMBINED` require the caller to be a `SellerServicer` whose `X-User-ID` is the selling portfolio's `seller_id`, and `TRANSFEREE_15DAY` requires a `BuyerServicer` whose `X-User-ID` is the agreement's `buyer_id`. A role that does not match the notice type at all is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_SENDER_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); a caller in the right role but acting for the wrong institution is refused earlier, on ownership grounds, with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Next, the disclosed `effective_date` must equal the agreement's `transfer_date` exactly, else `ERR_NOTICE_EFFECTIVE_DATE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Then the named loan must actually be on the awarded tape with its line `included`, else `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ON_TAPE` (HTTP 422) — the same rule, and the same code, guards the error cases of §3.8. That last check presumes the loan exists: a `loan_id` naming no loan at all is a body-referent miss resolved with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) at the §2.12 body-404 band, ahead of the ownership, transfer-state, sender-role, effective-date and on-tape checks alike. A line whose loan is no longer `ACTIVE` still counts as being on the tape, since only the line's `included` flag is consulted.

An accepted notice is created with `servicing_transfer_id`, `sent_at` set to the current logical clock, and `sent_by_role` recording which side issued it, either `SellerServicer` or `BuyerServicer`. A borrower notice is an immutable record: like an assumption set it carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Its compliance is then computed on calendar days only, discarding the time of day of `sent_at`. For a `TRANSFEROR_15DAY` or a `COMBINED` notice, `lead_days` is the number of calendar days from the date the notice was sent to the effective date — the effective date less the sent date — and `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` when `lead_days` is at least 15, and `LATE` otherwise. For a `TRANSFEREE_15DAY` notice the subtraction is reversed: `lead_days` is the sent date less the effective date, and `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` when `lead_days` is at least 0 and at most 15, and `LATE` otherwise. Because that figure is signed, a transferee notice sent *before* the effective date yields a negative `lead_days` and is `LATE` — the buyer cannot notice a transfer it has not yet received. Both boundaries are inclusive: exactly 15 days is `COMPLIANT` on both sides, while 14 days is `LATE` on the transferor side and `COMPLIANT` on the transferee side.

Each notice increments the transfer's `notice_count`, increments `late_notice_count` when the new notice is `LATE`, moves `notice_status` from `NOT_STARTED` to `NOTICES_IN_PROGRESS` on the first notice and leaves it in `NOTICES_IN_PROGRESS` for every subsequent one, re-derives `readiness`, and increments the transfer's `version` by 1. A `ComplianceOfficer` closes the dimension by attesting compliance, which moves `notice_status` `NOTICES_IN_PROGRESS → NOTICES_ATTESTED`, stamps `notice_attested_at` and records `notice_attested_by_user_id` — the identifier that later blocks the same officer from releasing the holdback. Attestation takes no request body, is not an ownership-checked operation — any `ComplianceOfficer` may attest any transfer — and first requires `notice_status` to be exactly `NOTICES_IN_PROGRESS`: attesting before the first notice has been sent, or attesting a second time, is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE` ahead of everything that follows. Past that, attestation has two gates, reported in this order: every loan with an included tape line must have at least one `TRANSFEROR_15DAY` or `COMBINED` notice on this transfer, else `ERR_NOTICE_COMPLIANCE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409); and `late_notice_count` must be exactly 0, else `ERR_NOTICE_LATE` (HTTP 409). That counter only ever rises — no later notice, however compliant, decrements it and no operation resets it — so a single late notice bars attestation, and therefore completion, permanently.

**Dimension two: data mapping and custodial funds.** `data_status` runs `NOT_STARTED` → `MAPPED` → `ESCROW_MAPPED` → `RECONCILED`, with a compensating edge from `ESCROW_MAPPED` back to `MAPPED`. Every step on this dimension belongs to the `BuyerServicer` whose `X-User-ID` is the agreement's `buyer_id`. The first step records the servicing-system mapping template as `mapping_scheme`, one of `MSP_TO_MSP`, `MSP_TO_LSAMS` or `CUSTOM`, and moves `data_status` `NOT_STARTED → MAPPED`; `mapping_scheme` is the only field its body accepts. On every step of this dimension the ownership check precedes the transfer-state and dimension-state checks, so a caller acting for the wrong institution is told `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` even when the step is also out of sequence.

The second step sweeps custodial funds and takes no request body. Its scope is the awarded package's `included` tape lines and nothing narrower — a loan whose status has moved on is swept exactly like an `ACTIVE` one — and each loan it touches is a mutation in its own right, refreshing that loan's `updated_at` and incrementing that loan's `version` by 1. Before anything moves, each loan on the included tape has its current balances snapshotted: `escrow_balance_at_mapping_cents` takes the value of `escrow_balance_cents` and `suspense_balance_at_mapping_cents` takes the value of `suspense_balance_cents`. The transfer's `escrow_transferred_cents` is then set to the sum of `escrow_balance_cents` over those loans and each of those loan balances is set to 0; `suspense_transferred_cents` is set to the sum of `suspense_balance_cents` over the same loans and each of those is likewise set to 0. `data_status` moves `MAPPED → ESCROW_MAPPED`, `readiness` is re-derived and the transfer's `version` increments by 1. Nothing is created or destroyed: the conserved custodial total of §2.11 is identical before and after the sweep. Attempting the sweep before the mapping template is recorded is a dimension-ordering violation refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`.

The sweep can be undone. A rollback, whose body carries a mandatory, non-blank, length-unbounded free-text `reason` and nothing else, version-bumps each loan it restores just as the sweep version-bumped each loan it drained, and restores each included tape loan's `escrow_balance_cents` and `suspense_balance_cents` from their `escrow_balance_at_mapping_cents` and `suspense_balance_at_mapping_cents` snapshots, resets both `escrow_transferred_cents` and `suspense_transferred_cents` to 0, moves `data_status` `ESCROW_MAPPED → MAPPED`, re-derives `readiness` to `NOT_READY`, and increments the transfer's `version` by 1. The custodial total returns to exactly its pre-sweep value with no drift, and a rollback attempted while `data_status` is anything other than `ESCROW_MAPPED` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`.

The third step reconciles. Reconciliation requires that the sweep has happened — `data_status` must be `ESCROW_MAPPED` — and it is one of the three deliberate carve-outs of §2.12: a reconcile filed too early passes the transfer state check so that it can be refused with the specific `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_MAPPED` (HTTP 409) instead of a generic state error. That carve-out is narrow, though: an already-`RECONCILED` transfer is caught by the ordinary repeat guard and answered `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`, so `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_MAPPED` is reserved for the two dimension states below `ESCROW_MAPPED`. The check itself is that the swept funds account for the pool: `escrow_transferred_cents` plus `suspense_transferred_cents` must equal the portfolio's `custodial_funds_total_cents` as frozen at tape lock, and a shortfall or excess reuses that very same `ERR_ESCROW_NOT_MAPPED` rather than any distinct reconciliation code, so the code alone does not distinguish filing too early from failing to balance. On success `reconciled_at` is stamped, `data_status` moves `ESCROW_MAPPED → RECONCILED`, `readiness` is re-derived and the `version` increments by 1.

**Dimension three: the loss-mitigation pipeline.** `pipeline_status` has a single edge, `NOT_STARTED → PIPELINE_TRANSFERRED`, driven by the `BuyerServicer` who owns the agreement. Handing over the pipeline takes no request body, sets `loss_mit_pipeline_count` to the number of included tape loans whose `loss_mit_status` is anything other than `NONE` — again by included flag alone, whatever those loans' statuses are — re-derives `readiness` and increments the `version` by 1. This dimension is genuinely independent: it may be driven first, last or anywhere in between, with identical results.

**Completion.** A `TransactionManager` completes the transfer. It is not an ownership-checked operation and takes no request body. Completion requires `readiness` to be `READY`, re-derived from the three dimensions at that moment rather than trusted as stored — any dimension short of its terminal value is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_READY` (HTTP 409) — and then requires the current logical clock date to have reached the agreement's `transfer_date`; a transfer cannot complete before its own effective date, and that second refusal reuses `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_READY` too rather than the `ERR_TRANSFER_DATE_TOO_SOON` that guards agreement drafting. Completion stamps `completed_at`, sets `transferred_loan_count` to the number of included tape loans, moves the transfer `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, increments its `version` by 1, and fires the completion propagation of §2.9: the included tape loans flip to `TRANSFERRED`, the portfolio follows to `TRANSFERRED` once no loan on it is still `ACTIVE`, and the portfolio's open hedge positions close with their marks realised.

### 3.8. Post-Transfer Error Resolution, Holdback Release and Closure

Borrowers notice the difference when their servicer changes, and a spike in complaints after a transfer is the desk's main early-warning signal. A `ComplianceOfficer` logs each borrower notice of error against the transfer as an `ErrorResolutionCase`, naming the `loan_id` complained about — which must have an included line on the awarded tape, else `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ON_TAPE` — the `category` of the asserted error (`ESCROW`, `SUSPENSE`, `LOSS_MIT`, `PAYMENT_MISAPPLIED` or `OTHER`) and the borrower's own `description` (non-blank, at most 500 characters). A case may be logged at any point in the transfer's life — there is no requirement that the transfer have `COMPLETED`, and one opened while it is still `IN_PROGRESS` is accepted and counts toward concentration exactly like any other — but a `loan_id` naming no loan at all is refused with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND` before the on-tape test is reached, and the on-tape test itself reads the line's `included` flag alone, without regard to the loan's status. The case records its `servicing_transfer_id`, opens in `OPEN` status with `opened_at` set to the current logical clock, and derives two statutory deadlines from that instant as instants rather than dates, preserving its time of day: `ack_deadline_at` five calendar days after it and `resolution_deadline_at` thirty calendar days after it. Both breach tests are strict comparisons of instants, so acting exactly at a deadline is not a breach. It starts with `acknowledged_at`, `resolved_at` and `resolution_note` null, `ack_breached` and `resolution_breached` false, and `remediation_cents` and `reopen_count` at 0. From the moment it is logged the case counts toward the complaint concentration ratio described below.

The buyer works the case. Acknowledging it — available to the `BuyerServicer` who owns the agreement while the case is `OPEN` or `REOPENED` — takes no request body, stamps `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock and sets `ack_breached` to true when that instant is later than `ack_deadline_at`. Resolving it — available while the case is `ACKNOWLEDGED` — stamps `resolved_at`, sets `resolution_breached` to true when that instant is later than `resolution_deadline_at`, records the narrative as the mandatory, non-blank, length-unbounded `resolution_note`, and adds the equally mandatory supplied `remediation_cents` (an integer, zero or more) to the case's cumulative remediation, which becomes chargeable against the holdback. On both operations the ownership check precedes the case-state check, so the wrong buyer is told `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` even on a case that is in no fit state to be worked. Both breach flags are **sticky**: once either is true it stays true for the life of the case, so a later on-time acknowledgement does not launder an earlier miss.

A resolution the compliance function does not accept can be reopened, and the cycle is bounded. A `ComplianceOfficer` may reopen a `RESOLVED` case only within 60 calendar days of its `resolved_at`; later than that the attempt is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). That window is measured on calendar dates alone, discarding both instants' times of day, and 60 days exactly is still inside it — only a difference strictly greater than 60 expires. Reopening is not ownership-checked, and only a `RESOLVED` or `ESCALATED` case reaches the window test at all: any other status, `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `REOPENED`, is refused first with `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`. Inside the window, a reopen supplies a mandatory, non-blank, length-unbounded `reason`, increments `reopen_count` by 1, clears `acknowledged_at` and `resolved_at` back to null while leaving both breach flags at their worst observed values, and moves the case `RESOLVED → REOPENED`; the buyer then acknowledges again, this time measured against the unchanged `ack_deadline_at`, so a second pass through the loop will usually set `ack_breached`. The loop `ACKNOWLEDGED → RESOLVED → REOPENED → ACKNOWLEDGED` may therefore run twice. A reopen attempted when `reopen_count` is already 2 does not cycle a third time: it moves the case `RESOLVED → ESCALATED`, which is terminal, leaving the counter at 2. That escalation is a status change and nothing more: unlike a reopen it clears neither `acknowledged_at` nor `resolved_at`, so an escalated case still shows the resolution that was rejected. `resolution_note` and `remediation_cents` survive both moves — a reopen clears only the two timestamps — and the reopen window of an `ESCALATED` case continues to be measured from the resolution that preceded the escalation. An escalated case still counts in the complaint concentration numerator but can no longer be acknowledged, resolved or reopened — acknowledgement and resolution are refused on state grounds with `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`, while a further reopen passes the state check by design and is refused with `ERR_MAX_REOPENS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). When both the window and the ceiling are violated at once, the window is reported first.

**Releasing the holdback.** The holdback exists to protect the buyer while the representations and warranties are live, and its release is a compliance decision, not a clerical one. A `ComplianceOfficer` releases against an agreement that is `SETTLED` or `HOLDBACK_RELEASED`, echoing `expected_purchase_agreement_version` as §2.6 requires. Four further gates apply in this order. The acting operator must not be the officer recorded in `notice_attested_by_user_id` (§2.10). The money must be eligible: the bound servicing transfer must be `COMPLETED` and the current logical clock date must have reached `holdback_release_eligible_at`, else `ERR_HOLDBACK_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409). The complaint concentration must be in a releasing tier, else `ERR_COMPLAINT_TIER_BLOCKS_RELEASE` (HTTP 409). And there must be something left to release: `holdback_retained_cents` must be greater than 0, else `ERR_HOLDBACK_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409).

The release amount is computed in exactly five ordered steps, and the order is normative because each step reads a figure the next step changes.

1. **Concentration ratio.** Count every error resolution case on the agreement's servicing transfer, in any status whatsoever — `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `RESOLVED`, `REOPENED` or `ESCALATED`. The complaint concentration ratio in percent is 0.00 when the transfer's `transferred_loan_count` is 0; otherwise it is that case count multiplied by 100, divided by `transferred_loan_count`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. Because the numerator ignores status, resolving cases never lowers the concentration.
2. **Tier lookup**, with inclusive upper bounds. A ratio of 25.00 percent or less releases 100 percent. A ratio above 25.00 and up to and including 50.00 releases 50 percent. A ratio strictly above 50.00 releases nothing, and the call itself fails with `ERR_COMPLAINT_TIER_BLOCKS_RELEASE`.
3. **Gross release**, taken against the *current* retained balance: `holdback_retained_cents` multiplied by the tier's release percentage, divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents.
4. **Remediation charge, capped.** Sum the cumulative `remediation_cents` of the cases on this transfer whose status is `RESOLVED` — and only those. The cap is 40 percent of *this* release's gross amount, truncated downward to whole cents so it can never round up past forty percent. The charge is the smaller of the remediation total and the cap.
5. **Net wire.** The seller receives the gross release less the charge.

The three holdback buckets then move together: `holdback_retained_cents` decreases by the gross release, `holdback_released_cents` increases by the net wire, and `holdback_applied_cents` increases by the charge — which is precisely why the identity of §2.11 survives every release. Note what step 4's wording implies: the cap is a fraction of this release, not of the original holdback, so a small second release carries a proportionally smaller cap.

A tier-two release deliberately leaves a residual: half the retained balance stays behind, the agreement moves `SETTLED → HOLDBACK_RELEASED`, and a later call releases against the residual while the state remains `HOLDBACK_RELEASED`. Closure is a branch on the *same* operation: when a release brings `holdback_retained_cents` to exactly 0 **and** the current logical clock date has reached the transfer date advanced by `indemnification_days` calendar days, the agreement moves to `CLOSED` instead — from `SETTLED` directly if a single tier-one release exhausts the balance, or from `HOLDBACK_RELEASED` if an earlier partial release got there first. A release that exhausts the balance *before* the indemnification period has run does **not** close the agreement; it lands in `HOLDBACK_RELEASED`, where it can still be clawed back. `CLOSED` is terminal and locks the agreement: any further release or clawback is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

**Clawing back.** A release taken in error is compensated rather than deleted. A `ComplianceOfficer` claws back against a `HOLDBACK_RELEASED` agreement, supplying `amount_cents` and a `reason`. The amount must be greater than 0 and must not exceed `holdback_released_cents`, else `ERR_CLAWBACK_EXCEEDS_RELEASED` (HTTP 422). The clawback subtracts `amount_cents` from `holdback_released_cents` and adds the same amount to `holdback_retained_cents`, so no value is created or destroyed and the holdback identity is preserved exactly. A clawback that returns the entire released balance — leaving `holdback_released_cents` at 0 — rewinds the agreement `HOLDBACK_RELEASED → SETTLED` so the release decision can be taken again; a partial clawback leaves the agreement in `HOLDBACK_RELEASED`.

### 3.9. Hedging the Servicing Asset

An MSR asset has negative effective duration: it gains value as rates rise, because borrowers stop refinancing and the fee strip lives longer. The `HedgeDesk` therefore holds long-duration instruments against it, which are deliberately signed to lose value as rates rise, and the desk watches the two legs together.

Opening a `HedgePosition` names the `portfolio_id` being hedged, the `instrument` — `TBA_LONG`, `SWAPTION_RECEIVER` or `TREASURY_FUTURE_LONG` — the face `notional_cents` (at least 1) and the `hedge_ratio_pct`, the fraction of that notional actually applied, in percent (at least 1, at most 200, at most 2 decimals). The named portfolio need only exist: a hedge may be opened against a portfolio in any status whatsoever, including one already `TRANSFERRED`, and the desk is never subjected to an ownership check. The position records `entry_base_rate_bps` from the `base_rate_bps` of the snapshot active at that moment — so opening a hedge requires an active curve, and fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATE_CURVE` when none exists, reported after the portfolio has been resolved — starts `OPEN` with `mark_to_market_cents` at 0, and leaves `close_base_rate_bps`, `realized_pnl_cents` and `closed_at` null.

The mark is a pure function of the current curve. Take `notional_cents`, multiply by `hedge_ratio_pct` and divide by 100 to get the applied notional; multiply by the instrument's option-adjusted duration factor, which is 4.5 for `TBA_LONG`, 6.0 for `SWAPTION_RECEIVER` and 5.5 for `TREASURY_FUTURE_LONG`; multiply by the difference between `entry_base_rate_bps` and the current active snapshot's base rate, taking entry less current so that a rate rise produces a negative mark; divide by 10,000; and round half up to whole cents on the magnitude, so a computed −0.5 cent becomes −1 cent. Because the mark depends only on the current curve and not on any accumulated history, it is fully reversible: returning the curve to the entry level restores the mark to 0 with no drift. Every published curve re-marks every `OPEN` position, as §2.8 notes.

A hedge-effectiveness read on a portfolio reports the two legs side by side: `effective_valuation_run_id` (the run selected as the portfolio's effective valuation, or null when none is eligible), `msr_value_cents` (that run's `value_cents`, or 0 when there is none), `open_hedge_mtm_cents` (the sum of `mark_to_market_cents` over that portfolio's `OPEN` positions), `net_hedged_position_cents` (the MSR value plus that aggregate mark) and `current_base_rate_bps` (the base rate of the currently active snapshot, or null when no snapshot is `ACTIVE` — the read never fails for want of a curve). Closed positions do not contribute to any of those aggregates.

Closing a position takes no request body and requires it to be `OPEN` — a second close, including one aimed at a position already closed by the completion cascade, is refused with `ERR_HEDGE_ALREADY_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), reported ahead of any curve check — and requires an active curve. A close stamps `closed_at`, sets `close_base_rate_bps` to the active snapshot's base rate, re-derives `mark_to_market_cents` against that same snapshot before freezing `realized_pnl_cents` at it — the two fields therefore always agree on a closed row — moves the position `OPEN → CLOSED`, and increments its `version` by 1. From then on the row is inert: later curves never re-mark it, and it stops contributing to the net hedged position. The completion cascade of §2.9 stamps a close identically, save that it has no active-curve precondition and falls back to the position's own `entry_base_rate_bps`, and it skips positions that are already `CLOSED` so a repeated portfolio transition can never re-stamp a realised result.
