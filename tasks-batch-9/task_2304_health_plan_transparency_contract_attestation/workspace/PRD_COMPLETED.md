# Health Plan Transparency Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employer plan sponsors now carry a set of transparency obligations that used to sit entirely with carriers and third-party administrators: negotiated rates and allowed amounts must be posted as machine-readable files and refreshed every month, a consumer-facing price comparison tool must stay available for covered items and services, every service provider contract must be scrubbed for prohibited gag clauses on cost and quality data, an annual gag-clause attestation must be prepared and accepted with documentary evidence of contract terms, broker and consultant compensation must be received and assessed for reasonableness, and prescription-drug data must be assembled from plan and pharmacy vendors and filed on time. Each of those obligations has its own clock, its own evidence trail, and its own penalty exposure — and today most sponsors track them in a patchwork of spreadsheets that cannot answer the one question the plan fiduciary actually asks: *how much are we exposed to right now, and what closes it fastest?*

This service is the compliance system of record for that program. It models a plan year as a two-dimensional object — an administrative lifecycle the sponsor drives forward, and a derived compliance posture the system computes from the live state of every child obligation — and it uses that posture to gate what the sponsor's staff is allowed to do. Deficiencies are priced into an append-only penalty ledger the moment they open, remediation plans abate that ledger without pretending the underlying deficiency never happened, and an independent auditor can freeze a plan year outright when the evidence stops being trustworthy. Separation of duties is enforced structurally rather than by convention: the officer who prepares an attestation may not submit it, and the auditor who adjudicates it may not have touched it earlier.

All resources are mounted under the `/compliance` prefix. The service is deliberately built for deterministic, replayable testing: there is no wall-clock dependency anywhere. Every deadline comparison, day count, month boundary and timestamp reads a single server-side logical clock that an operator advances explicitly.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Headers, and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the caller and `X-User-Role` names the capability set the caller is acting under. When `X-User-ID` is absent or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When `X-User-Role` is absent, holds a value that is not a recognised role, or holds a recognised role that is not listed for the addressed endpoint in §4, the request is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows.

- **`PlanSponsorAdmin`** — the employer plan sponsor's operator. Owns plan-year setup and lifecycle, vendor registration, contract creation, amendment proposal and execution, machine-readable file publication, and price comparison tool registration and launch. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` may not certify refresh cycles, assess compensation disclosures, prepare, submit or adjudicate attestations, or reverse penalty ledger entries.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — the compliance function. Runs gag-clause reviews, certifies refresh cycles, scores tool uptime, assesses compensation disclosures, validates and rejects vendor data packages, reconciles and files prescription-drug data, prepares, submits and revises attestations, drives compliance runs, and owns remediation plans. A `ComplianceOfficer` may not accept or reject an attestation, and may not submit an attestation they themselves prepared.
- **`ExternalAuditor`** — the independent adjudicator. Accepts or rejects submitted attestations, freezes and unfreezes plan years, and reverses penalty ledger entries. Read-only everywhere else, and never a preparer or submitter of an attestation.
- **`VendorContact`** — a service provider's representative. Submits gag-clause remediation, counters amendments, and delivers or resubmits its own prescription-drug data packages.
- **`PlatformOperator`** — the platform automation identity. Sets the logical clock, appends outage windows reported by monitoring, triggers tool uptime evaluations, and drives compliance runs. It holds no business judgement authority whatsoever: it cannot review contracts, assess disclosures, certify cycles, or touch attestations.

Four of the five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlanSponsorAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ExternalAuditor` or `PlatformOperator`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit anchor — as `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` on an attestation, as `certified_by_user_id` on a refresh cycle, as `assessed_by_user_id` on a compensation disclosure, and as `posted_by_user_id` on a penalty ledger entry — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

`VendorContact` is the one **entity-backed** role. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a vendor compare that resource's `vendor_id` to `X-User-ID`. Two distinct failures follow from this. If the addressed contract, amendment or data package belongs to a different vendor, the request is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) — the role is permitted on the endpoint, but the row is not the caller's. If instead the presented `X-User-ID` does not resolve to any registered vendor at all, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_ENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The scope violation is checked first, so a caller presenting a bogus vendor identity while reaching for another vendor's row receives `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, not `ERR_ROLE_ENTITY_MISMATCH`.

Beyond role and ownership, the attestation workflow enforces a segregation-of-duties rule that no role assignment can satisfy on its own: the `X-User-ID` submitting an attestation must differ from the attestation's `prepared_by_user_id`, and the auditor accepting or rejecting it must differ from both `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id`. Any breach is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the same logical clock of §2.5 as every persisted timestamp rather than from a wall clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` is an integer with a default of 10, a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is an integer with a default of 0 and a minimum of 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first — on every list endpoint, including the penalty ledger, whose chronological ordering is load-bearing rather than cosmetic. Because the logical clock is held still across a burst of writes, `created_at` ties are ordinary; rows sharing a `created_at` are ordered by server-side insertion sequence, so the earlier-created row still comes first. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows that match the supplied filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` set true when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and Version Discipline

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers are bare UUIDs with no type prefix and no embedded semantics; in particular a penalty ledger entry's `id` must never decide a business outcome, because ledger ordering is by `created_at` alone. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, read from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1. `PlanYear`, `Vendor`, `ServiceProviderContract`, `ContractAmendment`, `CompensationDisclosure`, `MachineReadableFile`, `RefreshCycle`, `PriceComparisonTool`, `RxDataSubmission`, `VendorDataPackage`, `Attestation` and `RemediationPlan` all carry both. `PenaltyLedgerEntry` is the one exception: a ledger entry carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is immutable — it is written once and never updated or deleted.

Version semantics are precise and matter to callers. A resource's `version` increments by exactly 1 on each mutation that changes at least one stored field, and `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock at the same moment. A recomputation that leaves every stored field equal to its prior value — a posture recompute that yields the same posture and the same point total, for example — is not a mutation: it neither increments `version` nor refreshes `updated_at`. Because a single request can cascade across several resources, a caller who needs a fresh parent version reads the parent immediately before issuing a create that carries a version expectation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Interval Conventions, and Deadline Comparisons

There is no wall clock in this service. A single server-side logical clock supplies every timestamp written to a resource, every day count, every month boundary and every deadline comparison. At startup the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PlatformOperator` moves it with a test-driving capability that accepts a `current_utc` instant and echoes back the new `current_utc`. The clock may be moved forward or held at its present value; a `current_utc` strictly earlier than the clock's present value is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409), so that day counts and deadline comparisons remain monotonic across a test run.

Three different interval conventions coexist in this domain, and confusing them produces wrong answers rather than obvious failures.

- A **contract term** runs from `term_start` to `term_end` as a half-open interval: the first day of the term is included, the end date is exclusive. `term_end` must be strictly after `term_start`.
- A **refresh period** runs from `period_start` to `period_end` as a closed interval: both endpoints are inside the period. Two refresh cycles of the same plan year therefore collide when one period's start falls on or before the other period's end and the second period's start falls on or before the first period's end — which means two cycles that share a single boundary day *do* overlap, and the second is refused with `ERR_REFRESH_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).
- An **outage window** runs from `window_start` to `window_end` as a half-open interval. A window that begins at exactly the instant another ends does not overlap it and is accepted; anything with genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The same half-open convention makes two touching windows one contiguous stretch of downtime when uptime is scored.

Deadlines are compared at millisecond resolution and are inclusive. A refresh cycle certified at exactly its `deadline_at` instant is on time; one millisecond later it is late. An amendment acted on at exactly its `expires_at` instant is still live; one millisecond later it has expired. Interval inputs that are malformed — a plan year whose `end_date` is not strictly after its `start_date`, a contract whose `term_end` is not strictly after `term_start`, a refresh cycle whose `period_end` precedes `period_start` or whose period falls outside the span from the plan year's `start_date` through its `end_date`, an outage window whose `window_end` is not strictly after `window_start`, or an amendment whose `expires_at` is not strictly after the logical clock — are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422).

### 2.6. Money, Points, and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this domain are integer counts of cents and are named accordingly: `annual_fee_cents`, `direct_comp_cents`, `indirect_comp_cents`, `benchmark_median_cents`, `penalty_exposure_cents`, `signed_amount_cents`, `daily_rate_cents`, `plan_paid_cents`, `member_paid_cents`, `rebate_cents`, `gross_spend_cents`, `rebate_reported_cents`, `rebate_credit_cents`, `net_plan_spend_cents` and `abatement_posted_cents`. No monetary field is ever a decimal.

Wherever rounding is required it is **round half up** — a tie rounds away from zero. Penalty amounts, abatement amounts and capped rebate credits are rounded half up to whole cents. Uptime percentages and the three compensation score components are rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. Deficiency point arithmetic is pure integer arithmetic with no rounding at all, and ledger balances are exact integer sums with no rounding at all.

The order in which rounding is applied is pinned in every calculation and is not an implementation detail. Where a specification says a multiplier is applied once to a summed raw figure and the result is then rounded, applying the multiplier and rounding per component instead produces a different, wrong number. Where a specification says three components are each rounded to two decimals and then added, summing the exact components and rounding once at the end produces a different, wrong number. §3 states the pinned order for each calculation explicitly.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Creation

Exactly two write operations in this service carry an optimistic-concurrency guard, and both are creates whose whole purpose is to displace an existing record.

Creating an attestation requires a non-optional `expected_plan_year_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PlanYear`. If that value does not equal the plan year's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_YEAR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a new draft attestation legitimately supersedes the prior draft for the same plan year — the create cannot be refused on state grounds, so the version check is what stops a writer from voiding a draft it never saw, and from attesting against a compliance posture snapshot that has since moved.

Creating a contract amendment requires a non-optional `expected_contract_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ServiceProviderContract`. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the create is state-legal: a new amendment of the same `amendment_type` legitimately withdraws the prior open one. The version check stops a writer from withdrawing a negotiation round it never observed, and from proposing terms against a `data_rights_status` or `gag_review_status` that has already changed.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version-expectation field. In particular, none of the transition actions — activating, certifying, executing, submitting, accepting, rejecting, delivering, validating, reversing or completing — take one.

### 2.8. Compliance Posture: Deficiency Points, Thresholds, and the Frozen Lockdown

A plan year's `compliance_posture` is never set by a caller. It is recomputed from `deficiency_points`, which is itself recomputed from the live state of the plan year's children after every mutation that opens or closes a deficiency and on every compliance run. Seven weighted contributions are summed:

- three points for each refresh cycle of the plan year whose `status` is `MISSED`;
- two points for each refresh cycle whose `status` is `LATE_CERTIFIED`;
- six points for each service provider contract of the plan year whose `gag_review_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT`;
- two points for each price comparison tool of the plan year whose `sla_breach_open` is true;
- five points for each prescription-drug data submission of the plan year whose `status` is anything other than `SUBMITTED`, counted only when the logical clock date is strictly after the plan year's `rxdc_deadline_date`;
- five points once for the plan year when `attestation_status` is `REJECTED`, or when `attestation_status` is anything other than `ACCEPTED` and the logical clock date is strictly after `attestation_deadline_date` — this contribution is either five or zero, never a multiple;
- three points for each compensation disclosure whose `status` is `UNREASONABLE`, counting disclosures reached through the plan year's contracts.

The posture ladder is then evaluated top down, and both thresholds are inclusive. If `freeze_flag` is true, or `deficiency_points` is 12 or more, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if `deficiency_points` is 6 or more, the posture is `DEFICIENT`. Otherwise, if `deficiency_points` is at least 1, the posture is `AT_RISK`. Otherwise the posture is `COMPLIANT`. A plan year starts life with `deficiency_points` of 0 and a posture of `COMPLIANT`. Exactly 6 points is `DEFICIENT`; exactly 12 is `FROZEN`. The same recomputation also refreshes `tool_sla_breach_count`, which is simply the number of price comparison tools of the plan year whose `sla_breach_open` is true. The draft-voiding consequence described in §3.8 fires only when a recomputation actually *changes* the posture into `DEFICIENT` or `FROZEN`; a recomputation that finds the plan year already sitting at `DEFICIENT` or `FROZEN` voids nothing further.

Money and deficiency state are deliberately independent axes. `penalty_exposure_cents` tracks what has been posted to the ledger; `deficiency_points` tracks what is currently wrong. An abatement lowers exposure without lowering points, and closing a deficiency lowers points without lowering exposure. Neither number is derivable from the other.

A posture of `FROZEN` is a lockdown, not a label. While the owning plan year's `compliance_posture` is `FROZEN`, every mutating endpoint scoped to that plan year is refused with `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). Five operations are exempt, because the whole point of a freeze is to allow the sponsor to dig out from under it: unfreezing the plan year, reversing a penalty ledger entry, creating a remediation plan, recording a milestone completion on a remediation plan, and completing a remediation plan. Every other mutation — activating, closing reporting, creating a service provider contract, staging or validating or revising a machine-readable file, opening or certifying a refresh cycle, registering or launching a tool, appending an outage window, running an evaluation, reviewing or remediating or clearing or escalating a contract's gag status, proposing or countering or revising or executing an amendment, recording or assessing a compensation disclosure, opening or reconciling or filing a prescription-drug submission, requesting or delivering or validating or rejecting or resubmitting a data package, creating or submitting or accepting or rejecting or revising an attestation, and triggering a compliance run — is refused.

### 2.9. The Rejection Ladder

When more than one thing is wrong with a request, the response is determined by the **first** failing check in the ordering below. No later check may pre-empt an earlier one, and the ordering is total across the whole service. The ladder is stated once here; §3 restates individual conditions in the business context where they matter.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** `X-User-Role` present and listed for the endpoint, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Path existence.** Every `{id}` path parameter resolves to an existing row of the addressed collection, on every endpoint that carries one — otherwise the entity-specific 404: `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MRF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFRESH_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RX_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REMEDIATION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`.
4. **Body schema.** On every write endpoint, the request body contains each required field with the declared type; a required string field is non-empty; enum fields carry a listed member; monetary and count fields are integers (a boolean or a decimal is not an integer); date fields are ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`; datetime fields are ISO 8601 with a `Z` offset; month fields are four digits, a hyphen, then `01` through `12`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The same code and status also cover the numeric-floor checks that §3 states without naming a code — `covered_lives` at least 1, `annual_fee_cents` at least 0, `covered_items_count` at least 0, `expected_record_count` at least 0, `contract_terms_doc_count` at least 0 at creation, and `milestone_count` at least 1 — each of which is evaluated at this rung, ahead of the referential-integrity rung below.
5. **Pagination.** On every list endpoint, `limit` is an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
6. **Referential integrity.** Every foreign key supplied in a request body or as a list filter — `plan_year_id`, `vendor_id`, `contract_id`, `rx_submission_id`, `reverses_entry_id`, `remediation_plan_id` — resolves to an existing row, on every create endpoint and every list endpoint that filters on a foreign key. Otherwise the same entity-specific 404 codes as step 3.
7. **Vendor scope.** A `VendorContact` may act only on rows whose owning `vendor_id` equals `X-User-ID`. This applies to gag-remediation submission, amendment counter, package delivery and package resubmission — four operations. Cross-vendor access is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403) even though the role itself is permitted on the endpoint.
8. **Concurrency.** Where the endpoint declares a version expectation, the supplied value equals the parent's current `version`. This applies to attestation creation and contract-amendment creation — two endpoints — yielding `ERR_STALE_PLAN_YEAR_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` respectively (412).
9. **Frozen plan year.** The owning plan year's `compliance_posture` is not `FROZEN`, on every mutating endpoint scoped to a plan year except the five exemptions named in §2.8 — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_FROZEN` (409).
10. **State legality.** The addressed resource is in a state from which the requested transition or state-requiring action is declared, including any parent `lifecycle_status` precondition — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).
11. **Posture gate on attestation submission.** An attestation may not be submitted while the plan year's `compliance_posture` is `DEFICIENT`; `FROZEN` has already been refused at step 9. Because this gate outranks segregation of duties, a deficient plan year returns `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_ATTESTATION` (409) even when the submitter is also the preparer.
12. **Segregation of duties.** The submitting `X-User-ID` differs from `prepared_by_user_id`; the accepting or rejecting auditor differs from both `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id`. This applies to attestation submission, acceptance and rejection — three operations — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
13. **Outstanding gag clauses.** On attestation submission only: no service provider contract of the plan year whose `contract_type` is `TPA`, `PBM`, `BROKER` or `CONSULTANT` may have a `gag_review_status` other than `COMPLIANT` — otherwise `ERR_GAG_CLAUSES_OUTSTANDING` (409).
14. **Contract-terms evidence.** `contract_terms_doc_count` is at least 1, on attestation submission and attestation revision — two operations — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMS_DOC_REQUIRED` (422).
15. **Published files for the period.** On refresh cycle certification only: a machine-readable file in `status` `PUBLISHED` exists for the same `plan_year_id` with `posting_month` equal to the cycle's `period_month`, for each of the two `file_type` values `IN_NETWORK_RATES` and `ALLOWED_AMOUNTS` — otherwise `ERR_MRF_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
16. **Secured data rights.** On package delivery and package resubmission — two operations — a service provider contract exists for the submission's `plan_year_id` and the package's `vendor_id` whose `data_rights_status` is `SECURED`, otherwise `ERR_DATA_RIGHTS_NOT_SECURED` (409).
17. **Validated package set.** On prescription-drug reconciliation and filing — two operations — the submission has at least one vendor data package and every one of them has `status` `VALIDATED`, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGES_NOT_VALIDATED` (409).
18. **TPA contract present.** On plan year activation only: at least one service provider contract of the plan year has `contract_type` `TPA`, otherwise `ERR_TPA_CONTRACT_REQUIRED` (409).
19. **Interval shape.** The interval rules of §2.5 hold, on plan year creation, contract creation, refresh cycle creation, outage window append and amendment creation — five operations — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422).
20. **Refresh period collision.** On refresh cycle creation only: the closed period does not intersect a sibling cycle's closed period, otherwise `ERR_REFRESH_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
21. **Outage window collision.** On outage window append only: the half-open window does not intersect an existing window of the same tool, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Because this check runs after step 19, a window that is both inverted and overlapping returns `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.
22. **Outage window range.** On outage window append only: `window_end` is at or before the logical clock and `window_start` is at or after the tool's `launched_at`, otherwise `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422).
23. **Elapsed evaluation month.** On tool evaluation only: `evaluation_month` is strictly before the logical clock's month and is not earlier than the month of `launched_at`, otherwise `ERR_EVALUATION_MONTH_NOT_ELAPSED` (422).
24. **Reporting period ended.** On close-reporting only: the logical clock date is strictly after the plan year's `end_date`, otherwise `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (409).
25. **Amendment not expired.** On amendment counter, revise-proposal and execute — three operations — the logical clock is at or before the amendment's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_AMENDMENT_EXPIRED` (409). This applies even while the amendment is still recorded as `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` because no compliance run has swept it yet, and it outranks the round-exhaustion check at step 27.
26. **Data rights actually granted.** On amendment execution only: an amendment whose `amendment_type` is `DATA_RIGHTS` has `data_rights_granted` true, otherwise `ERR_DATA_RIGHTS_NOT_GRANTED` (422).
27. **Negotiation rounds remaining.** On revise-proposal only: `round` is less than 3, otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409).
28. **Remediation rounds remaining.** On gag remediation request only: `remediation_round` is less than 2, otherwise `ERR_REMEDIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409).
29. **Clause count consistent with the action.** On gag clearance and gag escalation — two operations — clearance requires `prohibited_clause_count` of 0 and escalation requires `prohibited_clause_count` of at least 1, otherwise `ERR_PROHIBITED_CLAUSES_PRESENT` (409).
30. **Covered items sufficient.** On tool launch only: `covered_items_count` is at least 500, otherwise `ERR_COVERED_ITEMS_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
31. **Disclosure applicable.** On compensation disclosure creation only: the target contract's `compensation_disclosure_required` is true, otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_APPLICABLE` (409).
32. **Benchmark and score inputs.** On compensation disclosure creation only: `benchmark_median_cents` is at least 1 because it is a divisor, `services_scope_score` and `disclosure_completeness_score` are integers from 1 through 5 inclusive, and `direct_comp_cents` and `indirect_comp_cents` are each at least 0. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BENCHMARK` (422).
33. **Package totals.** On package delivery and package resubmission the supplied `record_count` is at least 1 and every supplied monetary total is at least 0; on package validation, where nothing is supplied, only the already-stored `record_count` is re-checked to be at least 1. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_TOTALS` (422).
34. **Reversible entry.** On ledger reversal only: the addressed entry's `entry_type` is `ACCRUAL` and the entry is not itself a reversal, otherwise `ERR_NOT_REVERSIBLE_ENTRY` (409).
35. **Not already reversed.** On ledger reversal only: no existing ledger entry carries a `reverses_entry_id` equal to the addressed entry's `id`, otherwise `ERR_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED` (409).
36. **Accrual available to abate.** On remediation plan completion only: at least one unreversed `ACCRUAL` entry of the plan's `target_deficiency_type` exists on the plan year, otherwise `ERR_NO_ACCRUAL_TO_REMEDIATE` (409).
37. **Milestone accounting.** On remediation plan completion and milestone completion — two operations — completion requires `completed_milestone_count` equal to `milestone_count`, and a milestone recorded beyond `milestone_count` is refused. Either way the code is `ERR_MILESTONES_INCOMPLETE` (409).
38. **Projection date in range.** On the penalty projection read only: a supplied `as_of_date` is at or after the plan year's `start_date` and at or before its `end_date`, otherwise `ERR_PROJECTION_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422).
39. **Machine-readable file inputs.** Each of the three operations checks exactly the inputs it carries: staging checks that `posting_month` falls within the months spanned by the plan year's `start_date` through `end_date` and that `row_count` is at least 1; validation checks that `validation_error_count` is at least 0; revision, which resupplies only `public_url` and `row_count`, checks that `row_count` is at least 1. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MRF_INPUT` (422).
40. **Vendor identity resolves.** On every endpoint reachable by `VendorContact`: the presented `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing vendor row, otherwise `ERR_ROLE_ENTITY_MISMATCH` (403). Step 7 pre-empts this rule whenever the addressed row belongs to a different vendor.
41. **Plan year label unique.** On plan year creation only: `plan_year_label` is not already used by another plan year, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_YEAR_LABEL` (409).
42. **One package per data section.** On package creation only: no package already exists for the same combination of `rx_submission_id`, `vendor_id` and `data_section`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DATA_SECTION` (409).
43. **Clause counts internally consistent.** On gag review and gag-remediation submission — two operations — `prohibited_clause_count` does not exceed `clause_inventory_count` and neither count is negative, otherwise `ERR_CLAUSE_COUNT_INCONSISTENT` (422).
44. **Reference year aligned.** On prescription-drug submission creation only: `reference_year` equals the calendar year of the plan year's `start_date` or the year immediately before it, otherwise `ERR_RX_REFERENCE_YEAR_INVALID` (422).
45. **Clock monotonicity.** On the clock-setting capability only: `current_utc` is not strictly earlier than the clock's present value, otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (409).

### 2.10. Flat Addressing and Reverse Reachability

Every resource is a peer addressed directly under `/compliance` by its own `id`; nothing is nested behind a parent path segment. Parent-child relationships travel instead through foreign-key fields in request bodies and through optional filters on the corresponding list endpoint, so any parent's children can be enumerated in one read. Where a path does carry more than one segment of hierarchy, path parameters are validated left to right, so the outer identifier's 404 wins over the inner one's.

The available filters are the natural ones. Plan years filter on `plan_year_label`, `lifecycle_status` and `compliance_posture`. Vendors filter on `vendor_type`. Contracts filter on `plan_year_id`, `vendor_id`, `contract_type`, `gag_review_status` and `data_rights_status`. Amendments filter on `contract_id`, `amendment_type` and `status`. Compensation disclosures filter on `contract_id`, `status` and `variant_applied`. Machine-readable files filter on `plan_year_id`, `file_type`, `posting_month` and `status`. Refresh cycles filter on `plan_year_id`, `status` and `period_month`. Price comparison tools filter on `plan_year_id`, `status` and `sla_breach_open`. Prescription-drug submissions filter on `plan_year_id`, `status` and `reference_year`. Vendor data packages filter on `rx_submission_id`, `vendor_id`, `data_section` and `status`. Penalty ledger entries filter on `plan_year_id`, `entry_type`, `deficiency_type`, `reverses_entry_id` and `remediation_plan_id`. Remediation plans filter on `plan_year_id`, `status` and `target_deficiency_type`. Every one of these filters is optional, and every list endpoint additionally accepts `limit` and `offset`. Filters match on exact equality against the stored field. Only the foreign-key filters named in §2.9 step 6 are validated: a `plan_year_id`, `vendor_id`, `contract_id`, `rx_submission_id`, `reverses_entry_id` or `remediation_plan_id` that resolves to nothing is a 404. Every other filter is unvalidated — an unrecognised enum member, an unparseable `reference_year`, or an `sla_breach_open` value that is not one of `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` simply matches no row and yields an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than an error.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Plan Year and Its Service Provider Roster

A compliance program begins when a `PlanSponsorAdmin` records the plan year itself. The creation payload names the employer in `sponsor_name`, gives the year a human label in `plan_year_label`, and bounds it with `start_date` and `end_date` as ISO calendar dates; `end_date` must fall strictly after `start_date`. Two regulatory deadlines are captured up front rather than derived: `attestation_deadline_date` is the ISO date by which the annual gag-clause attestation must have reached `ACCEPTED`, and `rxdc_deadline_date` is the ISO date by which the prescription-drug data submission must have reached `SUBMITTED`. The payload also carries `covered_lives`, the enrolled headcount, which must be at least 1 and which later drives every penalty amount through the size multiplier. Labels are a registry key rather than decoration: a second plan year reusing an existing `plan_year_label` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_YEAR_LABEL`.

A newly created plan year is inert. Its `lifecycle_status` is `SETUP`, its `compliance_posture` is `COMPLIANT`, its `deficiency_points`, `penalty_exposure_cents` and `tool_sla_breach_count` are all 0, its `attestation_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, its `freeze_flag` is false, and `freeze_reason` and `last_compliance_run_at` are both null.

Vendors are registered independently of any plan year, because the same third party typically serves several. Registration records the service provider's `legal_name`, a `vendor_type` drawn from `TPA`, `PBM`, `BROKER`, `CONSULTANT` and `NETWORK_VENDOR`, and a `contact_email` mailbox. Registering a vendor grants nothing by itself — a vendor becomes relevant to a plan year only once a contract links the two.

Contracts are where the roster actually forms. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` creates a service provider contract with a payload containing the `plan_year_id` it serves and the `vendor_id` it engages, a `contract_type` drawn from the same five categories, the term bounds `term_start` and `term_end`, and the contracted direct fee in `annual_fee_cents`, which must be at least 0. Every new contract starts with its gag dimension at `NOT_REVIEWED` and its data-rights dimension at `NOT_SECURED`; `clause_inventory_count`, `prohibited_clause_count` and `remediation_round` are all 0 and `gag_non_compliant_since` is null. One field on the contract is never supplied and never editable: `compensation_disclosure_required` is computed to be true exactly when `contract_type` is `BROKER` or `CONSULTANT`, and false for `TPA`, `PBM` and `NETWORK_VENDOR`. That single boolean is what decides whether a compensation disclosure can exist against the contract at all.

Activation is the sponsor's declaration that the plan year is live. It moves `lifecycle_status` from `SETUP` to `ACTIVE`, and it is refused unless the plan year is currently in `SETUP`, its `compliance_posture` is not `FROZEN`, and at least one service provider contract of that plan year has `contract_type` `TPA` — the third-party administrator is the counterparty through whom most transparency data actually flows, so a plan year with no TPA on the roster is refused with `ERR_TPA_CONTRACT_REQUIRED`. Activation increments the plan year's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`. The `ACTIVE` lifecycle then becomes a precondition for four downstream operations: certifying a refresh cycle, launching a price comparison tool, opening or filing a prescription-drug data submission, and submitting an attestation. Attempting any of them while the plan year is still in `SETUP` — or already in `REPORTING_CLOSED` — yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

At the other end, closing reporting moves `lifecycle_status` from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `REPORTING_CLOSED`. This is refused unless the plan year is `ACTIVE`, its posture is not `FROZEN`, and the logical clock date is strictly after `end_date`; closing on the last day of the plan year itself is refused with `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. Closing increments the plan year's `version` by 1 and recomputes `compliance_posture` exactly once, so that the closing snapshot reflects any deficiency that accrued on the plan year's final day rather than a stale posture from the previous mutation.

### 3.2. Publishing Machine-Readable Files and Certifying the Monthly Refresh

Two machine-readable files must be posted publicly each month: the negotiated in-network rates and the out-of-network allowed amounts. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` stages one by supplying the `plan_year_id` it discloses, a `file_type` of either `IN_NETWORK_RATES` or `ALLOWED_AMOUNTS`, the `posting_month` in four-digit-year-hyphen-two-digit-month form, the `public_url` at which the payload is reachable over https, the `row_count` of negotiated rate or allowed amount rows it contains, and the CMS `schema_version` string of the payload. `public_url` and `schema_version` are stored verbatim as any non-empty string; neither the URL scheme nor the schema identifier is parsed or checked against a permitted set. A staged file lands in `DRAFT` with `validation_error_count`, `published_at` and `superseded_at` all null. Staging rejects a `posting_month` that falls outside the months spanned by the plan year's `start_date` through `end_date`, and a `row_count` below 1, with `ERR_INVALID_MRF_INPUT`.

Validation is where a draft becomes a public posting. The sponsor submits the schema error count in `validation_error_count`, and the file moves `DRAFT → VALIDATING` while that count is stored, incrementing the file's `version` by 1. `VALIDATING` is an instantaneous intermediate state: the very same request continues on to a terminal outcome before it responds, so a caller never observes a file sitting in `VALIDATING`. When `validation_error_count` is 0 the file continues `VALIDATING → PUBLISHED`, stamping `published_at` with the logical clock and incrementing `version` by 1 again. When `validation_error_count` is 1 or more the file continues `VALIDATING → REJECTED`, `published_at` stays null, and `version` increments by 1. A negative `validation_error_count` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MRF_INPUT` before any transition occurs.

Publication carries one cross-resource consequence that is easy to miss. Reaching `PUBLISHED` displaces the incumbent: any *other* machine-readable file sharing the same `plan_year_id`, the same `file_type` and the same `posting_month` and currently in `PUBLISHED` transitions `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`, has its `superseded_at` stamped with the same logical clock instant, and has its own `version` incremented by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal; a superseded posting is history and cannot be revived. A rejected file, by contrast, is re-enterable: revision moves it `REJECTED → DRAFT`, replaces `public_url` and `row_count` from the request, resets `validation_error_count` back to null, and increments `version` by 1. A revision resupplies only those two fields, so the row-count check applies exactly as it did at staging while `posting_month` — which a revision cannot change — is not re-examined.

The monthly refresh obligation is tracked separately from the files themselves, because the obligation exists whether or not anyone posted anything. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` opens a refresh cycle with the `plan_year_id`, the `period_month` the cycle covers, and the closed period bounds `period_start` and `period_end`. The cycle's `period_month` is the key that ties it to the files certified: certification looks for postings whose `posting_month` equals the cycle's `period_month`. The service derives `deadline_at` on creation rather than accepting it: it is the instant five days after `period_end`, at 23:59:59.999Z on that fifth day — a five-day grace window on top of the period itself. A cycle opens in `PENDING` with `certified_at`, `certified_by_user_id`, `certification_note` and `on_time` all null. Because refresh periods are closed intervals, a new cycle whose period touches or overlaps a sibling cycle's period on even a single boundary day is refused with `ERR_REFRESH_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, and a period that falls outside the plan year's own span, or whose `period_end` precedes `period_start`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.

Only a `ComplianceOfficer` certifies, optionally attaching a `certification_note`. Certification requires the cycle to be `PENDING`, the plan year to be `ACTIVE` with a posture other than `FROZEN`, and — critically — a `PUBLISHED` machine-readable file to exist for that `plan_year_id` and that `posting_month` for *each* of the two required `file_type` values. One of the two present and the other missing is still `ERR_MRF_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Given all of that, the outcome is decided purely by the clock against the derived deadline, compared at millisecond resolution with an inclusive boundary. Certifying at an instant at or before `deadline_at` moves the cycle `PENDING → CERTIFIED`, stamps `certified_at` with the clock, records the certifying officer's `X-User-ID` in `certified_by_user_id`, sets `on_time` to true, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. Certifying at any instant after `deadline_at` moves it `PENDING → LATE_CERTIFIED` instead, stamps `certified_at`, sets `on_time` to false, increments `version` by 1, and additionally recomputes the plan year's `deficiency_points` and `compliance_posture` — a late-certified cycle carries two points for as long as it exists. Certifying at exactly `deadline_at` is on time; one millisecond later it is late. A cycle that is never certified is swept to the terminal `MISSED` state by a compliance run, as §3.9 describes, and a `MISSED` cycle's `on_time` stays null because it was never certified at all.

### 3.3. Keeping the Price Comparison Tool Available

Members must be able to look up what a covered item or service will cost them, and the sponsor is on the hook for the tool's availability. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` registers one with the `plan_year_id` it serves, a consumer-facing `tool_name`, the `covered_items_count` of items and services it prices — at least 0 at registration — and optionally an availability target in `sla_target_uptime_pct`, expressed as a percentage carrying two decimal places and defaulting to 99.00 when omitted. That target is accepted as any JSON number — integer or decimal — and stored as supplied; it is not range-checked against 0 or 100. A registered tool sits in `PENDING_LAUNCH` with `launched_at` null, an empty `outage_windows` array, and `last_evaluated_month`, `last_uptime_pct`, `last_downtime_minutes` and `breach_onset_date` all null while `sla_breach_open` is false.

Launching moves the tool `PENDING_LAUNCH → LIVE` and stamps `launched_at` with the logical clock, incrementing `version` by 1. Launch requires the plan year to be `ACTIVE` and not frozen, and it requires real breadth of coverage: a tool pricing fewer than 500 covered items and services is refused with `ERR_COVERED_ITEMS_INSUFFICIENT`. The threshold is on the currently recorded `covered_items_count`, so a thin tool must be re-registered with a fuller catalogue rather than launched and backfilled.

Outages are reported, not inferred. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` or a `PlatformOperator` appends one record to the tool's `outage_windows` array by supplying `window_start`, `window_end` and a `cause` string; each stored element is an object holding exactly those three keys. The array is append-only — there is no edit and no delete — so the outage history of a tool is a permanent record. Three checks guard the append, in this order. First the window's own shape: `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or the append is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Then collision: because windows are half-open, a window beginning at exactly the instant an existing window ends is accepted, while any genuine intersection with an existing window of the same tool is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Finally range: the window must lie entirely in observed history and inside the tool's operating life, meaning `window_end` at or before the logical clock and `window_start` at or after `launched_at`, or the append is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The ordering matters — a window that is both inverted and overlapping reports the shape error, not the collision error. The tool's own `status` is never a precondition for the append: a tool still in `PENDING_LAUNCH` has a null `launched_at`, so it fails this same range check with `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_OUT_OF_RANGE` rather than with a state-transition error.

Availability is scored one whole month at a time, by a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformOperator`, against a supplied `evaluation_month`. Only a `LIVE` tool can be evaluated; scoring a tool still in `PENDING_LAUNCH` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` before the month is examined at all. A month still in progress cannot be scored and neither can a month before the tool existed: an `evaluation_month` that is the clock's own month or any later month, or that precedes the month containing `launched_at`, is refused with `ERR_EVALUATION_MONTH_NOT_ELAPSED`.

The uptime computation proceeds in a fixed order. The month's window runs from the first instant of `evaluation_month` in UTC up to, but not including, the first instant of the following month. The month's total capacity, `month_minutes`, is the number of calendar days in `evaluation_month` multiplied by 1440 minutes per day — so a thirty-day month offers 43,200 minutes and a thirty-one-day month offers 44,640. Every recorded outage window is then clipped to that month's window and any window whose clipped result is empty is discarded. The surviving clipped windows are sorted by `window_start` ascending, and where two share the same start, by `window_end` ascending. The sorted windows are then merged: a window is merged into the one before it whenever its `window_start` falls at or before the running interval's `window_end` — so windows that overlap *and* windows that merely touch collapse into a single stretch of downtime. Three hours of downtime followed immediately by seventy-two more minutes is one interval of 252 minutes, not two intervals counted separately. `last_downtime_minutes` is the sum of the merged intervals' lengths in whole minutes — summed first in seconds across all merged intervals, then converted to minutes and rounded half up once.

From there, `last_uptime_pct` is 100 multiplied by the month's uptime minutes — that is, `month_minutes` less `last_downtime_minutes` — divided by `month_minutes`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `last_evaluated_month` is set to the evaluated month. The breach test is **strictly** less than: `sla_breach_open` becomes true when `last_uptime_pct` is strictly below `sla_target_uptime_pct`, and a tool landing exactly on its target is compliant. On a thirty-day month with a 99.00 target, 432 minutes of merged downtime yields exactly 99.00 percent and no breach, while 435 minutes yields 98.99 percent and a breach.

The consequences of an evaluation depend on which way the breach flag moves. When `sla_breach_open` flips from false to true, `breach_onset_date` is set to the first day of the month *after* `evaluation_month` — the sponsor is given the breached month itself and the deficiency is dated from the following day one — and the system posts one penalty ledger accrual of `deficiency_type` `TOOL_SLA` with `onset_date` equal to that `breach_onset_date`, priced as §3.9 describes. That posting then cascades: the plan year's `tool_sla_breach_count`, `penalty_exposure_cents`, `deficiency_points` and `compliance_posture` are all recomputed, and if the recomputed posture becomes `DEFICIENT` or `FROZEN`, every draft attestation of the plan year is voided as a further consequence. A further breaching evaluation while `sla_breach_open` is *already* true is not a new onset: the flag stays true, `breach_onset_date` is left exactly where the first breach put it, and no second accrual is posted — only `last_evaluated_month`, `last_uptime_pct` and `last_downtime_minutes` move. When a later evaluation comes back at or above target, `sla_breach_open` flips back to false, `breach_onset_date` is cleared to null, and the tool's two deficiency points fall away — but the accrual already posted to the ledger stays exactly where it is. Only an explicit reversal or a remediation abatement moves money once posted.

### 3.4. Scrubbing Service Provider Contracts for Prohibited Gag Clauses

Every service provider contract carries two entirely independent status dimensions. The gag dimension, `gag_review_status`, tracks whether the contract's language unlawfully restricts the sponsor's access to cost and quality data. The data-rights dimension, `data_rights_status`, tracks whether the sponsor has secured the contractual right to that data and the vendor's cooperation in producing it. Neither dimension constrains the other's transitions; a contract can be gag-`COMPLIANT` while its data rights are still `NOT_SECURED`, and vice versa. This subsection covers the first; §3.5 covers the second.

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens the review by recording the inventory. The review payload carries `clause_inventory_count`, the total number of clauses inventoried, and `prohibited_clause_count`, how many of them prohibit disclosure of cost or quality data, plus an optional `review_note`. Both counts are stored on the contract and the gag dimension moves `NOT_REVIEWED → IN_REVIEW`, incrementing the contract's `version` by 1. A review that claims more prohibited clauses than clauses inventoried, or that supplies a negative count for either, is refused with `ERR_CLAUSE_COUNT_INCONSISTENT`. A contract already past `NOT_REVIEWED` cannot be reviewed a second time from scratch.

From `IN_REVIEW` the officer has three moves, and which are legal depends on the recorded `prohibited_clause_count`.

Requesting remediation moves the contract `IN_REVIEW → REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` and increments `version` by 1. It requires at least one prohibited clause outstanding — requesting remediation on a contract whose `prohibited_clause_count` is 0 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, not with the clause-count code reserved for clearance and escalation — and it requires remediation headroom: `remediation_round` must be strictly below 2. The sponsor gets two remediation loops per contract and no more; a third request is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, at which point the officer must either clear the contract or escalate it. An optional `remediation_request_note` may accompany the request.

The vendor answers. A `VendorContact` acting for the contract's own vendor — its `X-User-ID` must equal the contract's `vendor_id` — submits remediation with a restated `prohibited_clause_count` and an optional `remediation_note`. This moves the contract `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW`, replaces `prohibited_clause_count` with the vendor's figure, increments `remediation_round` by 1, and increments `version` by 1. The restated count is checked against the recorded `clause_inventory_count` exactly as the original review was, so a vendor claiming more prohibited clauses than the inventory holds is refused with `ERR_CLAUSE_COUNT_INCONSISTENT`. This is the cycle edge: `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED → IN_REVIEW → REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` may repeat until `remediation_round` reaches 2.

Clearing ends the review favourably. It moves the contract `IN_REVIEW → COMPLIANT`, which is terminal, and it requires `prohibited_clause_count` to be exactly 0 — clearing a contract that still has prohibited language is refused with `ERR_PROHIBITED_CLAUSES_PRESENT`. Clearance sets `gag_non_compliant_since` back to null, increments the contract's `version` by 1, and recomputes the plan year's `deficiency_points` and `compliance_posture`, since a contract leaving `NON_COMPLIANT` (or never having reached it) removes its six-point weight. An optional `clearance_note` may be recorded.

Escalation ends it unfavourably and is the single most expensive action in this domain. It is reachable from either `IN_REVIEW` or `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` — both edges land on the terminal `NON_COMPLIANT` and both behave identically — and it requires `prohibited_clause_count` of at least 1, so escalating a clean contract is refused with `ERR_PROHIBITED_CLAUSES_PRESENT`. An optional `escalation_note` may be recorded. What follows is a five-link cascade, and every link is observable in the same response.

First, `gag_non_compliant_since` is set to the logical clock's UTC calendar date — this is the onset date of the `GAG_CLAUSE` deficiency — and the contract's `version` increments by 1. Second, because the gag dimension has reached `NON_COMPLIANT`, the system appends one penalty ledger accrual with `entry_type` `ACCRUAL`, `deficiency_type` `GAG_CLAUSE` and `onset_date` equal to that `gag_non_compliant_since` date, priced by the accrual formula of §3.9. Third, the newly posted entry triggers recomputation of the plan year's `penalty_exposure_cents` from the ledger and of its `deficiency_points` from live child state. Fourth, because a single non-compliant contract carries six points and the deficient threshold is six, the recomputed points flip `compliance_posture` to `DEFICIENT` — from `COMPLIANT` in one step if this was the plan year's only deficiency, or from `AT_RISK` if minor deficiencies were already open. Fifth, the posture flip itself voids every attestation of that plan year still in `DRAFT`, moving each `DRAFT → VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `POSTURE_DEFICIENT` and each attestation's `version` incremented by 1, and refreshes the plan year's `attestation_status` from its latest attestation by `created_at`. A single escalation therefore costs the sponsor a ledger accrual, a posture downgrade and a destroyed attestation draft in one request.

### 3.5. Negotiating Amendments to Secure Data Rights and Cooperation

The data-rights dimension moves only through amendments. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` proposes one with a payload naming the target `contract_id`, an `amendment_type` drawn from `DATA_RIGHTS`, `GAG_CLAUSE_REMOVAL` and `COOPERATION_SLA`, the sponsor's opening position in `proposed_terms_summary`, a boolean `data_rights_granted` stating whether the current proposal actually grants the sponsor data rights and cooperation, an `expires_at` instant after which the proposal lapses if unexecuted, and the stale-version guard `expected_contract_version` described in §2.7. An `expires_at` that is not strictly after the logical clock is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` — there is no point proposing terms that are already dead. A new amendment opens in `PROPOSED` at `round` 1, with `counter_terms_summary`, `executed_at`, `executed_terms_note` and `withdrawal_reason` all null.

Proposing has two side effects beyond the create itself. Any *prior* amendment of the same `contract_id` and the same `amendment_type` still sitting in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` is superseded: it moves to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state, its `withdrawal_reason` is set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_AMENDMENT`, and its `version` increments by 1. Separately, if the new amendment's `amendment_type` is `DATA_RIGHTS` and the contract's `data_rights_status` is `NOT_SECURED`, that dimension advances `NOT_SECURED → PENDING_AMENDMENT` and the contract's `version` increments by 1 — which is precisely what makes a second create carrying the pre-create contract version stale.

Negotiation is a two-party loop with a hard round cap. A `VendorContact` acting for the contract's own vendor counters a proposal by supplying `counter_terms_summary`; the amendment moves `PROPOSED → COUNTERED`, the counter text is stored, and `version` increments by 1. The sponsor answers a counter by revising, supplying a fresh `proposed_terms_summary` and a fresh `data_rights_granted`; the amendment moves `COUNTERED → PROPOSED`, both fields are replaced from the request, `round` increments by 1, and `version` increments by 1. Revision is refused once `round` has reached 3 — `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` — so a single amendment supports at most three sponsor positions before it must be executed, abandoned, or superseded by a fresh proposal. Both parties are also bound by the clock: a counter, a revision or an execution attempted after the amendment's `expires_at` is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_EXPIRED`, and this temporal refusal outranks the round cap, so an expired amendment sitting at `round` 3 reports the expiry rather than the exhaustion. Expiry is enforced on the action even while the amendment is still recorded as `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` because no compliance run has yet swept it.

Execution is the payoff, and only a `PROPOSED` amendment can be executed — a countered proposal must be revised back to `PROPOSED` first. Execution stamps `executed_at` with the logical clock, stores an optional `executed_terms_note`, moves the amendment to the terminal `EXECUTED`, and increments its `version` by 1. What it does to the contract depends on `amendment_type`. A `DATA_RIGHTS` execution advances the contract's data-rights dimension `PENDING_AMENDMENT → SECURED` and increments the contract's `version` by 1, and from that moment the vendor's prescription-drug data deliveries for that plan year are permitted. The execution simply writes `SECURED` from whatever the dimension currently holds — it is never refused because the dimension sat at `NOT_SECURED` instead of `PENDING_AMENDMENT` — and if the dimension already read `SECURED` no stored field changes, so the contract's `version` stays put. Such an amendment may not be executed at all while `data_rights_granted` is false: the entire purpose of the instrument is to grant those rights, so the attempt is refused with `ERR_DATA_RIGHTS_NOT_GRANTED`. A `GAG_CLAUSE_REMOVAL` execution sets the contract's `prohibited_clause_count` to 0 — the offending language has been struck contractually — and increments the contract's `version` by 1, which is what makes a subsequent gag clearance legal; if that count was already 0 nothing stored changes and the contract's `version` is left alone. A `COOPERATION_SLA` execution records the negotiated cooperation terms without touching either contract dimension.

Amendments that no one finishes die on their own schedule, but not spontaneously: the sweep is part of the compliance run described in §3.9. Any amendment in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` whose `expires_at` is strictly earlier than the logical clock is moved to the terminal `LAPSED` state with its `version` incremented by 1. Lapsing is what can walk the data-rights dimension backwards: once the sweep completes, if the contract has no remaining `DATA_RIGHTS` amendment in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` and its `data_rights_status` is `PENDING_AMENDMENT`, that dimension reverts `PENDING_AMENDMENT → NOT_SECURED` and the contract's `version` increments by 1. A contract that had already reached `SECURED` is unaffected — securing data rights is not undone by a later proposal lapsing.

### 3.6. Assembling and Filing Prescription-Drug Data

The prescription-drug data collection filing is an assembly problem: the sponsor must pull spending, totals, rebate and premium-impact sections out of several plan and pharmacy vendors, reconcile them into one set of figures, and file. A `PlanSponsorAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` opens the filing with the `plan_year_id` being reported and the `reference_year` of the data, which must be at least 2020 and must equal either the calendar year of the plan year's `start_date` or the year immediately before it — anything else is refused with `ERR_RX_REFERENCE_YEAR_INVALID`. Opening a submission requires the plan year to be `ACTIVE`. A new submission sits in `OPEN` with `assembled_record_count` 0 and `gross_spend_cents`, `rebate_reported_cents`, `rebate_cap_pct`, `rebate_credit_cents`, `net_plan_spend_cents`, `reconciled_at` and `submitted_at` all null.

Each expected contribution is registered as its own package before any data arrives. The request names the `rx_submission_id` it feeds, the `vendor_id` supplying it, the `data_section` drawn from `D2_SPENDING`, `D6_RX_TOTALS`, `D7_RX_REBATES` and `D8_PREMIUM_IMPACT`, and the `expected_record_count` the sponsor anticipates, which must be at least 0. Each combination of submission, vendor and data section may exist at most once; a duplicate request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DATA_SECTION`. Registration carries no precondition on the parent submission's own `status`, so a package may be requested against a submission sitting in `OPEN`, `ASSEMBLING`, `RECONCILED` or `SUBMITTED` alike. A registered package sits in `EXPECTED` with `record_count`, `plan_paid_cents`, `member_paid_cents`, `rebate_cents`, `rejection_reason` and `validated_at` all null and `delivery_round` at 0.

Delivery belongs to the vendor and is gated on the data-rights work of §3.5. A `VendorContact` whose `X-User-ID` equals the package's `vendor_id` delivers by supplying `record_count`, `plan_paid_cents`, `member_paid_cents` and `rebate_cents`. Before the delivery is accepted, the service looks for a service provider contract joining the submission's `plan_year_id` to the package's `vendor_id` whose `data_rights_status` is `SECURED`; if none exists the delivery is refused with `ERR_DATA_RIGHTS_NOT_SECURED`, which is the concrete reason a sponsor chases amendments in the first place. A delivery must also carry a `record_count` of at least 1 and non-negative monetary totals, or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_TOTALS`. An accepted delivery moves the package `EXPECTED → RECEIVED`, stores all four figures, increments `delivery_round` by 1 and increments the package's `version` by 1. It also bubbles up: if the parent submission is still `OPEN`, the first package to reach `RECEIVED` advances it `OPEN → ASSEMBLING` and increments the submission's `version` by 1.

A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates each delivery. Validation moves a package `RECEIVED → VALIDATED`, stamps `validated_at` with the logical clock, increments the package's `version` by 1, and — this is the conserved quantity in this domain — increases the parent submission's `assembled_record_count` by exactly this package's `record_count`. Validation also re-checks the stored `record_count` — and only that figure, since validation supplies no payload of its own — refusing with `ERR_INVALID_PACKAGE_TOTALS` when it is not at least 1. Rejection moves a package to `REJECTED` with the supplied `rejection_reason` stored and `version` incremented by 1, and it is reachable from either `RECEIVED` or `VALIDATED`. From `RECEIVED` it is cheap: `assembled_record_count` is unchanged, because a merely received package was never counted into it.

From `VALIDATED`, rejection is a four-link cascade. The `rejection_reason` is stored, the package's `status` becomes `REJECTED` and its `version` increments by 1. Because a validated package has left the validated set, the parent submission's `assembled_record_count` decreases by exactly that package's `record_count` — a compensating movement on the conserved record count, not a recomputation from scratch. That decrement leaves the submission without a fully validated package set, so a submission sitting in `RECONCILED` demotes `RECONCILED → ASSEMBLING`, its `version` increments by 1, and `gross_spend_cents`, `rebate_reported_cents`, `rebate_cap_pct`, `rebate_credit_cents`, `net_plan_spend_cents` and `reconciled_at` are all set back to null, because the reconciliation is no longer supported by validated data. Finally, if the logical clock date is strictly after the plan year's `rxdc_deadline_date` and no unreversed `RXDC_LATE` accrual already exists for that plan year, the demotion posts one penalty ledger accrual with `entry_type` `ACCRUAL`, `deficiency_type` `RXDC_LATE` and `onset_date` equal to the day after `rxdc_deadline_date`; that posting recomputes the plan year's `penalty_exposure_cents`, its `deficiency_points` — a submission short of `SUBMITTED` past the deadline carries five points — and its `compliance_posture`.

Rejected packages are re-enterable without limit. The owning `VendorContact` resubmits with corrected `record_count`, `plan_paid_cents`, `member_paid_cents` and `rebate_cents`; the package moves `REJECTED → RECEIVED`, all four values are replaced from the request, `delivery_round` increments by 1, `rejection_reason` is cleared to null, and `version` increments by 1. Resubmission re-checks the same secured-data-rights condition as the original delivery: if the vendor's contract for that plan year has slipped out of `SECURED`, the resubmission is refused with `ERR_DATA_RIGHTS_NOT_SECURED`. The `RECEIVED → REJECTED → RECEIVED` loop may repeat without bound.

Reconciliation is a `ComplianceOfficer` action that moves the submission `ASSEMBLING → RECONCILED`. It requires at least one package to exist and *every* package of the submission to be in `VALIDATED`; a submission with no packages at all, or with any package still in `EXPECTED`, `RECEIVED` or `REJECTED`, is refused with `ERR_PACKAGES_NOT_VALIDATED`. Only validated packages contribute to any total; expected, received and rejected packages are excluded from gross spend, from reported rebates and from the assembled record count alike. Taking the validated packages in `created_at` ascending order, the totals are computed in a pinned order. `gross_spend_cents` is the sum of every validated package's `plan_paid_cents` plus the sum of every validated package's `member_paid_cents`. `rebate_reported_cents` is the sum of every validated package's `rebate_cents`. The cap tier is then selected **from gross spend, before any rebate deduction whatsoever**: `rebate_cap_pct` is 0.25 when `gross_spend_cents` is below 10,000,000 cents, 0.20 when it is at least 10,000,000 cents and below 100,000,000 cents, and 0.15 when it is 100,000,000 cents or more. Choosing the tier from a rebate-net figure picks a different tier and a different credit, and is wrong. `rebate_credit_cents` is the smaller of `rebate_reported_cents` and the product of `rebate_cap_pct` and `gross_spend_cents` rounded half up to whole cents; the rounding happens before the comparison. `net_plan_spend_cents` is `gross_spend_cents` less `rebate_credit_cents`. `reconciled_at` is stamped with the logical clock and the submission's `version` increments by 1. As a worked illustration, two validated packages contributing 6,000,000 and 2,500,000 cents of plan-paid spend, 1,000,000 and 500,000 cents of member-paid spend, and 1,500,000 and 900,000 cents of rebates yield gross spend of 10,000,000 cents, reported rebates of 2,400,000 cents, a cap tier of 0.20, a credit of 2,000,000 cents because the capped figure is the smaller of the two, and net plan spend of 8,000,000 cents.

Filing closes the obligation. A `ComplianceOfficer` moves the submission `RECONCILED → SUBMITTED`, which is terminal, provided the plan year is `ACTIVE`, its posture is not `FROZEN`, and every package is still `VALIDATED` — the validated-set condition is re-checked at filing time and yields `ERR_PACKAGES_NOT_VALIDATED` if it has since broken. Filing stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock, increments the submission's `version` by 1, and recomputes the plan year's `deficiency_points`, because reaching `SUBMITTED` is exactly what closes the `RXDC_LATE` deficiency. Note what closing does *not* do: any `RXDC_LATE` accrual already posted stays on the ledger and `penalty_exposure_cents` is unchanged by the filing.

### 3.7. Receiving and Assessing Broker and Consultant Compensation

Brokers and consultants must disclose what they are paid, and the plan fiduciary must form a documented view on whether that compensation is reasonable for the services rendered. Receipt is recorded by either a `PlanSponsorAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` against the `contract_id` the disclosure covers. Only contracts whose `compensation_disclosure_required` is true — that is, contracts whose `contract_type` is `BROKER` or `CONSULTANT` — can carry a disclosure; a disclosure recorded against a TPA, PBM or network vendor contract is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

The receipt payload carries five figures. `direct_comp_cents` is the direct compensation reported for the plan year and `indirect_comp_cents` is the indirect compensation — carrier overrides, contingent bonuses and similar — both in cents and both at least 0. `benchmark_median_cents` is the market benchmark median for comparable services and must be at least 1, because it is used as a divisor. `services_scope_score` rates the breadth of services delivered and `disclosure_completeness_score` rates how complete the disclosure itself is; both are integers from 1 through 5 inclusive, where 5 means fullest scope and fully complete respectively. Any figure out of range — a benchmark below 1, a score outside 1 through 5, or a negative compensation amount — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BENCHMARK`. A recorded disclosure sits in `RECEIVED` with `variant_applied`, `component_price_score`, `component_scope_score`, `component_disclosure_score`, `unreasonableness_score`, `assessed_at` and `assessed_by_user_id` all null.

Assessment is a `ComplianceOfficer` action, optionally annotated with an `assessment_note`, and it computes an **unreasonableness score** on a 0-to-100 scale where higher is worse. Three weighted components make it up: price carries a weight of 40, breadth of services carries 25, and completeness of disclosure carries 35.

Which compensation figure feeds the price component depends on the parent contract's `contract_type`, and this is the one genuine branch in the calculation. For a `BROKER` contract the variant `TOTAL_WITH_INDIRECT` applies and the compensation base is `direct_comp_cents` plus `indirect_comp_cents`, because a broker is judged on all-in remuneration including carrier-paid overrides. For a `CONSULTANT` contract the variant `DIRECT_ONLY` applies and the compensation base is `direct_comp_cents` alone; `indirect_comp_cents` is still disclosed and still stored, but is deliberately excluded from the reasonableness base. Whichever applies is recorded in `variant_applied`. No other `contract_type` can reach this calculation, because no other `contract_type` can carry a disclosure at all.

From there the arithmetic is fixed. The price ratio is the compensation base divided by `benchmark_median_cents`, held as an exact rational value and never pre-rounded. `component_price_score` is 40 multiplied by that ratio, or by 3.0 if the ratio exceeds 3.0 — compensation more than triple the benchmark is capped rather than allowed to dominate the score — with the product rounded half up to two decimal places. `component_scope_score` is 25 multiplied by the difference of 5 less `services_scope_score`, divided by 3, rounded half up to two decimal places; a full-scope engagement scoring 5 therefore contributes nothing and the narrowest scope contributes the full 25. `component_disclosure_score` is 35 multiplied by the difference of 5 less `disclosure_completeness_score`, divided by 3, rounded half up to two decimal places, on the same principle.

The order of rounding is pinned and it changes answers. Each of the three components is rounded to two decimal places **first**, and `unreasonableness_score` is then the sum of the three already-rounded components. Summing the exact unrounded components and rounding once at the end stores a different score. A consultant disclosure with 541,600 cents of direct compensation against a benchmark of 1,000,000 cents, a `services_scope_score` of 4 and a `disclosure_completeness_score` of 2 produces components of 21.66, 8.33 and 35.00 and a stored score of 64.99 — where the unpinned order would have stored 65.00.

The threshold is 55.00 and the comparison is inclusive. A score of 55.00 or above moves the disclosure `RECEIVED → UNREASONABLE`; anything below moves it `RECEIVED → REASONABLE`. Both are terminal. Both branches store `variant_applied`, all three component scores, `unreasonableness_score`, the assessment instant in `assessed_at` and the assessing officer's `X-User-ID` in `assessed_by_user_id`, and both increment the disclosure's `version` by 1. Where they diverge is the plan year: the `UNREASONABLE` branch recomputes the plan year's `deficiency_points`, adding three points for this disclosure, and recomputes `compliance_posture` — while the `REASONABLE` branch touches no plan year field at all. Consider the same numbers assessed under each variant: a broker with 1,000,000 cents direct, 400,000 cents indirect, a benchmark of 1,000,000 cents, a scope score of 5 and a completeness score of 4 has a base of 1,400,000 cents, a ratio of 1.4, components of 56.00, 0.00 and 11.67, and a score of 67.67 — `UNREASONABLE`. A consultant with identical inputs has a base of 1,000,000 cents, a ratio of 1.0, components of 40.00, 0.00 and 11.67, and a score of 51.67 — `REASONABLE`. And a consultant with 875,000 cents direct against a 1,000,000-cent benchmark, a scope score of 4 and a completeness score of 4 produces 35.00, 8.33 and 11.67, landing exactly on 55.00, which is `UNREASONABLE` because the boundary is inclusive.

### 3.8. Preparing, Submitting and Adjudicating the Annual Attestation

The annual attestation is the sponsor's signed statement that its contracts contain no prohibited gag clauses, and it is the one workflow in this domain where three distinct people must touch the same record. A `ComplianceOfficer` creates the draft with the `plan_year_id` being attested to, an `attestation_type` — `GAG_CLAUSE_CEE` is the only recognised flavour — a `contract_terms_doc_count` giving how many contract-term documents are attached as evidence, which must be at least 0 at creation, and the `expected_plan_year_version` stale-version guard of §2.7. The creating officer's `X-User-ID` is captured in `prepared_by_user_id`, and that field is the segregation-of-duties anchor for everything that follows. A new attestation sits in `DRAFT` with `submitted_by_user_id`, `submitted_at`, `signature_statement`, `rejection_reason` and `void_reason` all null and `revision_round` at 0.

Creating a draft supersedes any earlier one. If the plan year already has an attestation in `DRAFT`, that earlier record moves `DRAFT → VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_DRAFT` and its `version` incremented by 1. The plan year's `attestation_status` field is a mirror of the `status` of its most recently created attestation, ordered by `created_at`, and it starts at `NOT_STARTED` when no attestation exists yet; it is refreshed whenever any attestation of the plan year changes state, so after a supersede it reads `DRAFT` from the new record rather than `VOIDED` from the displaced one. That refresh is an ordinary mutation of the plan year: the first create of a plan year moves the mirror `NOT_STARTED → DRAFT` and therefore increments the plan year's `version` by 1, whereas a create that supersedes an existing draft leaves the mirror reading `DRAFT` and so leaves the plan year's `version` untouched — which is exactly why §2.7 tells a caller to re-read the plan year before each create that carries `expected_plan_year_version`.

Submission is where the segregation of duties bites. A `ComplianceOfficer` submits by supplying a `signature_statement`, and five conditions must hold. The attestation must be in `DRAFT`. The plan year's `lifecycle_status` must be `ACTIVE`. The plan year's `compliance_posture` must be either `COMPLIANT` or `AT_RISK`; a `FROZEN` plan year has already been refused, and a `DEFICIENT` one is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_ATTESTATION` — and because that posture gate outranks the duties check, a deficient plan year returns the posture error even when the submitter happens to be the preparer. Every service provider contract of the plan year whose `contract_type` is `TPA`, `PBM`, `BROKER` or `CONSULTANT` must have a `gag_review_status` of exactly `COMPLIANT`; a single material contract still `NOT_REVIEWED`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` or `NON_COMPLIANT` is enough to refuse the submission with `ERR_GAG_CLAUSES_OUTSTANDING`. Contracts whose `contract_type` is `NETWORK_VENDOR` are not material to the attestation and are not examined. `contract_terms_doc_count` must be at least 1, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMS_DOC_REQUIRED`. And finally the submitting `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A successful submission moves the attestation `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, records the submitter in `submitted_by_user_id`, stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock, stores the `signature_statement`, sets the plan year's `attestation_status` to `SUBMITTED`, and increments both the attestation's `version` and the plan year's `version` by 1 each.

Adjudication belongs to the `ExternalAuditor`, who must differ from both `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` — an auditor who prepared or submitted the record is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — and the two outcomes have deliberately divergent consequences. The adjudicating auditor's own `X-User-ID` is never written onto the attestation itself: the record carries no adjudicator anchor field, so through both outcomes `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` remain its only user-identity fields.

Acceptance, optionally annotated with an `acceptance_note`, moves the attestation `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal. The plan year's `attestation_status` becomes `ACCEPTED`, its `deficiency_points` are recomputed because reaching `ACCEPTED` is exactly what closes the `ATTESTATION_LATE` deficiency, and both the attestation's `version` and the plan year's `version` increment by 1 each. Acceptance posts **no** ledger entry of any kind.

Rejection requires a `rejection_reason` and moves the attestation `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. The reason is stored, the plan year's `attestation_status` becomes `REJECTED`, and — the divergence from the acceptance branch — the system appends one penalty ledger accrual with `entry_type` `ACCRUAL`, `deficiency_type` `ATTESTATION_LATE` and `onset_date` equal to the logical clock's calendar date, priced by the accrual formula of §3.9. The plan year's `penalty_exposure_cents`, its `deficiency_points` — a `REJECTED` attestation carries five points regardless of whether the attestation deadline has passed — and its `compliance_posture` are all recomputed, and both the attestation's `version` and the plan year's `version` increment by 1 each.

A rejected attestation can be salvaged. A `ComplianceOfficer` revises it by supplying a replacement `contract_terms_doc_count`, which must be at least 1 or the revision is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERMS_DOC_REQUIRED`. Revision moves the attestation `REJECTED → DRAFT`, replaces `contract_terms_doc_count`, increments `revision_round` by 1, clears `rejection_reason` to null, resets `submitted_by_user_id` and `submitted_at` to null, sets the plan year's `attestation_status` back to `DRAFT`, and increments the plan year's `version` by 1. One further effect is easy to overlook and changes who may submit next: `prepared_by_user_id` is **replaced** with the revising officer's `X-User-ID`, so segregation of duties on the next submission is evaluated against the reviser rather than the original author. An officer who revises a rejected attestation has thereby disqualified themselves from submitting it.

Voiding is the terminal fate of drafts that the world moved past. An attestation in `DRAFT` is voided when the plan year's `compliance_posture` flips to `DEFICIENT` or `FROZEN`, with `void_reason` set to `POSTURE_DEFICIENT`, or when a newer attestation is created for the same plan year, with `void_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_DRAFT`. Either way the attestation's `version` increments by 1 and the plan year's `attestation_status` is refreshed from its latest attestation by `created_at`. `VOIDED` is terminal and irreversible: when a later remediation walks the posture back from `DEFICIENT` to `AT_RISK` or `COMPLIANT`, voided attestations are **not** revived — the officer must prepare a fresh draft.

### 3.9. Pricing Deficiencies: The Penalty Ledger, Projection, Remediation and the Compliance Run

Every deficiency this system tracks is priced into a single append-only ledger, one entry per event. A ledger entry records the `plan_year_id` it scores against, an `entry_type` of either `ACCRUAL` or `ABATEMENT`, the `deficiency_type` it prices drawn from `MRF_REFRESH`, `TOOL_SLA`, `RXDC_LATE`, `GAG_CLAUSE` and `ATTESTATION_LATE`, the `signed_amount_cents` it moves, the `daily_rate_cents` used to price it, the `days_open` it covers, the `size_multiplier` applied, the `onset_date` of the priced deficiency, an optional `reverses_entry_id` pointing at another entry, an optional `remediation_plan_id`, a human-readable `reason`, and a `posted_by_user_id` holding the `X-User-ID` of the actor whose call caused the posting — null when the posting came from a compliance run rather than a person's action on a specific record. Accrual entries carry a positive `signed_amount_cents`; abatement and reversal entries carry a negative one. Entries are never updated and never deleted, and as §2.4 states they carry no `updated_at` and no `version` at all. Their chronological order is `created_at` and nothing else — an entry's `id` must never decide a business outcome.

**Pricing an accrual.** Whenever a deficiency opens — a gag escalation, a missed refresh cycle, a tool SLA breach onset, prescription-drug lateness, or an attestation rejection — the accrual is priced in a fixed order from four inputs: the `deficiency_type`, the deficiency's `onset_date`, the logical clock date at posting time, and the plan year's `covered_lives`. The daily rate is looked up by deficiency family and stored in `daily_rate_cents`: a missed monthly refresh runs at 10,000 cents (100 dollars) per day, a price-comparison-tool availability breach at 5,000 cents (50 dollars) per day, prescription-drug lateness at 20,000 cents (200 dollars) per day, an outstanding gag clause at 15,000 cents (150 dollars) per day, and a late or rejected attestation at 12,000 cents (120 dollars) per day. The `size_multiplier` is selected from the plan year's enrolled headcount: a plan with fewer than 500 `covered_lives` takes 1.0, a plan with at least 500 and at most 4,999 takes 1.5, and a plan with 5,000 or more takes 2.0. `days_open` is the whole-day count from `onset_date` through the posting clock date, **inclusive of both endpoints** — a deficiency that opens and is priced on the same calendar day has `days_open` of 1, not 0. The raw amount is `daily_rate_cents` multiplied by `days_open`. `signed_amount_cents` is then that raw integer product multiplied by `size_multiplier` **exactly once**, rounded half up to whole cents, and finally clamped so that it never exceeds 100,000,000 cents. As an illustration, a gag escalation priced on its own onset day for a plan year with 6,000 covered lives posts 15,000 cents at a `days_open` of 1 and a `size_multiplier` of 2.0, giving `signed_amount_cents` of 30,000.

Accruals do not stack on the same deficiency instance. No second `ACCRUAL` is posted for a deficiency that already has an unreversed `ACCRUAL` of the same `deficiency_type` and the same `onset_date` on the same plan year. An accrual counts as reversed exactly when some other ledger entry carries a `reverses_entry_id` equal to that accrual's `id`. Entries that are not accruals carry a `daily_rate_cents` of 0, a `days_open` of 0, a `size_multiplier` of 1.0 and a null `onset_date`.

**Ledger balance.** The plan year's `penalty_exposure_cents` is the exact integer sum of `signed_amount_cents` over *every* entry of that plan year, with no rounding whatsoever, and it is recomputed after every insert. This equality is an invariant that holds at every observable instant: exposure is the ledger, and the ledger is exposure. That is precisely why a reversal must be a new compensating entry rather than a status flip on the original — flipping a field on the original would double-count against the sum.

**Reversal.** An `ExternalAuditor` who concludes an accrual was posted in error reverses it with a `reason`. The reversal appends a *new* entry whose `signed_amount_cents` is the arithmetic negation of the original's, whose `reverses_entry_id` is the original's `id`, whose `plan_year_id` and `deficiency_type` copy the original's, whose `entry_type` is `ABATEMENT`, and whose `daily_rate_cents`, `days_open`, `size_multiplier` and `onset_date` take the non-accrual values of 0, 0, 1.0 and null; `posted_by_user_id` records the auditor and `remediation_plan_id` stays null. The original entry itself is never touched. Once appended, the plan year's `penalty_exposure_cents` is recomputed from the ledger; `deficiency_points` and `compliance_posture` are deliberately left alone, and the exposure movement alone does *not* increment the plan year's `version` — a reversal is a ledger event, so the plan year row is refreshed in place without a version bump of its own. Only accruals are reversible: reversing an abatement, or reversing an entry that is itself a reversal, is refused with `ERR_NOT_REVERSIBLE_ENTRY`. And an accrual may be reversed at most once — if any entry already carries a `reverses_entry_id` equal to this entry's `id`, the request is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED`. Reversal is one of the five operations permitted while the plan year is frozen, because a frozen plan year is exactly the situation in which an auditor needs to correct the record.

**Remediation plans.** A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a remediation plan with the `plan_year_id` being remediated, the `target_deficiency_type` family the plan abates, a `milestone_count` of at least 1, and a narrative `plan_summary` of the corrective steps. A new plan is `ACTIVE` with `completed_milestone_count` at 0, `last_milestone_label` null, and `abatement_posted_cents` and `completed_at` both null. Progress is recorded one milestone at a time: each completion supplies a `milestone_label`, increments `completed_milestone_count` by 1, stores that label in `last_milestone_label`, and increments the plan's `version` by 1. A completion recorded beyond `milestone_count` is refused with `ERR_MILESTONES_INCOMPLETE`.

Completing the plan moves it `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, which is terminal, and requires two things: `completed_milestone_count` must equal `milestone_count` — a short plan is refused with `ERR_MILESTONES_INCOMPLETE` — and at least one unreversed `ACCRUAL` of the plan's `target_deficiency_type` must exist on the plan year, because there must be money to abate. With no such accrual the completion is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCRUAL_TO_REMEDIATE`. The abatement is priced at half the accrued base: the base is the sum of `signed_amount_cents` over the plan year's unreversed `ACCRUAL` entries whose `deficiency_type` equals `target_deficiency_type` — abatement entries never contribute to that base, and an accrual is excluded from it as soon as some entry carries a `reverses_entry_id` equal to its `id` — and `abatement_posted_cents` is 0.50 multiplied by that base, rounded half up to whole cents after the single multiplication. The officer may attach an optional free-text `completion_note` when closing the plan; that note is not stored as a field on the plan itself — it becomes the appended abatement entry's `reason`, and when it is omitted the service writes its own sentence naming the plan and its `target_deficiency_type` there instead. Completion stamps `completed_at` with the logical clock, stores `abatement_posted_cents`, increments the plan's `version` by 1, and appends exactly one ledger entry with `entry_type` `ABATEMENT`, `deficiency_type` equal to `target_deficiency_type`, `signed_amount_cents` equal to the negation of `abatement_posted_cents`, `remediation_plan_id` set to this plan's identifier, `daily_rate_cents` of 0, `days_open` of 0, `size_multiplier` of 1.0 and a null `onset_date`. The plan year's `penalty_exposure_cents` is then recomputed from the ledger, and as with a reversal that exposure movement does not increment the plan year's `version`. Crucially, `deficiency_points` are **not** reduced by a remediation completion: an abatement moves money, not deficiency state, and the underlying obligation must still be cured through its own workflow. A single unreversed gag-clause accrual of 30,000 cents therefore yields an abatement of 15,000 cents and a resulting ledger balance of 15,000 cents while the contract remains `NON_COMPLIANT` and its six points remain. Creating a remediation plan, recording a milestone on one, and completing one are all permitted while the plan year is frozen.

**Forward-looking projection.** Because the ledger only holds what was posted at the moment each deficiency opened, it systematically understates what a plan year currently owes. A read-only projection capability answers the fiduciary's real question. It accepts an optional `as_of_date`, defaulting to the logical clock's calendar date. The range rule bites only on a *supplied* value: a supplied `as_of_date` must fall between the plan year's `start_date` and `end_date` inclusive or the read is refused with `ERR_PROJECTION_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, while the defaulted clock date is used as-is and is never refused even when the clock has run past `end_date`. A supplied value that is not an ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the range is considered. The projection is derived from **live open child state, never from the ledger**, and it returns one object containing `statutory_maximum_cents`, `accrued_to_date_cents`, `open_deficiency_count` and the `size_multiplier` used.

The open deficiency set is derived family by family. One `GAG_CLAUSE` deficiency for each service provider contract of the plan year whose `gag_review_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT`, with its onset taken from that contract's `gag_non_compliant_since`. One `MRF_REFRESH` deficiency for each refresh cycle of the plan year whose `status` is `MISSED`, with its onset the day after the cycle's `deadline_at` date. One `TOOL_SLA` deficiency for each price comparison tool of the plan year whose `sla_breach_open` is true, with its onset taken from that tool's `breach_onset_date`. One `RXDC_LATE` deficiency for each prescription-drug submission of the plan year whose `status` is anything other than `SUBMITTED`, counted only when `as_of_date` is strictly after `rxdc_deadline_date`, with its onset the day after `rxdc_deadline_date`. And one `ATTESTATION_LATE` deficiency for the plan year itself when `attestation_status` is anything other than `ACCEPTED`, counted only when `as_of_date` is strictly after `attestation_deadline_date`, with its onset the day after `attestation_deadline_date`. Any deficiency whose onset falls after `as_of_date` is excluded from both variants.

Both variants are always returned in the same response object — the caller does not choose between them — and they differ **only** in where the day count stops. For each open deficiency the day count is inclusive of both endpoints, exactly as in accrual pricing. The accrued figure counts days from onset through `as_of_date`; the statutory figure counts days from onset through the plan year's `end_date`, on the assumption that every currently open deficiency stays open to the end of the year, which is the ceiling used for prioritising remediation. The order of operations is pinned: for each variant, first sum across all open deficiencies the product of that deficiency's daily rate and its day count, then multiply that single summed raw figure by the plan year's `size_multiplier` exactly once, then round half up to whole cents, then clamp at 100,000,000 cents. Rounding per deficiency and summing the rounded figures is wrong. `open_deficiency_count` is the number of deficiencies included above. For a plan year with 6,000 covered lives — multiplier 2.0 — an `end_date` of 2027-12-31, and a single gag-clause deficiency whose onset is 2027-07-10, a projection as of 2027-07-20 reports `accrued_to_date_cents` of 330,000, being 15,000 cents times 11 days times 2.0, and `statutory_maximum_cents` of 5,250,000, being 15,000 cents times 175 days times 2.0.

The projection deliberately disagrees with `penalty_exposure_cents`, and that disagreement is the feature: a deficiency open for eleven days projects eleven days of exposure while the ledger still holds only the single one-day accrual posted when it opened. Two asymmetries are worth calling out. A rejected attestation before `attestation_deadline_date` contributes five deficiency points but contributes **no** projected `ATTESTATION_LATE` deficiency, because the statutory deadline has not yet passed — while the accrual its rejection posted still counts in `penalty_exposure_cents`. Conversely, a deficiency that has since been cured contributes neither points nor projected exposure while its posted accrual remains on the ledger forever.

**Freezing and unfreezing.** An `ExternalAuditor` who no longer trusts a plan year's evidence freezes it with a `reason`. Freezing requires `freeze_flag` to be currently false — and because a plan year carrying that flag is by definition already at posture `FROZEN`, and freezing is *not* one of the five exemptions, a second freeze attempt is caught by the lockdown of §2.8 and answers `ERR_PLAN_YEAR_FROZEN` rather than a state-transition error. Freezing sets `freeze_flag` to true and `freeze_reason` to the supplied reason, forces `compliance_posture` to `FROZEN` regardless of the point total — the declared edges are `COMPLIANT → FROZEN` and `DEFICIENT → FROZEN`, and the posture ladder of §2.8 places any plan year with `freeze_flag` true at `FROZEN` from whatever posture it held — and increments the plan year's `version` by 1. Because the new posture is `FROZEN`, every attestation of that plan year still in `DRAFT` is voided with `void_reason` `POSTURE_DEFICIENT`. From that point the child-mutation lockdown of §2.8 applies in full.

Unfreezing requires `freeze_flag` to be currently true — unfreezing a plan year that was never frozen is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and optionally accepts a `note`, which is read for shape only and stored nowhere. It clears `freeze_flag` to false and `freeze_reason` to null, then recomputes `deficiency_points` and walks the posture ladder afresh, incrementing the plan year's `version` by 1. Where the plan year lands depends entirely on what is still wrong: `FROZEN → COMPLIANT` when the recomputed `deficiency_points` is 0, `FROZEN → AT_RISK` when it is at least 1 and below 6, and `FROZEN → DEFICIENT` when it is at least 6 and below 12 — in which case any remaining `DRAFT` attestations are voided with `void_reason` `POSTURE_DEFICIENT` as they would be for any other flip into `DEFICIENT`. A plan year still carrying 12 or more points returns to `FROZEN` under the ladder even though the flag has been cleared, since the ladder's top rung is inclusive at 12.

**System-driven posture movement.** Outside the auditor's explicit freeze, `compliance_posture` moves only as a consequence of recomputation after a mutation that opens or closes a deficiency. Six such edges exist, and every one of them increments the plan year's `version` by 1 when a stored field actually changes. Points rising from 0 to somewhere in the range 1 through 5 drives `COMPLIANT → AT_RISK` and stores the new `deficiency_points`. Points rising into the range 6 through 11 drives `AT_RISK → DEFICIENT`, or `COMPLIANT → DEFICIENT` in one step when a single mutation adds six or more points at once — a gag escalation, at six points, does exactly that. Both of those edges void every `DRAFT` attestation of the plan year with `void_reason` `POSTURE_DEFICIENT` and refresh `attestation_status` from the latest attestation, which reads `VOIDED` when the latest draft was the one voided. Points reaching 12 or more drives `DEFICIENT → FROZEN` and imposes the same lockdown as an explicit freeze. In the remediating direction, points falling back into the range 1 through 5 drives `DEFICIENT → AT_RISK` and points falling to 0 drives `AT_RISK → COMPLIANT` — and neither revives a voided attestation, because `VOIDED` is terminal.

**The compliance run.** Deadlines do not enforce themselves. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformOperator` triggers a compliance run against a plan year, and the run performs the sweeps that no user action would otherwise perform, in this order.

First it sweeps refresh cycles. Every cycle of the plan year still in `PENDING` whose `deadline_at` is strictly earlier than the logical clock moves `PENDING → MISSED` with its `version` incremented by 1. Each newly missed cycle then posts one ledger accrual with `entry_type` `ACCRUAL`, `deficiency_type` `MRF_REFRESH` and `onset_date` equal to the day after that cycle's `deadline_at` date, priced by the accrual formula above — unless the plan year already carries an unreversed `MRF_REFRESH` accrual whose `onset_date` equals that same day-after-the-deadline date, in which case no second accrual is posted. The suppression key is that `onset_date`, exactly as in the general no-stacking rule above, so two cycles covering different periods each get their own accrual even when they were swept by the same run.

Second it lapses amendments. Every contract amendment of the plan year's contracts still in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` whose `expires_at` is strictly earlier than the logical clock moves to `LAPSED` with its `version` incremented by 1. After the sweep, any contract left with `data_rights_status` `PENDING_AMENDMENT` and no remaining `DATA_RIGHTS` amendment in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` reverts `PENDING_AMENDMENT → NOT_SECURED`, incrementing that contract's `version` by 1.

Third it recomputes. The plan year's `penalty_exposure_cents` is recomputed from the ledger, its `deficiency_points` from live child state — each `MISSED` cycle now contributing three points — and its `compliance_posture` from the ladder. If the recomputation *changes* the posture into `DEFICIENT` or `FROZEN`, the flip voids every `DRAFT` attestation of the plan year exactly as any other posture flip would; a run that merely reconfirms a posture already at `DEFICIENT` voids nothing. The run also stamps `last_compliance_run_at` with the logical clock, and whatever the run changes on the plan year — exposure, points, posture and that stamp together — collapses into a single increment of the plan year's `version`, or into none at all when a repeat run at an unmoved clock leaves every one of those fields equal to its prior value.

The run's response is not the plan year but a summary object: a `run_id` identifying the run, freshly minted as a bare UUID for the response and not persisted anywhere, `cycles_missed_count` and `amendments_lapsed_count` reporting what the two sweeps touched, `accruals_posted_count` reporting how many ledger entries the run appended, `posture_before` and `posture_after` naming the posture on either side of the recomputation, and the resulting `deficiency_points` and `penalty_exposure_cents`. The run is idempotent for a fixed clock: triggering it a second time without advancing the clock sweeps nothing further and posts no further accruals, so `cycles_missed_count`, `amendments_lapsed_count` and `accruals_posted_count` all come back as 0 and `posture_before` equals `posture_after`. A compliance run is an ordinary mutating operation scoped to a plan year and is therefore refused while that plan year is frozen — it is not among the five exemptions.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/compliance` prefix. A request that creates a new resource responds with HTTP 201 and the created resource in `data`; every other successful request — retrievals, list reads, and all state-transition and mutation actions on existing resources — responds with HTTP 200. An action mounted on an existing resource stays in that 200 branch even when it appends new rows as a side effect: reversing a penalty ledger entry answers HTTP 200, carrying the newly appended reversal entry rather than the untouched original in `data`, and a compliance run that posts fresh accruals answers HTTP 200 as well. Mutating actions return the updated primary resource unless the description says otherwise; the compliance run and the penalty projection return their own summary objects, and the clock capability returns the clock value.
