# Landscape Supply Yard API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Bulk landscape material is a business of imperfect measurement. A stockpile that scaled in at forty cubic yards does not sell forty cubic yards: it settles, it compacts, it takes on rain, and a yard that quotes the scale ticket verbatim runs short by the third truck of the morning. Layered on top of that physical uncertainty is a commercial structure with just as many moving parts — contractor trade accounts carrying revolving credit lines, job-site purchase orders that must be tagged onto every ticket, seasonal price books that turn over in the middle of an open order, pre-buy contracts that lock a mulch or salt price for an entire season, and a truck fleet whose legal axle weight and physical bed volume each cap what may leave the gate. This document specifies the back-office API for a landscape supply yard that must reconcile all of it against one set of books.

Three recurring failures motivate the design. First, **volume drift**: the yard sells against a stockpile figure that is only as good as its last moisture reading, so the same lot must be able to report two different sellable volumes depending on whether it has been re-measured since intake. Second, **price drift**: a contractor confirms an order on Monday's mulch price and takes delivery on Thursday, after the spring surcharge landed, and the yard needs both numbers on the record — the quote it promised and the invoice it actually earned. Third, **credit and liability leakage**: unreturned pallets, unbilled deliveries, forgiven spillage claims and reversed cancellations all move a contractor's exposure, and every one of those movements has to land on the same credit line in the same direction the moment it happens.

The system is therefore built around a small number of long-lived aggregates — materials and their price rows, stockpile lots, contractor accounts, purchase orders, pre-buy contracts, orders and their lines, trucks, delivery loads, will-call tickets, claim cases and container returns — each of which sits at the root of the URL space and points at its neighbours through explicit identifier fields in the request body. There are no nested creation paths. What binds the aggregates together is not URL shape but cascade: a single delivery close-out can reprice an order, draw down a stockpile, deplete a lot, mint a container-deposit liability, tighten a credit line, freeze an account and evict that account's queued will-call tickets, all inside one request.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Yard Personnel, Trade Accounts and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal is acting under. Both must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must be one of exactly six declared roles — `Contractor`, `YardAdmin`, `CreditManager`, `DispatchPlanner`, `YardLoader`, `ClaimsAdjuster`. A request missing either header, sending either as an empty value, or naming a role outside those six is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is well-formed but is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The authoritative role list for each endpoint is the "Authorized Role(s)" column of the endpoint table in section 4.

The six roles divide the yard along strict lines of duty. A **`Contractor`** is the buying customer: it opens material orders, adds lines to them, confirms them, issues will-call tickets, files claims and supplies information the adjuster asks for. A **`YardAdmin`** runs yard operations — the material catalogue, the price book, stockpile intake and moisture readings, opening trade accounts, activating pre-buy contracts, forfeiting abandoned containers, and driving the yard's logical clock. A **`CreditManager`** works the credit desk: it resets credit lines, invoices fulfilled orders, records payment, and issues the credit memos that settle approved claims; it never touches fulfilment. A **`DispatchPlanner`** commissions trucks and runs the road: booking windows, dispatching, delivering, refusing, replanning and cancelling loads. A **`YardLoader`** works the loading yard: staging the will-call queue, running the equipment-safety briefing, loading and releasing contractor vehicles, and receiving containers back. A **`ClaimsAdjuster`** reviews damage and shortage claims, requests further information, and approves or denies them.

When `X-User-Role` is `Contractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ContractorAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ContractorAccount` compare the resource's account reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Contractor` may only act on a `JobPurchaseOrder`, `PreBuyContract`, `MaterialOrder`, `OrderLine`, `WillCallTicket`, `ClaimCase` or `ContainerReturn` whose owning `contractor_account_id` — reached directly on the resource, or through its `material_order_id` where the resource has no account field of its own — equals `X-User-ID`. A cross-account attempt is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `YardAdmin`, `CreditManager`, `DispatchPlanner`, `YardLoader` or `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is, however, recorded where the scaffolding of an operation demands an audit trail: dispatching a load writes the caller's identifier to that load's `dispatched_by_user_id`, staging a will-call ticket writes it to that ticket's `staged_by_user_id`, and approving or denying a claim writes it to that claim's `decided_by_user_id`. Those three recorded identifiers are compared against each other, not against entity ids. In particular, the yard enforces segregation of duties on claim decisions: when a `ClaimsAdjuster` decides a claim that names a `delivery_load_id`, and that load's `dispatched_by_user_id` equals the deciding adjuster's `X-User-ID`, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The planner who ran the truck does not get to judge the complaint about the truck.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint is paginated by the same two query-string parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100, and a value outside that inclusive range is a malformed request. `offset` controls how many matching rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum is 0, so a negative offset is likewise malformed. Both violations surface as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection states a different ordering for a specific collection, list results are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination was applied, and `has_more` set to true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters described alongside individual endpoints combine conjunctively: a query naming both a material and a status returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Timestamps and Record Versions

Every resource in this system — `Material`, `PriceSchedule`, `InventoryLot`, `ContractorAccount`, `JobPurchaseOrder`, `PreBuyContract`, `MaterialOrder`, `OrderLine`, `DeliveryTruck`, `DeliveryLoad`, `WillCallTicket`, `ClaimCase` and `ContainerReturn` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, including mutations the caller did not directly request — a cascade that only recomputes a derived money or volume field still advances the touched record's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`. There is no entity in this system that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Because so many figures in the yard are roll-ups of other records, it is worth being explicit about what "mutation" means for version accounting. When confirming an order pins an inventory lot to a line, the order's `version` advances, and so does the `version` of every `InventoryLot` whose `reserved_cubic_yards` moved, of every `PreBuyContract` whose draw changed, of the `ContractorAccount` whose credit position changed, and of the `JobPurchaseOrder` whose commitment changed. Each of those records also gets a fresh `updated_at`. A caller who wants to observe a cascade end-to-end must therefore re-read the neighbours, not only the record it wrote.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and each segment's existence is checked before the next one is consulted or before any body field is examined. A path naming a resource that does not exist yields the 404 error specific to that resource type: `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRICE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVENTORY_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREBUY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CONTAINER_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`. The same catalogue of 404 codes covers foreign keys arriving in a request body — `material_id`, `contractor_account_id`, `job_purchase_order_id`, `material_order_id`, `delivery_truck_id`, `delivery_load_id`, `will_call_ticket_id` — and those body references are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, so the first unresolvable reference in declaration order is the one reported.

### 2.5. The Yard Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time for business purposes. A single logical clock, settable only by a `YardAdmin`, supplies the instant that every timestamp side effect records and every date-window comparison consults: the instant a quote is frozen, the instant containers leave the yard, the instant a container return is received, the date compared against a pre-buy season, and the instant used to select which price row is in force. Until it is set for the first time, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

The clock may only move forward. A request to set it to an instant at or before the current reading is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_ADVANCE` (HTTP 422). The clock is set by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc` together with two arrays describing what the advance triggered: `promoted_price_schedule_ids` lists every price row that became effective as a consequence of the advance, and `expired_prebuy_contract_ids` lists every pre-buy contract whose season ended before the new clock date and which therefore expired. Both cascades are described in their business context in sections 3.1 and 3.3.

### 2.6. Volume, Weight and Money Conventions

The yard keeps three kinds of quantity and rounds each one the same way everywhere. Volumes are cubic yards carried to **2 decimal places, rounded half up**. Money is always an integer number of **cents**; there are no fractional-cent fields and every monetary rounding is likewise **half up**. Weight is an integer number of **pounds, rounded half up**. Percentages (`shrinkage_factor` excepted, which is a fraction rather than a percentage) are decimal numbers, and where a percentage participates in a money or volume calculation the rounding applies to the product, not to the percentage.

Two deliberate exceptions to plain half-up rounding exist, and both exist for safety rather than convenience. The payload headroom used when sizing a truck is truncated **downward** to 2 decimal places, so that a rounding-up can never authorise an overweight load. And the moisture multiplier that scales a stockpile's sellable volume is **clamped** before it is applied to the volume, never after. Both are spelled out where they are used, in sections 3.2 and 3.5.

Where a calculation runs in ordered steps, the order is part of the specification and not an implementation detail. Order pricing in particular extends each line at full precision, rounds the line, and only then sums the lines; summing at full precision and rounding once at order level is explicitly not the rule and produces different totals.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Catalogue and Credit Writes

Two write operations in this system supersede a value the caller must have read before writing, and both are guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the parent record's `version`.

Filing a new price row — a `POST /price-schedules` request — must carry `expected_material_version` as a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Material` named by `material_id`. If that number does not equal the material's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MATERIAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a newly effective price row retires the material's currently `EFFECTIVE` row: a writer holding an outdated view of the material would silently supersede a price row it never saw.

Resetting a credit line — a `POST /contractor-accounts/{contractorAccountId}/credit-adjustments` request — must carry `expected_contractor_account_version` as a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `ContractorAccount`. If that number does not equal the account's current `version`, the adjustment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a credit adjustment supersedes the prior limit: a writer whose view of `credit_used_cents` is stale would approve a line against a balance that no longer exists.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

When several rules could reject the same request, the order in which they are evaluated is fixed. It matters because a request can violate a cheap rule and an expensive rule at the same time, and the yard must always report the same one. The ladder below is evaluated top to bottom on every request, and the first rung that fails produces the response; no later rung is consulted.

1. **Header validity** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` present and non-empty and the role one of the six declared roles, on every endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization** — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list in section 4, on every endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Account scope** — for a `Contractor`, `X-User-ID` matches the `contractor_account_id` reachable from the target resource, whether that target is an order, a purchase order, a pre-buy contract, a will-call ticket, a claim or a container return; this applies to every `Contractor`-callable endpoint, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
4. **Segregation of duties** — on `POST /claim-cases/{claimCaseId}/decide`, when the claim carries a `delivery_load_id`, that load's `dispatched_by_user_id` differs from the acting `X-User-ID`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
5. **Request shape** — on every endpoint: required fields present, every enum value inside its declared set, numeric fields positive where the field declares positivity, a `BAGGED` `quantity` integral, `limit` inside 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0, and a claim decision naming either `APPROVE` or `DENY` accompanied by its matching companion field; else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Path-parameter existence** — every path segment resolves, checked left to right, on every endpoint carrying path parameters, else the matching 404 from the catalogue in section 2.4.
7. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key in the body resolves, checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, on every `POST` carrying foreign keys, else the matching 404.
8. **Material stale-version guard** — on `POST /price-schedules`, `expected_material_version` equals the material's `version`, else `ERR_STALE_MATERIAL_VERSION` (412).
9. **Account stale-version guard** — on `POST /contractor-accounts/{contractorAccountId}/credit-adjustments`, `expected_contractor_account_version` equals the account's `version`, else `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (412).
10. **Order must be draft** — on `POST /order-lines` and on `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/confirm`, the order's `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, else `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT` (409).
11. **Order not already terminal** — on `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/cancel`, the order's `fulfillment_status` is neither `FULFILLED` nor `CANCELLED`, else `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409).
12. **Billing eligibility** — on `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/invoice` the order's `fulfillment_status` is `FULFILLED`, and on `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/settle` its `settlement_status` is either `INVOICED` or `CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED`; either failure yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLED` (409).
13. **No dispatched load at cancel** — on `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/cancel`, no load of the order sits in `DISPATCHED`, else `ERR_ORDER_HAS_DISPATCHED_LOAD` (409).
14. **Load state gates** — on `POST /delivery-loads` and on every `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/*` action: `PLANNED` is required for dispatch and for cancel (`ERR_LOAD_NOT_PLANNED`), `DISPATCHED` for deliver and for refuse (`ERR_LOAD_NOT_DISPATCHED`), `REFUSED` for replan (`ERR_LOAD_NOT_REFUSED`), and for load creation the owning order's `fulfillment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ALLOCATED` (`ERR_ORDER_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`); all four are 409.
15. **Ticket state gates** — on every `POST /will-call-tickets/{willCallTicketId}/*` action: `STAGED` is required for safety acknowledgement (`ERR_TICKET_NOT_STAGED`), `LOADING` for loading (`ERR_TICKET_NOT_LOADING`), `LOADED` for release (`ERR_TICKET_NOT_LOADED`), and `QUEUED` or `STAGED` for cancellation (`ERR_TICKET_NOT_CANCELLABLE`); all four are 409.
16. **Claim state gates** — on every `POST /claim-cases/{claimCaseId}/*` action: `SUBMITTED` is required to start review, `UNDER_REVIEW` to request information and to decide, and `INFO_REQUESTED` to provide information — all three failures report `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_REVIEWABLE` — while settlement requires `APPROVED` and reports `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_APPROVED`; both are 409.
17. **Other lifecycle gates** — activation on `POST /prebuy-contracts/{preBuyContractId}/activate` requires `DRAFT` (`ERR_PREBUY_NOT_DRAFT`); `POST /container-returns/{containerReturnId}/receive` and `POST /container-returns/{containerReturnId}/forfeit` require a status that is neither `CLOSED` nor `FORFEITED` (`ERR_CONTAINER_RETURN_CLOSED`); `POST /job-purchase-orders/{jobPurchaseOrderId}/close` requires `OPEN` (`ERR_PO_NOT_OPEN`); all three are 409.
18. **Cross-entity readiness** — every line's material is `ACTIVE` at `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/confirm` (`ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_SELLABLE`); the pinned lot is `AVAILABLE` at `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/dispatch` (`ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`); the truck is `AVAILABLE` at `POST /delivery-loads`, at `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/dispatch` and at `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/replan` (`ERR_TRUCK_UNAVAILABLE`); the owning account is `ACTIVE` at confirm, at dispatch and at `POST /will-call-tickets/stage-next` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`); and a referenced pre-buy contract is `ACTIVE` at confirm (`ERR_PREBUY_NOT_ACTIVE`); all five are 409.
19. **Safety acknowledgement** — on `POST /will-call-tickets/{willCallTicketId}/safety-ack` the request's `ppe_confirmed` is true, and on `POST /will-call-tickets/{willCallTicketId}/load` the ticket's stored `ppe_confirmed` is true, before any material moves at the bay; else `ERR_SAFETY_ACK_REQUIRED` (422).
20. **Axle weight** — on `POST /delivery-loads` and `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/replan`, `planned_payload_lb` is at most the truck's `max_payload_lb`, equality being legal, else `ERR_AXLE_WEIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
21. **Driveway access** — on the same two endpoints, the access rank of the order's `driveway_access_class` is at least the access rank of the truck's `min_driveway_access_class`, else `ERR_DRIVEWAY_ACCESS_RESTRICTED` (422).
22. **Driveway protection** — on `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/deliver`, when the account's `driveway_protection_required` is true the report carries `driveway_protection_used` true, else `ERR_DRIVEWAY_PROTECTION_REQUIRED` (422).
23. **Volume fit** — on `POST /delivery-loads` and `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/replan`, `planned_cubic_yards` is at most the lesser of the truck's `bed_capacity_cubic_yards` and the driveway cap for the order's access class, else `ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_TRUCK_CAPACITY` (422).
24. **Truck window conflict** — on the same two endpoints, the requested window clears every other live window of the same truck under the turnaround-buffer rule of section 3.5, else `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
25. **Pre-buy season overlap** — on `POST /prebuy-contracts`, the new season shares no calendar day with another live contract for the same account and material, else `ERR_PREBUY_SEASON_OVERLAP` (409).
26. **Staging capacity** — on `POST /will-call-tickets/stage-next`, fewer than two bays are occupied over the requested window, else `ERR_STAGING_CAPACITY_FULL` (409).
27. **Queue not empty** — on the same endpoint, at least one eligible queued ticket exists, else `ERR_WILL_CALL_QUEUE_EMPTY` (409).
28. **Catalogue and mode constraints** — the order carries at least one line at `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/confirm` (`ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`); `sku` is unique at `POST /materials` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`); `po_number` is unique within its account at `POST /job-purchase-orders` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PO_NUMBER`); a line's `form` appears in the material's `offered_forms` at `POST /order-lines` (`ERR_FORM_NOT_OFFERED`); a ticket requires a `WILL_CALL` order at `POST /will-call-tickets` (`ERR_WILL_CALL_MODE_REQUIRED`); and a load requires a `DELIVERY` order at `POST /delivery-loads` (`ERR_DELIVERY_MODE_REQUIRED`); all six are 422.
29. **Referential coherence** — a `po_required` account's order carries `job_purchase_order_id` at `POST /material-orders` and at `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/confirm` (`ERR_PO_REQUIRED`); a supplied purchase order belongs to the order's own account (`ERR_PO_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`); a claim's `delivery_load_id` or `will_call_ticket_id` belongs to the claim's `material_order_id` at `POST /claim-cases` (`ERR_CLAIM_TARGET_MISMATCH`); and a pre-buy contract's material has `prebuy_eligible` true at `POST /prebuy-contracts/{preBuyContractId}/activate` (`ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_PREBUY_ELIGIBLE`); all four are 422.
30. **Window and dating rules** — `window_end` is strictly after `window_start` on `POST /delivery-loads`, on `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/replan` and for the staging window on `POST /will-call-tickets/stage-next` (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`); a new `effective_from` on `POST /price-schedules` is strictly later than that of the material's currently `EFFECTIVE` row (`ERR_PRICE_SCHEDULE_BACKDATED`); and `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` moves the clock strictly forward (`ERR_CLOCK_MUST_ADVANCE`); all three are 422.
31. **Pre-buy season currency** — a pre-buy-priced line requires the logical-clock date to fall inside the contract's inclusive season window at `POST /order-lines` and at `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/confirm`, and activation at `POST /prebuy-contracts/{preBuyContractId}/activate` requires the clock date to be at or before `season_end_date`; else `ERR_PREBUY_OUT_OF_SEASON` (422).
32. **Inventory sufficiency** — at `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/confirm`, every line finds one single available lot of its material with enough unreserved sellable volume, lots never being split, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY` (409).
33. **Pre-buy balance** — at confirm, a referenced contract's `remaining_cubic_yards` is at least the line's `line_volume_cubic_yards`, else `ERR_PREBUY_BALANCE_EXCEEDED` (422).
34. **Credit line** — at confirm, the account's `credit_available_cents` is at least `quoted_total_cents`, exact equality accepted, else `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402).
35. **Purchase-order ceiling** — at confirm, the purchase order's `committed_cents` plus `quoted_total_cents` is at most `po_limit_cents`, exact equality accepted, else `ERR_PO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402).
36. **Volume never exceeds what was promised** — at `POST /delivery-loads/{deliveryLoadId}/deliver`, `actual_net_cubic_yards` plus `spillage_cubic_yards` is at most `planned_cubic_yards` (`ERR_DELIVERED_EXCEEDS_PLANNED`); and at `POST /delivery-loads` and `POST /will-call-tickets/{willCallTicketId}/load`, the planned or loaded volume is at most the order's undelivered bulk balance net of volume already planned on other live loads (`ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_ORDER_BALANCE`); both are 422.
37. **Claim arithmetic** — at `POST /claim-cases/{claimCaseId}/decide`, `approved_amount_cents` is at most `claimed_amount_cents` (`ERR_APPROVED_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`), and at `POST /claim-cases/{claimCaseId}/settle` the claim's `payable_cents` is greater than 0 (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PAYABLE`); both are 422.
38. **Container return count** — at `POST /container-returns/{containerReturnId}/receive`, the cumulative `returned_count` already recorded plus the `returned_count` in the request is at most `issued_count`, else `ERR_RETURN_COUNT_EXCEEDS_ISSUED` (422).
39. **Payment sufficiency** — at `POST /material-orders/{materialOrderId}/settle`, `paid_amount_cents` is at least `final_total_cents` minus `credit_memo_cents`, else `ERR_UNDERPAYMENT` (402).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Authorization always outranks existence, so a `Contractor` reaching for another account's order learns only that it is forbidden, never whether the order exists. And within the delivery-load checks, the weight gate at rung 20 is evaluated before the volume gate at rung 23: a load that is both too heavy and too voluminous reports `ERR_AXLE_WEIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, never `ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_TRUCK_CAPACITY`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived Figures

A large fraction of the fields in this system are not stored by a caller but derived from other records, and they must be brought up to date within the request that disturbs their inputs. There is no background job and no eventual consistency: by the time a 2xx response is emitted, every figure the write disturbed has already been recomputed and every automatic state transition the new figures imply has already fired.

Cascades run in hops, and a later hop is caused by an earlier hop's output. A material order confirm illustrates the pattern: the first hop prices the lines and assembles the order total; the second hop, caused by the first, recomputes the reserved volume on every lot that was pinned, the drawn and remaining volume on every pre-buy contract that was drawn against, the credit position on the buying account, and the committed total on the tagged purchase order; the third hop, caused by the second, flips a pre-buy contract from `ACTIVE` to `EXHAUSTED` when its remaining volume has reached zero and flips a purchase order from `OPEN` to `EXHAUSTED` when its commitment has reached its limit. A delivery close-out runs five hops deep, ending in the cancellation of an unrelated will-call ticket. Each hop's writes advance the `version` and `updated_at` of the records it touches, as section 2.4 requires.

Compensating cascades are exact mirrors of the cascades they undo. Cancelling a confirmed order recomputes reserved lot volume, pre-buy draw, account credit usage and purchase-order commitment **downward**, and reverses the automatic transitions the confirm caused: an `EXHAUSTED` pre-buy contract with remaining volume above zero returns to `ACTIVE`, an `EXHAUSTED` purchase order with commitment below its limit returns to `OPEN`, and an account on credit hold is released when its usage has fallen far enough. Reversibility is the rule; the only figures that never move backwards are the ones the yard has physically consumed, namely the withdrawn volume on a stockpile lot.

### 2.10. Two Independent State Dimensions on a Material Order

A `MaterialOrder` carries two state fields that advance independently and are stored separately. `fulfillment_status` tracks the physical side and moves through `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED`, `ALLOCATED` and `FULFILLED`, with `CANCELLED` as its other terminal state; `FULFILLED` and `CANCELLED` are the two terminal values on this axis. `settlement_status` tracks the billing side and moves among `UNBILLED`, `INVOICED`, `SETTLED` and `CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED`; it has no terminal state at all, because a credit memo may be issued against an order that has already been paid and payment may then be re-recorded.

The two axes touch in exactly one place: an order cannot be invoiced until its `fulfillment_status` has reached `FULFILLED`. Everywhere else they are independent. Settling a claim moves `settlement_status` while `fulfillment_status` stays `FULFILLED`; the `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` revert that follows a refused delivery leaves `settlement_status` untouched; cancelling an order sets `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED` and does not disturb `settlement_status`. Each of these mutations advances the order's `version` by one regardless of which axis moved.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Material Catalogue and the Seasonal Price Book

Everything the yard sells starts as a `Material` registered by a `YardAdmin`. Registration supplies the yard's `sku` — one to thirty-two characters, unique across the whole catalogue, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` — a human-readable `name`, and the physical profile the rest of the system reasons about. `offered_forms` is a non-empty list drawn from `BULK` and `BAGGED`, declaring which sale forms the yard will write on a line for this material; a line naming a form outside that list is refused with `ERR_FORM_NOT_OFFERED`. `seasonal_category` is one of `MULCH`, `SALT`, `AGGREGATE` or `SOIL`, and it later decides which kind of reusable container the material ships in. `density_lb_per_cubic_yard` is the dry-basis bulk density, strictly greater than zero, from which every truck-payload figure descends. `shrinkage_factor` is the fraction of stockpile volume lost to compaction and settling when the pile is sold, and it lies between 0 and 0.5 inclusive. `moisture_pct` is the yard-wide moisture reading for the material, between 0 and 60 inclusive, defaulting to 15.0 when the registration omits it. `unit_bag_cubic_yards` is the loose volume a single bag occupies, strictly positive, defaulting to 0.037, and it exists so that a bagged quantity can be converted into cubic yards for the purpose of reserving stockpile. `prebuy_eligible` is a boolean defaulting to false that decides whether season contracts may be written against the material at all.

Registration leaves the material's `status` at `ACTIVE`, its `available_net_cubic_yards` at 0.0, and its `effective_density_lb_per_cubic_yard` computed from the moisture profile as described in section 3.5. Only two things about a registered material may later be changed by a `YardAdmin`: its `status`, which moves among `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `DISCONTINUED`, and its `moisture_pct`. Either change recomputes `effective_density_lb_per_cubic_yard` and advances the material's `version`. Suspending or discontinuing a material does not disturb orders already confirmed against it, but it does block new confirmations: any line referencing a material that is not `ACTIVE` stops the confirm with `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_SELLABLE`. Reads of the catalogue may be narrowed by `status`, by `seasonal_category` and by `prebuy_eligible`, and are available to every role in the yard.

Pricing lives in a separate dated ledger, the price book. Each `PriceSchedule` row names its `material_id`, the instant `effective_from` at which it takes over, a `bulk_price_per_cubic_yard_cents` and a `bagged_price_per_bag_cents` — both strictly positive integers — and a `seasonal_surcharge_pct` between 0 and 50 inclusive, defaulting to 0.0. The surcharge is a percentage uplift applied **only** to the bulk unit price; a bagged price is never surcharged, in any circumstance. A new row's `effective_from` must be strictly later than the `effective_from` of the material's currently `EFFECTIVE` row, and a row dated at or before it is refused with `ERR_PRICE_SCHEDULE_BACKDATED`. Filing the row also requires the material stale-version guard of section 2.7, and a successful filing advances the parent `Material`'s `version` by one — which is exactly why a second filing replaying the same `expected_material_version` fails.

A price row is born `SCHEDULED` unless its `effective_from` is already at or before the current logical clock, in which case it becomes `EFFECTIVE` the moment it is filed. When a row becomes `EFFECTIVE`, the material's previously `EFFECTIVE` row transitions `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, so at most one row per material is in force at any instant and `SUPERSEDED` is the terminal state of a price row. A `SCHEDULED` row that has not yet reached its date sits waiting, and a `YardAdmin` advancing the logical clock past its `effective_from` promotes it: the advance transitions it `SCHEDULED → EFFECTIVE`, supersedes its predecessor, and returns its identifier in the clock response's `promoted_price_schedule_ids`. Every one of these promotions advances the affected rows' `version`. Price rows may be listed filtered by `material_id` and by `status`, and read individually, by the yard admin, the credit desk and the buying contractor.

The rule the rest of the document leans on is this: **the price row in force at an instant is the row of that material with the greatest `effective_from` at or before that instant**, and exactly one such row exists because every promotion supersedes its predecessor. Which instant a given calculation uses is the interesting part, and it differs between quoting and invoicing — see section 3.4.

### 3.2. Stockpile Intake, Moisture Readings and Net Sellable Volume

Material arrives at the yard as a stockpile lot. Scaling one in creates an `InventoryLot` naming its `material_id`, the `gross_cubic_yards` that came off the scale — strictly positive — and the `moisture_pct_at_intake` measured on the truck scale, between 0 and 60 inclusive. `gross_cubic_yards` is the yard's permanent record of what was received and is **never** mutated after intake; everything else about the lot's volume is derived from it. Intake sets `current_moisture_pct` equal to `moisture_pct_at_intake`, sets `basis` to `INTAKE`, sets `status` to `AVAILABLE`, leaves `withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards` and `reserved_cubic_yards` at 0.0, computes `net_sellable_cubic_yards`, and refreshes the parent material's `available_net_cubic_yards`. Lots may be listed filtered by `material_id`, by `status` and by `basis`, and read individually, by the yard admin, the dispatcher, the loader and the credit desk.

Sellable volume is where the yard's physical honesty lives, and it turns on a **moisture multiplier** shared by both variants of the calculation. To obtain the multiplier for a moisture percentage, subtract the yard's reference moisture of 15.0 percent from that percentage, divide the difference by 100, and subtract the result from 1. The multiplier is then clamped into the inclusive range from 0.80 to 1.00 — a value below 0.80 is raised to 0.80 and a value above 1.00 is lowered to 1.00 — and the clamp is applied to the multiplier **before** it is multiplied by any volume, never afterwards. Drier-than-reference material therefore never earns a bonus above 1.00, and no matter how wet a pile is the yard never writes off more than twenty percent of it on moisture alone.

Which volume the multiplier scales depends on the lot's `basis`, and the two variants differ in three ways at once. While `basis` is `INTAKE` — meaning no moisture reading has been filed since the lot was scaled in — `net_sellable_cubic_yards` is the `gross_cubic_yards` multiplied by one minus the full `shrinkage_factor` of the material, multiplied by the moisture multiplier taken from `moisture_pct_at_intake`, with the product rounded half up to two decimal places. Once `basis` has flipped to `SETTLEMENT` — meaning at least one moisture reading has been filed — the pile has already settled, so only **half** the shrinkage factor is charged and the arithmetic runs on the volume still physically on the ground: `net_sellable_cubic_yards` becomes `gross_cubic_yards` minus `withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards`, multiplied by one minus half the `shrinkage_factor`, multiplied by the moisture multiplier taken from `current_moisture_pct`, with the product rounded half up to two decimal places. The same physical lot consequently reports a different sellable volume on the two bases, and that is intentional.

Filing a fresh reading is a `YardAdmin` act. The request supplies a new `current_moisture_pct` and a boolean `set_quarantine`. The reading overwrites `current_moisture_pct`, flips `basis` to `SETTLEMENT` — irreversibly, since nothing returns a lot to the intake basis — recomputes `net_sellable_cubic_yards` under the settlement variant, and recomputes the parent material's `available_net_cubic_yards`. When `set_quarantine` is true the lot moves `AVAILABLE → QUARANTINED`; when it is false the lot moves `QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE`, so a reading is also how a quarantine is lifted. Both directions perform the identical moisture, basis, volume and roll-up work and both advance the lot's `version`; the only difference between them is the resulting `status`.

`withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards` records what has physically left the pile and only ever grows. It is the sum, over every delivery load pinned to this lot that reached `DELIVERED`, of that load's `actual_net_cubic_yards` plus its `spillage_cubic_yards`, added to the sum, over every will-call ticket allocated to this lot whose status is `LOADED` or `RELEASED`, of that ticket's `loaded_cubic_yards`, with the total rounded half up to two decimal places. Spilled material counts against the pile even though, as section 3.7 explains, it is never charged to the contractor. `reserved_cubic_yards` records what has been promised but not yet withdrawn: the sum, over every order line pinned to this lot whose owning order sits in `CONFIRMED` or `ALLOCATED`, of that line's `line_volume_cubic_yards` minus its `delivered_volume_cubic_yards`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Lines on `DRAFT`, `FULFILLED` or `CANCELLED` orders reserve nothing.

The material-level figure a contractor sees is a roll-up across lots. `available_net_cubic_yards` on a `Material` is the sum, over that material's lots whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, of each lot's `net_sellable_cubic_yards` minus its `reserved_cubic_yards`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Lots in `QUARANTINED` or `DEPLETED` contribute nothing at all — a quarantine therefore removes a pile from the yard's sellable position immediately, which is precisely why a quarantined lot also blocks dispatch of any load pinned to it with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. A lot takes the `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED` step automatically at the end of a withdrawal — a delivery close-out or a will-call load — when its `net_sellable_cubic_yards` minus its `withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards` has fallen to zero or below; a depleted lot is excluded from all future allocation.

### 3.3. Trade Accounts, Job Purchase Orders and Season Pre-Buys

A `YardAdmin` opens a trade account by supplying the contractor's `business_name`, an approved `credit_limit_cents` of at least zero, and two site-policy switches. `po_required`, defaulting to false, declares that every order billed to this account must be tagged to a job purchase order. `driveway_protection_required`, defaulting to false, declares that the crew must lay plywood or mats before tipping on this contractor's driveways. A new account opens with `account_status` `ACTIVE`, `credit_used_cents` at 0, `credit_available_cents` equal to its `credit_limit_cents`, and `pallet_deposit_balance_cents` at 0. Accounts may be listed filtered by `account_status`, and each account may be read by the yard admin, the credit desk, the adjuster and the owning contractor.

The account's **credit position** is a pure roll-up over that contractor's orders, recomputed on every mutation that changes an order total, either of an order's two state fields, or an order's credit-memo figure, and on every credit adjustment. `credit_used_cents` is the sum, over the account's orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `ALLOCATED` or `FULFILLED` **and** whose `settlement_status` is `UNBILLED`, `INVOICED` or `CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED`, of that order's final total where one exists and its quoted total otherwise, minus that order's `credit_memo_cents`. Orders still in `DRAFT` and orders that were `CANCELLED` never consume credit, and an order whose `settlement_status` has reached `SETTLED` releases the credit it was consuming. `credit_available_cents` is then the `credit_limit_cents` minus `credit_used_cents`, and it is permitted to go negative — a delivery that comes in heavier than the quote can legitimately push an account past its line.

Credit posture is deliberately **asymmetric**, a hysteresis band that stops an account flapping in and out of hold on a few cents of movement. The account transitions `ACTIVE → CREDIT_HOLD` on any recompute where `credit_used_cents` is **strictly greater** than `credit_limit_cents`. It transitions `CREDIT_HOLD → ACTIVE` only once `credit_used_cents` has fallen to or below ninety percent of `credit_limit_cents`, that threshold being the product of the limit and 0.90 rounded half up to whole cents. Entry is therefore at the limit and release only at nine-tenths of it. Every transition on either direction advances the account's `version`. Entering `CREDIT_HOLD` also has teeth in the yard: every will-call ticket still sitting in `QUEUED` whose order belongs to that account is transitioned to `CANCELLED` with the credit-hold reason, and each cancelled ticket's order then re-evaluates the `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` revert described in section 3.6. An account in `CREDIT_HOLD` cannot confirm a new order, cannot have a load dispatched, and cannot have a ticket staged; all three refuse with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`.

A `CreditManager` may reset the line directly. The adjustment supplies `new_credit_limit_cents`, a free-text `memo` for the credit file, and the `expected_contractor_account_version` guard of section 2.7. The new figure replaces `credit_limit_cents`, `credit_available_cents` is recomputed against it, and where the account was on hold the release test is re-run against the **new** limit: the account transitions `CREDIT_HOLD → ACTIVE` when `credit_used_cents` is at or below the product of `new_credit_limit_cents` and 0.90 rounded half up. The adjustment advances the account's `version` by one, which — being an OCC-guarded write — is exactly why a second adjustment carrying the same expected version fails.

Separate from credit, the account carries a container liability. `pallet_deposit_balance_cents` is the sum, over every container-return record belonging to this account's orders, of that record's `issued_count` multiplied by its `deposit_per_unit_cents`, minus its `refund_cents`, minus its `forfeited_cents`. It is the outstanding deposit the yard is still holding against containers that are neither back in the yard nor written off, and section 3.8 traces how each term moves.

Job purchase orders give the contractor its own budget envelope. Either the `Contractor` itself or a `YardAdmin` registers a `JobPurchaseOrder` naming its `contractor_account_id`, the contractor-supplied `po_number` — unique within that account, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_PO_NUMBER` — the `job_code` that tags every order billed to it, and a strictly positive `po_limit_cents`. It opens with `status` `OPEN` and `committed_cents` at 0. `committed_cents` is the sum, over the orders tagged to this purchase order whose `fulfillment_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `ALLOCATED` or `FULFILLED`, of each order's final total where one exists and its quoted total otherwise, minus that order's `credit_memo_cents`. When a recompute leaves `committed_cents` at or above `po_limit_cents` the purchase order transitions `OPEN → EXHAUSTED`, and further orders tagged to it cannot confirm — they are stopped with `ERR_PO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. `EXHAUSTED` is not terminal: it is a reversible ceiling, and any event that lowers the commitment below the limit — an order cancellation, a delivery whose final total came in below the quote, or a claim settlement issuing a credit memo — transitions the purchase order `EXHAUSTED → OPEN`, restoring headroom. Both directions advance the purchase order's `version`.

Closing is the only terminal move. A `Contractor` or a `YardAdmin` may take the `OPEN → CLOSED` step on a purchase order that is currently `OPEN`, which sets `status` to `CLOSED` and freezes `committed_cents` at its then-current value; an `EXHAUSTED` purchase order must first fall back to `OPEN` before it can be closed, and any attempt to close one that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_PO_NOT_OPEN`. Purchase orders may be listed filtered by `contractor_account_id`, by `status` and by `job_code`, and read individually.

Pre-buy contracts lock a season price. Either the `Contractor` or a `YardAdmin` writes a `PreBuyContract` naming its `contractor_account_id`, the `material_id` whose price is being locked, the strictly positive `locked_price_per_cubic_yard_cents`, the strictly positive `contracted_cubic_yards` bought up front, and the season boundaries `season_start_date` and `season_end_date` as plain calendar dates in year-month-day form, where the end date must be no earlier than the start date. The contract opens in `DRAFT` with `drawn_cubic_yards` at 0.0 and `remaining_cubic_yards` equal to `contracted_cubic_yards`. `drawn_cubic_yards` is the sum of `line_volume_cubic_yards` over every order line priced off this contract whose owning order sits in `CONFIRMED`, `ALLOCATED` or `FULFILLED`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `remaining_cubic_yards` is `contracted_cubic_yards` minus `drawn_cubic_yards`, likewise rounded half up to two decimal places.

Seasons for the same account and the same material may not overlap, and the intervals are **fully closed with day granularity and no buffer**. A new contract is accepted only when either its `season_end_date` falls strictly before the other contract's `season_start_date`, or the other contract's `season_end_date` falls strictly before the new contract's `season_start_date`; every other arrangement is a conflict, so two seasons that merely touch on a single shared calendar day do overlap and the new contract is refused with `ERR_PREBUY_SEASON_OVERLAP`. The comparison considers other contracts of the same account and material whose status is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; contracts already `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` are ignored.

Activation is a `YardAdmin` act and moves the contract `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, which is what makes it drawable by order lines. It requires three things: the material's `prebuy_eligible` must be true, else `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_PREBUY_ELIGIBLE`; the owning account must be `ACTIVE` rather than on credit hold, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`; and the current logical-clock date must be at or before `season_end_date`, else `ERR_PREBUY_OUT_OF_SEASON`. Thereafter the contract follows its balance: it transitions `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED` automatically when a recompute of `drawn_cubic_yards` after an order confirm leaves `remaining_cubic_yards` at zero or below, and transitions `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` automatically when a recompute after an order cancellation leaves `remaining_cubic_yards` above zero and the clock date is still at or before `season_end_date`. Advancing the logical clock past `season_end_date` expires the contract, taking either the `DRAFT → EXPIRED` step or the `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` step with identical effect, and each expired contract's identifier appears in the clock response's `expired_prebuy_contract_ids`. `EXHAUSTED` and `EXPIRED` are the two terminal states of a pre-buy contract, and every transition among all of them advances its `version`. Contracts may be listed filtered by `contractor_account_id`, by `material_id` and by `status`.

### 3.4. Building an Order, Freezing the Quote, and Committing the Contractor

A `Contractor` opens a `MaterialOrder` naming its own `contractor_account_id` and the `fulfillment_mode` by which the material will leave the yard, either `DELIVERY` or `WILL_CALL`. A `job_purchase_order_id` may be supplied to tag the order to a job budget; it is optional in general, defaulting to null, but mandatory when the owning account has `po_required` true, and an untagged order on such an account is refused with `ERR_PO_REQUIRED` both at creation and again at confirm. A supplied purchase order must belong to the same account as the order, else `ERR_PO_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`. Three further fields are required precisely when `fulfillment_mode` is `DELIVERY` and default to null otherwise: `delivery_address`, the job-site address; `driveway_access_class`, the site access grade discussed in section 3.5; and `delivery_distance_miles`, the one-way haul distance, which must be at least zero. A new order begins with `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT` and `settlement_status` `UNBILLED`, with every money field at 0, with `final_total_cents` and `quote_locked_at` null, and with `paid_amount_cents` and `credit_memo_cents` at 0. Orders may be listed filtered by `contractor_account_id`, by `job_purchase_order_id`, by `fulfillment_status`, by `settlement_status` and by `fulfillment_mode`.

Lines are added one at a time to a `DRAFT` order — an attempt against an order in any other `fulfillment_status` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT`. Each `OrderLine` names its `material_order_id`, its `material_id`, its `form` (`BULK` or `BAGGED`, and it must appear in the material's `offered_forms`), and its `quantity`, which is a strictly positive number interpreted as cubic yards for a bulk line and as a whole count of bags for a bagged line — a non-integral bagged quantity is a malformed request. The optional boolean `use_prebuy` asks the yard to price the line off the account's season contract: when it is true, the line's `prebuy_contract_id` is set to the account's `ACTIVE` pre-buy contract for that material and the current logical-clock date must fall inside that contract's inclusive season window, else `ERR_PREBUY_OUT_OF_SEASON`; when it is absent or false, `prebuy_contract_id` stays null. Line creation immediately computes `line_volume_cubic_yards` — for a bulk line it is simply the `quantity`, and for a bagged line it is the `quantity` multiplied by the material's `unit_bag_cubic_yards`, rounded half up to two decimal places — because that figure is what the yard will later reserve out of a stockpile. Everything monetary stays empty at this stage: `unit_price_cents`, `line_price_cents`, `final_line_price_cents` and `inventory_lot_id` are all null on a draft line, and `delivered_volume_cubic_yards` sits at 0.0. Lines may be listed filtered by `material_order_id`, by `material_id`, by `prebuy_contract_id`, by `inventory_lot_id` and by `form`.

Confirming the order is the `Contractor`'s commitment point and the busiest single operation in the system: it prices every line, freezes the quote, allocates stockpile, and books credit and purchase-order capacity in one atomic step, transitioning the order `DRAFT → CONFIRMED`. Nine conditions must hold. The order must carry at least one line, else `ERR_ORDER_HAS_NO_LINES`. The owning account must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`. When the account has `po_required` true the order must carry a `job_purchase_order_id`, else `ERR_PO_REQUIRED`, and where one is carried that purchase order must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_PO_NOT_OPEN`. Every line's material must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_SELLABLE`. For every line drawing on a pre-buy contract, that contract must be `ACTIVE` (else `ERR_PREBUY_NOT_ACTIVE`), the current logical-clock date must fall within its inclusive season window from `season_start_date` through `season_end_date` (else `ERR_PREBUY_OUT_OF_SEASON`), and its `remaining_cubic_yards` must be at least the line's `line_volume_cubic_yards` (else `ERR_PREBUY_BALANCE_EXCEEDED`). Every line must find exactly one lot of its material that is `AVAILABLE` and whose `net_sellable_cubic_yards` minus `reserved_cubic_yards` is at least the line's `line_volume_cubic_yards`, choosing the earliest-created such lot; **lots are never split across a line**, so a line that could only be satisfied by combining two partly-full piles is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY`. The account's `credit_available_cents` must be at least the newly computed `quoted_total_cents`, exact equality being accepted, else `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. And where the order is tagged, the purchase order's `committed_cents` plus `quoted_total_cents` must be at most its `po_limit_cents`, again inclusively, else `ERR_PO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

**Line pricing at confirm.** Each line is priced against the price row in force at the confirm instant — the row of that line's material with the greatest `effective_from` at or before the instant the quote is frozen, which the order records as `quote_locked_at`. Where the line carries a `prebuy_contract_id`, the bulk unit basis is instead the contract's `locked_price_per_cubic_yard_cents` and the seasonal surcharge is treated as zero, because a locked season price is immune both to later price rows and to seasonal uplift. Otherwise the bulk unit basis is the in-force row's `bulk_price_per_cubic_yard_cents` and the surcharge is that row's `seasonal_surcharge_pct`. For a bulk line, `unit_price_cents` is the unit basis multiplied by one plus the surcharge percentage divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents, and `line_price_cents` is the billable quantity multiplied by the **unrounded** unit basis multiplied by one plus the surcharge percentage divided by one hundred, with that product rounded half up to whole cents. `unit_price_cents` is a display figure only and is never itself multiplied by a quantity. For a bagged line, `unit_price_cents` is simply the in-force row's `bagged_price_per_bag_cents` with no surcharge of any kind, and `line_price_cents` is that unit price multiplied by the whole bag count, which is exact and needs no rounding. Under the confirm-time variant the billable quantity is the line's ordered `quantity`.

**Charge assembly at confirm.** With every line priced, the order total is assembled in eight steps, and the sequence is pinned because the dependency between the discount tier and the minimum-load fee looks circular: the tier is chosen on the pre-discount bulk figure while the fee is decided on the post-discount one. Step one sets `bulk_subtotal_cents` to the sum of `line_price_cents` across the order's bulk lines. Step two sets `bagged_subtotal_cents` to the same sum across its bagged lines. Step three selects `volume_discount_pct` from the yard's volume tiers by looking up `bulk_subtotal_cents` — the pre-discount figure — where each tier's lower bound is inclusive and its upper bound exclusive: bulk business below one thousand dollars earns 0.0 percent, bulk business from one thousand dollars up to but not including two thousand five hundred dollars earns 3.0 percent, and bulk business of two thousand five hundred dollars or more earns 6.0 percent. Step four sets `volume_discount_cents` to `bulk_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `volume_discount_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents. Step five derives the discounted bulk figure as `bulk_subtotal_cents` minus `volume_discount_cents`. Step six sets `min_load_fee_cents` to the yard's seventy-five-dollar minimum-load fee when all three of the following hold — the order's `fulfillment_mode` is `DELIVERY`, `bulk_subtotal_cents` is greater than zero, and the discounted bulk figure from step five is **below** the yard's two-hundred-and-fifty-dollar minimum-load threshold — and to 0 otherwise; a `WILL_CALL` order is always exempt from this fee. Step seven sets `delivery_fee_cents` to 0 for a `WILL_CALL` order, and otherwise to the axle base for the applicable axle class plus `delivery_distance_miles` multiplied by four dollars twenty-five cents per mile, that per-mile term being rounded half up to whole cents before it is added. The axle bases are sixty-five dollars for `SINGLE`, ninety-five dollars for `TANDEM` and one hundred and thirty-five dollars for `TRI_AXLE`. Under the confirm-time variant no truck has been assigned yet, so the axle class used is the largest one the order's `driveway_access_class` admits: `TRI_AXLE` for `CLASS_A`, `TANDEM` for `CLASS_B` and `SINGLE` for `CLASS_C`. Step eight sets the total to the discounted bulk figure plus `bagged_subtotal_cents` plus `min_load_fee_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents`, and under the confirm-time variant that total is stored as `quoted_total_cents`. Rounding half up occurs at steps four and seven only; every other step is exact integer arithmetic. Every line of the order participates, with no status filter, because lines cannot be deleted.

**The confirm cascade.** The first hop writes the line pricing and the order assembly, sets `quote_locked_at` to the current logical clock, pins each line's `inventory_lot_id` to the earliest-created eligible available lot, and sets `fulfillment_status` to `CONFIRMED`. The second hop follows from what the first hop wrote: each pinned lot's `reserved_cubic_yards` is recomputed and that lot's `updated_at` and `version` advance; each drawn pre-buy contract's `drawn_cubic_yards` and `remaining_cubic_yards` are recomputed; the account's `credit_used_cents` and `credit_available_cents` are recomputed; and where the order is tagged, the purchase order's `committed_cents` is recomputed. The third hop follows from the second: a pre-buy contract whose `remaining_cubic_yards` has fallen to zero or below transitions `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`, and a purchase order whose `committed_cents` has reached or passed its `po_limit_cents` transitions `OPEN → EXHAUSTED`. The order's own `version` advances by one.

Once confirmed, an order sits waiting for the yard to act on it. The first delivery load planned against it, or the first will-call ticket staged for it, transitions it `CONFIRMED → ALLOCATED`; the specifics are in sections 3.5 and 3.6. Because both of those events are equally valid entries into `ALLOCATED`, the transition is guarded only by the order already being in `CONFIRMED`.

**Cancellation and compensation.** Either the `Contractor` or a `YardAdmin` may cancel an order that has not yet reached a terminal fulfilment state; an attempt against an order already `FULFILLED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and an attempt while any load of the order is `DISPATCHED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_HAS_DISPATCHED_LOAD` — material already on the road cannot be un-sold. Cancelling from `DRAFT` is trivial: the order transitions `DRAFT → CANCELLED` and its `version` advances, with nothing to compensate because nothing was ever reserved. The `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` and `ALLOCATED → CANCELLED` transitions run the identical compensating cascade as each other. The first hop sets `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED`. The second hop, caused by the order leaving the reserving set of `CONFIRMED` and `ALLOCATED`, recomputes every pinned lot's `reserved_cubic_yards` back to its pre-confirm value, recomputes every referenced pre-buy contract's `drawn_cubic_yards` downward — returning an `EXHAUSTED` contract with `remaining_cubic_yards` above zero to `ACTIVE` — recomputes the account's `credit_used_cents` downward, and recomputes the purchase order's `committed_cents` downward, returning an `EXHAUSTED` purchase order with `committed_cents` below `po_limit_cents` to `OPEN`. The third hop, caused by the second hop's credit movement, releases an account in `CREDIT_HOLD` back to `ACTIVE` once `credit_used_cents` is at or below ninety percent of `credit_limit_cents`, that threshold rounded half up. Alongside the ledger work, every `PLANNED` delivery load of the order transitions to `CANCELLED` and every will-call ticket of the order in `QUEUED` or `STAGED` transitions to `CANCELLED`. The order's `settlement_status` is deliberately left untouched throughout, and the order's `version` advances by one.

### 3.5. Truck Sizing, Driveway Access and the Delivery Run

A `DispatchPlanner` commissions each vehicle in the fleet as a `DeliveryTruck` carrying the `truck_code` painted on its door, its `axle_class` (`SINGLE`, `TANDEM` or `TRI_AXLE`), its strictly positive legal `max_payload_lb`, its strictly positive geometric `bed_capacity_cubic_yards`, and its `min_driveway_access_class` — the least permissive driveway grade the vehicle may enter. A commissioned truck starts `AVAILABLE`; the planner may later take it out of service or return it, moving `status` among `AVAILABLE`, `IN_SERVICE` and `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and advancing the truck's `version`. Trucks may be listed filtered by `axle_class`, by `status` and by `min_driveway_access_class`, and read individually by the planner, the yard admin and the loader.

Driveway grades are ranked, and the ranking is what makes site access comparable to truck capability: `CLASS_A` ranks 3, `CLASS_B` ranks 2, and `CLASS_C` ranks 1. A site is reachable by a truck when the rank of the order's `driveway_access_class` is at least the rank of the truck's `min_driveway_access_class`; a tri-axle rig restricted to `CLASS_A` driveways therefore cannot serve a `CLASS_B` site, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DRIVEWAY_ACCESS_RESTRICTED`. The grades also impose an outright volume cap per trip, independent of any truck: a `CLASS_A` driveway is effectively uncapped at nine hundred and ninety-nine cubic yards, a `CLASS_B` driveway is capped at twelve cubic yards, and a tight `CLASS_C` driveway at six cubic yards.

Three physical figures are computed whenever a load is planned or re-planned. The material's `effective_density_lb_per_cubic_yard` is its `density_lb_per_cubic_yard` multiplied by one plus the quantity obtained by subtracting the yard's reference moisture of 15.0 percent from the material's `moisture_pct` and dividing by one hundred, with the result rounded half up to two decimal places — wetter material is heavier per cubic yard, and this is the figure the scale house actually cares about. The load's `planned_payload_lb` is its `planned_cubic_yards` multiplied by that effective density, rounded half up to whole pounds. And the load's advisory `max_allowed_cubic_yards` is the **smallest** of three independent ceilings: the truck's `bed_capacity_cubic_yards`, the payload headroom, and the driveway cap for the order's access class. Payload headroom is the truck's `max_payload_lb` divided by the effective density, **truncated downward** to two decimal places rather than rounded, so that a rounding-up can never authorise an overweight load. Because geometry, legal weight and site access interact non-linearly, `max_allowed_cubic_yards` frequently comes from a different one of the three ceilings for the same truck on different materials.

Two safety gates and one capacity gate are then applied, in that order. The weight gate requires `planned_payload_lb` to be at most the truck's `max_payload_lb`, with exact equality legal, else `ERR_AXLE_WEIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The access gate requires the rank comparison described above, else `ERR_DRIVEWAY_ACCESS_RESTRICTED`. Only then does the capacity gate require `planned_cubic_yards` to be at most the lesser of the truck's `bed_capacity_cubic_yards` and the driveway cap for the order's access class, else `ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_TRUCK_CAPACITY`. The weight gate strictly precedes the capacity gate, so a load that is simultaneously overweight and over-volume reports the weight failure.

Planning a load is a `DispatchPlanner` act against an order whose `fulfillment_mode` is `DELIVERY` — a load planned against a will-call order is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_MODE_REQUIRED` — and whose `fulfillment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ALLOCATED`, else `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`. The request names the `material_order_id`, the `delivery_truck_id`, the strictly positive `planned_cubic_yards` for the trip, and the truck-occupation window bounded by `window_start` inclusive and `window_end` exclusive, where `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The truck must be `AVAILABLE`, else `ERR_TRUCK_UNAVAILABLE`. The planned volume must not exceed the order's undelivered bulk balance net of volume already planned on the order's other live loads, else `ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_ORDER_BALANCE`. Creation sets `status` to `PLANNED`, pins `inventory_lot_id` to the lot allocated to the order's **earliest-created** bulk line, computes `planned_payload_lb` and `max_allowed_cubic_yards`, leaves `actual_net_cubic_yards`, `driveway_protection_used`, `dispatched_by_user_id` and `refusal_reason` null and `spillage_cubic_yards` at 0.0, and transitions the order `CONFIRMED → ALLOCATED` when this is its first load. Loads may be listed filtered by `material_order_id`, by `delivery_truck_id`, by `inventory_lot_id` and by `status`.

A truck cannot be in two places at once, and the yard allows a **forty-five-minute turnaround buffer** on both sides of every booking. For every other load of the same truck whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `DISPATCHED`, the new window is rejected when the new window's `window_start` falls strictly before that load's `window_end` plus forty-five minutes **and**, at the same time, that load's `window_start` falls strictly before the new window's `window_end` plus forty-five minutes. The buffer is symmetric and the boundary is exact: a new window starting precisely at another load's `window_end` plus forty-five minutes is accepted. A conflict is reported as `ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**Dispatch.** Releasing a `PLANNED` load to the road transitions it `PLANNED → DISPATCHED` and requires three things to still be true at that moment: the truck must be `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_TRUCK_UNAVAILABLE`), the pinned lot must be `AVAILABLE` rather than `QUARANTINED` or `DEPLETED` (`ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`), and the buying account must be `ACTIVE` rather than on hold (`ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`). Dispatch writes the acting planner's `X-User-ID` to `dispatched_by_user_id` — the record that later bars that planner from adjudicating this load's claim — and moves the truck `AVAILABLE → IN_SERVICE`, advancing both the truck's `version` and the load's.

**Delivery close-out.** Closing out a dispatched load transitions it `DISPATCHED → DELIVERED` and is the deepest cascade in the system. The report carries `actual_net_cubic_yards`, the net cubic yards actually tipped at the site; `driveway_protection_used`, stating whether plywood or mats were laid; and optionally `spillage_cubic_yards`, the volume lost on site, which defaults to 0.0. Two preconditions guard it: where the buying account has `driveway_protection_required` true, `driveway_protection_used` must be true, else `ERR_DRIVEWAY_PROTECTION_REQUIRED`; and `actual_net_cubic_yards` plus `spillage_cubic_yards` must be at most the load's `planned_cubic_yards`, else `ERR_DELIVERED_EXCEEDS_PLANNED`.

The first hop of the close-out stores the three reported values, sets `status` to `DELIVERED`, and returns the truck to `AVAILABLE`. In the same hop the delivered volume is distributed across the order's bulk lines by a **waterfall in `created_at` ascending order**: working through the bulk lines from oldest to newest, each line takes the lesser of the volume still to be distributed and its own shortfall — its `line_volume_cubic_yards` minus its `delivered_volume_cubic_yards` — that amount is added to the line's `delivered_volume_cubic_yards`, and the remainder to distribute is reduced accordingly; if any volume is still left over after the newest bulk line has been filled, the whole leftover is added to that last line's `delivered_volume_cubic_yards`. Each bulk line's `final_line_price_cents` is then recomputed on the final basis, and the order's `final_total_cents` is assembled from those figures. The final basis differs from the confirm basis in three respects: the price row consulted is the one in force at the **dispatch instant** of the delivering load rather than at `quote_locked_at`; the billable quantity for a bulk line is its `delivered_volume_cubic_yards` rather than its ordered `quantity`; and the axle base in the delivery-fee step is the **actual delivering truck's** `axle_class` rather than the largest class the driveway admits. Bagged lines are **not** repriced — each keeps its quoted `line_price_cents` and contributes it unchanged to `bagged_subtotal_cents`. A seasonal promotion landing between confirm and dispatch therefore makes `final_total_cents` diverge from `quoted_total_cents` on identical order data, which is the intended behaviour. When every bulk line's `delivered_volume_cubic_yards` has reached its `line_volume_cubic_yards`, the order transitions `ALLOCATED → FULFILLED`.

Still within the first hop, the pinned lot's `withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards` grows by `actual_net_cubic_yards` plus `spillage_cubic_yards`, its `reserved_cubic_yards` is recomputed, and the lot transitions to `DEPLETED` when its `net_sellable_cubic_yards` minus its `withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards` has fallen to zero or below. Also in the first hop, when the order carries at least one bagged line and no container-return record exists for it yet, one is cascade-created: its `container_type` is derived from the `seasonal_category` of the material on the order's earliest-created bagged line — `MULCH` and `AGGREGATE` ship on a `PALLET`, `SOIL` ships in a `SUPER_SACK`, and `SALT` ships in a `SALT_TOTE` — its `issued_count` is the total bagged `quantity` across the order divided by the yard's forty-eight bags per container and rounded **up** to a whole container, its `deposit_per_unit_cents` is the deposit for that container type, its `issued_at` is the current logical clock, its `returned_count` is 0 and its `status` is `OUTSTANDING`.

The second hop, caused by the first hop writing `final_total_cents`, recomputes the account's `credit_used_cents` and the tagged purchase order's `committed_cents`, the latter transitioning `OPEN → EXHAUSTED` where the commitment has reached the limit; and, caused by the first hop creating the container record, it raises the account's `pallet_deposit_balance_cents` by the issued count multiplied by the per-container deposit. The third hop, caused by the second hop's credit movement, transitions the account `ACTIVE → CREDIT_HOLD` when `credit_used_cents` now exceeds `credit_limit_cents`. The fourth hop, caused by that status change, transitions every will-call ticket in `QUEUED` belonging to that account's orders to `CANCELLED`. The fifth hop, caused by those cancellations, has each affected order re-evaluate the `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` revert and recomputes the `reserved_cubic_yards` of the lots those orders had pinned. The load's own `version` advances by one. `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are the load's two terminal states.

**Refusal.** When the site turns the truck away, the planner records a refusal, transitioning the load `DISPATCHED → REFUSED` with a `refusal_reason` of `ACCESS_BLOCKED`, `CUSTOMER_REFUSED` or `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`. This branch is deliberately **disjoint** from the delivered branch: `actual_net_cubic_yards` stays null, no inventory is withdrawn, no line or order pricing is recomputed, no container record is created, and no credit figure moves. The truck returns to `AVAILABLE` — except when the `refusal_reason` is `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`, in which case the truck goes to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and must be returned to service by the planner before it can carry anything again. The order then re-evaluates the `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` revert, and the load's `version` advances.

**Replanning.** A `REFUSED` load is not dead; the planner may re-book it, transitioning it `REFUSED → PLANNED`. The re-booking supplies a fresh `delivery_truck_id`, a fresh `window_start` and `window_end`, and a fresh `planned_cubic_yards`, and it re-runs every check that guarded the original booking: the new truck must be `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_TRUCK_UNAVAILABLE`), the new window must clear the forty-five-minute turnaround rule (`ERR_TRUCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`) and be well-formed (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`), and the new truck-and-volume combination must pass the weight gate (`ERR_AXLE_WEIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), the access gate (`ERR_DRIVEWAY_ACCESS_RESTRICTED`) and the capacity gate (`ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_TRUCK_CAPACITY`) in that same order. The replan replaces the truck, window and planned volume, clears `refusal_reason` back to null, recomputes `planned_payload_lb` and `max_allowed_cubic_yards`, has the order re-evaluate `CONFIRMED → ALLOCATED`, and advances the load's `version`.

**Cancelling a load.** A `PLANNED` load may be dropped, transitioning it `PLANNED → CANCELLED`. This releases the truck window so a later load may occupy it and returns the truck to `AVAILABLE`, but it deliberately leaves the order's reservations alone: no inventory is withdrawn, so the pinned lot's `withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards` and `reserved_cubic_yards` are unchanged by the cancellation itself. The order then re-evaluates the `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` revert and the load's `version` advances.

**The revert rule.** An order sitting in `ALLOCATED` falls back to `CONFIRMED` whenever the last thing physically committed against it goes away — that is, after a load refusal, a load cancellation or a ticket cancellation, the order transitions `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` exactly when it is left with zero delivery loads in `PLANNED` or `DISPATCHED` **and** zero will-call tickets in `STAGED`, `LOADING` or `LOADED`. The revert leaves `settlement_status` untouched and advances the order's `version` by one. This is a genuine cycle: the same order may be allocated, reverted and allocated again any number of times.

### 3.6. The Will-Call Loading Queue and Equipment Safety

Contractors who send their own truck take the will-call route. A `Contractor` issues a `WillCallTicket` naming the `material_order_id` and the `requested_pickup_at` instant it intends to arrive; the order's `fulfillment_mode` must be `WILL_CALL`, and a ticket issued against a delivery order is refused with `ERR_WILL_CALL_MODE_REQUIRED`. A new ticket enters the yard queue in `QUEUED` with `staging_window_start`, `staging_window_end`, `safety_briefing_code`, `loaded_cubic_yards`, `staged_by_user_id` and `inventory_lot_id` all null and `ppe_confirmed` false. Tickets may be listed filtered by `material_order_id`, by `inventory_lot_id` and by `status`, and read individually by the loader, the yard admin and the owning contractor. `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are the queue's two terminal states.

Two derived figures order the queue. A ticket's `priority_class` is 2 when its order has at least one line carrying a `prebuy_contract_id`, and 1 otherwise — a contractor who paid for the season up front gets served ahead of a walk-up. A ticket's `queue_position` is its 1-based rank among **all** tickets in the yard whose `status` is `QUEUED`, sorted by `priority_class` descending, then by `requested_pickup_at` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending as the final discriminator; a record's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. `queue_position` is set to null the moment a ticket leaves `QUEUED`, and every remaining queued ticket is renumbered whenever any ticket's status changes.

Staging is where the queue meets the bay, and it is a `YardLoader` act that names no ticket at all: the loader supplies only a `staging_window_start` and a `staging_window_end` for the bay occupation it is offering, and the yard picks the ticket. Eligibility is an explicit predicate — a ticket is a candidate when its own `status` is `QUEUED`, **and** its order's `fulfillment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ALLOCATED`, **and** that order's account has `account_status` `ACTIVE`, **and** its `requested_pickup_at` is at or before the current logical clock. Candidates are sorted by the same three keys that produce `queue_position` — `priority_class` descending, `requested_pickup_at` ascending, `created_at` ascending — and the first row of that sorted list is the ticket staged. When the candidate set is empty, nothing is staged and the request is refused with `ERR_WILL_CALL_QUEUE_EMPTY`. Note the asymmetry that follows from the two definitions: `queue_position` is computed over every queued ticket in the yard, including ineligible ones, so the ticket the yard stages is not always the one showing position 1.

The yard has **two** staging bays, and their occupancy is measured over closed intervals. The request is refused with `ERR_STAGING_CAPACITY_FULL` when the number of tickets whose `status` is `STAGED` or `LOADING` and whose own closed interval from `staging_window_start` through `staging_window_end` intersects the requested window is already two or more. The offered window itself must be well-formed, with its end strictly after its start, else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Staging then transitions the selected ticket `QUEUED → STAGED`, stores the two window bounds, writes the acting loader's `X-User-ID` to `staged_by_user_id`, pins `inventory_lot_id` to the lot allocated to the order's earliest-created bulk line, nulls that ticket's `queue_position` and renumbers every remaining queued ticket, transitions the order `CONFIRMED → ALLOCATED` when this is its first staged ticket, and advances the ticket's `version`.

Nothing moves at the bay until the safety briefing is signed off. The loader records the briefing by supplying the `safety_briefing_code` issued at the bay and a boolean `ppe_confirmed` attesting that the contractor's personal protective equipment was checked. When `ppe_confirmed` in the request is not true, the acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_ACK_REQUIRED`; when it is true, both values are stored and the ticket transitions `STAGED → LOADING`. The same error guards the loading step itself, from the stored side: loading a ticket whose stored `ppe_confirmed` is false is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_ACK_REQUIRED`. The rule is deliberately checked twice, once against the request body and once against the persisted value, because the two are separate operations and material physically moves only at the second.

Loading transitions the ticket `LOADING → LOADED`. The request supplies `loaded_cubic_yards`, the volume actually put on the contractor's vehicle, and optionally `pallet_count`, the number of reusable containers handed over with it. The volume must be at most the order's outstanding bulk balance — the sum, across the order's bulk lines, of `line_volume_cubic_yards` minus `delivered_volume_cubic_yards` — else `ERR_LOAD_EXCEEDS_ORDER_BALANCE`. Loading stores the volume and runs the same `created_at` ascending waterfall the delivery path uses, filling each bulk line up to its shortfall in creation order and adding any leftover to the newest bulk line's `delivered_volume_cubic_yards`. The pinned lot's `withdrawn_gross_cubic_yards` grows by `loaded_cubic_yards`, its `reserved_cubic_yards` is recomputed, and the lot transitions to `DEPLETED` when its sellable volume net of withdrawals has fallen to zero or below. Where the order carries at least one bagged line and no container-return record exists for it yet, one is cascade-created here as well, and in this path its `issued_count` is the `pallet_count` supplied in the request rather than a computed figure. The ticket's `version` advances.

Release is the last step and settles the money. Transitioning `LOADED → RELEASED` requires the ticket to be in `LOADED`, else `ERR_TICKET_NOT_LOADED`; it frees the staging bay, runs the final-basis charge assembly on the order — using the ticket's `staging_window_start` as the instant whose price row is in force, and the delivered volumes as the billable bulk quantities — writes `final_total_cents` and each bulk line's `final_line_price_cents`, transitions the order `ALLOCATED → FULFILLED` once every bulk line is fully delivered, recomputes the account's `credit_used_cents`, and advances the ticket's `version`.

A ticket may be cancelled from either of its two live pre-loading states, by the owning `Contractor` or by a `YardLoader`; an attempt from any other status is refused with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_CANCELLABLE`. The `QUEUED → CANCELLED` step sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, nulls `queue_position` and renumbers the remaining queued tickets. The `STAGED → CANCELLED` step does all of that and additionally releases the staging bay, then has the order re-evaluate the `ALLOCATED → CONFIRMED` revert. Both directions advance the ticket's `version`. The `QUEUED → CANCELLED` transition is also driven by the system rather than by a person in two situations already described: when the owning account enters `CREDIT_HOLD`, and when the order itself is cancelled.

### 3.7. Damage, Spillage and Short-Load Claims

Things go wrong on driveways. A `Contractor` files a `ClaimCase` against the order it was billed on, naming the `material_order_id`, the `claim_type`, the strictly positive `claimed_amount_cents` it is asking for, and a free-text `description` of the damage or shortage. The claim may optionally point at the specific event it concerns through `delivery_load_id` or `will_call_ticket_id`; whichever is supplied must belong to the claim's own `material_order_id`, else `ERR_CLAIM_TARGET_MISMATCH`. The `claim_type` is one of three families, and the family is what selects the cap rule applied later: `DRIVEWAY_DAMAGE` for a cracked or rutted driveway, `SPILLAGE` for material lost on site, and `SHORT_LOAD` for a load that came in light. A filed claim starts in `SUBMITTED` with `review_round` at 0, with `approved_amount_cents` and `payable_cents` null, with `invoice_offset_cents`, `deposit_offset_cents` and `goodwill_credit_cents` at 0, and with `decided_by_user_id`, `denial_reason` and `info_request_note` null. Claims may be listed filtered by `material_order_id`, by `delivery_load_id`, by `will_call_ticket_id`, by `claim_type` and by `status`, and read individually by the adjuster, the yard admin and the owning contractor.

Adjudication is **cyclic**, and `review_round` is the counter that makes the cycle observable. A `ClaimsAdjuster` takes a submitted claim up, transitioning it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and incrementing `review_round` by 1. From review the adjuster may bounce it back by supplying an `info_request_note`, transitioning it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and storing the note. The owning `Contractor` — and only the owning contractor, an attempt by any other account being refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — then supplies a replacement `description`, transitioning the claim `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and incrementing `review_round` by 1 again. That loop may repeat any number of times, and `review_round` therefore counts every entry into review from either direction. Every one of these transitions advances the claim's `version`.

Deciding a claim under review is a single operation with two disjoint branches selected by a `decision` field carrying either `APPROVE` or `DENY`; a decision naming anything else is malformed. Both branches write the deciding adjuster's `X-User-ID` to `decided_by_user_id`, and both are blocked by segregation of duties: when the claim names a `delivery_load_id` whose `dispatched_by_user_id` equals the acting adjuster's `X-User-ID`, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval requires an `approved_amount_cents` at most equal to `claimed_amount_cents`, else `ERR_APPROVED_EXCEEDS_CLAIM`; it transitions the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, stores the approved figure, and runs the settlement waterfall described next, writing `payable_cents`, `invoice_offset_cents`, `deposit_offset_cents` and `goodwill_credit_cents`. Denial requires a `denial_reason`, whose absence is a malformed request reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it transitions the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, stores the reason, computes no payable amount at all — `payable_cents` is not written by a denial and therefore remains null — and moves no ledger field on any other entity whatsoever. `DENIED` is terminal.

**Stage one of the waterfall caps the approved figure by family.** For a `DRIVEWAY_DAMAGE` claim the yard applies a one-hundred-and-fifty-dollar deductible, except that the deductible is **waived entirely** — treated as zero — when the claim names a `delivery_load_id` whose `driveway_protection_used` is false, because a crew that skipped the mats does not get to bill the contractor for the first hundred and fifty dollars of the consequence; `payable_cents` is then the `approved_amount_cents` minus the applicable deductible, floored at zero so it never goes negative. For a `SPILLAGE` claim, `payable_cents` is the lesser of `approved_amount_cents` and the yard's four-hundred-dollar cleanup cap — the contractor is never charged for the spilled material itself, only the cleanup is in scope. For a `SHORT_LOAD` claim, `payable_cents` is the lesser of `approved_amount_cents` and the order's own value, taken as its `final_total_cents` where one exists and its `quoted_total_cents` otherwise; a short-load claim can never exceed the order it disputes.

**Stage two pours the payable amount through three ordered buckets**, and the three bucket figures always sum back to exactly `payable_cents`. Bucket one is the still-outstanding invoice, computed as the order's final total where one exists and its quoted total otherwise, minus its `credit_memo_cents`, minus its `paid_amount_cents`, floored at zero; `invoice_offset_cents` takes the lesser of the amount still to be allocated and that outstanding figure, and the amount still to be allocated is reduced by it. Bucket two is the deposit exposure, computed as the sum of `forfeited_cents` over every container-return record of this order taken in `created_at` ascending order; `deposit_offset_cents` takes the lesser of the amount still remaining and that exposure, and the remaining amount is reduced again. Bucket three, `goodwill_credit_cents`, takes whatever is left, and it is never negative. In plain terms: the yard first forgives what the contractor still owes on the invoice, then releases deposits it had written off, and only pays out real goodwill with what is left.

Settling the claim is a `CreditManager` act and requires the claim to be `APPROVED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_APPROVED` — and to have a `payable_cents` greater than zero, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PAYABLE`. The first hop transitions the claim `APPROVED → SETTLED`, which is its other terminal state. The second hop, caused by the claim entering the settled set, recomputes the order's `credit_memo_cents` as the sum of `payable_cents` over that order's settled claims and transitions the order's `settlement_status` to `CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED`, taking the `INVOICED → CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED` step or the `SETTLED → CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED` step according to which value it currently holds, while leaving `fulfillment_status` untouched. Both of those two steps carry identical effects and both require the settling claim to have a `payable_cents` above zero. The third hop, caused by the change in `credit_memo_cents`, recomputes the account's `credit_used_cents` downward. The fourth hop, caused by that credit movement, releases an account in `CREDIT_HOLD` back to `ACTIVE` once `credit_used_cents` sits at or below ninety percent of `credit_limit_cents`, that threshold rounded half up. Separately, where the claim's `deposit_offset_cents` is greater than zero, the targeted container-return record's `forfeited_cents` is reduced by that offset and the account's `pallet_deposit_balance_cents` is recomputed accordingly. The claim's `version` advances by one, and — because the settlement lowers the order's contribution to any tagged purchase order's commitment — an `EXHAUSTED` purchase order whose `committed_cents` has fallen below its `po_limit_cents` returns to `OPEN`.

### 3.8. Reusable Containers, Deposits and the Late-Return Decay

Bagged material leaves the yard on reusable carriers, and the yard holds a deposit until it gets them back. A `ContainerReturn` record is never created by a direct client request: it comes into existence only as a cascade of a delivery close-out or a will-call loading, and only when the order carries at least one bagged line and no such record exists for that order yet. Its `container_type` is derived from the `seasonal_category` of the material on the order's earliest-created bagged line, mapping `MULCH` and `AGGREGATE` to `PALLET`, `SOIL` to `SUPER_SACK`, and `SALT` to `SALT_TOTE`. Its `deposit_per_unit_cents` is the deposit for that carrier: twenty-five dollars for a `PALLET`, sixty dollars for a `SUPER_SACK`, and forty-five dollars for a `SALT_TOTE`. Its `issued_count` is strictly positive, and how it is arrived at depends on the path: on the delivery path it is the total bagged `quantity` across the order divided by the yard's forty-eight bags per container and rounded up to a whole container, while on the will-call path it is the `pallet_count` the loader recorded when handing the carriers over. Its `issued_at` is the logical-clock instant the carriers left the yard, and the grace window is measured from there. The record opens in `OUTSTANDING` with `returned_count` at 0, `refund_cents` at 0, `forfeited_cents` at 0 and `last_received_at` null. Records may be listed filtered by `material_order_id`, by `container_type` and by `status`.

Receiving carriers back is a `YardLoader` act that supplies a `returned_count` for the carriers physically handed in on that visit. The count must be at least 1, and the cumulative `returned_count` already recorded plus the count in the request must not exceed `issued_count`; either violation is refused with `ERR_RETURN_COUNT_EXCEEDS_ISSUED`. A receive on a record already `CLOSED` or `FORFEITED` is refused with `ERR_CONTAINER_RETURN_CLOSED`, as is a forfeiture on such a record. Each receive adds the request's count to `returned_count`, sets `last_received_at` to the current logical clock, and recomputes `refund_cents`.

Deposit refunds **decay with lateness**, and the decay is measured against the logical clock, never against a client-supplied timestamp. Compute the lateness in seconds as the receive instant minus `issued_at` minus the yard's thirty-day grace period, floored at zero. Convert that to whole weeks by dividing by the number of seconds in a week and rounding **up**, so any part of a week counts as a full week. The decay multiplier is then one minus a quarter for each whole week late — that is, one minus 0.25 multiplied by the number of whole weeks late — floored at zero, so a return four or more weeks past the grace window refunds nothing at all. The refund earned by this visit is the request's `returned_count` multiplied by `deposit_per_unit_cents` multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents, and it is **added** to the running `refund_cents`; refunds accumulate across visits and each visit is decayed by the lateness at the time of that visit. The grace boundary is exact: a return arriving at precisely thirty days after `issued_at` earns the full multiplier of 1.00, while one arriving a single second later earns 0.75.

Where the cumulative `returned_count` is still below `issued_count` after a receive, the record takes the `OUTSTANDING → PARTIALLY_RETURNED` step; a further partial receive is the self-loop `PARTIALLY_RETURNED → PARTIALLY_RETURNED`, in which `returned_count` and `refund_cents` both grow again by the same rules. Where a receive brings the cumulative `returned_count` to exactly `issued_count`, the record closes — the `OUTSTANDING → CLOSED` step and the `PARTIALLY_RETURNED → CLOSED` step being identical in effect — and `forfeited_cents` is written for the first time as `issued_count` multiplied by `deposit_per_unit_cents` minus the accumulated `refund_cents`. That figure captures whatever the decay withheld: a contractor who returns every carrier a month late gets some of the deposit back and forfeits the rest.

Carriers that never come back are written off by a `YardAdmin`, taking either the `OUTSTANDING → FORFEITED` step or the `PARTIALLY_RETURNED → FORFEITED` step, with identical effect in both cases: `forfeited_cents` is set to `issued_count` multiplied by `deposit_per_unit_cents` minus `refund_cents`, and the unreturned remainder is permanently retained by the yard. `CLOSED` and `FORFEITED` are the record's two terminal states, and `forfeited_cents` stays at 0 while the record is in either live state.

Every one of these movements cascades to the buying account. A receive recomputes the account's `pallet_deposit_balance_cents` **downward** by the refund just granted. Reaching `CLOSED` or `FORFEITED` recomputes it to zero for that record, because at that point the refund granted plus the amount forfeited exactly retires the liability the issue created. And a claim settlement that draws on bucket two of the claim waterfall reduces a record's `forfeited_cents` and recomputes the account balance again, as section 3.7 describes. Each of these advances the container record's `version`.

### 3.9. Invoicing, Payment and Releasing the Credit Line

The billing axis moves only under the `CreditManager`, and it moves in small, auditable steps. Invoicing transitions the order `UNBILLED → INVOICED` and is the one place the two state dimensions interact: the order's `fulfillment_status` must already be `FULFILLED`, else the request is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLED`. Invoicing touches nothing else — no money is recomputed, `fulfillment_status` is left exactly as it was, and the order's `version` advances by one.

Recording payment requires a `paid_amount_cents` in the request, and it is accepted only when that figure is at least the order's `final_total_cents` minus its `credit_memo_cents`; anything less is refused with `ERR_UNDERPAYMENT`. Payment is accepted from either of two settlement states, and the same rule and the same effects apply in both: from `INVOICED → SETTLED` in the ordinary case, and from `CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED → SETTLED` where a claim was settled before the contractor paid. An attempt while `settlement_status` is `UNBILLED` or already `SETTLED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FULFILLED`, since only an invoiced or credit-memoed order is payable.

The first hop of a payment stores `paid_amount_cents` on the order and sets `settlement_status` to `SETTLED`. The second hop follows from the order leaving the credit-consuming settlement set of `UNBILLED`, `INVOICED` and `CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED`: the account's `credit_used_cents` is recomputed downward, freeing the line for the contractor's next job. The third hop follows from that: an account sitting in `CREDIT_HOLD` transitions back to `ACTIVE` once `credit_used_cents` has fallen to or below ninety percent of `credit_limit_cents`, that threshold being the product of the limit and 0.90 rounded half up. The order's `version` advances by one. Because `settlement_status` has no terminal state, a later claim settlement may move a paid order to `CREDIT_MEMO_ISSUED` again, and a further payment may return it to `SETTLED`; the cycle is legitimate and each pass advances the order's `version`.
