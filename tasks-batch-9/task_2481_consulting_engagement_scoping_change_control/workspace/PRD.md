# Consulting Engagement Scoping & Change Control API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Management consulting firms lose money in two places, and both of them are upstream of delivery. The first is the pursuit that should never have been chased: a prospect with no funded budget, no named economic buyer, a procurement department that will grind the fee down, or an adverse-party relationship that the firm's own conflict rules forbid. The second is the engagement that was sold on a scope nobody wrote down precisely enough — where a client's casual "and while you're at it" quietly becomes forty hours of partner time that was never priced, never approved and never billed. This service exists to make both failures visible and expensive to commit, by holding the entire arc from opportunity qualification through scope baselining, statement-of-work pricing, change control and engagement closure inside a single auditable system of record.

The product's central idea is the **frozen baseline**. A pursuit is shaped freely while it is being scoped — deliverables added and removed, assumptions and exclusions drafted, client responsibilities negotiated — and then, at a single deliberate act, the scope is frozen and priced. Every downstream commercial number descends from that frozen baseline: the risk score, the quoted fee, the not-to-exceed ceiling, the volume discount that will price every future change request. Once a statement of work has been signed the baseline becomes contractually immutable, and the only legitimate route to a larger fee is a change request that is priced, measured against the signed fee, routed for approval according to how material it is, and accepted by the client in writing.

Alongside that commercial spine the service runs a delivery spine: weekly timesheet lines that accumulate into a burn ledger, deliverable acceptance that accumulates into a completion percentage, and a forward-looking margin forecast that charges every unburned contracted hour at a blended cost rate. When burn runs ahead of completion, or when the forecast turns the engagement unprofitable, the system raises a risk escalation to firm leadership on its own initiative — and firm leadership can hold delivery outright, suspending every open deliverable until the situation is resolved. Independent quality checkpoints, segregation-of-duties rules that forbid anyone from approving their own work, and a client satisfaction assessment that gates follow-on pursuit development complete the picture. Everything is mounted under `/consulting`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same code and status are returned when the supplied `X-User-ID` cannot be resolved to a known actor of the declared role. If the headers resolve cleanly but the named role is not one the target operation admits, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `EngagementPartner`, `EngagementManager`, `QualityReviewer`, `FirmLeadership`, `ClientContact` and `PracticeAdmin`.

Identity resolution differs by role, and the distinction matters because most authorisation in this system is an ownership comparison rather than a role comparison:

- When `X-User-Role` is `EngagementPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consultant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Consultant` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. In practice a partner is checked against `owner_partner_consultant_id` on the pursuit and against `lead_partner_consultant_id` on the engagement.
- When `X-User-Role` is `EngagementManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consultant`; ownership checks compare `scoping_manager_consultant_id` on the pursuit and `engagement_manager_consultant_id` on the engagement to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `QualityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consultant`; the ownership check compares `reviewer_consultant_id` on the quality review being decided to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FirmLeadership`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consultant`; leadership operations do not require an assignment match, but they do require that `X-User-ID` differ from the consultant whose work is being reviewed, which is why the identity must still resolve to a real `Consultant` row.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClientContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`. Every row a client contact touches must be reachable from that client: the `client_id` of an opportunity, the `client_id` of an engagement, or the client of the opportunity that owns a deliverable must equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque back-office operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. A practice administrator holds no engagement assignment whatsoever and therefore cannot approve, price, review or decide anything on a pursuit or an engagement; the role exists to maintain client and consultant master data and to drive the logical clock.

Two further authorisation families sit on top of role membership. **Assignment binding** requires that a firm actor be the specific individual nominated for the operation — the owning partner for partner-side pursuit operations, the scoping manager for scope editing and baselining, the lead partner for engagement partner operations, the engagement manager for delivery operations, and the named reviewer for outcome recording. A firm actor of the right role but the wrong identity is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_ENGAGEMENT` (HTTP 403). This rule guards budget verification, qualification, scope editing, scope baselining, deliverable lead assignment, deliverable status changes, engagement mobilisation, begin-closing, timesheet decisions, timesheet reversal, change-request impact assessment and quality-review outcome recording.

**Segregation of duties** forbids an actor from reviewing, approving or assuring their own work, and is enforced with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) on exactly four operations: `POST /consulting/sows/{id}/internal-decision` (the internal reviewer must not be the pursuit's `owner_partner_consultant_id`), `POST /consulting/change-requests/{id}/leadership-decision` (the leadership decision maker must not be the request's `requested_by_consultant_id`), `POST /consulting/time-entries/{id}/decision` (the approver must not be the line's own `consultant_id`) and `POST /consulting/quality-reviews` (the nominated reviewer must be neither the engagement's lead partner nor its engagement manager).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, a non-integer value for either parameter, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint narrative says otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the supplied filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client influence. Clients never supply an `id`.

Nearly every resource also carries three audit properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, with three fractional digits, at which the row was written. `updated_at` is the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that a cascade from another entity performs rather than a direct client write.

`SatisfactionAssessment` is the single exception. It is immutable once created — there is no operation that edits or withdraws one — and it therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a satisfaction assessment payload entirely.

All eleven other resource families — `Client`, `Consultant`, `Opportunity`, `Deliverable`, `Sow`, `Engagement`, `ChangeRequest`, `TimeEntry`, `QualityReview` and `RiskEscalation` — carry the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the error. A missing row yields HTTP 404 with the error code specific to the entity that was not found: `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TIME_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RISK_ESCALATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SATISFACTION_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

The same family of codes applies to foreign keys carried in a request body rather than a path. Every creating or patching operation that accepts a reference validates it: `client_id`, `owner_partner_consultant_id`, `scoping_manager_consultant_id`, `origin_engagement_id`, `opportunity_id`, `lead_consultant_id`, `depends_on_deliverable_id`, `supersedes_sow_id`, `engagement_id`, `deliverable_id`, `consultant_id`, `reviewer_consultant_id` and `supersedes_change_request_id` must each name an existing row of the corresponding family, otherwise the request fails with that family's not-found code at HTTP 404.

The entity topology is deliberately flat: every collection is a peer under `/consulting`, and no entity's identifier is ever nested inside another entity's path. Relationships travel in request bodies, which is why body-level referential integrity carries the same weight as path resolution here.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this service reads a server-held logical UTC clock, never real wall-clock time. Before the first clock write the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time under any circumstance. The clock is advanced only by a practice administrator supplying a `current_utc` instant, and it is forward-only: a submitted value earlier than the current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409). Setting the clock to exactly its current value is accepted. The response echoes the resulting `current_utc`.

Every deadline, review window, timesheet-week check and audit instant in the remainder of this document reads that clock. In particular `baselined_at`, `signed_at`, `approved_at`, `rescinded_at`, `resolved_at` and `closed_at` are all stamped from the logical clock at the moment their operation succeeds.

### 2.7. Version Pinning and Stale-Write Rejection

Two creation operations in this system price themselves against a parent row that other actors are actively moving, and both therefore require the writer to declare which version of that parent it believes it is working from. Optimistic concurrency here is a **staleness** contract, not a timing contract: the check asks whether the parent has advanced since the client last read it, regardless of how much time elapsed.

`POST /consulting/sows` requires a non-optional `expected_opportunity_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Opportunity`. If that value does not equal the opportunity's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OPPORTUNITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This check is load-bearing because a new statement of work legitimately supersedes any prior non-terminal one for the same opportunity — the prior row is written to `SUPERSEDED` and the opportunity simply remains `SOW_ISSUED` — so no state precondition would otherwise stop a second create. The stale-version check is the only signal that tells a drafting partner the scope baseline, or the statement-of-work set it priced against, has moved underneath it.

`POST /consulting/change-requests` requires a non-optional `expected_engagement_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Engagement`. A mismatch with the engagement's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the rule exists because nothing else would refuse the write: an engagement accepts any number of change requests, and a new request may carry `supersedes_change_request_id` to replace an earlier draft. The staleness check is what tells the writer that the contracted fee, the contracted hours or the cumulative change percentage against which its impact would be assessed has already moved.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.8. Money, Hours and Rounding Discipline

Money is always expressed as an integer number of cents. Hours are always expressed as numbers carrying at most two decimal places. Percentages and scores are numbers carrying two decimal places, with the sole exception of `risk_score`, which is a whole number of points.

Every rounding operation in this service rounds **half up**. A value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero, never to the nearest even digit. Money results are rounded half up to whole cents — that is, to zero decimal places. Scores, percentages and hour aggregates are rounded half up to two decimal places.

Two rounding disciplines matter enough to state as principles. First, **round per line, then sum**: where a total is built from several priced lines, each line is rounded to whole cents on its own and the rounded lines are then added. Summing the raw unrounded line amounts and rounding once at the end is wrong and diverges from the specified result by whole cents. Second, **round intermediates that become stored fields**: whenever a computation produces a value that is persisted and then re-used as the input of a later step, the later step consumes the already-rounded value, not the raw one.

The firm's rate card underpins nearly every money calculation. Billing rates per hour, by consultant grade, are 92,500 cents for `PARTNER`, 57,500 cents for `MANAGER`, 36,500 cents for `CONSULTANT` and 21,500 cents for `ANALYST` — that is, 925.00, 575.00, 365.00 and 215.00 currency units per hour respectively. These are the firm's external billing rates and are constant across all clients; a consultant's own `cost_rate_cents_per_hour` is a separate, per-person internal figure and is never used for billing.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

A single client request in this system routinely mutates several rows. The rule is that cascades run to completion within the same request, synchronously, before the response is emitted, and every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. There is no eventual consistency and no deferred recomputation: a read immediately following a write observes every consequence of that write.

Three cascade chains are deep enough to name here and are specified in full in the workflow sections below. Holding delivery on an engagement suspends every open deliverable of that engagement's opportunity and then recomputes the engagement's suspended-deliverable count. Recording a failed quality checkpoint raises a risk escalation, which — when the finding is the most severe grade — itself holds delivery and triggers the suspension chain, and then flips the engagement's leadership-review flag and moves a counter on the client row. Approving a change request moves five contracted figures on the engagement, re-runs the economics forecast, and may in turn drive the engagement into overrun, raise an escalation, and again move the client-level counter. Each of those secondary and tertiary effects is a required part of the operation, not an optional refinement.

### 2.10. Validation Order

When a request violates more than one rule, the response must carry the error of the highest-priority violated rule. The evaluation order is fixed and is as follows.

Authentication comes first: `X-User-ID` presence, then resolvability of that identifier to a known actor, both surfacing as `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Authorisation follows in four steps in this order — role membership (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`), client scoping for a `ClientContact` (`ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), assignment binding for a firm actor (`ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_ENGAGEMENT`), and finally segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Because assignment binding is evaluated before segregation of duties, an unassigned actor who also happens to be conflicted receives the assignment error.

Request well-formedness comes next: body shape and enum membership (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), then pagination bounds on list endpoints (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`), then submitted date ranges (`ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`). Existence checks follow — path identifiers first, then body foreign keys, both at HTTP 404.

Version pinning is checked immediately after existence and before any state reasoning, so a stale `expected_opportunity_version` or `expected_engagement_version` surfaces as its 412 even when the target row would also have failed a state check. State checks then run in three steps: the target row's own state per its transition table, then the governing parent row's state, then the scope-frozen check (`ERR_SCOPE_FROZEN`).

Business safety rules come next, in this order: conflict blocked (`ERR_CONFLICT_BLOCKED`), conflict not cleared (`ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_CLEARED`), a failed quality gate with an unresolved escalation (`ERR_QUALITY_GATE_FAILED`), then a pending quality gate (`ERR_QUALITY_GATE_PENDING`). Business referential rules follow in order: practice match (`ERR_CONSULTANT_PRACTICE_MISMATCH`), reviewer certification (`ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED`), cross-engagement scope of a referenced row (`ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_IN_ENGAGEMENT_SCOPE`), dependency cycles (`ERR_DEPENDENCY_CYCLE`), predecessor acceptance (`ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_ACCEPTED`), then lead assignment (`ERR_DELIVERABLE_LEAD_UNASSIGNED`).

Business constraint rules then run in this order: `ERR_CLIENT_CODE_TAKEN`, `ERR_SCOPE_HAS_NO_DELIVERABLES`, `ERR_ASSUMPTIONS_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA_MISSING`, `ERR_COMPLETION_WEIGHT_SUM_INVALID`, `ERR_OUTCOME_TARGET_BONUS_REQUIRED`, `ERR_TARGET_BONUS_NOT_APPLICABLE`, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIMESHEET_LINE`, `ERR_FINDINGS_SEVERITY_INCONSISTENT`, `ERR_DELIVERABLES_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_OPEN_CHANGE_REQUEST_BLOCKS_CLOSING`, `ERR_OPEN_ESCALATION_BLOCKS_CLOSE` and `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_SIGNATURE`. Note in particular that on scope baselining the four scope-completeness checks fire in exactly that sequence — deliverable count, then the assumption and exclusion and responsibility lists, then acceptance criteria, then the completion-weight sum — so a scope with no deliverables at all reports the count error rather than any of the later three.

Temporal rules follow, in this order: lead capacity overlap (`ERR_CONSULTANT_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`), review window spacing (`ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), review window openness (`ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`), the timesheet week falling inside the engagement window (`ERR_TIME_ENTRY_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT_WINDOW`), the timesheet week not being in the future (`ERR_FUTURE_TIMESHEET_LINE`), the opportunity target start date not being in the past (`ERR_TARGET_DATE_IN_PAST`) and clock rewind (`ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN`).

Fiscal rules are evaluated last, in this order: budget verification (`ERR_BUDGET_NOT_VERIFIED`), timesheet hour bounds (`ERR_TIMESHEET_HOURS_OUT_OF_RANGE`), change materiality (`ERR_CHANGE_EXCEEDS_MATERIALITY`), the not-to-exceed ceiling (`ERR_NOT_TO_EXCEED_BREACHED`) and follow-on eligibility (`ERR_FOLLOW_ON_NOT_ELIGIBLE`). On qualification, therefore, an unverified budget is reported before any score-based branch is taken; but because conflict screening sits in the earlier safety band, a blocked or uncleared conflict outranks the missing budget.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Master Data: Clients, Consultants and the Firm's Registers

Before any pursuit can exist, a practice administrator registers the two master-data families the rest of the system leans on. A client organisation is registered with its legal `name`, between 1 and 200 characters and required; a `client_code`, a short firm-wide code of between 2 and 10 characters drawn only from uppercase letters and digits; an `industry_code`, which must be one of `RETAIL`, `INDUSTRIAL`, `FINSVC`, `PUBLIC` or `HEALTH`; a `decision_process` describing how the organisation approves professional-services purchases, which must be `SINGLE_APPROVER`, `COMMITTEE` or `PROCUREMENT_LED`; and a `stated_budget_authority_cents` figure, the annual professional-services budget authority the client claims to hold, which must be zero or greater. The `client_code` must be unique across every client on the register; a repeat is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_CODE_TAKEN`.

Optionally the registration carries `adverse_party_codes`, a list of between zero and ten uppercase codes naming the parties this client considers adverse to itself. Omitted, it defaults to an empty list. That list is the raw material of conflict screening, and its content is the difference between a pursuit that proceeds and one the firm is forbidden to chase.

Three further client properties are never supplied at registration. A client's `status` — its commercial standing — begins at `ACTIVE` and can be moved to `CREDIT_HOLD` or `INACTIVE` only by a practice administrator's dedicated standing operation, which accepts the target `status` and increments the client's `version`. A client on credit hold, or an inactive client, cannot sign a statement of work. The two counters `completed_engagement_count` and `at_risk_engagement_count` both begin at 0 and are maintained entirely by the system: the former counts the client's engagements whose delivery has reached `CLOSED` and feeds the relationship term of the qualification score, while the latter counts the client's engagements currently carrying at least one risk escalation of severity `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` in `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DELIVERY_HELD` handling state. Clients can be listed and narrowed by `client_code` and by `status`.

Consultants are registered in the same back-office fashion. A consultant carries a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, required; a `grade`, which must be `PARTNER`, `MANAGER`, `CONSULTANT` or `ANALYST` and which selects the billing rate every fee formula in this system reaches for; a `practice_code`, one of `OPS`, `STRATEGY`, `DIGITAL` or `RISK`; and a `cost_rate_cents_per_hour`, the individual's internal cost rate, which must be greater than zero. The optional `quality_reviewer_certified` flag defaults to false and gates whether the person may ever be named as a quality reviewer. The computed `active_lead_deliverable_count` begins at 0 and always equals the number of deliverables anywhere in the firm whose lead is this consultant and whose status has not yet reached `ACCEPTED`. Consultants can be listed and narrowed by `practice_code`, by `grade` and by `quality_reviewer_certified`.

### 3.2. Pursuit Intake, Conflict Screening and Qualification

An `EngagementPartner` opens a pursuit, and in doing so nominates both halves of the pursuit team. The payload carries the `client_id` of the prospective buyer, the `owner_partner_consultant_id` — which the server takes from the acting partner's `X-User-ID`, making that partner the only one permitted to act on the row thereafter — and the `scoping_manager_consultant_id`, naming the single `EngagementManager` who will be allowed to shape scope on this pursuit. It also carries `requested_practice_code`, the practice the client is buying, drawn from the same four-value set as a consultant's practice; `estimated_value_cents`, the firm's own estimate of contract value, which must be greater than zero and which becomes the denominator of the budget-coverage term in scoring; `stated_budget_cents`, the budget the client verbally states at intake, which must be zero or greater; the boolean `decision_maker_identified`, true only when an economic buyer with signature authority has actually been named; the requested delivery window `target_start_date` and `target_end_date`, both plain `YYYY-MM-DD` dates; and `objective_summary`, a one-paragraph statement of the client's objective. A pursuit whose `target_start_date` falls before the current logical clock date is refused with `ERR_TARGET_DATE_IN_PAST`. A pursuit whose `target_end_date` is not strictly after its `target_start_date` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`; the same date-range code guards deliverable creation, deliverable editing and quality-checkpoint scheduling.

A pursuit may optionally carry `origin_engagement_id`, which marks it as follow-on development off an engagement the firm already delivered; left out, the field is null and the pursuit is a net-new one. A follow-on pursuit is only permitted when the named origin engagement already has a satisfaction assessment on file whose composite score is at least 7.50. A follow-on naming an engagement with no assessment at all, or one whose score falls below that bar, is refused with `ERR_FOLLOW_ON_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

Every new pursuit lands in `SCREENING` status with `conflict_status` at `NOT_CHECKED`, an empty `screened_adverse_party_codes` list, empty `assumptions`, `exclusions` and `client_responsibilities` lists, and null values for `verified_budget_cents`, `qualification_score_verified`, `total_estimated_hours`, `scope_discount_pct`, `baseline_effort_cost_cents`, `longest_dependency_chain` and `baselined_at`. The full pursuit lifecycle runs `SCREENING`, then `QUALIFIED`, then `SCOPING`, then `SCOPE_BASELINED`, then `SOW_ISSUED`, and terminates in `WON`, `LOST` or `DISQUALIFIED`; `WON`, `LOST` and `DISQUALIFIED` are terminal and admit no further transition. Any operation attempted against a pursuit whose status does not permit it is refused with `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATE_INVALID`.

**Qualification scoring.** The qualification score is a weighted composite on a 0.00 to 100.00 scale, computed from the pursuit and its client. It has four terms. The decision-process term contributes 30 points multiplied by a factor determined by the client's `decision_process`: that factor is 1.0 for a `SINGLE_APPROVER` client, 0.8 for a `COMMITTEE` client and 0.6 for a `PROCUREMENT_LED` client. The budget-coverage term contributes 40 points multiplied by the budget ratio, where the budget ratio is the applicable budget figure divided by `estimated_value_cents`, capped at 1 so that a budget exceeding the estimate earns no more than full marks. The sponsor term contributes 20 points when `decision_maker_identified` is true and nothing at all when it is false. The relationship term contributes 10 points multiplied by the client's `completed_engagement_count` divided by 3, again capped at 1 so that three or more closed engagements earn the full ten points. The four contributions are added together and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places; neither the budget ratio nor the relationship factor is pre-rounded, only the final total. A pursuit qualifies when that score is 60.00 or higher — the threshold is inclusive, so a score of exactly 60.00 qualifies.

The same formula runs twice against the same pursuit with a single substitution, and the two results are stored separately. At intake, the budget figure fed into the budget ratio is `stated_budget_cents`, the client's self-reported number, and the result is written to `qualification_score_intake` while `verified_budget_cents` remains null. Later, once the owning partner has confirmed in writing what is actually funded, the partner records that figure as `verified_budget_cents` and the formula is re-run with the verified number in place of the stated one, writing the result to `qualification_score_verified`; the pursuit's `version` advances. Because the confirmed figure may be lower or higher than the stated one, and because the qualification gate reads only `qualification_score_verified`, a pursuit whose intake score comfortably cleared 60.00 can still be disqualified once the verified figure lands, and a pursuit whose intake score fell short can qualify. Everything else — the same client, the same `decision_process`, the same `estimated_value_cents`, the same `decision_maker_identified` — is identical between the two runs.

**Conflict screening.** Separately from budget work, the owning partner records the adverse parties screened for the pursuit. The payload carries `screened_adverse_party_codes`, the list of codes checked, and optionally `waiver_obtained`, a boolean asserting that the firm has secured a waiver. The submitted list is persisted verbatim onto the pursuit's `screened_adverse_party_codes`, and `conflict_status` is resolved from the overlap between that list and the client's own `adverse_party_codes`: an overlap of two or more codes writes `BLOCKED`, an overlap of exactly one code with `waiver_obtained` true writes `CLEARED_WITH_WAIVER`, an overlap of exactly one code with `waiver_obtained` false or absent writes `WAIVER_REQUIRED`, and no overlap at all writes `CLEAR`. The pursuit's `version` advances on every screening. A `BLOCKED` outcome is permanent in effect: it stops qualification, it stops scope baselining and it stops statement-of-work drafting, each with `ERR_CONFLICT_BLOCKED`. `NOT_CHECKED` and `WAIVER_REQUIRED` are less severe but still blocking for qualification and for statement-of-work drafting, both of which insist that `conflict_status` be either `CLEAR` or `CLEARED_WITH_WAIVER` and otherwise return `ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_CLEARED`.

**The qualification decision.** With a verified budget and a cleared conflict position, the owning partner takes the qualification decision, which carries no request body at all. The pursuit must still be in `SCREENING`, the acting partner must be its `owner_partner_consultant_id`, `verified_budget_cents` must be non-null — a decision attempted without it fails with `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_VERIFIED` — and the conflict position must not be `BLOCKED`. The decision itself re-runs the verified-budget variant of the score, writes the result to `qualification_score_verified`, and branches purely on that freshly recomputed number: a score of 60.00 or above moves the pursuit `SCREENING → QUALIFIED`, and a score below 60.00 moves it `SCREENING → DISQUALIFIED`. The intake score recorded at creation plays no part in this branch. Either way the pursuit's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.3. Scope Definition: Narrative, Deliverables, Dependencies and Lead Capacity

Scoping belongs to the nominated `EngagementManager`, and to that manager alone — another manager attempting to edit the scope narrative is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_TO_ENGAGEMENT`. The scope narrative is edited as a partial update carrying any subset of `objective_summary`, `assumptions`, `exclusions` and `client_responsibilities`; whatever is submitted is written to the pursuit and the `version` advances. The `assumptions` and `exclusions` lists are the scope statements the fee depends on and the explicit out-of-scope declarations respectively, and their combined count later reduces the risk score. The `client_responsibilities` list enumerates the obligations the client must discharge for the plan to hold, and its count raises the risk score. The first such edit against a `QUALIFIED` pursuit is what moves it `QUALIFIED → SCOPING`; subsequent edits leave it in `SCOPING`.

That ordering is deliberate: because deliverables may only be created while the pursuit is in `SCOPING`, the scope narrative is always opened before any deliverable can exist. A deliverable submitted against a pursuit that has not yet reached `SCOPING` is refused with `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATE_INVALID`, and one submitted against a pursuit that has already advanced beyond `SCOPING` is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_FROZEN`. The same frozen-scope refusal applies to the scope-narrative edit and to deliverable editing once the baseline has been struck. Lead assignment is the one scoping-adjacent operation that deliberately stays open after freezing, because delivery staffing continues long after the win.

A deliverable is created against a named `opportunity_id` and carries a `name` of between 1 and 160 characters, required; a `primary_grade` from the four-grade set, which is the grade whose billing rate prices this deliverable's estimated effort; an `effort_hours_estimate` in hours to two decimal places, which must be greater than zero and at most 5000.00; a `completion_weight_pct` to two decimal places representing the share of engagement completion this deliverable stands for, which must be greater than zero and at most 100.00; an `acceptance_criteria` string, the objective statement the client will accept against; and a planned window of `planned_start_date` and `planned_end_date`, where the end must be strictly after the start. It may also carry `depends_on_deliverable_id`, naming a sibling predecessor that must reach `ACCEPTED` before this one is allowed to start. Newly created, a deliverable sits in `PLANNED` with `hours_consumed` at 0.00, a null `lead_consultant_id`, a null `prior_status` and a null `line_cost_cents`.

Dependencies are constrained in three ways at once, all reported as `ERR_DEPENDENCY_CYCLE`: the named predecessor must belong to the same pursuit and not to another, it must not be the deliverable itself, and it must not close a cycle anywhere in the pursuit's dependency graph. The same three constraints are re-checked whenever a deliverable is edited. Editing accepts any subset of `name`, `primary_grade`, `effort_hours_estimate`, `completion_weight_pct`, `acceptance_criteria`, `planned_start_date`, `planned_end_date` and `depends_on_deliverable_id`, and advances the deliverable's `version`.

**Lead assignment and firm-wide capacity.** Assigning a lead is a separate act from creating the deliverable. The payload carries a single `lead_consultant_id`, and two independent gates apply. First, the named consultant's `practice_code` must equal the pursuit's `requested_practice_code`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_PRACTICE_MISMATCH`. Second, a capacity rule limits how much concurrent lead work one person can carry. The deliverable's own planned window is treated as half-open — the start date is included and the end date is excluded — so a deliverable ending on 2026-10-01 does not overlap one starting on 2026-10-01. The system counts every other deliverable across all pursuits and all clients whose lead is the target consultant, whose status is anything other than `ACCEPTED`, and whose own half-open planned window overlaps the candidate window. This is a firm-wide count, not a per-engagement one. If that count is two or more, the assignment is refused with `ERR_CONSULTANT_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` — meaning two overlapping lead assignments are permitted and the third overlapping one is not. A successful assignment writes `lead_consultant_id`, advances the deliverable's `version`, and increments the consultant's `active_lead_deliverable_count` while advancing that consultant's `version` too.

### 3.4. Baselining: Freezing and Pricing the Scope

Baselining is the hinge of the whole product. Requested by the pursuit's scoping manager and carrying no request body, it converts a mutable draft scope into a frozen, priced commitment, and it is the last moment at which the shape of the work can change without a change request.

Six preconditions are checked. The acting manager must be the pursuit's `scoping_manager_consultant_id`. The pursuit must hold at least one deliverable; baselining a `SCOPING` pursuit that still has none is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_HAS_NO_DELIVERABLES`. The `assumptions`, `exclusions` and `client_responsibilities` lists must each hold at least one entry, and an empty one of any of the three is refused with `ERR_ASSUMPTIONS_REQUIRED`. Every deliverable must carry non-empty `acceptance_criteria`, otherwise `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA_MISSING`. The deliverables' `completion_weight_pct` values must add up to exactly 100.00 — not approximately, exactly — otherwise `ERR_COMPLETION_WEIGHT_SUM_INVALID`. And the `depends_on_deliverable_id` graph across the pursuit's deliverables must contain no cycle, otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_CYCLE`. A `BLOCKED` conflict position also blocks baselining with `ERR_CONFLICT_BLOCKED`.

**Baseline pricing.** When those gates pass, the system prices the scope over every deliverable belonging to the pursuit, regardless of each deliverable's current status. The total estimated hours are the sum of every deliverable's `effort_hours_estimate`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and stored as `total_estimated_hours`. That total then selects a volume discount, stored as `scope_discount_pct`, from three tiers whose lower bounds are inclusive: a total below 200 hours earns no discount at all, that is 0.00; a total of 200 hours or more but below 500 hours earns 0.05; and a total of 500 hours or more earns 0.10. The boundaries bite exactly where stated — a scope totalling 199.99 hours takes no discount, and one totalling exactly 200.00 hours takes the five-percent tier.

Each deliverable is then priced individually, in order of its `created_at` ascending. Its raw line amount is its `effort_hours_estimate` multiplied by the firm's hourly billing rate for its `primary_grade`, multiplied by one minus `scope_discount_pct`. That raw amount is rounded half up to whole cents and stored as that deliverable's `line_cost_cents`. Only after every line has been individually rounded are the rounded lines added together, and that integer sum is stored as the pursuit's `baseline_effort_cost_cents`. Adding the raw line amounts and rounding once at the end produces a different, incorrect answer that can differ by whole cents.

Baselining also measures dependency depth. The `longest_dependency_chain` is the number of deliverables on the longest path through the pursuit's `depends_on_deliverable_id` graph, counting nodes rather than edges — so a deliverable with no predecessor and no successor counts as a chain of 1, and a pair where one depends on the other counts as 2.

Alongside those computed figures, `baselined_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant, the pursuit moves `SCOPING → SCOPE_BASELINED`, and its `version` advances by 1. From this point the scope is frozen: both the scope-narrative edit and any deliverable edit return `ERR_SCOPE_FROZEN`, as does an attempt to add a further deliverable. The frozen `scope_discount_pct` and `baseline_effort_cost_cents` are re-read — never recomputed — by every later pricing call.

### 3.5. The Statement of Work: Risk Scoring, Pricing Models and the Negotiation Loop

The owning partner drafts a statement of work against the frozen baseline. The payload names the `opportunity_id`, selects a `pricing_model` from `FIXED_FEE`, `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` or `OUTCOME_BASED`, supplies an `acceptance_summary` of between 1 and 2000 characters carrying the acceptance criteria and dependency assumptions into the contract, and — as described in §2.7 — pins `expected_opportunity_version`. An `OUTCOME_BASED` document must also carry `target_bonus_cents`, the outcome bonus the client is willing to put at risk; omitting it, or supplying a value that is not positive, is refused with `ERR_OUTCOME_TARGET_BONUS_REQUIRED`. Conversely, a `FIXED_FEE` or `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` document must not carry `target_bonus_cents` at all, and supplying one is refused with `ERR_TARGET_BONUS_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

Drafting requires the pursuit to be either `SCOPE_BASELINED` or `SOW_ISSUED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATE_INVALID`. It also requires a clean conflict position — `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_CONFLICT_BLOCKED`, while `NOT_CHECKED` and `WAIVER_REQUIRED` are refused with `ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_CLEARED`. When a prior non-terminal statement of work already exists for the same pursuit — one sitting in `DRAFT`, `INTERNAL_REVIEW`, `CLIENT_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` — that earlier row is written to `SUPERSEDED`, its own `version` advances, and the new row's `supersedes_sow_id` is set to the superseded row's identifier. The first document of a pursuit leaves `supersedes_sow_id` null. A pursuit sitting at `SCOPE_BASELINED` moves `SCOPE_BASELINED → SOW_ISSUED`; a pursuit already at `SOW_ISSUED` stays there but still has its `version` incremented, which is precisely the movement the stale-version guard is there to expose. A fresh document begins in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `signature_name` null and `signed_at` null. Statements of work can be listed and narrowed by `opportunity_id`, by `supersedes_sow_id`, by `status` and by `pricing_model`.

**Delivery risk scoring.** Before any money is quoted, the system scores delivery risk as a whole number of points from five additive components. The pricing component contributes 25 points for `FIXED_FEE`, 10 points for `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` and 40 points for `OUTCOME_BASED` — the commercial construct is the single largest lever in the model. The decision component reads the client's `decision_process` and contributes 5 points for `SINGLE_APPROVER`, 15 points for `COMMITTEE` and 20 points for `PROCUREMENT_LED`. The specification component starts from 20 points and retires 4 points for every declared statement, counting the entries in `assumptions` and the entries in `exclusions` together; the result is floored at zero, so five or more combined statements retire the component entirely and a scope with no declared statements at all carries the full 20 points. The dependency component is 5 points for every deliverable on the longest dependency chain, that is 5 multiplied by `longest_dependency_chain`, capped at 15 points. The obligation component is 3 points for every entry in `client_responsibilities`, capped at 15 points. The five components are added and the total is capped at 100 to yield `risk_score`.

That score selects a tier whose lower bounds are inclusive: a score below 30 is the low tier, a score of 30 or more but below 60 is the medium tier, and a score of 60 or more is the high tier. The tier in turn fixes `risk_premium_pct` at exactly 0.05 for low, 0.12 for medium and 0.20 for high. These are exact constants and are never interpolated between tiers. Because the pricing component dominates, the same frozen scope can land in different tiers under different pricing models — which is exactly the commercial signal the firm wants. Once written, `risk_premium_pct` is pinned for the life of the contract.

**Quoting the fee.** Two figures are copied from the pursuit onto the document at creation and never re-derived afterwards: `pinned_scope_discount_pct` takes the pursuit's `scope_discount_pct`, and the document's own `baseline_effort_cost_cents` takes the pursuit's `baseline_effort_cost_cents`. Pinning them means a later re-baseline can never retroactively move a price that has already been signed, and it means every future change request reuses the same discount rate the original scope enjoyed.

From that pinned baseline cost the system computes a risk-loaded total: the baseline cost multiplied by one plus `risk_premium_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. What happens next depends entirely on the pricing model, and the three paths are genuinely different commercial constructs rather than variations on one.

Under `FIXED_FEE`, the risk premium is fully loaded into a single guaranteed number. `fixed_component_cents` becomes the risk-loaded total, `at_risk_component_cents` is 0, `not_to_exceed_cents` is null, and `quoted_fee_cents` equals `fixed_component_cents`.

Under `TIME_AND_MATERIALS`, the risk premium is discarded from the fee entirely — risk is passed through to the client hour by hour rather than pre-loaded — and the risk tier instead widens a billing ceiling. A not-to-exceed multiplier is selected from the tier: 1.05 for the low tier, 1.10 for the medium tier and 1.15 for the high tier. `not_to_exceed_cents` becomes the pinned baseline cost multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents. `fixed_component_cents` is 0, `at_risk_component_cents` is 0, and `quoted_fee_cents` equals `not_to_exceed_cents` — a ceiling rather than a commitment.

Under `OUTCOME_BASED`, the fee splits in two. `fixed_component_cents` is the pinned baseline cost multiplied by 0.70 — seventy percent of the unloaded baseline, with no risk premium applied to that share — rounded half up to whole cents. The at-risk share begins as the risk-loaded total minus that already-rounded `fixed_component_cents`, and is then clamped by two independent caps. The first cap is the risk-loaded total multiplied by 0.35, rounded half up to whole cents; note that this cap is evaluated against the risk-loaded total, not against the unloaded baseline. The second cap is `target_bonus_cents` multiplied by 0.20, rounded half up to whole cents. `at_risk_component_cents` is the smallest of those three candidates — the uncapped remainder, the risk-loaded cap and the benefit-share cap — so which cap binds varies with the client's bonus appetite. `not_to_exceed_cents` is null, and `quoted_fee_cents` is `fixed_component_cents` plus `at_risk_component_cents`. Because seventy percent of the fee carries no premium, an outcome-based quote comes out below a fixed-fee quote on identical scope.

Every intermediate that becomes a stored field is rounded to whole cents before it is used again, and the at-risk remainder is computed from the already-rounded risk-loaded total and the already-rounded fixed component rather than from raw products.

**The negotiation loop.** The document's own lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `INTERNAL_REVIEW`, `CLIENT_REVIEW`, and — when the client pushes back — `REVISION_REQUESTED` and back to `DRAFT`, forming a cycle that can turn several times on one row. Its terminal states are `SIGNED`, `DECLINED` and `SUPERSEDED`, and any operation attempted from a state that does not permit it is refused with `ERR_SOW_STATE_INVALID`.

The owning partner sends a `DRAFT` document into internal review, moving it `DRAFT → INTERNAL_REVIEW` with no request body; the pursuit's conflict position must still be `CLEAR` or `CLEARED_WITH_WAIVER`, otherwise `ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_CLEARED`. The internal review decision belongs to `FirmLeadership` and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `RETURN` plus an optional `note`. An approval moves the document `INTERNAL_REVIEW → CLIENT_REVIEW`, again requiring a cleared conflict position; a return moves it `INTERNAL_REVIEW → DRAFT`. Either way the reviewing leader must not be the pursuit's owning partner, and the `version` advances by 1.

The client's decision on a released document belongs to the `ClientContact` of the pursuit's own client and carries a `decision` of `SIGN`, `REQUEST_REVISION` or `DECLINE`, plus optionally `signature_name` and a `note`. All three require the document to be in `CLIENT_REVIEW`. A signature additionally requires `signature_name` to be present — its absence is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — requires the client's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, since a client on `CREDIT_HOLD` or one that is `INACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_SIGNATURE`, and requires a cleared conflict position. On success the document moves `CLIENT_REVIEW → SIGNED`, `signed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `signature_name` is persisted, the engagement described in §3.6 is created, and the pursuit moves `SOW_ISSUED → WON`; both the document's `version` and the pursuit's `version` advance.

A revision request moves the document `CLIENT_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, but only while its `revision_round` is below 3 — a request against a document that has already turned three revision loops is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. The revision request is also what re-opens the scope: the pursuit moves `SOW_ISSUED → SCOPING`, both the scope-narrative edit and deliverable editing become accepted again, and the frozen figures are wiped back to null — `total_estimated_hours`, `scope_discount_pct`, `baseline_effort_cost_cents`, `longest_dependency_chain` and `baselined_at` on the pursuit, and `line_cost_cents` on every one of its deliverables. The pursuit's `version` advances alongside the document's. A decline moves the document `CLIENT_REVIEW → DECLINED` and the pursuit `SOW_ISSUED → LOST`, again advancing both versions.

Re-drafting closes the loop. The owning partner re-opens a `REVISION_REQUESTED` document, moving it `REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`, and may optionally revise `pricing_model`, `target_bonus_cents` and `acceptance_summary` in the same call. The pursuit must by then have been re-baselined and therefore sit at `SCOPE_BASELINED`; a pursuit still being reworked is refused with `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATE_INVALID`, and a fourth re-drafting of the same document is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. On success `revision_round` increments by 1 and the entire pricing chain is re-run against the **new** baseline, overwriting `risk_score`, `risk_premium_pct`, `pinned_scope_discount_pct`, the document's `baseline_effort_cost_cents`, `quoted_fee_cents`, `fixed_component_cents`, `at_risk_component_cents` and `not_to_exceed_cents`. The pursuit moves `SCOPE_BASELINED → SOW_ISSUED` and both versions advance. The full cycle therefore reads `CLIENT_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT → INTERNAL_REVIEW → CLIENT_REVIEW` on the document, mirrored by `SOW_ISSUED → SCOPING → SCOPE_BASELINED → SOW_ISSUED` on the pursuit.

### 3.6. Signature, Mobilisation and Deliverable Execution

A signature is the only thing that creates an engagement — there is no operation that creates one directly, and listing engagements is the only way to discover the row a signature produced. The new engagement copies `sow_id` from the signed document, `opportunity_id` and `client_id` from the pursuit, and `lead_partner_consultant_id` from the pursuit's `owner_partner_consultant_id`. Its `engagement_manager_consultant_id` starts null. Its `pricing_model` is copied from the signed document. Its `original_fee_cents` and its `contracted_fee_cents` both start at the document's `quoted_fee_cents`, its `contracted_hours` starts at the pursuit's `total_estimated_hours`, and its `not_to_exceed_cents` starts at the document's `not_to_exceed_cents` — which means it starts null for anything other than a time-and-materials contract. Two rates are pinned onto the engagement so that later change pricing never has to reach back through the contract chain: `pinned_scope_discount_pct` from the document's own pinned discount, and `pinned_risk_premium_pct` from the document's `risk_premium_pct`. The delivery window copies across as `planned_start_date` from the pursuit's `target_start_date` and `planned_end_date` from its `target_end_date`. Everything else starts at its zero: `approved_hours_burned` at 0.00, `burned_cost_cents` at 0, `cumulative_change_pct` at 0.00, `suspended_deliverable_count` at 0, `schedule_relief_days` at 0, `leadership_review_required` false and `closed_at` null.

An engagement carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and this is central to how the system reasons about it. `delivery_state` records where delivery stands and runs `MOBILIZING`, `ACTIVE`, `ON_HOLD`, `CLOSING` and terminally `CLOSED`. `economics_state` records commercial health and takes `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK` or `OVERRUN`; it has no terminal value and is recomputed after every economics-bearing mutation. The two never gate each other: an engagement still `MOBILIZING` can already be `AT_RISK`, and an engagement held at `ON_HOLD` keeps recomputing its economics normally. Engagements can be listed and narrowed by `sow_id`, `opportunity_id`, `client_id`, `lead_partner_consultant_id`, `engagement_manager_consultant_id`, `delivery_state` and `economics_state`. An operation attempted against a delivery state that does not permit it is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, and no operation other than a read is accepted against a `CLOSED` engagement.

Mobilisation is the lead partner's act and staffs the delivery manager. The payload carries a single `engagement_manager_consultant_id`; the named consultant must exist, otherwise `ERR_CONSULTANT_NOT_FOUND`, and that consultant's `practice_code` must equal the pursuit's `requested_practice_code`, otherwise `ERR_CONSULTANT_PRACTICE_MISMATCH`. The acting partner must be the engagement's `lead_partner_consultant_id`. On success the field is written, delivery moves `MOBILIZING → ACTIVE`, and the engagement's `version` advances by 1. Mobilising an engagement twice is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

**Deliverable execution.** Deliverable state changes are driven by a single `target_status` value, and which role may request which transition is part of the contract. A deliverable's lifecycle is `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED`, and then either `ACCEPTED` — which is terminal — or `REWORK` and back to `IN_PROGRESS`. `SUSPENDED` sits outside that flow and is reachable only by the system. Every transition requires the engagement's delivery to be `ACTIVE`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. A target status not reachable from the deliverable's current status — including any attempt to move out of the terminal `ACCEPTED` — is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_STATE_INVALID`. A deliverable status change is additionally blocked while any quality checkpoint on the engagement sits at `FAILED` with an unresolved linked escalation, which surfaces as `ERR_QUALITY_GATE_FAILED`.

The engagement manager starts work, moving a deliverable `PLANNED → IN_PROGRESS`. Two further preconditions apply to that first move and to that move only: the deliverable's `lead_consultant_id` must not be null, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLE_LEAD_UNASSIGNED`, and if the deliverable names a `depends_on_deliverable_id`, that predecessor must already have reached `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENCY_NOT_ACCEPTED`. The manager later submits the work, moving it `IN_PROGRESS → SUBMITTED`.

The client contact then decides. Acceptance moves the deliverable `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and recomputes engagement economics, since the accepted deliverable's completion weight now counts towards completion — a recompute that may move `economics_state`. Returning the work moves it `SUBMITTED → REWORK`, after which the manager restarts it, moving it `REWORK → IN_PROGRESS` and reopening the submit-decide cycle. Both client-side transitions require `X-User-ID` to be the pursuit's `client_id`, otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Every one of these transitions advances the deliverable's `version` by 1.

**Suspension and restoration.** When delivery is held, the system suspends work rather than losing it. Any deliverable of the engagement's pursuit sitting in `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REWORK` is written to `SUSPENDED`, and the status it held immediately before is recorded in `prior_status`; each suspended row's `version` advances. When the hold lifts, each `SUSPENDED` deliverable is restored to exactly the status recorded in its `prior_status` — `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REWORK`, whichever it was — and `prior_status` is cleared back to null, with each restored row's `version` advancing again. Deliverables already at `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` are untouched by a hold in either direction. Deliverables can be listed and narrowed by `opportunity_id`, `lead_consultant_id`, `depends_on_deliverable_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Engagement Economics: Timesheets, the Burn Ledger and the Forward Forecast

Consultants of any firm role may log a weekly timesheet line — partners, managers, quality reviewers and firm leadership can all record time — but nobody may approve their own. A line names the `engagement_id` it charges to, the `deliverable_id` the effort belongs to, the `consultant_id` whose hours these are, the `week_ending_date` identifying the Saturday-ending week of the line as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` date, the `hours` charged to two decimal places, and the boolean `billable`. Setting `billable` to false marks the line as a write-off: it still consumes internal cost and still burns hours, but it never bills the client.

Several checks guard a new line. `hours` must be greater than 0.00 and at most 60.00; anything outside that range is refused with `ERR_TIMESHEET_HOURS_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The named deliverable must belong to the same pursuit as the named engagement, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_IN_ENGAGEMENT_SCOPE`. The combination of `engagement_id`, `deliverable_id`, `consultant_id` and `week_ending_date` must be unique among lines that are still `SUBMITTED` or already `APPROVED`; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIMESHEET_LINE`, while lines that were rejected or reversed do not block a fresh submission for the same week. The `week_ending_date` must fall inside the engagement's planned delivery window, from `planned_start_date` through `planned_end_date` inclusive, otherwise `ERR_TIME_ENTRY_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT_WINDOW`, and it must not be later than the current logical clock date, otherwise `ERR_FUTURE_TIMESHEET_LINE`. No line may be logged against a `CLOSED` engagement.

**Costing at creation.** A line is priced the moment it is created, even though the priced amounts stay inert until it is approved. Its `cost_cents` is `hours` multiplied by the consultant's own `cost_rate_cents_per_hour`, rounded half up to whole cents — this is the internal figure that drives the burn ledger. Its `billed_value_cents` is `hours` multiplied by the firm's hourly billing rate for the consultant's `grade`, multiplied by one minus the engagement's `pinned_scope_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents; when `billable` is false, `billed_value_cents` is 0 instead. One line therefore feeds two different thresholds through two different rates: the internal cost figure drives the margin forecast, and the billed value drives the time-and-materials ceiling. Neither amount is ever re-derived from an aggregate of hours; each is rounded once, per line. A new line begins at `SUBMITTED` with a null `reversal_reason`. Timesheet lines can be listed and narrowed by `engagement_id`, `deliverable_id`, `consultant_id`, `week_ending_date` and `status`.

**Approval, rejection and reversal.** The engagement manager decides each line by submitting a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. The deciding manager must be the engagement's `engagement_manager_consultant_id` and must not be the line's own `consultant_id` — self-approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The line must still be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_TIME_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`, and the engagement's delivery must be either `ACTIVE` or `CLOSING`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID` — deliberately permitting late timesheet clean-up during closing.

Approval moves the line `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and synchronises the ledger in three places at once: the line's `hours` are added to the engagement's `approved_hours_burned`, the line's `cost_cents` are added to the engagement's `burned_cost_cents`, and the line's `hours` are also added to the deliverable's `hours_consumed`. The economics forecast is then recomputed, which may move `economics_state`. Three rows are touched, so three counters advance: the line's `version`, the engagement's `version` and the deliverable's `version` each increment by 1. On a `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` engagement one further gate applies before any of this happens: the sum of `billed_value_cents` across the engagement's already-`APPROVED` lines, plus the `billed_value_cents` of the line being approved, must not exceed the engagement's `not_to_exceed_cents`; a breach is refused with `ERR_NOT_TO_EXCEED_BREACHED`. Rejection moves the line `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, changes nothing in the ledger, and advances only the line's own `version`.

Reversal undoes an approval exactly. Requested by the engagement manager with a `reversal_reason` in the body, it moves the line `APPROVED → REVERSED`, requires the line to be `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_TIME_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID` — and requires that the engagement not be `CLOSED`. The compensation is precise rather than approximate: the line's `hours` are subtracted from `approved_hours_burned`, its `cost_cents` are subtracted from `burned_cost_cents`, and its `hours` are subtracted from the deliverable's `hours_consumed`, restoring the exact pre-approval ledger values. The supplied `reversal_reason` is persisted, economics are recomputed, and the line's, the engagement's and the deliverable's `version` counters each advance by 1. A reversal removes the line's contribution outright; it does not post a compensating negative row. `REJECTED` and `REVERSED` are both terminal for a timesheet line.

**The forward-looking forecast.** Engagement economics are recomputed after every mutation that can move them — an approved timesheet line, a reversed timesheet line, a deliverable reaching `ACCEPTED`, an approved change request, and a rescinded change request — and the same figures are also available as a read-only computed view exposing `hours_burn_pct`, `completion_pct`, `progress_variance_pct`, `projected_cost_cents`, `projected_margin_pct` and `economics_state`.

The burn percentage, `hours_burn_pct`, is 100 multiplied by `approved_hours_burned` divided by `contracted_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The completion percentage, `completion_pct`, is the sum of `completion_weight_pct` across those deliverables of the engagement's pursuit whose status is `ACCEPTED`, rounded half up to two decimal places; deliverables at `SUBMITTED` or `REWORK` contribute nothing. The progress variance, `progress_variance_pct`, is `completion_pct` subtracted from `hours_burn_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places — a positive variance means hours are being consumed faster than value is being accepted.

The margin leg looks forward rather than back. Remaining hours are `approved_hours_burned` subtracted from `contracted_hours`, floored at zero so an over-burned engagement forecasts no negative remainder. Those remaining hours are multiplied by a blended forecast cost rate of 34,000 cents per hour — 340.00 currency units for every unburned contracted hour, regardless of who will actually work them — and that product is rounded half up to whole cents. `projected_cost_cents` is the already-integral `burned_cost_cents` plus that single rounded forecast leg. `projected_margin_pct` is then 100 multiplied by the difference of `contracted_fee_cents` minus `projected_cost_cents`, divided by `contracted_fee_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. All of these percentages are computed from the stored ledger fields rather than re-summed from individual timesheet rows.

Classification tests the two indicators against three bands, and **the first matching band wins**. The engagement is `OVERRUN` when `progress_variance_pct` is 30.00 or greater, or when `projected_margin_pct` is below 0.00. Failing that, it is `AT_RISK` when `progress_variance_pct` is 15.00 or greater, or when `projected_margin_pct` is below 15.00. Otherwise — variance below 15.00 and margin at 15.00 or above — it is `ON_TRACK`. Only `APPROVED` timesheet lines count towards the burn: `SUBMITTED` lines are excluded, and `REJECTED` and `REVERSED` lines are excluded too. `contracted_fee_cents` and `contracted_hours` always reflect every approved change request applied so far.

Because the forecast charges every unburned hour at the blended rate, the model is deliberately pessimistic about work not yet done: approving a change request that adds hours can push the projected margin negative even though the same request also raises the fee.

**Consequences of entering overrun.** The economics dimension moves freely among its three values, but only one boundary carries side effects. Whenever a recompute lands on `OVERRUN` from a state that was not already `OVERRUN` — whether `ON_TRACK → OVERRUN` in a single step or `AT_RISK → OVERRUN` — the system raises exactly one risk escalation on the engagement with `source` set to `ECONOMICS`, `severity` set to `HIGH`, handling status `OPEN`, and an `opened_reason` naming the threshold that was breached. Because that escalation exists at `HIGH`, the engagement's `leadership_review_required` is written true, and the owning client's `at_risk_engagement_count` is incremented by 1 with the client's `version` advancing, but only when this engagement's count of unresolved escalations at `HIGH` or above moves from zero to one. Both single-step and two-step routes into `OVERRUN` fire that identical cascade.

Leaving overrun is asymmetric by design. A recompute that lands on `AT_RISK` from `OVERRUN` creates no escalation and — critically — does not auto-resolve the escalation raised on entry; recovering economics never rescinds a warning firm leadership has already been given. `OVERRUN → ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK → ON_TRACK` and `ON_TRACK → AT_RISK` likewise carry no side effect beyond incrementing the engagement's `version` by 1, which every economics transition does.

### 3.8. Change Control: Identification, Impact, Routing and Approval

When a client asks for something the signed scope does not cover — or when the delivery team spots work that was never priced — the engagement manager raises a change request. The payload names the `engagement_id`, declares `origin` as either `CLIENT_REQUEST` or `DELIVERY_TEAM` depending on who identified the out-of-scope work, carries a `summary` of between 1 and 1000 characters describing the work, quantifies `added_hours` to two decimal places which must be greater than zero and at most 5000.00, names the `added_grade` from the four-grade set whose billing rate will price the effort, and states `requested_schedule_days` of calendar schedule relief, which must be at least 0 and at most 180. It also pins `expected_engagement_version` as described in §2.7. Optionally it carries `supersedes_change_request_id`, naming a prior request on the same engagement that this one replaces; a superseding request pointing at a request belonging to a different engagement is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_IN_ENGAGEMENT_SCOPE`.

A new request lands in `DRAFT`, records `requested_by_consultant_id` from the raising actor's `X-User-ID`, leaves `leadership_approver_consultant_id`, `routing`, `gross_delta_cents`, `fee_delta_cents`, `cumulative_change_pct_at_assessment`, `approved_at` and `rescinded_at` all null, and advances the target engagement's `version` by 1. The change-control lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, then `IMPACT_ASSESSED` as a branch point, then either `PENDING_LEADERSHIP_APPROVAL` or `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL`, then `APPROVED`; `REJECTED` and `RESCINDED` are terminal. Any operation attempted from a status that does not permit it is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. Change requests can be listed and narrowed by `engagement_id`, `requested_by_consultant_id`, `leadership_approver_consultant_id`, `supersedes_change_request_id` and `status`.

**Impact assessment.** The engagement manager assesses impact with no request body. The request must be in `DRAFT`, the acting manager must be the engagement's `engagement_manager_consultant_id`, and the engagement's delivery must be either `MOBILIZING` or `ACTIVE` — a request cannot be assessed once closing has begun, which is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Pricing runs in two rounded legs. `gross_delta_cents` is `added_hours` multiplied by the firm's hourly billing rate for `added_grade`, multiplied by one minus the engagement's `pinned_scope_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — the change reuses the volume discount the original scope earned, not a fresh one. `fee_delta_cents` is then the already-rounded `gross_delta_cents` multiplied by one plus the engagement's `pinned_risk_premium_pct`, again rounded half up to whole cents; the premium applies to the rounded gross, not to the raw product.

Materiality is measured cumulatively and against the **signed** price, never against the current contracted figure. The system sums `fee_delta_cents` across every sibling change request of the same engagement whose status is `APPROVED` — requests in `DRAFT`, `IMPACT_ASSESSED`, either pending state, `REJECTED` or `RESCINDED` contribute nothing — adds the `fee_delta_cents` of the request being assessed, multiplies the total by 100, divides by the engagement's `original_fee_cents`, and rounds half up to two decimal places. That figure is frozen onto the request as `cumulative_change_pct_at_assessment`. Because rescinded and rejected siblings drop out of the sum, rescinding an approved request lowers the cumulative figure that later requests are measured against.

Two thresholds then read that one number. If it is strictly greater than 40.00, the assessment is refused outright with `ERR_CHANGE_EXCEEDS_MATERIALITY` — a cumulative figure of exactly 40.00 is accepted, and 40.01 is not. Otherwise `routing` is written: `LEADERSHIP_THEN_CLIENT` when the figure is 20.00 or greater, and `CLIENT_ONLY` when it is below 20.00. The routing decision immediately determines where the request rests. When routing resolves to `LEADERSHIP_THEN_CLIENT` the status is written straight through `IMPACT_ASSESSED → PENDING_LEADERSHIP_APPROVAL`; when it resolves to `CLIENT_ONLY` the status is written straight through `IMPACT_ASSESSED → PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL`. In both branches the routed status arrives in the very same response that carries `gross_delta_cents`, `fee_delta_cents`, `cumulative_change_pct_at_assessment` and `routing`, so a request is never observed resting at `IMPACT_ASSESSED`. The request's `version` advances by 1.

**Leadership approval.** For a materially large change, firm leadership decides first, submitting a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` with an optional `note`. The request must be at `PENDING_LEADERSHIP_APPROVAL`, and the deciding leader must not be the request's `requested_by_consultant_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either decision writes `leadership_approver_consultant_id` from the deciding leader's `X-User-ID` and advances the request's `version` by 1. An approval moves the request `PENDING_LEADERSHIP_APPROVAL → PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL`, forwarding it to the client. A rejection moves it `PENDING_LEADERSHIP_APPROVAL → REJECTED` and changes no engagement field whatsoever.

**Client approval.** The client contact of the engagement's own client decides, submitting a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` with an optional `note`. The request must be at `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL` and `X-User-ID` must equal the engagement's `client_id`, otherwise `ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. A rejection moves the request `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL → REJECTED` with no engagement field changes.

An approval is the largest cascade in the system. On a `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` engagement the post-change forecast must first stay within the engagement's `not_to_exceed_cents`; a projection that would breach the ceiling is refused with `ERR_NOT_TO_EXCEED_BREACHED`. Engagements on the other two pricing models skip that gate entirely. When it passes, the request moves `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and `approved_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Then five contracted figures move on the engagement in one step: `fee_delta_cents` is added to `contracted_fee_cents`, `added_hours` is added to `contracted_hours`, `requested_schedule_days` is added to `schedule_relief_days`, `planned_end_date` is pushed out by that same number of calendar days, and `cumulative_change_pct` is overwritten with the request's frozen `cumulative_change_pct_at_assessment`; the engagement's `version` advances. On a time-and-materials engagement the ceiling moves with the contract: `not_to_exceed_cents` is raised by the same `fee_delta_cents`. The economics forecast is then recomputed against the new `contracted_fee_cents` and `contracted_hours`, and if that recompute lands on `OVERRUN` from a non-overrun state, the full escalation cascade of §3.7 follows — an `ECONOMICS` escalation at `HIGH` severity, `leadership_review_required` written true, and the client's `at_risk_engagement_count` incremented. The request's own `version` advances by 1.

**Rescission.** A change request can be withdrawn, and who may withdraw it depends on how far it has travelled. A `DRAFT` request may be rescinded by the manager who raised it — the acting identity must equal `requested_by_consultant_id` — moving it `DRAFT → RESCINDED`, stamping `rescinded_at` and touching no engagement field, because no impact was ever applied. An `APPROVED` request may be rescinded by firm leadership, moving it `APPROVED → RESCINDED`, provided the engagement is not `CLOSED`; a closed engagement is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Both routes accept an optional `note` and advance the request's `version` by 1.

Rescinding an approved request reverses its entire contracted impact by recomputation rather than by subtraction. Once the row has left `APPROVED`, the engagement's `contracted_fee_cents` is recomputed as `original_fee_cents` plus the sum of `fee_delta_cents` across the engagement's change requests that remain `APPROVED`; `contracted_hours` and `planned_end_date` are recomputed the same way from the surviving requests' `added_hours` and `requested_schedule_days` respectively, and `schedule_relief_days` follows from the same surviving set; and `cumulative_change_pct` is recomputed from the surviving approved set alone. `rescinded_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the economics forecast is recomputed — which may move `economics_state` in either direction — and the engagement's `version` advances by 1 alongside the request's.

### 3.9. Quality Assurance, Risk Escalation, Closure and Follow-On Development

Quality checkpoints are the firm's own assurance mechanism, and their defining property is independence. A checkpoint is scheduled by either the engagement manager or firm leadership, naming the `engagement_id` under review, the `reviewer_consultant_id` who will conduct it, the `checkpoint_type` — one of `SCOPE_GATE`, `MIDPOINT` or `PRE_DELIVERY` — and a review window given as `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`, where the end must fall on or after the start. Both window dates are inclusive: the window is a closed interval.

Three gates protect independence and spacing. The nominated reviewer must have `quality_reviewer_certified` set to true, otherwise `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The nominated reviewer must be neither the engagement's `lead_partner_consultant_id` nor its `engagement_manager_consultant_id`, since nobody may assure their own engagement; a violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the new window must keep a gap of at least 3 days from every other non-terminal checkpoint window on the same engagement — checkpoints must not be stacked back-to-back — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A newly scheduled checkpoint sits at `SCHEDULED` with `findings_severity` defaulting to `NONE` and a null `note`. Checkpoints can be listed and narrowed by `engagement_id`, `reviewer_consultant_id`, `checkpoint_type` and `status`.

**Recording the outcome.** Only the nominated reviewer records the outcome, and only inside the window. The payload carries the `outcome`, the `findings_severity` observed, and optionally a `note`. The checkpoint must still be `SCHEDULED`; recording an outcome on one that has already reached `PASSED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `FAILED` is refused with `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. The current logical clock date must lie inside the closed window from `window_start_date` through `window_end_date`, boundary days included; outside it, the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. And the severity must be consistent with the outcome: `NONE` for a `PASSED` outcome, either `LOW` or `MEDIUM` for a `CONDITIONAL` outcome, and either `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` for a `FAILED` outcome. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_FINDINGS_SEVERITY_INCONSISTENT`. `PASSED`, `CONDITIONAL` and `FAILED` are all terminal for a checkpoint.

A clean checkpoint moves `SCHEDULED → PASSED`, writing the submitted `findings_severity` and `note` and advancing the checkpoint's `version` by 1. A conditional checkpoint moves `SCHEDULED → CONDITIONAL`, writes the same two fields, advances the `version` by 1, and — this is worth stating explicitly — opens no escalation at all. Findings at `LOW` or `MEDIUM` are recorded for the file, not escalated.

A failed checkpoint moves `SCHEDULED → FAILED` and starts a three-hop cascade. First, one risk escalation is created on the engagement with `source` set to `QUALITY`, `source_quality_review_id` pointing back at this checkpoint, `severity` copied from the recorded `findings_severity`, and an `opened_reason` naming the `checkpoint_type` that failed. Second, the severity decides where that escalation starts: at `HIGH` it is created in `OPEN`, but at `CRITICAL` it is created directly in `DELIVERY_HELD`, which itself drives the engagement `ACTIVE → ON_HOLD` and triggers the deliverable suspension chain described in §3.6 along with the recomputation of `suspended_deliverable_count`. Third, the engagement's `leadership_review_required` is written true and the client's `at_risk_engagement_count` is incremented by 1 — with the client's `version` advancing — whenever the engagement's count of unresolved escalations at severity `HIGH` or above moves from zero to one. Both the checkpoint's `version` and the engagement's `version` advance by 1.

**Escalations and leadership response.** A risk escalation is never created by a client request; it is always system-raised, either by a failed quality checkpoint or by an engagement tipping into overrun, and listing escalations is the only way to discover one. Each row records the `engagement_id` it belongs to, its `source` as `QUALITY` or `ECONOMICS`, its `source_quality_review_id` when the source is a checkpoint and null otherwise, its `severity` as `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, and a machine-written `opened_reason` naming the triggering rule. Its handling status runs `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `DELIVERY_HELD` and terminally `RESOLVED`, and `leadership_response` records the most recent response as `ACKNOWLEDGE`, `HOLD_DELIVERY` or `RESOLVE`. Escalations can be listed and narrowed by `engagement_id`, `source_quality_review_id`, `source`, `severity` and `status`, and the `leadership_response` value may also be echoed on the response payload of a leadership action.

Firm leadership responds by submitting a `response` of `ACKNOWLEDGE`, `HOLD_DELIVERY` or `RESOLVE`. A response not reachable from the escalation's current status — including any response at all after `RESOLVED` — is refused with `ERR_RISK_ESCALATION_STATE_INVALID`. Every response writes `leadership_response` and advances the escalation's `version` by 1.

Acknowledging moves the escalation `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED` and does nothing else. Holding delivery is reachable from either of the two earlier states — `ACKNOWLEDGED → DELIVERY_HELD` and, skipping acknowledgement entirely, `OPEN → DELIVERY_HELD` — and both routes fire an identical cascade; acknowledgement is not a required intermediate step. Holding delivery requires the engagement's delivery to be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. On success it drives the engagement `ACTIVE → ON_HOLD`, suspends every deliverable of the engagement's pursuit that sits in `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REWORK` while recording each one's `prior_status`, and recomputes `suspended_deliverable_count` as the count of that pursuit's `SUSPENDED` deliverables. The engagement's `version` advances by 1 and each suspended deliverable's `version` advances by 1.

Resolving is reachable from all three earlier states — `DELIVERY_HELD → RESOLVED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED → RESOLVED` and `OPEN → RESOLVED` — and carries one conditional precondition. When the escalation's `source` is `QUALITY`, a later checkpoint on the same engagement with the same `checkpoint_type` must already have reached `PASSED` or `CONDITIONAL`; without such a re-review, resolution is refused with `ERR_QUALITY_GATE_PENDING`. Escalations whose source is `ECONOMICS` carry no such requirement. All three routes stamp `resolved_at` from the logical clock and write `leadership_response`. Resolving from `DELIVERY_HELD` additionally releases the hold, but only when no other escalation of the same engagement remains in `DELIVERY_HELD`: at that point the engagement moves `ON_HOLD → ACTIVE`, every `SUSPENDED` deliverable is restored to its recorded `prior_status` with `prior_status` cleared to null, and `suspended_deliverable_count` returns to 0. Resolving from `ACKNOWLEDGED` or from `OPEN` leaves `delivery_state` untouched, because no hold was ever placed. In all three cases the leadership reconciliation runs identically: when the engagement's count of unresolved escalations at severity `HIGH` or above reaches zero, `leadership_review_required` is written false and the client's `at_risk_engagement_count` is decremented by 1 with the client's `version` advancing.

**Closing an engagement.** Closing happens in two acts by two different roles, and each act has its own gate set. The lead partner begins closing, moving the engagement `ACTIVE → CLOSING` with no request body. Four conditions must hold. Every deliverable of the engagement's pursuit must have reached `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLES_NOT_ACCEPTED`. No change request of the engagement may sit in `DRAFT`, `IMPACT_ASSESSED`, `PENDING_LEADERSHIP_APPROVAL` or `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_CHANGE_REQUEST_BLOCKS_CLOSING`. No quality checkpoint of the engagement may still be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_QUALITY_GATE_PENDING`. And no quality checkpoint may sit at `FAILED` whose linked escalation has not reached `RESOLVED`, otherwise `ERR_QUALITY_GATE_FAILED`. The engagement's `version` advances by 1.

Firm leadership then closes the engagement, moving it `CLOSING → CLOSED`, again with no request body. One gate applies: no escalation of the engagement may remain in `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `DELIVERY_HELD`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_ESCALATION_BLOCKS_CLOSE`. On success `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the client's `completed_engagement_count` is incremented by 1 with the client's `version` advancing, and the engagement's own `version` advances by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal for delivery, and beyond reads the only operation a closed engagement still admits is the satisfaction assessment described next.

**Satisfaction and follow-on development.** Once an engagement is closed, its client contact files exactly one satisfaction assessment. The payload names the `engagement_id`, carries three integer scores each between 1 and 10 inclusive — `delivery_score` for delivery quality, `value_score` for value for money and `communication_score` for communication — carries the boolean `would_refer` recording whether the client would act as a reference, and optionally carries free-text `comments`. An assessment filed against an engagement that has not reached `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, and so is a second assessment against an engagement that already has one. The assessment is immutable once created and, as noted in §2.4, carries only `id` and `created_at` as audit properties. Assessments can be listed and narrowed by `engagement_id`.

The composite score, `csat_score`, weights the three ratings and adds a referral bonus outside that weighting. It is 0.40 multiplied by `delivery_score`, plus 0.35 multiplied by `value_score`, plus 0.25 multiplied by `communication_score`, plus a flat 0.25 points when `would_refer` is true and nothing when it is false. Because the three weights themselves add to exactly 1.00, the weighted part alone can never exceed 10.00 and the referral bonus is genuinely additional headroom — a quarter of a point that can carry a borderline engagement over the follow-on gate. The result is clamped at a maximum of 10.00 and then rounded half up to two decimal places, so a perfect ten-ten-ten with a referral still reports exactly 10.00.

That single number gates the firm's next pursuit. A follow-on opportunity — one naming a non-null `origin_engagement_id` — is admitted only when the origin engagement's assessment exists and its `csat_score` is 7.50 or greater; the bar is inclusive, so exactly 7.50 is eligible. Anything lower, or a missing assessment altogether, is refused with `ERR_FOLLOW_ON_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, closing the loop back to pursuit intake in §3.2.
