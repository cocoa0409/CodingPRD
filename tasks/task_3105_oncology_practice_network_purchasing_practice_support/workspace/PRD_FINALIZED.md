# Oncology Practice Network Purchasing and Practice Support API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Community oncology in the United States runs on an uncomfortable economic fact: the drug that a practice buys, stores, and infuses is frequently the single largest line on its balance sheet, and the practice pays for it weeks before a payer reimburses it. A network agreement exists to soften that exposure. By pooling the purchases of many independent practices, the network negotiates tiered acquisition pricing, rebate programs keyed to how much of a practice's spend actually flows through the agreement, and a pathways program that steers regimen selection toward clinically and financially preferred protocols. Alongside purchasing, the network supplies practice-management services — revenue cycle, staffing, and technology engagements — and opens clinical research trials to community sites that would otherwise never sponsor one.

This document specifies the API that the network's operations platform exposes for all of that activity. It covers the commercial spine (network agreements, practice affiliations, tier pricing agreements, drug purchase orders, inventory lots, treatments, and payer claims), the rebate and compliance machinery that audits how faithfully a practice routed and reported its business, the allocation of drugs when supply cannot meet demand, and the affiliation lifecycle including departure and contract unwind. The system is mounted under the top-level path prefix `/networks`; every resource path in §4 is relative to that mount. It operates on a logical clock rather than the wall clock, so that allocation epochs, expirations, grace periods, and effective windows are all deterministic and testable.

Two properties shape nearly every rule that follows. The first is that the platform is adversarial about its own numbers: rebates are computed under two variants that deliberately disagree, shortages are governed by conservation invariants that must hold at every step, and the price a practice observes is not always the price the network intended. The second is that authority and money move together — an operator who submitted a pricing agreement may not approve it, and a practice's dollars are held, reclaimed, and released as its affiliation standing changes.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section collects the rules that apply across the whole surface of the API: who may call it, what every response looks like, how lists are paged, and the cross-cutting invariants that individual workflows are expected to respect.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which it acts. A request missing either header, or naming a role the platform does not recognize, is refused with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER`. A request whose role is known but is not permitted to perform the operation being attempted is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS`.

The platform recognizes exactly six roles: `NetworkAdmin`, `PracticeAdmin`, `RevenueCycleManager`, `PayerAdjudicator`, `NetworkAnalyst`, and `PlatformAdmin`. Their identities are of two kinds, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Practice`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Practice` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a practice principal that names a different `Practice` in a path or body than the one it represents is acting outside its own scope. For the remaining roles — `NetworkAdmin`, `RevenueCycleManager`, `PayerAdjudicator`, `NetworkAnalyst`, and `PlatformAdmin` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier (for instance as `approved_by_user_id` or `submitted_by_user_id` on a `PracticePricingAgreement`) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

`NetworkAdmin` is the network operations owner: onboarding and suspending practices, approving pricing agreements, confirming and shipping orders, running shortage allocation, the whole rebate lifecycle, and contract unwind. `PracticeAdmin` owns the practice's own operational surface — submitting orders, receiving them into inventory, administering treatments, custody of lots, creating providers, drafting its own pricing agreements, adopting or withdrawing from the pathways program, issuing and withdrawing its own departure notices, and filing its compliance report. `RevenueCycleManager` drives the claim side: submitting claims, appealing denials, and working the management-services fee workflow. `PayerAdjudicator` is the external payer's processing identity, recording remittances and denials. `NetworkAnalyst` is a read-only analytics identity with no write authority at all. `PlatformAdmin` is a benchmark operator identity that owns only the test-driving clock endpoint; it is deliberately not a superuser and holds no business write authority. Reads across the network surface are shared by every role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

---

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept a `limit` and an `offset`. `limit` caps the number of items returned and defaults to 10; it may not be smaller than 1 nor larger than 100. `offset` skips that many leading items and defaults to 0. Unless a workflow below says otherwise, every list response is ordered by the resource's creation timestamp in ascending order.

Every list-shaped response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` reports the full size of the filtered result set before paging, and `has_more` reports whether further items remain past the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Each principal resource in this API — `OncologyNetwork`, `Practice`, `Provider`, `DrugProduct`, `PracticePricingAgreement`, `DrugPurchaseOrder`, `InventoryLot`, `LotConsumption`, `PatientTreatment`, `PayerClaim`, `RebateProgram`, `RebateQualification`, `PathwayProtocol`, `PracticeManagementAgreement`, `ResearchParticipation`, `DrugShortage`, `ShortageAllocation`, and `AffiliationContract` — carries a common set of system-managed fields. Its `id` is a unique, system-generated identifier formatted as a UUID with no type prefix or registry segment; nothing about a resource's kind is encoded in its identifier. Its `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits marking first creation. Its `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format marking last modification, and it is refreshed on every change to the resource's properties. Its `version` is a system-managed integer that increments with every modification to that resource; it is the value against which optimistic concurrency checks are made, as described in §2.8. A change to a computed field on a resource — a recomputed `tier_level`, a refreshed `unsettled_balance_cents` — is a mutation like any other and refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version`.

The platform reads time from a logical clock rather than the host's wall clock. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and until an operator moves it the clock continues to read that instant; it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. Every instant in this document — `administered_at`, `reported_at`, `expires_at`, `term_start`, term ends, effective windows, and the epoch boundaries described in §3.9 — is evaluated against that logical clock. A single test-driving operation, restricted to an operator holding `PlatformAdmin`, moves the clock to a supplied `current_utc`; it is the only way the clock moves.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Requests that target a resource nested in a path resolve the identifiers left to right. The platform validates that each path identifier names an existing resource before it evaluates anything about the deeper one, and rejects the request with the `404` not-found error belonging to the first identifier that fails. A request naming a `Provider` inside a `Practice` that does not exist surfaces the practice-level not-found error, never the provider-level one; the error identifiers used across the surface are `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MANAGEMENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHORTAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, and the generic fallback `ERR_NOT_FOUND`.

Cross-entity reference rules apply to identifiers supplied in a request body rather than in the path. Every body-supplied foreign key must resolve to a resource inside the same network as the request's context; a body key naming an entity that belongs to a different network is refused with `ERR_CROSS_NETWORK_REFERENCE`. Where a body key names a practice, it must name the same practice the path already identified; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PRACTICE_MISMATCH`. Missing required fields, fields carrying the wrong type, and values outside a closed enumeration are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. Effective Windows and the Temporal Conflict Model

Commercial instruments in this platform are windowed, and the boundary conventions matter. A `PracticePricingAgreement` governs the half-open interval that begins at its `effective_from` and ends at its `effective_to`, so the instant `effective_from` is inside the window and the instant `effective_to` is outside it. An `effective_to` left null means the window never closes. An `effective_to` that is present must be strictly later than `effective_from`; otherwise the agreement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW`; on submission this window-shape test is evaluated unconditionally and ahead of the state and affiliation conditions, alongside the overlapping-window check that follows. Windows of this kind may not be held loosely: for a given practice and a given `tier_level`, no two agreements may hold intersecting windows while either is in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `ACTIVE`; a submission that would create such an intersection is refused with `ERR_PRICING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. That conflict check is evaluated on the submitted agreement unconditionally — regardless of that agreement's own current status — and ahead of the state and affiliation conditions that would otherwise guard the submission, so a row that is not `DRAFT` still surfaces `ERR_PRICING_WINDOW_OVERLAP` before `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT` when its window intersects a live one.

`PracticeManagementAgreement` terms are closed intervals: both `term_start` and `term_end` are inside the engagement. A `term_end` earlier than `term_start` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MANAGEMENT_TERM`. Because the intervals are closed, two engagements for the same `service_line` at the same practice may not merely abut — a new engagement must begin at least 30 days after the previous engagement's `term_end`, and a shorter separation is refused with `ERR_MANAGEMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`. Affiliation contracts likewise use closed terms, an inverted term pair being refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_TERM`.

Two rules tie the commercial windows back to the affiliation relationship. A pricing agreement's `effective_from` may not precede the practice's earliest affiliation contract `term_start`, and an order may only be confirmed while the practice's `ACTIVE` affiliation contract covers the confirmation instant — broadly, `term_start` at or before the confirmation instant and the instant strictly before `term_end`. The first is refused with `ERR_PRICING_WINDOW_BEFORE_CONTRACT` and the second with `ERR_CONTRACT_LAPSED`.

### 2.7. Monetary Values, Rounding, and the Two Variants Principle

Every monetary quantity in this API is an integer number of cents. Where a formula produces a fractional cent, it is rounded half up to the nearest whole cent — `0.5` and above rounds away from zero, anything below rounds down. Ratios and percentages that are stored rather than paid — `market_share_pct`, `compliance_score`, `pathway_adherence_pct`, `denial_ratio` — are reported to four decimal places, rounded half up. `pathway_adherence_pct` in particular is persisted at four decimal places. Computed unit prices and costs reported on resources are integers, so no client ever has to guess a scale.

Rounding order is itself part of the contract, because two arithmetically plausible orders produce different answers on identical input. Where this document pins an order, the pinned order is authoritative and takes precedence over what a naive computation would do. Two named quantities express their own rounding-order pins explicitly: the rebate amount is computed by summing the per-order rebate contributions and rounding the sum once, and the claim's billed amount is assembled from its component lines as an exact sum that is rounded once at the end.

The platform also computes several quantities under two deliberately divergent variants. A dual-variant pair is not an ambiguity to be resolved; both variants are computed from the same underlying rows, both are legitimate readings, and the difference between them is observable through the API. This principle appears in network spend credited during versus after a measured period, in the denominator used for market share, in on-hand inventory counted physically versus counted as usable, in the dosage plan that bills a partial vial versus one that absorbs it, and in the write-off headroom available to a claim. Each pair is specified where its workflow lives.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Practice Record

Three write operations serialize against the `Practice` record's `version`. Submitting a pricing agreement, administering a treatment, and submitting a payer claim each require the caller to present the practice version it last observed, supplied as `expected_practice_version`. If the value presented does not equal the practice's current `version`, the request is refused with `ERR_VERSION_MISMATCH` and HTTP status 412; the check runs before any business logic for that operation is evaluated. This is a stale-view rejection, not a locking protocol: a caller that has re-read the practice and presents the current version proceeds normally. The field is not optional on these three operations. No other endpoint in the API accepts a version field of this kind.

The reason the practice's version is the right thing to pin is that the fields these operations read are precisely the ones other operations rewrite. Receiving an order posts to `trailing_12m_network_purchases_cents` and can therefore re-derive `tier_level`; a receipt, an adjudication, or a denial settlement moves `unsettled_balance_cents`. A client holding an older view of the practice would be pricing, drawing, or billing against a basis that no longer exists.

### 2.9. Conservation Invariants in Drug Shortage Allocation

Shortage allocation is governed by a conserved quantity the platform names `NETWORK_SHORTAGE_SUPPLY_UNITS`, and the invariant that protects it is checked at every allocation run and every grant revocation. The sum of `allocated_units` across a shortage's grants, counting every grant that is not in `REVOKED` status, plus the shortage's `unallocated_units`, equals the shortage's `supply_available_units`. In wording: the units on the shelf are never lost and never duplicated — every unit is either sitting in unallocated supply or held by a live grant.

A second quantity is conserved alongside it, `COMMITTED_SUPPLY_UNITS`. The sum of `committed_units` across a shortage's grants plus the `quantity_units` of every purchase order for that product that is in `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `BACKORDERED` status equals the shortage's `allocated_units`. Granting a unit out of unallocated supply and revoking it back are a compensating pair that leaves the first invariant intact; committing units on confirmation and releasing them on cancellation are a compensating pair that leaves the second intact. After an allocation run the grants created sum to exactly `supply_available_units` with `unallocated_units` at zero. A departure cascade that runs to completion but leaves either sum drifting is a defect.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and the Commercial Terms Freeze

Approval of a pricing agreement is separated from its authorship. The network user who submitted an agreement may not be the user who approves it, and the practice's own `PracticeAdmin` identity may not approve an agreement it belongs to. Either condition is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The agreement carries `submitted_by_user_id` recording who submitted it and `approved_by_user_id` recording who approved it, and the comparison is made between those two recorded values.

A network may be placed into a frozen commercial window by an operator holding `NetworkAdmin`. While `is_commercial_terms_frozen` is true, no new pricing agreement may be created or submitted — attempts are refused with `ERR_TERMS_FROZEN` — and the network may only be frozen once; a second freeze request while already frozen is refused with `ERR_TERMS_ALREADY_FROZEN`. A freeze is not a change to any agreement's own status. It changes the price: an agreement inside a frozen window keeps its `ACTIVE` status while the discount it nominates is not credited, as described where pricing is specified in §3.5. The flag is recomputed rather than merely toggled — it reads true while at least one practice of the network is `DEPARTED` or `DEPARTING`.

### 2.11. Validation Ordering

Validations that apply broadly are evaluated in a fixed order, so that a request failing several of them surfaces a predictable one. Authentication precedes authorization: the presence and validity of the user context headers is checked first, and only then whether the acting role is permitted for the operation. Path-resource existence follows, resolved left to right as described in §2.5. The stale-version check of §2.8 comes next, ahead of any business logic. Temporal conflicts — overlapping pricing windows, the management-term blackout, and the affiliation-coverage rules — follow. State preconditions come next, then the domain-safety rules: the oncology regimen guard, then stock sufficiency, then the shortage consumption cap and the shortage-restricted ordering rule, then the denial-ratio breach, then segregation of duties, then rebate holdback, then payer overpayment, then the commercial-terms freeze, then the shortage-eligibility and threshold rules, then cross-entity referential integrity, and finally field presence and typing. Where a workflow below names a specific ordering between two checks — as the departure cascade does, checking open management agreements before the active-contract condition, or as the pricing-window overlap does, running ahead of the state and affiliation checks — that named ordering governs.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Network and the Engine of Time

Everything begins with a network agreement holder. A `NetworkAdmin` creates one by supplying its `name`, its `network_agreement_code` — the GPO or network agreement identifier that the outside world will recognize it by — the `shortage_allocation_method` it will default to when supply must be rationed, and the `tier_market_share_boundaries` ladder that maps purchase volume to pricing tiers. Two rebate-qualification parameters come along at the same time: `rebate_market_share_threshold_pct`, the fraction of a practice's drug spend that must flow through network contracts before any rebate is owed, and `rebate_min_compliance_score`, the weighted compliance floor that must also be met. The network's buy-and-bill resale terms are set as `payment_terms_days`, which defaults to 60 when not supplied, and the window in which a late compliance report will still be accepted is `compliance_report_grace_days`, which defaults to 30 days. On creation, `allocation_epoch_index` starts at 0, `is_commercial_terms_frozen` starts false, and the ladder is stored in the ordered descending form it was supplied in.

The allocation epoch is the platform's coarsest unit of commercial time, and the canonical way it moves is an ops-run checkpoint that a `NetworkAdmin` invokes with a positive `by_epochs` value no larger than 12; `allocation_epoch_index` advances by that many epochs at once, and the network's `version` advances with it. Moving the epoch is rarely a bookkeeping formality, because several consequences are attached to it. Practices whose `affiliation_status` is `DEPARTING` and whose `departure_effective_epoch_index` has arrived at or below the new `allocation_epoch_index` become `DEPARTED` and run their full departure cascade — the epoch's arrival, not any caller's action, is what actually triggers a departure to take effect. Rebate qualifications still in `PENDING` whose `period_epoch_index` is below the new epoch and whose `compliance_report_verified` is false are moved to `DISQUALIFIED`. Qualifications that are `QUALIFIED` or `PAID` and whose period now sits strictly behind the current epoch have their `rebate_amount_cents` recomputed under the final variant described in §3.8. Shortage grants whose `expires_epoch_index` has arrived are `EXPIRED` and release their uncommitted units. Finally `is_commercial_terms_frozen` is recomputed across the network.

The advance is guarded. A `by_epochs` value that is not a positive integer, or that exceeds 12, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. More subtly, the advance is refused with `ERR_OPEN_REBATE_CLAIMS_EXIST` when any rebate qualification whose `period_epoch_index` is at or before the target epoch is still `PENDING`; the check is made before the epoch moves at all, so a half-advanced state is impossible. The logic is that a period should not be closed while its settlement is still unresolved.

### 3.2. Practice Onboarding and the Two Independent State Axes

A practice enters the network as a prospect. A `NetworkAdmin` creates it with a `practice_name`, an organizational `practice_npi` of ten digits, a two-letter `state_code`, a `drug_shortage_baseline_units` figure that defaults to zero and will later serve as its pro-rata weight when supply is rationed, a `pathway_steering_target_pct` representing the adherence level the pathways program will steer it toward and defaulting to 0.75, and its own `denial_ratio_threshold_pct` governing when its pricing is suspended, defaulting to 0.20. A new practice carries `affiliation_status` of `PROSPECTIVE`, `pathway_steering_status` of `NOT_ADOPTED`, `tier_level` of `TIER_1`, `trailing_12m_network_purchases_cents` of 0, `unsettled_balance_cents` of 0, and `is_rebate_qualified` false. Its `network_id` binds it to the owning network.

Two state axes then evolve on the same practice row, and they never consult each other. The first is `affiliation_status`, which begins at `PROSPECTIVE`, may pass through `AFFILIATED`, `SUSPENDED`, and `DEPARTING`, and terminates at `DEPARTED`. The second is `pathway_steering_status`, which begins at `NOT_ADOPTED`, moves between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, and is never terminal — a practice may withdraw from the pathways program and re-adopt it any number of times, and it may perfectly well be `DEPARTED` on the affiliation axis while its pathway axis still reads `SUSPENDED`.

Affiliation proper is a `NetworkAdmin` action, and it requires several things to be true. The practice must still be `PROSPECTIVE`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_PROSPECTIVE`. At least one pricing agreement for the practice must exist in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_PRICING_AGREEMENT`. No other practice in the same network may already carry the same `practice_npi`, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRACTICE_NPI` — uniqueness of the identifier is network-scoped, not global. When the action succeeds, `affiliation_status` becomes `AFFILIATED`, `tier_level` is recomputed from `trailing_12m_network_purchases_cents` against the network's ladder, the network's freeze flag is recomputed, and the practice's `version` advances.

The tier a practice is sold at and the tier it earns are two different things, and affiliation is where they are distinguished. The practice's `tier_level` is set directly from the `tier_level` of the most recently created pricing agreement on file for it — where several share a creation instant, the one with the latest `effective_from` wins, and if that also ties, the one whose identifier sorts greater lexicographically. The practice consumes the tier it was sold; it is not, at this moment, recomputed from purchase history. The recomputation from `trailing_12m_network_purchases_cents` happens on the affiliation action and again on every receipt, as §3.5 explains.

Suspension and reinstatement form the third leg of the affiliation lifecycle. A `NetworkAdmin` suspends an affiliated practice, moving `affiliation_status` to `SUSPENDED`; the action requires the practice to currently be `AFFILIATED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`, and it is reason-agnostic in one respect — if the practice's `pathway_steering_status` was `ACTIVE`, it becomes `SUSPENDED` as well. Reinstatement returns the practice to `AFFILIATED` and requires that the practice currently be `SUSPENDED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_SUSPENDED`. It additionally requires that the practice's count of practice-management agreements in `SUSPENDED` status be zero; a lingering suspended engagement is refused with `ERR_OPEN_MANAGEMENT_SUSPENSION`, because a practice may not return to good standing while a management engagement it owes money under is itself frozen. Reinstatement does not restore the pathway axis — `pathway_steering_status` remains `NOT_ADOPTED` or `SUSPENDED` exactly as it was.

A `NetworkAdmin` may also withdraw a prospect outright rather than let it lapse. Withdrawal is available only while `affiliation_status` is `PROSPECTIVE`, failing otherwise with `ERR_PRACTICE_DEPARTURE_NOT_ALLOWED`, and it requires an `ACTIVE` affiliation contract to exist for the practice, failing with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_AFFILIATION_CONTRACT`. The prospect becomes `DEPARTED`, its affiliation contract becomes `TERMINATED` with `rebate_holdback_cents` of 0 and `open_orders_closed` set true, and any purchase order still in `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `BACKORDERED` is `CANCELLED` without any return to supplier.

### 3.3. Pricing Agreements and the Tier Ladder

A pricing agreement is how a practice's commercial terms become concrete. A `PracticeAdmin` or a `NetworkAdmin` creates one in `DRAFT` by naming the `tier_level` it offers, the `discount_pct` applied off-invoice to the product's `network_base_price_cents`, the `effective_from` and `effective_to` instants bounding its window, and the routing term `routing_includes_out_of_scope` which decides how the practice's out-of-scope billed volume will be treated when market share is measured in §3.8. The discount may not exceed 0.35. On creation the agreement records `submitted_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and `status_changed_by` as `MANUAL`, and the practice it belongs to is identified by `practice_id`.

Submission moves the agreement from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_APPROVAL` and is a `PracticeAdmin` action — the three stale-version rules of §2.8 apply here, so the request must carry `expected_practice_version` matching the practice's current `version` and is refused with `ERR_VERSION_MISMATCH` at status 412 if it does not. Beyond the version check, submission requires the agreement to be `DRAFT`, failing with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`; requires the network not to be frozen, failing with `ERR_TERMS_FROZEN`; requires the practice to be `AFFILIATED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`; and requires that no other agreement of the same practice and same `tier_level` holding an intersecting window is already `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_PRICING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The window itself must be well formed per §2.6 and must not begin before the practice's earliest affiliation contract `term_start`.

Approval is a `NetworkAdmin` action and completes a loop that can also be reversed. It requires the agreement to be `PENDING_APPROVAL`, failing with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING`, and enforces the segregation of duties described in §2.10 — the approver may be neither the user recorded in `submitted_by_user_id` nor the practice's own `PracticeAdmin` identity, with either violation refused as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On approval the agreement becomes `ACTIVE`, `approved_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` while `submitted_by_user_id` keeps its value, and the practice's `tier_level` is set to this agreement's `tier_level`. Every other `ACTIVE` agreement of the same practice and same `tier_level` whose window overlaps is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, with `superseded_by_agreement_id` pointing at the newly approved agreement and `status_changed_by` set to `AGREEMENT_APPROVAL` on each superseded row. Because the routing term may have just changed, `market_share_pct` is recomputed for the practice's open rebate qualification rows. The practice's `version` advances.

Three reversion paths exist from a pending agreement. A `NetworkAdmin` may return it to `DRAFT`, which clears `approved_by_user_id` to null; may reject it outright, moving it to `REJECTED`; or, once it is `ACTIVE`, may return it to `DRAFT` again — but that last path is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_IN_USE` if any purchase order priced under the agreement has already reached `CONFIRMED`, `SHIPPED`, or `RECEIVED`. Orders already submitted under an agreement that is returned to draft keep the `pricing_tier_applied` and `unit_price_cents` they were pinned with, because pricing is fixed at submission and never revisited.

An `ACTIVE` agreement may also be superseded deliberately by a `NetworkAdmin`, or superseded automatically as part of a departure unwind, as §3.11 describes; both routes require the agreement to be `ACTIVE` and refuse otherwise with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A fourth path leads sideways into suspension: when the practice's denial ratio breaches its threshold — the mechanism is described with claims in §3.9 — an agreement in `ACTIVE` status becomes `SUSPENDED` with `status_changed_by` set to `DENIAL_RATIO_BREACH`. That suspension has a price consequence and a transparency consequence at once. The practice's `tier_level` recomputes to `TIER_1` while any of its agreements is `SUSPENDED`, so a suspended practice buys at the entry tier; and from the compliance-scoring standpoint the practice's price-transparency component of §3.8 drops to zero. Reinstatement of a suspended agreement is a `NetworkAdmin` action requiring the agreement to be `SUSPENDED`, failing with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SUSPENDED`, and requiring the practice's denial ratio to have returned to at or below its threshold — a ratio still strictly above it is refused with `ERR_DENIAL_RATIO_STILL_BREACHED`. Reinstatement recomputes `tier_level` from `trailing_12m_network_purchases_cents` against the ladder.

### 3.4. The Drug Catalogue, Prescribers, and the Allocating of Scarce Supply

The network's catalogue is maintained by a `NetworkAdmin`. A drug product is created with an 11-digit `ndc` that must be unique within the network, a `brand_name` and `generic_name`, a `therapeutic_class` drawn from `CYTOTOXIC`, `IMMUNOTHERAPY`, `SUPPORTIVE_CARE`, and `ORAL_TARGETED`, a `unit_type` of `VIAL`, `MG`, or `TABLET`, and a `network_base_price_cents` that is the acquisition base before any practice tier discount. The `is_shortage_candidate` flag, which defaults to false, marks products that may be placed under shortage allocation. A product begins `ACTIVE`. A `NetworkAdmin` may recall a product, which moves it to `RECALLED` and blocks ordering entirely — both order placement and order confirmation fail with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE` — but a recall is refused with `ERR_SHORTAGE_NOT_ACTIVE` while any shortage event for that product remains in `REPORTED`, `ALLOCATING`, or `ACTIVE` status, since a recalled product may not carry a live shortage.

Prescribers live under their employing practice and are created by a `PracticeAdmin` with a `full_name`, an individual `npi` of ten digits, a `specialty` of `MEDICAL_ONCOLOGY`, `HEMATOLOGY`, or `RADIATION_ONCOLOGY`, and an `is_billing_provider` flag that defaults true and governs whether administrations by this provider are billable. A provider starts `ACTIVE`. A `PracticeAdmin` may deactivate a provider, moving it to `INACTIVE`, but only while it has no `PatientTreatment` in `SCHEDULED` status; a scheduled administration blocks the deactivation and the request fails with `ERR_TREATMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The provider then leaves the candidate set for new treatments. Reactivation returns it to `ACTIVE`, and both operations fail with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` when the provider is not in the state the operation requires.

When a product becomes scarce, the network rations it. A `NetworkAdmin` reports a shortage against a `drug_product_id`, recording the `reported_at` instant, the `supply_available_units` the network can distribute in the current allocation epoch, and the `allocation_method` to apply. A reported shortage begins `REPORTED` with `allocated_units` of 0 and `unallocated_units` equal to the supplied `supply_available_units`, and it also carries an `unclaimed_authorization_cents` counter which tracks consumption authorization granted but not yet committed — the second conserved quantity described in §2.9. Allocation is refused with `ERR_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` when `supply_available_units` is zero, since a shortage with no supply cannot be allocated, and refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PRACTICES` when no practice in the network is `AFFILIATED`. Only affiliated practices receive grants; `PROSPECTIVE`, `DEPARTING`, `DEPARTED`, and `SUSPENDED` practices receive nothing, so a practice the run places no grant for holds no allocation for that shortage and a shortage-restricted order it places is refused with `ERR_NO_SHORTAGE_ALLOCATION` because no grant can cover it.

Running the allocation waterfall moves the shortage from `REPORTED` to `ALLOCATING`, failing with `ERR_SHORTAGE_NOT_REPORTED` from any other source state, or re-runs it in place while it is already `ALLOCATING` — a shortage may be re-allocated before activation, and each re-run must remain consistent with the conservation invariant. The run is supplied a `practice_consumption_authorization_cents` map from practice to cents; that figure sets each created grant's `consumption_authorization_cents` and seeds the shortage's `unclaimed_authorization_cents`. One `ShortageAllocation` is created per eligible practice, each carrying an `allocation_rank` reflecting its position in the method's ordering and an `expires_epoch_index` one epoch beyond the current `allocation_epoch_index`. On a re-run, every prior grant still `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` is first `REVOKED`, releasing its units back into unallocated supply, before fresh grants are created and ranked anew. Whichever method runs, the created grants sum to exactly `supply_available_units`, with `allocated_units` set to that sum and `unallocated_units` falling to zero.

The three methods are distinguished by how they weight and order, and the same product may be allocated under different methods on different shortages. Under `PRO_RATA_BASELINE`, each affiliated practice's weight is its `drug_shortage_baseline_units`; every practice's raw quota is the supply multiplied by its weight and divided by the sum of all weights, rounded half up to a whole unit. Because independent rounding does not generally reconcile to the supply, a remainder pass runs: if the rounded quotas undershoot the supply, single units are handed out in order of decreasing fractional part of the raw weight, ties broken by `Practice.created_at` ascending; if they overshoot, single units are clawed back in order of increasing integral part of the raw weight, ties broken by `Practice.created_at` descending. A practice with a baseline of zero receives nothing.

Under `PRIORITY_PATHWAY` the ordering is by clinical alignment rather than by weight. Practices are sorted by `pathway_adherence_pct` descending, ties broken by `Practice.created_at` ascending, and each is filled up to its own baseline demand of `drug_shortage_baseline_units`, bounded by whatever supply remains. When the remaining supply cannot cover a practice's baseline demand, that practice exhausts it and every later practice in the order takes whatever floor remains, which may be zero units. The ordering only re-orders; it never re-weights, which is why a high-adherence small practice can out-rank a large low-adherence one, and why a practice whose adherence sits exactly at the network's steering target still ranks strictly above one whose adherence is below it. Under `EQUAL_SPLIT` the supply is divided evenly and the remainder is distributed one unit per practice in `Practice.created_at` ascending order.

Activation moves the shortage from `ALLOCATING` to `ACTIVE`, refusing any other source state with `ERR_SHORTAGE_NOT_ALLOCATING`, and it is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_INCOMPLETE` unless the sum of `allocated_units` across the shortage's grants equals `supply_available_units` exactly. Activation also pushes the underlying `DrugProduct` to `SHORTAGE_RESTRICTED`. Resolution moves an `ACTIVE` shortage to `RESOLVED`, expires every grant still in `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` and releases their uncommitted units, and returns the product to `ACTIVE` provided no other shortage for it remains in `REPORTED`, `ALLOCATING`, or `ACTIVE`.

Every grant is bound to the shortage event that produced it through `shortage_id`, and to the practice that receives it through `practice_id`. Each also carries a `consumption_authorization_cents` ceiling supplied by the allocation run's per-practice map — the dollar figure the practice may commit against that grant — and a `consumed_authorization_cents` counter tracking how much of that ceiling confirmed orders have already absorbed. Unit allocation and dollar consumption are both enforced against the same grant, which is why a practice can be out of units while still holding authorization dollars, or the reverse.

Each grant carries its own lifecycle. A grant begins `GRANTED`. It becomes `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` when a shortage purchase order is confirmed against it and `0 < committed_units < allocated_units`, and `FULLY_COMMITTED` when `committed_units` reaches `allocated_units` exactly — from either `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED`. Cancelling the last order against a partially committed grant returns it to `GRANTED` when `committed_units` falls to zero. A grant expires when the network advances to an epoch at or beyond its `expires_epoch_index` and it is not yet fully committed, its uncommitted units, being those allocated but not committed, flowing back to `unallocated_units`. A grant is `REVOKED` when its practice reaches `DEPARTED`, and revocation returns the grant's entire `allocated_units` — committed or not — to unallocated supply.

### 3.5. Buying, Receiving, and the Price a Practice Actually Pays

Purchase orders are the practice's side of the buy-and-bill economics. A `PracticeAdmin` submits one naming a `practice_id`, a `drug_product_id`, a `quantity_units` in the product's `unit_type`, and optionally the `shortage_allocation_id` the order draws on, which is null for an unallocated order. On submission the order enters `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` records the logical clock, and its pricing is resolved immediately and pinned: `unit_price_cents` is the discounted unit price the practice observes, `extended_cost_cents` is that unit price multiplied by `quantity_units`, and `pricing_tier_applied` records the tier actually used. An order may be submitted with an idempotency key, in which case a repeat submission of the same key is honored as the same order rather than duplicated. Restating the shortfall: a shortage-restricted order must name a grant covering both its quantity and its dollar value, or it is refused with `ERR_NO_SHORTAGE_ALLOCATION`, and a supplied `shortage_allocation_id` must resolve to a grant of the same network, a cross-network identifier being refused with `ERR_CROSS_NETWORK_REFERENCE`; a grant that belongs to the same network but to a different practice is refused with `ERR_PRACTICE_MISMATCH`.

The price resolution is where the tier ladder becomes money. The platform seeks the single `ACTIVE` pricing agreement for the practice whose half-open window covers the order's effective instant, meaning `effective_from` at or before the submission instant and the submission instant strictly before `effective_to`, with a null `effective_to` meaning the window never closes. Where several `ACTIVE` agreements cover the instant, the one with the latest `effective_from` wins; where that ties, the latest `created_at` wins; where that also ties, the agreement whose identifier is greater lexicographically wins — identifiers serve only as a final determinism tie-break over an otherwise indistinguishable set, never as a primary key. The observed unit price is the product's `network_base_price_cents` multiplied by one minus the agreement's `discount_pct`, rounded half up once to a whole number of cents. The `pricing_tier_applied` recorded on the order is the resolved agreement's `tier_level`, which is the practice's current tier made concrete in the agreement row.

Three variants of that resolution exist, and they are the reason a practice's provisional and final spend can diverge. The `agreement_discount` variant applies when an active agreement resolves: the unit price is discounted and the tier is recorded. The `list_price_fallback` variant applies when no active agreement covers the instant: the unit price is the `network_base_price_cents` with no discount at all, and an order priced this way is the reason a rebate measured provisionally can shrink once reconciled, because dollars that were not routed at agreed contract pricing earn no rebate once the period closes. The `frozen_terms_list_price` variant applies when the order's instant sits inside a frozen commercial-terms window: the agreement keeps its `ACTIVE` status but the discount is deliberately not credited, so the order lands at the same list price as the fallback while having reached it through a live agreement. The distinction between the fallback and the frozen path matters because only orders whose `pricing_tier_applied` came from the fallback are excluded from final credited spend — a frozen-window order did resolve through an agreement.

Confirmation is a `NetworkAdmin` action and is where the order becomes a commitment. It requires the order to be `SUBMITTED`, failing with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SUBMITTED`; requires the practice to be `AFFILIATED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED` — a network-wide commercial-terms freeze is deliberately not a blocker here; requires the practice's `ACTIVE` affiliation contract to cover the confirmation instant, failing with `ERR_CONTRACT_LAPSED`; and requires the product to be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_ORDERABLE`. If the product is the subject of a shortage in `REPORTED`, `ALLOCATING`, or `ACTIVE` status, the order must name a grant for this practice covering its `quantity_units`, failing with `ERR_NO_SHORTAGE_ALLOCATION`, and that grant must carry that many uncommitted units, failing with `ERR_ALLOCATION_OVERCOMMIT` — the same cap applies when a shortage-restricted order is first placed. On confirmation the order becomes `CONFIRMED`; if it draws on a grant, the grant's `committed_units` rises by the order's quantity, the grant's status becomes `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` or `FULLY_COMMITTED` accordingly, and the parent shortage's `allocated_units` rises by the same quantity, with the shortage's `version` advancing. Crucially, `trailing_12m_network_purchases_cents` is untouched at confirmation: only receipts post to the trailing-window basis.

A `NetworkAdmin` may ship a confirmed order, moving it to `SHIPPED`; may backorder one, moving it to `BACKORDERED` while retaining its allocation commitment — the units are not released back to supply merely because the order is waiting. Both of those transitions require the order to be `CONFIRMED` and refuse any other status with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_CONFIRMED`. Cancellation is a `PracticeAdmin` action available from `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `BACKORDERED`, and any other status fails with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_CANCELLABLE`. Cancelling an order that drew on a grant decreases the grant's `committed_units` and the shortage's `allocated_units` by the order's quantity, restoring `unallocated_units` by exactly the same amount. A backordered order may be re-confirmed by a `NetworkAdmin`, which requires it to be `BACKORDERED` — the failure code is `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SUBMITTED`, treating a revive as a submission-like path — and requires an unallocated grant for the practice still to cover the quantity when the product is shortage-restricted, failing with `ERR_NO_SHORTAGE_ALLOCATION`. A `SHIPPED` order is received by a `PracticeAdmin`, supplying the `lot_number` and `expires_at`; receipt requires the order to be `SHIPPED`, failing with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SHIPPED`, and requires `quantity_units` to be greater than zero, failing with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Receipt is the moment the network's money memory moves. The order becomes `RECEIVED` with `received_at` at the logical clock. An `InventoryLot` is created under the practice, carrying `practice_id`, the `drug_product_id` it holds, the `purchase_order_id` of the receipt that created it, and the `lot_number` and `expires_at` supplied on the receipt, with `quantity_received` and `quantity_remaining` both equal to the order's `quantity_units`, an `acquisition_cost_cents_per_unit` copied from the order's `unit_price_cents`, `wastage_units`, `absorbed_cost_basis_cents`, and `waste_cost_basis_cents` at zero, and status `AVAILABLE`. The practice's `trailing_12m_network_purchases_cents` rises by the order's `extended_cost_cents` — gross, pre-discount dollars, so the trailing figure measures volume rather than net cost — and `tier_level` is recomputed against the network ladder, which is exactly why a submitting client's view of the practice may be stale by the time it next writes to it. The practice's `version` advances. If the order drew on a grant that is now fully committed, the grant becomes `FULLY_COMMITTED`.

### 3.6. Inventory Custody, Expiry, and the Two Ways of Counting Stock

A lot is `AVAILABLE` when it is in the practice's custody and eligible to be drawn. A `PracticeAdmin` may quarantine an available lot, moving it to `QUARANTINED` and removing it from the candidate set for draws; the action requires the lot to be `AVAILABLE`, failing with `ERR_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, and is refused with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINE_BLOCKED_BY_SHORTAGE` when the lot's drug product is currently the subject of a shortage in `ACTIVE` status, since a practice may not pull scarce product out of circulation while the network is rationing it. Releasing a quarantined lot returns it to `AVAILABLE`, requires the lot to be `QUARANTINED` — failing with `ERR_LOT_NOT_QUARANTINED` — and is refused with `ERR_LOT_EXPIRED` when `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, so a lot cannot be released back into circulation already dead. Expiry itself is evaluated against the logical clock, or against a supplied `as_of` instant when a `PracticeAdmin` or `NetworkAdmin` asks the platform to read a lot's expiry directly; a lot whose `expires_at` is at or before the evaluated instant moves to `EXPIRED` and is excluded from every future draw. Expiry also fires as a side effect of administering a treatment, for every one of the practice's available lots whose `expires_at` has been reached by the administration instant, and a quarantined lot that reaches its expiration instant while quarantined becomes `EXPIRED` too. A lot that is drawn down to zero remaining units becomes `DEPLETED`.

Because a lot can be physically present but commercially useless, the platform reports on-hand stock under two variants that are computed from the same rows and deliberately disagree at one boundary. Both filter to lots belonging to the practice and the named product with status `AVAILABLE`, so quarantined, expired, and depleted lots are excluded from both. The expiring variant sums `quantity_remaining` over those lots with no expiry filter at all — a lot whose `expires_at` has already passed still counts, because it is physically on the shelf. The usable variant sums `quantity_remaining` over lots whose `expires_at` is strictly later than the instant being evaluated, because a lot expiring exactly at that instant is not usable. Consider forty units sitting on a single lot expiring at `2026-09-10T00:00:00Z` with the logical clock reading that same instant: the expiring count is forty and the usable count is zero. Both figures are returned together for the same practice, product, and instant. Usable stock is what the administration workflow draws against, and expiring stock is what a practice physically holds and must eventually write off.

### 3.7. Administering a Treatment: Vial Rounding, FIFO Draws, and Wastage

A treatment begins its life scheduled. A `PracticeAdmin` creates one naming the `practice_id`, the administering `provider_id`, the `drug_product_id`, the `pathway_protocol_id` the regimen was selected under, a pseudonymous `patient_reference`, the `regimen_code` the prescriber chose, the `payer_name` to be billed after administration, the `administered_units` per dose, the `dose_count` of doses in this administration which defaults to one, the `vial_yield_units` obtainable from one vial which also defaults to one, an `admin_fee_cents` that defaults to zero, and the `administered_at` instant. The protocol named must resolve to a `PathwayProtocol` in `PUBLISHED` status for the administered product's drug class; a protocol that is not published for that class is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROTOCOL_FOR_PRODUCT`, and the guard is what makes a mis-classed protocol unreachable at administration time. The treatment begins `SCHEDULED`.

Administering is the operation that consumes inventory, and it is a `PracticeAdmin` action carrying the three-part stale-version guard of §2.8: the request supplies `expected_practice_version` and is refused with `ERR_VERSION_MISMATCH` at 412 if the practice has moved on. It also carries `dose_plan`, which selects between the two dosage variants described below. The operation requires the treatment to be `SCHEDULED`, failing with `ERR_TREATMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`; requires the practice to be `AFFILIATED` with `pathway_steering_status` of `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`; requires the administering provider to be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`; and requires `administered_at` to be the current logical clock or earlier, failing with `ERR_INVALID_ADMINISTRATION_TIME` when it is in the future.

Before any draw, the regimen is screened for oncology appropriateness. If the selected `regimen_code` falls outside the pathway protocol's declared regimen set entirely, the administration is refused outright with `ERR_OFF_PATHWAY_REGIMEN_REJECTED` at status 422. This is a different test from concordance: a regimen that is inside the protocol's declared set but outside its on-pathway subset is accepted and merely flagged non-concordant, as described below. Stock sufficiency is checked next, and it is checked against the usable count, not the expiring one. The sum of `quantity_remaining` across the practice's lots for this product that are `AVAILABLE` and whose `expires_at` is strictly later than `administered_at` must be at least the dose requirement that `administered_units` implies once `vial_yield_units` is accounted for; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_STOCK`. Note that the demanded quantity may exceed what is in stock — the endpoint accepts the request and lets this check reject it.

The draw is planned before it is made, and the plan is where vial rounding lives. Only products whose `unit_type` is `VIAL` carry a remainder at all. The vial count for one dose is `administered_units` divided by `vial_yield_units`, rounded up to a whole vial, since a vial cannot be half-opened. The units actually drawn for one dose are the vial count multiplied by the vial yield, and the leftover units in that dose are that drawn quantity minus `administered_units`. Across the whole administration, the total drawn quantity is the per-dose drawn quantity multiplied by `dose_count`, and the total remainder is the per-dose leftover multiplied by `dose_count`. The remainder is part of the total draw, never additional to it; both dose plans draw exactly the same units from the same lots in the same order and leave identical lot balances and an identical acquisition cost basis. The per-dose draw plan is recorded on the treatment as `dose_allocations`, one entry per dose carrying `vial_count`, `dose_units`, and the units obtainable from the rounded vials.

The two dose plans disagree only about who pays for the remainder. Under `WASTE_BILLED`, which is the default plan, the remainder is emitted as a final consumption row carrying `is_wastage` true, and the treatment carries it in `wastage_units` and `wastage_cost_basis_cents` so the claim can bill it. Under `WASTE_ABSORBED` the remainder is drawn from the same vials but is neither billed nor carried as treatment wastage: `wastage_units` and `wastage_cost_basis_cents` are both zero, and the dollars instead land on the lot as its `absorbed_cost_basis_cents`, never appearing on a claim. The practice's recovery under the absorbed plan is lower by exactly the cost of the remainder. Both plans are valid readings of the same administration; the divergence is visible on the treatment, on the lot, and on the claim. A few boundaries make the arithmetic concrete, all with `vial_yield_units` of 100: an `administered_units` of 0 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and produces neither a draw nor wastage; an `administered_units` of 100 leaves no remainder and so produces zero wastage under either plan; an `administered_units` of 250 leaves 50 remainder per dose, so the billed plan records 50 wastage units per dose while the absorbed plan records none; an `administered_units` of 99 leaves 1 per dose; and a `dose_count` of 3 at `administered_units` of 250 records 150 wastage units under the billed plan rather than 50, because the remainder scales with the dose count. Products whose `unit_type` is not `VIAL` record zero wastage under both plans.

The draw itself follows first-expiring-first-out order over the lots that are `AVAILABLE` with `expires_at` strictly after the administration instant. The order is fixed by two keys and never uses an identifier: lots are sorted by `expires_at` ascending and, where two lots share an expiration instant, by `created_at` ascending. Walking that order, the platform takes from each lot the smaller of the remaining need and that lot's `quantity_remaining`, emitting one consumption row per lot drawn — each row recording the `quantity_consumed` taken from that lot — with a one-based `consumption_sequence` and a `cost_basis_cents` equal to the quantity taken from that lot multiplied by that lot's `acquisition_cost_cents_per_unit`. Each row is bound to the lot through `inventory_lot_id` and to the administration through `treatment_id`. Under the billed plan the wastage draw is emitted as the last consumption row, from the terminal lot, flagged `is_wastage` true and priced at that lot's same per-unit cost basis. Each drawn lot's `quantity_remaining` falls by the quantity taken, and its `wastage_units` and `waste_cost_basis_cents` rise by any units discarded from it. The treatment's `acquisition_cost_basis_cents` is the sum of `cost_basis_cents` across every consumption row including the wastage row, and its `wastage_cost_basis_cents` is the sum over the rows flagged as wastage.

Concordance is decided at the drawing lot's instant, not at query time. The treatment's `is_pathway_concordant` is true when the `regimen_code` is a member of the on-pathway regimen codes of the pathway protocol that was in `PUBLISHED` status at `administered_at` for the product's indication. When the treatment is non-concordant, the practice's compliance contribution takes a hit: the rebate qualification covering this product accumulates one non-concordant administration, penalized by the protocol's `off_pathway_penalty_score`, into the compliance score recomputed in §3.8. The practice's `pathway_adherence_pct` is then recomputed over all of its treatments whose status is `ADMINISTERED` or `BILLED`. Finally a `PayerClaim` is created in `DRAFT` for the treatment, and the practice's expiring and usable on-hand figures are refreshed as of the administration instant, which is what makes the two counts in §3.6 differ at an expiry boundary.

### 3.8. Pathways, Rebate Qualification, and How Compliance Is Scored

Pathway protocols are the instrument through which the network steers regimen selection. A `NetworkAdmin` creates one in `DRAFT` with a `protocol_code`, an `indication` drawn from `NSCLC`, `BREAST`, `COLORECTAL`, or `LYMPHOMA`, a `version_label`, an `off_pathway_penalty_score` expressing the compliance penalty per non-concordant administration, an `on_pathway_regimen_codes` array naming the regimens that count as concordant, and a `declared_regimen_codes` array naming the protocol's full regimen set. The relationship between the two code sets is the whole point: a regimen outside `declared_regimen_codes` is rejected outright at administration, as §3.7 describes, while a regimen inside the declared set but outside `on_pathway_regimen_codes` is accepted and flagged non-concordant. Publishing a protocol is done by updating its `status` from `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED`, which requires the protocol to be `DRAFT`, failing with `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_DRAFT` otherwise, and requires `on_pathway_regimen_codes` to be a non-empty array, failing with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and which causes every practice in the network with `pathway_steering_status` of `ACTIVE` to have `pathway_adherence_pct` recomputed against the newly published regimen set. A `PUBLISHED` protocol may be updated to `RETIRED`, requiring the protocol to be `PUBLISHED` and failing with `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_PUBLISHED` otherwise; after retirement it no longer steers new administrations while historical concordance flags are left exactly as they were.

A practice adopts the pathways program through a `PracticeAdmin` or `NetworkAdmin` action available from `NOT_ADOPTED`, which requires at least one `PUBLISHED` protocol to exist for the network, failing with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_PROTOCOL`, and requires the practice's `pathway_steering_status` not already to be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_PATHWAY_ALREADY_ADOPTED`. Adoption sets `pathway_steering_status` to `ACTIVE` and re-evaluates every `SCHEDULED` treatment of the practice for concordance against the protocol in force at its `administered_at`, then recomputes `pathway_adherence_pct` over that re-evaluated set. Withdrawing is available from `ACTIVE` and requires exactly that, failing with `ERR_PATHWAY_NOT_ACTIVE`; it sets `pathway_steering_status` back to `NOT_ADOPTED` and zeroes `pathway_adherence_pct`, while leaving every historical treatment's concordance flag intact. Suspension is a `NetworkAdmin` action available from `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_PATHWAY_NOT_ACTIVE` otherwise; it sets the status to `SUSPENDED`, preserves `pathway_adherence_pct` rather than zeroing it, and diverts the practice's future shortage allocations to the non-pathway ordering branch in §3.4, so a suspended practice is treated as though it holds no adherence ranking. Re-adopting from `SUSPENDED` is available and shares the `ERR_PATHWAY_ALREADY_ADOPTED` code with the `NOT_ADOPTED` path, so a caller cannot rely on that identifier to distinguish the two situations.

The rebate program is the financial counterpart. A `NetworkAdmin` creates one in `DRAFT` against a network with a `program_name`, a `drug_ndc_scope` naming either one NDC or the literal `ALL`, a `market_share_threshold_pct` and a `compliance_min_score` that must both be met, a `rebate_pct` no greater than 0.10, and the `period_epoch_index` the program measures. That period is read as a half-open window against the network's own anniversary, running from the network's creation instant advanced by one month per period, up to and including the whole of the period in question. The program also carries three compliance weights: `weight_reporting_compliance`, `weight_adherence_steering`, and `weight_transparency`, each defaulting to 1. A program is `DRAFT` until a `NetworkAdmin` activates it, and may be closed once it is `ACTIVE` — but closing is refused with `ERR_OPEN_REBATE_CLAIMS_EXIST` while any qualification for it remains `PENDING`, and once `CLOSED` no further qualification may be created for it, an attempt failing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A qualification is the practice's claim against a program for one period. It is created by naming the `practice_id` and the `rebate_program_id`, and it begins `PENDING` with `period_epoch_index` copied from the program, `compliance_report_verified` false, `holdback_cents` at zero, and its `market_share_pct`, `compliance_score`, `compact_discount_credit_cents`, and `rebate_amount_cents` all computed at creation. The practice files its compliance evidence through a `PracticeAdmin` report action carrying a `compliance_attestation`. That action requires the qualification to be `PENDING`, failing with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_PENDING`; requires that no report has yet been recorded on it, since a report may be filed only once per epoch, failing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and must land no later than the network's `compliance_report_grace_days` after the epoch closed — a report arriving later is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REPORT_LATE`. On acceptance, `compliance_report_submitted_at` records the logical clock and the qualification's `version` advances. The report does not verify itself: a separate `NetworkAdmin` action sets `compliance_report_verified` true, and until it does, no rebate against that qualification can be paid.

Timeliness is scored from when the report landed. A report submitted on or before the epoch closed scores 1.0 for reporting timeliness; one submitted after the close but still inside the grace window scores 0.5; and a period whose report was never submitted scores 0.0. The grace window is the network's `compliance_report_grace_days` measured from the instant the epoch closed. Those three scores, together with the practice's adherence and transparency readings, feed the weighted compliance score below.

The compliance score is a weighted mean over exactly three registered score components. Reporting timeliness contributes the score just described. Pathway adherence contributes the practice's `pathway_adherence_pct`. Price transparency contributes 1.0 when every one of the practice's pricing agreements is `ACTIVE` and 0.0 when at least one is `SUSPENDED`, which is how the denial-ratio suspension in §3.3 reaches around to cost a practice rebate dollars. Each component is weighted by its program weight: reporting timeliness by `weight_reporting_compliance`, pathway adherence by `weight_adherence_steering`, and price transparency by `weight_transparency`. The weighted mean is the sum of each component's score multiplied by its weight, divided by the sum of the weights. A weight of zero removes its component from both the numerator and the denominator rather than merely zeroing its contribution — the practical consequence is that a network can set `weight_transparency` to zero and thereby let a practice carrying a suspended agreement still reach a competitive score, which is precisely the `adherence_heavy_weighting` reading of the same data. Unregistered component keys never contribute at all. The resulting ratio is reported to four decimal places, rounded half up. From that score the platform derives `compact_discount_credit_cents`, computed as the compliance score multiplied by the compact discount rate and by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents; the rate is zero in the current agreement, so the credit is zero today, but it remains a declared term and the qualification carries the field.

Qualification itself is a pair of inclusive comparisons. `is_qualified` is true when the practice's `market_share_pct` is at or above the program's `market_share_threshold_pct` and its `compliance_score` is at or above the program's `compliance_min_score`. Both bounds are inclusive, so a practice scoring exactly the minimum still qualifies, and at the default weighting that practice's `compact_discount_credit_cents` is a whole number. The network-level network-defined thresholds `rebate_market_share_threshold_pct` and `rebate_min_compliance_score` set the equivalent floors when the network rather than the program is the measuring authority; the program's own `market_share_threshold_pct` and `compliance_min_score` govern the qualification record.

Measuring market share requires deciding what the denominator is, and the platform computes it under two variants that can put the same practice on opposite sides of the threshold. The practice's network-routed spend in the window is the sum of `extended_cost_cents` over every one of its purchase orders whose `received_at` falls inside the measured window and whose status is `SHIPPED` or `RECEIVED` — orders that have not posted to the trailing-window basis, being `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `BACKORDERED`, are excluded. The base denominator is the practice's total drug spend in the window on the same basis, deliberately including orders for products outside the rebate program's NDC scope. When the practice's `ACTIVE` pricing agreement carries the `scope_excluded` routing term — that is, its `routing_includes_out_of_scope` is true — the denominator additionally includes the billed amounts of the practice's `ADJUDICATED_PAID` claims in the window, capturing dollar volume the practice bought and billed but did not route through the network contract. That makes the denominator larger and the resulting `market_share_pct` smaller. When `routing_includes_out_of_scope` is false the `scope_included_only` variant applies and the denominator is drug purchases alone. A denominator of zero yields a market share of zero rather than a division failure. The ratio is reported to four decimal places, rounded half up. Both readings are legitimate measurements of the share of drug spend routed through the network, and they disagree on identical data, which is why the routing term is pinned on the agreement at approval time.

The rebate amount itself is computed under two variants selected by where the period sits relative to the network's clock. The `provisional` variant governs while the period's `period_epoch_index` is at or beyond the network's current `allocation_epoch_index` — the period is still open, and the amount is provisional. The `final` variant governs once the period sits strictly behind the current epoch — the period has closed, and the amount is final. The distinction is produced by the network spend measure, which is itself dual. The provisional network spend is the sum of `extended_cost_cents` over purchase orders for the program's product scope, or over all rows when the program's `drug_ndc_scope` is `ALL`, whose `received_at` falls inside the half-open measured window and whose status is `SHIPPED` or `RECEIVED`. The final network spend is the same sum over the same rows with one exclusion: every order whose `pricing_tier_applied` came from the `list_price_fallback` variant is stripped out, because dollars not routed at agreed contract pricing earn no rebate once the period is closed. Both figures are computed from the same rows over the same window, and they diverge exactly to the extent fallback-priced orders are present.

Under the provisional variant the rebate amount is the provisional network spend multiplied by the program's `rebate_pct`, capped at 2,500,000 cents per practice, with the cap applied after rounding. The rounding order is pinned and is not the obvious one: the platform multiplies each order's `extended_cost_cents` by `rebate_pct`, sums those exact per-order products, and rounds the sum once to whole cents. The alternative order, rounding each order's contribution to whole cents and then summing the rounded values, is a recognized but rejected reading that differs on identical input; the pinned order is authoritative. Under the final variant the amount starts from the provisional figure and subtracts, first, every dollar of fallback-priced spend that was credited provisionally and later disallowed, computed as that fallback spend multiplied by `rebate_pct`, and second, the practice's `compact_discount_credit_cents`. A declared reconciliation fee term exists in the formula and is currently zero, so it contributes nothing today. If the subtraction would drive the amount below zero, the amount becomes zero and the qualification's status becomes `DISQUALIFIED`.

Evaluation is the `NetworkAdmin` action that resolves a pending qualification. It requires the qualification to be `PENDING`, failing with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_PENDING`, and requires the qualification's `period_epoch_index` to be at or before the network's `allocation_epoch_index`, failing with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` — a period that has not yet arrived cannot be settled. On evaluation the status becomes `QUALIFIED` when `is_qualified` is true and `DISQUALIFIED` otherwise, and `rebate_amount_cents` is pinned with the final variant when the period sits strictly before the current epoch and with the provisional variant otherwise. Disqualification also arises without any caller: when the epoch advances past a period whose compliance report was never verified, or when the qualification's own qualification test fails, the status moves to `DISQUALIFIED`, `rebate_amount_cents` is set to zero, and the practice's `is_rebate_qualified` mirror falls to false.

Payment closes the loop and is fenced by two conditions: the qualification must be `QUALIFIED`, failing with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_QUALIFIED`, its `compliance_report_verified` must be true, failing with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REPORT_NOT_VERIFIED`, and its `holdback_cents` must be zero, failing with `ERR_REBATE_ON_HOLDBACK`. Only then does the status become `PAID`. A qualified rebate can also be reclaimed rather than paid: a `NetworkAdmin` reclaim action requires the qualification to be `QUALIFIED`, failing with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_NOT_QUALIFIED`, and moves it to `RECLAIMED` with `holdback_cents` set to the full `rebate_amount_cents` and the amount itself zeroed, adds that same amount to the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents`, and drops `is_rebate_qualified` to false. Reclamation also happens on its own during a departure, as §3.11 describes.

### 3.9. Billing the Payer, Remittance, Denial, and the Denial Ratio

Submitting a claim is a `RevenueCycleManager` action, and it is the point at which the administration's cost is frozen into a receivable. It carries the stale-version field of §2.8, so the request supplies `expected_practice_version` matching the practice's current `version` and is refused with `ERR_VERSION_MISMATCH` at 412 otherwise. It also carries `write_off_tier` and `post_settlement_disclosure`, whose effects are described below. The action requires the parent treatment to be `ADMINISTERED`, failing with `ERR_TREATMENT_NOT_ADMINISTERED`; requires the claim itself to be `DRAFT`, failing with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DRAFT`; requires the practice to be `AFFILIATED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`; requires that the computed billed amount be greater than zero, failing with `ERR_CLAIM_NOTHING_TO_BILL`; and requires the claim's `payer_name` — copied from the treatment at administration — to be non-empty and to match the treatment's payer evidence, failing with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the claim becomes `SUBMITTED` with `billed_amount_cents` pinned, the parent treatment becomes `BILLED`, and the practice's `version` advances.

The billed amount is assembled from three lines and rounded in a pinned order. The first line is the patient's drug charge, computed as the draw unit cost multiplied by the dose count and by the administered units and then rounded half up to whole cents. The second line is the wastage charge, computed as the draw unit cost multiplied by the treatment's `wastage_units` and rounded half up. The third line is the administration fee, which is the whole-number `policy_admin_fee_cents` carried by the claim. The claim's `billed_amount_cents` is then the exact sum of those three lines, rounded half up once at the end — not each component rounded and then summed. That distinction is observable at a one-cent boundary: a ten-percent write-off against a billed amount of 1,000,001 cents is 100,000 cents rather than 100,000.1. The rate used throughout is the draw unit cost, which is the quantity-weighted average acquisition cost per unit across every lot of the practice for the drug rather than a lookup of the particular lot that supplied this dose. That choice is deliberate and has a consequence worth stating plainly: the rate coarsens as more lots accumulate in the trailing window, so two administrations drawing from the very same lot can carry different cost bases.

The administration fee line is not this treatment's own `admin_fee_cents`. It is the claim's `policy_admin_fee_cents`, a policy figure selected from the practice's history and pinned for the claim. The selection runs over the practice's treatments for this drug product that are in `ADMINISTERED` status, sorted by `administered_at` ascending, then by `admin_fee_cents` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and takes the fee sitting at the ordinal position given by rounding 0.41 times the count of those treatments half up to a whole number — a percentile pick, so it is only exceptionally the treatment's own fee. Under a uniform fee schedule, where every treatment carries 4,800 cents, the picked figure is 4,800 and nobody notices. When the practice's schedule is revised mid-batch, with five treatments at 4,800 followed by five at 6,000, the stratified pick lands at rank five in a ten-row batch and the selected figure is 4,800: the pre-revision fee schedule survives the revision for this claim. The competing reading, pricing the claim at the most recent fee schedule in the batch, would price the same claim at 6,000, which would make professional fees land differently purely by accident of timing.

Two variants govern how the claim's fee line is presented and settled. Under the `full_claim_remittance` reading, which applies when the practice's pricing agreement does not carry the post-settlement disclosure term, the claim's `billed_admin_fee_cents` equals `policy_admin_fee_cents` and the remittance covers all three lines. Under the `post_settlement_disclosure` reading, which applies when the agreement carries that term, the fee line is reconstructed after settlement rather than billed up front: `billed_admin_fee_cents` is zero, the remittance must equal the treatment's `acquisition_cost_basis_cents` exactly, and `written_off_admin_fee_cents` equals `policy_admin_fee_cents`. The same administration therefore settles at a materially different figure depending on which reading applies, and the field `written_off_admin_fee_cents` exists precisely to report the disclosed, uncollected portion.

The write-off headroom on the claim is likewise a two-tier menu. Under the `tier_w1` reading, available while the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents` is at or below the practice's own write-off cap, the claim carries ten percent of its billed amount as headroom; under `tier_w2`, which applies once `unsettled_balance_cents` exceeds that cap, the headroom is twenty-five percent. Only the second tier may be used in that above-cap condition, so the two tiers are reachable on the same claim shape, with the higher tier producing a correspondingly lower allowed amount. In both cases the allowed amount recorded at adjudication is the smaller of the billed amount and the sum of the payer's remittance and the pinned write-off.

A `PayerAdjudicator` records a remittance. Adjudication is accepted from `SUBMITTED` or from `APPEALED`, and any other status fails with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBMITTED`. The remittance must be greater than zero, failing with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and may not exceed the billed amount, failing with `ERR_PAYER_OVERPAYMENT` at status 422. On success the claim becomes `ADJUDICATED_PAID`, `allowed_amount_cents` is recorded as the smaller of `billed_amount_cents` and the sum of the remitted `paid_amount_cents` and the claim's write-off — the payer fee-schedule amount pinned at billing — the remittance is stored as remitted in `paid_amount_cents`, and `allowed_amount_cents` is posted to the practice's net-collections basis, which is the figure the management-fee accrual of §3.10 reads. The practice's `settled_claim_count` is recomputed.

A denial is recorded by the same `PayerAdjudicator` role against a claim in `SUBMITTED` or `APPEALED`, any other status failing with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and requires the supplied `denial_reason_code` to be a member of the declared denial code set, failing with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` otherwise. The claim becomes `DENIED` with the reason stored, the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents` rises by the claim's `billed_amount_cents`, and the practice's `denied_claim_count` and `settled_claim_count` are recomputed, with the practice's `version` advancing.

That recompute is what can suspend a practice's pricing. The practice's denial ratio is its count of claims in `DENIED` status divided by its count of claims in `DENIED` or `ADJUDICATED_PAID` status, evaluated as exact rational arithmetic with no intermediate rounding. Claims that are `VOIDED`, `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, or `APPEALED` are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator, so the ratio is a lifetime measure of settled outcomes and is unaffected by epoch advances. Pricing is suspended only when the ratio strictly exceeds the practice's `denial_ratio_threshold_pct`, which defaults to 0.20. The boundary is strict and not inclusive: a ratio of exactly 0.20 does not suspend, while 0.25 does. With a lifetime numerator of one, the suspension first fires at a denominator of four, where one divided by four is 0.25, and not at a denominator of five, where one divided by five is 0.20. The recomputed ratio is reported to four decimal places, rounded half up, but the comparison itself is made on exact rationals so that the boundary is never an artifact of rounding. When the ratio breaches, every `ACTIVE` pricing agreement of the practice becomes `SUSPENDED` with `status_changed_by` set to `DENIAL_RATIO_BREACH`, and the consequences of that suspension — the tier drop to `TIER_1`, the freeze flag, and the zeroed price-transparency component in the compliance score — follow as §3.3 and §3.8 describe.

A denied claim may be appealed, which is a `RevenueCycleManager` action. It requires the claim to be `DENIED`, failing with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_DENIED`, and requires fewer than two appeals already filed against it, failing with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`; two rounds is the ceiling. On appeal the claim becomes `APPEALED` and `appeal_round` advances by one, and the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents` falls by the claim's `billed_amount_cents`, because the denial is parked pending the appeal outcome rather than counted against the practice. An appealed claim that is later adjudicated becomes `ADJUDICATED_PAID` on the same terms as any other adjudication, with `allowed_amount_cents` posted to net collections; an appealed claim that is denied again returns to `DENIED` and adds the billed amount back to `unsettled_balance_cents`.

Two void paths close the claim side, and both are reached through the single `NetworkAdmin` void endpoint; a claim in any state outside the pair below fails with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VOIDABLE`. A claim that is still `DRAFT` may be voided as it stands, moving it to `VOIDED`. A `DENIED` claim may be voided only while it has not been appealed — that is, one with `appeal_round` still zero — and voiding a denied claim subtracts its billed amount from the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents`, reversing the earlier addition. The treatment side has its own void. A `NetworkAdmin` may void an `ADMINISTERED` treatment, but only while its claim is in `DRAFT` or `DENIED` and no appeal has been filed, failing with `ERR_TREATMENT_HAS_SUBMITTED_CLAIM`; the treatment becomes `VOIDED`, its claim is voided with it, and the drug basis is reversed in the sense that the acquisition cost basis is not re-posted to any network ledger while the practice's total `wastage_units` drops by the voided treatment's wastage. A `BILLED` treatment may also be voided, and here the claim must already be `VOIDED`, the failure code again being `ERR_TREATMENT_HAS_SUBMITTED_CLAIM`. Every treatment void recomputes `pathway_adherence_pct` over the remaining `ADMINISTERED` and `BILLED` rows. A treatment that has reached `ADMINISTERED` also carries a `settlement_priority`, the one-based position it holds in the claim-settlement order selected by a pinned three-key sort over all of the practice's treatments settled in the same batch — `administered_at` ascending, then `admin_fee_cents` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Separately, a practitioner can follow a treatment's consumption trail through the consumption rows that name its `treatment_id`, and can follow a lot's own trail through the rows naming its `inventory_lot_id`.

### 3.10. Practice Management Services and the Clinical Research Site

The network sells operational services alongside drugs. A `NetworkAdmin` creates a practice-management agreement in `DRAFT` naming the `practice_id` that will receive the services, the `service_line` — one of `REVENUE_CYCLE`, `STAFFING`, or `TECHNOLOGY` — the `fee_basis` of the engagement, and the term bounded by an inclusive `term_start` and an inclusive `term_end`. A `REVENUE_CYCLE` engagement is special-cased: it must be sold on a `PERCENT_OF_NET_COLLECTIONS` fee basis, and any other basis is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FEE_BASIS`. Activation requires the agreement to be `DRAFT`, failing with `ERR_MANAGEMENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`; requires the practice to be `AFFILIATED` or `SUSPENDED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`; requires `term_end` to be at or after `term_start`, failing with `ERR_INVALID_MANAGEMENT_TERM`; and respects the thirty-day blackout rule of §2.6, failing with `ERR_MANAGEMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`. On activation `active_month_count` is pinned as the whole calendar months between `term_start` and the earlier of `term_end` and the logical clock, inclusive of the month containing `term_start`, and `accrued_fee_cents` is computed as described below. A suspended engagement may be resumed through the same action, requiring the agreement to be `SUSPENDED` — the failure code is `ERR_MANAGEMENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`, again a case where the identifier does not distinguish the two source states.

Suspension freezes the accrual. A `NetworkAdmin` suspends an `ACTIVE` agreement, failing with `ERR_MANAGEMENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, moving it to `SUSPENDED` and stopping `accrued_fee_cents` from growing further. Termination is available from `ACTIVE` or from `SUSPENDED`, failing with `ERR_MANAGEMENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` otherwise, and sets the status to `TERMINATED`. Termination does not forgive the fee: the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents` rises by the agreement's `accrued_fee_cents`, because the fee liability survives the engagement that created it.

The fee itself depends on the basis. A term is counted in whole calendar months from `term_start` to the earlier of `term_end` and the logical clock, inclusive of the month containing `term_start`; a term spanning the first of January to the thirtieth of June is six months. The monthly fee under `FIXED_MONTHLY` is the agreement's `fixed_fee_cents`. Under `PER_FTE` it is the billable `fte_count` multiplied by `per_fte_fee_cents`. Under `PER_CLAIM` it is the practice's count of treatments in `ADMINISTERED` or `BILLED` status whose `administered_at` falls inside the term, multiplied by `per_claim_fee_cents`. Under `PERCENT_OF_NET_COLLECTIONS` the monthly fee is the practice's net collections for the month — the sum of `allowed_amount_cents` over claims in `ADJUDICATED_PAID` status whose adjudication month falls inside the term, counting each claim once even when it was appealed, since an appeal resolves the same claim rather than creating a new one — multiplied by the agreement's `fee_rate`, rounded half up per month. The `fee_rate`, capped at 0.15, is read only for this basis and ignored for the others. Whatever the basis, the accrued figure is the monthly fee multiplied by the month count.

Two adjustments sit on top of the accrual. Fee rates are tier-dependent: an agreement at `TIER_3` gets the full rate, an agreement at `TIER_1` gets three-quarters of the rate, and a `TIER_2` agreement takes the rate as written. A negative rate is rejected outright. There is also a fairness carve-out for revenue-cycle engagements: a `PERCENT_OF_NET_COLLECTIONS` month whose computed fee falls below the fairness floor of 50,000 cents uses the floor when the practice is not revenue-cycle-opaque, and uses the literal computed fee when the practice is, since access to the practice's collections detail has been excluded. The statement capability reports an agreement's `accrued_fee_cents`, its `monthly_fee_cents`, its `active_month_count`, and its `net_collections_cents` as of a supplied instant.

Research participation brings trials into community sites. A `NetworkAdmin` opens one at a practice with a `study_protocol_number`, a `study_phase` of `PHASE_I`, `PHASE_II`, or `PHASE_III`, a `sponsor_name`, and a `target_enrollment`. Its site-level status is carried on `site_status` and begins at `APPLIED` with `enrolled_participant_count` of zero; the participation is bound both to the practice through `practice_id` and, through `network_id`, to the owning network. Beginning feasibility requires the site to be `APPLIED`, failing with `ERR_SITE_NOT_APPLIED`, and requires the practice to be `AFFILIATED` with an `ACTIVE` pathway program, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`; the site moves to `FEASIBILITY_REVIEW`. Activation requires the site to be in feasibility — the failure code is `ERR_SITE_NOT_IN_FEASIBILITY` — and requires that the practice hold no other `ACTIVATED` participation, failing with `ERR_SITE_ALREADY_ACTIVE_TRIAL`, because one active trial per community site is the limit. On activation `enrolled_participant_count` resets to zero. The same activation action resumes a `SUSPENDED` site, and a `SUSPENDED` source also satisfies the feasibility requirement, so the identifier does not distinguish the two.

Suspending an activated site requires it to be `ACTIVATED`, failing with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ACTIVATED`, moves it to `SUSPENDED`, and places a research hold: the practice's affiliation contract gains a research flag, with the effect that the practice's rebate compliance contribution stays at whatever value it held on the day of suspension rather than drifting with the interruption. Deactivation is available from `ACTIVATED` or from `SUSPENDED` and fails with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ACTIVATED` otherwise; the site becomes `DEACTIVATED` and its `enrolled_participant_count` is frozen at its final value. A `NetworkAdmin` may enroll one participant at a time against a supplied `participant_reference`, advancing `enrolled_participant_count` by one.

### 3.11. Affiliation Contracts, Departure, and the Contract Unwind

The affiliation contract is the instrument that makes the relationship formal and that governs how it ends. A `NetworkAdmin` creates one in `DRAFT`, naming the `practice_id`, a `contract_type` of `FULL_AFFILIATION`, `MANAGEMENT_SERVICES_ONLY`, or `PURCHASING_ONLY`, an inclusive `term_start` and `term_end`, and a `notice_period_days` figure setting how much warning the practice must give before its departure takes effect. It starts with `pricing_agreements_unwound` and `open_orders_closed` false and `rebate_holdback_cents` at zero. Activation requires the contract to be `DRAFT`, failing with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`; requires the practice's `affiliation_status` to be `PROSPECTIVE` or `AFFILIATED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATABLE`; requires `term_end` to be at or after `term_start`, failing with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_TERM`; and requires `notice_period_days` to be at least zero, failing with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Activation makes the contract `ACTIVE` and, when the practice was still `PROSPECTIVE`, promotes it to `AFFILIATED`. A contract may be terminated outright, but only while the practice is still `PROSPECTIVE`; the action requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and requires the practice not to have progressed past prospect status, failing with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_TERMINABLE_WHILE_AFFILIATED`.

Departure begins with a notice. From `AFFILIATED`, either a `NetworkAdmin` or the practice's own `PracticeAdmin` may issue it; the practice must currently be `AFFILIATED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`, and an `ACTIVE` affiliation contract must exist for it, failing with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_AFFILIATION_CONTRACT`. From `SUSPENDED`, only a `NetworkAdmin` may issue it, and two further conditions apply on top: the practice must currently be `SUSPENDED`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_AFFILIATED`, and all of the practice's practice-management agreement rows must already be `TERMINATED`, failing with `ERR_OPEN_MANAGEMENT_AGREEMENT` — that check runs ahead of the contract check, so a practice carrying a live management engagement learns of that defect first. Both paths require an `ACTIVE` contract to exist as well.

When the notice is accepted, the practice's `departure_effective_epoch_index` is set to the current `allocation_epoch_index` plus the ceiling of `notice_period_days` divided by thirty — notice is counted in whole thirty-day months, always rounded up. The notice period is read from the `ACTIVE` affiliation contract with the latest `term_start`, ties broken by the latest `created_at`, so if a practice holds several contracts the most recently started one sets the terms of its exit. The cascade that follows is broad and worth enumerating. The practice's `affiliation_status` becomes `DEPARTING`. Its affiliation contract moves to `DEPARTURE_NOTICE`, and that status change recomputes the contract's `rebate_holdback_cents` as the sum of the practice's provisional and unverified rebate qualification amounts. That recomputed holdback then flags every rebate qualification of the practice in `PENDING` or `QUALIFIED` status: each one's `holdback_cents` is set to its own `rebate_amount_cents` when its compliance report was never verified, and to zero when it was. Every qualification so flagged with a positive holdback becomes `RECLAIMED` with `is_qualified` false, and the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents` rises by the same amount. Every one of the practice's shortage grants still `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` becomes `REVOKED`, releasing its `allocated_units` — committed or not — back into the shortage's unallocated supply. The practice's `is_rebate_qualified` mirror falls to false, the network's commercial-terms freeze flag is recomputed, and the practice's `version` advances.

A departure notice can be withdrawn as long as it has not yet taken effect. From `DEPARTING`, either a `NetworkAdmin` or the practice's own `PracticeAdmin` may withdraw it, requiring the practice to be `DEPARTING`, failing with `ERR_PRACTICE_NOT_DEPARTING`, and requiring `departure_effective_epoch_index` to be strictly greater than the current `allocation_epoch_index`; once the epoch has arrived the withdrawal is refused with `ERR_DEPARTURE_ALREADY_EFFECTIVE`. Withdrawal is deliberately a near-mirror of the notice. The practice returns to `AFFILIATED` with `departure_effective_epoch_index` back to null, the affiliation contract returns to `ACTIVE` with `rebate_holdback_cents` recomputed to zero, and every rebate qualification previously reclaimed has its holdback zeroed, its `is_qualified` restored to true, and its status returned to `QUALIFIED`, with each restored amount subtracted back out of the practice's `unsettled_balance_cents`. Every pricing agreement that had been superseded by the unwind and carried no successor is restored to `ACTIVE`, carrying `status_changed_by` of `DEPARTURE_WITHDRAWAL` to record why it came back. The network's freeze flag is recomputed and the practice's `version` advances. Revoked shortage grants are restored to `GRANTED`, and their units are deducted from unallocated supply again.

The departure actually lands when the epoch arrives rather than when anyone calls anything. Inside the epoch advance described in §3.1, a practice whose `affiliation_status` is `DEPARTING` and whose `departure_effective_epoch_index` has reached or fallen below the new `allocation_epoch_index` becomes `DEPARTED`. Its pricing agreements that are `ACTIVE` become `SUPERSEDED` — and here `superseded_by_agreement_id` stays null, because an unwind is a withdrawal rather than a replacement by a successor — with `status_changed_by` set to `DEPARTURE_UNWIND` on each superseded row. Its `pathway_steering_status` returns to `NOT_ADOPTED` and its `pathway_adherence_pct` is zeroed. Its affiliation contract moves to `UNWIND_IN_PROGRESS`, whose own `pricing_agreements_unwound` flag reads true once every pricing agreement of the practice has been superseded. The network's freeze flag is recomputed. Note that a departure is one-way on the affiliation axis while the pathway axis remains free: nothing prevents a practice from having departed the network while a suspended pathway program is still recorded against it.

Completing the unwind is the final `NetworkAdmin` step. It requires the contract to be `UNWIND_IN_PROGRESS`, failing with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_UNWINDING`, and requires the practice to be clear of four kinds of open commitments at once: no purchase order still in `SUBMITTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `BACKORDERED`, or `SHIPPED`, failing with `ERR_OPEN_ORDERS_EXIST`; no practice-management agreement still in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, or `SUSPENDED`, failing with `ERR_OPEN_MANAGEMENT_AGREEMENT`; and no research participation still in `APPLIED`, `FEASIBILITY_REVIEW`, `ACTIVATED`, or `SUSPENDED`, failing with `ERR_OPEN_RESEARCH_SITE`. On completion the contract becomes `TERMINATED`, the practice's rebate qualifications already in `RECLAIMED` status keep whatever holdback they recorded, and any `QUALIFIED` row carrying a zero holdback is left untouched — the sweep is not a second confiscation. The contract's `rebate_holdback_cents` is reported as the net amount withheld, `pricing_agreements_unwound` and `open_orders_closed` both read true, the practice's affiliation is unbound from the network, and any shortage grant of the practice still `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` becomes `REVOKED`, returning its `allocated_units` to unallocated supply.

### 3.12. Reporting: Spend Reconciliation, Rebate Share, and Stock Standing

Three read capabilities exist purely to expose the dual-variant computations described above, and they are all available to the read-only `NetworkAnalyst` alongside the operational roles that own the underlying data.

The first is a rebate-share report scoped to a network and optionally narrowed by a `practice_id`, a `rebate_program_id`, a `window_start_at`, and a `window_end_at`. It reports the practice's provisional network spend and final network spend over the measured window as `provisional_network_spend_cents` and `final_network_spend_cents`, the denominator used for the share as `routing_denominator_cents`, the resulting `market_share_pct`, and the practice's current `denial_ratio`. The window is half-open, so `window_start_at` is inside it and `window_end_at` is outside. Because the two spend figures come from the same order rows over the same window, their difference is exactly the fallback-priced dollars the reconciliation strips out — the report is where a practice can see, before its period closes, how much of its credited spend will not survive.

The second is a stock report scoped to a practice and, optionally, a `drug_product_id` and an `as_of` instant. It returns both on-hand counts — `expiring_on_hand_units` and `usable_on_hand_units` — for the same practice and product at the same instant, which is where the boundary disagreement described in §3.6 is directly observable. Alongside them it reports the demand side: `requested_doses`, `doses_required`, `unmet_dose_units`, the per-dose draw plan `dose_allocations`, and a `stock_sufficient` boolean. The demand side is parameterized by the caller — the report accepts the administered units per dose, the dose count (defaulting to one), and the vial yield (defaulting to one) as inputs, `requested_doses` and `doses_required` both read back the supplied dose count, and `dose_allocations` is the same per-dose vial-rounding plan the administration workflow would build. `unmet_dose_units` is the count of whole doses that could not be stocked after vial rounding is applied — the requested count less the number of complete per-dose draws the usable stock covers — and it is zero whenever demand can be met. `stock_sufficient` reads true exactly when the usable on-hand count is at least the vial-rounded total draw the supplied demand implies, which is the same comparison the sufficiency check that guards administration makes. The report is the read-only counterpart to that check.

The third is the management-fee statement scoped to a single practice-management agreement and, optionally, an `as_of` instant, reporting the agreement's `accrued_fee_cents`, its `monthly_fee_cents`, its `active_month_count`, and the `net_collections_cents` its revenue-cycle basis was measured against. All three reports are reads and never mutate the resources they describe.
