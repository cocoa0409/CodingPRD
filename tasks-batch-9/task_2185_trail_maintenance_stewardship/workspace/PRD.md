# Trail Maintenance Stewardship API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Volunteer trail stewardship sits at an awkward intersection. The people doing the work are unpaid, the land underneath them belongs to somebody else, the money that buys bridge timber and signage comes from grants that demand auditable proof of every donated hour, and the consequences of getting a safety call wrong are measured in ambulances rather than in support tickets. Most organisations run this on spreadsheets, email threads with a district ranger, and a clipboard at the trailhead. The result is predictable: crews show up to segments that a land manager quietly closed, chainsaw work happens on saturated tread, hours get double-counted into a grant match report, and nobody can reconstruct six months later who signed off on what.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces that clipboard. The system mounts under the `/stewardship` prefix and models the full arc of a stewardship season: an agency registers the land it controls and the policy it expects crews to honour; a crew leader adopts a segment under a dated agreement; a seasonal work plan carries a materials budget; hazards are reported from the field, triaged, scored and dispositioned; the agency issues a dated work permit; a work party is briefed, cleared, confirmed and run; volunteers register, check in, check out and have their hours verified and then independently certified for grant match; materials are requisitioned, priced, approved and received against the plan's ledger; and incidents are filed, reviewed and closed with a corrective action. Every one of those steps has a gate, and the gates interlock — a severe incident closes a segment, which suspends the adoption, which suspends the confirmed work parties, which withdraws their registered volunteers, all in one atomic sweep.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **the server owns time**: a logical clock, not wall-clock time, decides whether a permit has expired, whether a seasonal closure is in force, whether a wet observation is still fresh, and what timestamp lands on an audit field. Second, **the segment's posture is the master gate**: a single derived value on each trail segment summarises incident closure, seasonal closure, wet-weather restriction and hazard restriction, and every operation that would put people on the ground consults it before anything else about the crew is considered.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the endpoint table in section 4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `Volunteer`, `CrewLeader`, `StewardshipCoordinator`, `LandManagerOfficer`, `SafetyOfficer` and `GrantsAdministrator`.

A `Volunteer` is a trail volunteer who files condition reports and incidents, registers for work parties, checks in, and withdraws. A `CrewLeader` is the volunteer who signs an adoption agreement and runs work parties on the ground — planning, permitting, briefing, confirming, starting, completing, checking people out and raising material requisitions. A `StewardshipCoordinator` is stewardship-organisation staff who reviews adoptions and plans, triages and disposes of condition reports, approves requisitions and verifies volunteer hours. A `LandManagerOfficer` is an officer of the agency that owns the land, issuing, denying and revoking work permits, countersigning adoptions and recording weather observations. A `SafetyOfficer` is an independent safety reviewer who opens and closes incident reviews and clears escalated hazards. A `GrantsAdministrator` is grant-reporting staff who certifies verified hours for match and reads the grant-match figures, and who may never certify hours it verified itself.

Three of the six roles are **entity-backed**, which means `X-User-ID` is a real persisted identifier and ownership checks compare it against a foreign-key field on the resource:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `VolunteerProfile`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `VolunteerProfile` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — specifically, checking a shift in and withdrawing a shift compare `X-User-ID` against that shift's `volunteer_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CrewLeader`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `VolunteerProfile`; submitting an adoption agreement compares `X-User-ID` against that agreement's `crew_leader_volunteer_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `LandManagerOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `LandManager`; issuing or denying a permit, revoking a permit, activating an agreement and terminating a suspended agreement all compare `X-User-ID` against the `land_manager_id` that governs the resource in question.

Any of those ownership comparisons that fails yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The other three roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `StewardshipCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it — as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an agreement, as `approved_by_user_id` on a material order, as `hours_verified_by_user_id` on a shift — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SafetyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is similarly opaque, recorded as `closed_by_user_id` on an incident but never resolved against an entity. When `X-User-Role` is `GrantsAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is again opaque, recorded as `hours_certified_by_user_id` on a shift. The `briefing_leader_user_id` on a work party and the `issued_by_user_id` on a permit are recorded the same way, from the acting `X-User-ID` of the `CrewLeader` and `LandManagerOfficer` respectively, and the `reported_by_user_id` on an incident is recorded from whoever filed it.

Two operations are additionally guarded by **segregation of duties**, which compares two recorded operator identifiers rather than an identifier against an entity. Certifying a shift's hours requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `hours_verified_by_user_id` already recorded on that shift, and closing an incident requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `reported_by_user_id` recorded on that incident. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. The `offset` parameter defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` reflects the number of rows matching the supplied filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource in this system carries four common properties. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it. The `created_at` field records the logical-clock instant the resource was created, and `updated_at` records the instant of its most recent mutation; both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits. The `version` field is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, including mutations caused by a cascade originating elsewhere. All eleven entity types — land managers, volunteer profiles, trail segments, adoption agreements, work plans, condition reports, work permits, work parties, volunteer shifts, material orders and incident reports — carry the full set; none of them is reduced to `id` and `created_at` alone.

Whenever a rule below says a counter is recomputed or a status is set as a side effect on some *other* resource, that other resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by 1 as well. This matters more than it looks: the optimistic-concurrency rules in section 2.8 are built directly on that guarantee.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a **logical clock** and uses it for every temporal decision and every timestamp it writes. On startup the clock is fixed at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrative capability sets the clock forward to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant. The clock never runs backwards: a requested instant that is not strictly greater than the current clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422).

Advancing the clock is not a passive act. Each advance expires every `ISSUED` permit whose window has closed, re-evaluates every seasonal closure window and every wet-observation freshness horizon, and recomputes every trail segment's posture, processing segments in `created_at` ascending order. The response to a clock advance reports the resulting `current_utc`, together with `segments_recomputed`, `permits_expired` and `parties_blocked` counts describing what the sweep touched.

### 2.6. Segment Posture — The Master Gate

Each trail segment carries a derived `posture` that gates every child operation. It is a pure function of the segment's own fields plus the logical clock, evaluated top to bottom with the **first matching clause winning**. The precedence is strict and load-bearing: `INCIDENT_CLOSED` outranks `SEASONAL_CLOSED`, which outranks `WET_RESTRICTED`, which outranks `HAZARD_RESTRICTED`, which outranks `OPEN`.

1. If `open_severe_incident_count` is at least 1, the posture is `INCIDENT_CLOSED`.
2. Otherwise, if `seasonal_closure_start_mmdd` is not null and the logical clock's month-and-day falls inside the inclusive window running from `seasonal_closure_start_mmdd` to `seasonal_closure_end_mmdd` — a window that wraps across the year boundary when the start is later in the calendar than the end, as with a window from `12-01` to `03-31` — the posture is `SEASONAL_CLOSED`.
3. Otherwise, if `latest_soil_moisture_index` is not null, is greater than or equal to the governing land manager's `wet_threshold_index`, and the elapsed time from `latest_moisture_observed_at` to the logical clock is strictly less than 72 hours, the posture is `WET_RESTRICTED`.
4. Otherwise, if `hazard_index` is greater than or equal to 60, the posture is `HAZARD_RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

A posture recompute is triggered by: creating a trail segment; recording a soil-moisture observation; advancing the logical clock; triaging, dispositioning, resolving or voiding a condition report; and creating or closing an incident report.

Posture translates into hard refusals. Any operation that would put people on the ground — confirming a work party, starting a work party, deciding a permit, submitting an adoption agreement, activating an adoption agreement — is rejected with `ERR_SEGMENT_CLOSED` (HTTP 409) when the posture is `SEASONAL_CLOSED` or `INCIDENT_CLOSED`. Under a `WET_RESTRICTED` posture, confirming or starting a work party is permitted only when the party's `work_type` is `SIGNAGE` or `DRY_WORK`; under a `HAZARD_RESTRICTED` posture, confirming or starting is permitted for every work type except `CHAINSAW`. Either restriction violation is rejected with `ERR_WET_WEATHER_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409).

Each posture change refreshes the segment's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1, and each carries its own cascade. Entering `INCIDENT_CLOSED` moves every adoption agreement on that segment whose status is `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `SEGMENT_INCIDENT`. Entering `SEASONAL_CLOSED` sets `readiness_status` to `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `SEGMENT_CLOSED` on every work party on the segment whose `schedule_status` is `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`. Entering `WET_RESTRICTED` does the same, but only for parties whose `work_type` is neither `SIGNAGE` nor `DRY_WORK`; it additionally means that any condition report on the segment that is subsequently dispositioned will be re-scored with the wet surcharge in play, while reports already sitting in `TRIAGED` keep the score they were given at triage until that disposition happens. Entering `HAZARD_RESTRICTED` blocks only the narrower set: parties whose `schedule_status` is `PLANNED` and whose `work_type` is `CHAINSAW`.

Leaving a restricted posture is equally mechanical. When the last severe incident on a segment is closed and `open_severe_incident_count` falls to 0, the segment leaves `INCIDENT_CLOSED` — landing on `OPEN` only if no lower-precedence clause still matches, and otherwise on `SEASONAL_CLOSED`, `WET_RESTRICTED` or `HAZARD_RESTRICTED` as the clauses dictate — and every adoption agreement on that segment sitting in `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `SEGMENT_INCIDENT` returns to `ACTIVE`. A clock advance that carries the date out of the closure window returns the segment from `SEASONAL_CLOSED` to `OPEN` provided no higher-precedence clause matches and `hazard_index` is below 60. A fresh observation below the threshold, or simply the ageing of the last observation past the 72-hour freshness horizon, returns the segment from `WET_RESTRICTED` to `OPEN` on the same conditions. And a condition-report resolution that drops `hazard_index` below 60 returns the segment from `HAZARD_RESTRICTED` to `OPEN`.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder

Many operations are guarded by several checks at once, and a crew that is simultaneously unpermitted, over budget and short of first-aid cover must be told about exactly one of those problems. The ordering below is the single authority on which one, and it is deliberate: land-manager permission and human-safety checks are evaluated before parent-link checks, which are evaluated before temporal conflicts, which are evaluated before capacity and uniqueness checks, which are evaluated before money. A crew that is both unpermitted and over budget hears about the permission first, because that is the failure the land manager cares about.

Checks are evaluated in this order, and the first one that fails determines the response:

1. Authentication — both headers present, role among the six declared names (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorisation — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`).
3. Resource ownership for entity-backed roles, as described in section 2.1 (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`). This applies to shift check-in, shift withdrawal, agreement submission, agreement activation, agreement termination, permit decision and permit revocation.
4. Segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), applying to shift certification and incident closure.
5. Path-parameter existence, resolved left to right along the URL. The code names the segment of the path that failed: `ERR_LAND_MANAGER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRAIL_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONDITION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATERIAL_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`. This applies to every endpoint that takes a path parameter.
6. Request-body well-formedness (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`): the body parses, every required field is present, every enum value is a declared member, every number satisfies its declared range, `lines` is a non-empty array whose members each carry a `sku` string, a `unit_price_cents` integer of at least 0 and a `quantity` integer of at least 1, dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`, datetimes are ISO 8601 UTC, and pagination values are within their declared bounds.
7. Foreign-key existence for identifiers supplied in the request body, validated in the field order in which the entity declares them — on a material order creation, for instance, `work_plan_id` is resolved before `condition_report_id`. The codes are keyed by field: `land_manager_id` yields `ERR_LAND_MANAGER_NOT_FOUND`; `trail_segment_id` yields `ERR_TRAIL_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `crew_leader_volunteer_id`, `reported_by_volunteer_id`, `volunteer_id` and `injured_volunteer_id` each yield `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`; `adoption_agreement_id` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `work_plan_id` yields `ERR_WORK_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; `condition_report_id` yields `ERR_CONDITION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`; `work_permit_id` yields `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`; `work_party_id` and `linked_work_party_id` each yield `ERR_WORK_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`; a shift identifier yields `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`; a material order identifier yields `ERR_MATERIAL_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; an incident identifier yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`.
8. State legality — the target resource currently sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared, terminal states included, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).
9. Roster concurrency — `expected_work_party_version` matches the work party's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_WORK_PARTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This applies to volunteer registration only.
10. Ledger concurrency — `expected_work_plan_version` matches the work plan's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_WORK_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This applies to material order creation only.
11. Segment closure (`ERR_SEGMENT_CLOSED`), on work-party confirmation, work-party start, permit decision, agreement submission and agreement activation.
12. Wet-weather and hazard work-type restriction (`ERR_WET_WEATHER_RESTRICTED`), on work-party confirmation and work-party start.
13. Waiver currency (`ERR_WAIVER_NOT_SIGNED`), on volunteer registration, agreement submission and hour certification.
14. Certification cover (`ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`), on volunteer registration and work-party confirmation.
15. First-aid cover (`ERR_FIRST_AID_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`), on work-party confirmation and work-party start.
16. Briefing completeness (`ERR_BRIEFING_INCOMPLETE`), on the briefing itself, on work-party confirmation and on work-party start.
17. Engineering review (`ERR_ENGINEERING_REVIEW_REQUIRED`), on material-order submission.
18. Governing adoption is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ADOPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 409), on work-plan submission, work-plan review, work-party creation, work-party confirmation and permit request.
19. Governing work plan is `APPROVED` (`ERR_WORK_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`, HTTP 409), on permit request, material-order submission, material-order approval and material-order receipt.
20. Permit issued and covering (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`, HTTP 409), on work-party confirmation.
21. Permit lead time (`ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`), on permit request.
22. Permit window containment (`ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_MISMATCH`), on work-party confirmation.
23. Segment interval conflict (`ERR_SEGMENT_PARTY_OVERLAP`, HTTP 409), on work-party confirmation.
24. Volunteer interval conflict (`ERR_VOLUNTEER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, HTTP 409), on volunteer registration.
25. Deferral date reached (`ERR_DEFER_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, HTTP 409), on condition-report resolution.
26. Volunteer daily hour ceiling (`ERR_VOLUNTEER_DAILY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`), on volunteer registration.
27. Roster capacity (`ERR_ROSTER_FULL`, HTTP 409), on volunteer registration.
28. Duplicate registration (`ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`, HTTP 409), on volunteer registration.
29. Declared party size against the permit ceiling (`ERR_PARTY_SIZE_EXCEEDS_PERMIT`), on work-party confirmation.
30. Segment not already adopted (`ERR_SEGMENT_ALREADY_ADOPTED`, HTTP 409), on agreement submission, agreement review and agreement activation.
31. Revision ceiling (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), on agreement submission and work-plan submission.
32. Deferral priority ceiling (`ERR_PRIORITY_TOO_HIGH_TO_DEFER`), on condition-report disposition.
33. Escalation priority floor (`ERR_PRIORITY_TOO_LOW_TO_ESCALATE`), on condition-report disposition.
34. Linked-party completion (`ERR_LINKED_PARTY_NOT_COMPLETE`, HTTP 409), on condition-report resolution.
35. Escalation clearance (`ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_CLEARED`, HTTP 409), on condition-report resolution.
36. Clock monotonicity (`ERR_CLOCK_REWIND`), on the clock-setting capability.
37. Plan not over-committed (`ERR_PLAN_OVER_COMMITTED`, HTTP 409), on material-order submission and material-order approval.
38. Plan budget cap (`ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`), on material-order creation.

One consequence deserves calling out. The readiness gate on a work party is never reported as an opaque "not cleared" failure. When a crew leader tries to confirm or start a party that is not `CLEARED`, the ladder above surfaces the specific underlying cause: a missing briefing as `ERR_BRIEFING_INCOMPLETE`, a closed or restricted segment as `ERR_SEGMENT_CLOSED` or `ERR_WET_WEATHER_RESTRICTED`, a missing or non-covering permit as `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`, and an inactive adoption as `ERR_ADOPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Rosters and Ledgers

Two operations in this system are legitimate to perform many times in a row against the same parent, which means no state precondition can distinguish a well-informed writer from one working off a stale read. Both are therefore guarded by an explicit version check on the parent.

Registering a volunteer onto a work party's roster requires a non-optional `expected_work_party_version` field in the request, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that work party. If it does not equal the work party's current `version`, the registration is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WORK_PARTY_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. Because a roster holds many volunteers, a second registration is a perfectly ordinary product operation in its own right; only the version distinguishes a client that has seen the current roster from one that has not. Every roster mutation advances the work party's `version` — registration, withdrawal, cascade withdrawal, briefing, and any readiness recompute — so a client that registered once and then reuses its original version number is by definition stale.

Creating a material order requires a non-optional `expected_work_plan_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target work plan. If it does not equal the work plan's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WORK_PLAN_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. Adding a second requisition to a plan is likewise legitimate on its own, so no state precondition catches the stale writer. Every ledger movement — order creation, order cancellation, order receipt, and any budget-posture recompute — advances the work plan's `version`, so a client reusing an old version number is working from a stale view of `available_cents`.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.9. Precision, Rounding and Conserved Quantities

All rounding in this system is **round half up**. Money is held in integer cents throughout; every rate multiplication is closed with a round-half-up to zero decimal places immediately, before the result feeds the next step. Volunteer hours are held to two decimal places and are always a multiple of 0.25. Priority and hazard scores are integers in the range 0 through 100. Grant-match figures are US dollars to two decimal places.

Two invariants must hold with zero drift after every operation that touches them.

The **hour invariant** governs a volunteer's four hour buckets. `lifetime_credited_hours` is the running total of every hour ever credited to that volunteer by a check-out or by a dispute adjustment; `pending_hours`, `verified_hours` and `certified_hours` partition those hours by the state of the shift carrying them — pending while the shift is `CHECKED_OUT` or `HOURS_DISPUTED`, verified while it is `HOURS_VERIFIED`, certified while it is `HOURS_CERTIFIED`. Every movement between shift states moves the same number of hours out of one bucket and into another, and every credit adjustment moves `lifetime_credited_hours` and `pending_hours` by the identical delta.

The **budget invariant** governs a work plan's ledger: `approved_budget_cents` always equals `committed_cents` plus `spent_cents` plus `available_cents`, after every material-order creation, cancellation and receipt.

One rounding order is pinned and load-bearing. Hours are quantized to the nearest quarter hour **per shift first**, and every aggregate then sums the already-quantized per-shift values — that goes for `lifetime_credited_hours`, `pending_hours`, `verified_hours`, `certified_hours`, for a plan's `verified_volunteer_hours` and `certified_volunteer_hours`, and for every hour total inside the grant-match calculation. Summing raw billable minutes across shifts and quantizing the total is forbidden and yields a different answer: two shifts of 395 and 397 billable minutes give 6.50 plus 6.50, which is 13.00 under the pinned order, but 13.25 under the forbidden one.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system set off multi-level cascades, and their ordering is observable. Whenever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows — work parties under an agreement's plans, shifts on a party, agreements on a segment — the rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

The deepest cascade runs four levels. A severe incident raises a segment's `open_severe_incident_count`, which drives the posture to `INCIDENT_CLOSED`; that suspends every `ACTIVE` adoption agreement on the segment; that in turn moves every `CONFIRMED` work party under the agreement's plans to `SUSPENDED` while setting `readiness_status` to `BLOCKED`, and blocks (without moving the schedule axis of) every `PLANNED` work party; and finally, on each party that actually moved to `SUSPENDED`, every `REGISTERED` shift moves to `WITHDRAWN` and the affected volunteer's `active_registration_count` drops by 1. Parties that stayed `PLANNED` keep their rosters intact.

Recovery is deliberately asymmetric. When a suspension cause clears, agreements return to `ACTIVE`, blocked parties return to `readiness_status` `BRIEFED` with `block_reason` cleared to null, and parties that had been moved to `SUSPENDED` return to `CONFIRMED`. Shifts withdrawn by a suspension cascade are **not** restored — registration is a forward-only act, and a volunteer who was withdrawn must register again.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing the Register: Agencies, Volunteers and Segments

Nothing in this system happens until the land is on the books. A `StewardshipCoordinator` registers an agency or private landowner, supplying an `agency_name` and a `jurisdiction_code` — an opaque code such as a national-forest region identifier, between 1 and 40 characters long and unique across all registered land managers. The rest of the registration is policy, and each field has a default that applies when the caller omits it. The `permit_required` flag defaults to true and decides whether crews on this agency's land must hold an issued permit before a work party may be confirmed. The `permit_lead_days` value defaults to 14 and states the number of whole days that must separate the logical clock date from a requested permit's first authorised day. The `max_party_size` ceiling defaults to 20 and caps how many people the agency will allow on the ground. The `wet_threshold_index` defaults to 0.65, is expressed to two decimal places on a scale from 0.0 to 1.0, and names the soil-moisture level at or above which this agency's segments become wet-restricted. The `requires_engineering_review` flag defaults to false and, when true, forces a structural sign-off reference onto bridge requisitions. The `requires_certified_sawyer` flag defaults to true and, when true, insists that a chainsaw party keep at least one certified sawyer on its roster. Two further fields are maintained by the server rather than the caller: `policy_version` is initialised to 1 and is snapshotted onto each adoption at activation time, and `adopted_segment_count` is initialised to 0 and tracks how many of this agency's segments currently sit in `ADOPTED`. `permit_lead_days` must be at least 0 and `max_party_size` at least 1.

Volunteers are enrolled by a `StewardshipCoordinator` as well. A profile carries a `full_name` and an `email` that must be unique across all volunteers. Liability paperwork is captured as `waiver_signed_on`, a `YYYY-MM-DD` date that is null when the volunteer has never signed; from it the server derives `waiver_expires_on` as the day exactly 365 days after `waiver_signed_on`, likewise formatted `YYYY-MM-DD` and likewise null when nothing has been signed. The `certifications` array defaults to empty and may hold any subset of `CERT_FIRST_AID`, `CERT_WFA`, `CERT_SAWYER_B`, `CERT_SAWYER_C`, `CERT_TRAIL_SKILLS`, `CERT_ENGINEER` and `CERT_CREW_LEADER`. Separately, `first_aid_expires_on` records the `YYYY-MM-DD` date on which a held `CERT_FIRST_AID` or `CERT_WFA` lapses, and is null when the volunteer holds no first-aid certification at all.

From those certifications the server derives a `skill_class`, which is the volunteer's grant-match rate band and is never supplied by the caller. It is chosen by precedence, highest band first: a volunteer holding `CERT_ENGINEER` is `PROFESSIONAL`; failing that, a volunteer holding `CERT_SAWYER_B` or `CERT_SAWYER_C` is `CERTIFIED_SAWYER`; failing that, a volunteer holding `CERT_TRAIL_SKILLS` or `CERT_CREW_LEADER` is `SKILLED_TRAIL`; and a volunteer holding none of those is `GENERAL`. The four hour buckets described in section 2.9 — `lifetime_credited_hours`, `pending_hours`, `verified_hours` and `certified_hours` — all start at 0, as does `active_registration_count`, which tracks how many of the volunteer's shifts currently sit in `REGISTERED` or `CHECKED_IN`.

Segments are registered by a `StewardshipCoordinator` or a `LandManagerOfficer` against a `land_manager_id`, with a display `name`, a `length_miles` figure greater than 0 expressed to two decimal places, and a `surface_class` drawn from `NATIVE_SOIL`, `GRAVEL`, `BOARDWALK` and `ROCK`. A `traffic_class` of `LIGHT`, `MODERATE` or `HEAVY` defaults to `MODERATE` and feeds hazard scoring. The annual closure window is optional and given as two month-day strings, `seasonal_closure_start_mmdd` and `seasonal_closure_end_mmdd`, both inclusive; both are null on a segment that never closes, and the window is allowed to wrap the year boundary. On creation the server immediately computes the segment's `posture` from the logical clock and the closure window; `hazard_index`, `escalated_hazard_count` and `open_severe_incident_count` all start at 0, `adoption_status` starts at `UNADOPTED`, and `latest_soil_moisture_index` and `latest_moisture_observed_at` are both null until the first observation lands. Segment listings may be narrowed by `land_manager_id`, by `posture` and by `adoption_status`.

Weather is fed in by a `StewardshipCoordinator` or a `LandManagerOfficer`, who records an observation naming the `trail_segment_id`, a `soil_moisture_index` value and the `observed_at` instant of the reading. The server stores those as the segment's `latest_soil_moisture_index` and `latest_moisture_observed_at`, recomputes the posture against the governing agency's `wet_threshold_index`, and returns the segment with its new posture. Any work party the recompute blocks receives `readiness_status` `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `SEGMENT_CLOSED`. Note that a wet reading only bites while it is fresh: once more than 72 hours of logical time have passed since `latest_moisture_observed_at`, the wet clause stops matching even though the stored index is unchanged, so a segment can walk out of `WET_RESTRICTED` purely because the clock moved.

### 3.2. Adopting a Segment

A `CrewLeader` opens an adoption by drafting an agreement that names the `trail_segment_id` being adopted, the `crew_leader_volunteer_id` of the volunteer signing as crew leader, and the inclusive term boundaries `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, both `YYYY-MM-DD`, with the end strictly after the start. The agreement is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0 and `pinned_policy_version`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `activated_at` and `suspension_reason` all null. Agreement listings may be filtered by `trail_segment_id`, by `crew_leader_volunteer_id` and by `status`.

Submission moves the agreement `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is reserved to the signing crew leader — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `crew_leader_volunteer_id`. That crew leader's own waiver must be live: `waiver_signed_on` must be non-null and `waiver_expires_on` must be on or after the logical clock date, or the submission fails with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_SIGNED`. The segment must not be sitting in `INCIDENT_CLOSED`, and its `adoption_status` must be `UNADOPTED` — an attempt to adopt a segment where another agreement already holds `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_ALREADY_ADOPTED`. A successful submission refreshes `updated_at` and advances the agreement's `version` by 1.

Review is a `StewardshipCoordinator` decision carrying a `decision` value and, where the decision is a return, a `reason`. Returning the agreement moves it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, requires the `reason` to be a non-empty string, increments `revision_round` by 1 and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`. The crew leader may then resubmit, moving `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`, which re-checks that the segment is still `UNADOPTED`. That cycle — `SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` — is bounded: resubmission is permitted only while `revision_round` is at most 3, so the fourth submit, attempted when `revision_round` has already reached 4, is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Approving instead moves the agreement `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, again only while the segment's `adoption_status` is `UNADOPTED`, and records `reviewed_by_user_id`.

Activation is the land manager's countersignature and moves the agreement `APPROVED → ACTIVE`. Only a `LandManagerOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the segment's governing `land_manager_id` may perform it; the segment's posture must be neither `INCIDENT_CLOSED` nor `SEASONAL_CLOSED`; and the logical clock date must fall inside the inclusive span from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date`. Activation snapshots the agency's `policy_version` onto the agreement as `pinned_policy_version`, stamps `activated_at` with the logical clock, flips the segment's `adoption_status` to `ADOPTED`, and increments the agency's `adopted_segment_count` by 1.

An active adoption can be knocked sideways without anybody asking. When a work plan's `budget_posture` becomes `OVER_COMMITTED`, or when the segment's posture becomes `INCIDENT_CLOSED`, the agreement moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and `suspension_reason` is set to `BUDGET_OVER_COMMIT` or `SEGMENT_INCIDENT` accordingly. That suspension carries the cascade described in section 2.10: every work party under the agreement's plans whose `schedule_status` is `CONFIRMED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `readiness_status` `BLOCKED` and `block_reason` `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED`, while every party sitting in `PLANNED` keeps its `schedule_status` and merely acquires the same `readiness_status` and `block_reason`; both sets are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Then, on each party that actually moved to `SUSPENDED`, every shift in `REGISTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` and that volunteer's `active_registration_count` drops by 1, again in `created_at` ascending order. Parties that stayed `PLANNED` keep their rosters. When the last suspension cause clears — a requisition is cancelled and the budget recovers, or the final severe incident is closed — the agreement moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, `suspension_reason` returns to null, every party carrying `block_reason` `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED` has its `readiness_status` set to `BRIEFED` and its `block_reason` cleared to null, and those whose `schedule_status` is `SUSPENDED` additionally return to `CONFIRMED`. Withdrawn shifts stay withdrawn.

Termination ends the adoption for good. A `StewardshipCoordinator` may terminate an `ACTIVE` agreement, and a `LandManagerOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the segment's `land_manager_id` may terminate a `SUSPENDED` one; both require a non-empty `reason`, and both carry an identical set of consequences. The agreement lands in `TERMINATED`, which is terminal. The segment's `adoption_status` returns to `UNADOPTED` and the agency's `adopted_segment_count` drops by 1. Every work plan under the agreement whose status is `APPROVED` moves to `CLOSED`. And every work party under the agreement's plans whose `schedule_status` is `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `CANCELLED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order.

### 3.3. The Seasonal Work Plan and Its Budget Ledger

A `CrewLeader` drafts a seasonal work plan under an adoption, supplying the `adoption_agreement_id`, the `season_year` the plan covers, and a `requested_budget_cents` figure of at least 0. Only one plan may exist per adoption per season year, so the combination of `adoption_agreement_id` and `season_year` must be unique. The plan starts in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0 and every ledger field at 0: `approved_budget_cents`, `committed_cents`, `spent_cents`, `available_cents`, `verified_volunteer_hours` and `certified_volunteer_hours`, with `budget_posture` at `HEALTHY`. Plan listings may be narrowed by `adoption_agreement_id`, by `status` and by `budget_posture`.

Submission moves the plan `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and requires the governing adoption to be `ACTIVE` and `requested_budget_cents` to be at least 0; it refreshes `updated_at` and advances the plan's `version` by 1. A `StewardshipCoordinator` then answers with a `decision`. A revision request moves the plan `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, requires a non-empty `reason`, and increments `revision_round` by 1 — and then, inside the very same call, the plan drops straight back to `DRAFT` so the crew can resubmit. The response the coordinator receives therefore shows a `DRAFT` plan with the advanced `revision_round`, and the observable cycle is `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`. As with adoptions, the loop is capped at three rounds: the fourth submission, attempted once `revision_round` has reached 4, is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. An approval instead moves the plan `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, requires the governing adoption to still be `ACTIVE`, and requires an `approved_budget_cents` value of at least 0 in the payload, which is stored on the plan and immediately drives a full ledger recomputation. When the parent adoption terminates, an `APPROVED` plan moves to `CLOSED`, which is terminal — no further requisition and no further permit may reference it.

The **budget ledger** is recomputed on plan approval and on every material-order creation, cancellation and receipt. It works entirely in integer cents. The plan's `committed_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` over the plan's material orders whose status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`. Its `spent_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` over the plan's material orders whose status is `DELIVERED`. Cancelled orders count toward neither sum. The plan's `available_cents` is `approved_budget_cents` less `committed_cents` less `spent_cents`, and it is allowed to go negative. From `available_cents` the server derives `budget_posture` in four bands: it is `OVER_COMMITTED` when `available_cents` is below 0; `EXHAUSTED` when `available_cents` is exactly 0; `TIGHT` when `available_cents` is above 0 but strictly below a floor equal to one fifth of `approved_budget_cents` — that is, `approved_budget_cents` multiplied by 0.20 and rounded half up to whole cents; and `HEALTHY` in every remaining case.

Sitting alongside those bands is a hard admission gate applied when a new requisition is raised, evaluated **before** the commitment lands on the ledger. The plan will admit a new order only while the plan's existing `committed_cents` plus its `spent_cents` plus the new order's `total_cents` remains at or below one hundred and twenty percent of `approved_budget_cents` — that is, `approved_budget_cents` multiplied by 1.20 and rounded half up to whole cents. The cap is inclusive: a total that lands exactly on the cap is admitted. Anything above it is refused with `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. In other words, the plan tolerates a twenty percent overrun in commitments but not a cent more, and it is only when actual `available_cents` goes negative that `budget_posture` flips to `OVER_COMMITTED` and the adoption above it suspends.

### 3.4. Hazard Intake, Triage and Disposition

Any `Volunteer` or `CrewLeader` may file a condition report from the field. The report names the `trail_segment_id` carrying the defect and the `reported_by_volunteer_id` who saw it, classifies the defect with a `hazard_type` drawn from `BLOWDOWN`, `TREAD_EROSION`, `DRAINAGE_FAILURE`, `BRIDGE_DAMAGE`, `SIGNAGE_MISSING` and `BRUSH_ENCROACHMENT`, assigns a first-pass `severity_class` of `LOW`, `MODERATE`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, and carries a free-text `description` of the field notes. It lands in `SUBMITTED` with `priority_score` at 0, `priority_variant`, `exposure_class`, `traffic_class_snapshot`, `linked_work_party_id`, `defer_until_date` and `resolved_at` all null or zero. Reports may be listed filtered by `trail_segment_id`, by `reported_by_volunteer_id`, by `linked_work_party_id` and by `status`.

Triage is a `StewardshipCoordinator` act that moves the report `SUBMITTED → TRIAGED`. It requires an `exposure_class` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH` and optionally carries a `severity_override`, which when supplied overwrites the reporter's `severity_class` outright. Triage also freezes the segment's current `traffic_class` onto the report as `traffic_class_snapshot`, so that a later edit to the segment cannot silently re-score a historical report. It then scores the report and recomputes the segment's `hazard_index` and posture.

**Priority scoring** runs in two variants and both share the same skeleton. The score begins from a severity base: 20 for `LOW`, 50 for `MODERATE`, 80 for `HIGH` and 100 for `CRITICAL`, read from the post-override `severity_class` stored on the report. That base is multiplied by an exposure multiplier — 0.8 for `LOW` exposure, 1.0 for `MEDIUM`, 1.25 for `HIGH` — and then by a traffic multiplier read from `traffic_class_snapshot`: 0.9 for `LIGHT`, 1.0 for `MODERATE`, 1.15 for `HEAVY`. That three-way product is held exactly and is **not** rounded at this point. To it are added two increments. The first is an ageing bonus: the whole number of days from the report's `created_at` to the logical clock, divided by seven and rounded down to whole weeks, multiplied by 3 points per completed week, and then capped at 15 points. The second is a wet surcharge of 8 points, applied when and only when the segment's `posture` is `WET_RESTRICTED`, and 0 otherwise. The exact product plus the two increments gives a raw figure, and only then is a single round-half-up to a whole number applied, with the result capped at 100. That single, final rounding is the only rounding in the calculation.

The two variants differ in exactly which increments are live. The `INITIAL_TRIAGE` variant, used at triage, forces both the ageing bonus and the wet surcharge to 0 regardless of how old the report is or what posture the segment is in, so the triage score is a pure severity-by-exposure-by-traffic product. The `REASSESSED` variant, used on every disposition regardless of which disposition is chosen, has both increments live. The resulting integer is stored as `priority_score` and the variant that produced it is stored as `priority_variant`, whose value is `INITIAL_TRIAGE` or `REASSESSED`.

Disposition is where that difference bites. A `StewardshipCoordinator` dispositions a `TRIAGED` report with a `disposition` value, and the report is **re-scored with the `REASSESSED` variant first**; the gates below then read that fresh score, never the value stored at triage. A report triaged at 63 can therefore become undeferrable purely by ageing past a week boundary or by the segment turning `WET_RESTRICTED`.

Scheduling the report moves it `TRIAGED → SCHEDULED`. It requires a `linked_work_party_id` naming a work party on the same segment as the report — the party's `trail_segment_id` must equal the report's — and that party's `schedule_status` must be `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`. The identifier is stored on the report and the party's `linked_report_count` rises by 1.

Deferring the report moves it `TRIAGED → DEFERRED`. It requires a `defer_until_date` strictly after the logical clock date, and it requires the freshly reassessed `priority_score` to be strictly below the escalation threshold of 70; a report scoring 70 or higher is too urgent to park and is refused with `ERR_PRIORITY_TOO_HIGH_TO_DEFER`. The `defer_until_date` is stored on the report.

Escalating the report moves it `TRIAGED → ESCALATED` and is the mirror image: it requires the reassessed `priority_score` to be greater than or equal to 70, and a report scoring below that is refused with `ERR_PRIORITY_TOO_LOW_TO_ESCALATE`. Escalation raises the segment's `escalated_hazard_count` by 1.

The **segment hazard index** is recomputed after every triage, disposition, resolution and void, and on segment creation. It considers only the segment's condition reports whose status is `TRIAGED`, `SCHEDULED`, `DEFERRED` or `ESCALATED`; reports in `SUBMITTED`, `RESOLVED` and `VOIDED` are excluded entirely, and there is no time window — every non-terminal, already-triaged report participates. Each included report contributes its `priority_score` multiplied by a status weight: 1.0 for a report in `TRIAGED` or `SCHEDULED`, 0.5 for one in `DEFERRED`, and 1.5 for one in `ESCALATED`. Those weighted contributions are summed, the sum is divided by 3, and the quotient is rounded half up to a whole number and capped at 100 to give `hazard_index`. Separately, `escalated_hazard_count` is set to the number of included reports sitting in `ESCALATED`; and if that count reaches 2 or more, `hazard_index` is forced to 100 outright — an override applied **after** the rounded value has been computed, not before. Because the sum is order-independent, no tie-break is needed. A `hazard_index` of 60 or above is what drives the segment into `HAZARD_RESTRICTED`.

Resolution converges three branches on the same terminal state, and each branch carries a different precondition and a different set of consequences. A `SCHEDULED` report may be resolved by a `StewardshipCoordinator` only once its linked work party has reached `schedule_status` `COMPLETED`; otherwise the resolution is refused with `ERR_LINKED_PARTY_NOT_COMPLETE`. On success the report moves `SCHEDULED → RESOLVED`, `resolved_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the linked party's `linked_report_count` drops by 1. A `DEFERRED` report may be resolved only once the logical clock date has reached or passed its `defer_until_date`; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_DEFER_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. On success it moves `DEFERRED → RESOLVED`, `resolved_at` is stamped, and `defer_until_date` is deliberately left as recorded for the audit trail. An `ESCALATED` report may be resolved either by a `SafetyOfficer`, or by a `StewardshipCoordinator` when a material order naming this report through its `condition_report_id` has reached `APPROVED` or `DELIVERED`; a coordinator with neither of those is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_CLEARED`. On success it moves `ESCALATED → RESOLVED`, `resolved_at` is stamped, and the segment's `escalated_hazard_count` drops by 1. All three branches accept an optional `resolution_note`, and all three end by recomputing the segment's `hazard_index` with the resolved report now excluded, followed by a posture recompute.

A report that should never have been filed is voided by a `StewardshipCoordinator` with a non-empty `reason`, from either `SUBMITTED` or `TRIAGED`. Voiding from `SUBMITTED` moves the report `SUBMITTED → VOIDED` and recomputes the segment's `hazard_index` and posture; voiding from `TRIAGED` moves it `TRIAGED → VOIDED` and recomputes the same figures with the voided report now excluded from the included set. `RESOLVED` and `VOIDED` are both terminal.

### 3.5. Securing Land-Manager Permission

Before a crew can be allowed onto agency land, somebody at the agency has to say yes to a dated window. A `CrewLeader` requests a permit under an approved plan, naming the `work_plan_id` the request is made under, the `trail_segment_id` the permit will cover, the inclusive window boundaries `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` — both `YYYY-MM-DD`, with the end on or after the start — and a non-empty `requested_work_types` array drawn from `TREAD_WORK`, `CHAINSAW`, `BRIDGE_CONSTRUCTION`, `SIGNAGE`, `BRUSHING` and `DRY_WORK`. The request is refused with `ERR_ADOPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` if the governing adoption is not `ACTIVE`, and with `ERR_WORK_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED` if the plan is not `APPROVED`.

The agency's lead-time rule bites here. The number of whole days separating the logical clock date from `window_start_date` must be greater than or equal to the governing agency's `permit_lead_days`, and the comparison is inclusive — a gap of exactly the required number of days is accepted. Anything shorter is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`. For an agency running the default two-week notice period, a request logged on the first of a month for work starting on the fifteenth is exactly on the line and is accepted; work starting on the fourteenth is not.

On creation the permit copies the `land_manager_id` from the segment, lands in `REQUESTED`, and leaves `allowed_work_types`, `max_party_size`, `issued_by_user_id`, `issued_at`, `denial_reason` and `revocation_reason` null with `consumed_party_count` at 0. Permit listings may be narrowed by `work_plan_id`, by `trail_segment_id`, by `land_manager_id` and by `status`.

The decision belongs to a `LandManagerOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the permit's `land_manager_id`, and it carries a `decision` value. Issuing moves the permit `REQUESTED → ISSUED`. It is refused when the segment's posture is `INCIDENT_CLOSED`; it requires the supplied `allowed_work_types` to be a non-empty subset of the `requested_work_types` the crew asked for — the officer may authorise less than was asked for but never more — and it requires a `max_party_size` that is at most the agency's own `max_party_size` ceiling. Those two values, along with `issued_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID` and `issued_at` taken from the logical clock, are stamped onto the permit. Issuing also has a forward effect on scheduling: every work party referencing this permit whose `readiness_status` is `BRIEFED` advances to `CLEARED` if the clearance predicate now holds. Denying instead moves the permit `REQUESTED → DENIED`, requires a non-empty `denial_reason`, stores that reason together with `issued_by_user_id`, and pushes every referencing work party whose `schedule_status` is `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` into `readiness_status` `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `PERMIT_REVOKED`.

Revocation is the officer's emergency brake on an already-issued permit, moving it `ISSUED → REVOKED`. It requires the same ownership check and a non-empty `revocation_reason`, which is stored. Every referencing work party whose `schedule_status` is `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` acquires `readiness_status` `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `PERMIT_REVOKED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order — and among those, every party that was `CONFIRMED` additionally moves to `schedule_status` `SUSPENDED`. A `PLANNED` party is blocked but keeps its schedule axis where it is.

Two further exits are driven by the system rather than by a person. A clock advance that carries the date strictly past `window_end_date` moves an `ISSUED` permit to `EXPIRED`, and every referencing party in `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` acquires `readiness_status` `BLOCKED` with `block_reason` `PERMIT_EXPIRED`. And when a referencing work party completes, the permit's `consumed_party_count` rises by 1; once that count is at least 1 and no referencing party remains in `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, the permit moves `ISSUED → CONSUMED` with `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced by 1. `DENIED`, `CONSUMED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` are all terminal.

### 3.6. Standing Up a Work Party: Two Axes That Move Independently

A work party is the only object in this system that carries two state machines at once. The `schedule_status` axis tracks where the party sits in its calendar life — `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CANCELLED`, with `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` terminal — while the `readiness_status` axis tracks whether the crew is actually permitted and prepared to be out there: `PENDING`, `BRIEFED`, `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED`, none of which is terminal. Nothing on the readiness axis moves the schedule axis, and the briefing capability in particular leaves `schedule_status` completely untouched.

A `CrewLeader` schedules a party under an approved plan, supplying the `work_plan_id` funding it, the `trail_segment_id` being worked — which must equal the segment named on the plan's adoption — a half-open interval given as `start_at` and `end_at` in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, where `end_at` is strictly after `start_at` and falls on the same UTC calendar day, a single `work_type` drawn from the six declared work types, and a `max_participants` ceiling of at least 1. Three inputs are optional: `work_permit_id` names the permit the party relies on and may be left null only where the governing agency has `permit_required` set to false; `travel_allowance_minutes` defaults to 0, must be at least 0, and sets how many travel minutes each volunteer may claim on this party before the cap described in section 3.7 applies; and `required_certifications` defaults to empty and lists certifications every registrant must hold. Creation is refused with `ERR_ADOPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` when the governing adoption is not `ACTIVE`. The party is born with `schedule_status` `PLANNED` and `readiness_status` `PENDING`, with `briefing_delivered_at`, `briefing_leader_user_id` and `block_reason` null, `tool_checklist_complete` false, and `first_aid_kit_count`, `registered_count`, `checked_in_count`, `first_aid_capable_count` and `linked_report_count` all 0. Its `roster_capacity` is computed at creation and maintained thereafter as the smaller of `max_participants` and the referenced permit's `max_party_size` when a permit is referenced and that permit is `ISSUED`, and simply `max_participants` otherwise. Parties may be listed filtered by `work_plan_id`, by `trail_segment_id`, by `work_permit_id`, by `schedule_status` and by `readiness_status`.

The tool and safety briefing is delivered by a `CrewLeader` and is the only way onto the readiness ladder. The payload carries `tool_checklist_complete` and `first_aid_kit_count`; the briefing is accepted only when `tool_checklist_complete` is true and `first_aid_kit_count` is at least 1, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_BRIEFING_INCOMPLETE`. It may be delivered while `schedule_status` is `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `SUSPENDED`. A successful briefing moves the readiness axis `PENDING → BRIEFED`, stamps `briefing_delivered_at` with the logical clock and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `briefing_leader_user_id`.

Clearance is evaluated by the system, not requested — at the moment the briefing lands, and again when a permit is issued. The party advances `BRIEFED → CLEARED` when three things all hold: the governing agency either has `permit_required` false, or the referenced permit's status is `ISSUED` with the party's `work_type` inside that permit's `allowed_work_types` and both party dates inside the permit window; the segment's `posture` is `OPEN`, or is `HAZARD_RESTRICTED` with the party's `work_type` being anything other than `CHAINSAW`, or is `WET_RESTRICTED` with the party's `work_type` being `SIGNAGE` or `DRY_WORK`; and the governing adoption is `ACTIVE`. When that predicate already holds at briefing time, the readiness axis runs straight through to `CLEARED` inside the same call. Each clearance refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1.

Blocking runs the other way and is likewise system-driven. From either `CLEARED` or `BRIEFED`, a party moves to `BLOCKED` as soon as any blocking cause holds — the permit is revoked, denied or expired, the segment posture turns blocking, or the parent adoption suspends — and `block_reason` is set to `PERMIT_REVOKED`, `PERMIT_EXPIRED`, `SEGMENT_CLOSED` or `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED` to say which. A blocked party is recovered by re-briefing: the crew leader delivers the briefing again, which is accepted only once no blocking cause still holds — an attempt while a cause persists is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and again only when `tool_checklist_complete` is true and `first_aid_kit_count` is at least 1. That moves the readiness axis `BLOCKED → BRIEFED`, clears `block_reason` back to null, refreshes `briefing_delivered_at`, and once more runs straight through to `CLEARED` when the clearance predicate holds. The readiness cycle is therefore `BRIEFED → CLEARED → BLOCKED → BRIEFED`.

Confirmation moves the schedule axis `PLANNED → CONFIRMED` and is where the heaviest gate in the system sits. A `CrewLeader` requesting it must satisfy, in the ladder order of section 2.7: the segment must not be `SEASONAL_CLOSED` or `INCIDENT_CLOSED`; the work-type restriction under `WET_RESTRICTED` or `HAZARD_RESTRICTED` must be honoured; every registrant must hold every certification in `required_certifications`, and where `work_type` is `CHAINSAW` under an agency with `requires_certified_sawyer` true, the party must still retain at least one non-withdrawn shift whose volunteer holds `CERT_SAWYER_B` or `CERT_SAWYER_C`, failing which the confirmation is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`; first-aid cover must be adequate; a briefing must be on record, meaning `readiness_status` is no longer `PENDING`; the governing adoption must be `ACTIVE`; where the agency has `permit_required` true, the party must reference a permit that is `ISSUED` and whose `allowed_work_types` contains the party's `work_type`, failing which the confirmation is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ISSUED`; both the UTC date of `start_at` and the UTC date of `end_at` must fall inside the permit's inclusive window, failing which it is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_MISMATCH`; the segment interval rule below must be satisfied; and `max_participants` must be no greater than the referenced permit's `max_party_size`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_PARTY_SIZE_EXCEEDS_PERMIT`. A successful confirmation refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1.

**First-aid cover** is a ratio, not a flag. Counting only shifts in `REGISTERED` or `CHECKED_IN` whose volunteer holds `CERT_FIRST_AID` or `CERT_WFA` with a `first_aid_expires_on` on or after the logical clock date, the party's `first_aid_capable_count` must be at least the `registered_count` divided by eight and rounded up to a whole number, and must in any case be at least 1. A crew of eight therefore needs one qualified first-aider, and a crew of nine needs two. Falling short refuses the confirmation — and equally the start — with `ERR_FIRST_AID_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`.

**The segment interval rule** protects the ground itself. Work-party intervals on a segment are half-open, and the resource carries a mandatory 60-minute recovery buffer. The party being confirmed conflicts with any other work party on the same `trail_segment_id` whose `schedule_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS` when both of these hold at once: the new party's `start_at` is earlier than the other party's `end_at` advanced by 60 minutes, and the other party's `start_at` is earlier than the new party's `end_at` advanced by 60 minutes. A gap of exactly 60 minutes between two parties is therefore permitted. A conflict is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_PARTY_OVERLAP`.

Starting the party moves it `CONFIRMED → IN_PROGRESS`. It requires the same four underlying readiness causes that confirmation enforces, requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `start_at`, and requires the segment posture to be neither `SEASONAL_CLOSED` nor `INCIDENT_CLOSED`. Completing the party moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, again requiring the logical clock to be at or after `start_at`, and does three things at once: every shift still in `CHECKED_IN` is checked out at whichever is earlier of the logical clock and the party's `end_at`, with hours credited per shift and the shifts processed in `created_at` ascending order; every shift still sitting in `REGISTERED` moves to `NO_SHOW`; and the referenced permit's `consumed_party_count` rises by 1, with the permit moving to `CONSUMED` when no other referencing party remains in `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `IN_PROGRESS`.

Cancellation is available from `PLANNED` and `CONFIRMED` to a `CrewLeader`, and from `SUSPENDED` to a `StewardshipCoordinator`; all three require a non-empty `reason` and all three carry an identical consequence set. Every shift on the party in `REGISTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` and each affected volunteer's `active_registration_count` drops by 1, processed in `created_at` ascending order; and every condition report whose `linked_work_party_id` names this party returns from `SCHEDULED` to `TRIAGED` with `linked_work_party_id` cleared. A party may also be moved `CONFIRMED → SUSPENDED` by the system when its parent adoption suspends or its permit is revoked, which sets `readiness_status` to `BLOCKED` with the matching `block_reason` and withdraws the party's `REGISTERED` shifts exactly as above; and it returns `SUSPENDED → CONFIRMED` once no blocking cause holds and either the referenced permit is `ISSUED` or the agency does not require one, setting `readiness_status` back to `BRIEFED` and `block_reason` back to null.

### 3.7. The Roster, the Hour Ledger and the Grant Match

Registration puts a volunteer on a party's roster and is available to a `Volunteer` or a `CrewLeader`. The payload names the `volunteer_id` being registered, carries the mandatory `expected_work_party_version` described in section 2.8, and may carry a `role_on_crew` of `CREW_MEMBER`, `SAWYER`, `FIRST_AIDER` or `CREW_LEADER`, defaulting to `CREW_MEMBER`. The shift is created in `REGISTERED`, the party's `version` advances by 1, `registered_count` and `first_aid_capable_count` are recomputed, and the volunteer's `active_registration_count` rises by 1. Shift listings may be narrowed by `work_party_id`, by `volunteer_id` and by `status`.

Registration is heavily gated, and the gates fire in the ladder order of section 2.7. The volunteer's own paperwork comes first: `waiver_signed_on` must be non-null and `waiver_expires_on` must be on or after the logical clock date, otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_SIGNED`. Then certifications: the volunteer must hold every certification listed in the party's `required_certifications`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. Then the two interval rules, then the daily ceiling, then capacity, then uniqueness.

**The volunteer interval rule** differs deliberately from the segment rule. A volunteer's commitments are treated as closed intervals with **zero** buffer, so touching endpoints are allowed. The registration conflicts when the new party's interval and an existing party's interval satisfy both conditions: the new party's `start_at` is strictly earlier than the other party's `end_at`, and the other party's `start_at` is strictly earlier than the new party's `end_at`. The comparison ranges over that volunteer's shifts in `REGISTERED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `CHECKED_OUT` that belong to a **different** `work_party_id`; shifts on the party being registered for are excluded on purpose, so that a repeat registration surfaces as a duplicate rather than as a self-overlap. A new party starting at the exact instant an existing one ends is therefore accepted. A genuine conflict is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

**The daily ceiling** is an aggregate over the volunteer rather than over any one party. Take the scheduled duration — `end_at` minus `start_at` — of every shift that volunteer holds in `REGISTERED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `CHECKED_OUT` on **other** work parties whose party `start_at` falls on the same UTC calendar day as the new party's `start_at`, add the new party's own duration, and the total must not exceed ten hours. The comparison is inclusive, so a total landing on exactly 10.0 hours is accepted; anything above it is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_DAILY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`.

Capacity and uniqueness close the gate. The party's `registered_count` must be strictly below its `roster_capacity`, which as described above is the smaller of `max_participants` and the issued permit's `max_party_size` when a permit is referenced and simply `max_participants` otherwise; a full roster is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_FULL`. And no existing shift may already pair the same `work_party_id` and `volunteer_id` in any status other than `WITHDRAWN` or `NO_SHOW`; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`.

Attendance is straightforward. A shift moves `REGISTERED → CHECKED_IN` while the party's `schedule_status` is `IN_PROGRESS`, performed either by a `CrewLeader` or by the volunteer themselves — a `Volunteer` may only check in their own shift, meaning the acting `X-User-ID` equals the shift's `volunteer_id`. Check-in stamps `checked_in_at` with the logical clock and raises the party's `checked_in_count` by 1. A `CrewLeader` checks a volunteer out, moving the shift `CHECKED_IN → CHECKED_OUT` while the party's `schedule_status` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` and while the logical clock is strictly later than `checked_in_at`. Check-out stamps `checked_out_at`, runs the hour calculation below to set `worked_minutes`, `billable_minutes` and `credited_hours`, copies the volunteer's current `skill_class` onto the shift as `skill_class_snapshot` so that later certification edits cannot re-rate historical hours, raises both `lifetime_credited_hours` and `pending_hours` on the volunteer by the credited amount, and drops the party's `checked_in_count` by 1. A registration may also be abandoned: a `Volunteer` acting on their own shift, or a `CrewLeader`, may move a shift `REGISTERED → WITHDRAWN` with an optional `reason` at any time before the party reaches `COMPLETED`, which drops both the party's `registered_count` and the volunteer's `active_registration_count` by 1 and recomputes `first_aid_capable_count`. `HOURS_CERTIFIED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `NO_SHOW` are the terminal shift states.

**Crediting hours** works in minutes and quantizes once. The `worked_minutes` figure is the whole number of minutes from `checked_in_at` to `checked_out_at`, truncated toward zero. A meal break of 30 minutes is deducted from any shift of four hours or more — that is, whenever `worked_minutes` is at least 240 — and nothing is deducted from a shorter shift. Travel is credited from the party's `travel_allowance_minutes`, but never more than 60 minutes per volunteer, so the creditable travel is whichever is smaller of the party's allowance and 60. The `billable_minutes` figure is then the worked minutes less the break, floored at 0 so that a deduction can never drive the figure negative, plus the capped travel minutes. Finally the billable minutes are divided by 15, rounded half up to a whole number of quarter-hour units, and multiplied by 0.25 to give `credited_hours`, expressed to two decimal places. Only shifts that reach `CHECKED_OUT` ever receive credited hours; shifts in `REGISTERED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `NO_SHOW` hold `credited_hours` at 0. As section 2.9 insists, this quantization happens per shift, before any aggregate sums it.

Hour review is a `StewardshipCoordinator` act carrying a `decision`, and it has three outcomes. Verifying moves the shift `CHECKED_OUT → HOURS_VERIFIED` and requires the party to have reached `COMPLETED`; it records the acting `X-User-ID` as `hours_verified_by_user_id`, moves `credited_hours` out of the volunteer's `pending_hours` and into `verified_hours`, and adds the same amount to the plan's `verified_volunteer_hours`. Disputing moves the shift `CHECKED_OUT → HOURS_DISPUTED`; it requires an `adjusted_hours` value that is a multiple of 0.25 and at least 0, and a non-empty `dispute_reason`, both of which are stored. The difference between `adjusted_hours` and the shift's existing `credited_hours` is computed, `credited_hours` is overwritten with `adjusted_hours`, and both the volunteer's `lifetime_credited_hours` and `pending_hours` move by that same difference — upward if the correction added hours, downward if it removed them. Resetting is the compensating action for a dispute and moves the shift `HOURS_DISPUTED → CHECKED_OUT`: `credited_hours` is recomputed from scratch by the hour calculation above, `adjusted_hours` and `dispute_reason` are cleared to null, and the volunteer's `lifetime_credited_hours` and `pending_hours` each move by the difference between the recomputed figure and the stored one. The cycle `CHECKED_OUT → HOURS_DISPUTED → CHECKED_OUT` must leave the hour invariant holding with zero drift. A disputed shift may also be verified directly, moving `HOURS_DISPUTED → HOURS_VERIFIED` under exactly the same requirement and with exactly the same bucket movements as verification from `CHECKED_OUT`.

Certification is the `GrantsAdministrator`'s act and moves the shift `HOURS_VERIFIED → HOURS_CERTIFIED`. It is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the `hours_verified_by_user_id` already on the shift — the person who certifies hours may never be the person who verified them. It additionally re-checks the waiver against the work date rather than against today: the volunteer's `waiver_expires_on` must be on or after the date of the party's `start_at`, otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_SIGNED`. Certification records `hours_certified_by_user_id`, moves `credited_hours` out of the volunteer's `verified_hours` and into `certified_hours`, and correspondingly moves the same amount out of the plan's `verified_volunteer_hours` and into its `certified_volunteer_hours`.

**The grant match** is a read-only computed view over a plan, available to a `StewardshipCoordinator` or a `GrantsAdministrator`, and it persists nothing. It takes a `variant` parameter with two possible values, and the two answers are genuinely different figures rather than two renderings of one. Shifts are gathered through the plan's work parties, iterating in `created_at` ascending order. The response reports the `variant` used, the `included_shift_count`, the `total_credited_hours`, the `labor_match_usd`, the `material_match_usd`, the `grant_match_total_usd` and the `average_rate_usd`.

Under the `PROVISIONAL` variant — the figure a crew quotes to a funder before the audit closes — the included set is every shift under the plan's parties whose status is `CHECKED_OUT`, `HOURS_DISPUTED`, `HOURS_VERIFIED` or `HOURS_CERTIFIED`. The `total_credited_hours` is the sum of `credited_hours` across that set, each value already quantized per shift. Every one of those hours is valued at the flat general rate of 28.54 US dollars regardless of who worked them: `labor_match_usd` is the total hours multiplied by 28.54 and rounded half up to two decimal places — summed first, multiplied once, rounded once. Delivered materials are excluded entirely, so `material_match_usd` is 0.00.

Under the `CERTIFIED` variant — the figure actually submitted on the grant match report — the included set narrows to shifts whose status is `HOURS_CERTIFIED` only, but each shift is valued at its own `skill_class_snapshot` rate: 28.54 dollars per hour for `GENERAL`, 41.20 for `SKILLED_TRAIL`, 63.75 for `CERTIFIED_SAWYER` and 89.00 for `PROFESSIONAL`. Here the rounding order is inverted: each shift's `credited_hours` is multiplied by that shift's rate and rounded half up to two decimal places **first**, and those per-shift dollar figures are then summed to give `labor_match_usd`. The `total_credited_hours` is the sum of `credited_hours` over the same narrowed set. This variant also counts delivered materials as match: `material_match_usd` is the sum of `total_cents` over the plan's material orders in `DELIVERED`, divided by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places.

Both variants close the same way. The `grant_match_total_usd` is `labor_match_usd` plus `material_match_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `average_rate_usd` is 0.00 when `total_credited_hours` is 0, and otherwise `labor_match_usd` divided by `total_credited_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places. An empty included set yields `total_credited_hours` 0, `labor_match_usd` 0.00, `average_rate_usd` 0.00, and a `grant_match_total_usd` equal to `material_match_usd` — which is 0.00 under `PROVISIONAL`. Because the two variants draw on different shift sets and round in different orders, the same underlying work will produce a different `labor_match_usd`, a different hour total and a different `average_rate_usd` under each.

### 3.8. Materials Procurement and the Money It Moves

A `CrewLeader` raises a requisition against a plan, naming the `work_plan_id` whose budget it will commit against, a `category` drawn from `BRIDGE`, `SIGNAGE`, `TOOLS`, `DRAINAGE` and `SURFACING`, a non-empty `lines` array whose members each carry a `sku` string, a `unit_price_cents` integer of at least 0 and a `quantity` integer of at least 1, and the mandatory `expected_work_plan_version` described in section 2.8. Two inputs are optional: `condition_report_id` names the hazard the procurement answers and is null for routine stock, and `engineering_review_ref` carries a structural engineer's sign-off reference. The order is created in `DRAFT` with `approved_by_user_id`, `delivered_at` and `cancellation_reason` null. Order listings may be filtered by `work_plan_id`, by `condition_report_id` and by `status`.

Pricing happens once, at creation, and the evaluation order is pinned — each step consumes only values produced by the steps before it, and the intermediate figures are all stored on the order and readable afterwards.

Step one produces `line_subtotal_cents`: for each line, the `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the `quantity`, summed across all lines. Step two selects a volume discount from that **pre-discount** figure, storing it as `discount_pct`: it is 0.00 while `line_subtotal_cents` is below 50,000 cents, 0.04 once `line_subtotal_cents` reaches 50,000 cents and while it remains below 250,000 cents, and 0.07 once `line_subtotal_cents` reaches 250,000 cents. The `discount_cents` figure is `line_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Step three produces `discounted_subtotal_cents` as `line_subtotal_cents` less `discount_cents`, and that post-discount figure is the basis for both of the remaining tiers.

Step four sets `freight_cents`. Freight is waived entirely — 0 cents — once `discounted_subtotal_cents` reaches 200,000 cents. Below that threshold, freight is a base charge of 4,500 cents plus 2 percent of `discounted_subtotal_cents`, where that percentage is rounded half up to whole cents before being added to the base. It is worth being explicit about the trap here: freight is keyed on the **post-discount** subtotal, not the pre-discount one, and the difference is real money. A signage order with lines totalling 205,000 cents attracts a 4 percent discount of 8,200 cents, leaving a discounted subtotal of 196,800 cents, which sits below the free-freight threshold and therefore pays 4,500 plus 3,936, or 8,436 cents of freight — where keying the tier on the untouched 205,000 would have wrongly made freight free.

Step five sets `bridge_surcharge_cents`, the engineered-structure surcharge, which is non-zero only when `category` is `BRIDGE`: it is 6 percent of `discounted_subtotal_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and it is 0 for every other category. Step six sums the three to give `total_cents`: `discounted_subtotal_cents` plus `freight_cents` plus `bridge_surcharge_cents`. Only the order's own `lines` and `category` participate; no other order on the plan influences the price.

That `total_cents` is committed against the plan immediately on creation, subject to the admission cap of section 3.3 being checked first, and the plan's `version` advances by 1 as the ledger moves. If the commitment drives `budget_posture` to `OVER_COMMITTED`, the parent adoption suspends and its work parties suspend behind it, exactly as section 3.2 describes.

Submitting the order moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and enforces three checks in this order: the plan must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_WORK_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`; a `BRIDGE` order under an agency with `requires_engineering_review` true must carry a non-null `engineering_review_ref`, otherwise `ERR_ENGINEERING_REVIEW_REQUIRED`; and the plan's `budget_posture` must not be `OVER_COMMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_OVER_COMMITTED`. Approval by a `StewardshipCoordinator` moves the order `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, again requires the plan to be `APPROVED`, and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`; it also has a side effect elsewhere in the system, because an escalated condition report naming this order through `condition_report_id` becomes resolvable by a coordinator once the order reaches `APPROVED`. Receipt by a `CrewLeader` moves the order `APPROVED → DELIVERED`, requires the plan to be `APPROVED`, stamps `delivered_at` with the logical clock, and moves the money: the plan's `committed_cents` drops by `total_cents` and its `spent_cents` rises by the same amount, with `available_cents` and `budget_posture` recomputed and the plan's `version` advancing by 1.

Cancellation is the compensating action for creation and releases exactly `total_cents` back to the plan. A `CrewLeader` may cancel a `DRAFT` order; a `StewardshipCoordinator` may cancel one in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`; all three paths require a non-empty `reason`, which is stored as `cancellation_reason`, and all three carry an identical consequence set. The order lands in `CANCELLED`, which along with `DELIVERED` is terminal. The plan's `committed_cents` drops by `total_cents` and `available_cents` and `budget_posture` are recomputed so that the budget invariant is restored, with the plan's `version` advancing by 1. If that recomputation carries `budget_posture` out of `OVER_COMMITTED`, the parent adoption carrying `suspension_reason` `BUDGET_OVER_COMMIT` returns from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`, and every work party under that agreement's plans sitting in `schedule_status` `SUSPENDED` with `block_reason` `AGREEMENT_SUSPENDED` returns to `CONFIRMED` with `readiness_status` set to `BRIEFED`.

### 3.9. Incidents, Closure and Recovery

Anything that goes wrong on the ground is filed as an incident by a `Volunteer` or a `CrewLeader`. The report names the `work_party_id` during which it happened, classifies it with a `severity_class` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, records whether on-site first aid was given as `first_aid_rendered`, carries a free-text `narrative`, and stamps `occurred_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC instant that must be at or before the logical clock. An `injured_volunteer_id` is optional and is null for near-miss and property incidents. On creation the server copies the party's `trail_segment_id` onto the incident, records the filer's `X-User-ID` as `reported_by_user_id`, and lands the incident in `OPEN` with `closed_by_user_id`, `corrective_action` and `closed_at` null. Incident listings may be narrowed by `work_party_id`, by `trail_segment_id`, by `injured_volunteer_id` and by `status`.

A `SEVERE` incident is the heaviest event in the system. Filing one raises the segment's `open_severe_incident_count`, which drives the posture to `INCIDENT_CLOSED`; that suspends the segment's `ACTIVE` adoption with `suspension_reason` `SEGMENT_INCIDENT`; that suspends the agreement's `CONFIRMED` work parties and blocks its `PLANNED` ones; and that withdraws the `REGISTERED` shifts on the parties that actually moved to `SUSPENDED`, decrementing each volunteer's `active_registration_count`. Four levels, one atomic sweep, everything in `created_at` ascending order.

A `SafetyOfficer` opens formal review of an incident whose `severity_class` is `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, moving it `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW` with `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced by 1. A `MINOR` incident has no review step and is closed directly from `OPEN`; `MODERATE` and `SEVERE` incidents must pass through `UNDER_REVIEW` first, and an attempt to close them directly from `OPEN` finds no declared transition. Closing an incident, from either `OPEN` for a `MINOR` one or `UNDER_REVIEW` for the rest, is a `SafetyOfficer` act requiring a non-empty `corrective_action` and requiring that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the recorded `reported_by_user_id`; a closer who is also the reporter is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Closure stores `closed_by_user_id`, `corrective_action` and `closed_at`, and `CLOSED` is terminal.

Closing an incident from `UNDER_REVIEW` also unwinds the cascade. The segment's `open_severe_incident_count` is recomputed, and when it reaches 0 the segment's posture is recomputed and may leave `INCIDENT_CLOSED` — landing on `OPEN` only when no lower-precedence clause matches, and otherwise on `SEASONAL_CLOSED`, `WET_RESTRICTED` or `HAZARD_RESTRICTED`. Each adoption agreement on that segment carrying `suspension_reason` `SEGMENT_INCIDENT` returns from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`, which in turn returns its suspended work parties to `CONFIRMED` with `readiness_status` `BRIEFED` and `block_reason` cleared. As always, the volunteers withdrawn on the way down are not restored on the way back up.
