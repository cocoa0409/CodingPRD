# Charity Golf Outing Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A charity golf outing is, on the surface, a single afternoon of golf. Operationally it is four businesses running in parallel: a ticketed event with tiered pricing and a hard field capacity, a sponsorship inventory business with fulfillment obligations that must be proven rather than promised, a regulated fundraising operation that issues tax-relevant charitable receipts, and a contract relationship with a host course that bills on guaranteed minimums whether or not the golfers show up. The organizers who run these events today reconcile all four in spreadsheets, and the failure mode is always the same: the number reported to the beneficiary at the end does not survive audit, because a cancelled foursome, a waived hole sign, or a denied hole-in-one insurance claim silently moved a cost that nobody re-added.

This document specifies the Charity Golf Outing Operations API, the system of record for one outing from the day the course contract is drafted to the day the cheque is remitted to the charity. Every collection is mounted under the `/outing-ops` prefix. The system's governing design decision is that money is never entered twice: the beneficiary's net-proceeds figure is a derived quantity that is recomputed from first principles on every event that could possibly move it — a payment, a tier amendment, a cancellation, a sponsor waiver, an auction settlement, a bound insurance premium, a rain-date credit — and it is recomputed under an explicitly named variant so that a provisional mid-event estimate and the final audited figure can never be confused with one another.

The second design decision is that the outing's operational reality and its financial reality advance on separate tracks. An outing carries two independent lifecycle dimensions, and the tournament director who locks the field has no authority over the finance dimension, which belongs to the charity's treasurer. Layered on top of that separation is a segregation-of-duties rule enforced on the acting user's identity: the staff member who approves a sponsor's artwork may not be the one who certifies that the sign was actually installed, and the staff member who records an auction sale may not be the one who issues its charitable receipt. Readers implementing this specification should treat §2 as the invariant contract and §3 as the domain narrative in which the individual business rules live.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which they are acting. Six roles are recognized, and their spellings are exact: `TournamentDirector`, `SponsorshipManager`, `CharityTreasurer`, `CourseOperations`, `Participant`, and `Beneficiary`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of those six strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This is the very first check the server performs on any request, ahead of schema validation and ahead of any lookup of the resources the request names.

Once the caller is authenticated, the server checks whether the declared role appears on the endpoint being called. A role that is not listed for the endpoint is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The `TournamentDirector` runs the tournament itself: creating outings and package tiers, driving the play dimension, grouping foursomes, posting scores, and administering the skill contests. The `SponsorshipManager` owns the sponsorship inventory end to end — creating sellable units, withdrawing and restoring them, reserving them for sponsors, driving the fulfillment obligation, and binding prize insurance. The `CharityTreasurer` owns the money: the finance dimension of the outing, the fundraising channels, the charitable receipts, and the remittance to the beneficiary. `CourseOperations` acts for the host course and owns the contract, the course ratings, the tee sheet, the carts, score posting, and golfer check-in. Several selling and reading operations are deliberately shared between roles; §4 records the authorized roles for each endpoint.

Four of the six roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `TournamentDirector`, `SponsorshipManager`, `CharityTreasurer`, or `CourseOperations`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque staff operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any row's `id`. The server does record it where the specification calls for an audit stamp — as `recorded_by_user_id` on a registration or a fundraising item, as `artwork_approved_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a sponsor allocation, and as `issued_by_user_id` on a charitable receipt — and it compares those recorded stamps against each other to enforce segregation of duties, but it never resolves them to a row. The `Beneficiary` role likewise carries an opaque identifier; it is a read-only role limited to reading outings and the beneficiary proceeds report.

The `Participant` role is different: it is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Player` row, and ownership checks compare the addressed resource's identity to that value. Concretely, a `Participant` may update a handicap index only on their own player row; addressing any other player's row is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. A `Participant` may buy a package, add a player row, and read outings, package tiers, their own registration, their own player row, teams, the pairing sheet, contests, and receipts, but they hold no exclusive operation of their own.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters alongside its own filters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` states how many rows to skip and defaults to 0. A value outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's `created_at` timestamp in ascending order. The single documented exception is the pairing sheet, whose ordering is described in §3.5.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Ten of the eleven resource families in this system — outings, course contracts, package tiers, registrations, players, teams, sponsorship assets, sponsor allocations, contests, and fundraising items — carry the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and clients never supply it.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recorded when the row was first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.
- **`version`** (integer): a counter that starts at 1 and is incremented by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row. A cascade that rewrites a derived field is a mutation for this purpose: when a registration payment recomputes an outing's revenue figures, the outing's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though the treasurer never addressed the outing directly.

Charitable receipts are the exception. A `CharitableReceipt` is append-only and immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field, and no endpoint exists to update or delete one. Correcting a receipt is done by appending a reversal, never by editing the original.

All temporal comparisons in this system read a logical clock rather than wall-clock time. The server's clock starts at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability moves that clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, and it is available to all four staff roles. Every deadline in the specification — the contract cancellation notice window, the sponsorship fulfillment due date, the late-cancellation fee window — is evaluated against the logical clock, and the clock is what stamps `confirmed_at`, `signed_at`, `committed_at`, `sold_at`, `issued_at`, `insurance_bound_at`, and `score_posted_at`.

### 2.5. Amounts, Handicaps and Rounding

Money is carried in whole cents throughout, in integer fields whose names end in `_cents`. There is no sub-cent arithmetic anywhere in the system, and no monetary field is ever transmitted as a string. Rates and percentages are the only non-integer configuration values: `admin_fee_pct` and `rain_date_credit_pct` are plain numbers.

Wherever this document says an amount is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value sitting exactly on the .5 boundary rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Four rounding targets exist and they differ in their precision, so they are worth stating together:

- Monetary results are rounded to whole cents: the administrative fee, the partial rain-date credit, the late cancellation fee, the sponsor waiver credit, and the token-benefit ceiling.
- A player's course handicap is rounded to a whole stroke and then clamped, never truncated.
- A team's playing handicap is rounded to one decimal place, and the per-player allowances that feed it are themselves rounded to whole strokes before they are aggregated.
- A team's net score is carried to one decimal place, and its four matching-card tiebreak metrics are carried to two decimal places.

Requests are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when a required input is absent, when an enum-typed input carries a value outside its declared set, when a value has the wrong type, when a field whose name ends in `_cents` or `_count` is supplied as a negative number, when a timestamp is not ISO 8601 UTC with a trailing `Z`, when an array that must be non-empty is empty, or when the body attempts to write a field that the server computes or manages itself.

### 2.6. The Validation Ladder

A single request frequently violates more than one rule, and the response must be deterministic. The server therefore evaluates a fixed ladder and reports the **first** rule that fails; every later rule is left unevaluated. The ladder is identical for every endpoint:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and role recognized, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization** — the role is listed on the endpoint, and a `Participant` is acting on their own player row, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request schema** — the body and query conform as described in §2.5, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Path existence** — every identifier embedded in the path resolves to a live row. Identifiers are resolved left to right, so the outermost segment is reported first, and the reported error names the missing family: `ERR_OUTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_RECEIPT_NOT_FOUND`, all at HTTP 404.
5. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a live row, checked in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, and reported with the same family-specific 404 codes. This tier applies to registration creation, player creation, team creation, sponsor allocation creation, receipt issuance, contest award, fundraising sale recording, and tier amendment.
6. **Cross-outing referential integrity** — every row named by one request belongs to the same outing, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_OUTING_REFERENCE` (HTTP 409). This governs a registration and its tier, a team and its players, an allocation's registration and its asset, a contest winner's player row, and a receipt's subject. It applies to registration creation, team creation, sponsor allocation creation, receipt issuance, contest award, and tier amendment.
7. **Receipt subject arity** — a forward receipt names exactly one subject and a reversal names exactly one reversed receipt, otherwise `ERR_RECEIPT_SUBJECT_AMBIGUOUS` (HTTP 422). It applies to receipt issuance and to receipt reversal.
8. **State precondition** — the requested action is a declared edge out of the target row's current state in the relevant lifecycle, including the parallel dimensions carried by outings and contests, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). This applies to every action endpoint.
9. **Open-field precondition** — any request that sells, amends, or adds field capacity requires the owning outing's play dimension to be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). Six operations sit behind this gate: creating a registration, recording a registration payment, amending a registration's tier, creating a team, creating a sponsor allocation, and recording a fundraising sale.
10. **Receipt immutability** — a reversal may not itself be reversed and a receipt already carrying a live reversal may not be reversed a second time, otherwise `ERR_RECEIPT_IMMUTABLE` (HTTP 409).
11. **Optimistic concurrency** — the supplied version guard matches the row's current `version`, as described in §2.7. Because this tier sits below the state precondition, a request that is both wrongly sequenced and stale reports the state error, not the staleness error.
12. **Business rules**, evaluated in five sub-tiers in this order: **safety** first, then **referential**, then **temporal**, then **constraint**, then **fiscal**. Within each sub-tier the individual rules retain the relative order given in §3, and the specific orderings that matter operationally — the tee-assignment ladder, the sponsor-allocation ladder, and the withdrawn-before-capacity ordering — are stated where those workflows are described.

The safety sub-tier holds contract signature (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED`), insurance binding (`ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_BOUND`), score completeness (`ERR_MISSING_SCORES`), segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), the amateur cash cap (`ERR_AMATEUR_CASH_CAP_EXCEEDED`), and raffle non-deductibility (`ERR_RAFFLE_NOT_DEDUCTIBLE`), in that order. The referential sub-tier holds player-to-team uniqueness (`ERR_PLAYER_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`), entitlement type matching (`ERR_ASSET_TYPE_MISMATCH`), and entitlement balance (`ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_ENTITLED`). The temporal sub-tier holds the tee-sheet slot (`ERR_TEE_SLOT_CONFLICT`), the sponsor service window (`ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), the cart turnaround (`ERR_CART_DOUBLE_BOOKED`), the wave window (`ERR_WAVE_WINDOW_VIOLATION`), the fulfillment deadline (`ERR_FULFILLMENT_WINDOW_ELAPSED`), and the contract cancellation notice (`ERR_CANCELLATION_NOTICE_VIOLATION`), in that order. The constraint sub-tier holds the cart allowance (`ERR_CART_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`), asset withdrawal and capacity (`ERR_ASSET_WITHDRAWN` before `ERR_ASSET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), exclusivity (`ERR_EXCLUSIVE_ASSET_CONFLICT`), tier inventory (`ERR_TIER_INVENTORY_EXHAUSTED`), team size (`ERR_TEAM_SIZE_INVALID`), the reopen cap (`ERR_MAX_REOPENS_EXCEEDED`), duplicate receipts (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RECEIPT`), and contest hole conflicts (`ERR_CONTEST_HOLE_CONFLICT`), in that order. The fiscal sub-tier holds payment amount matching (`ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`), refund ceiling (`ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PAYMENT`), bid sufficiency (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BID`), prize-pool funding (`ERR_PRIZE_POOL_UNFUNDED`), non-negative net proceeds (`ERR_NEGATIVE_NET_PROCEEDS`), and remittance matching (`ERR_REMITTANCE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`), in that order.

Note the practical consequence of the safety sub-tier sitting first: a contest award that both exceeds the amateur cash cap and would leave the prize pool unfunded reports `ERR_AMATEUR_CASH_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and a field lock attempted on an outing whose contract was never signed reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED` rather than any later constraint failure.

### 2.7. Superseding Writes and Version Guards

Three operations in this system are legitimately repeatable: a tee assignment may be rewritten to supersede an earlier pairing, a registration may be re-amended from one tier to another any number of times while the field is open, and a finance reconciliation may be reopened and recomputed. Because each of these is a valid product operation rather than an error, no state precondition can distinguish a deliberate replacement from a client working blindly from figures that have since moved. Those three operations therefore carry an explicit version guard, supplied as a mandatory (never optional) integer in the request body:

- A tee assignment must carry `expected_team_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Team`. If it does not equal the team's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TEAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so the client cannot replace a pairing it has not seen.
- A tier amendment must carry `expected_registration_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Registration`. A mismatch with the registration's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so a client cannot compute a price delta from a tier the registration no longer holds.
- A finance transition must carry `expected_outing_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Outing`. A mismatch with the outing's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OUTING_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so the treasurer cannot publish a reconciliation computed from superseded figures.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Because cascades bump the `version` of every row they touch, a client that read a team, then watched an unrelated registration cancellation ripple through that team's handicap, holds a stale team version and must re-read before writing.

### 2.8. Cascade Propagation and Recomputation Discipline

Nearly every derived value in this system is a pure function of rows the client can see, and the server keeps those derived values continuously correct rather than computing them lazily on read. Whenever an operation changes an input to a derived value, the server recomputes that value in the same request, and then recomputes anything derived from it, until the chain closes. The three longest chains are worth naming, because an implementation that stops one hop short will produce figures that never reconcile.

The first begins at the course contract. Amending a contract's course rating, slope rating, or par rewrites the course handicap of every player of the outing; the new course handicaps rewrite the playing handicap of every team of the outing under whichever handicap variant that team currently carries; and the new playing handicaps rewrite the net score and all four net tiebreak metrics of every team that has already posted a gross score. Amending the contract's per-player rate, food-and-beverage minimum, or per-player food-and-beverage credit instead rewrites the outing's whole finance rollup.

The second begins at a registration cancellation and is the deepest in the system: the registration's own money fields, then every one of its sponsor allocations released, then each released allocation's asset counters and inventory posture, then the outing's aggregate reserved-slot counter, then the tier's sold count and the outing's confirmed slot count, then a possible waitlist promotion, then an automatic receipt reversal, and finally the outing's finance rollup. §3.2 walks it step by step.

The third begins at a postponement: the outing's dates move, the contract earns a rain credit and returns to an amended posture, the outing's direct costs absorb that credit, and its net proceeds change accordingly. Because the cascade rewrites the contract, the contract's own `version` advances even though nobody addressed the contract.

Each row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented exactly once for that request, whether the client addressed it or not. Cascades are applied immediately and synchronously: a read issued after the response is returned always observes the fully settled state, and the server never defers reconciliation to a later request.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two pairs of operations in this system must be performed by different people, and the rule is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` values recorded across separate requests rather than within one request. First, the user who approved a sponsor allocation's artwork — recorded as `artwork_approved_by_user_id` — may not be the user who verifies that the sponsorship was installed; an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and on success the verifying user is recorded as `verified_by_user_id`. Second, the user who recorded a fundraising sale — recorded as `recorded_by_user_id` on the item — may not be the user who issues that item's charitable receipt; the same error is returned, and on success the issuing user is recorded as `issued_by_user_id`. Both checks apply at the sponsor-allocation fulfillment endpoint and at the receipt issuance endpoint respectively.

### 2.10. Sponsorship Inventory Conservation

Sponsorship inventory is a conserved quantity, and the specification is written as an identity that must hold before and after every forward action and every compensating action. For each sponsorship asset, its declared `capacity` always equals `remaining_capacity` plus `reserved_count` plus `committed_count` plus `fulfilled_count`. The three counters are derived by classifying the asset's occupying allocations: `reserved_count` counts allocations sitting at `RESERVED`; `committed_count` counts those at `COMMITTED`, `ARTWORK_APPROVED`, or `INSTALLED`; and `fulfilled_count` counts those at `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`. An allocation that has reached `RELEASED` occupies nothing and is excluded from all three counters. `remaining_capacity` is the declared capacity minus the sum of the three counters, except that a withdrawn asset reads 0 regardless of its counters.

The asset's inventory posture is likewise derived, not chosen, with one exception. It reads `WITHDRAWN` when the unit was withdrawn manually. Otherwise it reads `AVAILABLE` when the sum of the three counters is zero; `RESERVED` when `reserved_count` is greater than zero while `committed_count` and `fulfilled_count` are both zero; `COMMITTED` whenever `committed_count` is greater than zero; and `FULFILLED` when `committed_count` and `reserved_count` are both zero while `fulfilled_count` is greater than zero.

At the outing level the same quantity is aggregated: `reserved_asset_slot_count` is the sum, over every sponsorship asset of the outing, of that asset's `reserved_count` plus `committed_count` plus `fulfilled_count`. It is this figure that must return to its prior value after a reservation is released, and it is the cheapest way to detect a cascade that stopped one hop short.

### 2.11. Parallel Lifecycle Dimensions

Two resource families in this system carry two lifecycle dimensions that advance independently on separate operations. An outing has a play dimension, `play_status`, and a finance dimension, `finance_status`; a contest has a lifecycle dimension, `status`, and an insurance dimension, `insurance_status`. Neither dimension of a pair is derived from the other, and neither shares a trigger with the other. The consequence is that a state precondition must be evaluated against the dimension the requested action actually drives: asking a contest's insurance dimension to move while the contest's lifecycle dimension is in a state that forbids binding is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and so is asking the lifecycle dimension to move to a state that only the insurance dimension can occupy.

There is exactly one place where a dimension reads its sibling as a precondition, and it is stated explicitly in §3.9: the reconciliation of an outing's finances requires that the play dimension already be `SCORED`. Beyond that single coupling, the dimensions do not consult each other, though several transitions of one dimension guard on the *other resource's* state — a contest may only open while its outing's play dimension is `OPEN` or `FIELD_LOCKED`, for example.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Founding the Outing and Contracting the Course

A `TournamentDirector` opens the season by creating an **Outing**, the aggregate root under which everything else in this system hangs. The creation payload carries the event's public `name`, the `beneficiary_name` of the charity that will receive the net proceeds, the tournament `format` — one of `SCRAMBLE`, `BEST_BALL`, or `STROKE_PLAY`, which selects the handicap allowance formula described in §3.3 — and the `start_format`, one of `SHOTGUN`, `WAVE_SPLIT`, or `TEE_TIME`. The director also supplies the `scheduled_date` of play, the wave A window as `wave_a_start` and `wave_a_end`, the `field_capacity_teams` count of groups the course has sold (which must be at least 1), and the `prize_pool_cents` budgeted for team placement prizes (which may be zero but never negative). Three inputs are optional: a contracted alternate date as `rain_date`, and the wave B window as `wave_b_start` and `wave_b_end`. Wave B's window is required in practice when the `start_format` is `WAVE_SPLIT`, since a split start has a second wave to place; all three optional inputs default to null when omitted.

A freshly created outing sits at `DRAFT` on its play dimension and `COLLECTING` on its finance dimension. Every one of its rollup figures — `gross_revenue_cents`, `refunded_cents`, `course_charge_cents`, `fnb_shortfall_cents`, `insurance_cost_cents`, `prize_cost_cents`, `fulfillment_credit_cents`, `direct_costs_cents`, `net_proceeds_before_fee_cents`, `admin_fee_pct`, `admin_fee_cents`, `net_proceeds_cents`, `unclaimed_prize_cents`, and `receipted_deductible_total_cents` — is initialised to 0, as are the operational counters `reopen_count`, `confirmed_player_slots`, `checked_in_player_count`, `waitlisted_registration_count`, and `reserved_asset_slot_count`. The `proceeds_variant` marker is initialised to `PROVISIONAL`, `postponed_from_date` to null, `beneficiary_remittance_cents` to 0, and `remittance_reference` to null. Outings can be listed with either dimension as a filter.

`CourseOperations` then drafts the **CourseContract**, and there may be at most one per outing: a second attempt against an outing that already has a contract is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The contract payload names its `outing_id` and the `course_name`, and it must state the `rate_per_player_cents` — the combined green-fee and cart rate the organizer pays for each billable player. Every other term has a default that stands in for the industry-standard club agreement: the `guaranteed_player_count` the organizer will pay for regardless of turnout defaults to 72 (that is, eighteen full foursomes); the `fnb_minimum_cents` food-and-beverage minimum spend defaults to 0; the `fnb_per_player_cents` credited against that minimum for each billable player defaults to 4500, which is forty-five dollars a head; the `course_rating` defaults to 71.4; the `slope_rating` defaults to 128; the `par` defaults to 72; the `tee_interval_minutes` length of one tee-sheet slot defaults to 10 and must be at least 1; the `cart_allowance_count` of distinct carts the course supplies defaults to 40 and must be at least 1; the `rain_date_terms` default to `NONE`, the alternatives being `FULL_CREDIT` and `PARTIAL_CREDIT`; the `rain_date_credit_pct` fraction credited under partial terms defaults to 0.5 and must lie between 0 and 1 inclusive; and the `cancellation_notice_days` window defaults to 30 whole days. A new contract begins at `DRAFT` with `signed_at` null, `amendment_count` 0, `rain_credit_cents` 0, and `settled_charge_cents` 0.

The contract's own lifecycle is driven by `CourseOperations` through a single operation taking an `action` of `SIGN`, `AMEND`, or `VOID`. Signing moves the contract from `DRAFT` to `SIGNED` and stamps `signed_at` from the logical clock. Amending a signed contract moves it `SIGNED → AMENDED`, and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` once the outing's play dimension has reached `SCORED` or `CANCELLED`. An amendment overwrites whichever of the negotiable terms the request supplies — `rate_per_player_cents`, `fnb_minimum_cents`, `fnb_per_player_cents`, `guaranteed_player_count`, `slope_rating`, `course_rating`, `par`, `tee_interval_minutes`, `cart_allowance_count`, `rain_date_terms`, `rain_date_credit_pct`, and `cancellation_notice_days` — and increments `amendment_count` by one. When any of the three rating fields changed, the full four-hop cascade of §2.8 runs; when a money term changed, the outing's finance rollup is recomputed under the outing's current `proceeds_variant`. Countersigning an amendment returns it `AMENDED → SIGNED`, which closes the amendment cycle and leaves the contract able to be amended again.

Voiding a contract is the course's way of walking away, and it is available from `DRAFT` and from `SIGNED`. In both cases no registration of the outing may be sitting at `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`; if one is, the void is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Voiding a `SIGNED` contract carries an additional temporal condition: the logical clock must be strictly earlier than the outing's `scheduled_date` minus `cancellation_notice_days` whole days, and a void attempted at or inside that notice window is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_NOTICE_VIOLATION`. When a signed contract is voided the outing loses its venue, so an outing whose play dimension is `DRAFT` or `OPEN` is moved to `CANCELLED` by the same request. A void request may carry a `void_reason` for the audit trail. Both `DRAFT → VOIDED` and `SIGNED → VOIDED` are terminal.

The play dimension is driven by a single director-only transition operation that takes an `action` naming one of seven moves — `OPEN_REGISTRATION`, `LOCK_FIELD`, `START_PLAY`, `FINALIZE_SCORING`, `POSTPONE`, `RESUME`, and `CANCEL_OUTING` — and may carry a replacement `rain_date`. Opening registration moves the outing `DRAFT → OPEN`, and it has two preconditions: the outing must have a contract whose status is `SIGNED` or `AMENDED`, and an outing with no contract at all fails the same check with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_SIGNED`; and the outing must already have at least one package tier, otherwise `ERR_NO_PACKAGE_TIER`. Locking the field moves it `OPEN → FIELD_LOCKED` and requires at least one team to exist and every team of the outing to hold exactly four players, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_INCOMPLETE`; its handicap cascade is described in §3.3. Starting play moves it `FIELD_LOCKED → IN_PLAY` and requires that every team of the outing has had a tee assignment written at least once and carries a non-null `starting_hole`, otherwise `ERR_MISSING_TEE_ASSIGNMENT`. Finalizing scoring moves it `IN_PLAY → SCORED`, which is terminal on the play dimension, and is described in §3.8.

Reopening the field is the reverse edge, `FIELD_LOCKED → OPEN`, and it is capped: an outing may be reopened at most twice. Each accepted reopen increments `reopen_count` by one, and a request that arrives when `reopen_count` already equals 2 is refused with `ERR_MAX_REOPENS_EXCEEDED`. A reopen also unwinds the lock's handicap work — it recomputes every team's `team_playing_handicap` under the provisional treatment and resets each team's `handicap_variant` to `PROVISIONAL`, and for any team that had already posted a gross score it recomputes `net_score` and the four net tiebreak metrics from the newly provisional playing handicap.

Weather is handled by postponement, available from both `OPEN` and `FIELD_LOCKED` — the edges `OPEN → POSTPONED` and `FIELD_LOCKED → POSTPONED` — and its side effects are identical from either origin. Two conditions must hold: the outing must carry a non-null `rain_date`, and the contract's `rain_date_terms` must be `FULL_CREDIT` or `PARTIAL_CREDIT`; if either fails the request is refused with `ERR_NO_RAIN_DATE_TERMS`. On acceptance the server captures the outgoing `scheduled_date` into `postponed_from_date`, promotes the `rain_date` into `scheduled_date`, and clears `rain_date` back to null. The contract earns a credit: `rain_credit_cents` is set to the outing's `course_charge_cents` as it stood immediately before the postponement when the terms are `FULL_CREDIT`, or to that same base multiplied by `rain_date_credit_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents when the terms are `PARTIAL_CREDIT`; terms of `NONE` would yield zero, but since `NONE` also blocks the transition, a zero credit can only ever arise from a later amendment that strips the terms away. The same request increments the contract's `amendment_count` by one and moves the contract's status to `AMENDED`, then feeds the fresh `rain_credit_cents` into the outing's finance rollup under its current `proceeds_variant`. Finally, because the field will be re-drawn on a new date, every team of the outing has its `wave`, `starting_hole`, `tee_time`, `cart_number`, `slot_occupancy_start`, and `slot_occupancy_end` cleared to null — but its `tee_assignment_count` is deliberately preserved, so the history of how many times a group has been placed survives the postponement. Resuming returns the outing `POSTPONED → OPEN` and does nothing else.

Cancelling the outing is available from `DRAFT`, from `OPEN`, and from `POSTPONED`, and in all three cases it requires the finance dimension to still be `COLLECTING` — money that has already been reconciled or disbursed cannot be unwound this way. From `OPEN` the cancellation additionally sweeps the unsettled sales: every registration of the outing sitting at `PENDING_PAYMENT` or `WAITLISTED` is moved to `CANCELLED`. From `DRAFT` and from `POSTPONED` no registration sweep occurs. `CANCELLED` is terminal on the play dimension.

### 3.2. Selling the Field: Package Tiers, Registrations and the Waitlist

Before registration can open, the `TournamentDirector` must publish at least one **PackageTier**. A tier names its `outing_id` and a short `code` that must be unique among that outing's tiers — a repeat is refused with `ERR_TIER_CODE_DUPLICATE` — along with the `kind` of registration it sells (`TEAM`, `INDIVIDUAL`, or `SPONSOR`), its `price_cents`, and the number of `player_slots` it includes, which must be between 0 and 4 inclusive. Three optional terms round out the tier: `gsv_cents`, the goods-and-services value of the whole package (rounds, meals, gifts) that the charitable-deduction rule of §3.7 subtracts, defaulting to 0; `inventory_cap`, the maximum number of registrations that may hold this tier in a revenue-recognized state, defaulting to 999 and never below 1; and the pair `entitled_asset_type` and `entitled_asset_count`, which grant the buyer sponsorship inventory at no extra charge. The entitled type is one of `HOLE_SIGN`, `CONTEST`, `CART`, `BEVERAGE_STATION`, or `NONE`, defaulting to `NONE`, and the entitled count defaults to 0. Each tier also exposes a live `sold_count`: the number of registrations currently holding it whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`. Tiers can be listed filtered by outing and by `kind`.

A **Registration** is the sale itself, and it may be created by a `TournamentDirector`, a `SponsorshipManager`, or a `Participant` buying for themselves. The payload names the `outing_id`, the `package_tier_id` being bought, the `buyer_name` of the company or person paying, and the `player_count` of slots being taken, which must be between 0 and 4 inclusive and must not exceed the `player_slots` of the tier being held. An optional `buyer_email` supplies the address the charitable receipt will be addressed to, defaulting to null. A new registration begins at `PENDING_PAYMENT` with `amount_paid_cents`, `amount_refunded_cents`, `late_cancel_fee_cents`, `fulfillment_credit_cents`, `entitled_asset_balance`, and `amendment_count` all at 0, `confirmed_at` null, and `receipt_issued` false. The creating user's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `recorded_by_user_id`. Creation does not touch the outing's `waitlisted_registration_count`, because a fresh registration is pending payment, not waitlisted.

Recording payment is where the field capacity is enforced, and it is the one operation in this system whose outcome state depends on arithmetic rather than on the caller's intent. The payment payload carries a single `amount_cents`, which must equal the held tier's `price_cents` to the cent, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 402). The outing's play dimension must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_CLOSED`, and the held tier's `sold_count` must be strictly below its `inventory_cap`, otherwise `ERR_TIER_INVENTORY_EXHAUSTED`. With those satisfied, the server compares the outing's current `confirmed_player_slots` plus this registration's `player_count` against the field's capacity in player slots, which is `field_capacity_teams` multiplied by four. When the sum is less than or equal to that capacity the registration moves `PENDING_PAYMENT → CONFIRMED`; when the sum strictly exceeds it, the registration moves `PENDING_PAYMENT → WAITLISTED` instead. The boundary matters: a payment that lands exactly on the capacity figure confirms, and only the first slot past it waitlists.

Both outcomes add `amount_cents` to `amount_paid_cents`. Confirmation goes further: it stamps `confirmed_at` from the logical clock, and for every player of the registration it snapshots that golfer's current `handicap_index` into `handicap_index_at_confirm` and then recomputes their `course_handicap` from that snapshot as §3.3 describes. It seeds the registration's `entitled_asset_balance` from the held tier's entitlement, re-derives the tier's `sold_count`, and re-derives the outing's `confirmed_player_slots`. Waitlisting deliberately does none of that handicap work — `confirmed_at` stays null and no snapshot is taken, because a waitlisted package has not consumed a slot — and instead re-derives the outing's `waitlisted_registration_count`.

Amending a registration's tier is the `SponsorshipManager`'s and `TournamentDirector`'s tool for upgrading or downgrading a sponsor after the sale. It moves a registration `CONFIRMED → AMENDED`, and from `AMENDED` it loops back to `AMENDED` so a package can be re-amended any number of times while the field is open; the preconditions and side effects of the self-loop are identical to those of the first amendment. The request supplies the new `package_tier_id`, the mandatory `expected_registration_version` guard of §2.7, an optional signed `amount_cents`, and an optional `player_count`. Five conditions are checked: the outing's play dimension must be `OPEN` or `FIELD_LOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_CLOSED`; the requested tier must belong to the same outing, otherwise `ERR_TIER_NOT_FOUND`; the registration's `player_count` must not exceed the new tier's `player_slots`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the supplied `amount_cents` must equal the new tier's `price_cents` minus the currently held tier's `price_cents` — the signed delta, with an omitted `amount_cents` read as zero — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; and the version guard must match, otherwise `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION`. On acceptance the registration's `package_tier_id` becomes the requested tier and `amendment_count` increments by one. A positive delta is added to `amount_paid_cents`; a negative delta has its absolute value added to `amount_refunded_cents`. The server then re-derives `sold_count` on both the old and the new tier, re-seeds `entitled_asset_balance` from the new tier and releases any entitlement allocations the new tier no longer covers, re-derives the outing's `confirmed_player_slots`, and recomputes the outing's finance rollup under its current `proceeds_variant`. An amendment is also subject to the new tier's `inventory_cap`, and is refused with `ERR_TIER_INVENTORY_EXHAUSTED` when accepting it would push that tier's `sold_count` past its cap.

The remaining registration moves travel on one lifecycle operation whose `action` is `CHECK_IN`, `RECONFIRM`, or `CANCEL`; check-in belongs to the course, reconfirmation and cancellation to the organizer. Reconfirming is the way back: it returns a registration `AMENDED → CONFIRMED` and closes the confirm-amend cycle. Its single condition is fiscal — `amount_paid_cents` minus `amount_refunded_cents` must equal the held tier's `price_cents` exactly, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` — which is what prevents a sponsor from being reconfirmed while a price delta is still outstanding.

Checking in is `CourseOperations`' morning-of operation. It moves a registration `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN` or `AMENDED → CHECKED_IN`, with identical effect from either origin, and requires the outing's play dimension to be `FIELD_LOCKED` or `IN_PLAY`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. It sets `checked_in` to true on every player of the registration and re-derives the outing's `checked_in_player_count` — the figure that becomes the billable headcount once the finances are reconciled.

Cancellation is the deepest cascade in the system, and it runs in nine strictly ordered steps. It is available from `PENDING_PAYMENT`, `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, `CHECKED_IN`, and `WAITLISTED`. From `PENDING_PAYMENT` the only condition is that `amount_paid_cents` is zero, and nothing beyond the state change happens. From `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, and `CHECKED_IN` — three origins whose cascade is identical — the outing's finance dimension must still be `COLLECTING`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The cascade then proceeds:

1. The late-cancellation test. When the logical clock is at or after the outing's `scheduled_date` minus the contract's `cancellation_notice_days` whole days, the organizer retains a quarter of what was paid: `late_cancel_fee_cents` is set to `amount_paid_cents` multiplied by 0.25 and rounded half up to whole cents, and the refund is `amount_paid_cents` minus that retained fee. Outside the notice window `late_cancel_fee_cents` stays 0 and the refund is the whole of `amount_paid_cents`.
2. The refund is added to `amount_refunded_cents`, and when the refund is greater than zero the row auto-advances one further step, `CANCELLED → REFUNDED`, in the same request. A refund of exactly zero leaves the registration at `CANCELLED`. No refund may push `amount_refunded_cents` above `amount_paid_cents` minus the retained late-cancellation fee; an attempt is refused with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PAYMENT`. A cancellation request may carry an explicit `refund_cents` figure, which is subject to that same ceiling.
3. Every sponsor allocation belonging to this registration whose status is not already `RELEASED` is moved to `RELEASED`.
4. Each release in step 3 decrements the occupancy counter of its sponsorship asset and re-derives that asset's `remaining_capacity` and inventory posture.
5. The asset counters written in step 4 re-derive the outing's `reserved_asset_slot_count`.
6. Every player of this registration has `team_id` set to null and `checked_in` set to false; every team that lost a player re-derives its `player_count` and recomputes its `team_playing_handicap` under whichever `handicap_variant` that team currently carries.
7. When the outing's play dimension is `OPEN`, the freed slots are offered to the waitlist as described below, and exactly one waitlisted registration may be promoted.
8. When a charitable receipt of kind `REGISTRATION` with no live reversal exists for this registration, a reversal receipt row is appended with negated amounts and the outing's `receipted_deductible_total_cents` is re-derived.
9. The outing's whole finance rollup is recomputed under its current `proceeds_variant`.

Cancelling a `WAITLISTED` registration is much simpler, because it never consumed a slot: the whole of `amount_paid_cents` is refunded with no late fee whatsoever, the row auto-advances to `REFUNDED` when that refund is greater than zero, and the outing's `waitlisted_registration_count` is re-derived. It too requires the finance dimension to be `COLLECTING`. Both `CANCELLED` and `REFUNDED` are terminal.

Waitlist promotion, step 7 above, is a selection rather than an arithmetic rule, and it is the reason a cancellation can confirm a package nobody touched. The number of freed slots is the cancelled registration's `player_count`. Candidates are the registrations of the same outing that are sitting at `WAITLISTED` at the instant step 6 completes and whose own `player_count` is less than or equal to the freed slot count. Those candidates are ordered by the `price_cents` of the tier they hold, highest first, and ties are broken by the registration's `created_at`, earliest first; a registration's `id` is never consulted as a tiebreaker. The first candidate in that ordering is the winner and moves `WAITLISTED → CONFIRMED`, which stamps its `confirmed_at`, snapshots `handicap_index_at_confirm` on every one of its players and recomputes their `course_handicap`, and re-derives the tier's `sold_count` along with the outing's `confirmed_player_slots` and `waitlisted_registration_count`. Exactly one registration is promoted per cancellation, however many slots were freed. If the candidate set is empty no promotion happens at all: the freed slots simply return to the outing's headroom and `waitlisted_registration_count` is left unchanged. A promotion also requires the outing's play dimension to be `OPEN`, which is why a cancellation after the field is locked frees capacity without filling it.

### 3.3. Golfers, Course Handicaps and the Sandbagging Cap

A **Player** is a named golfer occupying one of a registration's slots. Adding one is open to a `TournamentDirector`, a `SponsorshipManager`, or a `Participant`, and the payload names the funding `registration_id`, the golfer's `full_name` as it will be printed on the pairing sheet, and their `handicap_index` on file, which must lie between 0 and 54.0 inclusive. An optional `amateur_status` declares whether the golfer is an `AMATEUR` or a `PROFESSIONAL`, defaulting to `AMATEUR`; that single flag governs both what form of contest prize the golfer may accept and whether their team can take a cash placement prize at all. A player begins with `team_id` null — it stays null until the golfer is grouped into a foursome — `checked_in` false, and `handicap_index_at_confirm`, `effective_handicap_index`, and `course_handicap` all null. Adding a player beyond the owning registration's `player_count` is refused with `ERR_PLAYER_SLOTS_EXCEEDED`. When the owning registration is already `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN` at the moment the player is added, the snapshot is taken immediately rather than waiting for a confirmation that has already happened. Players can be listed filtered by `registration_id`, by `team_id`, by `amateur_status`, and by `checked_in`.

A golfer's index moves during the weeks before an outing, and the API accepts those updates through a dedicated capability that takes a fresh `handicap_index`. A `TournamentDirector` may update any player; a `Participant` may update only their own row. While the outing's play dimension is `OPEN` the update re-derives that player's `course_handicap` from the confirmation snapshot and rewrites the provisional playing handicap of whatever team the golfer belongs to. Once the field has reached `FIELD_LOCKED` the update is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — this is the rule that makes the sandbagging cap below impossible to dodge by simply reporting a lower index after the lock.

**Converting an index to a course handicap.** A golfer's `course_handicap` is the number of strokes they receive on this particular course, and it is derived from a single index value. The conversion multiplies the index in use by the contract's `slope_rating` divided by 113, then adds the contract's `course_rating` minus its `par`. That raw figure is rounded half up to a whole stroke, then floored at 0, then capped at 36 — so a negative raw figure becomes 0 and a raw figure above 36 becomes 36, and the cap and floor are applied after the rounding, not before. The conversion runs on registration confirmation, on a waitlist promotion, on the field lock, on a contract amendment that changed `slope_rating`, `course_rating`, or `par`, and on a handicap-index update. It runs only for players whose owning registration is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`; a player whose registration is `PENDING_PAYMENT`, `WAITLISTED`, `CANCELLED`, or `REFUNDED` keeps `course_handicap` null.

Which index feeds that conversion is the interesting part, because there are two answers. Before the field is locked — and again after any reopen — the index in use is `handicap_index_at_confirm`, the value snapshotted when the package was confirmed, and `effective_handicap_index` remains null. At the moment the field is locked the system applies a sandbagging cap: for every player whose owning registration is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`, `effective_handicap_index` is written as the larger of the golfer's current `handicap_index` and their `handicap_index_at_confirm` reduced by 3.0 strokes, and that capped value then becomes the index in use for the conversion. The practical effect is that a golfer whose index dropped by more than three strokes since confirmation is credited with only a three-stroke improvement, while a golfer whose index rose is taken at their honest current number. This is the first hop of the lock's three-hop cascade: the outing lock writes `effective_handicap_index` and `course_handicap` on players, which rewrites `team_playing_handicap` on teams.

**Teams.** A `TournamentDirector` groups golfers into a **Team** by naming the `outing_id`, a `team_name` for the pairing sheet, and the `player_ids` of the golfers to group. The outing's play dimension must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_CLOSED`. The `player_ids` array must be non-empty — an empty array is one of §2.5's schema faults, so it is caught at the schema tier and reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than ever reaching the team-size rule. The array must also not be longer than four, and must not be longer than the `player_count` of the registration that funded the players, otherwise `ERR_TEAM_SIZE_INVALID` — a team holds between one and four golfers. A golfer who already carries a `team_id` cannot be grouped again, and neither can a golfer whose owning registration is not `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`; both cases are refused with `ERR_PLAYER_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`. Players named in one request must belong to the same outing as the team, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_OUTING_REFERENCE`. On acceptance the server writes `team_id` on every listed player, derives the team's `player_count` as the count of players pointing at it, and computes the team's `team_playing_handicap` under the provisional treatment, recording `handicap_variant` as `PROVISIONAL`. All of a team's competitive fields — `gross_score`, `gross_back_9`, `gross_back_6`, `gross_back_3`, `gross_hole_18`, `net_score`, `net_back_9`, `net_back_6`, `net_back_3`, `net_hole_18`, `rank_position`, `prize_award_cents`, `score_posted_at` — start null, `prize_eligible` starts true, `prize_award_cents` starts at 0, and `tee_assignment_count` starts at 0. Teams can be listed filtered by outing, by `wave`, and by `starting_hole`.

**Computing a team's playing handicap.** The team figure is built from the course handicaps of the players currently pointing at the team whose owning registration is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`; players whose registration was cancelled have already been unlinked by the cancellation cascade, so they simply are not present. Those course handicaps are sorted into ascending order — lowest handicap first — and ties in value keep the players' `created_at` ascending order. The formula then depends on the outing's `format`, and in two of the three cases the per-player allowance is rounded to a whole stroke *before* anything is aggregated. That ordering is the contract: aggregating the raw products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not what this system does.

- Under `SCRAMBLE` the four sorted course handicaps are weighted 0.20, 0.15, 0.10, and 0.05 respectively, lowest handicap taking the largest weight. Each product is left unrounded, the four products are summed, and the sum is rounded half up to one decimal place.
- Under `BEST_BALL` each sorted course handicap is multiplied by 0.90 and rounded half up to a whole stroke, those whole-stroke allowances are summed, the sum is multiplied by 0.25, and the result is rounded half up to one decimal place.
- Under `STROKE_PLAY` the treatment is identical to best ball except that the per-player allowance percentage is 1.00 — the golfer's full course handicap — so each allowance is that course handicap rounded half up to a whole stroke, the allowances are summed, multiplied by 0.25, and rounded half up to one decimal place.

A team holding fewer than four golfers uses only the players present. Under `SCRAMBLE` the weight vector is applied from its first position upward and the unused weights are simply dropped — never redistributed across the remaining players. Under `BEST_BALL` and `STROKE_PLAY` the 0.25 multiplier is likewise applied unchanged to the sum of however many allowances exist.

The figure is recomputed under the provisional treatment on team creation, on any cascade that re-derives a team's `player_count`, and on a reopen of the field; and under the locked treatment at the moment the field is locked. The two treatments differ only in which course handicap feeds them: the provisional treatment reads course handicaps derived from the confirmation snapshot, while the locked treatment reads course handicaps derived from the sandbag-capped `effective_handicap_index`. A contract amendment that changed a rating field recomputes the figure under whichever treatment the team currently carries, leaving `handicap_variant` unchanged.

### 3.4. Sponsorship Inventory and the Fulfillment Obligation

A `SponsorshipManager` builds the sellable inventory as a set of **SponsorshipAsset** units, each one a physical or logical thing a sponsor's name can go on. A unit names its `outing_id`, its `asset_type` — one of `HOLE_SIGN`, `CONTEST`, `CART`, or `BEVERAGE_STATION` — a human `label` such as "Hole 7 tee sign", and its `list_price_cents`, the a-la-carte price when the unit is bought outside a package entitlement. Four terms are optional: `hole_number`, which must lie between 1 and 18 inclusive and is required for hole signs and contests but stays null for carts and beverage stations; `capacity`, the number of sponsors that may occupy the unit at once, defaulting to 1 and never below 1; `exclusive`, defaulting to true, which when set means no other exclusive unit of the same `asset_type` may occupy the same `hole_number` within the outing; and `fulfillment_due_at`, the deadline by which an occupying allocation must reach verification, defaulting to null. A new unit is created with `reserved_count`, `committed_count`, and `fulfilled_count` at 0, `remaining_capacity` equal to `capacity`, and its inventory posture at `AVAILABLE`. Units can be listed filtered by outing, by `asset_type`, and by posture.

Two manual inventory moves exist, selected by an `action` of `WITHDRAW` or `RESTORE`. Withdrawing a unit takes it off the market, moving it `AVAILABLE → WITHDRAWN`, and it requires that `reserved_count`, `committed_count`, and `fulfilled_count` all read zero; a unit with any occupancy at all is refused with `ERR_ASSET_OCCUPIED`. The withdrawal forces `remaining_capacity` to 0 so the unit leaves the sellable pool. Restoring reverses it, `WITHDRAWN → AVAILABLE`, and re-derives `remaining_capacity` back to the declared `capacity`. Because withdrawal demands zero occupancy, an occupied unit can never be withdrawn, which is why the fulfillment path below never has to defend against a withdrawn asset underneath it.

A **SponsorAllocation** is the junction between a sponsor's registration and one unit of inventory, and creating one is the `SponsorshipManager`'s reservation act. The payload names the `registration_id` of the sponsor package, the `sponsorship_asset_id` of the unit being occupied, and the `source` — `PACKAGE_ENTITLEMENT` when the unit is drawn from what the tier already grants, or `UPSELL` when it is bought on top. When the target unit's `asset_type` is `CART` or `BEVERAGE_STATION` the request must also carry `service_window_start` and `service_window_end` bounding the sponsor's service window; for hole signs and contests both default to null. The outing's play dimension must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_CLOSED`, and the registration and the asset must belong to the same outing, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_OUTING_REFERENCE`.

The remaining checks run in a fixed order, and the order is observable because several of them can fail at once. First, an allocation created with `source` `PACKAGE_ENTITLEMENT` must target a unit whose `asset_type` equals the held tier's `entitled_asset_type`, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Second, an entitlement allocation requires the registration's `entitled_asset_balance` to be at least 1, while an upsell allocation requires the unit to carry a positive `list_price_cents` and consumes no balance at all; either failure is `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_ENTITLED`. Third, the service window is checked for conflict. Fourth, a withdrawn unit is refused with `ERR_ASSET_WITHDRAWN` — this check deliberately precedes the capacity comparison, which a withdrawn unit would also fail, so a withdrawn unit never reports a capacity error. Fifth, the unit's `remaining_capacity` must be at least 1, and a live unit sitting exactly on the boundary value of 0 is refused with `ERR_ASSET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Sixth, a unit flagged `exclusive` accepts at most one non-released allocation regardless of the `capacity` it declares, so a second attempt is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVE_ASSET_CONFLICT`.

The service-window rule deserves its own statement because its inclusivity runs opposite to the tee sheet's. Two allocations on the same unit conflict when their windows, each padded by 30 minutes on both sides, intersect — and the padded interval is treated as **closed**, so two windows that merely touch at the padded boundary do conflict. A conflict is reported as `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. In practice that means a beverage-station sponsor finishing at noon and another starting at one o'clock are in conflict, because half an hour of changeover on each side closes the gap exactly.

An accepted allocation is created at `RESERVED`. It consumes one unit of the registration's `entitled_asset_balance` when its source is the package entitlement — that balance is always the held tier's `entitled_asset_count` minus the count of this registration's entitlement-sourced allocations whose status is not `RELEASED`. The unit's `reserved_count` increments by one, its `remaining_capacity` is re-derived downward, its posture moves `AVAILABLE → RESERVED`, and the outing's `reserved_asset_slot_count` increments. The allocation's `upsell_price_cents` is fixed at creation: it is 0 when the source is the package entitlement and equal to the unit's `list_price_cents` when the source is an upsell. That price is not yet revenue — it becomes recognized revenue only from the commitment step onward. `fulfillment_credit_cents` starts at 0, `waived_reason`, `artwork_approved_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id`, and `committed_at` all start null. Allocations can be listed filtered by `registration_id`, by `sponsorship_asset_id`, by status, and by `source`.

The obligation is driven by a single `SponsorshipManager` operation whose `action` is one of `COMMIT`, `APPROVE_ARTWORK`, `INSTALL`, `VERIFY`, `WAIVE`, or `RELEASE`. From `RESERVED` the obligation walks forward through a fork. Committing moves the allocation `RESERVED → COMMITTED` and requires the linked registration to be `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_REVENUE_RECOGNIZED`. It stamps `committed_at` from the logical clock; when the source is an upsell it turns `upsell_price_cents` into recognized revenue, so the outing's `gross_revenue_cents` and every figure downstream of it are recomputed; and it cascades the unit from `RESERVED` to `COMMITTED`, decrementing `reserved_count` by one and incrementing `committed_count` by one.

From `COMMITTED` the fork opens. The artwork path runs `COMMITTED → ARTWORK_APPROVED → INSTALLED → VERIFIED`. Approving the artwork records the approving user's `X-User-ID` as `artwork_approved_by_user_id`. Installing is a bare acknowledgement that the sign went up. Verifying is the segregation-of-duties point: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `artwork_approved_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and on success the acting user is recorded as `verified_by_user_id`. Verification also moves this allocation's occupancy from `committed_count` into `fulfilled_count` and re-derives the unit's posture, which reaches `FULFILLED` once no reserved or committed allocation remains on the unit and at least one fulfilled one does. Both artwork approval and verification are subject to the unit's `fulfillment_due_at`: they must be performed at or before that instant as read from the logical clock, and a later attempt is refused with `ERR_FULFILLMENT_WINDOW_ELAPSED`.

The waiver path is the other branch of the fork, and it is available from `COMMITTED`, from `ARTWORK_APPROVED`, and from `INSTALLED` with identical effect from all three origins. Waiving requires a non-empty `waived_reason` explaining why the obligation could not be met, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On acceptance the sponsor is credited for the undelivered benefit: the allocation's `fulfillment_credit_cents` is set to 0.60 of the unit's `list_price_cents` — sixty percent — rounded half up to whole cents. That credit then rolls up in three hops — it re-derives `fulfillment_credit_cents` on the linked registration as the sum of that registration's waived allocations, which re-derives the outing's `fulfillment_credit_cents` as the sum over all its waived allocations, which enters the outing's `direct_costs_cents` only under the final proceeds treatment, after which every downstream finance figure is recomputed. Like verification, waiving moves the occupancy from `committed_count` into `fulfilled_count` and re-derives the unit's posture. `VERIFIED` and `WAIVED` are both terminal.

Releasing is the compensating action, and it is deliberately available from every non-terminal fulfillment state — `RESERVED`, `COMMITTED`, `ARTWORK_APPROVED`, and `INSTALLED` — with identical effect from each, so the conservation identity of §2.10 can be exercised after a three-step forward walk. It is invoked either by the `SponsorshipManager` directly or by the system as step 3 of a registration cancellation. A release decrements the occupancy counter the allocation was sitting in, re-derives the unit's `remaining_capacity` and posture, and re-derives the outing's `reserved_asset_slot_count`. When the released allocation's source was an upsell and it had already reached `COMMITTED`, its `upsell_price_cents` is removed from the outing's `gross_revenue_cents` and the finance figures are recomputed. A unit whose last committed allocation is released while a reserved one remains falls back `COMMITTED → RESERVED`; a unit that loses its last occupying allocation of any kind falls back to `AVAILABLE`, with `remaining_capacity` re-derived to the full declared `capacity` and the outing's aggregate counter reduced accordingly. `RELEASED` is terminal for the allocation.

### 3.5. Drawing the Pairings: the Tee Sheet, the Waves and the Carts

Placing a foursome on the course is a single write, performed by a `TournamentDirector` or by `CourseOperations`, and it is the one operation in this system that is explicitly designed to be repeated. Writing a tee assignment supersedes whatever pairing the team held before, which is why the request carries the mandatory `expected_team_version` guard of §2.7 rather than relying on a state precondition. Beyond that guard the payload carries four required values: the `wave` the group starts in, either `A` or `B`; the `starting_hole`, between 1 and 18 inclusive; the `tee_time` at which the group takes its first swing; and the `cart_number` issued to the group.

Four business checks then run, in this order, and the order is what determines which error a badly formed assignment reports:

1. **The tee-sheet slot.** For a given outing and a given `starting_hole`, the interval running from a group's `tee_time` up to but not including that time plus the contract's `tee_interval_minutes` may hold only one team. The interval is half-open, so a group teeing off at exactly the instant the previous group's interval ends is accepted; any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_TEE_SLOT_CONFLICT`. With the default ten-minute interval, groups off the same hole at 08:00 and 08:10 coexist while 08:00 and 08:09 do not.
2. **The cart turnaround.** A cart may be reused across waves, but it needs tread time. When a `cart_number` is reissued to a group in a different wave, the previous holder's wave must end at least 30 minutes before the new holder's wave starts. Separately, a cart may never be issued less than 15 minutes before the start of its own wave, as read from the logical clock. Either failure is `ERR_CART_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.
3. **The wave window.** A group's `tee_time` must fall inside the window of the wave it was assigned to — between `wave_a_start` and `wave_a_end` inclusive for wave `A`, and between `wave_b_start` and `wave_b_end` inclusive for wave `B`. Additionally, when the outing's `start_format` is `SHOTGUN`, every group in a wave shares that wave's start instant exactly, so a shotgun group given any `tee_time` other than its wave's start is refused. That strict equality applies only to `SHOTGUN`; under `WAVE_SPLIT` and `TEE_TIME` a group's `tee_time` need only fall inside its wave's window, with `WAVE_SPLIT` sending each wave out across its own holes and `TEE_TIME` issuing sequential slots off a single hole. Any violation is `ERR_WAVE_WINDOW_VIOLATION`.
4. **The cart allowance.** The requested `cart_number` may not exceed the contract's `cart_allowance_count`, and the number of distinct `cart_number` values assigned within a single wave may not exceed that allowance either. Either failure is `ERR_CART_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`.

An accepted assignment writes the team's `wave`, `starting_hole`, `tee_time`, and `cart_number`; sets `slot_occupancy_start` equal to the `tee_time` and `slot_occupancy_end` to the `tee_time` advanced by `tee_interval_minutes`, which is the exclusive end of the occupancy window; increments `tee_assignment_count` by one; and increments the team's `version`, which is precisely what supersedes any client still holding the pre-write version. Starting play requires that every team of the outing has been through this at least once, as §3.1 states.

The **pairing sheet** is a computed read available to a `TournamentDirector`, to `CourseOperations`, and to a `Participant`. It returns every team of the outing ordered by `wave`, then by `starting_hole`, then by `tee_time`, then by `created_at`, each row carrying its cart number and its playing handicap. It accepts an optional `wave` filter alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`. Because a postponement clears the placement fields on every team while preserving `tee_assignment_count`, a pairing sheet read after a postponement shows an unplaced field even for groups that had been placed twice.

### 3.6. Skill Contests, Prize Insurance and Amateur Status

A `TournamentDirector` stages a **Contest** on a hole by naming the `outing_id`, the `contest_type` — one of `HOLE_IN_ONE`, `LONGEST_DRIVE`, `CLOSEST_TO_PIN`, or `PUTTING` — the `hole_number` between 1 and 18 inclusive, and the advertised `prize_value_cents`. Two inputs are optional: the measured `yardage` of the contest hole, defaulting to null, and the `prize_form` in which the advertised prize is paid, either `CASH` or `MERCHANDISE_CREDIT` and defaulting to `MERCHANDISE_CREDIT`. At most one non-voided contest may occupy any given `hole_number` within an outing; a second is refused with `ERR_CONTEST_HOLE_CONFLICT`. A new contest sits at `PLANNED` on its lifecycle dimension and at `NONE` on its insurance dimension, with `insurance_carrier` null, `insurance_premium_cents` 0, `insurance_coverage_cap_cents` 0, `insurance_min_yardage` 165, `insurance_required_witness_count` 2, `insurance_bound_at` null, `winner_player_id` null, `witness_count` 0, `award_form` null, `awarded_value_cents` 0, `insurance_recovery_cents` 0, `contest_payout_cents` 0, and `claim_denial_reason` at `NONE`.

Creation also derives one flag the client cannot set. `requires_insurance` is true exactly when the `contest_type` is `HOLE_IN_ONE` **and** the `prize_value_cents` is at least 500000 cents — that is, an advertised ace prize worth five thousand dollars or more — and false in every other case, including a five-figure longest-drive prize. Contests can be listed filtered by outing, by `winner_player_id`, by `contest_type`, and by either state dimension independently.

Binding the policy is the `SponsorshipManager`'s job and it drives the insurance dimension from `NONE` to `BOUND`. The request supplies the `insurance_carrier`, the `insurance_premium_cents` paid to bind the policy, and the `insurance_coverage_cap_cents` the carrier will reimburse at most, plus optionally an `insurance_min_yardage` (defaulting to 165 yards) and an `insurance_required_witness_count` (defaulting to 2). The contest's lifecycle dimension must be `PLANNED` or `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and the coverage cap must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Binding stamps `insurance_bound_at` from the logical clock, adds the premium to the outing's `insurance_cost_cents`, and recomputes the finance figures. It advances the contest's `version` without touching the lifecycle dimension at all — the two dimensions are independent in exactly this sense.

The lifecycle dimension is driven by the director through three actions — `OPEN`, `CLOSE`, and `VOID`. Opening a contest moves it `PLANNED → OPEN` and requires the outing's play dimension to be `OPEN` or `FIELD_LOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; it additionally requires that either `requires_insurance` is false or the insurance dimension already reads `BOUND`, otherwise `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_BOUND`. That single rule is what stops a five-thousand-dollar ace prize from being advertised uninsured. Closing moves the contest `OPEN → CLOSED` and requires the outing's play dimension to be `IN_PLAY` or `SCORED`. Voiding is available from `PLANNED`, from `OPEN`, and from `CLOSED` with identical effect from all three: the contest becomes `VOID`, and when its insurance dimension reads `BOUND` that dimension moves to `EXPIRED` — the premium already paid stays a direct cost of the outing, because the carrier does not refund it. `AWARDED` and `VOID` are the terminal lifecycle states; `CLAIM_PAID`, `CLAIM_DENIED`, and `EXPIRED` are terminal on the insurance dimension.

**Awarding a closed contest** is the busiest single request in the system, because it settles the prize, adjudicates the insurance claim, and moves both the finance rollup and a second state dimension in one atomic step. The director supplies the `winner_player_id` and optionally the `witness_count` attesting the winning shot. The named player must resolve, otherwise `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_FOUND`, and their registration must belong to this contest's outing, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_OUTING_REFERENCE`. The transition is `CLOSED → AWARDED`.

Amateur status governs the *form* of the award, never the amount. When the winner's `amateur_status` is `AMATEUR` and the contest's `prize_form` is `CASH`, the advertised value must not exceed 75000 cents — seven hundred and fifty dollars, the amateur cash ceiling — and a larger cash prize is refused outright with `ERR_AMATEUR_CASH_CAP_EXCEEDED`; within the ceiling the `award_form` resolves to `CASH`. When the winner is an `AMATEUR` and the contest's `prize_form` is not cash, the `award_form` resolves to `MERCHANDISE_CREDIT` — the non-cash form is converted rather than rejected. When the winner's `amateur_status` is `PROFESSIONAL`, the `award_form` is simply the contest's declared `prize_form`, because a professional may take cash without jeopardy. In every case `awarded_value_cents` is set to the contest's `prize_value_cents`, and the supplied `witness_count` is recorded.

The insurance claim is then adjudicated, and only for a hole-in-one contest. When the `contest_type` is `HOLE_IN_ONE` and the insurance dimension reads `BOUND`, three tests run in a fixed order and the **first** failing test names the denial:

- if the contest's `yardage` is below `insurance_min_yardage`, the claim is denied and `claim_denial_reason` becomes `YARDAGE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The comparison is inclusive on the policy's side: a hole measured at exactly the minimum yardage is covered.
- otherwise, if the recorded `witness_count` is below `insurance_required_witness_count`, the claim is denied and `claim_denial_reason` becomes `INSUFFICIENT_WITNESSES`.
- otherwise, if `awarded_value_cents` exceeds `insurance_coverage_cap_cents`, the claim is denied and `claim_denial_reason` becomes `COVERAGE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
- otherwise the claim is paid: the insurance dimension moves `BOUND → CLAIM_PAID`, `insurance_recovery_cents` is set to the smaller of `awarded_value_cents` and `insurance_coverage_cap_cents`, and `claim_denial_reason` is left at `NONE`.

A denial moves the insurance dimension `BOUND → CLAIM_DENIED` and sets `insurance_recovery_cents` to 0. A hole-in-one contest awarded with no policy bound at all is not an error — it simply records `claim_denial_reason` as `NO_POLICY`, leaves the insurance dimension where it stands, and recovers nothing. A contest of any other type recovers nothing and records no denial reason. Finally, `contest_payout_cents` is set to `awarded_value_cents` minus `insurance_recovery_cents` — the uninsured share the outing actually absorbs — and that fresh figure recomputes the outing's `prize_cost_cents` and every finance figure downstream of it.

### 3.7. Auctions, Raffles, Donations and the Charitable Receipt

The `CharityTreasurer` lists each fundraising opportunity as a **FundraisingItem**, naming the `outing_id`, the `item_type` — `AUCTION` for a silent-auction lot, `RAFFLE` for a raffle purchase, or `DONATION` for an outright appeal — and a `description` of the lot or appeal. Two money terms are optional: `fmv_cents`, the fair market value of whatever the buyer receives, which is the quid-pro-quo benefit on an auction lot and defaults to 0; and `ask_cents`, the reserve an offer must reach, likewise defaulting to 0. A listed item starts at `LISTED` with `buyer_registration_id` null, `bid_cents` 0, `collected_cents` 0, `recorded_by_user_id` null, `sold_at` null, and `void_count` 0. Items can be listed filtered by outing, by `buyer_registration_id`, by `item_type`, and by status.

Recording the sale — which a `CharityTreasurer` or a `SponsorshipManager` may do — moves the item `LISTED → SOLD`. The request names the `buyer_registration_id` of the buyer or donor and the `bid_cents` actually offered or pledged. The named registration must resolve, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, and must belong to this item's outing, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_OUTING_REFERENCE`; the outing's play dimension must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_CLOSED`. The offer must reach the reserve: `bid_cents` must be greater than or equal to `ask_cents`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BID` (HTTP 402). That rule is applied uniformly — an auction bid below the ask, a raffle purchase below the ask, and a pledge below the ask are all refused on the same rule, even though only the auction case reads naturally as a "bid". A recorded sale stores the `bid_cents` and the `buyer_registration_id`, stamps `sold_at` from the logical clock, and stamps the acting `X-User-ID` into `recorded_by_user_id` — which, by §2.9, permanently bars that user from issuing this item's receipt.

Collecting the cash moves the item `SOLD → PAID`. The payment request carries a single `amount_cents`, which must equal the recorded `bid_cents` exactly, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. On acceptance `collected_cents` is set to that amount and added to the outing's `gross_revenue_cents`, and the rest of the rollup is recomputed.

A retracted bid is handled by the item's lifecycle operation, whose `action` is either `VOID` or `RELIST`. Voiding moves the item `SOLD → VOID`. Voiding clears `buyer_registration_id`, `bid_cents`, and `recorded_by_user_id`, reverses any collected revenue out of the rollup, and increments `void_count` by one; the request may carry a `void_reason`. Relisting then returns the lot `VOID → LISTED`, closing the sold-void-relist cycle so the same lot can be offered again to a different buyer. The item's terminal state is `RECEIPTED`, which it reaches from `PAID` only as a cascade of receipt issuance.

**Charitable receipts** are the compliance artifact the beneficiary's auditors will read, and they are append-only in the strongest sense: a `CharitableReceipt` carries no `updated_at` and no `version`, no endpoint mutates one, and the only correction mechanism is to append a reversal. Only the `CharityTreasurer` may issue one. The request names the `outing_id` and the `receipt_kind` — `REGISTRATION`, `FUNDRAISING_ITEM`, or `REVERSAL` — plus exactly one subject. For kind `REGISTRATION` the `registration_id` is set and `fundraising_item_id` is null; for kind `FUNDRAISING_ITEM` the `fundraising_item_id` is set and `registration_id` is null; for kind `REVERSAL` the `reverses_receipt_id` alone is set and both of the others are null. Naming zero subjects, naming more than one, or naming a subject that does not match the declared `receipt_kind` is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_SUBJECT_AMBIGUOUS` (HTTP 422). At most one un-reversed forward receipt may exist per subject — per registration and per fundraising item — and a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECEIPT`. No receipt at all may be issued for a `RAFFLE` item, whatever the amount collected, because a raffle ticket buys a chance rather than making a gift; that request is refused with `ERR_RAFFLE_NOT_DEDUCTIBLE` (HTTP 422). And per §2.9, the user who recorded the item's sale may not issue its receipt.

Each accepted receipt is assigned a `receipt_number`, a per-outing sequential string in the form `GR-0001` assigned in `created_at` ascending order, so the first receipt of an outing reads `GR-0001`, the second `GR-0002`, and so on. The acting user is stamped into `issued_by_user_id` and the logical clock into `issued_at`. Issuing a receipt against a `PAID` fundraising item cascades that item `PAID → RECEIPTED`, and issuing one against a registration sets that registration's `receipt_issued` flag to true.

**Computing the deductible amount** is where the charitable rules live, and the three receipt kinds compute it differently. For a receipt of kind `REGISTRATION`, the `payment_cents` recorded on the receipt is the registration's `amount_paid_cents` minus its `amount_refunded_cents`, and the declared benefit is the `gsv_cents` of the tier the registration currently holds. The token-benefit rule then decides whether that benefit counts at all: a token ceiling is computed as the smaller of 1300 cents — thirteen dollars — and two percent of the receipt's `payment_cents` rounded half up to whole cents. If the declared benefit is less than or equal to that ceiling it is disregarded entirely and the receipt's `gsv_cents` is recorded as 0; only a benefit strictly above the ceiling is carried through at its declared value. A benefit sitting exactly on the ceiling is disregarded, because the comparison is inclusive. The `deductible_amount_cents` is then the `payment_cents` minus the applicable `gsv_cents`, floored at 0 so it is never negative.

For a receipt of kind `FUNDRAISING_ITEM` the `payment_cents` is the item's `collected_cents`, and the treatment depends on the channel. A `RAFFLE` item is refused as described above. For an `AUCTION` lot the receipt's `gsv_cents` is the item's `fmv_cents` — the token-benefit rule deliberately does not apply to auction lots, because the buyer received a specific thing of value — and the `deductible_amount_cents` is the `payment_cents` minus that fair market value, floored at 0. For a `DONATION` the `gsv_cents` is 0 and the whole `payment_cents` is deductible.

A receipt of kind `REVERSAL` mirrors the row it reverses with every sign flipped: its `payment_cents` is the negation of the original's `payment_cents`, its `gsv_cents` the negation of the original's `gsv_cents`, and its `deductible_amount_cents` the negation of the original's `deductible_amount_cents`. A reversal is created either by the treasurer through the dedicated reversal capability — which may carry a `reversal_reason` — or automatically as step 8 of the registration cancellation cascade. A reversal may never itself be reversed, and a receipt that already carries a live reversal may not be reversed a second time; both are refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_IMMUTABLE`. Appending a reversal clears the subject registration's `receipt_issued` flag back to false and returns a `RECEIPTED` fundraising item to `PAID`, which is what makes the outing eligible for a corrected receipt.

The outing's `receipted_deductible_total_cents` is the signed sum of `deductible_amount_cents` over every receipt of the outing, reversals included — which is why a forward receipt followed by its reversal returns the total to exactly the value it held before the forward receipt was issued. Receipts can be listed filtered by outing, by `registration_id`, by `fundraising_item_id`, by `reverses_receipt_id`, and by `receipt_kind`.

### 3.8. Scoring, Matching Cards and Prize Distribution

Scores are posted per team by a `TournamentDirector` or by `CourseOperations`, and the outing's play dimension must be `IN_PLAY`. Each posting supplies five gross figures: the team's 18-hole `gross_score`, the `gross_back_9` strokes taken on holes 10 through 18, the `gross_back_6` on holes 13 through 18, the `gross_back_3` on holes 16 through 18, and the `gross_hole_18` taken on the final hole alone. The posting immediately derives the team's net figures from its current `team_playing_handicap` and stamps `score_posted_at` from the logical clock.

The net figures are the gross figures less a fraction of the team's playing handicap, and the fraction differs per metric because each metric covers a different share of the round:

- `net_score` is the `gross_score` less the whole `team_playing_handicap`, rounded half up to one decimal place.
- `net_back_9` is the `gross_back_9` less half the `team_playing_handicap`, rounded half up to two decimal places.
- `net_back_6` is the `gross_back_6` less a third of the `team_playing_handicap`, rounded half up to two decimal places.
- `net_back_3` is the `gross_back_3` less a sixth of the `team_playing_handicap`, rounded half up to two decimal places.
- `net_hole_18` is the `gross_hole_18` less an eighteenth of the `team_playing_handicap`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Because those five figures are derived from the playing handicap, anything that rewrites the playing handicap rewrites them too: a reopen of the field, a contract amendment that changed a rating field, and a cancellation that removed a player from the team all recompute all five for every team that has already posted a gross score.

**Finalizing the scoring** moves the outing `IN_PLAY → SCORED`, and it has three gates. Every team of the outing must carry a non-null `gross_score`, `gross_back_9`, `gross_back_6`, `gross_back_3`, and `gross_hole_18`, and every contest that has not been voided must have left `OPEN`; a failure of either is `ERR_MISSING_SCORES`. Separately, the finalization enforces an aggregate funding check that no individual prize decision could catch: the outing's `prize_pool_cents` plus the sum of the settled `contest_payout_cents` across its contests may not exceed `gross_revenue_cents` minus `refunded_cents`. If it does, the finalization is refused with `ERR_PRIZE_POOL_UNFUNDED` even though every single prize was within its own limit.

On acceptance the server recomputes every team's `net_score` and four net tiebreak metrics, then ranks the field. **Ranking** applies a seven-key ordering strictly in this sequence, and it is total, so every team receives a distinct `rank_position` numbered from 1:

1. `net_score` ascending — the lowest net score leads.
2. `net_back_9` ascending — the USGA matching-cards convention.
3. `net_back_6` ascending.
4. `net_back_3` ascending.
5. `net_hole_18` ascending.
6. `team_playing_handicap` ascending — the lower-handicap team takes a dead heat.
7. the team's `created_at` ascending, as the final deterministic fallback. A team's `id` is never consulted.

Because a team with a null gross score blocks the transition entirely, the ranked set is always the full field.

**Prize distribution** then runs, immediately after every `rank_position` has been written. Eligibility comes first: a team's `prize_eligible` flag reads false when any player currently linked to it carries `amateur_status` `PROFESSIONAL`, because the placement pool pays cash and a professional on the card disqualifies the group; otherwise it reads true. The eligible teams are then taken in `rank_position` ascending order and paid from an ordered share vector of 0.50, 0.30, and 0.20 — the top three shares of the `prize_pool_cents`. The waterfall is over the *eligible* list, not over the raw ranking, so an ineligible team does not consume a share: if the second-placed team is ineligible, the third-placed eligible team takes the 0.30 share.

The first eligible team's `prize_award_cents` is 0.50 of `prize_pool_cents` rounded half up to whole cents, and the second eligible team's is 0.30 of `prize_pool_cents` rounded half up to whole cents. The third eligible team is paid the residual instead of a rounded percentage — the `prize_pool_cents` minus everything already awarded — so that when three or more teams are eligible the awarded total never drifts from the pool by a rounding cent. Every team beyond the third eligible one, and every ineligible team, keeps `prize_award_cents` at 0. When fewer than three teams are eligible only the shares that exist are funded, and no residual is paid out. When no team is eligible at all, nothing is awarded.

Whatever is not awarded lands in the outing's `unclaimed_prize_cents`, computed as `prize_pool_cents` minus the sum of `prize_award_cents` across all teams — so an outing whose eligible field is empty reports its entire pool as unclaimed. The finalization closes by recomputing the outing's whole finance rollup under its current `proceeds_variant`, which is what carries the newly written prize awards into the cost figures.

### 3.9. Reconciliation, the Net-Proceeds Rollup and Remittance to the Beneficiary

Every figure the beneficiary ever sees comes out of one rollup, and that rollup is recomputed in full — never incrementally patched — on every event that could move any of its inputs. Those events are: the finalization of scoring, every finance transition, a registration payment, a tier amendment, a registration cancellation, a sponsor allocation being committed, waived, or released, a fundraising payment, a contest insurance binding, a contest award, a contract amendment that touched a money term, and the rain-credit cascade of a postponement. The rollup runs in seven steps, in this order.

**Step 1 — gross revenue.** `gross_revenue_cents` is the sum of three streams: the `amount_paid_cents` of every registration whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN`; plus the `upsell_price_cents` of every sponsor allocation whose status is `COMMITTED`, `ARTWORK_APPROVED`, `INSTALLED`, `VERIFIED`, or `WAIVED`; plus the `collected_cents` of every fundraising item whose status is `PAID` or `RECEIPTED`. A registration still pending payment, waitlisted, cancelled, or refunded contributes nothing here, and neither does an allocation still merely reserved or already released.

**Step 2 — refunds.** `refunded_cents` is the sum of `amount_refunded_cents` over **every** registration of the outing, whatever its status. This is deliberately not filtered: a cancelled registration's refund must stay visible in the rollup even though its payment has dropped out of step 1.

**Step 3 — the course charge.** `course_charge_cents` is the larger of the billable player count and the contract's `guaranteed_player_count`, multiplied by the contract's `rate_per_player_cents`. The guarantee is a floor, so a poorly attended outing still pays for the players it promised.

**Step 4 — the food and beverage minimum.** The actual food-and-beverage spend credited is the billable player count multiplied by the contract's `fnb_per_player_cents`, with no guarantee floor applied here at all. `fnb_shortfall_cents` is then the contract's `fnb_minimum_cents` minus that credited spend, floored at 0 — the organizer owes the difference only when the field ate less than the minimum, and never earns anything back when it ate more.

**Step 5 — prize and insurance costs.** `insurance_cost_cents` is the sum of `insurance_premium_cents` over every contest of the outing whose insurance dimension reads `BOUND`, `CLAIM_PAID`, `CLAIM_DENIED`, or `EXPIRED` — note that an expired policy on a voided contest still costs its premium. `prize_cost_cents` depends on the proceeds treatment and is described below.

**Step 6 — direct costs.** `direct_costs_cents` is `course_charge_cents` plus `fnb_shortfall_cents` plus `insurance_cost_cents` plus `prize_cost_cents`, less the contract's `rain_credit_cents`, and — under the final treatment only — plus the outing's `fulfillment_credit_cents`.

**Step 7 — the administrative fee, in a pinned order.** `net_proceeds_before_fee_cents` is `gross_revenue_cents` minus `refunded_cents` minus `direct_costs_cents`. The fee *rate* is selected from that before-fee figure while the fee *base* is gross revenue, and the order is the contract: evaluating the tier against the after-fee net would be circular and is forbidden. `admin_fee_pct` is 0 when `net_proceeds_before_fee_cents` is zero or negative; otherwise 0.05 when it is below 2000000 cents (twenty thousand dollars); otherwise 0.035 when it is below 5000000 cents (fifty thousand dollars); otherwise 0.02. `admin_fee_cents` is then `admin_fee_pct` multiplied by `gross_revenue_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — the single rounded quantity in the entire rollup, rounded exactly once and only after the tier has been selected. `net_proceeds_cents` is `net_proceeds_before_fee_cents` minus `admin_fee_cents`.

**The two proceeds treatments.** The outing's `proceeds_variant` marker records which treatment last wrote the finance fields, and the two differ in exactly three places. Under `PROVISIONAL` — which is the state of a fresh outing, and the state an outing returns to after a finance reopen — the billable player count used by steps 3 and 4 is `confirmed_player_slots`, the slots actually sold; `prize_cost_cents` is the full `prize_pool_cents` plus the sum of `contest_payout_cents` across the outing's contests, because until the field is scored the whole pool is treated as payable; and the outing's `fulfillment_credit_cents` is **excluded** from direct costs. Under `FINAL` — written by reconciliation — the billable player count becomes `checked_in_player_count`, the turnout that actually walked the course, still floored by `guaranteed_player_count` in step 3 but with no floor applied in the step-4 food-and-beverage credit; `prize_cost_cents` becomes the sum of `prize_award_cents` actually written across the teams plus the sum of `contest_payout_cents`, so an unclaimed placement prize stops being a cost; and the outing's `fulfillment_credit_cents` **is** added to direct costs, because a waived sponsorship obligation is money the charity owes back.

The finance dimension is driven exclusively by the `CharityTreasurer` through three actions — `RECONCILE`, `REOPEN`, and `REMIT` — each of which carries the mandatory `expected_outing_version` guard described in §2.7 and each of which increments the outing's `version` on success.

**Reconciling** moves the finance dimension `COLLECTING → RECONCILED`. It requires that the play dimension has already reached `SCORED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — this is the single point at which one of the outing's two dimensions reads the other. On acceptance it switches `proceeds_variant` to `FINAL` and rewrites every finance field under the final treatment.

**Reopening** is the reverse edge, `RECONCILED → COLLECTING`, and it exists so that a late receipt reversal or a late correction can be absorbed. It switches `proceeds_variant` back to `PROVISIONAL` and rewrites the rollup under the provisional treatment. Unlike the play dimension's field reopen, the finance dimension carries no reopen cap whatsoever — it may be cycled as many times as the treasurer needs.

**Remitting** moves the finance dimension `RECONCILED → DISBURSED`, which is terminal, and it is the most heavily guarded transition in the system. The request carries a `remittance_cents` figure and a `remittance_reference` payment reference. Four conditions must hold, and when more than one of them fails the reported error follows the ladder of §2.6: the version guard is that ladder's tier-11 check and is therefore evaluated first, ahead of the three business conditions, which are themselves evaluated in the order stated here. Every registration of the outing whose status is `CONFIRMED`, `AMENDED`, or `CHECKED_IN` must have `receipt_issued` true, and every fundraising item of the outing sitting at `PAID` must have been receipted; an outstanding receipt anywhere refuses the remittance with `ERR_UNRECEIPTED_ITEMS_REMAIN`. The outing's `net_proceeds_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NEGATIVE_NET_PROCEEDS` — a charity is never asked to accept a loss. The supplied `remittance_cents` must equal the final-treatment `net_proceeds_cents` to the cent, otherwise `ERR_REMITTANCE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 402). And the version guard must match, otherwise `ERR_STALE_OUTING_VERSION`.

On acceptance the remittance is frozen: `beneficiary_remittance_cents` is set to the outing's `net_proceeds_cents` at that instant and `remittance_reference` records the supplied payment reference, so a later recomputation of the rollup can never silently rewrite what was actually paid. The same request settles the course: the outing's contract moves to `SETTLED` from either `SIGNED` or `AMENDED` with identical effect, and its `settled_charge_cents` is frozen as the outing's `course_charge_cents` plus its `fnb_shortfall_cents` less the contract's `rain_credit_cents`. `SETTLED` is terminal for the contract, alongside `VOIDED`.

The **beneficiary proceeds report** is a computed read available to the `CharityTreasurer`, the `TournamentDirector`, and the `Beneficiary`. It returns the whole rollup chain — gross revenue, refunds, each direct-cost component, the before-fee net, the administrative fee rate and amount, the net proceeds, the unclaimed prize money, and the receipted deductible total — together with the `proceeds_variant` that produced those figures, so the reader always knows whether they are looking at a mid-event estimate or the audited final number.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints below are mounted under the `/outing-ops` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint, which sits at the server root. Creation endpoints answer `201 Created`; every other successful call answers `200 OK`. Endpoints that drive a lifecycle take a discrete `action` value and return the updated resource. List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as query parameters along with the filters named in their descriptions, and report `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`. The Authorized Role(s) column is exhaustive: any other role receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and a request with no identity headers at all receives `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
