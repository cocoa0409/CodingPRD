# Warranty Reserve & Product Liability Accrual API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A durable-goods manufacturer carries two of the most judgement-heavy liabilities on its balance sheet: the accrued cost of honouring base warranty obligations on everything it has already shipped, and the reserve it holds against product-liability claims that have been asserted or that actuaries know are coming but have not yet been reported. Both numbers are estimates. Both are re-estimated every fiscal quarter as new claim history arrives, as engineering discovers new failure modes, as campaigns and recalls are launched, and as suppliers accept or refuse responsibility for the component defects that drove the claims in the first place. Today this work is done in spreadsheets that nobody can audit: the actual-versus-expected analysis that justifies a change in estimate lives in one workbook, the recall cost model in another, and the disclosure rollforward that ties them together is retyped by hand every close.

This service replaces that with a single, auditable ledger of record for warranty and product-liability accrual. It models the fiscal close as an explicit state machine, so that an accrual proposed by a warranty analyst cannot be posted by that same analyst, a period cannot be closed before its disclosure rollforward has been generated, and a liability reserve cannot be booked before an actuary has reviewed it. It models the estimation methods themselves as first-class, deterministic calculations — historical-claim-rate accrual for mature product families, an engineering/analog blend for new launches with thin history, a take-rate-weighted remedy cost model for campaigns and recalls, a four-step front-loaded curve for extended-service-contract revenue recognition, and a lag-factor IBNR model for product liability — so that any figure in the ledger can be reproduced from its source rows on demand.

The API is mounted under `/warranty`. Every monetary figure is an exact integer count of cents, every rate is an integer count of basis points or parts per million, and every derived figure is a full recompute from source rows rather than an incremental adjustment. That discipline is deliberate: the reserve rollforward disclosed to auditors must reconcile to the penny against the accruals, campaign accruals, changes in estimate, claims paid and supplier recoveries that produced it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting as. If either header is absent or carries the empty string, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). These two checks precede every other form of validation in the system.

The six roles are `WarrantyAnalyst`, `ReserveController`, `QualityEngineer`, `Actuary`, `SupplierRep` and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `WarrantyAnalyst` is the warranty engineering-finance analyst. The analyst maintains product and claim reference data, estimates and proposes accruals, runs adequacy analysis, sells and cancels extended service contracts, registers suppliers and asserts supplier recovery claims. A `ReserveController` sits in corporate accounting and owns the period calendar: the controller opens and closes fiscal periods, posts accruals, decides field actions, books liability reserves, closes recovery claims, applies adequacy true-ups and issues the rollforward disclosure. The controller is deliberately barred from authoring the estimates it approves. A `QualityEngineer` opens campaigns, recalls and bulletins, prices candidate remedy options, selects the remedy that will drive the accrual, submits the action for approval and drives field execution. An `Actuary` is a consulting actuary who creates and reviews product-liability reserves including the incurred-but-not-reported component, and reads the warranty ledger for context, but may not book its own review. A `SupplierRep` is a portal user acting on behalf of exactly one component supplier. A `PlatformAdmin` is an operations account that controls the server's logical clock and holds no business authority over reserves whatsoever.

Identity semantics differ between the supplier portal role and everything else. When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Supplier` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `SupplierRep` may act on a supplier recovery claim only when that claim's `supplier_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`, and when listing recovery claims the `supplier_id` filter must be supplied and must equal `X-User-ID`; any other read or write scope yields `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `WarrantyAnalyst`, `ReserveController`, `QualityEngineer`, `Actuary` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `proposed_by_user_id` and `posted_by_user_id` on a warranty accrual, as `applied_by_user_id` on an adequacy review, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a field action, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a liability reserve, and as `generated_by_user_id` on a rollforward snapshot — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`.

Attribution is not decoration. Three operations enforce **segregation of duties** by comparing the caller's `X-User-ID` against a previously recorded attribution field, and reject a match with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The full rule set is stated in §2.8.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. This same envelope carries the routing failures too: a request that resolves to no declared route answers `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 404, and a request naming a declared path with a method that path does not declare answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0, its minimum accepted value is 0, and it carries no maximum. A value outside those bounds, or a value that is not the plain decimal representation of an integer, is a request-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as applied to the query, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the returned page. Every filter named for a list endpoint is an optional query parameter matched by exact string equality against the named field; a filter supplied as the empty string is treated as absent, a filter value that matches no row — including a value outside the field's enum — simply yields an empty page rather than an error, and any query parameter an endpoint does not declare is ignored.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the service persists is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; the server mints it and returns it in the creation response. Alongside `id`, most resources carry three further system-managed properties. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written; it is read from the real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, and it is strictly increasing across rows, so ordering by `created_at` always reproduces insertion order. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, likewise read from the real wall clock, recording the most recent mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that only rewrite derived figures. Derived figures written as part of a creation are not mutations: a freshly created row reads `version` 1 and an `updated_at` equal to its `created_at` even when the create computed an exposure status, a maturity classification, an accrued amount, a recognition schedule, a per-unit remedy cost, an IBNR figure or a recognized recovery onto it.

Two resource kinds are deliberately narrower. A claim observation is an append-only historical fact and is never edited after it is written; it carries `id` and `created_at` only — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. A reserve rollforward snapshot is likewise an immutable disclosure artefact and carries `id` and `created_at` only, with neither `updated_at` nor `version` present. Every other resource — accrual periods, product families, production vintages, failure modes, warranty accruals, adequacy reviews, field actions, remedy options, service contracts, suppliers, supplier recoveries and liability reserves — carries the full set of four.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a **logical UTC clock** that, before any client has set it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`, which is the operation's only accepted field and is required; the target may lie before the clock's current position as well as after it, because no monotonicity is enforced. The operation returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object rather than a resource.

Moving the clock is not a passive act — it is the trigger for every time-dependent recomputation in the system, evaluated in this order. Each production vintage's `exposure_status` is recomputed against the new instant. Each product family's history-maturity classification is recomputed. Every extended service contract still in `ACTIVE` status has its `months_elapsed`, `recognized_revenue_cents` and `deferred_revenue_cents` recomputed at the new clock position, and any such contract whose recomputed `months_elapsed` has reached or passed its `term_months` auto-transitions from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`. Contracts are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

### 2.6. Integer Money, Basis Points and the Rounding Convention

No monetary figure in this system is ever a decimal. Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an exact integer count of cents. Every field whose name ends in `_bps` is an integer count of basis points, where 10000 basis points represent one whole unit, so 8000 basis points is 80 percent. Every field whose name ends in `_ppm` is an integer count of parts per million, so a rate of 250 parts per million means 250 claims per million units shipped. A labour figure named `labor_hours_centi` is an integer count of hundredths of an hour, so 150 means one and a half hours. No floating-point intermediate value is ever persisted.

Wherever a calculation in this document says a figure is **rounded**, exactly one rule applies: the exact rational value is rounded to the nearest integer, and a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero. Because a calculation's answer can depend on *where* in the sequence that rounding happens, every calculation description below pins its rounding order explicitly, and an implementation that rounds at a different point in the sequence will produce a different — and wrong — answer.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Period and Field-Action Versions

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a parent row. In each case the guard field is a required, non-optional member of the request body: it is not an opt-in, and a request that omits it is rejected as a request-shape violation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

First, `POST /warranty/accruals` must carry `expected_period_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `AccrualPeriod` named by `period_id`. If that value does not equal the period's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because another writer creating an accrual in the same period legitimately changes what the period's accrual set contains, and therefore changes what a duplicate check would see; a client whose view of the period is stale must re-read before it writes.

Second, `POST /warranty/periods/{periodId}/rollforward` must carry `expected_period_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of that same `AccrualPeriod`. A mismatch against the period's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A rollforward snapshot that has already been generated legitimately supersedes the client's view of the period's disclosure state, so a second generation request built on the earlier view carries a stale version and must be refused.

Third, `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/select-remedy` must carry `expected_field_action_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `FieldAction`. A mismatch against the field action's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FIELD_ACTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new remedy selection supersedes the prior selection and rewrites `specific_accrual_cents`, so the writer must prove it observed the selection currently in force.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these three must not be given one, and supplying an unknown field is itself an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` violation.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties in the Reserve Close

The reserve close is built on the principle that no single operator both authors an estimate and blesses it. Three checks enforce it, each comparing the caller's `X-User-ID` against a stored attribution field on the target row, and each rejecting an equal comparison with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Posting a warranty accrual is refused when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the accrual's `proposed_by_user_id`. Deciding a field action — whether the decision is approval or rejection — is refused when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the field action's `submitted_by_user_id`. Booking a product-liability reserve is refused when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the reserve's `reviewed_by_user_id`. The role boundaries reinforce the same intent: proposing an accrual is a `WarrantyAnalyst` capability while posting it is a `ReserveController` capability, submitting a field action is a `QualityEngineer` capability while deciding it is a `ReserveController` capability, and reviewing a liability reserve is an `Actuary` capability while booking it is a `ReserveController` capability.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, the response is determined by the **first** violated rule in the ordering below, and no later rule is evaluated. Implementations must honour this ladder exactly, because a request that is simultaneously (say) unauthorised, malformed and directed at a closed period must answer with the authorisation error and nothing else. Each rung names the scope it guards.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles — every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The acting role appears in the requested endpoint's authorised-role list — every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Supplier-portal scoping: when the acting role is `SupplierRep`, `X-User-ID` equals the `supplier_id` of the targeted recovery, and for list reads the `supplier_id` filter equals `X-User-ID` — guarding `POST /warranty/supplier-recoveries/{recoveryId}/respond`, `GET /warranty/supplier-recoveries/{recoveryId}` and `GET /warranty/supplier-recoveries` — otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On the paths that target one recovery by identifier the comparison needs a row to read a `supplier_id` from, so a `SupplierRep` naming a recovery identifier that resolves to nothing falls through to the 404 of rung 4 instead of answering `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; on the list read the filter comparison needs no row, so a `SupplierRep` that omits `supplier_id` or supplies a different one is refused here before anything else about the query is examined.
4. Every identifier embedded in the request path resolves to an existing row, validated strictly left to right, so that on a nested path the parent's absence is reported before the child is even looked for — guarding every endpoint whose path contains an id segment. The error is the one belonging to the first unresolvable segment: `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VINTAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAILURE_MODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADEQUACY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIELD_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECOVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIABILITY_RESERVE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ROLLFORWARD_NOT_FOUND` (all HTTP 404).
5. Request-shape conformance — every required field of the endpoint's input is present, no unknown field is present, and every value satisfies its declared type, enum member set and numeric range. This rung also carries the shape and ordering constraints stated with the fields themselves: `observation_period_code` matches the `YYYY-QN` shape, `months_in_service_to` is strictly greater than `months_in_service_from`, `period_end_date` is strictly after `period_start_date`, `affected_build_end_date` is the same as or later than `affected_build_start_date`, and every basis-point field lies between 0 and 10000 inclusive unless the owning entity declares a wider range. A request body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or is JSON that is not an object, is itself an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; an absent or empty body reads as the empty object, so an endpoint whose declared input is empty accepts no body field at all and rejects any that is supplied, while an endpoint all of whose fields are optional accepts an empty body happily. Integer-typed fields reject booleans and any non-integer number, and string-typed fields reject the empty string. Guards every creation and update endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. Every foreign-key identifier supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, and `observation_period_code` matches the `period_code` of an existing accrual period — guarding `POST /warranty/product-families`, `PATCH /warranty/product-families/{familyId}`, `POST /warranty/vintages`, `POST /warranty/failure-modes`, `POST /warranty/claim-observations`, `POST /warranty/accruals`, `POST /warranty/adequacy-reviews`, `POST /warranty/field-actions`, `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/remedy-options`, `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/select-remedy`, `POST /warranty/service-contracts`, `POST /warranty/supplier-recoveries` and `POST /warranty/liability-reserves`. The error is `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VINTAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAILURE_MODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIELD_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REMEDY_OPTION_NOT_FOUND` according to which reference failed (all HTTP 404).
7. A supplied `analog_family_id` is not the identifier of the family row being patched — guarding `PATCH /warranty/product-families/{familyId}` — otherwise `ERR_SELF_ANALOG_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422).
8. Code uniqueness within the declared scope — guarding `POST /warranty/periods`, `POST /warranty/product-families`, `POST /warranty/vintages`, `POST /warranty/failure-modes`, `POST /warranty/field-actions`, `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/remedy-options`, `POST /warranty/service-contracts` and `POST /warranty/suppliers` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409). The scopes are: `period_code` unique across all accrual periods, `family_code` unique across all product families, `action_code` unique across all field actions, `contract_number` unique across all service contracts, `supplier_code` unique across all suppliers, `vintage_code` unique within its `family_id`, `mode_code` unique within its `family_id`, and `option_code` unique within its `field_action_id`.
9. `expected_period_version` equals the current period `version` — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals` and `POST /warranty/periods/{periodId}/rollforward` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
10. `expected_field_action_version` equals the current field-action `version` — guarding `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/select-remedy` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_FIELD_ACTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
11. The accrual period that scopes the mutation — whether named directly by `period_id` in the body, inherited from the target row's `period_id`, or resolved through `observation_period_code` — does not have status `CLOSED`. This guards `POST /warranty/claim-observations`, `POST /warranty/accruals`, `POST /warranty/accruals/{accrualId}/propose`, `POST /warranty/accruals/{accrualId}/revise`, `POST /warranty/accruals/{accrualId}/post`, `POST /warranty/adequacy-reviews`, `POST /warranty/adequacy-reviews/{reviewId}/apply-true-up`, `POST /warranty/liability-reserves`, `POST /warranty/liability-reserves/{reserveId}/actuarial-decision`, `POST /warranty/liability-reserves/{reserveId}/book` and `POST /warranty/periods/{periodId}/rollforward` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).
12. The target row occupies a state from which the requested transition is declared, which includes every cross-entity state precondition and every terminal-state lockdown — guarding every state-transition endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Specifically this rung covers: the scoping period being `ANALYSIS_LOCKED` when an accrual is posted; the scoping period being `ANALYSIS_LOCKED` or `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` when a liability reserve is reviewed; the scoping period being `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` when a liability reserve is booked; a field action having a non-null `selected_remedy_option_id` when it is submitted; and the lockdown of the terminal states `SUPERSEDED` on a warranty accrual, `SETTLED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` on a supplier recovery, `BOOKED` on a liability reserve, `CLOSED` on an accrual period, and `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` on a service contract.
13. No warranty accrual of the period remains in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` — guarding `POST /warranty/periods/{periodId}/open-actuarial-review` — otherwise `ERR_UNPOSTED_ACCRUALS_REMAIN` (HTTP 409).
14. The period's `rollforward_generated` is already true — guarding `POST /warranty/periods/{periodId}/close` — otherwise `ERR_ROLLFORWARD_MISSING` (HTTP 409).
15. Every liability reserve of the period has status `BOOKED` — guarding `POST /warranty/periods/{periodId}/close` — otherwise `ERR_LIABILITY_RESERVE_NOT_BOOKED` (HTTP 409).
16. The field action's `approval_state` is `APPROVED` before its execution dimension may leave `NOT_STARTED` — guarding `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/execution-progress` — otherwise `ERR_EXECUTION_BEFORE_APPROVAL` (HTTP 409).
17. Safety-recall remedy adequacy: when a field action's `is_safety_recall` is true, the remedy option being selected — whether named explicitly or chosen by the server-side selector — has `remedy_class` equal to `PERMANENT_REPAIR`, and the option referenced by `selected_remedy_option_id` still has `remedy_class` equal to `PERMANENT_REPAIR` at submit time. This guards `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/select-remedy` and `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/submit` — otherwise `ERR_SAFETY_REMEDY_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).
18. When a field action's `is_safety_recall` is true, its `regulatory_notice_filed` is true before an approving decision may be recorded — guarding `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/decision` — otherwise `ERR_REGULATORY_NOTICE_MISSING` (HTTP 422).
19. Segregation of duties as described in §2.8 — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals/{accrualId}/post`, `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/decision` and `POST /warranty/liability-reserves/{reserveId}/book` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
20. An explicitly supplied `remedy_option_id` belongs to the field action named in the path — guarding `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/select-remedy` — otherwise `ERR_REMEDY_OPTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Note that rung 17 is evaluated before this rung: a safety recall pointed at a non-permanent remedy that also belongs to a different field action answers with `ERR_SAFETY_REMEDY_REQUIRED`.
21. At least one failure mode exists for the family of the vintage being accrued — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals` — otherwise `ERR_NO_FAILURE_MODES_DEFINED` (HTTP 422).
22. When the accrual `method` is `ANALOG_BLEND`, the vintage's family has both a non-null `analog_family_id` and a non-null `engineering_estimate_rate_ppm` — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals` — otherwise `ERR_ANALOG_BASIS_MISSING` (HTTP 422).
23. When a supplier recovery carries a non-null `field_action_id`, that field action's `approval_state` is `APPROVED` — guarding `POST /warranty/supplier-recoveries/{recoveryId}/assert` — otherwise `ERR_FIELD_ACTION_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422).
24. When the accrual `method` is `HISTORICAL_RATE`, the vintage's family has `history_maturity_status` equal to `MATURE` — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLAIM_HISTORY` (HTTP 422).
25. The referenced vintage has `exposure_status` equal to `ACTIVE` — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals` — otherwise `ERR_VINTAGE_EXPOSURE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422).
26. No warranty accrual already exists for the same period-and-vintage pair in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCRUAL` (HTTP 409).
27. No liability reserve already exists for the same period-and-family pair in `DRAFT`, `ACTUARY_REVIEWED` or `BOOKED` — guarding `POST /warranty/liability-reserves` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_LIABILITY_RESERVE` (HTTP 409).
28. The referenced supplier's `collectability_rating` is not `IMPAIRED` — guarding `POST /warranty/supplier-recoveries/{recoveryId}/assert` — otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_IMPAIRED` (HTTP 422).
29. When `remedy_option_id` is omitted, the server-side selector's eligibility filter yields at least one candidate — guarding `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/select-remedy` — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REMEDY_OPTION` (HTTP 422).
30. The booking-period selector finds at least one accrual period in status `OPEN` or `ANALYSIS_LOCKED` — guarding `POST /warranty/field-actions/{fieldActionId}/decision` and `POST /warranty/supplier-recoveries/{recoveryId}/close` — otherwise `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD` (HTTP 422).
31. The adequacy analysis produces an expected-to-date base strictly greater than zero — guarding `POST /warranty/adequacy-reviews` — otherwise `ERR_NO_EXPECTED_BASE` (HTTP 422).
32. Development-bucket non-overlap on claim observations — guarding `POST /warranty/claim-observations` — otherwise `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The geometry is spelled out in §3.2.
33. Buffered build-window non-conflict on field actions — guarding `POST /warranty/field-actions` — otherwise `ERR_FIELD_ACTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The geometry is spelled out in §3.5.
34. A service contract's `sold_date` is the same as or later than the covered family's `launch_date` — guarding `POST /warranty/service-contracts` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_PRECEDES_LAUNCH` (HTTP 422).
35. When a recovery is closed as settled, the supplied `settled_amount_cents` does not exceed the recovery's `asserted_amount_cents` — guarding `POST /warranty/supplier-recoveries/{recoveryId}/close` — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_ASSERTED` (HTTP 422).
36. The reserve ceiling: the family reserve balance that would result from counting this accrual as posted does not exceed the family's `reserve_cap_cents` — guarding `POST /warranty/accruals/{accrualId}/post` — otherwise `ERR_RESERVE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

### 2.10. The Family Reserve Ledger and Governance Posture

Every product family carries a single running figure, `warranty_reserve_balance_cents`, that represents the warranty reserve the company holds for that family. It is never adjusted incrementally. On every trigger it is recomputed in full from four source aggregates, all scoped to the one family:

- the **posted accrual total** — the sum of `accrued_amount_cents` over every warranty accrual whose status is `POSTED` and whose vintage belongs to this family;
- the **approved campaign total** — the sum of `specific_accrual_cents` over every field action of this family whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED`;
- the **applied true-up total** — the sum of `recommended_true_up_cents` over every adequacy review of this family whose `true_up_applied` is true;
- the **completed execution total** — the sum of `executed_cost_cents` over every field action of this family whose `execution_state` is `COMPLETED`.

The balance is the posted accrual total plus the approved campaign total plus the applied true-up total, minus the completed execution total. The status filters are exhaustive and exclusive, and the exclusions matter as much as the inclusions: accruals in `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` contribute nothing; field actions in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing to the approved campaign total; adequacy reviews whose `true_up_applied` is false contribute nothing; and supplier recoveries never contribute at all, because a recovery is a separate receivable asset rather than an offset to the reserve. Because the applied true-up total is a sum of signed recommendations, a redundant reserve pulls the balance down and a deficient one pushes it up. The balance is recomputed when any accrual of the family moves from `PROPOSED` to `POSTED`, when any field action of the family moves from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`, when any field action of the family moves from `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED` on its execution dimension, and when an adequacy review of the family has its true-up applied.

Immediately after every single evaluation of that balance, the family's governance posture is re-derived. Take the balance, or zero if the balance is negative, whichever is greater; multiply by 10000; divide by the family's `reserve_cap_cents`; and round the result to the nearest integer number of basis points to obtain the utilisation ratio. If that ratio is at least 10000 basis points, `reserve_posture` becomes `CONSTRAINED`; otherwise, if it is at least 8000 basis points, `reserve_posture` becomes `ELEVATED`; otherwise it becomes `NORMAL`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a balance exactly equal to the cap yields `CONSTRAINED` and a balance exactly eighty percent of the cap yields `ELEVATED`. Because `reserve_cap_cents` is constrained to at least 1, the division is always defined. A freshly registered family sits at a balance of zero and therefore at `NORMAL`, and re-pointing the cap through a family update re-derives the posture against the new ceiling without touching the balance.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. Where they do, the cascade is part of the contract, not an implementation detail, and each affected row whose stored values the cascade actually moves experiences a real mutation: its `updated_at` is refreshed and, where the row carries one, its `version` increases by 1. The row an operation directly targets is always mutated in that sense; a row reached only by cascade or by recomputation is mutated only when the recomputation lands on a different figure than the row already held, so a family whose recomputed balance and posture are unchanged, a remedy option whose recomputed `per_unit_expected_cents` is unchanged, a supplier whose recomputed aggregates and rating are unchanged, and a vintage, family or service contract that a clock move leaves numerically identical all keep their existing `updated_at` and `version`. Cascades are single-pass and never iterate to a fixed point — a re-valuation triggered by a rating change does not itself trigger a second rating recomputation. Where a cascade touches a set of rows rather than one row, the rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and where a chain of consequences is described as numbered hops, the hops are evaluated in the stated order, because a later hop reads the value a previous hop wrote.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Fiscal Accrual Period Calendar

Everything the finance organisation does in this system is anchored to a fiscal **accrual period**. A `ReserveController` opens one by supplying a `period_code` in the form `YYYY-QN`, a `period_start_date` and a `period_end_date`, both as calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, with the end date strictly after the start date. That `YYYY-QN` form is a convention the caller is trusted to honour rather than a shape the server polices: `period_code` is validated only as a non-empty string, so a code departing from the form is accepted at face value. The `period_code` is unique across every period ever opened, and its lexical ordering is guaranteed to match its chronological ordering — a property the system relies on heavily when it needs to pick "the latest period" or "every period up to and including this one". A freshly opened period has `status` of `OPEN`, a `reopen_count` of 0, a `version` of 1, and a null `locked_at` and `closed_at`.

The period then walks a four-state cycle. Locking analysis moves it from `OPEN` to `ANALYSIS_LOCKED`, which is the point at which new claim observations stop being coded into the period; the transition requires the period to be in `OPEN`, stamps `locked_at` with the current logical-clock instant and advances the period's `version` by 1. Opening actuarial review moves it from `ANALYSIS_LOCKED` to `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` and advances `version` by 1, but only if the estimation work is genuinely finished: if even one warranty accrual of the period still sits in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED`, the request is refused with `ERR_UNPOSTED_ACCRUALS_REMAIN`.

Actuarial review can be handed back. Reopening analysis moves the period from `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` back to `ANALYSIS_LOCKED`, adds 1 to `reopen_count`, re-stamps `locked_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and advances `version` by 1 — and it drags the actuarial work back with it. Every product-liability reserve of that period sitting in `ACTUARY_REVIEWED` is returned to `DRAFT`, has 1 added to its `return_count`, and has its `reviewed_by_user_id` cleared to null. This cascade is conditional and narrow: reserves already in `BOOKED` are untouched, and reserves already in `DRAFT` are untouched, so a reopen after some reserves have been booked leaves those booked figures intact.

Closing the period moves it from `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` to `CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` with the current logical-clock instant and advances `version` by 1. Two guards protect the close, and they answer with distinct codes. The period's `rollforward_generated` flag must already be true, meaning at least one rollforward disclosure snapshot exists for the period; otherwise the close is refused with `ERR_ROLLFORWARD_MISSING`. And every product-liability reserve of the period must have reached `BOOKED`; a reserve still in `DRAFT` or in `ACTUARY_REVIEWED` refuses the close with `ERR_LIABILITY_RESERVE_NOT_BOOKED`. `CLOSED` is terminal — no transition leaves it, and any attempt to move a closed period answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Two workflows elsewhere in the system need to know which period a newly recognised obligation should be booked into, and both use the same **booking-period selector**. Its candidate set is every accrual period whose status is `OPEN` or `ANALYSIS_LOCKED`; periods in `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` and `CLOSED` are excluded outright. Candidates are ranked by `period_code` descending, so the chronologically latest still-bookable period wins; since `period_code` is unique a tie cannot arise, but were two rows ever to share a code, the earlier `created_at` would decide. When the candidate set is empty the calling operation fails with `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD` and nothing is persisted.

Reading a period back returns not only its dates and lifecycle fields but three computed disclosure counters. `rollforward_generated` is true exactly when at least one rollforward snapshot names this period. `significant_exposure_count` is the number of field actions whose `approved_period_id` is this period, whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED`, and whose `specific_accrual_cents` is at least 50,000,000 cents — that is, half a million dollars of campaign exposure or more. `requires_significant_exposure_disclosure` is true exactly when `significant_exposure_count` is 1 or greater. All three are derived at read time from whatever rows exist when the period is read, so a field action's approval is reflected in them on the very next read without the period row itself being written: approving a field action does not refresh the period's `updated_at` and does not advance its `version`.

### 3.2. Product Families, Production Vintages, Failure Modes and Claim History

The reference data that all estimation stands on is maintained by a `WarrantyAnalyst`. A **product family** is registered with a `family_code` unique across all families, a human-readable `name`, a `launch_date` giving the first customer shipment date, a `base_warranty_months` of at least 1 stating the length of base (non-extended) coverage, a `standard_cost_per_claim_cents` of at least 1 giving the family-level standard cost of remedying one warranty claim, and a `reserve_cap_cents` of at least 1 acting as the governance ceiling described in §2.10. Two further inputs are optional and exist only to support new-launch estimation: `engineering_estimate_rate_ppm`, which is either null or a non-negative engineering-estimated lifetime claim rate in claims per million units, and `analog_family_id`, which is either null or the identifier of an existing product family that is *not* the row's own identifier and whose observed lifetime claim rate will be borrowed as an analogue. Families can be listed filtered by `analog_family_id` or by `history_maturity_status`.

A family's engineering, analogue and governance attributes are revisable. An update accepts any of `name`, `base_warranty_months`, `standard_cost_per_claim_cents`, `engineering_estimate_rate_ppm`, `analog_family_id` and `reserve_cap_cents`, every one of them optional, so only the fields actually supplied are written and a body carrying none of them is legal and still counts as a mutation; `engineering_estimate_rate_ppm` and `analog_family_id` may be supplied as null to clear them, and a null `analog_family_id` skips both the existence check and the self-reference check. It re-derives the family's history maturity, re-derives its `reserve_posture` against whatever ceiling `reserve_cap_cents` now holds — without recomputing `warranty_reserve_balance_cents`, which the update never touches — and advances the family's `version` by 1. Pointing `analog_family_id` at the very row being updated is a circular reference and is refused with `ERR_SELF_ANALOG_REFERENCE`.

Two computed exposure metrics and a maturity classification live on the family. `observed_history_months` is 0 when no claim observation exists for any vintage of the family; otherwise it is the largest `months_in_service_to` found across all such observations. Because claim observations are append-only and carry no status of their own, every observation row counts. `observed_exposure_units` is the sum of `units_shipped` over every production vintage of the family whose `exposure_status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` — which, since those are the only two values the field takes, means every vintage of the family. `units_sold_lifetime` is an alias metric holding exactly the same number as `observed_exposure_units`; it exists because the product-liability model reads the family's unit exposure under that name. The family's `history_maturity_status` is then `MATURE` when `observed_history_months` is at least 12 **and** `observed_exposure_units` is at least 5000; in every other case it is `LIMITED`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so exactly one year of observed development on exactly five thousand shipped units already classifies as `MATURE`. This classification is re-derived on family registration, on family update, on vintage registration, on claim-observation append, and whenever the logical clock moves.

A **production vintage** is a manufacturing cohort — a model year, typically — and is registered against a family by supplying `family_id`, a `vintage_code` unique within that family, a `units_shipped` count of at least 1 that becomes the exposure denominator for every claim rate computed from the cohort, a `ship_period_code` naming the period in which the cohort shipped — conventionally in `YYYY-QN` form, but validated only as a non-empty string and never resolved against any existing accrual period — and a `warranty_expiry_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form giving the date base warranty coverage ends. The cohort's `exposure_status` is derived, never supplied: it is `ACTIVE` while the logical clock is strictly earlier than `warranty_expiry_date` and `EXPIRED` from that date onward. It is computed when the vintage is registered and recomputed for every vintage whenever the logical clock moves. Vintages can be listed filtered by `family_id` or by `exposure_status`.

A **failure mode** captures one way the family's design fails in the field. It is registered with `family_id`, a `mode_code` unique within that family, an engineering `description`, a `severity` that is one of `COSMETIC`, `FUNCTIONAL` or `SAFETY`, and a `cost_per_claim_cents` of at least 1 giving the average total cost of remedying one claim coded to this mode. An optional `supplier_id` names the component supplier whose defect drives the mode; it is left null when the root cause is in-house. That single field is what later makes supplier recovery possible at all. Failure modes can be listed filtered by `family_id` or by `supplier_id`.

Claim history arrives as **claim observations**, each an immutable statement that a given cohort, coded to a given failure mode, produced a given number of claims at a given cost within a given band of months in service. An observation is appended with `vintage_id`, `failure_mode_id`, an `observation_period_code` in `YYYY-QN` form naming the period in which those claims were paid (and which must match the `period_code` of an existing accrual period), a `months_in_service_from` of at least 0, a `months_in_service_to` strictly greater than `months_in_service_from`, a `claim_count` of at least 0 and a `paid_cost_cents` of at least 0 recording the cash actually paid. Appending an observation is barred when the period identified by `observation_period_code` is `CLOSED`, which answers `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`. The `failure_mode_id` need only resolve to an existing failure mode; the server never requires it to belong to the cohort's own family, so a cross-family pairing is accepted; such a row still counts towards the cohort's `paid_to_date_cents` and towards the family's `observed_history_months`, but it falls outside the per-failure-mode frequency loop of `HISTORICAL_RATE`, which enumerates only failure modes of the cohort's own family. Observations can be listed filtered by `vintage_id` or by `failure_mode_id`.

Development buckets must not double-count, and the geometry is **half-open**: the bucket spans from `months_in_service_from` inclusive to `months_in_service_to` exclusive. A new bucket must not intersect the bucket of any existing observation sharing the same cohort *and* the same failure mode; two buckets intersect exactly when the new bucket's lower bound is strictly less than the existing bucket's upper bound and the existing bucket's lower bound is strictly less than the new bucket's upper bound. Adjacency is therefore perfectly legal. Against an existing bucket running from 0 to 12, a new bucket from 12 to 24 is accepted and so is one from 24 to 36, while a bucket from 11 to 13 is refused and an exact repeat of 0 to 12 is refused, both with `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Buckets belonging to a different cohort, or to the same cohort but a different failure mode, never conflict.

### 3.3. Estimating and Posting the Base Warranty Accrual

A `WarrantyAnalyst` opens a **warranty accrual** for exactly one period-and-cohort pair, supplying the `period_id` of the period the estimate belongs to, the `vintage_id` of the cohort being accrued for, a `method` naming the estimation variant, an `elapsed_exposure_bps` between 0 and 10000 inclusive stating what fraction of the cohort's warranty exposure has already been earned, and the `expected_period_version` guard described in §2.7. The row is born in `status` `DRAFT` with a `revision_index` of 0 and both `proposed_by_user_id` and `posted_by_user_id` null. The create runs the estimate immediately, so the `DRAFT` row already carries its computed `expected_claim_rate_ppm`, `cost_per_claim_basis_cents`, `paid_to_date_cents` and `accrued_amount_cents`; computing them is part of the creation rather than a subsequent mutation, so the new accrual's own `version` is 1. The create advances the parent period's `version` by 1.

Several conditions gate the create beyond the version guard. The scoping period must be in `OPEN` or `ANALYSIS_LOCKED`; a period in `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` or `CLOSED` answers `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`. The referenced cohort must still be under warranty — an `exposure_status` of `EXPIRED` answers `ERR_VINTAGE_EXPOSURE_EXPIRED`. The cohort's family must have at least one failure mode on file, because an accrual with nothing to price answers `ERR_NO_FAILURE_MODES_DEFINED`. And no other accrual for the same period-and-cohort pair may already sit in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED`, which otherwise answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCRUAL`; note that a prior accrual in `POSTED` or `SUPERSEDED` does *not* block a new one, because re-estimating a cohort in the same period is exactly how a change in estimate is recorded.

The `method` selects one of two estimation variants, and each carries its own admission gate. `HISTORICAL_RATE` is permitted only when the family's `history_maturity_status` is `MATURE`; attempting it on a family still classified `LIMITED` answers `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLAIM_HISTORY`. `ANALOG_BLEND` is permitted whenever the family has both a non-null `analog_family_id` and a non-null `engineering_estimate_rate_ppm`, and is refused with `ERR_ANALOG_BASIS_MISSING` when either is absent — but it is not restricted to immature families. A `MATURE` family that also carries an analogue basis may legally be accrued under either variant on identical data, and the two variants will return different figures; choosing between them is a deliberate accounting judgement, not a fallback.

Both variants first establish what has already been spent. `paid_to_date_cents` is the sum of `paid_cost_cents` over every claim observation of this accrual's own cohort, regardless of failure mode or development bucket.

Under **`HISTORICAL_RATE`**, frequency is measured per failure mode from the cohort's own history, so the mix of failure modes drives the answer. Assemble the set of failure modes belonging to the cohort's family for which at least one claim observation exists naming both this cohort and that mode, and enumerate them in `mode_code` ascending order. For each mode in turn: sum `claim_count` over the observations naming this cohort and this mode to obtain the mode's observed claim count; multiply that count by one million, divide by the cohort's `units_shipped`, and round to the nearest integer to obtain the mode's observed rate in parts per million; then multiply that rounded rate by `units_shipped`, multiply by the lifetime development factor of 25000 basis points — a factor of two and a half, reflecting that a cohort observed part-way through its warranty life will accumulate claims well beyond what has been seen — divide by the product of one million and ten thousand, and round to the nearest integer to obtain the mode's ultimate claim count; finally multiply that ultimate claim count by that failure mode's own `cost_per_claim_cents` to obtain the mode's gross ultimate cost. The rounding order here is pinned and observable: the rate and the ultimate claim count are each rounded **per failure mode, before any summation**, never summed as exact rationals and rounded once at the end. The gross ultimate cost for the accrual is the sum of the per-mode gross ultimate costs. The accrual's `expected_claim_rate_ppm` is the sum of the per-mode rounded rates. The accrual's `cost_per_claim_basis_cents` is 0 when the summed ultimate claim count across all modes is 0, and otherwise the gross ultimate cost divided by that summed ultimate claim count, rounded to the nearest integer — an effective blended cost per claim reflecting the observed failure-mode mix.

Under **`ANALOG_BLEND`**, frequency comes from blending engineering judgement with a predecessor product's real experience, and every claim is priced at one single family-level figure. Take the analogue family named by the accruing family's `analog_family_id`. Sum `claim_count` over every claim observation whose cohort belongs to that analogue family to obtain the analogue claim count, and sum `units_shipped` over every vintage of that analogue family whose `exposure_status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` to obtain the analogue exposure. The analogue's observed rate in parts per million is 0 when the analogue exposure is 0, and otherwise the analogue claim count multiplied by one million, divided by the analogue exposure, rounded to the nearest integer. Develop that observed rate to ultimate by multiplying it by the same lifetime development factor of 25000 basis points and dividing by 10000, rounding to the nearest integer. The blended rate then weights the engineering estimate at 6000 basis points — sixty percent — and the developed analogue rate at the remaining 4000 basis points: multiply `engineering_estimate_rate_ppm` by 6000, multiply the developed analogue rate by 4000, add the two products as exact whole numbers, divide the sum by 10000, and round **once** to the nearest integer. Rounding each weighted term separately before adding them is wrong and will produce a different answer. That blended figure becomes the accrual's `expected_claim_rate_ppm`. The ultimate claim count is the blended rate multiplied by the cohort's `units_shipped`, divided by one million, rounded to the nearest integer. The accrual's `cost_per_claim_basis_cents` is simply the family's `standard_cost_per_claim_cents`, and the gross ultimate cost is the ultimate claim count multiplied by that same standard cost. Note the asymmetry in what each variant reads: `HISTORICAL_RATE` reads only observations of this accrual's own cohort, whereas `ANALOG_BLEND` reads no observation of this cohort other than for `paid_to_date_cents` and instead reads the analogue family's observations across all of its vintages.

Whichever variant ran, the accrual's `accrued_amount_cents` is the gross ultimate cost less `paid_to_date_cents`, floored at 0 — a cohort that has already paid out more than its developed ultimate accrues nothing rather than a negative figure.

The accrual then walks a cycle. Proposing it advances `DRAFT` to `PROPOSED`, records the acting analyst's identifier as `proposed_by_user_id`, and re-runs the whole estimate against whatever claim data now exists, so that a proposal always reflects current history rather than the state of the world at draft time; it requires the scoping period to be in `OPEN` or `ANALYSIS_LOCKED` and answers `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` otherwise. Revising it loops `PROPOSED` back to `DRAFT`, adds 1 to `revision_index` and clears `proposed_by_user_id` to null while leaving every stored estimate figure exactly as the last proposal computed it — revising re-runs no arithmetic at all — and carries the same period requirement with the same `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` refusal. There is no limit on how many times an accrual may cycle between the two states.

Posting is where the estimate becomes a booked liability, and it belongs to a `ReserveController` rather than the analyst. The accrual must be in `PROPOSED`. The scoping period must be precisely in `ANALYSIS_LOCKED` — not merely non-closed — and a period still `OPEN`, already in `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW`, or `CLOSED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The caller's identifier must differ from `proposed_by_user_id`, as described in §2.8. And the family reserve balance that would result from counting this accrual as posted must not exceed the family's `reserve_cap_cents`; if it would, the post is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_CAP_EXCEEDED` and nothing is written. That hypothetical balance models the whole effect of the post, not merely the addition: any other accrual of the same period-and-cohort pair currently in `POSTED` is counted as already superseded and therefore already out of the posted-accrual filter, so a re-estimate is measured against the cap on its net movement rather than on the sum of old and new. That ceiling deliberately applies to accrual posting alone: field-action approvals and adequacy true-ups are legal obligations rather than discretionary estimates and are never cap-gated, so they may drive the balance past the cap.

A successful post records the controller's identifier as `posted_by_user_id`, then does three further things. Every *other* accrual for the same period-and-cohort pair that was sitting in `POSTED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal — a superseded row can never move again, and any operation against it answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Because the superseded row's `accrued_amount_cents` thereby drops out of the posted-accrual filter while the new row's enters it, the family reserve balance moves by exactly the difference between the new and the old accrued amounts. That balance is then recomputed in full as described in §2.10, and immediately afterwards the family's `reserve_posture` is re-derived from the new balance. Accruals can be listed filtered by `period_id`, by `vintage_id` or by `status`.

### 3.4. Reserve Adequacy: Actual versus Expected, and the Trend True-Up

An accrual is only ever an estimate, and the discipline that keeps the reserve honest is the periodic **adequacy review**: a comparison of what the posted accruals said would have been spent by now against what was actually paid, converted into a signed change-in-estimate recommendation. A `WarrantyAnalyst` runs one by naming just two things — the `period_id` as of which adequacy is being measured and the `family_id` under review. Everything else is computed and snapshotted. The review is barred on a `CLOSED` period, which answers `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`. A newly created review has `true_up_applied` of false and a null `applied_by_user_id`, and reviews can be listed filtered by `period_id` or `family_id`. Nothing bars a second review of the same period-and-family pair: each run is an independent snapshot, and if both have their true-ups applied both signed recommendations count in the family's applied-true-up total.

The analysis runs as a fixed seven-step sequence, and every intermediate is persisted on the review row so that an auditor can retrace it. Its working set is every warranty accrual in `POSTED` status whose cohort belongs to the family under review, enumerated in `created_at` ascending order. Crucially, that working set is *not* restricted to the review's own period: accruals of other periods are included, because the reserve is measured cumulatively. Accruals in `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded.

**Step one** establishes the expected spend. For each accrual in the working set, multiply its `accrued_amount_cents` by its `elapsed_exposure_bps`, divide by 10000, and round to the nearest integer to obtain that accrual's earned portion; the review's `expected_to_date_cents` is the sum of those per-accrual earned portions. The rounding happens per accrual, before summing. **Step two** establishes the actual spend: the review's `actual_paid_cents` is the sum of `paid_cost_cents` over every claim observation whose cohort belongs to the family under review and whose `observation_period_code` is lexically less than or equal to the review period's `period_code` — an inclusive upper bound, so the review period's own paid claims are counted. **Step three** forms the ratio. If `expected_to_date_cents` is 0 there is no base to compare against, the review is refused with `ERR_NO_EXPECTED_BASE`, and nothing whatsoever is persisted. Otherwise `ave_ratio_bps` is `actual_paid_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `expected_to_date_cents`, rounded to the nearest integer — 10000 basis points meaning actuals landed exactly on expectation.

**Step four** selects the trend tier from the ratio computed in step three, and it sets `trend_adjustment_bps` and `adequacy_status` together. A ratio of 8000 basis points or less means claims are running at eighty percent of expectation or better, so `trend_adjustment_bps` becomes -1000 and `adequacy_status` becomes `REDUNDANT`. Otherwise a ratio of 12000 basis points or less means actuals are within a fifth of expectation, so `trend_adjustment_bps` becomes 0 and `adequacy_status` becomes `SUFFICIENT`. Otherwise a ratio of 15000 basis points or less means a material overrun, so `trend_adjustment_bps` becomes 1500 and `adequacy_status` becomes `DEFICIENT`. Any higher ratio sets `trend_adjustment_bps` to 3000 and `adequacy_status` to `DEFICIENT`. Every threshold is an inclusive upper bound, so a ratio of exactly 8000 is `REDUNDANT`, exactly 12000 is `SUFFICIENT`, and exactly 15000 draws the 1500 basis-point adjustment rather than the 3000 one.

**Step five** computes the unearned base: `remaining_base_cents` is the sum, over the same working set, of each accrual's `accrued_amount_cents` less that accrual's earned portion — reusing the *rounded* earned figure computed in step one rather than recomputing it. **Step six** applies the tier: `adjusted_remaining_cents` is `remaining_base_cents` multiplied by the quantity 10000 plus `trend_adjustment_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest integer. **Step seven** produces the recommendation: `recommended_true_up_cents` is the amount by which step six moved the remaining base — that is, `adjusted_remaining_cents` less `remaining_base_cents` — plus the amount by which actuals have already diverged from expectation, that is `actual_paid_cents` less `expected_to_date_cents`. The result is signed: a redundant reserve produces a negative recommendation that will release reserve, and a deficient one produces a positive recommendation that will strengthen it.

The evaluation order above is pinned to break an apparent circularity, and implementations must not "improve" it. The trend tier in step four is selected from the ratio computed in step three against the pre-true-up posted-accrual base, and step six applies that tier to step five's remaining base. Once the review row is written it is an immutable snapshot of the numbers as of its creation: applying its true-up later changes the family's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents`, but it must not re-run any of the seven steps, and the review's stored intermediates never move again.

Applying the true-up is a `ReserveController` capability, refused with `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` on a closed period. It flips the review's `true_up_applied` from false to true — a review whose flag already reads true has no declared transition left, so a second attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and changes nothing — records the acting controller's identifier as `applied_by_user_id`, and — because the row has now entered the applied-true-up filter — triggers a full recomputation of the family's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents`, immediately followed by a re-derivation of the family's `reserve_posture` from that new balance.

### 3.5. Campaigns, Recalls and Field Actions

When engineering discovers a defect that warrants going out to the installed base, a `QualityEngineer` opens a **field action**. Three flavours exist, chosen by `action_type`: a `SERVICE_CAMPAIGN`, a `SAFETY_RECALL`, or a `FIELD_BULLETIN`. Alongside the type the engineer supplies a `family_id`, an `action_code` unique across every field action ever opened, an `affected_build_start_date` and `affected_build_end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form bounding the affected production window with both endpoints inclusive and the end date the same as or later than the start date, an `affected_unit_count` of at least 1 giving the units in scope of the remedy, and optionally `regulatory_notice_filed`, which defaults to false when omitted. The system derives `is_safety_recall` as true exactly when `action_type` is `SAFETY_RECALL` and false for the other two types; that single derived flag drives every safety guard in this subsection.

A new action starts with `approval_state` of `DRAFT`, `execution_state` of `NOT_STARTED`, a null `selected_remedy_option_id`, a `specific_accrual_cents` of 0, an `executed_cost_cents` of 0, a null `approved_period_id`, and null `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id`. Field actions can be listed filtered by `family_id`, by `selected_remedy_option_id`, by `approved_period_id` or by `approval_state`.

Overlapping campaigns on the same family are an operational hazard — two notification waves colliding in the field confuse dealers and double-bill the reserve — so the affected-build window is protected by a **buffered closed-interval conflict check**. The new window runs from `affected_build_start_date` to `affected_build_end_date` with both endpoints inclusive. It must not intersect the window of any existing field action on the same family whose `approval_state` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, after that existing window has been expanded outward by a quiet buffer of 30 calendar days on both sides. Concretely, a conflict exists when the new window's start date falls on or before the existing window's end date advanced by 30 days, and simultaneously the existing window's start date retreated by 30 days falls on or before the new window's end date. Adjacency is therefore illegal here — unlike the half-open development buckets of §3.2 — and so is any gap of 30 days or fewer between the two windows; only a gap of 31 days or more is accepted. Field actions whose `approval_state` is `REJECTED` are excluded from the check entirely, so a rejected campaign does not block its own replacement. A conflict answers `ERR_FIELD_ACTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Costing a field action means pricing candidate **remedy options**. The engineer adds each candidate under the parent action with an `option_code` unique within that action, a `remedy_class` that is one of `PERMANENT_REPAIR`, `INTERIM_REPAIR`, `PART_REPLACEMENT` or `BUYBACK`, a `parts_cost_cents` of at least 0 covering parts per remedied unit, a `labor_hours_centi` of at least 0 giving labour per remedied unit in hundredths of an hour, an `admin_cost_cents` of at least 0 covering administration and customer notification per unit in scope, an `expected_take_rate_bps` between 0 and 10000 inclusive stating what share of the affected population is expected to actually present for the remedy, and optionally `is_engineering_approved`, which defaults to false and records whether engineering has released the option for field use. Each option's `field_action_id` is taken from the parent action it is created under, and the candidates of one action can be listed on their own, in `created_at` ascending order and with no filter. A candidate's `per_unit_expected_cents` is computed by the cost model below and stored on the row the moment the option is created, so even a never-selected candidate carries the figure; adding a candidate never mutates the parent action, whose `specific_accrual_cents` and `version` are both left exactly as they were.

The cost model runs per option, and its rounding order is fixed. Convert labour first: multiply `labor_hours_centi` by the standard field labour rate of 9500 cents per hour — ninety-five dollars an hour — divide by 100 to undo the hundredths-of-an-hour scaling, and round to the nearest integer to obtain per-unit labour cost in cents. Add `parts_cost_cents`, that per-unit labour cost, and `admin_cost_cents` to obtain the per-unit gross cost of remedying one unit. Then weight for take-up: multiply the per-unit gross cost by `expected_take_rate_bps`, divide by 10000, and round to the nearest integer; that figure is stored on the option as `per_unit_expected_cents`. Finally, the action's `specific_accrual_cents` is that stored, already-rounded `per_unit_expected_cents` multiplied by the action's `affected_unit_count`. The order is pinned and observable: the take-rate weighting is rounded to whole cents **per unit first**, and only then multiplied up by the unit count. Multiplying the unrounded per-unit gross by the unit count and by the take rate and rounding once at the end is wrong, and yields a different total whenever the product of the per-unit gross and the take rate is not an exact multiple of 10000. Only the option currently selected feeds the action's accrual; non-selected options of the same action contribute nothing, and `specific_accrual_cents` stays at 0 for as long as `selected_remedy_option_id` is null.

Selecting the remedy is guarded by the field-action version check of §2.7 and comes in two forms. In the **explicit** form the engineer names a `remedy_option_id`. If no remedy option carries that identifier the request answers `ERR_REMEDY_OPTION_NOT_FOUND`. Two checks then run in a fixed order: first, if the action's `is_safety_recall` is true and the named candidate's `remedy_class` is anything other than `PERMANENT_REPAIR`, the request answers `ERR_SAFETY_REMEDY_REQUIRED`; second, if the candidate's `field_action_id` is not this action, the request answers `ERR_REMEDY_OPTION_MISMATCH`. A candidate that fails both checks answers with the safety code, not the mismatch code.

In the **implicit** form the engineer omits `remedy_option_id` — or supplies it as null, which the server treats as exactly equivalent to omitting it — and the server picks the cheapest eligible candidate. The eligibility filter admits remedy options belonging to this action whose `is_engineering_approved` is true, whose `expected_take_rate_bps` is at least 1, and — when the action's `is_safety_recall` is true — whose `remedy_class` is `PERMANENT_REPAIR`; when `is_safety_recall` is false, the remedy class is unrestricted. Surviving candidates are ranked by `per_unit_expected_cents` ascending, each candidate's figure being computed by the cost model above. Ties are broken by `expected_take_rate_bps` ascending, and any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending; the row identifier is never a ranking or tie-break input. If the eligibility filter yields no candidate at all, the request answers `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REMEDY_OPTION` — and in that case no field of the action is mutated and the action's `version` is **not** incremented.

A successful selection writes `selected_remedy_option_id`, recomputes the selected option's `per_unit_expected_cents` and the action's `specific_accrual_cents`, and advances the action's `version` by 1; the response body is the updated field action, not the option. A new selection supersedes any prior one outright. Selection is legal from two starting points on the approval dimension. From `DRAFT` the action stays in `DRAFT` — a self-loop that lets the engineer re-price freely before submitting. From `REJECTED` the action returns to `DRAFT` for re-work, and in that case `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` are both cleared to null so that the segregation-of-duties comparison starts clean on the next round.

Submitting advances the approval dimension from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and records the acting engineer's identifier as `submitted_by_user_id`; the execution dimension is untouched. An action with a null `selected_remedy_option_id` cannot be submitted and answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A safety recall is re-checked at submit time: if the option currently referenced by `selected_remedy_option_id` has a `remedy_class` other than `PERMANENT_REPAIR`, the submit answers `ERR_SAFETY_REMEDY_REQUIRED`.

The decision is a `ReserveController` capability, takes a `decision` value of either `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, requires the action to be in `SUBMITTED`, and in both branches requires the caller's `X-User-ID` to differ from `submitted_by_user_id` on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The two branches then diverge completely.

The **approving** branch carries two further preconditions. When `is_safety_recall` is true, `regulatory_notice_filed` must already be true, or the approval answers `ERR_REGULATORY_NOTICE_MISSING` — the company will not book a recall accrual before it has notified the regulator. And the booking-period selector of §3.1 must find a period, or the approval answers `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD`. When both hold, the approval advances the action to `APPROVED`, which is terminal on the approval dimension, and sets off a four-hop cascade that must be evaluated in order. **Hop one** records the controller's identifier as `decided_by_user_id`, writes the period chosen by the booking-period selector into `approved_period_id`, and recomputes `specific_accrual_cents` from the currently selected remedy option. **Hop two**, because that accrual has now entered the approved-campaign filter, recomputes the family's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` in full. **Hop three**, because the balance changed, re-derives the family's `reserve_posture`. **Hop four** fires only when hop three left `reserve_posture` at `CONSTRAINED`: every warranty accrual sitting in `PROPOSED` whose cohort belongs to this family is pushed back to `DRAFT`, with 1 added to its `revision_index` and its `proposed_by_user_id` cleared to null, forcing the analysts to re-estimate in light of a reserve that has hit its ceiling. When hop three left the posture at `NORMAL` or `ELEVATED`, hop four has no effect at all and no accrual is touched. Independently of those hops, the period named by `approved_period_id` reports refreshed `significant_exposure_count` and `requires_significant_exposure_disclosure` from the next read onward, because those counters are derived at read time; the approval writes nothing into the period row and leaves its `updated_at` and `version` alone.

The **rejecting** branch is deliberately inert on the ledger. It advances the action to `REJECTED`, records the controller's identifier as `decided_by_user_id`, and forces `specific_accrual_cents` back to 0. `approved_period_id` remains null, and precisely because the action never enters the approved-campaign filter, the family's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` is unchanged, no posture re-derivation occurs, no proposed accrual is reverted, and no disclosure counter moves. A rejected action is not dead, though: re-selecting a remedy returns it to `DRAFT`, as described above.

Field execution runs on a second, independent dimension. `execution_state` starts at `NOT_STARTED` and advances through `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED`, with the desired target supplied as `to_execution_state` and an optional `incurred_cost_cents` of at least 0 that defaults to 0 when omitted; each progress call adds the supplied `incurred_cost_cents` to the action's running `executed_cost_cents`. Only those two hops are declared, and the declared-transition test is evaluated first, so a `to_execution_state` of `NOT_STARTED`, a jump from `NOT_STARTED` straight to `COMPLETED`, and any call at all against an action already in `COMPLETED` all answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than `ERR_EXECUTION_BEFORE_APPROVAL`. Leaving `NOT_STARTED` requires the approval dimension to already read `APPROVED`; attempting it on an action still in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` answers `ERR_EXECUTION_BEFORE_APPROVAL`. Advancing to `COMPLETED` requires the execution dimension to currently read `IN_PROGRESS`. Reaching `COMPLETED` is also a ledger event: because the completed action's `executed_cost_cents` now enters the deduction term of the family reserve, the family's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` is recomputed in full and its `reserve_posture` is then re-derived. The two dimensions genuinely are independent — the execution dimension advances while `approval_state` remains `APPROVED` throughout, and a progress call never moves the approval dimension.

### 3.6. Extended Service Contracts and Revenue Deferral

An extended service contract is a sale, not a liability estimate, and the system keeps the two ledgers strictly apart: no warranty accrual, claim observation or field action ever affects extended-service-contract revenue, and no service contract ever affects the warranty reserve. A `WarrantyAnalyst` records a sale with the `family_id` of the covered product family, a `contract_number` unique across all contracts, a `sold_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, a `term_months` coverage term of at least 1, a `total_contract_price_cents` of at least 1 giving the gross price collected from the customer, and a `recognition_method` of either `STRAIGHT_LINE` or `CLAIMS_PATTERN`. A contract may not predate the product it covers: a `sold_date` earlier than the covered family's `launch_date` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_PRECEDES_LAUNCH`. New contracts begin with `status` of `ACTIVE`, a `refunded_revenue_cents` of 0 and a null `cancelled_at`; recognition is evaluated at once against the logical clock, so a contract whose `sold_date` lies far enough in the logical past that its `months_elapsed` already reaches `term_months` is created directly in `EXPIRED`, with `recognized_revenue_cents` equal to `total_contract_price_cents` and `deferred_revenue_cents` of 0. Contracts can be listed filtered by `family_id` or by `status`.

Recognition tracks the logical clock, and the first thing computed is elapsed time. Count whole months between the `sold_date` and the calendar date of the current logical clock instant as follows: multiply the difference in years by 12, add the difference in months, and then subtract 1 if the clock date's day-of-month is strictly less than the `sold_date`'s day-of-month. Clamp that count to be no less than 0 and no greater than `term_months`; the result is `months_elapsed`.

Under **`STRAIGHT_LINE`** revenue accrues in equal monthly slices, so `recognized_revenue_cents` is `total_contract_price_cents` multiplied by `months_elapsed`, divided by `term_months`, rounded to the nearest integer. Recognition is therefore exactly proportional to elapsed months and is 0 at inception.

Under **`CLAIMS_PATTERN`** revenue follows a four-step front-loaded curve that mirrors when service claims actually arrive. First express elapsed time as a fraction of term: multiply `months_elapsed` by 10000, divide by `term_months`, and round to the nearest integer to obtain the elapsed fraction in basis points. Then select the cumulative recognition fraction from that figure: 1000 basis points when the elapsed fraction is 2500 basis points or less, 3000 basis points when it is 5000 or less, 6000 basis points when it is 7500 or less, and 10000 basis points otherwise. Each bucket's upper bound is inclusive, so an elapsed fraction of exactly 2500 recognizes 1000 basis points and exactly 5000 recognizes 3000 basis points. `recognized_revenue_cents` is then `total_contract_price_cents` multiplied by that cumulative fraction, divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest integer. Because the curve recognizes a tenth of the contract immediately — infant-mortality claims arrive early — the two methods return different `recognized_revenue_cents` and different `deferred_revenue_cents` for an identical price, term, sold date and clock position.

Under either method, `deferred_revenue_cents` is `total_contract_price_cents` less `recognized_revenue_cents` less `refunded_revenue_cents`. Recognition is re-evaluated when the contract is sold, whenever the logical clock moves, and when the contract is cancelled.

Cancelling is a `WarrantyAnalyst` capability and refunds exactly the unearned balance. The transition takes an `ACTIVE` contract to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal. `refunded_revenue_cents` is set to whatever `deferred_revenue_cents` held immediately before the transition, `deferred_revenue_cents` is then set to 0, `cancelled_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and `recognized_revenue_cents` is frozen at its pre-cancellation value so that it no longer tracks the logical clock. The identity that recognized plus deferred plus refunded equals `total_contract_price_cents` holds both before and after the cancellation — this is a compensating action, not a write-off.

A contract that simply runs its course ends differently. When the logical clock advances far enough that the recomputed `months_elapsed` reaches or exceeds `term_months`, an `ACTIVE` contract auto-transitions to `EXPIRED`, which is also terminal; at that point `recognized_revenue_cents` equals `total_contract_price_cents` in full and `deferred_revenue_cents` is 0. Contracts are evaluated for expiry in `created_at` ascending order on each clock move. Neither `CANCELLED` nor `EXPIRED` admits any further transition, and any attempt answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.7. Supplier Recovery, Negotiation and Collectability

When the root cause of a wave of claims sits in a purchased component, the manufacturer has a claim against its supplier — and, in accounting terms, a recoverable asset whose value depends entirely on how likely the supplier is to pay. A `WarrantyAnalyst` registers a **supplier** with a `supplier_code` unique across all suppliers, a legal `name`, an external `credit_rating` of `AAA`, `BBB`, `CCC` or `D`, an `annual_purchase_volume_cents` of at least 0 recording annual spend with that counterparty, and a `contractual_recovery_cap_bps` between 0 and 10000 inclusive stating the contractual ceiling on annual recoveries as a share of that spend.

Six aggregates and two ratings are maintained on the supplier row and are never client-supplied. `gross_asserted_recovery_cents` is the sum of `asserted_amount_cents` over that supplier's recovery claims whose status is `ASSERTED`, `NEGOTIATION`, `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. `open_recovery_asset_cents` is the sum of `recognized_recovery_cents` over claims still in `ASSERTED` or `NEGOTIATION` — the live receivable. `realized_recovery_cents` is the sum of `recognized_recovery_cents` over claims in `SETTLED`. `written_off_recovery_cents` is the sum of `asserted_amount_cents` over claims in `WRITTEN_OFF`. `closed_recovery_count` is the number of claims in `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. Claims still in `DRAFT` contribute to none of these.

From those, the supplier's `realization_bps` and `collectability_rating` are derived. The base rating comes straight from the credit rating: `AAA` gives a base of `HIGH`, `BBB` gives `MEDIUM`, `CCC` gives `LOW`, and `D` gives `IMPAIRED`. Realization measures the supplier's own track record: let the denominator be `realized_recovery_cents` plus `written_off_recovery_cents`; when that denominator is 0, `realization_bps` is 10000, and otherwise `realization_bps` is `realized_recovery_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by that denominator, rounded to the nearest integer. The rating then follows: when `closed_recovery_count` is 0 the base rating stands; otherwise, when `realization_bps` is at least 7500 the base rating stands; otherwise, when `realization_bps` is at least 4000 the base rating is downgraded by one notch; and in every remaining case the base rating is downgraded by two notches. The downgrade ladder runs `HIGH` to `MEDIUM` to `LOW` to `IMPAIRED` and is floored at `IMPAIRED`, so a two-notch downgrade from `LOW` lands on `IMPAIRED` rather than falling off the end. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a realization of exactly 7500 basis points holds the base rating and exactly 4000 costs one notch. Only claims in `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` affect the rating; claims in `DRAFT`, `ASSERTED` or `NEGOTIATION` do not. The rating is derived when the supplier is registered — with no closed history it equals the base rating — and re-derived on every recovery transition into `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`.

A **supplier recovery** claim is opened by a `WarrantyAnalyst` with the `supplier_id` of the counterparty, the `failure_mode_id` of the component defect driving the claims being recovered, an `asserted_amount_cents` of at least 1 giving the gross amount claimed, and optionally a `field_action_id` naming the campaign or recall whose cost is being recovered — left null for ordinary warranty-claim recovery. The row is born in `status` `DRAFT` with an `assert_count` of 0, a null `settled_amount_cents`, a null `supplier_response_note` and a null `settled_period_id`, and it is valued immediately on creation under the `EXPECTED_VALUE` variant described below, so a brand-new claim already carries a `valuation_variant` of `EXPECTED_VALUE`, a `collectability_rating_at_valuation` snapshot and a computed `recognized_recovery_cents`. Claims can be listed filtered by `supplier_id`, `failure_mode_id`, `field_action_id`, `settled_period_id` or `status`.

Valuation of the claim runs through one of two variants, and which one applies is recorded on the row as `valuation_variant` alongside `collectability_rating_at_valuation`, a snapshot of the supplier's `collectability_rating` as it stood at the moment of that valuation. Both variants first compute a contractual ceiling: multiply the supplier's `annual_purchase_volume_cents` by its `contractual_recovery_cap_bps`, divide by 10000, and round to the nearest integer. That ceiling is a supplier-level annual figure applied to each recovery row individually; it is not shared or apportioned across the supplier's rows.

The **`EXPECTED_VALUE`** variant applies while the claim's status is `DRAFT`, `ASSERTED` or `NEGOTIATION`. It probability-weights the full asserted claim by the supplier's collectability: a rating of `HIGH` weights at 9000 basis points, `MEDIUM` at 6000, `LOW` at 2500, and `IMPAIRED` at 0. Multiply `asserted_amount_cents` by that weighting, divide by 10000, round to the nearest integer, and then take the lesser of that figure and the contractual ceiling; the result is `recognized_recovery_cents`. Rounding happens before the ceiling comparison, not after. Because the weighting reads a live supplier attribute, the recoverable asset moves whenever the supplier's rating moves, even with no change at all to the claim itself.

The **`CONTRACTUAL_FLOOR`** variant applies once the claim's status is `SETTLED`. It uses the agreed figure with no probability weighting whatsoever: `recognized_recovery_cents` is the lesser of the contractual ceiling and `settled_amount_cents`. An identical asserted amount against an identical supplier therefore yields a different recognized figure under this variant than it did under `EXPECTED_VALUE` one transition earlier. When a claim's status is `WRITTEN_OFF`, `recognized_recovery_cents` is 0 outright.

Asserting the claim is the analyst's move and is legal from two starting points, both of which land in `ASSERTED`. From `DRAFT` it is the initial assertion; from `NEGOTIATION` it is a fresh negotiation round in which the claim is re-asserted, usually at a revised figure. In both cases the supplier's `collectability_rating` must not be `IMPAIRED`, or the request answers `ERR_SUPPLIER_IMPAIRED` — the company does not book a receivable against a counterparty it has already written off. From `DRAFT` there is one additional guard: when the claim carries a non-null `field_action_id`, that field action's `approval_state` must be `APPROVED`, or the assertion answers `ERR_FIELD_ACTION_NOT_APPROVED`. An optional `revised_asserted_amount_cents` of at least 1 in the request replaces `asserted_amount_cents` when supplied and leaves it unchanged when omitted. Either way, 1 is added to `assert_count`, the `valuation_variant` is set to (or remains) `EXPECTED_VALUE`, `recognized_recovery_cents` and `collectability_rating_at_valuation` are recomputed under that variant, and the supplier's `gross_asserted_recovery_cents` and `open_recovery_asset_cents` are recomputed. The re-assertion path additionally clears `supplier_response_note` to null, since the supplier's previous answer no longer applies to a claim that has just been restated.

Responding is the one operation a `SupplierRep` may perform, and it moves the claim from `ASSERTED` to `NEGOTIATION`. The caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the claim's `supplier_id`, or the request answers `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The `response_note` free text is required and must be non-empty; it is stored as `supplier_response_note`, `recognized_recovery_cents` is recomputed under the `EXPECTED_VALUE` variant, and the supplier's `open_recovery_asset_cents` is then recomputed because the row's recognized figure may have moved. No other supplier aggregate and no rating is touched.

Closing the claim is a `ReserveController` capability and takes an `outcome` of either `SETTLED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`. Both outcomes are legal from `ASSERTED` and from `NEGOTIATION`, and both land in a terminal state from which nothing further is possible.

Closing as settled requires a `settled_amount_cents` of at least 0 — the field is optional in the endpoint's shape, but its absence under this outcome is itself an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — that does not exceed the claim's current `asserted_amount_cents`, or the request answers `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_ASSERTED`, and requires the booking-period selector of §3.1 to find a period, or the request answers `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD`. The booking-period check is the earlier of the two, so a settlement that both overshoots the asserted amount and finds no bookable period answers `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD`. It then runs a five-hop cascade in order. **Hop one** writes the supplied `settled_amount_cents`, writes the selected booking period into `settled_period_id`, switches `valuation_variant` to `CONTRACTUAL_FLOOR`, and recomputes `recognized_recovery_cents` under that variant. **Hop two**, because the row has left the open set and entered `SETTLED`, recomputes the supplier's `open_recovery_asset_cents`, `realized_recovery_cents` and `closed_recovery_count`. **Hop three**, because `closed_recovery_count` and `realized_recovery_cents` changed, recomputes the supplier's `realization_bps` and `collectability_rating`. **Hop four**, because the supplier's `collectability_rating` may have changed in hop three, re-values every *other* recovery claim of the same supplier whose status is `ASSERTED` or `NEGOTIATION` under the `EXPECTED_VALUE` variant, updating each row's `recognized_recovery_cents` and `collectability_rating_at_valuation`; those rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and exactly one re-valuation pass runs — the cascade never iterates to a fixed point. **Hop five**, because hop four changed those rows' recognized figures, recomputes the supplier's `open_recovery_asset_cents` once more.

Closing as written off needs no settlement amount and no booking period; a `settled_amount_cents` supplied anyway is accepted and then ignored. It sets `settled_amount_cents` to 0, `recognized_recovery_cents` to 0, `valuation_variant` to `CONTRACTUAL_FLOOR` and `collectability_rating_at_valuation` to the supplier's rating as it stands at that instant, before the re-rating below; recomputes the supplier's `open_recovery_asset_cents`, `written_off_recovery_cents` and `closed_recovery_count`; then, because `closed_recovery_count` and `written_off_recovery_cents` changed, recomputes the supplier's `realization_bps` and `collectability_rating`; then, because the rating may have changed, re-values every other `ASSERTED` or `NEGOTIATION` claim of the supplier under `EXPECTED_VALUE` in `created_at` ascending order; and finally recomputes `open_recovery_asset_cents` once more. Note what is conserved: the amount does not vanish from `gross_asserted_recovery_cents`, it simply moves out of `open_recovery_asset_cents` and into `written_off_recovery_cents`. A write-off is the compensating action for an assertion.

### 3.8. Product-Liability Reserves and Incurred-but-not-Reported Claims

Product-liability exposure is measured independently of the warranty reserve, on actuarial rather than engineering inputs. An `Actuary` opens a **liability reserve** for one period-and-family pair, supplying the `period_id` the reserve is measured for, the `family_id` it covers, a `reported_claim_count` of at least 0 giving the asserted product-liability claims reported to date, an `asserted_case_reserve_cents` of at least 0 holding the legal department's case reserves for those asserted claims, a `reporting_lag_months` between 0 and 24 inclusive giving the actuarial average reporting lag, and a `severity_trend_bps` between -5000 and 10000 inclusive applying an actuarial severity trend to average case severity — note that this is the one basis-point field permitted to be negative, because severity can trend down. The row is born in `status` `DRAFT` with a `return_count` of 0 and a null `reviewed_by_user_id`. The scoping period must not be `CLOSED`, which otherwise answers `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`, and only one reserve may exist per period-and-family pair across the states `DRAFT`, `ACTUARY_REVIEWED` and `BOOKED` — a second answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_LIABILITY_RESERVE`. Reserves can be listed filtered by `period_id`, `family_id` or `status`.

The reserve's arithmetic runs in a fixed sequence. `avg_case_severity_cents` is 0 when `reported_claim_count` is 0, and otherwise `asserted_case_reserve_cents` divided by `reported_claim_count`, rounded to the nearest integer. `lag_factor_bps` translates reporting lag into an IBNR frequency uplift: multiply `reporting_lag_months` by 1200 basis points per month — twelve percent of reported frequency for each month of lag — and take the lesser of that product and a hard ceiling of 6000 basis points, so a lag of five months already saturates the factor and a longer lag adds nothing. `ibnr_claim_count` is `reported_claim_count` multiplied by `lag_factor_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest integer. Trended severity is `avg_case_severity_cents` multiplied by the quantity 10000 plus `severity_trend_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest integer. The raw IBNR figure is `ibnr_claim_count` multiplied by that trended severity.

That raw figure is then clamped between two bounds, and **the clamp order is pinned and observable**. The cap is `asserted_case_reserve_cents` multiplied by 15000 basis points — one and a half times the asserted case reserves — divided by 10000, rounded to the nearest integer. The exposure floor is the covered family's `units_sold_lifetime` multiplied by 25 cents per unit. Apply the cap first, taking the lesser of the raw IBNR figure and the cap; then apply the floor to that capped value, taking the greater of it and the floor. The result is `ibnr_reserve_cents`. Because the floor is applied outermost, the floor **wins** over the cap: for a family with large unit exposure relative to its case reserves, `ibnr_reserve_cents` may legitimately exceed one and a half times `asserted_case_reserve_cents`. Finally, `total_liability_reserve_cents` is `asserted_case_reserve_cents` plus `ibnr_reserve_cents`. No claim observation, warranty accrual or field action participates in any of this — the four request-supplied actuarial inputs and the family's `units_sold_lifetime` are the whole input set.

The reserve then walks its own cycle, driven by a `decision` value. A decision of `REVIEWED` advances `DRAFT` to `ACTUARY_REVIEWED`, records the acting actuary's identifier as `reviewed_by_user_id`, and re-runs the whole computation above so that `avg_case_severity_cents`, `lag_factor_bps`, `ibnr_claim_count`, `ibnr_reserve_cents` and `total_liability_reserve_cents` all reflect the family's exposure as it stands at review time. That transition additionally requires the scoping period to be in `ANALYSIS_LOCKED` or `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW`; a period still `OPEN` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A decision of `RETURNED` loops `ACTUARY_REVIEWED` back to `DRAFT`, adds 1 to `return_count` and clears `reviewed_by_user_id` to null; it recomputes nothing. Recall from §3.1 that reopening the period's analysis stage forces the same return on every `ACTUARY_REVIEWED` reserve of that period.

Booking is a `ReserveController` capability. It advances `ACTUARY_REVIEWED` to `BOOKED`, which is terminal, and requires the scoping period to be precisely in `ACTUARIAL_REVIEW` — anything else answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `reviewed_by_user_id`, as described in §2.8. Booking writes no figure of its own; its whole significance is that the row now satisfies the period-close guard requiring every liability reserve of the period to be booked.

### 3.9. The Rollforward Disclosure and Significant Campaign Exposures

The final act of a close is the **reserve rollforward**: an immutable disclosure snapshot that explains, in the language auditors expect, how the warranty reserve moved from the beginning of the period to its end. A `ReserveController` generates it for a period, supplying only the `expected_period_version` guard of §2.7; every figure on the snapshot is computed. Because the call brings a brand-new row into existence, it answers HTTP 201 with the snapshot itself — not the period — in `data`. Generation is barred on a `CLOSED` period, which answers `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

`beginning_balance_cents` chains the disclosure to the prior close. Look among accrual periods whose status is `CLOSED` and which have at least one rollforward snapshot, and take the one whose `period_code` is the greatest `period_code` strictly less than this period's `period_code`; the prior snapshot is that period's rollforward, and if that period somehow has two snapshots, the one with the greater `created_at` wins. `beginning_balance_cents` is that prior snapshot's `ending_balance_cents`, or 0 when no such prior snapshot exists.

Five movement figures follow, each scoped to this period by an explicit reference and each carrying its status filter in full. `accruals_cents` is the sum of `accrued_amount_cents` over warranty accruals whose `period_id` is this period and whose status is `POSTED`. `campaign_accruals_cents` is the sum of `specific_accrual_cents` over field actions whose `approved_period_id` is this period and whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED`. `changes_in_estimate_cents` is the sum of `recommended_true_up_cents` over adequacy reviews whose `period_id` is this period and whose `true_up_applied` is true. `claims_paid_cents` is the sum of `paid_cost_cents` over claim observations whose `observation_period_code` equals this period's `period_code`. `supplier_recoveries_cents` is the sum of `recognized_recovery_cents` over supplier recoveries whose `settled_period_id` is this period and whose status is `SETTLED`. Superseded accruals, rejected field actions, unapplied adequacy reviews and non-settled recoveries are all excluded.

`ending_balance_cents` then ties out: `beginning_balance_cents` plus `accruals_cents` plus `campaign_accruals_cents` plus `changes_in_estimate_cents`, minus `claims_paid_cents`, plus `supplier_recoveries_cents`. Every input is already an exact integer count of cents, so no rounding occurs anywhere in the rollforward. The snapshot also carries `significant_exposure_count`, the number of field actions whose `approved_period_id` is this period, whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED` and whose `specific_accrual_cents` is at least 50,000,000 cents — the half-million-dollar threshold at which a campaign becomes individually disclosable — and `generated_by_user_id`, the identifier of the controller who generated it.

Generating a snapshot also flips the period's `rollforward_generated` to true and advances the period's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a second generation attempt built on the client's earlier view of the period answers `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION`. Snapshots are read individually — an unresolvable identifier answering `ERR_ROLLFORWARD_NOT_FOUND` — or listed, optionally filtered by `period_id`. Once written, a snapshot is never modified: it carries no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no operation that edits or deletes one.
