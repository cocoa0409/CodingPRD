# CMBS Special Servicing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a commercial mortgage loan inside a securitization stops performing, responsibility for it moves from the master servicer to a **special servicer**, and the rulebook changes completely. From that moment forward every action taken on the asset — ordering an appraisal, funding an advance out of the servicer's own pocket, granting a borrower a rate reduction, foreclosing, selling the building — has to be justified against the pooling and servicing agreement, tested against the net present value it produces for the trust, cleared with the representative of the controlling class of certificates, and reported to the trustee, the rating agencies and the certificate holders. Today that work is done in spreadsheets, e-mail threads and a filing cabinet, which is why two analysts looking at the same loan routinely disagree about how much the trust has written down and whether the controlling class still has a veto.

This document specifies the service that replaces those spreadsheets. It is a single system of record for the entire special-servicing lifecycle of a securitization trust: the loans it holds, the servicing transfer events that pull a loan out of master servicing, the appraisals that establish collateral value, the appraisal reductions that flow from those appraisals into the master servicer's advance obligation and into the controlling class's own notional balance, the advances themselves, the borrower's requests for relief, the competing workout alternatives and the arithmetic that ranks them, the consultation and consent machinery that surrounds a recommendation, the real-estate-owned asset a foreclosure produces, the fee accruals the servicer earns along the way, and the periodic distribution reports that summarise all of it. Every collection is mounted under the prefix `/special-servicing`.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **arithmetic is exact and reproducible**: a one-cent difference in an appraisal reduction is enough to terminate the controlling class's consent rights, so rounding order, boundary inclusivity and evaluation sequence are pinned down rather than left to the implementer. Second, **authorship and approval are always separated**: the analyst who ordered an appraisal may not accept it, the asset manager who wrote a workout plan may not approve it, and the operator who accrued a fee may not waive it. The service is expected to refuse such requests even when the caller's role would otherwise permit the operation.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which it is acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven roles below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. This is the very first thing the service checks: a request that both omits a header and names a resource that does not exist is answered with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, never with a not-found error.

The seven roles are `MasterServicer`, `SpecialServicerAssetManager`, `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer`, `ControllingClassRepresentative`, `Borrower`, `Trustee` and `PlatformAdmin`.

- The `MasterServicer` is the primary servicer. It boards loans, records delinquency and servicing transfer events, funds advances out of its own funds, hands distressed assets to the special servicer, and collects the fees that have been accrued.
- The `SpecialServicerAssetManager` is the special servicer's asset manager and the authoring party throughout: intake, servicing standard analysis, appraisal ordering, plan drafting and scoring, consultation issuance, real-estate-owned marketing.
- The `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer` is the special servicer's credit committee. It blesses what the asset manager authored — making appraisal reductions effective, approving workout plans, deciding borrower requests, deeming advances non-recoverable, closing sales, waiving fees.
- The `ControllingClassRepresentative` speaks for the controlling class of certificates and responds to consultations.
- The `Borrower` owes the mortgage loan, submits and withdraws its own requests, and reads its own loan.
- The `Trustee` certifies, distributes and voids the periodic reports.
- The `PlatformAdmin` operates the servicing platform: it boards trusts, runs the deemed-consent sweep and drives the logical clock. It holds no business judgement at all and cannot analyse, recommend, approve or dispose of anything.

A valid role that is not listed for the endpoint being called is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. This coarse check happens before the service looks for the addressed resource, so a role that may not touch an endpoint receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` even when the identifier in the path does not correspond to any stored row.

Two of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not an opaque operator string but the identifier of a stored row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Borrower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Loan`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Loan` compare that resource's `loan_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Borrower` submitting a request must therefore present a `loan_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`, may only withdraw a request whose `loan_id` equals its `X-User-ID`, and may only read its own loan and its own requests.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ControllingClassRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Trust`; ownership checks compare the resource's `trust_id` to `X-User-ID`. A representative may only respond to a consultation whose `trust_id` equals its `X-User-ID`.

A mismatch in either case is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Ownership is evaluated *after* existence, so a caller addressing a row that does not exist is told the row is missing, and is told `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` only for a row that exists but belongs to someone else.

The other five roles — `MasterServicer`, `SpecialServicerAssetManager`, `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer`, `Trustee` and `PlatformAdmin` — carry an **opaque** identity: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The service records it where a workflow demands an audit trail (as `recorded_by_user_id` on a servicing transfer event, `ordered_by_user_id` and `accepted_by_user_id` on an appraisal, `made_effective_by_user_id` on an appraisal reduction, `prepared_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a workout plan, `decided_by_user_id` on a borrower request, `responded_by_user_id` on a consultation, `accrued_by_user_id` on a fee accrual, `certified_by_user_id` on a distribution report) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Authorisation is enforced at three increasingly narrow levels. `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` covers the endpoint as a whole. `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS` covers a specific requested target state: an endpoint's role list is the union over all the target states it can drive, and this narrower rule decides whether *this* role may request *this* target — it is also the refusal used for every target state that only the system may reach. `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` covers the individual human: the same `X-User-ID` may not both author and bless one artefact even when its role permits both operations.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. Both values are echoed back in `meta`, alongside `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied — and `has_more`, which is true whenever rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Several collections deliberately deviate because their natural reading order carries business meaning, and the deviation is stated with the collection in §4: trusts are returned newest first by `created_at` descending; a loan's servicing transfer event log is returned oldest first by `occurred_on` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; advances are returned by `advanced_on` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, which is exactly the order in which the liquidation waterfall reimburses them; consultations are returned by `deadline_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, which is exactly the order in which the deemed-consent sweep processes them; and workout alternatives are returned by `rank` ascending with unranked alternatives last, again with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break.

Filter parameters combine conjunctively: a list request that supplies two filters returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Row Versions

Every stored resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Client-supplied identifiers are never honoured.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every write that touches the row. A version bump is not limited to writes the client aimed at the row directly: when a cascade originating in another aggregate rewrites a field, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes just the same.

There is one deliberate exception. The servicing transfer event log is append-only and immutable: a `ServicingTransferEvent` row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, there is no endpoint that changes it, and the only mutation the service ever makes to an event is the internal marking of `consumed_by_transfer` when a transfer consumes it.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Date Arithmetic

Nothing in this domain is measured against wall-clock time. The service maintains a **logical clock**, and every deadline, coverage window, day count, accrual and overdue calculation is evaluated against it. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that value is the mandatory server startup default. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it, echoing back the new `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no stored row, but every subsequent business-day count, coverage-window test, thirty-over-three-sixty accrual and deadline comparison uses the new instant.

Four distinct date conventions coexist and must not be confused with one another.

**Calendar addition.** Adding calendar days, calendar months or calendar years is ordinary calendar arithmetic. An appraisal's turnaround deadline is its order date plus sixty calendar days. An asset business plan is due sixty calendar days after the loan was transferred to special servicing. An appraisal's coverage window runs for a whole number of calendar months. A real-estate-owned asset's disposition deadline is three calendar years after title vested.

**Business-day counting.** The controlling class's response deadline is counted in business days forward from the instant of issuance. The issue date itself is never counted, whether or not it happens to be a business day. The count then advances one calendar day at a time; a day is counted only if it is neither a Saturday nor a Sunday and is not one of the three pinned holidays `2026-09-07`, `2026-11-26` and `2026-12-25`. When the required number of business days has been counted, the deadline is that final date at `23:59:59.999Z`.

**Thirty-over-three-sixty accrual.** Interest on servicer advances uses the US thirty-over-three-sixty day count with end-of-month clamping, described in full in §3.5.

**Interval inclusivity.** Three different interval conventions are in play and each is load-bearing. An appraisal's coverage window is **half-open**: the window includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, so an appraisal is current while the clock is at or after `coverage_start_date` and strictly before `coverage_end_date`, and a clock exactly equal to `coverage_end_date` is already stale. A proposed lease term is a **closed** interval: both `lease_commencement_date` and `lease_expiration_date` belong to the term, so a new term beginning on the day an existing term expires does overlap it. A fee accrual period is likewise a **closed** interval: both `period_start` and `period_end` belong to the period.

### 2.6. Money, Rates and Rounding

All monetary amounts are decimal numbers carried to two decimal places and rounded **half up**. Every intermediate result that the specification names as a stored field is rounded to two decimal places before it is stored or compared, and every comparison against a threshold is performed on the rounded figure.

Two fields carry finer precision because they are ratios rather than amounts. An appraisal reduction's `ara_percentage` is rounded half up to four decimal places. A fee accrual's `rate_pct` is rounded half up to six decimal places.

One calculation pins its rounding *order* rather than merely its precision: when a workout alternative's projected cash flows are discounted, each individual period is rounded to two decimal places **before** it is added to the running total. Discounting the whole stream and rounding once at the end is wrong; it can differ by one cent, and one cent is enough to reorder two alternatives and change which one the servicing standard obliges the special servicer to put forward.

Threshold comparisons are inclusive or exclusive exactly as stated where each threshold is introduced, and the specification never leaves this to inference. Day counts, ranks, revision counters, information-request counters and fall-through counters are integers.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Aggregates

Two creation operations rewrite state on a parent row that the client must have read first, and both therefore require the client to prove which version of that parent it observed.

Creating a workout plan supersedes any plan already sitting on the loan in `DRAFT` or `SCORED`, moves the loan's servicing dimension and rewrites `active_workout_plan_id`. The request body must therefore carry `expected_loan_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Loan`. The service compares it to the loan row's current `version`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_LOAN_VERSION` and HTTP status 412, and no plan row is created. A caller that re-reads the loan and resubmits with the fresh version succeeds. Note that a loan's `version` advances on *every* write touching the loan row, including writes driven from other aggregates — an appraisal reduction becoming effective, an advance being reversed, a fee being collected — so any such cascade invalidates a previously observed `expected_loan_version`.

Creating a distribution report freezes five snapshot figures off the trust row, and a report built from a stale read would misstate the trust to the trustee, the rating agencies and the certificate holders. The request body must therefore carry `expected_trust_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Trust`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_TRUST_VERSION` and HTTP status 412, and no report row is created. A trust's `version` advances on every recomputation of its appraisal-reduction total, its outstanding-advance total, its realized loss, its control status and its count of loans in special servicing, so the value goes stale readily.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Both checks are evaluated after the requested state change has been found legal, so a caller holding a stale version while attempting an illegal transition is told about the transition first.

### 2.8. The Order in Which Refusals Are Decided

Many requests fail more than one rule at once. The service evaluates its checks in a strict, fixed precedence and returns the **first** rule that fails; no later rule runs, and no later rule's error can mask an earlier one. The precedence is as follows.

1. Both authentication headers are present, non-empty and name one of the seven roles, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The role appears in the endpoint's permitted list, or `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Every identifier appearing as a path segment resolves to a stored row, or the 404 not-found error naming the entity of the *failing segment* — `ERR_TRUST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BORROWER_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_REDUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADVANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORKOUT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORKOUT_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REO_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` respectively. Path segments are validated left to right, so where a loan owns the addressed sub-collection the loan is resolved before the child and the loan's not-found error wins.
4. An entity-backed caller owns the addressed row, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
5. The request body parses; every required field is present; every field has its declared type; every enumerated value is a member of its declared set; every numeric constraint holds; and every conditionally-forbidden field is absent. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An unknown extra field is ignored rather than rejected.
6. Every foreign key in the request body resolves to a stored row, or the 404 not-found error naming that entity: a dangling `trust_id` gives `ERR_TRUST_NOT_FOUND`, a dangling `loan_id` gives `ERR_LOAN_NOT_FOUND`, a dangling `appraisal_id` gives `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`, a dangling `workout_plan_id` gives `ERR_WORKOUT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, a dangling `selected_alternative_id` or `workout_alternative_id` gives `ERR_WORKOUT_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FOUND`, and a dangling `borrower_request_id` gives `ERR_BORROWER_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. Because this ranks below the schema check, a body that is both malformed and carries a dangling foreign key is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. Cross-references agree with one another, or `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. Both rows exist, so this is not a not-found condition; they simply do not belong together.
8. The requested transition from the row's current state to the requested target is a declared transition, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
9. The body's expected parent version matches the parent row's current `version`, or `ERR_STALE_LOAN_VERSION` / `ERR_STALE_TRUST_VERSION`.
10. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the row's authoring user identifier on a blessing operation, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
11. The role is permitted to request this particular target state, or `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`.
12. A borrower request's loan is in special servicing or in workout, or `ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_SPECIAL_SERVICING`.
13. The controlling class's rights on the subject have been satisfied, or `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_SATISFIED`. Approving over a live consent right is the single most serious breach in this domain, which is why it outranks every fiscal and temporal rule beneath it.
14. The servicing standard is satisfied, or `ERR_SERVICING_STANDARD_VIOLATION`.
15. A real-estate-owned sale closes on or before the REMIC disposition deadline, or `ERR_REMIC_DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`.
16. A current accepted appraisal covers the clock, or `ERR_STALE_APPRAISAL`.
17. A lease approval's closed term does not overlap an already-approved term on the same suite, or `ERR_LEASE_TERM_OVERLAP`.
18. A received appraisal's half-open coverage window does not overlap another live window on the loan, or `ERR_APPRAISAL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
19. A special servicing fee accrual's closed period does not overlap a live accrual of the same type on the loan, or `ERR_FEE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
20. A consultation response arrives no later than the deadline, or `ERR_CONSULTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Because this ranks below ownership, a representative of the wrong trust responding after the deadline is told `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
21. The information-request loop has not been used twice already, or `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
22. The revision loop has not been used three times already, or `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
23. The plan being scored has at least one eligible alternative, or `ERR_NO_ALTERNATIVES`.
24. The loan being transferred has an unconsumed qualifying transfer event, or `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_TRANSFER_EVENT`.
25. The loan carries no live accrual of the conflicting fee type, or `ERR_FEE_TYPE_CONFLICT`.
26. The processing deposit meets its type-specific floor, or `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Because this ranks below rule 12, a request against a master-serviced loan with a sub-floor deposit is answered with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_SPECIAL_SERVICING`.
27. A principal-and-interest advance does not exceed the loan's reduced obligation, or `ERR_ADVANCE_EXCEEDS_ARA_ADJUSTED_OBLIGATION`.
28. No advance on the loan has been deemed non-recoverable, or `ERR_NONRECOVERABLE_ADVANCE_OUTSTANDING`.
29. A workout fee is being accrued on a loan that qualifies for one, or `ERR_WORKOUT_FEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
30. The executed plan's selected alternative type agrees with the operation, or `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_TYPE_MISMATCH`. This ranks below rule 7, so naming an alternative belonging to a different plan reports the mismatch of ownership rather than the mismatch of type.
31. A corrected loan returning to master servicing has enough consecutive timely payments, or `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TIMELY_PAYMENTS`.
32. Liquidation expenses are credible and, with the computed liquidation fee, do not exceed gross proceeds, or `ERR_LIQUIDATION_EXPENSE_REQUIRED`.
33. A loan number is unique within its trust, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOAN_NUMBER`.

### 2.9. Flat Collections, Foreign-Key Ownership and Reverse Discovery

Every resource in this service is a top-level collection beneath `/special-servicing`. Ownership is never expressed by nesting a child inside its parent's URL; it is expressed only by a foreign-key field carried in the request body — `trust_id`, `loan_id`, `workout_plan_id`, `appraisal_id`, `source_workout_plan_id`, `borrower_request_id`, `supersedes_alternative_id`. Two read endpoints are the sole exceptions to the flat rule: a loan's servicing transfer event log and a loan's computed asset business plan both hang beneath the loan. On those two, path segments are resolved strictly left to right, so an unknown loan identifier is reported as a missing loan before anything about the sub-resource is considered.

A consequence of the flat topology is that several resources are created by cascade rather than by a client call, and are therefore observable only through the filtered list reads. The provisional appraisal reduction created when an appraisal is accepted, the servicing transfer event auto-recorded when a loan rolls to sixty days delinquent or matures unpaid, and the liquidation fee accrual created by the liquidation waterfall are all invisible until the caller lists the relevant collection filtered by `loan_id`. Every filtered list read exists precisely so that each foreign key is reverse-reachable: loans by `trust_id`, by `servicing_status`, by `payment_status`, by `active_workout_plan_id` and by `correction_source_plan_id`; borrower requests by `loan_id`, `request_type`, `status` and `suite_code`; appraisals by `loan_id`, `appraisal_type` and `status`; appraisal reductions by `loan_id`, `appraisal_id` and `status`; advances by `loan_id`, `advance_type` and `status`; workout plans by `loan_id`, `status`, `recommended_alternative_id`, `selected_alternative_id` and `npv_basis`; workout alternatives by `workout_plan_id`, `supersedes_alternative_id`, `alternative_type` and `status`; consultations by `trust_id`, `workout_plan_id`, `borrower_request_id`, `subject_type`, `rights_type` and `status`; real-estate-owned assets by `loan_id`, `source_workout_plan_id`, `acquisition_type` and `status`; fee accruals by `loan_id`, `fee_type` and `status`; distribution reports by `trust_id`, `report_type` and `status`; and a loan's transfer event log by `event_code`.

A foreign key in a request body that names no stored row is a not-found condition naming the missing entity, as rule 6 of §2.8 sets out. A foreign key that names an existing row belonging to the wrong parent is a different and later failure: `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. The cross-reference agreements the service enforces are these — a consultation's subject must sit inside the named trust (the subject workout plan's loan, or the subject borrower request's loan, must carry that `trust_id`); a workout alternative's `supersedes_alternative_id` must name a sibling in the same plan; a `selected_alternative_id` must name an alternative of the plan being recommended; an appraisal reduction's `appraisal_id` must name an appraisal on the same loan; and a real-estate-owned asset's `source_workout_plan_id` must name a plan on the same loan.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The special servicer's own governance forbids one person from both preparing and blessing the same artefact. Three blessing operations therefore compare the acting `X-User-ID` against the identifier stored when the artefact was authored, and refuse the request with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when they are equal:

- accepting or rejecting an appraisal, compared against the appraisal's `ordered_by_user_id`;
- approving a workout plan, compared against the plan's `prepared_by_user_id`;
- waiving a fee accrual, compared against the accrual's `accrued_by_user_id`.

These are the only three operations that carry the check, and the check bites even when the caller's role plainly permits the operation — the barrier is the individual, not the capacity. A fee accrual created by the liquidation waterfall records the literal string `system` as its `accrued_by_user_id`, so no human is ever blocked from waiving it.

### 2.11. Cascades, Compensation and Ledger Consistency

Many operations in this domain touch more than one aggregate. When they do, every affected row's `version` increments by one and its `updated_at` refreshes, and the roll-up fields that depend on the changed data are recomputed **in full** rather than adjusted incrementally. Four trust-level roll-ups and five loan-level roll-ups behave this way, and their definitions are fixed:

- A trust's `total_ara_usd` is the sum of `ara_amount_usd` over every appraisal reduction whose loan belongs to that trust and whose status is `EFFECTIVE`. It is recomputed from scratch every time any appraisal reduction changes status.
- A trust's `total_advances_outstanding_usd` is the sum, over every advance on a loan of that trust whose status is `OUTSTANDING` or `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED`, of that advance's `amount_usd` less its `reimbursed_principal_usd`.
- A trust's `loans_in_special_servicing_count` is the number of its loans whose `servicing_status` is `SPECIAL_SERVICING`, `IN_WORKOUT` or `REO`.
- A trust's `realized_loss_usd` accumulates and never decreases; only the liquidation waterfall and a write-off add to it. Its `excess_liquidation_proceeds_usd` likewise only accumulates, gathering the residue the waterfall leaves after principal has been fully repaid.
- A loan's `advances_outstanding_usd` is the sum, over its own advances in status `OUTSTANDING` or `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED`, of `amount_usd` less `reimbursed_principal_usd`. Its `fees_payable_usd` is the sum of `amount_usd` over its fee accruals still in status `ACCRUED`. Its `ara_amount_usd` mirrors the single `EFFECTIVE` appraisal reduction on the loan and is `0` when there is none. Its `last_appraised_value_usd` mirrors the `value_usd` of the single `ACCEPTED` appraisal on the loan and is null when there is none. Its own `realized_loss_usd` is written by the waterfall or by a write-off and feeds the trust's figure.

Some cascades are **compensating**: they undo an earlier cascade's effect exactly, restoring the affected figures to the values they held before. Reversing an appraisal reduction and reversing an advance both work this way, and the specification says so explicitly at each site. One downgrade is deliberately *not* compensated: once the controlling class's rights on a pending consultation have been downgraded and that consultation waived, restoring the trust's control status does not restore the consultation. Downgrades of consultation rights are one-way.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Boarding a Securitization Trust and Its Loans

A trust is boarded by the platform operator with a `deal_name` such as "CMBS 2024-C1", a `closing_date` and an `original_controlling_class_balance_usd`, which must be greater than zero and which becomes the denominator of every subsequent control test. Six economic parameters may be supplied and each has a default, so a minimal body is legal: `special_servicing_fee_annual_pct` defaults to a quarter of one percent per year, `workout_fee_rate_pct` and `liquidation_fee_rate_pct` both default to one percent, `advance_interest_rate_pct` defaults to five percent per year, `fee_basis_convention` defaults to `STATED_PRINCIPAL` (its only alternative being `ARA_ADJUSTED`), and `consultation_response_business_days` defaults to ten. On creation the control test runs once and yields `control_status` of `CONTROLLING`; `controlling_class_notional_usd` therefore starts equal to `original_controlling_class_balance_usd`, while `total_ara_usd`, `realized_loss_usd`, `total_advances_outstanding_usd`, `excess_liquidation_proceeds_usd` and `loans_in_special_servicing_count` all start at zero.

Loans are boarded either by the master servicer or by the platform operator. A loan names its `trust_id` and a `loan_number`, and carries the economics of the note: `original_balance_usd` and `current_principal_balance_usd` (the stated principal balance), `note_rate_pct`, `monthly_debt_service_usd` (the scheduled monthly principal-and-interest payment), `maturity_date` and `securitization_value_usd` (the appraised value at securitization, retained purely for reporting). `accrued_unpaid_interest_usd` is optional and defaults to zero. Both `original_balance_usd` and `current_principal_balance_usd` must be greater than zero at creation, and `accrued_unpaid_interest_usd` may not be negative. The `loan_number` must be unique within the trust; a second loan in the same trust bearing an existing number is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOAN_NUMBER`, while the same number in a different trust is perfectly acceptable.

Both balance fields are supplied once at creation and are thereafter server-maintained: `current_principal_balance_usd` is reduced only by step 5 of the liquidation waterfall, and `accrued_unpaid_interest_usd` only by step 4. A new loan starts with `servicing_status` of `MASTER_SERVICING` and `payment_status` of `CURRENT`, has `required_monthly_pi_advance_usd` equal to its `monthly_debt_service_usd`, has `consecutive_timely_payments` at zero, has `ara_amount_usd`, `advances_outstanding_usd`, `fees_payable_usd` and `realized_loss_usd` at zero, has `appraisal_required` and `is_modified` both false, and leaves `transferred_to_special_on`, `transfer_event_code`, `asset_business_plan_due_date`, `last_appraised_value_usd`, `active_workout_plan_id` and `correction_source_plan_id` null. Boarding a loan does not touch the trust's count of loans in special servicing, because a new loan is master-serviced.

### 3.2. Two Status Dimensions, Delinquency, and the Move into Special Servicing

A loan carries **two independent status dimensions on one row**, and neither gates the other except where a precondition says so in as many words. The servicing dimension, `servicing_status`, takes the values `MASTER_SERVICING`, `SPECIAL_SERVICING`, `IN_WORKOUT`, `CORRECTED`, `REO`, `LIQUIDATED` and `WRITTEN_OFF`, of which `LIQUIDATED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are terminal. The payment dimension, `payment_status`, takes the values `CURRENT`, `DELINQUENT_30`, `DELINQUENT_60`, `DELINQUENT_90_PLUS`, `MATURED_UNPAID` and `PAID_OFF`, of which `PAID_OFF` is terminal. A loan deep in workout can be perfectly current on its payments; a master-serviced loan can be ninety days down.

Only the `MasterServicer` drives the payment dimension. Each such call names a `to_status` and an `as_of_date`, and may carry `timely_payment_recorded`, which is consulted only when the target is `CURRENT`. The declared moves are these. `CURRENT → DELINQUENT_30`, `DELINQUENT_30 → DELINQUENT_60` and `DELINQUENT_60 → DELINQUENT_90_PLUS` walk the delinquency ladder one rung at a time; each resets `consecutive_timely_payments` to zero. The cure edge to `CURRENT` is legal from `DELINQUENT_30`, from `DELINQUENT_60`, from `DELINQUENT_90_PLUS` and from `MATURED_UNPAID`; on a cure, `consecutive_timely_payments` increases by one when `timely_payment_recorded` is true and is otherwise reset to zero. There is additionally a self-loop `CURRENT → CURRENT`, which requires `timely_payment_recorded` to be true and increases `consecutive_timely_payments` by one — this is how the three timely payments that a corrected loan needs are accumulated. `MATURED_UNPAID` is reachable from `CURRENT`, `DELINQUENT_30`, `DELINQUENT_60` or `DELINQUENT_90_PLUS`, and only when the supplied `as_of_date` is at or after the loan's `maturity_date` and `current_principal_balance_usd` is strictly greater than zero; the counter resets. `PAID_OFF` is reachable from any state other than `PAID_OFF` itself, and only when both `current_principal_balance_usd` and `accrued_unpaid_interest_usd` are exactly zero. Any pair that is not in this list is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Two rungs of the ladder do more than change a status. Reaching `DELINQUENT_60` **auto-records a servicing transfer event** on the loan: its `event_code` is `MONETARY_DEFAULT_60D`, its `occurred_on` is the `as_of_date` from the request, its `days_delinquent_at_event` is 60, its `evidence_note` reads `auto-recorded on 60-day monetary delinquency`, and its `recorded_by_user_id` is the calling `X-User-ID`. Because that event immediately qualifies (see below), the second consequence of the same call is that the loan becomes eligible for transfer to special servicing. Reaching `MATURED_UNPAID` auto-records an event in the same way, with `event_code` of `MATURITY_DEFAULT`, `occurred_on` equal to `as_of_date`, and `evidence_note` reading `auto-recorded on maturity default`; it too qualifies immediately. Reaching `DELINQUENT_90_PLUS` instead sets `appraisal_required` to true, flagging the asset for a fresh valuation; that flag is cleared only when an appraisal on the loan is accepted. Auto-recorded events are not returned by the call that creates them — the loan's event log is the only place they surface.

Events may also be recorded deliberately, by either the `MasterServicer` or the `SpecialServicerAssetManager`, by naming a `loan_id`, an `event_code`, an `occurred_on`, an `evidence_note` and, where relevant, `days_delinquent_at_event` (which defaults to 0 and may not be negative) or `imminent_default_certified` (which defaults to false). The six recognised event codes are `MONETARY_DEFAULT_60D`, `IMMINENT_DEFAULT`, `MATURITY_DEFAULT`, `BANKRUPTCY`, `RECEIVERSHIP` and `OTHER_MATERIAL_DEFAULT`. On recording, the service decides `triggers_transfer` — whether this event, standing alone, compels transfer — as follows: a `MONETARY_DEFAULT_60D` event qualifies when its `days_delinquent_at_event` is at least sixty, so fifty-nine days does not qualify and sixty days does; an `IMMINENT_DEFAULT` event qualifies only when `imminent_default_certified` is true, which is the master servicer's certification that default is imminent; a `MATURITY_DEFAULT` event qualifies when its `occurred_on` is at or after the loan's `maturity_date`; `BANKRUPTCY` and `RECEIVERSHIP` always qualify; and `OTHER_MATERIAL_DEFAULT` never qualifies. An `OTHER_MATERIAL_DEFAULT` event is still recordable, and still belongs in the servicing file, but a loan whose only event is of that kind cannot be transferred. Every newly recorded event starts with `consumed_by_transfer` false.

Transfer itself — `MASTER_SERVICING → SPECIAL_SERVICING` — is reserved to the `MasterServicer`; any other role reaching the servicing-dimension endpoint for that target is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`. The loan must currently be in `MASTER_SERVICING`, and at least one event on it must have `triggers_transfer` true and `consumed_by_transfer` false; without one, the transfer is refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_TRANSFER_EVENT`. On success the service selects the **earliest** such event, ordering by `occurred_on` ascending and breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, and marks its `consumed_by_transfer` true — a consumed event can never justify a second transfer. The loan's `transfer_event_code` is set to that event's `event_code`, `transferred_to_special_on` is stamped with the current logical clock, and `asset_business_plan_due_date` is set to sixty calendar days after that instant, which is the deadline by which the asset business plan is owed. The trust's `loans_in_special_servicing_count` is recomputed, and both the loan's and the trust's `version` advance by one. The transfer is re-entrant: a loan that completed the correction cycle and returned to `MASTER_SERVICING` may be transferred again on a new, unconsumed qualifying event.

The asset business plan itself is exposed as a computed read rather than a stored resource. It reports the loan's `loan_id`, its `transferred_to_special_on` and `asset_business_plan_due_date`, its `active_workout_plan_id`, the `recommended_alternative_type` of that plan's recommended alternative, its current `ara_amount_usd` and `required_monthly_pi_advance_usd`, plus two derived figures: `days_until_due`, the whole-day difference between the current logical clock's date and the due date, which turns negative once the deadline has passed; and `is_overdue`, which is true exactly when the clock is strictly past the due date. When the loan has never been transferred to special servicing, every one of these fields is null.

### 3.3. Valuing the Collateral: Ordering, Receiving and Accepting Appraisals

Only the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` orders appraisals. An order names the `loan_id`, an `appraisal_type`, the `ordered_on` date on which the engagement letter was issued, the `appraiser_firm` engaged, and optionally `valuation_valid_months`, the length in whole months of the coverage window the eventual valuation will occupy; that field defaults to 12 and must be at least 1. The three appraisal types are `FULL_NARRATIVE`, `UPDATE` and `INTERNAL_VALUATION`, and the choice is consequential: it decides which variant of the appraisal reduction arithmetic will run, and whether liquidation expenses must be reported. A new appraisal starts in status `ORDERED`, has its `due_date` stamped at sixty calendar days after `ordered_on` — the turnaround the pooling and servicing agreement allows — and records the ordering `X-User-ID` in `ordered_by_user_id`. That identifier is what bars the same human from later accepting or rejecting the result.

An appraisal's lifecycle runs `ORDERED`, `RECEIVED`, `ACCEPTED`, with `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `CANCELLED` as terminal outcomes; `SUPERSEDED` is reached only by the system and is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS` if a client asks for it. From `ORDERED`, the asset manager may abandon the engagement outright, moving it to `CANCELLED`, or may report the appraiser's result, moving `ORDERED → RECEIVED`. Reporting the result carries `value_usd`, the as-is value the appraiser reported, which may not be negative, and `as_of_date`, the valuation date. Both are mandatory on that transition, and `as_of_date` must be at or after the appraisal's `ordered_on`; a missing field or a valuation dated before the engagement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The third figure, `estimated_liquidation_expenses_usd`, is the appraiser's estimate of the costs of liquidating the collateral: it is **required** when the `appraisal_type` is `FULL_NARRATIVE` and **forbidden** otherwise, and it may not be negative. Supplying it on an `UPDATE` or `INTERNAL_VALUATION` appraisal, or omitting it on a `FULL_NARRATIVE` one, is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A received valuation claims a **coverage window** on the loan, and windows may not overlap. The window is half-open: `coverage_start_date` is set equal to `as_of_date` and is inside the window, while `coverage_end_date` is set to `as_of_date` plus `valuation_valid_months` calendar months and is outside it. Before storing anything, the service checks every other appraisal on the same `loan_id` whose status is `RECEIVED` or `ACCEPTED` and refuses the transition with `ERR_APPRAISAL_WINDOW_OVERLAP` if any of their windows overlaps the new one. Because the interval is half-open, a new window beginning exactly on an existing window's `coverage_end_date` is adjacent rather than overlapping and is accepted, whereas beginning one day earlier is refused. Appraisals in `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED` release their windows entirely, so a later appraisal may occupy the same span. On success the supplied figures are stored, `coverage_start_date` and `coverage_end_date` are computed as above, `received_on` is stamped with the current logical clock, and the appraisal's `version` advances by one.

Accepting a received appraisal — `RECEIVED → ACCEPTED` — is the single most consequential read of collateral value in the system, and it may not be performed by the person who ordered it: an `X-User-ID` equal to `ordered_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On acceptance the service records the acting user in `accepted_by_user_id` and then performs a four-part cascade. First, any *other* appraisal on the same loan that was itself `ACCEPTED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. Second — caused by the first — if an appraisal reduction pointing at that now-superseded appraisal was `EFFECTIVE`, it is moved to `RECALCULATED`, its `advance_reduction_applies` is set false, the trust's `total_ara_usd` is recomputed and the control test re-runs. Third, a brand-new appraisal reduction is created automatically in status `PROVISIONAL`, carrying the accepted appraisal's `loan_id` and, as its `appraisal_id`, that appraisal's own identifier, with every computed figure filled in by the appraisal reduction arithmetic of §3.4 and its `calculation_basis` chosen from the appraisal's `appraisal_type`. Fourth, the loan's `last_appraised_value_usd` is set to the appraisal's `value_usd` and its `appraisal_required` flag is cleared. The appraisal's `version` and the loan's `version` each advance by one. Because the new reduction is only `PROVISIONAL`, the trust's `total_ara_usd` is **not** yet affected by it; that happens only when the committee makes it effective.

Rejection — `RECEIVED → REJECTED` — is the deliberate counterweight and is deliberately not symmetrical with acceptance. It requires a narrative `rejection_reason` in the body, absent which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it is subject to the same segregation-of-duties bar against `ordered_by_user_id`. Its consequences are the ones acceptance does *not* have: the coverage window is released so that a later appraisal may occupy the same span without triggering `ERR_APPRAISAL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, no appraisal reduction is created, and the loan's `appraisal_required` flag is **not** cleared — the asset still needs a valuation. Only the appraisal's own `version` advances.

### 3.4. Appraisal Reductions, the Reduced Advance Obligation, and Control Termination

An **appraisal reduction amount** is the shortfall between what the trust is owed on a loan and ninety percent of what the collateral is now worth. The arithmetic runs automatically when an appraisal is accepted, and it proceeds in three steps.

The first step establishes the **deficiency base**, and is identical under both variants. Take the loan's `current_principal_balance_usd`; add its `accrued_unpaid_interest_usd`; add the outstanding principal of every advance on the loan whose status is `OUTSTANDING` or `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED`, where each advance's outstanding principal is its `amount_usd` less its `reimbursed_principal_usd`; and add the accrued interest of those same advances, each refreshed to the current logical clock by the advance interest rule of §3.5 before it is used. Round the total half up to two decimal places and store it as `deficiency_base_usd`. That figure is frozen at `calculated_on`, the logical-clock instant at which the reduction was computed, and is never revisited.

The second step establishes the **appraisal credit**, and this is where the two variants diverge. The credit is always ninety percent of a value, but the value differs. When the driving appraisal's `appraisal_type` is `FULL_NARRATIVE`, the `calculation_basis` is `NET_LIQUIDATION_BASIS`: the appraiser's `estimated_liquidation_expenses_usd` is subtracted from `value_usd` first, and ninety percent of that net figure, rounded half up to two decimal places, becomes `appraisal_credit_usd`. When the `appraisal_type` is `UPDATE` or `INTERNAL_VALUATION`, the `calculation_basis` is `GROSS_APPRAISED_BASIS`: ninety percent of the raw `value_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places, becomes `appraisal_credit_usd`. The gross basis always yields the smaller reduction; the net basis shrinks the credit by ninety percent of the estimated liquidation expenses and grows the reduction by exactly the same amount, so two appraisals reporting an identical `value_usd` produce materially different reductions depending on their type.

The third step is the reduction itself, floored at zero: subtract `appraisal_credit_usd` from `deficiency_base_usd`, round half up to two decimal places, and take the greater of that result and zero to obtain `ara_amount_usd`. Separately, `ara_percentage` is the reduction expressed against the stated principal balance — `ara_amount_usd` divided by the loan's `current_principal_balance_usd`, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to **four** decimal places.

A reduction begins life `PROVISIONAL`. Only the `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer` may act on it, moving it `PROVISIONAL → EFFECTIVE` or moving it to `REVERSED`; the fourth status, `RECALCULATED`, is reached only by the system, and both `RECALCULATED` and `REVERSED` are terminal. Making a reduction effective requires that it still be `PROVISIONAL`, that the appraisal it points at still be `ACCEPTED`, and that **no other** reduction on the same loan already be `EFFECTIVE`; a violation of either of the latter two is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. On success the service stamps `effective_on` with the current logical clock, records the acting user in `made_effective_by_user_id`, and sets `advance_reduction_applies` to true — that flag is true exactly while the reduction is `EFFECTIVE` and false at every other time.

What follows is the deepest cascade in the domain, and it runs in four linked stages. First, the loan's `ara_amount_usd` is set to the reduction's `ara_amount_usd` and the loan's `required_monthly_pi_advance_usd` is recomputed. Second — caused by the first — the trust's `total_ara_usd` is recomputed, which in turn recomputes `controlling_class_notional_usd`. Third — caused by the second — the control test re-runs and may move the trust's `control_status` from `CONTROLLING` to `CONSULTING_ONLY`. Fourth — caused by the third — every consultation on that trust that is still `PENDING` with `rights_type` of `CONSENT_RIGHT` has its `rights_type` downgraded to `CONSULTATION_ONLY` and its status set to `WAIVED`, and every workout plan that was sitting in status `CONSULTATION` as the subject of one of those consultations advances to `CONSENT_SATISFIED`. The reduction's `version`, the loan's `version` and the trust's `version` each advance by one.

The **reduced advance obligation** is what the master servicer is actually required to advance each month. While the loan's `ara_amount_usd` is zero, `required_monthly_pi_advance_usd` is simply `monthly_debt_service_usd` — the unreduced scheduled payment. Once a reduction is effective, the obligation is prorated: multiply `monthly_debt_service_usd` by the loan's `current_principal_balance_usd` less its `ara_amount_usd`, divide by that same `current_principal_balance_usd`, and round the result half up to two decimal places. It is recomputed on every change to `ara_amount_usd` — that is, whenever a reduction reaches `EFFECTIVE`, `RECALCULATED` or `REVERSED`.

The **control test** decides whether the controlling class still holds a veto. Compute `controlling_class_notional_usd` as the trust's `original_controlling_class_balance_usd` less its `total_ara_usd` less its `realized_loss_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places and then floored at zero so it never goes negative. Compute the control threshold as twenty-five percent of `original_controlling_class_balance_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. When the notional is greater than or equal to that threshold, `control_status` is `CONTROLLING`; when it is strictly below, `control_status` is `CONSULTING_ONLY`. The boundary is inclusive on the controlling side: a notional exactly equal to the threshold retains control, and one cent less loses it. The test re-runs on every write that changes `total_ara_usd` or `realized_loss_usd` — a reduction reaching `EFFECTIVE`, `RECALCULATED` or `REVERSED`, and the liquidation waterfall or a write-off recording realized loss. Control is genuinely cyclic: it terminates as valuations deteriorate and is restored when a reduction is reversed or recalculated downward, and on restoration future consultations are again issued with `rights_type` of `CONSENT_RIGHT`. What restoration does **not** do is undo the past: consultations already `WAIVED` are not returned to `PENDING`, and rights already downgraded are not upgraded.

Reversal is the compensating counterpart, and it is available from `PROVISIONAL` as well as from `EFFECTIVE`. It requires a narrative `reversal_reason` in the body, absent which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On reversal, `advance_reduction_applies` becomes false, the loan's `ara_amount_usd` returns to zero, and `required_monthly_pi_advance_usd` is restored to `monthly_debt_service_usd`. The trust's `total_ara_usd` returns to exactly the value it held before this reduction became effective, and `controlling_class_notional_usd` and `control_status` are recomputed from that restored total — so a trust that had dropped to `CONSULTING_ONLY` can return to `CONTROLLING`. The one effect that is deliberately not compensated is the consultation downgrade: waived consultations stay waived. The reduction's `version`, the loan's `version` and the trust's `version` each advance by one.

### 3.5. Funding the Asset: Servicer Advances and Advance Interest

When a borrower stops paying, the master servicer keeps the certificate holders whole out of its own funds, and it is entitled to be repaid with interest. Only the `MasterServicer` records an advance, naming the `loan_id`, an `advance_type`, the `amount_usd` advanced — which must be strictly greater than zero — and `advanced_on`, the date the funds actually left the servicer. That date matters twice over: it is the start of interest accrual and the primary sort key by which the liquidation waterfall decides which advance gets repaid first. The three advance types are `PI_ADVANCE` for a scheduled principal-and-interest payment, `TAX_INSURANCE_ADVANCE` for taxes and insurance premiums, and `PROPERTY_PROTECTION_ADVANCE` for expenditure protecting the collateral. A new advance starts in status `OUTSTANDING` with `reimbursed_principal_usd`, `reimbursed_interest_usd` and `accrued_interest_usd` all zero.

Two fiscal guards sit on advance creation. A `PI_ADVANCE` may not exceed the loan's `required_monthly_pi_advance_usd`; one that does is refused with `ERR_ADVANCE_EXCEEDS_ARA_ADJUSTED_OBLIGATION`. The comparison is inclusive, so an amount exactly equal to the obligation is permitted and a single cent more is not — and since the obligation shrinks the moment an appraisal reduction becomes effective, an advance that would have been fine yesterday can be refused today. The second guard is blunter: if **any** advance on the loan has been deemed non-recoverable, no further advance of **any** type may be created, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NONRECOVERABLE_ADVANCE_OUTSTANDING`. Once the servicer has written an asset off as unfundable, it stops funding it entirely. Creating an advance recomputes the loan's `advances_outstanding_usd` and the trust's `total_advances_outstanding_usd`.

Interest on an outstanding advance accrues on a **thirty-over-three-sixty** basis at the trust's `advance_interest_rate_pct`, and the day count is computed with end-of-month clamping. Take the calendar year, month and day of the advance's `advanced_on` and of the current logical clock's date. Clamp each of the two day-of-month values to a maximum of thirty. The day count is then three hundred and sixty times the difference in years, plus thirty times the difference in months, plus the difference between the two clamped day-of-month values. The clamping is what makes the thirty-first of a month accrue exactly as the thirtieth does. If the day count is zero or negative, `accrued_interest_usd` is zero. Otherwise, take the advance's unreimbursed principal — its `amount_usd` less its `reimbursed_principal_usd` — multiply by the trust's `advance_interest_rate_pct` divided by one hundred, multiply by the day count, divide by three hundred and sixty, and round half up to two decimal places. Whenever the figure is refreshed, `interest_accrued_through` is stamped with the logical clock it was measured to. The refresh happens on every read of the advance, on every appraisal reduction computation that includes the advance in its deficiency base, and immediately before the liquidation waterfall reimburses it — which means a read of an advance, or of the loan that owns it, is not a pure read: it moves `accrued_interest_usd` and `interest_accrued_through` forward.

An advance's status may be `OUTSTANDING`, `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED`, `REIMBURSED`, `DEEMED_NONRECOVERABLE` or `REVERSED`, the last three being terminal. `REIMBURSED` and `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED` are reachable only through the liquidation waterfall and are refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS` when a client asks for them directly. Both client-driven exits require a narrative `reversal_reason` alongside the `to_status`, and neither may be requested without one.

The `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer` deems an advance non-recoverable, moving it from `OUTSTANDING` or `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED` to `DEEMED_NONRECOVERABLE`. The service stamps `nonrecoverable_determined_on` with the current logical clock. The unreimbursed principal leaves both the loan's `advances_outstanding_usd` and the trust's `total_advances_outstanding_usd`, but it is emphatically **not** returned to the borrower's obligation — the trust has simply absorbed it. From that moment the loan accepts no new advances at all. The advance's, the loan's and the trust's `version` each advance by one.

The `MasterServicer` reverses an advance, moving it from `OUTSTANDING` to `REVERSED`, and this is the compensating operation: a reversed advance is treated as though it had never been funded. It is available only while nothing has been repaid — both `reimbursed_principal_usd` and `reimbursed_interest_usd` must be exactly zero, and an attempt to reverse an advance that has already received money is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. On reversal, `accrued_interest_usd` is zeroed, and the loan's `advances_outstanding_usd` and the trust's `total_advances_outstanding_usd` return to exactly the values they held before the advance existed. The advance's, the loan's and the trust's `version` each advance by one.

### 3.6. Borrower Requests: Modification, Assumption and Lease Approval

A borrower in distress asks the special servicer for one of three things, and the `request_type` field names which: a `MODIFICATION` of the note's economics, an `ASSUMPTION` by a purchaser of the property, or a `LEASE_APPROVAL` for a proposed tenancy. Requests are submitted either by the `Borrower` itself or by the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` on the borrower's behalf; a `Borrower` caller must present an `X-User-ID` equal to the `loan_id` it names, or be refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Intake is only open on a loan the special servicer actually controls: the loan's `servicing_status` must be `SPECIAL_SERVICING` or `IN_WORKOUT`, and a request against a master-serviced loan is refused with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_IN_SPECIAL_SERVICING`, because such a borrower's requests are the master servicer's business.

Every request carries a `requested_terms_note` describing the relief sought in the borrower's own words and a `processing_fee_deposit_usd`, the non-refundable deposit wired with the request, which may not be negative. The deposit has a **floor that depends on the request type**: twenty-five thousand dollars for a `MODIFICATION`, fifteen thousand for an `ASSUMPTION` and five thousand for a `LEASE_APPROVAL`. A deposit strictly below its floor is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`; a deposit exactly equal to the floor is accepted. The deposit is never refunded — not on denial and not on withdrawal — so it is stored exactly as supplied and never recomputed.

The remaining fields are conditional on the type. A `MODIFICATION` names the `proposed_rate_pct` the borrower wants and the `proposed_maturity_date` it wants to extend to, both null for the other two types. An `ASSUMPTION` names the `assumption_buyer_name`, the proposed transferee, and is otherwise null. A `LEASE_APPROVAL` names the `suite_code` identifying the rentable suite, the `lease_commencement_date` on which the term begins, the `lease_expiration_date` on which it ends, and the `lease_base_rent_annual_usd` the tenant will pay; all four are null for the other two types. The pairing of a loan with a `suite_code` is the occupancy resource the overlap rule guards. A new request starts in status `SUBMITTED` with `info_request_count` at zero.

The intake workflow moves through `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ANALYSIS`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `RECOMMENDED` and `CONSULTATION_PENDING`, ending in one of the three terminal outcomes `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN`. The `SpecialServicerAssetManager` drives the analysis stages, the `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer` decides, the `Borrower` may withdraw, and `CONSULTATION_PENDING` is reached only by the system when a consultation is issued. A role asking for a target it does not own is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`. Each call names a `to_status` and may carry an explanatory `note`.

Analysis begins when the asset manager moves the request to `UNDER_ANALYSIS`, which is legal from `SUBMITTED` and also from `INFO_REQUESTED`. From `UNDER_ANALYSIS` the manager may ask the borrower for more paperwork, `UNDER_ANALYSIS → INFO_REQUESTED`, which increases `info_request_count` by one and **invalidates any analysis already recorded**, clearing both `servicing_standard_margin_usd` and `servicing_standard_conclusion` back to null. Returning from `INFO_REQUESTED` to `UNDER_ANALYSIS` closes the loop. The loop is capped at two round trips: an attempt to request information a third time, when `info_request_count` has already reached two, is refused with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` even though the transition itself is otherwise legal.

The **servicing standard analysis** is recorded by the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` while the request is `UNDER_ANALYSIS`, and it may be recorded repeatedly, each call overwriting the previous conclusion. It carries three figures: `npv_recovery_with_request_usd`, the net present value of the trust's recovery assuming the relief is granted; `npv_recovery_without_request_usd`, the same figure assuming it is refused; and `analysis_discount_rate_pct`, the discount rate the analyst used, which is retained for the servicing file but does not enter the arithmetic. The service derives `servicing_standard_margin_usd` as `npv_recovery_without_request_usd` subtracted from `npv_recovery_with_request_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. It then derives the margin the standard demands: the greater of a fixed floor of fifty thousand dollars and one half of one percent of the loan's `current_principal_balance_usd` rounded half up to two decimal places. When the achieved margin is greater than or equal to the demanded margin, `servicing_standard_conclusion` is `SERVICING_STANDARD_MET`; when it is strictly below, the conclusion is `SERVICING_STANDARD_NOT_MET`. The comparison is inclusive at the boundary, and the two components of the demanded margin swap dominance with loan size: on a small loan the absolute floor governs, on a large loan the relative test does.

Only a `SERVICING_STANDARD_MET` conclusion opens the door to `UNDER_ANALYSIS → RECOMMENDED`; attempting that move on a `SERVICING_STANDARD_NOT_MET` conclusion, or before any analysis has been recorded at all, is refused with `ERR_SERVICING_STANDARD_VIOLATION`. Recommendation additionally requires a **current** valuation: there must exist an appraisal on the request's loan whose status is `ACCEPTED` and whose half-open coverage window contains the current logical clock, meaning the clock is at or after `coverage_start_date` and strictly before `coverage_end_date`. Without one the move is refused with `ERR_STALE_APPRAISAL`; a clock exactly equal to `coverage_end_date` is already stale.

A request that has been recommended is put to the controlling class, which moves it `RECOMMENDED → CONSULTATION_PENDING`. From there the committee decides. `CONSULTATION_PENDING → APPROVED` requires that the controlling class's rights on the request have been satisfied: when the consultation's `rights_type` is `CONSENT_RIGHT`, its status must be `CONSENTED`, `DEEMED_CONSENTED` or `WAIVED`; when its `rights_type` is `CONSULTATION_ONLY`, any status other than `PENDING` suffices. Otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_SATISFIED`. For a `LEASE_APPROVAL`, approval carries one further test: no other request on the same loan and the same `suite_code` that is already `APPROVED` may have a lease term overlapping this one, where both terms are treated as **closed** intervals running from `lease_commencement_date` through `lease_expiration_date` inclusive. A new term starting on the very day an existing term expires therefore does overlap and is refused with `ERR_LEASE_TERM_OVERLAP`; starting the following day is accepted. On approval the service stamps `decided_on` with the current logical clock and records the deciding officer in `decided_by_user_id`, and for a `MODIFICATION` it sets the loan's `is_modified` flag to true.

Denial short-circuits the ladder: `DENIED` is reachable from `UNDER_ANALYSIS`, from `RECOMMENDED` and from `CONSULTATION_PENDING` alike, and it too stamps `decided_on` and `decided_by_user_id`. Withdrawal belongs to the borrower and is legal from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_ANALYSIS` or `INFO_REQUESTED`; the withdrawing caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the request's `loan_id`, and the `processing_fee_deposit_usd` is untouched — the borrower does not get its money back. Every one of these moves advances the request's `version` by one.

### 3.7. Workout Alternatives, Net Present Value Testing and the Consultation Cycle

A workout plan is the special servicer's formal comparison of the routes out of a defaulted loan. Only the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` creates one, naming the `loan_id`, the `discount_rate_pct` at which future recoveries will be discounted — which must be strictly greater than zero — and optionally the `npv_basis`, which selects the discounting variant and defaults to `CONTRACTUAL`, its only alternative being `DELAY_STRESSED`. Because creating a plan supersedes any live plan already on the loan and rewrites the loan's `active_workout_plan_id`, the body must also carry `expected_loan_version` as described in §2.7. On creation the plan starts in status `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at zero, records the creating `X-User-ID` in `prepared_by_user_id`, and leaves `recommended_alternative_id`, `selected_alternative_id`, `scored_on` and `consent_requirement` null. The loan moves `SPECIAL_SERVICING → IN_WORKOUT` and its `active_workout_plan_id` becomes the new plan; any pre-existing plan on the same loan whose status was `DRAFT` or `SCORED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. That servicing-dimension move is system-driven only: a client asking for `IN_WORKOUT` directly is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS` regardless of role.

Alternatives are then added to the draft plan, again by the asset manager, each naming its `workout_plan_id`, its `alternative_type` — one of `MODIFICATION`, `DEED_IN_LIEU`, `NOTE_SALE` or `FORECLOSURE` — its `upfront_cost_usd`, the undiscounted period-zero cost of pursuing it, which may not be negative, and its `projected_cash_flows`. That last field is an array of entries, each an object with a `period_index` and an `amount_usd`; the periods are annual, `period_index` must be at least 1, `amount_usd` may not be negative, the array may not be empty, and no two entries may share a `period_index`. An empty array, a `period_index` below one, or a duplicated `period_index` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An alternative may optionally carry `supersedes_alternative_id`, naming a sibling in the **same** plan that it replaces; naming an alternative belonging to a different plan is refused with `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. When the sibling is validly named, that sibling — which must itself have been `PROPOSED` or `SCORED` — moves to `SUPERSEDED`, its `rank` is cleared to null, and it is permanently excluded from the ranking pool. A new alternative starts in status `PROPOSED` with `discounted_pv_usd`, `npv_usd` and `rank` all null; the five statuses an alternative can hold are `PROPOSED`, `SCORED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `SELECTED` and `DISCARDED`, of which `SUPERSEDED` and `DISCARDED` are terminal.

The plan's workflow is `DRAFT`, `SCORED`, `RECOMMENDED`, `CONSULTATION`, then either `CONSENT_SATISFIED` or `OBJECTED`, then `APPROVED` and finally `EXECUTED`, with `ABANDONED` and `SUPERSEDED` as the other terminal outcomes. The asset manager drives the moves to `SCORED`, `RECOMMENDED`, back to `DRAFT` on revision, to `EXECUTED` and to `ABANDONED`; the `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer` alone drives the move to `APPROVED`; and `CONSULTATION`, `CONSENT_SATISFIED`, `OBJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are system-only targets refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`. Each call names a `to_status` and may carry a `note`; `selected_alternative_id` is supplied only when the target is `RECOMMENDED`.

Scoring — `DRAFT → SCORED` — requires at least one alternative on the plan that is still `PROPOSED` and is not named by any sibling's `supersedes_alternative_id`; a plan whose only alternative has been superseded presents an empty pool and the transition is refused with `ERR_NO_ALTERNATIVES` rather than producing a null recommendation. On success the **net present value arithmetic** runs for every `PROPOSED` alternative in the plan. The discount factor's base is one plus the plan's `discount_rate_pct` divided by one hundred. Under the `CONTRACTUAL` basis, the cash flow of period *n* is divided by that base raised to the power *n*; under the `DELAY_STRESSED` basis every recovery is assumed to slip one full annual period, so the cash flow of period *n* is divided by the base raised to the power *n* plus one. The entries are processed in ascending `period_index` order, and — as §2.6 insists — **each period's discounted figure is rounded half up to two decimal places before it is added to the running total**, which becomes `discounted_pv_usd`. The alternative's `npv_usd` is then `upfront_cost_usd` subtracted from `discounted_pv_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because the stressed basis pushes every flow one period further out, it punishes back-loaded alternatives more than front-loaded ones and can genuinely reverse which alternative comes first. Each scored alternative moves `PROPOSED → SCORED`; alternatives named by a sibling's `supersedes_alternative_id` are moved to `SUPERSEDED` with `rank` null and are excluded from the pool.

**Ranking** then runs immediately over the pool of scored, non-superseded alternatives in the plan. They are sorted first by `npv_usd` descending; ties are broken by the ordinal of the `alternative_type`, ascending, where `MODIFICATION` is 1, `DEED_IN_LIEU` is 2, `NOTE_SALE` is 3 and `FORECLOSURE` is 4 — an ordering that encodes the pooling and servicing agreement's preference for keeping the loan alive; and only when both the net present value and the type are equal does `created_at` ascending decide. An alternative's `id` is never used as a tie-break. Ranks are assigned as consecutive integers starting at 1 in that order, `rank` is written on every alternative in the pool, superseded alternatives keep a null `rank`, and the plan's `recommended_alternative_id` is set to the identifier of the rank-1 alternative. The plan's `scored_on` is stamped with the current logical clock, and the plan's `version` and every touched alternative's `version` each advance by one. Re-scoring after a revision loop recomputes every `npv_usd` from scratch and may well recommend a different alternative.

Recommendation — `SCORED → RECOMMENDED` — requires `selected_alternative_id` in the body, absent which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That identifier must name an existing alternative, or the request is a not-found condition for a workout alternative; the alternative must belong to the plan being recommended, or the request is `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`; and it must equal the plan's `recommended_alternative_id`, or the request is `ERR_SERVICING_STANDARD_VIOLATION` — putting forward anything other than the highest-ranked alternative is a breach of the servicing standard, not a matter of taste. On success the plan's `selected_alternative_id` is set to the supplied value, that alternative moves `SCORED → SELECTED`, and every other scored sibling moves `SCORED → DISCARDED`, retaining its `rank` for the servicing file.

Consultation of the controlling class is issued by the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` against either a recommended workout plan or a recommended borrower request, and the polymorphic `subject_type` decides which. It names the `trust_id` whose controlling class is being consulted; when `subject_type` is `WORKOUT_PLAN` the body must carry `workout_plan_id` and must **not** carry `borrower_request_id`, and when `subject_type` is `BORROWER_REQUEST` the body must carry `borrower_request_id` and must **not** carry `workout_plan_id`. Getting that pairing wrong is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; naming a subject that exists but whose loan does not belong to the named trust is `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. At issue the service stamps `issued_at` with the current logical clock and snapshots the controlling class's standing into `rights_type`: `CONSENT_RIGHT` when the trust's `control_status` is `CONTROLLING`, and `CONSULTATION_ONLY` when it is `CONSULTING_ONLY`. That snapshot may later be downgraded in place but is never upgraded. `deadline_at` is computed as the business-day count of §2.5 from `issued_at` using the trust's `consultation_response_business_days`, and the consultation starts in status `PENDING` with `responded_at`, `response_note` and `responded_by_user_id` null. The subject advances at the same time: a workout plan moves `RECOMMENDED → CONSULTATION` and takes the consultation's `rights_type` as its own `consent_requirement`, while a borrower request moves `RECOMMENDED → CONSULTATION_PENDING`.

The `ControllingClassRepresentative` — and no one else — responds, supplying a `response` of either `CONSENTED` or `OBJECTED` and optionally a narrative `response_note`. The consultation must still be `PENDING`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the consultation's `trust_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and the current logical clock must be at or before `deadline_at` — a response arriving strictly after the deadline is refused with `ERR_CONSULTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The response stamps `responded_at` with the logical clock and records the responder in `responded_by_user_id`. A consent moves the consultation `PENDING → CONSENTED` and cascades a workout-plan subject sitting in `CONSULTATION` to `CONSENT_SATISFIED`, which also refreshes the plan's `consent_requirement` to the consultation's final `rights_type`. An objection moves the consultation `PENDING → OBJECTED`, and what happens to the subject depends entirely on the snapshot: when `rights_type` is `CONSENT_RIGHT` a workout-plan subject in `CONSULTATION` moves to `OBJECTED`, sending the plan back for revision, whereas when `rights_type` is `CONSULTATION_ONLY` the objection is advisory only and the subject moves to `CONSENT_SATISFIED` instead. The four terminal consultation statuses are `CONSENTED`, `OBJECTED`, `DEEMED_CONSENTED` and `WAIVED`.

Silence is treated as consent, but only once the deadline has genuinely passed. The `PlatformAdmin` runs a sweep capability that finds every `PENDING` consultation whose `deadline_at` is **strictly** before the current logical clock — a consultation whose `deadline_at` is exactly equal to the clock is left `PENDING`, and a response is still accepted at that instant — and moves each `PENDING → DEEMED_CONSENTED`. The sweep may be narrowed to one trust by supplying `trust_id`, processes matching consultations in `deadline_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and reports `swept_count` together with `deemed_consented_ids`, the identifiers it swept. Each swept consultation cascades a workout-plan subject in `CONSULTATION` to `CONSENT_SATISFIED`.

The fourth way a consultation ends is `PENDING → WAIVED`, which only the system performs, and it happens in two circumstances: the control-termination cascade of §3.4, in which case the consultation's `rights_type` is also downgraded to `CONSULTATION_ONLY`; and the abandonment of the subject plan. In both cases a workout-plan subject in `CONSULTATION` advances to `CONSENT_SATISFIED`.

Revision closes the cycle. `OBJECTED → DRAFT` is driven by the asset manager and is permitted while the plan's `revision_round` is below three; the fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, leaving abandonment as the only exit. On revision, `revision_round` increases by one, the plan's `recommended_alternative_id`, `selected_alternative_id` and `scored_on` are all cleared to null, and every alternative in the plan whose status is `SCORED`, `SELECTED` or `DISCARDED` returns to `PROPOSED` with `rank`, `npv_usd` and `discounted_pv_usd` all cleared to null. The plan's `version` and every touched alternative's `version` advance by one.

Approval — `CONSENT_SATISFIED → APPROVED` — belongs to the `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer`. The plan must actually be in `CONSENT_SATISFIED`; attempting approval from any other state, including from `CONSULTATION` while the controlling class's consent right is still live, is refused with `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_SATISFIED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the plan's `prepared_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success the approver is recorded in `approved_by_user_id`.

Execution — `APPROVED → EXECUTED` — is driven by the asset manager and requires a current valuation on the plan's loan on exactly the terms described in §3.6: an `ACCEPTED` appraisal whose half-open coverage window contains the current logical clock, failing which the move is refused with `ERR_STALE_APPRAISAL`. On execution `executed_on` is stamped with the logical clock. What execution unlocks depends on the selected alternative's type: a `MODIFICATION` sets the loan's `is_modified` flag to true and makes the loan eligible to be corrected; a `FORECLOSURE` or a `DEED_IN_LIEU` makes it eligible for a real-estate-owned acquisition; and a `NOTE_SALE` makes it eligible for liquidation. The plan's `version` and the loan's `version` each advance by one.

Abandonment is available from `DRAFT`, `SCORED`, `RECOMMENDED`, `CONSULTATION`, `OBJECTED`, `CONSENT_SATISFIED` or `APPROVED`. It clears the loan's `active_workout_plan_id` to null while leaving the loan in `IN_WORKOUT`, and it moves every still-`PENDING` consultation on that plan to `WAIVED`. Both the plan's and the loan's `version` advance by one.

### 3.8. Disposition: Correction, Real-Estate-Owned Management and the Liquidation Waterfall

An executed workout dictates the route out, and the route may not be improvised. Whenever the operation being attempted implies a particular strategy, the service checks the executed plan's selected alternative against it and refuses a mismatch with `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_TYPE_MISMATCH`: correcting a loan requires a `MODIFICATION`, liquidating it directly requires a `NOTE_SALE`, and taking title requires the acquisition to match the selected `FORECLOSURE` or `DEED_IN_LIEU`. A foreclosure plan cannot be used to correct a loan.

Correction — `IN_WORKOUT → CORRECTED` — is the `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer`'s call. The loan must have a non-null `active_workout_plan_id` whose plan is `EXECUTED` with a selected alternative of type `MODIFICATION`, and it must additionally be current on its payments: a `payment_status` other than `CURRENT` blocks the move with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. This is one of the few places where the two status dimensions interact, and it is deliberate — a loan is not corrected until the borrower is actually paying. On success the plan that did the work is remembered in `correction_source_plan_id` and `active_workout_plan_id` is cleared to null. Two linked cascades follow: any appraisal reduction on the loan that was `EFFECTIVE` moves to `REVERSED` with a `reversal_reason` of `loan corrected`, and — caused by that reversal — the trust's `total_ara_usd` is recomputed and the control test re-runs, which may flip the trust's `control_status` back to `CONTROLLING`. The trust's `loans_in_special_servicing_count` is recomputed, and both the loan's and the trust's `version` advance by one.

Handing the asset back — `CORRECTED → MASTER_SERVICING` — belongs to the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` and requires proof that the modification actually worked: the loan's `consecutive_timely_payments` must be at least three. Exactly three succeeds and two does not; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TIMELY_PAYMENTS`. The move recomputes the trust's `loans_in_special_servicing_count`, advances both the loan's and the trust's `version` by one, and is what makes the loan eligible for a workout fee. It also closes the cycle: `MASTER_SERVICING → SPECIAL_SERVICING → IN_WORKOUT → CORRECTED → MASTER_SERVICING`, after which a fresh qualifying event can start the whole sequence again.

Taking title is a separate resource. Only the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` acquires a real-estate-owned asset, naming the `loan_id`, the `source_workout_plan_id` of the executed plan that authorised it, the `acquisition_type` — either `FORECLOSURE` or `DEED_IN_LIEU`, which must equal the source plan's selected alternative type — the `acquired_on` date on which title vested in the trust, and `initial_noi_annual_usd`, the annualized net operating income at acquisition, retained for the disposition plan. The source plan must sit on the same loan, or the request is `ERR_SUBJECT_REFERENCE_MISMATCH`. Acquisition stamps `remic_disposition_deadline` at three calendar years after `acquired_on` — the REMIC rules give the trust that long to dispose of the property — and starts the asset in status `ACQUIRED` with `contract_fallthrough_count` at zero and `list_price_usd`, `gross_sale_proceeds_usd`, `liquidation_expenses_usd` and `closing_date` all null. The loan moves `IN_WORKOUT → REO` and its `active_workout_plan_id` is cleared to null; that servicing-dimension move is system-driven, so a client naming `REO` directly is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`.

The disposition lifecycle then runs `ACQUIRED`, `STABILIZING`, `MARKETED`, `UNDER_CONTRACT` and finally `SOLD`, with `WRITTEN_OFF` as the other terminal outcome. `STABILIZING`, `MARKETED` and `UNDER_CONTRACT` may be driven by either the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` or the `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer`; `SOLD` and `WRITTEN_OFF` belong to the committee alone, and a role asking for a target it does not hold is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`. Stabilisation is legal only from `ACQUIRED`. Going to market is legal from `ACQUIRED`, from `STABILIZING` and from `UNDER_CONTRACT`, and it requires `list_price_usd` in the body, absent which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the supplied asking price is stored. When the previous status was `UNDER_CONTRACT`, the return to `MARKETED` is a contract fall-through and `contract_fallthrough_count` increases by one — the `UNDER_CONTRACT → MARKETED → UNDER_CONTRACT` cycle may repeat without limit. Going under contract is legal only from `MARKETED`.

Closing the sale — `UNDER_CONTRACT → SOLD` — carries three figures: `gross_proceeds_usd`, `liquidation_expenses_usd` and `closing_date`, all three mandatory on that transition and otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `closing_date` must be at or before the asset's `remic_disposition_deadline`; a closing exactly on the third anniversary is permitted and one day later is refused with `ERR_REMIC_DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`. The expenses must be strictly greater than zero — a disposition with no closing costs is not credible — and the expenses together with the computed liquidation fee must not exceed the gross proceeds, since that would drive the waterfall negative; either failure is refused with `ERR_LIQUIDATION_EXPENSE_REQUIRED`. On success the three figures are stored as the asset's `gross_sale_proceeds_usd`, `liquidation_expenses_usd` and `closing_date`, and the **liquidation waterfall** runs.

The waterfall allocates gross proceeds through seven fixed steps, each rounded half up to two decimal places, with every step's remainder feeding the next exactly.

1. **The liquidation fee.** Multiply `gross_proceeds_usd` by the trust's `liquidation_fee_rate_pct` divided by one hundred, round half up to two decimal places, and take the lesser of that figure and the fee cap of one million dollars. The base is pinned to **gross** proceeds and never to the net figure, because taking the fee on a post-fee amount would be circular. A `LIQUIDATION_FEE` fee accrual is created on the loan in status `ACCRUED` with `basis_amount_usd` equal to `gross_proceeds_usd`, `rate_pct` equal to the trust's `liquidation_fee_rate_pct`, `amount_usd` equal to the computed fee, and `accrued_by_user_id` recorded as the literal string `system`.
2. **Available proceeds.** Subtract `liquidation_expenses_usd` and the fee from `gross_proceeds_usd`. Because the guard above has already run, this figure may be zero but is never negative.
3. **Advance reimbursement, oldest first.** Process every advance on the loan whose status is `OUTSTANDING` or `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED`, ordered by `advanced_on` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Refresh the advance's `accrued_interest_usd` to the current logical clock first. Its interest due is that refreshed `accrued_interest_usd` less its `reimbursed_interest_usd`; its principal due is its `amount_usd` less its `reimbursed_principal_usd`. **Interest is always paid before principal within a single advance**: pay the lesser of the available proceeds and the interest due, deduct that from the available proceeds, then pay the lesser of what remains and the principal due and deduct that too, adding both payments to `reimbursed_interest_usd` and `reimbursed_principal_usd` respectively. An advance that receives both its full interest and its full principal moves to `REIMBURSED`; one that receives something but not everything moves to `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED`; one that receives nothing keeps its status. The loan's `advances_outstanding_usd` and the trust's `total_advances_outstanding_usd` are recomputed as this proceeds. The oldest-first ordering is observable: reversing it would fully repay a later advance and leave an earlier one short.
4. **Accrued unpaid note interest.** Pay the lesser of the remaining proceeds and the loan's `accrued_unpaid_interest_usd`, deducting the payment from both the proceeds and that balance.
5. **Principal.** Pay the lesser of the remaining proceeds and the loan's `current_principal_balance_usd`, deducting the payment from both the proceeds and that balance.
6. **Realized loss.** Sum, over the advances still short after step 3, each one's `amount_usd` less its `reimbursed_principal_usd`. The loan's `realized_loss_usd` is that sum added to whatever `current_principal_balance_usd` remains, rounded half up to two decimal places, and the trust's `realized_loss_usd` increases by the loan's figure. Every advance still `OUTSTANDING` or `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED` at this point moves to `DEEMED_NONRECOVERABLE`.
7. **Excess proceeds.** Whatever remains after principal has been fully repaid — zero in the ordinary case — is added to the trust's `excess_liquidation_proceeds_usd`.

Once the waterfall has run, one further cascade completes the disposition: the loan's `servicing_status` becomes `LIQUIDATED`, any appraisal reduction on the loan that was `EFFECTIVE` moves to `REVERSED`, the trust's `total_ara_usd` and `loans_in_special_servicing_count` are recomputed, and the control test re-runs against the newly recorded realized loss — which, depending on the numbers, can restore `CONTROLLING` or confirm `CONSULTING_ONLY`. The asset's, the loan's and the trust's `version` each advance by one.

The same waterfall, in exactly the same seven steps with exactly the same cascade, runs on the direct route out: `IN_WORKOUT → LIQUIDATED`, driven by the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` on a loan whose active plan is `EXECUTED` with a selected alternative of type `NOTE_SALE`. That call supplies `gross_proceeds_usd`, `liquidation_expenses_usd` and `closing_date` in the body — all three mandatory for that target and otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and is subject to the same expense guard, refused with `ERR_LIQUIDATION_EXPENSE_REQUIRED`. A note sale never produces a real-estate-owned asset, because the trust never takes title.

Writing the property off is the other ending, available from `ACQUIRED`, `STABILIZING` or `MARKETED` and reserved to the committee. It has its own cascade rather than the waterfall's: the loan's `realized_loss_usd` becomes its `current_principal_balance_usd` plus its `advances_outstanding_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places; its `current_principal_balance_usd` is set to zero; the trust's `realized_loss_usd` is recomputed and the control test re-runs; every advance on the loan whose status is `OUTSTANDING` or `PARTIALLY_REIMBURSED` moves to `DEEMED_NONRECOVERABLE`; and the loan's `servicing_status` becomes `WRITTEN_OFF`. The asset's, the loan's and the trust's `version` each advance by one. As with `REO` and `IN_WORKOUT`, `WRITTEN_OFF` is not a target a client may name on the loan directly; asking for it is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`.

### 3.9. Fees, Reporting and the Servicing File

The special servicer earns three kinds of fee, and the `fee_type` field names which: a `SPECIAL_SERVICING_FEE` accrued monthly for as long as it holds the asset, a `WORKOUT_FEE` earned on collections received after it has successfully corrected a loan, and a `LIQUIDATION_FEE` earned on a disposal. Accruals are created by either the `MasterServicer` or the `SpecialServicerAssetManager`, naming the `loan_id` and the `fee_type`, and the remaining body fields are conditional on the type. A `SPECIAL_SERVICING_FEE` **requires** `period_start` and `period_end`, the inclusive first and last days of the closed accrual period, and forbids `collection_amount_usd`. A `WORKOUT_FEE` **requires** `collection_amount_usd`, the collection the percentage fee is applied to, which may not be negative, and forbids `period_start` and `period_end`. A `LIQUIDATION_FEE` may never be created here at all — only the liquidation waterfall of §3.8 creates one. Supplying a forbidden field or omitting a required one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every accrual records the creating `X-User-ID` in `accrued_by_user_id`, starts in status `ACCRUED` with `collected_on` and `waived_reason` null, and causes the loan's `fees_payable_usd` to be recomputed.

The monthly **special servicing fee** is a dual-variant calculation whose behaviour the trust chooses through `fee_basis_convention`. Begin with the monthly rate: take the trust's `special_servicing_fee_annual_pct`, divide by one hundred to convert the percentage to a fraction, and divide by twelve months. Then choose the basis. Under `STATED_PRINCIPAL` the `basis_amount_usd` is the loan's full `current_principal_balance_usd`, unaffected by any appraisal reduction, so the servicer keeps earning on the written-down portion of the asset. Under `ARA_ADJUSTED` the `basis_amount_usd` is that balance less the loan's `ara_amount_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places, so the fee shrinks as collateral value falls. Multiply the chosen basis by the monthly rate and round half up to two decimal places; the accrual's `amount_usd` is the **greater** of that raw figure and a monthly floor of five hundred dollars, so a small loan is charged the floor under either convention. The accrual's `rate_pct` records the monthly rate actually used, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to six decimal places.

The same month may not be charged twice. Before storing a `SPECIAL_SERVICING_FEE` accrual the service compares its period against every other accrual on the same loan of the same `fee_type` whose status is not `WAIVED`, treating both periods as closed intervals from `period_start` through `period_end` inclusive, and refuses an overlap with `ERR_FEE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A waived accrual releases its period, so a month whose accrual was waived may be accrued again.

The **workout fee** rewards handing a corrected loan back to the master servicer, and it is available only on a loan that genuinely completed that cycle: the loan's `servicing_status` must be `MASTER_SERVICING` and its `correction_source_plan_id` must be non-null, failing which the accrual is refused with `ERR_WORKOUT_FEE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Its `basis_amount_usd` is the supplied `collection_amount_usd`, its `rate_pct` is the trust's `workout_fee_rate_pct`, and its `amount_usd` is the **lesser** of a per-accrual cap of one million dollars and the collection multiplied by that rate divided by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Workout fee and liquidation fee are mutually exclusive on one loan, because one rewards keeping the loan alive and the other rewards disposing of it. Accruing a `WORKOUT_FEE` on a loan that already carries a `LIQUIDATION_FEE` accrual in any status other than `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_FEE_TYPE_CONFLICT`, and the reverse is equally forbidden. Waiving the earlier accrual clears the conflict.

An accrual ends in one of two terminal states. The `MasterServicer` collects it, `ACCRUED → COLLECTED`, which stamps `collected_on` with the current logical clock and removes the fee from the loan's `fees_payable_usd`; both the accrual's and the loan's `version` advance by one. The `SpecialServicingCommitteeOfficer` waives it, `ACCRUED → WAIVED`, which requires a narrative `waived_reason` in the body — absent which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and which is barred to the very user who created the accrual, refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when `X-User-ID` equals `accrued_by_user_id`. Waiving compensates the loan's `fees_payable_usd` back to exactly its pre-accrual value and releases the combination of loan, fee type and accrual period so that the same month may be accrued afresh; both the accrual's and the loan's `version` advance by one.

Reporting closes the loop back to the capital markets. A distribution report is opened by either the `SpecialServicerAssetManager` or the `Trustee`, naming the `trust_id`, the `report_type` — one of `DELINQUENT_LOAN_STATUS`, `REO_STATUS` and `FINAL_ASSET_DISPOSITION`, the CREFC-style reports the agreement calls for — the `period_end` marking the last day of the reporting period, and the `expected_trust_version` required by §2.7. At creation the service freezes five figures off the trust row so that later trust activity cannot retroactively change what was reported: `loans_in_special_count` from the trust's count of loans in special servicing, `total_ara_snapshot_usd` from its `total_ara_usd`, `total_advances_outstanding_snapshot_usd` from its `total_advances_outstanding_usd`, `realized_loss_snapshot_usd` from its `realized_loss_usd`, and `control_status_snapshot` from its `control_status`, which is therefore either `CONTROLLING` or `CONSULTING_ONLY`. The report starts in status `DRAFT` with `recipients`, `certified_by_user_id` and `distributed_at` null.

Only the `Trustee` drives a report's workflow; any other role naming any of its targets is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_STATUS`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must not be the literal string `system`. Certification, `DRAFT → CERTIFIED`, records the certifying trustee in `certified_by_user_id` and freezes the snapshot permanently — subsequent trust mutations do not touch it. Distribution, `CERTIFIED → DISTRIBUTED`, requires a `recipients` array in the body that is present, non-empty, and drawn entirely from `TRUSTEE`, `RATING_AGENCIES`, `CERTIFICATE_HOLDERS` and `CONTROLLING_CLASS_REPRESENTATIVE`; an empty array or an unrecognised member is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the array is stored and `distributed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock. A report may also be voided, `VOID` being reachable from `DRAFT` and from `CERTIFIED`; `DISTRIBUTED` and `VOID` are the two terminal states. Every one of these moves advances the report's `version` by one.
