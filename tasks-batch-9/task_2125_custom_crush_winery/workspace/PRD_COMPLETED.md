# Custom Crush & Bonded Winery Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A custom crush facility in Napa is, in regulatory terms, a single bonded winery premise that holds other people's wine. Dozens of client wineries deliver fruit to the same crush pad, ferment in the same tanks, age in the same barrel hall and bottle on the same lines — yet every gallon in the building belongs to exactly one client, must be traceable back to the vineyard block it came from, and must be accounted for to the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau on that client's own periodic report. The facility's commercial life and its compliance life are therefore the same ledger seen from two angles: a tank-day is simultaneously a storage charge and a line on a bonded-inventory reconciliation.

This document specifies the API that runs that facility. It governs the full arc of a harvest: signing a client onto a harvest-year contract with a fixed cooperage entitlement, reserving and committing tanks and barrels, weighing trucks in at the crush pad and pricing the crush against a marginal tonnage tier, minting lots and deriving their label eligibility from what actually went into them, trialling and executing blends with full component traceability, scheduling cellar work orders and billing them onto the contract ledger, accruing storage fees against the aging timeline, scheduling bottling runs with a waterfall draw on client-owned dry goods, and finally moving wine out of bond either as a taxpaid removal or as a transfer in bond to another bonded premise.

Two design pressures shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the person who executes cellar work may not bill it, and the officer who prepares a bonded report may not file it. The second is **stale-read protection**: several ledgers in this system are shared by many writers — a contract's running billed total, a vessel's booking calendar — and a writer who reasoned about a figure that has since moved must be told to re-read rather than allowed to overwrite. Both pressures show up throughout as concrete, testable rejections rather than as advisory guidance.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts — authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and versioning, evaluation order, concurrency, and rounding — that apply uniformly to every endpoint in the API. All collections are mounted at the API root as peers; there is no URL nesting between resources, and relationships are carried in payload fields rather than in path structure.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is missing or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The same rejection applies when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the four roles the facility recognises: `FacilityAdmin`, `CellarSupervisor`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `ClientRep`.

A `FacilityAdmin` is the facility's operations manager. This role owns the commercial surface — clients, contracts, cooperage, bottling lines and dry goods — and is the only role permitted to bill a work order onto the contract ledger. A `CellarSupervisor` is the cellar floor lead who runs the physical wine: weigh-ins, sampling, lot creation, vessel commits and releases, production transitions and bottling runs. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns bonded reporting and the removal-from-bond gate; because filing requires two distinct officer user identities, one officer prepares the figures and a different officer files them. A `ClientRep` is the client winery's own winemaker, acting for exactly one client.

Authorization is checked at two levels. First, the caller's role must appear in the authorized-role list published for the target endpoint in §4; a caller whose role is absent from that list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Second, several endpoints multiplex a family of state-machine actions behind a single `action` field in the body. On those endpoints the caller's role must additionally equal the actor declared for the specific action requested, and a role mismatch at this level is also `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. For example, on the blend-trial transitions endpoint only a `ClientRep` may send `SUBMIT` while only a `FacilityAdmin` may send `REQUEST_REVISION`, `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `UNWIND`; on the release-order transitions endpoint only a `ComplianceOfficer` may send `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `EXECUTE` while only a `ClientRep` may send `CANCEL`; on the vessel-condition endpoint only a `CellarSupervisor` may send `CLEAN` while only a `FacilityAdmin` may send `DECOMMISSION` or `RECOMMISSION`; on the vessel-allocation transitions endpoint only a `CellarSupervisor` may send `RELEASE` while only a `FacilityAdmin` may send `CANCEL`; and on the work-order transitions endpoint `SCHEDULE`, `START` and the `IN_PROGRESS` completion are the `CellarSupervisor`'s actions, the completion of a disputed order is the `FacilityAdmin`'s, and `DISPUTE` and `CANCEL` are the `ClientRep`'s.

Identity semantics differ by role. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityAdmin`, `CellarSupervisor` or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a state transition asks for it — as `executed_by_user_id` and `billed_by_user_id` on a `WorkOrder`, and as `prepared_by_user_id` and `filed_by_user_id` on a `TTBReport` — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `ClientRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`. Ownership checks on any resource that resolves back to a client compare that resolution to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). The resolution paths are: a `Client` through its own `id`; a `Contract` through its `client_id`; a `Lot`, `BlendTrial`, `HarvestIntake` or `VesselAllocation` through its `contract_id` to that contract's `client_id`; a `WorkOrder`, `BottlingRun` or `ReleaseOrder` through its `lot_id` to that lot's `contract_id` and on to the contract's `client_id`; a `DryGoodsItem` through its own `client_id`; and a `TTBReport` through its own `client_id`. `Vessel` and `BottlingLine` are facility assets that resolve back to no client at all, so reads of them are never scoped. A `ClientRep` also records identity on the resources it originates: `submitted_by_user_id` on a submitted or revised blend trial, `approved_by_user_id` on an approved trial (stamped by the reviewing `FacilityAdmin`), and `requested_by_user_id` on a release order. List reads performed by a `ClientRep` are always narrowed to that caller's own client, so a `ClientRep` listing clients sees only its own `Client` record; the two collections that resolve to no client — vessels and bottling lines — are returned unnarrowed.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, a `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band, or a negative `offset` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with records sharing one `created_at` — the ordinary case, since the logical clock only moves when it is repositioned — ordered by the sequence in which they were stored. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every record matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, each list endpoint accepts optional equality filters drawn from the resource it returns, and any filter a caller omits simply does not narrow the result. Clients may be narrowed by `status`; contracts by `client_id`, `status` and `harvest_year`; vessels by `vessel_type`, `condition` and `facility_zone`; vessel allocations by `contract_id`, `vessel_id`, `lot_id` and `status`; harvest intakes by `contract_id`, `lot_id` and `status`; lots by `contract_id`, `current_vessel_id`, `parent_lot_id`, `production_status`, `bond_status` and `origin`; blend trials by `contract_id`, `result_lot_id` and `status`; work orders by `lot_id`, `status` and `operation_type`; bottling lines by `status`; bottling runs by `lot_id`, `line_id` and `status`; dry-goods items by `client_id` and `item_type`; TTB reports by `client_id` and `status`; and release orders by `lot_id`, `status` and `release_type`. The read-only storage projection is not a list endpoint: it takes a required `through_at` instant and an optional `variant`, and returns a single object.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Facility Clock

Every stored resource in this system — `Client`, `Contract`, `Vessel`, `VesselAllocation`, `HarvestIntake`, `Lot`, `BlendTrial`, `WorkOrder`, `BottlingLine`, `BottlingRun`, `DryGoodsItem`, `TTBReport` and `ReleaseOrder` — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by a client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the resource was first stored.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative while cascading a change from another resource.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation. A resource that is recomputed twice inside one request — for example a contract whose allocation counters are recomputed after its own status changes — advances its `version` once per recomputation, as the individual workflow descriptions in §3 spell out.

There are no resources in this system that omit `updated_at` or `version`; all thirteen resource types carry the full set.

All server-stamped instants are read from a single logical clock rather than from wall-clock time. That clock governs `created_at` and `updated_at` on every resource, and equally `received_at` on a harvest intake, `committed_at` and `released_at` on a vessel allocation, `aging_start_at` on a lot, `executed_at` on a release order, and `filed_at` on a TTB report. Every window, buffer, calendar-day and deadline rule in this specification is evaluated against the same clock. Before the clock is ever repositioned it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability, available to every role, repositions the clock to a supplied instant carried in the `to_iso8601` field and answers with the resulting `current_utc`; no stored resource is mutated by that call.

### 2.5. The Request Evaluation Ladder

Requests are validated in a strictly ordered ladder, and the **first** rung that fails decides the response. Two rungs are never both reported: if a caller sends an unauthorized role together with a malformed body and a non-existent path id, the role rejection wins, because it sits higher on the ladder. Implementations must therefore evaluate in this order rather than in whatever order is convenient.

1. **Authentication headers present** (rung 1, all endpoints). Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must be present and non-empty, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Known role** (rung 2, all endpoints). `X-User-Role` must be one of `FacilityAdmin`, `CellarSupervisor`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `ClientRep`, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. **Endpoint and action authorization** (rung 3, all endpoints). The caller's role must be authorized for the endpoint and, on action-multiplexing endpoints, must equal the actor declared for the requested `action`; otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
4. **Request shape** (rung 5, all endpoints). The body must carry every required field for the endpoint; each field must have its declared type; every enum-valued field must carry a declared member; every numeric constraint must hold, including the "greater than zero", "at least zero", bounded-range and decimal-place constraints stated per field in §3; and the pagination `limit` must lie between 1 and 100 with `offset` at least 0. Unknown body fields are ignored rather than rejected, and fields that the server itself derives or stamps are likewise ignored when a client supplies them. A failure here is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. **Path identifier existence** (rung 10, every endpoint with an id in its path). Each identifier in the URL must resolve to a stored resource, validated left to right, and the rejection names the collection that failed: `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VESSEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTAKE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRY_GOODS_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TTB_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RELEASE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` — each with status 404.
6. **Body foreign-key existence** (rung 15). Every reference supplied in a request body must resolve: `client_id`, `contract_id`, `vessel_id`, `lot_id`, `line_id`, each entry of `intake_ids`, and each `source_lot_id` inside `components`. The rejection is the matching not-found code above, with status 404. This rung guards `POST /contracts`, `POST /vessel-allocations`, `POST /vessel-allocations/{id}/commit`, `POST /harvest-intakes`, `POST /lots`, `POST /blend-trials`, `POST /blend-trials/{id}/revise`, `POST /work-orders`, `POST /bottling-runs`, `POST /dry-goods-items`, `POST /ttb-reports` and `POST /release-orders`.
7. **Client scoping** (rung 20, every endpoint on which `ClientRep` is an authorized role). A `ClientRep` whose `X-User-ID` does not resolve to the owning client of the target resource is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
8. **Declared transition** (rung 30, every action endpoint). The requested action must be a transition declared out of the target's current state. Repeating a trigger that has already fired, and any action attempted out of a terminal state, both fail here with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
9. **Parent version match** (rung 35). Where an endpoint requires the caller's last observed parent `version`, the supplied value must equal the parent's current `version`: `expected_vessel_version` against the target `Vessel` on `POST /vessel-allocations`, and `expected_contract_version` against the governing `Contract` on `POST /blend-trials/{id}/execute`, `POST /work-orders/{id}/bill` and `POST /contracts/{id}/storage-accruals`. A mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VESSEL_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`, both with status 412.
10. **Cross-entity state gates** (rung 40). The governing `Contract` must be `ACTIVE` where the operation requires it (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`); the governing `Client` must be `ACTIVE` where the operation requires it (`ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`); a vessel receiving a lot must be `CLEAN` (`ERR_VESSEL_NOT_CLEAN`); a line receiving a run must be `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_LINE_UNAVAILABLE`); and a lot that must be housed to advance must have a non-null `current_vessel_id` (`ERR_LOT_NOT_HOUSED`). All five carry status 409. This rung guards `POST /contracts/{id}/transitions`, `POST /vessel-allocations`, `POST /vessel-allocations/{id}/commit`, `POST /harvest-intakes/{id}/transitions`, `POST /lots/{id}/transitions`, `POST /blend-trials/{id}/transitions`, `POST /blend-trials/{id}/revise`, `POST /blend-trials/{id}/execute`, `POST /work-orders/{id}/transitions`, `POST /work-orders/{id}/bill`, `POST /bottling-runs`, `POST /ttb-reports/{id}/transitions` and `POST /release-orders/{id}/transitions`.
11. **Segregation of duties** (rung 50). On `POST /work-orders/{id}/bill` the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `executed_by_user_id`; on `POST /ttb-reports/{id}/transitions` with the file action the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the report's `prepared_by_user_id`. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
12. **Bonded-book freeze** (rung 55, `POST /release-orders/{id}/transitions` with the execute action). The owning client must have no `TTBReport` in status `PREPARED` whose half-open window contains the logical clock's current instant, otherwise `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_LOCKED` (409).
13. **Cross-aggregate ownership** (rung 60). Every intake named in `intake_ids` must belong to the same contract as the lot being created; every `source_lot_id` inside `components` must name a lot on the trial's own contract; the lot named when committing an allocation must belong to that allocation's contract; and every dry-goods item drawn must belong to the client that owns the run's lot. A violation is `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE` (422). This rung guards `POST /lots`, `POST /blend-trials`, `POST /blend-trials/{id}/revise`, `POST /vessel-allocations/{id}/commit` and `POST /bottling-runs/{id}/transitions`.
14. **Uniqueness** (rung 62). `ttb_permit_number`, `vessel_code`, `line_code` and `delivery_ticket` are each unique across all stored rows of their type, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VESSEL_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_CODE` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_DELIVERY_TICKET` respectively; and a new reporting window must not overlap another `TTBReport` for the same client in status `OPEN`, `PREPARED`, `FILED` or `AMENDED`, failing with `ERR_TTB_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. All five carry status 409, and this rung guards `POST /clients`, `POST /vessels`, `POST /bottling-lines`, `POST /harvest-intakes` and `POST /ttb-reports`.
15. **Interval sanity and window containment** (rung 65). An allocation's `end_at` must be strictly after its `start_at` and a report's `period_end` strictly after its `period_start`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; an intake's `received_at` at acceptance, and an allocation's occupancy interval at reservation, must lie inside the governing contract's effective window, otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_WINDOW`; and a report's `period_end` must be at or before the logical clock's current instant when filing, otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. All three carry status 422, and this rung guards `POST /vessel-allocations`, `POST /ttb-reports`, `POST /contracts/{id}/transitions`, `POST /harvest-intakes/{id}/transitions` and `POST /ttb-reports/{id}/transitions`.
16. **Resource temporal overlap** (rung 70, `POST /vessel-allocations` and `POST /bottling-runs`). Vessel occupancy and line occupancy each have their own collision geometry, described in §3.2 and §3.7; the rejections are `ERR_VESSEL_OVERLAP` and `ERR_LINE_SLOT_CONFLICT`, both 409.
17. **Aggregate daily capacity** (rung 72, `POST /bottling-runs`). A line's committed hours for one UTC calendar day may not be pushed past its declared daily capacity, otherwise `ERR_LINE_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
18. **Vessel and contract entitlement** (rung 75, `POST /vessel-allocations` and `POST /vessel-allocations/{id}/commit`). Capacity and entitlement ceilings are enforced with `ERR_VESSEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_TANK_ENTITLEMENT_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_BARREL_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`, all 422, as detailed in §3.2.
19. **Volume sufficiency** (rung 78). Intake eligibility fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_INTAKES` and every draw that would take more wine than a lot holds fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOT_VOLUME`, both 422. This rung guards `POST /lots`, `POST /blend-trials`, `POST /blend-trials/{id}/revise`, `POST /blend-trials/{id}/execute`, `POST /lots/{id}/transitions`, `POST /bottling-runs/{id}/transitions` and `POST /release-orders/{id}/transitions`.
20. **Dry-goods sufficiency** (rung 80, `POST /bottling-runs/{id}/transitions` with the reserve-supplies action). The client's eligible pool must cover every required item type, all-or-nothing, otherwise `ERR_DRY_GOODS_SHORTAGE` (409).
21. **Terminal safety gates** (rung 85). A report may be filed only when its variance is exactly zero (`ERR_TTB_VARIANCE_UNBALANCED`); a client may be closed only when none of its lots is still in bond holding wine (`ERR_CLIENT_HAS_BONDED_INVENTORY`); a contract may be terminated only when it owns no live allocation (`ERR_ALLOCATIONS_OUTSTANDING`). All three carry status 422, and this rung guards `POST /ttb-reports/{id}/transitions`, `POST /clients/{id}/status` and `POST /contracts/{id}/transitions`.
22. **Label and compliance shape** (rung 90). A blend trial may be approved only when its projected appellation label is not the generic one (`ERR_APPELLATION_INELIGIBLE`); a transfer-in-bond release order must carry a destination permit and a taxpaid removal must not (`ERR_DESTINATION_PERMIT_REQUIRED`); an executed blend may be unwound only while its result lot is untouched (`ERR_BLEND_ALREADY_CONSUMED`). All three carry status 422, and this rung guards `POST /blend-trials/{id}/transitions`, `POST /release-orders` and `POST /release-orders/{id}/transitions`.

### 2.6. Stale-Parent Rejection on Shared Ledgers

Four write operations in this system read a figure from a parent resource, reason about it, and then post against it. Because other writers legitimately move those same figures, each of these four operations requires the caller to declare the parent `version` it last observed, and the server rejects the write when that declaration no longer matches. This is not an advisory field: it is a required input on those four endpoints, and it appears on no others. Endpoints outside this list accept no version field at all, and supplying one there is simply ignored as an unknown body field.

- **Reserving cooperage.** A `POST /vessel-allocations` request must carry `expected_vessel_version` equal to the target `Vessel`'s current `version`. A writer that has since booked another window on the same vessel, cleaned it, or taken it out of service has advanced that vessel, which means the current caller's picture of the vessel's booking calendar and `condition` is stale even though the window it asks for might otherwise be perfectly bookable. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VESSEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Note that a successful reservation itself increments the vessel's `version`, so a caller booking two windows back to back must re-read the vessel between them.
- **Executing a blend.** A `POST /blend-trials/{id}/execute` request must carry `expected_contract_version` equal to the governing `Contract`'s current `version`. Another approved trial on the same contract may have executed and drawn down the very source lots this trial names, so the volumes the winemaker reasoned about are stale. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Billing a work order.** A `POST /work-orders/{id}/bill` request must carry `expected_contract_version` equal to the governing `Contract`'s current `version`. Billing selects a volume-discount tier from the contract's `billed_total_cents`, and any other posting — a crush fee, a storage accrual, another work order — legitimately moves that total and would silently reprice this order. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Posting a storage accrual.** A `POST /contracts/{id}/storage-accruals` request must carry `expected_contract_version` equal to the `Contract`'s current `version`. The accrual window opens at the contract's `storage_billed_through` watermark, and any posting that moved the contract forward may already have advanced that watermark; re-posting from a stale watermark would double-bill the client. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.7. Gallons, Cents and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is always an integer count of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere. Wherever a monetary figure is derived, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. Where a formula is defined as a sequence of numbered steps, rounding happens at each numbered step before the result feeds the next, rather than once at the end; and where a formula sums per-subject or per-tier segments, each segment is rounded to whole cents **before** being added to the running total. That ordering is deliberate and pinned: summing unrounded segments and rounding once at the end can differ by a cent, and is not the reading this system uses.

Volumes are carried in gallons to two decimal places, rounded half up, with two documented exceptions: a contract's `tons_received` is carried to four decimal places, and the per-tier tonnage segments used in crush pricing are likewise carried to four decimal places before being priced. Tonnage derived from a truck weight is not rounded before it is multiplied into a yield figure. Percentage figures — varietal and appellation shares, and the volume-discount percentage — are carried to two decimal places, rounded half up, and are compared against their thresholds inclusively, so a share that lands exactly on a threshold satisfies it. A bottling run's `run_hours` is always an exact multiple of one quarter hour and is always rounded **up**. The reconciliation figure `variance_gallons` is compared for exact equality with zero at two decimal places when a report is filed.

Timestamps returned by the API are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional-second digits, including derived instants such as a bottling run's `scheduled_end` and instants the client itself supplied: a supplied instant is parsed, carried to UTC and re-rendered in that shape — any precision finer than a millisecond is dropped rather than rounded — before it is stored on a resource or echoed back, so a client string written in any other shape does not come back the way it was written. Comparisons the server makes against a supplied instant are made against the instant itself at full precision, not against its rendering. Elapsed-day counts — a lot's `aging_days`, an allocation's `occupied_days`, and the day count inside a storage accrual — are whole days obtained by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86400 and discarding any remainder, and are never negative.

### 2.8. Cascade Reconciliation Across the Bonded Estate

Because this is one physical building holding many clients' wine, a single request routinely mutates several resources. The rule is that every touched resource is a full mutation: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1, even when the client never named it in the request. Cascades in this system are described as numbered hops, and each hop is mandatory — an implementation that performs the first hop and skips the second has produced an inconsistent building.

Several fields exist purely as reconciled projections of other resources and must be recomputed the moment their inputs move, never left to drift: a contract's `gallons_allocated` and `barrel_slots_used` are always recomputed from that contract's surviving allocations in status `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`; a client's `bond_gallons_in_bond` is recomputed from that client's lots whenever a lot's `volume_gallons` or `bond_status` changes; a dry-goods item's `reserved_units` and `available_units` are recomputed whenever a reservation is taken, consumed or returned; a client's `last_filed_period_end` is recomputed whenever any of that client's reports is filed or amended; a client's three derived figures are in fact answered from the current state of its lots, release orders and reports every time the client is read, so they can never be stale, and the client counts as a touched resource — advancing its `updated_at` and its `version` by 1 — exactly when a lot of its is created or pressed, when a blend of its is executed or unwound, when one of its bottling runs completes, when one of its release orders is executed, and when one of its reports is filed or amended, whereas a bond-status move that leaves every volume alone, such as approving or cancelling a taxpaid removal, changes the figure the next read returns without advancing the client's `version`; and a lot's `aging_days` is derived from the logical clock afresh on every read rather than stored as an independent fact, while an allocation's `occupied_days` is a stored figure that sits at 0 for the whole life of the booking and is written once, from the clock, at the moment the occupancy ends.

Two conservation invariants fall out of these cascades and are worth stating outright, because compensating operations are specified to preserve them. Within a contract, the total across its lots of `volume_gallons` plus `loss_gallons` is unchanged by executing a blend and unchanged again by unwinding one. Within a client, the total across its dry-goods items of `available_units` plus `reserved_units` is unchanged by reserving supplies and unchanged again by cancelling a reservation.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties and the Bonded-Book Freeze

Two operations in this system may never be performed by the same person who performed the incompatible prior step. A work order is executed by one cellar user and billed by another: the `X-User-ID` billing an order must differ from the `executed_by_user_id` recorded when the order started. A bonded report is prepared by one compliance user and filed by another: the `X-User-ID` filing a report must differ from the `prepared_by_user_id` recorded at preparation. Both violations are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Because these are user-identity comparisons and not role comparisons, two different `ComplianceOfficer` user ids satisfy the filing rule while one officer acting twice does not.

The bonded books are additionally frozen while a period is under preparation. When a client has any `TTBReport` sitting in status `PREPARED` whose window contains the logical clock's current instant, no release order for that client may be executed, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_LOCKED` (409). Recalling the report or filing it lifts the freeze.

### 2.10. Resolving Path Identifiers

Every collection in this API is mounted at the root and every addressable resource is reached through a single identifier segment in the path. Identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the 404 that is returned — the server does not continue to a later segment or to body validation once a path segment has failed. Each collection has its own not-found code, so a missing allocation reports `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` while a missing lot reports `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, and the same codes are reused when the identifier arrives in the body rather than the path.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding: Clients, Contracts and the Entitlement Envelope

Everything in the facility hangs off a **Client** — a bonded winery customer whose wine the facility holds. A `FacilityAdmin` registers one by supplying the client's legal `name`, its `ttb_permit_number`, and a `contact_email` for billing and compliance correspondence. A permit number is only accepted in the federal basic-permit form used by California bonded wineries: the literal characters `BW-CA-` followed by exactly five digits. Permit numbers are unique across the whole facility, and re-using one already held by another client is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER`. A newly registered client opens in `status` `ACTIVE`, with `bond_gallons_in_bond` and `taxpaid_gallons_ytd` both at 0 and `last_filed_period_end` null.

Three figures on a client are reconciled rather than supplied. `bond_gallons_in_bond` is the total `volume_gallons` across that client's lots whose `bond_status` is `IN_BOND`, carried to two decimal places and recomputed on every mutation of a lot's volume or bond status. `taxpaid_gallons_ytd` accumulates the gallons removed under a `TAXPAID_REMOVAL` release order that has reached `EXECUTED`, counting only orders whose `executed_at` falls in the same calendar year as the logical clock's current instant, and is likewise carried to two decimal places. `last_filed_period_end` is the `period_end` of that client's most recent report in status `FILED`, chosen by ordering the client's filed reports by `period_end` descending and breaking ties by `created_at` ascending; it is null when the client has filed nothing.

A client's account moves on a small lifecycle that a `FacilityAdmin` drives. Suspending an `ACTIVE` client sets its `status` to `SUSPENDED` and starts a three-hop cascade. In the first hop, every contract of that client currently in `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED`, each incrementing its own `version`. In the second hop, each contract that just entered `SUSPENDED` cancels every one of its own allocations that stood in `RESERVED`; those allocations keep a null `lot_id` and a null `released_at`, because nothing was ever housed in them. In the third hop, each contract whose allocations were just cancelled recomputes `gallons_allocated` and `barrel_slots_used` from the allocations that survive in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`, incrementing that contract's `version` a second time. Allocations already in `COMMITTED` are deliberately left alone: wine that is physically in a vessel stays housed regardless of the commercial dispute that triggered the suspension.

Reinstating a `SUSPENDED` client returns it to `ACTIVE` and returns every contract of that client that sits in `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`, each incrementing its `version`. Reinstatement is not a full undo — the allocations cancelled by the earlier suspension are **not** restored, and the client must reserve fresh cooperage. Closing a client is the terminal step and may be taken from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, but only when no lot on any of that client's contracts still carries `bond_status` `IN_BOND` together with a `volume_gallons` greater than zero; a client with wine still in bond is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_HAS_BONDED_INVENTORY`. A successful close sets `status` to `CLOSED` and terminates every contract of that client standing in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. `CLOSED` is terminal; no action moves a client out of it.

A **Contract** is the harvest-year agreement between one client and the facility, and it is where every entitlement and rate lives. A `FacilityAdmin` creates one against a `client_id` with a `harvest_year` between 2020 and 2035 inclusive, the `committed_tons` the client undertakes to deliver (greater than zero), and the cooperage envelope: `tank_gallons_entitled` (greater than zero) caps the aggregate gallons the client may hold in tanks at one time, and `barrel_slots_entitled` (zero or more) caps the number of barrels the client may occupy at one time. Three rates are fixed at signing: `crush_rate_cents_per_ton` is the tier-one crush rate in cents per ton, `storage_rate_cents_per_gallon_month` is the storage rate in cents per gallon for a thirty-day month, and `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` is the cellar labour rate in cents per crew-hour; all three must be greater than zero. The commercial window runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive.

A new contract opens in `DRAFT` with `tons_received`, `gallons_allocated`, `barrel_slots_used` and `billed_total_cents` all at 0 and `storage_billed_through` null. Submitting it for signature moves `DRAFT` to `PENDING_SIGNATURE` and requires that `effective_to` be strictly after `effective_from`. From `PENDING_SIGNATURE` the admin may revise, sending it back to `DRAFT`; that loop may repeat any number of times. Activation moves `PENDING_SIGNATURE` to `ACTIVE` and requires the owning client to be `ACTIVE`, failing otherwise with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Suspending an `ACTIVE` contract moves it to `SUSPENDED` and, exactly as in the client-driven cascade, cancels that contract's `RESERVED` allocations and recomputes `gallons_allocated` and `barrel_slots_used` from the survivors. Reinstating a `SUSPENDED` contract returns it to `ACTIVE`, again requiring the client to be `ACTIVE`. Termination may be taken from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, but only when the contract owns no allocation in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATIONS_OUTSTANDING`. `TERMINATED` is terminal. Every one of these six transitions increments the contract's `version` by 1, and a suspension that actually cancelled at least one allocation increments it a second time for the recomputation that follows.

Two contract figures are ledgers rather than settings. `tons_received` is the running total of `net_weight_lbs` divided by 2000 across that contract's intakes standing in `ACCEPTED` or `ASSIGNED`, advanced at the moment an intake is accepted and carried to four decimal places. `billed_total_cents` is the contract-to-date charge: crush fees posted when fruit is accepted, work-order amounts posted when an order is billed, and storage accruals posted against the aging timeline, reduced again when a billed work order is disputed.

### 3.2. Cooperage: Registering Vessels, Reserving Windows and Committing Wine

The facility's cooperage is registered as **Vessels**. A `FacilityAdmin` records each one with a `vessel_code` asset tag — unique facility-wide, with a repeat refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_VESSEL_CODE` — a `vessel_type` of either `TANK` or `BARREL`, a working `capacity_gallons` greater than zero, and a `facility_zone` of `ZONE_A`, `ZONE_B` or `BARREL_HALL`. A vessel enters service with `condition` `CLEAN` and `last_released_at` null.

A vessel's `condition` cycles for the life of the asset. It goes `CLEAN` to `DIRTY` automatically, never by direct request, whenever an allocation on that vessel moves from `COMMITTED` to `RELEASED`; that same event stamps `last_released_at` with the releasing allocation's `end_at`. A `CellarSupervisor` returns it `DIRTY` to `CLEAN` by cleaning it. A `FacilityAdmin` may decommission it from either `CLEAN` or `DIRTY` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, but only when no allocation on that vessel stands in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` — a vessel that still carries one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and may recommission it from `OUT_OF_SERVICE` back to `CLEAN`. Each of these transitions increments the vessel's `version` by 1, and none of them is terminal.

A **VesselAllocation** is a booked occupancy window: one contract's claim on one vessel for a stretch of time. Either a `FacilityAdmin` or a `CellarSupervisor` may reserve one, naming the `contract_id`, the `vessel_id`, the occupancy interval from `start_at` inclusive to `end_at` exclusive, and the `allocated_gallons` of working capacity being held (greater than zero). The reservation must also declare `expected_vessel_version` as described in §2.6. The governing contract must be `ACTIVE`, or the reservation is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The interval must be sane — `end_at` strictly after `start_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` — and must lie inside the contract's effective window, meaning `start_at` at or after `effective_from` and `end_at` at or before `effective_to`, otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_WINDOW`.

Vessel occupancy is exclusive and carries a trailing cleaning buffer. The requested interval, treated as half-open from `start_at` up to but not including `end_at`, must not intersect any other live allocation's interval on the same vessel **extended by a two-day cleaning buffer** — that is, the other allocation is treated as occupying from its `start_at` up to its `end_at` plus 48 hours. Only allocations in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED` are considered live for this test. An intersection is refused with `ERR_VESSEL_OVERLAP`. Three ceilings then apply: `allocated_gallons` may not exceed the vessel's `capacity_gallons` (`ERR_VESSEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); for a `TANK` vessel, the contract's current `gallons_allocated` plus the newly requested `allocated_gallons` may not exceed `tank_gallons_entitled` (`ERR_TANK_ENTITLEMENT_EXCEEDED`); and for a `BARREL` vessel, the contract's current `barrel_slots_used` plus one may not exceed `barrel_slots_entitled` (`ERR_BARREL_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`).

A successful reservation is stored in `status` `RESERVED` with `lot_id`, `committed_at` and `released_at` all null and `occupied_days` at 0. It recomputes the contract's `gallons_allocated` (the total `allocated_gallons` over that contract's `RESERVED` and `COMMITTED` allocations pointing at `TANK` vessels) and `barrel_slots_used` (the count of that contract's `RESERVED` and `COMMITTED` allocations pointing at `BARREL` vessels), incrementing the contract's `version`, and it increments the vessel's `version` as well.

Committing an allocation is the act of physically placing a specific lot into that vessel, and only a `CellarSupervisor` may do it. The request names the `lot_id` being housed. The allocation must be in `RESERVED`, the governing contract must be `ACTIVE`, and the vessel's `condition` must be `CLEAN` — a `DIRTY` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` vessel is refused with `ERR_VESSEL_NOT_CLEAN`. The named lot must belong to the same contract as the allocation, or the request is refused with `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`, and it must not already be housed: a lot whose `current_vessel_id` is already set is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Finally the lot's `volume_gallons` may not exceed the allocation's `allocated_gallons`, which is the second reading of `ERR_VESSEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the allocation moves `RESERVED` to `COMMITTED`, takes the supplied `lot_id`, stamps `committed_at` from the logical clock, and writes the allocation's `vessel_id` onto the lot as its `current_vessel_id`, incrementing the lot's `version`; the request answers with the updated allocation rather than with the housed lot.

Ending an occupancy has two branches that leave the building in materially different states, and the difference matters. **Cancelling** a `RESERVED` allocation — a `FacilityAdmin`'s action — sets it to `CANCELLED`, leaves `released_at` null, recomputes the owning contract's `gallons_allocated` and `barrel_slots_used` from the survivors and increments that contract's `version`, and leaves the vessel entirely untouched: its `condition` does not become `DIRTY` and its `last_released_at` does not move, because no wine was ever in it. **Releasing** a `COMMITTED` allocation — a `CellarSupervisor`'s action — sets it to `RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` from the logical clock, and computes `occupied_days` as the whole days between `committed_at` and `released_at`. Releasing also clears the housed lot's `current_vessel_id` to null and increments that lot's `version`; sets the vessel's `condition` to `DIRTY`, stamps its `last_released_at` with the allocation's `end_at`, and increments the vessel's `version`; and recomputes the contract's `gallons_allocated` and `barrel_slots_used`, incrementing the contract's `version`. Both `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

The same release side effects — including dirtying the vessel and recomputing the contract counters — also fire without any direct request whenever the housed lot leaves the vessel by itself: when a blend merge draws that lot to `CONSUMED`, or when a bottling run drives it to `BOTTLED`. Those two paths are described in §3.5 and §3.7.

### 3.3. The Crush Pad: Weigh-In, Sampling, Acceptance and the Crush Fee

Harvest begins at the scale. A `CellarSupervisor` records each truckload as a **HarvestIntake** against a `contract_id`, capturing the scale's `delivery_ticket` weigh-tag number, the `varietal` delivered — one of `CABERNET_SAUVIGNON`, `MERLOT`, `CHARDONNAY`, `PINOT_NOIR` or `ZINFANDEL` — the `appellation` of the source block, which is one of `NAPA_VALLEY`, `RUTHERFORD`, `OAKVILLE`, `STAGS_LEAP` or `HOWELL_MOUNTAIN`, and the vineyard `block_code` that keeps the fruit traceable. Weights are recorded as `gross_weight_lbs` for the loaded truck (greater than zero) and `tare_weight_lbs` for the empty one (at least zero, and strictly less than the gross weight). Delivery tickets are unique facility-wide; a repeated one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DELIVERY_TICKET`.

The intake is stored in `WEIGHED` with `net_weight_lbs` set to the gross weight less the tare weight, `received_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `brix`, `ph` and `rejection_reason` all null, `lot_id` null, and `estimated_gallons` and `crush_fee_cents` both at 0. The lab then samples the load, moving it `WEIGHED` to `SAMPLED`; sampling requires both a `brix` reading, which must fall between 10.00 and 35.00 inclusive at two decimal places, and a `ph` reading between 2.50 and 4.50 inclusive, and both are stored on the intake.

Acceptance is the commercially significant step, moving the intake `SAMPLED` to `ACCEPTED`. It requires the governing contract to be `ACTIVE`, failing otherwise with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and it requires the load's `received_at` to fall inside the contract's effective window — at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to` — failing otherwise with `ERR_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_WINDOW`. A load may instead be rejected outright from either `WEIGHED` or `SAMPLED`, which stores the supplied `rejection_reason` — an optional field, stored as null when it is omitted — sets the status to `REJECTED`, and moves no contract counter whatsoever. Both `REJECTED` and `ASSIGNED` are terminal states for an intake; `ASSIGNED` is reached only by the system, when a lot is created naming this intake, and is described in §3.4.

Acceptance writes two figures. The first is `estimated_gallons`, the brix-adjusted juice yield. Start from the load's tonnage: the net weight in pounds divided by 2000 pounds to the short ton, carried at full precision and never rounded before it is used. The facility's base yield is 165.0 gallons of juice per ton, and the sugar reading adjusts it. When `brix` is at least 25.00, the yield per ton is reduced — and the curve steps down discontinuously at exactly 25.00, because crossing that line costs a flat 5.0 gallons per ton before any per-degree penalty accrues, and a further 2.5 gallons per ton is then lost for each degree of Brix by which the reading exceeds 25.00, fractional degrees counting proportionally. Otherwise, when `brix` is at most 22.00, the yield per ton is increased by 3.0 gallons. Between those bounds — above 22.00 and below 25.00 — there is no adjustment at all. The load's `estimated_gallons` is its tonnage multiplied by the adjusted gallons-per-ton figure, rounded half up to two decimal places. An intake that never reaches acceptance keeps `estimated_gallons` at 0.

The second figure is `crush_fee_cents`, and it is priced **marginally** against the contract's running tonnage rather than at a single average rate. Read the contract's `tons_received` as it stands immediately **before** this acceptance and call that the starting point; the ending point is that starting figure plus this load's own tonnage. The contract's tonnage runs through three bands: the first 25 tons of the harvest year price at the full `crush_rate_cents_per_ton`, the tons from 25 up to 75 price at nine tenths of that rate, and every ton beyond 75 prices at eight tenths of it. For each band, take the portion of the interval from the starting point to the ending point that falls inside that band; where that portion is greater than zero, multiply its tonnage — carried to four decimal places — by the contract's `crush_rate_cents_per_ton` and by that band's multiplier, round the result half up to whole cents, and only then add it to the running fee. The per-band rounding happens before summation, never after: summing unrounded band amounts and rounding once at the end is a different answer on a load that straddles a band boundary, and is not the reading this system uses.

Acceptance then posts both figures. The contract's `tons_received` advances by this load's net weight divided by 2000, its `billed_total_cents` advances by the computed `crush_fee_cents`, and its `version` increments by 1. When two loads are accepted at the same logical-clock instant, the tonnage accumulator advances in ascending `created_at` order of the intakes, so the marginal tier each one sees is deterministic.

### 3.4. Lot Creation, Composition and the Cellar Production Axis

A **Lot** is the unit of wine the cellar actually works with, and a `CellarSupervisor` mints one from accepted fruit. Creation names the owning `contract_id`, the cellar's `lot_code`, the declared `vintage_year` as a whole number, and the `intake_ids` being folded in. That array must not be empty and every intake it names must stand in `ACCEPTED`; an empty array or an intake in any other status is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_INTAKES`. Every named intake must also belong to the same contract as the new lot, or the request is refused with `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`.

Composition is derived from the fruit, not declared. The lot's `volume_gallons` is the total `estimated_gallons` of the named intakes, rounded half up to two decimal places. Its `brix_at_crush` is the volume-weighted average sugar of that fruit: total each intake's `estimated_gallons` multiplied by its `brix`, divide by the total `estimated_gallons`, and round half up to two decimal places. The dominant `varietal` is the varietal value with the greatest total `estimated_gallons` among the contributing intakes; where two varietals tie on gallons, the winner is the one whose earliest contributing intake has the smaller `created_at`, never the one with the smaller identifier. The dominant `appellation` is found the same way.

Label eligibility follows federal share rules and is expressed in two derived strings. The dominant varietal's share is 100 multiplied by its gallons and divided by the lot's `volume_gallons`, rounded half up to two decimal places; when that share is at least 75.00 percent the lot's `varietal_label` is that varietal's name, and otherwise it is the literal string `RED_BLEND`. The dominant appellation's share is computed the same way; when it is at least 85.00 percent the `appellation_label` is that appellation's name, and otherwise it is the literal string `CALIFORNIA`.

A newly created lot carries `origin` `INTAKE`, `production_status` `JUICE`, `bond_status` `IN_BOND`, `loss_gallons` 0, `cases_bottled` 0, `aging_days` 0, and null values for `current_vessel_id`, `parent_lot_id`, `alcohol_pct` and `aging_start_at`. Its `source_intake_ids` records, in order, the intakes it was built from, and its `component_manifest` is an empty array because it is not a blend. Each named intake moves `ACCEPTED` to `ASSIGNED` and takes the new lot's identifier as its own `lot_id`, and the owning client's `bond_gallons_in_bond` is recomputed.

A lot carries **two independent status axes**, and this is the single most important structural fact about it. The `production_status` axis tracks what the cellar is doing to the wine; the `bond_status` axis tracks its position relative to the federal bond. Neither axis constrains the other's value, and a transition on one axis never writes the other: a lot may sit at `AGING` or `BOTTLED` on the production axis while moving from `IN_BOND` to `TAX_DETERMINED` on the bond axis. The bond axis is described in §3.8.

On the production axis, a `CellarSupervisor` starts fermentation, moving `JUICE` to `FERMENTING`; the lot must be housed at that moment, meaning its `current_vessel_id` is not null, and an unhoused lot is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_HOUSED`. Pressing moves `FERMENTING` to `PRESSED` and books the processing loss: the supplied `loss_gallons` must be at least zero and no greater than the lot's current `volume_gallons` (a larger figure is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOT_VOLUME`), and on success that quantity is added to the lot's cumulative `loss_gallons` and subtracted from its `volume_gallons`. Pressing also fixes the wine's strength for an `INTAKE`-origin lot: its `alcohol_pct` becomes `brix_at_crush` multiplied by 0.55, rounded half up to two decimal places. Starting aging moves `PRESSED` to `AGING` and stamps `aging_start_at` from the logical clock, which is the moment the storage meter starts running. Each of these transitions increments the lot's `version` by 1, and because pressing moves wine off the lot the owning client's `bond_gallons_in_bond` is recomputed as part of the same operation.

Two further production transitions are never requested directly. A lot in `AGING` moves to `CONSUMED` when a blend execution draws its entire remaining volume, and a lot in `AGING` moves to `BOTTLED` when a bottling run drives its `volume_gallons` to exactly zero; both are covered where those workflows are described. `CONSUMED` is the terminal production state, with exactly one compensating way out — the unwinding of the blend that consumed the lot. The derived `aging_days` figure is the number of whole days elapsed from `aging_start_at` to the logical clock's current instant, and is 0 whenever `aging_start_at` is null.

Provenance can be read back at any time. The traceability view of a lot answers with the lot's own `lot_id`, its `origin`, the `source_intake_ids` it was built from, its `component_manifest`, the `ancestor_lot_ids` reachable transitively through blend lineage, and a `total_source_gallons` figure. For an `INTAKE`-origin lot, `ancestor_lot_ids` is empty and `total_source_gallons` is the total `estimated_gallons` of the intakes named in `source_intake_ids`. For a `BLEND`-origin lot, `total_source_gallons` is the total of the component volumes recorded in its `component_manifest`, and `ancestor_lot_ids` lists every source lot named in that manifest and then, transitively, every lot named in those lots' own manifests.

### 3.5. Blending Trials: Projection, Revision Rounds, Execution and Unwind

Blending is a negotiation between the client's winemaker and the facility, and the system models it as a **BlendTrial** that carries its own projected outcome long before any wine moves. A `ClientRep` opens a trial against a `contract_id`, giving it a `trial_name`, the `target_lot_code` the resulting blend lot will carry, a `declared_loss_pct` for racking and blending losses that must lie between 0 and 5 inclusive at two decimal places, and a `components` array. Each entry of that array names a `source_lot_id` and the `volume_gallons` to draw from it, which must be greater than zero; the array must hold at least two entries and no `source_lot_id` may repeat. All three of those are request-shape rules, so a list of fewer than two entries, a repeated `source_lot_id` or a non-positive component volume is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than with any domain code. A new trial opens in `DRAFT` at `round_number` 1, with `result_lot_id`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `revision_note` all null, and its projections already computed.

Those projections are recomputed from scratch at creation, at every submission, and at every revision. The `total_component_gallons` is simply the total of the component `volume_gallons` entries, rounded half up to two decimal places like every other gallon figure. The `projected_loss_gallons` is that total multiplied by `declared_loss_pct` and divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places; the facility does not execute a trial declaring more than a five-percent loss. The `projected_blend_gallons` is `total_component_gallons` less `projected_loss_gallons`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `projected_alcohol_pct` is volume-weighted over the gallons actually drawn rather than over each source lot's whole volume: total, across components, each component's `volume_gallons` multiplied by its source lot's `alcohol_pct`, divide by `total_component_gallons`, and round half up to two decimal places; when any named source lot still has a null `alcohol_pct` the projection is null.

Label projection follows the same share rules the cellar applies to intake lots, but measured over the draw. For each varietal value, total the component `volume_gallons` whose source lot carries that varietal; take the largest such total, multiply by 100, divide by `total_component_gallons` and round half up to two decimal places. When that share is at least 75.00 percent, `projected_varietal_label` is the dominant varietal's name; otherwise it is `RED_BLEND`. The appellation share is computed identically and, at a threshold of at least 85.00 percent, sets `projected_appellation_label` to the dominant appellation's name and otherwise to `CALIFORNIA`. Where two values tie for dominance, the winner is the one contributed by the source lot with the earliest `created_at`, never the one with the smaller identifier. Only lots whose `production_status` is `PRESSED` or `AGING` may be named as components, and naming one that is not is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; other lots on the contract are ignored entirely by the projection because they may not be named in the first place. The three component checks — same-contract ownership, then component `production_status`, then per-lot volume sufficiency — are made in exactly that order, and in exactly that order at creation, at submission and at every revision alike, so a component list that offends more than one of them reports the earliest offence.

Submitting a `DRAFT` trial is the `ClientRep`'s action and moves it to `SUBMITTED`. Submission re-checks everything: the governing contract must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`); the component list must still hold at least two entries with distinct `source_lot_id` values; every source lot must belong to the trial's own contract (`ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`); every source lot must stand in `PRESSED` or `AGING` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`); and every source lot's `volume_gallons` must be at least the component volume being drawn from it (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOT_VOLUME`). On success the acting user's identifier is stored as `submitted_by_user_id` and every projected figure is recomputed.

Review is the `FacilityAdmin`'s. Requesting a revision moves `SUBMITTED` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and stores the supplied `revision_note` — optional, and stored as null when it is omitted — on the trial. The winemaker answers with a resubmission carrying a replacement `components` array and, optionally, a replacement `declared_loss_pct`; that resubmission moves `REVISION_REQUESTED` back to `SUBMITTED`, and the replacement list must satisfy every precondition listed for the original submission. Each resubmission increments `round_number` by 1, clears `revision_note` back to null, re-stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting user, and recomputes every projected figure. This `SUBMITTED` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` to `SUBMITTED` cycle may repeat as many times as the parties need.

Approval moves `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`, stamping `approved_by_user_id` with the approving admin's identifier. It requires `projected_blend_gallons` to be greater than zero, refusing a projection that is not with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` before the label test is reached, and it requires the blend to be AVA-eligible: the facility only executes blends under its bonded label programme when a dominant appellation clears the 85.00 percent share, so a trial whose `projected_appellation_label` has fallen back to `CALIFORNIA` is refused with `ERR_APPELLATION_INELIGIBLE`. A trial may instead be rejected from either `SUBMITTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, which is terminal.

Execution is the `ClientRep`'s action on an `APPROVED` trial and is the largest cascade in the system. It must carry `expected_contract_version` matching the contract's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`; the contract must be `ACTIVE`; and every source lot must **still** hold at least its component volume, failing with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOT_VOLUME`. On success a new lot is minted with `origin` `BLEND`, its `lot_code` taken from the trial's `target_lot_code`, the trial's `contract_id`, `volume_gallons` set to `projected_blend_gallons`, `loss_gallons` set to `projected_loss_gallons`, `alcohol_pct` set to `projected_alcohol_pct`, `varietal_label` set to `projected_varietal_label` and `appellation_label` set to `projected_appellation_label`, its `varietal` and `appellation` set to the dominant values the projection found, a null `vintage_year`, a null `current_vessel_id` and a null `parent_lot_id`, `cases_bottled` at 0, a null `brix_at_crush` and an empty `source_intake_ids`, `production_status` `AGING` with `aging_start_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `bond_status` `IN_BOND`. Its `component_manifest` pins traceability as an array of objects, one per component, each carrying the `source_lot_id`, the `volume_gallons` drawn from it, and a `pct_of_blend` equal to that component's volume multiplied by 100 and divided by `total_component_gallons`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The trial itself moves to `EXECUTED` and records the new lot's identifier as its `result_lot_id`.

The draw-down then cascades through four hops. In the first, each source lot has its component volume subtracted from its `volume_gallons`; a source lot drawn to exactly zero gallons moves `AGING` to `CONSUMED`, takes the new blend lot's identifier as its `parent_lot_id`, and has its `current_vessel_id` cleared to null inside that same mutation, so the source lot's `version` advances exactly once across the whole hop. In the second, each source lot that just entered `CONSUMED` forces its own `COMMITTED` allocation — the one whose `lot_id` names that lot — to move `COMMITTED` to `RELEASED`, stamping `released_at` and computing `occupied_days`. In the third, each allocation that just entered `RELEASED` sets its vessel's `condition` to `DIRTY` and its `last_released_at` to that allocation's `end_at`, incrementing the vessel's `version`. In the fourth, each contract behind those vessels recomputes `gallons_allocated` and `barrel_slots_used` from the allocations surviving in `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`, incrementing the contract's `version`. The owning client's `bond_gallons_in_bond` is recomputed at the end, and the request answers with the executed trial rather than with the newly minted blend lot.

An executed blend can be unwound by a `FacilityAdmin`, but only while the result lot is untouched: its `volume_gallons` must still equal the trial's `projected_blend_gallons` and its `loss_gallons` must still equal `projected_loss_gallons`. If any wine has been bottled out of it, blended onward, or released from it, the unwind is refused with `ERR_BLEND_ALREADY_CONSUMED`. A permitted unwind is a true compensation: each source lot's `volume_gallons` is restored by exactly the component volume drawn at execution, each source lot's `parent_lot_id` returns to null, and a source lot sitting in `CONSUMED` returns to `AGING` — the only path out of that terminal production state. The housing side of the execution is deliberately **not** compensated: the allocation that was forced to `RELEASED` stays released, the vessel it dirtied stays `DIRTY`, and a restored source lot's `current_vessel_id` stays null, so the wine must be re-housed by a fresh reservation. The result lot is emptied, with `volume_gallons` and `loss_gallons` set to 0, `production_status` set to `CONSUMED` and `component_manifest` set to an empty array. The trial moves to `UNWOUND`, which is terminal, and its `result_lot_id` returns to null. The client's `bond_gallons_in_bond` is recomputed, and the contract-level conservation invariant of §2.8 holds across the whole operation.

### 3.6. The Contract Ledger: Cellar Work Orders, Billing Disputes and Storage Accruals

Fermentation protocols are executed as **WorkOrders**, and either a `ClientRep` or a `CellarSupervisor` may raise one. An order names the `lot_id` it acts on and the `operation_type` requested — one of `PUNCHDOWN`, `PUMPOVER`, `RACKING`, `PRESS`, `ADDITION`, `TOPPING` or `FILTRATION` — together with the `scheduled_at` instant the work is wanted, the estimated `labor_hours` per crew member (greater than zero, at two decimal places) and the `crew_size` required (at least one worker). Two optional inputs shape the eventual charge: `material_cost_cents` passes additive and material costs straight through as a whole number of cents of at least zero and defaults to 0 when omitted, and `rush` is a boolean that defaults to false and, when true, adds a quarter again to the base labour charge at billing time. A new order opens in `REQUESTED` with every billing figure at 0 and `actual_labor_hours`, `executed_by_user_id`, `billed_by_user_id` and `dispute_reason` all null.

A `CellarSupervisor` schedules the order, moving `REQUESTED` to `SCHEDULED`; the target lot must not already be finished with — a lot standing in `CONSUMED` or `BOTTLED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and the governing contract must then be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`). The lot test is the earlier of the two, so a finished lot on a lapsed contract reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Starting the work moves `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS` and records the acting supervisor's identifier as `executed_by_user_id`. Completion moves `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED` and requires the actual hours worked per crew member, supplied as `actual_labor_hours` and greater than zero. Because the permitted completing actor depends on the state the completion is taken from — the `CellarSupervisor`'s from `IN_PROGRESS`, the `FacilityAdmin`'s from `DISPUTED` — that particular actor test cannot be made at rung 3 and is made after the declared-transition test instead, though a mismatch is still `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. An order may be cancelled by a `ClientRep` from either `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED`, and `CANCELLED` is terminal.

Billing a `COMPLETED` order is reserved to a `FacilityAdmin` and is subject to the segregation rule of §2.9: the biller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `executed_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Billing must also carry `expected_contract_version` matching the contract's current `version`, and the contract must be `ACTIVE`. The charge is then computed in five pinned steps, each rounded before the next consumes it.

First, `base_labor_cents` is `actual_labor_hours` multiplied by `crew_size` multiplied by the contract's `labor_rate_cents_per_hour`, rounded half up to whole cents. Second, `rush_surcharge_cents` is 25 percent of `base_labor_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, when `rush` is true, and is 0 when it is false. Third, `subtotal_cents` is `base_labor_cents` plus `rush_surcharge_cents` plus `material_cost_cents`. Fourth, the volume-discount tier is selected — once — from an **undiscounted** basis: take the contract's `billed_total_cents` as it stands immediately before this bill and add `subtotal_cents`. If that basis is at least 3,000,000 cents (thirty thousand dollars of contract-to-date business) then `volume_discount_pct` is 10; otherwise, if it is at least 1,000,000 cents (ten thousand dollars) then `volume_discount_pct` is 5; otherwise it is 0. Fifth, `billable_amount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` less the discount, where the discount is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `volume_discount_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents. The tier is never re-selected after the discount is applied, so the result stays well defined even when discounting would drop the running total back below the threshold that selected the tier. The order then moves `COMPLETED` to `BILLED`, `billed_by_user_id` records the acting admin, the contract's `billed_total_cents` increases by `billable_amount_cents`, and the contract's `version` increments by 1. Orders standing in `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `DISPUTED` or `CANCELLED` are never priced.

Billing is not the end of the story, because clients dispute charges. A `ClientRep` may dispute a `BILLED` order, which moves it to `DISPUTED` and stores the supplied `dispute_reason` — optional, and stored as null when it is omitted. The disputed charge is compensated out of the ledger immediately: the contract's `billed_total_cents` is reduced by that order's `billable_amount_cents` and the contract's `version` increments, while `billable_amount_cents`, `subtotal_cents` and `volume_discount_pct` on the order are all reset to 0. A `FacilityAdmin` then settles the dispute by completing the order again from `DISPUTED`, supplying the corrected `actual_labor_hours` (present and greater than zero); that returns the order to `COMPLETED` and clears `dispute_reason` to null. The whole `COMPLETED` to `BILLED` to `DISPUTED` to `COMPLETED` cycle may repeat until the client accepts the charge, and each fresh billing re-selects the discount tier from whatever the contract's ledger reads at that moment.

Storage is the other half of the contract ledger, and it accrues against the aging timeline rather than against any explicit event. A read-only projection answers, for a contract and a `through_at` instant, how much storage would be charged; `through_at` is required and `variant`, when supplied, must name one of the two readings, and a missing, unparseable or unrecognised value of either is a malformed request answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the contract identifier is even resolved; it returns the `contract_id`, the `variant` used, the `through_at` echoed back in that same rendered shape, a `subject_count` naming how many subjects the reading found, and the `charge_cents` those subjects generate. The projection posts nothing.

Every subject's billing window opens at the later of that subject's own clock start and the contract's `storage_billed_through` watermark when it is set, or the contract's `effective_from` when it is not; the window closes at `through_at`. The billable day count is the whole 86400-second days between those two instants, discarding any remainder and never falling below zero. Two readings of "what is being stored" are available, and they answer different commercial questions. The **`OCCUPANCY`** reading bills the wine actually held: its subjects are the contract's lots whose `bond_status` is `IN_BOND` or `TAX_DETERMINED`, whose `production_status` is not `CONSUMED`, and whose `aging_start_at` is not null, and each such lot's clock starts at its own `aging_start_at`. Each lot is charged its `volume_gallons` multiplied by the contract's `storage_rate_cents_per_gallon_month` multiplied by the day count and divided by the thirty days the rate is quoted against, rounded half up to whole cents per lot, and the per-lot charges are summed after rounding. The **`VESSEL_FOOTPRINT`** reading bills the cooperage tied up instead: its subjects are the contract's allocations standing in `COMMITTED` or `RELEASED` with a non-null `committed_at`, each clocking from its own `committed_at`, and each charged its `allocated_gallons` multiplied by the same monthly rate multiplied by the day count and divided by thirty, again rounded half up per allocation and then summed. A released allocation keeps being charged for the days it was held. Whenever a lot under-fills its vessel the two readings diverge, and `VESSEL_FOOTPRINT` returns the strictly larger figure.

`OCCUPANCY` is the default reading: the projection uses it whenever the `variant` query parameter is absent or names it, and `VESSEL_FOOTPRINT` is only ever produced when explicitly requested. Posting an accrual to the ledger is a `FacilityAdmin`'s action, takes `through_at` and `expected_contract_version`, and **always** uses the `OCCUPANCY` reading regardless of anything the projection was asked for. A successful posting adds that reading's `charge_cents` to the contract's `billed_total_cents`, advances `storage_billed_through` to the supplied `through_at` so the next accrual cannot double-bill the same days, and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

### 3.7. Bottling: Line Scheduling, the Dry-Goods Waterfall and Run Completion

Packaging capacity is registered as **BottlingLines**. A `FacilityAdmin` records each one with a `line_code` asset tag that must be unique facility-wide — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_CODE` — a nameplate `cases_per_hour` throughput that is a whole number greater than zero, the fixed `changeover_minutes` appended to every run's occupancy window (a whole number, at least zero), and optionally a `daily_capacity_hours` ceiling on the aggregate hours schedulable in one UTC calendar day, which defaults to 16 hours and must be greater than zero. A line opens `AVAILABLE`, and the same admin may take it out of service to `MAINTENANCE` and return it to `AVAILABLE` as often as the maintenance calendar requires, each move incrementing the line's `version` by 1. Taking a line down does not disturb runs already standing in `SCHEDULED` or `SUPPLIES_RESERVED`: their windows survive untouched.

Dry goods are client-owned and never shared. A `FacilityAdmin` registers a **DryGoodsItem** against a `client_id` with its supplier `sku`, its `item_type` — one of `BOTTLE`, `CORK`, `CAPSULE`, `LABEL` or `CASE_BOX` — and the supplier pack size `units_per_case` (at least one), which is informational for receiving. An optional `initial_units` seeds the shelf as a whole number of at least zero and defaults to 0, so the item is created with `on_hand_units` equal to `initial_units`, `reserved_units` at 0 and `available_units` equal to `on_hand_units`. Later deliveries are received by the same role by supplying the `units` arriving, a whole number greater than zero; the receipt raises `on_hand_units` by that quantity, recomputes `available_units` as `on_hand_units` less `reserved_units`, and increments the item's `version`.

A `CellarSupervisor` schedules a **BottlingRun** by naming the `lot_id` to be packaged, the `line_id` to run it on, the `scheduled_start` of the occupancy window, the `bottle_size_ml` format — 375, 750 or 1500 millilitres — and the `cases_planned` (at least one case). The line must be `AVAILABLE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LINE_UNAVAILABLE`. Scheduling derives three figures. The run's `run_hours` is `cases_planned` divided by the line's `cases_per_hour`, rounded **up** to the next quarter hour, so a run needing two hours and one minute of line time occupies two and a quarter hours. The run's `scheduled_end` is `scheduled_start` advanced by `run_hours` hours and then by the line's `changeover_minutes` minutes, so the changeover is already baked into the window. The run's `gallons_consumed` is the wine the run will draw: the case count multiplied by the bottles in a case multiplied by `bottle_size_ml`, divided by 3785.411784 millilitres to the gallon, rounded half up to two decimal places. A case holds 24 bottles at the 375 millilitre format, 12 at 750, and 6 at 1500. At scheduling the case count used is `cases_planned`.

Line occupancy is a **closed** interval running from `scheduled_start` through `scheduled_end` inclusive, and two runs on the same line collide even when they merely touch at an endpoint — the changeover minutes are already inside the window, so back-to-back scheduling with no gap is a conflict rather than a convenience. A collision with any other run on that line standing in `SCHEDULED` or `SUPPLIES_RESERVED` is refused with `ERR_LINE_SLOT_CONFLICT`; runs that have been cancelled or completed are ignored by the test. A second, aggregate ceiling then applies: total the `run_hours` of that line's runs in `SCHEDULED` or `SUPPLIES_RESERVED` whose `scheduled_start` falls on the same UTC calendar day as the new run, add the new run's own `run_hours`, and refuse the request with `ERR_LINE_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that total exceeds the line's `daily_capacity_hours`. A new run is stored in `SCHEDULED` with `cases_actual` null and `supply_reservations` an empty array.

Reserving supplies moves the run `SCHEDULED` to `SUPPLIES_RESERVED` and draws on the client's shelf through a strict waterfall. First the requirement is sized from the plan: the bottle count is `cases_planned` multiplied by the bottles in a case for the chosen format, and the run needs that many `BOTTLE` units, that many `CORK` units, that many `CAPSULE` units, twice that many `LABEL` units — every bottle carries a front and a back label — and `cases_planned` units of `CASE_BOX`. For each required item type, the eligible pool is the dry-goods items belonging to the client that owns the run's lot (resolved through the lot's contract) whose `item_type` matches and whose `available_units` is greater than zero; items belonging to any other client are never drawn even when the `sku` matches — they are simply not eligible, so a requirement that only another client's shelf could have covered surfaces as the shortage below rather than as `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`, and that cross-reference rejection is reserved for a run whose lot resolves to no owning client at all. The eligible items are ranked by `available_units` descending and, where two items tie, by `created_at` ascending. The waterfall then drains the top-ranked item first, taking the smaller of that item's `available_units` and the units still outstanding, recording a reservation entry naming the `dry_goods_item_id`, the `item_type` and the `units_reserved`, and moving on to the next item until the requirement is met.

The reservation is all-or-nothing across every required type. If any single type cannot be covered in full by the client's eligible pool, the entire reservation is refused with `ERR_DRY_GOODS_SHORTAGE` and no dry-goods item anywhere is touched — a run short only on capsules does not consume the bottles it could have had. On success the run's `supply_reservations` is set to the draw list, and each named item has its `reserved_units` raised by that entry's `units_reserved`, its `available_units` recomputed as `on_hand_units` less `reserved_units`, and its `version` incremented.

Completing a run moves `SUPPLIES_RESERVED` to `COMPLETED` and reconciles plan against reality. The supplied `cases_actual` must be present, at least one case, and no greater than `cases_planned`; all three are request-shape rules answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the `cases_planned` ceiling in particular is tested after the declared-transition test, so a completion aimed at a run in the wrong state reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when the case count is also too high. The run's `gallons_consumed` is then recomputed from `cases_actual` using the same bottles-per-case and millilitre arithmetic, and the lot must still hold at least that much wine or the completion is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOT_VOLUME`. On success each reserved item has both its `on_hand_units` and its `reserved_units` reduced by exactly its `units_reserved` — the glass has physically left the building — with `available_units` recomputed and `version` incremented. Three hops then run against the wine: first, the lot's `volume_gallons` is reduced by the recomputed `gallons_consumed`, its `cases_bottled` is raised by `cases_actual`, and its `version` increments; second, a lot whose `volume_gallons` reached exactly zero in that first hop moves `AGING` to `BOTTLED` and has its `current_vessel_id` cleared to null, which is a second mutation of the lot and advances its `version` again; third, a lot that just entered `BOTTLED` forces its `COMMITTED` allocation to `RELEASED`, which sets that allocation's vessel `condition` to `DIRTY` and stamps `last_released_at`, and recomputes the owning contract's `gallons_allocated` and `barrel_slots_used`. The client's `bond_gallons_in_bond` is recomputed at the end.

Cancelling a run has two readings depending on where it stands. Cancelling from `SCHEDULED` simply moves it to `CANCELLED` and frees the line window for later scheduling; nothing else moves, because nothing was reserved. Cancelling from `SUPPLIES_RESERVED` is a compensation: every dry-goods item named in `supply_reservations` has its `reserved_units` reduced by exactly its `units_reserved` and its `available_units` recomputed, while `on_hand_units` is deliberately left untouched because no glass was consumed, and each such item's `version` increments. The run's `supply_reservations` is then emptied to an empty array and the run moves to `CANCELLED`. Both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and the per-client unit conservation invariant of §2.8 holds across the reserve-and-cancel pair.

### 3.8. Removal from Bond: Release Orders, the Bond Axis and Excise Tax

Wine leaves a bonded premise in exactly one of two ways, and a **ReleaseOrder** is how a client asks for either. A `ClientRep` raises one against a `lot_id`, choosing a `release_type` of `TAXPAID_REMOVAL` — the wine is taxpaid and leaves the bond — or `TRANSFER_IN_BOND` — the wine moves to another bonded premise with the tax still deferred. The order states the `gallons` to remove (greater than zero, at two decimal places) and may state the `cases` shipped as a whole number of at least zero, which is informational and stays null for a bulk removal. A transfer must name the receiving winery's `destination_permit_number`; a taxpaid removal must omit that field or send it as null. Either violation of that pairing is refused with `ERR_DESTINATION_PERMIT_REQUIRED`. A new order opens in `REQUESTED` with `excise_tax_cents` at 0, `executed_at`, `approved_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` all null, and `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the acting winemaker's `X-User-ID`.

Approval belongs to a `ComplianceOfficer` and gates the wine's regulatory position. The order must stand in `REQUESTED`; the lot's `bond_status` must be `IN_BOND` and its `production_status` must be either `AGING` or `BOTTLED`, with either failure refused as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the lot must hold at least the requested `gallons`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOT_VOLUME`; and the owning client must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id` and diverges by type: for a `TAXPAID_REMOVAL` the lot moves along the bond axis from `IN_BOND` to `TAX_DETERMINED`, because the tax has now been determined against it; for a `TRANSFER_IN_BOND` the lot is deliberately **left** at `IN_BOND`, because a transfer never determines tax. In neither case does the lot's `production_status` move.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may instead reject a `REQUESTED` order, which stores the supplied `rejection_reason` — optional, and stored as null when it is omitted — sets the order to `REJECTED` and leaves the lot completely untouched. A `ClientRep` may cancel its own order from either `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`; cancelling an approved taxpaid order is a compensation, returning a lot that this order left at `TAX_DETERMINED` back to `IN_BOND` while leaving `volume_gallons` untouched. `EXECUTED`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal.

Execution is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s final act on an `APPROVED` order and is gated twice: the owning client must have no report standing in `PREPARED` whose window contains the logical clock's current instant, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REPORTING_PERIOD_LOCKED`, and the lot must still hold at least the requested `gallons`, or it is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_LOT_VOLUME`. The two branches then write genuinely different outcomes. On a `TAXPAID_REMOVAL`, the federal excise tax is computed and stored in `excise_tax_cents`, the lot's `volume_gallons` is reduced by the removed `gallons`, the client's `taxpaid_gallons_ytd` advances by the same quantity, and the lot's `bond_status` becomes `REMOVED_TAXPAID` when the removal drains it to exactly zero gallons or returns from `TAX_DETERMINED` to `IN_BOND` when wine remains. On a `TRANSFER_IN_BOND`, `excise_tax_cents` stays 0, the lot's `volume_gallons` is reduced by the removed `gallons`, the client's `taxpaid_gallons_ytd` is **not** touched at all, and the lot's `bond_status` becomes `TRANSFERRED_IN_BOND` when the removal drains it to exactly zero and is otherwise left at `IN_BOND`. Both branches stamp `executed_at` from the logical clock, move the order to `EXECUTED`, increment the lot's `version`, and recompute the client's `bond_gallons_in_bond`.

The bond axis is therefore a small cycle plus two exits. A lot sits at `IN_BOND` until a taxpaid removal is approved, at which point it moves to `TAX_DETERMINED`; a partial removal, or the cancellation of the approved order, returns it to `IN_BOND`, and that `IN_BOND` to `TAX_DETERMINED` to `IN_BOND` cycle may repeat for every partial removal. A taxpaid removal that empties the lot lands it at `REMOVED_TAXPAID`; a transfer in bond that empties it lands it at `TRANSFERRED_IN_BOND`. None of these moves ever writes `production_status`, and none of the production transitions in §3.4 ever writes `bond_status`.

Excise tax is priced marginally against the client's year-to-date taxpaid removals, exactly as the crush fee is priced against contract tonnage. A transfer in bond prices at zero cents and never advances the accumulator. For a taxpaid removal, read the client's `taxpaid_gallons_ytd` as it stands immediately **before** this execution as the starting point, and add the order's `gallons` for the ending point; the accumulator counts only that client's executed taxpaid removals whose `executed_at` falls in the same calendar year as the logical clock. The federal credit structure gives three bands: the first 30,000 gallons of the year price at 7 cents per gallon, the gallons from 30,000 up to 130,000 price at 17 cents per gallon, and everything beyond 130,000 gallons prices at 107 cents per gallon. For each band, take the portion of the interval from the starting point to the ending point falling inside that band; where it is greater than zero, multiply those gallons by that band's cents-per-gallon rate, round half up to whole cents, and add the rounded segment to the running tax. As with the crush fee, each segment is rounded before it is summed.

### 3.9. Bonded Reporting: Periods, Reconciliation Variants and Filing

Each client's bonded inventory is reported to the Bureau on its own periodic **TTBReport**, which a `ComplianceOfficer` opens against a `client_id` for a window running from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive. The window must be sane — `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` — and it must not overlap any other report for the same client standing in `OPEN`, `PREPARED`, `FILED` or `AMENDED`, otherwise `ERR_TTB_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A new report opens in `OPEN` with `variant` at `BONDED`, every gallon line at 0, `amendment_count` at 0, and `prepared_by_user_id`, `filed_by_user_id` and `filed_at` all null. A report has no terminal state; it can always be amended and re-prepared.

Preparing a report runs the full reconciliation and moves it from `OPEN` to `PREPARED`, or from `AMENDED` back to `PREPARED` after a correction. The client must be either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. The preparation action carries an optional `variant` field selecting which reading to apply; the supplied value is stored on the report's `variant`, and when the action omits it the report's `variant` is left exactly where it already stands — `BONDED` on a report no preparation has yet moved off it, and otherwise whatever reading the last preparation stored, so a re-preparation after an amendment keeps `GROSS` unless it names `BONDED` again. Preparation stamps `prepared_by_user_id` with the acting officer's identifier. An officer who is unhappy with the figures recalls the report, moving `PREPARED` back to `OPEN`, which resets every computed figure to 0 and clears `prepared_by_user_id` to null; that recall loop may repeat until the figures balance.

Every line is filtered to the reporting client: a lot belongs to the client through its contract, and a release order belongs through its lot's contract, so another client's wine never enters any line. The `opening_gallons` line is the `closing_gallons` of that client's own previously `FILED` reports whose `period_end` falls at or before this report's `period_start`, choosing the one with the latest `period_end` and breaking ties by the earliest `created_at`; it is 0 when no such report exists. The `removed_taxpaid_gallons` line totals the `gallons` of that client's `EXECUTED` release orders of type `TAXPAID_REMOVAL` whose `executed_at` falls inside the window, and `removed_in_bond_gallons` totals the same for orders of type `TRANSFER_IN_BOND`. The `closing_gallons` line is a physical count taken at preparation time: the total `volume_gallons` across that client's lots whose `bond_status` is `IN_BOND` at that instant.

The two remaining lines depend on the `variant`, because the industry recognises two defensible readings of what a blend merge is. Under **`BONDED`**, the default, a blend merge is an internal bond-to-bond move that produces nothing: `produced_gallons` totals the `volume_gallons` at creation of that client's lots whose `origin` is `INTAKE` and whose `created_at` falls inside the window, and `loss_gallons` totals the losses booked inside the window — press losses plus declared blending losses — plus the total `gallons_consumed` of that client's runs completed inside the window, because bulk wine packaged into cased goods leaves the bulk bonded account and must appear on that line or the physical closing count will not reconcile. Under **`GROSS`**, every minted lot counts as production and every gallon drawn into a blend counts as a removal to internal transfer: `produced_gallons` is the `BONDED` figure plus the total `volume_gallons` at creation of that client's lots whose `origin` is `BLEND` and whose `created_at` falls inside the window, and `loss_gallons` is the `BONDED` figure less the total `projected_loss_gallons` of the trials executed inside the window plus the total `total_component_gallons` of those same trials. For identical data `GROSS` reports a larger `produced_gallons` and a larger `loss_gallons` than `BONDED`, and both readings still reconcile to a zero variance.

The `variance_gallons` line closes the books: take `closing_gallons` and subtract from it the quantity formed by `opening_gallons` plus `produced_gallons`, less `removed_taxpaid_gallons`, less `removed_in_bond_gallons`, less `loss_gallons`; round the result half up to two decimal places. Filing is permitted only when that figure is exactly 0.00 — an unbalanced report is refused with `ERR_TTB_VARIANCE_UNBALANCED`, which is the point of the recall loop.

Filing moves `PREPARED` to `FILED` and is the second segregation-of-duties gate: the filing officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the report's `prepared_by_user_id`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The period must also be closed — `period_end` at or before the logical clock's current instant — or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. A successful filing stamps `filed_by_user_id` and `filed_at`, and recomputes the client's `last_filed_period_end`. Corrections are made by amending a `FILED` report, which moves it to `AMENDED`, increments `amendment_count` by 1, leaves `filed_by_user_id` and `filed_at` standing from the filing being amended, and recomputes the client's `last_filed_period_end` because this report no longer counts as filed; preparing it again under whatever `variant` the correction calls for returns it to `PREPARED`, from which it can be filed once more. That `FILED` to `AMENDED` to `PREPARED` to `FILED` cycle repeats for every correction the Bureau requires.
