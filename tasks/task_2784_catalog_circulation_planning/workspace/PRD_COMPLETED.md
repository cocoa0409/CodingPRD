# Catalog Circulation Planning API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A catalog retailer lives or dies by circulation economics. Every book that leaves the bindery costs real money in printing, paper and postage before a single order arrives, and the decision of *which* names receive *which* book on *which* in-home date is the single largest controllable lever on the season's profit. Today that decision is spread across a circulation planner's spreadsheet, a list broker's email thread, a mail shop's presort report and an analyst's matchback workbook — four artefacts that disagree with one another by the time the plan is approved. The Catalog Circulation Planning service replaces all four with one authoritative model of the season: the housefile segmentation, the rented prospect universe, the merge-purge that reconciles them, the drop-by-drop circulation plan that spends the budget, and the measurement reads that tell the retailer whether the spend worked.

The service is built around a small number of hard commitments. Money never leaks: a plan's budget is always exactly the sum of what it has committed and what it still has free, and every void, suppression or cancellation credits back precisely what it debited. Names never multiply: the gross universe a merge-purge run inspects always equals the mailable universe plus the names it suppressed plus the duplicates it removed. Page space never overflows: a drop's square-inch capacity is always exactly the space its placements consume plus the space still open. And nobody signs their own work: the operator who costs a plan and the operator who submits it for review are both barred from approving it.

This document is the complete specification of that service. It is written for the engineer who will implement it, and it assumes no access to any other artefact. All resources are mounted under the `/catalog-circulation` prefix, and a path is matched whether or not it carries a trailing slash. All monetary amounts are integer cents, all rates are integer basis points (one hundredth of one percent), all name and piece counts are integers, and all timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional-second digits.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. A request whose `X-User-ID` header is absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before anything else about the request is examined. A request whose role is not listed among the endpoint's authorized roles is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, and so is a request whose `X-User-Role` header is absent, empty or holds a string that is not one of the five role names — a missing role is an authorization failure rather than an authentication failure, so it yields the 403 and never `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. This role check happens *before* any identifier in the URL path is resolved, so a caller in the wrong role who also names a non-existent resource receives the 403 role rejection rather than a 404 — the service never confirms the existence of a resource to a caller who is not entitled to touch that class of resource at all.

Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `CirculationPlanner`, `CirculationDirector`, `ListBroker`, and `MarketingAnalyst`.

There is no user entity in this domain. For all five roles — `PlatformAdmin`, `CirculationPlanner`, `CirculationDirector`, `ListBroker` and `MarketingAnalyst` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server does record it in three places where a transition explicitly stamps the acting operator onto a `CirculationPlan`: `costed_by_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id`. Those three fields hold raw `X-User-ID` strings, and the segregation-of-duties guard on approval compares the incoming `X-User-ID` against the first two of them. No other ownership or tenancy check exists: any operator holding a given role may act on any resource that role is authorized for.

The division of labour is deliberate and each role is fenced off from work it should not judge or perform. A `PlatformAdmin` owns the configuration surface — the season rate card, the contact-frequency governance rules, the logical clock and the ingested ecommerce order feed — and deliberately cannot plan, cost, approve, buy lists or measure. A `CirculationPlanner` authors and executes circulation: housefile segments, plans, drops, audience lines, allocation, page placements, presort batches and the production transitions through mail and delivery; a planner may cost a plan and submit it for review, but may never approve one. A `CirculationDirector` holds budget authority — approve, send back for revision, or kill the plan — and cannot author any of the artefacts being judged. A `ListBroker` owns outside-list procurement, cooperative-database modeling and the merge-purge universe build, but cannot allocate circulation against the names it delivers. A `MarketingAnalyst` owns measurement: manual contact logging, test cells, and matchback attribution reads. All five roles may perform every read (`GET`) operation the service exposes, subject to the per-endpoint role lists in §4, which are narrower for a handful of reads.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the host's real clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.11.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx and 5xx status codes alike) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Deterministic Collection Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0, may not be negative, and has no upper bound — an `offset` at or past the end of the matching set is legitimate and simply returns an empty `data` array. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not the decimal spelling of an integer, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters across all pages and `has_more` is `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned being strictly less than `total_count` — so a page that runs off the end of the collection reports `has_more` false. Every other query parameter a list endpoint declares is an optional filter, and filters compose conjunctively: a value outside a filter's enumerated domain, a filter declared as an integer whose value is not an integer, or a filter spelled as a boolean that is none of `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` (case-insensitively), is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while an identifier-valued filter is not existence-checked at all — an identifier that matches nothing yields an empty page rather than a 404, and an identifier-valued filter supplied as the empty string is treated as though it had been omitted. That indulgence is confined to identifier-valued filters: the empty string supplied for a filter drawn from an enumerated domain, or for an integer- or boolean-valued filter, is a value outside that filter's domain and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than ignored.

Unless a specific endpoint declares otherwise, collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order, and because `created_at` is only millisecond-resolution every ordering in this subsection — the default one and each domain-meaningful one below — takes the row's own insertion sequence as its final tie-break, so rows created inside the same millisecond come back in the order they were created. Several collections carry a domain-meaningful ordering instead, and these orderings are part of the contract because they determine which rows land on which page. Housefile segments are ordered by `segment_code` ascending. Circulation lines are ordered by `allocation_rank` ascending with never-ranked lines placed last, then by `created_at` ascending. A season's circulation plans are ordered by `plan_code` ascending. A season's suppression rules are ordered by `priority` ascending and then `rule_code` ascending. A plan's drops are ordered by `drop_number` ascending. A drop's page placements are ordered by `page_number` ascending and then `sq_in_offset` ascending. Contact events, whether read through a drop or through a segment, are ordered by `contact_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. A line's test cells are ordered by `cell_code` ascending. Merge-purge runs and matchback runs are ordered by `created_at` **descending**, so the newest run of each kind appears first. A season's prospect list rentals and a drop's presort batches use the default `created_at` ascending order.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no client influence over its value. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional-second digits recording when the row came into existence. Storage holds nothing at process start and nothing is ever seeded: every row any read can return was created through a declared endpoint in the same process lifetime.

Both `created_at` and `updated_at` are stamped from the host's real clock, not from the logical clock of §2.11, which governs only the three domain stamps that subsection names. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` equals `created_at` on a freshly created row and is refreshed to the current instant on every mutation of the row, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation — including mutations the row's owner did not request, such as a cascade reaching it from another aggregate. Two entities are deliberate exceptions because they are immutable audit rows rather than living resources: a **postal presort batch** and a **contact event** carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, because neither has any update path at all — a presort batch is created, read, and eventually deleted wholesale when its drop reverts out of the presorted state, and a contact event is never rewritten once logged.

Version increments are the observable trace of the service's cascades, and the specification is precise about which rows move. When a request mutates several entities, each mutated entity's own `version` advances by one. A row whose *derived* figures a recompute merely rewrites in place is not a mutated entity for this purpose: wherever a subsection of §3 says explicitly that such a recompute leaves a row's `updated_at` and `version` alone, they stay exactly where they stood even though the row's stored figures moved. A pure read never advances a version: the plan profitability read recomputes and returns refreshed figures without changing the plan's `status` and without incrementing the plan's `version`.

### 2.5. The Season Rate Card

A `CatalogSeason` is the configuration root of the entire model. It is created by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `season_code` — a human-readable identifier of 1 to 32 characters such as `FALL2026A`, unique across the whole service, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEASON_CODE` — and a `fiscal_year` between 2020 and 2100 inclusive. Everything else on the season is a rate-card value with a defined default, so a season can be created with two fields and still price a plan.

The printing and paper economics come from four values. `print_cost_per_page_per_thousand_cents` is the web-offset printing charge in cents for one catalog page across one thousand books and defaults to 2600, meaning twenty-six dollars per page per thousand books; it may not be negative. `paper_cost_per_lb_cents` is the delivered paper cost in cents per pound and defaults to 92; it may not be negative. `paper_oz_per_page_milli` expresses basis weight as thousandths of an ounce of paper per catalog page and defaults to 40 — that is, forty thousandths of an ounce, or one twenty-fifth of an ounce, per page; it must be greater than zero. `usable_sq_in_per_page` is the sellable area on one page after trim and gutter, defaulting to 88 square inches, and must be greater than zero.

Postage, margin and prospect assumptions supply four more. `default_postage_estimate_cents_per_piece` is the planning-stage postage figure per book used before any presort batch exists, defaulting to 55 cents and never negative. `cogs_rate_bps` is cost of goods sold expressed in basis points of demand, defaulting to 5800 — fifty-eight percent — and constrained to the range 0 through 10000; the gross margin rate is whatever remains after subtracting `cogs_rate_bps` from ten thousand basis points. `prospect_base_response_bps` is the baseline response rate in basis points for a rented prospect name before any cooperative-model lift, defaulting to 110 basis points, or one and one-tenth percent, and never negative. `prospect_avg_order_value_cents` is the average order value assumed for every rented prospect name, defaulting to 11800 cents — one hundred eighteen dollars — and never negative.

Three governance values close the rate card. `min_inhome_gap_days` is the minimum whole-day separation the service requires between two in-home dates that target the same audience, defaulting to 21 days — three weeks; it may not be negative. `fatigue_window_days` is the look-back window in whole days over which cross-channel contact pressure is aggregated, defaulting to 90 days — one quarter — and must be greater than zero. `fatigue_suppression_threshold` is the weighted fatigue point total at or above which a housefile segment is forced into suppression, defaulting to 100 points, and must be greater than zero.

### 2.6. Integer Money, Basis Points and Pinned Rounding

There are no decimal fields anywhere in this service. Money is always an integer number of cents. Rates, shares, lifts and overlaps are always integer basis points, where ten thousand basis points is one hundred percent. Names, pieces, pages, square inches and points are plain integers. Wherever a computation produces a fractional intermediate value, the specification names the reduction to an integer explicitly, and there are only two reductions in use.

The first is **round half up**: the fractional value is rounded to the nearest whole unit, and a value exactly halfway between two whole units rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Round half up governs the rental unit cost per name, the rental expected response rate, the per-piece weight surcharge, each of the three drop cost-per-book components, the per-line gross margin, each contact event's weighted fatigue points, a test cell's response rate and lift, and both page-placement productivity outputs.

The second is **floor**: the fractional value is truncated downward to the next whole unit. Floor governs both merge-purge duplicate components, the merge-purge and matchback whole-day computations, the allocation waterfall's budget cap, the holdout carve-out, the forecast order count, the drop's automation share in basis points, and the fractional matchback share.

Two further conventions are contractual because they are observable. First, **rounding happens where the specification says it happens, not at the end**. A drop's cost per book is the sum of three components each rounded to whole cents on its own; it is not the rounding of the exact sum. To make the distinction concrete: a 94-page book with a planned quantity of 50000 and actual presorted postage of 2580000 cents over those 50000 pieces has exact components of 232.18, 21.62 and 51.6 cents. Rounding the exact sum of 305.40 would give 305 cents; the specification's per-component reading gives 232 plus 22 plus 52, which is 306 cents. 306 is correct. The same principle governs a plan's contribution roll-up, which is the sum of already-rounded per-line contributions rather than a contribution recomputed from summed demand, and it governs a placement's space productivity, which divides the already-rounded forecast rather than recomputing from scratch. Second, **each contact event's fatigue points are rounded individually before accumulation**, so three identical mail contacts each worth ten points sum to thirty rather than being derived from a summed exact value — the difference matters precisely at the suppression threshold.

### 2.7. Three Conserved Ledgers

Three quantities in this domain are conserved, and the conservation is an invariant that must hold before and after every single request rather than something reconciled later.

**The plan budget.** A `CirculationPlan` is created with a `budget_cents` figure greater than zero. At every observable moment, `budget_cents` equals `committed_cost_cents` plus `uncommitted_budget_cents` with no drift whatsoever. Allocation debits `committed_cost_cents`; voiding a line, suppressing a line through the fatigue cascade, and cancelling a plan each credit back *exactly* the amount that line froze, never a recomputed approximation of it.

**The mailable name universe.** A merge-purge run's `gross_input_names` always equals `net_mailable_names` plus `suppressed_names` plus `duplicates_removed`. This must hold when the run completes and must still hold after the run is recomputed because one of its input rentals was cancelled.

**Drop page space.** A drop's `square_inch_capacity` always equals `allocated_square_inches` plus `unallocated_square_inches`. Adding a placement moves space from unallocated to allocated; deleting one moves exactly the same space back.

Every cascade, derived-field recompute and compensating credit in this service completes inside the request that triggered it. There is no read repair, no background reconciliation, and no eventual consistency: when a request returns 2xx, every downstream figure it touched has already been rewritten.

### 2.8. Nested Path Resolution and Foreign-Key Existence

Several endpoints address a resource through a parent, and path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right after the authentication and role checks have passed. The leftmost identifier is resolved first; if it does not exist, that resource's own not-found code is returned and no further identifier is examined. Each entity family has its own 404 code: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND` for a catalog season, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a housefile segment, `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_FOUND` for a prospect list rental, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` for a circulation plan, `ERR_DROP_NOT_FOUND` for a drop, `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND` for a circulation line, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND` for a page placement, `ERR_TEST_CELL_NOT_FOUND` for a test cell, and `ERR_MATCHBACK_RUN_NOT_FOUND` for a matchback run.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are checked the same way and produce the same codes, one step later in the ladder. This applies to `season_id`, `parent_segment_id`, `housefile_segment_id`, `prospect_list_rental_id`, `drop_id`, and every element of `input_rental_ids`. A `season_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `parent_segment_id` or `housefile_segment_id` yields `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `prospect_list_rental_id` or any unresolvable element of `input_rental_ids` yields `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `drop_id` on a contact event yields `ERR_DROP_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level foreign-key existence is enforced on segment creation, segment amendment (its `parent_segment_id`), rental creation, plan creation, merge-purge run creation, circulation-line creation, contact-event creation, and the online-order ingest (each element's `housefile_segment_id`). This pass inspects only references supplied as non-empty strings; a reference supplied as a number, a boolean or an empty string is not a missing row but a malformed field, and it falls through to the schema pass to be rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Where one body carries two references, they are resolved in the order the endpoint's narrative introduces them — on segment creation the `season_id` is resolved before the `parent_segment_id`, so a body in which neither resolves reports `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Two Write Paths

Exactly two write paths in this service require the caller to prove it is working from a current view of a parent resource, because both retire work that a stale caller would never have seen.

Creating a merge-purge run against a circulation plan — `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/merge-purge-runs` — requires a non-optional `expected_plan_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on that `CirculationPlan`. If `expected_plan_version` does not equal the plan's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. The rule exists because a completed run legitimately supersedes the plan's prior completed universe and re-nets every rental it lists: a caller working from a stale view of the plan would retire a universe it never saw and re-net rentals another broker has already consumed. When the check passes, the accepted value is persisted on the run as `observed_plan_version`.

Creating a matchback run against a drop — `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/matchback-runs` — requires a non-optional `expected_drop_version` field, an integer that may not be negative, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on that `Drop`. If `expected_drop_version` does not equal the drop's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DROP_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. The rule exists because a new run legitimately supersedes every prior open or finalized run on the drop: a caller working from a stale view would retire an attribution read produced from a cost basis or line set it never observed. When the check passes, the accepted value is persisted on the run as `observed_drop_version`.

No other endpoint in this service takes a version field of any kind. Endpoints not named in this subsection must not require, and must not honour, an `expected_plan_version`, an `expected_drop_version`, or any similar field.

### 2.10. The Order in Which Requests Are Rejected

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one rejection is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks run in the fixed order below; the first violated check wins and short-circuits every later check.

1. **`X-User-ID` present and non-empty** on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorized for the endpoint** on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Every path identifier resolves**, on every endpoint that has one — otherwise the matching 404 from §2.8.
4. **Every body foreign key resolves**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/housefile-segments`, `PATCH /catalog-circulation/housefile-segments/{id}`, `POST /catalog-circulation/prospect-list-rentals`, `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans`, `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/merge-purge-runs`, `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/circulation-lines`, `POST /catalog-circulation/contact-events` and `POST /catalog-circulation/_online-orders/ingest` — otherwise the matching 404 from §2.8. On the ingest endpoint this pass sweeps every element of the `orders` array before any element is validated against its schema.
5. **Request body satisfies its schema** on every write endpoint: required fields present, enum values in range, integers inside their declared bounds, no negative money or quantities — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Four conventions make this pass exact. A request that carries no body at all is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose every field is optional accepts it; a body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or is JSON that is not an object, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422. An explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as an omitted value: it fails the check on a required field and falls back to the declared default on an optional one, with the single exception of an amendment's `parent_segment_id`, where §3.1 gives `null` the meaning "detach from the parent". An integer field admits only a JSON integer — a boolean, a numeric string such as `"5"`, and a whole-valued float such as `2.0` are all rejected, just as a string field rejects a non-string and a boolean field rejects anything but `true` or `false`. And a field the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected, so an unrecognised key never by itself fails a request. A timestamp field accepts any ISO 8601 instant — a trailing `Z`, an explicit numeric offset, or no zone at all, in which case UTC is assumed, and with any number of fractional digits — and is normalised to UTC before use, while every timestamp the service renders back carries exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`.
6. **Audience specification coherent** on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/circulation-lines` — otherwise `ERR_AUDIENCE_SPEC_INVALID` (422).
7. **Segment hierarchy acyclic and same-season** on `POST /catalog-circulation/housefile-segments` and `PATCH /catalog-circulation/housefile-segments/{id}` — otherwise `ERR_SEGMENT_CYCLE` (422).
8. **Owning plan editable** — its `status` is `DRAFT` or `COSTED` — for any structural mutation of its drops, lines, placements, presort batches or merge-purge runs: `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/drops`, `PATCH /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}`, `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/circulation-lines`, `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/allocate`, `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-lines/{id}/void`, `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/page-placements`, `DELETE /catalog-circulation/page-placements/{id}`, `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/presort-batches` and `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/merge-purge-runs` — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_EDITABLE` (409).
9. **The requested transition exists from the entity's current state**, on every action endpoint — otherwise the matching 409 code: `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DROP_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RENTAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LINE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TEST_CELL_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_MATCHBACK_STATE`.
10. **The supplied expected parent version matches**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/merge-purge-runs` and `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/matchback-runs` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_DROP_VERSION` (412). Because this sits below the editability and state guards, an uneditable plan reports `ERR_PLAN_NOT_EDITABLE` even when the version is also stale.
11. **Approver is neither the coster nor the submitter**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/approve` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
12. **The targeted segment is not suppression-only**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/circulation-lines` and `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/allocate` — otherwise `ERR_SEGMENT_SUPPRESSED` (422).
13. **The contact-frequency cap is respected**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/contact-events` — otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
14. **Rentals have reached the netted state**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/allocate` and `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/merge-purge-runs` — otherwise `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_NETTED` (409).
15. **The plan has a completed merge-purge run**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/allocate` and `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/cost` — otherwise `ERR_MERGE_PURGE_REQUIRED` (409).
16. **In-home date not in the past**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/drops` and `PATCH /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}` — otherwise `ERR_INHOME_DATE_IN_PAST` (422).
17. **In-home spacing respected**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/circulation-lines` — otherwise `ERR_INHOME_SPACING_VIOLATION` (409).
18. **Drop number unique in the plan**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/drops` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DROP_NUMBER` (409).
19. **Code uniqueness**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/seasons`, `POST /catalog-circulation/housefile-segments`, `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans`, `POST /catalog-circulation/seasons/{id}/suppression-rules`, `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-lines/{id}/test-cells` and `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/page-placements` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEASON_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEGMENT_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPPRESSION_RULE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEST_CELL_CODE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLACEMENT_SKU` (409).
20. **No second live line for the same audience**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/circulation-lines` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_AUDIENCE_LINE` (409).
21. **Placement intervals do not overlap**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/page-placements` — otherwise `ERR_PAGE_SPACE_OVERLAP` (409).
22. **Placement fits inside one page**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/page-placements` — otherwise `ERR_PAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
23. **Drop square-inch capacity respected**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/page-placements` and `PATCH /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}` — otherwise `ERR_DROP_SQ_IN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
24. **At most one control cell per line**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-lines/{id}/test-cells` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTROL_CELL` (409).
25. **Test cell quantities within the line's allocation**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-lines/{id}/test-cells` — otherwise `ERR_TEST_CELL_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_LINE` (422).
26. **Presort coverage exact**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/presort` — otherwise `ERR_PRESORT_COVERAGE_MISMATCH` (422).
27. **Budget sufficient for the top-priority line**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/drops/{id}/allocate` — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` (422).
28. **Plan contribution not negative**, on `POST /catalog-circulation/circulation-plans/{id}/approve` — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_CONTRIBUTION_NEGATIVE` (422).

### 2.11. The Logical Clock

Three of this service's rules are measured against "now": the contact-fatigue decay bands, the in-home spacing buffer, and the matchback attribution window. None of them can be exercised deterministically against wall-clock time, so the service maintains a **logical clock** whose value stands in for the current instant everywhere those rules read it. At process start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it by supplying a required `current_utc` instant, and the endpoint answers 200 with an object whose only member is `current_utc`, the clock's new value — not a persisted resource, so it carries no `id`, `created_at` or `version`. The clock may be moved to any instant, later or earlier than the one it currently holds, and moving it recomputes nothing by itself: each rule reads the clock at the moment that rule next runs. Every timestamp the service stamps itself — `suppressed_at`, `mailed_at`, `finalized_at` — is taken from the logical clock rather than from the host's real time.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Season Setup and the Housefile Segmentation Hierarchy

Everything begins with a season. Once a `PlatformAdmin` has laid down the rate card described in §2.5, a `CirculationPlanner` builds the season's **housefile segmentation** — the recency, frequency and monetary partition of the retailer's own customer file. Registering a segment requires the owning `season_id`, a `segment_code` such as `HF_BEST_0_90` of at least one character that must be unique within that season (a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEGMENT_CODE`), and six representative figures for the names the segment holds: `recency_days`, the whole days since the segment's representative most-recent order; `orders_12mo`, the representative order count over the trailing twelve months; `ltv_cents`, the representative lifetime monetary value in cents; `housefile_count`, the mailable name count the segment owns directly, excluding any descendants; `avg_order_value_cents`, the average order value used to convert forecast orders into forecast demand; and `historical_response_rate_bps`, the observed catalog response rate in basis points. None of the first five may be negative, and the response rate must fall between 0 and 10000 basis points inclusive.

The service scores each segment the moment it is registered, and again whenever any of its scoring inputs is amended. Scoring awards points on three independent step scales. Recency is worth 40 points when `recency_days` is 90 or fewer, 30 points when it is 180 or fewer, 18 points when it is 365 or fewer, 8 points when it is 730 or fewer, and 0 points beyond that. Frequency is worth 30 points when `orders_12mo` is 6 or more, 22 points at 3 or more, 14 points at 2 or more, 6 points at 1 or more, and 0 points for a segment with no orders in the year. Monetary value is worth 30 points when `ltv_cents` is 50000 or more, 22 points at 25000 or more, 14 points at 12000 or more, 6 points at 5000 or more, and 0 points below five thousand cents. The three point awards are added together and the sum is multiplied by ten to yield `rfm_score`, an exact integer between 0 and 1000 that never involves rounding. Every band edge is inclusive on the favourable side, so a segment whose `recency_days` is exactly 90 earns the full 40 recency points while one at 91 earns 30.

`rfm_score` then selects the segment's `segment_tier`: `BEST` at 800 or above, `CORE` at 600 or above, `OCCASIONAL` at 400 or above, `LAPSED` at 200 or above, and `DORMANT` below 200. Here too the boundary is inclusive on the favourable side — a score of exactly 200 is `LAPSED`, not `DORMANT`. The tier in turn supplies a default `contact_strategy` when the planner does not name one: `BEST` defaults to `PREMIUM_FULL`, both `CORE` and `OCCASIONAL` default to `STANDARD`, `LAPSED` defaults to `REACTIVATION`, and `DORMANT` defaults to `SUPPRESS_ONLY`. A planner may instead supply `contact_strategy` explicitly at registration, in which case the supplied value stands and the tier default is not applied.

Segments nest. Supplying `parent_segment_id` files the new segment underneath an existing one; omitting it leaves the segment at the top level, where `parent_segment_id` is null. A parent must belong to the same season as its child, a segment may never name itself as its own parent, and no chain of parent links may close into a cycle — a segment can never be its own ancestor. Any of those three violations is rejected with `ERR_SEGMENT_CYCLE`. Two derived attributes track the shape of the tree. `is_leaf` is true exactly when no other segment in the service names this segment as its parent; a newly registered segment is therefore always a leaf, and registering a child immediately turns its parent's `is_leaf` false. `rollup_housefile_count` is a segment's own `housefile_count` plus the transitive sum of every descendant segment's `housefile_count`, so it is recomputed for a parent whenever a descendant is added, re-parented, or has its own count amended. Both derived fields are in fact refreshed across the entire segment forest on every registration and every amendment, and a segment *other* than the one the request named has its own `version` incremented by exactly one precisely when that refresh actually rewrote its `is_leaf` or its `rollup_housefile_count`; a segment whose derived pair comes out unchanged is left completely alone, with neither its `version` nor its `updated_at` moving. So registering the first child under a leaf parent advances that parent by one and advances each further ancestor whose roll-up total moved, while registering a child whose `housefile_count` is 0 under a parent that already had children advances nothing above it. The newly registered segment is itself born at `version` 1 even though the same refresh writes its roll-up, and an amended segment takes exactly one increment however far the refresh reaches. A segment with at least one child is a **roll-up node**, and roll-up nodes carry a special significance for the merge-purge universe described in §3.3: only leaves contribute names, because counting a roll-up alongside its children would double-count the housefile.

Amending a segment re-scores it. A planner may revise `recency_days`, `orders_12mo`, `ltv_cents`, `housefile_count`, `avg_order_value_cents`, `historical_response_rate_bps`, `parent_segment_id` or `contact_strategy` on an existing segment; the amendment re-runs the recency-frequency-monetary scoring and rewrites `rfm_score` and `segment_tier`. An explicit `contact_strategy` in the amendment overrides the tier default, and this is the **only** way to lift a suppression that the contact-fatigue cascade has imposed. When the amendment names no `contact_strategy` at all, the re-scored tier's default is applied afresh, overwriting whatever value the segment was carrying — so a segment that re-scores from `BEST` down to `CORE` moves from `PREMIUM_FULL` to `STANDARD`, and a segment whose re-score leaves its tier where it was simply lands on that tier's default again even if a planner had once named something else. The single carve-out is a segment whose `contact_strategy` is already `SUPPRESS_ONLY`: it keeps that value, whatever the re-scored tier would have defaulted to. Supplying `parent_segment_id` as `null` is how an amendment detaches a segment back to the top level; omitting the key entirely leaves the existing link alone. The amendment cannot move a segment to another season or rename its `segment_code` — a `season_id` or `segment_code` supplied in the payload is simply ignored. When `parent_segment_id` changes, both the old parent and the new parent have their `rollup_housefile_count` and `is_leaf` recomputed. Every amendment increments the segment's `version` by one — including an amendment that names no amendable field at all, and one whose values leave every stored figure exactly where it stood. Two further fields sit on every segment and are never written by the planner directly: `fatigue_score`, which starts at 0 and is maintained by the contact-pressure aggregation in §3.8, and `suppressed_at`, which starts null and records the logical-clock instant at which the fatigue cascade forced the segment into suppression. A re-score of the recency-frequency-monetary bands never reverts a fatigue-imposed `SUPPRESS_ONLY`.

Segments are read back either individually or as a filtered collection. The collection read accepts `season_id`, `parent_segment_id`, `segment_tier`, `contact_strategy` and `is_leaf` as optional filters, which together serve as the reverse lookup from a season to its segments and from a parent to its children.

### 3.2. Prospect List Procurement and Cooperative Modeling

The housefile alone cannot grow a catalog business, so a `ListBroker` buys outside names. Each purchase is registered as a **prospect list rental** against a `season_id`, carrying the `list_source_name` the list owner or cooperative database knows it by — at least one character, and not required to be unique in any scope — a `requested_gross_names` quantity greater than zero, a `cost_per_thousand_cents` price — the rental CPM, in cents per thousand names, never negative — and two modelled overlap figures in basis points, each between 0 and 10000 inclusive: `housefile_overlap_bps`, the expected collision between this list and the retailer's own file, and `inter_list_overlap_bps`, the expected collision against rentals already processed earlier in the same merge-purge run. A freshly registered rental sits in status `REQUESTED` with `coop_model_score`, `expected_response_rate_bps`, `gross_names`, `net_names_delivered` and `unit_cost_cents_per_name` all zero.

A rental then walks a four-step procurement path — `REQUESTED → MODELED → ORDERED → DELIVERED` — before a fifth, system-driven step retires it into `NETTED`. Each step is a broker action, and each increments the rental's `version` by one.

**Modeling** moves the rental `REQUESTED → MODELED` and is the point at which cooperative-database intelligence enters the plan. The broker supplies a `coop_model_score` between 0 and 1000 inclusive, which is a decile rank rather than a rate: the score selects a multiplicative lift band applied to the season's baseline prospect response. A score of 800 or above earns a lift of positive 4000 basis points; 600 or above earns positive 2000; 400 or above earns no lift at all; and anything below 400 earns a lift of *negative* 3000 basis points, so an unmodelled-quality list responds below the season base. The rental's `expected_response_rate_bps` is then the season's `prospect_base_response_bps` multiplied by the quantity ten thousand plus the lift, divided by ten thousand, with the result rounded half up to a whole basis point.

**Ordering** moves the rental `MODELED → ORDERED` and takes no payload. It freezes the rental's per-name price: `unit_cost_cents_per_name` becomes `cost_per_thousand_cents` divided by one thousand, rounded half up to whole cents. This is the figure that will be added on top of the drop's cost per book for every rental line.

**Delivery** moves the rental `ORDERED → DELIVERED` and records a required `delivered_gross_names` from the payload into the rental's `gross_names` field; the figure may not be negative but may legitimately be 0, a list owner who shipped nothing still closing the delivery step. The list owner may ship more or fewer names than were ordered, so `gross_names` is authoritative from this point on and `requested_gross_names` remains as the original order of record.

The final procurement step is not a broker action at all: a rental moves `DELIVERED → NETTED` as a consequence of a merge-purge run completing with that rental in its input set, at which point `net_names_delivered` is written as the rental's `gross_names` less its own housefile and inter-list duplicate removals. Only a `NETTED` rental may back an allocated circulation line.

Cancellation is available from every live status — `REQUESTED`, `MODELED`, `ORDERED`, `DELIVERED` and `NETTED` all lead to `CANCELLED` — and takes an optional free-text `reason`, which is recorded nowhere on the rental but must be a string when it is supplied. Attempting it on an already-`CANCELLED` rental is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RENTAL_STATE`. Cancellation is the most destructive single action a broker can take, and it unwinds in five steps regardless of which live status it started from. First, the rental's `gross_names` and `net_names_delivered` are both set to 0. Second, every merge-purge run in `COMPLETED` status whose input set contains this rental moves to `STALE` and recomputes its name aggregates with the cancelled rental excluded from the set the recomputation reads and removed from the run's persisted `processing_order`, while the run's own `input_rental_ids` array is left exactly as it was — it remains the audit record of what the run originally consumed, so a stale run still lists the cancelled rental there — and no surviving rental's own `net_names_delivered` is rewritten. Third — because the rental's names have left the mailable universe — every circulation line pointing at this rental in status `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` moves to `VOIDED`, with `allocated_quantity`, `holdout_quantity`, `mailable_quantity`, `forecast_demand_cents`, `gross_margin_cents`, `circulation_cost_cents` and `contribution_cents` all set to 0. Fourth — because each of those lines has left `ALLOCATED` — each line's `committed_cost_cents` is credited back to its plan's `committed_cost_cents` and then zeroed on the line, and the plan's `uncommitted_budget_cents` is recomputed. Fifth — because those lines no longer contribute names — every affected drop's `requested_quantity_total` and `planned_quantity` are recomputed and its cost per book is re-run at the drop's *current* `cost_basis` — and where that re-run reads the presorted-actual basis on a drop that still holds its presort batches, but the void has driven that drop's `planned_quantity` down to 0, `postage_cents_per_book` is taken as 0 rather than dividing by zero — after which every line of that drop still sitting in `ALLOCATED` has its `circulation_cost_cents`, `gross_margin_cents` and `contribution_cents` re-derived at the drop's new cost per book, rewritten in place without that surviving line's own `updated_at` or `version` moving and with its two forecast figures untouched, and then every owning plan's `planned_cost_cents`, `planned_demand_cents` and `planned_contribution_cents` are recomputed, with any such plan sitting in `COSTED` falling back to `DRAFT`. Unlike the mailing cascade in §3.7, this unwind spares no line on account of its drop's production state: a line whose drop has already reached `PRESORTED`, `MAILED`, `DELIVERED` or even `CANCELLED` is voided too, though that drop's `cost_basis` is preserved rather than deliberately reverted — with the one exception that a drop whose presort batch rows have since been deleted has no actual postage left to divide, so its re-run falls back to the estimated reading and rewrites `cost_basis` to `ESTIMATED_POSTAGE` under the rule of §3.7. Each voided line's `version` and each affected drop's `version` increment by one, and the rental's own `version` increments by one. When the rental was cancelled from `REQUESTED`, `MODELED` or `ORDERED`, no names were ever delivered and no netting ever happened, so all five steps are no-ops unless a circulation line already referenced the rental.

Rentals are read back individually, or as a season's collection filtered by `status`.

### 3.3. Building the Mailable Universe with Merge-Purge

No circulation may be allocated, and no plan may be costed, until the plan knows how many distinct, mailable names actually exist. Establishing that number is the `ListBroker`'s **merge-purge run**, created against a circulation plan and identified internally by its `circulation_plan_id`. The request carries two fields, both of them required: the `expected_plan_version` demanded by §2.9, an integer that may not be negative, and `input_rental_ids` — an ordered-at-rest array of prospect list rental identifiers, which may hold between 0 and 20 elements and may not repeat an element. An `input_rental_ids` that is omitted, supplied as `null`, or supplied as anything other than a JSON array, one holding more than 20 elements, one holding an element that is not a string, and a repeated element are each rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An element that *is* a string is instead handed to the step-4 foreign-key pass without being trimmed or emptiness-checked first, so an element supplied as the empty string reports `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the one place in the service where an empty-string reference is read as a missing row rather than a malformed field. An empty array is entirely legitimate: a housefile-only universe still needs a completed run before circulation can be allocated or the plan costed.

Three referential guards protect the input set. The owning plan must be editable, in `DRAFT` or `COSTED`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_EDITABLE`. Every element of `input_rental_ids` must resolve to a prospect list rental belonging to the *same* season as the plan, otherwise `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_FOUND` — but the two halves of that guard sit at different rungs of the §2.10 ladder. Bare existence is settled by the step-4 foreign-key pass, ahead of everything else; the same-season condition is only tested once the editability and `expected_plan_version` guards have both passed, so a run naming a rental from another season against an uneditable plan reports `ERR_PLAN_NOT_EDITABLE`, and against a plan whose version is stale reports `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`. And every resolved rental must be sitting in `DELIVERED` — a rental still in `REQUESTED`, `MODELED` or `ORDERED`, or one already `CANCELLED`, is rejected with `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_NETTED`.

The run itself is a single deterministic computation. It starts by collecting every **leaf** housefile segment in the plan's season — every segment for which no other segment of that season names it as parent — and records how many such segments entered the computation as `housefile_leaf_segment_count`. The housefile gross contribution is the sum of `housefile_count` over those leaf segments; roll-up nodes contribute nothing, because their names are already counted through their children. `suppressed_names` is the sum of `housefile_count` over just those leaf segments whose `contact_strategy` is `SUPPRESS_ONLY`. `gross_input_names` is the housefile gross contribution plus the sum of `gross_names` over every rental in the input set.

Deduplication then runs in a pinned order, and the order is the whole point. The rentals are sorted by `coop_model_score` descending, with `created_at` ascending breaking ties, and the resulting sequence of rental identifiers is persisted as the run's `processing_order`. The housefile is always deduplicated against first, so every rental is compared against the housefile; rentals are then additionally compared against rentals already processed earlier in the same run. Walking the processing order from its first element, each rental's housefile duplicates are its `gross_names` multiplied by its `housefile_overlap_bps`, divided by ten thousand, floored to a whole number of names. Its inter-list duplicates are zero for the very first rental in processing order — by construction there is no previously-processed rental to collide with — and for every later rental they are its `gross_names` less its housefile duplicates, multiplied by its `inter_list_overlap_bps`, divided by ten thousand, floored. That rental's post-dedup count is its `gross_names` less its housefile duplicates less its inter-list duplicates. The run's `duplicates_removed` accumulates both duplicate components of every rental. Both dedup steps floor and neither ever rounds. Because the first list in processing order pays no inter-list penalty, swapping two rentals with different `gross_names` or different `inter_list_overlap_bps` genuinely changes `duplicates_removed` and therefore the universe.

`net_mailable_names` is `gross_input_names` less `suppressed_names` less `duplicates_removed`, and it is the finite universe the allocation waterfall in §3.5 draws from. The conservation invariant of §2.7 must hold on the persisted figures.

A run is born in `PENDING` and immediately forks on that final number. When `net_mailable_names` is greater than zero the run takes the `PENDING → COMPLETED` branch: `gross_input_names`, `suppressed_names`, `duplicates_removed`, `net_mailable_names`, `processing_order` and `housefile_leaf_segment_count` are all persisted; every rental in the input set moves `DELIVERED → NETTED` with its `net_names_delivered` written to its post-dedup count; every *other* merge-purge run of the same plan sitting in `COMPLETED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`; the accepted version is stored as `observed_plan_version`; and the plan's `version` increments by one. When `net_mailable_names` is exactly zero the run takes the `PENDING → FAILED` branch instead, and the two branches diverge in ways that outlive the status field: the four name aggregates are still persisted for audit, and so are `processing_order` and `housefile_leaf_segment_count`, but **no** rental moves to `NETTED` and no `net_names_delivered` is written, and **no** other merge-purge run of the plan is superseded — a previously completed universe survives a failed re-run intact. The failed branch still stores `observed_plan_version` and still increments the plan's `version` by one.

Two system-driven exits leave `COMPLETED`. A run moves `COMPLETED → SUPERSEDED` when a later run on the same plan takes the completed branch, or when the plan itself is cancelled; nothing happens beyond the status change and the row's `updated_at` refresh. A run moves `COMPLETED → STALE` when one of the rentals in its `input_rental_ids` is cancelled, and in that case it recomputes `gross_input_names`, `suppressed_names`, `duplicates_removed` and `net_mailable_names` over the surviving rentals only and rewrites `processing_order` without the cancelled one — the promotion of whichever rental now sorts first genuinely changes `duplicates_removed`, since the first list pays no inter-list penalty — and `housefile_leaf_segment_count` is re-derived from the season's segment forest as it now stands, while `input_rental_ids` is left as it was, with the conservation invariant still holding afterwards. `STALE`, `SUPERSEDED` and `FAILED` are all terminal: a stale universe is no longer trustworthy, and a fresh run must be created to restore a completed one.

Because `input_rental_ids` is an identifier array rather than a single foreign key, the plan's merge-purge run collection — filterable by `status` and returned newest first — is the only way to read back which rentals a given run consumed.

### 3.4. Drops, Book Weight and the Page-Space Ledger

A plan spends its budget through **drops**: individual mailings, each with its own book and its own in-home date. A `CirculationPlanner` adds a drop to a plan by supplying a `drop_number` between 1 and 99 that must be unique within that plan — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DROP_NUMBER` — an `in_home_date` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, and a `page_count`. The page count must be an even integer between 8 and 240 inclusive, because a catalog signature always comes in pairs of pages; an odd or out-of-range value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `in_home_date` may not be earlier than the logical clock's current value, and a date in the past is rejected with `ERR_INHOME_DATE_IN_PAST`. The drop is created with `production_status` `PLANNED` and `measurement_status` `PENDING`, and the two dimensions advance entirely independently of one another thereafter.

Creation immediately derives two exact integer products from the page count and the season rate card. The drop's `weight_oz_milli` is `page_count` multiplied by the season's `paper_oz_per_page_milli`, giving thousandths of an ounce of paper per book. The drop's `square_inch_capacity` is `page_count` multiplied by the season's `usable_sq_in_per_page`. At creation `allocated_square_inches` is 0 and `unallocated_square_inches` therefore equals the full capacity. Every other computed field on a fresh drop starts at its zero value: `requested_quantity_total`, `planned_quantity`, `print_rebate_bps`, `printing_cents_per_book`, `paper_cents_per_book`, `postage_cents_per_book`, `cost_per_book_cents`, `provisional_cost_per_book_cents`, `postage_total_cents` and `automation_share_bps` are all 0, `automation_qualified` is false, `cost_basis` reads `ESTIMATED_POSTAGE`, and `mailed_at` is null.

A planner may amend a drop's `in_home_date` or `page_count` while its plan is still editable and the drop itself is still in `PLANNED` production status; a mutation against a drop that has moved beyond `PLANNED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DROP_STATE`. Changing `page_count` recomputes `weight_oz_milli`, `square_inch_capacity` and `unallocated_square_inches`, then re-runs the drop's cost per book at the drop's *current* `cost_basis`, then recomputes the owning plan's `planned_cost_cents`, `planned_demand_cents` and `planned_contribution_cents`; a plan sitting in `COSTED` falls back to `DRAFT` as a result. The drop's `version` increments by one on every accepted amendment — including one that names neither field, and one whose values leave every stored figure exactly where it stood — and the plan's ledger refresh and its `COSTED → DRAFT` edge run on every accepted amendment too, whether or not `page_count` actually moved. A page-count reduction may never strand space that is already committed: shrinking `page_count` below the square inches already allocated, or below the highest occupied `page_number`, is rejected with `ERR_DROP_SQ_IN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` rather than silently producing a negative remainder.

Inside the book, merchandise competes for square inches. A **page placement** puts one SKU in one rectangle of one page, and it carries a `product_sku` of at least one character that must be unique within the drop (a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLACEMENT_SKU`), a `product_name` for display of at least one character, a `unit_price_cents` catalog selling price and a `unit_cogs_cents` cost of goods — neither negative — a `demand_per_sq_inch_cents` figure, itself never negative, recording the historical demand in cents this SKU generates per square inch when placed in an ordinary inside position, a `page_number` that carries no schema bound of its own because its range is a page-capacity concern rather than a schema one, a `sq_in_offset`, never negative, measuring the left edge of the occupied area in square inches consumed from the page origin, a `square_inches` area greater than zero, and a `placement_position` drawn from `FRONT_COVER`, `HOT_SPOT`, `INSIDE` and `BACK_COVER`.

Square-inch analysis is the catalog merchant's unit economics, and position is what makes it interesting: the same SKU in the same space earns far more on the front cover than buried inside. The position selects a multiplier in basis points as `position_multiplier_bps` — 18000 for `FRONT_COVER`, 14000 for `HOT_SPOT`, 13000 for `BACK_COVER`, and 10000 for `INSIDE`. The placement's `forecast_space_demand_cents` is its `square_inches` multiplied by its `demand_per_sq_inch_cents`, multiplied by `position_multiplier_bps`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to whole cents. Its `space_productivity_cents_per_sq_in` is then `forecast_space_demand_cents` divided by `square_inches`, rounded half up — a deliberate re-division of the already-rounded forecast rather than an independent computation from the exact product.

Three space constraints guard every placement, and they are checked in the order §2.10 fixes. A placement occupies the half-open interval that begins at `sq_in_offset` and extends for `square_inches`, so the interval includes its left edge and excludes its right; two live placements on the same `page_number` of the same drop may not have overlapping intervals, though intervals that merely touch — where one ends exactly where the next begins — are permitted. An overlap is rejected with `ERR_PAGE_SPACE_OVERLAP`. A placement must also fit inside a single page: `sq_in_offset` plus `square_inches` may not exceed the season's `usable_sq_in_per_page`, and `page_number` must lie between 1 and the drop's `page_count` inclusive; either violation is rejected with `ERR_PAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Finally, the drop's total allocated square inches may never exceed its `square_inch_capacity`, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_DROP_SQ_IN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful placement adds its `square_inches` to the drop's `allocated_square_inches` and subtracts the same amount from `unallocated_square_inches`, and increments the drop's `version` by one.

Removing a placement is the exact inverse: its `square_inches` return to `unallocated_square_inches` and leave `allocated_square_inches` with no drift, and the drop's `version` increments by one. The removal deliberately returns the parent drop rather than the deleted placement, so the reconciled square-inch figures are directly observable in the response. Both adding and removing a placement require the owning plan to be editable and the drop to still be in `PLANNED` production status. A drop's placements are read back as a collection filterable by `page_number`, and a plan's drops as a collection filterable by `production_status` and `measurement_status`.

### 3.5. Audience Lines, Holdouts and the Allocation Waterfall

A drop reaches an audience through **circulation lines**. Each line names exactly one audience and asks for a quantity of names from it. The planner supplies an `audience_type` of either `HOUSEFILE` or `RENTAL`, a `requested_quantity` greater than zero, and precisely one of two audience references: `housefile_segment_id` for a housefile line, or `prospect_list_rental_id` for a rental line. The unused reference is null. Supplying both references, supplying neither, or supplying an `audience_type` that disagrees with the reference actually provided is rejected with `ERR_AUDIENCE_SPEC_INVALID`. A line may also be marked as a measurement holdout: `is_holdout` defaults to false, and `holdout_pct_bps` — the share of the eventual allocation withheld from the mail, expressed in basis points between 0 and 5000 inclusive — defaults to 0. A non-zero `holdout_pct_bps` on a line whose `is_holdout` is false is a contradiction and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Three business guards sit on line creation beyond the plan-editable and drop-in-`PLANNED` requirements. A housefile segment whose `contact_strategy` is `SUPPRESS_ONLY` may not be given circulation at all, and naming one is rejected with `ERR_SEGMENT_SUPPRESSED`. A drop may not carry two live lines for the same audience — where "live" means status `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` — and a second such line is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AUDIENCE_LINE`. And a single housefile segment may not be mailed by two drops whose in-home dates crowd each other: the whole-day distance between this drop's `in_home_date` and the `in_home_date` of any other drop that already carries a live line for the same segment must be at least the season's `min_inhome_gap_days`. The buffer is symmetric — it applies whether the other drop mails before or after this one — and a separation of exactly `min_inhome_gap_days` whole days is permitted. A violation is rejected with `ERR_INHOME_SPACING_VIOLATION`. Only lines in status `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED`, sitting on drops whose `production_status` is not `CANCELLED`, occupy the buffer; a `VOIDED` or `SUPPRESSED` line releases it immediately.

A newly created line lands in status `PROPOSED` with `allocation_rank` 0 and every allocated, committed, margin and cost field at 0. At creation the service computes the **planning view** of the line's demand — the pre-merge gross variant. First it resolves the line's `effective_response_bps` from whichever single audience reference is non-null: a housefile line takes the segment's `historical_response_rate_bps`, and a rental line takes the rental's `expected_response_rate_bps`. It also resolves the average order value from the same reference: a housefile line uses the segment's `avg_order_value_cents`, while a rental line uses the season's `prospect_avg_order_value_cents`. The gross order count is `requested_quantity` multiplied by `effective_response_bps`, divided by ten thousand, floored to a whole number of orders, and `gross_forecast_demand_cents` is that order count multiplied by the resolved average order value. This variant prices the universe the planner *asked for*, before merge-purge removed any duplicate and before the waterfall clamped anything, and it ignores the holdout carve-out entirely. Creating the line also recomputes the drop's `requested_quantity_total` — the sum of `requested_quantity` over the drop's lines in status `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` — and a plan sitting in `COSTED` falls back to `DRAFT`. That recompute rewrites the drop's figure in place: the drop's own `updated_at` and `version` do not move on line creation, because the line is the only row the request actually mutates.

Allocation is where the budget is actually spent, and it is a strict waterfall rather than a proportional split. Before any line is funded the service prices the drop **provisionally**, off the requested universe, because a printer quotes a volume rebate band before a single book has been allocated. The provisional rebate band is selected from `requested_quantity_total`: 800 basis points at 100000 books or more, 500 basis points at 40000 or more, and no rebate below that. The provisional printing component is the drop's `page_count` multiplied by the season's `print_cost_per_page_per_thousand_cents`, divided by one thousand, then multiplied by ten thousand less the rebate basis points and divided by ten thousand, with the result rounded half up to whole cents. The provisional paper component is `page_count` multiplied by the season's `paper_oz_per_page_milli`, divided by one thousand to convert thousandths of an ounce into ounces, divided by sixteen to convert ounces into pounds, then multiplied by the season's `paper_cost_per_lb_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The provisional postage component is simply the season's `default_postage_estimate_cents_per_piece`. `provisional_cost_per_book_cents` is the sum of those three whole-cent components, and once set it is **never** recomputed after allocation. The service recognises "already set" purely by the stored `provisional_cost_per_book_cents` being non-zero, so the degenerate rate card whose three components sum to 0 leaves the drop looking unpriced and the figure is struck afresh on every later allocation of that drop — the only case in which the provisional price is computed more than once. That immobility is deliberate: recomputing it would change every line's unit cost, which would change every allocated quantity, which would change the drop's planned quantity and therefore the rebate band all over again.

The waterfall then walks the drop's `PROPOSED` lines in a pinned priority order: every `HOUSEFILE` line before every `RENTAL` line; within the housefile lines, by the segment's `rfm_score` descending; within the rental lines, by the rental's `coop_model_score` descending; and, as the final tie-break in every case, by the line's own `created_at` ascending. Remaining budget starts at the plan's `uncommitted_budget_cents`. For each line in turn, the service assigns the next `allocation_rank`, counting from 1. It sets the line's `unit_cost_cents` to the drop's `provisional_cost_per_book_cents` plus, for a rental line only, that rental's `unit_cost_cents_per_name`; a housefile line adds nothing. It computes an audience cap — the segment's `housefile_count` for a housefile line, the rental's `net_names_delivered` for a rental line — and a budget cap, which is the remaining budget divided by the line's `unit_cost_cents` and floored, because a partial book cannot be printed — or, in the degenerate case where a rate card drives that unit cost to zero, no budget cap at all, since a book that costs nothing consumes no budget. The line's `allocated_quantity` is the smallest of its `requested_quantity`, its audience cap and its budget cap.

If that smallest value is zero or below, the line is left in `PROPOSED` with `allocated_quantity` 0 and `committed_cost_cents` 0, and the waterfall moves on to the next line — the unfunded line nevertheless keeps the `allocation_rank` and the `unit_cost_cents` the walk just wrote onto it, and its own `version` increments by one for having been walked at all. Otherwise the line moves `PROPOSED → ALLOCATED`, and four figures are frozen: `holdout_quantity` becomes `allocated_quantity` multiplied by `holdout_pct_bps`, divided by ten thousand, floored; `mailable_quantity` becomes `allocated_quantity` less `holdout_quantity`; and `committed_cost_cents` becomes `allocated_quantity` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`. The remaining budget is reduced by exactly that committed amount before the next line is considered, so the highest-priority audience fills to its own cap before any lower-ranked line sees a single name or a single cent. Note that the holdout is carved out of the allocated quantity *after* the cost has been committed: a holdout group is paid for in the cost model but never mailed, which is exactly why forecast demand needs two variants. When the highest-priority `PROPOSED` line cannot afford even one book out of the plan's `uncommitted_budget_cents` — that is, when `uncommitted_budget_cents` divided by that one line's `unit_cost_cents` and floored comes out below 1 — the whole allocation request is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` rather than committing nothing and reporting success. The guard is read off that single top-priority line and no other, and it does not apply at all in two cases: a drop with no `PROPOSED` line left to fund clears it and allocates nothing, and a top-priority line whose `unit_cost_cents` is zero clears it too, a book that costs nothing always being affordable.

Allocation only runs when the ground is prepared. The owning plan must be editable, the drop must be in `PLANNED` production status, and the plan must already have a merge-purge run in `COMPLETED` status — otherwise `ERR_MERGE_PURGE_REQUIRED`. Both audience guards inspect only the lines this run would actually fund — the drop's lines still in `PROPOSED` — and never re-examine a line that is already `ALLOCATED`: any such housefile line whose segment has since become `SUPPRESS_ONLY` triggers `ERR_SEGMENT_SUPPRESSED`, and any such rental line whose rental has not yet reached `NETTED` triggers `ERR_RENTAL_NOT_NETTED`.

Once the waterfall finishes, the consequences fan out inside the same request. The drop's `planned_quantity` becomes the sum of `allocated_quantity` over its `ALLOCATED` lines. The plan's `committed_cost_cents` grows by the total newly frozen, and `uncommitted_budget_cents` is recomputed as `budget_cents` less `committed_cost_cents`. The drop's cost per book is then re-derived from the new `planned_quantity` as described in §3.6, at the drop's *current* `cost_basis`, and every line of the drop now in `ALLOCATED` — those this waterfall just funded and any funded by an earlier allocation of the same drop alike — gets its **committed view** of demand: the line's `effective_response_bps` is first re-resolved from its audience reference exactly as that reference stands now, so a segment or rental whose response rate was amended after the line was created is picked up here; then `forecast_orders` is the line's `mailable_quantity` multiplied by that `effective_response_bps`, divided by ten thousand, floored, and `forecast_demand_cents` is that order count multiplied by the resolved average order value. This second variant prices only the books that will actually land in a mailbox, and it is the figure the profitability roll-up and the matchback expectation use. Both variants are persisted side by side on the line: the gross figure is what the plan was justified with, the net figure is what the plan will be judged on, and they coincide only when a line was allocated its full request with a `holdout_pct_bps` of zero.

Per-line profitability follows immediately. `gross_margin_cents` is `forecast_demand_cents` multiplied by ten thousand less the season's `cogs_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up. `circulation_cost_cents` is the line's `mailable_quantity` multiplied by the drop's *current* `cost_per_book_cents` plus, for a rental line, that rental's `unit_cost_cents_per_name`. `contribution_cents` is `gross_margin_cents` less `circulation_cost_cents`, and it may legitimately be negative — a prospect list that loses money on its first mailing is still bought for the housefile names it creates. There is a deliberate asymmetry here worth internalising: the budget was committed at the *provisional* cost per book on the *allocated* quantity including the holdout, while profitability is measured at the *final* cost per book on the *mailable* quantity only. Contribution therefore moves when a drop is presorted even though `committed_cost_cents` does not. Finally the plan's roll-up figures are refreshed, a plan sitting in `COSTED` falls back to `DRAFT`, and the drop's `version` increments by one; the response carries the reconciled drop rather than any line. The line-level version accounting is exact. Every line the waterfall walked takes exactly one increment — the funded ones and the ones it left in `PROPOSED` at `allocated_quantity` 0 alike — and takes it once only, the later committed-view and profitability rewrite deliberately not bumping a line the walk already bumped. A line that was already `ALLOCATED` from an earlier allocation of the same drop was not walked, so it takes one increment precisely when this request's rewrite actually moved one of its committed-view or profitability figures, and none at all when those figures come out unchanged.

A planner may retire a line at any point while the plan is editable and the drop is still `PLANNED`, optionally supplying a free-text `reason` which is recorded nowhere on the line but must be a string when it is supplied. Retiring an allocated line takes it `ALLOCATED → VOIDED` and credits the plan's `committed_cost_cents` back by *exactly* the line's `committed_cost_cents` before zeroing that field on the line, then recomputes `uncommitted_budget_cents`; the compensation is exact in both directions, so the budget identity of §2.7 holds before and after with no drift. The line's `allocated_quantity`, `holdout_quantity`, `mailable_quantity`, `forecast_demand_cents`, `gross_margin_cents`, `circulation_cost_cents` and `contribution_cents` are all set to 0. Its `allocation_rank`, `unit_cost_cents`, `effective_response_bps` and `gross_forecast_demand_cents` are pointedly *not* zeroed — the retired line keeps them exactly as they stood, as the record of what it was once ranked and priced at. Retiring a line that was still `PROPOSED` takes it `PROPOSED → VOIDED` and needs no budget credit at all, since its `committed_cost_cents` was already 0. Either way the drop's `requested_quantity_total`, its `planned_quantity` and its cost per book are recomputed — the last at the drop's *current* `cost_basis` — after which every *other* line of that drop still in `ALLOCATED` has its `circulation_cost_cents`, `gross_margin_cents` and `contribution_cents` re-derived at the drop's new cost per book, rewritten in place with that surviving line's two forecast figures untouched and with neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moving; then the plan's roll-up figures are refreshed, with a `COSTED` plan falling back to `DRAFT`. The retired line's own `version` and the drop's `version` each increment by one. Retiring a line whose status is neither `PROPOSED` nor `ALLOCATED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LINE_STATE`, and doing so on a drop that has moved past `PLANNED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DROP_STATE`. `SUPPRESSED` and `VOIDED` are both terminal for a line.

Lines are read back individually or as a collection filterable by `drop_id`, `housefile_segment_id`, `prospect_list_rental_id` and `status`, which is how the reverse path from a drop, a segment or a rental to its lines is served.

### 3.6. Costing, Review, Approval and the Revision Cycle

A `CirculationPlanner` opens a **circulation plan** against a `season_id` with a `plan_code` such as `FALL2026A-CIRC-V1`, at least one character long and unique within that season — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_CODE` — and a `budget_cents` figure greater than zero. The plan is born in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0, `committed_cost_cents` 0, `uncommitted_budget_cents` equal to `budget_cents`, all three of `costed_by_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null, and `planned_cost_cents`, `planned_demand_cents` and `planned_contribution_cents` all 0. A season's plans are read back as a collection filterable by `status`.

Costing takes the plan `DRAFT → COSTED` and is the moment the plan's numbers become quotable. Four preconditions apply. The plan must be in `DRAFT`. At least one drop must exist under the plan whose `production_status` is not `CANCELLED`. At least one circulation line in status `ALLOCATED` must exist somewhere across the plan's non-cancelled drops — individual drops may still be empty or wholly unallocated, but a plan with nothing allocated anywhere cannot be costed and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. And the plan must already carry a merge-purge run in `COMPLETED` status, otherwise `ERR_MERGE_PURGE_REQUIRED`.

Costing recomputes the drop cost per book for every drop of the plan whose `production_status` is `PLANNED` or `PRESORTED`, and it does so with the **estimated-postage** reading, forcing each such drop's `cost_basis` back to `ESTIMATED_POSTAGE` even if that drop had previously been priced from actual presort figures. The cost per book is three whole-cent components summed, and the rebate band this time is selected from the drop's `planned_quantity` — the allocated universe — rather than from the requested universe the provisional figure used: 800 basis points at 100000 books or more, 500 basis points at 40000 or more, and no rebate below that, persisted on the drop as `print_rebate_bps`. `printing_cents_per_book` is the drop's `page_count` multiplied by the season's `print_cost_per_page_per_thousand_cents`, divided by one thousand, then multiplied by ten thousand less `print_rebate_bps` and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents on its own. `paper_cents_per_book` is `page_count` multiplied by the season's `paper_oz_per_page_milli`, divided by one thousand to reach ounces, divided by sixteen to reach pounds, multiplied by the season's `paper_cost_per_lb_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents on its own. `postage_cents_per_book` under the estimated reading is simply the season's `default_postage_estimate_cents_per_piece`. `cost_per_book_cents` is the sum of those three independently rounded components — not the rounding of their exact sum, as §2.6 insists. Because the rebate band comes from the allocated universe, which is generally smaller than the requested universe, the final figure normally *exceeds* the provisional figure the budget was actually spent at.

With every drop repriced, costing recomputes `circulation_cost_cents`, `gross_margin_cents` and `contribution_cents` for every `ALLOCATED` line of those drops, then rolls the plan up. The roll-up's input set is the whole difficulty: it takes every line in status `ALLOCATED` that belongs to a drop of the plan whose `production_status` is not `CANCELLED`, and it excludes `VOIDED` lines, `SUPPRESSED` lines, lines still sitting in `PROPOSED`, and every line of a cancelled drop. `planned_cost_cents` is the sum of `circulation_cost_cents` across that set, `planned_demand_cents` the sum of `forecast_demand_cents`, and `planned_contribution_cents` the sum of `contribution_cents`. Because it sums already-rounded per-line contributions, `planned_contribution_cents` is deliberately *not* the same number a contribution recomputed from summed demand would produce; the per-line reading is the pinned one. Costing then stamps `costed_by_user_id` with the acting operator's `X-User-ID` and increments the plan's `version` by one. The plan row is the *only* row costing advances: a drop whose three cost components moved and a line whose profitability moved each have their figures rewritten in place, leaving that drop's and that line's own `updated_at` and `version` exactly where they stood. Costing explicitly does **not** re-run the allocation waterfall, it does not touch any drop's `provisional_cost_per_book_cents` or any line's `committed_cost_cents`, and it does not refresh the plan's `committed_cost_cents` or `uncommitted_budget_cents` either.

Submitting for review takes the plan `COSTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, incrementing `version` by one. A `CirculationDirector` then holds three options.

**Approval** takes the plan `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, and two guards stand in front of it. Segregation of duties requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from *both* `costed_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id`; matching either one is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Fiscal responsibility requires `planned_contribution_cents` to be zero or greater, read exactly as it currently stands on the plan row — approval recomputes no roll-up of its own before testing it; a negative figure is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_CONTRIBUTION_NEGATIVE`, so a single deeply unprofitable rental line can block approval of an otherwise healthy plan. On success the director's `X-User-ID` is recorded in `approved_by_user_id` and the plan's `version` increments by one.

**Requesting revision** is the plan's cycle back-edge, and it exists from two states, taking an optional `note`. From `UNDER_REVIEW → DRAFT` it increments `revision_round` by one and sets both `costed_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, incrementing `version` by one. Clearing both operator identities matters: it means the segregation-of-duties check on the *next* approval is evaluated only against the operators of the new cost-and-submit pair. From `APPROVED → DRAFT` the same back-edge is legal only while no book has entered the mail stream — if any drop of the plan has `production_status` `MAILED` or `DELIVERED` the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. When it is legal, it increments `revision_round`, clears `costed_by_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id` *and* `approved_by_user_id`, and additionally unwinds every presorted drop: each drop of the plan whose `production_status` is `PRESORTED` reverts `PRESORTED → PLANNED`, its presort batch rows are deleted, its `postage_total_cents` and `automation_share_bps` reset to 0 with `automation_qualified` reset to false, and its cost per book is recomputed under the estimated-postage reading with `cost_basis` set to `ESTIMATED_POSTAGE`, after which every `ALLOCATED` line of that drop has its `circulation_cost_cents`, `gross_margin_cents` and `contribution_cents` re-derived at the drop's new cost per book without that line's own `version` moving; each such drop's `version` increments by one. The plan's `planned_cost_cents`, `planned_demand_cents` and `planned_contribution_cents` are then recomputed — a recompute the `UNDER_REVIEW` back-edge skips entirely, having no presorted drop to unwind — and the plan's `version` increments by one on either back-edge. Neither back-edge refreshes the plan's `committed_cost_cents` or `uncommitted_budget_cents`, because no line has left `ALLOCATED`.

**Cancellation** is reachable from `DRAFT`, `COSTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` and `APPROVED`, takes an optional `reason`, and is likewise barred once any drop of the plan has reached `MAILED` or `DELIVERED` — that attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. The side-effect set is identical from all four origin states. Every circulation line of the plan in status `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` moves to `VOIDED` with `allocated_quantity`, `holdout_quantity`, `mailable_quantity`, `forecast_demand_cents`, `gross_margin_cents`, `circulation_cost_cents` and `contribution_cents` all set to 0, and each such line credits its `committed_cost_cents` back to the plan's `committed_cost_cents` before that field is zeroed on the line. Every drop of the plan whose `production_status` is `PLANNED` or `PRESORTED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and a drop cancelled out of `PRESORTED` also has its presort batch rows deleted — though, unlike the revision back-edge, cancellation leaves that drop's `postage_total_cents`, `automation_share_bps`, `automation_qualified` and `cost_basis` exactly as the presort left them, and re-derives neither its quantities nor its cost per book; each such drop's `version` increments by one. Every merge-purge run of the plan in `COMPLETED` status moves to `SUPERSEDED`. The plan's `uncommitted_budget_cents`, `planned_cost_cents`, `planned_demand_cents` and `planned_contribution_cents` are recomputed and its `version` increments by one. `CANCELLED` is terminal for a plan.

One transition on the plan has no caller at all. A costed plan's figures are only trustworthy while its drop and line structure is frozen, so **any structural mutation of a drop or a line belonging to a plan in `COSTED` status silently drives the plan `COSTED → DRAFT`**, recomputing the three roll-up figures and incrementing the plan's `version` by one. The mutations that trigger this invalidation edge are: amending a drop, adding a circulation line, running allocation on a drop, retiring a circulation line, and any cascade that voids or suppresses one of the plan's lines. Every one of those mutations refreshes the plan's `uncommitted_budget_cents` alongside the three roll-up figures, and the plan's `version` advances by exactly one whenever that refresh actually rewrote one of those four figures or moved the plan's `status` — and not otherwise: a mutation that leaves all four figures exactly where they stood and finds no status edge to take leaves the plan row, its `updated_at` and its `version` untouched, which is why adding a still-`PROPOSED` line to a `DRAFT` plan does not advance the plan's `version` while allocating that line does. A plan already in `DRAFT` is unaffected by the status edge because it is already there. A plan in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` never takes the status edge either: it rejects the four planner-driven mutations outright with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_EDITABLE` before they can happen, while a cascade that reaches it from another aggregate — a rental cancellation under §3.2, or the mailing cascade of §3.7 — still refreshes those four figures and still advances its `version` when they move, without ever touching its `status`.

Separately from the lifecycle, a plan's profitability may be read on demand. That read recomputes the roll-up from the plan's currently allocated lines and returns the plan with `planned_cost_cents`, `planned_demand_cents` and `planned_contribution_cents` refreshed, and those three refreshed figures are written through to the stored plan row, so an ordinary read of the plan afterwards reports them too. It is a pure read in every way that leaves a trace: it does not change the plan's `status`, it leaves `committed_cost_cents` and `uncommitted_budget_cents` untouched, and it moves neither the plan's `updated_at` nor its `version`.

### 3.7. Postal Presort, Mailing, and the Five-Hop Fatigue Cascade

A drop's `production_status` and its `measurement_status` are two parallel dimensions that advance independently. A drop can sit at `DELIVERED` while its `measurement_status` is still `PENDING`, and it can advance `MAILED → DELIVERED` while its measurement side is already `MATCHBACK_OPEN`. Exactly one guard in the whole service couples them, and it is described in §3.9.

On the production side, the first real-world step after approval is the mail shop's presort. A planner records the mail stream as a set of **postal presort batches** under the drop, each carrying a `declared_tier` — the tier the mail shop *claims* for that batch, one of `FIVE_DIGIT`, `THREE_DIGIT`, `AADC` or `MIXED_AADC` — and a `piece_count` greater than zero. A batch is an immutable audit row: it has no version and no update path, and the only thing that ever removes one is the wholesale deletion described in §3.6 when a presorted drop reverts. Recording one also leaves the parent drop's own row alone — the drop's `version` does not move until the drop is actually presorted — and a drop's batches are read back as a collection filterable by `declared_tier` and `effective_tier`.

Each batch is qualified the moment it is recorded. The three fine-grained tiers each require a minimum of 150 pieces; `MIXED_AADC` has no minimum at all. A batch whose `piece_count` clears its declared tier's minimum keeps that tier as its `effective_tier`; a batch that falls short of the minimum drops to `MIXED_AADC` instead. The effective tier then sets `rate_cents_per_piece`: 42 cents for `FIVE_DIGIT`, 47 for `THREE_DIGIT`, 51 for `AADC`, and 58 for `MIXED_AADC`. `weight_surcharge_cents_per_piece` is derived from the drop's book weight rather than from the batch: the excess over a free allowance of four thousand thousandths of an ounce — that is, four ounces — is the drop's `weight_oz_milli` less 4000, or zero if the book weighs no more than four ounces; that excess is multiplied by 9 and divided by one thousand, giving nine cents per whole ounce of excess weight, and the result is rounded half up to whole cents. `batch_postage_cents` is the batch's `piece_count` multiplied by the sum of `rate_cents_per_piece` and `weight_surcharge_cents_per_piece` — the surcharge is folded into the per-piece rate *before* the multiplication, never applied to the batch total afterwards. `is_automation_qualified` is true exactly when the batch's `effective_tier` is `FIVE_DIGIT`, `THREE_DIGIT` or `AADC`; `MIXED_AADC` is not an automation tier, so a batch that missed its minimum loses automation credit as well as paying a higher rate.

Presorting the drop takes it `PLANNED → PRESORTED` and demands three things. The drop's owning plan must be in `APPROVED` status, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE` — presort is the first step that spends real money and it is gated on the director's signature. The drop's `planned_quantity` must be greater than zero, and a drop with nothing allocated to it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DROP_STATE`. And the mail stream must cover the mailing exactly: the sum of `piece_count` across the drop's batches must equal the drop's `planned_quantity`, and any shortfall or excess is rejected with `ERR_PRESORT_COVERAGE_MISMATCH`.

Presorting then computes the drop's actual postage. The service sums `batch_postage_cents` across the drop's batches, and separately sums the `piece_count` of just those batches whose `effective_tier` is an automation tier. `automation_share_bps` is that automation piece count multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the drop's `planned_quantity`, floored. `automation_qualified` is true exactly when `automation_share_bps` is 9000 or greater. The automation discount is all-or-nothing at exactly that line: when the drop qualifies, the discount is 2 cents for each automation-qualified piece, and when it does not qualify the discount is zero. `postage_total_cents` is the batch postage sum less that discount. Two thresholds therefore compose here, and both are worth internalising: a batch that misses its own tier minimum both pays a higher rate *and* removes its pieces from the automation numerator, and because the drop-level share is floored, a single piece moved out of an automation batch can push the share below the line and forfeit the entire discount.

With postage known, the drop is repriced under the **presorted-actual** reading. `printing_cents_per_book` and `paper_cents_per_book` are computed exactly as in §3.6, but `postage_cents_per_book` now becomes `postage_total_cents` divided by the drop's `planned_quantity`, rounded half up to whole cents, and the drop's `cost_basis` is set to `PRESORTED_ACTUAL`. That substitution only happens when the drop actually has at least one presort batch; a drop with no batch falls back to the season's `default_postage_estimate_cents_per_piece` with `cost_basis` `ESTIMATED_POSTAGE`. `cost_per_book_cents` remains the sum of the three independently rounded components. The presort then recomputes `circulation_cost_cents`, `gross_margin_cents` and `contribution_cents` for every `ALLOCATED` line of this drop — rewriting those figures in place without moving any line's own `updated_at` or `version` — and recomputes the owning plan's `planned_cost_cents`, `planned_demand_cents` and `planned_contribution_cents` — note carefully that this recompute does **not** change the plan's `status`, so an `APPROVED` plan stays approved through presort even though its contribution figure moves, and it does **not** advance the plan's `version` either. The drop's `version` increments by one.

Mailing takes the drop `PRESORTED → MAILED`, requires the drop's `in_home_date` to be at or before the logical clock's current value (an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DROP_STATE`), and stamps `mailed_at` with the logical clock's current instant. It also sets off the deepest cascade in the service — five hops, each caused by the hop before it rather than by the mail action itself.

**Hop one.** For every circulation line of this drop in status `ALLOCATED` whose `housefile_segment_id` is non-null, a contact event is created carrying that `housefile_segment_id`, this drop's identifier as its `drop_id`, a `channel` of `MAIL`, a `contact_date` equal to the drop's `in_home_date`, and a `source` of `DROP_MAIL_CASCADE`. Rental lines create no contact event, because a rented name is not a housefile name whose contact pressure the retailer tracks. These synthesized rows are not subject to the contact-frequency caps of §3.8 — a mailing that the director approved is not blocked by a cap.

**Hop two.** Because each of those contact event rows now exists, the contact-pressure aggregation described in §3.8 is re-run for each affected housefile segment, rewriting its `fatigue_score`. Each such segment's `version` increments by one, whether or not the recomputed score actually moved.

**Hop three.** Because a recomputed `fatigue_score` may have crossed the line, any segment whose `fatigue_score` is now at or above the season's `fatigue_suppression_threshold` has its `contact_strategy` forced to `SUPPRESS_ONLY` and its `suppressed_at` stamped with the logical clock's current instant.

**Hop four.** Because a segment has flipped to `SUPPRESS_ONLY`, every circulation line referencing that segment in status `PROPOSED` or `ALLOCATED` whose *own* parent drop has `production_status` `PLANNED` transitions to `SUPPRESSED`, with `allocated_quantity`, `holdout_quantity`, `mailable_quantity`, `forecast_demand_cents`, `gross_margin_cents`, `circulation_cost_cents` and `contribution_cents` all set to 0. Lines on drops that are already `PRESORTED` or beyond are deliberately spared — those books are already committed to production and cannot be recalled.

**Hop five.** Because each of those lines has left `ALLOCATED`, its `committed_cost_cents` is credited back to its plan's `committed_cost_cents` and then zeroed on the line, its parent drop's `requested_quantity_total` and `planned_quantity` are recomputed, that drop's cost per book is re-run at the drop's *current* `cost_basis`, and the owning plan's three roll-up figures plus its `uncommitted_budget_cents` are recomputed — with a plan sitting in `COSTED` falling back to `DRAFT`. Because that drop's cost per book has moved, every line of the same drop that *survived* in `ALLOCATED` has its `circulation_cost_cents`, `gross_margin_cents` and `contribution_cents` re-derived at the new figure as well — rewritten in place, with its two forecast figures untouched and with neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moving, so a surviving line's profitability legitimately changes without any trace on its own row. Each suppressed line's `version` and its parent drop's `version` each increment by one.

The mailed drop's own `version` increments by one as well. Delivery is the quiet final step: `MAILED → DELIVERED` increments the drop's `version` by one and does nothing else, leaving `measurement_status` deliberately untouched because the two dimensions never synchronise. `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal on the production dimension.

### 3.8. Contact Governance, Fatigue Pressure and Sticky Suppression

Catalog contact is a shared resource across three channels, and the retailer governs it two ways: a hard per-window cap that refuses individual contacts, and a soft weighted pressure score that eventually suppresses a whole segment.

The hard cap lives in **suppression rules**, which a `PlatformAdmin` registers against a season. Each rule carries a `rule_code` such as `CAP_EMAIL_30D`, at least one character long and unique within the season with a repeat rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUPPRESSION_RULE_CODE`; a `channel_scope` of `MAIL`, `EMAIL`, `DIGITAL` or `ALL`, where `ALL` counts every channel together; a `window_days` trailing window between 1 and 365 whole days inclusive; a `max_contacts_per_window` ceiling that may not be negative; a `priority` of at least 1 that fixes evaluation order among a season's rules, with lower numbers evaluated first and `created_at` ascending breaking ties; and an `is_enabled` flag that defaults to true and determines whether the rule participates at all. The collection read for a season's rules accepts `channel_scope` and `is_enabled` as filters.

A `MarketingAnalyst` logs a **contact event** by naming a `housefile_segment_id`, a `channel` of `MAIL`, `EMAIL` or `DIGITAL`, and a `contact_date` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. An optional `drop_id` ties the contact to a catalog drop; it is null for email and digital touches that no drop produced. An optional `source` may be supplied, and a contact posted through this path is recorded as `MANUAL_LOG`, as distinct from the `DROP_MAIL_CASCADE` rows the mailing cascade synthesizes. Before the row is written, the season's enabled suppression rules are consulted in ascending `priority`: the **first** enabled rule whose `channel_scope` covers the contact's channel — either matching it exactly or being `ALL` — is the one that decides, and no later rule is consulted. That rule counts the segment's existing in-scope contact events within its `window_days` trailing window — an event whose whole-day age against the logical clock is exactly `window_days` still counts, one strictly older does not, and the count is taken over the events already stored regardless of where the incoming `contact_date` itself falls — and if admitting the new contact would push the count past `max_contacts_per_window` the request is rejected with `ERR_CONTACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

The soft pressure measure is the segment's `fatigue_score`, recomputed from scratch every time a contact event is created — whether logged manually or synthesized by the mailing cascade. Each contact carries a raw channel weight: a `MAIL` touch is worth 40 points, an `EMAIL` touch 12, and a `DIGITAL` touch 6, because a catalog in the mailbox fatigues a household far more than a display impression. Each contact's weight then decays in steps according to how many whole days separate its `contact_date` from the logical clock's current instant: a gap of 14 days or fewer retains the full 10000 basis points, 30 days or fewer retains 7500, 60 days or fewer retains 5000, 90 days or fewer retains 2500, and anything beyond that retains nothing. The decay is a step function, not a continuous curve — every contact inside a band contributes identically — and the band edges are inclusive on the fresher side, so a contact exactly 14 days old still retains the full weight while one at 15 days retains three quarters. A contact whose whole-day age exceeds the season's `fatigue_window_days` is skipped entirely. Each surviving contact's contribution is its channel weight multiplied by its decay factor, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole points, and `fatigue_score` is the exact sum of those individually rounded contributions. Three mail contacts each 61 days old therefore contribute ten points apiece for a total of thirty, rather than thirty being derived from a summed exact value — and at the threshold that difference decides a suppression.

Each contact event row also stores its own `channel_weight`, `decay_factor_bps` and `weighted_points`, and those three columns are written exactly once, at creation, from the clock as it stood at that moment. The row is immutable thereafter. A later recompute derives all three values locally for its own summation and never rewrites the stored row, so a row's frozen `weighted_points` may legitimately disagree with the contribution that same row is currently making to its segment's score. Contact events are read back through the drop that produced them or through the segment that received them, in either case filterable by `channel`.

When a recomputed `fatigue_score` reaches or exceeds the season's `fatigue_suppression_threshold`, the segment's `contact_strategy` is forced to `SUPPRESS_ONLY` and its `suppressed_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant; creating a contact event increments the segment's `version` by one whether or not the score moved. The threshold is re-tested on every recompute rather than only on the crossing one, so a segment already sitting in `SUPPRESS_ONLY` has its `suppressed_at` re-stamped to the clock's current value each further time a recompute leaves its score at or above the threshold, and the line-retirement hops of §3.7 run again on that recompute too — finding, ordinarily, no live line left to retire. Because contacts strictly older than the window are dropped from the sum, a segment's `fatigue_score` can *fall* as the logical clock advances, and a suppression can become unjustified in hindsight. Suppression is nevertheless sticky: once `contact_strategy` is `SUPPRESS_ONLY` the service never reverts it automatically — not when the score falls, and not when a recency-frequency-monetary re-score would have assigned a friendlier default. Only an explicit amendment by a `CirculationPlanner`, naming a new `contact_strategy` as described in §3.1, restores the segment to circulation.

### 3.9. Reading the Result: Test Cells and Matchback Attribution

Circulation is only worth planning if it can be read back afterwards, and a `MarketingAnalyst` owns two independent reads: the in-mail test design, and the attribution of website orders back to the books that caused them.

**Test cells** split an allocated line's names into competing treatments. A cell is created against a circulation line that must already be in status `ALLOCATED` — creating one on a line that is not allocated is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LINE_STATE`. The cell records that owning line in its own `circulation_line_id`, the line whose names the cell splits out. It carries a `cell_code` such as `OFFER_FREESHIP_T1`, unique within its line with a repeat rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_TEST_CELL_CODE`; a `test_type` naming what the cell varies, one of `SEGMENT`, `FORMAT` or `OFFER`; a `cell_role` of `TEST`, `CONTROL` or `HOLDOUT`; and a `quantity` of names greater than zero. A line may carry at most one cell whose `cell_role` is `CONTROL`, and a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTROL_CELL`. The names a test design consumes cannot exceed the names the line actually won: the sum of `quantity` across all of a line's cells may not exceed that line's `allocated_quantity`, and a breach is rejected with `ERR_TEST_CELL_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_LINE`. A new cell lands in status `PLANNED` with `significance` `UNMEASURED` and with `responders`, `demand_cents`, `response_rate_bps` and `lift_bps` all 0. A line's cells are read back filterable by `cell_role` and `status`.

Measuring a cell takes it `PLANNED → MEASURED` and is only possible once the mail has actually gone out: the cell's line must resolve to a drop whose `production_status` is `MAILED` or `DELIVERED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DROP_STATE`. A cell already in `MEASURED` cannot be measured again and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TEST_CELL_STATE`; `MEASURED` is terminal. The analyst supplies `responders` — which may not exceed the cell's `quantity`, a breach being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and `demand_cents`. The cell's `response_rate_bps` becomes `responders` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by `quantity`, rounded half up to whole basis points.

Lift is measured relative to the line's control. The service looks for the `MEASURED` cell on the same line whose `cell_role` is `CONTROL`. If the cell being measured *is* the control, or no measured control exists on the line, or the located control is the cell itself, then `lift_bps` is 0 and `significance` is `INSUFFICIENT_CONTROL`. If a distinct measured control exists but its own `response_rate_bps` is 0, lift is undefined — a relative comparison against a zero base is neither a finite number nor an infinite one worth reporting — so again `lift_bps` is 0 and `significance` is `INSUFFICIENT_CONTROL`. Otherwise `lift_bps` is this cell's `response_rate_bps` less the control's `response_rate_bps`, multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the control's `response_rate_bps`, rounded half up. The verdict then follows two tests in a fixed order. The sample-size guard runs **first** and inspects *both* cells: if either this cell's `quantity` or the control's `quantity` is below 1000 names, `significance` is `INSUFFICIENT_SAMPLE`, so a spectacular lift measured on a 900-name cell reports insufficient sample rather than significance. Only when both cells clear a thousand names does the magnitude test run: an absolute `lift_bps` of 1500 or more — fifteen percent in either direction — yields `SIGNIFICANT`, and anything smaller yields `NOT_SIGNIFICANT`. Measuring a cell also re-scores every *other* already-measured cell on the same line, because a sibling's lift depends on a control rate that may not have existed when the sibling was measured; each such sibling's `response_rate_bps`, `lift_bps` and `significance` are rewritten in place, moving neither that sibling's `updated_at` nor its `version`. Only the measured cell's `version` increments, by one.

**Matchback attribution** credits website orders back to mail. Those orders originate in an external ecommerce system and no business workflow in this service creates them, so a `PlatformAdmin` feeds them in: the ingest capability accepts a required `orders` array — anything else, including a missing key or a non-array value, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while an empty array is accepted and ingests nothing — whose elements must each be a JSON object carrying an `external_order_id` of at least one character, a `housefile_segment_id` that must resolve to an existing segment (otherwise `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`), an `order_date`, and a `demand_cents` amount that may not be negative. The `external_order_id` is the feed's identity key: re-ingesting one that was ingested before overwrites that stored order's segment, date and demand in place rather than adding a second row. The whole call is atomic in three sweeps — every element's `housefile_segment_id` is resolved first, then every element is validated against the schema, and only once both sweeps have passed for the entire array is anything written — so a single bad element leaves the store exactly as it was. The response reports an `ingested_count`, the count of elements accepted whether they created a row or overwrote one, and the `external_order_ids` accepted, echoed in the order they appeared in the request.

An analyst then opens a **matchback run** against a drop. The run requires the `expected_drop_version` of §2.9; an `attribution_variant` of either `LAST_TOUCH_MAIL` or `FRACTIONAL_MULTI_TOUCH`; and an optional `attribution_window_days`, the look-back in whole days from an online order back to a qualifying contact, constrained to the range 1 through 180 inclusive with an out-of-range value rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and defaulting to 90 whole days when the field is omitted or supplied as `null`. The drop's `production_status` must be `MAILED` or `DELIVERED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DROP_STATE` — this is the single guard that couples the drop's two dimensions, and it reads `production_status` without changing it. On the measurement dimension, a drop in `PENDING` moves `PENDING → MATCHBACK_OPEN` and the run is created in status `OPEN`. A drop already in `MATCHBACK_OPEN` takes a self-loop: every existing run of that drop in status `OPEN` or `FINALIZED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its own `version` incremented by one, and a fresh run is created in `OPEN`. A drop in `MATCHBACK_CLOSED` accepts no new run at all and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_STATE` — reaching the closed state does not unlock a re-read. Either way the drop's `version` increments by one.

The attribution itself walks every ingested online order. For each order, the qualifying contacts are the contact events for that order's `housefile_segment_id` whose `contact_date` falls inside a half-open window: strictly later than the order date minus the run's `attribution_window_days` whole days, and at or before the order date itself. A contact landing exactly on the order date therefore qualifies, while a contact landing exactly `attribution_window_days` before the order date does not. An order with no qualifying contact is unattributed and contributes nothing to any drop.

The two variants then distribute the order's `demand_cents` in genuinely different ways. Under `LAST_TOUCH_MAIL`, only mail can take credit and only the freshest mail touch: the qualifying contacts are narrowed to those whose `channel` is `MAIL`, and if none remain the order is unattributed; otherwise the winner is the mail contact with the greatest `contact_date`, with the smallest `created_at` breaking a tie, and the order's entire `demand_cents` is credited to that contact's `drop_id`. A manually logged `MAIL` contact carrying no `drop_id` at all competes for that last touch on equal terms, and when such a contact wins it the credit lands on no drop whatsoever: no run counts that order among its `attributed_order_count`, and the drop that really mailed the segment earlier in the window collects nothing either. Under `FRACTIONAL_MULTI_TOUCH`, every qualifying contact of every channel shares equally: the share is the order's `demand_cents` divided by the number of qualifying contacts, floored, and each qualifying contact's `drop_id` is credited that share — credits accumulate by drop at one share per qualifying contact, so a drop that contacted the same segment twice inside the window collects two shares, and a share credited to a contact with no `drop_id` again lands nowhere; the whole remainder — the order's `demand_cents` less the share multiplied by the contact count — is then credited additionally to the contact with the **earliest** `contact_date`, with the smallest `created_at` breaking a tie. So on a twenty-thousand-cent order with three qualifying contacts on three different drops, each drop receives 6666 cents except the earliest-contact drop, which receives 6668. Cents are conserved under both models: the credits summed across every recipient equal the order's demand exactly, never more — and a credit that falls to a contact carrying no `drop_id` is simply held by no drop, so it never surfaces on any run's figures.

Four figures are then persisted on the run. `attributed_order_count` is the number of ingested orders that credited at least one whole cent to *this* run's drop, and `attributed_demand_cents` is the total cents credited to this drop. The one-cent floor is a real threshold rather than a turn of phrase: an order whose credit to this drop works out to 0 — a `FRACTIONAL_MULTI_TOUCH` order whose `demand_cents` is smaller than its qualifying contact count, so that the floored share is 0 and the remainder fell to a contact of some other drop, or an order whose `demand_cents` is 0 outright — is not counted in `attributed_order_count` and adds nothing to `attributed_demand_cents`, even though a qualifying contact of this very drop took part in the split. `matched_order_count` is the number of ingested orders whose segment matches a segment this drop contacted, counted regardless of whether the window admitted any contact or which drop ultimately won the credit. `unmatched_order_count` is `matched_order_count` less `attributed_order_count`.

Finalizing a run takes it `OPEN → FINALIZED`, requires the run to be in `OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MATCHBACK_STATE`) and requires its drop to be in `MATCHBACK_OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_STATE`). It stamps the run's `finalized_at` with the logical clock's current instant, moves the drop's measurement dimension `MATCHBACK_OPEN → MATCHBACK_CLOSED`, and increments both the run's `version` and the drop's `version` by one. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a run, and `MATCHBACK_CLOSED` is terminal on the drop's measurement dimension. A drop's runs are read back filterable by `status`, newest first.
