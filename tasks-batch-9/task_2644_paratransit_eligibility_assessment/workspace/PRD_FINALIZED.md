# Paratransit Eligibility Assessment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every transit agency that runs fixed-route service is obliged to certify who may ride its complementary paratransit service, and the accessibility office that performs that certification lives permanently between two pressures. On one side sits a statutory processing deadline: a rider whose complete application has been sitting for three weeks acquires service by operation of law whether or not the office has finished looking at it. On the other side sits an evidentiary burden — functional ability questionnaires, attestations from licensed professionals, an in-person functional assessment that the agency itself must provide a ride to, and a written determination that survives independent review. Today this work is tracked in spreadsheets, wall calendars and email threads, which means the office cannot answer the two questions that matter most: *which packets are about to breach the twenty-one-day window*, and *is this rider's suspension actually supportable on the conduct record as it stands right now*.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record covering that whole certification lifecycle. It handles intake and completeness screening, professional verification with its revision cycles, evaluator slot publication with turnaround buffers, wheelchair-accessible vehicle reservation for assessment rides, scoring with independent sign-off, determination issuance with unconditional, conditional, temporary and ineligible outcomes, trip-by-trip condition evaluation for conditional riders, a sixty-day appeal window heard by a reviewer who took no part in the original decision, a points-based no-show and late-cancellation policy with full due-process protections before any suspension takes effect, visitor eligibility for riders certified by another agency, and referral into fixed-route travel training.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **segregation of duties is enforced by the server, not by convention**: the evaluator who scores an assessment cannot sign it off, the specialist who determined eligibility cannot review its appeal, and the officer who proposes a suspension cannot decide it. Second, **time is explicit and server-controlled**: every deadline, rolling window and expiry is measured against a logical clock the agency drives, and every obligation that would otherwise fire "overnight" fires inside an operator-invoked sweep whose step order is part of the contract. Nothing in this system depends on wall-clock time or on background timers.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles the addressed endpoint authorizes, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before everything else — before resource lookup, before request-shape validation, and before any state check.

The eight roles are `Applicant`, `IntakeClerk`, `Evaluator`, `EligibilitySpecialist`, `IndependentReviewer`, `ConductOfficer`, `TravelTrainer`, and `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin`.

An `Applicant` is the rider (or their authorized representative) acting on their own eligibility. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant` record; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` compare that resource's owning applicant reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `Applicant` may read and act only where the owning applicant matches: an `Application` through its `applicant_id`, a `Determination` through its `applicant_id`, an `Appeal` through the applicant of the determination it contests, a `SuspensionProceeding` through its `applicant_id`, a `VisitorEligibility` through its `applicant_id`, and a `FunctionalAssessment` through the `applicant_id` of the application being assessed. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership rule guards exactly fifteen operations: fetching one applicant, fetching one application, listing applications, fetching one determination, listing determinations, listing a determination's trip conditions, listing a determination's appeals, listing a rider's trip events, listing a rider's suspension proceedings, listing a rider's visitor-eligibility records, opening an application, submitting an application, filing an appeal, responding to a suspension notice, and responding to a travel-training referral.

The remaining seven roles are staff roles, and for all of them `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeClerk`, `Evaluator`, `EligibilitySpecialist`, `IndependentReviewer`, `ConductOfficer`, `TravelTrainer`, or `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin`, the server may *record* the supplied `X-User-ID` on a resource — as `evaluator_user_id` on a published slot, `scored_by_user_id` and `signed_off_by_user_id` on an assessment, `determined_by_user_id` on a determination, `reviewer_user_id` on an appeal, and `proposed_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a suspension proceeding — and it may compare one recorded operator identifier against another to enforce independence, but it never resolves an opaque identifier to a stored row. `IntakeClerk` staff assemble packets, chase verification, book assessments and arrange rides. An `Evaluator` conducts the in-person assessment and records observed scores, and may never sign off on their own scores. An `EligibilitySpecialist` signs off scores, drafts determinations and issues them, and is barred from reviewing appeals of their own determinations. An `IndependentReviewer` hears an appeal and records its disposition. A `ConductOfficer` ingests trip outcomes, excuses misses, proposes suspensions and decides them, but never both proposes and decides the same one. A `TravelTrainer` enrolls riders in fixed-route training and records post-training competency. An `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` publishes assessment slots and vehicles, assigns independent reviewers, and drives the logical clock and the deferred-obligation sweep.

One further authorization rule is specific to appeals: on the two endpoints an `IndependentReviewer` owns — opening the review and recording the decision — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `reviewer_user_id` recorded on that appeal. Any other reviewer, even one holding the `IndependentReviewer` role, is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER` (HTTP 403). Separately, on the five endpoints where one operator's identity is compared against an identity recorded at an earlier segregated step — assessment sign-off, determination issuance, reviewer assignment, appeal decision, and suspension decision — the headers must be present and usable for that comparison; a request that cannot supply a comparable actor identity is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The substantive independence rules themselves are stated in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` is an integer bounded to the inclusive range 1 through 100 and defaults to 10 when omitted. `offset` is an integer that must be zero or greater and defaults to 0 when omitted. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400); this check is declared for the five top-level collection listings — the application list, the functional-assessment list, the transportation-booking list, the determination list, and the travel-training-referral list — and the same parameter names, defaults and bounds apply to every other listing described in §4.

Unless a listing's description in §4 states another ordering, results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Three listings carry an explicitly different or reinforced order: a rider's trip events are ordered by `trip_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; an application's professional verifications are ordered by `created_at` ascending; and a determination's trip conditions are ordered by `created_at` ascending, which is also the order in which those conditions are evaluated during a trip-by-trip check.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. The `total_count` is the number of records matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource is assigned a server-minted UUID as its `id`. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded at creation from the logical clock.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that record — including mutations produced as a side effect of an operation addressed at a different resource. There is exactly one exception. A `TripCondition` is immutable once written: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. Trip conditions are created only as part of drafting a determination and are never edited, re-ordered or deleted afterwards.

Two mirror fields deserve special mention because they are *not* declared foreign keys and must not be treated as the authoritative link. `active_determination_id` on a rider is a denormalized pointer to the determination that currently governs that rider; the authoritative child-to-parent link runs the other way, from a determination's own `applicant_id`, and the supported way to discover a rider's governing grant is to list determinations filtered by that rider with `status` restricted to `ISSUED`. Likewise, cascade-created records — the successor determination synthesized by an appeal remedy, and the travel-training referral auto-created for a conditional grant — are not echoed in the response body of the request that created them; they are discoverable only through the determination listing (filtering on the rider and on `origin` equal to `APPEAL_REMEDY`) and the travel-training-referral listing (filtering on the rider) respectively.

### 2.5. Request Screening Order and Path Resolution

When a single request would fail more than one rule, the rejection the client receives is fixed, not arbitrary. The server screens every request through the following stages in this exact order, and returns the first failure it finds:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` among the eight declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role is listed for the addressed operation, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Applicant ownership.** For the fifteen operations enumerated in §2.1, an acting `Applicant` owns the addressed resource, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
4. **Actor-identity usability.** On the five segregated-step operations named in §2.1 the acting identity must be usable for the independence comparison, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. **Assigned-reviewer authorization.** On opening a review and on recording an appeal decision, the actor is the appeal's assigned reviewer, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER` (403).
6. **Path existence.** Every identifier appearing in the request path must resolve to a stored record, and the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so the outermost segment's 404 always wins over an inner segment's. The code names the entity that could not be found: `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIP_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROCEEDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_VISITOR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404.
7. **Body reference existence.** Every reference carried in a request body — `applicant_id`, `application_id`, `slot_id`, `vehicle_id`, `assessment_id`, `determination_id` — must resolve to a stored record. The affected operations are opening an application, booking an assessment, reserving a ride, filing an appeal, ingesting a trip event, and referring a rider to travel training; the codes are the corresponding not-found identifiers with HTTP 404. A `rescheduled_from_assessment_id` that names no stored assessment is likewise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
8. **Parentage.** Where a record is addressed through a parent, it must actually belong to that parent. Recording a professional's response addresses a verification, identified by `verification_id`, underneath an application; a verification that exists but whose `application_id` differs from the application named in the path is `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404), not a 403 or a 409.
9. **Request shape.** All required fields present; every enum-typed field carrying one of its declared values; every numeric field inside its declared range (observed scores in 0 through 4, `distance_meters_walked` zero or greater, `threshold_value` zero or greater, `fixed_route_competency_score` in 0 through 100); every instant a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC value; and every interval satisfying a start strictly earlier than its end. Failures are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
10. **Pagination shape.** As described in §2.3, yielding `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
11. **State preconditions.** The addressed record is in a status from which the requested transition is declared, yielding the 409 family described alongside each workflow in §3.
12. **Version reconciliation.** The optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.6, yielding HTTP 412.
13. **Business rules.** Everything else — accessibility and safety guards, independence rules, due-process gates, cross-entity referential requirements, uniqueness, capacity and interval conflicts, and temporal windows — evaluated in the relative order stated with each rule in §3.

Within stage 11 the state checks are ordered by the entity being driven: the application's own workflow status is checked first, then a professional verification's status, then a functional assessment's two dimensions and a determination's issuance status, then an appeal's appealability and status, then a suspension proceeding's and a referral's status, and finally a booking's cancellability and a visitor record's activity. That within-stage-11 ranking orders only the statuses of records the addressed operation actually drives. A status requirement an operation places on some *other* record — the parent packet's `ASSESSMENT_COMPLETE` at determination issuance, for instance — is one of the stage-13 cross-entity referential requirements instead, and is therefore screened after the driven record's own status has been accepted.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Packets and Rider Records

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a legitimate supersede rule exists that would otherwise let a writer working from a stale read silently overwrite work it never observed.

A `POST /applications/{id}/determinations` request must carry `expected_application_version` as a required field of its body, holding the `version` of the target `Application` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the application's current `version`, the draft is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because another specialist may already have drafted a determination on the same packet — a draft that would legitimately be superseded when a determination is issued — so a writer whose view of the packet's `status`, `completeness_established_at` and existing drafts predates that work must re-read before drafting.

A `POST /applicants/{id}/suspension-proceedings` request must carry `expected_applicant_version` as a required field of its body, holding the `version` of the target `Applicant` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the applicant's current `version`, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a newer proposal voids any proceeding still sitting in `PROPOSED`, so a proposal written from a stale view of the rider's conduct mirrors — the point balances, the miss ratio, `prior_upheld_suspension_count` — would consolidate the wrong review window into a decision the office then has to defend.

No other operation accepts or requires a version field. Endpoints outside these two never take `expected_application_version`, `expected_applicant_version`, or any other expected-version input.

### 2.7. The Logical Clock and the Day-Close Sweep

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every deadline, rolling window, expiry and elapsed-day count is measured against a server-held logical clock that an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` sets explicitly by supplying `current_utc`; the response echoes the accepted `current_utc` and no stored record is mutated by setting the clock. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time.

Obligations that mature with the passage of time do not fire on their own. They fire when an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` invokes the day-close sweep, which walks the whole database once and advances everything that has become due at the clock's current instant. The sweep executes exactly five steps in exactly this order:

- **Step 1 — hearings held.** Every `Appeal` in `HEARING_SCHEDULED` whose `hearing_at` is at or before the current instant advances to `HEARD`. Its `heard_at` is set to the current instant and its `decision_due_date` is set to the calendar date of `heard_at` plus 30 days.
- **Step 2 — suspension due process and service windows.** Every `SuspensionProceeding` in `NOTICE_SENT` whose `response_deadline_date` falls strictly before today's date advances to `RESPONSE_LAPSED`, changing nothing but its status and `updated_at` — the lapse itself is the due-process evidence. Every proceeding in `UPHELD_PENDING` whose `suspension_start_date` is at or before today's date advances to `ACTIVE` and sets the rider's `service_status` to `SUSPENDED`. Every proceeding in `ACTIVE` whose `suspension_end_date` falls strictly before today's date advances to `SERVED`, sets `days_served` equal to `duration_days` and `days_forgiven` to zero, and returns the rider's `service_status` to `ACTIVE`.
- **Step 3 — determination expiry.** Every `Determination` in `ISSUED` with a non-null `expires_at` that is at or before today's date expires, triggering the three-hop cascade described in §3.5.
- **Step 4 — presumptive eligibility.** Applications that have been pending at least 21 days receive presumptive eligibility, under the conditions and in the order described in §3.2.
- **Step 5 — overdue appeals.** Every `Appeal` in `HEARD` whose `decision_due_date` falls strictly before today's date and whose `outcome` is still null has `continued_service_extended` set to true, and the rider's `current_eligibility_status` becomes `PRESUMPTIVE`.

Several steps may write the same rider's `current_eligibility_status` in one sweep. There is no precedence table: the last writer in step order simply wins. The ordering of steps 3 and 4 is therefore contractual and outcome-changing. Consider a rider whose five-year unconditional grant expires today while their recertification packet has been pending 25 days. Because expiry runs in step 3, the grant is gone before step 4 evaluates its "holds no issued determination" guard, so step 4 fires and the rider ends the sweep `PRESUMPTIVE` rather than `EXPIRED`. Had step 4 run first, it would have seen the not-yet-expired grant, declined to grant presumptive eligibility, and left the rider `EXPIRED` with no service at all — the exact outcome the statute forbids.

The sweep is idempotent at a fixed clock: invoking it twice without moving the clock changes nothing on the second call. Its response body reports six counters — `appeals_heard_count`, `proceedings_advanced_count`, `determinations_expired_count`, `referrals_withdrawn_count`, `presumptive_granted_count` and `appeals_overdue_count` — corresponding respectively to step 1 advances, step 2 advances, step 3 expiries, referrals withdrawn by the step 3 cascade, step 4 grants and step 5 extensions.

### 2.8. Day Counting, Rounding and Window Arithmetic

Two different kinds of time arithmetic appear in this domain and they must not be confused. **Elapsed-day counts between two instants** are computed as the whole number of days in the difference, discarding any remainder — that is, the difference in seconds divided by 86,400 and floored. This governs `wait_days` on a professional verification, `applicant_delay_days` on an application, and both pending-day variants. **Calendar-date windows** are computed by adding or subtracting whole days to a date, and their boundaries are stated inclusively or exclusively rule by rule: the statutory determination window is 21 days, the suspension response window is 14 days, the travel-training referral response window is 14 days, the appeal filing window is 60 days, and the appeal decision window is 30 days.

Rolling windows are inclusive at both ends and are anchored deliberately. The conduct window spans 90 calendar days ending on today's date, so its first day is today's date minus 89 days and a trip falling on either boundary date is inside the window. The visitor allowance window spans 365 calendar days ending on the service date being requested, so its first day is that date minus 364 days.

Only one quantity in the system is a rounded decimal: the pooled miss percentage `no_show_ratio_pct` is carried to **one decimal place, rounded half up**, in percent units. Every other computed quantity is an exact integer — points, day counts, term lengths, capacities, reservation counters — or an exact enum, and no rounding is applied to any of them. Interval comparisons on instants are exact to the second, except for the evaluator turnaround buffer, which is expressed and compared at minute resolution.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties Across the Certification Chain

Three points in this workflow require two different human beings, and the server enforces each of them by comparing recorded operator identifiers. First, the person who signs off an assessment must not be the person who scored it: if the signing `X-User-ID` equals the assessment's `scored_by_user_id`, the sign-off is rejected with `ERR_SIGNOFF_NOT_INDEPENDENT` (HTTP 422). Second, an appeal's reviewer must have had no hand in the decision under review: the `reviewer_user_id` being assigned must differ both from the contested determination's `determined_by_user_id` and from the `scored_by_user_id` of the assessment that produced the scores, and a violation of either comparison is `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_INDEPENDENT` (HTTP 422). Third, the officer who proposed a suspension must not be the one who decides it: a deciding `X-User-ID` equal to the proceeding's `proposed_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_DECIDER_NOT_INDEPENDENT` (HTTP 422).

The third rule has one structural consequence worth stating plainly. A proceeding whose `origin` is `AUTO_THRESHOLD` was raised by the conduct cascade rather than by a person, so its `proposed_by_user_id` is null and the independence comparison is always satisfied — any `ConductOfficer` may decide it. Only a proceeding whose `origin` is `MANUAL` can trip the rule.

### 2.10. Derived Mirrors and Conserved Quantities

A rider's record is deliberately fat with denormalized state, because the accessibility office needs one screen that answers "may this person ride today, and what is their conduct record". None of these mirror fields is ever written directly by a client; each is maintained by the side effects of the operations described in §3 and is recomputed on read where the rule says so.

The eligibility mirrors are `current_eligibility_status`, which takes one of `NONE`, `PRESUMPTIVE`, `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE`, `EXPIRED` or `VISITOR` and starts at `NONE` for a freshly registered rider; `eligibility_category`, which mirrors the category of the governing determination and is null whenever `current_eligibility_status` is `NONE`, `PRESUMPTIVE` or `EXPIRED`; and `active_determination_id`, which starts null. The service mirror is `service_status`, which reads `SUSPENDED` for as long as any of the rider's suspension proceedings sits in `ACTIVE` and reads `ACTIVE` otherwise; it starts at `ACTIVE`. Two further booleans track protections in flight: `continued_service_during_appeal`, true for as long as one of the rider's appeals sits in `FILED`, `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED` or `HEARD`, and `fixed_route_competency_confirmed`, true once any travel-training referral for that rider has reached `COMPLETED` with a `fixed_route_competency_score` of at least 80. Both start false.

The conduct mirrors are recomputed together, on every trip-event ingestion, on every excusal, and on every read of the rider: `no_show_points_event_basis` and `no_show_points_report_basis` (both starting at 0), `chargeable_miss_count_90d` and `scheduled_trip_count_90d` (both starting at 0), and `no_show_ratio_pct` (starting at 0.0). Alongside them sit two cumulative counters that survive the rolling window: `prior_upheld_suspension_count`, starting at 0, and `suspension_days_ledger_total`, also starting at 0.

Three other records carry mirrors of their own. An `AssessmentSlot` maintains `booked_count`, the number of assessments on that slot whose `attendance_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ATTENDED`, and derives `status` from it: `FULL` exactly when `booked_count` equals `capacity`, `OPEN` otherwise, starting at `OPEN` with a `booked_count` of 0. A `Vehicle` maintains two conserved counters, `open_wheelchair_reservations` and `open_ambulatory_reservations`, each counting that vehicle's bookings still in `RESERVED` with `requires_wheelchair_position` true and false respectively; both start at 0, and a booking that leaves `RESERVED` by any route always releases exactly the one counter it consumed. A `Determination` maintains `under_appeal`, true while one of its appeals sits in `FILED`, `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED` or `HEARD`; `trip_condition_count`, the number of trip conditions attached to it; and `fixed_route_competency_confirmed`, a mirror of the rider's own competency flag taken at evaluation time. All three start at false, 0 and false respectively.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Rider and Opening the Intake Packet

Registration is the cheapest step in the process and carries no gates beyond request shape. An `IntakeClerk` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` records the rider's `full_name` and `date_of_birth` — the latter as an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form — together with `residence_zone`, which is either `IN_SERVICE_AREA` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE_AREA` and states whether the rider lives inside the agency's ADA complementary service area. Three further attributes are optional. `mobility_aid` names the primary aid the rider uses outdoors and takes one of `NONE`, `CANE`, `WALKER`, `MANUAL_WHEELCHAIR`, `POWER_WHEELCHAIR` or `SCOOTER`, defaulting to `NONE`; it is the field that later drives the wheelchair-position safety checks on rides and assessment sites. `primary_language` is the notice language code and defaults to `EN`. `home_agency_name` names the transit agency that certified an out-of-area rider and defaults to null for local applicants. All of the rider's derived mirrors take the starting values listed in §2.10, and the new record's `version` is 1.

An intake packet is opened by an `Applicant` acting on their own behalf or by an `IntakeClerk`, with a payload naming the `applicant_id` it belongs to, an `application_type` of either `NEW` for first-time certification or `RECERTIFICATION` for an expiring or already expired grant, the `functional_answers` questionnaire, and optionally `claimed_conditions`, a list of free-text condition labels the rider asserts, which defaults to an empty list. The packet is created in `DRAFT` with `version` 1, `submitted_at`, `completeness_established_at`, `determination_due_date`, `presumptive_granted_at` and `attests_permanent` all null, `missing_items` an empty list, and `resubmission_count`, `verification_excluded_days`, `applicant_delay_days`, `days_pending_statutory` and `days_pending_net_agency` all zero.

One rider may have only one packet open at a time. If the rider already holds an application whose `status` is anything other than `DETERMINED`, opening another is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_APPLICATION`. Because `DETERMINED` is the only terminal status of the intake workflow, this is exactly the rule "finish or abandon your current packet before starting a new one", and it is what forces a recertification to wait for the previous cycle to close.

The `functional_answers` object is the heart of completeness screening. It is considered complete only when all five of its keys are present and non-null: `can_board_bus_steps` and `can_navigate_unfamiliar_route`, both booleans; `max_walk_distance_meters`, a whole number of meters that is zero or greater; `uses_mobility_aid_outdoors`, a boolean; and `has_cognitive_impairment`, a boolean. A packet may be created and left in `DRAFT` with an incomplete questionnaire — the five-key test bites at submission, not at creation.

### 3.2. Submission, Return for Incomplete Items, and the Statutory Clock

Submitting a packet advances it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, and either an `Applicant` acting on their own packet or an `IntakeClerk` may do it. The submitter may optionally supply a replacement `functional_answers` object with the request; whether the questionnaire arrives with this request or was recorded earlier, it must satisfy the five-key completeness test of §3.1, and a questionnaire missing any of the five keys or carrying a null for any of them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Submission also requires that no *other* packet of the same rider is anywhere in the active pipeline — that is, none of the rider's other applications sits in `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_INCOMPLETE`, `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`, `ASSESSMENT_SCHEDULED` or `ASSESSMENT_COMPLETE`; a submission that would violate this is refused with the same `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_APPLICATION` code the office sees when opening a duplicate packet. Attempting to submit from a status that has no declared submit edge — from `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, say, or from the terminal `DETERMINED` — is `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` (409).

A successful submission does four things. It stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock's current instant. It sets `completeness_established_at` equal to `submitted_at`, which is the moment the statutory determination window begins. It sets `determination_due_date` to the calendar date of `completeness_established_at` plus 21 days. And it clears `missing_items` back to an empty list. The packet's `version` increments, as it does on every one of the intake transitions in this section.

When an `IntakeClerk` reviews a submitted packet and finds it deficient, the packet goes from `SUBMITTED` to `RETURNED_INCOMPLETE`. The request must carry `missing_items` as a non-empty array of item labels — an empty array or a non-array value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and those labels are stored verbatim on the packet so the rider knows what to supply. Returning a packet stops the statutory clock hard: `completeness_established_at` is set back to null and `determination_due_date` becomes null with it, and `days_pending_statutory` freezes at whatever value it held at the moment of the return.

Resubmission is the cycle edge of this workflow: `RETURNED_INCOMPLETE` returns to `SUBMITTED` under the same submit operation and the same actors. Here the crucial rule is that the statutory clock is **reset, not resumed**. `completeness_established_at` is set to the new `submitted_at`, so the 21-day count restarts from zero and `determination_due_date` becomes the date of the new `submitted_at` plus 21 days; days that elapsed before the return-incomplete never enter the count again. If the request supplies a `functional_answers` object it replaces the stored one wholesale. `resubmission_count` increases by one on this edge and only on this edge, so it reads as the number of completed return-and-resubmit cycles, and `missing_items` is cleared.

Two elapsed-day counters are published on every packet, and they answer different questions. `days_pending_statutory` is the governing number: it is zero whenever `completeness_established_at` is null, and otherwise it is the whole number of days elapsed from `completeness_established_at` to the logical clock's current instant, discarding any partial day. It counts every calendar day since completeness was established, including days the agency spent waiting on a professional's attestation and days lost because the rider missed an assessment. `days_pending_net_agency` is the internal performance number reported to management as agency-controlled processing time: take `days_pending_statutory`, subtract `verification_excluded_days`, subtract `applicant_delay_days`, and if the result is below zero report zero instead. Whenever either exclusion is non-zero the net figure is strictly smaller than the statutory figure on the same clock.

The two exclusion counters accrue independently of the statutory clock and are never reset by resubmission, which is why a freshly restarted cycle can already show a net figure below its statutory figure. `verification_excluded_days` grows by the whole number of days a professional verification waited — from its `requested_at` to its `received_at`, discarding any partial day — every time a response is recorded, whichever way that response goes. `applicant_delay_days` grows by the whole number of days between a missed assessment's slot start and the replacement slot's start, every time a replacement assessment is booked against a prior no-show. Neither counter shrinks.

Presumptive eligibility is the statute's answer to a slow office, and it is granted only by step 4 of the day-close sweep — never synchronously by a submission or a read. The sweep walks the rider's applications in `created_at` ascending order and grants presumptive eligibility to a packet when **all four** of the following hold at that moment: `days_pending_statutory` is at least 21; the packet's `status` is none of `DRAFT`, `RETURNED_INCOMPLETE` or `DETERMINED`; `presumptive_granted_at` is still null; and the rider holds no determination in `ISSUED` at that instant. When they hold, `presumptive_granted_at` is stamped with the sweep's instant and the rider's `current_eligibility_status` becomes `PRESUMPTIVE`. Because `presumptive_granted_at` is checked and set exactly once, a rider cannot be granted presumptive eligibility twice for the same packet; and because the sweep tests for an issued determination at the moment it runs, a rider whose determination lands the day before the breach never becomes `PRESUMPTIVE` at all. When a determination is subsequently issued the rider leaves `PRESUMPTIVE`; `presumptive_granted_at` is retained on the packet as an audit record of the breach even though it no longer governs the rider's status.

### 3.3. Professional Verification of Functional Limitations

Once a packet is `SUBMITTED`, an `IntakeClerk` asks a licensed professional to verify the claimed functional limitations, which moves the packet from `SUBMITTED` to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and creates a verification record. The request identifies the professional by `provider_name`, by `provider_type` — one of `PHYSICIAN`, `PHYSICAL_THERAPIST`, `OCCUPATIONAL_THERAPIST`, `PSYCHOLOGIST`, `SOCIAL_WORKER` or `OTHER` — and by `provider_license_number`, which is kept purely for the audit record. The new record starts in `REQUESTED` with `requested_at` stamped from the logical clock, `received_at` null, `wait_days` zero, and `attests_conditions`, `attests_permanent` and `insufficiency_reason` all null. Only one request may be outstanding at a time: if any verification of this packet is still in `REQUESTED`, the new request is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_PENDING` (409).

Recording the professional's response addresses the verification through its parent packet, and the `outcome` supplied — either `ACCEPTED` or `INSUFFICIENT`, any other value being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — decides which of the two branches out of `REQUESTED` is taken. The verification must still be in `REQUESTED`; a second response against an already-answered verification is `ERR_VERIFICATION_STATE_INVALID` (409). Both branches are terminal for the verification record itself — `RECEIVED` and `INSUFFICIENT` are both end states, and neither can be revisited.

On the **accepted** branch the professional attests to the limitations, and the request must carry `attests_conditions` as a non-empty array — the subset of the packet's `claimed_conditions` the professional is willing to stand behind — together with a non-null `attests_permanent` stating whether the attested limitation is permanent. Either omission is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The verification moves `REQUESTED → RECEIVED`, `received_at` is stamped from the clock, and `wait_days` is computed as the whole days elapsed from `requested_at` to `received_at`, discarding any partial day. That figure is added to the packet's `verification_excluded_days`. The packet copies the professional's `attests_permanent` onto its own `attests_permanent` field — this is the single input the scoring calculation later reads from outside the assessment itself — and the packet advances `PENDING_VERIFICATION → ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`.

On the **insufficient** branch the professional's answer does not carry the packet forward, and the request must carry `insufficiency_reason`; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The verification moves `REQUESTED → INSUFFICIENT`, `received_at` is stamped from the clock, and `wait_days` is computed by the same whole-day rule and added to the packet's `verification_excluded_days` exactly as on the accepted branch. The packet's `attests_permanent` is left untouched. Crucially, the packet stays in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` — this is a self-loop, not a regression — and `completeness_established_at` is not disturbed, so an insufficient attestation costs the agency statutory days without buying it any progress.

The office recovers from an insufficient answer by requesting verification again. That re-request is the second self-loop on `PENDING_VERIFICATION`: it is permitted when no verification of the packet is in `REQUESTED` and at least one is in `INSUFFICIENT`, it creates a fresh record in `REQUESTED` with a new `requested_at`, and it leaves the packet's `status` and `completeness_established_at` exactly as they were. Together the insufficient-response edge and the re-request edge form the verification revision cycle, which may repeat as many times as the professional's answers require. Both response recording and re-requesting increment the packet's `version`.

### 3.4. Publishing Assessment Slots, Booking the Assessment, and the Provided Ride

Assessment capacity is published by an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` as slots. A slot names its `site_name`, the `evaluator_user_id` of the evaluator who staffs it, a `starts_at` and `ends_at` pair of UTC instants with the end strictly after the start, and a `capacity` of at least one assessment. `site_is_wheelchair_accessible` is optional and defaults to true; publishing it as false marks a site that cannot receive riders who need a securement position. A new slot always begins with `booked_count` zero and `status` `OPEN`. A slot whose `starts_at` is not strictly after the logical clock's current instant is refused with `ERR_SLOT_IN_PAST` (422).

Evaluators need turnaround time between appointments, so two slots staffed by the same `evaluator_user_id` must be separated by a mandatory quarter-hour buffer. The conflict test treats each slot as a closed interval expanded by that buffer on both sides: the new slot conflicts with an existing one when the new slot's start falls earlier than the existing slot's end extended forward by 15 minutes **and** the existing slot's start falls earlier than the new slot's end extended forward by 15 minutes. All slots sharing that evaluator are considered, whether they are `OPEN` or already `FULL`. A conflict is refused with `ERR_EVALUATOR_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409). Note that back-to-back slots — where one ends exactly when the next begins — do conflict here, and a gap of exactly 15 minutes is the smallest gap that is accepted.

Booking the in-person assessment is an `IntakeClerk` action that advances the packet from `ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` to `ASSESSMENT_SCHEDULED`. The payload names the `slot_id` being reserved, may set `requires_wheelchair_position` (default false) to declare that the rider needs a securement position on the provided ride and an accessible site, and may set `rescheduled_from_assessment_id` to point at a prior assessment that ended in `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED_BY_AGENCY`; that field defaults to null and is the self-referential link that lets the office walk a reschedule chain. The chosen slot must be `OPEN` — a slot at capacity is `ERR_SLOT_FULL` (409) — and must start strictly after the current instant, else `ERR_SLOT_IN_PAST` (422). When `requires_wheelchair_position` is true the chosen slot must have `site_is_wheelchair_accessible` true, else `ERR_SITE_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` (422). A successful booking creates the assessment with `attendance_status` `SCHEDULED` and `scoring_status` `NOT_STARTED`, all five score inputs null, `recommended_category`, `recommended_term_days`, `scored_by_user_id`, `signed_off_by_user_id` and `attendance_recorded_at` null; it increments the slot's `booked_count` by one and flips the slot's `status` to `FULL` when `booked_count` reaches `capacity`; and it increments the packet's `version`. When `rescheduled_from_assessment_id` is supplied, the packet's `applicant_delay_days` additionally grows by the whole days between the prior assessment's slot start and this slot's start.

Because the agency is obliged to get the rider to the assessment, it also runs a small accessible fleet. An `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` registers a vehicle by its `vehicle_number`, with `wheelchair_positions` counting securement positions on board and `ambulatory_seats` counting non-securement seats; both are optional, default to zero, and may not be negative. `status` is optional and defaults to `IN_SERVICE`; the only other value is `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Both open-reservation counters start at zero.

Reserving the ride is done by an `IntakeClerk` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` with a payload naming the `assessment_id` the ride serves, the `vehicle_id` being reserved, and the pickup window as `pickup_window_start` (inclusive) and `pickup_window_end` (exclusive), the end strictly after the start. `requires_wheelchair_position` is optional, defaults to false, and decides whether the ride consumes a securement position or an ambulatory seat. The booking is created in `RESERVED` with `cancelled_reason` null, and it increments `open_wheelchair_reservations` on the vehicle when it consumes a securement position and `open_ambulatory_reservations` when it does not.

Four rules guard a reservation, and when more than one is violated they are evaluated in this order. First, the chosen vehicle must be `IN_SERVICE`; a vehicle marked `OUT_OF_SERVICE` is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_OUT_OF_SERVICE` (409). Second, a ride that needs a securement position requires the vehicle to have at least one `wheelchair_positions`, else `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` (422). Third comes the window-capacity test described next, refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Fourth, the ride must actually get the rider there in time: `pickup_window_end` must fall at or before the assessment slot's `starts_at`, and a later window is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_LATE` (422).

The window-capacity test treats pickup windows as half-open intervals that include their start and exclude their end. Two bookings overlap when the first one's start falls strictly before the second one's end and the second one's start falls strictly before the first one's end; consequently two windows that merely touch — one ending exactly when the other begins — never overlap and are always allowed. The two lanes on a vehicle are counted separately: a booking that needs a securement position is measured against the vehicle's `wheelchair_positions`, and one that does not is measured against its `ambulatory_seats`. The server counts the vehicle's bookings that are still in `RESERVED`, that sit in the same lane as the new request, and that overlap the requested window; if adding the new booking to that set would exceed the lane's capacity, the reservation is refused. Bookings in `CANCELLED`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW_CLOSED` never occupy a lane.

A reserved ride may be cancelled by an `IntakeClerk` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin`, moving it `RESERVED → CANCELLED` with a `cancelled_reason` recorded from the request. Only a booking still in `RESERVED` is cancellable; anything else is `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (409). Cancellation decrements exactly the one vehicle counter the booking had consumed — `open_wheelchair_reservations` when the booking needed a securement position, `open_ambulatory_reservations` otherwise — returning it to its pre-booking value.

### 3.5. Attendance, Scoring and Independent Sign-Off

A functional assessment carries two independent state dimensions that evolve separately. The attendance dimension starts at `SCHEDULED` and ends at one of `ATTENDED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED_BY_AGENCY`. The scoring dimension starts at `NOT_STARTED`, passes through `SCORED`, and ends at `SIGNED_OFF`. Scoring is guarded on attendance, but attendance never consults the scoring dimension.

Recording attendance is an `Evaluator` action, shared with `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` for the agency-cancellation case, and the `attendance_status` supplied selects the branch. The assessment must still be `SCHEDULED`; recording attendance twice is `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE` (409). All three branches stamp `attendance_recorded_at` from the logical clock and increment the parent packet's `version`.

Marking a rider `ATTENDED` requires that the logical clock has reached the slot's `starts_at` — the transition is permitted only when the current instant is at or after that start. It leaves the slot's `booked_count` unchanged, because an attended assessment still occupies its seat, and it completes the ride: every booking on this assessment still in `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → COMPLETED`, and each such completion releases the vehicle counter that booking consumed, decrementing `open_wheelchair_reservations` or `open_ambulatory_reservations` accordingly.

Marking a rider `NO_SHOW` likewise requires that the current instant is at or after the slot's `starts_at`, and it triggers the widest cascade in this part of the system. The assessment's attendance dimension becomes `NO_SHOW`. The slot's `booked_count` decreases by one, and a slot that was `FULL` returns to `OPEN` as soon as its `booked_count` falls below its `capacity`. Every booking on this assessment still in `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → NO_SHOW_CLOSED`, each releasing the vehicle counter it consumed. And the parent packet regresses from `ASSESSMENT_SCHEDULED` back to `ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` so a replacement assessment can be booked — the assessment stage can be re-entered without the packet ever leaving the intake workflow.

Marking an assessment `CANCELLED_BY_AGENCY` needs only that the assessment is still `SCHEDULED`. Its effects mirror the no-show branch with one deliberate difference: the assessment's attendance dimension becomes `CANCELLED_BY_AGENCY`, the slot's `booked_count` decreases by one with a `FULL` slot returning to `OPEN` once `booked_count` is below `capacity`, every booking on this assessment still in `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → CANCELLED` with its vehicle counter released, and the packet regresses from `ASSESSMENT_SCHEDULED` to `ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`. The difference is that `applicant_delay_days` is left alone: agency fault is never charged to the rider, whereas a rider's own no-show is charged when the replacement is booked.

Scoring belongs to the `Evaluator` who conducted the assessment, and it is permitted only once the attendance dimension reads `ATTENDED`; attempting to score a `SCHEDULED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED_BY_AGENCY` assessment is `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE` (409). The evaluator records `board_alight_score`, the observed ability to board and alight a fixed-route vehicle on a five-point scale where 0 means unable and 4 means fully independent; `wayfinding_score`, the observed ability to navigate an unfamiliar route on the same scale; `distance_meters_walked`, the distance walked unaided during the assessment in whole meters, which may be zero but never negative; and `heat_sensitivity_flag`, a non-null boolean recording whether heat materially degrades the rider's function. `evaluator_notes` is optional free text. A score outside 0 through 4, a negative distance, or a missing `heat_sensitivity_flag` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Recording scores moves the scoring dimension to `SCORED` and writes `scored_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. Re-scoring is explicitly allowed while the assessment is still `SCORED` and not yet signed off: it is a self-loop that overwrites the four observations, recomputes the recommendation, and rewrites `scored_by_user_id` to whoever performed the latest scoring.

The recommendation itself — `recommended_category` and the `recommended_term_days` that follows from it — is derived from exactly five inputs: the four observations just recorded plus the parent packet's `attests_permanent`, copied there when the professional's attestation was accepted. No other assessment, no prior determination and no conduct data participate. The rules are evaluated in the following order and **the first rule that matches decides the outcome**; later rules are not consulted.

1. If the packet's `attests_permanent` is false, the recommendation is `TEMPORARY`.
2. Otherwise, if the lower of `board_alight_score` and `wayfinding_score` is at most 1, the recommendation is `UNCONDITIONAL`.
3. Otherwise, if `distance_meters_walked` is below 400 meters and the higher of `board_alight_score` and `wayfinding_score` is at least 3, the recommendation is `CONDITIONAL`.
4. Otherwise, if the two scores added together come to at most 4, the recommendation is `UNCONDITIONAL`.
5. Otherwise, if `distance_meters_walked` is 800 meters or more and `heat_sensitivity_flag` is false, the recommendation is `INELIGIBLE`.
6. Otherwise the recommendation is `CONDITIONAL`.

Branch order here is part of the contract and not an implementation detail. A rider scoring 0 on boarding and 4 on wayfinding who walked 900 meters with no heat sensitivity is `UNCONDITIONAL` by rule 2; testing the 800-meter ceiling of rule 5 first would have made the same rider `INELIGIBLE`. `recommended_term_days` is then set from the recommended category using the same term table the determination itself uses: 1,825 days (five years) for `UNCONDITIONAL`, 1,095 days (three years) for `CONDITIONAL`, 180 days for `TEMPORARY`, and 0 days for `INELIGIBLE`.

Sign-off is the segregation point. An `EligibilitySpecialist` signs off an assessment whose scoring dimension reads `SCORED`; a sign-off attempted while the assessment is still `NOT_STARTED`, or after it is already `SIGNED_OFF`, is `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE` (409). The signer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `scored_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SIGNOFF_NOT_INDEPENDENT` (422). A successful sign-off writes `signed_off_by_user_id` from the acting header, moves the scoring dimension `SCORED → SIGNED_OFF`, and bubbles up to the parent packet: an application sitting in `ASSESSMENT_SCHEDULED` whose assessment has attendance `ATTENDED` and scoring `SCORED` advances to `ASSESSMENT_COMPLETE`, and the packet's `version` increments with it.

### 3.6. Drafting and Issuing the Determination

Only an `EligibilitySpecialist` may draft a determination, and only against a packet that has reached `ASSESSMENT_COMPLETE`; drafting against a packet in any other status is `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` (409). Beyond the workflow status, the packet must own exactly one functional assessment whose attendance reads `ATTENDED` and whose scoring has reached `SIGNED_OFF` — the substantive evidentiary requirement — and a draft attempted before that sign-off exists is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_SIGNED_OFF` (422). The draft payload carries `expected_application_version` as described in §2.6, the `category` being granted, an `effective_date` as an ISO calendar date, a `rationale` giving the written basis for the category (required, because the determination is an appealable public record), and optionally a `trip_conditions` array.

`category` takes one of four values. `UNCONDITIONAL` grants paratransit for every trip. `CONDITIONAL` grants it only for trips satisfying at least one of the attached trip conditions. `TEMPORARY` grants a short-term certification, and is what a non-permanent professional attestation produces. `INELIGIBLE` records a denial. Two symmetric rules tie the category to the conditions payload: a `CONDITIONAL` draft must carry at least one `trip_conditions` entry, else `ERR_CONDITIONAL_REQUIRES_CONDITIONS` (422); and an `UNCONDITIONAL` or `INELIGIBLE` draft must carry either no `trip_conditions` key at all or an empty array, else `ERR_UNCONDITIONAL_FORBIDS_CONDITIONS` (422). A `TEMPORARY` draft is free either way — it may carry conditions or not.

Each entry in `trip_conditions` becomes one immutable trip condition attached to the determination, and the number created is recorded on the determination as `trip_condition_count`. An entry names a `condition_type`, one of `HEAT_INDEX_ABOVE`, `COLD_BELOW`, `PRECIPITATION`, `PATH_OF_TRAVEL_BARRIER`, `DISTANCE_OVER_METERS`, `COGNITIVE_UNFAMILIAR_ROUTE` or `NIGHT_TRAVEL`; a `comparator`, one of `GTE`, `GT`, `LTE`, `LT` or `IS_TRUE`; an optional `threshold_value`; and an optional `note` carrying the plain-language wording printed on the rider's eligibility letter. The comparator and the threshold are coupled: `threshold_value` is required whenever `comparator` is anything other than `IS_TRUE`, and must be null when `comparator` is `IS_TRUE`. A violation of that coupling, or a `threshold_value` below zero, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A successful draft creates the determination in `DRAFT`, copies `applicant_id` down from the parent packet so the grant is addressable by rider, records `determined_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` — the identity the appeal reviewer must later differ from — leaves `issued_at` and `expires_at` null, sets `under_appeal` false, `origin` to `SPECIALIST_DETERMINATION`, and `fixed_route_competency_confirmed` false. The parent packet's `version` increments.

Issuance is a separate act, also reserved to an `EligibilitySpecialist`, and it is where the whole system's state converges. Its substantive guards are screened in the order in which they are stated here. The determination must still be in `DRAFT`, and no other determination of the same packet may already be `ISSUED`; either violation is `ERR_DETERMINATION_ALREADY_ISSUED` (409). The determination is the entity being driven here, so its own issuance status is screened before anything is asked of the parent packet — which is what keeps `ERR_DETERMINATION_ALREADY_ISSUED` reachable at all: a second issue call against an already-`ISSUED` determination is answered with that code, even though the first issuance has by then already carried the packet to the terminal `DETERMINED`. Only after those two checks pass is the parent packet examined, as the stage-13 cross-entity referential requirement §2.5 classifies it as: the packet must be in `ASSESSMENT_COMPLETE`, else `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` (409), and it must own the same single attended, signed-off assessment the drafting step required, else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_SIGNED_OFF` (422). The category-to-conditions coupling is re-checked after those, against the stored `trip_condition_count`: a `CONDITIONAL` determination must have at least one trip condition, and an `UNCONDITIONAL` or `INELIGIBLE` one must have none.

Issuance then performs seven writes. It stamps `issued_at` with the logical clock and computes the term: `term_days` is 1,825 for `UNCONDITIONAL`, 1,095 for `CONDITIONAL`, 180 for `TEMPORARY` and 0 for `INELIGIBLE`, and `expires_at` is the `effective_date` advanced by `term_days` calendar days — except for an `INELIGIBLE` determination, whose `expires_at` stays null because a denial does not expire. It moves every *other* determination of the same rider that was in `ISSUED` to `SUPERSEDED`. It rewrites the rider's mirrors: `current_eligibility_status` becomes `ELIGIBLE`, or `INELIGIBLE` when the category is `INELIGIBLE`; `eligibility_category` becomes the issued category; and `active_determination_id` becomes this determination's `id`. It advances the parent packet to the terminal `DETERMINED` and increments its `version`, freezing `days_pending_statutory` and `days_pending_net_agency` at the values they held at issuance. When the category is `CONDITIONAL` it creates a travel-training referral for the rider with `referral_reason` `CONDITIONAL_ELIGIBILITY_AUTO`, `determination_id` pointing at this determination, and `status` `REFERRED`. And it closes out visitor service: every visitor-eligibility record of this rider still in `ACTIVE` moves to `SUPERSEDED_BY_LOCAL`, because a local grant governs from its `effective_date` and visitor days stop accruing. A rider who was `PRESUMPTIVE` leaves that status here; the packet's `presumptive_granted_at` is kept for audit but no longer governs.

A determination that has been superseded is frozen for appeal purposes: its `under_appeal` value stays whatever it was, its own `status` is not otherwise disturbed, and no new appeal may be filed against it.

Expiry is the other way an issued determination ends, and it happens only in step 3 of the day-close sweep, for every determination in `ISSUED` whose `expires_at` is at or before today's date. The effect is a three-hop cascade. **Hop 1:** the determination moves `ISSUED → EXPIRED`. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1: the rider's `current_eligibility_status` becomes `EXPIRED`, `eligibility_category` becomes null, and `active_determination_id` becomes null. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2 because the rider is no longer eligible: every travel-training referral of that rider sitting in `REFERRED` or `ENROLLED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` `ELIGIBILITY_LAPSED`. Each referral withdrawn this way is counted in the sweep's `referrals_withdrawn_count`, and each determination expired in its `determinations_expired_count`.

### 3.7. Trip-by-Trip Conditions, Travel Training and Fixed-Route Competency

A conditional grant is only useful if the office can answer, for a specific proposed trip, whether paratransit applies. That question is answered by a read-only evaluation that mutates nothing and is available to an `Applicant` on their own grant as well as to `IntakeClerk`, `EligibilitySpecialist` and `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` staff. The request supplies the `trip_date` being asked about, plus whichever trip facts the attached conditions need: `forecast_heat_index_f` for temperature conditions, `precipitation_flag`, `path_barrier_flag`, `is_night_trip`, `route_familiarity` (either `FAMILIAR` or `UNFAMILIAR`), and `origin_distance_to_stop_meters`. All of those trip facts are optional; only `trip_date` is required.

Evaluation proceeds in a fixed order. The determination must be in `ISSUED`; a check against a `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED`, `VACATED` or `EXPIRED` determination is refused outright with `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) rather than answered with a structured denial, which is precisely why conditions attached to a superseded or vacated grant are never consulted. If the determination is `ISSUED` but its `category` is `INELIGIBLE`, the answer is a successful response reporting `eligible` false with `denial_reason` `CATEGORY_INELIGIBLE`. If the `category` is `UNCONDITIONAL`, the answer is `eligible` true with an empty `applied_condition_ids` list and a null `denial_reason`, without examining any condition. Otherwise, if `expires_at` is non-null and the requested `trip_date` falls later than `expires_at`, the answer is `eligible` false with `denial_reason` `TERM_EXPIRED`.

Only when none of those short-circuits applies does the server walk the determination's trip conditions, in `created_at` ascending order — never in identifier order. A condition is satisfied as follows. A `HEAT_INDEX_ABOVE` condition compares the request's `forecast_heat_index_f` against the condition's `threshold_value`. A `COLD_BELOW` condition compares that same `forecast_heat_index_f` value against its own `threshold_value` — the temperature reading in the request serves both warm-weather and cold-weather conditions. A `DISTANCE_OVER_METERS` condition compares the request's `origin_distance_to_stop_meters` against its `threshold_value`. In all three numeric cases the `comparator` decides the test: `GTE` is satisfied when the supplied value is greater than or equal to the threshold, `GT` when it is strictly greater, `LTE` when it is less than or equal, and `LT` when it is strictly less. The remaining condition types are boolean and use `IS_TRUE`, which is why `IS_TRUE` is never valid on a numeric condition type and is rejected when the determination is drafted: a `PRECIPITATION` condition is satisfied when the request's `precipitation_flag` is true, a `PATH_OF_TRAVEL_BARRIER` condition when `path_barrier_flag` is true, and a `NIGHT_TRAVEL` condition when `is_night_trip` is true. A `COGNITIVE_UNFAMILIAR_ROUTE` condition is satisfied only when the request's `route_familiarity` is `UNFAMILIAR` **and** the determination's own `fixed_route_competency_confirmed` is false; once competency has been confirmed, that condition can never be satisfied again.

The response reports `applied_condition_ids` as the identifiers of the satisfied conditions in the order they were evaluated, `eligible` as true when that list holds at least one entry, `denial_reason` as null when eligible and `NO_CONDITION_MET` when the list is empty, `evaluated_condition_count` as the number of the determination's conditions that were examined, and `category` as the determination's own category.

Travel training is the mechanism by which a conditional rider can move some or all of their trips back onto fixed route. A referral is opened by an `EligibilitySpecialist`, a `TravelTrainer` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` against a rider, naming a `referral_reason` of `CONDITIONAL_ELIGIBILITY_AUTO`, `STAFF_REFERRAL` or `RIDER_REQUEST`, and optionally a `determination_id` linking the referral to the grant that motivated it (null for a pre-determination referral). The rider must hold a determination in `ISSUED` whose category is `CONDITIONAL` or `TEMPORARY`; a rider whose issued grant is `UNCONDITIONAL` or `INELIGIBLE`, or who holds none at all, is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_TRAINABLE` (422). The referral is created in `REFERRED`, with `referral_response_deadline_date` set to the calendar date of `created_at` plus 14 days, and with `fixed_route_competency_score`, `completed_at` and `withdrawal_reason` all null.

Responding to a referral is done by the rider themselves or by a `TravelTrainer`, supplying a `decision` of either `ENROLLED` or `DECLINED`. The referral must still be in `REFERRED`; a response to a referral already resolved is `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID` (409). The response must arrive on or before `referral_response_deadline_date`; a later response is `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). A decision of `ENROLLED` moves the referral `REFERRED → ENROLLED`, and it re-checks the category guard at that moment — a rider whose eligibility has changed to `UNCONDITIONAL` or `INELIGIBLE`, or lapsed entirely, since the referral was written cannot enroll, and is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_TRAINABLE`. A decision of `DECLINED` moves the referral `REFERRED → DECLINED`, which is terminal and carries no further effects.

Completion is a `TravelTrainer` action on a referral in `ENROLLED`; attempting it on any other status is `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID` (409). The trainer supplies `fixed_route_competency_score`, a whole number from 0 through 100 — anything outside that range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the referral moves `ENROLLED → COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped from the logical clock. A score of at least 80 confirms fixed-route competency, and that confirmation cascades: the rider's `fixed_route_competency_confirmed` flips to true, and because the rider flag has flipped, the `fixed_route_competency_confirmed` mirror on the rider's determination that is still in `ISSUED` flips to true as well, which permanently suppresses `COGNITIVE_UNFAMILIAR_ROUTE` conditions in every subsequent trip-by-trip evaluation of that grant. A score below 80 records the completion and leaves both flags as they were. Either way the rider's `version` increments.

A referral can also end without the rider doing anything. When step 3 of the day-close sweep expires the rider's governing determination, every referral of that rider in `REFERRED` moves `REFERRED → WITHDRAWN` and every referral in `ENROLLED` moves `ENROLLED → WITHDRAWN`, each recording `withdrawal_reason` `ELIGIBILITY_LAPSED`. A referral already `COMPLETED` or `DECLINED` is untouched.

### 3.8. Appeals and Independent Review

A rider who disagrees with a determination may contest it, and either the rider themselves or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` (for walk-in filings) may lodge the appeal, naming the `determination_id` under challenge, the written `grounds`, and optionally `request_hearing`, which defaults to false. Two gates apply, in this order: the determination must currently be in `ISSUED` — a `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED`, `VACATED` or `EXPIRED` determination is not appealable and yields `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_APPEALABLE` (409) — and only then is the filing window checked. The window is 60 days: the appeal is accepted when the calendar date of `filed_at` falls on or before the determination's `effective_date` plus 60 days, and a filing even one day later is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).

A filed appeal starts in `FILED` with `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock, `filing_deadline_date` recorded as `effective_date` plus 60 days, and `reviewer_user_id`, `hearing_at`, `heard_at`, `decision_due_date`, `outcome`, `granted_category`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` all null, and `continued_service_extended` false. Filing also flips two mirrors: the contested determination's `under_appeal` becomes true, and the rider's `continued_service_during_appeal` becomes true. That second flag is the substantive protection — service continues at the previously granted level for as long as the appeal sits in `FILED`, `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED` or `HEARD`. The rider's `current_eligibility_status` is not changed by filing.

Assigning the reviewer is an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` action on an appeal in `FILED`; from any other status it is `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (409). The supplied `reviewer_user_id` must satisfy the independence rule of §2.9 — different from the contested determination's `determined_by_user_id` and different from the `scored_by_user_id` of the assessment behind it — else `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_INDEPENDENT` (422). Success moves the appeal `FILED → REVIEWER_ASSIGNED` and records the reviewer.

From `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED` the appeal forks, and which branch is available was fixed at filing time by `request_hearing`. Only the assigned reviewer may open the review, and the appeal must be in `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED`, else `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (409). On the **hearing branch**, where `request_hearing` is true, the reviewer must supply `hearing_at`; omitting it is `ERR_HEARING_REQUIRED` (422). The supplied instant must be strictly later than the logical clock's current instant, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The appeal moves `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED → HEARING_SCHEDULED` with `hearing_at` recorded, and it does **not** reach `HEARD` synchronously — step 1 of the day-close sweep advances `HEARING_SCHEDULED → HEARD` once the clock has passed `hearing_at`, stamping `heard_at` with the sweep instant and setting `decision_due_date` to the date of `heard_at` plus 30 days. On the **paper branch**, where `request_hearing` is false, `hearing_at` must be omitted; supplying it is `ERR_HEARING_NOT_REQUESTED` (422). The appeal moves `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED → HEARD` immediately, `heard_at` is stamped from the current instant, `hearing_at` stays null, and `decision_due_date` is set to the date of `heard_at` plus 30 days. The two branches thus join at `HEARD` with identical deadline arithmetic and differ only in how they get there.

The decision is recorded by the assigned reviewer on an appeal in `HEARD`; from `FILED`, `REVIEWER_ASSIGNED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED` or an already-`DECIDED` appeal it is `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (409). The reviewer supplies an `outcome` of `UPHELD`, `OVERTURNED` or `MODIFIED`, optionally a `decision_note` giving the written reasoning, and a `granted_category` whose presence is strictly coupled to the outcome: `OVERTURNED` and `MODIFIED` both require a non-null `granted_category` of `UNCONDITIONAL`, `CONDITIONAL` or `TEMPORARY`, while `UPHELD` requires `granted_category` to be null. Any mismatch is `ERR_GRANTED_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` (422).

An `UPHELD` decision confirms the original determination. The appeal moves `HEARD → DECIDED` with `outcome` recorded and `decided_at` stamped from the clock. The contested determination's `under_appeal` returns to false and its own `status` is left exactly as it was — an upheld appeal does not vacate anything. The rider's `continued_service_during_appeal` returns to false, and where the category under appeal was `INELIGIBLE` the rider's `current_eligibility_status` returns to `INELIGIBLE`, ending the continued-service protection that filing had granted.

An `OVERTURNED` or `MODIFIED` decision grants a remedy, and it runs as a four-hop cascade inside the single request. **Hop 1:** the appeal moves `HEARD → DECIDED` with `outcome` set from the request and `decided_at` stamped from the clock. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1: the contested determination moves `ISSUED → VACATED` with `under_appeal` set to false, and a successor determination is created already in `ISSUED`, with `origin` `APPEAL_REMEDY`, `category` equal to the appeal's `granted_category`, `effective_date` equal to the calendar date of `decided_at`, `term_days` and `expires_at` computed from that category and effective date by the same term table used at ordinary issuance, `application_id` and `applicant_id` copied from the vacated determination, and `determined_by_user_id` set to the appeal's own `reviewer_user_id`. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2 because a new issued determination now exists: the rider's `current_eligibility_status` becomes `ELIGIBLE`, `eligibility_category` becomes the granted category, `active_determination_id` becomes the successor's identifier, and `continued_service_during_appeal` returns to false. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3 because the rider now holds conditional eligibility: when the granted category is `CONDITIONAL`, a travel-training referral is created for the rider with `referral_reason` `CONDITIONAL_ELIGIBILITY_AUTO`, `determination_id` pointing at the successor determination, and `status` `REFERRED`.

One deliberate omission in that cascade must not be "helpfully" fixed. Trip conditions are **not** copied from the vacated determination onto the successor: the successor carries a `trip_condition_count` of 0, and when the granted category is `CONDITIONAL` the reviewer's `decision_note` stands as the written condition basis until a specialist drafts a fresh determination with real conditions attached. A trip-by-trip check against that successor therefore reports `eligible` false with `denial_reason` `NO_CONDITION_MET` until conditions exist.

Finally, a reviewer who lets the clock run out does not cost the rider their service. Step 5 of the day-close sweep finds every appeal in `HEARD` whose `decision_due_date` falls strictly before today's date and whose `outcome` is still null, sets that appeal's `continued_service_extended` to true, and moves the rider's `current_eligibility_status` to `PRESUMPTIVE`. Each such appeal is counted in the sweep's `appeals_overdue_count`, and appeals advanced by step 1 are counted in `appeals_heard_count`.

### 3.9. Trip Conduct, No-Show Points and Suspension Due Process

Riders who repeatedly fail to take trips they booked degrade service for everyone, so the agency operates a points-based conduct policy. A `ConductOfficer` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` ingests one trip outcome at a time, naming the `applicant_id`, the `trip_date` the trip was scheduled for, and an `event_type` of `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `LATE_CANCEL`, `CANCEL_IN_ADVANCE` or `AGENCY_FAULT_NO_SHOW`. History may be backfilled but the future may not be anticipated: a `trip_date` later than today's date is refused with `ERR_TRIP_DATE_FUTURE` (422). The event is stored with `recorded_at` stamped from the logical clock, `excused` false, `excuse_reason` null, and `points_assessed` set to 2 for an unexcused `NO_SHOW`, 1 for an unexcused `LATE_CANCEL`, and 0 for every other event type. The rider's `version` increments.

A miss beyond the rider's control can be excused afterwards by the same two roles, supplying an `excuse_reason`. Excusing sets `excused` to true and `points_assessed` to 0, and increments the rider's `version`. The subtlety worth internalizing is that an excused event **stays in the denominator and leaves the numerator** of the miss ratio — the trip still happened and still consumed a slot, it just no longer counts against the rider.

Both ingestion and excusal recompute the whole conduct block, as does every read of the rider. The conduct window is the 90 calendar days ending on today's date, so it opens on today's date minus 89 days and both boundary dates are inside it. Point totals are computed twice, over the same events, on two different membership tests, and the two totals can legitimately disagree. The **event-date basis** tests window membership against the event's `trip_date`, so a miss ingested late still counts in the period in which it actually happened; this is the governing total and it is published as `no_show_points_event_basis`. The **report-date basis** tests membership against the calendar date of `recorded_at`, so a trip that happened before the window but was keyed in during it counts here and not on the event basis; this total is published as `no_show_points_report_basis` and is used only for the monthly conduct report — it never gates anything. On either basis, the total is the sum of the points of that rider's events that fall in the window and are not excused, accumulated in chronological order by `trip_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending so the audit trail is reproducible. Events scored at 0 points — `COMPLETED`, `CANCEL_IN_ADVANCE` and `AGENCY_FAULT_NO_SHOW` — contribute nothing to either total.

The miss ratio is computed on the event-date basis only, and its rounding order is pinned. Take the rider's events whose `trip_date` falls inside the inclusive 90-day window: `scheduled_trip_count_90d` is how many there are, counting every event type including excused ones, and `chargeable_miss_count_90d` is how many of them are unexcused and of type `NO_SHOW` or `LATE_CANCEL`. When `scheduled_trip_count_90d` is zero, `no_show_ratio_pct` is 0.0. Otherwise pool the entire window, divide once, and round once: multiply `chargeable_miss_count_90d` by 100, divide by `scheduled_trip_count_90d`, and round the single quotient half up to one decimal place. Computing a ratio per calendar month, rounding each, and averaging the monthly percentages is expressly forbidden because it produces a different answer — one miss out of two trips in May and no misses out of one trip in July average to 25.0 monthly but pool to 33.3, and 33.3 is the authoritative figure.

Three thresholds together define a suspendable pattern, and all three must hold simultaneously: `no_show_points_event_basis` at least 6, `chargeable_miss_count_90d` at least 3, and `no_show_ratio_pct` strictly greater than 10.0 percent. Whenever a trip event is ingested and all three hold, the system opens a proceeding on its own, provided no proceeding of that rider is already live — that is, none of the rider's proceedings sits in `PROPOSED`, `NOTICE_SENT`, `RIDER_RESPONDED`, `RESPONSE_LAPSED`, `UPHELD_PENDING` or `ACTIVE`. The auto-created proceeding starts in `PROPOSED` with `origin` `AUTO_THRESHOLD`, `proposed_by_user_id` null, `grounds_note` null, `review_window_end_date` set to today's date as the last date of the window just evaluated, and `points_at_proposal` holding the rider's `no_show_points_event_basis` at that instant.

A `ConductOfficer` may also propose a suspension by hand, supplying `expected_applicant_version` as described in §2.6, the `review_window_end_date` naming the last calendar date of the 90-day window being reviewed, and optionally a `grounds_note` narrative. A manual proposal starts in `PROPOSED` with `origin` `MANUAL`, `proposed_by_user_id` set from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `points_at_proposal` snapshotting `no_show_points_event_basis`. Creating it also consolidates the record: every *other* proceeding of that rider still in `PROPOSED` moves `PROPOSED → VOID`, keeping `duration_days`, `days_served` and `days_forgiven` at zero, because the newer review supersedes the earlier proposal rather than running alongside it. The rider's `version` increments — which is exactly why this operation is version-guarded.

Due process comes before consequence. A `ConductOfficer` sends the written notice on a proceeding in `PROPOSED`, moving it `PROPOSED → NOTICE_SENT`, stamping `notice_sent_at` from the logical clock, and setting `response_deadline_date` to the calendar date of `notice_sent_at` plus 14 days. Sending a notice from any other status is `ERR_PROCEEDING_STATE_INVALID` (409), and that same code guards every other transition in this workflow whose source status is wrong.

From `NOTICE_SENT` the proceeding forks on whether the rider answers. The rider — and only the rider whose `applicant_id` the proceeding names — may record a written response, supplying `rider_response_note`, which moves the proceeding `NOTICE_SENT → RIDER_RESPONDED` and stamps `rider_response_at` from the clock. The response must arrive on or before `response_deadline_date`; a later one is `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). If the rider stays silent, step 2 of the day-close sweep moves the proceeding `NOTICE_SENT → RESPONSE_LAPSED` once `response_deadline_date` falls strictly before today's date, changing nothing else — the lapse itself is the due-process evidence the office relies on. The two branches carry equal weight: a decision may be taken from either `RIDER_RESPONDED` or `RESPONSE_LAPSED`, with the same options, the same guards and the same effects; only the evidence in the file differs.

Deciding a proceeding is a `ConductOfficer` action, and when several of its guards fail at once they are evaluated in this order. The proceeding must be in `RIDER_RESPONDED` or `RESPONSE_LAPSED`, else `ERR_PROCEEDING_STATE_INVALID` (409). The deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `proposed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_DECIDER_NOT_INDEPENDENT` (422). Due process must be complete — `notice_sent_at` must be set and the status must be `RIDER_RESPONDED` or `RESPONSE_LAPSED` — else `ERR_DUE_PROCESS_INCOMPLETE` (422). And only then is the conduct record re-tested: upholding requires that, recomputed at this very instant, `no_show_points_event_basis` is at least 6, `chargeable_miss_count_90d` is at least 3 and `no_show_ratio_pct` is strictly above 10.0; if any of the three has fallen below its threshold — because misses aged out of the rolling window, or because an officer excused one in the meantime — the uphold is refused with `ERR_SUSPENSION_UNSUPPORTED` (422). The `decision` supplied is either `UPHELD` or `RESCINDED`, and `suspension_start_date` is coupled to it: required when the decision is `UPHELD`, and required to be null when the decision is `RESCINDED`, with a violation of either coupling being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). An `UPHELD` decision additionally requires `suspension_start_date` to fall on or after today's date; an earlier date is `ERR_SUSPENSION_START_IN_PAST` (422).

An `UPHELD` decision moves the proceeding to `UPHELD_PENDING`, records `decision` and `decided_by_user_id`, and computes the length of the suspension from an escalating ladder of 7, 14, 21 and 30 days. The index into that ladder is the rider's `prior_upheld_suspension_count` **as it stands before this decision increments it**: a first offence draws 7 days, a second 14, a third 21, and a fourth or any subsequent offence draws 30 days. That prior count includes only the rider's proceedings whose `decision` is `UPHELD` and whose `status` is `UPHELD_PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `SERVED` or `VOID`; a proceeding that was `RESCINDED`, and one voided while still merely `PROPOSED`, never count, so a rescinded first offence does not escalate the second. `suspension_end_date` is then the `suspension_start_date` advanced by `duration_days` and pulled back one day, because the end date is the last suspended date and is itself suspended. Finally the rider's `prior_upheld_suspension_count` increases by one and `suspension_days_ledger_total` grows by `duration_days`.

A `RESCINDED` decision moves the proceeding to the terminal `RESCINDED` and closes it with nothing owed: `duration_days`, `days_served` and `days_forgiven` are all zero and `suspension_end_date` stays null. Either way the rider's `version` increments.

An upheld suspension does not bite immediately — it waits for its start date, and step 2 of the day-close sweep carries it through. Once `suspension_start_date` is at or before today's date the proceeding moves `UPHELD_PENDING → ACTIVE` and the rider's `service_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`. Once `suspension_end_date` falls strictly before today's date the proceeding moves `ACTIVE → SERVED`, `days_served` is set equal to `duration_days`, `days_forgiven` is set to zero, and the rider's `service_status` returns to `ACTIVE`. Both kinds of advance, along with the response-window lapses, are counted together in the sweep's `proceedings_advanced_count`.

The office may also cut a suspension short. A `ConductOfficer` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` may rescind a proceeding in `UPHELD_PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, moving it to `VOID`; from any other status it is `ERR_PROCEEDING_STATE_INVALID` (409). Rescinding a suspension already running freezes `days_served` at the number of days already served, computed as the smaller of `duration_days` and the number of days from `suspension_start_date` through today's date counted inclusively — that is, the day difference plus one. `days_forgiven` takes the remainder, `duration_days` minus `days_served`. Rescinding one that has not yet started leaves `days_served` at zero and forgives the whole term. The rider's `service_status` returns to `ACTIVE`, `suspension_days_ledger_total` is left untouched, and the rider's `version` increments.

That ledger is a conserved quantity and the invariant is worth stating explicitly, because it is the office's audit hook. For every proceeding, whatever its status, `days_served` plus `days_forgiven` equals `duration_days`. A proceeding in `PROPOSED`, `NOTICE_SENT`, `RIDER_RESPONDED` or `RESPONSE_LAPSED` has all three at zero. One in `UPHELD_PENDING` has `days_served` zero, so its whole `duration_days` sits in `days_forgiven` until it starts. One in `ACTIVE` has `days_served` equal to the smaller of `duration_days` and the inclusive day count from `suspension_start_date` through today. One in `SERVED` has `days_served` equal to `duration_days` and `days_forgiven` zero. One in `VOID` after being rescinded mid-service keeps the `days_served` figure frozen at rescind time. One in `RESCINDED`, and one in `VOID` because a newer proposal superseded it while it was still `PROPOSED`, have `duration_days` and `days_served` both zero. Summed across all of a rider's proceedings, `days_served` plus `days_forgiven` equals `suspension_days_ledger_total`, which in turn equals the sum of every proceeding's `duration_days`.

### 3.10. Visitor Eligibility for Out-of-Area Riders

A rider certified by another transit agency is entitled to a limited run of service while visiting, without going through local certification. An `IntakeClerk` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` opens a visitor record against the rider, supplying the `home_agency_name` of the certifying agency in the rider's home jurisdiction, `home_agency_certification_provided` stating whether documentation of that agency's ADA paratransit eligibility was actually presented, and `visit_start_date`, the first calendar date of the visit.

Two gates apply and they are evaluated in this order. The rider must genuinely be a visitor: their `residence_zone` must be `OUT_OF_SERVICE_AREA` and `home_agency_certification_provided` must be true, else the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_OUT_OF_AREA` (422). Only then is local eligibility considered: a rider who already holds a determination in `ISSUED` whose category is anything other than `INELIGIBLE` does not need visitor service and is refused with `ERR_LOCAL_ELIGIBILITY_EXISTS` (422). A rider whose only issued determination is `INELIGIBLE` may still be granted visitor service, because a local denial says nothing about the home agency's certification.

A new visitor record starts in `ACTIVE` with an empty `service_dates` list, `days_used_365` at 0 and `days_remaining_365` at 21. Where the rider holds no determination in `ISSUED` at all, their `current_eligibility_status` becomes `VISITOR`; where they hold an `INELIGIBLE` one, that status is left as it stands.

The allowance is 21 service days in any rolling 365 days, and days are recorded one at a time by an `IntakeClerk` or an `AccessibilityOfficeAdmin` supplying a single `service_date`. The record must be in `ACTIVE`: both `EXHAUSTED` and `SUPERSEDED_BY_LOCAL` records are frozen and reject new days with `ERR_VISITOR_RECORD_INACTIVE` (409). The requested date must fall on or after `visit_start_date` and no later than today's date plus 90 days, else `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422).

The accounting order is pinned, and pinning it is what makes the answer well defined at all — the window must not depend on the outcome of the very grant being decided. First, the window is fixed from the **request**: it ends on the requested `service_date` and opens 364 days earlier, so it spans 365 calendar days inclusive of both ends. Second, the server counts the distinct dates already present in `service_dates` that fall inside that window; the date being requested is not yet among them. Third, the day is granted only when that count is below 21, and otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_VISITOR_DAYS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Fourth, and only after granting, the requested `service_date` is appended to `service_dates` — appending is idempotent, so a date already present is not stored twice and does not consume a day of the allowance. Fifth, `days_used_365` is recomputed as the number of distinct dates in `service_dates` inside the window fixed in step one, and `days_remaining_365` becomes 21 minus that figure, reported as zero rather than a negative number. When `days_used_365` reaches exactly 21 the record moves `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`, `days_remaining_365` reads 0, and no further service day can be recorded against it.

A visitor record also ends when the rider becomes a local rider. Issuing any determination for that rider moves every one of their visitor records still in `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED_BY_LOCAL`: the local determination governs from its `effective_date` and visitor days stop accruing from that point.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint is mounted at the top level; there is no shared API prefix. Status codes follow one rule throughout: an operation that brings a new resource into existence answers with **201 Created**, and an operation that modifies, advances or reads an existing resource answers with **200 OK**. The code follows the resource the request addresses, not the cascade behind it: an operation that advances an existing record answers 200 even when it creates further records as a side effect. Both carry the standard success envelope of §2.2.
