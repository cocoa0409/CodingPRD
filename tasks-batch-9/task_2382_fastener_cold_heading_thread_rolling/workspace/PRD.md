# Fastener Cold Heading & Thread Rolling API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A cold-heading fastener plant converts mill-certified steel rod into threaded parts that hold aircraft engines and vehicle suspensions together, and the plant's commercial value is only partly in the machines. It is in the paper trail: which heat the wire came from, whether that heat's mill test report was ever independently verified, how far into its life the die set was when the blanks were formed, whether the relief bake after plating started soon enough to drive hydrogen back out of a high-strength part, and which destructive test the certificate of conformance actually cites. When any link in that chain is missing or falsified, the correct response is not a warning — it is the immediate freezing of every downstream artefact, up to and including recalling packs that have already left the dock.

This document specifies the API that carries that chain. The system tracks material from steel heat through wire drawing, cold heading on a progressive die set, secondary operations (thread rolling, drilling, slotting, pointing), heat treatment to a strength class, plating with hydrogen-embrittlement relief baking, destructive lot testing against a sampling plan, certification, and finally packaging and shipment. Two concerns dominate the design. The first is **contagion containment**: a suspected counterfeit mill certificate must propagate, in one transitive chain, from the heat to its wire lots, from those wire lots to their production lots, from those lots to their certificates, and from those certificates to the packs printed with them. The second is **segregation of duties**: the inspector who destroys the sample pieces may record what the sample showed, but may never be the operator whose identifier ends up signing the certificate that cites that sample.

A third, quieter concern runs through every calculation in this specification: the same physical tool, furnace or lot must yield different answers depending on the qualification programme the part is built under. An aerospace-qualified part budgets die life against the tool's *observed* history; an automotive part budgets it against the nameplate. Implementers should expect the arithmetic, not merely the state machine, to branch.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, the logical clock, record versioning, rounding discipline, the two conserved ledgers, cascade ordering, and the fixed order in which rules are evaluated.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the operator performing the action, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles the plant recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the declared role is not listed as authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `ProcessEngineer`, `HeatTreatOperator`, `QualityInspector`, `QualityManager`, `ShippingClerk`, and `PlantAdmin`. A `ProcessEngineer` owns wire preparation, tooling and the heading floor: registering part specifications and revising them, recording wire lots, registering die sets and furnaces, mounting and regrinding dies, opening and aborting production lots, reporting heading completion, and queueing and closing secondary operations. A `HeatTreatOperator` runs furnaces, plating lines and relief bakes. A `QualityInspector` verifies mill certificates, releases wire to heading, records heat treatment results, performs destructive lot tests, records their results, and may draft certificates. A `QualityManager` owns counterfeit flagging, hold release, and the whole certificate lifecycle — issue, revoke and reissue. A `ShippingClerk` owns packaging and shipment. A `PlantAdmin` is a master-data administrator: it may register reference data (part specifications and their revisions, steel heats, wire lots, die sets, furnaces) but may not operate the process, test, certify or ship. Every role may read every resource through the read endpoints, and every role may drive the logical clock.

Identity in this system is deliberately shallow. When `X-User-Role` is `ProcessEngineer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the same is true when `X-User-Role` is `HeatTreatOperator`, `QualityInspector`, `QualityManager`, `ShippingClerk`, or `PlantAdmin`. The server records the acting identifier where the process demands an audit trail — as `verified_by_user_id` on a steel heat that a `QualityInspector` verifies, as `performed_by_user_id` on a lot test that a `QualityInspector` opens, and as `signed_by_user_id` on a certificate that a `QualityManager` issues — but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`, and no resource is "owned" by a user in the ownership-check sense. The only comparison the system ever makes between operator identifiers is the segregation-of-duties check described in §2.11, which compares the acting `X-User-ID` against a stored `performed_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` is the number of records skipped before the page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0, and it may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. There is exactly one exception in this API: the list of a lot's plating runs is returned newest first, sorted by `created_at` descending, because a strip-and-replate cycle makes the most recent run the operationally interesting one.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object. Both `total_count` and `has_more` are computed against the *filtered* result set, not against the whole collection: when a caller filters wire lots by `steel_heat_id` and `status`, `total_count` reports how many wire lots match those filters, and `has_more` reports whether records matching those filters remain beyond the current page. Filters combine conjunctively — every supplied filter must match.

The filters available per collection are as follows. Wire lots may be filtered by `steel_heat_id` and by `status`. Production lots may be filtered by `part_spec_id`, `wire_lot_id`, `heading_tool_id`, `traceability_heat_id`, and by either state dimension independently — `process_state` or `disposition`. A lot's secondary operations may be filtered by `status`. Heat treatment runs may be filtered by `production_lot_id`, `furnace_id` and `status`. A lot's plating runs may be filtered by `status`. A lot's tests may be filtered by `result`. Certificates may be filtered by `production_lot_id`, `lot_test_id` and `status`. Pack units may be filtered by `production_lot_id`, `certification_document_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. The Logical Plant Clock

Every timestamp the server writes, every furnace and press window comparison, and every elapsed-time rule in this specification reads a single server-side logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. A test-driving capability — `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, available to all six roles — accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it, returning that instant. Setting the clock mutates no entity; it only changes what subsequent operations observe as "now".

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Record Versioning

Every resource in this system — part specifications, steel heats, wire lots, heading tools, furnaces, production lots, secondary operations, heat treatment runs, plating runs, lot tests, certification documents and pack units alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at the moment the record was created.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed from the logical clock on every mutation of the record.
- **`version`** (integer): starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record.

No entity in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`; all twelve resource types carry the full set.

The `version` field is the plant's change counter, and it is deliberately sensitive. A cascade that touches five records increments the `version` of all five, not just the record the client named. A derived-field refresh counts as a mutation: when a heading completion snapshots `tool_utilization_pct` onto a lot and advances the die set's life counter, both the lot and the die set have their `version` incremented and their `updated_at` refreshed. Clients that hold a stale `version` will discover it through the stale-read protection described in §2.7.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. The number of retained decimal places depends on the quantity:

- **Mass in kilograms** is stored to 3 decimal places. This covers the wire lot's `available_mass_kg`, `reserved_mass_kg` and `consumed_mass_kg`, a lot's `wire_reserved_kg` and `wire_consumed_kg`, a heat treatment run's `charge_mass_kg`, and a plating run's `load_mass_kg`.
- **Drawing reduction** (`reduction_pct`) is stored to 2 decimal places, and every comparison against the acceptance band is made on the rounded stored value, never on the unrounded intermediate.
- **Decarburization depth** (`total_decarb_depth_mm`) is stored to 3 decimal places.
- **Bake delay** (`bake_delay_hours`) is stored to 3 decimal places.
- **Tool life statistics** — `observed_avg_cycle_pieces` and `tool_utilization_pct` — are stored to 2 decimal places.
- **Risk scores** — `risk_score_pre_test` and `risk_score_final` — are stored to 2 decimal places. Their weighted sum is rounded exactly once, at the end, after all four terms have been weighted and added; the individual weighted terms are never rounded on their own.
- **Piece counts, life budgets, sample sizes and tolerances** are integers. Where a budget or tolerance is derived from a product, it is truncated downward (`rated_derated_life_pieces`, `conservative_life_pieces`, `count_tolerance_pieces`). Where a demand is derived from a product, it is raised upward to the next whole piece (the heading demand checked against remaining die life, and both sampling-plan sample sizes). A pack's `declared_count` derived from weight is rounded half up to a whole piece.

Bounds in this specification are inclusive unless the text says "strictly". Where a limit is exclusive, that is called out explicitly at the rule.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Shared Material and Lot Records

Two write operations act against a parent record that other writers legitimately mutate in parallel, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent's `version`.

**Opening a production lot** — `POST /production-lots` — requires a non-optional `expected_wire_lot_version` field carrying the `version` the client last observed on the wire lot named by `wire_lot_id`. If that value does not equal the wire lot's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WIRE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Note that the create would otherwise be perfectly legal: several production lots may reserve mass from the same wire lot simultaneously, so nothing about the lot's own state blocks it. What the system rejects is the client's stale view — its picture of `available_mass_kg` predates whatever earlier reservation, release or quarantine advanced the wire lot's `version`.

**Drafting a certificate** — `POST /certification-documents` — requires a non-optional `expected_production_lot_version` field carrying the `version` the client last observed on the production lot named by `production_lot_id`. If that value does not equal the lot's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again, a fresh draft legitimately supersedes an earlier draft for the same lot, so no state precondition blocks the write; the rejection exists because the drafting client's picture of the lot's test result, risk score and disposition predates the earlier mutation.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above must not require one and must not reject a request for an absent or mismatched version.

### 2.8. Two Conserved Ledgers

The plant keeps two accounting identities that must hold after every single write, including the compensating ones.

**The wire mass ledger.** For every wire lot, the mass received from the mill equals the mass still free, plus the mass reserved by lots in setup, plus the mass already burned into blanks: `gross_mass_kg` equals `available_mass_kg` plus `reserved_mass_kg` plus `consumed_mass_kg`. Opening a production lot moves mass from available to reserved. Reporting heading completion moves the reservation out of reserved, adds the actually consumed mass to consumed, and returns the difference to available — which means that a lot that over-consumed its reservation can drive `available_mass_kg` negative, and the system permits that rather than breaking the identity. Aborting a lot still in setup reverses its reservation exactly, moving the reserved mass back to available and leaving the identity untouched. A lot that has been aborted contributes nothing to either the reserved or the consumed side; lots in any other process state contribute their reservation or their consumption.

**The lot piece ledger.** For every production lot, everything that came off the header is accounted for somewhere: `headed_count` plus `heading_scrap_count` equals `available_count` plus `scrap_count` plus `destructive_test_count` plus `packed_count`. Secondary-operation scrap moves pieces from available into scrap. A destructive test moves pieces from available into destructive test consumption. Sealing a pack moves pieces from available into packed. Reopening a sealed pack moves them back, exactly. Note that `heading_scrap_count` is itself a component of `scrap_count`, and that recalled pack units keep contributing to `packed_count` — recalled pieces have left the lot's available inventory and stay accounted for as packed.

### 2.9. Contagion Containment and Cascade Ordering

Quality events in this plant travel forward through material, and the system propagates them automatically rather than waiting for a client to walk the graph. Three properties govern every cascade.

First, **cascades are transitive, and each link is caused by the mutation the previous link performed** — not by the original request. Flagging a mill certificate as suspected counterfeit quarantines the heat's wire lots; the wire lots' quarantine quarantines their production lots; the lots' quarantine revokes their certificates; the certificates' revocation recalls the packs printed with them. An implementation that fires all four links directly off the flagging request, rather than chaining them off each other's results, will produce the wrong result whenever an intermediate link matches nothing.

Second, **within each link, affected records are processed in ascending `created_at` order**. This gives cascades a deterministic, reproducible sequence of writes.

Third, **every record a cascade mutates has its own `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed**, exactly as if it had been written directly.

Compensating cascades are scoped by the tag that caused the original freeze. A wire lot quarantined by a counterfeit flag records `quarantine_source` as `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`; a wire lot quarantined by an outright rejection records `HEAT_REJECTED`; and a production lot frozen by either records `hold_source` as `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`. When a heat is later re-verified, only the records carrying the counterfeit tag are restored, so material frozen for an unrelated reason is never accidentally released. Restoration is also deliberately incomplete: revoked certificates stay revoked and recalled packs stay recalled, because a document that was withdrawn from a customer cannot be silently un-withdrawn.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and the Rule Evaluation Ladder

For any endpoint whose URL contains resource identifiers, the system resolves them strictly left to right and fails on the *first* one that does not exist. A request against a secondary-operations collection nested under a non-existent production lot reports `ERR_PRODUCTION_LOT_NOT_FOUND` and never attempts to look at anything to the right of it. Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved after all path identifiers, and among themselves in the order in which the fields appear in the endpoint's declared input list.

Rules are evaluated in a strict ascending ladder. **The first rule violated decides the response, and no later rule runs.** This ordering is normative — it is what makes an error response predictable when a request breaks several rules at once.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present, and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Schema.** Required fields present, enum values within their declared sets, numbers within their declared constraints, datetimes well-formed ISO 8601 UTC, `press_window_end_at` strictly after `press_window_start_at`, `window_end_at` strictly after `window_start_at`, `planned_pieces` at least 1, `sequence_no` at least 1, `limit` within its declared range and `offset` not negative — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Path existence**, left to right, yielding the 404 code for whichever resource type failed to resolve: `ERR_PART_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STEEL_HEAT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WIRE_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEADING_TOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FURNACE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECONDARY_OPERATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEAT_TREAT_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLATING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERT_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACK_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path placeholder.
5. **Body foreign-key existence**, in input-field order, yielding `ERR_PART_SPEC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STEEL_HEAT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WIRE_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEADING_TOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FURNACE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_TEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CERT_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to wire-lot registration, production-lot opening, heat-treat-run booking, plating-run scheduling, lot-test opening, certificate drafting and pack-unit opening.
6. **Uniqueness.** A steel heat whose `mill_cert_serial` already exists in the register is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MILL_CERT_SERIAL` (409) — the counterfeit-prevention register admits each mill certificate serial exactly once. Every other business identifier collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (409): `part_number` on part-spec registration, `lot_code` on lot opening, `tool_code` on die-set registration, `furnace_code` on furnace registration, `cert_serial` on certificate drafting and on certificate reissue, `label_serial` on pack-unit opening — each unique across the whole plant — and `sequence_no` on secondary-operation queueing, unique within its production lot.
7. **Stale-read protection**, per §2.7: `ERR_STALE_WIRE_LOT_VERSION` on lot opening and `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_LOT_VERSION` on certificate drafting, both HTTP 412. Note that this tier sits *above* the hold, state and window rules — a client presenting a stale parent version is told so even when the operation would also have failed a later check.
8. **Quarantine freeze.** The production lot in scope must not have `disposition` of `QUARANTINED` — otherwise `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED` (409). Quarantined material is frozen ahead of every other business check, including its own hold state. This rule guards every mutating operation whose target or parent is a production lot: heading completion, heading abort, placing a hold, releasing a hold, queueing a secondary operation, completing a secondary operation, booking a heat treatment run, starting a run, recording run results, scheduling a plating run, completing plating, starting a bake, completing a bake, opening a lot test, recording lot-test results, drafting a certificate, issuing a certificate, reissuing a certificate, opening a pack unit, sealing a pack, reopening a pack, and shipping a pack — twenty-two operations in all. Certificate revocation is deliberately *not* in this list: a quarantined lot's certificate can still be withdrawn.
9. **Hold freeze.** The production lot in scope must not have `disposition` of `HOLD` — otherwise `ERR_LOT_ON_HOLD` (409). This rule guards the same twenty-two operations as tier 8 minus two: releasing a hold is exempt (it is the operation that clears the hold) and placing a hold is exempt (it has its own precondition).
10. **Own-entity state gates.** The target's own state must permit the requested transition. A production lot's `process_state` must admit the transition, including the secondary-operation queue gate (the lot must be in `HEADED`, `HEAT_TREATED` or `SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS`) and the lot-test entry gate (`BAKED`, or `HEAT_TREATED` or `SECONDARY_DONE` when the part has no plating, or `TESTING` when every existing test of the lot came back `REJECT`) — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE` (409). A heading tool must be `AVAILABLE` to mount, `MOUNTED` to condemn, and `WORN` to regrind — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TOOL_STATE` (409). A steel heat must be `PENDING` or `SUSPECTED_COUNTERFEIT` to be verified, and `PENDING` or `VERIFIED` to be flagged counterfeit, with `REJECTED` terminal — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_HEAT_STATE` (409). A wire lot must be `DRAWN` to be released, so quarantined and already-released lots are refused — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WIRE_LOT_STATE` (409). A secondary operation must still be `PENDING` to be completed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SECONDARY_OP_STATE` (409). A heat treatment run must be `PLANNED` to start and `RUNNING` to accept results, and a plating run must be `SCHEDULED` to complete plating, `PLATED` to start a bake, and either `BAKING` or `PLATED`-with-no-bake-required to complete a bake, with `SUPERSEDED` runs refusing everything — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROCESS_RUN_STATE` (409). A certificate must be `DRAFT` or `REISSUE_PENDING` to be issued, `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `REISSUE_PENDING` to be revoked, and `REVOKED` to be reissued — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CERT_STATE` (409). A pack unit must be `OPEN` to seal and `SEALED` to reopen or ship, with `RECALLED` terminal — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PACK_UNIT_STATE` (409).
11. **Cross-entity state gates.** The lot's die set must be `MOUNTED` when heading completion is reported — a tool condemned or worn out mid-lot blocks the completion report with `ERR_INVALID_TOOL_STATE` (409). The wire lot named at lot opening must be `RELEASED` — otherwise `ERR_WIRE_LOT_NOT_RELEASED` (409). The steel heat behind the material must have `cert_status` of `VERIFIED` at wire-lot release, at lot opening, at certificate drafting, and at hold release when the hold's `hold_source` is `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT` — otherwise `ERR_STEEL_HEAT_NOT_VERIFIED` (409). The furnace named by a run must be `IDLE` when the run starts — otherwise `ERR_FURNACE_OCCUPIED` (409). Heat treatment must have completed with a passing result before plating is scheduled or completed — otherwise `ERR_HEAT_TREAT_NOT_PASSED` (409).
12. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) on certificate issue and certificate reissue, per §2.11.
13. **Process safety.** The relief-bake delay window (`ERR_HE_BAKE_WINDOW_MISSED`, 422) and bake parameters (`ERR_HE_BAKE_PARAMS_INVALID`, 422) on bake start; the elapsed soak (`ERR_BAKE_DURATION_NOT_ELAPSED`, 409) on bake completion; the achieved strength class (`ERR_STRENGTH_CLASS_NOT_ACHIEVED`, 422) and the aerospace die-utilisation ceiling (`ERR_AEROSPACE_TOOL_UTILIZATION_EXCEEDED`, 422) on certificate drafting; thread-rolling timing (`ERR_THREAD_TIMING_VIOLATION`, 422) on secondary-operation queueing; lubrication sufficiency (`ERR_LUBRICATION_INSUFFICIENT`, 422) and drawing reduction (`ERR_DRAW_REDUCTION_OUT_OF_RANGE`, 422) on wire-lot release; and mill certificate serial agreement (`ERR_MILL_CERT_SERIAL_MISMATCH`, 422) on mill certificate verification.
14. **Referential business rules.** On certificate drafting, the cited test must belong to the same production lot as the certificate (`ERR_TEST_LOT_MISMATCH`, 409) and must have result `ACCEPT` (`ERR_TEST_NOT_ACCEPTED`, 409) — and the mismatch rule is evaluated *before* the not-accepted rule, so citing a foreign test reports the mismatch and only a same-lot, non-accepted test can reach the second rule. On pack-unit opening and on pack shipment, the named certificate must belong to the same production lot as the pack (`ERR_CERT_LOT_MISMATCH`, 409). At shipment the certificate must be `ISSUED` (`ERR_CERT_NOT_ISSUED`, 409). At certificate issue, the certificate's `pinned_part_spec_version` must still equal the part specification's current `version` (`ERR_PART_SPEC_VERSION_DRIFT`, 409).
15. **Temporal rules.** Press window overlap on the same die set (`ERR_TOOL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409) on lot opening; furnace window overlap including cooldown (`ERR_FURNACE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409) on run booking; and starting a run outside its booked window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_RUN_WINDOW`, 422) on run start.
16. **Business constraints.** Steel grade permitted by the part (`ERR_GRADE_NOT_ALLOWED`, 422) on lot opening; operation sequencing (`ERR_OP_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT`, 409) on secondary-operation queueing; required secondary operations complete (`ERR_SECONDARY_OPS_INCOMPLETE`, 422) on run booking and on lot-test opening; bake applicability (`ERR_BAKE_NOT_REQUIRED`, 409) on bake start; piece-count consistency (`ERR_PIECE_COUNT_INVALID`, 422) on heading completion, secondary-operation completion, lot-test results and pack sealing; sampling feasibility (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SAMPLE_PIECES`, 422) on lot-test opening; pack count tolerance (`ERR_PACK_COUNT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, 422) on pack sealing; label completeness (`ERR_PACK_LABEL_INCOMPLETE`, 422) on pack shipment; and the certifiable risk ceiling (`ERR_RISK_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`, 422) on certificate issue.
17. **Capacity and material sufficiency.** Wire mass sufficiency (`ERR_WIRE_MASS_INSUFFICIENT`, 422) and remaining die life (`ERR_TOOL_LIFE_EXHAUSTED`, 422) on lot opening; and furnace charge capacity (`ERR_FURNACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 422) on run booking.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Counterfeit Prevention

The plant's audit posture rests on two people never being the same person. When a `QualityInspector` opens a destructive lot test, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored on that test as `performed_by_user_id`. Later, when a certificate citing that test is issued or reissued, the system compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the stored `performed_by_user_id` of the cited test; if they are equal, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The inspector who destroyed the sample pieces cannot sign or reissue the certificate that rests on them.

Counterfeit prevention rests on the mill certificate serial. Each steel heat carries a `mill_cert_serial` that no other heat may repeat, so a forged duplicate cannot be quietly registered alongside the genuine article. A verification must present the same serial the heat already records; presenting a different one is treated as a counterfeit signal rather than a typo and is refused with `ERR_MILL_CERT_SERIAL_MISMATCH`. And once a heat's paperwork is doubted, no downstream artefact of that heat may continue as though nothing happened — see §2.9 for the propagation and §3.2 for the details.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Defining the Part: Specifications and the Derived Bake Duty

Everything in this plant is measured against a part specification, registered by a `ProcessEngineer` or a `PlantAdmin`. The payload carries the customer's `part_number` (unique across the plant), the thread callout in `thread_designation` (a value such as M8x1.25), the target `strength_class` — one of `CLASS_8_8`, `CLASS_10_9`, `CLASS_12_9`, `GRADE_5` or `GRADE_8` — and the `qualification_program` under which the part is built, which is `NONE`, `AUTOMOTIVE_PPAP` or `AEROSPACE_NADCAP` and defaults to `NONE` when omitted. Material eligibility is expressed as `allowed_steel_grades`, a non-empty list of steel grades permitted for the part. The mass of one headed blank is `blank_mass_g`, given in grams and strictly positive; every mass conversion in the system divides grams by 1000 to reach kilograms. A `scrap_allowance_pct` expresses, as a fraction of planned output, how much heading overrun and scrap the process may produce; it defaults to 0.05 when omitted and must be at least 0 and no more than 0.20.

The routing side of the specification says how the part is made. `station_count_required` states how many progressive stations the die set must provide and must be at least 1; `required_secondary_ops` lists the subset of thread rolling, drilling, slotting and pointing the part needs, drawn from `THREAD_ROLL`, `DRILL`, `SLOT` and `POINT`, and it must always contain `THREAD_ROLL`. `thread_timing` decides which side of heat treatment the thread rolling belongs on, and is either `BEFORE_HEAT_TREAT` or `AFTER_HEAT_TREAT`. The metallurgical acceptance band is given by `min_core_hardness_hv` and `max_core_hardness_hv`, both inclusive bounds in Vickers hardness, with `max_decarb_depth_mm` as the inclusive ceiling on total decarburization depth. Finish is declared as `plating_spec`, one of `NONE`, `ZINC_CLEAR`, `ZINC_NICKEL` or `CADMIUM`. Testing draws on `proof_load_kn`, the proof load in kilonewtons applied to sample pieces during lot testing.

Packaging is specified up front. `packaging_mode` is either `BY_COUNT` or `BY_WEIGHT`; `nominal_pack_count` is the target number of pieces per pack and must be at least 1; `pieces_per_kg` is the nominal piece count per kilogram used to derive a declared count from a weighed pack and must be strictly positive; and `count_tolerance_pct` is the fraction of the nominal pack count tolerated as deviation, defaulting to 0.02 when omitted.

Two fields on the specification are never supplied by the client. `he_bake_required` is true exactly when the part is plated with something — that is, `plating_spec` is not `NONE` — *and* its `strength_class` is one of the three embrittlement-prone classes `CLASS_10_9`, `CLASS_12_9` and `GRADE_8`; in every other combination it is false. `he_bake_min_minutes` follows from it: zero when no bake is required; a full eight hours, expressed as 480 minutes, for `CLASS_12_9` and `GRADE_8`; and four hours, expressed as 240 minutes, for `CLASS_10_9`. A `CADMIUM`-plated `CLASS_8_8` part therefore needs no relief bake at all, while an otherwise identical `CLASS_12_9` part needs the longest soak the plant runs.

Specifications may be revised later by a `ProcessEngineer` or a `PlantAdmin`, and a revision may touch `min_core_hardness_hv`, `max_core_hardness_hv`, `max_decarb_depth_mm`, `nominal_pack_count`, `count_tolerance_pct`, `plating_spec` and `proof_load_kn`, each optional. A revision increments the specification's `version` by 1, and when `plating_spec` changes it re-derives both `he_bake_required` and `he_bake_min_minutes` from the new finish and the unchanged strength class. Crucially, a revision never re-pins anything: production lots keep the `pinned_part_spec_version` they captured when they were opened, and certificates keep theirs. That is by design — it is how a specification quietly revised after a certificate was drafted surfaces as `ERR_PART_SPEC_VERSION_DRIFT` at signing time instead of silently changing what the certificate means.

### 3.2. Incoming Material: Steel Heats, Wire Drawing and Release to the Floor

Each coil of rod arriving from the mill is registered as a steel heat by a `ProcessEngineer`, a `QualityInspector` or a `PlantAdmin`, recording the mill's `heat_number` (unique across the plant), the producing mill's `mill_code`, the `grade` — one of `SAE_1018`, `SAE_1038`, `SAE_10B21` or `SAE_4037` — the ladle chemistry as `carbon_pct` and `boron_ppm` (the latter defaulting to zero when omitted), the `gross_mass_kg` received, and the `mill_cert_serial` of the mill test report. A new heat begins with `cert_status` of `PENDING`, with `lab_report_reference`, `verified_at`, `verified_by_user_id` and `counterfeit_reason` all empty.

A `QualityInspector` adjudicates the paperwork by submitting an `outcome` of either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, along with the `mill_cert_serial` being attested and a `lab_report_reference` naming the independent laboratory report relied on. On an outcome of `VERIFIED`, the heat moves `PENDING → VERIFIED`, stamping `verified_at` from the logical clock, `verified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `lab_report_reference` from the request, and incrementing the heat's `version` by 1. On an outcome of `REJECTED`, the heat moves `PENDING → REJECTED`, recording `lab_report_reference`, and `REJECTED` is terminal — no path leads out of it. Rejection also fires a two-link cascade. The first link takes every wire lot drawn from the heat whose `status` is `DRAWN` or `RELEASED`, in ascending `created_at` order, to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_source` set to `HEAT_REJECTED`. The second link is caused by the first: for each wire lot the first link quarantined, every production lot consuming that wire lot whose `process_state` is neither `SHIPPED` nor `ABORTED` moves, in ascending `created_at` order, to `disposition` of `QUARANTINED` with `hold_source` of `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`. The heat's `version` increments by 1, and so does the `version` of every record the cascade touched.

Suspicion is a `QualityManager`'s call. Flagging a heat whose `cert_status` is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED` moves it to `SUSPECTED_COUNTERFEIT`, records the supplied `reason` as `counterfeit_reason`, and fires the plant's longest chain — four links, each caused by the write the previous link performed rather than by the original request. Link one: every wire lot of the heat in `status` `DRAWN` or `RELEASED` becomes `QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_source` of `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`, in ascending `created_at` order. Link two, caused by link one: every production lot whose wire lot was quarantined by link one and whose `process_state` is not `ABORTED` becomes `disposition` `QUARANTINED` with `hold_source` `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`, in ascending `created_at` order. Link three, caused by link two: every certificate of a lot quarantined by link two whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `REISSUE_PENDING` becomes `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` recorded as `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`, in ascending `created_at` order. Link four, caused by link three: every pack unit printed with a certificate revoked by link three whose `status` is `SEALED` or `SHIPPED` becomes `RECALLED` with `recall_reason` recorded as `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`, in ascending `created_at` order. The heat's `version` increments by 1, as does that of every wire lot, production lot, certificate and pack unit the chain touched.

A suspected heat can be cleared. When a `QualityInspector` re-verifies a heat sitting in `SUSPECTED_COUNTERFEIT` against an independent lab report — outcome `VERIFIED`, with a `mill_cert_serial` equal to the one the heat records — the heat moves `SUSPECTED_COUNTERFEIT → VERIFIED`, refreshing `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id`, and a compensating cascade runs. Every wire lot of the heat tagged `quarantine_source` of `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT` returns, in ascending `created_at` order, to `status` `DRAWN` with `quarantine_source` reset to `NONE` and `inspected_at` cleared — the lot goes back to being merely drawn, and a fresh inspection release is required before it can feed heading again. Every production lot in `disposition` `QUARANTINED` with `hold_source` `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT` whose wire lot was among those restored returns to `disposition` `IN_PROCESS` with `hold_source` reset to `NONE`. Certificates revoked by the original chain are *not* restored; they stay `REVOKED`, and the packs recalled under them stay `RECALLED`. Every mutated record's `version` increments by 1. The inspector may instead conclude the worst: an outcome of `REJECTED` on a heat in `SUSPECTED_COUNTERFEIT` moves it `SUSPECTED_COUNTERFEIT → REJECTED` and increments its `version` by 1, with no cascade beyond the freeze the flagging already imposed.

Wire lots come next. A `ProcessEngineer` or `PlantAdmin` records a drawn and coated lot against a `steel_heat_id`, giving the `draw_pass_count` (at least 1), the `inlet_diameter_mm` entering the bench and the `outlet_diameter_mm` leaving it (both strictly positive, with the outlet strictly smaller than the inlet), the `lubricant_coating` applied — `PHOSPHATE`, `POLYMER`, `LIME` or `NONE` — the `coating_weight_gsm` in grams per square metre (not negative), and the `gross_mass_kg` of drawn wire (strictly positive). The server derives `reduction_pct` from the diameters: divide the outlet diameter by the inlet diameter, square that ratio to get the surviving cross-sectional area fraction, subtract it from one, multiply by one hundred, and round the result half up to two decimal places. The three mass ledger members are initialised so the identity holds trivially — `available_mass_kg` equals `gross_mass_kg`, while `reserved_mass_kg` and `consumed_mass_kg` are both zero — and `status` starts at `DRAWN` with `quarantine_source` of `NONE` and `inspected_at` empty.

Only a `QualityInspector` can put wire on the heading floor, and three gates stand in the way of the `DRAWN → RELEASED` transition. The wire must actually be lubricated: `lubricant_coating` must be something other than `NONE` and `coating_weight_gsm` must be at least 2.0 grams per square metre, or the release is refused with `ERR_LUBRICATION_INSUFFICIENT`. The drawing must have been neither too gentle nor too aggressive: the stored, already-rounded `reduction_pct` must be at least 8.0 and at most 35.0 — both bounds inclusive, so exactly 8.00 and exactly 35.00 both pass — or the release is refused with `ERR_DRAW_REDUCTION_OUT_OF_RANGE`. And the paperwork behind the material must be clean: the steel heat the lot was drawn from must have `cert_status` of `VERIFIED`, or the release is refused with `ERR_STEEL_HEAT_NOT_VERIFIED`. On success the lot moves `DRAWN → RELEASED`, `inspected_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the lot's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.3. Die Sets, Life Cycles and the Two Life Budgets

A progressive cold-heading die set is registered by a `ProcessEngineer` or `PlantAdmin` with a unique `tool_code`, the number of progressive stations it provides as `station_count` (at least 1), its nameplate `rated_life_pieces` for a fresh set (at least 1), and optionally `max_regrinds`, the number of regrind cycles it may take before condemnation, which defaults to 3 and may not be negative. A fresh set begins `AVAILABLE`, with `regrind_count` and `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle` at zero, an empty `cycle_pieces_history`, `completed_cycle_count` and `lifetime_pieces` at zero, `observed_avg_cycle_pieces` empty, and `mounted_press_code` and `condemn_reason` both empty.

Die life is bookkept in cycles. `cycle_pieces_history` holds one integer per *closed* life cycle, appended in chronological order as each cycle ends; `completed_cycle_count` is simply how many entries that history holds; `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle` counts the pieces headed since the last regrind, that is, in the still-open cycle; and `lifetime_pieces` is the sum of every entry in the history plus the open cycle's count. From these, two life budgets are derived, and the difference between them is the sharpest branch in this specification.

The first budget is the nameplate, derated for wear that regrinding cannot restore. `rated_derated_life_pieces` is the nameplate `rated_life_pieces` multiplied by one minus 0.15 for each completed regrind — that is, by one minus the product of `regrind_count` and 0.15 — with the result truncated downward to a whole number of pieces. A set with a nameplate of 100,000 pieces and two regrinds behind it therefore offers a derated 70,000.

The second budget listens to what the tool has actually achieved. When `completed_cycle_count` is zero there is no history to listen to: `observed_avg_cycle_pieces` is empty and `conservative_life_pieces` simply equals `rated_derated_life_pieces`. Once at least one cycle has closed, `observed_avg_cycle_pieces` is the sum of every entry in `cycle_pieces_history` divided by `completed_cycle_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `conservative_life_pieces` is the smaller of `rated_derated_life_pieces` and 0.90 multiplied by `observed_avg_cycle_pieces`, truncated downward to a whole number of pieces. A set whose real cycles have been running short is thus budgeted well below its nameplate.

Which budget applies is decided by the part, not by the tool — see §3.4 for the capacity gate that reads it. Both budgets, along with `observed_avg_cycle_pieces`, are computed when the set is first registered and recomputed whenever a regrind closes a cycle.

A `ProcessEngineer` mounts an `AVAILABLE` set on a press by supplying the `press_code`, taking it `AVAILABLE → MOUNTED`, recording the press in `mounted_press_code`, clearing any previous `condemn_reason`, and incrementing the set's `version` by 1. A mounted set leaves service by one of two `MOUNTED → WORN` routes, and both do the same damage downstream. The counter-driven route fires automatically when heading completion pushes `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle` to or past `rated_derated_life_pieces`: `status` becomes `WORN`, `mounted_press_code` is cleared, `condemn_reason` stays empty because no operator gave a reason, and every *other* production lot queued on that set whose `process_state` is `SETUP` moves, in ascending `created_at` order, to `disposition` of `HOLD` with `hold_source` of `TOOL_WEAR`. The operator route is a `ProcessEngineer` or `QualityInspector` condemning a chipped or cracked die with a supplied `reason`: `status` becomes `WORN`, that `reason` is recorded as `condemn_reason`, `mounted_press_code` is cleared, and every production lot on that set in `process_state` `SETUP` moves, in ascending `created_at` order, to `disposition` `HOLD` with `hold_source` `TOOL_WEAR`. In both routes the set's `version` increments by 1, as does the `version` of every lot put on hold. The operator route ends a cycle early on purpose — that is precisely how `observed_avg_cycle_pieces` falls below the derated nameplate and how the conservative budget starts to bite.

Regrinding is a `ProcessEngineer` action on a `WORN` set, and it branches on the regrind allowance. When `regrind_count` is still below `max_regrinds`, the set goes `WORN → AVAILABLE`: the open cycle's `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle` is appended to `cycle_pieces_history`, `completed_cycle_count` and `regrind_count` each increment by 1, `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle` resets to zero, `observed_avg_cycle_pieces`, `rated_derated_life_pieces` and `conservative_life_pieces` are all recomputed, `condemn_reason` is cleared, and the set's `version` increments by 1 — closing the cycle `AVAILABLE → MOUNTED → WORN → AVAILABLE`. When `regrind_count` has already reached `max_regrinds`, the very same request takes the set `WORN → SCRAPPED` instead: the open cycle's count is still appended to `cycle_pieces_history` and `completed_cycle_count` still increments by 1, but `regrind_count` does not advance, the set's `status` becomes `SCRAPPED`, and the `version` increments by 1. `SCRAPPED` is terminal; a scrapped set never mounts again.

### 3.4. Opening a Production Lot and Heading It

A `ProcessEngineer` opens a production lot with a unique `lot_code`, the `part_spec_id` being produced, the `wire_lot_id` to be consumed, the `heading_tool_id` to be used, the `press_code` the lot will run on, an inclusive `press_window_start_at` and an exclusive `press_window_end_at` bounding the die set's occupation, the `planned_pieces` of good output (at least 1), and the `expected_wire_lot_version` described in §2.7. The lot is born with `process_state` of `SETUP` and `disposition` of `IN_PROCESS`, `hold_source` of `NONE`, every piece counter — `headed_count`, `heading_scrap_count`, `available_count`, `scrap_count`, `destructive_test_count` and `packed_count` — at zero, and `inspection_severity`, `achieved_strength_class`, `risk_score_pre_test`, `risk_score_final`, `abort_reason` and `wire_consumed_kg` all still empty.

Two values are frozen onto the lot at that moment and never re-derived afterwards. `pinned_part_spec_version` copies the part specification's current `version`, which is what later makes a mid-flight revision visible as drift rather than as silent change. And `traceability_heat_id` copies the wire lot's `steel_heat_id`, denormalising the provenance edge so the lot's origin survives even as the wire lot's own record moves on.

Opening a lot must clear a series of gates beyond the ladder in §2.10. The named wire lot must be in `status` `RELEASED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WIRE_LOT_NOT_RELEASED`. The steel heat behind it must still be `VERIFIED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STEEL_HEAT_NOT_VERIFIED`. The wire lot's steel grade must appear in the part's `allowed_steel_grades`, or the request is refused with `ERR_GRADE_NOT_ALLOWED`. The requested press window must not overlap the window of any other non-aborted production lot already booked on the same die set; windows are half-open, running from the inclusive start to the exclusive end, so a window beginning at the exact instant another one ends is accepted, while any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_TOOL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Then comes the die-life capacity gate, and this is where the qualification programme decides the arithmetic. The demand is the planned output inflated by the scrap allowance — `planned_pieces` multiplied by one plus the part's `scrap_allowance_pct`, raised upward to the next whole piece. The supply is the remaining life in the open cycle: the applicable life budget minus `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle`. For a part whose `qualification_program` is `NONE` or `AUTOMOTIVE_PPAP`, the applicable budget is `rated_derated_life_pieces`, and the tool's observed history is ignored entirely — a set whose real cycles ran short still offers its full nameplate minus the regrind derate. For a part whose `qualification_program` is `AEROSPACE_NADCAP`, the applicable budget is `conservative_life_pieces`, the smaller of the derated nameplate and 0.90 multiplied by the observed cycle average. Where a history of short cycles exists the aerospace budget is strictly smaller, so the same die set will accept an automotive lot and refuse an identically sized aerospace one. If the demand exceeds the remaining life, the request is refused with `ERR_TOOL_LIFE_EXHAUSTED`.

Finally the wire is reserved. The required mass is `planned_pieces` multiplied by the part's `blank_mass_g`, multiplied by one plus `scrap_allowance_pct`, divided by 1000 to convert grams to kilograms, and rounded half up to three decimal places. The wire lot's `available_mass_kg` must be at least that amount, or the request is refused with `ERR_WIRE_MASS_INSUFFICIENT`. On success, that amount is subtracted from `available_mass_kg`, added to `reserved_mass_kg`, and stored on the lot as `wire_reserved_kg`; the wire lot's `version` increments by 1.

Reporting heading completion is the single busiest write in the system. A `ProcessEngineer` supplies `headed_count`, the good pieces the header produced, and `heading_scrap_count`, the pieces it spoiled. The lot must be in `process_state` `SETUP` with `disposition` `IN_PROCESS` — a lot on hold reports `ERR_LOT_ON_HOLD` and a quarantined lot reports `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED` — and its die set must still be `MOUNTED`, or the report is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TOOL_STATE`. The reported output must also stay inside the allowance: `headed_count` plus `heading_scrap_count` may not exceed `planned_pieces` multiplied by one plus `scrap_allowance_pct`, truncated downward to a whole number of pieces, or the report is refused with `ERR_PIECE_COUNT_INVALID`.

On success, the lot moves `SETUP → HEADED` and a chain of effects fires. The reported counts land: `headed_count` and `available_count` both take the reported good-piece count, while `heading_scrap_count` and `scrap_count` both take the reported scrap count. The wire ledger settles: the actually consumed mass is the sum of the two reported counts multiplied by `blank_mass_g`, divided by 1000, rounded half up to three decimal places; the lot's original `wire_reserved_kg` is subtracted from the wire lot's `reserved_mass_kg`, the consumed mass is added to `consumed_mass_kg`, and the difference between the reservation and the consumption is added back to `available_mass_kg` — which will be negative when the lot over-consumed. The consumed mass is stored as `wire_consumed_kg` and `wire_reserved_kg` drops to zero. The first cascade link advances the die set: `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle` grows by the sum of the two reported counts and `lifetime_pieces` is recomputed. The lot then snapshots its own wear picture as `tool_utilization_pct`, which is 100 multiplied by the set's new `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle`, divided by its `rated_derated_life_pieces`, rounded half up to two decimal places — note that wear is always measured against the *rated derated* budget, never against the conservative one, whatever the part's qualification programme. The second cascade link is caused by the first: if `cumulative_pieces_current_cycle` has reached or passed `rated_derated_life_pieces`, the set transitions `MOUNTED → WORN`. The third link is caused by the second: every other production lot on that set still in `process_state` `SETUP` moves to `disposition` `HOLD` with `hold_source` `TOOL_WEAR`, in ascending `created_at` order. The lot's `version`, the wire lot's `version` and the die set's `version` each increment by 1, plus the `version` of every lot put on hold.

A lot that never runs is aborted instead. A `ProcessEngineer` supplies a `reason`; the lot must be in `process_state` `SETUP`. The abort exactly compensates the reservation made at opening: `wire_reserved_kg` is subtracted from the wire lot's `reserved_mass_kg` and added back to `available_mass_kg`, leaving the mass identity of §2.8 exactly where it was before the lot existed. The lot's `wire_reserved_kg` drops to zero, the supplied reason is stored as `abort_reason`, and and the two state dimensions move together: the lot's `process_state` goes `SETUP → ABORTED` while its `disposition` goes `IN_PROCESS → SCRAPPED`. Both `ABORTED` and `SCRAPPED` are terminal on their respective dimensions. The lot's `version` and the wire lot's `version` each increment by 1.

The `disposition` dimension is deliberately independent of `process_state`: a lot can be frozen and thawed without moving one step along its process route. A `QualityInspector` or `QualityManager` may place a hold with a `reason` on any lot whose `disposition` is `IN_PROCESS`, taking it `IN_PROCESS → HOLD` with `hold_source` of `QUALITY` and incrementing its `version` by 1. The system places the same hold by itself on three occasions, differing only in the tag it leaves: `TOOL_WEAR` when a die set wears out or is condemned under the lot, `HT_FAILURE` when a heat treatment run comes back failing, and `TEST_REJECT` when a lot test is rejected. Only a `QualityManager` releases a hold, optionally recording a `note`; the lot must be in `disposition` `HOLD`, and when the hold carries `hold_source` of `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT` the release additionally requires that the lot's traceability heat now has `cert_status` of `VERIFIED`, or it is refused with `ERR_STEEL_HEAT_NOT_VERIFIED`. A successful release takes the lot `HOLD → IN_PROCESS`, resets `hold_source` to `NONE`, and increments the `version` by 1. Quarantine is stricter still: a lot in `disposition` `IN_PROCESS` or `HOLD` can be pushed to `QUARANTINED` by the counterfeit or rejection cascades (with `hold_source` `HEAT_COUNTERFEIT`) so long as its `process_state` is not `ABORTED`, and the only route back out of `QUARANTINED` is the system's own compensating cascade when the heat is re-verified. No operator can release a quarantine by hand.

### 3.5. Secondary Operations, Heat Treatment and Thread Timing

Secondary operations are queued one at a time by a `ProcessEngineer`, who supplies the `op_type` — `THREAD_ROLL`, `DRILL`, `SLOT` or `POINT` — the `sequence_no` giving the operation's order within the lot (at least 1, and unique within the lot), and the `machine_code` the operation runs on. Queueing requires the lot's `disposition` to be `IN_PROCESS` and its `process_state` to be `HEADED`, `HEAT_TREATED` or `SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. The new operation is created in `status` `PENDING` with `pieces_in` taken from the lot's current `available_count` — the client never supplies it — and with `performed_after_heat_treat` capturing whether the lot's `heat_treat_completed` flag was already true at the moment of creation. Its `pieces_out` and `scrap_pieces` stay empty while the operation is `PENDING`.

Where the lot goes depends on where it was. From `HEADED` the lot moves `HEADED → SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS`. From `HEAT_TREATED` the lot moves `HEAT_TREATED → SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS`, and this route is reserved for parts whose thread rolling belongs after heat treatment: queueing a `THREAD_ROLL` operation here requires the part's `thread_timing` to be `AFTER_HEAT_TREAT`, and the created operation carries `performed_after_heat_treat` as true. Already inside `SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS`, queueing the next operation is a self-loop — `SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS → SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS` — which is how a multi-operation route works its way through drilling, slotting and pointing one operation at a time. The self-loop imposes two extra conditions: no other operation of the lot may still be in `status` `PENDING`, and the new `sequence_no` must be strictly greater than every `sequence_no` already used on the lot; a violation of either is refused with `ERR_OP_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT`. In every case the lot's `version` increments by 1.

Thread timing is enforced symmetrically, and it is the one rule that reads the part's routing intent rather than its state. A `THREAD_ROLL` operation created *after* heat treatment has completed requires `thread_timing` to be `AFTER_HEAT_TREAT`; a `THREAD_ROLL` operation created *before* heat treatment has completed requires `thread_timing` to be `BEFORE_HEAT_TREAT`. Either mismatch is refused with `ERR_THREAD_TIMING_VIOLATION`.

Closing an operation is also a `ProcessEngineer` action: the payload carries `pieces_out`, the pieces that came back off the machine, which may not be negative and may not exceed `pieces_in` — a larger value is refused with `ERR_PIECE_COUNT_INVALID`. The operation must still be `PENDING` and the lot's `disposition` must be `IN_PROCESS`. On success the operation moves `PENDING → DONE` (a terminal status), `pieces_out` is recorded, and `scrap_pieces` is set to `pieces_in` minus `pieces_out`. The lot absorbs the yield: its `available_count` is replaced by `pieces_out` and its `scrap_count` grows by `scrap_pieces`, keeping the piece ledger of §2.8 balanced. Both the operation's `version` and the lot's `version` increment by 1.

Completion also bubbles the lot up. When the lot has no operation left in `status` `PENDING` *and* the operations due at this stage are all `DONE`, the lot moves `SECONDARY_IN_PROGRESS → SECONDARY_DONE`. What "due at this stage" means depends on the part's thread timing. For a part with `thread_timing` of `BEFORE_HEAT_TREAT`, every `op_type` listed in `required_secondary_ops` must have a `DONE` operation. For a part with `thread_timing` of `AFTER_HEAT_TREAT` whose `heat_treat_completed` is still false, every `op_type` in `required_secondary_ops` *except* `THREAD_ROLL` must have a `DONE` operation — the thread rolling is legitimately still outstanding, and the lot proceeds to the furnace without it. The bubble-up increments the lot's `version` by 1. Should a lot be charged into a furnace or opened for testing while a required operation for its stage is missing or still `PENDING`, the request is refused with `ERR_SECONDARY_OPS_INCOMPLETE`.

Heat treatment runs on furnaces, which a `ProcessEngineer` or `PlantAdmin` registers with a unique `furnace_code`, a strictly positive `max_charge_mass_kg` per run, and an optional `cooldown_minutes` — the mandatory idle buffer after a run window before the next one may open — which defaults to 30 minutes and may not be negative. A furnace starts `IDLE` with `last_run_end_at` empty.

A `HeatTreatOperator` or `ProcessEngineer` books a run against a `production_lot_id` and a `furnace_id`, declaring an inclusive `window_start_at` and an inclusive `window_end_at` (the end strictly after the start), together with the recipe: `austenitize_temp_c`, the `quench_medium` (`OIL`, `WATER` or `POLYMER`), `temper_temp_c`, `temper_time_min` (at least 1 minute of soak), and `carbon_potential_pct`, the furnace atmosphere's carbon potential. The run's `charge_mass_kg` is derived, not supplied: it is the lot's `available_count` multiplied by the part's `blank_mass_g`, divided by 1000, rounded half up to three decimal places, and it may not exceed the furnace's `max_charge_mass_kg` — the comparison is inclusive, so a charge exactly at capacity is accepted, and anything heavier is refused with `ERR_FURNACE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. One run charges exactly one lot, so no aggregation across lots occurs. Booking also respects the cooldown: the requested window must not collide with any non-terminal run already booked on the same furnace, where each existing window is treated as extended by that furnace's `cooldown_minutes` and both endpoints count as touching. Concretely, a conflict exists when the requested start is at or before the other run's end plus the cooldown *and* the requested end is at or after the other run's start; such a booking is refused with `ERR_FURNACE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A booked run begins in `status` `PLANNED` with `started_at`, `completed_at`, `core_hardness_hv`, `surface_decarb_depth_mm`, `total_decarb_depth_mm` all empty and `result` of `PENDING`; booking does not occupy the furnace.

Starting the run is exclusively a `HeatTreatOperator` action and takes the run `PLANNED → RUNNING`. The furnace must be `IDLE`, or the start is refused with `ERR_FURNACE_OCCUPIED`. The logical clock must lie inside the booked window — at or after `window_start_at` and at or before `window_end_at` — or the start is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_RUN_WINDOW`. The lot must have `disposition` `IN_PROCESS` and `process_state` `SECONDARY_DONE`. On success `started_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the furnace flips `IDLE → OCCUPIED`, and both the run's `version` and the furnace's `version` increment by 1.

Results are recorded by a `QualityInspector` on a `RUNNING` run, supplying the coupon's `core_hardness_hv` and its measured `surface_decarb_depth_mm`. The system first amplifies the measured surface loss for a lean furnace atmosphere. The carbon deficit is the greater of 0 and 0.40 minus the run's `carbon_potential_pct` — so an atmosphere whose carbon potential is at or above 0.40 contributes no deficit at all. `total_decarb_depth_mm` is then the measured `surface_decarb_depth_mm` multiplied by one plus 2.0 multiplied by that deficit, rounded half up to three decimal places. Evaluation order is pinned, hardness first and decarburization second. If `core_hardness_hv` falls outside the part's band — below `min_core_hardness_hv` or above `max_core_hardness_hv`, both bounds inclusive so a coupon exactly on either bound passes — the `result` is `FAIL_HARDNESS`. Otherwise, if `total_decarb_depth_mm` is strictly greater than the part's `max_decarb_depth_mm`, the `result` is `FAIL_DECARB`; a depth exactly equal to the limit passes. Otherwise the `result` is `PASS`. Both the hardness band and the decarburization ceiling are read from the part specification version pinned on the lot, never from the specification's current state.

Recording results always takes the run `RUNNING → COMPLETED` (a terminal status), stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock, returns the furnace to `IDLE`, and sets the furnace's `last_run_end_at` to the same instant. What happens to the lot depends on the result. On `PASS`, the lot's `heat_treat_completed` flag becomes true and the lot moves `SECONDARY_DONE → HEAT_TREATED`. On either `FAIL_HARDNESS` or `FAIL_DECARB`, the lot's `disposition` moves to `HOLD` with `hold_source` of `HT_FAILURE` while its `process_state` stays exactly where it was. In every case the run's `version`, the furnace's `version` and the lot's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.6. Plating and the Hydrogen-Embrittlement Relief Bake

Plating is scheduled by a `HeatTreatOperator` or `ProcessEngineer` against a `production_lot_id`, and it may not begin before metallurgy has been proven: the lot's `heat_treat_completed` flag must be true both when the run is scheduled and when plating is completed, or the request is refused with `ERR_HEAT_TREAT_NOT_PASSED`. Although the payload names `plating_spec` and `load_mass_kg`, both are server-derived on the created run: `plating_spec` is copied from the part specification's own `plating_spec` — one of `ZINC_CLEAR`, `ZINC_NICKEL` or `CADMIUM`, since an unplated part never has a plating run — and `load_mass_kg` is the lot's `available_count` multiplied by the part's `blank_mass_g`, divided by 1000, rounded half up to three decimal places. A new run starts in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `plated_at`, `bake_started_at`, `bake_temp_c`, `bake_duration_min`, `bake_delay_hours` and `bake_completed_at` all empty.

Scheduling a second run for the same lot is the plant's strip-and-replate path, and it retires the earlier attempt automatically: any existing run of that lot in `status` `SCHEDULED` or `PLATED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` and has its `version` incremented by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal and a superseded run refuses every subsequent operation with `ERR_INVALID_PROCESS_RUN_STATE`. Every rule in this section that speaks of "the lot's plating run" means the newest run that has not been superseded; superseded runs never gate a bake and never feed a risk score.

Completing plating is a `HeatTreatOperator` action on a `SCHEDULED` run whose lot has `disposition` `IN_PROCESS`. The run moves `SCHEDULED → PLATED` and stamps `plated_at` from the logical clock, which starts the embrittlement relief clock ticking. The lot follows on its process dimension: a part that came straight from the furnace moves `HEAT_TREATED → PLATED`, while a part whose thread rolling ran after heat treatment — and which therefore re-entered secondary operations and came back through the bubble-up — moves `SECONDARY_DONE → PLATED`. Both branches require `heat_treat_completed` to be true, both require `disposition` `IN_PROCESS`, and both increment the run's `version` and the lot's `version` by 1 each.

The relief bake is where high-strength fasteners are saved or lost. A `HeatTreatOperator` starts it on a `PLATED` run, supplying `bake_temp_c` and `bake_duration_min`. Four things are checked. The part must actually need a bake: `he_bake_required` must be true, or the request is refused with `ERR_BAKE_NOT_REQUIRED`. The bake must start promptly: `bake_delay_hours` is the elapsed time from `plated_at` to now, expressed in hours — the elapsed seconds divided by three thousand six hundred — rounded half up to three decimal places, and it must be at most 4.0 hours; the bound is inclusive, so a delay of exactly 4.000 hours still passes, while anything longer is refused with `ERR_HE_BAKE_WINDOW_MISSED`. The parameters must be right: `bake_temp_c` must be at least 190 and at most 230 degrees Celsius — both bounds inclusive, so exactly 190 and exactly 230 both pass — and `bake_duration_min` must be at least the part's `he_bake_min_minutes`; either violation is refused with `ERR_HE_BAKE_PARAMS_INVALID`. On success the run moves `PLATED → BAKING`, `bake_started_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `bake_delay_hours` and both bake parameters are recorded, and the run's `version` increments by 1.

Closing the bake has two sibling branches with different preconditions and different effects. For a part that needed the bake, the run must be in `status` `BAKING` and the logical clock must have reached or passed `bake_started_at` plus the committed `bake_duration_min` in minutes; closing early is refused with `ERR_BAKE_DURATION_NOT_ELAPSED`. The run moves `BAKING → BAKE_COMPLETE` and `bake_completed_at` is stamped. For a part whose `he_bake_required` is false, there is no soak to wait for: the run is still in `status` `PLATED` and moves `PLATED → BAKE_COMPLETE` directly, stamping `bake_completed_at` while `bake_delay_hours` stays empty and no bake parameters are recorded at all. `BAKE_COMPLETE` is terminal on both branches. In both branches the lot moves `PLATED → BAKED`, the lot's pre-test risk score is computed, and the run's `version` and the lot's `version` each increment by 1.

That pre-test score, stored as `risk_score_pre_test`, is a weighted picture of process risk assembled *before* any test evidence exists, and it is built from four terms. The **heat term** is 0 when the lot's traceability heat has `cert_status` of `VERIFIED` and 25 otherwise. The **tool term** is the lot's snapshotted `tool_utilization_pct`, capped at 100. The **bake term** is 0 for a part whose `he_bake_required` is false; otherwise it depends on `bake_delay_hours`: 0 when that delay is at most 2.0 hours, and otherwise the excess over 2.0 hours multiplied by 40, capped at 100 — so a bake started 3.5 hours after plating contributes 60. The **decarburization term** is 100 multiplied by the governing run's `total_decarb_depth_mm`, divided by the part's `max_decarb_depth_mm`, rounded half up to two decimal places and then capped at 100. The score is 0.30 multiplied by the heat term, plus 0.20 multiplied by the tool term, plus 0.25 multiplied by the bake term, plus 0.25 multiplied by the decarburization term, with the sum rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once at the end — the individual weighted terms are never rounded on their own. The governing heat treatment run for the decarburization term is the lot's run in `status` `COMPLETED` with `result` `PASS` having the greatest `window_end_at`, breaking ties by earliest `created_at`; the governing plating run is the newest non-superseded one.

For a part whose `plating_spec` is `NONE` there is no plating run at all, so the lot never reaches `BAKED` and the pre-test score is instead computed when the lot test is opened, with the bake term taken as zero. Once computed, `risk_score_pre_test` is frozen: the second scoring pass described in §3.7 never recomputes it.

### 3.7. Lot Sampling, Acceptance and the Achieved Strength Class

Only a `QualityInspector` may open a destructive lot test, and the payload names nothing but the `production_lot_id`. The lot must be admissible: normally its `process_state` must be `BAKED`, but a part whose `plating_spec` is `NONE` may be tested straight from `HEAT_TREATED` or from `SECONDARY_DONE` — the latter covering parts whose thread rolling ran after heat treatment — and a lot already in `TESTING` may be tested again only when every existing test of that lot came back with `result` `REJECT`. Anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. The lot's `disposition` must be `IN_PROCESS` throughout, which means a lot placed on hold by a rejected test must have that hold released before it can be retested. The lot's required secondary operations for its stage must all be `DONE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SECONDARY_OPS_INCOMPLETE`.

The sampling plan is derived before the test row is written, and it begins with `severity`. Severity is `TIGHTENED` when either of two things is true: the lot's `risk_score_pre_test` is at least 40.0, or a rejected test already exists anywhere in the plant on a production lot sharing both the same `part_spec_id` and the same `traceability_heat_id` as this lot. Otherwise severity is `NORMAL`. Note the width of that second condition — it looks at every test in the database, not just this lot's, so one bad lot taints the plan for every sibling lot built from the same part number and the same steel heat.

Severity then selects the plan, and the two variants differ in both the sample size and the tolerance. Under **normal inspection**, `sample_size` is the greater of 8 and the square root of the lot's `available_count` raised upward to the next whole piece, and `acceptance_number` is 1 when that `sample_size` is at least 20 and 0 otherwise — so a single failure is tolerated on any lot holding 400 or more available pieces, and a lot holding fewer than 8 available pieces cannot be sampled at all. Under **tightened inspection**, `sample_size` is the greater of 13 and 1.5 multiplied by the square root of `available_count`, raised upward to the next whole piece, and `acceptance_number` is 0 unconditionally — the very same measured failure count that accepts a lot under normal inspection rejects it under tightened inspection, and the sampling floor of 13 pieces is itself out of reach for a nearly exhausted lot. The lot must hold at least `sample_size` available pieces, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SAMPLE_PIECES`.

The chosen severity is copied onto the test as `severity` and simultaneously frozen onto the lot as `inspection_severity`; later movement in the lot's risk score never re-derives it, and it is always the pass-one score that feeds the decision. The test's `destructive` flag defaults to true and the create payload offers no way to change it, so the sample is consumed: when `destructive` is true, the lot's `destructive_test_count` grows by `sample_size` and its `available_count` shrinks by the same amount, keeping the piece ledger balanced. The acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `performed_by_user_id` — the segregation-of-duties key of §2.11. A new test starts with `result` `PENDING`, with `proof_load_failures`, `tensile_failures`, `min_measured_core_hardness_hv`, `total_failures` and `completed_at` all empty. The lot moves into `TESTING`: from `BAKED → TESTING` in the ordinary case, from `HEAT_TREATED → TESTING` or `SECONDARY_DONE → TESTING` for an unplated part (both of which also compute `risk_score_pre_test` at this moment, with the bake term taken as zero), and `TESTING → TESTING` as a self-loop on a retest. On a retest the severity re-freezes as `TIGHTENED`, because a rejected test now exists on the same part-number-and-heat pair by construction. The lot's `version` increments by 1.

Results are recorded by a `QualityInspector` on a test whose `result` is still `PENDING`, supplying `proof_load_failures`, `tensile_failures` and `min_measured_core_hardness_hv`. Neither failure count may be negative, neither may exceed `sample_size`, and their sum may not exceed `sample_size` either — a violation is refused with `ERR_PIECE_COUNT_INVALID`. `total_failures` is their sum. The verdict is a straight comparison against the frozen plan: `ACCEPT` when `total_failures` is at most `acceptance_number`, and `REJECT` when it is greater. Either way `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and both `ACCEPT` and `REJECT` are terminal on the test.

On `ACCEPT` the test moves `PENDING → ACCEPT`, the lot's `achieved_strength_class` is derived, `risk_score_final` is computed, and the lot moves `TESTING → TESTED`. On `REJECT` the test moves `PENDING → REJECT`, `risk_score_final` is still computed, the lot's `disposition` moves to `HOLD` with `hold_source` of `TEST_REJECT`, and the lot's `process_state` stays at `TESTING` — which is exactly why a certificate drafted after a retest can still be pointing at the first, rejected test. In both cases the test's `version` and the lot's `version` each increment by 1.

`risk_score_final` adds test evidence to the frozen pre-test value: it is `risk_score_pre_test` plus 12.5 points for each failure counted in `total_failures`, capped at 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places.

The achieved strength class is read out of the sample's weakest piece — `min_measured_core_hardness_hv` — against five overlapping hardness bands, each stated in Vickers with both bounds inclusive: `CLASS_8_8` spans 242 to 318, `CLASS_10_9` spans 304 to 361, `CLASS_12_9` spans 366 to 414, `GRADE_5` spans 241 to 302, and `GRADE_8` spans 321 to 352. The lot's own specified class gets first refusal: if the measured hardness falls inside the band of the part's `strength_class`, then `achieved_strength_class` is that class. Only when it does not are the bands scanned in a fixed order — `CLASS_12_9` first, then `CLASS_10_9`, then `GRADE_8`, then `CLASS_8_8`, then `GRADE_5` — and the first band containing the hardness wins. Because the bands overlap, that order is normative, not incidental: a hardness of 330 lands in `GRADE_8` rather than `CLASS_8_8` for a part specified as neither. When no band contains the measured hardness, `achieved_strength_class` is `NONE`. The part specification consulted is always the version pinned on the lot.

### 3.8. Certification and Traceability

A certificate of conformance may be drafted by a `QualityInspector` or a `QualityManager`, citing the `production_lot_id` being certified and the `lot_test_id` whose evidence it rests on, printing a plant-unique `cert_serial`, and carrying the `expected_production_lot_version` of §2.7. The draft is where every remaining gate is enforced at once. The cited test must belong to the same lot as the certificate, or the draft is refused with `ERR_TEST_LOT_MISMATCH`; only then is the test's `result` examined, and a test that is not `ACCEPT` is refused with `ERR_TEST_NOT_ACCEPTED`. The lot's traceability heat must have `cert_status` of `VERIFIED`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_STEEL_HEAT_NOT_VERIFIED` — the counterfeit gate. The lot's `achieved_strength_class` must equal the part's specified `strength_class`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_STRENGTH_CLASS_NOT_ACHIEVED`; a lot that came out one grade low may not be certified to the grade the customer ordered. And for a part whose `qualification_program` is `AEROSPACE_NADCAP` only, the lot's snapshotted `tool_utilization_pct` may be at most 80.00 percent of derated die life — the bound is inclusive, so exactly 80.00 passes — or the draft is refused with `ERR_AEROSPACE_TOOL_UTILIZATION_EXCEEDED`. Non-aerospace parts face no die-utilisation ceiling at draft time.

A fresh draft starts in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision` of 1 and `signed_by_user_id`, `issued_at` and `revoked_reason` all empty. It copies `pinned_part_spec_version` from the lot's own `pinned_part_spec_version`, and sets `certified_strength_class` from the lot's `achieved_strength_class` as it stands at draft time. Drafting increments the lot's `version` by 1.

The certificate's `traceability_chain` is a frozen provenance object projected at draft time (and re-projected on reissue), and the same projection is available as a computed read on the lot itself even before any certificate exists. It gathers, from the lot's steel heat, the `heat_number`, `mill_code`, `mill_cert_serial` and current `cert_status`; from the lot's wire lot, the drawing reduction — exposed in the projection as `wire_lot_reduction_pct` — and the `lubricant_coating`; from the lot's die set, the `tool_code` and its regrind count, exposed as `tool_regrind_count`; from the lot itself, `tool_utilization_pct` and `pinned_part_spec_version`; from the governing heat treatment run, the `austenitize_temp_c`, `core_hardness_hv` and `total_decarb_depth_mm`; and from the governing plating run, the `bake_delay_hours` and `bake_duration_min`. Which runs govern is defined narrowly: the heat treatment run must be in `status` `COMPLETED` with `result` `PASS`, and among those the one with the greatest `window_end_at` wins, breaking ties in favour of the earliest `created_at`; the plating run must be in `status` `BAKE_COMPLETE`, and among those the one with the greatest `bake_started_at` wins, again breaking ties in favour of the earliest `created_at`, with the plating contribution empty altogether when the part's `plating_spec` is `NONE`. Failed runs, running runs and superseded runs are excluded even when they are the most recent rows on the lot. Every value is copied as stored, with no re-rounding at projection time.

Signing is exclusively a `QualityManager` act, and it is where segregation of duties bites: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `performed_by_user_id` of the cited test, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The certificate's `pinned_part_spec_version` must still equal the part specification's live `version`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PART_SPEC_VERSION_DRIFT` — a specification revised since drafting forces a deliberate reissue rather than a silent re-pin. And the lot's `risk_score_final` may be at most 55.00; the bound is inclusive, so exactly 55.00 is certifiable, while a higher score is refused with `ERR_RISK_SCORE_TOO_HIGH`.

Issue has two sibling entry points, and both are fully specified. Signing a certificate in `status` `DRAFT` requires the lot to be in `process_state` `TESTED` with `disposition` `IN_PROCESS`; the certificate moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`, `issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `signed_by_user_id` takes the acting `X-User-ID`, the lot moves `TESTED → CERTIFIED`, and the certificate's `version` and the lot's `version` each increment by 1. Signing a certificate in `status` `REISSUE_PENDING` requires only that its `pinned_part_spec_version` still match the specification's `version`; the certificate moves `REISSUE_PENDING → ISSUED`, `issued_at` is stamped, `signed_by_user_id` takes the acting `X-User-ID`, and the certificate's `version` increments by 1.

Withdrawal is also a `QualityManager` act. Revoking a certificate in `status` `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `REISSUE_PENDING` with a supplied `reason` moves it to `REVOKED` and records that reason as `revoked_reason`. The revocation cascades: every pack unit printed with that certificate whose `status` is `SEALED` or `SHIPPED` becomes `RECALLED`, in ascending `created_at` order, with its `recall_reason` copied from the certificate's `revoked_reason`. When the revocation came from the operator endpoint and the lot's `disposition` was `IN_PROCESS`, the lot additionally moves to `disposition` `HOLD` with `hold_source` of `QUALITY`. The certificate's `version`, the lot's `version` and the `version` of every recalled pack unit each increment by 1.

A revoked certificate can be reopened. A `QualityManager` reissues it by supplying a new, plant-unique `cert_serial`; the certificate must be in `status` `REVOKED` and the lot's `disposition` must be `IN_PROCESS`, so a lot still on hold reports `ERR_LOT_ON_HOLD` and a quarantined lot reports `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. Reissue is also subject to segregation of duties against the cited test's `performed_by_user_id`. On success the new serial replaces the old one, `revision` increments by 1, the `status` moves `REVOKED → REISSUE_PENDING`, `signed_by_user_id` is cleared, the `traceability_chain` is re-projected from the lot's current provenance, and the certificate's `version` increments by 1 — closing the cycle `ISSUED → REVOKED → REISSUE_PENDING → ISSUED`.

### 3.9. Packaging, Labelling and Shipment

Packing belongs to the `ShippingClerk`, who opens a pack unit against a `production_lot_id` and a `certification_document_id`, printing a plant-unique `label_serial`. The named certificate must belong to the same production lot as the pack, or the request is refused with `ERR_CERT_LOT_MISMATCH`. The pack's `pack_mode` is copied from the part specification's `packaging_mode` and is therefore either `BY_COUNT` or `BY_WEIGHT`, and which one it is decides which measurement the payload must carry: a `BY_COUNT` pack supplies `actual_count`, the pieces physically counted into the box, which may not be negative and leaves `net_weight_kg` empty; a `BY_WEIGHT` pack supplies `net_weight_kg`, the weighed net mass, which must be strictly positive and leaves `actual_count` empty. The payload may also carry `label_fields_complete`, the operator's confirmation that the printed label actually carries the certificate serial, the lot code and the declared count; it defaults to false, and a pack opened without that confirmation can never ship.

From those inputs the server derives two numbers. `count_tolerance_pieces` is the part's `nominal_pack_count` multiplied by its `count_tolerance_pct`, truncated downward to a whole number of pieces — so a nominal pack of 500 at a `count_tolerance_pct` of 0.02 tolerates 10 pieces of deviation, and a nominal pack of 40 at the same tolerance tolerates none at all, because 0.8 of a piece truncates downward to 0. `declared_count`, the number printed on the label, is `actual_count` verbatim for a `BY_COUNT` pack, and for a `BY_WEIGHT` pack it is the pack's own `net_weight_kg` multiplied by the part's `pieces_per_kg`, rounded half up to a whole piece. That rounding happens per pack and never on a summed weight: a lot's declared total is the sum of the already-rounded per-pack values, so the plant accepts the small accumulation of per-pack rounding rather than re-deriving a total from aggregate mass. A new pack starts in `status` `OPEN` with `sealed_at`, `shipped_at` and `recall_reason` all empty.

Sealing moves pieces out of the lot's available inventory. The pack must be `OPEN`, the lot must be in `process_state` `CERTIFIED` or already `PACKED` with `disposition` `IN_PROCESS`, the pack's `declared_count` must sit within `count_tolerance_pieces` of the part's `nominal_pack_count` in either direction — a deviation larger than the tolerance in absolute terms is refused with `ERR_PACK_COUNT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` — and `declared_count` may not exceed the lot's `available_count`, or the seal is refused with `ERR_PIECE_COUNT_INVALID`. On success the pack moves `OPEN → SEALED`, `sealed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the lot's `packed_count` grows by `declared_count` while its `available_count` shrinks by the same amount, the lot moves `CERTIFIED → PACKED`, and the pack's `version` and the lot's `version` each increment by 1.

Reopening a sealed pack compensates the seal exactly. The pack must be `SEALED`; it moves `SEALED → OPEN`, `sealed_at` is cleared, and a `reason` is supplied for the record. The lot's `packed_count` shrinks by `declared_count` and its `available_count` grows by the same amount, leaving the conserved piece ledger of §2.8 exactly as it was before the seal. The lot's `process_state` then moves `PACKED → CERTIFIED` only when no other pack unit of that lot remains in `status` `SEALED`, `SHIPPED` or `RECALLED`; otherwise the lot stays `PACKED`. Both the pack's `version` and the lot's `version` increment by 1.

Shipping a `SEALED` pack requires the label and the paperwork to line up. `label_fields_complete` must be true, or the shipment is refused with `ERR_PACK_LABEL_INCOMPLETE`. The pack's certificate must be in `status` `ISSUED`, or the shipment is refused with `ERR_CERT_NOT_ISSUED`. And that certificate must belong to the same production lot as the pack, or the shipment is refused with `ERR_CERT_LOT_MISMATCH`. On success the pack moves `SEALED → SHIPPED` and `shipped_at` is stamped from the logical clock; the lot moves `PACKED → SHIPPED` only once every pack unit of the lot is in `status` `SHIPPED` and at least one such pack exists. `SHIPPED` is terminal on the lot's process dimension. The pack's `version` and the lot's `version` each increment by 1.

A pack's last possible move is out of the plant's hands. When a certificate is revoked — whether by a `QualityManager` directly or by link four of the counterfeit chain — every pack unit printed with it in `status` `SEALED` or `SHIPPED` moves to `RECALLED`, recording the withdrawal's reason as `recall_reason` and incrementing its own `version` by 1. `RECALLED` is terminal. Recalled pieces do not come back into inventory: the lot's `packed_count` is left exactly as it was, because those pieces have left the lot for good.
