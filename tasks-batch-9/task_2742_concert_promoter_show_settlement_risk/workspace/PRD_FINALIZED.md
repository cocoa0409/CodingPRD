# Concert Show Settlement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A national concert promoter lives and dies by the gap between what it promised an act and what the box office actually delivered. Every date on the calendar begins as an offer — a guarantee wired against a projection, sometimes sweetened with a percentage of the upside — and ends, weeks later, as a settlement book reconciled on a folding table in a loading dock at one in the morning. Between those two moments the promoter must build priced inventory inside a building it usually does not own, budget production, marketing, staffing and hospitality, paper the house with comps without giving away the margin, capture vendor invoices with enough documentation to survive an audit, and then compute — to the cent, under a deal structure agreed months earlier — exactly what the artist is owed. The finance organisation currently does this in spreadsheets that disagree with each other, and the risk organisation discovers that a market has over-committed its guarantee capacity only after the cheques have gone out.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. It is a ledger-reconciliation system with a negotiation front end: offers are countered and re-submitted under a round cap, budget variances escalate to different approvers depending on how badly they blew through, invoices must be verified by one person and approved by another, and settlements can be reopened by a risk officer who did not calculate them. Above the individual show sits a portfolio view — each market carries a hard ceiling on the guarantees it may have outstanding at any moment, and the service re-bands that market's risk posture on every write that moves committed money, projected margin or realised sales. Shows are pulled and rescheduled; refunds are owed at face value while the promoter's deductions become sunk loss; insurance claims recover part of the damage inside a filing window.

The service is a JSON API over HTTP. Its correctness bar is arithmetic: two different deal structures over identical sales data must produce two different, exactly reproducible artist payments, and the same show valued forward (against its full sellable allocation at today's prices) must produce a different number than the same show valued backward (against the tickets that actually sold, at the prices they sold for). Where a figure can be derived two plausible ways, this document names the normative one.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including the list and read endpoints and the two test-driving endpoints — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check precedes everything else, including request-body parsing and resource lookup.

Exactly five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `TalentBuyer`, `ArtistRep`, `VenueOperator`, `TourAccountant`, and `RiskOfficer`. If `X-User-Role` holds any other string, or holds one of the five but that role is not listed as authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). The authorised role list for each endpoint appears in §4.

A `TalentBuyer` is the promoter-side buyer: it books dates, writes and submits offers, builds and prices ticket inventory, papers the house, and pulls or reschedules shows. A `TourAccountant` owns the budget, the vendor-invoice ledger and the settlement book; it may not commit a guarantee and may not reopen a settlement it calculated. A `RiskOfficer` owns portfolio risk: it opens markets and sets their exposure ceilings, grants show-scoped exposure overrides, decides escalated budget variances, reopens settlements, and runs the insurance-claim path; it may not verify an invoice and may not calculate or pay a settlement. An `ArtistRep` is the artist-side representative who counters, accepts, signs and disputes. A `VenueOperator` is the venue-side operator who submits and disputes vendor invoices and records walk-up sales.

Three of the five roles carry an opaque identity and two are entity-backed, and the difference is load-bearing:

- When `X-User-Role` is `TalentBuyer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for instance as `released_by_user_id` on a `TicketHold` released or converted by that buyer — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `TourAccountant`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `verified_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a `VendorInvoice`, as `variance_decided_by_user_id` on a `BudgetLine`, and as `calculated_by_user_id` on a `Settlement`, but it is never compared to an entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `RiskOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `exposure_override_by_user_id` on a `Show`, as `variance_decided_by_user_id` on a `BudgetLine`, and as `filed_by_user_id` on a `CancellationEvent`, but it is never compared to an entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ArtistRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Artist`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Artist` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, on any offer, settlement or hold action the acting rep's `X-User-ID` must equal the `artist_id` of the show in question. It is also recorded as `accepted_by_user_id` on an accepted `Offer` and as `signed_by_user_id` on a signed `Settlement`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `VenueOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Venue`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Venue` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the acting operator's `X-User-ID` must equal the `venue_id` of the show being invoiced, disputed or held against.

When an entity-backed role acts on a resource it does not own, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards offer acceptance, offer counters, the artist's settlement response, hold creation, hold release, vendor-invoice creation and vendor-invoice dispute.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first — unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 states otherwise. List responses set `response_type` to `"array"`, place the page of resources in `data`, and populate four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page). Single-resource endpoints set `response_type` to `"object"` and omit the four pagination fields from `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client; a client-supplied `id` in a creation body is ignored.

Every persisted resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. All eleven resource types in this system — `Market`, `Artist`, `Venue`, `Show`, `Offer`, `BudgetLine`, `VendorInvoice`, `TicketTier`, `TicketHold`, `Settlement` and `CancellationEvent` — additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. There is no resource type in this system that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

`version` starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that resource, and `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock on each mutation. A "mutation" here includes mutations the caller did not directly request: when a cascade recomputes a parent's derived money fields, that parent's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed as well. §2.10 describes the cascade discipline in detail, and each workflow narrative in §3 names the specific rows whose `version` moves.

The two test-driving endpoints in the `/_` namespace are the only endpoints that return no resource. They respond with `response_type` set to `"object"` and an empty `data` object.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this service is entirely logical. On startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance. An operator moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting endpoint; the clock then reads that instant until it is set again. Every deadline in this specification — offer expiry, ticket-hold expiry, the announce gate on going on sale, the event-date gate on recording a performance, the insurance-claim filing window, and the `paid_at` and `occurred_at` stamps — is measured against this clock and no other.

Setting the clock moves nothing else. Rows whose deadlines have passed do not change state merely because the clock advanced past them; expiries are applied only when the housekeeping endpoint is run, and that endpoint is idempotent — running it twice at the same clock instant changes nothing the second time.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer count of cents. Field names ending in `_cents` are integers; a fractional or negative value supplied where a non-negative amount is expected is a malformed request. Several computed monetary fields are nonetheless **signed**: `variance_cents`, `payment_due_cents`, `projected_promoter_net_cents` and `promoter_net_cents` may all legitimately be negative, and a loss must be reported as a negative number rather than floored at zero.

Percentages are decimal numbers with two decimal places. Where this document says a percentage is applied to an amount, the operation is: multiply the amount by the percentage, divide by 100, and round the result **half up** to a whole cent — a value of exactly one-half cent rounds away from zero, upward in magnitude. Two derived percentages, `exposure_utilization_pct` on a market and `variance_pct` on a budget line, are themselves rounded half up to two decimal places, and every threshold comparison in this document reads the **rounded** value, never the raw ratio. One derived figure, `performance_index`, is rounded half up to a whole percent.

Where a formula sums several rounded terms, this document states explicitly whether the rounding happens once per term before summation or once on the total; the two differ by cents and the difference is observable. Where a formula runs a sequence of deductions, this document states the order and the base each deduction is computed against; deductions are never re-derived from the net.

### 2.7. Stale-View Rejection on Show-Scoped Writes

Two write endpoints reach into a show's state in a way no state precondition can guard, because the operation itself is always legitimate on its merits. Both therefore require the client to prove that its view of the parent `Show` is current, by supplying `expected_show_version` — a non-optional integer field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of that show.

A `POST /offers` request must carry `expected_show_version` matching the target show's current `version`. Offer creation legitimately supersedes every other `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED` offer on the same show, so a writer holding a stale view of the show would silently invalidate negotiation rounds it never saw. If the supplied value does not equal the show's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SHOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A `POST /ticket-tiers/{ticket_tier_id}/price-adjustments` request must likewise carry `expected_show_version` matching the current `version` of the show that owns the tier. A price revision is always permitted on an `ON_SALE` or `PAUSED` tier, so no state check can catch a writer whose view of the show predates another revision, a hold release or a budget change — yet the revision rewrites the show's `projected_adjusted_gross_cents` and therefore the market's risk posture. If the supplied value does not equal the show's current `version`, the revision is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SHOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. Endpoints not named above must not require one, and must not reject a request because a version field was omitted.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Four paired steps in this system must be performed by two different users. In each pair, the second step compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the identifier the first step recorded, and rejects a match with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403):

- Approving a vendor invoice requires an `X-User-ID` different from the invoice's `verified_by_user_id`.
- Paying a settlement requires an `X-User-ID` different from the settlement's `signed_by_user_id`.
- Reopening a settlement requires an `X-User-ID` different from the settlement's `calculated_by_user_id`.
- Settling or denying an insurance claim requires an `X-User-ID` different from the event's `filed_by_user_id`.

These four checks are independent of role authorisation. A user may hold a role that authorises the second step and still be refused because it performed the first.

### 2.9. Validation Order

Where several rules could reject the same call, they are evaluated in a fixed order and the **first** violated rule determines the response. No later rule is evaluated once one has fired, and no side effect of the operation runs. The global order is:

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role recognition and endpoint authorisation — `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN`.
3. Request-body well-formedness — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every required field must be present, every enum-valued field must hold a declared member, every `_cents` field must be an integer that is not negative, every `_pct` field must be a number between 0 and 100 inclusive, every `_at` field must be a valid ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, and every quantity field must be an integer of at least 1. This check applies to all endpoints that accept a body.
4. Existence of every referenced resource, in the order market, artist, venue, show, offer, budget line, vendor invoice, ticket tier, ticket hold, settlement, cancellation event — each with its own error code, listed in §2.11.
5. Resource ownership for entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
6. Stale-view rejection — `ERR_STALE_SHOW_VERSION`.
7. State legality — `ERR_INVALID_STATE` when the target sits in a non-terminal state from which the requested trigger has no declared transition, including sub-resource guards such as a show's ticketing state or a budget line's own lifecycle state.
8. Terminality — `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` when the target already sits in one of its own terminal states. Because a target already in a terminal state is reported here and a target in a non-terminal but wrong state is reported by the preceding rule, the two state errors never compete for the same call.
9. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
10. Everything else — the domain-specific checks, whose relative order among themselves is stated in the §3 subsection that describes each workflow.

The domain-specific checks that follow, in ascending order of evaluation, are: invoice-to-budget-line coherence (`ERR_BUDGET_LINE_SHOW_MISMATCH`), show window coherence (`ERR_INVALID_SHOW_WINDOW`), event date in the future (`ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_PAST`), venue occupancy overlap (`ERR_VENUE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), artist routing (`ERR_ARTIST_ROUTING_CONFLICT`), deal-term coherence (`ERR_DEAL_TERMS_INCOHERENT`), negotiation round cap (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`), offer expiry (`ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`), the announce gate (`ERR_ANNOUNCE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`), the event-date gate (`ERR_SHOW_NOT_YET_OCCURRED`), the required-children check (`ERR_NO_TICKET_TIERS`), the comp allowance (`ERR_COMP_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`), tier inventory sufficiency (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TIER_INVENTORY`), variance approval authority (`ERR_VARIANCE_APPROVAL_AUTHORITY`), variance cap validity (`ERR_VARIANCE_CAP_INVALID`), unresolved invoices (`ERR_UNRESOLVED_INVOICES`), unresolved variances (`ERR_UNRESOLVED_VARIANCE`), replacement-show validity (`ERR_REPLACEMENT_SHOW_INVALID`), the claim window (`ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`), market posture (`ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_FROZEN`), market exposure (`ERR_MARKET_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED`), the cash payment cap (`ERR_CASH_PAYMENT_LIMIT`), exposure-override validity (`ERR_EXPOSURE_OVERRIDE_INVALID`), venue capacity (`ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), tier-name uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_NAME`), and the one-open-settlement rule (`ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_OPEN`).

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

This system has no eventual consistency. When a write changes a figure that another figure is derived from, every dependent figure is recomputed **within the same request**, and the response reflects the fully settled state. Reads never trigger recomputation of persisted values, with one deliberate exception noted in §3.3.

Dependency runs in one direction, from the smallest unit of inventory or expense up to the portfolio:

- A ticket sale, hold, release, conversion, expiry or price revision changes a `TicketTier`'s counters, which changes its `Show`'s aggregate counters, which changes that show's adjusted and projected gross, which changes its market's at-risk exposure and risk posture.
- An approved vendor invoice changes a `BudgetLine`'s actual and recognised expense, which changes that line's variance state, which changes its `Show`'s expense totals, budget posture and breakeven, which changes its market's at-risk exposure and risk posture.
- An accepted offer changes a `Show`'s committed guarantee, which changes its market's committed exposure, utilisation and posture.

Each hop that alters a row increments that row's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`. A single call therefore commonly bumps four `version` counters — the acted-on row, a tier or line, the show, and the market — and each §3 narrative states which.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Existence

Every path variable is resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. The variables and their codes are: `market_id` → `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, `artist_id` → `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_FOUND`, `venue_id` → `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `show_id` → `ERR_SHOW_NOT_FOUND`, `offer_id` → `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `budget_line_id` → `ERR_BUDGET_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `vendor_invoice_id` → `ERR_VENDOR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ticket_tier_id` → `ERR_TICKET_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ticket_hold_id` → `ERR_TICKET_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `settlement_id` → `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `cancellation_event_id` → `ERR_CANCELLATION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`. All eleven are HTTP 404.

The same codes apply to identifiers supplied in a request body or as a list filter rather than in the path. A body or filter naming a market that does not exist yields `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`; one naming a missing artist yields `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_FOUND`; one naming a missing venue yields `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`. A missing show — whether named as `show_id`, as `rescheduled_from_show_id` at show creation, or as `replacement_show_id` at reschedule time — yields `ERR_SHOW_NOT_FOUND`. A missing `budget_line_id` on an invoice body or filter yields `ERR_BUDGET_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing `ticket_tier_id` on a hold body or filter yields `ERR_TICKET_TIER_NOT_FOUND`.

Resources in this system are flat: each is its own top-level collection and links to its neighbours by identifier fields in the request body rather than by nesting in the URL. The action segments that do appear beneath a resource — accept, submit, on-sale, calculate, pay and their siblings — are state-transition triggers on that one resource, not nested collections.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Portfolio Foundations and Booking a Date

A `RiskOfficer` opens the books on a geography by creating a **market** — a reporting and risk container such as "Pacific Northwest". The creation payload supplies a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `region_code` of between 2 and 8 uppercase characters used on reports, and `guarantee_exposure_limit_cents`, an integer of at least 1 that is the hard ceiling on the artist guarantees this market may carry outstanding at any one moment. A newly opened market starts empty in every derived respect: `committed_guarantee_cents` 0, `at_risk_guarantee_cents` 0, `exposure_utilization_pct` 0, `risk_posture` `OPEN`, `settled_show_count` 0, and `performance_index` 0. Markets can be listed and narrowed by `risk_posture`.

Acts are registered by a `TalentBuyer`. An **artist** record carries a `name` and an `agency` of record, each 1 to 120 characters; a commercial `tier` that is one of `CLUB`, `THEATER`, `ARENA` or `HEADLINE` and drives routing rules and reporting; a `historical_sellthrough_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals, used when projecting; and an optional `withholding_pct` between 0.00 and 40.00 with two decimals, the statutory rate deducted from the act's earnings at settlement — omit it and it defaults to 0. The act's `confirmed_show_count` starts at 0 and tracks how many of its shows currently sit in the `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SALE` or `PERFORMED` window. Artists can be listed and narrowed by `tier`.

Buildings are registered by a `TalentBuyer` too. A **venue** names the market it reports into by `market_id`, carries a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `gross_capacity` of at least 1 seat, a `facility_fee_cents_per_ticket` of 0 or more retained by the building on each **paid** ticket (comps never attract it), and a `ticket_tax_pct` between 0.00 and 25.00 with two decimals — the amusement or ticket tax levied on gross receipts. Venues can be listed and narrowed by `market_id`.

Booking a date creates a **show**, again by a `TalentBuyer`. The payload names the act by `artist_id` and the building by `venue_id`, and the server copies the venue's market into the show's own `market_id` at creation and never re-derives it afterwards — moving a venue between markets later does not move its already-booked shows. Four instants describe the date: `event_at` is the show start, `load_in_at` and `load_out_at` bound the venue occupancy interval (inclusive of `load_in_at`, exclusive of `load_out_at`), and `announce_at` is the earliest instant tickets may go on sale. A replacement date created for a postponed original additionally names the original in `rescheduled_from_show_id`; on every other show that field is null.

The payload also fixes how the building is paid, through `venue_arrangement`. Under `RENTAL` the promoter bears every expense and the building takes a flat fee: `rental_fee_cents` is required and must be greater than 0, while `copromote_venue_share_pct`, `venue_expense_pct_production`, `venue_expense_pct_marketing`, `venue_expense_pct_staffing` and `venue_expense_pct_hospitality` must all be 0. Under `CO_PROMOTE` the building reimburses declared shares of the promoter's expense and takes a percentage of promoter net: `copromote_venue_share_pct` is required, must be greater than 0, and lies between 0.00 and 50.00 with two decimals, while `rental_fee_cents` must be 0 and each of the four category percentages lies between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals, each naming the fraction of every budget line in that category the building reimburses. A payload that contradicts its own `venue_arrangement` is malformed.

Three date checks then run, in this order, and the first one violated determines the answer. First, window coherence: `load_in_at` must be strictly earlier than `event_at`, `event_at` must be strictly earlier than `load_out_at`, and `announce_at` must be no later than `event_at`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SHOW_WINDOW`. Second, `event_at` must be strictly after the current logical clock — booking into the past yields `ERR_EVENT_DATE_IN_PAST`. Third, venue occupancy: the new show's half-open interval from `load_in_at` up to but not including `load_out_at` must not overlap the interval of any other show at the same venue whose `show_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED` or `RESCHEDULED`; an overlap yields `ERR_VENUE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Exact adjacency is deliberately permitted — one show's `load_out_at` may equal the next show's `load_in_at`, because the trucks pass in the alley.

A newly booked show starts in `show_status` `PLANNED` and `sales_status` `PRE_SALE`, commits no guarantee, and contributes nothing to its market's exposure. Its two status dimensions then advance independently. The lifecycle dimension `show_status` moves through `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SALE`, `PERFORMED` and terminally `SETTLED`, or leaves the happy path for terminal `CANCELLED`, or for `POSTPONED` and then terminal `RESCHEDULED`. The ticketing dimension `sales_status` moves through `PRE_SALE`, `SELLING`, and cycles between `SELLING` and `PAUSED` as often as the promoter likes, ending terminally at `CLOSED`. Pausing or resuming sales never touches `show_status`: a paused show is still `ON_SALE` and still settles normally. Shows may be listed and narrowed by any of `market_id`, `artist_id`, `venue_id`, `rescheduled_from_show_id`, `show_status` and `sales_status`.

### 3.2. Offer Construction and the Negotiation Cycle

An **offer** is the promoter's written proposal against a `PLANNED` show, authored by a `TalentBuyer`. It names its show by `show_id` and carries the deal terms: a `deal_structure` that is one of `FLAT_GUARANTEE`, `VERSUS` or `GUARANTEE_PLUS_BACKEND`; a `guarantee_cents` of at least 1, the floor the promoter owes regardless of what the box office does; a `deposit_cents` of 0 or more, wired at confirmation and credited back at settlement; and an `expense_cap_cents` of at least 1, the ceiling on artist-deductible show expenses that will be recognised when the books close. Two conditional terms complete the structure: `versus_pct`, the artist's percentage of net-after-expenses, and `backend_split_artist_pct`, the artist's percentage of gross above breakeven; each lies between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals and each defaults to 0.

Deal terms must be coherent with the structure chosen, and this check applies both when the offer is written and when the artist side counters it. `FLAT_GUARANTEE` forbids both `versus_pct` and `backend_split_artist_pct`. `VERSUS` requires `versus_pct` to be present and greater than 0 and forbids `backend_split_artist_pct`. `GUARANTEE_PLUS_BACKEND` requires `backend_split_artist_pct` to be present and greater than 0 and forbids `versus_pct`. In every structure `deposit_cents` may not exceed `guarantee_cents`, and `expense_cap_cents`, when supplied, must be greater than 0. Any of these violations yields `ERR_DEAL_TERMS_INCOHERENT` (HTTP 422).

Writing an offer is also the moment older paper goes stale, which is why the create body requires `expected_show_version` as described in §2.7. On success the new offer is created in status `DRAFT` with `round_index` 1, and every **other** offer on the same show whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. A sibling still in `DRAFT` is deliberately left alone — it was never put in front of the agent, so there is nothing to supersede. The show's `version` increments by 1 and each superseded offer's `version` increments by 1.

Sending the paper to the artist side moves the offer `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and may only be done while the show is still `PLANNED`. The submission stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock and sets `expires_at` to exactly 72 hours — three days — after that instant. From `SUBMITTED` the artist side may counter, which moves the offer `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED`. A counter is authored by an `ArtistRep` whose `X-User-ID` equals the show's `artist_id`, requires the show to still be `PLANNED`, and records what the artist side asked for: `counter_guarantee_cents` is required, `counter_versus_pct` and `counter_backend_split_artist_pct` are optional, and a required `counter_note` carries free text. Countering also clears `expires_at` to null — the clock stops while the ball is in the promoter's court.

The loop closes when the promoter answers a counter: re-submitting moves the offer `COUNTERED → SUBMITTED` again. The re-submission may carry revised `guarantee_cents`, `versus_pct` and `backend_split_artist_pct`, and any of those three that is supplied replaces the current term while any omitted field keeps the value it already holds. `round_index` increments by 1, `submitted_at` is re-stamped from the logical clock and `expires_at` is set 72 hours later, and the `counter_*` fields are left untouched as the historical record of the round just answered. Negotiation is capped at three rounds: neither a counter nor a re-submission may proceed once `round_index` has reached 3, and an attempt to go a fourth round is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). Separately, a counter or an acceptance attempted at or after the offer's `expires_at` is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409); the current logical clock must be strictly earlier than `expires_at`.

Two exits close an offer without a deal. The promoter may withdraw it from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED`, supplying a `reason`, which moves it to `WITHDRAWN`. And housekeeping expires it: any offer sitting in `SUBMITTED` whose `expires_at` is non-null and has been reached by the logical clock moves `SUBMITTED → EXPIRED`, its `version` increments, and the show remains `PLANNED`. `ACCEPTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal — an offer in any of them is never moved again, and is untouched even by the cancellation cascade that withdraws open paper. Offers may be listed and narrowed by `show_id`, by `status` and by `deal_structure`.

### 3.3. Acceptance, the Guarantee Exposure Book and Portfolio Risk

Acceptance is the money-committing moment of the whole system. An `ArtistRep` whose `X-User-ID` equals the show's `artist_id` accepts a `SUBMITTED` offer on a show that is still `PLANNED`; the offer moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and, in the same request, the show moves `PLANNED → CONFIRMED`. Because accepting books real money against a market's capacity, four domain checks guard it, and they are evaluated in this order so that the most specific business objection surfaces first.

The first is **artist routing**. For every other show of the same act whose `show_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SALE`, `PERFORMED` or `POSTPONED`, the absolute difference between that show's `event_at` and this show's `event_at` must be at least 36 hours — a day and a half of rest and travel. A gap of exactly 36 hours is permitted; anything shorter yields `ERR_ARTIST_ROUTING_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Note carefully that this routing set **includes** `POSTPONED`: the act still holds that night while a replacement date is being found, so a postponed show keeps blocking the calendar even though, as described below, it has already left the market's exposure book. These two sets differ by exactly that one state and must be evaluated as two separate predicates.

The second check is offer expiry, described in §3.2. The third is **market posture**: if the market's `risk_posture` is `FROZEN`, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). The posture read here is the market's posture **before** this acceptance is applied. The fourth is **market exposure**: the market's current `committed_guarantee_cents` plus this offer's `guarantee_cents` must not exceed the applicable ceiling, and equality with the ceiling passes. The applicable ceiling is the show's own `exposure_override_limit_cents` when that field is non-null, and the market's `guarantee_exposure_limit_cents` otherwise. Exceeding it yields `ERR_MARKET_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

Once the checks pass, the write fans out. The offer's `accepted_by_user_id` records the accepting rep's `X-User-ID`. Every **other** offer on the show that sits in `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. The show's `committed_guarantee_cents` is set to the accepted offer's `guarantee_cents` — this field is defined as the guarantee of the single offer on the show whose status is `ACCEPTED`, and 0 when no such offer exists. That change cascades: the show's `breakeven_cents` and `projected_promoter_net_cents` are recomputed; the show, now inside the exposure set, causes its market's `committed_guarantee_cents` and `at_risk_guarantee_cents` to be recomputed; and that in turn causes the market's `exposure_utilization_pct` to be recomputed and its `risk_posture` to be re-banded. The act's `confirmed_show_count` increases by 1. The `version` of the offer, the show, the artist and the market each increment by 1.

The market's exposure figures are defined over a precise show set. **In scope** are the market's shows whose `show_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SALE` or `PERFORMED`. `PLANNED` shows contribute nothing, because no guarantee is committed until an offer is accepted, and `SETTLED`, `CANCELLED`, `POSTPONED` and `RESCHEDULED` shows are all released from the book — the guarantee comes off the moment the date is pulled or the cheque clears. Over that set, `committed_guarantee_cents` is the sum of each in-scope show's `committed_guarantee_cents`. `at_risk_guarantee_cents` is the sum, over the same in-scope set, of the amount by which each show's `breakeven_cents` exceeds its `adjusted_gross_cents` — taking zero for any show whose adjusted gross already covers its breakeven — and then reduced by the total `insurance_recovery_cents` of every `CLAIM_SETTLED` cancellation event belonging to this market's shows, with the final result floored at 0 so it never goes negative. Because a show's `breakeven_cents` folds in recognised deductible expense, approving a budget variance moves this figure.

`exposure_utilization_pct` is the market's `committed_guarantee_cents` multiplied by 100, divided by its `guarantee_exposure_limit_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `risk_posture` is then banded from that **rounded** percentage: `OPEN` when it is at most 70, `WATCH` when it is greater than 70 and at most 90, `RESTRICTED` when it is greater than 90 and strictly below 100, and `FROZEN` at 100 or above. The boundaries are inclusive on the low side of each band, so a market sitting at exactly 70.00 is still `OPEN` and one at exactly 90.00 is still `WATCH`. Committing exactly to the limit lands at 100.00 and freezes the market — and because the exposure check permits equality, that acceptance itself still succeeds; it is the *next* acceptance that fails, and it fails on posture rather than on exposure.

Committed exposure is a strictly conserved quantity. Accepting an offer adds exactly the offer's `guarantee_cents` to the market's committed total, and each compensating operation — opening a cancellation event of either kind, or paying the settlement — subtracts exactly the same amount. A confirm-then-cancel round trip therefore returns `committed_guarantee_cents`, `exposure_utilization_pct` and `risk_posture` to precisely their pre-acceptance values with no drift.

Occasionally a deal is worth breaching the market's ceiling for. A `RiskOfficer` may grant a show-scoped ceiling that replaces the market limit for **that one show's** guarantee check only, supplying `exposure_override_limit_cents` and a written `exposure_override_justification`. The override may not exceed twice the market's own `guarantee_exposure_limit_cents`, and the justification may not be empty; either violation yields `ERR_EXPOSURE_OVERRIDE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). On success the show records `exposure_override_limit_cents`, `exposure_override_justification` and `exposure_override_by_user_id` (the granting officer's opaque `X-User-ID`), and the show's `version` increments by 1. The override never changes the market's own `guarantee_exposure_limit_cents`, its `exposure_utilization_pct` or its posture bands — it only widens the ceiling that one acceptance is measured against.

Alongside the plain market read, the service offers a **portfolio risk** read: a computed read that recomputes the market's exposure figures and its performance index for a requested valuation and returns the market carrying those figures. It takes an optional `valuation` query parameter whose value is `TRAILING_SETTLED` or `MARK_TO_MARKET`, defaulting to `TRAILING_SETTLED`. This read is the one deliberate exception to §2.10's rule that reads never recompute — and even here, a `MARK_TO_MARKET` valuation is returned in the response body only and never overwrites the stored `Market.performance_index`, which is always the `TRAILING_SETTLED` figure.

The `performance_index` is a realisation ratio expressed as a whole percent: how much of the projected promoter margin the market has actually delivered. Under the **`TRAILING_SETTLED`** valuation, the numerator is the sum of `promoter_net_cents` over the settlements of the market's shows whose `show_status` is `SETTLED`, and the denominator is the sum of `projected_promoter_net_cents` over those same settled shows. Backward-looking by design: a market with no settled show reports 0. Under the **`MARK_TO_MARKET`** valuation, the settled shows contribute exactly as before, and every **live** show is additionally marked in. A live show is one whose `show_status` is `ON_SALE` or `PERFORMED` and which has no `PAID` settlement. Each live show's contribution to the numerator is its `projected_promoter_net_cents` multiplied by its `paid_tickets_sold`, divided by the total `allocation_qty` across that show's tiers, rounded half up to whole cents — each show's mark is rounded individually **before** the marks are summed. The denominator becomes the sum of `projected_promoter_net_cents` over the settled shows and the live shows together. In both valuations the tail is the same: if the denominator is zero or negative, the index is 0; otherwise it is the numerator multiplied by 100, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to a whole percent. Shows that are `CANCELLED`, `RESCHEDULED`, `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` appear in neither valuation. A market also carries `settled_show_count`, the count of its shows whose settlement has reached `PAID`.

### 3.4. Building, Opening and Pricing the House

Inventory is expressed as **ticket tiers**. A `TalentBuyer` allocates a tier onto a show by naming the show in `show_id` and supplying a `tier_name` of 1 to 40 characters (GA, PIT, BALCONY and the like), a `price_cents` face price of at least 1, and an `allocation_qty` of at least 1 seat — the conserved quantity for that tier. Two checks guard the create, in this order: the sum of `allocation_qty` across the show's tiers, counting the tier being created, may not exceed the venue's `gross_capacity`, or the create is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); and `tier_name` must be unique among that show's tiers, or the create is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_NAME` (HTTP 409).

A new tier is born in status `DRAFT` with `sold_qty` 0, `gross_sold_cents` 0, `comp_qty` 0, `held_qty` 0, `available_qty` equal to its `allocation_qty`, `price_revision_count` 0 and `last_price_cents` null. Creating it recomputes the show's `projected_adjusted_gross_cents` and `projected_promoter_net_cents`, then recomputes the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents` and re-bands its `risk_posture`; the show's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1. Tiers may be listed and narrowed by `show_id` and by `status`, whose members are `DRAFT`, `ON_SALE`, `PAUSED` and terminally `CLOSED`.

Going on sale is a single call by a `TalentBuyer` that advances **both** of a show's status dimensions at once: `show_status` moves `CONFIRMED → ON_SALE` and, in the same request, `sales_status` moves `PRE_SALE → SELLING`. Two gates apply, evaluated in this order. First, the current logical clock must have reached the show's `announce_at`; an earlier attempt yields `ERR_ANNOUNCE_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). Second, the show must have at least one ticket tier; an empty tier set yields `ERR_NO_TICKET_TIERS` (HTTP 422). The total `allocation_qty` across the show's tiers must also be within the venue's `gross_capacity`. On success, every tier of the show sitting in `DRAFT` cascades `DRAFT → ON_SALE` and has its `available_qty` re-derived as its `allocation_qty` less its `sold_qty`, its `comp_qty` and its `held_qty`; the show's `version` and each touched tier's `version` each increment by 1.

Sales can be halted without disturbing the deal. Pausing — available to a `TalentBuyer` or a `RiskOfficer`, and requiring a `reason` — moves `sales_status` `SELLING → PAUSED` and cascades every tier in `ON_SALE` to `PAUSED`, while leaving `show_status` completely untouched at `ON_SALE`. Resuming, available to a `TalentBuyer`, moves `sales_status` `PAUSED → SELLING` provided `show_status` is `ON_SALE`, and cascades every `PAUSED` tier back to `ON_SALE`. Both calls increment the show's `version` and each touched tier's `version` by 1. This is a true cycle: a show may travel `SELLING → PAUSED → SELLING` any number of times, for instance between price revisions, and a round trip leaves every tier counter untouched.

Sales themselves are recorded in blocks against a tier by a `TalentBuyer` or a `VenueOperator`, supplying `qty`, the number of paid seats sold. The tier must be `ON_SALE` — a `DRAFT`, `PAUSED` or `CLOSED` tier accepts no sales — and `qty` must not exceed the tier's `available_qty`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TIER_INVENTORY` (HTTP 422). On success the tier's `sold_qty` increases by `qty`, its `available_qty` decreases by `qty`, and its `gross_sold_cents` **accumulates** the quantity multiplied by the tier's price **at that instant**.

That accumulation rule is normative and easy to get wrong. Sales are applied in `created_at` order, and each sale contributes its own quantity multiplied by the price the tier carried when that sale was recorded. `gross_sold_cents` is therefore an accumulator, never a recomputation of `sold_qty` multiplied by the tier's current `price_cents`: a later revision never revalues history. Consider 800 tickets sold at 5000 cents each, then a revision to 6000, then 600 more tickets: the tier has accumulated 4,000,000 plus 3,600,000, or 7,600,000 cents. Re-deriving 1,400 tickets at the current 6,000 would give 8,400,000 — wrong by 800,000 cents.

Each sale then rolls upward. The show's `gross_sold_cents` is the sum of `gross_sold_cents` across **its own** tiers, and its `paid_tickets_sold` is the sum of `sold_qty` across its own tiers; the amounts transferred in from a postponed predecessor are deliberately **not** folded into these two fields but are added later, in the receipts waterfall. The show's `comp_tickets_issued` is the sum of `comp_qty` across its tiers and its `held_tickets` is the sum of `held_qty`. From these the show's `adjusted_gross_cents` is recomputed, then the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents` is recomputed and its `risk_posture` re-banded. The tier's `version`, the show's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1. Tiers in `ON_SALE`, `PAUSED` or `CLOSED` contribute gross; a `DRAFT` tier contributes its allocation to the comp allowance but never contributes gross.

The **adjusted gross receipts waterfall** is the single deduction ladder used everywhere in this system, and its order and bases are normative. Start from a gross receipts figure that is the show's own `gross_sold_cents` plus its `transferred_in_gross_cents`, and a paid ticket count that is its `paid_tickets_sold` plus its `transferred_in_paid_tickets`. Then take three deductions:

1. **Ticket tax.** The gross receipts figure multiplied by the venue's `ticket_tax_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. This is `ticket_tax_cents`.
2. **Facility fee.** The venue's `facility_fee_cents_per_ticket` multiplied by the paid ticket count. This is a flat unit charge on paid tickets only and involves no rounding. This is `facility_fee_cents`.
3. **Card fees.** A card-processing charge of 2.5 percent, applied to a base that is the gross receipts figure **less the ticket tax** — that is, post-tax but **before** the facility fee has been taken out — divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents. This is `credit_card_fee_cents`.

`adjusted_gross_cents` is then the gross receipts figure less all three deductions. Each bucket is rounded independently and no bucket is ever re-derived from the net. The card-fee base is the subtle part: charging cards on the raw gross, or on the fully netted amount, both produce a different settlement than the declared order does.

Repricing a tier in response to sales pace is a `TalentBuyer` capability that supplies `new_price_cents`, a `reason`, and the `expected_show_version` demanded by §2.7. On success the tier's `last_price_cents` records the price it held immediately before the revision, its `price_cents` becomes `new_price_cents`, and its `price_revision_count` increases by 1. Its `gross_sold_cents` and `sold_qty` are explicitly **unchanged** — history keeps its as-sold prices. Forward-looking figures do move: the show's `projected_adjusted_gross_cents` and `projected_promoter_net_cents` are recomputed at the new price, and the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents` is recomputed and its `risk_posture` re-banded. The tier's `version`, the show's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.5. Papering the House: Holds, Comps and the Conservation of Seats

Seats are pulled out of sellable inventory by a **ticket hold**. The payload names the pool by `ticket_tier_id`, states a `kind`, a `qty` of at least 1 seat, and a `holder` of 1 to 120 characters describing who the seats are being held for, and may carry an optional `expires_at` instant at which the hold auto-releases. `kind` is one of `COMP` (papered seats, counted as comps from the moment the hold is created), `ARTIST_HOLD`, `PRODUCTION_HOLD` or `PROMOTER_HOLD`. A hold is created in status `HELD` with `released_by_user_id` null.

Holds may be created by a `TalentBuyer`, an `ArtistRep` or a `VenueOperator`, but the two entity-backed roles are narrowly scoped: an `ArtistRep` may only create `ARTIST_HOLD` seats, and only on shows whose `artist_id` equals its `X-User-ID`; a `VenueOperator` may only create `PRODUCTION_HOLD` seats, and only on shows whose `venue_id` equals its `X-User-ID`. Acting outside that scope yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Two quantitative checks guard a hold, in this order. First the **comp allowance**, which applies when the hold's `kind` is `COMP`. Total allocation is the sum of `allocation_qty` across **every** tier of the show regardless of tier status — `DRAFT` tiers included. The show's comp allowance in whole tickets is 3 percent of that total allocation, rounded half up. The show's current `comp_tickets_issued` plus the requested quantity must not exceed that allowance, or the request is rejected with `ERR_COMP_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A 1,000-seat show therefore allows 30 comps and refuses the 31st; a 2,000-seat show allows 60. Second, the requested `qty` must not exceed the tier's `available_qty`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TIER_INVENTORY` (HTTP 422) — inventory is never allowed to go negative.

On success, a `COMP` hold increases the tier's `comp_qty` by the held quantity while every other kind increases the tier's `held_qty`, and either way the tier's `available_qty` decreases by the same quantity. The show's `comp_tickets_issued` and `held_tickets` are refreshed from its tiers and its `projected_adjusted_gross_cents` is recomputed. The tier's `version`, the show's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1. Holds may be listed and narrowed by `ticket_tier_id`, by `kind` and by `status`.

Releasing a hold puts the seats back. The hold must be in `HELD`, and the show's `sales_status` must not yet be `CLOSED`. A `TalentBuyer` may release any hold on any show; an `ArtistRep` may release only `ARTIST_HOLD` holds on shows whose `artist_id` matches its own identity; a `VenueOperator` may release only `PRODUCTION_HOLD` holds on shows whose `venue_id` matches its own identity. The hold moves `HELD → RELEASED`, its `released_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the inventory movement is exactly reversed: the tier's `held_qty` — or its `comp_qty`, when the hold's kind is `COMP` — decreases by the hold's `qty` and its `available_qty` increases by the same amount. The show's `comp_tickets_issued` and `held_tickets` are refreshed and its `projected_adjusted_gross_cents` recomputed; the hold's `version`, the tier's `version`, the show's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1.

Converting a hold to comps is the one movement that changes a seat's **class** without changing how many seats are sellable. Only a `TalentBuyer` may do it. The hold must be in `HELD`, its `kind` must be something other than `COMP`, and the show's `sales_status` must be `PRE_SALE`, `SELLING` or `PAUSED`. The comp allowance is re-checked against the show's total allocation exactly as above, and a conversion that would push the show past its allowance is rejected with `ERR_COMP_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. On success the hold's `kind` becomes `COMP` and its status moves `HELD → CONVERTED`, `released_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, the tier's `held_qty` decreases by the hold's `qty` while its `comp_qty` increases by the same amount, and the tier's `available_qty` is **unchanged**. The show's `comp_tickets_issued` increases by that quantity and its `held_tickets` decreases by it. The hold's `version`, the tier's `version` and the show's `version` each increment by 1. Converted seats count as comps from the instant of conversion and stay comps forever.

Housekeeping expires holds. Any hold in `HELD` whose `expires_at` is non-null and has been reached by the current logical clock moves `HELD → EXPIRED`; the tier's `held_qty` — or `comp_qty` for a comp hold — decreases by the hold's `qty` and its `available_qty` increases by the same amount, the show's `held_tickets` and `comp_tickets_issued` decrease accordingly, and the hold's, the tier's and the show's `version` each increment by 1. `RELEASED`, `CONVERTED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal for a hold.

Underneath all of this sits one invariant that must hold after every hold creation, release, conversion, expiry, tier creation, housekeeping run and recorded sale, in **every** tier status including `DRAFT` and `CLOSED`: a tier's `allocation_qty` always equals its `sold_qty` plus its `comp_qty` plus its `held_qty` plus its `available_qty`. The two hold-derived counters are defined precisely: `comp_qty` is the total `qty` of the tier's holds that either have `kind` `COMP` and status `HELD`, or have status `CONVERTED` regardless of kind; `held_qty` is the total `qty` of the tier's holds whose `kind` is anything other than `COMP` and whose status is `HELD`. `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` holds contribute to neither. Every movement is compensating and exact: creating a hold moves seats from available to held, releasing or expiring one moves exactly the same seats back, converting moves seats from held to comp while leaving available alone, and recording a sale moves seats from available to sold. A hold-then-release round trip on a 1,000-seat tier with 400 sold returns `held_qty` to its prior value and `available_qty` to 600 with no drift, and the invariant closes at every intermediate step — which is precisely how an implementation that double-counts a converted hold in both `comp_qty` and `held_qty` reveals itself.

### 3.6. Show Budgets, Vendor Invoices and the Variance Ladder

A `TourAccountant` budgets a show's costs as **budget lines**. Each line names its show in `show_id`, falls into exactly one `category` — `PRODUCTION`, `MARKETING`, `STAFFING` or `HOSPITALITY` — carries a `description` of 1 to 200 characters saying what the line pays for, a `budgeted_cents` amount of at least 1, and an optional `is_artist_deductible` boolean declaring whether the line may enter the artist's deductible expense base at settlement. Omit `is_artist_deductible` and it defaults to false.

A new line is created with `actual_cents` 0, `variance_cents` 0, `variance_pct` 0, `variance_status` `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`, `required_approver_role` `NONE`, `approved_cap_cents` null, `variance_decided_by_user_id` null, `variance_justification` null, `settlement_expense_cents` 0, and lifecycle `status` `ACTIVE`. Creating it recomputes the show's `total_budgeted_expense_cents`, `breakeven_cents` and `projected_promoter_net_cents`, then the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents` and its `risk_posture`; the show's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1. Budget lines may be listed and narrowed by `show_id`, by `category`, by `variance_status` and by `status`.

A line's own lifecycle `status` is separate from its variance state and takes exactly three values. It is `ACTIVE` while the show's books are open, becomes `SETTLED` when the show's settlement is paid, and becomes `VOID` when the show is cancelled outright. A show's `total_budgeted_expense_cents` is the sum of `budgeted_cents` over its lines whose `status` is not `VOID`, and its `total_actual_expense_cents` is the sum of `actual_cents` over the same set — the invoiced reality. Note that neither figure is what reaches the artist; the recognised figure is frozen onto the settlement instead, as §3.7 explains.

The show's `breakeven_cents` is derived here too: it is the show's `committed_guarantee_cents` plus the **lesser** of two amounts — the sum of `settlement_expense_cents` across the show's artist-deductible lines, and the accepted offer's `expense_cap_cents`. While no offer on the show is `ACCEPTED`, the show's `breakeven_cents` is 0. Because this figure feeds the market's at-risk exposure, any change to a line's recognised expense re-prices the portfolio.

**Vendor invoices** are how reality arrives. A `TourAccountant` or a `VenueOperator` captures one against a line, supplying `show_id`, `budget_line_id`, a `vendor_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, an `invoice_date`, and a `documentation_ref` of 1 to 64 characters pointing at the settlement-book packet page or scan. `invoice_date` is a required string whose content this document deliberately leaves unconstrained: it must be a string that is non-empty once surrounding whitespace is trimmed, it carries no maximum length, and — because it is not an `_at`-suffixed field — the ISO 8601 UTC instant rule of §2.9 does not reach it, so a plain calendar date such as `2026-10-09` is accepted. The server neither parses nor reformats the value: it stores and echoes back on read exactly the string the client supplied. A `VenueOperator` may only invoice shows whose `venue_id` equals its own identity. Cross-reference coherence is enforced: the `BudgetLine` named by `budget_line_id` must belong to the same show as the `show_id` on the invoice, or the create is rejected with `ERR_BUDGET_LINE_SHOW_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The invoice is created in status `SUBMITTED` with `resubmit_count` 0, `verified_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` null, and `dispute_reason` null — and pointedly, **no** budget line figure moves, because an unapproved invoice is invisible to the ledger. Invoices may be listed and narrowed by `show_id`, by `budget_line_id` and by `status`.

An invoice then travels through a two-person control. A `TourAccountant` verifies it against the settlement book, moving it `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` and recording `verified_by_user_id`; verification requires the show's `show_status` to be `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SALE` or `PERFORMED` and the target line's `status` to be `ACTIVE`. Approval — available to a `TourAccountant` or a `RiskOfficer` — moves it `VERIFIED → APPROVED` and records `approved_by_user_id`, and requires an acting `X-User-ID` different from the recorded `verified_by_user_id` (see §2.8), the line still `ACTIVE`, and the show still in one of those same three lifecycle states. `APPROVED` is terminal.

An invoice that has not yet been approved can be disputed by a `TourAccountant` or the `VenueOperator` that owns the venue, moving it `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` or `VERIFIED → DISPUTED` and recording a `dispute_reason`. When the dispute arrives from `VERIFIED`, the invoice's `verified_by_user_id` is cleared to null — the verification is void, so whoever verified it before may verify it again after re-papering. Resolving a dispute is a `TourAccountant` capability that supplies an `outcome`. With `outcome` `RESUBMIT` the invoice cycles `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`: both `amount_cents` and `documentation_ref` must be supplied and they replace the recorded values, `resubmit_count` increases by 1, and `dispute_reason` and `verified_by_user_id` are both cleared to null. A row may travel `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED → DISPUTED → SUBMITTED` any number of times. With `outcome` `REJECT` a `reason` must be supplied and the invoice moves `DISPUTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal; the target line's `actual_cents` is refreshed (a rejected invoice never counts), its variance is re-derived, and the show's `total_actual_expense_cents` and `budget_posture` are recomputed. The invoice's `version`, and on a rejection the line's `version` and the show's `version`, each increment by 1.

Approving an invoice is the four-hop cascade at the centre of this system. **Hop one:** the line's `actual_cents` is refreshed to the total `amount_cents` of the line's invoices whose status is `APPROVED` — only `APPROVED` invoices count, so `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED`, `DISPUTED` and `REJECTED` amounts are all excluded, which is exactly why approving and then rejecting an invoice returns the line to its prior recognition. From that, `variance_cents` becomes `actual_cents` less `budgeted_cents` (negative when the line came in under budget), and `variance_pct` becomes `variance_cents` multiplied by 100, divided by `budgeted_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. **Hop two:** the refreshed, **rounded** `variance_pct` drives the line's `variance_status` machine and re-derives `settlement_expense_cents`. **Hop three:** the line's new recognised expense recomputes the show's `total_actual_expense_cents`, `budget_posture` and `breakeven_cents`. **Hop four:** the show's `breakeven_cents` and `projected_promoter_net_cents` recompute the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents`, which re-bands its `risk_posture`. The invoice's, the line's, the show's and the market's `version` each increment by 1.

The banding in hop two reads the rounded percentage against two tolerance thresholds. A variance of at most 10.00 percent leaves the line `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` with `required_approver_role` `NONE` and `settlement_expense_cents` equal to `actual_cents`. Anything above 10.00 percent puts the line in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, sets `settlement_expense_cents` to `budgeted_cents` while the variance is unresolved, and sets `required_approver_role` to `TOUR_ACCOUNTANT` when the variance is at most 25.00 percent and `RISK_OFFICER` when it is above 25.00 percent. The boundaries are inclusive, and they read the rounded value: a line budgeted at 1,500,000 with actual 1,650,000 lands on exactly 10.00 and stays `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`; the same line with actual 1,650,074 has a raw ratio just above ten percent that **rounds** to 10.00 and therefore also stays `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`; with actual 1,651,000 it lands on 10.07 and goes to `PENDING_APPROVAL` needing a `TOUR_ACCOUNTANT`; a line budgeted at 200,000 with actual 260,000 lands on 30.00 and goes to `PENDING_APPROVAL` needing a `RISK_OFFICER`. A line sitting on exactly 25.00 stays with `TOUR_ACCOUNTANT`.

A pending variance is decided by a `TourAccountant` or a `RiskOfficer`, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and a `variance_justification`, and optionally an `approved_cap_cents`. The line must be in `PENDING_APPROVAL` and the show's `show_status` must be `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SALE` or `PERFORMED`. Two checks then apply, in this order. First, the acting role must satisfy the line's `required_approver_role`: a `RiskOfficer` may decide any line, while a `TourAccountant` may decide only lines whose `required_approver_role` is `NONE` or `TOUR_ACCOUNTANT` and is refused on a `RISK_OFFICER` line with `ERR_VARIANCE_APPROVAL_AUTHORITY` (HTTP 403). Second, on `APPROVE`, `approved_cap_cents` must be present and at least the line's `budgeted_cents`; on `REJECT`, `approved_cap_cents` must be absent; either violation yields `ERR_VARIANCE_CAP_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

The two branches then diverge in money, and this is deliberate. On `APPROVE` the line moves `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED`, records `approved_cap_cents`, `variance_justification` and `variance_decided_by_user_id`, and its `settlement_expense_cents` becomes the **lesser** of `actual_cents` and `approved_cap_cents` — the approver's ceiling binds. On `REJECT` the line moves `PENDING_APPROVAL → REJECTED`, leaves `approved_cap_cents` null, records `variance_justification` and `variance_decided_by_user_id`, and its `settlement_expense_cents` is clamped back to `budgeted_cents` — the promoter absorbs the whole overage and it never reaches the artist's deductible base. Both branches then recompute the show's `budget_posture` and `breakeven_cents`, recompute the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents` and re-band its `risk_posture`, and increment the line's, the show's and the market's `version` by 1 each.

A decided variance is not permanent. If a later invoice approval refreshes an `APPROVED` line's `actual_cents` to a value above its `approved_cap_cents`, the line falls back `APPROVED → PENDING_APPROVAL`: `approved_cap_cents` is cleared to null, `variance_decided_by_user_id` is cleared to null, `required_approver_role` is re-derived from the new `variance_pct`, `settlement_expense_cents` reverts to `budgeted_cents` until the new variance is decided, the show's `budget_posture` becomes `VARIANCE_PENDING` and its `breakeven_cents` is recomputed, and the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents` is recomputed. An approved cap does not survive a later invoice that breaches it. Symmetrically, if a later invoice approval makes a `REJECTED` line's `actual_cents` strictly greater than the value it held when the rejection was recorded, the line falls back `REJECTED → PENDING_APPROVAL`: `required_approver_role` is re-derived, `variance_decided_by_user_id` is cleared to null, `settlement_expense_cents` stays at `budgeted_cents` while the new variance is pending, and the show's `budget_posture` becomes `VARIANCE_PENDING`. And in the compensating direction, when rejecting a disputed invoice lowers a `PENDING_APPROVAL` line's `actual_cents` far enough that the rounded `variance_pct` falls back to 10.00 percent or below, the line moves `PENDING_APPROVAL → WITHIN_TOLERANCE`, its `required_approver_role` becomes `NONE`, its `settlement_expense_cents` becomes `actual_cents`, and the show's `budget_posture` returns to `ON_BUDGET` provided no other line deviates.

The show's `budget_posture` summarises all of this in one enum. It is `ON_BUDGET` when every one of the show's lines is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`; it is `VARIANCE_PENDING` whenever **any** line is `PENDING_APPROVAL`, which takes precedence; and it is `OVER_BUDGET` otherwise — meaning at least one line has been `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and none is pending.

### 3.7. Performance, Settlement Calculation and the Money Waterfall

The show happens. A `TalentBuyer` or a `TourAccountant` records it, moving `show_status` `ON_SALE → PERFORMED`, and the current logical clock must have reached the show's `event_at` — an earlier attempt yields `ERR_SHOW_NOT_YET_OCCURRED` (HTTP 422). The same call closes ticketing: `sales_status` moves to `CLOSED`, and every tier of the show sitting in `DRAFT`, `ON_SALE` or `PAUSED` moves to `CLOSED` with `available_qty` set to 0. A tier created **after** the show went on sale never saw the on-sale cascade and is therefore still `DRAFT` at this point — it is closed here all the same, because `DRAFT` is not a state that survives a show going terminal. The show's `adjusted_gross_cents` is recomputed and thereafter frozen, since no further sale can change it. The show's `version` and each touched tier's `version` increment by 1.

Opening the settlement book is a `TourAccountant` call that names the show in `show_id`. A show may have at most one settlement that is not yet `PAID`; attempting to open a second while one is outstanding yields `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The new **settlement** is created in status `DRAFT` with `reopen_count` 0, `deal_structure_applied` null, every money field 0, and `calculated_by_user_id`, `signed_by_user_id`, `paid_at`, `payment_method`, `payment_reference`, `dispute_reason` and `reopen_reason` all null. Settlement statuses are `DRAFT`, `CALCULATED`, `ARTIST_SIGNED`, `DISPUTED`, `REOPENED` and terminally `PAID`. Settlements may be listed and narrowed by `show_id` and by `status`.

Calculating the settlement is the heaviest write in the system, and only a `TourAccountant` may run it. It is reachable from `DRAFT`, from `DISPUTED` and from `REOPENED`, all three landing on `CALCULATED`, and it may be run as many times as those cycles allow — each run re-reads the ledger and may produce different money. The show's `show_status` must be `PERFORMED`, and exactly one offer on the show must be in status `ACCEPTED`; that offer supplies every deal term. Three further checks then run in this order: the show must have at least one budget line, or the call is rejected with `ERR_NO_TICKET_TIERS` (HTTP 422); no vendor invoice on the show may still be `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED` or `DISPUTED` — every invoice must have reached `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` before the books close — or the call is rejected with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_INVOICES` (HTTP 409); and no budget line on the show may be `PENDING_APPROVAL`, or the call is rejected with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_VARIANCE` (HTTP 409). A recalculation arriving from `DISPUTED` additionally clears `dispute_reason` back to null.

The calculation copies the accepted offer's `deal_structure` into `deal_structure_applied` and then runs four stages in sequence, freezing every figure onto the settlement row. Throughout, only budget lines whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SETTLED` participate; `VOID` lines are excluded entirely.

**Stage one — receipts.** `gross_receipts_cents` is the show's accumulated `gross_sold_cents` at calculate time, valued at time-of-sale prices, plus its `transferred_in_gross_cents`. The three deductions of the adjusted gross receipts waterfall described in §3.4 are then computed and frozen as `ticket_tax_cents`, `facility_fee_cents` and `credit_card_fee_cents`, using a paid ticket count that is the show's `paid_tickets_sold` plus its `transferred_in_paid_tickets`. The settlement's `adjusted_gross_cents` is `gross_receipts_cents` less all three.

**Stage two — expense recognition.** `total_settlement_expense_cents` is the sum of `settlement_expense_cents` across the show's participating lines — the recognised figure, which differs from the show's `total_actual_expense_cents` wherever a variance was approved with a cap or rejected. `allowed_expense_cents` is the artist's deductible base: take the sum of `settlement_expense_cents` across the participating lines whose `is_artist_deductible` is true, and if the accepted offer carries an `expense_cap_cents`, use the lesser of that sum and the cap; if no cap is set, use the sum unchanged. `breakeven_cents` is then `allowed_expense_cents` plus the offer's `guarantee_cents` — the guarantee is part of breakeven, including for backend deals.

**Stage three — artist earnings, forked by deal structure.** All three structures compute `breakeven_cents` the same way, but the artist's base differs:

- Under `FLAT_GUARANTEE`, the base is simply the offer's `guarantee_cents`. Adjusted gross is ignored entirely — the artist is indifferent to ticket sales and the promoter absorbs the whole downside.
- Under `VERSUS`, first form net-after-expense as `adjusted_gross_cents` less `allowed_expense_cents`. The base is the **greater** of the offer's `guarantee_cents` and the offer's `versus_pct` applied to that net-after-expense figure (multiply, divide by 100, round half up). The percentage applies to the **whole** net, not just the part above breakeven.
- Under `GUARANTEE_PLUS_BACKEND`, form the overage as `adjusted_gross_cents` less `breakeven_cents`, taking 0 when adjusted gross has not reached breakeven. The base is the offer's `guarantee_cents` plus the offer's `backend_split_artist_pct` applied to that overage (multiply, divide by 100, round half up). Below breakeven the base collapses to the guarantee.

That base is written to `artist_base_earnings_cents`. Then comes the **marketing co-op contribution**, and the order of its two legs matters because the cap leg is keyed on the base the previous step has just produced. First form the recognised marketing expense: the sum of `settlement_expense_cents` across the participating lines whose `category` is `MARKETING`. The **share leg** is half of that recognised marketing expense — 50 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. The **cap leg** is 5 percent of `artist_base_earnings_cents`, likewise rounded half up to whole cents. `artist_coop_contribution_cents` is the **lesser** of the two legs, and each leg is rounded independently **before** the comparison, never after. Which leg binds genuinely varies with the numbers, so neither may be assumed.

`artist_earnings_cents` is `artist_base_earnings_cents` less `artist_coop_contribution_cents`. `withholding_cents` is the artist's `withholding_pct` applied to `artist_earnings_cents` (multiply, divide by 100, round half up). `deposit_credit_cents` is the accepted offer's `deposit_cents`, already wired at confirmation. `payment_due_cents` is `artist_earnings_cents` less `withholding_cents` less `deposit_credit_cents` — and it is signed: a negative `payment_due_cents` means the artist was overpaid at deposit time and the promoter has money to claw back.

**Stage four — venue reimbursement and the promoter/venue split.** Under `RENTAL`, `venue_expense_reimbursement_cents` is 0 — the promoter bears every expense — so `promoter_borne_expense_cents` equals `total_settlement_expense_cents`, and `venue_share_cents` is the show's flat `rental_fee_cents`, paid regardless of outcome. `promoter_net_cents` is then `adjusted_gross_cents` less `artist_earnings_cents` less `promoter_borne_expense_cents` less `venue_share_cents`.

Under `CO_PROMOTE`, `venue_expense_reimbursement_cents` is computed **per line and rounded per line, then summed**: for each participating budget line, take its `settlement_expense_cents`, multiply by whichever of `venue_expense_pct_production`, `venue_expense_pct_marketing`, `venue_expense_pct_staffing` or `venue_expense_pct_hospitality` matches the line's `category`, divide by 100, round half up to whole cents — and add those per-line results together. This is normative: rounding a category total once, or rounding a blended percentage against the aggregate expense, differs by cents from the per-line mode and the difference is observable. `promoter_borne_expense_cents` is `total_settlement_expense_cents` less `venue_expense_reimbursement_cents`. Then form net-before-venue-share as `adjusted_gross_cents` less `artist_earnings_cents` less `promoter_borne_expense_cents`; `venue_share_cents` is the show's `copromote_venue_share_pct` applied to that net-before-venue figure **floored at zero** (multiply the greater of zero and the net figure by the percentage, divide by 100, round half up); and `promoter_net_cents` is net-before-venue less `venue_share_cents`.

One closing invariant must hold on every `CALCULATED` settlement, under either arrangement: `adjusted_gross_cents` equals `artist_earnings_cents` plus `promoter_borne_expense_cents` plus `venue_share_cents` plus `promoter_net_cents`. `venue_share_cents` is the only rounded term in the split, and `promoter_net_cents` absorbs the rounding remainder so the invariant closes exactly. `promoter_net_cents` is signed and a loss must be reported as a negative figure, never floored at zero. On a co-promote loss the building's share is zero — the floor in the venue-share step guarantees it — and the whole loss stays with the promoter.

Finally, the settlement's `calculated_by_user_id` records the calculating accountant's opaque `X-User-ID`, and the settlement's `version` increments by 1.

Alongside the final calculation the service offers a **provisional settlement projection**: a computed read, available to a `TalentBuyer`, a `TourAccountant` or a `RiskOfficer`, that answers "what would this show settle at if the house sold out at today's prices and the budget held?" It may be run at any `show_status` — before the show, during the on-sale, after the performance — and it neither creates, reads nor mutates any settlement row. Its result is a settlement-shaped transient object in which `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` are all null, `status` is reported as `DRAFT`, and `show_id` echoes the show whose projection was requested.

The two variants disagree everywhere on purpose. The **provisional** variant values the entire sellable allocation at the **current** price: for each tier, take its `allocation_qty` less its `comp_qty` less its `held_qty`, multiply by that tier's current `price_cents`, and sum across tiers to get a projected gross; the projected paid ticket count is the sum of those same per-tier sellable quantities. Those two figures are then run through the full adjusted-gross deduction ladder of §3.4 exactly as the final variant runs it, producing the show's `projected_adjusted_gross_cents`. Sales history is deliberately ignored. On the expense side the provisional variant uses **budgeted** amounts rather than recognised ones: the deductible base is the sum of `budgeted_cents` across the artist-deductible lines (still subject to the offer's `expense_cap_cents`), the marketing co-op share leg is computed against budgeted marketing expense, the artist-earnings fork of stage three runs against the projected adjusted gross and that budgeted base, and the venue reimbursement is computed per line against `budgeted_cents`. Unapproved variances are ignored. Nothing is persisted.

The **final** variant, which is what the settlement calculation runs, values only the tickets that actually sold, at the price each sale carried, and deducts recognised expenses — capped for `APPROVED` variances, clamped back to budget for `REJECTED` ones — freezing every figure onto the settlement row. Because the provisional variant's co-op contribution can land on its share leg while the final variant's lands on its cap leg, even the binding constraint can switch between the two.

Separately from either variant, the show carries `projected_promoter_net_cents`, and it is deliberately a **gross** promoter margin taken before any venue split, so that the market's exposure view is independent of how each deal pays its building: it is the show's `projected_adjusted_gross_cents` less its `committed_guarantee_cents` less its `total_budgeted_expense_cents`, and it may be negative.

### 3.8. Artist Response, Payment on Site and Closing the Books

Once the settlement is `CALCULATED`, the artist side answers it. An `ArtistRep` whose `X-User-ID` equals the show's `artist_id` supplies a `response` of either `SIGN` or `DISPUTE`, and the two branches leave the same state for different targets with different effects. `SIGN` moves the settlement `CALCULATED → ARTIST_SIGNED` and records `signed_by_user_id` as the responding rep's `X-User-ID`, unlocking payment. `DISPUTE` moves it `CALCULATED → DISPUTED`, requires a `dispute_reason`, and leaves `signed_by_user_id` null — the settlement goes back for recalculation, and the recalculation clears the dispute reason again. Either branch increments the settlement's `version` by 1.

A `RiskOfficer` may reopen a settlement that is `CALCULATED` or `ARTIST_SIGNED`, moving it to `REOPENED` with a required `reopen_reason`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the settlement's `calculated_by_user_id`. Reopening increases `reopen_count` by 1, records the `reopen_reason`, and clears `signed_by_user_id` to null — an artist signature does not survive a reopen and must be given again. `reopen_count` is a running total and is never reset, so it also serves as the loop counter for the recalculation cycle.

Payment on site is the terminal act. A `TourAccountant` pays an `ARTIST_SIGNED` settlement, supplying a required `payment_method` of `WIRE`, `CHECK` or `CASH` and a required `payment_reference` capturing the wire or cheque reference. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the settlement's `signed_by_user_id`. Cash is capped: when `payment_method` is `CASH`, the settlement's `payment_due_cents` may not exceed 1,000,000 cents — ten thousand dollars — or the payment is rejected with `ERR_CASH_PAYMENT_LIMIT` (HTTP 422). Wire and cheque payments carry no such cap.

Payment moves the settlement `ARTIST_SIGNED → PAID`, which is terminal, stamps `paid_at` with the current logical clock and records the `payment_method` and `payment_reference`. Then the books close in three hops. **Hop one:** the show's `show_status` moves `PERFORMED → SETTLED`. **Hop two:** every budget line on the show whose lifecycle `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SETTLED`. **Hop three:** the market is re-priced — its `committed_guarantee_cents` decreases by the show's `committed_guarantee_cents` (a `SETTLED` show is outside the in-scope exposure set), its `at_risk_guarantee_cents` drops that show's contribution, its `settled_show_count` increases by 1, its `exposure_utilization_pct` and `risk_posture` are re-derived, and its `performance_index` is recomputed using the `TRAILING_SETTLED` valuation. The act's `confirmed_show_count` also decreases by 1, because a `SETTLED` show is outside the `CONFIRMED`, `ON_SALE`, `PERFORMED` window that field counts. The settlement's `version`, the show's `version`, each touched budget line's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.9. Pulling a Date: Cancellation, Postponement, Refunds, Insurance and Rescheduling

Shows die, and they die in two different ways. A `TalentBuyer` or a `RiskOfficer` opens a **cancellation event** naming the show in `show_id`, a `kind`, and a `reason` of 1 to 200 characters explaining why the date was pulled. `kind` `CANCELLED` kills the show outright and refunds are owed; `kind` `POSTPONED` keeps the deal alive for a reschedule and defers the refunds. A show may carry at most one cancellation event ever — if one already exists for the show, the request is refused. A `CANCELLED` event may be opened against a show whose `show_status` is `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SALE`; announce-cancelled-before-the-deal-closed is a real scenario, and a `PLANNED` show with no accepted offer can still be pulled, in which case its committed guarantee is already 0 and market exposure does not move. A `POSTPONED` event requires the show to be `CONFIRMED` or `ON_SALE` — there is nothing to postpone before the deal closed. The event is created in status `OPENED` with `occurred_at` stamped from the current logical clock, and that stamp starts the insurance filing window.

Both kinds share most of the damage. The show's `refund_liability_cents` becomes its `gross_sold_cents` plus its `transferred_in_gross_cents` — every paid ticket is refunded at **face value**, because the ticket tax, the facility fee and the card fees are the promoter's sunk loss and not the buyer's problem — and the event's own `refund_liability_cents` mirrors that figure. The show's `sales_status` moves to `CLOSED` and every tier of the show moves to `CLOSED` with `available_qty` 0; no further sale, hold, conversion or price adjustment is accepted afterwards. The show leaves the market's in-scope exposure set either way, so the market's `committed_guarantee_cents` decreases by exactly the show's `committed_guarantee_cents`, its `at_risk_guarantee_cents` is recomputed, and its `exposure_utilization_pct` and `risk_posture` are re-derived. The act's `confirmed_show_count` decreases by 1 under both kinds. The show's `version`, the market's `version`, and every touched budget line's and ticket tier's `version` each increment by 1.

Where the two kinds part company is expense and paper. Under `CANCELLED` the show's `show_status` moves to `CANCELLED` (terminal), every budget line whose lifecycle `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `VOID`, and every offer on the show sitting in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` — an offer already in a terminal state is never touched by that cascade. Under `POSTPONED` the show's `show_status` moves to `POSTPONED` and budget lines are explicitly **not** voided, because they carry across to the replacement date. And note the asymmetry already flagged in §3.3: a `POSTPONED` show has left the market's exposure book but is still inside the artist-routing set, so it keeps blocking that act's calendar within the 36-hour buffer even though its `confirmed_show_count` contribution is gone. Cancellation events may be listed and narrowed by `show_id`, by `replacement_show_id`, by `kind` and by `status`.

From `OPENED`, the event forks into two paths that never rejoin. The **refund and insurance path** begins with a `TourAccountant` processing refunds, which requires the show's `show_status` to be `CANCELLED` or `POSTPONED` and moves the event `OPENED → REFUNDS_PROCESSED`. That call writes three figures. `refund_liability_cents` on the event is taken from the show's `refund_liability_cents`. `refunded_ticket_count` is the show's `paid_tickets_sold` plus its `transferred_in_paid_tickets`. And `unrecovered_cost_cents` — the insurable base — is the sum of `settlement_expense_cents` across the show's budget lines whose lifecycle `status` is `ACTIVE` or `VOID`, plus the accepted offer's `deposit_cents`. Lines already voided by the cancellation still count, because the money was spent before the show died; rejected vendor invoices never reach `settlement_expense_cents` and are therefore never recoverable. Processing refunds also zeroes the show's `gross_sold_cents` and `adjusted_gross_cents`, while each tier's `sold_qty` stays put as the historical record. The event's `version` and the show's `version` each increment by 1.

A `RiskOfficer` then files an insurance claim, moving the event `REFUNDS_PROCESSED → CLAIM_FILED`, supplying a `claim_amount_cents` greater than 0 and a `claim_documentation_ref` filed with the insurer, and recording `filed_by_user_id`. The filing must land inside the claim window: the current logical clock must be within 30 days of the event's `occurred_at`, inclusive of the final instant, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422).

The insurer's answer arrives as a second `RiskOfficer` call carrying an `outcome`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the event's `filed_by_user_id`. With `outcome` `SETTLE`, an `insurer_reference` is required and recorded and the event moves `CLAIM_FILED → CLAIM_SETTLED`. `covered_loss_cents` becomes the **smallest** of three amounts: the event's `claim_amount_cents`, its `unrecovered_cost_cents`, and the policy cap of 25,000,000 cents. `insurance_recovery_cents` is then `covered_loss_cents` less the policy deductible of 500,000 cents, further limited to the 25,000,000-cent policy cap, and floored at 0 so a claim smaller than the deductible recovers nothing rather than producing a negative figure. Consider a small cancelled show with 500,000 cents of recognised expense and a 500,000-cent deposit: `unrecovered_cost_cents` is 1,000,000, a claim of 1,000,000 covers 1,000,000 and recovers 500,000, while a claim of exactly 500,000 covers 500,000 and recovers 0. Settling also relieves the portfolio: the market's `at_risk_guarantee_cents` decreases by the event's `insurance_recovery_cents`, floored at 0, and its `risk_posture` is re-banded; the event's `version` and the market's `version` each increment by 1. With `outcome` `DENY`, a `reason` is required, the event moves `CLAIM_FILED → CLAIM_DENIED`, both `covered_loss_cents` and `insurance_recovery_cents` are 0, the promoter absorbs `unrecovered_cost_cents` in full, and the market's at-risk exposure is left untouched — the exposure stays on the book. Only the event's `version` increments. Note also that a show's face-value `refund_liability_cents` is never insurable; only unrecovered cost is.

The **reschedule path** is the alternative fork and skips refunds entirely, which is why `REFUNDS_PROCESSED` and `RESCHEDULED` are mutually unreachable from one another. A `TalentBuyer` links a postponed date to its replacement by supplying `replacement_show_id`, and the event moves `OPENED → RESCHEDULED`. The event's `kind` must be `POSTPONED` — a cancelled event can never be rescheduled. The replacement must be a genuine replacement: the show it names must carry a `rescheduled_from_show_id` equal to the event's own `show_id`, must itself be in `show_status` `PLANNED`, and must not be the original show; any violation yields `ERR_REPLACEMENT_SHOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

On success the event records `replacement_show_id`, the original show moves `POSTPONED → RESCHEDULED` (terminal), and the money moves rather than being refunded: the replacement show's `transferred_in_gross_cents` increases by the original's `gross_sold_cents` and its `transferred_in_paid_tickets` increases by the original's `paid_tickets_sold`, which recomputes the replacement's `gross_receipts_cents` base and therefore its `adjusted_gross_cents` and projected figures. Both the original show's `refund_liability_cents` and the event's `refund_liability_cents` are set to 0. The market's `committed_guarantee_cents` is **unchanged** by this call: the guarantee already left the book at postponement, and the `PLANNED` replacement books nothing until a new offer is accepted on it — only `at_risk_guarantee_cents`, `exposure_utilization_pct` and `risk_posture` are re-derived. The event's `version`, both shows' `version` values and the market's `version` each increment by 1. `CLAIM_SETTLED`, `CLAIM_DENIED` and `RESCHEDULED` are the event's three terminal states.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The table below is the complete surface of the API. Every endpoint requires the `X-User-ID` header described in §2.1, and every response — success or error — uses the envelope defined in §2.2. Resource creation endpoints return HTTP 201 with the created object in `data`; every other endpoint, including all state-transition and workflow endpoints, returns HTTP 200 with the affected object in `data`. List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3 in addition to the filters named in their descriptions, and their `meta` block carries the pagination fields. Path parameters are resolved in the order given in §2.11.
