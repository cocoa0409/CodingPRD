# Nonprofit Newsroom Funding API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A nonprofit newsroom lives or dies on the credibility of its money. Reader memberships, six-figure major gifts, multi-year foundation grants, ticketed events and syndication deals all arrive with different strings attached, and every one of them is a potential argument that coverage was bought. This document specifies the backend service that runs that funding operation end to end: a recurring-giving membership program with tiered benefits and renewal cadences, a major-donor cultivation pipeline governed by gift acceptance criteria, a foundation grant pipeline with restricted funding scopes and reporting obligations, collaborative reporting partnerships with shared byline credit and shared cost, and the fiscal-period accounting container all of it is recognized in.

Two concerns cut across everything. The first is the **editorial independence firewall**: no funder may direct coverage, every funder-to-beat relationship must be publicly disclosed, and every attempt by a funder to influence editorial judgment must be logged and scored. The service treats independence as a hard precondition on money movement, not as a report generated afterwards — a funder whose risk score crosses the hard bar cannot be cleared, a beat whose disclosures have gone stale cannot receive new restricted funding, and a single logged request for editorial direction is enough to restrict a funder and claw back principal already released against its grants. The second is **financial sustainability**: revenue concentration is measured continuously, the operating-reserve target escalates when concentration breaks through policy thresholds, and the resulting posture caps how much restricted grant principal finance may release into operations in any single request.

The service is designed for board-level auditability. Every material decision names the acting user, and the users who cultivate money are structurally barred from being the users who clear it, attest to its disclosure, or ratify it at board level. Time is explicit rather than ambient: a logical clock drives renewals, grace expiry, report due dates and cooling-off windows, and it advances only when a caller advances it. The remainder of this document specifies the global contracts (§2), the business workflows and their rules (§3), the endpoint surface (§4) and the error catalogue (§5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, if `X-User-ID` is present but empty or holds only whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the supplied role is not among the roles authorized for the requested endpoint (§4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are:

- **`DevelopmentOfficer`** — fundraising staff. Builds membership tiers, enrols members, cultivates funders, solicits gifts, drafts proposals, submits grant reports and issues receipts. Never rules on independence and never moves money.
- **`EditorialDirector`** — newsroom editorial leadership, and the owner of the independence firewall. Clears or restricts funders, rules on gift offers and grant proposals, attests firewall disclosures, and runs collaborative reporting partnerships. Never solicits and never releases funds.
- **`FinanceController`** — finance staff. Owns fiscal periods, contribution settlement, refunds, restricted-fund releases, grant report review and grant closeout. Never rules on independence and never solicits.
- **`BoardOversight`** — board-level oversight. Ratifies large or elevated-risk gifts and records reserve/concentration overrides. Read-only everywhere else, and never originates funding activity.
- **`Member`** — a member acting on their own record.

`DevelopmentOfficer`, `EditorialDirector`, `FinanceController` and `BoardOversight` are all **opaque operator identities**: when `X-User-Role` is any of these four, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server does record these identifiers where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `cultivated_by_user_id` and `cleared_by_user_id` on a funder, `solicited_by_user_id`, `cleared_by_user_id` and `board_ratified_by_user_id` on a gift offer, `drafted_by_user_id` and `cleared_by_user_id` on a grant proposal, `attested_by_user_id` on a firewall disclosure, and `logged_by_user_id` on an influence contact — and it compares those recorded identifiers against each other to enforce segregation of duties (§2.5), but it never resolves them to a row.

`Member` is different. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member` row, and ownership is enforced by comparing the addressed resource against that id. A `Member` caller may only read the member row whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`, may only drive lifecycle actions and tier changes on that same row, may only create a contribution whose `member_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only read a contribution whose `member_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Any of those four operations addressed at another member's data is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). Note that `editorial_lead_user_id` on a coverage area is likewise an opaque editor identifier and is never compared against any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request: the literal `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn at random from the uppercase letters A through Z and the digits 0 through 9.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the service taken from the server's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it neither moves with `current_utc` nor bears any relation to the `created_at` of the row being returned.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Transport-level failures are rendered in this same envelope: a request whose path matches no endpoint, or whose HTTP method the endpoint does not accept, carries `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with the transport status code (404 and 405 respectively) rather than a 422. A trailing slash on any path is ignored when matching.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of matching items to skip before the page begins, it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A value outside the accepted range for either parameter, or one that is not an integer at all, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), while an empty value is treated as absent and takes the default. The filter parameters of §4 behave the same way: a filter carrying a value outside its declared enum, or a boolean filter carrying anything other than `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an empty value is ignored rather than matched.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 names a different ordering, list endpoints return their rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. Several collections do name a different ordering — fiscal periods come back newest `start_date` first, influence contacts by `occurred_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, grant reports by `due_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending, contributions by `received_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and collaborations by `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending — and in those cases the endpoint's stated ordering wins. Because `created_at` is minted from the logical clock (§2.4), rows created without an intervening clock advance all carry the same `created_at`; wherever an ordering's sort key ties — two rows sharing a `created_at`, or two fiscal periods sharing a `start_date` — the tie is settled by the order in which the server created the rows, so a `created_at` ordering is in practice creation order.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` counts every row matching the request's filters, not merely the rows on the returned page, and `has_more` is true exactly when unreturned matching rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this service carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, a system-managed integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations that only touch computed fields, and including mutations produced by a cascade the client never asked for directly. Both `created_at` and `updated_at` are read from the logical clock described below rather than from wall-clock time, so a row created or touched without an intervening clock advance carries the clock's present value. A write that leaves every stored field at the value it already held is not a mutation: a re-derivation whose inputs have not actually moved refreshes neither `updated_at` nor `version`, whereas the declared transitions and action self-loops of §3 count as mutations and bump `version` even where what they write matches what was already there.

There is one exception. An influence contact is an immutable, append-only record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from an influence contact, and no operation ever modifies one after it is written.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never wall-clock time. On a fresh server the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it only ever moves when a caller sets it, supplying the new instant as `current_utc`. The clock may never move backwards: a `current_utc` earlier than the clock's present value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422), while a value equal to the present value is accepted. Setting the clock returns both the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced, and mutates no entity — elapsed time only becomes state through the daily sweep described in §3.3 and §3.8. Every deadline in this specification — renewal dates, grace expiry, report due dates, the funder cooling-off window, and collaboration production windows — is evaluated against the logical clock.

### 2.5. Segregation of Duties Across the Funding Chain

Because the same human may legitimately hold several roles, the firewall cannot be enforced by role names alone. It is enforced on `X-User-ID`: the user who cultivated money may never be the user who clears it, attests to its disclosure, or ratifies it at board level, regardless of which role header that user presents.

Concretely, filing a firewall disclosure requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the funder's `cultivated_by_user_id`; re-attesting a stale disclosure carries the same requirement. Accepting a gift offer requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the offer's `solicited_by_user_id`, and so does ruling on that offer in independence review. Board ratification of a gift offer requires that the acting `X-User-ID` be neither the offer's `solicited_by_user_id` nor its `cleared_by_user_id`. Ruling on a grant proposal in internal review requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the proposal's `drafted_by_user_id`. Clearing or restricting a funder requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from that funder's `cultivated_by_user_id`. A violation of dual control is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The duties check is deliberately ranked **below** the state checks and below the concurrency check in the validation ladder of §2.9. A request that is both in the wrong state and in breach of dual control reports the state error, not the duties error; likewise a request carrying a stale version reports the stale-version error even if its acting user would also have failed dual control.

### 2.6. Amounts, Basis Points, Rounding and Whole-Cent Discipline

Every monetary amount in this service is an integer count of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the API surface, in storage, or in any intermediate result that is persisted. Wherever a computation would otherwise produce a fraction, the rounding rule is stated explicitly with the calculation; the default and by far the most common rule is **round half up to the nearest whole cent** (a value ending in exactly one half rounds away from zero).

Proportions are expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — as integers, so 10000 basis points is the whole of something and 2500 basis points is a quarter of it. Every basis-point input must be an integer inside the range its own field declares: 0 to 10000 inclusive for `lead_credit_bps`, `lead_cost_share_bps` and `award_probability_bps`, and 1 to 10000 inclusive for a partner's `credit_share_bps` and `cost_share_bps`, since a partner with a zero share is not a partner. `reporting_reliability_bps` is derived rather than supplied and is never range-checked against a request. The `audited_advocacy_index` is not a basis-point value but is range-checked in the same pass: it must be an integer between 0 and 10 inclusive. A violation of any of those ranges is reported as `ERR_INVALID_BPS_RANGE` (HTTP 422); the rule guards funder independence clearance, grant proposal creation, collaboration creation and partner addition, which are the only four operations that accept such a field — neither fiscal-period creation nor funder registration takes one.

Dates supplied as date-only values use the `YYYY-MM-DD` form and are interpreted in UTC. Where a date pair defines a span, the start must be strictly earlier than the end — this holds for a fiscal period's `start_date` and `end_date`, a grant proposal's `restriction_start_date` and `restriction_end_date`, and a collaboration's `window_start` and `window_end` — and a violation is reported as `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (HTTP 422). Fiscal-period and restriction ranges are **inclusive** of both endpoints. A collaboration's production window is **half-open**: it includes `window_start` and excludes `window_end`, so two windows that merely touch at an instant do not overlap.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Supersede-Style Writes

Two writes in this service replace something a client has already observed, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the *parent* row rather than on the row being written.

`POST /firewall-disclosures` requires a non-optional `expected_funder_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Funder`. If that value does not equal the funder's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FUNDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because a new disclosure for a `(funder_id, coverage_area_id)` pair retires the pair's prior disclosure whenever it names that prior row in `supersedes_disclosure_id` — retirement is never automatic, so a filing that omits the field leaves the earlier row exactly as it stood and the pair then holds two `ACTIVE` disclosures at once; a writer holding a stale view of the funder would silently supersede an attestation it never read — one filed moments earlier by another editor, or one invalidated by a funder-restriction cascade.

`POST /members/{id}/tier-change` requires a non-optional `expected_member_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Member`. If that value does not equal the member's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Repointing a member at a new tier supersedes the prior tier assignment and rewrites the deductible split on every one of that member's unacknowledged contributions, so a client acting on a stale member row would be acting on a stale view of the cadence, the benefit bundle and the unacknowledged receipt set.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints that are not one of these two never accept `expected_funder_version` or `expected_member_version`, and never perform a version check.

### 2.8. Derived Fields, Cascades and Hierarchical Consistency

A large fraction of this specification consists of **computed** fields — values the client never supplies and never writes, which the server recomputes whenever any of their inputs change. Computed fields are visible on reads exactly like input fields, and recomputing one to a value different from the one stored is a mutation of its owning row: `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` is incremented by 1. A recomputation that lands back on the value the row already carried writes nothing and bumps no version, so a cascade hop that reaches a row whose derived picture has not actually changed leaves that row untouched.

Recomputation is transitive. Several operations set off cascades in which each hop is caused by the preceding hop rather than by the original request, and every hop mutates its own rows and bumps their own versions. Restricting a funder, for instance, drives its active disclosures to require re-attestation, which blocks the coverage areas those disclosures cover, which suspends awarded grants on those beats with a clawback of released principal, which in turn forces the affected fiscal period to re-derive its entire sustainability picture. The individual hops are specified in §3.5, §3.7 and §3.8; the principle here is that a client is never required to fire a follow-up request to bring the graph back into agreement, and that a read of any touched row after such an operation reflects the fully settled result.

Cascades are **not** symmetric by default. Curing the cause of a suspension does not un-suspend anything: re-attesting the disclosures that blocked a beat returns the beat's gate to open, but the grants that were suspended stay suspended until finance explicitly reinstates each one. Where a compensating action exists, it is specified as its own operation, and this document names it as such.

### 2.9. Validation Ordering

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Validation proceeds in a fixed ladder and the **first** violated rule decides the response; no later rule is evaluated and no partial mutation is ever written.

The ladder is:

1. Authentication — headers present and role recognized (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Authorization — the supplied role appears on the endpoint's role list (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. Path-parameter existence, resolved strictly left to right. Each entity has its own not-found code, and the code returned is the one belonging to the *first* path segment that fails to resolve: `ERR_FISCAL_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVERAGE_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INFLUENCE_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GIFT_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLABORATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND` (all HTTP 404).
4. Body foreign-key existence, evaluated after every path id and in the order the fields are declared on the endpoint's input list, again returning the not-found code of the missing target entity. This applies to member enrolment, coverage-area registration, funder registration, disclosure filing, influence-contact logging, gift-offer creation, grant-proposal creation, contribution creation, collaboration creation and member tier changes. A field that is absent or null is not resolved at all; one that is present but is not a string is treated as unresolvable and reports the same not-found code rather than a shape error. Where the endpoint nevertheless requires that reference, its absence is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at the moment the field is reached in this pass, before any later-declared reference is resolved — so a gift offer or a grant proposal filed with no `funder_id` at all and an unresolvable `fiscal_period_id` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not `ERR_FISCAL_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, and a collaboration filed with no `coverage_area_id` and an unresolvable `fiscal_period_id` does the same.
5. Member self-scope (`ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN`).
6. Request shape — required fields present and, where they are strings, non-empty; enums holding declared values, integers actually integers (a boolean is not an integer), and cent amounts strictly positive where the endpoint requires an amount (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 422). A missing or unparseable request body is treated as an empty JSON object, so what such a request reports is the absence of its required fields, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The uniqueness of a natural key supplied on a create — a fiscal period `label`, a `tier_code`, a member `email`, an `area_code`, a funder `legal_name` — is enforced in this same pass, and a duplicate is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. Basis-point and index ranges (`ERR_INVALID_BPS_RANGE`).
8. Date-pair ordering (`ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`).
9. Reported spend not exceeding released funds (`ERR_SPEND_EXCEEDS_RELEASE`, HTTP 422).
10. Award amount and award date validity (`ERR_AWARD_AMOUNT_INVALID`, HTTP 422).
11. Fiscal-period openness (`ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`, HTTP 409).
12. Terminal-state guard (`ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, HTTP 409).
13. Declared-transition guard (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`, HTTP 409).
14. Unsettled contributions blocking a period close (`ERR_UNSETTLED_CONTRIBUTIONS`).
15. Board override warranted and not already recorded (`ERR_BOARD_OVERRIDE_NOT_WARRANTED`).
16. Tier published (`ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED`).
17. Member in an acceptable status (`ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`).
18. Optimistic concurrency (`ERR_STALE_FUNDER_VERSION` / `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION`, HTTP 412) — placed after every state check so a stale version never masks a wrong-state request.
19. Segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
20. Then the domain rules of §3, in this relative order: funder restriction, then the funder risk hard bar, then the disclosure-required check, then the disclosure-stale check, then the publication gate, then the remaining editorial, referential, temporal, constraint and fiscal rules described alongside the workflows they guard.

The relative rankings that matter most in practice are called out again where those workflows are specified, because several pairs of rules can fire on the same request and only one ranking produces the documented error.

### 2.10. Sub-Resource Reads and Reverse Reachability

Almost every collection in this service is a flat top-level collection joined by explicit identifier fields in request bodies; there is no deep URL nesting. Two sub-resource reads exist anyway, because some rows are created by cascade rather than by a client request and would otherwise be unreachable: a grant proposal's reporting obligations, and a collaboration's partners. For both, the parent identifier in the path is resolved first, and a request naming a parent that does not exist fails with that parent's not-found code before the child collection is consulted at all.

A third row is created by cascade and reachable only by filtering: accepting a gift offer writes a contribution that no client requested, and the only way to find it is to list contributions filtered by the originating `gift_offer_id`.

### 2.11. The Independence Firewall as a Precondition on Money

Three numbers govern the whole firewall and they are worth stating once, centrally, because they are referenced throughout §3. A funder's `independence_risk_score` is an integer between 0 and 100. At or above the **disclosure floor of 40**, the funder may still be cleared and may still fund the newsroom, but only if an active firewall disclosure exists for the exact funder-and-beat pair being funded. At or above the **hard bar of 60**, no clearance, no gift review and no proposal review may succeed at all, and no disclosure of any kind waives that bar — the rejection is `ERR_FUNDER_RISK_TOO_HIGH` (HTTP 409). Separately, a funder that has been driven into a restricted state is barred outright from new funding activity with `ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409), and for 30 days after the instant of that restriction it remains barred from grant submission and award even after being rehabilitated.

Disclosure is a two-sided artifact: it names a funder and a coverage area, and its text is the newsroom's public statement of how editorial decisions on that beat stay independent of that funder. The coverage area — not the funder — carries the consequence. While every disclosure touching a beat is active, that beat's `disclosure_status` reads `CURRENT` and its `publication_gate` reads `OPEN`. The moment any of them needs re-attestation, the beat's `disclosure_status` becomes `STALE` and its `publication_gate` becomes `BLOCKED`, and a blocked gate suspends new funding activity on that beat with `ERR_PUBLICATION_GATE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening and Governing the Fiscal Year

Everything the newsroom recognizes as revenue is bound to a **fiscal period**, and finance opens one before anything else can happen. A `FinanceController` opens a period by supplying a `label` such as "FY2027" — unique across all periods, between 1 and 32 characters — together with the period's first calendar day as `start_date` and its last calendar day as `end_date`, both inclusive and both date-only. The board-approved monthly operating expense base is supplied as `monthly_expense_base_cents` and must be at least 1, because it is the denominator of every reserve metric. Unrestricted operating reserve cash on hand is supplied as `reserve_balance_cents`; it is optional, defaults to 0, and may never be negative. A period opens in status `OPEN` with `board_override_active` false, `board_override_justification` null and `locked_at` null, and with its full sustainability picture derived immediately — over an empty revenue input set, so `recognized_revenue_cents`, `concentration_hhi` and `top_source_share_bps` all start at 0, `reserve_months_target` starts at the base policy figure of three months, and `operating_reserve_target_cents` starts at three times the supplied expense base. `stream_shares_bps` is an object keyed by the five revenue streams — `MEMBERSHIP`, `MAJOR_GIFT`, `GRANTS`, `EVENTS` and `SYNDICATION` — and `reserve_months_ratio_bps`, `sustainability_posture` and `restricted_release_cap_pct_bps` are derived from the supplied balance and expense base by the reserve-policy rules of §3.9. Opening the period is a single mutation even though that derivation runs inside it, so a freshly created period always reads `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to its own `created_at`, whatever the derivation wrote over the seeded defaults.

The period moves through three statuses: `OPEN`, `REVIEW_LOCKED` and `CLOSED`, the last of which is terminal. Finance drives it with a single action-bearing operation whose `action` selects the move. `LOCK` takes an `OPEN` period to `REVIEW_LOCKED`; before doing so it re-derives every sustainability field on the recognized basis, and it then stamps `locked_at` with the logical clock. A lock is refused with `ERR_CONCENTRATION_BOARD_REVIEW_REQUIRED` when the freshly computed `concentration_hhi` has reached 4500 or more and no board override is on file for the period — concentration that severe is a matter for the board before the books are frozen for review. `UNLOCK` is the revert edge: it returns a `REVIEW_LOCKED` period to `OPEN` and clears `locked_at` back to null, which is what makes period governance a branching machine with a way back rather than a one-way pipeline. `CLOSE` takes a `REVIEW_LOCKED` period to `CLOSED` and freezes every computed sustainability field at the value it then holds.

Two gates guard the close, and their relative order is fixed. First, no contribution of the period may still be sitting in settlement status `INITIATED`; if any is, the close fails with `ERR_UNSETTLED_CONTRIBUTIONS`. Only then is the reserve floor examined: a period may not close while `reserve_months_ratio_bps` is below 10000 — that is, while the reserve holds less than one full month of the expense base, which is precisely the condition that puts `sustainability_posture` at `CRITICAL` — unless `board_override_active` is true. That failure is `ERR_RESERVE_FLOOR_BREACH`. Both of these sit *below* the declared-transition guard, so asking to close a period that is still `OPEN` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than either fiscal complaint. Requesting `UNLOCK` on an `OPEN` period, or `LOCK` on a `REVIEW_LOCKED` one, likewise reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and any action at all against a `CLOSED` period reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

The board's own lever is a documented override, and it is the only fiscal-period operation `BoardOversight` may perform. The override is a self-loop: it leaves the period's status exactly as it found it — it is legal both from `OPEN` and from `REVIEW_LOCKED` — and it sets `board_override_active` to true while storing the supplied `justification` text into `board_override_justification`. It is not available on demand. The board may only file an override where one is warranted: either `concentration_hhi` has reached 4500 or more, or `sustainability_posture` is `CONSTRAINED` or `CRITICAL`. It also may not be filed twice; `board_override_active` must still be false. Any of those failures is `ERR_BOARD_OVERRIDE_NOT_WARRANTED`, while an override addressed at a `CLOSED` period is stopped earlier in the ladder by the terminal-state guard and reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` instead. Once on file, the override is what lets a severely concentrated period lock and a reserve-thin period close.

A locked or closed period is fiscally inert. Every financial write bound to a period in `REVIEW_LOCKED` or `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`, and that covers eight operations: recording a contribution, settling a contribution, refunding a contribution, accepting a gift offer, submitting a grant proposal to its funder, recording a grant outcome, releasing restricted grant funds, and driving a collaboration's status. Cascades are not covered by that rejection and are not stopped by the close: a hop that reaches a `REVIEW_LOCKED` or `CLOSED` period — the suspension clawback debiting `reserve_balance_cents`, or any of the re-derivations the delinquency and firewall cascades trigger — still writes to that period and still bumps its `version`, so the freeze the close applies binds only the eight client-driven writes just listed. Reads are unaffected — a period may always be read individually or listed, optionally filtered by `status`, and its computed metrics may always be read through the sustainability projection described in §3.9.

### 3.2. Membership Tiers and the Valuation of Benefits

The membership program is built from **tiers**, each of which is a recurring-giving offer plus the bundle of thank-you benefits that comes with it. A `DevelopmentOfficer` defines a tier with a `tier_code` — unique across all tiers, uppercase letters A through Z and underscores only, between 2 and 32 characters, for example "SUSTAINER" — a member-facing display `name`, a `renewal_cadence` of either `MONTHLY` or `ANNUAL`, and the recurring `contribution_amount_cents` charged each cadence period, which must be at least 100. An optional `grace_period_days` sets how long a member lingers after a missed renewal before lapsing; it defaults to 30 and must fall between 0 and 120 inclusive.

The `benefits` array carries the benefit definitions, defaults to empty, and may hold at most eight items. Each item is an object with three keys: `benefit_code`, a `fulfillment_type` drawn from `TOTE_BAG`, `EVENT_ACCESS`, `NEWSLETTER`, `BOOK` and `MERCH_DISCOUNT`, and `annual_fair_market_value_cents`, which may not be negative. Within a single tier the `benefit_code` values must all be distinct; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_BENEFIT_CODE_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 422).

Tax law requires the newsroom to separate the deductible gift from the value of what the donor got back, and the tier is where that split is fixed. Two constants govern it: benefits worth **2 percent of the recurring contribution** or less are treated as insubstantial, and that percentage test is itself capped at **130 dollars**. Concretely, `insubstantial_threshold_cents` is the smaller of two quantities: the recurring `contribution_amount_cents` multiplied by 200 basis points and rounded half up to whole cents, and the flat ceiling of 13000 cents.

Each benefit is then valued **per period**, and the order of operations is pinned. Walking the `benefits` array in exactly the order the request supplied it, an `ANNUAL` tier takes each benefit's `annual_fair_market_value_cents` unchanged as that benefit's per-period fair market value, while a `MONTHLY` tier divides that annual figure by 12 and rounds the result half up to whole cents **for that benefit individually**. A benefit is insubstantial when its per-period value is less than *or equal to* `insubstantial_threshold_cents`; a benefit sitting exactly on the threshold is insubstantial. Only after each benefit has been rounded on its own are the substantial ones added together, and that sum is `quid_pro_quo_per_period_cents`. Rounding per benefit first and summing afterwards is the required order and it is not interchangeable with the alternative: three benefits worth 1000 cents a year each on a `MONTHLY` tier contribute 83 plus 83 plus 83, which is 249 cents — not the 250 cents that summing the annual values and dividing the total by 12 would produce. The `benefit_code` of every insubstantial benefit is collected into `insubstantial_benefit_codes`, and `benefit_count` records the number of items in `benefits` — zero for a benefit-free tier.

All four of those derived values are computed once, when the tier is created, and are immutable from then on; every membership contribution simply reads them. A new tier is created in status `DRAFT`, with `published_at` null. Publishing it is a separate step by a `DevelopmentOfficer` that takes the tier `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and stamps `published_at` with the logical clock. Publication has one substantive precondition: `quid_pro_quo_per_period_cents` must be **strictly less than** `contribution_amount_cents`. A benefit bundle worth the entire contribution — or more — leaves nothing deductible and makes the tier unpublishable, which is reported as `ERR_BENEFIT_VALUE_EXCEEDS_CONTRIBUTION` (HTTP 409). Publishing an already-`PUBLISHED` tier reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Tiers may be read individually or listed, with optional `status` and `renewal_cadence` filters.

### 3.3. The Member Lifecycle: Enrolment, Renewal, Grace and Reinstatement

A `DevelopmentOfficer` enrols a member against a tier by supplying that tier's identifier as `tier_id`, an `email` that must be unique across all members and must contain an "@" character, and a display `full_name`. Two optional fields round out the record: `employer`, the member's self-reported employer, which exists so that employer-matched giving can be surfaced in transparency reporting, and `linked_funder_id`, set when this same person is also being cultivated as an individual major donor and left null otherwise. The referenced tier must already be `PUBLISHED`; enrolling against a `DRAFT` tier is rejected with `ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). A new member is created in status `PENDING` with `lifetime_giving_cents` at 0, `is_major_donor` false, `cultivation_locked` false, `consecutive_renewals` at 0, and `cycle_start_date`, `next_renewal_date` and `grace_expires_at` all null.

Enrolment alone does not start the clock. A member becomes `ACTIVE` only when their first membership contribution actually settles, and that transition is a side effect of settlement rather than an action anyone requests on the member. The settling contribution must name this member and its `amount_cents` must be at least the tier's `contribution_amount_cents`. On `PENDING → ACTIVE`, `cycle_start_date` is set to the contribution's `received_date`, `next_renewal_date` is derived from it, `lifetime_giving_cents` and `is_major_donor` are recomputed, `cultivation_locked` is re-derived, and the member's `version` increments by 1.

**Deriving the renewal date.** The step is one calendar month for a `MONTHLY` cadence and twelve calendar months for an `ANNUAL` one, and it is a *calendar* step: `next_renewal_date` is `cycle_start_date` advanced by that many months. Where the anchor day-of-month does not exist in the target month, the date clamps to the **last day of the target month** and never spills into the month after. The anchor is always the day-of-month of the *current* `cycle_start_date`, and because every renewal advances `cycle_start_date` to the `next_renewal_date` it just consumed, a clamp is sticky and the original day never recovers: a member activated on 2026-08-31 on a monthly cadence renews next on 2026-09-30, and after that renewal — with `cycle_start_date` now 2026-09-30 — the following renewal falls on 2026-10-30, not 2026-10-31. Adding a fixed 30 or 31 days in place of one calendar month is not a permitted substitute.

**Falling behind.** Two of the member transitions are driven by elapsed time rather than by a request, and both happen inside the daily sweep that a `FinanceController` runs. A member in `ACTIVE` whose `next_renewal_date` now lies strictly in the past — the logical clock's date is greater than `next_renewal_date` — moves `ACTIVE → GRACE`, and `grace_expires_at` is stamped as the tier's `grace_period_days` counted forward from `next_renewal_date` taken at `00:00:00Z`; that instant is when grace runs out. A member in `GRACE` whose `grace_expires_at` the logical clock has reached or passed moves `GRACE → LAPSED`, which resets `consecutive_renewals` to 0 and clears `grace_expires_at` back to null. The sweep runs those two moves as two ordered passes over the members — every `ACTIVE → GRACE` move first, then every `GRACE → LAPSED` move, and only then the report pass below — so a member whose freshly stamped `grace_expires_at` the clock has already passed travels `ACTIVE → GRACE → LAPSED` inside one sweep and is counted once in `members_moved_to_grace` and once in `members_moved_to_lapsed`. The sweep is idempotent within a single clock value: running it twice without advancing the clock moves nothing the second time. It reports what it did as `swept_at` plus five counters — `members_moved_to_grace`, `members_moved_to_lapsed`, `reports_moved_to_overdue`, `proposals_suspended` and `funders_restricted`.

**Catching up.** Three lifecycle actions are available to either a `DevelopmentOfficer` or the member themselves, selected by the `action` field. `RENEW` is legal from both `ACTIVE` and `GRACE` and lands the member in `ACTIVE` either way; the tier must still be `PUBLISHED`. In both cases `cycle_start_date` advances to the `next_renewal_date` the member had been carrying, a fresh `next_renewal_date` is derived from it, `grace_expires_at` is cleared to null, and `consecutive_renewals` increments by 1. `REINSTATE` is legal only from `LAPSED`, requires the tier to still be `PUBLISHED`, and requires `cultivation_locked` to be false — the locked member's reinstatement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, checked after the tier-published gate; it sets `cycle_start_date` to the logical clock's date, derives `next_renewal_date` from that, and leaves `consecutive_renewals` at 0 — a reinstated member starts their renewal streak over. Reinstatement is what closes the `ACTIVE → GRACE → LAPSED → ACTIVE` cycle. `CANCEL` is legal from `ACTIVE`, `GRACE` and `LAPSED` with identical effects in all three cases: the member lands in the terminal status `CANCELLED` and both `next_renewal_date` and `grace_expires_at` are cleared to null. Every one of these actions increments the member's `version` by 1, and requesting an action that is not a declared transition out of the member's current status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, while any action against a `CANCELLED` member is `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

**Lifetime giving and the major-donor latch.** `lifetime_giving_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every contribution of this member whose settlement status is `SETTLED`, across all fiscal periods. Contributions that fail or are refunded leave that set, so a refund reduces the total by exactly the refunded amount with no separate subtraction step. `consecutive_renewals` counts the renewals recorded since the member most recently entered `LAPSED` or `CANCELLED`, and is 0 for a member who has never renewed. A member is a major donor — `is_major_donor` true — once `lifetime_giving_cents` reaches **5,000 dollars**; the comparison is inclusive, so a member sitting at exactly 500000 cents is a major donor. Both `is_major_donor` and `cultivation_locked` **latch**: once `is_major_donor` has become true it stays true even if a later refund drops `lifetime_giving_cents` back under the threshold, and `cultivation_locked` is true exactly when `is_major_donor` is true and is likewise never cleared by a refund. The lock exists because a member who has crossed into major-donor territory and is also being cultivated as an individual funder through `linked_funder_id` is a firewall matter, and their tier may not be quietly rearranged while that is unresolved.

**Changing tier.** Moving a member to a different tier is the one member write under optimistic concurrency, and it takes exactly two fields: `new_tier_id` and `expected_member_version`. The member must be in `ACTIVE` or `GRACE` — `LAPSED` and `CANCELLED` members are refused with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), as is a `PENDING` member — the target tier must be `PUBLISHED` (otherwise `ERR_TIER_NOT_PUBLISHED`), `new_tier_id` must differ from the member's current `tier_id`, and `cultivation_locked` must be false. Those last two refusals are both `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and both are ranked *below* the unpublished-tier and member-status gates rather than at the declared-transition rung of §2.9, so a `LAPSED` member repointed at their own tier still reports `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The version check of §2.7 is evaluated after all of those, so a stale `expected_member_version` never masks a wrong-status or unpublished-tier request. On success the member's `tier_id` is repointed at the new tier, `next_renewal_date` is re-derived under the new cadence from the `cycle_start_date` the member already carried — which the tier change itself leaves exactly where it was — `grace_expires_at` is re-derived for a member sitting in `GRACE`, as the new tier's `grace_period_days` counted forward from that fresh `next_renewal_date` taken at `00:00:00Z`, and stays null for an `ACTIVE` one, and — this is the cross-entity part that is easy to miss — the quid-pro-quo and deductible split is recomputed on **every** contribution of this member whose acknowledgment status is still `UNACKNOWLEDGED`. Contributions already receipted or disclosed keep the split they were frozen with. The member's `version` increments by 1.

A member row may be read individually, and the collection may be listed with optional `tier_id`, `linked_funder_id`, `status` and `is_major_donor` filters. A caller presenting the `Member` role is confined to their own row and their own contributions as described in §2.1.

### 3.4. Coverage Areas, Funder Cultivation and the Independence Firewall

**Beats.** An `EditorialDirector` registers each editorial beat the newsroom covers as a **coverage area**, supplying an `area_code` that is unique across all coverage areas and consists of uppercase letters A through Z and underscores, between 2 and 32 characters, such as "HOUSING"; a display `name`; and `editorial_lead_user_id`, the opaque identifier of the editor accountable for the beat. A newly registered beat begins with `disclosure_status` at `CURRENT`, `publication_gate` at `OPEN`, and both `restricted_grant_count` and `disclosed_funder_count` at 0. `restricted_grant_count` counts the grant proposals pointing at this beat that are in status `AWARDED` or `SUSPENDED` and whose restriction type is anything other than `UNRESTRICTED`; that is exactly the population the aggregate cap of §3.7 limits. `disclosed_funder_count` counts the distinct funders that currently hold an `ACTIVE` firewall disclosure for this beat. Coverage areas may be listed with optional `publication_gate` and `disclosure_status` filters, and every role in the system — including `Member` — may read them.

The beat is also where the newsroom publishes its funding transparency. A transparency read of a coverage area returns its `area_code`, its current `publication_gate` and `disclosure_status`, its `disclosed_funder_count` and `restricted_grant_count`, the array of distinct funder identifiers holding an `ACTIVE` disclosure on the beat as `disclosed_funder_ids`, ordered by the creation of the disclosure that first put each funder into the array, and `total_committed_cents`, which is the sum of the `lifetime_committed_cents` of exactly those funders — a funder-level aggregate that counts everything each of them has committed anywhere, not merely what it committed to this beat. It is a read available to every role. It returns a single object rather than a page, but a `limit` or `offset` on the query string is still validated exactly as §2.3 requires before being ignored.

**Registering a funder.** A `DevelopmentOfficer` registers a prospective funder with a `legal_name` that must be unique across all funders and a `funder_type` of `FOUNDATION`, `INDIVIDUAL`, `CORPORATE` or `GOVERNMENT`. Optionally the officer records `affiliated_coverage_area_id`, the beat the funder has declared an interest in, which is left null for an unaffiliated funder; and `self_attested_advocacy`, the funder's own statement that it engages in advocacy on the beat it funds, which defaults to false. A new funder is created in status `PROSPECT` with `risk_basis` at `SELF_ATTESTED`, `reporting_reliability_bps` at 10000, `audited_advocacy_index` null, `prior_editorial_requests_count` and `lifetime_committed_cents` at 0, `cleared_by_user_id` and `restricted_at` null, and `cultivated_by_user_id` stamped with the registering officer's `X-User-ID`. Its `independence_risk_score` is scored immediately on the self-attested basis. Registration is nonetheless one single mutation however much scoring runs inside it, so a freshly registered funder always reads `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to its own `created_at`, whatever the scoring wrote over the seeded 0. Funders may be read individually or listed with optional `affiliated_coverage_area_id`, `status`, `funder_type` and `risk_basis` filters. `lifetime_committed_cents` tracks everything this funder has actually committed: the sum of `amount_cents` over its `SETTLED` contributions plus the `awarded_amount_cents` of its proposals in status `AWARDED` or `CLOSED`. The stored figure is refreshed only by the operations that write the funder row itself — registration, an independence ruling, a disclosure filing, a logged influence contact, a grant award, a gift acceptance, the settlement of a `MAJOR_GIFT` contribution, the refund of any contribution that names the funder directly or through a member's `linked_funder_id`, and the report-delinquency cascade — so suspending or reinstating one of its awards, neither of which touches the funder row, leaves the figure standing at whatever the last such write computed even though the underlying sum has moved.

**Scoring independence risk.** The `independence_risk_score` is a weighted integer between 0 and 100, assembled from five terms and then capped. The first term is the funder-type weight: 10 for a `FOUNDATION`, 5 for an `INDIVIDUAL`, 25 for a `CORPORATE` funder and 20 for a `GOVERNMENT` one. The second term is 4 multiplied by the funder's advocacy index, whose meaning depends on the basis and is described below. The third term is 8 multiplied by `prior_editorial_requests_count`, which is the number of logged influence contacts for this funder whose contact type is `EDITORIAL_DIRECTION_REQUEST` or `CONDITION_ATTEMPT` — every such contact, regardless of how long ago it happened. The fourth term is a flat 10 whenever `affiliated_coverage_area_id` is non-null, and 0 when it is null. The fifth term penalises unreliable reporting: it is 25 multiplied by the shortfall of `reporting_reliability_bps` below 10000, divided by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole number **before** it is added to the other terms. `reporting_reliability_bps` itself starts at 10000 and falls by 2000 for every grant report under this funder's proposals that has ever entered `OVERDUE`, with a floor of 0; that history is read across the funder's proposals in every status except `WITHDRAWN`. The five terms are summed and the result is capped at 100, giving `independence_risk_score`.

The advocacy index is where the score has two variants, and which one applies is recorded on the funder as `risk_basis`. Under the `SELF_ATTESTED` basis — the default at registration, and the basis in force whenever a clearance is filed declaring it — the advocacy index is 3 when `self_attested_advocacy` is true and 0 when it is false, and `audited_advocacy_index` is ignored even if a value is stored. Under the `AUDITED` basis — established by a clearance that declares it and supplies an `audited_advocacy_index` between 0 and 10 — the advocacy index is that supplied integer taken verbatim, and `self_attested_advocacy` is ignored. The two bases can put the same funder on opposite sides of a policy line: a `FOUNDATION` with an affiliated beat and `self_attested_advocacy` true scores 32 on the self-attested basis, but the same funder audited at an `audited_advocacy_index` of 7 scores 48, which crosses the disclosure floor of 40 and flips both the disclosure precondition described below and the board-ratification flag of §3.6.

**Clearing and restricting a funder.** Only an `EditorialDirector` rules on a funder, and never the officer who cultivated it: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the funder's `cultivated_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The ruling carries a `decision` of either `CLEAR` or `RESTRICT`, a mandatory `basis` of `SELF_ATTESTED` or `AUDITED`, the `audited_advocacy_index` — optional in general but mandatory whenever `basis` is `AUDITED`, where omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` raised after the index's own range check but before the state guard, the segregation check and every threshold guard — and an optional free-text `reason`. Either decision sets `risk_basis` to the submitted `basis`, stores `audited_advocacy_index` when the basis is `AUDITED`, and re-scores the funder on that variant before any threshold is examined.

`CLEAR` is legal from `PROSPECT` and from `RESTRICTED`, landing the funder in `CLEARED` either way. It has three substantive gates. The recomputed `independence_risk_score` must be strictly below the hard bar of 60; at 60 or above the clearance fails with `ERR_FUNDER_RISK_TOO_HIGH`, and no disclosure can rescue it. If the recomputed score has reached the disclosure floor of 40 or more, then `affiliated_coverage_area_id` must be non-null and an `ACTIVE` firewall disclosure must exist for that exact funder-and-beat pair; a missing one is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`, and a disclosure for some *other* beat does not satisfy the requirement. And when the funder is being rehabilitated out of `RESTRICTED`, none of its disclosures may still be sitting in `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` — the firewall paperwork has to be current again first, and a stale one is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STALE`. Those three fire in exactly that order: hard bar, then disclosure-required, then disclosure-stale. On success `cleared_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, the funder's `version` increments by 1, and — this is deliberate and load-bearing — `restricted_at` is **left untouched**, so the cooling-off window of §3.7 keeps running against the instant of the original restriction even after rehabilitation. `restricted_at` is null only for a funder that has never been restricted.

`RESTRICT` is legal from `PROSPECT` and from `CLEARED`, landing the funder in `RESTRICTED`. It re-scores the funder under the submitted basis, sets `restricted_at` to the logical clock, and then runs the firewall cascade described below. `RESTRICTED` is not terminal; the funder machine cycles `CLEARED → RESTRICTED → CLEARED` and has no terminal state at all.

**The five-hop firewall cascade.** Restricting a funder is the deepest cascade in the system, and each hop is caused by the hop before it, not by the original request.

1. The funder itself: `prior_editorial_requests_count` and `independence_risk_score` are recomputed, `status` becomes `RESTRICTED`, and `restricted_at` is stamped with the logical clock. `cleared_by_user_id` is not among the fields a restriction rewrites, so a funder that had already been cleared keeps the identifier of the director who cleared it while it sits in `RESTRICTED`.
2. Because the funder is restricted, every firewall disclosure of that funder currently in `ACTIVE` moves to `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` and records why in `reattestation_reason` — `INFLUENCE_CONTACT` when and only when a logged influence contact drove the restriction (§3.5), and `FUNDER_RESTRICTED` on every other path into restriction, which covers both an editorial director filing it directly and the daily sweep's report-delinquency cascade pushing the funder over the hard bar (§3.7).
3. Because those disclosures left `ACTIVE`, every coverage area they referenced re-derives `disclosure_status` to `STALE` and `publication_gate` to `BLOCKED`.
4. Because those beats are now blocked, every grant proposal on one of them that stood in `AWARDED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `FIREWALL_BREACH`. Each such suspension claws back the principal already released: the whole of `released_cents` is added to `returned_cents`, `released_cents` returns to 0, and the proposal's fiscal period has its `reserve_balance_cents` debited by exactly the clawed amount.
5. Because those proposals left `AWARDED`, their fiscal period re-derives `recognized_revenue_cents`, `stream_shares_bps`, `concentration_hhi`, `top_source_share_bps`, `reserve_months_target`, `operating_reserve_target_cents`, `reserve_months_ratio_bps`, `sustainability_posture` and `restricted_release_cap_pct_bps`, and every affected coverage area re-derives `restricted_grant_count`. The reserve-balance debit of the previous hop and this re-derivation are folded into a single write of the period row — that holds wherever a balance movement forces a re-derivation, the release credit of §3.7 included — so a period that one suspension both debits and re-derives bumps its `version` by exactly 1.

Every row a hop actually changes has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed; per §2.8 a hop that re-derives a row already holding the values it would write — a beat, say, whose gate some other stale disclosure had already driven to `BLOCKED` — leaves that row's `version` alone. Nothing in this cascade reverses itself when the cause is cured: re-attesting the disclosures unblocks the beats, but the suspended proposals stay suspended until finance reinstates them one at a time (§3.7).

### 3.5. Documenting the Firewall: Disclosures and Logged Influence Contacts

A **firewall disclosure** is the newsroom's published statement, for one funder and one beat, of how editorial decisions on that beat stay independent of that funder's money. An `EditorialDirector` files one by supplying `funder_id`, `coverage_area_id`, the published `disclosure_text` — which must be between 20 and 2000 characters — and `expected_funder_version`, the optimistic-concurrency field described in §2.7. Optionally the filing names `supersedes_disclosure_id`, the earlier disclosure this one replaces; it is left null for the first disclosure of a pair. A new disclosure is created in status `ACTIVE`, with `attested_by_user_id` stamped with the filing director's `X-User-ID`, `attested_at` stamped with the logical clock, and `reattestation_reason` null.

Filing a disclosure has consequences beyond its own row. The funder's `version` increments by 1 — which is why the concurrency field is on the funder rather than on the disclosure. Where the filing supersedes an earlier disclosure, that earlier row moves to the terminal status `SUPERSEDED`. And the referenced coverage area re-derives `disclosed_funder_count`; if no disclosure of that beat remains in `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED`, the beat also returns to `disclosure_status` `CURRENT` and `publication_gate` `OPEN`.

Three rules guard the filing. Dual control requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the funder's `cultivated_by_user_id`, reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. When `supersedes_disclosure_id` is supplied, the target must be in status `ACTIVE` or `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` and must carry the *same* `funder_id` and `coverage_area_id` as the new disclosure; a target that is already `SUPERSEDED` or `VOID`, or that covers a different pair, is `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (HTTP 409). And the stale-funder-version check of §2.7 sits between the two, ranked above dual control and below the referential checks. Disclosures may be read individually or listed, with optional `funder_id`, `coverage_area_id`, `status` and — for walking a supersede chain — `supersedes_disclosure_id` filters.

Re-attestation is the way back from staleness, and it uses a single operation whose behaviour is selected by the optional boolean `void`. With `void` absent or false, a disclosure sitting in `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` returns to `ACTIVE`: `reattestation_reason` is cleared, `attested_by_user_id` and `attested_at` are refreshed to the acting director and the current logical clock, and the disclosure's `version` increments by 1. Once no disclosure of that coverage area remains in `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED`, the beat re-derives `disclosure_status` back to `CURRENT` and `publication_gate` back to `OPEN`. Dual control applies here too: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the funder's `cultivated_by_user_id`. This closes the `ACTIVE → REATTESTATION_REQUIRED → ACTIVE` cycle. It does **not** revive suspended grants — those need finance's explicit reinstatement.

With `void` set to true the same operation retires the disclosure instead, moving it from `ACTIVE` or from `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` to the terminal status `VOID`, and re-deriving the coverage area's `disclosed_funder_count`, `disclosure_status` and `publication_gate`. A void writes nothing else: `reattestation_reason`, `attested_by_user_id` and `attested_at` all keep whatever values the row already carried, so a disclosure voided out of `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` retires still naming the reason it was flagged. Voiding is also the one attestation path that carries no dual-control requirement at all — the director who voids may be the funder's own `cultivated_by_user_id`. Voiding has one precondition of its own: no grant proposal in status `AWARDED` may still reference that funder-and-beat pair, because retiring the disclosure under a live award would leave awarded money undisclosed. That refusal is `ERR_DISCLOSURE_IN_USE` (HTTP 409). Requesting a plain re-attestation on a disclosure that is already `ACTIVE` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and any attestation against a `SUPERSEDED` or `VOID` disclosure is `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. The move from `ACTIVE` to `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` is never requestable: it is produced only by the funder-restriction cascade.

**Logging donor influence.** The donor-influence-prevention policy requires that every substantive contact from a funder be written down, and either a `DevelopmentOfficer` or an `EditorialDirector` may log one. A logged contact carries `funder_id`, a `contact_type`, the instant it happened as `occurred_at`, and a staff `summary` of what the funder asked for, between 10 and 1000 characters. Optionally it carries `coverage_area_id`, the beat the contact touched, left null for general stewardship contacts. `occurred_at` may not be later than the logical clock; a future-dated contact is refused as an invalid request. The server stamps `logged_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and derives `severity_weight` from the contact type: `ROUTINE_STEWARDSHIP` weighs 0, `COVERAGE_INQUIRY` weighs 1, `CONDITION_ATTEMPT` weighs 2 and `EDITORIAL_DIRECTION_REQUEST` weighs 3. A logged contact is immutable and append-only — as noted in §2.4 it carries no `updated_at` and no `version` — and `triggered_restriction` records, as false or true, whether logging it was what drove the funder into restriction. Contacts may be read individually or listed with optional `funder_id`, `coverage_area_id` and `contact_type` filters.

Every logged contact re-derives the funder's `prior_editorial_requests_count` and re-scores `independence_risk_score` on whatever `risk_basis` the funder currently carries. Two situations then restrict the funder outright, moving it `PROSPECT → RESTRICTED` or `CLEARED → RESTRICTED`: the logged `contact_type` is `EDITORIAL_DIRECTION_REQUEST`, or the freshly recomputed score has reached the hard bar of 60. Both situations require the funder to be standing in `PROSPECT` or `CLEARED`: a contact logged against a funder already in `RESTRICTED` is recorded with `triggered_restriction` false and sets no cascade off, however severe the contact is. Otherwise `triggered_restriction` on the new contact is set to true and the same five-hop cascade of §3.4 runs in full — disclosures to `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` with `reattestation_reason` `INFLUENCE_CONTACT`, beats to `STALE` and `BLOCKED`, awarded grants to `SUSPENDED` with `FIREWALL_BREACH` and a principal clawback, and the fiscal period's whole sustainability picture re-derived. One asked-for favour, written down honestly, can therefore pull released cash back out of operations; that is the intended design.

### 3.6. Major Gift Cultivation: Offer, Negotiation, Ratification and Acceptance

A major gift begins as an **offer**. A `DevelopmentOfficer` records one against a funder and a fiscal period, supplying `funder_id`, `fiscal_period_id`, the gross `offered_amount_cents` the donor has put on the table — which must be at least 100000, so a thousand dollars is the floor for a major gift — and a `designation` of `UNRESTRICTED`, `PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` or `PROJECT_RESTRICTED`. A designated gift names the beat it is earmarked for as `coverage_area_id`; an `UNRESTRICTED` gift leaves it null. Whatever conditions the donor has attached are recorded verbatim as `donor_conditions`, which is optional and null when the donor attached none. The offer is created in status `PROPOSED` with `conditions_round_count` at 0, `acceptance_gate_score` at 0, its three netting outputs seeded rather than computed — `stewardship_fee_cents` 0, `net_acceptable_amount_cents` 0 and `board_ratification_required` false, all of which stay at those seeds until the first submission for review computes them — `cleared_by_user_id` and `board_ratified_by_user_id` null, and `solicited_by_user_id` stamped with the soliciting officer's `X-User-ID`. An offer may not be recorded against a funder in status `RESTRICTED`, which is `ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED`. Offers may be read individually or listed with optional `funder_id`, `coverage_area_id`, `fiscal_period_id` and `status` filters.

**Sending an offer into review.** The soliciting side moves a `PROPOSED` offer to `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW`, and the same operation re-submits an offer whose conditions have been renegotiated, moving it `CONDITIONS_REQUESTED → INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW`. The only field it takes is the optional `donor_conditions`: supplying a value replaces whatever conditions are stored, and omitting it clears them to null — which is the mechanism by which a donor withdraws conditions. The funder must not be in status `RESTRICTED`. For a designated gift going in from `PROPOSED`, `coverage_area_id` must be non-null and the beat's `publication_gate` must be `OPEN`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PUBLICATION_GATE_BLOCKED`.

Submission is also where the money is netted. **Stewardship netting** is a three-step calculation whose evaluation order is pinned, because the fee tier is keyed on the **gross** amount and must be selected before the net exists — keying the tier on the net, which itself depends on the fee, would be circular and is not permitted. First the fee rate is chosen from `offered_amount_cents`: a gift below **1000000 cents** (10,000 dollars) carries no stewardship fee at all; a gift from 1000000 cents up to but not including **5000000 cents** (50,000 dollars) carries 150 basis points; and a gift at 5000000 cents or above carries 250 basis points. The tier floors are inclusive, so a gift of exactly 5000000 cents is charged at 250 basis points, not 150, and a gift of exactly 1000000 cents is charged at 150 basis points, not 0. Second, `stewardship_fee_cents` is `offered_amount_cents` multiplied by the chosen rate in basis points, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents, and `net_acceptable_amount_cents` is `offered_amount_cents` minus that fee — the net is what will actually be posted as revenue. Third, and also keyed on the gross, `board_ratification_required` becomes true when either `offered_amount_cents` has reached **2500000 cents** (25,000 dollars) or the funder's current `independence_risk_score` has reached 40; otherwise it is false. All three outputs are rewritten on every submission, including a re-submission out of `CONDITIONS_REQUESTED`, and the offer's `version` increments by 1.

**The independence ruling.** An `EditorialDirector` who is not the soliciting officer rules on an offer under review, supplying a `decision` and an optional `review_note`. The `decision` may be `CLEAR`, `REQUEST_CONDITIONS` or `DECLINE`.

`REQUEST_CONDITIONS` moves the offer `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW → CONDITIONS_REQUESTED`, increments `conditions_round_count` by 1, and stores the `review_note` into `donor_conditions` as the conditions the newsroom is asking the donor to accept or drop — the note is optional, and a ruling that omits it overwrites `donor_conditions` with null rather than leaving the stored conditions alone. Negotiation is bounded: `conditions_round_count` must be strictly below 2 for another round to be requested, so at most two rounds are possible, and an attempt beyond that is `ERR_CONDITION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The pairing of `REQUEST_CONDITIONS` with re-submission is what closes the `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW → CONDITIONS_REQUESTED → INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW` negotiation cycle.

`CLEAR` moves the offer `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW → CLEARED` and has four gates, evaluated in this order. The funder must be in status `CLEARED`, or the ruling fails with `ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED`. The funder's `independence_risk_score` must be strictly below 60, or the ruling fails with `ERR_FUNDER_RISK_TOO_HIGH`. For a designated gift, an `ACTIVE` firewall disclosure must exist for the exact pair of this funder and this `coverage_area_id`, otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`; and no disclosure covering that pair may be sitting in `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED`, otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STALE` — a stale disclosure blocks clearance even when a separate `ACTIVE` disclosure also exists for the pair. Finally, the offer must carry no non-empty `donor_conditions`: a gift with conditions still attached cannot clear, and the refusal is `ERR_EDITORIAL_CONDITION_PRESENT`. Conditions have to be withdrawn by the donor — expressed as a re-submission with `donor_conditions` omitted — before clearance is possible. On success the funder's `independence_risk_score` is snapshotted into `acceptance_gate_score`, `cleared_by_user_id` is set to the ruling director's `X-User-ID`, and the offer's `version` increments by 1.

`DECLINE` is legal from `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW` and from `CONDITIONS_REQUESTED`, and lands the offer in the terminal status `DECLINED`. It records the deciding reviewer as `cleared_by_user_id` and creates no contribution — ever.

**Board ratification.** A `BoardOversight` member ratifies a `CLEARED` offer that crossed the ratification threshold. Ratification is a self-loop: the offer stays in `CLEARED` and only `board_ratified_by_user_id` changes, stamped with the ratifier's `X-User-ID`, alongside an optional `ratification_note`. It is available only where `board_ratification_required` is true and `board_ratified_by_user_id` is still null; an offer that does not require ratification, or that has already been ratified, reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Dual control here is three-cornered: the ratifier's `X-User-ID` may be neither the offer's `solicited_by_user_id` nor its `cleared_by_user_id`, and a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

**Acceptance.** A `DevelopmentOfficer` — specifically, one who is not the offer's `solicited_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — accepts a `CLEARED` offer, supplying the `received_date` on which the funds arrived — a date that is only checked for `YYYY-MM-DD` shape and is never range-checked against the fiscal period, unlike the `received_date` of a contribution recorded directly. Four conditions must hold, and they are examined in this order. First the offer's fiscal period must still be `OPEN`, or `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED` — this is the fiscal rung of §2.9, so it is reached before the terminal-state check, the `CLEARED`-status check and the segregation check, and a locked period therefore masks all three. Then the funder must still be in status `CLEARED`, or `ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED`. Then, for a designated gift, the beat's `publication_gate` must still be `OPEN`, or `ERR_PUBLICATION_GATE_BLOCKED`. And last, if `board_ratification_required` is true, `board_ratified_by_user_id` must be non-null, or `ERR_BOARD_RATIFICATION_REQUIRED`.

Acceptance takes the offer to the terminal status `ACCEPTED` and writes a contribution the client never asked for: a row on the `MAJOR_GIFT` revenue stream carrying this offer's `funder_id` and `gift_offer_id`, an `amount_cents` equal to `net_acceptable_amount_cents` — the **net**, not the gross — a `source_label` copied from the funder's `legal_name`, `member_id` and `collaboration_id` null, `receipt_number` and `refunded_at` null, settlement status `INITIATED` and acknowledgment status `UNACKNOWLEDGED`. The funder's `lifetime_committed_cents` is re-derived, and the offer's `version` increments by 1. Because nothing in the response points at the new contribution, the only way to find it is to list contributions filtered by this offer's identifier as `gift_offer_id`, as noted in §2.10.

### 3.7. The Grant Pipeline: Proposal Development, Award, Release and Reporting

**Drafting.** A `DevelopmentOfficer` drafts a foundation or government grant proposal naming `funder_id`, `fiscal_period_id`, a `title`, the `requested_amount_cents` — at least 100000 — and a `restriction_type` of `UNRESTRICTED`, `PROGRAM_RESTRICTED` or `PROJECT_RESTRICTED`. A restricted request also carries the beat it covers as `coverage_area_id` and the inclusive bounds of its restricted funding window as `restriction_start_date` and `restriction_end_date`; all three are expected to be null for an unrestricted request, but the server neither requires that nor forces it — whatever the request supplies is stored verbatim, and the restricted-scope gates simply never fire for a proposal whose `restriction_type` is `UNRESTRICTED`. The reporting obligation the award will generate is declared up front as `reporting_cadence`, one of `NONE`, `SEMIANNUAL` or `ANNUAL`, defaulting to `NONE`. Development's own probability estimate is `award_probability_bps`, an integer between 0 and 10000 that defaults to 5000 and is used by nothing except the pipeline revenue projection of §3.9. Only institutional money can be a grant: the named funder's `funder_type` must be `FOUNDATION` or `GOVERNMENT`, and an individual or corporate donor is refused with `ERR_FUNDER_NOT_INSTITUTIONAL` (HTTP 422) — a refusal ranked below both the basis-point range check and the date-pair check, so a corporate funder named on a draft that also carries an out-of-range `award_probability_bps` reports `ERR_INVALID_BPS_RANGE`, and one whose restriction window runs backwards reports `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The funder's `status` is not consulted at drafting at all: a proposal may be drafted against a `RESTRICTED` funder, and the firewall gates only begin at internal review and `SUBMIT`. A new proposal sits in `DRAFT` with `revision_round_count` at 0, `awarded_amount_cents`, `released_cents`, `unreleased_cents`, `returned_cents` and `report_count` all at 0, `compliance_flag` at `COMPLIANT`, `award_date`, `cleared_by_user_id` and `suspended_reason` all null, and `drafted_by_user_id` stamped with the drafting officer's `X-User-ID`. Proposals may be read individually or listed with optional `funder_id`, `coverage_area_id`, `fiscal_period_id`, `status` and `compliance_flag` filters.

**Development-side pipeline moves.** Four moves belong to the fundraising side and are selected by an `action` field. `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW` sends a proposal into `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW` from either `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; for a restricted proposal it insists that `coverage_area_id`, `restriction_start_date` and `restriction_end_date` are all non-null, refusing a proposal missing any of them with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a shape complaint that is nonetheless evaluated *after* the terminal-state and declared-transition guards on this endpoint, so a `CLEARED` proposal with no restriction window asked to `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Neither this move nor `REVISE` nor `WITHDRAW` consults the funder at all. `REVISE` returns a `REVISION_REQUESTED` proposal to `DRAFT`, which is what closes the `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT → INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW` development cycle. `SUBMIT` takes a `CLEARED` proposal to `SUBMITTED` and is the point at which the proposal starts counting toward the pipeline revenue projection weighted by its `award_probability_bps`. `WITHDRAW` is legal from `DRAFT`, `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CLEARED` and `SUBMITTED`, landing the proposal in the terminal status `WITHDRAWN`, dropping it out of the pipeline projection and forcing the fiscal period to re-derive its sustainability fields.

`SUBMIT` is the most heavily gated of the four. The funder must not be in status `RESTRICTED` (`ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED`). The **cooling-off rule** also applies: at least 30 days must have elapsed since the funder's `restricted_at` instant before any proposal for that funder may be submitted, and because `restricted_at` survives rehabilitation, a funder cleared back to `CLEARED` two days after being restricted is still barred for another 28. The same rule also refuses a submission when another proposal of the same funder in status `SUBMITTED` or `AWARDED` has a restriction window falling within 30 days of this one's — that is, when the two inclusive windows either overlap or are separated by a gap of 30 days or less. That second limb only bites where both proposals carry a restriction window: a proposal without one, its own or the other's, is compared on `restricted_at` alone. Either way the refusal is `ERR_FUNDER_COOLING_OFF_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409), and it is ranked strictly *below* the restricted-funder check, so a funder that is still restricted and also inside its cooling-off window reports `ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED`. For a restricted proposal, the beat's `publication_gate` must be `OPEN` (`ERR_PUBLICATION_GATE_BLOCKED`), and that gate is examined *above* the cooling-off rule, exactly as §2.9 ranks the two, so a restricted proposal on a `BLOCKED` beat whose funder is also inside its cooling-off window reports `ERR_PUBLICATION_GATE_BLOCKED`. And the proposal's fiscal period must be `OPEN` — the one place where the fiscal check is not lifted to the top of the ladder but sits at the very bottom of this endpoint's, below the funder, gate and cooling-off checks, so a proposal in a `CLOSED` period whose funder is `RESTRICTED` reports `ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED` rather than `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`, and one asked to `SUBMIT` from a status other than `CLEARED` inside a locked period reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Editorial internal review.** An `EditorialDirector` who is not the proposal's `drafted_by_user_id` rules on a proposal in `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW`, supplying a `decision` of `CLEAR` or `REQUEST_REVISION` plus an optional `review_note`. `REQUEST_REVISION` moves the proposal to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and increments `revision_round_count` by 1; it requires `revision_round_count` to be strictly below 3, so at most three revision rounds are possible and a fourth attempt is `ERR_REVISION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. `CLEAR` moves the proposal to `CLEARED` and sets `cleared_by_user_id` to the ruling director's `X-User-ID`. Its gates mirror the gift-offer ruling and run in the same relative order: the funder must be in status `CLEARED` (`ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED`), its `independence_risk_score` must be strictly below 60 (`ERR_FUNDER_RISK_TOO_HIGH`), and for a restricted proposal an `ACTIVE` firewall disclosure must exist for the exact funder-and-beat pair (`ERR_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`) with none covering that pair sitting in `REATTESTATION_REQUIRED` (`ERR_DISCLOSURE_STALE`).

**Recording the funder's decision.** The outcome of a `SUBMITTED` proposal is recorded with an `outcome` of `AWARDED` or `DECLINED`. `DECLINED` lands the proposal in the terminal status `DECLINED`, leaves `awarded_amount_cents` at 0, drops the proposal out of the pipeline projection, and re-derives every sustainability field of its fiscal period. A declination passes through only the period-openness check and the `SUBMITTED` state guard: it needs no `awarded_amount_cents` or `award_date`, and it consults neither the funder's status nor its cooling-off window, so bad news may always be recorded against a funder that has since been restricted.

`AWARDED` requires `awarded_amount_cents` and `award_date`. The amount must be present, at least 1, and no greater than `requested_amount_cents`, and the `award_date` must fall inside the fiscal period's inclusive date range; any of those failing is `ERR_AWARD_AMOUNT_INVALID` (HTTP 422). The period must still be `OPEN`, the funder must not be `RESTRICTED`, and the cooling-off rule applies to awarding exactly as it applies to submission. One further gate is an **aggregate** one: for a restricted proposal, the beat may not already carry two proposals in status `AWARDED` whose restriction windows overlap this one's — each of those proposals is individually valid and only the collective count offends, and the refusal is `ERR_RESTRICTED_GRANT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The cap of two also governs `restricted_grant_count`, which counts restricted proposals on the beat in status `AWARDED` or `SUSPENDED`.

An award writes a great deal. `awarded_amount_cents` and `award_date` are stored, `unreleased_cents` is set equal to `awarded_amount_cents`, and `released_cents` and `returned_cents` are set to 0 — establishing the ledger invariant that `awarded_amount_cents` always equals `released_cents` plus `unreleased_cents` plus `returned_cents`, at every instant, for the rest of the proposal's life. The reporting obligations are generated (see below) and `report_count` is set to the number produced, which is 0 when `reporting_cadence` is `NONE`. Release capacity is allocated across those obligations. The funder's `lifetime_committed_cents` and the beat's `restricted_grant_count` are re-derived, every sustainability field of the fiscal period is re-derived on the recognized basis, and the proposal's `version` increments by 1.

**Generating the reporting obligations.** When `reporting_cadence` is not `NONE`, the award generates a contiguous series of reporting periods. The series spans the restricted funding window for a restricted proposal — from `restriction_start_date` to `restriction_end_date` — and, for an `UNRESTRICTED` proposal, from the `award_date` to the fiscal period's `end_date`. The interval is six months for `SEMIANNUAL` and twelve months for `ANNUAL`. The span in months is computed as the difference in years between the window's end and its start multiplied by 12, plus the difference in their month numbers, plus one; the number of reports is that span divided by the interval and **rounded up**, so a partial trailing interval still produces its own report. Each report `i`, counting from 1, receives `sequence_no` equal to `i`; a `period_start` equal to the window start advanced by `i` minus one intervals, clamped to the last day of the target month where the anchor day does not exist there; and a `period_end` equal to the earlier of the window end and the day before the window start advanced by `i` intervals under the same month-end clamp. Its `due_date` is `period_end` plus **45 days** of grace. The periods are contiguous and non-overlapping by construction: each report's `period_start` is exactly the previous report's `period_end` plus one day. Every generated report begins in status `SCHEDULED` with `consumed_release_cents` at 0 and `spend_reported_cents`, `variance_bps` and `submitted_at` all null. Reports may be read individually, listed with optional `grant_proposal_id` and `status` filters, or read as a sub-resource of their proposal with an optional `status` filter — the latter being the only discovery channel for rows the award cascade created.

**Allocating release capacity.** The award also gives each reporting obligation a share of the principal, and those shares are the buckets that later releases draw down. The buckets are the proposal's reports ordered by `due_date` ascending, with `created_at` ascending as the only tie-break — identifiers are never compared. A base share is the awarded amount divided by `report_count` and **floored**; every bucket except the last receives exactly that base as its `allocated_release_cents`, and the last bucket — the one with the highest `due_date` — receives the base plus the entire leftover, that is the awarded amount minus the base times the report count. The remainder always lands in the last bucket and is never spread across buckets. A proposal whose cadence is `NONE` has no reports and therefore behaves as though it had a single implicit bucket holding the whole awarded amount, releasable subject only to the posture cap.

**Releasing restricted principal.** A `FinanceController` draws principal down against those buckets by naming a `request_amount_cents`. This is a self-loop that leaves the proposal in `AWARDED`, and its four money gates are evaluated in a fixed order that matters because more than one can be offended at once:

1. The fiscal period's `restricted_release_cap_pct_bps` must be greater than 0 — that is, `sustainability_posture` must not be `CRITICAL`. A `CRITICAL` posture blocks a release of any size, which is why this is checked before the cap amount. Failure is `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_RELEASE` (HTTP 409).
2. The request must not exceed `unreleased_cents` multiplied by `restricted_release_cap_pct_bps` divided by 10000, rounded half up, using the posture computed from the reserve balance **as it stands before** this release is credited. This is a cascading check: it consumes the reserve posture, which itself consumes the revenue diversification index. It is **skipped entirely when the cap is 10000 bps** (a `HEALTHY` posture), because the cap then equals the whole unreleased principal and adds nothing — an over-large request under a `HEALTHY` posture is reported by the next gate instead. Failure is `ERR_RELEASE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
3. The waterfall must be able to absorb the request: `request_amount_cents` must not exceed the sum, over all buckets, of `allocated_release_cents` minus `consumed_release_cents`. Overflow rejects the whole request and no partial release is ever written. Failure is `ERR_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_AWARD` (HTTP 409), and this is the binding overflow guard under a `HEALTHY` posture where the previous gate is skipped.
4. Only last does the delinquency gate run: `compliance_flag` must be `COMPLIANT`, meaning no report of the proposal is `OVERDUE`. A delinquent grant can neither release funds nor be reinstated. Failure is `ERR_PROPOSAL_DELINQUENT` (HTTP 409). A delinquent proposal under a `CRITICAL` posture therefore reports `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_RELEASE`, not the delinquency.

The proposal's fiscal period must also be `OPEN`, and that check is not part of this ladder at all: as the fiscal rung of §2.9 it is evaluated *before* the terminal-state and `AWARDED` guards and therefore before all four money gates, so a release aimed at a `SUSPENDED` proposal in a `CLOSED` period reports `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`. When the request survives, the buckets are filled strictly in `due_date` ascending order: each bucket absorbs the lesser of the amount still to place and its own remaining headroom, its `consumed_release_cents` rises by that fill, and the walk stops as soon as nothing is left to place. `request_amount_cents` moves from `unreleased_cents` to `released_cents`, so that `unreleased_cents` becomes the awarded amount less `released_cents` less `returned_cents`. The fiscal period's `reserve_balance_cents` is **credited** by the released amount and its `reserve_months_ratio_bps` and `sustainability_posture` are recomputed, which means a large release can improve the posture that governs the next release.

**Delinquency and the suspension clawback.** The daily sweep moves any `SCHEDULED` report whose `due_date` is strictly earlier than the logical clock's date to `OVERDUE`, and that single move sets off a four-hop cascade. First the report becomes `OVERDUE`. Second, its proposal's `compliance_flag` becomes `DELINQUENT` and, if the proposal is in `AWARDED`, the proposal itself moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `REPORT_DELINQUENCY`; the clawback then adds `released_cents` to `returned_cents`, sets `released_cents` to 0, and **debits** the fiscal period's `reserve_balance_cents` by the same amount, all while preserving the ledger invariant. What the clawback does *not* touch is the buckets: every report keeps the `consumed_release_cents` it had accumulated, so a suspended grant still shows which obligations the withdrawn principal had been drawn against and can still have its reports submitted against those figures. Only reinstatement clears them. Third, because the proposal left `AWARDED`, the funder's `reporting_reliability_bps` drops by 2000 floored at 0 and its `independence_risk_score` is recomputed; if that new score reaches 60 and the funder is still in `PROSPECT` or `CLEARED`, the funder moves to `RESTRICTED` with `restricted_at` stamped from the logical clock — so the cooling-off window starts running from the sweep — and the whole funder-restriction cascade of §3.4 runs from its second hop onward, which can suspend the funder's *other* awards in turn; a funder already sitting in `RESTRICTED` is left as it is, sets no cascade off a second time, and adds nothing to `funders_restricted`. Fourth, the proposal's fiscal period recomputes every sustainability field and its coverage area recomputes `restricted_grant_count`. A proposal can also arrive in `SUSPENDED` from the funder-restriction cascade, in which case `suspended_reason` is `FIREWALL_BREACH`; the clawback is identical.

`SUSPENDED` is not terminal. A `FinanceController` reinstates the award once the breach or the delinquency is cured, with no request body. The proposal must be `SUSPENDED`, the funder must be back in status `CLEARED`, a restricted proposal's beat must have `publication_gate` `OPEN`, and `compliance_flag` must be `COMPLIANT`. Reinstatement is the exact compensating action for the clawback: `returned_cents` is added back to `unreleased_cents` and set to 0, **every** report's `consumed_release_cents` is reset to 0 and the waterfall is re-run over the buckets, `suspended_reason` is cleared, and the beat's `restricted_grant_count` and the period's sustainability fields are recomputed. Note the asymmetry with the suspension: suspension moves money from `released_cents`, reinstatement returns it to `unreleased_cents`, so a reinstated grant must be released again from the beginning.

**Closing out an award.** A `FinanceController` closes an `AWARDED` proposal, optionally recording a `closure_justification`. Every report of the proposal must be in `ACCEPTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `WAIVED` — an obligation still sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `OVERDUE` blocks closure with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and either `unreleased_cents` must already be 0 or a non-empty `closure_justification` must be supplied, its absence being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and an empty string not counting as a justification. Closure waives every still-`SCHEDULED` report, adds any remaining `unreleased_cents` to `returned_cents` and zeroes it, recomputes the fiscal period's sustainability fields and the beat's `restricted_grant_count`, and leaves the proposal in the terminal status `CLOSED`. A waived report keeps its bucket capacity, so a reinstated or re-examined award's total stays reconcilable against the sum of its buckets.

**Submitting and reviewing a report.** A `DevelopmentOfficer` submits a report from `SCHEDULED`, `OVERDUE` or `RETURNED`, declaring `spend_reported_cents`, which is required and must be an integer of at least 0 — a zero-spend report is perfectly legal, a negative figure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `ACCEPTED` and `WAIVED` are the report's two terminal statuses, so a submission or a review addressed at a report in either of them reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, while a submission of an already-`SUBMITTED` report reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The proposal must be in `AWARDED` or `SUSPENDED` — a suspended grant still owes its reports — and a proposal in any other status refuses the submission with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, ranked below the report's own terminal and state guards. Three gates apply, in this order. The declared spend may not exceed the report's own `consumed_release_cents`, because a newsroom cannot report spending money it never received: `ERR_SPEND_EXCEEDS_RELEASE` (HTTP 422) — a gate that sits at the §2.9 spend rung, which is above every state guard on this endpoint, so a report already in `ACCEPTED`, `WAIVED` or `SUBMITTED` that is handed an impossible spend reports `ERR_SPEND_EXCEEDS_RELEASE` and not `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Reports must be submitted in order, meaning every report of the same proposal with a lower `sequence_no` must already be in `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`: `ERR_REPORT_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409). And the reporting period must have elapsed, meaning the logical clock's date must be on or after `period_end`: `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (HTTP 409). Submission stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock and computes `variance_bps` as 10000 times the difference between `spend_reported_cents` and `consumed_release_cents`, divided by `consumed_release_cents`, rounded half up — a signed figure, negative when the newsroom underspent — and defined as 0 when `consumed_release_cents` is 0.

A `FinanceController` then reviews a `SUBMITTED` report with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` and an optional `review_note`. `ACCEPT` requires the absolute value of `variance_bps` to be no more than 2000; a wider swing must be returned for rework instead, and accepting it is refused with `ERR_VARIANCE_UNACCEPTABLE` (HTTP 409). Acceptance is terminal for the report. Either decision recomputes the proposal's `compliance_flag` from the reports still sitting in `OVERDUE`, and that recomputation is what finally lifts a `DELINQUENT` flag: submitting an overdue report is not enough on its own, because submission leaves the flag alone, but the review that follows clears it — `RETURN` just as much as `ACCEPT`, since the report has by then left `OVERDUE` either way — unless another report of the same proposal is still `OVERDUE`. `RETURN` moves the report to `RETURNED` and clears `spend_reported_cents` and `variance_bps` back to null, closing the `SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` revision cycle, which is unbounded.

### 3.8. Collaborative Investigations and the Conservation of Shared Cost

**Opening a collaboration.** An `EditorialDirector` opens a collaborative reporting project on a beat by naming `coverage_area_id`, `fiscal_period_id`, a `title`, the production window as `window_start` and `window_end` — both ISO 8601 UTC **instants** rather than date-only values, though a date-only string is accepted and read as midnight UTC of that day, and both are stored and returned in the millisecond instant form of §2.6 — the `total_cost_cents` to be split — at least 1 — and the newsroom's own two shares, `lead_credit_bps` for byline credit and `lead_cost_share_bps` for money. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, and the window is the one **half-open** interval in this specification: `window_start` is inclusive and `window_end` is exclusive. The window must lie inside its fiscal period, meaning `window_start` on or after the period's `start_date` and `window_end` on or before its `end_date`; otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). That containment test compares calendar dates, not instants — only the date part of each bound is taken — so a `window_end` at any time of day on the period's own `end_date` lies inside the period even though the interval is half-open, and this check follows the date-pair ordering check. A new collaboration opens in `DRAFT` with `partner_count` at 0, `lead_cost_cents`, `allocated_cost_cents` and `syndication_revenue_cents` at 0, `settled_at` null, and `lead_cost_share_bps` seeded from the request. Collaborations are read individually or listed with optional `coverage_area_id`, `fiscal_period_id` and `status` filters, ordered by `window_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending.

**Enrolling partner newsrooms.** A partner is added under its collaboration with a `partner_org_name`, a `credit_share_bps` and a `cost_share_bps`, each of the two shares being between 1 and 10000 — a partner with a zero share is not a partner. The organisation name must be unique among the collaboration's **`CONFIRMED`** partners, so a name freed by a withdrawal may be used again; a clash is `ERR_PARTNER_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). Partners may be added while the collaboration is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` — an attempt against a `SETTLED` or `DISSOLVED` collaboration is `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` — and adding one to an `ACTIVE` collaboration immediately re-runs the cost split. A new partner is `CONFIRMED` with `cost_owed_cents` and `remainder_rank` at 0 and `withdrawn_at` null; where the split runs inside that same request, the row the create returns already carries the `cost_owed_cents` and `remainder_rank` the split assigned it, and it is still at `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to its own `created_at`, because a create is one single mutation however much derivation runs inside it. Meanwhile the collaboration's `partner_count` — the count of `CONFIRMED` partners only — rises by 1 along with its `version`. Partners are read individually or as a sub-resource of their collaboration ordered by `created_at` ascending with an optional `status` filter; that sub-resource read is the discovery channel for the allocation the split writes.

**Driving the lifecycle.** A single action-bearing operation carries an `action` of `ACTIVATE`, `SETTLE` or `DISSOLVE`.

`ACTIVATE` moves a `DRAFT` collaboration to `ACTIVE` and is where the arithmetic is checked. In ascending priority order: no other `ACTIVE` collaboration on the same beat may have a half-open window overlapping this one — adjacency is expressly permitted, so a window starting at the exact instant a previous one ended is legal — otherwise `ERR_COLLABORATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409); credit shares must close at exactly 10000 bps, that is `lead_credit_bps` plus the `credit_share_bps` of every `CONFIRMED` partner, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_SHARE_SUM_INVALID` (HTTP 409); cost shares must likewise close at exactly 10000 bps, otherwise `ERR_COST_SHARE_SUM_INVALID` (HTTP 409); and at least one `CONFIRMED` partner must exist, otherwise `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_PARTNER` (HTTP 409). The beat's `publication_gate` must be `OPEN`, a check that outranks all four of the arithmetic gates just listed, so a `BLOCKED` beat reports `ERR_PUBLICATION_GATE_BLOCKED` however badly the shares fail to close. And the fiscal period must be `OPEN`, which — being the fiscal rung of §2.9 — outranks even the terminal-state and declared-transition guards, so any of the three actions aimed at a collaboration whose period is `REVIEW_LOCKED` or `CLOSED` reports `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED` first, including one aimed at an already-`SETTLED` collaboration. Activation then runs the cost split.

`SETTLE` moves an `ACTIVE` collaboration to the terminal status `SETTLED`. The production window must have closed, meaning the logical clock is at or after `window_end` — a collaboration whose window is still open is refused with `ERR_COLLABORATION_WINDOW_OPEN` (HTTP 409) — and `allocated_cost_cents` must equal `total_cost_cents`, a reconciliation that fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, checked after the window-closed gate. Settlement stamps `settled_at` with the logical clock and recomputes `syndication_revenue_cents` as the sum of `amount_cents` over every `SETTLED` contribution on the `SYNDICATION` revenue stream pointing at this collaboration.

`DISSOLVE` is legal from either `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` and lands the collaboration in the terminal status `DISSOLVED`. Every `CONFIRMED` partner moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `cost_owed_cents` reset to 0 — but not `remainder_rank`, which a dissolution leaves untouched, unlike a single partner's withdrawal, so each dissolved-out partner keeps the rank its last split gave it — and their shares fold into the lead — each partner's `credit_share_bps` is added to `lead_credit_bps` and each partner's `cost_share_bps` is added to `lead_cost_share_bps` — after which `lead_cost_cents` and `allocated_cost_cents` are recomputed so that `allocated_cost_cents` still equals `total_cost_cents`. Because the shares closed at 10000 bps, dissolving leaves the newsroom carrying the entire cost.

**The largest-remainder cost split.** Whenever the split runs — on activation, on adding a partner to an `ACTIVE` collaboration, and on a withdrawal — it apportions `total_cost_cents` across the lead plus every `CONFIRMED` partner. `WITHDRAWN` partners are excluded from the split entirely. The participants are the lead, whose share is `lead_cost_share_bps` and whose sort key is the collaboration's own `created_at`, followed by the `CONFIRMED` partner rows in `created_at` ascending order. For each participant, the exact value is `total_cost_cents` multiplied by that participant's share in bps divided by 10000, held as an **exact rational and not rounded**; the participant's floor is that value truncated downward and its remainder is the fractional part left over. The leftover to distribute is `total_cost_cents` minus the sum of all the floors, a quantity that is always at least 0 and strictly less than the number of participants. Participants are ranked by remainder descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the first *leftover* ranked participants each receive exactly one extra cent. A partner's `cost_owed_cents` is its floor plus that possible extra cent; the lead's resulting value is `lead_cost_cents`; and `remainder_rank` is the participant's 1-based position in the ranking, stored on partner rows only. `allocated_cost_cents` is `lead_cost_cents` plus the sum of the `cost_owed_cents` of every `CONFIRMED` partner, and the invariant is that this equals `total_cost_cents` exactly whenever the participating shares close at exactly 10000 basis points — which activation enforces, so it holds for every collaboration from `ACTIVE` onward, and it survives every withdrawal and the dissolution because both fold the departing shares back into the lead. A split run over a `DRAFT` collaboration whose shares do not yet close is not refused; it simply apportions the shares it is given, and `allocated_cost_cents` then lands wherever that apportionment falls. Rounding each share independently with round half up is **forbidden**, because independently rounded shares do not sum to the total.

**Withdrawal.** A `CONFIRMED` partner of a `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` collaboration may withdraw, optionally recording a `withdrawal_reason`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for a partner row, so withdrawing the same partner twice reports `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`; a still-`CONFIRMED` partner of a collaboration that has moved on to `SETTLED` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` instead, ranked immediately below that terminal check, and no period check runs on this endpoint at all, so a partner may withdraw out of a collaboration whose fiscal period is locked. An `ACTIVE` collaboration must retain at least one `CONFIRMED` partner, so the last remaining partner cannot withdraw — the collaboration must be dissolved instead — and the attempt is refused with `ERR_LAST_PARTNER_WITHDRAWAL` (HTTP 409). Withdrawal is the compensating action for adding a partner and the conservation invariant must hold both before and after it. The partner moves to `WITHDRAWN`, `withdrawn_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and its `cost_owed_cents` and `remainder_rank` are reset to 0; its `credit_share_bps` is added to the collaboration's `lead_credit_bps` and its `cost_share_bps` to `lead_cost_share_bps`, so the two sums still close at 10000 bps; the split is re-run over the remaining `CONFIRMED` participants; `partner_count` decrements; and both the collaboration's and the partner's `version` increment by 1. A withdrawn partner's historical share of the money is *not* preserved on the row — its `cost_owed_cents` is zeroed — and it is excluded from every subsequent split.

### 3.9. The Contribution Ledger: Two Independent Dimensions

**Recording revenue.** Incoming money is recorded as a contribution naming `fiscal_period_id`, a `revenue_stream` of `MEMBERSHIP`, `MAJOR_GIFT`, `EVENTS` or `SYNDICATION`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, and a `received_date` that must fall inside the fiscal period's inclusive date range — a `received_date` outside it is a shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not one of the date-pair violations of §2.6, and it is reported ahead of the period's own lockedness. Four optional foreign keys — `member_id`, `funder_id`, `gift_offer_id` and `collaboration_id` — plus a free-text `source_label` carry the source. Which of those keys is required is decided by the stream: `MEMBERSHIP` requires `member_id` and forbids `funder_id`; `MAJOR_GIFT` requires `funder_id` and forbids `member_id`; `SYNDICATION` requires `collaboration_id`; and `EVENTS` forbids `gift_offer_id`. A mismatch is `ERR_STREAM_SOURCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and it outranks the tier-minimum check named below, so a `MEMBERSHIP` row that both carries a `funder_id` and falls short of its tier reports the mismatch. `MAJOR_GIFT` rows are in ordinary operation written by the gift-acceptance cascade of §3.6, but nothing forbids recording one directly on this endpoint under the same source rules; and whichever stream carries a `funder_id`, that funder may not be in status `RESTRICTED`, which is `ERR_FUNDER_RESTRICTED` and is ranked ahead of the source rules per §2.9. The `source_label` carries the descriptive source for `EVENTS` revenue and for transparency listings. A `MEMBERSHIP` contribution requires its member to be in `PENDING` (the enrolment payment that drives activation), `ACTIVE` or `GRACE` — `LAPSED` and `CANCELLED` members reject it with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` — and its `amount_cents` must be at least the member's tier `contribution_amount_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRIBUTION_BELOW_TIER_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422). A `Member`-role caller may record their own contribution and read their own, but may not list contributions at all. The list read carries every foreign-key filter plus `revenue_stream`, `settlement_status` and `acknowledgment_status` filters, ordered by `received_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and is the only discovery channel for the contributions gift acceptance creates.

**The deductible split.** At creation — and for the `MAJOR_GIFT` row the gift-acceptance cascade writes — the contribution's `quid_pro_quo_cents` and `deductible_cents` are computed from its stream. `EVENTS` and `SYNDICATION` are earned revenue and never deductible: both fields are 0. A `MAJOR_GIFT` carries no benefits, so `quid_pro_quo_cents` is 0 and `deductible_cents` is the whole `amount_cents`. A `MEMBERSHIP` contribution takes `quid_pro_quo_cents` from the `quid_pro_quo_per_period_cents` of the tier the member references *at the moment of evaluation*, and `deductible_cents` is `amount_cents` minus that value floored at 0. Both operands are integers, so no rounding arises. The split is recomputed by a tier change, but only while `acknowledgment_status` is `UNACKNOWLEDGED`: rows already `RECEIPTED` or `DISCLOSED` are excluded from recomputation, because a receipt already told the donor a number.

**Two dimensions that move independently.** A contribution carries two orthogonal state fields. `settlement_status` tracks money movement through `INITIATED`, `SETTLED`, `FAILED` and `REFUNDED`; `acknowledgment_status` tracks donor acknowledgment through `UNACKNOWLEDGED`, `RECEIPTED` and `DISCLOSED`. They advance on their own schedules: a contribution may be `RECEIPTED` while its money is still merely `INITIATED`, and a `REFUNDED` contribution keeps whatever receipt record it had. `FAILED` and `REFUNDED` are terminal on the settlement dimension, and `DISCLOSED` is terminal on the acknowledgment dimension.

**Settlement.** A `FinanceController` resolves an `INITIATED` contribution with an `outcome` of `SETTLED` or `FAILED`. The fiscal period must be `OPEN` for either outcome, not only for the settling one. `FAILED` recognizes nothing and leaves a `PENDING` member `PENDING`. `SETTLED` is where money becomes real: a `MEMBERSHIP` settlement recomputes the member's `lifetime_giving_cents`, `is_major_donor` and `cultivation_locked` and, for a `PENDING` member, sets `cycle_start_date` to the contribution's `received_date` and runs the renewal schedule of §3.3 to activate them; a `MAJOR_GIFT` settlement recomputes the funder's `lifetime_committed_cents`; a `SYNDICATION` settlement recomputes the collaboration's `syndication_revenue_cents`; and every settlement re-derives all nine sustainability fields of the fiscal period on the recognized basis.

**Refund as compensating action.** A `SETTLED` contribution may be refunded by a `FinanceController` with an optional `refund_reason`, while the fiscal period is `OPEN`. The refund window is bounded by the *other* dimension: a refund is permitted only while `acknowledgment_status` is `UNACKNOWLEDGED` or `RECEIPTED`, because a `DISCLOSED` contribution has entered public transparency reporting and is frozen — the refusal is `ERR_REFUND_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The refund runs a four-hop cascade. `settlement_status` becomes `REFUNDED` and `refunded_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Because the contribution left `SETTLED`, the member's `lifetime_giving_cents` drops by `amount_cents` and `is_major_donor` and `cultivation_locked` are recomputed — but, as §3.3 establishes, both are latches and **never un-latch**, so a member who crossed the major-donor threshold stays a major donor and stays cultivation-locked after the money is returned. Because the member is no longer counted, the linked funder — the member's own `linked_funder_id` whenever the refunded row names a `member_id` at all, and otherwise the row's own `funder_id`, as on a `MAJOR_GIFT` row — recomputes `lifetime_committed_cents`; a refunded `SYNDICATION` row additionally recomputes its collaboration's `syndication_revenue_cents`. And because those source totals changed, the fiscal period recomputes `recognized_revenue_cents`, `stream_shares_bps`, `concentration_hhi`, `top_source_share_bps`, `reserve_months_target`, `operating_reserve_target_cents`, `reserve_months_ratio_bps`, `sustainability_posture` and `restricted_release_cap_pct_bps`. `acknowledgment_status` and `receipt_number` are left untouched.

**Acknowledgment.** A `DevelopmentOfficer` advances the acknowledgment dimension with a boolean `disclose_publicly`. With `disclose_publicly` false, an `UNACKNOWLEDGED` contribution whose `settlement_status` is anything other than `FAILED` becomes `RECEIPTED`: it is assigned a `receipt_number` of the literal `RCPT-` followed by a zero-padded six-digit counter drawn from one server-wide sequence that starts at 1, advances by exactly 1 for each receipt issued in the order the acknowledgment calls arrive, and is never reset for a new fiscal period, and its `quid_pro_quo_cents` and `deductible_cents` are frozen against later tier changes. With `disclose_publicly` true, a `RECEIPTED` contribution becomes `DISCLOSED` and appears in the beat's transparency report; this requires `settlement_status` to be exactly `SETTLED` — that refusal, like the refusal to receipt a `FAILED` row, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and for a `MAJOR_GIFT` row whose originating offer had a `designation` other than `UNRESTRICTED`, an `ACTIVE` firewall disclosure must exist for the pair of that offer's `funder_id` and `coverage_area_id`, or the publication is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`; a `MAJOR_GIFT` row carrying no `gift_offer_id` has no such precondition. The two calls are ordered: publishing a contribution that was never receipted is not possible.

**Revenue diversification.** Every settlement, refund, award, decline, suspension, reinstatement and closure, plus the fiscal-period lock and the sustainability read itself, re-derives the period's concentration picture. Five stream totals are computed over the period. `MEMBERSHIP`, `MAJOR_GIFT`, `EVENTS` and `SYNDICATION` are each the sum of `amount_cents` over the period's contributions on that stream whose `settlement_status` is in the basis's settled set. `GRANTS` is the sum of `awarded_amount_cents` over the period's grant proposals in status `AWARDED` or `CLOSED`, plus the basis's pipeline term. Every other proposal status — `SUSPENDED`, `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `SUBMITTED`, `CLEARED`, `DRAFT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` and `INDEPENDENCE_REVIEW` — contributes 0 on the recognized basis, which is why a suspension moves money *out* of recognized revenue.

`recognized_revenue_cents` is the sum of those five totals. Each entry of `stream_shares_bps` is 0 when the total is 0, and otherwise 10000 times that stream's revenue divided by the total, rounded half up. `concentration_hhi` is the sum of the squares of the five share figures, divided by 10000, with round half up applied **once at the very end and never per term** — a whole number between 0 and 10000. `top_source_share_bps` measures the largest single **institutional** source: for each funder, that funder's source total is the sum of `amount_cents` over the period's contributions carrying its `funder_id` whose settlement status is in the settled set, plus the sum of `awarded_amount_cents` over the period's proposals of that funder in status `AWARDED` or `CLOSED`, plus the basis's per-funder pipeline term; `top_source_share_bps` is then 0 when the total is 0 and otherwise 10000 times the largest of those source totals divided by the total, rounded half up. Members are retail sources and are excluded from this measure by policy, so a single enormous member never raises `top_source_share_bps`. Ties for the largest source do not matter because only the value is reported.

The calculation has two bases. The **recognized** basis is the persisted one: its settled set is exactly `SETTLED`, both pipeline terms are 0, and it counts cash and signed awards only. Its results are written to the fiscal period's `recognized_revenue_cents`, `stream_shares_bps`, `concentration_hhi` and `top_source_share_bps` on every triggering mutation, and are what the sustainability read replays when `basis` is omitted or `RECOGNIZED`. The **pipeline** basis is a read-only projection that is never persisted: its settled set is `SETTLED` **and** `INITIATED`, and its grant pipeline term is the sum, over the period's proposals in status `SUBMITTED`, of `requested_amount_cents` multiplied by `award_probability_bps` divided by 10000 and rounded half up — the same term also entering each funder's source total. Identical stored rows therefore yield a different `concentration_hhi`, a different `top_source_share_bps` and possibly a different `reserve_months_target` on the two bases.

**Reserve policy posture.** Immediately after every diversification evaluation, and on the sustainability read, the reserve posture is derived in a **pinned order** in which each step consumes only the steps above it. First `concentration_hhi` and then `top_source_share_bps`, both from the diversification calculation on the *same* basis. Third, `reserve_months_target` starts at a base of 3 months and gains one month if `concentration_hhi` is at least 3000 and one further month if `top_source_share_bps` is at least 3500 — so the target is 3, 4 or 5 months. Fourth, `operating_reserve_target_cents` is `monthly_expense_base_cents` multiplied by `reserve_months_target`. Fifth, `reserve_months_ratio_bps` is 10000 times `reserve_balance_cents` divided by `monthly_expense_base_cents`, rounded half up — measured against one month of expenses, not against the target. Sixth, `sustainability_posture` is decided by the **first matching branch** of an ordered ladder: the posture is `CRITICAL` if `reserve_balance_cents` is strictly below `monthly_expense_base_cents`; otherwise `CONSTRAINED` if `reserve_balance_cents` is strictly below `operating_reserve_target_cents` divided by 2 and rounded half up — a balance exactly equal to that half-target is **not** constrained; otherwise `WATCH` if `reserve_balance_cents` is strictly below `operating_reserve_target_cents` **or** `concentration_hhi` is at least 4000, so a fully funded reserve can still sit at `WATCH` on concentration alone; otherwise `HEALTHY`. Seventh, `restricted_release_cap_pct_bps` is read off the posture: 10000 for `HEALTHY`, 6000 for `WATCH`, 2500 for `CONSTRAINED` and 0 for `CRITICAL`. The inputs are the period's own `monthly_expense_base_cents` and `reserve_balance_cents` together with the concentration outputs of the same basis, and the balance used is the value **before** the current request's release or clawback is applied — the posture is recomputed from the post-mutation balance afterwards, so a release is always capped by the pre-release posture.

**Reading the picture.** The sustainability read of a fiscal period accepts an optional `basis`. Omitted or `RECOGNIZED` replays the persisted values; `PIPELINE` returns the probability-weighted projection and persists nothing. The response carries `basis`, `recognized_revenue_cents`, the five stream shares as `membership_share_bps`, `major_gift_share_bps`, `grants_share_bps`, `events_share_bps` and `syndication_share_bps`, then `concentration_hhi`, `top_source_share_bps`, `reserve_months_target`, `operating_reserve_target_cents`, `reserve_months_ratio_bps`, `sustainability_posture` and `restricted_release_cap_pct_bps`.
