# Drone Pilot Certification & Airspace Authorization API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Recreational and small commercial drone flying has outgrown the paper-and-goodwill model that used to govern it. A club flyer with a sub-250-gram toy and a survey contractor with a 12-kilogram multirotor now share the same low-altitude airspace, the same controlled-airspace gateways around airports, and the same temporary flight restrictions that appear over a stadium or a wildfire with a few hours' notice. What differs is the rule set each of them operates under, the evidence each must hold before taking off, and the financial exposure each carries when something goes wrong. Today that evidence is scattered across knowledge-test certificates, registration cards, broadcast-module receipts, waiver letters, insurance binders and hand-written logbooks, and nobody — least of all the pilot on the field — can reliably answer the only question that matters: *am I legal to fly this airframe, in this airspace, at this altitude, right now?*

This document specifies the Drone Operations Service, a REST API that makes that question answerable and auditable. The service owns the whole compliance chain: it registers pilots and moves them through knowledge testing into a certificate that expires; it tracks currency separately from certification, because a commercial operator who has not flown in three months is no longer current even though their certificate is valid for another year; it registers airframes and verifies their remote-identification broadcast modules; it publishes airspace zones and temporary flight restrictions; it adjudicates controlled-airspace authorization requests against a finite number of concurrent slots per zone; it processes waiver applications for flights over people, at night and beyond visual line of sight, each backed by a reviewed safety case; it verifies liability insurance and selects the policy that covers a given commercial mission; and it records every flight, every pre-flight sweep, every accident and every settlement paid out of a policy's coverage.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is that eligibility is evaluated at the last possible moment and re-evaluated at every gate: a clearance obtained an hour ago is worthless if the pilot's currency lapsed, the broadcast module's verification expired, or a restriction was published in the meantime, so the service re-runs its gates at launch and cancels or lapses the dependent records itself rather than trusting a stale flag. The second is that authority is deliberately divided. The officer who registers airframes cannot approve airspace; the reviewer who clears a safety case cannot then grant the waiver; the authority who approved a flight's airspace access cannot close that flight's accident investigation. Every rule below exists to make one of those two commitments enforceable, and the whole surface is mounted under the `/drone-ops` path prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint in the service: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, the logical clock, resource identity, reference resolution, optimistic concurrency, cascade semantics, arithmetic precision, the conserved quantities the service must never let drift, and the precedence order in which all validations run.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, or present but empty or whitespace-only, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six roles below, or names a role that is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- **`Pilot`** — the operator. Pilots own airframes, broadcast modules, waiver applications and their safety cases, insurance filings, airspace authorization requests, flights and the accident reports filed against their own flights. A pilot may read and act only on their own resources.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — certification registrar and airworthiness authority. Registers pilots, schedules and grades knowledge tests, reviews currency, registers, grounds and returns airframes to service, adjudicates broadcast-module verification, and verifies insurance filings. Holds the suspension, reinstatement and revocation powers. Cannot approve airspace, review or decide waivers, or investigate accidents.
- **`AirspaceAuthority`** — publishes airspace zones and temporary flight restrictions and decides controlled-airspace authorization requests, which is where zone occupancy slots are consumed and released. Cannot certify pilots, touch waivers, or close accident investigations.
- **`SafetyReviewer`** — takes submitted waiver applications under review, sends them back for revision inside the revision budget, and clears the safety case. Barred by design from deciding the waiver whose safety case they cleared.
- **`WaiverApprover`** — grants or denies cleared waivers, writing the granted ceiling and the validity window. Cannot review or clear safety cases.
- **`InvestigationOfficer`** — opens accident investigations, drives the remediation loop, closes reports with the itemised final settlement, and withdraws reports filed in error. Cannot certify pilots, approve airspace or decide waivers.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the pilot role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Pilot`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Pilot` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Pilot` compare the resource's own pilot reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, that means `Aircraft.owner_pilot_id` for an airframe, the `owner_pilot_id` of the parent airframe for a broadcast module, `Waiver.pilot_id` for a waiver and the parent waiver's pilot for a safety case, `AirspaceAuthorization.pilot_id` for an authorization, `Flight.pilot_id` for a flight and its pre-flight checks, and the pilot of the referenced flight for an accident report or an insurance policy. The same comparison applies to the body: any `pilot_id` or `owner_pilot_id` a pilot supplies in a request payload must equal their own `X-User-ID`. A mismatch on either the addressed resource or the submitted body is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `AirspaceAuthority`, `SafetyReviewer`, `WaiverApprover` or `InvestigationOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server does record it where a transition says so — `AirspaceAuthorization.approved_by_user_id`, `Waiver.approved_by_user_id`, `SafetyCase.cleared_by_user_id`, `AccidentReport.closed_by_user_id` and `TemporaryFlightRestriction.published_by_user_id` all hold the `X-User-ID` of the operator who performed the step — but those recorded values exist to support segregation of duties, not ownership.

Segregation of duties is enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against one of those recorded operator identifiers, and both checks return `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A `WaiverApprover` deciding a waiver must have an `X-User-ID` that differs from the `cleared_by_user_id` recorded on that waiver's safety case. An `InvestigationOfficer` closing an accident report whose flight cites an airspace authorization must have an `X-User-ID` that differs from that authorization's `approved_by_user_id`; when the flight cites no authorization, there is nothing to compare and the check passes.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 to 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current window.

Unless an endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. There is exactly one deliberate exception in this service: the pilot roster is returned newest-first, ordered by `created_at` descending, because the registrar's working view is the pilots who just enrolled. Every other collection — knowledge tests, certifications, airframes, broadcast modules, airspace zones, temporary flight restrictions, airspace authorizations, waivers, safety cases, insurance policies, flights, pre-flight checks and accident reports — is `created_at` ascending.

Filters offered on a list endpoint narrow the result set before pagination is applied. The pilot roster filters on `status` and `operator_category`; knowledge tests on `pilot_id` and `status`; certifications on `pilot_id`, `originating_test_id`, `supersedes_certification_id` and `status`; airframes on `owner_pilot_id` and `registration_status`; broadcast modules on `aircraft_id` and `status`; zones on `class_code`; restrictions on `zone_id` and `status`; authorizations on `pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, `zone_id`, `waiver_id` and `status`; waivers on `pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, `waiver_type` and `status`; safety cases on `waiver_id` and `review_status`; policies on `pilot_id` and `coverage_status`; flights on `pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, `zone_id`, `authorization_id`, `waiver_id`, `covering_policy_id`, `status` and `investigation_status`; and accident reports on `flight_id`, `policy_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Time in this service is explicit, not ambient. The server maintains a single logical UTC clock whose value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back on real wall-clock time for any decision. Every deadline, currency window, certificate expiry, module validity window, restriction window, authorization window and turnaround comparison is evaluated against this logical clock, referred to throughout this document as the current instant.

An operator role advances the clock by supplying a target instant in the `to_iso8601` field; the response reports the two values `current_utc` (the clock after the move) and `previous_utc` (the clock before it). The clock is monotonic and forward-only: a target instant that is at or before the present clock value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 409), so the supplied instant must be strictly later than the current one. Advancing the clock writes no entity rows at all — nothing lapses, expires or cascades at the moment of the move. Instead, every stale record is discovered lazily, at the next gate that reads it, and is transitioned there; §2.8 lists which gates perform which lazy transition.

All datetimes the service accepts and emits are ISO 8601 UTC instants at whole-second precision with a literal `Z` suffix and no fractional part — for example `2026-06-13T12:00:00Z`. This applies to every stored datetime field, including `scheduled_at`, `graded_at`, `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `certified_at`, `currency_expires_at`, `last_completed_flight_end`, `verified_at`, `verified_until`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `decided_at`, `valid_until`, `effective_from`, `effective_until`, `planned_start`, `planned_end`, `actual_start`, `actual_end`, `occurred_at` and `checked_at`. The `api_request_timestamp` inside the envelope's `meta` object is the one exception and keeps the three fractional digits shown in §2.2.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries a `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation taken from the logical clock.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current instant on every mutation of that row, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. A mutation includes writing a derived field: when a completed flight pushes a pilot's `currency_expires_at` forward, or an approval bumps a zone's `reserved_slot_count`, that is a mutation of the pilot and of the zone respectively, and each one increments the version of the row it touched. The service exposes `version` on reads precisely so clients can echo it back on the two optimistically concurrent writes described in §2.7.

`PreflightCheck` is the single exception. A pre-flight check record is an immutable audit artefact: once appended it is never modified, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither key appears in its representation.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Identifiers embedded in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A request that names a pilot and then a nested resource under that pilot fails with the pilot's not-found code before the nested identifier is examined at all. Each entity has its own dedicated code, all of them HTTP 404: `ERR_PILOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KNOWLEDGE_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFETY_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_TFR_NOT_FOUND`.

The same catalogue governs references supplied in a request body. Every reference a payload carries — `pilot_id`, `owner_pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, `zone_id`, `authorization_id`, `waiver_id`, `flight_id`, and each id listed in `acknowledged_tfr_ids` — must resolve to an existing row, and an unresolvable reference returns the code naming the entity that could not be found. A body reference to a missing airframe therefore yields `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_FOUND`, and an acknowledged restriction id that names no restriction yields `ERR_TFR_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Zones and on Pilots

Two write operations in this service are optimistically concurrent, and both are concurrent on a **parent** row rather than on the row being written. No other endpoint accepts or requires a version field of any kind.

The first is the creation of an airspace authorization request, which is concurrent on the target `AirspaceZone`. The request body must carry `expected_zone_version` — the field is required, not optional — holding the `version` the client last read from that zone. If it does not equal the zone's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ZONE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The point of the check is that such a request would otherwise succeed on its own terms: the zone still shows nominal capacity and the pilot is eligible, but the client's picture of the zone's occupancy predates another writer who has already lodged or had approved a request that consumed part of that capacity and advanced the zone's version. Because approvals, revocations, expiries and consumptions all bump the zone, a client that wants to lodge a request must read the zone immediately beforehand.

The second is the creation of a waiver application, which is concurrent on the applicant `Pilot`. The request body must carry `expected_pilot_version` — again required, not optional — holding the `version` the client last read from that pilot. A value that does not equal the pilot's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PILOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the stale read is dangerous because creating a waiver of a `waiver_type` the pilot already holds silently supersedes the prior `APPROVED` waiver of that type; a client working from a stale pilot has an out-of-date view of `open_waiver_count` and of which waiver it is about to displace. A grading, a currency review, a waiver decision, a completed flight or any cascade that touches the pilot will have advanced the pilot's version and invalidated the client's read.

### 2.8. Cascading Consistency and Lazy State Discovery

A single operator action in this service routinely mutates rows several levels away from the one addressed, and the propagation is transitive: each link fires *because the previous link mutated something*, not because the original caller asked for it. Five cascades exist, and each of them processes its victims in `created_at` ascending order so that the resulting sequence of versions is deterministic.

**Suspending a pilot** is the deepest. The pilot moves to `SUSPENDED` and `suspension_reason` is recorded; because the pilot is suspended, every airframe they own whose `registration_status` is `REGISTERED` moves to `GROUNDED` with `grounding_reason` set to `OWNER_SUSPENDED`; because each of those airframes is now grounded, every airspace authorization bound to one of them whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `REVOKED`, and each such revocation decrements that zone's `reserved_slot_count` by 1 and increments the zone's `version` by 1; and because those authorizations are revoked, every flight citing one of them whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED`. What the cascade deliberately leaves alone matters as much as what it touches: flights already `COMPLETED` or `IN_PROGRESS` are untouched, authorizations already `DENIED`, `CONSUMED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` are untouched, and airframes still in `PENDING_REGISTRATION` are untouched.

**Grounding a single airframe** directly runs the same lower two links: the airframe's `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` authorizations move to `REVOKED`, each releasing one slot and bumping the zone's version, and the `PLANNED` or `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` flights citing those authorizations move to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED`.

**Revoking one authorization** cancels only the flights that cited it, again limited to those in `PLANNED` or `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` and again writing `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED` into `cancellation_reason`. A flight already `IN_PROGRESS` when its authorization is revoked stays `IN_PROGRESS` — the aircraft is in the air and the record must reflect that — while the zone slot is released immediately.

**Filing an accident report** is the second depth-four cascade. The report is created; because the report exists, the named flight's `investigation_status` moves from `NONE` to `OPEN` while its operational `status` is left exactly as it was; because the report holds a provisional reserve, the covering policy's `open_reserved_cents_total` rises and its `remaining_coverage_cents` falls by that reserve, bumping the policy's `version`; and because the policy's remaining cover may now be too thin for commercial work, a policy that drops below the commercial minimum moves from `ACTIVE` to `INSUFFICIENT` — that demotion is a *further* mutation of the same policy row and therefore bumps the policy's `version` a second time, so a filing that both erodes the ledger and demotes the policy advances that policy's `version` by 2 in total, while a filing that only erodes it advances it by 1 — which in turn cancels every `PLANNED` commercial flight covered by that policy with `cancellation_reason` set to `COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. Note the asymmetry with the suspension cascade: this last link takes only flights in `PLANNED`, leaving `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` flights alone.

**Revoking a pilot** terminates the whole holding. Every certification of that pilot in `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED` moves to `REVOKED`; every airframe of that pilot in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `REGISTERED` or `GROUNDED` moves to `DEREGISTERED` with `grounding_reason` set to `OWNER_REVOKED`; and every waiver of that pilot in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CLEARED` or `APPROVED` moves to `REVOKED`.

Cascades are one-way. Reinstating a suspended pilot does not return their grounded airframes to service — each airframe must be individually returned. Returning an airframe to service does not resurrect the authorizations that were revoked when it was grounded. A policy climbing back from `INSUFFICIENT` to `ACTIVE` does not un-cancel the flights that were cancelled while it was under-covered. Lifting a restriction does not resurrect the flights it blocked, though subsequent pre-flight sweeps stop reporting it.

Alongside the cascades, the service performs **lazy state discovery**: because advancing the clock writes nothing, a row that has gone stale is discovered and transitioned by the next gate that reads it, and that transition happens *before* the gate's own verdict is formed. The pre-flight sweep and the launch gate each perform four such transitions: a broadcast module in `VERIFIED` whose `verified_until` is at or before the current instant moves to `EXPIRED`; an authorization in `APPROVED` whose `window_end` is at or before the current instant moves to `EXPIRED`, releasing its zone slot and bumping the zone's version; a restriction in `ACTIVE` whose `effective_until` is at or before the current instant moves to `EXPIRED`; and a pilot in `CERTIFIED` whose recomputed currency has run out moves to `LAPSED`. Airframe registration and return-to-service perform the module expiry and then reject the call with `ERR_REMOTE_ID_EXPIRED`. Currency review expires certifications whose `expires_at` has passed and moves the pilot between `CERTIFIED` and `LAPSED`. Flight creation expires insurance policies whose `effective_until` has passed while the covering-policy selector walks the pilot's policies.

### 2.9. Arithmetic, Cents and Rounding Discipline

There is no floating-point money in this service. Every monetary quantity — `coverage_limit_cents`, `deductible_cents`, `remaining_coverage_cents`, `open_reserved_cents_total`, `settled_cents_total`, `reported_property_damage_cents`, `reserved_cents`, `settlement_cents` and the `amount_cents` of each damage line item — is a whole number of cents, and every one of them is a non-negative integer. Where a rate or factor multiplies a cent figure, the product is rounded to a whole number of cents by the **round-half-up** rule (a result of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, upward) **at the point in the calculation where the multiplication occurs, never later**. Section 3.8 makes the ordering explicit for the settlement waterfall, where rounding each covered line before summing yields a different total from summing first and rounding once, and only the former is correct.

Altitudes are whole feet above ground level throughout — `class_ceiling_ft`, `max_altitude_ft`, `requested_ceiling_ft`, `granted_ceiling_ft`, `planned_max_altitude_ft`, `max_altitude_reached_ft`, `max_permitted_altitude_ft` and `effective_ceiling_ft` — and because every input is an integer, no rounding ever arises in the ceiling arithmetic. Knowledge-test scores are integers from 0 to 100 inclusive. `sail_level` is an integer from 1 to 6 inclusive. `risk_score` is an integer clamped to the range 0 to 100 inclusive. `logged_minutes` is a whole number of minutes obtained by discarding any part-minute remainder, that is by rounding down. `revision_round` and `remediation_round` are integer counters. Coordinates are decimal degrees: `launch_latitude` between -90.0 and 90.0 inclusive and `launch_longitude` between -180.0 and 180.0 inclusive. The only fractional number the service reports is `average_duration_minutes`, carried to one decimal place under the same round-half-up rule.

Threshold comparisons are stated with their inclusivity in the workflow sections and must be honoured exactly. Where this document says a value must be *at least* a bound, the bound itself qualifies; where it says *strictly above*, it does not.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities

Two quantities in this service are conserved, and the service must be able to demonstrate the conservation at any moment because both are exposed on reads.

The first is **airspace occupancy on a zone**. A zone's `reserved_slot_count` must at all times equal the number of airspace authorizations on that zone whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`. Authorizations in `REQUESTED`, `DENIED`, `CONSUMED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` contribute nothing. The count is never negative. Approval is the only action that adds a slot, and each approval is undone by exactly one of three mutually exclusive terminations of the same row — revocation, consumption at flight completion, or expiry when the window closes — never by two of them. Consequently, for any zone and any authorization on it, the slot count after approving and then revoking that authorization equals the slot count before the approval, exactly.

The second is **insured money on a policy**. A policy's `coverage_limit_cents` must at all times equal the sum of its `remaining_coverage_cents`, its `open_reserved_cents_total` and its `settled_cents_total`, where `open_reserved_cents_total` is the sum of `reserved_cents` over the accident reports against that policy whose `status` is `FILED`, `INVESTIGATING` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, and `settled_cents_total` is the sum of `settlement_cents` over the reports against that policy whose `status` is `CLOSED`. Reports in `WITHDRAWN` contribute nothing to either sum. The three terms add up to the limit with zero drift: filing a report that reserves an amount and then withdrawing that report returns `remaining_coverage_cents` to precisely its pre-filing value and leaves `settled_cents_total` untouched.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When a single request could fail several ways, the outcome is not a matter of implementation taste. The service evaluates checks in the fixed order below — a strictly lower rung always wins, and the first rung that fails determines both the `error_id` and the HTTP status. The ordering is what makes a stale-version rejection visible instead of being masked by a capacity check, and what makes an authorization gate surface before an altitude gate. The rungs, in evaluation order:

1. **Header presence**, on every endpoint: `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and are non-empty, non-whitespace strings — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role admissibility**, on every endpoint: `X-User-Role` names one of the six declared roles and that role appears in the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Pilot ownership**, on every endpoint a `Pilot` may call: `X-User-ID` equals the owning pilot of the addressed resource and equals any `pilot_id` or `owner_pilot_id` in the body — otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403).
4. **Waiver-decision segregation**, on `POST /drone-ops/waivers/{id}/decide` only: `X-User-ID` differs from the `cleared_by_user_id` of the waiver's safety case — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
5. **Accident-close segregation**, on `POST /drone-ops/accident-reports/{id}/close` only: when the report's flight cites an authorization, `X-User-ID` differs from that authorization's `approved_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
6. **Path existence**, on every endpoint carrying a path id: each id resolves, left to right, with the code naming the entity of the failing segment as catalogued in §2.6 — for example `ERR_PILOT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
7. **Body shape**, on every endpoint: all required fields present, every enum value inside its declared member set, every integer inside its declared range, every string non-blank, arrays well-formed, and `limit` within 1 to 100 with `offset` not negative — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
8. **Interval ordering**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`, `POST /drone-ops/flights`, `POST /drone-ops/insurance-policies` and `POST /drone-ops/tfrs`: `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, `planned_end` strictly after `planned_start`, `effective_until` strictly after `effective_from`, and on flight creation `planned_start` at or after the current instant — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (400).
9. **Body reference existence**, on every creating endpoint carrying references: `pilot_id`, `owner_pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, `zone_id`, `authorization_id`, `waiver_id` and `flight_id` each resolve, with the code naming the unresolved entity — for example `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
10. **Uniqueness**, each with its own code, all HTTP 409: `email` unique across all pilots (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PILOT_EMAIL`) on `POST /drone-ops/pilots`; `serial_number` unique within `owner_pilot_id` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_AIRCRAFT_SERIAL`) on `POST /drone-ops/aircraft`; `module_serial` unique within `aircraft_id` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MODULE_SERIAL`) on `POST /drone-ops/aircraft/{id}/remote-id-modules`; `designator` unique across all zones (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ZONE_DESIGNATOR`) on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-zones`; and `policy_number` unique within `pilot_id` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_NUMBER`) on `POST /drone-ops/insurance-policies`.
11. **Zone optimistic concurrency**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations` only: `expected_zone_version` equals the zone's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_ZONE_VERSION` (412).
12. **Pilot optimistic concurrency**, on `POST /drone-ops/waivers` only: `expected_pilot_version` equals the pilot's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PILOT_VERSION` (412).
13. **Declared source state**, on every state-changing endpoint: the addressed resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared, with the code naming the entity whose state blocks it — `ERR_PILOT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TEST_ALREADY_GRADED`, `ERR_MODULE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AIRCRAFT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_WAIVER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FLIGHT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_POLICY_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_TFR_INVALID_STATE`, each HTTP 409. There is exactly one carve-out: on `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch`, a flight whose `status` is `PLANNED` is **not** rejected at this rung but is passed down to rung 14, which owns the clearance message; every other launch-time status (`IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`) is rejected here with `ERR_FLIGHT_INVALID_STATE`.
14. **Pre-flight clearance**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch` only: the flight's `status` is `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED`. A flight still in `PLANNED` — whether because its most recent check failed or because it has never been checked — is rejected here with `ERR_PREFLIGHT_NOT_CLEARED` (409).
15. **Pilot suspension gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/aircraft`, `POST /drone-ops/aircraft/{id}/register`, `POST /drone-ops/aircraft/{id}/return-to-service`, `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`, `POST /drone-ops/waivers`, `POST /drone-ops/flights` and `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch`: the acting or target pilot's `status` is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `REVOKED` — otherwise `ERR_PILOT_SUSPENDED` (409).
16. **Certification gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`, `POST /drone-ops/waivers/{id}/submit`, `POST /drone-ops/flights`, `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch` and `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations/{id}/decide`: the pilot's `status` is `CERTIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_PILOT_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409).
17. **Currency gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`, `POST /drone-ops/flights` and `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch`: the pilot's recomputed `currency_expires_at` is strictly after the current instant, and on flight creation strictly after the flight's `planned_end` as well. A `CERTIFIED` pilot found lapsed here is first transitioned to `LAPSED` and the call is then rejected with `ERR_PILOT_CURRENCY_LAPSED` (409).
18. **Registration gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`, `POST /drone-ops/flights`, `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch` and `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations/{id}/decide`: the airframe's `registration_status` is `REGISTERED`; `PENDING_REGISTRATION` and `DEREGISTERED` airframes are rejected with `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_REGISTERED` (409).
19. **Grounding gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`, `POST /drone-ops/flights` and `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch`: the airframe's `registration_status` is not `GROUNDED` — otherwise `ERR_AIRCRAFT_GROUNDED` (409).
20. **Remote-ID presence**, on `POST /drone-ops/aircraft/{id}/register`, `POST /drone-ops/aircraft/{id}/return-to-service` and `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch`: when the airframe's `remote_id_required` is true, at least one of its broadcast modules is in `VERIFIED` **or in `EXPIRED`**. A module sitting in `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFICATION_PENDING` or `VERIFICATION_FAILED`, or the absence of any module row, fails here with `ERR_REMOTE_ID_NOT_VERIFIED` (422). `EXPIRED` is deliberately absent from that list: an expired module counts as *present* for this rung and its verdict belongs to rung 21, which is what keeps rung 21 reachable and makes the code independent of whether the §2.8 lazy module expiry has already moved a stale `VERIFIED` module to `EXPIRED` earlier in the same request.
21. **Remote-ID validity**, on the same three endpoints as rung 20: at least one of the airframe's modules is in `VERIFIED` with a `verified_until` strictly after the current instant. When none is, every module of that airframe still sitting in `VERIFIED` is first transitioned to `EXPIRED` and the call is then rejected with `ERR_REMOTE_ID_EXPIRED` (422); a module that is already in `EXPIRED` — whether from an earlier request or from the §2.8 lazy expiry of this one — yields the same code here, with nothing left to transition.
22. **Restriction gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights/{id}/launch` only: for every restriction on the flight's zone whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose window overlaps the flight's planned window, the restriction must be non-blocking — its `max_permitted_altitude_ft` is not null **and** the flight's `planned_max_altitude_ft` is at most that cap. A null `max_permitted_altitude_ft` is a total ban that no acknowledgement can clear. A blocking restriction yields `ERR_TFR_ACTIVE` (422).
23. **Special-operation waiver gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` only: when exactly one of `over_people`, `night_operation` or `beyond_visual_line_of_sight` is true, `waiver_id` must be supplied and must reference a waiver of the matching `waiver_type` in `status` `APPROVED`, belonging to the same `pilot_id` and `aircraft_id`, whose `valid_until` is strictly after `planned_end` — otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED` (422). Setting more than one of the three flags on a single flight is a shape error caught earlier, at rung 7.
24. **Controlled-airspace gate**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` only: when the zone's `requires_authorization` is true, `authorization_id` must be supplied and must reference an authorization in `status` `APPROVED` whose `pilot_id`, `aircraft_id` and `zone_id` all match the flight's — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED` (422).
25. **Authorization window containment**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` only: the flight's `planned_start` is at or after the authorization's `window_start` and its `planned_end` is strictly before the authorization's `window_end` — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_WINDOW_MISMATCH` (422).
26. **Altitude ceiling**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations` and `POST /drone-ops/flights`: on an authorization request, `requested_ceiling_ft` is at most the smaller of the zone's `class_ceiling_ft` and the airframe's `max_altitude_ft`; on flight creation, `planned_max_altitude_ft` is at most the flight's effective ceiling, which is the cited authorization's `granted_ceiling_ft` when an `authorization_id` is supplied and 400 feet otherwise. A breach yields `ERR_ALTITUDE_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (422).
27. **Rule-set distinction**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` and `POST /drone-ops/pilots/{id}/knowledge-tests`: a flight whose `operation_category` is `COMMERCIAL` requires a pilot whose `operator_category` is `COMMERCIAL`; a pilot whose `operator_category` is `RECREATIONAL` may sit only the `TRUST_RECREATIONAL` exam and may fly only `RECREATIONAL` operations. A breach yields `ERR_RECREATIONAL_OPERATION_FORBIDDEN` (403).
28. **Zone capacity**, on `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations/{id}/decide` only: the number of overlapping `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` authorizations on the zone is strictly below the zone's `max_concurrent_authorizations` — otherwise `ERR_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
29. **Airframe turnaround**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` only: no other live flight on the same airframe collides with the candidate window once that window is padded on both ends — otherwise `ERR_AIRCRAFT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409).
30. **Safety case presence**, on `POST /drone-ops/waivers/{id}/submit` only: at least one safety case is attached to the waiver — otherwise `ERR_SAFETY_CASE_MISSING` (422).
31. **Safety case acceptability**, on `POST /drone-ops/waivers/{id}/clear-safety-case` only: the safety case's `sail_level` is at most 5 — otherwise `ERR_SAIL_LEVEL_UNACCEPTABLE` (422).
32. **Loop budgets**, on `POST /drone-ops/waivers/{id}/request-revision` and `POST /drone-ops/accident-reports/{id}/require-remediation`: the waiver's `revision_round` is strictly below 2, respectively the report's `remediation_round` is strictly below 2 — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
33. **One live report per flight**, on `POST /drone-ops/accident-reports` only: no report already exists against that `flight_id` in `FILED`, `INVESTIGATING`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` or `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_ACCIDENT_REPORT_DUPLICATE` (409). Only a `WITHDRAWN` report leaves the flight free for a new filing.
34. **Attempt cap**, on `POST /drone-ops/pilots/{id}/knowledge-tests` only: the pilot has fewer than three knowledge tests whose `scheduled_at` falls in the trailing 365 days of the current instant — otherwise `ERR_TEST_ATTEMPT_LIMIT` (409).
35. **Commercial insurance presence**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` and `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`: the pilot holds a policy in `coverage_status` `ACTIVE` spanning the requested window — otherwise `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_ACTIVE` (402). This rung is evaluated for commercial flights and for authorization requests lodged by pilots whose `operator_category` is `COMMERCIAL`.
36. **Client indemnification**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` only: the covering policy's `indemnification_clause_accepted` is true — otherwise `ERR_INDEMNIFICATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` (422).
37. **Coverage adequacy**, on `POST /drone-ops/flights` and `POST /drone-ops/airspace-authorizations`: the covering policy's `remaining_coverage_cents` is at least 1,000,000 cents — otherwise `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (402).
38. **Clock monotonicity**, on `POST /drone-ops/_clock/set_current_utc` only: `to_iso8601` is strictly later than the current clock value — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (409).

Rungs 35, 36 and 37 sit in that order for a reason, and together they pin exactly which of the three insurance codes a rejected commercial flight receives: `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_ACTIVE` when the pilot has no `ACTIVE` policy spanning the window at all, `ERR_INDEMNIFICATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` when such a policy exists but has not accepted the indemnification clause, and `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` when an active, indemnified policy spans the window but its remaining cover is too thin.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Becoming a Certified Remote Pilot

Enrolment is a registrar's act, not a self-service one. A `ComplianceOfficer` creates a pilot record by supplying the operator's `full_name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank), an `email` (which must contain an `@` and is unique across all pilots, compared case-insensitively), and the `operator_category` that fixes the rule set the operator will fly under — either `RECREATIONAL` or `COMMERCIAL`. The new pilot lands in `UNCERTIFIED` with no certificate and no currency: `certified_at` is null, `currency_expires_at` is null, `suspension_reason` is null, and the three derived counters `risk_score`, `knowledge_attempt_count` and `open_waiver_count` all start at 0. `currency_basis` is derived immediately from the declared category — `RECREATIONAL_BASIS` for a recreational operator and `COMMERCIAL_BASIS` for a commercial one — even though there is nothing yet for it to govern. A pilot's `status` moves only through the states `UNCERTIFIED`, `KNOWLEDGE_PENDING`, `CERTIFIED`, `LAPSED`, `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED`, and `REVOKED` is terminal.

Testing begins when the registrar books a sitting for the pilot, supplying the `test_code` and the `scheduled_at` instant the sitting is booked for. Two exams exist and the pilot's category decides which one they may sit: a recreational operator may sit only `TRUST_RECREATIONAL`, and a commercial operator may sit only `PART107_COMMERCIAL`; the wrong pairing is refused with `ERR_RECREATIONAL_OPERATION_FORBIDDEN`. Booking is admissible only from `UNCERTIFIED` or from `LAPSED` — a pilot who is already `KNOWLEDGE_PENDING`, already `CERTIFIED`, or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_PILOT_INVALID_STATE` — and it moves the pilot to `KNOWLEDGE_PENDING`. That the transition is declared from `LAPSED` as well as from `UNCERTIFIED` is what makes recurrent training possible: the loop `CERTIFIED → LAPSED → KNOWLEDGE_PENDING → CERTIFIED` is the intended path back to currency, not an edge case.

Attempts are rationed. At most three sittings may be booked for one pilot inside any trailing 365 days, counted over the pilot's knowledge tests whose `scheduled_at` falls at or after the instant exactly 365 days before the current clock value and at or before the current clock value itself, in any state. A fourth booking inside that window is refused with `ERR_TEST_ATTEMPT_LIMIT`. Each new sitting is stamped with an `attempt_number` equal to one plus the number of knowledge tests that already exist for that pilot in any state, so a pilot's first booking is attempt 1 and the numbering never reuses a value. The sitting is created in `SCHEDULED` with `raw_score`, `passed` and `graded_at` all null. Note that the pilot's `knowledge_attempt_count` counts something narrower — the number of that pilot's knowledge tests whose `status` is `PASSED` or `FAILED` — so booking a sitting leaves it unchanged and grading is what moves it.

Grading is also the registrar's act, and it takes a single input: the `raw_score`, an integer from 0 to 100. The pass mark depends entirely on which exam was sat: the recreational knowledge test is graded against a mark of 100, and the commercial exam against a mark of 70. The comparison is inclusive — a score equal to the mark passes. A score at or above the mark writes `passed` true and moves the sitting from `SCHEDULED` to `PASSED`; a score below the mark writes `passed` false and moves it to `FAILED`. Either way `graded_at` is set to the current instant and both destinations are terminal, so a second grading attempt on the same sitting is refused with `ERR_TEST_ALREADY_GRADED`. A failure returns the pilot from `KNOWLEDGE_PENDING` to `UNCERTIFIED`, issues no certificate, and leaves `currency_expires_at` exactly as it was, which is what allows the `UNCERTIFIED → KNOWLEDGE_PENDING → UNCERTIFIED` cycle to repeat up to the three-attempt ceiling.

A pass sets a longer chain in motion. A certificate is minted in `ACTIVE` for the pilot, with `originating_test_id` pointing at the sitting that produced it, `category` mirroring the pilot's `operator_category` at the moment of issuance, `issued_at` set to the current instant, and a `certificate_number` formatted as the literal prefix `CERT-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded issuance sequence — `CERT-000001` for the first certificate the service ever issues, `CERT-000002` for the second, and so on in `created_at` ascending order across the whole service. Its `expires_at` is the issuance instant advanced by 24 calendar months, that is by two calendar years: the arithmetic is calendar-month arithmetic on the UTC instant, so the day-of-month is clamped down to the last day of the target month when the target month is shorter, and the time-of-day is preserved unchanged. If the pilot already held a certificate in `ACTIVE`, that older certificate moves to `SUPERSEDED` and its `id` is written into the new certificate's `supersedes_certification_id`; a first issuance leaves `supersedes_certification_id` null. The pilot then moves from `KNOWLEDGE_PENDING` to `CERTIFIED`, `certified_at` is set to the new certificate's `issued_at`, and `currency_expires_at` together with `currency_basis` are recomputed as described in §3.2. `knowledge_attempt_count` is recomputed at this point too.

A certificate's own `status` moves through `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED`, with `REVOKED` terminal. Nothing creates a certificate directly — issuance is always a consequence of a passing grade — so reading certificates individually or listing them, optionally narrowed by `pilot_id`, `originating_test_id`, `supersedes_certification_id` or `status`, is the only channel through which a client discovers a certificate's `id` and `certificate_number`.

### 3.2. Currency, Lapse and the Recurrent-Training Cycle

Holding a valid certificate is necessary but not sufficient to fly. Currency is a second, independent clock, and its value depends on which rule set the pilot operates under. The service therefore computes currency in two variants, and records which one it used in `currency_basis`.

Two anchors feed the computation. The **base expiry** is the pilot's single `ACTIVE` certificate's `issued_at` advanced by 24 calendar months, using the same calendar-month arithmetic and day clamping described in §3.1 — it is exactly the `expires_at` stored on that certificate. The **recency expiry** is a recency anchor advanced by 90 days, where the recency anchor is the pilot's `last_completed_flight_end` when the pilot has ever completed a flight, and the certificate's `issued_at` when they have not. `last_completed_flight_end` itself is the greatest `actual_end` over that pilot's flights whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, breaking any tie on `created_at` ascending, and it is null while the pilot has completed no flight.

Under `RECREATIONAL_BASIS`, which applies whenever the pilot's `operator_category` is `RECREATIONAL`, certificate validity alone governs: the projected expiry is the base expiry, and `last_completed_flight_end` is ignored entirely. Under `COMMERCIAL_BASIS`, which applies whenever the `operator_category` is `COMMERCIAL`, both constraints bite and the earlier of the two wins: the projected expiry is the base expiry when the base expiry is the earlier instant, and the recency expiry otherwise. The practical effect is that a commercial operator's two-year certificate is clamped down to ninety days past their last completed flight — or to ninety days past issuance if they have never flown — so identical stored data yields an earlier instant under `COMMERCIAL_BASIS` than under `RECREATIONAL_BASIS` whenever the recency window closes first. When the pilot holds no `ACTIVE` certificate at all, there is no currency to project and `currency_expires_at` is null.

Currency is recomputed and written at four moments: when a passing grade issues a certificate, when a currency review is run, when a flight is completed (a fresh `actual_end` moves the recency anchor forward), and, read-only, when a client asks for a projection. Expiry comparisons against the clock are strict in one direction only: currency has lapsed exactly when `currency_expires_at` is at or before the current instant, and remains valid while it is strictly after it.

A `ComplianceOfficer` runs a **currency review** on a pilot to re-settle these values against the clock. The review rewrites `currency_expires_at` and `currency_basis`; moves the pilot from `CERTIFIED` to `LAPSED` when the recomputed expiry is at or before the current instant; moves the pilot from `LAPSED` back to `CERTIFIED` when the recomputed expiry is strictly after the current instant, which happens once a fresh certificate or a fresh completed flight has pushed the anchor forward; transitions any of that pilot's certificates in `ACTIVE` whose `expires_at` is at or before the current instant to `EXPIRED`; and recomputes `risk_score`. The `CERTIFIED → LAPSED → CERTIFIED` cycle closes here. A review on a pilot who is neither `CERTIFIED` nor `LAPSED` is refused with `ERR_PILOT_INVALID_STATE`.

Because the same review recomputes `risk_score`, and because a lapsed pilot carries a risk penalty (§3.9), a currency review can itself push a pilot over the automatic suspension threshold, at which point the suspension cascade of §2.8 fires from inside the review.

Pilots and officers can also ask the service to project currency **without writing anything**. The projection accepts an optional `basis` selector naming either `RECREATIONAL_BASIS` or `COMMERCIAL_BASIS`, and when it is omitted the basis implied by the pilot's `operator_category` is used. The response reports `projected_expires_at` (the instant the chosen variant yields), `basis_applied` (the basis actually used, as a string), `base_expiry` and `recency_expiry` (both anchors, reported regardless of which one the chosen basis consults), `days_remaining` (the whole number of days from the current instant to `projected_expires_at`, discarding any part-day remainder, and negative when the projection already lies in the past), and `lapse_due` (true exactly when `projected_expires_at` is at or before the current instant). When the pilot holds no `ACTIVE` certificate, `projected_expires_at`, `base_expiry` and `recency_expiry` are null, `days_remaining` is 0 and `lapse_due` is true. This read never mutates the pilot: it is the diagnostic that lets an operator see both variants over the same stored data.

### 3.3. Airframes, Registration and Remote Identification

An airframe is created against its owner: the request carries `owner_pilot_id`, a `model` (1 to 80 characters, not blank), a `serial_number` (1 to 40 characters, unique within that owner — two different pilots may hold the same manufacturer serial, the same pilot may not), a `takeoff_weight_g` (integer 1 to 25000) and a `max_altitude_ft` (integer 1 to 5000, the manufacturer's altitude limit). The airframe begins in `PENDING_REGISTRATION` with `registration_number` null and `grounding_reason` null. `owner_pilot_id` is the only pilot permitted to fly the airframe; ownership is not transferable.

At creation the service computes `remote_id_required` once and never again: it is true exactly when `takeoff_weight_g` is **strictly greater** than 250 grams. An airframe of exactly 250 grams is exempt and may be registered and launched with no broadcast module attached at all; an airframe of 251 grams is not. Because `takeoff_weight_g` cannot be changed after creation, the flag is stable for the airframe's life, but it is *re-read* at every registration, return-to-service and launch gate, which is what makes an expiring module able to block an airframe that was previously flying legally.

A broadcast module is attached to a specific airframe and inherits `aircraft_id` from the path — it is never supplied in the body. The request carries `module_serial` (1 to 40 characters, unique within that airframe), a `broadcast_protocol` of either `WIFI_NAN` or `BLUETOOTH_LE`, a `firmware_version` (1 to 20 characters, not blank), and optionally a `broadcast_sample_ref`. The module is created in `UNVERIFIED` with `verified_at`, `verified_until` and `failure_reason` all null. Its `status` moves through `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFICATION_PENDING`, `VERIFIED`, `VERIFICATION_FAILED` and `EXPIRED`, and no state is terminal — every dead end has a way back into the queue.

The owning pilot submits a captured broadcast sample for adjudication, supplying `broadcast_sample_ref`. Submission is accepted from `UNVERIFIED`, from `VERIFICATION_FAILED` and from `EXPIRED`, and always lands in `VERIFICATION_PENDING`, overwriting any previous `broadcast_sample_ref` and clearing `failure_reason` back to null. A module already in `VERIFICATION_PENDING` or already `VERIFIED` is refused with `ERR_MODULE_INVALID_STATE`. Only the airframe owner may submit; any other pilot is refused for ownership. The re-entry from `VERIFICATION_FAILED` and from `EXPIRED` is the mechanism that makes the module lifecycle cyclic: `VERIFICATION_PENDING → VERIFICATION_FAILED → VERIFICATION_PENDING` for a rejected sample, and `VERIFIED → EXPIRED → VERIFICATION_PENDING` for a module whose validity ran out.

A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates a `VERIFICATION_PENDING` module by supplying an `outcome` of either `VERIFIED` or `FAILED`. An outcome of `VERIFIED` requires `verified_until` to be supplied and to be strictly after the current instant; it moves the module to `VERIFIED`, sets `verified_at` to the current instant, stores `verified_until`, and clears `failure_reason`. An outcome of `FAILED` requires `failure_reason` to be supplied; it moves the module to `VERIFICATION_FAILED`, stores the reason, and leaves both `verified_at` and `verified_until` null. Adjudication is admissible only from `VERIFICATION_PENDING`. Separately and lazily, a module in `VERIFIED` whose `verified_until` is at or before the current instant is moved to `EXPIRED` at the moment a preflight check or a launch reads it (§2.8) — the expiry is discovered, not scheduled.

Registration is a `ComplianceOfficer` act and takes no request body: the service assigns the `registration_number` itself, a non-null marking unique across all airframes, and moves the airframe from `PENDING_REGISTRATION` to `REGISTERED`. Two gates guard it. The owner must not be `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`. And when `remote_id_required` is true, the airframe must have at least one broadcast module in `VERIFIED` whose `verified_until` is strictly after the current instant; if the only candidate module is `VERIFIED` but its `verified_until` has passed, that module is first moved to `EXPIRED` and the registration is then refused with `ERR_REMOTE_ID_EXPIRED` — the lazy transition happens even though the request fails, so a retry sees the module in `EXPIRED` and is refused with `ERR_REMOTE_ID_EXPIRED` again — never with `ERR_REMOTE_ID_NOT_VERIFIED`, because an `EXPIRED` module still counts as a present module for the presence rung (§2.11 rungs 20 and 21) — and the officer knows to demand a fresh sample. An airframe with `remote_id_required` true and no module at all is refused with `ERR_REMOTE_ID_NOT_VERIFIED`.

Grounding takes a `grounding_reason` and moves a `REGISTERED` airframe to `GROUNDED`, and it is deliberately destructive downstream: every airspace authorization bound to that airframe in `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` is revoked, each revocation releasing a zone slot, and every planned or preflight-cleared flight that cited a revoked authorization is cancelled. §2.8 fixes the ordering and the `cancellation_reason` recorded. Return-to-service moves the airframe back from `GROUNDED` to `REGISTERED`, clears `grounding_reason` to null, and re-runs both registration gates — owner not suspended or revoked, and remote-ID compliance re-checked — so an airframe whose module expired while it sat grounded cannot fly again until the module is re-verified. Nothing that grounding destroyed is restored: the revoked authorizations stay revoked, the cancelled flights stay cancelled, and the pilot must request fresh authorizations. `DEREGISTERED` is reachable from all three of `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `REGISTERED` and `GROUNDED`, is written only by the system when the owning pilot is revoked, records `grounding_reason` as `OWNER_REVOKED`, and is terminal.

### 3.4. Airspace Structure, Restrictions and Controlled-Airspace Authorization

An `AirspaceAuthority` publishes a zone with a globally unique `designator` (1 to 30 characters), a `class_code` drawn from `B`, `C`, `D`, `E` and `G`, a `class_ceiling_ft` (integer 100 to 2000) that is the hard regulatory ceiling for that airspace, a `requires_authorization` flag that marks the airspace as controlled, a `max_concurrent_authorizations` capacity (integer 1 to 20), and an optional `structure_relief_eligible` flag that defaults to false. A new zone starts with `reserved_slot_count` 0 and `version` 1. `reserved_slot_count` is a denormalised count of that zone's authorizations in `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`, maintained under the conservation rule of §2.10, and the zone's `version` is the value an authorization request must echo (§2.7).

The same authority publishes temporary flight restrictions over a zone: `zone_id`, a `reason` (1 to 200 characters, not blank), an `effective_from` and an `effective_until` bounding a half-open window, and optionally a `max_permitted_altitude_ft` (integer 0 to 2000). The distinction the optional field draws is the important one: a restriction that names a `max_permitted_altitude_ft` is a partial restriction — flying below that altitude remains permitted once the pilot has acknowledged the restriction — whereas **omitting** it (leaving it null) declares a total ban that no acknowledgement can ever clear. A restriction is created in `ACTIVE` with `published_by_user_id` set to the publishing operator's `X-User-ID`. The authority may lift an `ACTIVE` restriction ahead of its `effective_until`, moving it to `LIFTED`; and a restriction whose `effective_until` is at or before the current instant is lazily moved to `EXPIRED` when a preflight check or a launch reads it. Both `LIFTED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. Lifting a restriction does not resurrect the flights it blocked — it only stops new preflight checks from reporting it.

A pilot requests controlled-airspace access for one airframe over one zone in one window, supplying `pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, `zone_id`, an optional `waiver_id`, a `requested_ceiling_ft` (integer 1 to 2000), a `window_start`, a `window_end` and the `expected_zone_version`. The request is created in `REQUESTED` with `granted_ceiling_ft`, `approved_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `denial_reason` all null, and — this is the point of the design — **no slot is reserved at request time**. `reserved_slot_count` moves only on approval, so a zone can carry an unbounded queue of `REQUESTED` rows and the capacity check is deferred to the decision. The occupation window is half-open: `window_start` is inclusive, `window_end` is exclusive, and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`. An authorization's `status` moves through `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `ACTIVE`, `CONSUMED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`, of which `DENIED`, `CONSUMED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

Request-time validation checks the requesting pilot's eligibility, the airframe's, and the ceiling — but only against the two hard caps. A `requested_ceiling_ft` greater than the lesser of the zone's `class_ceiling_ft` and the airframe's `max_altitude_ft` is refused with `ERR_ALTITUDE_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. Category limits and waiver relief are **not** applied at request time; they clamp the granted value at decision time. The consequence is intentional and visible: a request the service accepts may still be granted a lower ceiling than it asked for, without any error being raised.

The `AirspaceAuthority` decides a `REQUESTED` authorization by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY`, with an optional `denial_reason`. The two branches have deliberately disjoint effects.

A `DENY` requires `denial_reason` to be supplied, moves the authorization to `DENIED`, stores the reason, sets `decided_at` to the current instant, leaves `granted_ceiling_ft` null, and touches neither `reserved_slot_count` nor the zone's `version` — a denial is invisible to every other request in flight against that zone.

An `APPROVE` is admissible only when the authorization is `REQUESTED`, the requesting pilot is `CERTIFIED`, the airframe is `REGISTERED`, and the zone has capacity. Capacity is measured over the requested window rather than over the zone as a whole: the service counts the zone's *other* authorizations — same `zone_id`, different `id` — whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` and whose window overlaps the candidate's, where overlap means the existing `window_start` is strictly before the candidate's `window_end` **and** the existing `window_end` is strictly before neither, that is strictly after the candidate's `window_start`. Rows in `REQUESTED`, `DENIED`, `CONSUMED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are excluded from the count entirely. Approval is permitted only while that count is strictly less than the zone's `max_concurrent_authorizations`; at or above it the decision is refused with `ERR_ZONE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the comparison is strictly half-open, two windows that merely touch — one ending at exactly the instant the other begins — do not collide and may both be approved even in a zone of capacity 1.

Approval then computes the ceiling actually granted, by propagating four constraints and writing the result to `granted_ceiling_ft`. First, two hard caps: the **class cap** is the zone's `class_ceiling_ft`, and the **airframe cap** is the airframe's `max_altitude_ft`. Second, the **waiver cap**: if the authorization named a `waiver_id`, and that waiver's `status` is `APPROVED`, and that waiver's `valid_until` is strictly after the authorization's `window_end`, then the waiver cap is that waiver's `granted_ceiling_ft`; in every other case — no waiver cited, a waiver that is not `APPROVED`, or a waiver whose validity expires at or before the window ends — the waiver cap is 400 feet. Third, the **category cap**, which branches on the pilot's rule set: for a `RECREATIONAL` operator it is 400 feet flat, ignoring the zone's relief flag entirely; for a `COMMERCIAL` operator it is 600 feet when the zone's `structure_relief_eligible` is true and 400 feet when it is false. Fourth, the four are combined: relief is the **greater** of the waiver cap and the category cap, and the granted ceiling is the **least** of the `requested_ceiling_ft`, the class cap, the airframe cap, and that relief figure. All values are integer feet and no rounding arises. The asymmetry is worth stating plainly: structure relief can lift a commercial operator above the base 400 feet, but never a recreational one, and a waiver can lift either — but only if its validity outlasts the whole requested window.

Approval also sets `approved_by_user_id` to the deciding operator's `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` to the current instant, increments the zone's `reserved_slot_count` by one and the zone's `version` by one. That `approved_by_user_id` is retained for a reason far downstream: it is the value the accident-closure segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1 compares against.

An approved authorization is then consumed by the flight that cites it. Launch moves the authorization from `APPROVED` to `ACTIVE`, admissible only while `window_start` is at or before the current instant and the current instant is strictly before `window_end`; slot occupancy is unchanged, because `APPROVED` and `ACTIVE` both hold a slot. Completing the flight moves it from `ACTIVE` to `CONSUMED` and releases the slot, decrementing `reserved_slot_count` and incrementing the zone's `version`. Two other exits release the slot as well. The authority may revoke an authorization in either `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`, optionally recording a `denial_reason`; the slot is released, the zone's `version` advances, and every planned or preflight-cleared flight citing the authorization is cancelled — but a flight already `IN_PROGRESS` is left running, since the aircraft is in the air. And an authorization still in `APPROVED` whose `window_end` is at or before the current instant is lazily moved to `EXPIRED` when a preflight check or a launch reads it, also releasing the slot and advancing the zone's `version`. Cancelling a flight, by contrast, releases nothing: the authorization stays `APPROVED` and keeps its slot, because the pilot may still fly inside the window.

### 3.5. Special-Operation Waivers and the Safety Case Review Loop

Flying over people, at night, or beyond visual line of sight requires a waiver, and a waiver is not simply granted — it is documented, reviewed, possibly sent back, cleared by one officer and then decided by a different one. A pilot opens an application supplying `pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, a `waiver_type` of `OVER_PEOPLE`, `NIGHT` or `BVLOS`, a `requested_ceiling_ft` (integer 1 to 2000), an `operation_description` (1 to 500 characters, not blank) and the `expected_pilot_version`. The application is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0 and with `granted_ceiling_ft`, `valid_until`, `approved_by_user_id` and `denial_reason` all null. Creation also supersedes: if the pilot already holds a waiver in `APPROVED` of the *same* `waiver_type`, that older waiver is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. This is precisely why the request is optimistically concurrent on the pilot — a second application filed concurrently would otherwise silently discard the first's grant. Either way the pilot's `open_waiver_count` is recomputed and the pilot's `version` is incremented, which is what makes the echoed `expected_pilot_version` go stale for any concurrent caller.

A waiver's `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CLEARED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `REVOKED`; `DENIED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `REVOKED` are terminal. `open_waiver_count` is always the count of that pilot's waivers whose `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CLEARED` or `APPROVED` — note that `APPROVED` counts as open, so a decision that approves does not reduce the count while a decision that denies does. Every event that changes a waiver's status recomputes the counter from that definition rather than adjusting it by one.

Safety case documentation is attached to the application while it is in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; attaching to a waiver in any other state is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_INVALID_STATE`. The safety case inherits `waiver_id` from the path and carries a `mitigation_summary` (1 to 1000 characters, not blank), a `ground_risk_class` (integer 1 to 4), an `air_risk_class` of `ARC_A`, `ARC_B`, `ARC_C` or `ARC_D`, and `contingency_procedures` (1 to 1000 characters, not blank). It is created with `review_status` `PENDING`, `reviewer_findings` null and `cleared_by_user_id` null.

At creation the service computes the case's `sail_level`, the specific assurance and integrity level, once. Translate the `air_risk_class` to an index: `ARC_A` is 0, `ARC_B` is 1, `ARC_C` is 2, `ARC_D` is 3. Add a beyond-visual-line-of-sight uplift of 1 when the parent waiver's `waiver_type` is `BVLOS`, and 0 for `OVER_PEOPLE` and `NIGHT`. Sum the `ground_risk_class`, the air-risk index and the uplift, and cap the total at 6 — the result is the `sail_level`, always an integer from 1 to 6. The cap matters because the highest inputs overflow it: ground risk class 4 with `ARC_D` and a beyond-visual-line-of-sight operation sums to 8 and is reported as 6.

The pilot submits the application, which is admissible from `DRAFT` or from `REVISION_REQUESTED` and lands in `SUBMITTED`. Submission requires the pilot to be `CERTIFIED` — a lapsed or suspended pilot cannot push an application forward, which is the cross-entity guard that keeps waiver review tied to live certification — and requires at least one safety case to exist for the waiver, otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_CASE_MISSING`. Resubmission from `REVISION_REQUESTED` additionally returns every safety case of the waiver from `REVISION_REQUESTED` to `PENDING` and clears its `reviewer_findings` back to null, so the reviewer sees a clean slate. `open_waiver_count` is recomputed.

A `SafetyReviewer` takes a `SUBMITTED` application to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and from `UNDER_REVIEW` has exactly two moves. The first is to request a revision, supplying `reviewer_findings`: the waiver returns to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `revision_round` is incremented by one, and every safety case of the waiver moves from `PENDING` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` with the findings stored on it. This is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly less than 2, so an application may be sent back at most twice; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and — importantly — the waiver stays in `UNDER_REVIEW`, leaving the reviewer to either clear it or let the approver deny it. The cycle `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` is the review loop, and it closes at most twice.

The reviewer's second move is to clear the safety case, optionally recording `reviewer_findings`. Clearance requires the `sail_level` to be at most 5; a case at `sail_level` 6 is refused with `ERR_SAIL_LEVEL_UNACCEPTABLE` and can never be cleared, because `sail_level` is fixed at creation and no revision recomputes it — the applicant must file a fresh application with a lower risk classification. Clearance moves the waiver to `CLEARED` and moves every safety case of the waiver from `PENDING` to `CLEARED`, stamping the reviewer's own `X-User-ID` into `cleared_by_user_id`. That stamp is readable through the safety-case reads, which is what makes the segregation-of-duties rule observable to clients rather than a hidden trap.

A `WaiverApprover` then decides a `CLEARED` application by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, with `granted_ceiling_ft` and `denial_reason` as conditional inputs. Both branches require the deciding `X-User-ID` to differ from the safety case's `cleared_by_user_id`; the same human may not both clear and approve, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An `APPROVE` requires `granted_ceiling_ft` to be supplied and to be at most the `requested_ceiling_ft` — an approver may grant less than was asked for but never more, and a missing or larger figure is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It moves the waiver to `APPROVED`, stores the granted ceiling, sets `approved_by_user_id` to the approver's `X-User-ID`, and sets `valid_until` to the current instant advanced by 180 whole days with the time-of-day preserved. A `DENY` requires `denial_reason`, moves the waiver to `DENIED`, and leaves `granted_ceiling_ft` and `valid_until` null. Either outcome recomputes `open_waiver_count` and increments the pilot's `version`.

An approved waiver's relief is consumed in two places, and both comparisons against `valid_until` are strict. An airspace authorization that cites the waiver contributes the waiver's `granted_ceiling_ft` to its ceiling computation only when `valid_until` is strictly after the authorization's `window_end` (§3.4). A flight that cites the waiver requires `valid_until` to be strictly after the flight's `planned_end`, so a flight ending at exactly the instant the waiver expires is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. Finally, revoking a pilot moves that pilot's waivers in any of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CLEARED` and `APPROVED` to `REVOKED` (§2.8).

### 3.6. Insurance Cover and Commercial Indemnification

Recreational flying needs no insurance. Commercial flying does, and the service models cover as a ledger rather than as a flag, because an accident consumes cover and the consumption has to be visible before the next flight is planned.

A policy is filed for a pilot with `insurer_name` (1 to 80 characters, not blank), `policy_number` (1 to 40 characters, unique within that pilot), a `coverage_limit_cents` (integer, at least 0), a `deductible_cents` (integer, at least 0), an `effective_from` and `effective_until` bounding the period of cover, and an `indemnification_clause_accepted` boolean recording whether the client indemnification clause required for commercial work has been accepted. Filing grants nothing: the policy is created in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with all three ledger figures — `open_reserved_cents_total`, `settled_cents_total` and `remaining_coverage_cents` — at 0, so an unverified policy covers no flight even if its limit is enormous. `coverage_status` moves through `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `ACTIVE`, `REJECTED`, `INSUFFICIENT` and `EXPIRED`, with `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` terminal.

A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates the filing with an `outcome` of `ACTIVE` or `REJECTED`, and only from `PENDING_VERIFICATION`. An outcome of `ACTIVE` additionally requires `effective_until` to be strictly after the current instant — a policy whose cover has already run out cannot be activated, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_POLICY_INVALID_STATE`. Activation opens the ledger: `remaining_coverage_cents` becomes `coverage_limit_cents` less `open_reserved_cents_total` less `settled_cents_total`, which for a fresh policy is the full limit. An outcome of `REJECTED` moves the policy to `REJECTED` terminally and leaves `remaining_coverage_cents` at 0.

The three ledger figures are always definitionally derived, never incrementally patched. `open_reserved_cents_total` is the sum of `reserved_cents` over the accident reports filed against this policy whose `status` is `FILED`, `INVESTIGATING` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`. `settled_cents_total` is the sum of `settlement_cents` over the accident reports against this policy whose `status` is `CLOSED`. And `remaining_coverage_cents` is the `coverage_limit_cents` minus both totals. Every event that files, closes or withdraws an accident report recomputes all three from these definitions, which is what keeps the conservation invariant of §2.10 exactly true after every event rather than merely close.

Erosion of the ledger changes the policy's own state. When a filing pushes the recomputed `remaining_coverage_cents` below 1000000 cents, an `ACTIVE` policy moves to `INSUFFICIENT` and every commercial flight in `PLANNED` that named the policy as its cover is cancelled (§2.8). When a closure or a withdrawal restores the recomputed `remaining_coverage_cents` to 1000000 cents or above, an `INSUFFICIENT` policy moves back to `ACTIVE` — the `ACTIVE → INSUFFICIENT → ACTIVE` cycle — but the flights that were cancelled while cover was short are not restored. Independently, a policy in `ACTIVE` or `INSUFFICIENT` whose `effective_until` is at or before the current instant is moved to `EXPIRED` at the moment the covering-policy selector evaluates it during flight creation.

Flight creation is where cover is actually selected, and only for a `COMMERCIAL` operation. The selector runs over the flight's pilot's policies and keeps a policy as a candidate only when *all* of the following hold: its `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE`; its `indemnification_clause_accepted` is true; its `effective_from` is at or before the flight's `planned_start`; its `effective_until` is strictly after the flight's `planned_end`; and its `remaining_coverage_cents` is at least 1000000 cents. Policies in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `REJECTED`, `INSUFFICIENT` or `EXPIRED` are excluded outright, and any policy whose `effective_until` has already passed is first moved to `EXPIRED` and thereby drops out of the candidate set. Among the survivors the selector takes the policy with the **greatest** `remaining_coverage_cents`, breaking a tie on `created_at` ascending and never on `id`, and writes that policy's `id` into the flight's `covering_policy_id`. The choice is deliberately greedy on remaining cover, not on limit and not on recency, so a large policy that has been eroded by an open accident reserve loses to a smaller untouched one.

When no candidate survives, the flight is refused, and which of three errors is returned is pinned by the ladder of §2.11 rather than by the selector: `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_ACTIVE` when the pilot has no `ACTIVE` policy at all; `ERR_INDEMNIFICATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` when an `ACTIVE` policy does cover the window but its `indemnification_clause_accepted` is false; and `ERR_INSURANCE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` when an `ACTIVE`, indemnified policy covers the window but its `remaining_coverage_cents` is below 1000000 cents. A `RECREATIONAL` flight never runs the selector at all and its `covering_policy_id` stays null — none of these three errors can arise on a recreational operation.

### 3.7. Planning, Clearing and Flying a Mission

A flight is planned, checked, launched and completed, and each of those four steps re-tests eligibility against the clock as it stands at that moment. That repetition is the core of the design: a plan that was legal when filed can become illegal before it flies, and the service is built to notice at the last possible moment rather than the first.

Planning carries `pilot_id`, `aircraft_id`, `zone_id`, optionally an `authorization_id` and a `waiver_id`, an `operation_category` of `RECREATIONAL` or `COMMERCIAL`, a `planned_start` and `planned_end`, a `planned_max_altitude_ft` (integer 1 to 2000), a `launch_latitude` (decimal degrees, -90.0 to 90.0) and `launch_longitude` (decimal degrees, -180.0 to 180.0), and the three special-operation flags `over_people`, `night_operation` and `beyond_visual_line_of_sight`, each defaulting to false. The planned window is a **closed** interval — both `planned_start` and `planned_end` are inclusive — and `planned_end` must be strictly after `planned_start`, while `planned_start` must be at or after the current instant, so no flight may be filed into the past. The flight is created in `PLANNED` with `investigation_status` `NONE`, `logged_minutes` 0, `altitude_exceedance` false, and `actual_start`, `actual_end`, `max_altitude_reached_ft`, `incident_occurred`, `incident_summary` and `cancellation_reason` all null. A flight's operational `status` moves through `PLANNED`, `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Creation runs the full eligibility ladder of §2.11 — the pilot must be `CERTIFIED`, current, and not suspended; the airframe must be `REGISTERED`, not grounded, and owned by the flying pilot; each raised special-operation flag must be backed by an `APPROVED` waiver of the matching `waiver_type` cited in `waiver_id` and still valid past `planned_end`; controlled airspace must be backed by an `APPROVED` authorization covering the planned window; and `planned_max_altitude_ft` must sit inside the ceilings. Note that only **one** waiver may be cited per flight, so a flight raising two special-operation flags cannot be backed twice and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Creation also enforces airframe separation, and it is the one interval rule in the service with a buffer. Pad the candidate's window by 30 minutes on each end: the padded start is `planned_start` less 30 minutes and the padded end is `planned_end` plus 30 minutes. The candidate conflicts when some other flight on the same `aircraft_id`, whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, has a `planned_start` at or before the padded end **and** a `planned_end` at or after the padded start. Flights in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` never conflict. Both comparisons are inclusive, so touching endpoints *do* collide: a gap of exactly 30 minutes between two flights of the same airframe conflicts and is refused with `ERR_AIRCRAFT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, while a gap of 31 minutes is clear. This is the deliberate opposite of the zone-slot rule of §3.4, which is strictly half-open and lets touching windows coexist — the two interval conventions must not be conflated.

For a `COMMERCIAL` operation, creation finally selects the covering policy and writes `covering_policy_id` (§3.6); for a `RECREATIONAL` operation it stays null.

Before a flight may launch it must be cleared. The pre-flight check accepts an optional `acknowledged_tfr_ids` array — the restriction ids the pilot declares awareness of, defaulting to an empty array — and appends an **immutable** check record to the flight. That record carries `flight_id` from the path, a `checked_at` instant, a `result` of `PASSED` or `FAILED`, a `blocking_findings` array, an `active_tfr_ids` array, the `acknowledged_tfr_ids` as submitted, an `airspace_class_code` snapshotting the zone's `class_code` at check time, and an `effective_ceiling_ft`. The `effective_ceiling_ft` is the cited authorization's `granted_ceiling_ft` when the flight names an `authorization_id`, and 400 feet otherwise. `active_tfr_ids` holds the ids of the restrictions on the flight's zone that are still `ACTIVE` and whose half-open window overlaps the flight's window, ordered by the restriction's `created_at` ascending. A check record is the only record in the service with no `updated_at` and no `version`, because nothing ever amends it (§2.5), and the only way to read one is to list the checks of its flight.

The check first runs the lazy transitions of §2.8 — an expired module, an expired authorization, an expired restriction, a lapsed pilot — and then evaluates the gates against the post-transition state. Each unmet gate contributes one code to `blocking_findings`, and the array is sorted ascending by code with no duplicates. The seven codes are: `AIRCRAFT_NOT_REGISTERED` when the airframe's `registration_status` is not `REGISTERED`; `AUTHORIZATION_NOT_APPROVED` when the zone's `requires_authorization` is true and either no `authorization_id` is cited or the cited authorization's `status` is not `APPROVED`; `CURRENCY_LAPSED` when the pilot's `status` is not `CERTIFIED` or the recomputed `currency_expires_at` is at or before the current instant; `REMOTE_ID_INVALID` when the airframe's `remote_id_required` is true and it has no module in `VERIFIED` whose `verified_until` is strictly after the current instant; `TFR_TOTAL_BAN` when some overlapping `ACTIVE` restriction blocks the flight outright, meaning its `max_permitted_altitude_ft` is null or is below the flight's `planned_max_altitude_ft`; `TFR_UNACKNOWLEDGED` when some id in `active_tfr_ids` is absent from `acknowledged_tfr_ids`; and `WAIVER_MISSING` when a special-operation flag is raised and no `APPROVED` waiver of the matching type, still valid past `planned_end`, is cited.

The check's `result` is `PASSED` exactly when `blocking_findings` is empty, and `FAILED` otherwise. A `PASSED` check moves the flight from `PLANNED` to `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` and increments the flight's `version`. A `FAILED` check is **not** an error response — it is a recorded advisory returned with a success status: the flight stays in `PLANNED`, the flight's `version` is deliberately *not* incremented, and the pilot may fix the cause and re-check as often as they like, each attempt appending another immutable record. The hard gate lives on launch, not here: a flight still in `PLANNED` at launch time, whether because its last check failed or because it was never checked at all, is refused with `ERR_PREFLIGHT_NOT_CLEARED`.

Launch takes no request body and re-runs every gate against the clock as it now stands: the pilot must be `CERTIFIED` with a recomputed `currency_expires_at` strictly after the current instant; the airframe must be `REGISTERED`; a `remote_id_required` airframe must still have a `VERIFIED` module with `verified_until` strictly after the current instant; no `ACTIVE` restriction on the zone may block the flight, where a restriction blocks unless its `max_permitted_altitude_ft` is non-null **and** the flight's `planned_max_altitude_ft` is at most that value, so a null ceiling is a ban that acknowledgement cannot clear and the refusal is `ERR_TFR_ACTIVE`; and if the flight cites an authorization, that authorization must be `APPROVED` with `window_start` at or before the current instant and the current instant strictly before `window_end`. On success the flight moves from `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, `actual_start` is set to the current instant, and the cited authorization moves from `APPROVED` to `ACTIVE`, keeping its zone slot. The lazy transitions run first here too, so a launch that fails on a stale gate still leaves the stale row correctly transitioned.

Completion carries an `actual_end`, a `max_altitude_reached_ft`, an `incident_occurred` boolean and, when an incident occurred, an `incident_summary`. `actual_end` must be strictly after `actual_start`. The service writes all four fields, then computes `logged_minutes` as the whole minutes between `actual_start` and `actual_end`, discarding any part-minute remainder, and computes `altitude_exceedance` as true exactly when `max_altitude_reached_ft` is strictly greater than the flight's effective ceiling — the cited authorization's `granted_ceiling_ft` when the flight names one, and 400 feet otherwise. The cited authorization moves from `ACTIVE` to `CONSUMED`, releasing the zone slot and advancing the zone's `version`. The pilot's `last_completed_flight_end` is set to the new `actual_end`, `currency_expires_at` is recomputed (§3.2), `risk_score` is recomputed (§3.9) and the pilot's `version` is incremented; when the recomputed `risk_score` reaches 70 the pilot is suspended on the spot and the depth-four suspension cascade of §2.8 fires from inside the completion. A single completed flight can therefore ground the airframe and cancel the pilot's remaining plans.

Cancellation carries a `cancellation_reason` and is admissible from `PLANNED` or `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED`. It moves the flight to `CANCELLED` and releases nothing on the zone, because the slot belongs to the authorization and not to the flight — the cited authorization keeps its `APPROVED` status and its slot, and the pilot may plan a fresh flight inside the same window. The reason is normally the caller's own text, but the cascades of §2.8 write the fixed values `AUTHORIZATION_REVOKED` and `COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` into it.

A pilot's flying record is available as a computed read that writes nothing. It accepts an optional `window_start` and `window_end`; `window_start` defaults to the pilot's `created_at` and `window_end` to the current instant. The source set is that pilot's flights whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `actual_end` falls in the half-open window — at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`. Flights in `PLANNED`, `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely, and the keying is on `actual_end`, not on `planned_end` and not on `created_at`. From that set the response reports `completed_flight_count` (the number of flights), `total_logged_minutes` (the sum of their `logged_minutes`), `max_altitude_reached_ft` (the greatest `max_altitude_reached_ft` among them), `incident_flight_count` (how many have `incident_occurred` true), `exceedance_flight_count` (how many have `altitude_exceedance` true), `average_duration_minutes` (the `total_logged_minutes` divided by the `completed_flight_count`, rounded half-up to one decimal place), and the `window_start` and `window_end` actually applied. The empty-set behaviour is fixed and must not be improvised: when no flight qualifies, every count and total is 0, `max_altitude_reached_ft` is 0, and `average_duration_minutes` is 0.0 — never null, and never a division by zero.

### 3.8. Accidents, Investigation and Settlement

An accident report is filed against a flight, carrying `flight_id`, an `occurred_at` instant, an `injury_level` of `NONE`, `MINOR` or `SERIOUS`, a `reported_property_damage_cents` figure (integer, at least 0) and a `narrative` (1 to 2000 characters, not blank). Filing is admissible only when the flight's operational `status` is `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` and its `investigation_status` is `NONE`; a flight still in `PLANNED` or `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED` has nothing to investigate, and at most one non-withdrawn report may exist per flight — a second is refused with `ERR_ACCIDENT_REPORT_DUPLICATE`. The report is created in `FILED` with `remediation_round` 0, and with `remediation_findings`, `damage_line_items`, `settlement_cents`, `probable_cause` and `closed_by_user_id` all null. Its `policy_id` is **copied** from the flight's `covering_policy_id` at filing and is null when the flight had no covering policy; the copy is deliberate, so that later reselection on the flight cannot move an accident's cover.

A report's `status` moves through `FILED`, `INVESTIGATING`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `CLOSED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `CLOSED` and `WITHDRAWN` terminal. The flight's `investigation_status` is a genuinely parallel dimension: filing moves it from `NONE` to `OPEN` and closure or withdrawal moves it from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`, and **neither ever touches the flight's operational `status`**. A `COMPLETED` flight can be `OPEN`, and an `IN_PROGRESS` flight can be `OPEN` — the aircraft may still be in the air while the paperwork is running.

Filing immediately takes a **provisional reserve** against the covering policy: a hold, not a payment. Select the injury reserve from the `injury_level` — 0 cents for `NONE`, 250000 cents for `MINOR`, 1500000 cents for `SERIOUS`. Multiply the `reported_property_damage_cents` by 1.25 and round half-up to whole cents, then add the injury reserve; that sum is the raw reserve. The report's `reserved_cents` is the lesser of the policy's `remaining_coverage_cents` and that raw reserve — so a reserve can never exceed the cover actually left. When `policy_id` is null there is nothing to hold against and `reserved_cents` is 0. The policy's ledger is then recomputed, which raises `open_reserved_cents_total` and lowers `remaining_coverage_cents` by the reserve, and may push the policy into `INSUFFICIENT` with the flight cancellations that entails (§3.6, §2.8). Filing also recomputes the pilot's `risk_score` and increments the pilot's `version`, and a recomputed score that reaches 70 suspends the pilot on the spot with the depth-four cascade.

An `InvestigationOfficer` opens the formal investigation, moving the report from `FILED` to `INVESTIGATING`; the reserve is untouched, since it was taken at filing. From `INVESTIGATING` the officer may send the report back for operator remediation, supplying `remediation_findings`: the report moves to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` and `remediation_round` is incremented. This is permitted only while `remediation_round` is strictly less than 2, so a report may be sent back at most twice and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, the same code the waiver revision budget uses. The officer returns a `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` report to `INVESTIGATING` once the operator has responded, closing the `INVESTIGATING → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → INVESTIGATING` cycle. The reserve is held untouched throughout the loop, which is why a policy driven into `INSUFFICIENT` by a filing stays there for the whole remediation cycle no matter how many rounds it takes.

Closure is the only path that produces a payment, and it takes `damage_line_items` — an array of objects each carrying a `label` string and an `amount_cents` integer of at least 0, kept in the order supplied — together with a `probable_cause`. Closure is admissible only from `INVESTIGATING`, and it is segregated: when the report's flight cites an airspace authorization, the closing operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from that authorization's `approved_by_user_id`. The officer who granted the airspace may not sign off the accident that happened in it, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The final settlement is computed as a waterfall, and its order is pinned. First, apply coinsurance **per line**: for each entry of `damage_line_items` in the order supplied, multiply its `amount_cents` by 0.85 and round half-up to whole cents. Then sum those rounded line figures to get the subtotal. Rounding each line before summing is required; summing the raw amounts and rounding once at the end is wrong and produces a different cent figure. Second, subtract the policy's `deductible_cents` from the subtotal and floor the result at 0 — this is the net figure. Third, select the surcharge tier **from the net figure**, before any surcharge is added, and never from the post-surcharge total even though the surcharge becomes part of it: a net at or below 500000 cents attracts no surcharge; a net above 500000 cents and at or below 2000000 cents attracts the net multiplied by 0.02, rounded half-up; a net above 2000000 cents attracts the net multiplied by 0.05, rounded half-up. Fourth, add the surcharge to the net to get the uncapped total. Finally, cap: the headroom is the policy's `coverage_limit_cents` less its `settled_cents_total` computed over that policy's *other* reports in `CLOSED` — the report being closed is excluded from that total while the figure is computed — and the report's `settlement_cents` is the lesser of the uncapped total and that headroom.

Closure then writes `settlement_cents`, zeroes `reserved_cents`, and sets `closed_by_user_id` to the closing operator's `X-User-ID`. The policy's `open_reserved_cents_total`, `settled_cents_total` and `remaining_coverage_cents` are recomputed from their definitions, and a policy in `INSUFFICIENT` whose recomputed `remaining_coverage_cents` reaches 1000000 cents returns to `ACTIVE`. The flight's `investigation_status` moves from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`, and the pilot's `risk_score` is recomputed — the open-accident term drops out, so closing a report normally lowers the score. Because the two variants work off different inputs, the same accident yields a different figure at close than it reserved at filing: the reserve inflates a single reported figure by 25 percent and adds a flat injury reserve, while the settlement itemises the damage at 85 percent with per-line rounding, deducts the deductible, adds a tier surcharge and caps against unused limit. They are not expected to agree.

Withdrawal is the compensating action for filing and is admissible from `FILED` or `INVESTIGATING`, optionally recording a `probable_cause`. It moves the report to `WITHDRAWN`, zeroes `reserved_cents`, and leaves `settlement_cents` null — no payment is ever made. The policy's `open_reserved_cents_total` and `remaining_coverage_cents` are recomputed, restoring exactly the pre-filing values with zero drift; a policy in `INSUFFICIENT` returns to `ACTIVE` on the same threshold as closure; the flight's `investigation_status` moves from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`; and the pilot's `risk_score` is recomputed with the pilot's `version` incremented. Flights cancelled while cover was short are not resurrected. Because withdrawal releases the reserve in full and closure replaces it with a settlement, the ledger of §2.10 balances after either exit.

### 3.9. Enforcement: Risk, Suspension, Reinstatement and Revocation

The pilot's `risk_score` is the service's single enforcement signal, and it is recomputed — never adjusted — at six moments: on flight completion, on accident filing, on accident closure, on accident withdrawal, on a currency review, and on reinstatement.

Compute it from four counts over the pilot's own records. The incident count is the number of that pilot's flights whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `incident_occurred` is true. The exceedance count is the number whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `altitude_exceedance` is true. The open-accident count is the number of accident reports against that pilot's flights whose `status` is `FILED`, `INVESTIGATING` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`; reports in `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded. The clean-flight count is the number of that pilot's flights whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `incident_occurred` and `altitude_exceedance` are **both** false. Flights in `PLANNED`, `PREFLIGHT_CLEARED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `CANCELLED` never contribute to any of the four counts.

Then take the raw score as 25 times the incident count, plus 15 times the exceedance count, plus 30 times the open-accident count, plus a lapsed-currency term of 10 when the pilot's `status` is `LAPSED` and 0 otherwise, minus 5 times the clean-flight count. Clamp that raw value into the range 0 to 100 — negative raws become 0 and raws above 100 become 100 — and the clamped integer is the `risk_score`. Note that a flight with an incident *and* an exceedance contributes both weights, 40 in total, and is not a clean flight; and that clean flying decays the score, so a pilot can work a bad record back down.

Suspension fires when the recomputed `risk_score` reaches 70. The comparison is inclusive: exactly 70 suspends. The automatic path applies only to a pilot whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` or `LAPSED`, and it records `suspension_reason` as `RISK_THRESHOLD_REACHED`. A `ComplianceOfficer` may also suspend such a pilot manually, supplying the `suspension_reason` text. Either way the pilot moves to `SUSPENDED` and the depth-four cascade of §2.8 fires: the pilot's registered airframes are grounded, the live authorizations on those airframes are revoked with their zone slots released, and the planned or preflight-cleared flights depending on those authorizations are cancelled. A suspended pilot can do nothing that requires certification: every gate that demands `CERTIFIED` refuses them, and `ERR_PILOT_SUSPENDED` is the refusal the ladder of §2.11 reaches first.

Reinstatement lifts the suspension and is admissible only from `SUSPENDED`. It has two substantive preconditions. Every accident report filed against any flight of this pilot must be in `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN` — an open investigation blocks reinstatement, refused with `ERR_PILOT_INVALID_STATE`. And the pilot's recomputed `currency_expires_at` must be strictly after the current instant, so a pilot whose currency lapsed while suspended must first regain currency; the refusal is again `ERR_PILOT_INVALID_STATE`, since both are unmet preconditions of the reinstatement transition rather than gates of the ladder. On success the pilot returns to `CERTIFIED`, `suspension_reason` is cleared to null, `risk_score` is recomputed from the definition above, and the pilot's `version` is incremented. Because reinstatement requires every accident report to be `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN`, the open-accident term is necessarily zero at that moment, and because the pilot is being returned to `CERTIFIED` the lapsed-currency term is zero too — so the recomputed score reflects only historical incidents and exceedances net of clean-flight decay. The automatic suspension rule is **not** re-fired by reinstatement: a pilot whose historical record still scores 70 or more is reinstated anyway, rather than bouncing straight back into `SUSPENDED`. Reinstatement is also deliberately narrow in scope: grounded airframes are **not** returned to service automatically and must each be returned individually, and cancelled flights are never resurrected.

Revocation is the end of the line and is admissible only from `SUSPENDED` — a `CERTIFIED` pilot cannot be revoked directly, they must be suspended first. It takes a `suspension_reason`, which overwrites whatever reason the suspension recorded, and moves the pilot to `REVOKED`, which is terminal. It then terminates everything the pilot holds, in the order fixed by §2.8: every certification of theirs in `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED` becomes `REVOKED`; every airframe of theirs in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `REGISTERED` or `GROUNDED` becomes `DEREGISTERED` with `grounding_reason` set to `OWNER_REVOKED`; and every waiver of theirs in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `CLEARED` or `APPROVED` becomes `REVOKED`. Note that revocation reaches airframes still in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, which the suspension cascade leaves alone, and that no transition anywhere returns a pilot from `REVOKED`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are mounted under the `/drone-ops` prefix. Creation endpoints that bring a new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201; every other successful call responds with HTTP 200. Thirteen endpoints are creations and therefore return 201: pilot registration, knowledge-test scheduling, airframe creation, broadcast-module attachment, zone publication, restriction publication, authorization request, waiver application, safety-case attachment, policy filing, flight planning, the pre-flight check, and accident-report filing. Note that the pre-flight check returns 201 even when its `result` is `FAILED`, because a new immutable check record was created — a failed check is a recorded advisory, not an error. All other state transitions, including every decision, verification, cascade-triggering enforcement action and computed read, return 200. Responses carry the affected resource in `data` under the envelope of §2.2; the two computed reads return their own field sets, and the clock endpoints return `current_utc` and `previous_utc`.
