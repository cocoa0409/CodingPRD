# Earnings Disclosure Committee Process API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every quarter a public registrant has to convert a closed set of books into a filed periodic report, and every quarter the same failure modes recur: a sub-certification that nobody chased, a non-GAAP measure that was presented ahead of the figure it is supposed to bridge to, an earnings-call answer that wandered into forward-looking territory without a safe-harbor legend, a reported line item that never got tied back to the underlying accounting record. The disclosure committee exists to catch all of that before the filing goes out, and the officers who sign the certifications depend entirely on the committee's paper trail. Today that paper trail lives in spreadsheets and mail threads, which is why the same control deficiencies keep surfacing in post-filing corrections.

This document specifies the Earnings Disclosure Committee Process API: a single system of record for the reporting calendar, the committee charter and its seats, the sub-certification collection that supports the officer certifications, the review and tie-out of the draft earnings release and periodic filing, the reconciliation and prominence discipline applied to adjusted measures, the assessment of between-period events for current-report triggers, the preparation of the earnings-call script and question bank, and — after the filing — the correction, restatement-evaluation and control-deficiency remediation workflow. The service is mounted under `/disclosure`, with one additional administrative endpoint outside that prefix for driving the server's logical clock during testing.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, the system is a **gate machine**: nothing advances because a person says it is ready, it advances because every declared precondition on the transition is satisfied, and each precondition maps to exactly one error identifier so that a blocked cycle can always explain itself. Second, every derived quantity — slack days, coverage percentages, variance in basis points, deficiency risk weight — is recomputed in full from its source rows on every touch rather than adjusted incrementally, so that a compensating action such as withdrawing a sub-certification or rescinding a correction returns the affected roll-up to exactly the value it held before.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Committee Seats, Roles and Request Authentication

Every request to every endpoint, reads included, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, empty, or made up entirely of whitespace the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names declared below, or is a declared role that the called endpoint does not list as authorised, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ComplianceAdmin`, `DisclosureCommitteeChair`, `Controller`, `LegalCounsel`, `InvestorRelations`, `CertifyingOfficer` and `BusinessLeader`.

`ComplianceAdmin` is the securities-compliance operations function. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and never resolves it to a committee seat.

The other six roles are all seat-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `DisclosureCommitteeChair`, `Controller`, `LegalCounsel`, `InvestorRelations`, `CertifyingOfficer` or `BusinessLeader`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CommitteeMember` row; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references a committee member compare that resource's member reference to `X-User-ID`. If `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing `CommitteeMember` row on an endpoint whose authorised-role list includes at least one seat-backed role, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403) — the same code used for an unrecognised or unauthorised role, since from the caller's perspective the credential simply is not usable here.

A seat-backed credential must additionally satisfy that role's **function gate**, evaluated against the acting member's own record. `Controller` requires that the acting member's `function` is `FINANCE`. `LegalCounsel` requires `LEGAL`. `InvestorRelations` requires `INVESTOR_RELATIONS`. `CertifyingOfficer` requires that the acting member's `is_officer` flag is true. `DisclosureCommitteeChair` requires that the acting member's `voting` flag is true. `BusinessLeader` accepts a member of any function. A credential that fails its function gate is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_FUNCTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Several endpoints accept an `action` discriminator and multiplex more than one transition, so authorisation is checked a second time against the specific action requested. On a document, `REQUEST_CHANGES` and `APPROVE` are open to `Controller` and `LegalCounsel` only, while `SUBMIT_REVIEW` additionally accepts `InvestorRelations`. On a reporting cycle, `DECLARE_FILING_READY` is reserved to `DisclosureCommitteeChair`, while `OPEN_CLOSE`, `OPEN_REVIEW`, `OPEN_CERTIFICATION` and `ABANDON` accept either `Controller` or `DisclosureCommitteeChair`. On a sub-certification, `SUBMIT` is reserved to `BusinessLeader` and `WITHDRAW` to `DisclosureCommitteeChair`. A role that is listed on the endpoint but not permitted to invoke the supplied `action` is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_ACTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

The complete action vocabulary, endpoint by endpoint, is this. A charter accepts `ADOPT` and `SUPERSEDE`. A committee member accepts `RECUSE`, `REINSTATE` and `VACATE`. A reporting cycle accepts `OPEN_CLOSE`, `OPEN_REVIEW`, `OPEN_CERTIFICATION`, `DECLARE_FILING_READY` and `ABANDON`. A milestone accepts `START` and `COMPLETE`. A sub-certification request accepts `SUBMIT` and `WITHDRAW`. A draft document accepts `SUBMIT_REVIEW`, `REQUEST_CHANGES` and `APPROVE`. A material event accepts `ASSESS` and `DISCLOSE`. A post-filing correction accepts `ASSESS` and `RESCIND`. Any other value in the `action` field is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, that prefix being followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.6 rather than from wall-clock time, so two responses produced while the clock sits still carry the same value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` is an integer with a default of 10, a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 200. `offset` is an integer with a default of 0 and a minimum of 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 200, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422). A parameter that is supplied but empty or blank counts as a non-integer and is rejected the same way, while whitespace surrounding an otherwise valid integer is tolerated and a parameter left off the query string entirely simply takes its default.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain after the current window. Whatever filters a collection endpoint accepts, they are all exact-match equality filters, and they are evaluated after the pagination check rather than before it: an enum-typed filter whose value is not a member of its declared domain, and a boolean-typed filter whose value is neither `true` nor `false` compared case-insensitively, are both rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while an identifier-typed filter supplied as an empty or whitespace-only string is ignored rather than matched and an identifier that matches no row simply yields an empty page.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, collections are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two collections override that default: the charter list is returned newest first, sorted by `created_at` descending and then by `id` ascending as a stable tie-break; and the per-cycle milestone list is sorted by `planned_start_utc` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock routinely stamps several rows with the same `created_at`, that column is not a total order on its own: wherever it is used as a sort key — the default ordering and the milestone ordering alike — a remaining tie is broken by ascending order of creation, so rows created earlier in the run sort earlier. The charter list needs no such fallback, its `id` tie-break already being total.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties

Every persisted record in this system — charters, committee members, reporting cycles, milestones, sub-certification requests, officer certifications, draft documents, tie-out items, adjusted measures, question items, material events, filings, post-filing corrections and control deficiencies alike — carries the same four common properties, and no record type omits any of them.

`id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. `updated_at` has the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations reached through a cascade rather than through a direct request. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, again including cascaded mutations — the one declared exception being the reporting cycle's slack pair as refreshed by the readiness dashboard read of §3.2. When a cascade touches several rows in one request, every touched row's `version` increments and every touched row's `updated_at` is refreshed.

### 2.5. Numeric Precision, Basis Points and Rounding

Monetary amounts, weights and percentages are carried to **2 decimal places and rounded half up**. Half-up rounding is applied exactly once per derived value, at the point the specification for that value says so — never repeatedly at intermediate steps, because rounding a sum of already-rounded terms produces a different answer than rounding the sum of the raw terms, and this system pins the latter wherever the two diverge.

Variance and tolerance figures are expressed in **basis points**, where one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, and are carried as integers. A raw variance in basis points is produced by half-up rounding to zero decimal places. An effective tolerance in basis points is produced by truncation toward zero — the floor of the product, never a rounding.

Slack figures, prominence indices, quorum counts, buffer day counts and event counts are integers with no rounding at all. Computed instants are rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits normalised to `.000`; the system resolves deadlines to a whole calendar date and then attaches a fixed cut-off time of day, so a computed deadline never carries a sub-second remainder.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and the Business-Day Calendar

The server maintains a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability, reserved to `ComplianceAdmin`, sets the clock to an arbitrary instant supplied as `to_iso8601`; every subsequent timestamp stamped by the server, every milestone breach sweep, every sub-certification due-window check and every filing lateness evaluation reads the clock's current value. The clock may be set forward or backward with equal freedom. An instant supplied anywhere in this API — `to_iso8601` included — parses when it carries a trailing `Z` or an explicit numeric UTC offset, in which case it is normalised to UTC before use, and an instant written with no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC; every instant the server renders back is that normalised UTC value with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`.

A second shared primitive is the **business-day calendar**. A calendar date is a business day when it falls neither on a Saturday nor on a Sunday and is not one of the following nine recognised holidays: 2026-01-01, 2026-01-19, 2026-02-16, 2026-05-25, 2026-07-03, 2026-09-07, 2026-11-26, 2026-12-25 and 2027-01-01. Three operations are built on that predicate and are used identically everywhere they appear.

**Rolling forward** a date means: while the date is not a business day, advance it by one calendar day, and return the first business day reached. A date that is already a business day is returned unchanged; the roll only ever moves forward, never backward.

**Adding a count of business days** to a date means: repeat the count that many times, and on each repetition advance the date by one calendar day and then keep advancing by one calendar day for as long as the date is not a business day. The starting date itself is never counted, so adding one business day to a Friday yields the following Monday when neither is a holiday.

**Counting business days after one date through another** means: count the number of dates strictly after the first date and at or before the second date that are business days. The lower bound is exclusive and the upper bound is inclusive, and the count is 0 whenever the second date is at or before the first.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Referential Validation Order

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. Each path segment's identifier is looked up in turn, and the first segment that does not resolve determines the 404 that is returned; segments to its right are never examined. The code emitted is the not-found variant belonging to the failing segment: `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBCERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFICER_CERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TIE_OUT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEASURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QA_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CORRECTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. Those typed codes belong to declared routes; a path that matches no declared route at all, and an HTTP method that the matched path does not declare, are instead both answered with HTTP 404 and the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND`, which is the only context in which that identifier appears.

References supplied inside a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved, and among themselves they are checked in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields. The same not-found codes are reused: an unresolvable `charter_id` or `successor_charter_id` yields `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `member_id`, `officer_member_id`, `author_member_id`, `preparer_member_id` or `reviewer_member_id` yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `cycle_id` yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `document_id` or `linked_document_id` yields `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`; and an unresolvable `amends_filing_id` yields `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`.

Request-shape validation follows: every required input field must be present and non-null, every enum-typed value must be a member of its declared domain, numeric fields must be numbers and must respect the non-negativity their descriptions state, ISO 8601 instants must parse, and `reconciliation_items` must be a list of objects each carrying a `label` string and an `amount` number. A field this document calls non-empty must contain at least one non-whitespace character; a boolean is never accepted where a number is required; and an integer-typed field additionally refuses a value that arrives as a fraction or as a float. A request body that is absent, that is not valid JSON, or that is valid JSON but not an object is read as an empty object rather than failing on the parse itself, so what such a body produces is this rule's missing-required-field failure and never an earlier rule's. Validation reads only the fields the endpoint declares; a field the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected, so a spurious extra property never by itself fails a request. Any failure here is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). The same code covers the conditionally-required fields: a missing `exception_note` on a submission that notes an exception, a missing `change_note` on a change request, a missing `determination` on an event assessment, and a missing `successor_charter_id` on a charter supersession.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations re-derive state that belongs to a parent record rather than only to the row being created, and both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency against the parent's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required, non-optional** input on the request — omitting it is a request-shape failure, not a silent pass — and the value the client supplies must equal the parent's current `version` at the moment the request is processed.

Creating a draft document requires `expected_cycle_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ReportingCycle`. If the cycle's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a newly created document legitimately supersedes any earlier document of the same cycle and the same `doc_type`; a writer holding a stale view of the cycle would retire a document it had never seen.

Creating a tie-out item requires `expected_document_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `DraftDocument`. If the document's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DOCUMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a tie-out create re-derives every sibling item's effective tolerance from a document-wide tier multiplier; a writer holding a stale view of the document would re-classify siblings it had never read.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Reviews, transitions, sign-offs, reconciliations, clearances, filings, corrections and mitigations are all unguarded by optimistic concurrency, and supplying a version field to them has no defined effect.

### 2.9. Terminal Records and Transition Legality

Each record type declares terminal states beyond which no further transition is possible. An attempt to mutate a row that already sits in one of its terminal states is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). The terminal states are: `SUPERSEDED` for a charter; `VACATED` for a committee member; `ABANDONED` on a reporting cycle's workflow axis, the remediation axis declaring no terminal state at all; `COMPLETE` or `BREACHED` for a milestone; `WITHDRAWN` or `VOID` for a sub-certification request; `VOID` for an officer certification; `FILED` or `SUPERSEDED` for a draft document; `WAIVED` for a tie-out item; `DISCLOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` for a material event; `AMENDED_SUPERSEDED` for a filing; and `RESCINDED` or `CLOSED` for a post-filing correction.

Control deficiencies are deliberately **excluded** from that generic test. Their end states `MITIGATED` and `REVERSED` are reported instead by the domain-specific `ERR_DEFICIENCY_ALREADY_MITIGATED`, which sits far later in the rule order, so a second mitigation attempt never surfaces `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. Adjusted measures and question items declare no terminal state whatsoever, so an attempt to re-clear an already-cleared question or re-reconcile a measure that admits no such edge is rejected as an illegal transition rather than as a terminal record.

"The target record" for this purpose means the row the request path names, plus the parent row that a creation endpoint writes into as named in the body. It does **not** mean a row that is merely referenced by a body field. On a filing creation the targets are the reporting cycle named by `cycle_id` and the draft document named by `document_id`; a filing named by `amends_filing_id` that has already reached `AMENDED_SUPERSEDED` is reported by the domain-specific `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID` and never by `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`.

Separately, a requested transition must be declared from the row's current state, and the parent row must be in a state that admits the write. A violation of either is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The parent-state gates are exactly these. A reporting cycle admits milestone creation while it is `PLANNING` or `CLOSE_OPEN`; document creation and a document's submission for review while it is `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `FILED`, the last because an amended report is prepared after the original filing has gone out; sub-certification creation and sub-certification submission while it is `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`; sub-certification withdrawal in any workflow state other than `FILED` and `AMENDED`; milestone start while it is `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`; officer-certification registration and material-event logging in any workflow state other than `ABANDONED`; officer signing only while it is `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`; and filing creation only while it is `FILING_READY` when the request omits `amends_filing_id`, or only while it is `FILED` or `AMENDED` when the request supplies one — so a cycle whose workflow state does not admit the filing is reported here at rule 13, ahead of the document-class check of rule 36 and of every later gate on that endpoint. A draft document admits tie-out item creation and tie-out review while it is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` — a late-discovered tie-out break on an already-approved document is precisely the situation the re-opening cascade exists for — but admits adjusted-measure creation, question-item creation, measure reconciliation and question clearance only while it is `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Control-deficiency mitigation carries **no** parent-state gate: an open deficiency can only exist beneath an assessed correction, because rescission reverses every open row and mitigating the last open row is itself what closes the correction, so the redundant gate is dropped and a mitigation attempt against an already-resolved deficiency surfaces `ERR_DEFICIENCY_ALREADY_MITIGATED` instead of being masked here.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three segregation rules are enforced across the disclosure process, each with its own code, all HTTP 409.

A tie-out line must be reviewed by someone other than the person who prepared it: the `reviewer_member_id` on a review must differ from that item's `preparer_member_id`, or the review is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SELF_REVIEW`.

A document must be approved, or sent back for changes, by someone other than its author: the acting `X-User-ID` on an approve or request-changes action must differ from that document's `author_member_id`, or the action is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SELF_APPROVAL`.

A certifying officer must not sub-certify to itself: a committee member who already holds a non-`VOID` officer certification on a cycle cannot also be assigned a sub-certification on that same cycle, and the assignment is rejected with `ERR_SOD_OFFICER_SUBCERT`. The same rule is enforced from the other direction at signing time — an officer certification cannot be signed while a sub-certification request on the same cycle is held by that certification's own officer and is still in `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED` — and that rejection also carries `ERR_SOD_OFFICER_SUBCERT`.

### 2.11. The Order in Which Rules Fire

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and it is always the violation that sits **earliest** in the following ladder. The ladder is normative: it is what makes a blocked operation diagnosable, and it is what the filing-readiness dashboard reports against. Rules are numbered from 1, and a lower number always wins.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty. This applies to every endpoint without exception, reads included — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles and appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list. Applies to every endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. For a seat-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing committee member; the opaque `ComplianceAdmin` role is exempt. Applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list contains at least one seat-backed role — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. The acting member satisfies the role's function gate described in §2.1. Applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list contains at least one seat-backed role — `ERR_MEMBER_FUNCTION_MISMATCH`.
5. The supplied `action` is permitted for the acting role. Applies to all eight action-multiplexed transition endpoints — those on charters, committee members, reporting cycles, milestones, sub-certifications, documents, material events and corrections — `ERR_ROLE_ACTION_MISMATCH`. An `action` that is absent, or that is not a member of the endpoint's declared vocabulary at all, carries no role mapping and is therefore not judged here; it falls through untouched to rule 8.
6. Every path identifier resolves, left to right. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter — the not-found variant of the failing segment.
7. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, in declaration order. Applies to cycle creation (`charter_id`), sub-certification creation (`cycle_id` then `member_id`), officer-certification registration (`cycle_id` then `officer_member_id`), document creation (`cycle_id` then `author_member_id`), tie-out item creation (`preparer_member_id`), tie-out review (`reviewer_member_id`), filing creation (`cycle_id`, then `document_id`, then `amends_filing_id`), a material-event transition (`linked_document_id`, resolved whenever the field is present, including on an `ASSESS` that makes no use of it) and a charter transition (`successor_charter_id`, which is resolved whenever the field is present, whatever `action` accompanies it and even where the accompanying action makes no use of it) — the not-found variant of the failing reference.
8. Request shape and required fields, as described in §2.7. Applies to every endpoint — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
9. `limit` is an integer between 1 and 200 inclusive and `offset` is a non-negative integer. Applies to every list endpoint — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
10. `expected_cycle_version` equals the reporting cycle's current `version`. Applies to document creation — `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION`.
11. `expected_document_version` equals the draft document's current `version`. Applies to tie-out item creation — `ERR_STALE_DOCUMENT_VERSION`.
12. The target record is not already terminal, as scoped in §2.9. Applies to every mutation endpoint that acts on an existing row — `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`.
13. The requested transition is declared from the row's current state and the parent admits the write, as enumerated in §2.9. Applies to every mutation endpoint — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
14. Uniqueness: at most one non-`VOID` officer certification per cycle and `cert_type`, and at most one non-terminal sub-certification request per cycle, member and `scope_area`. Applies to officer-certification registration, which reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERTIFICATION`, and to sub-certification creation, which reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBCERT`.
15. The cycle's pinned charter is in `ADOPTED` status. Applies to cycle creation and to every cycle workflow transition — `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_ADOPTED`.
16. Every committee member named in the request — through `member_id`, `officer_member_id`, `author_member_id`, `preparer_member_id` or `reviewer_member_id` — belongs to the same charter as the reporting cycle the write targets. Applies to sub-certification creation, officer-certification registration, document creation, tie-out item creation and tie-out review — `ERR_MEMBER_CHARTER_MISMATCH`.
17. Every committee member named in the request holds a usable seat: `status` is `ACTIVE`, with `RECUSED` and `VACATED` both rejected, and additionally `is_officer` is true when the field is `officer_member_id`. Applies to sub-certification creation, officer-certification registration, officer signing, document creation, tie-out item creation, tie-out review and sub-certification transitions — `ERR_MEMBER_SEAT_INVALID`.
18. Tie-out self-review is refused. Applies to tie-out review — `ERR_SOD_SELF_REVIEW`.
19. Document self-approval and self-change-request are refused. Applies to document transitions — `ERR_SOD_SELF_APPROVAL`.
20. An officer may not be assigned a sub-certification on a cycle where it already holds a non-`VOID` officer certification. Applies to sub-certification creation — `ERR_SOD_OFFICER_SUBCERT`. The mirrored enforcement at signing time described in §2.10 shares that identifier but not this position: on the signing endpoint it is judged last of all, after the coverage test of rule 43.
21. The charter has at least `min_quorum_count` `ACTIVE` voting members at the moment of the gate. Applies to a charter adoption, a filing-readiness declaration and an officer signature — `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`.
22. Every function in the charter's `required_functions` is covered by at least one `ACTIVE` member of that function. Applies to a charter adoption and a filing-readiness declaration — `ERR_FUNCTION_COVERAGE_GAP`.
23. Safe-harbor language is present where required. Applies to a document approval — `ERR_SAFE_HARBOR_MISSING`.
24. The call script's question gate is cleared. Applies to a document approval — `ERR_QA_ITEM_UNCLEARED`.
25. No adjusted measure blocks the document on reconciliation. Applies to a document approval — `ERR_MEASURE_NOT_RECONCILED`.
26. No adjusted measure blocks the document on prominence. Applies to a document approval — `ERR_MEASURE_PROMINENCE`.
27. The document's tie-out has passed. Applies to a document approval — `ERR_TIE_OUT_NOT_PASSED`.
28. The cycle has no open material events. Applies to a filing-readiness declaration and an officer signature — `ERR_OPEN_MATERIAL_EVENT`.
29. The event determination is consistent with the computed severity. Applies to a material-event assessment — `ERR_EVENT_DETERMINATION_CONFLICT`.
30. The cycle's deficiency gate is clear. The gate has two limbs — no unresolved deficiency weight, and a remediation axis that is not mid-restatement — and the two callers read different subsets of it: a filing-readiness declaration requires both limbs, while an amending filing tests the weight limb alone and never inspects `restatement_stage`, so an amendment is legal against a cycle sitting at `UNDER_EVALUATION` or `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` provided the weight is exactly 0.00. Applies to a filing-readiness declaration and to an amending filing — `ERR_DEFICIENCY_OPEN`.
31. The candidate milestone window does not overlap a same-kind sibling. Applies to milestone creation — `ERR_MILESTONE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
32. The candidate milestone respects the ordering buffer against the preceding kind. Applies to milestone creation — `ERR_MILESTONE_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.
33. The cycle carries a milestone of the kind the transition requires. Applies to cycle workflow transitions — `ERR_MILESTONE_MISSING`.
34. The prerequisite milestone is `COMPLETE`. Applies to cycle workflow transitions — `ERR_MILESTONE_INCOMPLETE`.
35. `successor_charter_id` names a different charter that is `ADOPTED`. Applies to a charter supersession — `ERR_SUCCESSOR_CHARTER_INVALID`.
36. The document named on a filing, or on a material-event disclosure, is of the right class, on the right cycle and `APPROVED`; the same identifier also reports the `APPROVED` `PERIODIC_FILING` document that opening certification requires the cycle to already carry, which is why that gate too sits here rather than earlier. Applies to filing creation, to a material-event disclosure and to the `OPEN_CERTIFICATION` cycle transition — `ERR_FILING_DOCUMENT_INVALID`.
37. `amends_filing_id`, when supplied, names a filing on the same cycle in `SUBMITTED` status. Applies to filing creation — `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID`.
38. The control deficiency is still `OPEN`. Applies to deficiency mitigation — `ERR_DEFICIENCY_ALREADY_MITIGATED`.
39. The candidate milestone window ends no later than the statutory deadline, and starts strictly before it ends. Applies to milestone creation — `ERR_MILESTONE_PAST_DEADLINE` (HTTP 422).
40. The sub-certification `due_utc` falls inside the cycle's permitted assignment window. Applies to sub-certification creation — `ERR_SUBCERT_DUE_OUT_OF_WINDOW`.
41. The member's concurrent preparation-window load is below cap. Applies to sub-certification creation — `ERR_MEMBER_PREP_WINDOW_CAPACITY`.
42. The cycle's sub-certification scope is non-empty. Applies to officer signing — `ERR_CERT_SCOPE_EMPTY`.
43. The cycle's sub-certification coverage is complete. Applies to officer signing — `ERR_CERT_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`.
44. Every officer certification the cycle's `form_type` requires is `SIGNED`. Applies to a filing-readiness declaration and to filing creation — `ERR_CERTIFICATIONS_INCOMPLETE`.
45. The cycle's business-day slack is sufficient. Applies to a filing-readiness declaration — `ERR_MILESTONE_SLACK_EXHAUSTED`.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth naming outright, because implementations get them wrong in opposite directions. Because the seat check at rule 17 precedes the self-review check at rule 18, a tie-out review naming a `VACATED` reviewer who also happens to be the item's preparer reports `ERR_MEMBER_SEAT_INVALID`, not `ERR_SOD_SELF_REVIEW`. And because the empty-scope check at rule 42 precedes the coverage check at rule 43, signing against a cycle that has no sub-certification requests at all reports `ERR_CERT_SCOPE_EMPTY` even though the coverage percentage of an empty set reads 0.00 and would otherwise trip rule 43.

### 2.12. Cascades, Conserved Roll-Ups and Recomputation Discipline

Many operations in this system mutate more than one record. A cascade is described throughout §3 as a chain of hops, each hop caused by the previous one, and every hop is mandatory: an implementation that performs the first hop and skips the third has not implemented the operation. Every record a cascade touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, including records reached two or three hops away from the request's nominal target. The increment is counted once per row per request rather than once per write, so a row a request writes to twice — a draft document written by the tie-out re-derivation of §3.6 and again by the failure cascade that follows it, say — still advances its `version` by exactly 1.

Two roll-ups on a reporting cycle are **conserved quantities**, meaning they are always recomputed in full from their source rows and never adjusted by an increment or a decrement. `total_assigned_scope_weight` is recomputed as the half-up-rounded, two-decimal sum of the `scope_weight` of every sub-certification request on the cycle whose `status` is `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED`; withdrawn and voided rows contribute nothing. `unresolved_deficiency_weight` is recomputed as the half-up-rounded, two-decimal sum of the `deficiency_weight` of every control deficiency whose `status` is `OPEN` and that is reachable from the cycle through a filing whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `AMENDED_SUPERSEDED` and then through a correction whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ASSESSED` or `CLOSED`; mitigated and reversed deficiencies are excluded, and deficiencies under a rescinded correction are excluded. Because both are full recomputations over unordered sets, order of evaluation cannot change the answer, and an empty sum is exactly 0.00. This is what guarantees that assigning a sub-certification and then withdrawing it, or assessing a correction and then rescinding it, returns the roll-up to precisely its earlier value with no accumulated drift.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing the Committee: Charters and Seats

A disclosure committee exists in this system as a **charter**, and a charter is created by `ComplianceAdmin` in `DRAFT`. The payload carries a `name` — non-empty, at most 160 characters — a human-readable `version_label` such as "v3.1", non-empty and at most 40 characters, a `min_quorum_count` that must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 12, and a `required_functions` list that must be non-empty and must contain distinct values drawn from `FINANCE`, `LEGAL`, `OPERATIONS` and `INVESTOR_RELATIONS`, and which is stored and echoed back in the order the client supplied rather than sorted. A freshly created charter has `status` `DRAFT`, a null `superseded_by_charter_id`, a null `adopted_at_utc`, an `active_voting_member_count` of 0 and an empty `covered_functions` list.

Seats are added to a charter by `ComplianceAdmin` one at a time, each seat naming the member's `full_name` — non-empty, at most 120 characters — and the `function` the member represents on the committee, one of `FINANCE`, `LEGAL`, `OPERATIONS` or `INVESTOR_RELATIONS`. Two optional flags shape what the seat can do: `is_officer`, which defaults to false and marks a corporate officer eligible to sign officer certifications, and `voting`, which defaults to true and determines whether the seat counts toward charter quorum. Membership is charter-scoped, so the `charter_id` a member carries is the charter the seat was created against and is never re-pointed. Every new seat is persisted with `status` `ACTIVE`. A charter admits new seats while it is `DRAFT` and equally while it is `ADOPTED` — a committee can be enlarged after adoption — and only a `SUPERSEDED` charter refuses them, which it does as the terminal record it is, with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`.

Two derived attributes on the charter are maintained from its seats and are refreshed whenever a seat is created or changes state. `active_voting_member_count` is the number of committee members on this charter whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `voting` flag is true. `covered_functions` is the list of distinct `function` values among the charter's `ACTIVE` members, sorted ascending in alphabetical order — a recused or vacated seat contributes nothing to either. Seating a member also increments the charter's `version`.

Adoption is what turns a charter from a draft into a governing instrument, and it is performed by the `DisclosureCommitteeChair`, moving `status` from `DRAFT` to `ADOPTED`. Two conditions gate it. The charter's `active_voting_member_count` must be greater than or equal to its `min_quorum_count`, otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. And every function named in `required_functions` must appear in `covered_functions`, otherwise `ERR_FUNCTION_COVERAGE_GAP`. On success the charter's `adopted_at_utc` is stamped from the current logical clock and its `version` increments.

Superseding an adopted charter — also a chair action — moves it from `ADOPTED` to `SUPERSEDED` and requires a `successor_charter_id`. The successor must exist, must itself be in `ADOPTED` status, and must not be the charter being superseded; any of those failing is `ERR_SUCCESSOR_CHARTER_INVALID`. On success the outgoing charter's `superseded_by_charter_id` is set to the successor's identifier, forming a successor edge that can be walked in either direction, and every `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED` member of the superseded charter is moved to `VACATED`, each such seat's `version` incrementing. Because those seats are now empty, the outgoing charter's own two derived attributes are recomputed over what survives and therefore fall to an `active_voting_member_count` of 0 and an empty `covered_functions` list. The successor's own record is not written to at all: its `version` and its derived attributes are left exactly as they were. What supersession explicitly does **not** do is re-point the charter reference on any reporting cycle: a cycle stays pinned to the charter version it was created against, for the whole of its life. The outgoing charter's `version` increments.

Seat state is managed by the chair through three actions. Recusal moves a seat from `ACTIVE` to `RECUSED`; reinstatement moves it from `RECUSED` to `ACTIVE`; and vacating moves it to `VACATED` from either `ACTIVE` or `RECUSED`. All three recompute the owning charter's `active_voting_member_count` and `covered_functions` and increment both the member's `version` and the charter's `version`. Vacating carries a further cascade, and it is deliberately asymmetric. Once the seat empties, every sub-certification request held by that member whose `status` is `PENDING` moves from `PENDING` to `VOID`, while rows already in `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED` are left untouched — work already delivered is not undone by a later vacancy. Then, for every reporting cycle that lost a row, `total_assigned_scope_weight` and `certification_coverage_pct` are recomputed and the cycle's `version` increments. Note the counter-intuitive direction of that last step: dropping a `PENDING` row removes weight from the coverage denominator while leaving the numerator alone, so coverage **rises**. Recusal and reinstatement trigger no such sub-certification cascade; only vacating does.

### 3.2. Opening a Reporting Cycle and Fixing Its Deadlines

A **reporting cycle** is the unit of work that produces one periodic report, and `ComplianceAdmin` opens it. The creation payload names a `fiscal_period_label` such as "FY2026-Q2" — non-empty, at most 40 characters — the `form_type` the cycle will produce, either `FORM_10Q` or `FORM_10K`, the `period_end_date` as a valid ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form, the registrant's `filer_status`, which is one of `LARGE_ACCELERATED`, `ACCELERATED` or `NON_ACCELERATED`, a strictly positive `materiality_base_amount` representing the pretax income base that later serves as the denominator for event severity scoring, and the `charter_id` of the committee charter the cycle will be governed by. One optional field, `internal_buffer_days`, defaults to 5 and must be an integer of at least 0 and at most 30.

The pinned charter must already be in `ADOPTED` status, both at creation and at every subsequent cycle workflow transition; a charter still in `DRAFT`, or one that has since been superseded, causes `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_ADOPTED`.

Creation resolves the cycle's two calendar anchors once and never re-derives them. First a statutory day count is selected from the combination of form and filer category: a quarterly report allows 40 calendar days for a large accelerated filer, 40 for an accelerated filer and 45 for a non-accelerated filer, while an annual report allows 60 calendar days for a large accelerated filer, 75 for an accelerated filer and 90 for a non-accelerated filer. That day count is added to the `period_end_date` as plain calendar days, and the resulting date is then rolled forward to the next business day if it lands on a weekend or a recognised holiday — the roll is forward only and never pulls the date earlier. The cycle's `statutory_deadline_utc` is that rolled date at a fixed cut-off time of 17:30:00 UTC. Then, and this asymmetry is deliberate, the cycle's `internal_target_utc` is obtained by subtracting `internal_buffer_days` **calendar** days from `statutory_deadline_utc` with no business-day roll of any kind, so an internal target can legitimately fall on a Saturday or a holiday.

Beyond the two computed instants, a new cycle is initialised with `status` `PLANNING`, `restatement_stage` `NONE`, `min_slack_days_calendar` and `min_slack_days_business` both 9999, `total_assigned_scope_weight` 0.00, `certification_coverage_pct` 0.00, `open_material_event_count` 0 and `unresolved_deficiency_weight` 0.00.

A cycle carries **two independent state axes on the same record**, and this is the single most important structural fact about it. The workflow axis is `status`, which ranges over `PLANNING`, `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN`, `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`, `FILING_READY`, `FILED`, `AMENDED` and `ABANDONED`. The remediation axis is `restatement_stage`, which ranges over `NONE`, `UNDER_EVALUATION`, `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` and `REMEDIATED`. The two evolve independently: a cycle may legitimately sit at `FILED` on the workflow axis while sitting at `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` on the remediation axis, or at `AMENDED` while `REMEDIATED`. No transition on one axis constrains the current value of the other except where a precondition names it explicitly. The workflow axis is driven only by cycle transitions and by filing creation; the remediation axis is driven exclusively by post-filing correction and control-deficiency activity, described in §3.9.

Cycle workflow transitions accept an `action` and an optional free-text `note` — a string of at most 2000 characters which may legitimately be empty, and which the server validates and then discards rather than storing it anywhere on the cycle. Every one of them sweeps the calendar for breaches and recomputes slack, as described in §3.3, once the terminal check of rule 12 and the transition-legality check of rule 13 have passed and before any gate of its own is evaluated — so a transition refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` or `ERR_INVALID_STATE` sweeps nothing, while one refused by any later gate has already swept — and every one increments the cycle's `version`.

Abandonment is the escape hatch, available to the chair or the controller, and it is legal from `PLANNING`, `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN`, `CERTIFICATION_OPEN` and `FILING_READY` with identical effects from each. An attempt to abandon a cycle already at `FILED` or `AMENDED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the cycle moves to `ABANDONED` and four cascades run: every sub-certification request on the cycle in `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED` moves to `VOID`; every officer certification on the cycle in `BLOCKED`, `ELIGIBLE`, `SIGNED` or `REVOKED` moves to `VOID`; every draft document on the cycle in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`; and every material event on the cycle whose `assessment_status` is `UNASSESSED`, `NOT_REPORTABLE` or `REPORTABLE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which drives `open_material_event_count` to 0. Both `total_assigned_scope_weight` and `certification_coverage_pct` then recompute, and because their source sets are now empty both fall to exactly 0.00.

Alongside the ordinary single-cycle read, a **filing-readiness dashboard** answers the question the chair actually asks: what would block a filing-readiness declaration right now. It is a computed read, and unusually for a read it performs work before answering — it runs the breach sweep and re-runs the slack computation against the current logical clock, so the slack figures it reports are never stale. That work is not free of side effects, and they are asymmetric: any milestone the sweep moves to `BREACHED` keeps that status and has its own `version` incremented, while the cycle row's refreshed `min_slack_days_calendar` and `min_slack_days_business` are written in place — this read alone neither increments the cycle's `version` nor refreshes its `updated_at`. Its response carries `cycle_id`, the workflow axis as `cycle_status`, the remediation axis as `restatement_stage`, `statutory_deadline_utc`, `internal_target_utc`, `min_slack_days_calendar`, `min_slack_days_business`, `total_assigned_scope_weight`, `certification_coverage_pct`, `open_material_event_count`, `unresolved_deficiency_weight`, plus three lists and one boolean. `missing_milestone_kinds` lists only those milestone kinds that some remaining workflow transition of this cycle requires and that the cycle does not have, ordered `CLOSE`, `REVIEW`, `CERTIFICATION`, `FILING`; because no transition ever requires a `CERTIFICATION` milestone, that kind never appears in the list. `unsigned_cert_types` lists the certification instruments required by the cycle's `form_type` that are not yet signed, in the fixed order `CEO_302`, `CFO_302`, `CEO_906`, `CFO_906`. `blocking_error_ids` lists, de-duplicated and in ascending order of the rule ladder of §2.11, the distinct error identifiers a filing-readiness declaration would raise at this instant — so its first element is exactly the code the declaration would return. Each of that declaration's own gates is evaluated independently rather than the assembly stopping at the first failure, so a cycle failing several of them lists all of them: the terminal check and the workflow-state check, which are mutually exclusive so that an `ABANDONED` cycle contributes `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` and never also `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; then the charter's adoption, quorum and function-coverage gates; then the open-event count, the deficiency gate, the `FILING` milestone, certification completeness and business-day slack. Nothing from rules 1 through 5 ever appears — the list reports the declaration's domain gates, not the authentication, role and action gates that the dashboard's own caller has already passed. `filing_ready_eligible` is true if and only if `blocking_error_ids` is empty.

### 3.3. The Close-to-Filing Calendar: Windows, Buffers and Slack

The `Controller` builds the cycle's calendar out of **milestones**, and a cycle accepts new milestones only while it is `PLANNING` or `CLOSE_OPEN`. Each milestone declares its `kind` — `CLOSE`, `REVIEW`, `CERTIFICATION` or `FILING` — and a planned window given by `planned_start_utc`, which is the inclusive start, and `planned_end_utc`, which is the **exclusive** end and must be strictly later than the start. New milestones are persisted with `status` `PLANNED` and with both `actual_start_utc` and `actual_end_utc` null.

Three admission rules are evaluated on creation, always in this order.

The first rule is **same-kind exclusivity**. The candidate window may not overlap the window of any existing milestone of the same `kind` on the same cycle, and the sibling set for this test includes milestones in `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETE` and `BREACHED` alike, because a calendar slot that was historically occupied is still occupied. Overlap is judged under half-open geometry: two windows overlap when the candidate's start is strictly before the sibling's end and the sibling's start is strictly before the candidate's end. A candidate that starts exactly at the instant a sibling ends therefore does **not** overlap it. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

The second rule is the **ordering buffer**, which enforces breathing room between consecutive phases of the close. The phases run in the order `CLOSE`, then `REVIEW`, then `CERTIFICATION`, then `FILING`; the predecessor of the candidate's kind is the kind immediately before it in that sequence, and a `CLOSE` milestone has no predecessor and so skips this rule entirely. The rule is also skipped when the cycle carries no milestone of the predecessor kind at all. Otherwise the system takes the latest `planned_end_utc` among the cycle's milestones of the predecessor kind, regardless of their status, and requires that the gap from that instant to the candidate's `planned_start_utc` be at least the buffer for that pair: two full days — 48 hours — from the close phase into review, two full days again from review into certification, and half a day — 12 hours — from certification into filing. The comparison is inclusive, so a gap of exactly the required number of hours is accepted; a shorter gap is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONE_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. When two predecessor milestones share the same `planned_end_utc` the latest value is identical either way, so the outcome does not depend on which one is inspected.

The third rule is **deadline containment**: the candidate's `planned_end_utc` must fall at or before the cycle's `statutory_deadline_utc`, and its `planned_start_utc` must be strictly before its `planned_end_utc`. Either failing yields `ERR_MILESTONE_PAST_DEADLINE`, which — unlike the first two rules — is reported as HTTP 422 rather than 409.

**Slack** is how the calendar reports its own health, and it is computed in two parallel variants that answer the same question with different arithmetic. For each milestone of the cycle, `slack_calendar_days` is the number of whole days in the interval from that milestone's `planned_end_utc` to the cycle's `statutory_deadline_utc`, truncated toward zero so any partial day is dropped; a milestone planned to end after the deadline yields a negative number. Its sibling `slack_business_days` counts only business days, and counts them strictly after the calendar date of `planned_end_utc` through the calendar date of `statutory_deadline_utc` inclusive, ignoring time of day altogether; it is 0 whenever the deadline date falls at or before the planned-end date. For identical inputs the business-day variant returns a strictly smaller number whenever the interval spans a weekend or a recognised holiday. At cycle level, `min_slack_days_calendar` and `min_slack_days_business` are the minimum of the corresponding per-milestone value taken over the **open** milestones only — those whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Milestones in `COMPLETE` or `BREACHED` are excluded from the aggregate while keeping their own per-row slack figures frozen at their last computation. When no open milestone remains, both cycle-level figures are set to 9999. Because the aggregate is a minimum over integers, ties need no tie-break: two milestones tying on the minimum produce the same value regardless of the order they are inspected. Slack is recomputed on milestone creation, on every milestone transition, and on every cycle workflow transition that has passed its terminal and state checks; because creating a milestone recomputes the owning cycle's pair, the create touches the cycle too and increments its `version`, whether or not either aggregate figure actually moves.

A milestone's own life is short. Starting it moves from `PLANNED` to `IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `actual_start_utc` from the logical clock; the controller may only start a milestone while the owning cycle is in `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`, and an attempt outside those states is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Completing it moves from `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETE`, stamps `actual_end_utc`, and — because the row now drops out of the open input set — re-runs the slack computation for the cycle. Both actions increment the milestone's `version` and the owning cycle's `version`.

Breach is not an action anybody takes; it is a sweep. On every cycle workflow transition that has passed its terminal and state checks, and again whenever the filing-readiness dashboard is read, every milestone on the cycle whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose `planned_end_utc` lies strictly before the current logical clock is moved to `BREACHED`. Each swept milestone's `version` increments, and because breached rows also drop out of the open input set, the cycle's slack figures are recomputed immediately afterwards. Neither effect is undone by a later refusal: a transition that sweeps and is then rejected by one of its own gates answers with that error and still leaves the milestones it moved in `BREACHED` and the recomputed slack figures standing. A breach is terminal: a breached milestone can never be started, completed, or brought back.

The calendar is what unlocks the workflow axis. Opening the close moves the cycle from `PLANNING` to `CLOSE_OPEN` and requires that at least one `CLOSE` milestone exist on the cycle, otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_MISSING`. Opening review moves it from `CLOSE_OPEN` to `REVIEW_OPEN` and requires that every `CLOSE` milestone on the cycle be `COMPLETE`, otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_INCOMPLETE`; the existence of a `CLOSE` milestone is re-checked here too, though it can only fail if the close was never opened, so that clause is defensive. Opening certification moves it from `REVIEW_OPEN` to `CERTIFICATION_OPEN` and requires that at least one `REVIEW` milestone exist — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_MISSING` — that every `REVIEW` milestone be `COMPLETE` — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_INCOMPLETE` — and that at least one draft document on the cycle with `doc_type` `PERIODIC_FILING` be in `APPROVED` status, otherwise `ERR_FILING_DOCUMENT_INVALID`. Declaring filing readiness requires a `FILING` milestone to exist, again `ERR_MILESTONE_MISSING`. Every one of these existence tests, and the `missing_milestone_kinds` list of §3.2 alike, is satisfied by a milestone of the required kind in any status: `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETE` and `BREACHED` all count as the cycle having one. No transition anywhere requires a `CERTIFICATION` milestone; the kind exists so that the certification phase can be scheduled and can accrue slack, not so that it can gate anything.

### 3.4. Collecting Sub-Certifications from Business and Functional Leaders

The officers who sign the certifications do not personally verify every disclosure; they rely on **sub-certifications** collected from business and functional leaders. The `DisclosureCommitteeChair` assigns one by naming the `cycle_id` it supports, the `member_id` of the leader who owes it, the `scope_area` being sub-certified — one of `REVENUE`, `LEGAL_CONTINGENCY`, `OPERATIONS`, `IR_COMMUNICATIONS` or `IT_CONTROLS` — and a `due_utc`, which is an inclusive submission deadline. A cycle accepts new assignments while it is `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`.

Four eligibility checks stand between the request and a persisted row, and they fire in the ladder order of §2.11. The named member must belong to the same charter the cycle is pinned to, or the assignment is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_CHARTER_MISMATCH`. The named member's seat must be `ACTIVE`; a `RECUSED` or `VACATED` seat yields `ERR_MEMBER_SEAT_INVALID`. There may be at most one non-terminal request for the same combination of cycle, member and `scope_area`, and a second one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBCERT`. And the member must not already hold a non-`VOID` officer certification on this cycle, because an officer cannot supply the evidence it will itself rely on — that rejection is `ERR_SOD_OFFICER_SUBCERT`.

Two further constraints concern time. The `due_utc` must fall strictly after the current logical clock — a deadline that has already passed cannot be assigned — and at or before the cycle's `internal_target_utc`, so that submissions land before the committee's own buffer runs out rather than merely before the statutory deadline. A `due_utc` outside that window is rejected with `ERR_SUBCERT_DUE_OUT_OF_WINDOW`.

The second temporal constraint protects the leaders themselves from being over-committed. Each request implies a **preparation window** of the 72 hours running up to its deadline: the row's `prep_window_start_utc` is its `due_utc` less 72 hours, and the window spans from that instant to `due_utc` with **both ends inclusive**. Before persisting a new assignment the system counts, across **all** cycles rather than just this one — a leader's workload is charter-wide — the requests already held by the same member whose `status` is `PENDING` or `SUBMITTED` and whose window intersects the candidate's. Two closed windows intersect when each one's start falls at or before the other one's end, which means windows that merely touch at a single instant **do** conflict. If two or more such occupants already exist, the assignment is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_PREP_WINDOW_CAPACITY`; a leader may carry at most two concurrent preparation windows. Requests in `EXCEPTION_NOTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `VOID` are not occupants — the leader has either already delivered or the assignment is gone.

Every request carries a `scope_weight`, which is how much of the cycle's certification burden it represents, and it is derived rather than supplied. The base weight comes from the scope area: 30 for `REVENUE`, 25 for `LEGAL_CONTINGENCY`, 20 for `OPERATIONS`, 15 for `IR_COMMUNICATIONS` and 10 for `IT_CONTROLS`. That base is multiplied by an officer-support factor of 1.5 when the named member's `is_officer` flag is true, and by 1.0 otherwise. It is multiplied again by an escalation factor drawn from the request's `exception_severity`: 1.0 for `NONE`, 1.1 for `LOW`, 1.4 for `MEDIUM` and 2.0 for `HIGH`. The order of the final three steps is pinned and matters: take the larger of the base weight and that product, round the result half up to two decimal places **once**, and only then cap it at 45. Because the cap is applied after rounding, a capped row reads exactly 45.00. A new request is persisted with `status` `PENDING`, `exception_severity` `NONE`, a null `exception_note` and a null `submitted_utc`, so its initial weight is simply its base possibly lifted by the officer factor. Creation then recomputes the cycle's `total_assigned_scope_weight` and `certification_coverage_pct` and increments the cycle's `version`.

`certification_coverage_pct` is the cycle's headline readiness number, and its evaluation order is pinned. The denominator set is every request on the cycle whose `status` is `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED`; the numerator set is the subset of those whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED`. When the denominator set is empty the coverage is **0.00**, not 100.00 — an empty scope is not a covered scope. Otherwise the raw `scope_weight` values of the numerator set are summed, the raw `scope_weight` values of the denominator set are summed, the first sum is divided by the second, the quotient is multiplied by 100, and the result is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once. Computing a per-scope-area completion percentage, rounding each of those, and averaging them unweighted is explicitly forbidden and gives a different answer: for four requests weighted 30.00, 25.00, 20.00 and 15.00 with the first two submitted, the pinned order yields 61.11 while the forbidden order yields 50.00.

Submission is the leader's own act, performed by a `BusinessLeader` whose `X-User-ID` must equal the request's `member_id` and whose own seat must be `ACTIVE`; either failing is `ERR_MEMBER_SEAT_INVALID`. The owning cycle must be in `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`. Submission then **forks into two outcomes with genuinely different consequences**, selected by the `exception_severity` supplied on the request.

A clean submission — `exception_severity` omitted, or explicitly `NONE` — moves the request from `PENDING` to `SUBMITTED`. It stamps `submitted_utc` from the logical clock, leaves `exception_severity` at `NONE`, and leaves `scope_weight` untouched. Coverage is recomputed: because the row was already in the denominator and has now entered the numerator, the numerator grows and the denominator does not move, so coverage rises. `total_assigned_scope_weight` is recomputed as well and, since the row's weight has not changed, comes back to the same value.

A submission that notes an exception — `exception_severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH` — moves the request from `PENDING` to `EXCEPTION_NOTED` instead. It requires a non-empty `exception_note` of at most 2000 characters, and its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It stamps `submitted_utc`, records `exception_severity` and `exception_note`, and — this is what diverges from the clean branch — re-runs the weight derivation with the escalation factor now in play, so the row's `scope_weight` rises. Both roll-ups therefore move: `total_assigned_scope_weight` grows, and `certification_coverage_pct` shifts because the same increased weight enters **both** the numerator and the denominator. Both branches increment the request's `version` and the cycle's `version`.

Withdrawal is the chair's compensating action for an assignment that should never have been made. It moves a request from `PENDING` to `WITHDRAWN` and is legal while the cycle is in any workflow state other than `FILED` and `AMENDED`; from those two it is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Because withdrawal removes the row from both the numerator and the denominator, and because `total_assigned_scope_weight` is always recomputed in full from the surviving rows, the cycle's assigned weight returns to exactly the value it held before the assignment was made. Coverage is recomputed alongside it.

Voiding is never requested; it happens to a request. A cycle moving to `ABANDONED` voids every request on it in `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED`, while a member being vacated voids only that member's `PENDING` rows. In both cases the owning cycle's `total_assigned_scope_weight` and `certification_coverage_pct` are recomputed and the voided request's `version` increments.

### 3.5. Drafting and Reviewing the Disclosure Documents

Four classes of document pass through the committee, distinguished by `doc_type`: the `EARNINGS_RELEASE`, the `PERIODIC_FILING` itself, the `CALL_SCRIPT` used on the earnings call, and the `CURRENT_REPORT` used to disclose a between-period event. A `Controller`, an `InvestorRelations` member or a `LegalCounsel` member may start any of them. The payload names the `cycle_id` the document belongs to, its `doc_type`, a `title` that must be non-empty and at most 200 characters, the `author_member_id` of the drafting member, an optional `safe_harbor_present` flag that defaults to false and records whether forward-looking-statement safe harbor language is included, and the mandatory `expected_cycle_version`.

Drafting is not permitted against a cycle that is still `PLANNING`: the cycle must be in `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `FILED`, and the last of those is admitted deliberately, because an amended report is drafted and reviewed only after the original filing has gone out. Any other cycle state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, with the single exception of `ABANDONED`: because the cycle named in the body is a target row in the sense of §2.9, an abandoned cycle is refused one rule earlier with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. The named author must belong to the same charter the cycle is pinned to, or `ERR_MEMBER_CHARTER_MISMATCH`, and must hold an `ACTIVE` seat, or `ERR_MEMBER_SEAT_INVALID`.

Creation is also the point at which an earlier draft of the same thing is retired. When a new document is created with the same `cycle_id` and the same `doc_type` as an existing document of that cycle, the existing one moves to `SUPERSEDED` — from `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` alike — and its `version` increments. That silent retirement is exactly why the create carries `expected_cycle_version`: a writer holding a stale view of the cycle would otherwise supersede a document it had never read.

A new document is persisted with `status` `DRAFT`, `revision_round` 0, a null `change_note`, `tie_out_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `tie_out_total_variance_bp` 0, `tie_out_tier_multiplier` 1.00, `measure_gate_status` `NO_MEASURES` and `qa_gate_status` `CLEARED`. The owning cycle's `version` increments.

Review is a **loop, not a line**, and the loop counter is observable. Submitting a document for review moves it from `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW` on its first pass and from `CHANGES_REQUESTED` to `IN_REVIEW` on every subsequent pass; the acting role may be `Controller`, `LegalCounsel` or `InvestorRelations`, and the owning cycle must again be in `CLOSE_OPEN`, `REVIEW_OPEN` or `FILED`. Requesting changes moves it from `IN_REVIEW` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, is open to `Controller` and `LegalCounsel` only, and requires a non-empty `change_note` of at most 2000 characters — its absence, or a longer one, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Entry into `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is where `revision_round` increments by exactly 1, and the supplied `change_note` is stored on the document, overwriting whatever the previous round left there. The return edge back into `IN_REVIEW` does **not** increment `revision_round` a second time; the counter counts change requests, not review submissions. Requesting changes is also subject to the author-separation rule: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the document's `author_member_id`, or the action is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SELF_APPROVAL`.

Approval moves a document from `IN_REVIEW` to `APPROVED` and is the single densest gate in the system. It is available to `Controller` and `LegalCounsel`, and it evaluates five substantive conditions after the author-separation rule has already been applied — the acting member must not be the document's `author_member_id`, or the approval is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SELF_APPROVAL`. The remaining conditions are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.11, so a document failing several of them reports only the earliest.

First, **safe harbor**. A document whose `doc_type` is `EARNINGS_RELEASE` or `CALL_SCRIPT` cannot be approved while its `safe_harbor_present` flag is false, and a `CALL_SCRIPT` that carries any question item with `requires_safe_harbor` true likewise cannot be approved unless `safe_harbor_present` is true. Either failing is `ERR_SAFE_HARBOR_MISSING`. A `PERIODIC_FILING` or a `CURRENT_REPORT` is not subject to this rule at all.

Second, the **question gate**: the document's `qa_gate_status` must be `CLEARED`, and a value of `BLOCKED` yields `ERR_QA_ITEM_UNCLEARED`.

Third and fourth, the **adjusted-measure gate**: `measure_gate_status` must be either `NO_MEASURES` or `CLEARED`. A value of `BLOCKED_RECONCILIATION` yields `ERR_MEASURE_NOT_RECONCILED`, and a value of `BLOCKED_PROMINENCE` yields `ERR_MEASURE_PROMINENCE` — two distinct codes for two distinct defects, and reconciliation failures are reported ahead of prominence failures.

Fifth, the **tie-out gate**: `tie_out_status` must be exactly `PASSED`, and both `NOT_STARTED` and `FAILED` block with `ERR_TIE_OUT_NOT_PASSED`. The `NOT_STARTED` case has a consequence worth stating plainly: a document that carries no tie-out lines at all can never be approved, because tie-out is not optional.

An approved document has three ways out. It moves from `APPROVED` to `FILED` when a filing is submitted against it, which is only possible for a `PERIODIC_FILING`. It moves from `APPROVED` to `SUPERSEDED` when a newer document of the same cycle and `doc_type` is created, or when the owning cycle is abandoned — and the same supersede edge applies from `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` and `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. And it moves from `APPROVED` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` when its tie-out later fails, which is the cascade described next. Both `FILED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

### 3.6. Tying the Reported Figures Back to the Records

Every figure printed in a draft has to be traceable to an underlying accounting record, and a **tie-out item** is one such trace. The `Controller` adds them to a document that is in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. Each carries a `line_label` naming the caption being tied out — non-empty, at most 120 characters, and unique within the document across all rows that have not been waived — the `reported_value` as printed in the draft, the `source_record_value` read from the accounting record, a base `tolerance_bp` expressed in basis points which must be an integer of at least 0 and at most 10000, the `preparer_member_id` of the member who performed the trace, and the mandatory `expected_document_version`. The preparer must belong to the cycle's charter and hold an `ACTIVE` seat. A duplicate `line_label` is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and unusually for that identifier it is judged not at rule 8 but last of all on this endpoint, after the charter and seat checks of rules 16 and 17. A new row is persisted with `reviewer_member_id` null, `review_decision` null, and a nominal `status` of `MATCHED` that the derivation below immediately overwrites; the created row is nevertheless returned at `version` 1, that derivation's write to its own `raw_variance_bp`, `effective_tolerance_bp` and `status` being folded into the create rather than counted as a second mutation. The owning document is touched on every such create, so its `version` increments whether or not any figure it carries actually moves — and a second tie-out create must therefore quote the incremented value as its `expected_document_version`.

What makes tie-out the hardest calculation in the system is that it **looks circular**: each item's outcome depends on a tolerance multiplier derived from a document-wide variance total, and that total is drawn from the same items. The following evaluation order breaks the loop and is normative — no other order is permitted — and the whole sequence is re-run over the document's entire non-waived item set on every item creation and on every review, not merely against the row that was touched. Waived rows are outside the input set entirely: they contribute nothing to the total and keep their own `raw_variance_bp` and `effective_tolerance_bp` frozen at whatever they held when they were waived.

**Step one** derives each item's `raw_variance_bp` independently of any status. When the `source_record_value` is zero, `raw_variance_bp` is set to the ceiling value of 10000 basis points, because a proportional variance against a zero base is undefined and is treated as maximally wrong. Otherwise it is the absolute difference between `reported_value` and `source_record_value`, divided by the absolute value of `source_record_value`, multiplied by 10000, and rounded half up to a whole number of basis points.

**Step two** sets the document's `tie_out_total_variance_bp` to the sum of the step-one `raw_variance_bp` values across the non-waived set. This is a snapshot of step one only; the statuses that step four will assign must never feed back into this sum.

**Step three** selects the document's `tie_out_tier_multiplier` from that snapshot total. A total of at most 50 basis points earns the tight tier and a multiplier of 1.00. A total above 50 but at most 200 basis points earns the medium tier and a multiplier of 0.75. Anything above 200 basis points earns the wide tier and a multiplier of 0.50. The escalation is intentionally counter-intuitive: a document with a lot of small variances gets a **tighter** effective tolerance on every line, not a looser one.

**Step four** derives, for every item in the non-waived set, an `effective_tolerance_bp` equal to that item's own `tolerance_bp` multiplied by the document's tier multiplier and then floored to a whole number of basis points by truncation. The item's `status` becomes `MATCHED` when its `raw_variance_bp` is less than or equal to its `effective_tolerance_bp`, and `VARIANCE` otherwise. The comparison is inclusive, so a variance landing exactly on the effective tolerance **passes**.

**Step five** sets the document's `tie_out_status`. It is `NOT_STARTED` when the non-waived set is empty, `PASSED` when every item in that set is `MATCHED`, and `FAILED` otherwise.

Because the tier is document-wide, `MATCHED` and `VARIANCE` are a **two-way** pairing rather than a one-way slide. Adding a new item can push the snapshot total into a stricter tier and flip previously matched siblings to `VARIANCE`; waiving an item can drop the total into a looser tier and flip siblings back from `VARIANCE` to `MATCHED`. Every item whose status changes has its `version` incremented.

Independent review is the second control on the tie-out, and it belongs to the `Controller` as well — but not to the same person. A review names the `reviewer_member_id` and a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `WAIVE`. The reviewer's seat is checked before the separation rule is: a reviewer who is `RECUSED` or `VACATED` yields `ERR_MEMBER_SEAT_INVALID`, and only once the seat is usable does the system reject a reviewer equal to the item's `preparer_member_id` with `ERR_SOD_SELF_REVIEW`. The owning document must be in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. Both decisions record `reviewer_member_id` and `review_decision` on the item and increment both the item's `version` and the document's `version`.

An `ACCEPT` decision is available on an item that is `MATCHED` and leaves it `MATCHED` — the row's status is simply re-derived by step four and comes back unchanged. A `WAIVE` decision is available from either `MATCHED` or `VARIANCE` and moves the item to the terminal `WAIVED`, which pulls it out of the step-one-through-four input set; the document's `tie_out_total_variance_bp`, its `tie_out_tier_multiplier`, every surviving sibling's `effective_tolerance_bp` and `status`, and the document's `tie_out_status` are then all recomputed.

When a tie-out failure lands on a document that had already been approved, a five-hop cascade unwinds the work downstream of it, and every hop is mandatory. The first hop is the item itself moving to `VARIANCE`. The second is the document's `tie_out_status` recomputing to `FAILED`. The third, caused by that failure, is the document moving from `APPROVED` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, with its `revision_round` incremented by 1 and its `change_note` set by the system to the literal text `auto: tie-out variance detected`. The fourth, caused by the document leaving `APPROVED`, applies only when the document's `doc_type` is `PERIODIC_FILING` and the owning cycle is in `FILING_READY`: the cycle moves from `FILING_READY` to `REVIEW_OPEN`. The fifth, caused by the cycle leaving `FILING_READY`, is that every officer certification on the cycle whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE` or `SIGNED` moves to `REVOKED`; a revoked certification has its `signed_utc` and `attestation_text` cleared, while `coverage_pct_at_signing` is deliberately **retained** as an audit trace of what the officer relied on. The cycle's `certification_coverage_pct` is then recomputed, and every record touched along the chain has its `version` incremented.

### 3.7. Adjusted Measures, Prominence and the Earnings-Call Question Bank

Two further kinds of child record hang off a draft document, and both maintain a gate the approval in §3.5 reads. Unlike tie-out items, neither is accepted onto an already-approved document: an `APPROVED` document admits tie-out work but refuses adjusted-measure creation, question-item creation, measure reconciliation and question clearance, all of which require the document to be in `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`.

An **adjusted measure** is a non-GAAP or otherwise adjusted figure presented in the release. The `Controller` attaches one by supplying its `measure_label` — non-empty, at most 120 characters, for instance "Adjusted EBITDA" — the `adjusted_value` as presented, the `comparable_gaap_value` of the most directly comparable reported measure, a `reconciliation_items` list of bridging items where each item is an object carrying a `label` string — at most 200 characters, and permitted to be empty or blank — and an `amount` number, any further property supplied on such an item being dropped rather than stored, and the list may legitimately be empty, and two presentation-order indices: `adjusted_prominence_index`, the one-based position of the adjusted figure in the presentation where 1 is the most prominent, and `gaap_prominence_index`, the one-based position of the comparable reported figure. Both indices must be integers of at least 1 and at most 50. A newly attached measure is persisted in `status` `DRAFT` with `reconciliation_sum`, `reconciliation_gap` and `prominence_ok` all null, and — precisely because it is `DRAFT` and therefore not yet reconciled — attaching it immediately drives the owning document's `measure_gate_status` to `BLOCKED_RECONCILIATION`. The document's `version` increments.

Reconciling a measure is the controller's act of making the bridge add up. The request may optionally carry corrected `reconciliation_items`, a corrected `adjusted_prominence_index` and a corrected `gaap_prominence_index`; any of the three supplied overwrites the stored value before the derivation runs, and any omitted leaves the stored value in place. The derivation then proceeds in a fixed order. `reconciliation_sum` becomes the sum of the `amount` of every bridging item, rounded half up to two decimal places. `reconciliation_gap` becomes the `adjusted_value` less the total of `comparable_gaap_value` and `reconciliation_sum`, again rounded half up to two decimal places — and the term entering that subtraction is the already-rounded `reconciliation_sum` just derived, not the raw item total behind it, this specification naming that derived field explicitly just as the deficiency scoring of §3.9 names an already-rounded `materiality_pct`. A gap of zero means the bridge is exact. `prominence_ok` becomes true exactly when `gaap_prominence_index` is less than or equal to `adjusted_prominence_index`, that is, when the reported figure is presented at least as prominently as the adjusted one. The measure reconciles when the absolute value of `reconciliation_gap` is at most 0.01; the comparison is inclusive, so a gap of exactly 0.01 reconciles while a gap of 0.02 does not.

Those three derived values select the measure's `status`, and reconciliation is judged before prominence. A measure whose gap exceeds the tolerance becomes `REJECTED` regardless of its prominence indices. A measure whose gap is within tolerance but whose `prominence_ok` is false becomes `PROMINENCE_FAILED`. A measure that satisfies both becomes `RECONCILED`. The reconcile action is idempotent in shape and re-derives the status from whatever the current field values are, so it is legal from `DRAFT`, from `PROMINENCE_FAILED` and from `REJECTED` — a rejected measure returns to `RECONCILED` once corrected bridging items are supplied, and a prominence-failed measure returns to `RECONCILED` once corrected indices are supplied. There is no declared edge out of `RECONCILED`, and no state of an adjusted measure is terminal, so reconciling a measure that already reads `RECONCILED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Each reconcile increments both the measure's `version` and the owning document's `version`.

The document-level `measure_gate_status` is then re-derived over **all** the document's measures regardless of their individual status, with a pinned precedence in which reconciliation failures outrank prominence failures. It reads `NO_MEASURES` when the document has no measures at all; `BLOCKED_RECONCILIATION` when at least one measure is in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`; `BLOCKED_PROMINENCE` when no measure is in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` but at least one is in `PROMINENCE_FAILED`; and `CLEARED` when every measure is `RECONCILED`. The gate is re-derived both when a measure is attached and when one is reconciled.

The **question bank** is the earnings call's other half. An `InvestorRelations` member adds prepared questions to a call script, and the target document is recorded on the question as `script_document_id`. That target is a `CALL_SCRIPT` by intent only: the endpoint does not inspect `doc_type` at all, so a question may be attached to a document of any of the four classes, and the document-level question gate it feeds then constrains that document's own approval exactly as it would a script's, while the safe-harbor rule of §3.5 that reads `requires_safe_harbor` across a document's questions remains confined to a `CALL_SCRIPT`. Each question carries its `question_text`, non-empty and at most 400 characters, a `topic` drawn from `GUIDANCE`, `MARGIN`, `LIQUIDITY`, `LITIGATION` and `SEGMENT_PERFORMANCE`, a `risk_tier` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, and an optional `requires_safe_harbor` flag, defaulting to false, that marks a prepared answer containing forward-looking statements. Its `clearance_note` starts null.

Clearance state is decided inside the creation request rather than afterwards. A question is persisted directly in `status` `BLOCKED` when its `risk_tier` is `HIGH` **or** its `requires_safe_harbor` flag is true, and in `status` `DRAFT` otherwise. Creation also re-derives the script's `qa_gate_status` and increments the document's `version`.

The distinction between a blocked *row* and a blocked *gate* is subtle and load-bearing. `qa_gate_status` reads `BLOCKED` exactly while some question on the script has `risk_tier` `HIGH` and a `status` other than `CLEARED`; it reads `CLEARED` in every other case, including the case of a script with no questions at all. So an uncleared `LOW` or `MEDIUM` question does not block approval, and a `LOW`-tier question that merely sets `requires_safe_harbor` sits in `BLOCKED` as a row while leaving the gate `CLEARED` — that scenario is caught by the safe-harbor rule and reported as `ERR_SAFE_HARBOR_MISSING`, not by the question gate and `ERR_QA_ITEM_UNCLEARED`.

Clearing a question is reserved to `LegalCounsel`, requires a non-empty `clearance_note` of at most 2000 characters, and moves the question to `CLEARED` from either `DRAFT` or `BLOCKED`. One condition attaches to the blocked path: a `BLOCKED` question can only be cleared when the owning script's `safe_harbor_present` flag is true, and an attempt against a script lacking the legend is rejected with `ERR_SAFE_HARBOR_MISSING`. A question in `DRAFT` carries no such condition. Clearing records the `clearance_note`, re-derives the script's `qa_gate_status` — which returns to `CLEARED` once no `HIGH`-tier question on the script is left uncleared — and increments both the question's `version` and the document's `version`.

### 3.8. Material Events, Officer Certification and the Filing-Readiness Gate

A **material event** is something that happens between reporting periods and may require its own current report rather than waiting for the periodic filing. Either a `ComplianceAdmin` or `LegalCounsel` may log one against any cycle that is not `ABANDONED`, supplying a `category` drawn from `ACQUISITION`, `DIVESTITURE`, `DEBT_COVENANT_BREACH`, `LITIGATION_JUDGMENT`, `AUDITOR_CHANGE` and `IMPAIRMENT`, the `occurred_utc` instant at which the event happened, the `discovered_utc` instant at which the company became aware of it — which must be at or after `occurred_utc`, an ordering enforced as a request-shape constraint — a `magnitude_amount` of at least zero expressing the absolute financial size of the event, and a `description` that is non-empty and at most 400 characters.

Logging the event scores it. Its `severity_score` is the absolute value of `magnitude_amount` divided by the cycle's `materiality_base_amount`, expressed as a percentage, capped at 100.00 and rounded half up to two decimal places. The row is then persisted with `assessment_status` `UNASSESSED`, `determination_note` null, `linked_document_id` null and `current_report_deadline_utc` null.

The count that certification hangs on is `open_material_event_count`, and it is defined as the number of events on the cycle whose `assessment_status` is `UNASSESSED` or `REPORTABLE`. The inclusion of `UNASSESSED` is the whole point: an event legal has not yet looked at is an open disclosure risk and blocks certification exactly as a confirmed reportable one does. So merely logging an event pushes the count up, and every `OfficerCertification` on the cycle sitting in `ELIGIBLE` is driven back to `BLOCKED`; the cycle's `version` increments.

Assessment is reserved to `LegalCounsel` and branches on a supplied `determination`, with the two branches producing **opposite** effects on the cycle. On the `NOT_REPORTABLE` branch the `current_report_deadline_utc` stays null, the event leaves the open set, `open_material_event_count` recomputes downward, and — when the count reaches zero and the cycle is in `CERTIFICATION_OPEN` — every `BLOCKED` certification on the cycle is released to `ELIGIBLE`. On the `REPORTABLE` branch the deadline is computed, the event stays inside the open set so the count holds its current value, this edge sustains the existing block rather than creating a new one, and every `ELIGIBLE` certification is driven to `BLOCKED` (a no-op when the event's own creation already blocked them). Either branch records the `determination_note` — an optional string of at most 2000 characters which may legitimately be empty and which is written straight from the request, so that omitting it on a re-assessment clears whatever note an earlier assessment left on the row — and increments the event's `version` and the cycle's `version`.

A materiality floor constrains which branch legal may choose: an event whose `severity_score` is greater than or equal to 5.00 percent cannot be determined `NOT_REPORTABLE`, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_DETERMINATION_CONFLICT`. The comparison reads the **rounded** score, so a raw ratio of 4.99999 percent rounds to 5.00 and is already reportable. There is no corresponding floor on the `REPORTABLE` branch: legal may always elect to treat a small event as reportable.

On the `REPORTABLE` branch the `current_report_deadline_utc` is set to the instant four business days after the calendar date of `discovered_utc`, at 17:30:00 UTC on that resulting date. The business-day arithmetic is the one described in §2.6, so weekends and the published holidays are skipped. Re-assessment is permitted in one direction: an event in `NOT_REPORTABLE` may be re-assessed as `REPORTABLE` when new information arrives, which recomputes the deadline, drives `open_material_event_count` back up — from zero to one when this is the cycle's only open event — and re-blocks every `ELIGIBLE` certification. There is no edge back from `REPORTABLE` to `NOT_REPORTABLE`; indeed an event already reading `REPORTABLE` admits no `ASSESS` of either determination, and a `NOT_REPORTABLE` determination is available only from `UNASSESSED`, so re-affirming an event that already reads `NOT_REPORTABLE` is refused too. Both refusals are `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Disclosure closes an event out. `LegalCounsel` records a `REPORTABLE` event as `DISCLOSED` by naming the `linked_document_id` of the current report it went out on; that document must have `doc_type` `CURRENT_REPORT`, must belong to the same cycle and must be in `APPROVED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_FILING_DOCUMENT_INVALID`. Disclosure stores the link, drops the event out of the open set, recomputes `open_material_event_count`, and releases `BLOCKED` certifications to `ELIGIBLE` on the same terms as the not-reportable branch. It differs from the assessment branches on one detail: a `determination_note` is overwritten only when the disclosure request actually supplies one, so an omitted note leaves the assessment's note standing rather than clearing it. `DISCLOSED` is terminal, as is `SUPERSEDED`, which is where every non-terminal event lands when the owning cycle is abandoned.

An **officer certification** is a required signature slot rather than a document. A `ComplianceAdmin` registers one against a cycle in any status other than `ABANDONED`, naming the `officer_member_id` of the signing officer and the `cert_type`, one of `CEO_302`, `CFO_302`, `CEO_906` and `CFO_906`. The named seat must be `ACTIVE` and must carry `is_officer` true, or the registration is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_SEAT_INVALID`; the cycle may hold at most one non-`VOID` certification per `cert_type`, and a second registration of the same instrument is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CERTIFICATION`. The slot is persisted in `status` `BLOCKED` with `attestation_text`, `signed_utc` and `coverage_pct_at_signing` all null, and the cross-entity gate is then evaluated at once: if the cycle is already in `CERTIFICATION_OPEN` with `certification_coverage_pct` exactly 100.00, `open_material_event_count` zero and `unresolved_deficiency_weight` exactly 0.00, the slot becomes `ELIGIBLE` immediately rather than waiting for a later event; that determination is folded into the create itself, so the new row is returned already reading `ELIGIBLE` at `version` 1 rather than as a second mutation of a `BLOCKED` row. The cycle's `version` increments.

Eligibility is otherwise entirely system-driven, and `BLOCKED` and `ELIGIBLE` form a two-way edge that the rest of the workflow flips back and forth. A slot moves from `BLOCKED` to `ELIGIBLE` when the cycle enters `CERTIFICATION_OPEN` with `open_material_event_count` at zero, and when a material event resolution brings that count down to zero while the cycle is in `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`. It moves from `ELIGIBLE` back to `BLOCKED` whenever the count rises above zero, whether because an event was logged or because one was re-assessed as reportable. Neither state is terminal and neither direction is ever requested by a client.

Signing is the `CertifyingOfficer`'s act and is the most heavily gated write in the system. It requires an `attestation_text` of at most 4000 characters, and beyond the seat and role checks of §2.1 and §2.10 it stacks five conditions. The slot must be `ELIGIBLE`. The acting member must be the very officer the slot names, and that member's `is_officer` must be true, else `ERR_MEMBER_FUNCTION_MISMATCH`. The cycle must be in `CERTIFICATION_OPEN`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The cycle's sub-certification scope must be non-empty — at least one request in `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `EXCEPTION_NOTED` — else `ERR_CERT_SCOPE_EMPTY`. The cycle's `certification_coverage_pct` must be exactly 100.00, else `ERR_CERT_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. And `open_material_event_count` must be zero, else `ERR_OPEN_MATERIAL_EVENT`. A sixth condition is the segregation rule of §2.10: an officer who himself holds a live sub-certification request on the cycle cannot sign, and that is `ERR_SOD_OFFICER_SUBCERT`. On success the slot moves to `SIGNED`, `signed_utc` is stamped from the logical clock, `attestation_text` is stored, and `coverage_pct_at_signing` is frozen at the cycle's `certification_coverage_pct` as it reads at that instant — a snapshot that is deliberately **not** recomputed afterwards, so it remains an audit trace of what the officer actually relied on.

Two further states complete the machine. `REVOKED` is where `ELIGIBLE` and `SIGNED` slots land when the cycle is knocked out of `FILING_READY` by the tie-out cascade of §3.6; a revoked slot returns to `BLOCKED` when the cycle re-enters `CERTIFICATION_OPEN` from `REVIEW_OPEN`, from where it can become eligible and be signed again. `VOID` is terminal and is where a slot in any of `BLOCKED`, `ELIGIBLE`, `SIGNED` or `REVOKED` lands when the cycle is abandoned. Every one of these system moves increments the slot's `version`.

**Declaring a cycle filing-ready** is the `DisclosureCommitteeChair`'s final gate before submission, moving the workflow axis from `CERTIFICATION_OPEN` to `FILING_READY`. Its conditions read across nearly every aggregate in the system. The charter must still be `ADOPTED` and must still satisfy both the quorum test and the function-coverage test of §3.1, so a committee that has lost members since adoption cannot declare readiness. The cycle must carry a `FILING` milestone, else `ERR_MILESTONE_MISSING`. Every officer certification its `form_type` requires must be `SIGNED` — a quarterly cycle requires `CEO_302` and `CFO_302`, an annual cycle additionally requires `CEO_906` and `CFO_906` — else `ERR_CERTIFICATIONS_INCOMPLETE`. `open_material_event_count` must be zero, else `ERR_OPEN_MATERIAL_EVENT`. `min_slack_days_business` — the business-day variant, never the calendar one — must be at least 1, else `ERR_MILESTONE_SLACK_EXHAUSTED`. And the deficiency gate of rule 30 must be clear on both of its limbs: `unresolved_deficiency_weight` must be exactly 0.00 **and** the remediation axis must not be mid-restatement, meaning `restatement_stage` is neither `UNDER_EVALUATION` nor `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` while `NONE` and `REMEDIATED` both pass. Either limb failing is `ERR_DEFICIENCY_OPEN`, which is how a control deficiency raised against a *previous* filing of the same cycle blocks the amended one. Declaring readiness also runs the breach sweep of §3.3 and increments the cycle's `version`.

### 3.9. Filing, Amendment, Post-Filing Correction and Restatement Remediation

Submitting the filing is reserved to `ComplianceAdmin` and is the only write that moves the cycle onto its filed states. The request names the `cycle_id`, the `document_id` of the report going out, and optionally the `amends_filing_id` of a prior filing this one supersedes. The named document must belong to the same cycle, must have `doc_type` `PERIODIC_FILING` and must be in `APPROVED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_FILING_DOCUMENT_INVALID`. The certification completeness rule of §3.8 applies here a second time, so a filing attempted while a required instrument is unsigned is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATIONS_INCOMPLETE` even if the cycle was somehow declared ready earlier.

An **original filing** omits `amends_filing_id` and is legal only from `FILING_READY`; it moves the cycle's workflow axis to `FILED` and the named document from `APPROVED` to `FILED`. An **amending filing** supplies `amends_filing_id`, which must name a filing on the same cycle currently in `SUBMITTED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID`; it also requires the cycle's `unresolved_deficiency_weight` to be exactly 0.00, else `ERR_DEFICIENCY_OPEN` — the company may not file an amended report while a control weakness raised by a correction on this cycle is still open. That weight is the **only** limb of the rule-30 gate this path reads: an amending filing never inspects `restatement_stage`, so it is legal against a cycle whose remediation axis still sits at `UNDER_EVALUATION` or `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` as long as the weight itself is 0.00. The document an amendment names moves from `APPROVED` to `FILED` exactly as on an original filing. An amendment from `FILED` moves the cycle to `AMENDED`; an amendment from `AMENDED` leaves the cycle in `AMENDED` but still increments its `version`, so successive amendments are legal without limit. In every amending case the superseded filing moves from `SUBMITTED` to the terminal `AMENDED_SUPERSEDED`. That last detail produces the trap worth stating explicitly: once Amendment No. 1 has gone out, the original filing is `AMENDED_SUPERSEDED`, so Amendment No. 2 must point at the most recent `SUBMITTED` filing and not back at the original, and pointing back at the original yields `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID` rather than a terminal-record error, because a row named only by `amends_filing_id` is a referenced row and not a target row in the sense of §2.9.

Creating a filing computes four fields. Its `form_type` is copied from the cycle's `form_type` at creation and thereafter never tracks the cycle. Its sequence number is one greater than the number of filings already on the cycle, counting **every** filing row regardless of status and ordering those rows by `created_at` ascending, never by `id`. Its `accession_label` is the `form_type`, then a hyphen, then the cycle's `fiscal_period_label`, then a hyphen, then that sequence number — so a quarterly cycle labelled `2026-Q2` yields `FORM_10Q-2026-Q2-1` for the original and `FORM_10Q-2026-Q2-2` for the first amendment. Its `filed_utc` is stamped from the logical clock. Its `late_flag` is true when `filed_utc` is strictly after the cycle's `statutory_deadline_utc`, so a filing stamped at exactly the deadline instant is **not** late. The filing is persisted in `status` `SUBMITTED`.

A **post-filing correction** is how a misstatement discovered after submission is assessed. A `ComplianceAdmin` opens one against a filing, supplying a `description` that is non-empty and at most 400 characters, the signed `error_amount` of the misstatement in the filed period, the `current_period_pretax_income` and `prior_period_pretax_income` denominators — each of which must have an absolute value greater than zero — and optionally the `cumulative_prior_uncorrected_amount` of misstatements carried into the filed period, defaulting to 0. Because a filing in `AMENDED_SUPERSEDED` is terminal and is the path-named target here, corrections can only be opened against a filing still in `SUBMITTED`; a correction opened while the filing was `SUBMITTED` nonetheless keeps counting after that filing is later superseded, exactly as §2.12 defines the source set. The row is persisted with `status` `DRAFT` and `method`, `materiality_pct` and `classification` all null. Opening it also nudges the cycle's remediation axis: `restatement_stage` moves from `NONE` to `UNDER_EVALUATION`, and when the stage is already `UNDER_EVALUATION`, `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` or `REMEDIATED` it is left exactly as it is and no error is raised. The cycle's `version` increments either way.

Assessing a correction is where the second dual-variant calculation lives, and the variant is chosen per request rather than stored on the record. The `method` supplied on the assess request may be `ROLLOVER` or `IRON_CURTAIN`, and defaults to `ROLLOVER` when omitted. Under the rollover method, `materiality_pct` is the absolute value of `error_amount` divided by the absolute value of `current_period_pretax_income`, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to two decimal places; `cumulative_prior_uncorrected_amount` is ignored entirely. Under the iron-curtain method, `materiality_pct` is the absolute value of the total of `error_amount` and `cumulative_prior_uncorrected_amount`, divided by the absolute value of `prior_period_pretax_income`, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to two decimal places. The `classification` then follows from that single percentage: `RESTATEMENT` when `materiality_pct` is greater than or equal to 5.00, otherwise `REVISION` when it is greater than or equal to 1.00, otherwise `IMMATERIAL`. Both tests are inclusive at their boundary.

The two methods are not cosmetic alternatives; they routinely disagree on the same stored row. For an `error_amount` of 400000, a `current_period_pretax_income` of 10000000, a `cumulative_prior_uncorrected_amount` of 300000 and a `prior_period_pretax_income` of 12000000, the rollover method returns 4.00 and classifies the correction as a `REVISION`, while the iron-curtain method returns 5.83 and classifies the identical data as a `RESTATEMENT`. An implementation that stores one method's answer and reports the other will diverge on exactly this kind of row.

Assessment then cascades in four hops, and the request must be prepared for the deficiency branch. Alongside `method`, the assess request may carry `likelihood_pct` — a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive expressing the chance the control failure recurs — a `magnitude_amount` of at least zero, and a `compensating_control_present` flag. These three are consumed only on the non-immaterial branch, and they become required there: an assess request whose computed `classification` is `REVISION` or `RESTATEMENT` but which omits any of the three is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Hop one sets `method`, `materiality_pct` and `classification` and moves the correction from `DRAFT` to `ASSESSED`. Hop two, caused by the classification landing on `REVISION` or `RESTATEMENT`, creates exactly one control deficiency row whose `correction_id` names this correction, in `status` `OPEN`, copying `likelihood_pct`, `magnitude_amount` and `compensating_control_present` from the request into the new row and scoring it; an `IMMATERIAL` classification creates no deficiency at all. Hop three, caused by that open deficiency existing, recomputes the cycle's `unresolved_deficiency_weight` upward. Hop four, caused by that weight rising above 0.00 while the classification is `RESTATEMENT`, moves the cycle's `restatement_stage` from `UNDER_EVALUATION` to `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED`. A `REVISION` classification therefore raises a deficiency and a weight but does **not** declare a restatement. The owning cycle is touched on every assessment whatever the branch, so its `version` increments even on the `IMMATERIAL` path where no roll-up and no stage of its own has moved.

Scoring the cascaded deficiency proceeds in a fixed order and mixes a rounded input with unrounded ones, which is the detail implementations get wrong. First, the magnitude ratio is the absolute value of the deficiency's `magnitude_amount` divided by the absolute value of the owning correction's `current_period_pretax_income`. Second, the exposure percentage is the deficiency's `likelihood_pct` multiplied by that ratio, with both factors left unrounded. Third, the compensating credit is 0.60 when `compensating_control_present` is true and 1.00 otherwise. Fourth, `severity_score` is the greater of the correction's `materiality_pct` and the exposure percentage, capped at 100.00, multiplied by the credit, and rounded half up to two decimal places — and the `materiality_pct` entering that comparison is the **already-rounded** two-decimal value produced above, not the unrounded ratio behind it. Fifth, `severity` is selected from the rounded `severity_score`: `MATERIAL_WEAKNESS` when the score is greater than or equal to 5.00, otherwise `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` when it is greater than or equal to 1.50, otherwise `DEFICIENCY`. Sixth, `deficiency_weight` is `severity_score` multiplied by a factor fixed by the selected tier — 1.0 for a `DEFICIENCY`, 2.0 for a `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` and 4.0 for a `MATERIAL_WEAKNESS` — and rounded half up to two decimal places. Rounding is applied to `severity_score` before the tier is chosen and again to `deficiency_weight`, and `remediation_note` starts null.

**Rescinding** a correction is the compensating action for assessing it, and it is required to restore the system exactly. It is legal only from `ASSESSED`, and only while every control deficiency raised by that correction is still `OPEN`; a rescission attempted after any of them has been mitigated is rejected with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_ALREADY_MITIGATED`, because a remediation that has already been performed cannot be un-performed. Rescission cascades in four hops of its own. Hop one moves the correction to the terminal `RESCINDED` and clears `method`, `materiality_pct` and `classification` back to null. Hop two moves every `OPEN` deficiency of that correction to `REVERSED`, which is the only way a deficiency ever reaches that state. Hop three recomputes the cycle's `unresolved_deficiency_weight` from its source rows, which — by the conservation property of §2.12 — returns it to precisely the value it held before the assessment, not to an approximation of it. Hop four returns the remediation axis: `restatement_stage` moves from `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` to `NONE` once the weight is back to 0.00 and no correction on the cycle remains `ASSESSED`, and moves from `UNDER_EVALUATION` to `NONE` once no correction on the cycle remains in `DRAFT` **or** `ASSESSED`. The two conditions are deliberately different: a declared restatement is stood down on assessed corrections alone, while an evaluation that never got as far as a declaration is only stood down when no draft correction is left waiting either.

**Mitigating** a deficiency is the forward path. A `ComplianceAdmin` records a non-empty `remediation_note` of at most 2000 characters against a deficiency in `OPEN`, moving it to the terminal `MITIGATED`. There is no parent-state gate on this write, for the reason given in §2.9, so an attempt against a row already `MITIGATED` or `REVERSED` is reported as `ERR_DEFICIENCY_ALREADY_MITIGATED` and never as a terminal-record or illegal-transition error. Mitigation recomputes the cycle's `unresolved_deficiency_weight` downward. When no `OPEN` deficiency remains beneath the correction, the correction itself moves from `ASSESSED` to the terminal `CLOSED`. And when the cycle's weight has returned to 0.00 with at least one `CLOSED` correction on the cycle, the remediation axis moves from `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED` to `REMEDIATED`. Each of these steps increments the `version` of the row it touches, including the cycle's.

The two axes remain independent throughout, exactly as §3.2 describes. A cycle can legitimately read `FILED` on its workflow axis while its remediation axis reads `RESTATEMENT_DECLARED`, and `AMENDED` while the remediation axis reads `REMEDIATED`. Nothing on the remediation axis moves the workflow axis, and no workflow transition alters `restatement_stage`; the only coupling between them is the deficiency gate of rule 30, and even that reads the two axes asymmetrically — a filing-readiness declaration consults `unresolved_deficiency_weight` **and** `restatement_stage`, while an amending filing consults the weight alone.
