# Hospital Case Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every inpatient stay ends somewhere, and the distance between "medically stable" and "actually out the door" is where hospitals lose the most money and patients lose the most confidence. A frail patient who is clinically ready on Tuesday morning may still be occupying a bed on Friday afternoon because a skilled-nursing authorization is pending, because no wheelchair van window could be found, or because the family has not yet chosen between three facilities they were never properly shown. Each of those days is an avoidable delay — recorded nowhere, coded inconsistently, and invisible to the throughput reports that hospital leadership actually reads. This document specifies the Hospital Case Management & Discharge Planning API, the system of record that makes discharge planning an auditable, measurable workflow rather than an oral tradition passed between case managers.

The system tracks a patient's **episode** from admission through discharge along two independent axes at once: how far the case has progressed toward discharge, and how badly it is currently obstructed. Around that spine it coordinates four separate negotiations that historically happen in parallel and out of band — readmission-risk screening and the interventions that follow from it, the post-acute placement search in which multiple facilities bid on a bed, insurance authorization for the days that placement will consume, and the durable-medical-equipment and transport logistics that must be in place before the patient can physically leave. Each of these produces obligations that the others depend on, and the API's job is to make those dependencies explicit: a placement cannot be documented without live coverage, a discharge cannot happen while a transport order sits unconfirmed, and a denial from a payer reaches all the way back to cancel an ambulance that was already booked.

Two regulatory concerns shape the design as much as the operational ones. Patient choice must be demonstrably free — the system enforces a no-steering rule on which facilities are invited to bid and a minimum breadth of choice, and it records an explicit attestation at the moment a choice is documented. And responsibility must be separable — the operator who declares an episode ready may not be the operator who discharges it, and the reviewer who waives a barrier may not be the person who raised it. Alongside these controls the system continuously derives a length-of-stay picture that separates the stay the patient clinically needed from the stay the process imposed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. When either header is absent, or when `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else — before path resolution, before body parsing, before any state inspection.

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `CaseManager`, `UtilizationReviewNurse`, `PayerRepresentative`, `PostAcuteLiaison`, and `DischargeCoordinator`. Once authentication succeeds, the acting role is matched against the roles authorized for the requested operation (see the endpoint table in §4); a role that is not listed for an operation receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

A `PlatformAdmin` owns the operational registry. It seeds payers, patients, post-acute providers, network contracts and transport vehicles, drives the logical clock, and reads throughput reports — but it holds no clinical authority whatsoever: it cannot screen a patient, cannot place one, cannot decide an authorization, and cannot discharge. A `CaseManager` owns the case from admission through readiness — screening, barrier identification, placement search, authorization submission, equipment and transport arrangement, readmission-risk interventions, and the mark-ready declaration — but may not waive a barrier, may not decide an authorization, and may not discharge. A `UtilizationReviewNurse` is the independent compliance reviewer: it is the only role that may waive a barrier, it files authorization appeals, and it may force a ready episode back into planning. A `DischargeCoordinator` executes the discharge itself, confirming and cancelling logistics orders and performing the discharge on an episode that a different operator declared ready. A `PayerRepresentative` acts on behalf of exactly one payer and decides that payer's authorization requests and nothing else. A `PostAcuteLiaison` acts on behalf of exactly one post-acute provider, answering referral rounds with bed offers and retracting its own outstanding offers; it may never document a patient's choice, and that separation is precisely what keeps referral fairness auditable.

Roles differ in how `X-User-ID` is interpreted. When `X-User-Role` is `PayerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Payer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Payer` compare the resource's payer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so deciding an authorization requires `X-User-ID` to equal that authorization's `payer_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PostAcuteLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PostAcuteProvider`; ownership checks compare the resource's provider foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so creating a bed offer requires the offering `provider_id` to be `X-User-ID`, and withdrawing an offer requires that offer's `provider_id` to be `X-User-ID`. When either of these two entity-backed roles acts on a resource it does not own, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `CaseManager`, for `UtilizationReviewNurse`, and for `DischargeCoordinator`: for all three, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that the server may record — as `assigned_case_manager_id` and `marked_ready_by_user_id` on an `Episode`, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a `RiskScreening`, and as `opened_by_user_id` on a `Barrier` — but never resolves against any entity table. Those recorded identifiers exist so that the four-eyes controls described in §2.10 can compare one operator against another, not so that they can be looked up.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100; a supplied `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). `offset` defaults to 0 and counts records to skip from the start of the ordered result set.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. Each list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters offered by individual list endpoints — such as `patient_id`, `case_status`, `primary_payer_id`, `mrn`, `provider_type`, `status`, `episode_id`, `payer_id`, `provider_id`, `referral_id`, `chosen_bed_offer_id`, `vehicle_id`, `supplier_provider_id`, and `destination_provider_id` — are all optional, and when several are supplied they combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and Versioning

Every resource in this system is identified by a server-minted UUID exposed as `id`, with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; they receive one. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was minted.

All twelve resource types in this system — `Payer`, `Patient`, `Episode`, `RiskScreening`, `Barrier`, `PostAcuteProvider`, `NetworkContract`, `Referral`, `BedOffer`, `AuthorizationRequest`, `TransportVehicle`, and `LogisticsOrder` — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. Derived fields that are recomputed on read — posture, rank scores, cost projections, throughput figures — do not by themselves constitute a mutation and do not advance `version`.

The `version` integer is not decorative. Two write operations described in §2.5 require the client to prove it has observed the parent resource's current `version` before it is allowed to write, and several workflow steps in §3 explicitly note where a `version` advances, because those advances are exactly what invalidates a stale client view.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Offer Creation and Logistics Creation

Two write paths in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent resource's `version`, and no others. An endpoint not named in this subsection accepts no version field at all.

Creating a bed offer against a referral requires the request body to carry `expected_referral_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `Referral` as the liaison last observed it. If `expected_referral_version` does not equal that referral's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REFERRAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An advanced referral version means the round the liaison is answering has already moved on — a competing offer opened the round, a choice was documented, or the case manager rebroadcast a fresh round — so the liaison's view of `round_index` and of `broadcast_provider_ids` is stale, and the offer would otherwise attach to the wrong round.

Creating a logistics order against an episode requires the request body to carry `expected_episode_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `Episode` as the case manager last observed it. If `expected_episode_version` does not equal that episode's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EPISODE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard matters because a patient-transport create is destructive: it supersedes any prior patient-transport order on the same episode, so the version check forces the caller to re-read the episode — including `logistics_committed_cents` and `case_status` — before overwriting arrangements it may never have seen.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, expiry, hold window and delay clock in this system reads a single server-side logical UTC clock rather than real wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host machine's wall clock. A `PlatformAdmin` may replace the logical clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Replacing the clock mutates no rows at all — it does not sweep expiries, does not close delay intervals, and does not re-derive any stored field. The `current_utc` that response reports is the instant echoed back exactly as the `PlatformAdmin` spelled it in `to_iso8601`, unchanged. Stored fields are a separate matter: wherever a row field records the logical clock instant, or an instant derived from it — `Barrier.clock_start_at` and `Barrier.clock_stop_at`, `RiskScreening.submitted_at`, `Episode.marked_ready_at` and `Episode.discharged_at`, and `AuthorizationRequest.decided_at` and `AuthorizationRequest.expires_at` — the value is written in the same rendering as `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, no matter how the instant happened to be spelled when the clock was set.

Expiry is instead **lazy**. Bed holds that have run out, authorizations that have passed their expiry instant, and referrals that have passed their needed-by instant are all evaluated at the start of the next request that reads or writes the affected row (or its parent), and only then. A request that reads an episode therefore performs three sweeps before it builds its response — bed-offer hold expiry, authorization expiry, and referral needed-by expiry — and reports the post-sweep truth. A request that reads a provider sweeps that provider's bed-offer holds first. A request that reads a referral sweeps its offers' holds and its own needed-by deadline first. A request that reads or writes a bed offer sweeps that offer's hold first. A request that reads or writes an authorization sweeps its expiry first.

### 2.7. Derived Fields and Recomputation Discipline

A substantial share of the fields this API exposes are computed rather than stored-and-trusted. Episode posture, risk scores and bands, the avoidable-delay union, the readmission bundle flag, throughput figures, provider counters, offer rank scores and cost projections are all re-derived from their inputs whenever the owning resource is read or written. Two consequences follow, and both are load-bearing.

First, a derived field never drifts. `Episode.barrier_posture` is recomputed at the start of every request that reads or writes an episode or any of its barriers. `Referral.recommended_bed_offer_id` and `BedOffer.rank_score` are recomputed on every read of the offer or of its referral. `BedOffer.projected_gross_cents`, `projected_patient_share_cents`, `projected_plan_share_cents` and `rate_variant_applied` are recomputed on every read of the offer or its referral as well, which means a network contract signed after an offer was made changes what that offer projects.

Second, there are deliberate exceptions where a value is **pinned** at creation and never re-derived. `LogisticsOrder.cost_cents` is fixed at create time and never recalculated, so a later edit to a vehicle's rates cannot disturb the committed ledger. The four snapshot fields on a `RiskScreening` — `prior_admissions_snapshot`, `lives_alone_snapshot`, `has_pcp_snapshot`, and the screening's own clinical scores — are frozen when the screening row is created, so later edits to the patient record never retroactively rescore a screening that already exists. And a superseded screening's `composite_risk_score` and `risk_band` stay frozen at their submitted values forever.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Validation

Resources named in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. A request naming an episode and then a nested resource under it reports the episode's absence first. Each resource type has its own 404 code: `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BED_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOGISTICS_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_NETWORK_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

Checks across the whole API run in a fixed precedence, and the ordering is normative because different checks on the same request would otherwise produce different error codes depending on implementation whim. The ladder is: authentication of the two headers first; then the endpoint's role list; then, for the entity-backed roles, the ownership comparison that yields `ERR_NOT_OWNER` (this applies to bed-offer creation, offer withdrawal, and authorization decisions); then left-to-right existence of every path resource; then request-body well-formedness, meaning required fields present, enum values that are declared members, integers inside their declared ranges — the three screening scores between 0 and 10 inclusive, `quality_star_rating` between 1 and 5 inclusive, `coinsurance_pct` between 0.000 and 0.500 inclusive, rates and day counts strictly positive — and `limit` inside 1 through 100, all failing as `ERR_VALIDATION`; then existence of every resource id referenced in the request **body** rather than the path, covering `patient_id`, `primary_payer_id`, `provider_id`, `vehicle_id`, `supplier_provider_id`, `destination_provider_id`, `chosen_bed_offer_id`, and every member of `provider_ids`, each failing with that resource type's own 404 code; then the two uniqueness constraints described below; then the optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.5; then state-machine admissibility; then the safety, referential, temporal and fiscal rules described throughout §3.

Two uniqueness constraints sit in that ladder. A `Patient`'s `mrn` must be unique across all patient rows; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN` (HTTP 409). At most one `NetworkContract` row may exist for a given combination of `payer_id` and `provider_id`; a second one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NETWORK_CONTRACT` (HTTP 409). Both are checked after body well-formedness and body-referenced existence, and before any concurrency or state check.

Beyond that, whenever an operation targets a resource whose current state does not admit the requested transition, the rejection carries the state code belonging to that resource type — `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SCREENING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BARRIER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AUTHORIZATION_STATE`, or `ERR_INVALID_LOGISTICS_STATE` — each with HTTP 409.

### 2.9. Money, Time, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer cents. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fraction of a cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent. Where a rate is a percentage it is expressed as a decimal fraction — `coinsurance_pct` carries three decimals between 0.000 and 0.500 inclusive, and `volume_discount_pct` takes one of three tier values.

Rounding **order** is pinned wherever it could change an answer, and §3 states the pinned order in each case. Two rules recur. Hour quantities — `avoidable_delay_hours`, `los_hours`, `adjusted_los_hours`, `expected_los_hours` — are carried to one decimal place, rounded half up. The `observed_expected_ratio` is carried to three decimal places, rounded half up. Risk scoring is pure integer arithmetic with no rounding at any step, and the same is true of offer rank scoring, where the only non-integer operation is a floor applied to a cost penalty.

### 2.10. Conserved Ledgers, Compensation, and Four-Eyes Control

Three running totals in this system are **conserved**: every credit has a matching debit, and after a compensating action the total returns exactly to its pre-action value. `Episode.logistics_committed_cents` is the sum of `cost_cents` across that episode's logistics orders in `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` status; confirming an order credits it, cancelling a confirmed order debits it by the same amount, and completing a confirmed order leaves it untouched because completion stays inside the committed set. `Episode.authorized_days_total` is the sum of `approved_days` across that episode's authorization requests in `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` status; an approval credits it and an expiry debits the same number of days back out. `PostAcuteProvider.held_bed_units` is the count of that provider's bed offers in `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED_BY_PATIENT` status; making an offer holds a unit, and every path out of `OFFERED` other than acceptance releases it.

Segregation of duties is enforced at two points, both under `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The `DischargeCoordinator` performing a discharge must have an `X-User-ID` different from the episode's `marked_ready_by_user_id`. The `UtilizationReviewNurse` waiving a barrier must have an `X-User-ID` different from that barrier's `opened_by_user_id`. Separately, waiving is restricted by role as well as by identity: only a `UtilizationReviewNurse` may waive, and a `CaseManager` attempting a waiver is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_ROLE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403) even though the same endpoint admits that role for every other barrier transition. The role check precedes the identity check, so a `CaseManager` who also opened the barrier sees `ERR_WAIVER_ROLE_REQUIRED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Operational Registry: Payers, Patients, Providers, Contracts and Vehicles

Nothing clinical can happen until a `PlatformAdmin` has stood up the registry. Registering an insurance payer captures its `name` and its `coinsurance_pct` — the member's cost share, expressed with three decimals somewhere between 0.000 and 0.500 inclusive — together with three coverage-policy switches. `snf_auth_required` states whether a placement at a skilled-nursing, inpatient-rehab or long-term-acute-care facility may only be chosen once coverage has been granted; `hh_auth_required` states the same for home health. Both default to `true` when omitted from the request. `auth_expiry_days` is the number of days added to a decision instant to produce the moment coverage lapses, and it defaults to 30 when omitted.

Patient registration is shared between `PlatformAdmin` and `CaseManager`. Each patient carries an `mrn` — the medical record number, which must be unique across every patient row, on pain of `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN` — plus `full_name` and `date_of_birth` as an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form. The `primary_payer_id` names the payer whose coverage rules will gate every authorization on this patient's episodes. Three further attributes feed the readmission-risk model directly rather than being administrative colour: `prior_admissions_180d`, a non-negative count of the patient's inpatient admissions over the trailing half-year; `lives_alone`, the social-support flag; and `has_pcp`, whether the patient has an established primary care provider. The patient list is filterable by `primary_payer_id` or by `mrn`.

Post-acute providers are registered with a `name`, a `provider_type` of `SNF`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `IRF`, `LTACH`, or `DME_SUPPLIER`, a `quality_star_rating` between 1 and 5 inclusive, a whole-mile `distance_miles_from_hospital` of zero or more, and `bed_unit_codes`, the list of bed-unit code strings the facility can hold beds under. A durable-medical-equipment supplier holds no beds and is registered with an empty `bed_unit_codes` list. Providers begin life with `status` of `ACTIVE`, with `held_bed_units`, `offers_received_count`, `offers_submitted_count` and `offers_accepted_count` all at zero and `acceptance_rate_pct` at 0.0. Those five counters are the fairness telemetry: `offers_received_count` counts the broadcast rounds this provider was invited into, `offers_submitted_count` counts every bed offer it has ever created regardless of outcome, `offers_accepted_count` counts the ones a patient chose, and `acceptance_rate_pct` is `offers_accepted_count` multiplied by 100, divided by `offers_submitted_count`, rounded half up to one decimal place — with the special case that a provider which has submitted nothing reads exactly 0.0 rather than erroring on the division. Providers can be filtered by `provider_type` and by `status`.

Suspending a provider moves its `status` to `SUSPENDED` and removes it from every future referral candidate pool, but deliberately leaves any bed holds it already has outstanding untouched. Transport vehicles are registered with an `operator_name`, a `vehicle_type` of `WHEELCHAIR_VAN`, `BLS_AMBULANCE` or `ALS_AMBULANCE`, a strictly positive flat dispatch fee in `base_rate_cents`, and a `per_mile_cents` marginal charge of zero or more; they too start `ACTIVE`, and taking one out of service moves `status` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Already-confirmed orders keep their occupied intervals when a vehicle goes out of service, but no new order may be confirmed against it. Whenever an action reaches for a `SUSPENDED` provider or an `OUT_OF_SERVICE` vehicle — in a broadcast slate, in a bed offer, or in a logistics confirmation — the request is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 422).

The bridge between the payer world and the provider world is the network contract, which links one `payer_id` to one `provider_id` and records whether the relationship is `in_network` along with the contracted per-diem in `negotiated_daily_rate_cents`, which must be strictly positive. At most one such row may exist for any given payer-and-provider pair; the second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_NETWORK_CONTRACT`. Contracts can be listed and filtered by `payer_id` or `provider_id`. That single row, or its absence, is what decides which rate variant applies to every cost figure this system produces.

### 3.2. Admission, Risk Screening, and the Two Scoring Variants

A `CaseManager` opens an episode for an admitted patient by naming the `patient_id`, the `admission_type` — `EMERGENT`, `ELECTIVE`, or `OBSERVATION` — the `drg_code` for reporting, the `drg_gmlos_days` geometric-mean length of stay for that diagnosis-related group (strictly greater than zero, carried to at most two decimals), the `attending_unit` code, and the `admitted_at` instant, which may not be later than the logical clock at create time; an admission instant in the future is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A new episode starts with `case_status` of `ADMITTED`, `barrier_posture` of `CLEAR`, `assigned_case_manager_id` set to the creating operator's `X-User-ID`, `current_risk_score` of 0, `current_risk_band` of `LOW`, `risk_variant_applied` of `ADMISSION_VARIANT`, `logistics_committed_cents` and `authorized_days_total` both at zero, `discharge_disposition` null, and all four intervention flags false. Episodes may be listed and filtered by `patient_id` or `case_status`.

The episode's `case_status` axis runs `ADMITTED → SCREENED → PLANNING → READY_FOR_DISCHARGE → DISCHARGED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from the four non-terminal states and `DISCHARGED` and `CANCELLED` both terminal. Running in parallel and entirely independently is the `barrier_posture` axis, which takes the values `CLEAR`, `ACTIVE` and `ESCALATED` and is described in §3.3. Neither axis constrains the other's vocabulary; both constrain what operations are legal.

Risk screening is the first clinical act. A `CaseManager` creates a screening under an episode by supplying a `screening_type` of `ADMISSION` or `RESCREEN` and four clinical inputs: `functional_score`, independence in activities of daily living from 0 for fully dependent to 10 for fully independent; `cognitive_score` on the same 0-to-10 scale; `social_support_score`, available caregiver support, again 0 to 10; and `medication_count`, the discharge medication count, which must be zero or greater. At the moment the screening row is created the system takes three snapshots from the patient — `prior_admissions_snapshot` from `prior_admissions_180d`, `lives_alone_snapshot` from `lives_alone`, and `has_pcp_snapshot` from `has_pcp` — and it is these frozen copies, never the live patient record, that the scoring model reads. A fresh screening begins in `DRAFT` status with `composite_risk_score` of 0 and `risk_band` of `LOW`. An episode's screenings can be listed in creation order.

Submitting a draft screening is what scores it. Submission requires the screening to be in `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_SCREENING_STATE` — and requires the owning episode's `case_status` to be one of `ADMITTED`, `SCREENED`, or `PLANNING`. There is one additional guard specific to admission screenings: when `screening_type` is `ADMISSION`, no other screening on the same episode may already be `SUBMITTED` with `screening_type` of `ADMISSION`, and a second one is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_SCREENING_STATE`. On success the screening's `status` becomes `SUBMITTED`, `submitted_at` records the logical clock instant, and `submitted_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. Any other screening on the same episode that was `SUBMITTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and its `composite_risk_score` and `risk_band` are frozen at whatever they were.

The score itself is a weighted integer tally with no rounding anywhere. The base begins with prior-admission history: the smaller of `prior_admissions_snapshot` and a cap of four admissions, multiplied by eight points each — so a patient with seven prior admissions contributes the same 32 points as one with four. To that are added three deficit terms, each computed as ten minus the corresponding score so that a lower clinical score contributes more risk: the functional deficit weighted at three points per unit, the cognitive deficit at two points per unit, and the social-support deficit at two points per unit. Six further points are added when `lives_alone_snapshot` is true. Five further points are added when `has_pcp_snapshot` is false. And seven polypharmacy points are added when `medication_count` is at least ten — exactly ten qualifies.

On top of that base sits a variant adjustment, and this is where the two variants of the model diverge. Under `ADMISSION_VARIANT` — which applies when `screening_type` is `ADMISSION`, that is, a screening submitted while the case is still at admission or freshly screened — the adjustment is exactly zero: neither barrier history nor placement credit exists yet, so neither is consulted. Under `PREDISCHARGE_VARIANT` — which applies when `screening_type` is `RESCREEN`, and again as a side effect of the mark-ready declaration — the adjustment adds three points for each barrier in the episode's accumulated history, counting at most five barriers, so at most fifteen points; and it subtracts a twelve-point placement credit when the episode has a referral in `PLACED` status whose `requested_level_of_care` is `SNF`, `IRF`, or `LTACH`. Home-health placements earn no credit. The barrier history term reads `barriers_ever_opened_count`, which counts every barrier row ever created on the episode in any status, waived ones included, and does not double-count a barrier that was reopened. The consequence is that identical clinical inputs produce a different score and often a different band depending on which variant ran, and `variant_applied` on the screening — together with `risk_variant_applied` on the episode — records which one it was.

The base and the adjustment are summed and then clamped into the range 0 through 100 inclusive, and the clamp is applied last, after the adjustment. The resulting `composite_risk_score` maps to a `risk_band` by threshold: 70 or above is `VERY_HIGH`, otherwise 50 or above is `HIGH`, otherwise 30 or above is `MODERATE`, and anything below 30 is `LOW`. The episode adopts the submitting screening's figures into `current_risk_score`, `current_risk_band`, and `risk_variant_applied`.

Submission of an admission screening also advances the episode: when `case_status` is `ADMITTED` and the submitted screening belongs to that episode and carries `screening_type` of `ADMISSION`, `case_status` moves `ADMITTED → SCREENED`. Immediately after the score is computed, barrier derivation runs, and immediately after that the episode's `barrier_posture` and `readmission_bundle_complete` are recomputed.

### 3.3. Barriers, Delay Coding, and the Escalation Posture

A **barrier** is the unit of accountability for a stalled discharge. Both a `CaseManager` and a `UtilizationReviewNurse` may record one against an episode by supplying a `barrier_code` drawn from `HOUSING`, `TRANSPORT`, `INSURANCE_AUTH`, `DME_DELIVERY`, `GUARDIANSHIP`, `BEHAVIORAL`, `FAMILY_DECISION`, `PHYSICIAN_ORDER`, or `BED_AVAILABILITY`; a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`; and a `delay_reason_code` drawn from `AWAITING_BED`, `AWAITING_AUTH`, `AWAITING_DME`, `AWAITING_TRANSPORT`, `AWAITING_FAMILY`, `AWAITING_PHYSICIAN`, `CLINICAL_INSTABILITY`, or `PATIENT_REFUSAL`. A manually recorded barrier begins with `status` of `OPEN`, `clock_start_at` set to the logical clock instant, `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `origin` of `MANUAL`, and `reopen_count` of zero. The `avoidable` flag is derived, not supplied: it is true exactly when `delay_reason_code` is one of the six codes beginning "awaiting" — `AWAITING_BED`, `AWAITING_AUTH`, `AWAITING_DME`, `AWAITING_TRANSPORT`, `AWAITING_FAMILY`, `AWAITING_PHYSICIAN` — and false for the two clinical codes, `CLINICAL_INSTABILITY` and `PATIENT_REFUSAL`. Recording a barrier recomputes the episode's `barrier_posture`, but it deliberately does **not** advance a `SCREENED` episode to `PLANNING`: barriers are not planning artifacts. An episode's barriers can be listed in creation order and filtered by `status`.

Some barriers are raised by the system rather than by a person, and screening submission is one such source. Immediately after a screening is scored, two derivation rules are evaluated in this exact order, each capable of creating at most one barrier. The first rule fires when the freshly computed `risk_band` is `HIGH` or `VERY_HIGH` **and** `has_pcp_snapshot` is false; it creates a barrier with `barrier_code` of `PHYSICIAN_ORDER`, `severity` of `MEDIUM`, and `delay_reason_code` of `AWAITING_PHYSICIAN`. The second rule fires when `lives_alone_snapshot` is true **and** `functional_score` is 5 or below; it creates a barrier with `barrier_code` of `HOUSING`, `severity` of `HIGH`, and `delay_reason_code` of `AWAITING_FAMILY`. Both derived barriers carry `origin` of `SCREENING_AUTO`, `clock_start_at` at the logical clock instant, and the literal string `system` as `opened_by_user_id`. A rule is skipped when the episode already carries a barrier with the same `barrier_code` in `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` status — the derivation deduplicates rather than piling up. When both rules fire, the rows are created in rule order, so the physician-order barrier's `created_at` strictly precedes the housing barrier's.

Barriers move through a cyclic lifecycle. A `CaseManager` or `UtilizationReviewNurse` drives it by naming a `to_status`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `note`. From `OPEN` a barrier may go to `MITIGATING`, leaving `clock_start_at` untouched so the delay interval keeps accruing. From either `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` it may go to `RESOLVED`, which sets `clock_stop_at` to the logical clock instant and closes out `accrued_delay_minutes`, the per-barrier running total of all its closed intervals plus its currently open interval up to the logical clock. From `RESOLVED` it may return to `OPEN` — this is the revision loop, and it may repeat without bound — incrementing `reopen_count` by one, setting `clock_start_at` to the logical clock instant and `clock_stop_at` back to null. And from either `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` it may be waived, which sets `clock_stop_at` and moves it to the terminal `WAIVED` status. Any `to_status` not reachable from the barrier's current status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BARRIER_STATE`: `WAIVED` is terminal and admits no exit, and the step from `RESOLVED` directly to `MITIGATING` is not a declared transition.

Waiving is the compliance escape hatch and is guarded twice, as §2.10 sets out: it demands the `UtilizationReviewNurse` role, and it demands a different operator than the one recorded in `opened_by_user_id`. A waiver does more than close the current interval — it erases the barrier from the delay accounting entirely, including intervals that had already been closed before the waiver.

Reopening a barrier has a consequence that reaches beyond the barrier itself: when the episode's `case_status` is `READY_FOR_DISCHARGE` at the moment of the reopen, the episode is cascaded back to `PLANNING`. That cascaded revert creates no additional barrier, because the barrier that caused it already exists. Every barrier status change recomputes both `Episode.avoidable_delay_hours` and `Episode.barrier_posture`.

The posture axis is a rollup over the episode's barrier children, recomputed at the start of every request that reads or writes an episode or any of its barriers. Consider the set of that episode's barriers whose `status` is `OPEN` or `MITIGATING`; `RESOLVED` and `WAIVED` rows never participate. When that set is empty, `barrier_posture` is `CLEAR`. When the set is non-empty and contains at least one barrier whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`, or at least one whose age — the logical clock now less its `clock_start_at`, measured in whole hours — has reached 72 hours or more, `barrier_posture` is `ESCALATED`; the age comparison is inclusive, so a barrier that has been open for exactly 72 hours escalates. Otherwise `barrier_posture` is `ACTIVE`. Every transition among the three values falls out of that rule: an episode goes `CLEAR → ACTIVE` when the first non-critical, non-aged barrier opens, `CLEAR → ESCALATED` directly when a `CRITICAL` barrier is created on an episode whose open set was empty, `ACTIVE → ESCALATED` when a remaining barrier turns critical or ages past the threshold, `ESCALATED → ACTIVE` when the escalating barrier is resolved or waived while other non-critical barriers remain open, and `ACTIVE → CLEAR` or `ESCALATED → CLEAR` when the last open barrier is resolved or waived.

While `barrier_posture` reads `ESCALATED`, two operations are blocked outright with `ERR_ESCALATED_POSTURE` (HTTP 409): declaring the episode ready for discharge, and discharging it. No other operation is gated on posture.

Avoidable delay is not a simple sum of barrier durations, because barriers overlap and double-counting would inflate every report. The episode's `avoidable_delay_hours` is computed as a union. Each barrier on the episode contributes a candidate half-open interval, except that a barrier whose `delay_reason_code` is `CLINICAL_INSTABILITY` or `PATIENT_REFUSAL` is skipped — non-avoidable delay never counts — and a barrier whose `status` is `WAIVED` is skipped entirely, its whole history erased. For each remaining barrier the interval starts at `clock_start_at` and ends at `clock_stop_at` when one is set, or at the logical clock now when the barrier is still running. An interval whose end is at or before its start contributes nothing and is dropped, which is exactly what happens to a barrier reopened and re-resolved at the same logical instant. Only the barrier's **current** interval is modelled, since `clock_start_at` is reset on every reopen; a reopened barrier therefore contributes only its newest interval, and a freshly reopened barrier contributes a zero-length interval that leaves the union unchanged.

The surviving intervals are then sorted by start ascending and, where starts tie, by end ascending — never by `id`, so the result is deterministic. Walking the sorted list, two intervals merge into one whenever the next interval's start is at or before the current interval's end; intervals that merely touch, with a gap of zero, do merge. The lengths of the merged intervals are summed in whole minutes, and only at that very end is the total divided by 60 and rounded half up to one decimal place to yield `avoidable_delay_hours`.

### 3.4. The Placement Search: Broadcast Rounds, Fairness, and Bed Holds

Post-acute placement is run as a repeated, auditable negotiation rather than a phone call. A `CaseManager` opens a placement search against an episode by declaring the `requested_level_of_care` — `SNF`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `IRF`, or `LTACH` — the `projected_stay_days`, which must be at least 1 and which becomes the covered-day count every cost projection uses, and `needed_by`, the instant by which a bed must be available. The `needed_by` instant must be strictly later than the logical clock at create time, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A new search begins with `status` of `DRAFT`, `round_index` of 0, an empty `broadcast_provider_ids` list, and null `chosen_bed_offer_id`, `choice_basis`, and `patient_choice_attested` of false. Creating a placement search is a planning artifact, so an episode sitting at `SCREENED` advances to `PLANNING`. Placement searches may be listed and filtered by `episode_id`, by `chosen_bed_offer_id`, or by `status`.

Broadcasting opens a round. The case manager supplies `provider_ids`, an ordered list of the facilities to be invited, and the referral moves `DRAFT → BROADCAST`. Two fairness rules govern that slate, and both are refusals at HTTP 422. First, the breadth rule: the slate must contain at least three distinct providers whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `provider_type` equals the referral's `requested_level_of_care`. Exactly three passes; two fails, and so does an empty slate. The refusal is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PROVIDER_CHOICE`, and its purpose is that patient choice must be presented from a genuine pool rather than from a foregone conclusion. Second, the no-steering rule: the slate must contain **every** `ACTIVE` provider whose `provider_type` equals the requested level of care and which holds a network contract with `in_network` set to true for the episode's payer. Omitting even one such provider is `ERR_REFERRAL_FAIRNESS_VIOLATION`. A hospital may add out-of-network or non-contracted facilities to the slate freely; what it may not do is quietly leave an in-network competitor off it. A slate naming a `SUSPENDED` provider is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`, and a slate naming a provider id that resolves to nothing at all is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`.

A successful broadcast increments `round_index` by one — from 0 to 1 on the first round — replaces `broadcast_provider_ids` with the supplied list in the order given, increments each listed provider's `offers_received_count` by one, and advances the referral's `version` by one. Broadcasting again later is how a fresh round opens, and that is the negotiation cycle: `BROADCAST → CHOICE_PENDING → BROADCAST` repeats one round per iteration. A rebroadcast is only permitted from `CHOICE_PENDING`, and only once every bed offer carrying the current `round_index` has reached a terminal, non-accepted status — `DECLINED_BY_PATIENT`, `WITHDRAWN`, or `EXPIRED`. If any offer of the current round is still `OFFERED`, or one has already been accepted, the rebroadcast is refused with `ERR_ROUND_STILL_OPEN` (HTTP 422). A rebroadcast re-runs both fairness rules against the new slate, increments `round_index` again, replaces `broadcast_provider_ids`, credits each newly listed provider's `offers_received_count`, and advances `version`. Attempting a broadcast from a status that admits neither the first round nor a rebroadcast is `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE`. The first broadcast also requires the episode's `case_status` to be `PLANNING`.

A `PostAcuteLiaison` answers a round by creating a bed offer. The offer body carries `expected_referral_version` as described in §2.5, a `bed_unit_code`, a `bed_available_from` instant, a `hold_expires_at` instant, and a `daily_rate_cents` billed per-diem that must be strictly positive. The bed hold the offer creates is **half-open**: it includes `bed_available_from` and excludes `hold_expires_at`, and `hold_expires_at` must be strictly after `bed_available_from` or the request is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Four referential guards apply. The offering provider must be the liaison itself, so `X-User-ID` must equal the provider the offer is attributed to, and the offer's `provider_id` is taken from that identity rather than from the body. The offering provider's `provider_type` must equal the referral's `requested_level_of_care`, and a mismatch is `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The offering provider must be a member of the referral's current `broadcast_provider_ids` — a liaison may only answer a round it was invited to, and answering an uninvited round is `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_IN_BROADCAST`. And `bed_unit_code` must be one of the strings in that provider's `bed_unit_codes` list, or the request is `ERR_UNKNOWN_BED_UNIT`. An offer from a `SUSPENDED` provider is `ERR_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`.

Bed holds are exclusive per bed unit. The new offer's half-open interval must not intersect the interval of any other bed offer on the same combination of `provider_id` and `bed_unit_code` whose `status` is `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED_BY_PATIENT`; an intersection is `ERR_BED_HOLD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Because the intervals are half-open, touching endpoints do not intersect: a new hold whose `bed_available_from` equals an existing hold's `hold_expires_at` is accepted, and the two sit back to back.

A created offer starts with `status` of `OFFERED`, copies the referral's current `round_index` into its own `round_index`, increments the provider's `held_bed_units` and `offers_submitted_count` by one each, computes its cost projection as described in §3.5, and advances the referral's `version` by one. The round's first offer also moves the referral `BROADCAST → CHOICE_PENDING`. Offers may be listed and filtered by `referral_id`, by `provider_id`, or by `status`.

Three exits from `OFFERED` do not involve a patient choosing. A liaison may withdraw its own outstanding offer, which moves it to `WITHDRAWN`, decrements that provider's `held_bed_units`, and makes the hold interval immediately reusable; withdrawing an offer that belongs to another provider is `ERR_NOT_OWNER`, and withdrawing one that is no longer `OFFERED` is `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. When the withdrawn offer was the last non-terminal offer of the round, the referral nevertheless stays at `CHOICE_PENDING` — a rebroadcast is what reopens the negotiation. Separately, an offer whose `hold_expires_at` the logical clock has reached moves lazily `OFFERED → EXPIRED`, again releasing `held_bed_units` and the interval; that sweep runs before the state check on any request touching the offer or its referral, so an attempt to withdraw or to choose a hold that has run out reads the offer as `EXPIRED` and is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). And when the logical clock reaches a referral's `needed_by` while the referral is still `BROADCAST` with no offer of the current round outstanding, the referral itself moves lazily to the terminal `EXPIRED` status and a barrier is raised on the episode with `barrier_code` of `BED_AVAILABILITY`, `severity` of `HIGH`, `delay_reason_code` of `AWAITING_BED`, and `origin` of `REVERT_AUTO` — unless such a barrier is already open on that episode, in which case none is added.

### 3.5. Ranking Offers, Projecting Cost, and Documenting the Patient's Choice

Every outstanding bed offer carries a `rank_score`, an integer advisory figure recomputed on every read of the offer or of its referral. It is assembled from four terms. The provider's `quality_star_rating` is multiplied by ten, so a five-star facility starts fifty points ahead of a zero baseline. A flat bonus of twenty-five points is added when a network contract exists between the episode's payer and the offering provider with `in_network` set to true. A proximity term adds whatever remains of a twenty-mile allowance after subtracting the provider's `distance_miles_from_hospital`, floored at zero — so a facility eight miles away earns twelve points, one exactly twenty miles away earns nothing, and one thirty miles away is not penalised below zero. Finally a cost penalty is subtracted: the offer's `daily_rate_cents` divided by five thousand and rounded down to a whole number, so each full fifty dollars of daily rate costs the offer one point. No rounding occurs anywhere else in the calculation.

Those scores drive `recommended_bed_offer_id` on the referral, an advisory selector output that is recomputed on every read and is never a stored relationship. The eligible set is the offers belonging to that referral whose `round_index` equals the referral's current `round_index`, whose `status` is `OFFERED`, whose `hold_expires_at` is strictly later than the logical clock now, and whose provider's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Those are ordered by `rank_score` descending; ties are broken by `daily_rate_cents` ascending, and any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. The top entry's identifier becomes `recommended_bed_offer_id`. When the eligible set is empty, `recommended_bed_offer_id` is null and no error is raised on read. This value is advice only: it never constrains which offer a patient may choose.

Alongside the rank, each offer carries a cost projection with the same recompute-on-read discipline. The projection first decides which rate variant applies by looking for the single network contract joining the episode's patient's `primary_payer_id` to the offer's `provider_id`. When such a contract exists and its `in_network` is true, `rate_variant_applied` is `NETWORK_RATE` and the raw daily rate is that contract's `negotiated_daily_rate_cents`. When no contract row exists, or the row exists with `in_network` false, `rate_variant_applied` is `BILLED_RATE` and the raw daily rate is the offer's `daily_rate_cents` increased by a quarter — that is, multiplied by 1.25 and rounded half up to whole cents. The same offer therefore projects a materially larger gross, patient share and plan share when the facility is out of network.

The gross is tiered by day, and the rounding order is pinned. The covered-day count is the referral's `projected_stay_days`. Each covered day is priced separately: the first twenty days are priced at the raw daily rate, and every day from the twenty-first onward is priced at eighty-five percent of the raw daily rate, rounded half up to whole cents **for that day**. Only after every day has been individually rounded are the day prices summed into `projected_gross_cents`. Summing first and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer. From the gross, `projected_patient_share_cents` is the gross multiplied by the payer's `coinsurance_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents, and `projected_plan_share_cents` is simply the gross less the patient share, so the two always add back to the gross exactly.

Documenting the choice is the act that closes the negotiation, and it belongs to the `CaseManager` alone — never to a liaison. The body names the `chosen_bed_offer_id`, sets `patient_choice_attested`, and records a `choice_basis` of `PATIENT_PREFERENCE`, `FAMILY_PREFERENCE`, `CLINICAL_MATCH`, or `ONLY_AVAILABLE`. Any offer of the current round that is still `OFFERED` may be chosen; the advisory recommendation is not a constraint, and `choice_basis` exists precisely to record why a lower-ranked offer was preferred.

The checks on that request run in a fixed order, and the order is what determines which error a caller sees. The referral must be in `CHOICE_PENDING`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE`. The chosen offer must resolve, must belong to this referral — an offer belonging to a different referral is `ERR_BED_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` — must carry the referral's current `round_index`, and must be `OFFERED`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. The lazy hold sweep runs before that state check, so a hold the logical clock has already reached surfaces as `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` rather than as an invalid state. Then, **before any coverage or cost consideration whatsoever**, the compliance attestation is tested: `patient_choice_attested` must be true in the body, and a false or absent attestation is `ERR_CHOICE_NOT_ATTESTED` (HTTP 422). This ordering is deliberate — an unattested choice must never be masked by a coverage problem discovered later. Only after the attestation passes are the two coverage gates evaluated, and in this order: first, when the episode's payer requires authorization for the chosen level of care — `snf_auth_required` governing `SNF`, `IRF` and `LTACH`, `hh_auth_required` governing `HOME_HEALTH` — there must exist an authorization request on this episode for the chosen offer's provider whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` and whose `expires_at` is strictly later than the logical clock now, or the request is `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); second, that same authorization's `approved_days` must be at least the referral's `projected_stay_days`, or the request is `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). When the payer requires no authorization for that level of care, neither gate applies.

A successful documentation of choice fires a four-hop cascade. The chosen offer moves `OFFERED → ACCEPTED_BY_PATIENT`; its provider's `offers_accepted_count` increments and `acceptance_rate_pct` is recomputed; and the bed-hold interval stays occupied, so `held_bed_units` for that provider is unchanged. Every **other** offer of the same round still in `OFFERED` moves to `DECLINED_BY_PATIENT`; each decline decrements that provider's `held_bed_units` by one and releases its hold interval for reuse, and each declining provider's `acceptance_rate_pct` is recomputed even though neither its numerator nor its denominator moved. The referral moves `CHOICE_PENDING → PLACED`, recording `chosen_bed_offer_id`, `choice_basis` and `patient_choice_attested`, and its `version` advances by one. Then every barrier on the episode with `barrier_code` of `BED_AVAILABILITY` whose `status` is `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` moves to `RESOLVED` with `clock_stop_at` at the logical clock instant — and that resolution in turn recomputes the episode's `avoidable_delay_hours` and `barrier_posture`.

### 3.6. Insurance Authorization: Awards, Appeals, and the Denial Cascade

Coverage for post-acute days is negotiated on its own track. A `CaseManager` submits an authorization request against an episode naming the `provider_id` the days are requested for, the `level_of_care` sought — `SNF`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `IRF`, or `LTACH` — the `requested_days`, which must be at least 1, and the `billed_daily_rate_cents`, the provider's billed per-diem, which must be strictly positive and which is used whenever no in-network contract exists. The `level_of_care` must equal the named provider's `provider_type`, and a disagreement is `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The request's `payer_id` is never supplied by the caller: it is copied at create time from the episode's patient's `primary_payer_id`. A new request begins with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `appeal_round` of 0, and null `approved_days`, `denial_reason`, `decided_at` and `expires_at`. Submitting an authorization request is a planning artifact, so an episode at `SCREENED` advances to `PLANNING`. Requests may be listed and filtered by `episode_id`, `payer_id`, `provider_id`, or `status`.

Only the `PayerRepresentative` whose `X-User-ID` equals the request's `payer_id` may decide it; anyone else in that role receives `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. The decision body carries a `decision` of `PEND`, `APPROVE`, `PARTIAL`, or `DENY`. `approved_days` is required when the decision is `PARTIAL` and must be absent for every other decision; `denial_reason` is required when the decision is `DENY` and must be absent otherwise, and its value is one of `NOT_MEDICALLY_NECESSARY`, `OUT_OF_NETWORK`, or `INCOMPLETE_CLINICALS`. Deciding a request that is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `PENDED` is `ERR_INVALID_AUTHORIZATION_STATE`.

Pending is only available as a first response: `SUBMITTED → PENDED` sets `decided_at` to the logical clock instant and leaves `approved_days` null. Approval is reachable from either `SUBMITTED` or `PENDED`; it sets `approved_days` equal to `requested_days`, sets `decided_at` to the logical clock instant, and sets `expires_at` to that instant plus the payer's `auth_expiry_days` in days. Partial approval is likewise reachable from either `SUBMITTED` or `PENDED` and requires the supplied `approved_days` to be at least 1 and strictly fewer than `requested_days`; it carries exactly the same ledger and barrier consequences as a full approval, using the smaller day count. Denial is reachable from either `SUBMITTED` or `PENDED`, sets `approved_days` to 0, records the `denial_reason` and `decided_at`, and leaves `expires_at` null.

Both approval flavours credit the episode: `authorized_days_total` grows by the `approved_days` just granted. Both also unblock the case — every barrier on the episode with `barrier_code` of `INSURANCE_AUTH` whose `status` is `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` moves to `RESOLVED` with `clock_stop_at` at the logical clock instant, after which the episode's `avoidable_delay_hours` and `barrier_posture` are recomputed.

The award figures are computed on approval and partial approval in a pinned three-step order. Step one establishes the rate: exactly as with the placement projection, the single network contract joining the request's `payer_id` and `provider_id` is consulted, and when it exists with `in_network` true, `rate_variant_applied` is `NETWORK_RATE` and the raw daily rate is the contract's `negotiated_daily_rate_cents`; otherwise `rate_variant_applied` is `BILLED_RATE` and the raw daily rate is `billed_daily_rate_cents` multiplied by 1.25 and rounded half up to whole cents. Then `gross_projection_cents` is that raw daily rate multiplied by `approved_days` — a flat product, with no day tiering at all; the tiering that appears in the placement projection has no counterpart here. Step two reads the volume-discount tier from that **pre-discount** gross and from nothing else: a gross of fifteen thousand dollars or more sets `volume_discount_pct` to 0.05, otherwise a gross of five thousand dollars or more sets it to 0.03, otherwise it is 0.00. The comparisons are inclusive, so a gross of exactly five hundred thousand cents already qualifies for the lower tier. The tier is deliberately **not** re-keyed on the discounted amount, even though the discounted amount is what the payer ultimately owes — that dependency would be circular, and the order pinned here is what makes it well defined. Step three subtracts: `authorized_amount_cents` is `gross_projection_cents` less the gross multiplied by `volume_discount_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents. Before any decision, `gross_projection_cents` reads 0.

A denial is not the end of the conversation. Either a `UtilizationReviewNurse` or a `CaseManager` may file an appeal on a `DENIED` request, supplying an `appeal_note`, which returns it to `SUBMITTED` for a second look. Appealing anything that is not `DENIED` is `ERR_INVALID_AUTHORIZATION_STATE`. `appeal_round` increments by one and must be strictly below two before a new appeal is accepted — so a request may travel the `SUBMITTED → DENIED → SUBMITTED` loop at most twice, and a third attempt is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). Filing an appeal clears `denial_reason`, `decided_at` and `approved_days` back to null and resets the award fields to zero: `gross_projection_cents` to 0, `volume_discount_pct` to 0.00, and `authorized_amount_cents` to 0.

Coverage lapses on its own. Once the logical clock reaches an `APPROVED` or `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` request's `expires_at`, that request moves lazily to the terminal `EXPIRED` status the next time it or its episode is read or written, and the episode's `authorized_days_total` is debited by exactly the `approved_days` it had been credited, so the conserved total returns to its pre-approval value.

A denial, by contrast, cascades outward through four hops, and every hop must fire. The first hop is the decision itself: `approved_days` becomes 0 and the request lands in `DENIED`. The second hop raises a barrier on the episode with `barrier_code` of `INSURANCE_AUTH`, `severity` of `HIGH`, `delay_reason_code` of `AWAITING_AUTH`, and `origin` of `AUTH_DENIAL_AUTO`, with `clock_start_at` at the logical clock instant — except that when such a barrier already exists in `RESOLVED` status, it is reopened instead, incrementing its `reopen_count` and restarting its clock rather than creating a duplicate row. The third hop recomputes `barrier_posture`, which becomes `ACTIVE`, or `ESCALATED` should the new barrier be critical or should another open barrier have aged past seventy-two hours; and when the episode's `case_status` is `READY_FOR_DISCHARGE`, the episode is reverted to `PLANNING`. Because this revert is system-driven rather than operator-driven, it creates **no** additional barrier — the insurance barrier from hop two is the reason, and duplicating it would double-count the delay. The fourth hop follows from that revert: every logistics order on the episode with `order_type` of `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` and `status` of `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` of `READINESS_REVERTED`, each cancellation releasing that order's occupied vehicle interval and debiting `logistics_committed_cents` by that order's `cost_cents`.

### 3.7. Equipment and Transport Logistics

The last physical dependency before a patient can leave is logistics: a wheelchair van or an ambulance to carry them, and a hospital bed or an oxygen concentrator waiting at the other end. Both are modelled as one polymorphic order created by a `CaseManager` against an episode, and the `order_type` — `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` or `DME_DELIVERY` — selects which half of the body is required and which half is forbidden.

Every order carries `expected_episode_version` as described in §2.5 and a service window bounded by `window_start` and `window_end`. Unlike a bed hold, a service window is **closed**: both endpoints are inclusive. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or the request is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` order additionally requires `vehicle_id` and `mileage_miles` — whole miles, zero or more — and must not carry `supplier_provider_id` or `item_code`. A `DME_DELIVERY` order additionally requires `supplier_provider_id` and `item_code` and must not carry `vehicle_id` or `mileage_miles`. Supplying a field the order type forbids, or omitting one it requires, is `ERR_ORDER_SHAPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The `item_code` is one of `HOSPITAL_BED`, `OXYGEN_CONCENTRATOR`, `WHEELCHAIR`, `WALKER`, or `CPAP`. A `DME_DELIVERY` order's supplier must be a provider whose `provider_type` is `DME_SUPPLIER`, and anything else is `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

`destination_provider_id` is optional on either type and names where the patient or the equipment is headed. When the episode already has a referral in `PLACED` status and a supplied `destination_provider_id` differs from the provider of that referral's chosen offer, the request is refused with `ERR_TRANSPORT_DESTINATION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — the hospital may not book transport to a facility the patient did not choose.

The order's `cost_cents` is computed once, at create time, and never re-derived, so later edits to a vehicle's rates cannot disturb an already-committed ledger. For a `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` order the cost is the vehicle's `base_rate_cents` plus its `per_mile_cents` multiplied by the order's `mileage_miles`, in exact integer cents with no rounding. For a `DME_DELIVERY` order the cost is a flat published rate keyed on `item_code`: a hospital bed costs 45000 cents, an oxygen concentrator 32000 cents, a wheelchair 21000 cents, a walker 8000 cents, and a CPAP unit 27000 cents.

An order is created in `REQUESTED` status with `cancel_reason` null, and creating one is a planning artifact, so an episode at `SCREENED` advances to `PLANNING`; the episode's `version` advances by one. Creating a `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` order is also destructive by design: it supersedes any prior `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` order on the same episode whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED`, moving that prior order to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` of `SUPERSEDED`, releasing its vehicle interval, and — when that prior order had been `CONFIRMED` — debiting `logistics_committed_cents` by its `cost_cents`. This is exactly why the create is version-guarded. A `DME_DELIVERY` create supersedes nothing. Orders may be listed and filtered by `episode_id`, `vehicle_id`, `supplier_provider_id`, `destination_provider_id`, or `status`.

Confirmation belongs to the `DischargeCoordinator` and is where the cost is committed and the vehicle actually reserved. The order must be `REQUESTED`, or the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_LOGISTICS_STATE`, and the episode's `case_status` must be `PLANNING` or `READY_FOR_DISCHARGE`. On the transport branch, the vehicle's `status` must be `ACTIVE` — an `OUT_OF_SERVICE` vehicle yields `ERR_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` — and the vehicle must be free. Vehicle exclusivity is enforced with a symmetric turnaround buffer of forty-five minutes on each side: the order's window is expanded to run from three quarters of an hour before `window_start` to three quarters of an hour after `window_end`, and that expanded window must not intersect the identically expanded window of any other `CONFIRMED` `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` order on the same vehicle. Because both ends of these windows are inclusive, sharing even a single instant is a conflict, and the refusal is `ERR_VEHICLE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). On the delivery branch, the supplier's `status` must be `ACTIVE` and its `provider_type` must be `DME_SUPPLIER`, and when the episode has a referral in `PLACED` status the delivery's `window_end` must be at or before that referral's `needed_by`; a delivery landing after the bed is needed is `ERR_DELIVERY_AFTER_NEEDED_BY` (HTTP 422).

A successful confirmation moves the order `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`, adds its `cost_cents` to the episode's `logistics_committed_cents`, advances the episode's `version` by one, and — on the transport branch only — marks the buffered vehicle interval occupied.

Cancellation is available to both the `DischargeCoordinator` and the `CaseManager`, and the body names a `reason_code`. Only `USER_REQUEST`, `CLINICAL_CHANGE`, and `VEHICLE_UNAVAILABLE` may be supplied by a caller; `SUPERSEDED` and `READINESS_REVERTED` are reserved for the system-driven cancellations described elsewhere in §3, and the value is recorded on the order as `cancel_reason`. Cancelling a `REQUESTED` order simply records the reason and leaves `logistics_committed_cents` untouched, because an unconfirmed order was never committed. Cancelling a `CONFIRMED` order is a compensating action: it subtracts that order's `cost_cents` from `logistics_committed_cents`, restoring the conserved total exactly, and on the transport branch releases the buffered vehicle interval so that it is immediately reusable by another order. Cancelling from any other status is `ERR_INVALID_LOGISTICS_STATE`.

The final exit is completion. When the episode is discharged, every order still `CONFIRMED` moves to `COMPLETED`; `logistics_committed_cents` is left unchanged, because a completed order remains inside the committed set, and on the transport branch the vehicle interval remains occupied for its historical window rather than being released.

### 3.8. Readmission Interventions, Discharge Readiness, Reverts, and Discharge

Readmission-risk interventions are the bridge between a screening score and a safe discharge, and how many of them are mandatory scales with the band. A `CaseManager` records them by supplying any subset of four booleans: `pcp_followup_booked`, `med_reconciliation_completed`, `tcm_call_scheduled` for transitional-care-management outreach, and `home_monitoring_enrolled`. A flag omitted from the body keeps whatever value it already had; supplying `false` explicitly clears a flag that was previously set. Recording interventions recomputes `readmission_bundle_complete`, advances the episode's `version` by one, and — being a planning artifact — moves an episode sitting at `SCREENED` to `PLANNING`.

The required set is keyed on `current_risk_band` at the moment of evaluation. A `LOW` band requires nothing at all, and because the required set is empty it is vacuously satisfied, so `readmission_bundle_complete` reads true. A `MODERATE` band requires `pcp_followup_booked`. A `HIGH` band requires `pcp_followup_booked` and `med_reconciliation_completed`. A `VERY_HIGH` band requires all four: `pcp_followup_booked`, `med_reconciliation_completed`, `tcm_call_scheduled`, and `home_monitoring_enrolled`. `readmission_bundle_complete` is true exactly when every flag in the required set is true, and it is re-derived on every read or write of the episode.

Declaring an episode ready is the `CaseManager`'s final act on the case, and the body carries the `discharge_disposition` — one of `HOME_SELF_CARE`, `HOME_WITH_HH`, `SNF`, `IRF`, `LTACH`, `HOSPICE`, or `AMA`. Until this moment `discharge_disposition` is null. Five preconditions guard the `PLANNING → READY_FOR_DISCHARGE` transition, and they are evaluated in this order, which matters because several of them can be false at once. First, the episode's `case_status` must be `PLANNING`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`. Second, `barrier_posture` must not be `ESCALATED`, or the refusal is `ERR_ESCALATED_POSTURE` — so a critical or long-aged barrier surfaces as an escalation rather than being masked by the generic unresolved-barrier check that follows. Third, no barrier on the episode may be in `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` status at all, failing which the refusal is `ERR_BARRIER_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 422). Fourth, `readmission_bundle_complete` must be true, evaluated against the `current_risk_band` **in effect before** this transition's predischarge rescore, or the refusal is `ERR_READMISSION_BUNDLE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Fifth, when the chosen disposition is `SNF`, `IRF`, `LTACH`, or `HOME_WITH_HH`, the episode must have at least one referral in `PLACED` status, or the refusal is `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 422); the three remaining dispositions carry no placement requirement.

The ordering of that fourth check deserves emphasis, because the transition itself changes the band. Bundle completeness is judged on the **pre-transition** band, and the predischarge rescore happens strictly after every precondition has passed. Once they have, the episode records `discharge_disposition` from the body, sets `marked_ready_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` and `marked_ready_at` to the logical clock instant, and then rescores: `current_risk_score` and `current_risk_band` are recomputed with the predischarge variant of the risk model described in §3.2, drawing on the episode's most recent `SUBMITTED` screening, and `risk_variant_applied` becomes `PREDISCHARGE_VARIANT`. `readmission_bundle_complete` is then re-derived against the **new** band, and it may legitimately turn false without blocking anything, because the gate it guards has already been passed. Finally the episode's throughput figures are recomputed and its `version` advances by one.

Readiness is reversible, and a single capability serves both the reversal and the outright cancellation of a case; a `CaseManager` or a `UtilizationReviewNurse` invokes it with a `reason_code`. Four reason codes drive a `READY_FOR_DISCHARGE → PLANNING` revert, each mapping to an auto-raised barrier: `CLINICAL_DETERIORATION` maps to a barrier with `barrier_code` of `PHYSICIAN_ORDER` and `delay_reason_code` of `CLINICAL_INSTABILITY`; `AUTH_LOST` maps to `INSURANCE_AUTH` with `AWAITING_AUTH`; `TRANSPORT_FAILED` maps to `TRANSPORT` with `AWAITING_TRANSPORT`; and `FAMILY_WITHDREW` maps to `FAMILY_DECISION` with `AWAITING_FAMILY`. In every case the auto-raised barrier carries `severity` of `HIGH`, `origin` of `REVERT_AUTO`, and `clock_start_at` at the logical clock instant. The revert clears `marked_ready_by_user_id` and `marked_ready_at` back to null but **retains** `discharge_disposition`. It then cancels every logistics order on the episode with `order_type` of `PATIENT_TRANSPORT` and `status` of `CONFIRMED`, recording `cancel_reason` of `READINESS_REVERTED`, releasing each vehicle interval, and debiting `logistics_committed_cents` by each cancelled order's `cost_cents`. `barrier_posture` is recomputed and the episode's `version` advances. This loop is unbounded: `PLANNING → READY_FOR_DISCHARGE → PLANNING` may repeat as often as the case demands. A revert cascaded by the system — from an authorization denial, or from a barrier reopen — performs the same clearing and transport cancellation but raises no extra barrier, because in both those cases the barrier that forced the revert already exists.

The fifth reason code, `PATIENT_LEFT_AMA`, does something else entirely: it cancels the episode outright, and it is the only cancellation path in the system. It may be invoked while the episode is `ADMITTED`, `SCREENED`, `PLANNING`, or `READY_FOR_DISCHARGE`, and it drives `case_status` to the terminal `CANCELLED`, sets `discharge_disposition` to `AMA`, and sets `discharged_at` to the logical clock instant. No auto-barrier is raised on this path. Every barrier on the episode in `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` status moves to `WAIVED` with `clock_stop_at` at the logical clock instant. When the case had progressed past admission and planning artifacts exist, two further cascades fire: every referral on the episode with `status` of `DRAFT`, `BROADCAST`, or `CHOICE_PENDING` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and each such withdrawal moves that round's `OFFERED` bed offers to `WITHDRAWN`, decrementing `held_bed_units` for every affected provider; and every logistics order with `status` of `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` of `CLINICAL_CHANGE`, debiting `logistics_committed_cents` for those that had been `CONFIRMED`. From `ADMITTED` neither cascade has anything to act on, since no referral and no order can yet exist.

Discharge itself belongs to the `DischargeCoordinator`, takes no body, and is guarded in this order. The episode's `case_status` must be `READY_FOR_DISCHARGE`, or the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`. `barrier_posture` must not be `ESCALATED`, or the refusal is `ERR_ESCALATED_POSTURE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the episode's `marked_ready_by_user_id`, or the refusal is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the four-eyes control that keeps one operator from both declaring and executing a discharge. And no logistics order on the episode may still be sitting in `REQUESTED`, or the refusal is `ERR_LOGISTICS_NOT_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 422): every arrangement must be either confirmed or cancelled before the patient leaves.

A successful discharge moves `case_status` to the terminal `DISCHARGED` and sets `discharged_at` to the logical clock instant. Every `CONFIRMED` logistics order becomes `COMPLETED`, with `logistics_committed_cents` unchanged. Every bed offer on this episode's referrals still in `OFFERED` moves to `EXPIRED`, each expiry decrementing that provider's `held_bed_units` and releasing its bed-hold interval so the bed returns to circulation. Every barrier in `OPEN` or `MITIGATING` status moves to `RESOLVED` with `clock_stop_at` at the logical clock instant — a set that must be empty in practice, since mark-ready demanded it be empty and any barrier raised afterwards forces a revert, but the rule is stated so the outcome is defined. Finally the episode's throughput figures are finalised against `discharged_at`, and its `version` advances by one.

### 3.9. Length-of-Stay and Throughput Reporting

The point of coding every delay is to be able to separate the stay a patient clinically needed from the stay the process imposed. Throughput figures are recomputed as a side effect of the mark-ready declaration and again at discharge, and they are also available on demand as a per-episode report exposing `los_hours`, `avoidable_delay_hours`, `adjusted_los_hours`, `expected_los_hours`, `observed_expected_ratio`, `throughput_grade`, `current_risk_score`, `current_risk_band`, and `barriers_ever_opened_count`. No cross-episode data participates in any of it: one episode and its own barrier rows are the entire input.

The seven steps look mutually dependent — the band feeds the expected stay, barrier history feeds the band, and barriers also feed the delay union — so their order is normative and must be followed exactly as given. **Step one** establishes the risk figures: `current_risk_score` and `current_risk_band` are set from the composite readmission risk model, using the predischarge variant once the episode has ever reached `READY_FOR_DISCHARGE`, and otherwise using the variant of the episode's most recent `SUBMITTED` screening. **Step two** computes `avoidable_delay_hours` by the interval-union procedure of §3.3. **Step three** computes `los_hours`: the elapsed time from `admitted_at` to `discharged_at` when the episode has been discharged, or to the logical clock now when it has not, measured in whole minutes, divided by 60 and rounded half up to one decimal place. **Step four** computes `adjusted_los_hours` as `los_hours` less `avoidable_delay_hours`, rounded half up to one decimal place and floored at 0.0, so an episode whose avoidable delay exceeds its stay reports zero rather than a negative figure. **Step five** computes `expected_los_hours` as `drg_gmlos_days` multiplied by 24 to convert days into hours and then multiplied by an acuity multiplier keyed on the band from step one — 0.90 for `LOW`, 1.00 for `MODERATE`, 1.15 for `HIGH`, and 1.30 for `VERY_HIGH` — with the product rounded half up to one decimal place. **Step six** computes `observed_expected_ratio` as `adjusted_los_hours` divided by `expected_los_hours`, rounded half up to three decimal places.

**Step seven** grades the result. A ratio of 0.900 or below is `EXEMPLARY`. Otherwise a ratio of 1.050 or below is `ON_TARGET`. Otherwise a ratio of 1.250 or below is `ABOVE_TARGET`. Anything higher is an `OUTLIER`. Each comparison is inclusive at its boundary, so a ratio of exactly 1.050 grades as `ON_TARGET` rather than `ABOVE_TARGET`. Note what the grade measures: because `adjusted_los_hours` has already had the avoidable delay union subtracted from it, an episode that sat idle for three days awaiting an authorization is not punished in its grade for those days — the delay shows up instead in `avoidable_delay_hours`, where the case-management organisation, rather than the clinical team, owns it.
