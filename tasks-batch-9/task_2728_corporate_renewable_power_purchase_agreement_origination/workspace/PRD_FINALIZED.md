# Corporate Renewable PPA Origination API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Clean energy project developers no longer sell their output to a single regulated utility. They sell it to Fortune 500 buyers who arrive with decarbonisation pledges, thin balance sheets, treasury departments that insist on virtual settlement, and sustainability teams who need a registry serial number before they can put a renewable claim in an annual report. Originating one of these corporate Power Purchase Agreements means running ten coupled disciplines at once — buyer qualification, product structuring, price construction, credit underwriting, collateral management, milestone conditions precedent, settlement arithmetic, environmental attribute conveyance, risk allocation and termination — and every one of them reads and writes state that the others depend on. A rating downgrade on a parent guarantor changes how much collateral a subsidiary's deal must post; an accepted change-in-law claim reprices the contract and can push an already-approved deal back into shortfall; a curtailed hour becomes money only if a committee has accepted a curtailment claim and allocated it to the buyer.

This document specifies the API that runs that origination desk. The system is the single book of record for every buyer, project, agreement, negotiation round, collateral instrument, condition precedent, settlement period, metered hour, attribute batch and risk claim on the developer's platform. It is deliberately opinionated: money is never estimated, every threshold is inclusive or exclusive by design, every deadline is measured against a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, and every derived figure is recomputed from its inputs rather than incremented in place. Where two reasonable conventions exist — closed versus half-open intervals, ceiling versus half-up rounding, floor versus round for whole megawatt-hours — the specification picks one and states it.

The API is mounted under `/ppa`, with a single test-driving clock endpoint outside that prefix. Readers should treat sections 2 and 3 as the specification and sections 4 and 5 as an index back into it: the endpoint table gives the HTTP contract and the authorized roles, but the request payloads, side effects and rejection conditions live in the narrative.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that operator acts. If either header is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before anything else happens — before the path is resolved, before the body is parsed, and before any resource is looked up, so a request for a non-existent identifier that also omits its headers returns 401 rather than 404. Once the headers are present, the role named in `X-User-Role` must appear among the roles the target endpoint authorizes; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and this refusal precedes request-shape parsing, so a wrong-role request carrying a malformed body still returns 403 rather than 400.

Six roles exist. An **`Originator`** is the developer-side origination lead: it builds pricing nodes, buyers, projects and deals, tables developer term sheets, proposes aggregated-buyer allocations, registers credit-support instruments, submits credit packages, advances project milestones, resolves conditions precedent and claims risk events on the seller's behalf. A **`CreditOfficer`** underwrites: it owns every buyer qualification decision, every rating change, every deal credit decision and the entire posting and release lifecycle of collateral. A **`DealCommittee`** operator is the signing authority: it executes and terminates agreements, waives conditions precedent and rules on risk-event claims. A **`SettlementAnalyst`** runs the settlement ledger: opening periods, posting metered hours, calculating the netting, invoicing and settling. A **`RegistryAgent`** is the environmental attribute registry operator, and the only role that can mint, transfer, cancel a transfer of, or retire an attribute batch. An **`OfftakerRep`** is the corporate buyer acting for itself: answering term sheets, confirming or withdrawing its own capacity share, disputing an invoice and filing its own risk claims.

The five developer-side and registry-side roles — `Originator`, `CreditOfficer`, `DealCommittee`, `SettlementAnalyst` and `RegistryAgent` — carry an **opaque** identity. For each of them, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail (as `created_by_user_id` on `Offtaker`, `Project` and `Deal`, as `decision_by_user_id` on `Offtaker`, as `credit_approved_by_user_id` on `Deal`, as `resolved_by_user_id` on `ConditionPrecedent`, as `responded_by_user_id` on `TermSheetRound`, as `posted_by_user_id` on `MeterInterval` and as `reviewed_by_user_id` on `RiskEvent`) and it compares that recorded value against later actors for segregation-of-duties purposes, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

`OfftakerRep` is different, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `OfftakerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Offtaker`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Offtaker` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `OfftakerRep` acting on a deal must be either that deal's `anchor_offtaker_id` or the `offtaker_id` of one of that deal's `CONFIRMED` allocations; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_DEAL_COUNTERPARTY`. That check is applied after existence resolution — an unknown deal still returns its 404 — but before any state precondition, so a non-counterparty acting on a wrongly-stated deal receives 403 rather than 409.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unless a list endpoint's own narrative states a different order, results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly one on every mutation of that row. `Offtaker`, `Project`, `Deal`, `DealAllocation`, `TermSheetRound`, `CreditSupportInstrument`, `ConditionPrecedent`, `SettlementPeriod`, `MeterInterval`, `EnvironmentalAttributeBatch` and `RiskEvent` all carry the full set. `PricingNode` is the single exception: a settlement point is immutable once registered, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely, and there is no operation that amends one.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock** whose startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and an administrative test-driving capability sets that clock to an arbitrary UTC instant supplied as `current_utc`. Before any such call the clock reads the startup default and must never fall back to the host's real time. Every deadline, expiry, tenor, window and "has it lapsed yet" comparison in this specification is evaluated against the logical clock and nothing else. Day arithmetic is exact: adding N days means adding N times 86400 seconds to a whole-second UTC datetime, and boundary comparisons are inclusive unless the text says otherwise.

### 2.5. The Order in Which a Request Fails

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The system evaluates its checks in a strict ascending priority order and stops at the first violation; no later check is evaluated, and no error is aggregated with another. The order is:

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization for the endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Request shape: every required field present, every enum value a declared member, every numeric inside its declared range, every datetime parseable as ISO-8601 UTC, and pagination bounds respected — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The numeric ranges enforced here are that `capacity_mw` is greater than zero, `contracted_mw` is greater than zero, `term_years` is between 1 and 25, `escalation_pct_per_year` is between 0 and 10, `base_fixed_price_usd_per_mwh` is greater than zero, `face_amount_usd` is greater than zero, `quantity_mwh` is greater than zero, `metered_mwh` is at least zero, `net_debt_to_ebitda` is at least zero and `reported_ebitda_usd` is greater than zero. Any field whose value the server derives — a computed or system field — is silently ignored when a client supplies it, never rejected.
4. Path existence: every identifier appearing in the request path must resolve to a live row, and the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right — the outer segment first. The error is the specific per-entity not-found code: `ERR_PRICING_NODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFTAKER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDIT_SUPPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONDITION_PRECEDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RISK_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Body foreign-key existence: `project_id`, `anchor_offtaker_id`, `settlement_point_id`, `offtaker_id`, `guarantor_offtaker_id`, `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id` and `transferred_to_offtaker_id` must each resolve to a live row, again returning the referenced entity's own not-found code. Body foreign keys are validated in the order in which the endpoint declares its input fields, and always after path existence — so a request with both a bad path identifier and a bad body foreign key returns the path entity's 404. This applies to buyer creation, project creation, deal creation, allocation proposal, credit-support registration and attribute transfer.
6. Guarantor-graph well-formedness — `ERR_GUARANTOR_CYCLE`, on buyer creation and on a rating change.
7. Buyer-side counterparty ownership for an `OfftakerRep` — `ERR_NOT_DEAL_COUNTERPARTY`.
8. The state gate — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. This check owns one question and only one: does the subject entity's own current state, on the dimension being moved, have a declared transition for the requested action? Every other precondition — another entity's state, a money comparison, a deadline, a collision — is owned by its own rule further down this list and returns that rule's error. Because the state gate sits at priority 8, executing a `DRAFT` deal whose anchor buyer is also still `UNDER_REVIEW` returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409), not `ERR_OFFTAKER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (422).
9. Optimistic concurrency: the supplied expected parent version equals the parent's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Because this runs after the state gate, a stale version supplied against an already-`TERMINATED` deal yields 409 rather than 412.
10. Segregation of duties on `X-User-ID` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
11. Buyer qualification standing — `ERR_OFFTAKER_NOT_QUALIFIED`.
12. Buyer credit tier not `DECLINED` — `ERR_CREDIT_TIER_DECLINED`.
13. Attribute recipient holds a `CONFIRMED` allocation — `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_ALLOCATION`.
14. No undecided risk claim over the period being invoiced — `ERR_OPEN_RISK_CLAIM`.
15. Attribute recipient holds a registry account — `ERR_REGISTRY_ACCOUNT_MISSING`.
16. Retirement claim year inside the vintage window — `ERR_CLAIM_YEAR_OUT_OF_WINDOW`.
17. Window and date well-formedness — `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Well-formedness is checked before any collision check, so a backwards window that would also overlap an existing claim returns `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.
18. Condition-precedent deadlines — `ERR_CP_DEADLINE_PASSED`.
19. Term-sheet round response deadline — `ERR_ROUND_EXPIRED`.
20. Instrument issuer or guarantor eligibility — `ERR_INELIGIBLE_ISSUER`. Eligibility is decided before tenor, so a `BB`-rated letter of credit with only thirty days left returns `ERR_INELIGIBLE_ISSUER` only when the rating is the limb that fails; an `AAA` letter of credit with thirty days left returns `ERR_INSTRUMENT_TENOR_TOO_SHORT` instead.
21. Instrument remaining tenor — `ERR_INSTRUMENT_TENOR_TOO_SHORT`.
22. Risk-claim window collision — `ERR_RISK_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
23. Settlement-period window collision — `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
24. Metered hour inside the period and on the hour — `ERR_HOUR_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`.
25. Metered hour not already loaded — `ERR_DUPLICATE_METER_HOUR`. Because this is checked after the in-window test, a duplicate hour that is also out of range returns `ERR_HOUR_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`.
26. No live duplicate allocation for the same buyer — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ALLOCATION`.
27. Settlement point name uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRICING_NODE`.
28. Change-in-law price adjustment validity — `ERR_PRICE_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID`.
29. Project capacity headroom at deal creation — `ERR_PROJECT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
30. Allocation confirmation within contracted capacity — `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_CONTRACTED_MW`.
31. Full allocation at execution — `ERR_ALLOCATION_INCOMPLETE`.
32. Posted collateral covers the requirement at execution — `ERR_CREDIT_SUPPORT_SHORTFALL`.
33. Release does not breach coverage — `ERR_RELEASE_BREACHES_COVERAGE`.
34. Attribute quantity within the period's remaining mintable volume — `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_MINTABLE`.
35. Termination right vested — `ERR_TERMINATION_RIGHT_NOT_VESTED`. This sits last because both the state gate at position 8 and the segregation-of-duties gate at position 10 must beat it, and no fiscal rule applies to termination at all.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Sign Conventions

Rounding is never left to the implementer's judgement. Unless a specific calculation says otherwise, every monetary amount is carried to two decimal places and rounded half up. Scores — `credit_score`, `sustainability_score` and `cp_completion_pct` — are two decimal places rounded half up on a nought-to-one-hundred scale. Megawatt quantities — `contracted_mw`, `allocated_mw`, `confirmed_allocated_mw`, `unallocated_mw`, `contracted_mw` and `available_mw` on a project, and every megawatt-hour total on a settlement period or metered hour — are carried to three or four decimal places as their own narrative states, rounded half up. `allocation_share_pct` is carried to four decimal places. Both `weighted_avg_index_usd_per_mwh` and the intermediate escalated base price are carried to four decimal places, rounded half up.

Three places in the system deliberately do **not** round half up, and getting them wrong changes the answer. The collateral requirement rounds the residual exposure **up** to the next whole collateral increment — a ceiling, never a half-up round. The mintable attribute count **truncates** a megawatt-hour total to a whole number — a floor, never a half-up round. And an attribute batch is split across buyers by taking each buyer's floor and then handing out the leftover units one at a time, never by rounding each share independently.

Sign conventions matter in settlement. A positive `net_settlement_usd` means the index exceeded the strike and the seller owes the buyer that amount; a negative one means the buyer owes the seller. The two payable fields are therefore always non-negative: when the net is at or above zero, `seller_pays_usd` carries the net and `buyer_pays_usd` is 0.00; when the net is below zero, `seller_pays_usd` is 0.00 and `buyer_pays_usd` carries the magnitude of the net. A net of exactly 0.00 leaves both at 0.00 and nobody pays. `energy_settlement_usd`, `basis_adjustment_usd` and `net_settlement_usd` may each be negative; `seller_pays_usd` and `buyer_pays_usd` may not.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Aggregates

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent aggregate's `version`, because in each case a client that has not observed the parent's latest state would compute the wrong arithmetic while passing every state precondition. Each of the three requires a version token as a **non-optional** input field, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Proposing an aggregated-buyer allocation against a deal requires `expected_deal_version` in the payload, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Deal`. If any writer has advanced the deal's `version` since the client read it, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Aggregated arrangements let many allocations coexist on one deal, so nothing about the deal's state blocks a second proposal — what blocks it is that the proposer's view of `unallocated_mw` is stale, and honouring the proposal could oversubscribe the contracted capacity.

Posting a registered credit-support instrument likewise requires `expected_deal_version` matching the parent deal's current `version`. Instruments legitimately stack, so a later post is not blocked by any state precondition; it is blocked because the poster's view of `posted_credit_support_usd` and `credit_support_required_usd` is stale, and the coverage arithmetic that decides whether the deal's credit standing cures from `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` back to `APPROVED` would otherwise be evaluated against a superseded requirement. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Posting a metered hour into a settlement period requires `expected_settlement_period_version` matching that `SettlementPeriod`'s current `version`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Hourly intervals accumulate by design, so no state precondition stands in the way; the danger is that a poster whose view of `interval_count` and the period's computed totals is stale is precisely the poster who would re-load an hour another writer already posted.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Where a payload does not name `expected_deal_version` or `expected_settlement_period_version` in its narrative, no concurrency token is expected and none is required.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Holding the right role is necessary but never sufficient. Four named checks compare `X-User-ID` against an identifier the system recorded at an earlier step, and any of them failing returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. First, the operator recorded as an `Offtaker`'s `created_by_user_id` may not render that same buyer's qualification decision. Second, the operator recorded as a `Deal`'s `created_by_user_id` may not render that deal's credit decision. Third, the operator who failed a condition precedent — recorded as that condition's `resolved_by_user_id` — may not be the operator who terminates the deal citing `CP_FAILURE`. Fourth, the operator recorded as a deal's `credit_approved_by_user_id` may not execute that deal. The last check exists because the same natural person may legitimately hold both the `CreditOfficer` and the `DealCommittee` role; the header pair is what makes that visible to the server. These four checks guard buyer qualification decisions, deal credit decisions, deal termination and deal execution.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Nested Path Resolution

Several operations in this system are not single-row writes. A project milestone advance, a deal execution, a rating change, a termination and a risk-event acceptance each propagate through two, three, four or five hops of dependent state, and every hop is part of the same request: there is no deferred queue and no eventual consistency. A caller who reads the affected resources immediately after such a request observes every hop already applied. Whenever a hop mutates a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one — including rows the caller never named, such as the sibling deals of a downgraded buyer or the guarantor-supported children of a downgraded parent. The response body of a cascading request is the primary subject of the request; the dependent rows are discovered through the relevant list operations.

Nested resources are addressed through hierarchical paths, and path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. A request naming a settlement period inside a deal resolves the deal first; if the deal does not exist the answer is `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, even if the inner identifier is also bogus. When an inner resource exists but belongs to a different parent than the path claims, it is treated as not found under that path.

### 2.10. The Day-Count Calendar

Every deadline in the lifecycle is a fixed number of days added to the logical clock at the moment of the triggering transition, and each one has a home field. A successor negotiation round created by a buyer counter gets a `response_deadline` fourteen days out. A deal that falls into credit support shortfall gets a `support_cure_deadline` ten days out. A condition precedent that fails gets a `cure_deadline` fifteen days out, and when it is re-cured its `deadline_date` is pushed to fifteen days from the moment of the re-cure. Waiving a condition precedent extends the deal's `commercial_operation_deadline` by thirty days beyond its prior value. A force-majeure termination right vests only once an accepted force-majeure claim spans at least one hundred and eighty whole days. A retirement claim year must fall in a two-year window: not earlier than the batch's vintage year and not later than one year after it. A posted letter of credit or parent guarantee must have at least three hundred and sixty-five whole days of remaining tenor measured from the logical clock to its `expiry_date`, where whole days are the truncated count of 86400-second periods; exactly three hundred and sixty-five days passes and three hundred and sixty-four fails. All of these comparisons are inclusive at the boundary.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Settlement Points, Generation Projects and Development Milestones

Nothing can be priced until the desk knows where it settles. A settlement point is registered by an `Originator` or a `SettlementAnalyst` with three attributes: a `name` between 1 and 64 characters, the `iso` that publishes it — one of `CAISO`, `ERCOT`, `PJM` or `MISO` — and a `settlement_point_type` that is either `HUB` or `NODE`. Because these names are identifiers published by the independent system operator and must never be re-used, a second registration under an existing `name` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRICING_NODE`. Settlement points are immutable: they carry no `version` and there is no operation that amends one. Operators browse them with optional `iso` and `settlement_point_type` filters, or fetch one directly.

Generation projects are created by an `Originator` and always begin in `PRE_DEVELOPMENT`. The payload carries the project `name` (1 to 128 characters), the `technology` — `SOLAR`, `WIND` or `STORAGE_HYBRID` — the nameplate AC `capacity_mw` available for contracting, the `node_id` of the physical injection point where generation is metered, a `cod_target_date` for commercial operation, the `p50_annual_mwh` annual energy estimate, and optionally the `interconnection_queue_position`, which is at least 1. On creation, `contracted_mw` is 0 and `available_mw` equals `capacity_mw`; `last_milestone_at` is null and `created_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting operator's identifier. Thereafter `contracted_mw` is always the sum of `contracted_mw` over the project's deals whose `status` is `EXECUTED` or `EFFECTIVE`, and `available_mw` is `capacity_mw` less `contracted_mw`. Projects are listed with optional `node_id`, `status` and `technology` filters — the `node_id` filter is how an operator walks backwards from a settlement point to the projects injecting there — and fetched individually with that derived capacity ledger attached.

Development progress is recorded one milestone at a time. An `Originator` advances a project by naming a `milestone` and supplying an `evidence_ref` document reference. The milestone must be the single legal successor of the project's current `status`: `PRE_DEVELOPMENT` becomes `PERMITTED`, `PERMITTED` becomes `INTERCONNECTION_APPROVED`, `INTERCONNECTION_APPROVED` becomes `NTP_ISSUED` when the milestone `NOTICE_TO_PROCEED` is named, and `NTP_ISSUED` becomes `OPERATIONAL`, which is terminal. Requesting any other jump is refused by the state gate with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Each advance sets `last_milestone_at` to the logical clock and increments the project's `version` by one.

The first three advances are also the head of a five-hop cascade, and the hops matter more than the milestone itself. Hop one is the project's own move and the `last_milestone_at` stamp. Hop two, caused by hop one, reaches into every condition precedent whose `cp_type` matches the milestone just reached — `PERMITTING` for the `PERMITTED` advance, `INTERCONNECTION` for the `INTERCONNECTION_APPROVED` advance, `NOTICE_TO_PROCEED` for the notice-to-proceed advance — and, for every such condition that is still `PENDING` on a deal of this project whose own `status` is `EXECUTED`, moves it `PENDING → SATISFIED`, copying the request's `evidence_ref` onto the condition and stamping its `resolved_at` with the logical clock. Hop three, caused by hop two, recomputes each affected deal's `cp_completion_pct`; any deal whose recomputed figure reaches exactly 100.00 moves `EXECUTED → EFFECTIVE` with `effective_at` stamped at the logical clock. Hop four, caused by hop three, takes every newly effective deal and moves each of its credit-support instruments whose `purpose` is `DEVELOPMENT_SECURITY` and whose `status` is `POSTED` from `POSTED → RELEASED`, stamping `closed_at`. Hop five, caused by hop four, recomputes `posted_credit_support_usd` and `released_credit_support_usd` on those deals. The final advance to `OPERATIONAL` carries no condition-precedent cascade; it recomputes `contracted_mw` and `available_mw` on the project and nothing more.

### 3.2. Onboarding, Scoring and Qualifying the Corporate Buyer

A corporate buyer enters the system as a `DRAFT` record created by an `Originator`. The payload states the buyer's `legal_name` (1 to 128 characters), its senior unsecured issuer `credit_rating` on the agency scale — `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BBB`, `BB`, `B`, `CCC` or `NR` — the `rating_agency` that published it (`SP`, `MOODYS`, `FITCH` or `NONE`), the most recent fiscal year's `reported_ebitda_usd`, and `net_debt_to_ebitda` carried to at most two decimal places and never below zero. Sustainability posture arrives in the same payload: the `disclosure_framework` the buyer reports under, which is `CDP_AND_TCFD`, `TCFD_ONLY`, `CDP_ONLY` or `NONE`; the optional booleans `has_science_based_target` and `re100_member`, each defaulting to false; the optional `scope2_target_year`, which is either null or a year from 2026 through 2060 and defaults to null; the optional `registry_account_id` naming the buyer's attribute registry account, defaulting to null; and the optional `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id` pointing at another buyer record whose balance sheet may credit-support this one, also defaulting to null. On creation `qualification_status` is `DRAFT`, `conditions` is the empty array, `claimed_attributes_mwh` is 0, `watchlist_since` is null, `decision_by_user_id` is null, and `credit_score`, `credit_score_variant`, `credit_tier`, `sustainability_score` and `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` are all null — no scoring runs at creation. The `created_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting operator and is what the qualification-decision segregation check later compares against.

Guarantor links must form a forest, never a graph with a cycle. A supplied `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id` may not be the buyer's own identifier, and walking the existing guarantor chain upward from the proposed parent must terminate at a null guarantor without ever revisiting a node; a violation is `ERR_GUARANTOR_CYCLE`. Since a freshly created record can only ever be attached as a leaf, the cycle case is in practice reachable only by re-parenting an existing record during a rating change. Buyers are listed with optional `qualification_status`, `credit_tier` and `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id` filters — that last one is how an operator finds every subsidiary hanging off a given parent — and fetched individually with all computed credit and sustainability figures attached.

Submitting a buyer for review is an `Originator` action that runs the entire scoring chain and moves the buyer `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`. The submission carries no payload. From `DRAFT` it additionally requires that the buyer either names a real rating agency — that is, `rating_agency` is not `NONE` — or carries the `credit_rating` value `NR`; a record claiming a real rating with no agency behind it cannot be underwritten. The same action is also the revision loop: a buyer sitting at `CONDITIONAL` after a conditional decision re-submits along `CONDITIONAL → UNDER_REVIEW` once it has remediated its conditions, and on that path the `conditions` array is cleared to empty **before** the scores are recomputed. Both paths recompute `credit_score`, `credit_score_variant`, `credit_tier`, `sustainability_score` and `unsecured_credit_limit_usd`, and both increment the buyer's `version` by one.

The creditworthiness score is a weighted blend of three components on a nought-to-one-hundred scale. The **rating component** is a direct lookup on the buyer's own `credit_rating`: `AAA` is worth 100 points, `AA` 92, `A` 84, `BBB` 72, `BB` 55, `B` 38, `CCC` 20 and `NR` 10. The **leverage component** starts at 100 points and subtracts ten points for every full turn of `net_debt_to_ebitda`; the result is then clamped so it can be no lower than 0 and no higher than 100. So a buyer at 3.5 turns scores 65 leverage points, and a buyer at 12 turns scores 0 rather than a negative figure. The **disclosure component** is a lookup on `disclosure_framework`: `CDP_AND_TCFD` is worth 100 points, `TCFD_ONLY` 80, `CDP_ONLY` 70 and `NONE` 30. The three effective component values are then combined by weight — the rating component at 0.55, the leverage component at 0.30 and the disclosure component at 0.15 — and the weighted sum is rounded half up to two decimal places to give `credit_score`. Component values themselves are carried into the weighted sum unrounded; only the final sum is rounded.

Which components get used depends on the guarantor, and this is where `credit_score_variant` comes in. The variant is `STANDALONE` whenever `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id` is null, **or** whenever the linked guarantor's own `qualification_status` is anything other than `QUALIFIED` — mere linkage is not enough, the parent must itself have cleared qualification. In the `STANDALONE` variant the effective rating component is the buyer's own rating points and the effective leverage component is the buyer's own leverage points. The variant is `GUARANTOR_SUPPORTED` when a guarantor is linked **and** that guarantor's `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED`. In that variant the guarantor's own rating points and leverage points are computed by the same two lookups, and then the effective rating component becomes the greater of the buyer's own rating points and the guarantor's rating points reduced by 8 notch-points, while the effective leverage component becomes the greater of the buyer's own leverage points and the guarantor's leverage points. The disclosure component is **never** inherited: it is the buyer's own in both variants, because it is the buyer's own reporting that substantiates a renewable claim.

The commercial tier follows immediately from the score, on the same request. A `credit_score` of at least 80.00 is `PRIME`; failing that, at least 62.00 is `STANDARD`; failing that, at least 45.00 is `SUBPRIME`; anything below 45.00 is `DECLINED`. Every comparison is inclusive at the lower bound, and it is performed on the two-decimal `credit_score` with no further rounding — a score of exactly 62.00 is `STANDARD` and 61.99 is `SUBPRIME`.

The sustainability score is purely additive and also runs on submission. It starts at zero. A validated science-based target — `has_science_based_target` being true — adds 40 points. Being an RE100 signatory, that is `re100_member` being true, adds 30 points. A `scope2_target_year` that is not null **and** falls no later than 2030 adds 20 points; a target of exactly 2030 does contribute, and a null target contributes nothing and is not an error. Reporting under any framework other than `NONE` adds 10 points. The total is rounded half up to two decimal places into `sustainability_score`, giving a theoretical maximum of 100.00.

The unsecured limit is computed immediately after the tier, and re-computed on every rating change. It is the **lesser** of two ceilings. The first is a hard cap by tier: 25,000,000.00 USD for `PRIME`, 10,000,000.00 for `STANDARD`, 2,000,000.00 for `SUBPRIME` and 0.00 for `DECLINED`. The second is a share of reported earnings: `reported_ebitda_usd` multiplied by 5 percent for `PRIME`, 2 percent for `STANDARD`, one-half of one percent for `SUBPRIME` and zero for `DECLINED`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` is whichever of the two is smaller, and a `DECLINED` buyer therefore lands on 0.00 by both limbs.

A `CreditOfficer` then renders the decision on a buyer under review, supplying a `decision` of `QUALIFY`, `CONDITIONAL` or `REJECT` and, on the conditional path, a `conditions` array of free-text remediation items. The acting operator must not be the one recorded as `created_by_user_id` on that buyer. `QUALIFY` moves the buyer `UNDER_REVIEW → QUALIFIED` and requires both that `credit_tier` is `PRIME` or `STANDARD` and that `sustainability_score` is at least 70.00; it sets `conditions` to the empty array and stamps `decision_by_user_id` with the acting operator. `CONDITIONAL` moves the buyer `UNDER_REVIEW → CONDITIONAL` and requires that `credit_tier` is anything other than `DECLINED`, that `sustainability_score` is at least 40.00, and that the request supplies a non-empty `conditions` array; the supplied array is stored verbatim and `decision_by_user_id` is stamped. `REJECT` moves the buyer `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, which is terminal, stamps `decision_by_user_id`, and cascades: every deal anchored by this buyer whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `TERM_SHEET_ISSUED` or `UNDER_NEGOTIATION` has its `credit_status` set to `DECLINED` and its `version` incremented by one. All three decisions increment the buyer's `version` by one.

### 3.3. Rating Migration, the Watchlist and the Downgrade Cascade

A buyer's credit standing does not stand still, and recording a change is a `CreditOfficer` action available only while `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `WATCHLIST`. The payload always carries a fresh `credit_rating`, and may optionally carry a new `rating_agency`, `net_debt_to_ebitda`, `reported_ebitda_usd`, `disclosure_framework` or `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id`. A supplied `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id` re-parents the guarantor edge **before** any scoring runs, and must both exist and satisfy the forest rule — neither equal to this buyer's own identifier nor closing a cycle when the upward chain is walked — or the request is refused with `ERR_GUARANTOR_CYCLE`. Omitting the field leaves the existing edge untouched. Whatever the payload, the entire chain re-runs: `credit_score`, `credit_score_variant`, `credit_tier`, `sustainability_score` and `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` are all recomputed from the post-update inputs.

What happens next depends on where the recomputed tier lands. If the tier stays inside the band it was already in, `qualification_status` does not move — but the deal-side consequences still fire. Every non-terminated deal anchored by this buyer re-snapshots `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` and `collateral_threshold_usd` from the buyer and re-sizes `credit_support_required_usd`. Crucially, `credit_status` is deliberately **not** re-opened on this path. A deal can therefore sit at `APPROVED` carrying a freshly enlarged requirement, and the discrepancy is caught later, at signing, by `ERR_CREDIT_SUPPORT_SHORTFALL`.

A drop out of `QUALIFIED` into a recomputed `credit_tier` of `SUBPRIME` or `DECLINED` moves the buyer `QUALIFIED → WATCHLIST` and fires a four-hop cascade. Hop one recomputes `credit_score`, `credit_score_variant`, `credit_tier` and `unsecured_credit_limit_usd`, and stamps `watchlist_since` with the logical clock. Hop two, caused by hop one's smaller unsecured limit, visits every deal anchored by this buyer whose `status` is `TERMS_AGREED`, `EXECUTED` or `EFFECTIVE`, re-snapshots that deal's `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` and re-sizes its `credit_support_required_usd`. Hop three, caused by hop two's larger requirement, takes each of those deals whose `credit_status` is `APPROVED` and whose `posted_credit_support_usd` now falls below the recomputed `credit_support_required_usd`, moves it `APPROVED → SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` and sets `support_cure_deadline` to ten days after the logical clock. Hop four, caused by hop one's new tier, visits every buyer whose own `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id` points at this buyer and whose `credit_score_variant` is `GUARANTOR_SUPPORTED`, recomputes that child's `credit_score`, `credit_score_variant`, `credit_tier` and `unsecured_credit_limit_usd`, and increments the child's `version` by one. The downgraded buyer's own `version` increments by one as well.

Recovery runs the same machinery in reverse. A rating change on a watchlisted buyer whose recomputed `credit_tier` is `PRIME` or `STANDARD` moves it `WATCHLIST → QUALIFIED`, recomputes the four credit figures, and sets `watchlist_since` back to null. Every deal anchored by this buyer whose `status` is `TERMS_AGREED`, `EXECUTED` or `EFFECTIVE` again re-snapshots `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` and re-sizes `credit_support_required_usd`; and each such deal whose `credit_status` is `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` and whose `posted_credit_support_usd` now reaches or exceeds the recomputed requirement moves `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL → APPROVED` with `support_cure_deadline` cleared to null. The buyer's `version` increments by one.

A buyer already on the watchlist has no second chance at a `DECLINED` tier. When a rating change on a buyer whose `qualification_status` is `WATCHLIST` and whose `watchlist_since` is not null recomputes `credit_tier` to `DECLINED`, the buyer moves `WATCHLIST → REJECTED`. The four credit figures are recomputed, every deal anchored by this buyer whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `TERM_SHEET_ISSUED` or `UNDER_NEGOTIATION` has its `credit_status` set to `DECLINED`, and the buyer's `version` increments by one.

Three list views exist purely to walk the buyer's relationships backwards. One returns every aggregated-buyer share this buyer holds across all deals, filterable by `status`. One returns every attribute batch conveyed into this buyer's registry account, filterable by `status` and `claim_year`. One returns every parent guarantee this entity has issued for a subsidiary's agreement, filterable by `status`.

### 3.4. Structuring and Pricing the Agreement

An `Originator` structures a new agreement in `DRAFT`. The payload names the `project_id` supplying the energy, the `anchor_offtaker_id` of the lead corporate buyer that will sign, and the `settlement_point_id` whose published price settles the contract. It declares the `product_type` — `PHYSICAL_DELIVERY` for real energy delivery, `VIRTUAL_CFD` for a financial contract for differences, or `AGGREGATED` for a multi-buyer arrangement — the total `contracted_mw` across all buyers, carried to at most three decimals and strictly above zero, the `term_years` (from 1 to 25 inclusive, exactly the range section 2.5 enforces), the `first_delivery_year` (from 2026 through 2040), the `commercial_operation_deadline` that is the outside date by which the project must reach `OPERATIONAL`, and the `settlement_frequency` electing either `HOURLY` or `MONTHLY` netting. It declares the commercial terms too: `base_fixed_price_usd_per_mwh` above zero and at most two decimals, `escalation_pct_per_year` from 0.0 to 10.0 inclusive — again exactly the range section 2.5 enforces — at most two decimals, the `shape_product` the seller commits to — `AS_GENERATED`, `SOLAR_SHAPED_16X5` or `BASELOAD_FIRM` — and the `basis_responsibility` allocating node-to-hub basis risk to `SELLER`, `SHARED` or `BUYER`.

Two gates guard creation. The anchor buyer's `qualification_status` must be `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OFFTAKER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. And the requested `contracted_mw` must not exceed the parent project's `available_mw` at the moment of creation, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; equality is allowed, so a deal may take exactly the remaining headroom. The `commercial_operation_deadline` must additionally fall strictly after the logical clock, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.

Creation initialises a great deal. `status` is `DRAFT` and `credit_status` is `NOT_SUBMITTED`; `round_count` is 0; `confirmed_allocated_mw` is 0 and `unallocated_mw` equals `contracted_mw`; `cp_total_count` is 0; `posted_credit_support_usd`, `released_credit_support_usd`, `drawn_credit_support_usd`, `attributes_minted_mwh`, `attributes_transferred_mwh` and `attributes_retired_mwh` are all 0; `credit_approved_by_user_id`, `support_cure_deadline`, `effective_at`, `terminated_at`, `termination_reason` and `cp_completion_pct` are all null; and `created_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting operator, which is the identifier the credit-decision segregation check later compares against. Creation also prices the deal, projects its volume, snapshots the buyer's unsecured limit into `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` and `collateral_threshold_usd`, and sizes `exposure_usd` and `credit_support_required_usd`. Finally it recomputes the parent project's `contracted_mw` and `available_mw` and increments that project's `version` by one.

Pricing is assembled from four components **in a fixed order**, and the order is load-bearing. Step one escalates the base price. The number of escalation years is the `first_delivery_year` minus the pricing base year 2026; the escalated base is `base_fixed_price_usd_per_mwh` multiplied by the quantity one plus `escalation_pct_per_year` divided by one hundred, raised to the power of that year count, and the product is rounded half up to four decimal places. The year count may be zero — first delivery in 2026 — in which case the escalated base equals the base price to four decimals. Step two selects `price_tier` **from the escalated base alone**: an escalated base below 40.00 is `LOW`, an escalated base of at least 40.00 but below 60.00 is `MID`, and an escalated base of 60.00 or more is `HIGH`. Selecting the tier from the pre-escalation base price mis-tiers every deal whose escalation carries it across a tier edge, and selecting it from the finished contract price is circular because the shape premium is itself tier-dependent; neither is correct.

Step three looks up two adders. The shape premium is keyed on **both** `shape_product` and the `price_tier` just selected: `AS_GENERATED` costs nothing at any tier; `SOLAR_SHAPED_16X5` adds 2.00 at `LOW`, 3.10 at `MID` and 4.00 at `HIGH`; `BASELOAD_FIRM` adds 4.50 at `LOW`, 6.25 at `MID` and 8.00 at `HIGH`, all in USD per MWh. The basis adder is keyed on `basis_responsibility` alone: 1.50 USD per MWh when the seller bears basis risk, 0.75 when it is shared, and nothing when the buyer bears it. Step four assembles the answer: `contract_price_usd_per_mwh` is the escalated base plus the shape premium plus the basis adder, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Volume projection is deterministic and independent of the price. `expected_annual_mwh` is `contracted_mw` multiplied by the technology capacity factor of the parent project — 0.28 for `SOLAR`, 0.42 for `WIND`, 0.35 for `STORAGE_HYBRID` — multiplied by the 8,760 hours in a year, rounded half up to two decimal places. It is recomputed on deal creation and on any change to `contracted_mw`.

Deals are listed with optional `project_id`, `anchor_offtaker_id`, `settlement_point_id`, `status` and `credit_status` filters; those first three are the reverse-reachability channels from a project, a buyer or a settlement point back to the agreements that reference them. A single deal is fetched with both state dimensions, the full collateral ledger and the attribute ledger attached, and its `version` is the concurrency token that allocation proposals and instrument postings must carry.

### 3.5. The Term Sheet Negotiation Cycle

Commercial terms are agreed through alternating rounds of paper. An `Originator` tables a developer round by supplying `proposed_by` — which must be `DEVELOPER` on this path — together with `proposed_fixed_price_usd_per_mwh` above zero, `proposed_escalation_pct_per_year` from 0.0 to 10.0 inclusive — the same range section 2.5 enforces, and the same bounds a buyer's `counter_escalation_pct_per_year` must respect — `proposed_shape_product`, `proposed_basis_responsibility`, a `response_deadline` instant after which the round can no longer be answered, and optionally a `redline_items` array of contract clause labels the tabling party has marked up, which defaults to empty. The new round is created with `round_number` equal to the deal's `round_count` plus one, `status` `OPEN`, `responded_at` null and `responded_by_user_id` null; the deal's `round_count` then increments and its `version` increments by one. From `DRAFT` the deal must have an anchor buyer whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, and it moves `DRAFT → TERM_SHEET_ISSUED`.

Tabling a developer round from `UNDER_NEGOTIATION` returns the deal `UNDER_NEGOTIATION → TERM_SHEET_ISSUED` — this alternation is the redline loop, and it is why the commercial dimension is cyclic rather than linear. That path also sweeps whatever is still outstanding: any round of this deal whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `response_deadline` has already fallen before the logical clock moves `OPEN → EXPIRED`, leaving `responded_at` and `responded_by_user_id` null; any round whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `response_deadline` is at or after the logical clock moves `OPEN → COUNTERED` with `responded_by_user_id` set to the tabling operator. Both sweeps increment each affected round's `version` by one.

Tabling a developer round from `TERMS_AGREED` re-opens settled terms, moving the deal `TERMS_AGREED → UNDER_NEGOTIATION` and setting both `contract_price_usd_per_mwh` and `price_tier` back to null. This is permitted **only** while the deal's `credit_status` is anything other than `APPROVED`; once credit is approved the terms are locked and the attempt is refused by the state gate with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The full negotiation history of a deal is available as a list in `round_number` order, filterable by `status` and `proposed_by`.

The buyer answers through its `OfftakerRep`, who must be the deal's `anchor_offtaker_id`. The answer supplies a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `COUNTER`, and on a counter may carry `counter_fixed_price_usd_per_mwh`, `counter_escalation_pct_per_year`, `counter_shape_product`, `counter_basis_responsibility` and a fresh `redline_items` array. The round must be `OPEN` and the logical clock must be at or before its `response_deadline`; answering a lapsed round is refused with `ERR_ROUND_EXPIRED`, and a lapsed round must be expired and re-issued rather than answered late.

An `ACCEPT` moves the round `OPEN → ACCEPTED` with `responded_at` set to the logical clock and `responded_by_user_id` set to the answering buyer, and drives the deal `TERM_SHEET_ISSUED → TERMS_AGREED`. The round's `proposed_fixed_price_usd_per_mwh`, `proposed_escalation_pct_per_year`, `proposed_shape_product` and `proposed_basis_responsibility` are copied onto the deal's `base_fixed_price_usd_per_mwh`, `escalation_pct_per_year`, `shape_product` and `basis_responsibility`, and then `contract_price_usd_per_mwh` and `price_tier` are recomputed by the four-step pricing assembly. A `COUNTER` moves the answered round `OPEN → COUNTERED` with the same two stamps, drives the deal `TERM_SHEET_ISSUED → UNDER_NEGOTIATION`, and creates a successor round with `proposed_by` `OFFTAKER`, `round_number` equal to `round_count` plus one, `status` `OPEN`, the counter terms from the request carried onto its proposed fields, and a `response_deadline` fourteen days after the logical clock. Either way `round_count` increments, the round's `version` increments by one and the deal's `version` increments by one. The response body differs between the two: a counter returns the newly created successor round, while an accept returns the answered round itself.

### 3.6. Aggregated Buyer Arrangements

An aggregated arrangement lets one agreement carry several corporate buyers, each taking a slice of the contracted capacity. An `Originator` proposes a slice by naming the `offtaker_id` of the buyer taking it, the `allocated_mw` that buyer is taking (strictly above zero, at most three decimals), the mandatory `expected_deal_version` concurrency token described in section 2.7, and optionally `joint_and_several`, a boolean defaulting to false that records whether this buyer is jointly and severally liable for the whole agreement. The deal's `status` must be `TERM_SHEET_ISSUED`, `UNDER_NEGOTIATION` or `TERMS_AGREED`, and the named buyer's `qualification_status` must be `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`. If that buyer already holds a slice on this deal whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED`, the proposal is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ALLOCATION`; a `WITHDRAWN` slice does not block a fresh proposal.

A new slice is created with `status` `PROPOSED`, `confirmed_at` null, `deal_id` taken from the path rather than the payload, and `created_by_user_id` stamped from the proposing operator. Its `allocation_share_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `allocated_mw` divided by the deal's `contracted_mw`, rounded half up to four decimal places. The deal's `unallocated_mw` is then recomputed as `contracted_mw` less the sum of `allocated_mw` over the slices whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED`, both the slice's `version` and the deal's `version` increment by one. Every slice on a deal is listable, filterable by `status` and by `offtaker_id`.

Each buyer decides on its own slice, and only its own: the acting `OfftakerRep`'s identifier must equal the slice's `offtaker_id`. The payload carries a single `decision` of `CONFIRM` or `WITHDRAW`. A `CONFIRM` moves the slice `PROPOSED → CONFIRMED`, requires that the deal's `status` is `TERM_SHEET_ISSUED`, `UNDER_NEGOTIATION` or `TERMS_AGREED` and that the referenced buyer's `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, and stamps `confirmed_at` with the logical clock. It also carries an aggregate check that no individual proposal could catch: if confirming would push the sum of `allocated_mw` over the deal's `CONFIRMED` slices above `contracted_mw`, the request is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_CONTRACTED_MW`. Equality is allowed, and confirming exactly to the contracted total drives `unallocated_mw` to 0.000.

`WITHDRAW` is the exact compensating action, and it is legal from either live state. From `PROPOSED` it moves the slice `PROPOSED → WITHDRAWN` and recomputes the deal's `unallocated_mw`; from `CONFIRMED` it moves the slice `CONFIRMED → WITHDRAWN`, requires that the deal's `status` is `TERM_SHEET_ISSUED`, `UNDER_NEGOTIATION` or `TERMS_AGREED`, and recomputes both `confirmed_allocated_mw` and `unallocated_mw`. `confirmed_allocated_mw` is always the sum of `allocated_mw` over the deal's `CONFIRMED` slices. A confirm followed by a withdraw must leave `unallocated_mw` exactly where it stood before the slice was proposed. Every decision increments both the slice's `version` and the deal's `version` by one.

### 3.7. Credit Underwriting and the Collateral Stack

Credit runs on its own dimension, `credit_status`, which starts at `NOT_SUBMITTED` and evolves entirely independently of the commercial `status`. An `Originator` submits the credit package with an empty payload. Submission is legal from `NOT_SUBMITTED`, from `DECLINED` — the re-underwriting loop after a refused package — and from `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL`, and in every case the deal moves to `SUBMITTED`. It requires that `contract_price_usd_per_mwh` is not null, so an unpriced deal cannot be underwritten, and from `NOT_SUBMITTED` it additionally requires the anchor buyer's `qualification_status` to be `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`. It also requires the anchor buyer's `credit_tier` to be anything other than `DECLINED`; a declined tier carries a 0.00 unsecured limit and has no eligible support path, so review is refused outright with `ERR_CREDIT_TIER_DECLINED`.

Submission does four things before it changes the state. It sweeps stale collateral: every instrument on this deal whose `status` is `POSTED` and whose `expiry_date` falls at or before the logical clock moves `POSTED → EXPIRED` with `closed_at` stamped and its own `version` incremented, and the deal's `posted_credit_support_usd` drops by that instrument's `eligible_credit_usd`. It re-snapshots `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` and `collateral_threshold_usd` from the anchor buyer. It re-sizes `expected_annual_mwh`, `exposure_usd` and `credit_support_required_usd`. And it recomputes `posted_credit_support_usd` over the instruments that survived the sweep. Arriving from `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` additionally clears `support_cure_deadline` to null. The deal's `version` increments by one.

Sizing the requirement is a three-step calculation. First, `exposure_usd` is one year of contract value: `expected_annual_mwh` multiplied by `contract_price_usd_per_mwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Second, the buyer's unsecured appetite is netted off — `collateral_threshold_usd` on the deal equals the `unsecured_credit_limit_usd` snapshotted from the anchor buyer, and it is the threshold below which no collateral is called at all. If `exposure_usd` is at or below `collateral_threshold_usd`, then `credit_support_required_usd` is 0.00. Third, otherwise, the residual is `exposure_usd` less `collateral_threshold_usd`, that residual is divided by the collateral increment of 250,000.00 USD and the quotient is rounded **up** to the next whole number of increments — a ceiling, never a half-up round — and `credit_support_required_usd` is that whole count of increments multiplied by 250,000.00. The consequence at the boundary is sharp: at an exposure exactly equal to the threshold the requirement is 0.00, and one cent above it the requirement jumps a full 250,000.00.

Instruments are registered before they are posted, and registration computes nothing. Either an `Originator` or a `CreditOfficer` registers one by declaring the `instrument_type` — `LETTER_OF_CREDIT`, `PARENT_GUARANTEE` or `CASH_COLLATERAL` — the `purpose`, which is either `DEVELOPMENT_SECURITY` (released when the deal becomes effective) or `PERFORMANCE_SECURITY`, the `face_amount_usd` above zero, the `effective_date` from which it is in force and the `expiry_date` on which it lapses, plus optionally the `issuer_name` (1 to 128 characters, the issuing bank for a letter of credit or the depositary for cash), the `issuer_rating` of that bank, and the `guarantor_offtaker_id` naming the guaranteeing entity. `expiry_date` must fall strictly after `effective_date` or the registration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. A registered instrument arrives with `status` `PROPOSED` and with `haircut_pct`, `eligible_credit_usd`, `posted_at` and `closed_at` all null; registration deliberately does **not** move `posted_credit_support_usd` and does **not** increment the deal's `version`. The full collateral stack of a deal is listable, filterable by `status`, `purpose` and `instrument_type`.

Posting is a `CreditOfficer` action, carries the `expected_deal_version` token, and is where the eligibility rules and the haircut arithmetic land. The instrument must be `PROPOSED`, and the deal's `credit_status` must be `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` or `DECLINED` — since `NOT_SUBMITTED` is the only other value that dimension takes, posting against a deal whose credit package has never been submitted is refused by the state gate with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, exactly as a post against an instrument that is not `PROPOSED` is. Both of those refusals are decided before the `expected_deal_version` comparison, so a stale token supplied against a `NOT_SUBMITTED` deal yields 409 rather than 412. Eligibility is tested first. A `LETTER_OF_CREDIT` requires an `issuer_name` and an `issuer_rating` among `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BBB` and `BB`; an unrated issuer or one rated `B`, `CCC` or `NR` has no haircut row and is refused with `ERR_INELIGIBLE_ISSUER`. A `PARENT_GUARANTEE` requires a `guarantor_offtaker_id` that is present, equal to the anchor buyer's own `parent_guarantor_offtaker_id`, and belonging to a guarantor whose `credit_tier` is not `DECLINED`; any of those failing is also `ERR_INELIGIBLE_ISSUER`. `CASH_COLLATERAL` consults neither issuer nor guarantor fields. Tenor is tested only after eligibility: a letter of credit or a parent guarantee must have at least 365 whole days from the logical clock to its `expiry_date`, or the post is refused with `ERR_INSTRUMENT_TENOR_TOO_SHORT`; cash collateral is exempt from the tenor rule entirely.

The haircut itself is a lookup, and `haircut_pct` is stored as a **percentage** rather than a fraction, so a ten percent discount is recorded as 10.00. Cash collateral is undiscounted at 0.00. A letter of credit is discounted by the issuing bank's rating: 0.00 for `AAA`, `AA` and `A`, 10.00 for `BBB` and 35.00 for `BB`. A parent guarantee is discounted by the guarantor's own commercial tier: 0.00 for `PRIME`, 25.00 for `STANDARD` and 60.00 for `SUBPRIME`, which is why a weak parent contributes little even behind a large face amount. `eligible_credit_usd` is then `face_amount_usd` multiplied by the quantity one minus `haircut_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places.

A successful post moves the instrument `PROPOSED → POSTED`, stamps `posted_at` with the logical clock, and recomputes the deal's `posted_credit_support_usd` as the sum of `eligible_credit_usd` over that deal's `POSTED` instruments. If the deal's `credit_status` was `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` and the recomputed coverage now reaches or exceeds `credit_support_required_usd`, the deal moves `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL → APPROVED` and `support_cure_deadline` is cleared to null — the cure loop, which closes without touching the commercial `status` at all. Both the instrument's `version` and the deal's `version` increment by one.

Releasing is the compensating half, also a `CreditOfficer` action and with no payload. The instrument must be `POSTED` and the deal's `status` must be anything other than `TERMINATED`. When the deal's `credit_status` is `APPROVED`, a release that would drop `posted_credit_support_usd` below `credit_support_required_usd` is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_BREACHES_COVERAGE` — the coverage invariant is enforced on the reverse action, not only on the forward one. A permitted release moves the instrument `POSTED → RELEASED`, stamps `closed_at`, and recomputes both `posted_credit_support_usd` and `released_credit_support_usd`, so that posted plus released plus drawn is conserved across the deal's whole stack. `released_credit_support_usd` is always the sum of `eligible_credit_usd` over the deal's `RELEASED` instruments and `drawn_credit_support_usd` the sum over its `DRAWN` ones. Both the instrument's `version` and the deal's `version` increment by one.

The credit decision itself belongs to a `CreditOfficer` who is not the operator recorded as the deal's `created_by_user_id`. The payload carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DECLINE` and optionally a free-text `decision_note`. The deal's `credit_status` must be `SUBMITTED`. `APPROVE` requires that `posted_credit_support_usd` is at least `credit_support_required_usd`, moves the deal `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, and stamps `credit_approved_by_user_id` with the deciding operator — the identifier that later bars that same person from executing. Approval explicitly does **not** renegotiate `collateral_threshold_usd`; it keeps the value snapshotted at submission. `DECLINE` moves the deal `SUBMITTED → DECLINED`, sets `credit_approved_by_user_id` back to null, and leaves the commercial `status` untouched, which is exactly what makes the two dimensions independent. Either decision increments the deal's `version` by one.

One further transition on the credit dimension has no operator behind it at all. Whenever a rating-change cascade or an accepted change-in-law repricing raises `credit_support_required_usd` above `posted_credit_support_usd` on a deal whose `credit_status` is `APPROVED`, the system itself moves that deal `APPROVED → SUPPORT_SHORTFALL`, sets `support_cure_deadline` to ten days after the logical clock and increments the deal's `version` by one.

### 3.8. Execution, Conditions Precedent, Effectiveness and Termination

Signing is a `DealCommittee` act with no payload, and it is the most heavily guarded operation in the system. The deal's `status` must be `TERMS_AGREED` and its `credit_status` must be `APPROVED`. The acting operator must not be the one recorded as `credit_approved_by_user_id`. The anchor buyer's `qualification_status` must be exactly `QUALIFIED` — a `CONDITIONAL` buyer may be structured, negotiated and even cleared through credit, but it may not sign, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_OFFTAKER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. That buyer's `credit_tier` must not be `DECLINED`, or the answer is `ERR_CREDIT_TIER_DECLINED`. `confirmed_allocated_mw` must equal `contracted_mw` exactly, or the answer is `ERR_ALLOCATION_INCOMPLETE`; a single-buyer deal satisfies this with one confirmed slice equal to the contracted total. `posted_credit_support_usd` must be at least `credit_support_required_usd`, or the answer is `ERR_CREDIT_SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` — equality passes, and a requirement of 0.00 passes with nothing posted at all. And the project's `available_mw` must be at least the deal's `contracted_mw`. Notably, the project's own development `status` is deliberately **not** a precondition: signing before permitting is exactly the situation the conditions-precedent set exists to govern.

The conditions-precedent set is built at execution from a template that is conditional on two other entities, so two deals with identical commercial terms can execute with entirely different obligations. The template is evaluated in a fixed order of four rows. A `PERMITTING` condition is included when the parent project's `status` is `PRE_DEVELOPMENT`; it is not waivable, and its `deadline_date` is the earlier of the deal's `commercial_operation_deadline` and 270 days after the logical clock. An `INTERCONNECTION` condition is included when the project's `status` is `PRE_DEVELOPMENT` or `PERMITTED`; it is not waivable either, and its `deadline_date` is the earlier of `commercial_operation_deadline` and 540 days after the logical clock. A `NOTICE_TO_PROCEED` condition is included whenever the project's `status` is anything other than `OPERATIONAL`; it is waivable, and its `deadline_date` is the earlier of `commercial_operation_deadline` and 720 days after the logical clock. A `REGISTRY_ACCOUNT` condition is included when the anchor buyer's `registry_account_id` is null; it is waivable, and its `deadline_date` is 60 days after the logical clock with no cap against the commercial operation deadline. Each included row is created with `status` `PENDING`, its computed `deadline_date`, its `is_waivable` flag, `evidence_ref` and `waiver_justification` null, `cure_deadline` null, `resolved_at` null and `resolved_by_user_id` null. These rows are created only by this cascade, which is why the deal's condition list is their only discovery channel; that list is filterable by `status` and `cp_type`.

Execution then sets `cp_total_count` to the number of rows included and computes `cp_completion_pct`, which is one hundred multiplied by the count of the deal's conditions whose `status` is `SATISFIED` or `WAIVED`, divided by `cp_total_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places; both `FAILED` and `PENDING` count as not cleared. When `cp_total_count` is zero the figure is 100.00 by vacuous truth. That is the case that matters most: an already-`OPERATIONAL` project whose anchor buyer already holds a registry account produces the empty template, so `cp_total_count` is 0, `cp_completion_pct` is 100.00, and the deal moves `TERMS_AGREED → EXECUTED → EFFECTIVE` inside the very same request, stamping `effective_at` and releasing every `POSTED` instrument whose `purpose` is `DEVELOPMENT_SECURITY`. Execution also recomputes the project's `contracted_mw` and `available_mw` and increments the deal's `version` by one. With three conditions of which two are cleared, by contrast, the figure is 66.67 — the half-up rounding of 66.666… — which is not 100.00, so no effectiveness transition fires.

Resolving a condition is available to an `Originator` or a `DealCommittee` operator, and the payload carries a `resolution` of `SATISFY`, `WAIVE`, `FAIL` or `RECURE`, together with `evidence_ref` or `waiver_justification` as the branch requires. The four branches are deliberately asymmetric. `SATISFY` moves the condition `PENDING → SATISFIED`; it requires that the deal's `status` is `EXECUTED`, that the logical clock is at or before the condition's `deadline_date` — satisfying after the deadline is refused with `ERR_CP_DEADLINE_PASSED`, and an expired condition can then only be failed or waived — and that the request supplies `evidence_ref`, which is stored along with `resolved_at` at the logical clock and `resolved_by_user_id` from the acting operator. `WAIVE` moves the condition `PENDING → WAIVED`; it requires that the deal's `status` is `EXECUTED`, that `is_waivable` is true, and that the request supplies `waiver_justification`, which is stored along with `resolved_at` and `resolved_by_user_id` while `evidence_ref` stays null. `WAIVE` carries one side effect that `SATISFY` never applies: it extends the deal's `commercial_operation_deadline` by 30 days beyond its prior value.

`FAIL` moves the condition `PENDING → FAILED` and is legal while the deal's `status` is `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATED`. It stamps `resolved_at` at the logical clock and `resolved_by_user_id` from the acting operator, sets `cure_deadline` to 15 days after the logical clock, and vests the `CP_FAILURE` termination right on the deal. `RECURE` is the cure loop that makes this machine non-linear: it moves the condition `FAILED → PENDING`, requires that the deal's `status` is `EXECUTED` and that the logical clock is at or before the condition's `cure_deadline` — otherwise `ERR_CP_DEADLINE_PASSED` — clears both `cure_deadline` and `resolved_at` back to null, and pushes `deadline_date` out to 15 days after the logical clock. Every one of the four resolutions recomputes the deal's `cp_completion_pct` and increments both the condition's `version` and the deal's `version` by one. Any resolution whose recomputed figure reaches exactly 100.00 drives the deal `EXECUTED → EFFECTIVE`: `effective_at` is stamped with the logical clock, every instrument on the deal whose `purpose` is `DEVELOPMENT_SECURITY` and whose `status` is `POSTED` moves `POSTED → RELEASED` with `closed_at` stamped, and `posted_credit_support_usd` and `released_credit_support_usd` are recomputed.

Termination is a `DealCommittee` act citing a single `termination_reason`, and there is no unconditional right to walk away — all five reasons are predicated, so a request citing a reason whose predicate is false is always refused with `ERR_TERMINATION_RIGHT_NOT_VESTED`. `CP_FAILURE` is vested when some condition of the deal has `status` `FAILED` and either its `cure_deadline` is null or that `cure_deadline` falls before the logical clock. `BUYER_DEFAULT` is vested when the deal's `credit_status` is `SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` and its `support_cure_deadline` falls before the logical clock. `SELLER_DEFAULT` is vested when the logical clock has passed the deal's `commercial_operation_deadline` and the parent project's `status` is still anything other than `OPERATIONAL`. `COD_DEADLINE_MISSED` is vested when the logical clock has passed `commercial_operation_deadline` and the deal's own `status` is anything other than `EFFECTIVE`. `EXTENDED_FORCE_MAJEURE` is vested when some risk claim on the deal has `status` `ACCEPTED`, `event_type` `FORCE_MAJEURE` and a `duration_days` of at least 180. When the cited reason is `CP_FAILURE`, the acting operator must additionally differ from the `resolved_by_user_id` recorded on the failed condition, or the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Termination is legal from `TERMS_AGREED`, from `EXECUTED` and from `EFFECTIVE`, and each of the three does something different. From all three, `terminated_at` is stamped with the logical clock and `termination_reason` is stored. From `TERMS_AGREED` the deal moves `TERMS_AGREED → TERMINATED` and every slice on the deal whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, after which `confirmed_allocated_mw` and `unallocated_mw` are recomputed. From `EXECUTED` the deal moves `EXECUTED → TERMINATED` and a three-branch cascade fires off that stamp. The first branch fails the outstanding conditions: every condition of the deal whose `status` is `PENDING` moves `PENDING → FAILED` with `resolved_at` set to the logical clock and `cure_deadline` left null, and then `cp_completion_pct` is recomputed over the new statuses. The second branch resolves the collateral: every instrument whose `status` is `POSTED` moves `POSTED → DRAWN` when the `termination_reason` is `BUYER_DEFAULT` and `POSTED → RELEASED` for every other reason, with `closed_at` stamped in both cases, and then `posted_credit_support_usd`, `released_credit_support_usd` and `drawn_credit_support_usd` are all recomputed. The third branch voids the unconveyed attributes: every attribute batch of the deal whose `status` is `MINTED` moves `MINTED → CANCELLED`, and then `attributes_minted_mwh` is recomputed, dropping by each cancelled batch's `quantity_mwh`; batches already `TRANSFERRED` or `RETIRED` are untouched. Termination from `EFFECTIVE` moves the deal `EFFECTIVE → TERMINATED` and runs that identical three-branch cascade, with one extra guard and one explicit non-effect: it is refused while any settlement period of the deal has `status` `DISPUTED`, and periods that are `OPEN`, `CALCULATED` or `INVOICED` are left exactly as they stand. Every termination path also recomputes the project's `contracted_mw` and `available_mw` and increments the deal's `version` by one, along with the `version` of every row the cascade touched.

### 3.9. Settlement, Risk Allocation and Environmental Attribute Conveyance

Money only starts moving once a deal is `EFFECTIVE`. A `SettlementAnalyst` opens a settlement window by supplying `period_start`, the inclusive beginning of the window, and `period_end`, its exclusive end. The deal's `status` must be `EFFECTIVE`; `period_end` must fall strictly after `period_start` or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`; and the new window must not intersect any existing period of the same deal, or the request is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Overlap here uses **half-open** semantics on the range from `period_start` up to but not including `period_end`, so a period beginning at exactly the instant a previous one ends is accepted — deliberately the opposite convention from the one that governs risk claims. On creation the period's `netting_method` is computed as a snapshot of the deal's `settlement_frequency` and never changes afterwards; since a deal is created with one `settlement_frequency` and no operation amends it, comparing the two netting variants requires two deals. The period arrives with `status` `OPEN`, `interval_count` 0, `total_metered_mwh`, `total_deemed_mwh` and `total_delivered_mwh` at 0, and `weighted_avg_index_usd_per_mwh`, `energy_settlement_usd`, `basis_adjustment_usd`, `net_settlement_usd`, `seller_pays_usd`, `buyer_pays_usd`, `mintable_attributes_mwh`, `calculated_at`, `invoiced_at` and `dispute_reason` all null. Periods on a deal are listable with optional `status` and `netting_method` filters, and a single period is fetched with its computed totals; that period's `version` is the concurrency token that interval posting must carry.

Metered hours are loaded one at a time by a `SettlementAnalyst`. The payload carries `hour_start`, the inclusive start of a one-hour slot spanning from `hour_start` up to but not including one hour later; `metered_mwh`, the generation actually delivered in that hour, at least zero and at most three decimals; `node_price_usd_per_mwh` and `hub_price_usd_per_mwh`, the published prices at the project's injection node and at the trading hub for that hour, each at most two decimals and each permitted to be negative; the mandatory `expected_settlement_period_version` token; and optionally `curtailment_flag`, which is `NONE`, `ECONOMIC`, `GRID_EMERGENCY` or `BUYER_REQUESTED` and defaults to `NONE`. The period's `status` must be `OPEN`, `CALCULATED` or `DISPUTED`. The `hour_start` must land exactly on the hour and must lie inside the period's half-open window — an `hour_start` equal to `period_end` is outside the period — or the request is refused with `ERR_HOUR_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`. Only after that in-window test does the duplicate test run: an hour already loaded into this period is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_METER_HOUR`, so a duplicate hour that is also out of range returns the out-of-range error instead. A new interval carries `settlement_period_id` set to the period it was loaded into, `posted_by_user_id` stamped from the acting analyst, `expected_mwh` and `delivered_mwh` null and `deemed_mwh` 0 until the period is calculated; the period's `interval_count` increments by one and its `version` increments by one. The hours of a period are listable in `hour_start` order, filterable by `curtailment_flag`.

Running the settlement is a `SettlementAnalyst` action with no payload, and it is the only route by which an accepted risk claim reaches the money. It requires that the period's `interval_count` is at least 1 and that the deal's `status` is `EFFECTIVE`. It is legal from `OPEN`, moving the period `OPEN → CALCULATED`; from `CALCULATED`, where it re-runs in place as `CALCULATED → CALCULATED`; and from `DISPUTED`, moving the period `DISPUTED → CALCULATED` and additionally clearing `invoiced_at` and `dispute_reason` back to null. Re-running overwrites every computed value rather than accumulating onto it. Every run rewrites `expected_mwh`, `deemed_mwh` and `delivered_mwh` on each of the period's intervals, incrementing each interval's `version` by one; recomputes the period's nine totals and money fields; recomputes `mintable_attributes_mwh`; stamps `calculated_at` with the logical clock; and increments the period's `version` by one.

Deemed generation is where curtailment becomes money, and the filter on its inputs is the whole point. For each hour, the contract-shape expectation `expected_mwh` is the deal's `contracted_mw` multiplied by the parent project's technology capacity factor — 0.28 for `SOLAR`, 0.42 for `WIND`, 0.35 for `STORAGE_HYBRID` — rounded half up to four decimal places. An hour earns deemed volume only when two things hold together: its `curtailment_flag` is something other than `NONE`, and there exists a risk claim on this deal whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, whose `event_type` is `CURTAILMENT`, and whose window contains `hour_start` inclusive of both endpoints. At most one such claim can exist, because two live curtailment claims on one deal may not share any instant, so no tie-break is ever needed. The three curtailment causes `ECONOMIC`, `GRID_EMERGENCY` and `BUYER_REQUESTED` all mark the hour as curtailed; `NONE` does not. When both conditions hold, the deemed share is read off the covering claim's `allocation_outcome`: nothing at all when the outcome is `SELLER_BEARS`, one half when it is `SHARED`, and the whole shortfall when it is `BUYER_BEARS`. `deemed_mwh` is then the shortfall — `expected_mwh` less `metered_mwh`, floored at zero so a curtailed hour that out-produced its expectation yields 0.0000 rather than a negative — multiplied by that share, rounded half up to four decimal places. An hour that fails either condition has `deemed_mwh` of 0.0000. `delivered_mwh` is `metered_mwh` plus `deemed_mwh`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and it is the quantity the settlement legs multiply. A `SELLER_BEARS` outcome therefore excuses the seller without creating any deemed volume at all: the accepted claim is visible on the claim record and invisible in the money.

Netting itself has two variants, selected by the period's own `netting_method` snapshot, and the same intervals produce materially different money under the two. Both begin from the same index choice and the same three totals. The hourly index is the `hub_price_usd_per_mwh` when the deal's settlement point has `settlement_point_type` `HUB`, and the `node_price_usd_per_mwh` when it has `settlement_point_type` `NODE`. `total_metered_mwh` is the sum of `metered_mwh` across the period's intervals, `total_deemed_mwh` the sum of `deemed_mwh`, and `total_delivered_mwh` the sum of `delivered_mwh`, each rounded half up to four decimal places.

Under the `HOURLY` variant each hour settles on its own. The effective index for an hour is that hour's index floored at 0.00, because the buyer never pays a negative index under a contract for differences. Each hour's amount is that hour's `delivered_mwh` multiplied by the quantity the effective index less the deal's `contract_price_usd_per_mwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places; `energy_settlement_usd` is the sum of those per-hour amounts, rounded half up to two decimal places. `weighted_avg_index_usd_per_mwh` is still reported under this variant, as the sum over the hours of `delivered_mwh` multiplied by the unfloored index, divided by `total_delivered_mwh`, rounded half up to four decimal places — but it is reported only and takes no part in the hourly arithmetic. Under the `MONTHLY` variant there is a single netting. `weighted_avg_index_usd_per_mwh` is computed by that same volume-weighted formula over the **unfloored** index, and `energy_settlement_usd` is `total_delivered_mwh` multiplied by the quantity that weighted average less `contract_price_usd_per_mwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because negative hours are not floored here, they pull the average down and shift money toward the seller's side, which is precisely the divergence between the two variants.

The basis leg is identical under both variants. When the deal's settlement point has `settlement_point_type` `NODE`, `basis_adjustment_usd` is 0.00, because the contract already settles at the node and there is no differential to allocate. Otherwise the differential is the sum, over the period's hours, of `delivered_mwh` multiplied by the quantity that hour's `node_price_usd_per_mwh` less that hour's `hub_price_usd_per_mwh`; `basis_adjustment_usd` is that differential multiplied by the buyer's basis share — nothing when `basis_responsibility` is `SELLER`, one half when it is `SHARED`, and all of it when it is `BUYER` — rounded half up to two decimal places. `net_settlement_usd` is `energy_settlement_usd` plus `basis_adjustment_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it splits into `seller_pays_usd` and `buyer_pays_usd` by the sign convention of section 2.6. If `total_delivered_mwh` is zero, `weighted_avg_index_usd_per_mwh` is 0.0000 and every money field is 0.00; there is no division by zero. Finally, `mintable_attributes_mwh` is `total_metered_mwh` truncated to a whole number — a floor, never a half-up round — and `total_deemed_mwh` is deliberately excluded from that input, because deemed generation is a financial make-whole and minting a certificate against it would leave the buyer's claim unsubstantiated.

A calculated period is then advanced by naming an `action`. `INVOICE` requires `status` `CALCULATED` and moves the period `CALCULATED → INVOICED`, stamping `invoiced_at` with the logical clock; it is refused with `ERR_OPEN_RISK_CLAIM` while any risk claim of the deal whose `status` is `CLAIMED` has a window intersecting the period's half-open window from `period_start` up to but not including `period_end`. An unreviewed curtailment, change-in-law or force-majeure claim over the period must be decided first; a claim already `REJECTED` or `ACCEPTED` does not block invoicing. `SETTLE` requires `status` `INVOICED` and moves the period `INVOICED → SETTLED`, which is terminal. `DISPUTE` requires `status` `INVOICED`, moves the period `INVOICED → DISPUTED`, and stores the supplied `dispute_reason`. `INVOICE` and `SETTLE` are available only to a `SettlementAnalyst`; `DISPUTE` is available only to an `OfftakerRep`, who must be the deal's `anchor_offtaker_id`, and who must supply a `dispute_reason`. Every advance increments the period's `version` by one. The dispute loop — `INVOICED → DISPUTED → CALCULATED → INVOICED` — is what makes the settlement machine cyclic.

Risk allocation runs alongside settlement on the same deal. Either an `Originator` or an `OfftakerRep` files a claim, and an `OfftakerRep` claimant must be the deal's anchor buyer or the holder of a confirmed slice on it. The payload names the `event_type` — `CHANGE_IN_LAW`, `FORCE_MAJEURE` or `CURTAILMENT` — the `claimed_by` party, which is `DEVELOPER` or `OFFTAKER`, the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the claimed event, and optionally a `description` narrative of 1 to 512 characters. The deal's `status` must be `EFFECTIVE` or `EXECUTED`. The window must be well formed, with `window_end` at or after `window_start`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. It must then not collide with any existing claim of the same deal carrying the same `event_type` whose `status` is `CLAIMED` or `ACCEPTED`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_RISK_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Collision here is **closed-interval**: the window runs from `window_start` through `window_end` inclusive of both ends, so touching endpoints do collide and a claim starting at exactly the instant another ends is rejected. A `REJECTED` claim frees its window for re-use. A new claim arrives with `status` `CLAIMED`, `allocation_outcome`, `price_adjustment_usd_per_mwh` and `duration_days` all null, `excused_hours` 0, and `reviewed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_at` null. Claims on a deal are listable with optional `event_type` and `status` filters.

A `DealCommittee` operator reviews a claim whose `status` is `CLAIMED`, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. `ACCEPT` requires an `allocation_outcome` of `SELLER_BEARS`, `BUYER_BEARS` or `SHARED`, and when the `event_type` is `CHANGE_IN_LAW` it additionally requires a `price_adjustment_usd_per_mwh` between negative 10.00 and positive 10.00 inclusive, at most two decimals; a change-in-law acceptance that omits the adjustment, or one whose adjustment would drive the deal's `contract_price_usd_per_mwh` to zero or below, is refused with `ERR_PRICE_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID` — the adjustment may be negative but the resulting price must remain strictly positive. `REJECT` moves the claim `CLAIMED → REJECTED`, stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the acting operator and `reviewed_at` with the logical clock, leaves both `allocation_outcome` and `price_adjustment_usd_per_mwh` null and leaves `excused_hours` at 0, and increments the claim's `version` by one.

`ACCEPT` moves the claim `CLAIMED → ACCEPTED` and is the head of a three-hop cascade. Hop one stores the `allocation_outcome` and, where supplied, the `price_adjustment_usd_per_mwh`, stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_at`, and sizes the claim: `duration_days` is the whole days from `window_start` to `window_end` counted inclusively of both endpoints — that is, the truncated count of 86400-second periods between them, plus one — and `excused_hours` is the number of metered hours belonging to any settlement period of this deal whose `hour_start` falls at or after `window_start` and at or before `window_end`, the window being closed on both ends so an hour starting exactly at `window_end` is excused. Hop two fires only on the `CHANGE_IN_LAW` path: the deal's `contract_price_usd_per_mwh` becomes its prior value plus `price_adjustment_usd_per_mwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places, after which `exposure_usd` and `credit_support_required_usd` are re-sized by the three-step requirement calculation. Hop three, caused by hop two, catches the coverage consequence: a deal whose `credit_status` is `APPROVED` and whose `posted_credit_support_usd` now falls below the recomputed `credit_support_required_usd` moves `APPROVED → SUPPORT_SHORTFALL` with `support_cure_deadline` set to ten days after the logical clock. The claim's `version` increments by one, and the deal's `version` increments by one on the change-in-law path. One thing acceptance pointedly does **not** do is rewrite periods that are already `CALCULATED`: neither the new deemed generation nor the adjusted price reaches the money until a `SettlementAnalyst` re-runs the calculation on the affected period.

Environmental attributes are the last leg, and they are the reason a corporate buyer signed in the first place. A `RegistryAgent` mints a batch from a period whose `status` is `CALCULATED`, `INVOICED` or `SETTLED`, supplying the whole-megawatt-hour `quantity_mwh` (at least 1) and the `registry` holding the certificates — `WREGIS`, `MRETS` or `NAR`. The requested `quantity_mwh` may not exceed the period's `mintable_attributes_mwh` less whatever earlier batches have already minted from that same period, or the request is refused with `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_MINTABLE`; because deemed generation is excluded from the mintable count, a period whose delivered volume was largely deemed mints far fewer certificates than its settlement dollars would suggest. The batch's `vintage_month` is computed as the calendar year and month of the source period's `period_start`, written in four-digit-year-hyphen-two-digit-month form. It arrives with `status` `MINTED`, `claim_substantiation_status` `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, `allocation_breakdown` the empty array, and `registry_serial_range`, `transferred_to_offtaker_id`, `claim_year`, `transferred_at` and `retired_at` all null. The deal's `attributes_minted_mwh` increases by `quantity_mwh` and the deal's `version` increments by one. `attributes_minted_mwh` is always the sum of `quantity_mwh` over the deal's batches whose `status` is `MINTED`, `TRANSFERRED` or `RETIRED`; `attributes_transferred_mwh` the sum over those that are `TRANSFERRED` or `RETIRED`; and `attributes_retired_mwh` the sum over those that are `RETIRED`. A deal's batches are listable with optional `status` and `vintage_month` filters.

Conveyance into a buyer's registry account is also a `RegistryAgent` act. The payload names the `transferred_to_offtaker_id` receiving the batch and the `registry_serial_range` the registry assigned. The batch's `status` must be `MINTED`. The named recipient must hold a slice on this batch's deal whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, or the transfer is refused with `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_ALLOCATION` — aggregated deals convey attributes only to buyers whose allocation was confirmed. That recipient must also have a non-null `registry_account_id`, or the transfer is refused with `ERR_REGISTRY_ACCOUNT_MISSING`, because a claim without a receiving account and a registry serial cannot be substantiated at all. A successful transfer moves the batch `MINTED → TRANSFERRED`, stores the recipient and the serial range, stamps `transferred_at` with the logical clock, populates `allocation_breakdown`, increases the deal's `attributes_transferred_mwh` by `quantity_mwh`, and increments both the batch's `version` and the deal's `version` by one.

The split recorded in `allocation_breakdown` uses the largest-remainder method, and the order in which the leftover units are handed out is load-bearing. The basis is the sum of `allocated_mw` over the deal's slices whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` only; `PROPOSED` and `WITHDRAWN` slices are excluded from the basis entirely. For each confirmed slice, an exact share is the batch's `quantity_mwh` multiplied by that slice's `allocated_mw` divided by the basis; the slice's floor is that exact share truncated to a whole number, and its remainder is the exact share less that floor. The leftover to distribute is `quantity_mwh` less the sum of all the floors. The confirmed slices are then sorted by remainder descending; ties are broken by `allocated_mw` descending; and any remaining tie is broken by `created_at` ascending, which is the final arbiter — the chain never falls through to the row identifier. One additional whole megawatt-hour is given to each of the first slices in that order, as many as the leftover count. `allocation_breakdown` is emitted in that same sort order, as objects each carrying an `offtaker_id` and a `quantity_mwh`, and the parts sum to the batch's `quantity_mwh` exactly. Rounding each exact share independently would not conserve the batch — it can hand out more or fewer megawatt-hours than were minted — and is therefore wrong.

Cancelling a transfer is the exact compensating action, available to a `RegistryAgent` with no payload. The batch's `status` must be `TRANSFERRED`, and the batch moves `TRANSFERRED → MINTED`. `transferred_to_offtaker_id`, `registry_serial_range` and `transferred_at` are all set back to null and `allocation_breakdown` is reset to the empty array. The deal's `attributes_transferred_mwh` decreases by `quantity_mwh`, returning to its pre-transfer value, while `attributes_minted_mwh` is left untouched — the pair must conserve attribute megawatt-hours across the minted, transferred and retired buckets. Both the batch's `version` and the deal's `version` increment by one.

Retirement is what finally substantiates the buyer's renewable claim, and only a `RegistryAgent` can do it. The batch's `status` must be `TRANSFERRED` and its `claim_substantiation_status` must still be `UNSUBSTANTIATED`. The payload supplies the `claim_year`, the reporting year the buyer will claim the certificates against, which must be no earlier than the batch's vintage year and no later than one year after it; a `claim_year` outside that two-year window is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_YEAR_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. Retirement moves the batch `TRANSFERRED → RETIRED`, stamps `retired_at` with the logical clock, stores the `claim_year`, and flips `claim_substantiation_status` to `SUBSTANTIATED`. It increases the deal's `attributes_retired_mwh` by `quantity_mwh` and increases the recipient buyer's `claimed_attributes_mwh` by the same amount, incrementing that buyer's `version` by one; `claimed_attributes_mwh` is the running total of environmental attribute megawatt-hours retired in that buyer's name across every deal it participates in. Both the batch's `version` and the deal's `version` increment by one.

Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence answer with HTTP status code 201; every other successful call, including all of the state-advancing actions and all of the list and fetch reads, answers with HTTP status code 200. The single exception to the `/ppa` mount is the logical-clock control endpoint, which sits outside the domain prefix because it is a test-driving facility rather than part of the business surface.
