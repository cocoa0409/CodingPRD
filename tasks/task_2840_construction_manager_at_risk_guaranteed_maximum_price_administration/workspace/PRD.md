# CMAR Guaranteed Maximum Price Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A construction-manager-at-risk (CMAR) engagement is unusual among construction delivery methods in that the builder is hired long before the drawings are finished, prices the work progressively as the design matures, and then — at a single moment — converts a moving estimate into a **guaranteed maximum price** it is contractually bound not to exceed. Everything that happens after that moment is an argument about which side of a fixed line a dollar falls on. Our administrators currently manage that argument in spreadsheets: one workbook for progressive estimates and design-milestone reconciliation, another for the assumptions-allowances-clarifications log that qualifies the guaranteed price, a third for the two contingency pots, and a fourth for monthly cost reporting. When a subcontractor defaults or an owner directs a change, the reconciliation between those workbooks is done by hand, and the firm's exposure is discovered weeks later.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that replaces all of it. The system tracks a CMAR project from preconstruction estimating through guaranteed-price establishment, trade buyout with open-book cost transparency, subcontract administration including default and surety recovery, change-event classification, monthly cost reconciliation, and finally the shared-savings settlement at closeout under the owner's audit rights. Two ideas govern the whole design. First, the guaranteed maximum price is a **closed envelope**: once executed, dollars may move between the budget, the two contingency pots and the spent bucket, but the envelope total only moves when the owner executes a formal amendment. Second, the parties who benefit from a determination may not be the parties who make it — the operator who prepares a self-perform bid may not open it, and the operator who submits a change event may not classify it.

The API is a flat set of ten peer collections mounted at the server root; relationships are expressed as identifiers inside request bodies rather than as nested URL segments. Every temporal rule — solicitation windows, mobilization buffers, reporting periods, ledger timestamps — is evaluated against a server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that a full project lifecycle can be driven deterministically end to end.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the five roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check precedes every other check in the system.

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `ConstructionManager`, `ProcurementOfficer`, `OwnerRepresentative`, and `IndependentAuditor`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `ProgramAdmin` is the owner-side program administrator. It registers CMAR projects and controls the logical clock, and holds no authority over pricing, procurement or the contingency ledger.
- A `ConstructionManager` is the construction manager at risk. It produces progressive estimates and guaranteed-maximum-price proposals together with their qualification log, buys out trade packages, submits sealed bids, writes subcontracts, administers defaults and files bond claims, logs and submits change events, posts contingency draws, and files monthly cost reports.
- A `ProcurementOfficer` is an independent procurement officer holding the sealed-bid opening and award authority, and publishing open-book breakdowns to the owner. The role is deliberately separated from `ConstructionManager` so that the construction manager cannot open the bids it competes against, and from `OwnerRepresentative` so that the hand which buys out the trades is not the hand which approves contingency.
- An `OwnerRepresentative` is the owner's contract administrator. It reconciles design-milestone estimates, decides guaranteed-maximum-price proposals and amendments, reviews and classifies change events, approves, denies and voids contingency draws, reconciles and releases monthly cost reports, exercises audit rights, and settles shared savings.
- An `IndependentAuditor` is a third-party auditor engaged under the owner's audit rights. It posts signed cost adjustments, certifies open-book breakdowns, and closes the engagement. It cannot itself settle the shared savings it audits.

**Operator identity.** All five roles carry an *opaque* identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the same is true when `X-User-Role` is `ConstructionManager`, `ProcurementOfficer`, `OwnerRepresentative`, or `IndependentAuditor`. The server never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`, and no resource is "owned" by an operator in the ownership-check sense. The server does, however, **record** the acting `X-User-ID` on specific resources where a later rule needs to compare two operators against each other: `prepared_by_user_id` and `opened_by_user_id` on a self-perform bid, `submitted_by_user_id` and `classified_by_user_id` on a change event, and `approved_by_user_id` on a contingency draw. Those recorded values are compared to a subsequent request's `X-User-ID` string, never to a role name and never to an entity identifier.

If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being invoked, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Two endpoints are shared between roles at the endpoint level but partitioned by action underneath, and the same `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) is returned when an authorized role requests an action it does not own:

- On the work-package transparency endpoint, advancing the disclosure axis to `DISCLOSED` is `ProcurementOfficer`-only, and certifying it to `AUDITED` is `IndependentAuditor`-only.
- On the subcontract bond-claim endpoint, the `FILE` action is `ConstructionManager`-only, while `ACKNOWLEDGE`, `SETTLE` and `DENY` are `OwnerRepresentative`-only.

Without this second layer, the disclosure step that the construction manager competes under could be certified by the very party that published it, and a surety settlement could be recorded by the party that filed the claim.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of records to skip before the page begins, and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the record's creation timestamp, ascending — oldest first.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

All ten collections in this system — CMAR projects, progressive estimates, guaranteed-maximum-price proposals, qualification items, work packages, self-perform bids, subcontracts, change events, contingency draws and cost reports — share the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must never construct one.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. A cascade that mutates three rows increments three separate `version` counters.

A cascade-driven write is a mutation like any other. When classifying a change event rewrites the project's rolled-up proposed price, the project's `updated_at` and `version` both move even though the client addressed the change event, not the project.

**The logical clock.** All temporal comparisons in this system — a bid's opening eligibility against a solicitation close, a subcontract's mobilization buffer, a ledger timestamp such as `posted_at`, `voided_at`, `opened_at`, `classified_at`, `reconciled_at` or `default_notice_at` — read a single server-side logical clock rather than real wall-clock time. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that value until it is moved. It never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdmin` may set the clock to an arbitrary ISO 8601 UTC instant by supplying `current_utc`; doing so writes no entity and advances no state machine.

### 2.5. Money, Ratios and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is decimal, never floating point. Every stored money field carries exactly 2 decimal places, and every rounding step uses the round-half-up convention. Money supplied by a client is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) if it is negative or carries more than 2 decimal places.

Ratios are held to 4 decimal places, also round-half-up. This applies to `cm_fee_pct`, `insurance_rate`, `markup_pct`, an estimate's computed `variance_pct`, and an allowance's computed `allowance_unit_rate`; a client-supplied ratio carrying more than 4 decimal places is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unit-rate inputs — a cost line's `unit_rate` and an allowance reconciliation's `actual_unit_cost` — are likewise non-negative decimals with at most 4 decimal places.

Where a formula has more than one step, **the rounding is applied at every step the formula names, not once at the end.** This is not a cosmetic preference. Two of this system's calculations are deliberately specified so that rounding early and rounding late produce different published numbers, and only the early-rounding answer is correct: the four-step guaranteed-price build-up in §3.2, and the per-line extension in monthly cost reporting in §3.8. Where a comparison is made against a rounded ratio, the ratio is rounded **before** the comparison, never after.

### 2.6. The Guaranteed Maximum Price as a Closed Envelope

Once a project's `gmp_status` reaches `EXECUTED`, its `gmp_amount` is a closed envelope, and the system asserts a seven-term identity after every write that touches any money field of the project — guaranteed-price execution, a draw posting, a draw void, a bond-claim settlement, and an amendment execution:

> `gmp_amount` equals the sum of `cost_of_work_budget_total`, `general_conditions_total`, `insurance_amount`, `cm_fee_amount`, `owner_contingency_remaining`, `construction_contingency_remaining`, and `contingency_drawn_to_cost_total`.

The equality is exact at 2 decimal places; there is no tolerance band. The consequence for any single posted draw of some amount against some pot is that the pot's remaining balance decreases by exactly that amount while `contingency_drawn_to_cost_total` increases by exactly that amount, and `gmp_amount` does not move at all. Voiding that same draw restores both fields to their pre-posting values exactly: after a post followed by a void, all seven terms are identical to their values before the post, with no residue in either direction.

An amendment execution is the only operation that moves the envelope, and it moves `gmp_amount` and `cost_of_work_budget_total` by the same signed `amount_delta`, so the identity survives amendments as well. Contingency dollars never leave the envelope; they only move between the two unspent pots and the spent bucket.

### 2.7. Contingency Posture: A Derived Project-Wide Gate

Each project carries a `contingency_posture` that is not set by any client. It is re-derived by the server after every contingency-ledger movement — a draw reaching `POSTED`, a posted draw being `VOIDED`, a bond-claim settlement crediting the construction pot, and the execution of the base guaranteed price — and it gates operations on the project's children.

The derivation reads only the project's own two construction-contingency fields plus its `closeout_status`; the owner pot never influences the posture. First, a remaining percentage is computed: when `construction_contingency_original` is 0.00 the remaining percentage is taken to be 0.0000, and otherwise it is `construction_contingency_remaining` divided by `construction_contingency_original`, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places. Then:

- If `closeout_status` is `SETTLEMENT_EXECUTED`, the posture is `FROZEN`.
- Otherwise, if the remaining percentage is less than or equal to ten percent — that is, at most 0.1000 after the 4-decimal rounding — the posture is `CONSTRAINED`. The boundary is inclusive: a remaining percentage of exactly 0.1000 is `CONSTRAINED`. A completely exhausted construction pot yields a remaining percentage of 0.0000 and therefore also lands on `CONSTRAINED`; there is no separate exhausted posture, and exhaustion is reported as `CONSTRAINED`.
- Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

**The freeze cascades; the thaw does not.** When the posture moves from `OPEN` to `CONSTRAINED`, every cost report of that project whose status is `OPEN` is immediately moved to `RECONCILIATION_HELD` with its `held_reason` set to the string `CONSTRUCTION_CONTINGENCY_CONSTRAINED`, and each such report's own `version` increments. When the posture later recovers from `CONSTRAINED` back to `OPEN` — which only a void or a bond-claim settlement credit can achieve — reports already sitting in `RECONCILIATION_HELD` are **not** released automatically. Each one requires an explicit release by an `OwnerRepresentative`, and that release is itself refused with `ERR_POSTURE_NOT_RECOVERED` (HTTP 409) while the posture is still `CONSTRAINED` or `FROZEN`. This asymmetry is intentional: automatic tightening protects the owner, and deliberate loosening keeps a human in the loop.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties on the Operator Identifier

Two determinations in this system are separated at the level of the individual operator, not merely at the level of the role, because a single person could plausibly hold two roles:

- The operator who opens a sealed bid must not be the operator who prepared it. The comparison is between the acting `X-User-ID` and the bid's recorded `prepared_by_user_id`.
- The operator who classifies a change event must not be the operator who most recently submitted it. The comparison is between the acting `X-User-ID` and the event's recorded `submitted_by_user_id`.

Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that classification is deliberately shared between `ConstructionManager` and `OwnerRepresentative` precisely so that either side of the contract may make the cost-responsibility determination — but never the operator who asked for it.

### 2.9. Referential Integrity and Path Resolution Order

Because the collections are peers rather than nested, most relationships arrive as identifiers inside request bodies, and each one is resolved before the operation proceeds. Identifiers that appear in a URL path are resolved left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error: a project identifier in a path that does not resolve yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a work-package identifier `ERR_WORK_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a subcontract identifier `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a change-event identifier `ERR_CHANGE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and any other identifier — an estimate, a proposal, a qualification item, a self-perform bid, a contingency draw or a cost report — `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The same four-way split applies to identifiers supplied in a request body: an unresolvable `project_id` yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `work_package_id` yields `ERR_WORK_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, and every other unresolvable body identifier — `prior_estimate_id`, `based_on_estimate_id`, `gmp_proposal_id`, `awarded_bid_id`, `bid_id`, `qualification_item_id`, `source_subcontract_id`, or a cost line's `work_package_id` — yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

Beyond mere resolvability, **every referenced row must belong to the same CMAR project as the row being written.** This applies to the project behind a `prior_estimate_id` or a `based_on_estimate_id`, the project behind a `gmp_proposal_id`, the project behind the proposal that owns a `qualification_item_id`, the project behind the work package that owns a `source_subcontract_id`, and the project behind a cost line's `work_package_id`. A cross-project reference is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). A tighter sibling rule governs bids: the bid named by a subcontract's `awarded_bid_id` must sit on the same work package as the subcontract itself, and the bid named by an award's `bid_id` must belong to the package in the path; either mismatch is rejected with `ERR_WORK_PACKAGE_PROJECT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Creation

Three creation operations require the client to prove it priced its write against the parent row it actually read, by supplying the parent's `version` as a mandatory field. These fields are not optional and may not be omitted.

- Creating a progressive estimate requires `expected_project_version` to equal the target project's current `version`. If it does not, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new estimate for a milestone legitimately supersedes the prior non-reconciled estimate for that same milestone, so no state precondition blocks the second write — the stale-version check is what makes the supersede decision safe.
- Creating a base guaranteed-maximum-price proposal likewise requires `expected_project_version` to equal the project's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a newer base proposal supersedes the prior draft, under-review or returned proposal, a stale writer would otherwise silently discard pricing it never saw.
- Creating a subcontract requires `expected_work_package_version` to equal the target work package's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WORK_PACKAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A replacement subcontract written after a default supersedes the terminated one's committed scope, and the termination cascade rewrites the package's `awarded_amount`, `committed_amount` and `replacement_reserve_amount` behind the client's back; the version the client holds is the only evidence it priced against the current package.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a stale-version check.

### 2.11. Order of Precedence Among Validations

When a single request could fail several ways, the system reports the failure that comes first in the following order, and stops. The ordering matters because it decides which error identifier a client sees; a later check must never mask an earlier one.

1. Header presence and role recognition — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Endpoint-level role authority — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Action-level authority on the two role-partitioned shared endpoints described in §2.1 — also `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
4. Request-shape validation — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This covers required fields being present, enums taking declared values, money at no more than 2 decimal places and non-negative, unit rates and ratios at no more than 4 decimal places, datetimes parsing as ISO 8601 UTC instants, an interval's start being strictly before its end, and conditionally-required fields being present when their condition holds — `allowance_amount`, `allocated_units` and `unit_of_measure` when the item type is `ALLOWANCE`; `bond_amount` when `bond_required` is true; `void_reason` on a draw void; `recovered_amount` on a bond-claim settlement; and `adjustment_amount` on an audit adjustment.
5. Existence of identifiers in the path, resolved left to right, per §2.9.
6. Existence of identifiers in the request body, per §2.9.
7. Stale-version rejection on the three creation operations listed in §2.10.
8. State preconditions — the family of `409 Conflict` conditions detailed in §3, in this internal order: the project's `gmp_status` gates, then the per-entity status gates for estimates, proposals, work packages, bids, subcontracts, bond claims, change events, contingency draws, cost reports and the audit engagement, then `ERR_CLOSEOUT_ALREADY_SETTLED`, then `ERR_CONTINGENCY_FROZEN`, then `ERR_POSTURE_NOT_RECOVERED`.
9. Segregation-of-duties checks on the operator identifier — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
10. Open-book compliance — the disclosure gate `ERR_OPEN_BOOK_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED`, then the breakdown-arithmetic check `ERR_OPEN_BOOK_BREAKDOWN_MISMATCH`.
11. Referential-integrity rules — `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE`, then `ERR_WORK_PACKAGE_PROJECT_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_MILESTONE_RANK_REGRESSION`.
12. Temporal rules — `ERR_SOLICITATION_STILL_OPEN`, then `ERR_COST_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_PERFORMANCE_WINDOW_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.
13. Business-constraint rules — `ERR_VARIANCE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_ALLOWANCE_UNITS_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_ALLOWANCE_OVERRUN_UNAUTHORIZED`, then `ERR_ALLOWANCE_REVERSAL_INVALID`, then `ERR_NO_RECONCILED_COST_REPORT`, then `ERR_COST_REPORTS_NOT_RECONCILED`, then `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_CLOSED`.
14. Fiscal rules — `ERR_OWNER_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_CONTINGENCY_INSUFFICIENT`.

One consequence of this ordering deserves calling out. The rule that blocks money-moving operations on a project whose `closeout_status` has reached `SETTLEMENT_EXECUTED` (`ERR_CLOSEOUT_ALREADY_SETTLED`, HTTP 409) covers a settlement execution itself, opening a cost report, appending a cost line, and classifying a change event — but deliberately **not** the posting or voiding of a contingency draw. A settled project always carries `contingency_posture` equal to `FROZEN`, so those two operations are owned by the frozen-posture rule and report `ERR_CONTINGENCY_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) instead. Listing them under the settled-closeout rule would make `ERR_CONTINGENCY_FROZEN` unreachable.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Preconstruction: Progressive Estimating and Milestone Reconciliation

A `ProgramAdmin` opens the engagement by registering the CMAR project itself, supplying a project `name` and the `owner_org` of the owner organization holding the CMAR contract (each between 1 and 200 characters), together with the two contractual rates that will later price the guaranteed maximum price: `cm_fee_pct`, the construction manager's fee rate, which must be greater than 0 and no greater than 0.1500 at 4 decimal places, and `insurance_rate`, the builder's-risk rate, which must be at least 0 and no greater than 0.0500 at 4 decimal places. A lump-sum `precon_fee_amount` for preconstruction services may also be supplied; it must be at least 0.00, defaults to 0.00 when omitted, and is invoiced **outside** the guaranteed maximum price, so it never appears in any of the envelope identities. A freshly registered project arrives at `version` 1 with all four of its state axes at their initial values — `gmp_status` at `PRECONSTRUCTION`, `contingency_posture` at `OPEN`, `audit_status` at `NOT_REQUESTED`, `closeout_status` at `NOT_STARTED` — with `pending_amendment_count` at 0 and with every money field at 0.00.

Estimating then proceeds milestone by milestone. A `ConstructionManager` files a **progressive estimate** naming the `project_id` it belongs to, the `milestone_code` it prices, and the `submitted_cost_of_work` estimated at that milestone, which must be greater than 0 at 2 decimal places. The milestone codes, in ascending design maturity, are `SD`, `DD`, `CD_50`, `CD_100` and `GMP_BASIS`; the server stamps a `milestone_rank` on the row from a fixed ranking in which `SD` ranks 1, `DD` ranks 2, `CD_50` ranks 3, `CD_100` ranks 4 and `GMP_BASIS` ranks 5. The client may additionally declare a `prior_estimate_id` naming the predecessor it reconciled against, and may pre-supply a `justification_note`. Because a new estimate for a milestone displaces the previous one, the write also carries `expected_project_version` as described in §2.10, and a successful create increments the project's own `version` by 1.

**Milestone variance is computed at creation and recomputed at submission.** The server selects a reconciliation basis in exactly this order. If `prior_estimate_id` was supplied, that estimate is the basis and `variance_basis` is recorded as `DECLARED_PRIOR`. Otherwise the server gathers every other estimate of the same project whose status is `RECONCILED` and whose `milestone_rank` is strictly lower than the new estimate's rank; drafts, submissions, returns and superseded rows are never eligible as a basis even when their rank is lower. If that candidate set is empty, there is no basis, `variance_basis` is recorded as `NO_BASIS`, `variance_basis_estimate_id` stays null and `variance_pct` is 0.0000. If the set is non-empty, the candidate with the highest `milestone_rank` wins, ties among equal ranks being broken in favour of the latest `created_at`, and `variance_basis` is recorded as `HIGHEST_RECONCILED_MILESTONE`. Whenever a basis is selected, `variance_basis_estimate_id` records that basis row's identifier and `variance_pct` is the basis-relative signed ratio: the new estimate's `submitted_cost_of_work` less the basis estimate's `submitted_cost_of_work`, divided by the basis estimate's `submitted_cost_of_work`, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places.

A declared `prior_estimate_id` must have a `milestone_rank` strictly lower than the estimate being created. Declaring a predecessor at the same or a later design milestone is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONE_RANK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 422) — a milestone may not be priced against a later or equal milestone.

Filing a newer estimate for the same `project_id` and the same `milestone_code` supersedes its predecessor: any prior estimate of that project and milestone whose status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, has its `superseded_by_estimate_id` set to the new row's identifier, and has its `version` incremented. Estimates already at `RECONCILED` are never superseded; they remain the reconciliation basis for everything downstream.

Submission by the `ConstructionManager` advances the estimate `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and the same action re-entered from a returned estimate advances it `RETURNED → SUBMITTED` — a loop that may repeat without bound. Submission is refused from any other status with `ERR_ESTIMATE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Submission re-runs the variance computation against the current reconciled basis, writes a supplied `justification_note` onto the row, and then applies **the justification gate**: when the recomputed `variance_pct` is strictly greater than five percent — that is, strictly greater than 0.0500 — a non-empty `justification_note` must be present, and its absence is rejected with `ERR_VARIANCE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The comparison is strict on purpose. A variance of exactly 0.0500 does not require a justification, and a negative variance, where the milestone came in under its basis, never requires one.

Reconciliation is the owner's move. An `OwnerRepresentative` decides a submitted estimate with a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `RETURN`, optionally recording a `reconciliation_note` which is written onto the row in either case. `ACCEPT` advances the estimate `SUBMITTED → RECONCILED` and clears `reconciliation_stale` to false; `RETURN` advances it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, sending it back to the construction manager for revision. Either decision increments the estimate's `version`. Both decisions are permitted only from `SUBMITTED`; anything else is `ERR_ESTIMATE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The `reconciliation_stale` flag is not merely decorative — §3.6 describes the one cascade that raises it, marking every previously reconciled estimate of a project as needing a fresh look once the scope of the work has formally changed.

### 3.2. Establishing the Guaranteed Maximum Price

Once a milestone estimate has been reconciled, the `ConstructionManager` builds the **base guaranteed-maximum-price proposal** from it. The create names the `project_id`, the `based_on_estimate_id` of the reconciled estimate it is priced from, and the four money components the construction manager is proposing: `cost_of_work_total`, `general_conditions_total`, `owner_contingency_amount` and `construction_contingency_amount`. It also carries `expected_project_version` per §2.10. On a base row `cost_of_work_total` and `construction_contingency_amount` must both be greater than 0, while `general_conditions_total` and `owner_contingency_amount` need only be at least 0; all four carry 2 decimal places. A base proposal is minted with `proposal_type` recorded as `BASE`, `status` at `DRAFT`, `amount_delta` at 0.00 and `change_event_id` null, and the create increments the project's `version` by 1. If the named `based_on_estimate_id` does not name an estimate whose status is `RECONCILED`, the create is rejected with `ERR_ESTIMATE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); if the project's `gmp_status` is already `EXECUTED` or `CLOSED`, it is rejected with `ERR_GMP_ALREADY_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). As with estimates, a newer base proposal supersedes its predecessor: any prior base proposal of the same project whose status is `DRAFT`, `OWNER_REVIEW` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_by_proposal_id` set to the new row's identifier and its `version` incremented.

**The four-step pricing build-up.** The construction manager's fee is contractually a percentage of the cost of the work *including insurance*, while the insurance premium is a rate on the contract sum *including fee*. Read literally, those two definitions are mutually recursive, and the contract resolves the recursion by pinning one evaluation order — which is not the naive one. The server evaluates exactly four steps and rounds half-up to 2 decimal places at each of the first three:

1. Take the **base** as the proposal's `cost_of_work_total` plus its `general_conditions_total`. Compute a *provisional* fee as the project's `cm_fee_pct` multiplied by that base, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and retain it on the proposal as `provisional_fee_amount`. This figure exists only to gross up the insurance base; it is never itself a component of the guaranteed maximum price.
2. Compute `insurance_amount` as the project's `insurance_rate` multiplied by the sum of the base and `provisional_fee_amount`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places.
3. Compute the **binding** `cm_fee_amount` as the project's `cm_fee_pct` multiplied by the sum of the base and `insurance_amount`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The fee rate is applied to base-plus-insurance — not to base-plus-provisional-fee, and not to base-plus-insurance-plus-provisional-fee. The provisional fee is consumed by step 2 and then discarded.
4. Compute the proposal's `gmp_amount` as the exact sum of five already-rounded terms: the base, `insurance_amount`, `cm_fee_amount`, `owner_contingency_amount` and `construction_contingency_amount`.

Only the proposal's own four money inputs plus `cm_fee_pct` and `insurance_rate` from its project participate. No other proposal, estimate, work package or contingency draw enters the computation. A collapsed single-pass reading that omits insurance from the fee base produces a materially different number, which is exactly why the order is pinned.

While the proposal is still editable — that is, while its status is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` — the `ConstructionManager` builds out its qualification log as described in §3.3. Submitting the proposal, with an optional `cover_note`, advances it `DRAFT → OWNER_REVIEW`, and the same action re-entered after a return advances it `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → OWNER_REVIEW`; that revision loop may repeat without bound. Submitting from any other status is `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A base submission is additionally gated on the estimate behind `based_on_estimate_id` still being `RECONCILED`, and it drags the project's own axis along with it: `gmp_status` advances `PRECONSTRUCTION → GMP_UNDER_REVIEW` and the project's `version` increments. Submitting an amendment proposal instead requires that the change event behind it is `CLASSIFIED` with a classification of `SCOPE_CHANGE`.

The owner then decides, supplying a `decision` of `EXECUTE`, `RETURN` or `REJECT` together with an optional `owner_decision_note`, which is recorded on the row. Every decision is permitted only from `OWNER_REVIEW` and otherwise fails with `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); each increments the proposal's `version`.

A `RETURN` advances the proposal `OWNER_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, increments its `revision_count` by 1, and for a base proposal walks the project back `GMP_UNDER_REVIEW → PRECONSTRUCTION` so that the construction manager can revise its pricing while the project sits in preconstruction again.

A `REJECT` advances the proposal `OWNER_REVIEW → REJECTED` and is permitted only on an amendment proposal. Rejecting an amendment decrements the project's `pending_amendment_count` by 1 and recomputes `proposed_gmp_amount`, leaves the linked change event at `CLASSIFIED`, and draws no contingency at all — the estimated impact simply never becomes money.

An `EXECUTE` on a **base** proposal is the moment the guaranteed maximum price comes into existence. It requires the project's `gmp_status` to be `GMP_UNDER_REVIEW`; attempting it on a project already `EXECUTED` or `CLOSED` yields `ERR_GMP_ALREADY_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). The proposal advances `OWNER_REVIEW → EXECUTED`, the project advances `GMP_UNDER_REVIEW → EXECUTED`, and the proposal's figures are copied onto the project: `gmp_amount` from the proposal's `gmp_amount`, `cost_of_work_budget_total` from `cost_of_work_total`, `general_conditions_total` from `general_conditions_total`, `insurance_amount` from `insurance_amount`, and `cm_fee_amount` from `cm_fee_amount`. Both contingency pots are funded at once: `owner_contingency_original` and `owner_contingency_remaining` are both set to the proposal's `owner_contingency_amount`, and `construction_contingency_original` and `construction_contingency_remaining` are both set to the proposal's `construction_contingency_amount`, while `contingency_drawn_to_cost_total` is set to 0.00. The project's `proposed_gmp_amount` is then rolled up — with no pending amendments it equals the executed `gmp_amount` — the `contingency_posture` is re-derived (a fully funded construction pot yields `OPEN`), and the project's `version` increments by 1.

An `EXECUTE` on an **amendment** proposal requires the project's `gmp_status` to be `EXECUTED`. The proposal advances `OWNER_REVIEW → EXECUTED`, the project's `gmp_amount` increases by the proposal's signed `amount_delta` and its `cost_of_work_budget_total` increases by the same `amount_delta` — which is precisely why the seven-term envelope identity of §2.6 survives amendments — `pending_amendment_count` decrements by 1, and `proposed_gmp_amount` is recomputed. An amendment row never runs the four-step build-up: its `provisional_fee_amount`, `insurance_amount` and `cm_fee_amount` all stay 0.00, and its own `gmp_amount` is the project's `gmp_amount` at the amendment's creation time plus its `amount_delta`.

**The proposed-price rollup.** Whenever an amendment proposal is minted, decided or superseded, and on every base execution, the server rewrites two derived project fields together. `proposed_gmp_amount` becomes the project's `gmp_amount` plus the sum of `amount_delta` over every amendment proposal of that project whose status is `DRAFT`, `OWNER_REVIEW` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, and `pending_amendment_count` becomes the count of those same rows. Executed amendments are excluded because their delta has already been folded into `gmp_amount`; rejected and superseded amendments are excluded because their delta never will be. The sum is exact over already-rounded deltas and is held at 2 decimal places. Recomputing this pair is itself the cascade step that raises `reconciliation_stale` on the project's reconciled estimates, as §3.6 describes.

### 3.3. The Qualification Log: Assumptions, Allowances, Clarifications and Exclusions

A guaranteed maximum price is only meaningful alongside the qualifications that bound it, so every proposal carries a log of **qualification items** written by the `ConstructionManager` while the proposal is still editable. Each entry names its `gmp_proposal_id`, an `item_type` of `ASSUMPTION`, `ALLOWANCE`, `CLARIFICATION` or `EXCLUSION`, and the `text` of the qualification language as it will appear in the GMP exhibit, between 1 and 1000 characters. An entry may only be added while the parent proposal's status is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`; adding one to a proposal that is no longer editable is rejected with `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

An `ALLOWANCE` entry is special: it carries dollars *and* a quantity, and therefore behaves as a small ledger of its own. Three fields are required when — and only when — `item_type` is `ALLOWANCE`, and must be absent otherwise: `allowance_amount`, the carried allowance dollars, greater than 0 at 2 decimal places; `allocated_units`, the allowance quantity carried in the guaranteed price, greater than 0 at 2 decimal places; and `unit_of_measure`, the unit label for that quantity, such as `SF` or `EA`. From those two numbers the server derives `allowance_unit_rate` as `allowance_amount` divided by `allocated_units`, rounded half-up to 4 decimal places, and opens the ledger with `remaining_units` equal to `allocated_units`, `reconciled_units` at 0.00, `reconciled_amount` at 0.00, `variance_amount` at 0.00, `reconciliation_seq` at 0, `last_action` at `NONE` and `status` at `OPEN`. For every other item type the entire allowance ledger sits at zero — `allowance_unit_rate` at 0.0000 and `remaining_units` at 0.00 — and `status` stays `OPEN` permanently.

As the trade actually gets built, the `ConstructionManager` books actuals against the allowance by supplying an `action` of `RECONCILE` together with the `units` consumed and the `actual_unit_cost` those units were bought at, and optionally an `overrun_authorized` acknowledgement. A booking proceeds as follows: the requested `units` must not exceed the item's current `remaining_units`, and a request for more is rejected with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_UNITS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422); if the `actual_unit_cost` exceeds the item's `allowance_unit_rate`, the request must carry `overrun_authorized` set to true, acknowledging that the difference will be routed through a change event, and its absence is rejected with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_OVERRUN_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 422). Because a non-allowance item carries `remaining_units` of 0.00, any positive unit count booked against one necessarily fails the units check and is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_UNITS_EXCEEDED`.

A successful booking moves four fields together: `reconciled_units` increases by the booked units, `remaining_units` decreases by the same units, `reconciled_amount` increases by the booked dollars — the units multiplied by the `actual_unit_cost`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places — and `variance_amount` is rewritten as the item's `reconciled_amount` less the expected dollars at the carried rate, where the expected figure is `reconciled_units` multiplied by `allowance_unit_rate`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. A positive `variance_amount` means the allowance is overrunning. The booking also increments `reconciliation_seq` by 1 and records `last_action` as `RECONCILE`.

The compensating move supplies an `action` of `REVERSE`, which undoes the most recent booking and only the most recent booking: exactly the four field movements of that last `RECONCILE` are backed out, so `remaining_units` rises by the reversed units, `reconciled_units` falls by them, `reconciled_amount` falls by the reversed booking, and `variance_amount` is recomputed on the restored figures. A reversal requires `last_action` to be `RECONCILE` and `reconciliation_seq` to be at least 1; a reversal cannot itself be reversed, an untouched allowance cannot be reversed, and a non-allowance item cannot be reversed — all three are rejected with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_REVERSAL_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Crucially, `reconciliation_seq` is **not** decremented by a reversal: it counts every `RECONCILE` ever posted, and it is the sequence number a reversal names as the booking it undoes. `last_action` becomes `REVERSE`.

After either action, the item's `status` is re-derived from the ledger rather than transitioned by hand: it is `OPEN` when `reconciled_units` is 0, `FULLY_RECONCILED` when `reconciled_units` equals `allocated_units`, and `PARTIALLY_RECONCILED` in between. Bookings therefore drive `OPEN → PARTIALLY_RECONCILED`, `PARTIALLY_RECONCILED → FULLY_RECONCILED`, and — when a single call consumes every allocated unit at once — `OPEN → FULLY_RECONCILED`. Reversals drive `PARTIALLY_RECONCILED → OPEN`, `FULLY_RECONCILED → PARTIALLY_RECONCILED` when earlier bookings remain, and `FULLY_RECONCILED → OPEN` when the reversal undoes the only booking. The round trip `OPEN → FULLY_RECONCILED → OPEN` is reachable and must leave the ledger exactly whole. **The invariant, asserted after every action of either kind, is that `allocated_units` equals `remaining_units` plus `reconciled_units`.** Every action of either kind increments the item's `version` by 1.

### 3.4. Trade Buyout: Packages, Sealed Bids and Open-Book Disclosure

Buyout is organised into **work packages**, one per trade. A `ConstructionManager` creates a package naming its `project_id`, its `trade_division` — a CSI trade division label such as `03-CONCRETE`, between 1 and 60 characters — and the `budget_amount` carried for that package inside the cost of work, greater than 0 at 2 decimal places. A `self_perform_intent` flag may be supplied, defaulting to false, marking that the construction manager intends to bid the package with its own forces. A new package begins with `procurement_status` at `BUDGETED`, `transparency_status` at `SEALED`, `awarded_bid_id` null, `awarded_amount` at 0.00, `committed_amount` at 0.00, `replacement_reserve_amount` at 0.00, `bid_count` at 0, and both `solicitation_opens_at` and `solicitation_closes_at` null.

**Two axes, moving independently.** A package's `procurement_status` tracks buyout progress through `BUDGETED`, `SOLICITING`, `AWARDED`, `REPROCUREMENT` and finally `CLOSED`. Its `transparency_status` tracks open-book disclosure through `SEALED`, `DISCLOSED` and `AUDITED`. Neither axis constrains the other's position: a package may be `DISCLOSED` while still `BUDGETED`, or while already `CLOSED`.

Opening the bid market advances the package `BUDGETED → SOLICITING`, and the `ConstructionManager` supplies both `solicitation_opens_at` and `solicitation_closes_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, which are recorded on the package; the close must be strictly after the open, and a window that is not is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The project must have reached at least owner review of its base price — `gmp_status` must be `GMP_UNDER_REVIEW` or `EXECUTED` — and a project still in `PRECONSTRUCTION`, or already `CLOSED`, is rejected with `ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). The same action is also how a package that has fallen into `REPROCUREMENT` after a subcontractor default reopens its market, advancing `REPROCUREMENT → SOLICITING` with a fresh window; from any other `procurement_status` the request is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Bids arrive as sealed envelopes. A `ConstructionManager` submits a bid against a package that is `SOLICITING`, naming the `work_package_id`, the `bidder_type` — `CM_SELF_PERFORM` for the construction manager's own forces or `THIRD_PARTY` for the open market — the `bidder_name` of the legal bidding entity between 1 and 200 characters, the lump-sum `base_bid_amount` greater than 0 at 2 decimal places, and the `open_book_cost_breakdown`. That breakdown is an object of five components — `labor`, `material`, `equipment`, `overhead` and `profit` — and it is required when `bidder_type` is `CM_SELF_PERFORM` and must be null when `bidder_type` is `THIRD_PARTY`. **The five components must sum exactly to `base_bid_amount`**; any discrepancy is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_BOOK_BREAKDOWN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A bid is minted at `SEALED` with `prepared_by_user_id` taken from the submitting operator's `X-User-ID`, `opened_by_user_id` null and `opened_at` null; the parent package's `bid_count` increases by 1 and its `version` increments. Submitting a bid against a package that is not `SOLICITING` is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A sealed bid's breakdown is suppressed on read: `open_book_cost_breakdown` is populated in responses only for bids whose status is `OPENED`, `AWARDED` or `NOT_AWARDED`, and reads as null while the bid is still `SEALED`.

Disclosure is the owner's window into that breakdown, and it is a two-step ratchet driven by a `target_status` and an optional `disclosure_note`. A `ProcurementOfficer` publishes the cost breakdowns to the owner by naming `target_status` `DISCLOSED`, advancing the package `SEALED → DISCLOSED`; this requires the package to have attracted at least one bid, that is `bid_count` of 1 or more. An `IndependentAuditor` then certifies them by naming `target_status` `AUDITED`, advancing `DISCLOSED → AUDITED`. Neither step may be skipped and neither may be regressed — a request to reach `AUDITED` from `SEALED`, or to return to `SEALED` from anywhere, is rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — and the package's `procurement_status` is never touched by either step. Each step increments the package's `version`.

Opening a sealed bid is reserved to the `ProcurementOfficer`, who may attach an `open_note`. Four conditions guard it. The bid must be `SEALED`, and a bid in any other status is rejected with `ERR_BID_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The logical clock must have reached the package's `solicitation_closes_at`, with an inclusive boundary — a clock reading exactly equal to the close instant is permitted — and an earlier reading is rejected with `ERR_SOLICITATION_STILL_OPEN` (HTTP 409). A bid whose `bidder_type` is `CM_SELF_PERFORM` may only be opened once the package's `transparency_status` has reached `DISCLOSED` or `AUDITED`, and opening one while the package is still `SEALED` is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_BOOK_DISCLOSURE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); third-party bids are exempt, because the gate exists specifically for the case where the construction manager is bidding against the trades it also administers. Finally, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the bid's `prepared_by_user_id`, per §2.8. A successful open advances the bid `SEALED → OPENED`, records `opened_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `opened_at` from the logical clock, and increments the bid's `version`.

Award is likewise the `ProcurementOfficer`'s call. The request names the winning `bid_id` and may attach an `award_note`. The package must be `SOLICITING` — or `REPROCUREMENT`, when a replacement is being selected after a default — the named bid must be `OPENED`, and the logical clock must have reached the package's `solicitation_closes_at`. A bid that is not `OPENED` is rejected with `ERR_BID_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), a bid belonging to a different package with `ERR_WORK_PACKAGE_PROJECT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and a package in the wrong `procurement_status` with `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); the project must also be `EXECUTED`, failing which the award is rejected with `ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). The award advances the package `SOLICITING → AWARDED`, writes `awarded_bid_id` from the supplied `bid_id` and `awarded_amount` from that bid's `base_bid_amount`, advances the winning bid `OPENED → AWARDED`, and moves **every other** bid on that package that is currently `OPENED` to `NOT_AWARDED`. Both buyout-savings figures on the project are then recomputed as described in §3.8, and the package's and the project's `version` counters each increment by 1. When the award is instead the replacement selection that advances the package `REPROCUREMENT → AWARDED`, it additionally clears `replacement_reserve_amount` back to 0.00; that cycle may repeat once for each successive default.

### 3.5. Subcontracts, Default, and the Surety

With a bid awarded, the `ConstructionManager` writes the **subcontract**. The write names the `work_package_id` it executes under, the `awarded_bid_id` it is written from, the `subcontractor_name` between 1 and 200 characters, the `contract_amount` inside the guaranteed maximum price greater than 0 at 2 decimal places, the on-site performance window as `performance_start` and `performance_end` (ISO 8601 UTC instants bounding a **closed**, inclusive interval, with the end strictly after the start), a `bond_required` boolean defaulting to false, and `expected_work_package_version` per §2.10. When `bond_required` is true a `bond_amount` greater than 0 must accompany it, giving the face amount of the performance bond; when `bond_required` is false the `bond_amount` is 0.00. The project must be `EXECUTED`, failing which the write is rejected with `ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). The named `awarded_bid_id` must reference a bid whose status is already `AWARDED` — a losing `NOT_AWARDED` bid on the same package is rejected with `ERR_BID_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — and that bid's own `work_package_id` must equal the subcontract's, a mismatch drawing `ERR_WORK_PACKAGE_PROJECT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

**Mobilization buffer.** A new subcontract's closed performance window must be separated from every *blocking* sibling window on the same work package by at least 72 hours — three full days — of clear time on whichever side it falls. Blocking siblings are those whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE`, `DEFAULT_NOTICED` or `COMPLETED`. Siblings at `TERMINATED_FOR_DEFAULT` are deliberately non-blocking, so that a reprocurement can reuse the window the defaulted trade vacated. Any window that overlaps a blocking sibling, or that clears it by less than 72 hours, is rejected with `ERR_PERFORMANCE_WINDOW_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409).

A new subcontract begins with `contract_status` at `ACTIVE`, `paid_to_date` at 0.00, `unpaid_committed_amount` equal to its `contract_amount`, `replacement_cost_amount` at 0.00, `bond_claim_amount` at 0.00, `bond_recovered_amount` at 0.00, `default_notice_at` null and `cure_count` at 0. Its **surety axis branches on the creation payload**: when `bond_required` is true the `bond_claim_status` is initialised to `NONE`, meaning a claim is possible but has not been filed; when `bond_required` is false it is initialised to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, a terminal dead axis on which no bond-claim action will ever be accepted. The write also recounts the parent package's `committed_amount` and increments the package's `version` by 1. That recount is defined once and used everywhere: a package's `committed_amount` is the sum of `contract_amount` over its subcontracts whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE`, `DEFAULT_NOTICED` or `COMPLETED`, plus the sum of `paid_to_date` over its subcontracts whose `contract_status` is `TERMINATED_FOR_DEFAULT`.

Monthly progress is applied by the `ConstructionManager` supplying a `payment_amount`, which increases the subcontract's `paid_to_date` by that amount and rewrites `unpaid_committed_amount` as `contract_amount` less `paid_to_date` — reducing, payment by payment, the exposure a default would have to replace. A payment is accepted only while the subcontract is `ACTIVE` or `DEFAULT_NOTICED`; against a terminated or completed subcontract it is rejected with `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

The contract-performance axis is driven by an `action` of `NOTICE_DEFAULT`, `CURE`, `TERMINATE_FOR_DEFAULT` or `COMPLETE`, with an optional `default_reason` or `cure_note` recorded alongside. Noticing a default advances `ACTIVE → DEFAULT_NOTICED` and stamps `default_notice_at` from the logical clock. Curing advances `DEFAULT_NOTICED → ACTIVE`, increments `cure_count` by 1 and clears `default_notice_at` back to null; that loop may repeat without bound and `cure_count` records how many times it did. Completing advances `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` and requires that `paid_to_date` has reached `contract_amount` exactly. Each of these actions is permitted only from the status named, and any other combination is rejected with `ERR_SUBCONTRACT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Each increments the subcontract's `version`.

**Termination for default is the head of a five-level cascade**, and it is the single most consequential operation in the system. It is permitted only from `DEFAULT_NOTICED` and only while the project is `EXECUTED`. It runs as follows.

1. On the subcontract itself, `unpaid_committed_amount` is frozen at `contract_amount` less `paid_to_date`, and `replacement_cost_amount` is written as that frozen unpaid amount multiplied by 1.15 — a fifteen-percent replacement premium — rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The **replacement premium** is `replacement_cost_amount` less `unpaid_committed_amount`, and it is the premium, not the whole replacement cost, that the guaranteed price must absorb, because the unpaid committed amount was already sitting inside the cost of work. The subcontract advances `DEFAULT_NOTICED → TERMINATED_FOR_DEFAULT`. If `bond_required` is true, the `bond_claim_status` stays exactly where it was at `NONE` — termination does not file a claim, and the claim must still be filed explicitly.
2. The parent work package advances `AWARDED → REPROCUREMENT`. Its `replacement_reserve_amount` is set to the subcontract's `replacement_cost_amount`; its `committed_amount` drops by the terminated subcontract's `unpaid_committed_amount`, which is exactly what the recount rule above yields once the terminated row contributes only its `paid_to_date`; and its `awarded_amount` increases by the replacement premium.
3. Because `awarded_amount` moved, both buyout-savings figures on the project are recomputed per §3.8.
4. Because `awarded_amount` moved, a contingency draw is minted for the premium: `contingency_type` `CONSTRUCTION`, `origin` `SUBCONTRACTOR_DEFAULT_PREMIUM`, `amount` equal to the replacement premium, `requires_owner_approval` true, `source_subcontract_id` naming the terminated subcontract and `change_event_id` null — and, uniquely among draws, it is minted **already at** `POSTED` with `posted_at` stamped from the logical clock. Posting it moves `construction_contingency_remaining` down by the premium and `contingency_drawn_to_cost_total` up by the premium, leaving `gmp_amount` untouched exactly as §2.6 requires.
5. Because the contingency ledger moved, `contingency_posture` is re-derived, and if it lands on `CONSTRAINED` every cost report of the project whose status is `OPEN` is held at `RECONCILIATION_HELD` with the reason `CONSTRUCTION_CONTINGENCY_CONSTRAINED`.

A single termination therefore increments the subcontract's `version`, the work package's `version` and the project's `version`, plus a `version` on each held cost report.

**The surety axis moves entirely independently of the contract axis.** A `ConstructionManager` files the claim with an `action` of `FILE` and a `claim_amount`, which must not exceed the subcontract's `bond_amount`; filing requires `bond_required` to be true — so the axis is at `NONE` rather than `NOT_APPLICABLE` — and requires the `contract_status` to be `TERMINATED_FOR_DEFAULT`. It advances `NONE → FILED` and records `bond_claim_amount` from the supplied `claim_amount`. An `OwnerRepresentative` then acknowledges the claim with `ACKNOWLEDGE`, advancing `FILED → ACKNOWLEDGED`. From there the owner either settles or denies. `DENY` advances `ACKNOWLEDGED → DENIED`, and is also permitted directly from `FILED`, advancing `FILED → DENIED`; an optional `denial_reason` may be recorded, `bond_recovered_amount` stays at 0.00, and no contingency credit occurs — the construction contingency simply stays depleted. Any action attempted from a status that does not permit it, and any action at all against a `NOT_APPLICABLE` axis, is rejected with `ERR_BOND_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). This endpoint never touches `contract_status`: a denied claim leaves the contract `TERMINATED_FOR_DEFAULT`, and a settled claim does not resurrect it.

`SETTLE` advances `ACKNOWLEDGED → SETTLED` and carries a `recovered_amount` that must not exceed the `bond_claim_amount`. The subcontract's `bond_recovered_amount` is written as the lesser of the supplied `recovered_amount` and the `bond_claim_amount`. The server then looks for the at-most-one contingency draw of that project whose `source_subcontract_id` names this subcontract, whose `origin` is `SUBCONTRACTOR_DEFAULT_PREMIUM` and whose status is `POSTED`. If no such draw exists the credit is 0.00; otherwise the credit is the lesser of `bond_recovered_amount` and that draw's `amount`. The credit is added to the project's `construction_contingency_remaining` and subtracted from its `contingency_drawn_to_cost_total`, so the envelope identity of §2.6 is preserved exactly — the surety's money refills the construction manager's contingency rather than reducing the guaranteed maximum price. Capping the credit at the posted draw amount is what prevents an over-recovery from manufacturing contingency the project never spent. Afterwards `contingency_posture` is re-derived, and a settlement is one of only two paths in the entire system that can move it `CONSTRAINED → OPEN`. A settlement increments both the subcontract's `version` and the project's `version`.

### 3.6. Change Events and the Cost-Responsibility Fork

Every cost impact that arises after the guaranteed maximum price is executed enters the system as a **change event**, and the whole point of the record is that *who bears the cost* is a reviewed determination rather than something the claimant asserts. A `ConstructionManager` logs the event against a `project_id` with a `description` between 1 and 1000 characters, an `estimated_amount` greater than 0 at 2 decimal places giving the construction manager's estimated cost impact, and an `origin` declaring what gave rise to it — one of `OWNER_DIRECTIVE`, `DESIGN_DEVELOPMENT`, `FIELD_CONDITION`, `SUBCONTRACTOR_DEFAULT` or `ALLOWANCE_OVERRUN`. Two optional links may be supplied: a `qualification_item_id` naming the assumption, allowance, clarification or exclusion the event relies on, which must belong to a proposal of the same project, and a `source_subcontract_id` naming the defaulted subcontract the event arises from, which must belong to a work package of the same project; either cross-project link is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). A new event is minted with `status` at `DRAFT`, `classification` deliberately null, `revision_count` at 0, and `submitted_by_user_id`, `classified_by_user_id`, `classified_at` and `review_note` all null.

Submission, with an optional `submit_note`, advances the event `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` when it is a resubmission after a revision request. Submission requires the project's `gmp_status` to be `EXECUTED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409), and it requires — when the event's `origin` is `ALLOWANCE_OVERRUN` — that its `qualification_item_id` names a qualification item whose `item_type` is `ALLOWANCE`. Every submission overwrites `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, which is the anchor of the segregation-of-duties rule at classification time; a resubmission by a different operator therefore moves the anchor. Submission from any status other than `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_CHANGE_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Owner review actions that do *not* decide cost responsibility are driven by an `action` of `BEGIN_REVIEW`, `REQUEST_REVISION` or `REJECT`, with an optional `review_note`. An `OwnerRepresentative` beginning review advances the event `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and records the supplied `review_note` on the row. Requesting a revision advances `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and increments `revision_count` by 1; the loop from submission through review to revision request and back to submission may repeat without bound. Rejecting advances `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, and nothing downstream is minted — no amendment proposal and no contingency draw. Each review action is permitted only from the status named and otherwise draws `ERR_CHANGE_EVENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and each increments the event's `version`.

**Classification is the fork.** It is shared between `OwnerRepresentative` and `ConstructionManager`, precisely so that either side of the contract may make the determination, and it carries a `classification` plus an optional `classification_note`. It is permitted only from `UNDER_REVIEW` (otherwise `ERR_CHANGE_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409), only while the project is `EXECUTED` (otherwise `ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED`, HTTP 409), only before the project's `closeout_status` reaches `SETTLEMENT_EXECUTED` (otherwise `ERR_CLOSEOUT_ALREADY_SETTLED`, HTTP 409), and only by an operator whose `X-User-ID` differs from the event's `submitted_by_user_id` (otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403). Whichever branch is taken, the event advances `UNDER_REVIEW → CLASSIFIED`, its `classification` is written from the payload, `classified_by_user_id` is recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`, `classified_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and both the event's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1. The three branches then diverge completely.

`SCOPE_CHANGE` means the owner pays, via a formal amendment to the guaranteed maximum price. An amendment proposal is minted with `proposal_type` `AMENDMENT`, `status` `DRAFT`, `based_on_estimate_id` null, `change_event_id` naming this event, `amount_delta` equal to the event's `estimated_amount`, and its own `gmp_amount` equal to the project's current `gmp_amount` plus that `amount_delta`. Its four proposed money components — `cost_of_work_total`, `general_conditions_total`, `owner_contingency_amount` and `construction_contingency_amount` — are all 0.00, as are `provisional_fee_amount`, `insurance_amount` and `cm_fee_amount`, because the entire impact is carried by `amount_delta`. Minting that row is itself the trigger for the rollup of §3.2, which increments the project's `pending_amendment_count` by 1 and rewrites `proposed_gmp_amount`. And that rewrite is itself the trigger for a third-level cascade: **every** progressive estimate of the project whose status is `RECONCILED` has `reconciliation_stale` raised to true, because the scope those estimates were reconciled against has formally moved. No contingency is drawn on this branch. Because the amendment lands at `DRAFT`, it must still be submitted and then executed by the owner before the envelope actually moves; listing proposals filtered by `change_event_id` is the only discovery channel for a cascade-minted amendment.

`CONTINGENCY_USE_OWNER` means the owner's contingency absorbs the cost without changing the guaranteed price. A contingency draw is minted with `contingency_type` `OWNER`, `origin` `CHANGE_EVENT_CLASSIFICATION`, `status` `REQUESTED`, `amount` equal to the event's `estimated_amount`, `change_event_id` naming this event, `source_subcontract_id` null, and `requires_owner_approval` set to true unconditionally — it is the owner's money, so the owner always signs for it. No amendment proposal is minted, and the project's `pending_amendment_count` and `proposed_gmp_amount` are untouched.

`CONTINGENCY_USE_CONSTRUCTION` means the construction manager absorbs the cost inside the guaranteed price. A contingency draw is minted with `contingency_type` `CONSTRUCTION`, `origin` `CHANGE_EVENT_CLASSIFICATION`, `status` `REQUESTED`, `amount` equal to the event's `estimated_amount`, `change_event_id` naming this event and `source_subcontract_id` null, with `requires_owner_approval` determined by the approval rule in §3.7 rather than being forced true. No amendment proposal is minted and the guaranteed maximum price does not move at all.

### 3.7. The Contingency Ledger: Draws, Approval, Posting and Compensation

There is deliberately **no creation endpoint for a contingency draw.** Every draw in the system is minted by a cascade — either the classification of a change event onto one of the two contingency branches described in §3.6, or the termination-for-default cascade described in §3.5 — and listing draws, filterable by `project_id`, by the `change_event_id` that produced them, by the `source_subcontract_id` that produced them, by `contingency_type`, by `origin` and by `status`, is the only channel through which a client discovers one.

**Whether the owner must sign is decided once, at mint time.** If the draw's `contingency_type` is `OWNER`, `requires_owner_approval` is true unconditionally. If it is `CONSTRUCTION`, `requires_owner_approval` is true when any one of three conditions holds: the draw's `amount` is strictly greater than 50,000.00; or the project's `contingency_posture` at mint time is `CONSTRAINED`; or the draw's `origin` is `SUBCONTRACTOR_DEFAULT_PREMIUM`. Otherwise it is false. The threshold comparison is strict, on 2-decimal money: a construction draw of exactly 50,000.00 on an `OPEN`-posture project arising from a change-event classification does not require approval, while a draw of 50,000.01 does.

An `OwnerRepresentative` decides a requested draw with an `approve` boolean and an optional `approval_note`, on a project whose `gmp_status` is `EXECUTED`. Approving advances the draw `REQUESTED → OWNER_APPROVED` and records `approved_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. Declining advances it `REQUESTED → DENIED` with no contingency movement whatsoever. Either decision is permitted only from `REQUESTED`, and any other starting status is rejected with `ERR_DRAW_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); either increments the draw's `version`.

Posting is the `ConstructionManager`'s move, with an optional `post_note`, and it is the moment the money actually leaves the pot. It is permitted from `REQUESTED` only when `requires_owner_approval` is false, and otherwise from `OWNER_APPROVED`; from any other status it draws `ERR_DRAW_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Four further conditions apply. The project must be `EXECUTED`, failing which the post is rejected with `ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). The project's `contingency_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, and a frozen posture is rejected with `ERR_CONTINGENCY_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). A post attempted straight from `REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_OWNER_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) both when the draw's `requires_owner_approval` is true and, independently, whenever the posture is `CONSTRAINED` — a constrained project forces every draw down the owner-approved path regardless of how the flag was computed at mint time. And the draw's `amount` must not exceed the remaining balance of its own pot: `owner_contingency_remaining` for an `OWNER` draw, `construction_contingency_remaining` for a `CONSTRUCTION` draw. Overdrawing a pot would breach the guaranteed maximum price and is rejected with `ERR_CONTINGENCY_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

A successful post advances the draw `REQUESTED → POSTED` or `OWNER_APPROVED → POSTED` with identical ledger effects in both cases: `posted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the drawn pot's remaining balance decreases by the draw's `amount`, and the project's `contingency_drawn_to_cost_total` increases by the same `amount`, leaving `gmp_amount` unmoved. The `contingency_posture` is then re-derived, and when it lands on `CONSTRAINED` every cost report of the project sitting at `OPEN` is held. Both the draw's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1.

Voiding is the compensating action, reserved to the `OwnerRepresentative`, and it requires a `void_reason`. It is permitted only from `POSTED` (otherwise `ERR_DRAW_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409), only while the project's `closeout_status` has not reached `SETTLEMENT_EXECUTED`, and only while the posture is not `FROZEN` (otherwise `ERR_CONTINGENCY_FROZEN`, HTTP 409). The void advances the draw `POSTED → VOIDED`, stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock, records the supplied `void_reason`, returns the draw's `amount` to its pot's remaining balance and subtracts the same `amount` from `contingency_drawn_to_cost_total` — restoring both fields, and therefore all seven terms of the envelope identity, to exactly their pre-posting values. The posture is re-derived afterwards and may move `CONSTRAINED → OPEN`; as §2.7 insists, cost reports already at `RECONCILIATION_HELD` are not released by that recovery and must be released explicitly. Both the draw's `version` and the project's `version` increment by 1.

### 3.8. Monthly Cost Reporting, Per-Line Rounding and Buyout Savings

Open-book cost reporting is how the construction manager evidences what the work actually cost, and it is the input to every savings figure the contract turns on. The `ConstructionManager` opens a monthly report against a `project_id` over a **half-open** period `[period_start, period_end)`: `period_start` is an inclusive ISO 8601 UTC instant and `period_end` is the exclusive end, which must be strictly after `period_start`. Opening a report requires the project's `gmp_status` to be `EXECUTED` (`ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED`, HTTP 409) and is refused once the project's `closeout_status` has reached `SETTLEMENT_EXECUTED` (`ERR_CLOSEOUT_ALREADY_SETTLED`, HTTP 409). The new period must not overlap the period of **any** existing report of the same project in **any** status, including reports already at `RECONCILED`, `DISPUTED` or `RECONCILIATION_HELD`; because the interval is half-open, **adjacency is explicitly allowed** — a period whose `period_start` equals another period's `period_end` abuts it and is not an overlap, so consecutive monthly periods tile without gaps. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_COST_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A new report is minted with `status` `OPEN`, an empty `lines` array, `line_count` 0, all three of `reported_cost_total`, `undisputed_cost_total` and `disputed_cost_total` at 0.00, `buyout_savings_to_date` 0.00, and `held_reason`, `reconciled_at` and `reconciliation_note` all null.

Cost lines are appended one at a time by the `ConstructionManager`, each carrying a `cost_code` identifying the cost classification, a `quantity`, a `unit_rate`, a `markup_pct`, and optionally a `work_package_id` tying the line to a bought-out trade package, a `qualification_item_id` tying it to an allowance or assumption, and a `disputed` flag. The appended line object exposes `line_seq` set to the report's `line_count` plus 1, together with `work_package_id`, `quantity`, `unit_rate`, `markup_pct`, the computed `line_amount` and `disputed`. Any `work_package_id` on a line must reference a work package of the same project, and a cross-project reference is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PROJECT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). Appending a line is permitted from `OPEN` or `DISPUTED`, and is refused after settlement with `ERR_CLOSEOUT_ALREADY_SETTLED` (HTTP 409). The `lines` array is **append-only**: there is no line-deletion capability anywhere in the system, so a mistaken line is corrected by appending an offsetting one.

**The rounding order on a cost line is normative and is not an implementation detail.** Each line's extended amount is computed and rounded to 2 decimal places, half-up, *before it enters any total*:

`line_amount = round(quantity × unit_rate × (1 + markup_pct), 2)`

The report's `reported_cost_total` is then the sum of `line_amount` over every line, `undisputed_cost_total` is the sum of `line_amount` over lines whose `disputed` is false, `disputed_cost_total` is the sum over lines whose `disputed` is true, and `line_count` is the number of entries in `lines`. Summing the exact unrounded products and rounding once at the end produces a different number, and that number is wrong. Every subtotal the response exposes must be a sum of the **same** rounded `line_amount` values the response exposes, which guarantees the invariant that `reported_cost_total` equals `undisputed_cost_total` plus `disputed_cost_total` exactly, with no residual cent. All four figures are recomputed from the rounded line amounts on every append, and the report's `version` increments by 1.

The `OwnerRepresentative` closes out a monthly report with a `decision` of either `RECONCILE` or `DISPUTE`, plus an optional `reconciliation_note`. A `RECONCILE` decision is permitted from `OPEN` or from `DISPUTED` — the latter being how a dispute is resolved after the parties settle it — and requires the report's `line_count` to be at least 1; it advances the report to `RECONCILED` and stamps `reconciled_at` from the logical clock. A `DISPUTE` decision is permitted only from `OPEN` and only when the report's `disputed_cost_total` is strictly greater than 0.00 — there is nothing to dispute on a report with no disputed lines — and advances the report to `DISPUTED`. **Either** decision writes `reconciliation_note` from the payload and snapshots the project's `buyout_savings_trade_level` onto the report's `buyout_savings_to_date`, freezing the savings picture as at the moment the report was decided; a later award that changes the project figure does not retrospectively alter a decided report's snapshot. A report sitting at `RECONCILIATION_HELD` accepts neither new lines nor a reconcile decision, and any action attempted from a status that does not permit it is rejected with `ERR_COST_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

Releasing a held report is the `OwnerRepresentative`'s move, with an optional `release_note`. It is permitted only from `RECONCILIATION_HELD` (`ERR_COST_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409) and only once the project's `contingency_posture` has recovered to `OPEN`; a release attempted while the posture is still `CONSTRAINED` or `FROZEN` is rejected with `ERR_POSTURE_NOT_RECOVERED` (HTTP 409). The release returns the report to `OPEN`, clears `held_reason` to null, and increments the report's `version` by 1. This completes the `OPEN → RECONCILIATION_HELD → OPEN` cycle described in §2.7, and it is worth restating that the two directions are asymmetric by design: the freeze cascades automatically to every `OPEN` report the moment the posture constrains, while the thaw is manual, one report at a time.

**Buyout savings are published two different ways from identical data.** For each work package of the project, the per-package delta is its `budget_amount` minus its `awarded_amount`, so a positive delta is an underrun. Only packages whose `procurement_status` is `AWARDED`, `REPROCUREMENT` or `CLOSED` are included; packages still at `BUDGETED` or `SOLICITING` are excluded outright, because nothing has been bought out yet. The **trade-level** figure (the `TRADE_LEVEL` variant) floors each package at zero before summing — it is the sum of the greater of the package delta and 0.00 across the included packages — and is written to the project's `buyout_savings_trade_level`. This is the number reported to the owner trade by trade: a package that came in over budget is treated as a separate problem, not as a reduction of the savings already banked on packages that came in under. The **project-net** figure (the `PROJECT_NET` variant) sums every included package delta signed, so overruns offset underruns and the result may be negative; it is written to the project's `buyout_savings_project_net`. Both are money at 2 decimal places, half-up. Both are recomputed on every award, on every subcontract execution, and on every termination-for-default cascade that rewrites a package's `awarded_amount`. The distinction matters at settlement: it is the project-net figure — never the trade-level one — that the shared-savings settlement audits against, while it is the trade-level figure that is snapshotted onto each monthly report.

### 3.9. Audit Rights, Shared Savings and Settlement

The owner's audit rights over the open-book records run on the project's `audit_status`, which begins at `NOT_REQUESTED`. An `OwnerRepresentative` exercises those rights with an optional `audit_scope_note`, moving `audit_status` `NOT_REQUESTED → IN_PROGRESS`. Two preconditions apply: the project's `gmp_status` must be `EXECUTED` (`ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED`, HTTP 409), and at least one cost report of the project must be at `RECONCILED` — there must be something to audit, and an empty reconciled set is rejected with `ERR_NO_RECONCILED_COST_REPORT` (HTTP 422) rather than opening an audit over nothing. A request from any status other than `NOT_REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

The `IndependentAuditor` — and only that role — works the open audit with an `action` of `POST_ADJUSTMENT` or `CLOSE`. A `POST_ADJUSTMENT`, permitted only while `audit_status` is `IN_PROGRESS`, carries an `adjustment_amount` and an optional `adjustment_note`, and adds the amount **signed** to the project's `audit_adjustment_total`. The `adjustment_amount` is the one deliberate exception to the non-negative money rule of §2.5: an auditor may post a negative adjustment to strike disallowed cost, and `audit_adjustment_total` is therefore a signed running sum that may itself be negative. A `CLOSE`, also permitted only from `IN_PROGRESS`, moves `audit_status` `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED` and freezes `audit_adjustment_total` at its accumulated value; no further adjustments are accepted afterwards. Either action from a status that does not permit it is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and each increments the project's `version` by 1.

Shared savings are computed by the `OwnerRepresentative` under a declared `basis` of `PRE_AUDIT_PROVISIONAL` or `POST_AUDIT_FINAL`, together with an `execute` boolean that decides whether the figures are merely stored or the settlement is actually written. Both bases require the project's `gmp_status` to be `EXECUTED` (`ERR_GMP_NOT_EXECUTED`, HTTP 409) and both require at least one cost report of the project at `RECONCILED`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_RECONCILED_COST_REPORT` (HTTP 422); a project with no reconciled reports is never settled as though the entire guaranteed maximum price had been saved.

The computation is a five-step build-up, identical under both bases apart from how the settled cost is assembled:

1. The **recoverable base** is the project's `gmp_amount` minus its `cm_fee_amount` minus its `insurance_amount`. Fee and insurance are earned regardless of performance, so they are carved out of the envelope that is capable of being underrun.
2. The **settled cost** is assembled per basis, as described below.
3. The **savings pool** is the recoverable base minus the settled cost. If that pool is less than or equal to 0.00, the pool, the construction manager's share and the owner's share are all recorded as 0.00 and the computation stops immediately — no tier is selected, no cap is evaluated, and an overrun never produces a negative share for either party.
4. Otherwise the **savings percentage** is the pool divided by the project's `gmp_amount`, rounded to 4 decimal places half-up. Note that the denominator here is the full `gmp_amount`, not the recoverable base. The percentage is **rounded before the tier comparison**, and both tier boundaries are non-strict on their upper edge: a percentage of at most 0.02 selects a 25% construction-manager rate; otherwise a percentage of at most 0.05 selects 35%; anything above 0.05 selects 50%.
5. The uncapped construction-manager share is the selected rate times the pool, rounded to 2 decimal places half-up. The **cap** is 25% of the project's `cm_fee_amount`, rounded to 2 decimal places half-up. The construction manager's share is the lesser of the uncapped share and the cap, and the owner's share is the pool minus the construction manager's share — so the owner absorbs whatever the cap withholds, and the two shares always add back to the pool exactly.

Under `PRE_AUDIT_PROVISIONAL`, the settled cost is the sum of `reported_cost_total` over the project's cost reports whose status is `RECONCILED` or `DISPUTED`. Disputed dollars are counted **against** the construction manager — treated as real cost — and `audit_adjustment_total` is ignored entirely. This is the deliberately conservative reading, and it understates the savings the construction manager may claim. It may be run while the audit is anywhere at all, including `NOT_REQUESTED`, and while cost reports remain `OPEN`, `DISPUTED` or held. It writes `provisional_savings_pool`, `provisional_cm_savings_share` and `provisional_owner_savings_share`, and moves `closeout_status` `NOT_STARTED → PROVISIONAL_COMPUTED`. It may also be re-run from `PROVISIONAL_COMPUTED` back to `PROVISIONAL_COMPUTED` as further reports reconcile, overwriting the three provisional figures with the fresh calculation each time.

Under `POST_AUDIT_FINAL`, the settled cost is the sum of `undisputed_cost_total` over the project's cost reports whose status is `RECONCILED`, **plus** the project's `audit_adjustment_total`. Disputed line amounts drop out of the cost base entirely and the auditor's signed adjustment is added back in. Because both the pool and therefore the selected tier can move, the two bases can select different construction-manager rates from exactly the same project data, and the cap may bind on one basis while sitting idle on the other. This basis is permitted only when the project's `audit_status` is `CLOSED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), and only when no cost report of the project remains at `OPEN`, `DISPUTED` or `RECONCILIATION_HELD`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_COST_REPORTS_NOT_RECONCILED` (HTTP 422). With `execute` false it writes `final_savings_pool`, `final_cm_savings_share` and `final_owner_savings_share` and moves `closeout_status` to `FINAL_COMPUTED`, from either `NOT_STARTED` or `PROVISIONAL_COMPUTED`. The provisional pass is therefore optional: an owner may go straight to the audited final figure, in which case the three provisional fields are simply never computed and stay at 0.00.

**Settlement** is `POST_AUDIT_FINAL` with `execute` true, and it is permitted only when the project's `closeout_status` is already `FINAL_COMPUTED` — the audited figures must have been computed and stored before they can be settled. Settlement advances `closeout_status` `FINAL_COMPUTED → SETTLEMENT_EXECUTED`, which is terminal, and cascades three further changes: the project's `gmp_status` moves `EXECUTED → CLOSED`; the project's `contingency_posture` moves to `FROZEN` from whichever of `OPEN` or `CONSTRAINED` it held; and every work package of the project whose `procurement_status` is `AWARDED` moves to `CLOSED`. The project's `version` increments by 1. Attempting settlement on a project already at `SETTLEMENT_EXECUTED` is rejected with `ERR_CLOSEOUT_ALREADY_SETTLED` (HTTP 409), and — as §2.11 sets out — after settlement the frozen posture, not the settled closeout, is what turns away any attempt to post or void a contingency draw.

The table below enumerates every endpoint of the API.
