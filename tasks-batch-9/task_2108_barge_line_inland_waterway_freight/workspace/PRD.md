# Barge Line & Inland Waterway Freight API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Mississippi River barge line moves bulk cargo in units that are assembled, priced, restricted and billed by entirely different departments, and the paperwork that binds them together has historically lived in spreadsheets, radio traffic and the dispatcher's memory. A single loaded hopper barge is at once a hull with a cleaning certificate, a line on a freight contract, a draft number that the current river stage may or may not permit, a slot in a lock chamber queue, an occupant of a harbor fleeting berth, and — after outturn weights come back light — the subject of a cargo claim. This document specifies the REST API that consolidates all of it into one system of record, so that the tow the dispatcher seals in Cairo, the tariff the rate bureau made effective last week, and the invoice the biller issues after discharge all agree on the same numbers.

Three pressures shape the design. The first is **physical**: the river's stage dictates how deep a barge may be loaded and how many barges a towboat may push, and a falling gauge must be able to force tonnage out of an already-built tow rather than merely warn about it. The second is **temporal**: tariffs have effective windows, diesel index postings have posting dates, lock chambers have occupancy windows that must not overlap, laytime clocks run against tendered and finished instants, and cargo claims expire. Every one of these reads from a single server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that an entire commercial season can be replayed deterministically. The third is **fiscal**: freight, fuel, fleeting, cleaning and demurrage roll into one invoice against a shipper's credit line, and a claim approved after the invoice was cut must credit the bill without silently re-pricing it.

The API is therefore written as a set of small, explicit state transitions rather than as editable records. Nothing is patched into a new shape; a tow is sealed, departed, delayed, resumed, arrived and broken up, and each of those steps carries its own preconditions, its own cascade onto sibling entities, and its own rejection code when the preconditions do not hold. Readers implementing this specification should expect every numeric outcome — a draft in feet, a fee in cents, a settlement in cents — to be pinned to an exact evaluation order and an exact rounding rule, because two defensible readings of the same charter language routinely produce different money, and this document names which reading is billed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Users, Roles, and Operating Authority

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the roles declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before anything else about the request is examined.

Nine roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `WaterwayAdmin`, `TariffAnalyst`, `RateAuthority`, `TowboatDispatcher`, `LockMaster`, `HarborAgent`, `ContractManager`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, and `Shipper`.

`WaterwayAdmin` is the shore-side reference-data administrator: it owns commodities, the prior-cargo compatibility matrix, hulls, fleeting berths, lock chambers, gauge readings, and the logical clock. `TariffAnalyst` drafts tariff filings and posts the diesel index, but may never make its own filing effective. `RateAuthority` exists solely to make filed tariffs effective. `TowboatDispatcher` builds tows, assigns barges by commodity, light-loads to the river stage, moves tows, requests lockages, and records discharge events. `LockMaster` operates one lock chamber. `HarborAgent` runs the fleeting harbor and the wash rack. `ContractManager` owns shipper accounts and the freight contract lifecycle including billing. `ClaimsAdjuster` handles cargo loss and short claims end to end. `Shipper` is the customer account. Every role, without exception, may call every read endpoint in the system.

Two roles are **entity-backed**, meaning their `X-User-ID` is not a free-form operator string but the identifier of a stored row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `LockMaster`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the `Lock` the caller masters. Ownership checks compare the target resource's chamber reference to `X-User-ID`: granting, denying or rescheduling a lock reservation compares the reservation's `lock_id` to `X-User-ID`, and changing a chamber's posture compares the chamber's own `id` to `X-User-ID`. A `LockMaster` acting on another chamber's reservation, or on another chamber, is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Shipper`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Shipper` row. A `Shipper` may file a cargo claim or withdraw one only when the claim's freight contract carries a `shipper_id` equal to `X-User-ID`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Reads are not narrowed by ownership.

The remaining seven roles — `WaterwayAdmin`, `TariffAnalyst`, `RateAuthority`, `TowboatDispatcher`, `HarborAgent`, `ContractManager` and `ClaimsAdjuster` — carry an **opaque** identity: their `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any row's `id`. The server does record such an identifier where a transition says so — as `filed_by_user_id` and `effectuated_by_user_id` on a tariff filing, and as `assessed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a cargo claim — but it stores those values purely so that a later step can require a *different* operator.

That last point is the line's segregation-of-duties rule, and it applies in exactly two places. A tariff filing may not be made effective by the same `X-User-ID` that filed it. A cargo claim may neither be approved nor denied by the same `X-User-ID` that assessed it. Both violations are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that segregation of duties is checked *after* entity-backed ownership, so a caller who fails both sees the ownership code first.

Chamber posture is the one operation with a split authority model: both `LockMaster` and `WaterwayAdmin` may change a chamber's operational posture, but the `LockMaster` may only do so for its own chamber, while `WaterwayAdmin` carries no such restriction. Filing a cargo claim and withdrawing one are likewise shared, between `ClaimsAdjuster` (unrestricted) and `Shipper` (restricted to its own contracts and claims).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Conventions

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as a non-integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List responses always sort by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest row first — unless the endpoint's own description says otherwise, and no endpoint in this system says otherwise. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current page. Every filter parameter named in the endpoint table narrows the result set by exact match, and multiple filters combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and Version Counters

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a request body is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped at creation and never changed thereafter.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations that a cascade performs on the row rather than a direct call from a client. If a single operation touches three rows, all three rows have their `version` incremented and their `updated_at` refreshed.

Four resource kinds are deliberately immutable reference records and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all: commodities, compatibility rules, fuel index postings, and river stage readings. Implementations must not emit `updated_at` or `version` on those four. Every other resource — shippers, tariff filings, barges, cleaning orders, fleeting berths, fleeting assignments, locks, lock reservations, tows, barge assignments, freight contracts and cargo claims — carries the full set of four common properties.

### 2.5. The Logical River Clock

Time in this system is a server-held value, not the host's wall clock. The logical clock is the **only** time source consulted for tariff effectiveness, fuel index selection, river stage selection and cargo claim filing windows. Before any client repositions it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance.

A `WaterwayAdmin` repositions the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 instant. The call responds with the resulting clock value in a payload shaped as `{ current_utc: string }`, and it is a pure control operation: no entity is mutated, no `version` anywhere is incremented, and no cascade fires. The clock may be moved forward or backward freely; the system draws no distinction.

Note carefully that `created_at` and `updated_at` are stamped from the logical clock as well, which is what makes the tie-break rules in the selectors below reproducible across a replayed season.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Foreign-Key Admission

Several resources are addressed beneath a parent segment, and two are created beneath one: a cleaning order is created under a hull's path segment, taking its `barge_id` from that segment rather than from the body, and a barge assignment is created under a tow's path segment, taking its `tow_id` from that segment rather than from the body. Everything else is addressed at the top level and joined by foreign key.

Path segments are resolved strictly left to right. The leftmost identifier is looked up first; if it names no row, the request fails immediately with that entity's not-found code and no segment to its right is examined. The path variables used across the API are `shipper_id`, `commodity_id`, `tariff_filing_id`, `barge_id`, `cleaning_order_id`, `fleeting_berth_id`, `fleeting_assignment_id`, `lock_id`, `lock_reservation_id`, `tow_id`, `barge_assignment_id`, `freight_contract_id` and `cargo_claim_id`, and each resolves to the not-found code of its own entity: `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMODITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TARIFF_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BARGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLEANING_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLEETING_BERTH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLEETING_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOCK_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BARGE_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FREIGHT_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CARGO_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` respectively, each with HTTP 404.

Foreign keys arriving in a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved, and they are resolved **in the order the fields are declared in that endpoint's input list**, not in alphabetical order and not in the order the client happened to serialize them. A create call that names both a missing barge and a missing contract reports whichever of the two is declared first for that endpoint. The same 404 codes apply.

### 2.7. The Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: the checks below are evaluated in strictly ascending order and **the first failing check decides the response**. This ordering is not advisory. A caller who sends a malformed body to an endpoint their role may not use sees the authorization failure, not the body failure; a caller who sends a stale version alongside a cargo incompatibility sees the stale version failure, because concurrency sits above safety on this ladder.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and `X-User-Role` names a declared role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorized list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** The body carries every required field, every enum-typed value is a declared member of its enumeration, every numeric field satisfies its declared range, and every datetime parses as ISO 8601 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
4. **Pagination shape.** `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
5. **Path existence.** Every resource named by a path segment exists, validated left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves to an existing row, validated in the order the fields are declared in the endpoint's input list — otherwise the matching not-found code (404). Applies to every write endpoint carrying foreign keys.
7. **Resource ownership.** For entity-backed roles the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the ownership field of the target — a `LockMaster` against the reservation's `lock_id` or against the chamber's own `id`, a `Shipper` against the contract's `shipper_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This check guards exactly six operations: granting a lock reservation, denying a lock reservation, rescheduling a lock reservation, changing a chamber's posture, filing a cargo claim, and withdrawing a cargo claim.
8. **Segregation of duties.** The caller's `X-User-ID` differs from the identity recorded for the preceding step — the `filed_by_user_id` at effectuation, the `assessed_by_user_id` at approval or denial — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This check guards exactly three operations: making a tariff filing effective, approving a cargo claim, and denying a cargo claim.
9. **State precondition.** The target resource stands in a state from which the requested transition is declared, including the hull's `operational_status` when it is being fleeted, assigned or washed — otherwise the entity's invalid-state code (409), or `ERR_BARGE_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409) where the obstruction is the hull's operational posture. Applies to every transition endpoint.
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_tow_version` equals the tow's current `version`, or `expected_shipper_version` equals the shipper's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_TOW_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SHIPPER_VERSION` (412). This check guards exactly three operations: requesting a lock reservation, adjusting a tow's load, and issuing an invoice.
11. **Prior-cargo prohibition.** The compatibility rule for the pair formed by the hull's prior cargo group and the contract's commodity group does not carry `prohibited` set to true — otherwise `ERR_CARGO_INCOMPATIBLE` (422). Guards two operations: adding a barge assignment to a tow, and sealing a tow.
12. **Cleanliness sufficiency.** The hull's cleanliness rank is at least the rank of the cleaning level the matrix requires for the target commodity — otherwise `ERR_BARGE_CLEANLINESS_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Guards the same two operations: adding a barge assignment, and sealing a tow.
13. **Stage reading availability.** A river stage reading exists for the tow's `controlling_gauge_station` whose `reading_at` is at or before the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_NO_RIVER_STAGE_READING` (422). Guards two operations: sealing a tow, and adjusting a tow's load.
14. **Draft restriction.** The tow's `max_draft_feet` is at or below the controlling band's permitted `max_draft_feet` — otherwise `ERR_DRAFT_EXCEEDS_RESTRICTION` (422). Guards sealing a tow.
15. **Tow size cap.** The tow's `barge_count` is at or below the controlling band's `tow_size_cap_barges` — otherwise `ERR_TOW_SIZE_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Guards sealing a tow.
16. **Hull capacity.** `loaded_tons` does not exceed the hull's `capacity_tons` — otherwise `ERR_BARGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Guards adding a barge assignment to a tow.
17. **Effective tariff.** An `EFFECTIVE` tariff filing for the contract's commodity group has `effective_from` at or before the logical clock and `effective_to` at or after it — otherwise `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_TARIFF` (422). Guards activating a contract.
18. **Fuel index availability.** A fuel index posting exists whose `posting_date` is at or before the logical clock's calendar date — otherwise `ERR_NO_FUEL_INDEX_POSTING` (422). Guards activating a contract.
19. **Tariff window overlap.** No other `EFFECTIVE` filing for the same `commodity_group` has an effective window overlapping this filing's window, other than the filing this one supersedes — otherwise `ERR_TARIFF_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Guards making a filing effective.
20. **Chamber window conflict.** The reservation's chamber occupation window does not overlap that of another reservation on the same lock whose status is `GRANTED` or `IN_CHAMBER` — otherwise `ERR_LOCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Guards two operations: requesting a lock reservation, and granting one.
21. **Berth capacity.** Berth occupancy across the requested interval never exceeds `capacity_barges` once the candidate assignment is added — otherwise `ERR_BERTH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards creating a fleeting assignment.
22. **Chronological sequence.** `unload_started_at` is at or after `arrived_at`; `unload_finished_at` is at or after `unload_started_at`; `released_at` is at or after `start_at`; `actual_end_at` is at or after `actual_start_at`; and the tow's `arrived_at` is at or after its `departed_at` — otherwise `ERR_UNLOAD_SEQUENCE_INVALID` (422). Guards five operations: starting an unload, finishing an unload, releasing a fleeting assignment, clearing a chamber, and recording a tow's arrival.
23. **Claim filing window.** The logical clock is no more than 30 days after the named assignment's `unload_finished_at` — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). Guards filing a cargo claim.
24. **Chamber not closed.** The lock's status is anything other than `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_LOCK_CLOSED` (409). Guards three operations: requesting a lock reservation, granting one, and entering the chamber.
25. **Cut position uniqueness.** The requested `cut_position` is not already occupied by a non-`DROPPED` assignment of the same tow — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CUT_POSITION` (409). Guards adding a barge assignment to a tow.
26. **Single active assignment per hull.** The hull does not already hold an assignment in status `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `LOADED` on any tow — otherwise `ERR_BARGE_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (409). Guards adding a barge assignment to a tow.
27. **Reference-data uniqueness.** `hull_number` is unique across all barges and `code` is unique across all commodities — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_HULL_NUMBER` (409). Guards two operations: registering a barge, and registering a commodity. The same code covers both collisions.
28. **One live claim per assignment.** No cargo claim whose status is neither `WITHDRAWN` nor `DENIED` already exists for the named `barge_assignment_id` — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_FILED` (409). Guards filing a cargo claim.
29. **Revision limit.** The claim's `revision_round` is below 2 — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_REVISION_LIMIT` (409). Guards requesting further information on a claim.
30. **Claim line coherence.** The named assignment's `freight_contract_id` equals the claim's `freight_contract_id` — otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` (409). Guards filing a cargo claim.
31. **Shortage support.** For a claim whose `claim_type` is `SHORTAGE`, `claimed_tons` is at most the named assignment's `loaded_tons` minus its `discharged_tons`, and that assignment's `discharge_status` is `COMPLETED` or `DISPUTED` — otherwise `ERR_SHORTAGE_NOT_SUPPORTED` (422). Guards filing a cargo claim.
32. **Billable lines exist.** At least one assignment of the contract has `discharge_status` of `COMPLETED` or `DISPUTED` — otherwise `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_LINES` (422). Guards issuing an invoice.
33. **Credit posture.** The shipper's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_SHIPPER_ON_CREDIT_HOLD` (409). Guards activating a contract.
34. **Credit headroom.** The shipper's `outstanding_balance_cents` plus the projected charge for this contract is at most the shipper's `credit_limit_cents` — otherwise `ERR_SHIPPER_CREDIT_EXCEEDED` (402). Guards activating a contract.
35. **Payment exactness.** `paid_amount_cents` equals the contract's `total_cents` exactly — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Guards recording payment.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Tow and the Shipper Ledger

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and only those three. Each requires a non-optional version field in its request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of a *parent* entity — not of the resource being written. Every other endpoint in this system takes no version field at all, and supplying one there has no effect.

**Requesting a lock reservation** requires `expected_tow_version`, carrying the `version` of the tow named by `tow_id` as the dispatcher last read it. The chamber window is sized from the tow's cut count, so a makeup that has moved since the dispatcher looked — another barge added, a load adjustment applied, an earlier slot booked — invalidates the request. If `expected_tow_version` does not equal the tow's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Under the product's ordinary semantics the reservation would otherwise be perfectly legal, because one tow may legitimately hold any number of slots at different chambers; the rejection exists purely to stop a slot being sized from a composition the caller never observed.

**Adjusting a tow's load** likewise requires `expected_tow_version`. Light-loading rewrites `loaded_tons` on every over-draft assignment of the tow, so the request carries the `version` the dispatcher based the decision on; if the makeup has advanced since then, the adjustment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than applied to a composition the caller never saw. A repeated adjustment against a freshly read `version` is entirely legitimate, so this is a staleness rule and not a state precondition.

**Issuing an invoice** requires `expected_shipper_version`, carrying the `version` of the `Shipper` row that owns the contract as the biller last read it. Issuing an invoice moves the shipper's outstanding balance and may put the account on credit hold, so if another contract of the same shipper has been invoiced, paid, or credited by an approved claim since that read, the stored `version` no longer matches and the issue is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SHIPPER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Issuing a second, different invoice for the same shipper is otherwise entirely legitimate.

### 2.9. Money, Tonnage, and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is carried in whole cents as integers; there are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field or a response. Wherever a monetary quantity is derived from a multiplication, it is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value ending in exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, upward.

Where rounding order matters, this document pins it explicitly and the pinned order is part of the contract. Two conventions recur and must not be confused. First, when many lines are summed, each line is rounded to whole cents **before** the summation, never after; summing raw line values and rounding once at the end is expressly not the rule and produces a different total. Second, when a per-item value feeds an aggregate comparison, the per-item value is rounded first and the aggregate is computed over the rounded values — so a tow's deepest draft is the maximum of rounded barge drafts, not the rounding of an unrounded maximum.

Non-monetary precision is fixed as follows. Barge drafts and the tow's maximum draft are carried at two decimal places, rounded half up. Gauge stage readings are compared at one decimal place, and the maximum draft permitted by a band is carried at one decimal place. A trade tolerance in tons is carried at three decimal places, rounded half up. A light-loading tonnage allowance is truncated downward to a whole ton. Laytime is rounded **up** to whole hours per barge before any comparison is made against a free allowance. Delay is measured in whole minutes truncated toward zero. Fleeting occupancy is measured in whole days rounded up, with a floor of one day.

### 2.10. Parallel State Axes and Cascade Discipline

Three resources carry two independent state axes that advance on disjoint sets of triggers, and implementers must resist the temptation to collapse them. A barge carries `operational_status` — where the hull physically is — alongside `cleanliness_status`, the cleaning certificate it currently holds; a fleeting reservation moves the first axis without touching the second, while a passing wash inspection moves the second without necessarily touching the first. A barge assignment carries `status`, the makeup lifecycle driven by its tow, alongside `discharge_status`, the unload lifecycle driven by dock events and by claim approval. A freight contract carries `movement_status`, the physical lifecycle, alongside `billing_status`, the money lifecycle; issuing an invoice moves only the billing axis, and an approved claim moves only the billing axis.

Cascades are immediate and complete. When an operation is specified to touch sibling or parent rows, those rows are updated within the same request, their `version` counters increment and their `updated_at` timestamps refresh, and the effects are visible to the very next read. There is no deferred settlement, no queue, and no eventual convergence. Where a cascade is described as a chain of hops, each hop is caused by the hop before it and fires only if that predecessor's own condition held: a delay that does not push a tow over its threshold stops at hop one and never reaches the assignments or the contracts.

Aggregate counters maintained on parent rows — a berth's `occupied_barge_count`, a chamber's `granted_reservation_count`, a tow's `barge_count`, `total_loaded_tons` and `pending_lock_reservation_count`, a shipper's `outstanding_balance_cents` and `open_claim_count` — are maintained incrementally by the transitions that move their children, and every increment or decrement named in this document is mandatory. A counter that is merely recomputed on read will disagree with the specification wherever a transition is declared to leave it untouched.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Cargoes, the Prior-Cargo Matrix, Hulls, Berths, and Chambers

Before a single ton moves, a `WaterwayAdmin` populates the reference layer. A **commodity** is registered with a short trade `code` such as CORN or UAN32, which must be unique across all commodities — a repeat registers as `ERR_DUPLICATE_HULL_NUMBER`, the same code that covers a repeated hull number. Each commodity declares the `commodity_group` that prices and groups it, one of `GRAIN`, `COAL`, `CHEMICAL`, `PETROLEUM`, `FERTILIZER` or `AGGREGATE`; a `residue_class` describing what the cargo leaves behind in a hopper after discharge, one of `CLEAN`, `DUSTY`, `OILY` or `TOXIC`; a `hazard_flag` marking regulated hazardous cargo; and a `stowage_factor` in cubic feet per ton, which must be greater than zero and is informational for load planning only. Commodities are immutable reference rows and may be listed narrowed by `commodity_group`.

The **prior-cargo compatibility matrix** is the safety spine of the whole barge-assignment process. Each rule covers one ordered pair — a `prior_commodity_group` that was last carried in a hull and a `next_commodity_group` about to be loaded into it — and declares the `required_cleaning_level` that the hull must hold before the next cargo may load, one of `NONE`, `SWEEP`, `WASH` or `STEAM_CERT`, plus a `prohibited` flag that defaults to false and, when true, forbids the sequence outright no matter how thoroughly the hull is cleaned. At most one rule may exist per ordered pair, and the list may be narrowed by either group.

Resolving a requirement works as follows. If the hull has never carried cargo — that is, its `last_cargo_commodity_id` is null — the required level resolves to `NONE` and the sequence is never prohibited. Otherwise the prior group is the `commodity_group` of the commodity the hull last carried, the next group is the `commodity_group` of the commodity the target contract moves, and the matrix is searched for the rule matching that ordered pair. When no rule exists for the pair, the required level defaults to `WASH` and the sequence is not prohibited. When a rule exists, its `required_cleaning_level` and its `prohibited` flag govern. A prohibited pair is refused with `ERR_CARGO_INCOMPATIBLE`.

Cleanliness is then compared by rank. A hull's `cleanliness_status` ranks `DIRTY` at 0, `SWEPT` at 1, `WASHED` at 2 and `STEAM_CERTIFIED` at 3, while a required level ranks `NONE` at 0, `SWEEP` at 1, `WASH` at 2 and `STEAM_CERT` at 3. The hull passes only when its cleanliness rank is greater than or equal to the rank of the required level; a hull that falls below is refused with `ERR_BARGE_CLEANLINESS_INSUFFICIENT`. Both gates are applied twice in the life of a tow — once when the barge is added to the makeup and again when the makeup is sealed — because the matrix, the hull's certificate, or the contract's commodity may all have moved in between.

A **barge** is registered with a `hull_number` painted on the hull and unique across the fleet, a `capacity_tons` ceiling greater than zero, a `light_draft_feet` figure for the empty hull greater than zero, and a `tons_per_inch_immersion` figure greater than zero describing how much cargo sinks the hull one further inch. Two further attributes are optional at registration and exist so that an operator can record a hull arriving from elsewhere with a known history: `cleanliness_status`, which defaults to `WASHED` when omitted, and `last_cargo_commodity_id`, which is null when omitted. Registration always sets `operational_status` to `IDLE` — the axis moves only through fleeting, tow makeup and wash-rack events — and leaves `last_cleaning_certificate_number` empty until a passing inspection writes one. Hulls may be listed narrowed by `operational_status`, by `cleanliness_status`, or by `last_cargo_commodity_id`, the last of which answers "which hulls last carried this cargo".

A **fleeting berth** models harbor parking. It carries a `harbor_name`, a `river_mile` locating it, a `capacity_barges` head count greater than zero, a `daily_fee_cents_per_barge` charge of at least zero levied per billable day per barge, and a `switch_fee_cents` charge of at least zero levied once per fleeting assignment. Its `occupied_barge_count` starts at 0 and tracks, at every instant, the number of reservations on the berth that are still in `RESERVED` status. Berths may be listed narrowed by `harbor_name`.

A **lock** models one chamber. It carries a `lock_name`, a `river_mile`, a `chamber_capacity_barges` figure greater than zero for how many barges fit in one cut, a `cycle_minutes` figure greater than zero for how long one cut takes to lock through, and a `turnback_buffer_minutes` figure of at least zero for how long the chamber remains unavailable after a tow clears before the next may start. A newly registered chamber opens in status `OPEN` with `granted_reservation_count` at 0. One detail deserves emphasis because the authorization model depends on it: a chamber's own `id` doubles as the `LockMaster` identity for that chamber, so the operator who masters a chamber authenticates with that chamber's identifier.

### 3.2. Publishing Rates, Posting the Diesel Index, and Reading the River

A **tariff filing** is the published price sheet, and it is the only thing that ever prices a contract. A `TariffAnalyst` drafts one with a published `tariff_code`, the `commodity_group` it prices, a `base_rate_cents_per_ton_mile` greater than zero and carried to four decimal places, a `min_charge_cents` floor of at least zero, a `demurrage_rate_cents_per_hour` of at least zero, a `fuel_surcharge_table`, and an inclusive effective window running from `effective_from` to `effective_to`, where `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`. Optionally the draft names `supersedes_tariff_filing_id`, pointing at an earlier filing this one is intended to replace. The `fuel_surcharge_table` is a non-empty ascending list of tiers, each carrying a `threshold_cents_per_gallon` and a `pct`; thresholds must be strictly ascending across the list. A new filing is born in status `DRAFT`, with `filed_by_user_id` and `effectuated_by_user_id` both null.

Filing the draft with the rate bureau moves it `DRAFT → FILED` and is the moment the filer's identity is captured: the acting `X-User-ID` is written to `filed_by_user_id`, and the filing's `version` increments. That capture exists for one reason only — the next step reads it. Filing is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_FILING_INVALID_STATE` unless the filing stands in `DRAFT`, and the window is re-checked here as well: the filing's `effective_to` must still be strictly later than its `effective_from`.

Making the filing effective moves it `FILED → EFFECTIVE` and is reserved to the `RateAuthority` role. Three things must hold. The filing must stand in `FILED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_FILING_INVALID_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `filed_by_user_id`, or the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the analyst who wrote the rate may not be the authority who publishes it. And no *other* filing for the same `commodity_group` that already stands in `EFFECTIVE` may have an effective window overlapping this one's window, with a single exception: the filing named by `supersedes_tariff_filing_id` is excluded from that overlap test, since it is precisely the filing being displaced. An overlap that is not excused this way is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. On success, `effectuated_by_user_id` is written from the acting `X-User-ID` and the filing's `version` increments.

When `supersedes_tariff_filing_id` is set, effectuation also drives the named filing from `EFFECTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED` and truncates that older filing's `effective_to` to the new filing's `effective_from`, so the two windows abut instead of overlapping. Both filings have their `version` incremented, since both rows were touched. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. Crucially, freight contracts that already pinned the superseded filing keep pointing at it — pinning is never re-resolved, and a later rate change can never retroactively re-price a contract that was already activated. Filings may be listed narrowed by `status`, by `commodity_group`, or by `supersedes_tariff_filing_id`, the last of which finds the successors of a given filing.

The **fuel index** is posted separately by the same analyst role: each posting names a `posting_date` as a plain calendar date in year-month-day form and a `diesel_price_cents_per_gallon` greater than zero, expressed in whole cents per gallon. Postings are immutable reference rows carrying no `version`. Posting a new price never disturbs an already-activated contract, because that contract pinned its price at activation.

The **river stage** is recorded by the `WaterwayAdmin` as a gauge reading: a `gauge_station` name, the instant `reading_at` at which the stage was observed, and the `stage_feet` on that station's datum. The server immediately stamps three derived values onto the reading. The `draft_restriction_band` is resolved from `stage_feet` with lower-edge-inclusive boundaries: a stage of 25.0 feet or higher is `HIGH_WATER`; a stage of at least 15.0 feet but below 25.0 is `NORMAL`; a stage of at least 8.0 feet but below 15.0 is `LOW`; anything below 8.0 feet is `CRITICAL`. Stages are compared at one decimal place. The band then fixes the other two: `max_draft_feet` is 12.0 feet in `HIGH_WATER`, 12.0 feet in `NORMAL`, 10.5 feet in `LOW` and 9.0 feet in `CRITICAL`, while `tow_size_cap_barges` is 20 barges in `HIGH_WATER`, 40 in `NORMAL`, 30 in `LOW` and just 6 in `CRITICAL`. Note the deliberate asymmetry: a flooding river permits the same loaded draft as a normal river but caps the tow at half the normal size, because handling is the constraint rather than depth.

Whenever a tow needs to know its restriction, exactly one reading governs, chosen by a fully pinned selector. The candidates are the readings whose `gauge_station` equals the tow's `controlling_gauge_station` and whose `reading_at` is at or before the current logical clock; readings dated in the future are excluded entirely. Those candidates are ordered by `reading_at` descending, and ties on `reading_at` are broken by `created_at` descending; the first row in that ordering is the controlling reading. If the candidate set is empty, the caller is refused with `ERR_NO_RIVER_STAGE_READING`. Readings are immutable and may be listed narrowed by `gauge_station`.

### 3.3. The Shipper Account and the Road to an Active Contract

A `ContractManager` opens a **shipper account** with a legal `name` and a `credit_limit_cents` of at least zero, being the maximum outstanding freight balance the line is willing to carry for that customer. A fresh account is born with `status` `ACTIVE`, `outstanding_balance_cents` of 0, `open_claim_count` of 0 and `version` 1. Neither balance nor counters are ever written directly by a client: `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` across that shipper's contracts whose `billing_status` is `INVOICED` or `ADJUSTED`, and `open_claim_count` is the number of cargo claims against that shipper's contracts whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `ASSESSED`. Both are maintained incrementally by the transitions described later in this section. The `status` axis has exactly two values, `ACTIVE` and `CREDIT_HOLD`, and it gates the activation of new contracts. Accounts may be listed narrowed by `status`.

A **freight contract** is drafted against that account. It names the `shipper_id` moving the cargo and the `commodity_id` being moved, commits a `contracted_tons` figure greater than zero, and fixes the geography with an `origin_mile` loading point and a `destination_mile` discharge point. It also declares `laytime_hours`, the free laytime allowance of at least zero hours granted per barge before demurrage begins to accrue, and `declared_value_cents_per_ton` greater than zero, the per-ton value used to price loss and short claims. One further input, `claim_settlement_basis`, is optional and defaults to `DEDUCTIBLE`; its only other legal value is `FRANCHISE`, and it decides which of two assessed settlement variants is actually paid when a claim is approved. A new contract begins with `movement_status` `DRAFT` and `billing_status` `NOT_BILLED`, and its `distance_miles` is not yet resolved.

Booking is a formality that moves `movement_status` from `DRAFT` to `BOOKED`; it takes no input and increments the contract's `version`. It is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE` from any other movement state.

**Activation is where the contract's economics are frozen**, and it is the most heavily guarded transition in the system. It moves `movement_status` from `BOOKED` to `ACTIVE` and requires, in the ladder order of §2.7, that the contract stands in `BOOKED`; that the owning shipper's `status` is `ACTIVE` rather than `CREDIT_HOLD`, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_SHIPPER_ON_CREDIT_HOLD`; that an `EFFECTIVE` tariff filing for the contract's commodity group covers the current logical clock, meaning its `effective_from` is at or before the clock and its `effective_to` is at or after it, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_TARIFF`; that at least one fuel index posting exists whose `posting_date` is at or before the logical clock's calendar date, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_NO_FUEL_INDEX_POSTING`; and finally that the shipper has credit headroom.

The headroom test is a forward-looking projection, computed at the moment of activation and never stored. First, `distance_miles` is the absolute difference between `destination_mile` and `origin_mile`. Then the projected charge is `contracted_tons` multiplied by `distance_miles` multiplied by the pinned filing's `base_rate_cents_per_ton_mile`, rounded half up to whole cents. Note that the projection deliberately uses the *contracted* tonnage rather than any assignment tonnage, because at activation time no barge has been assigned yet. If the shipper's current `outstanding_balance_cents` plus that projected charge exceeds the shipper's `credit_limit_cents`, activation is refused with `ERR_SHIPPER_CREDIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402); the comparison is inclusive, so a projection that lands exactly on the credit limit passes.

On success, activation writes three pins onto the contract and sets `distance_miles`. The `pinned_tariff_filing_id` records the `EFFECTIVE` filing that satisfied the tariff test, and every later charge computation reads its rates from that filing and no other. The `pinned_fuel_price_cents_per_gallon` records the diesel price from the controlling posting, chosen by a pinned selector: among the postings whose `posting_date` is at or before the logical clock's calendar date, order by `posting_date` descending with ties broken by `created_at` descending and take the first. The `pinned_fuel_surcharge_pct` records the percentage read from the pinned filing's `fuel_surcharge_table` at that price: the applicable tier is the **highest** tier whose `threshold_cents_per_gallon` is less than or equal to the pinned price, and a price exactly equal to a threshold selects that tier rather than the one below it. When the pinned price falls below every threshold in the table, the pinned percentage is 0.0. The contract's `version` increments. Because all three values are pinned, a diesel posting made tomorrow and a tariff superseded next week leave this contract's bill untouched.

Cancelling a contract moves `movement_status` to `CANCELLED` from `DRAFT`, `BOOKED` or `ACTIVE`, and takes an optional `reason`. It is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE` from any later movement state, and it is likewise refused when any barge assignment of the contract stands in `CONFIRMED` or `LOADED` — once steel is committed to a sealed tow, the commercial paper cannot simply vanish. Cancellation moves the billing axis from `NOT_BILLED` to `VOID`, drives every assignment of the contract that still stands in `PLANNED` to `DROPPED` — which releases that tonnage back out of its tow's rollup exactly as an ordinary drop would — and increments the contract's `version`. `CANCELLED` and `CLOSED` are the two terminal movement states. Contracts may be listed narrowed by `shipper_id`, by `commodity_id`, by `pinned_tariff_filing_id`, by `movement_status` or by `billing_status`.

### 3.4. Harbor Services: Fleeting the Hull and Working the Wash Rack

A `HarborAgent` parks a barge by reserving berth space over a half-open interval. The reservation names the `barge_id` being parked and the `fleeting_berth_id` receiving it, an inclusive `start_at` and an exclusive `end_at` that must be strictly later than `start_at`, and optionally a `freight_contract_id` — when that optional link is present, the released assignment's fee eventually rolls into that contract's `fleeting_fee_cents` bucket at invoice time, and when it is absent the fee is a harbor cost that no freight bill ever sees. The hull must stand in `operational_status` `IDLE`; a hull that is already `FLEETED`, `IN_TOW` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` is refused with `ERR_BARGE_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

Admission to a berth is an **aggregate head-count test, not a pairwise overlap test** — any number of reservations may overlap so long as the berth is never over-subscribed at any instant. Consider the candidate interval and the set of reservations already standing in `RESERVED` on that same berth; reservations in `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` never occupy the berth and are ignored entirely. Collect the boundary instants: every `start_at` belonging to that reserved set together with the candidate's own `start_at`, keeping only those that fall inside the candidate interval — at or after the candidate's `start_at` and strictly before its `end_at`. At each such instant, occupancy is 1 for the candidate itself plus the count of reserved rows whose `start_at` is at or before the instant and whose `end_at` is strictly after it. Intervals are closed on the left and open on the right, so a reservation ending exactly at the instant under test does not occupy the berth at that instant. If the greatest occupancy over those instants exceeds the berth's `capacity_barges`, the reservation is refused with `ERR_BERTH_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

On admission the reservation is created in status `RESERVED` and a provisional `estimated_fee_cents` is computed from the planned interval: take the elapsed seconds from `start_at` to `end_at`, divide by the 86,400 seconds in a day, round the quotient up to a whole day, take the greater of that figure and one day, multiply by the berth's `daily_fee_cents_per_barge`, and add the berth's one-time `switch_fee_cents`. The berth's `occupied_barge_count` increments by one, the hull moves `IDLE → FLEETED` on its operational axis while its `cleanliness_status` is left completely untouched, and the `version` of both the hull and the berth increments alongside the new reservation.

Releasing the hull requires a `released_at` instant, which must be at or after the reservation's `start_at` or the call is refused with `ERR_UNLOAD_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. The reservation must stand in `RESERVED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_FLEETING_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Release moves the reservation `RESERVED → RELEASED` and fixes the real money: `billable_days` is the elapsed seconds from `start_at` to `released_at` divided by 86,400, rounded up to a whole day, with a floor of one day; `fee_cents` is `billable_days` multiplied by the berth's `daily_fee_cents_per_barge` plus the berth's `switch_fee_cents`. The berth's `occupied_barge_count` decrements by one, and the hull returns `FLEETED → IDLE` unless it has meanwhile been taken `IN_TOW`, in which case its operational axis is left alone. The reservation's `version` increments.

Cancelling a reservation is the same shape with the opposite money: it moves `RESERVED → CANCELLED`, leaves both `fee_cents` and `billable_days` at 0 forever — a cancelled reservation is never billable under any circumstance — decrements the berth's `occupied_barge_count` by one, returns the hull `FLEETED → IDLE` when it is not `IN_TOW`, and increments `version`. `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal. Reservations may be listed narrowed by `barge_id`, by `fleeting_berth_id`, by `freight_contract_id` or by `status`.

The wash rack runs on **cleaning orders**, which are created beneath the hull they clean, so the order takes its `barge_id` from the hull it is filed under rather than from the body. The body names `required_for_commodity_id`, the next cargo the hull is being prepared for, and optionally a `freight_contract_id` that the eventual fee should roll into as `cleaning_fee_cents`. At creation the server resolves `required_cleaning_level` for the pair formed by the hull's prior cargo group and the target commodity's group, exactly as §3.1 describes, and the order is born in status `ORDERED` with `fee_cents` at 0.

Moving the order onto the rack advances it `ORDERED → IN_PROGRESS`, and the same operation is also how an order that failed inspection returns to the rack, advancing `FAILED_INSPECTION → IN_PROGRESS` — the failed order is re-worked rather than replaced by a fresh one. Any other starting status is refused with `ERR_CLEANING_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. The hull must stand in `operational_status` `IDLE` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` to go on the rack; a hull that is `IN_TOW` or `FLEETED` is refused with `ERR_BARGE_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

Closing the order carries the surveyor's verdict and three inputs: the `performed_level` actually worked, one of `NONE`, `SWEEP`, `WASH` or `STEAM_CERT`; the `certificate_number` issued; and the boolean `inspection_passed`. The order must stand in `IN_PROGRESS`. The two verdicts are mirror images and must be implemented with equal care. **On a pass**, the order moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` — a terminal state — and `fee_cents` is fixed from the performed level: `NONE` costs nothing, a `SWEEP` costs 45,000 cents, a `WASH` costs 180,000 cents, and a `STEAM_CERT` costs 420,000 cents. The hull's `cleanliness_status` is set from the performed level through a fixed mapping in which `NONE` yields `DIRTY`, `SWEEP` yields `SWEPT`, `WASH` yields `WASHED` and `STEAM_CERT` yields `STEAM_CERTIFIED`, and the hull's `last_cleaning_certificate_number` is set from the supplied `certificate_number`. A hull that was `OUT_OF_SERVICE` returns to `IDLE`; a hull that was already `IDLE` stays `IDLE`, because the two axes are independent and a passing wash advances cleanliness without moving the hull anywhere. **On a failure**, the order moves `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED_INSPECTION`, `fee_cents` stays at 0 because a failed inspection is not billable, the hull's `cleanliness_status` is left exactly as it was since a failed inspection grants no certificate, and the hull is driven `IDLE → OUT_OF_SERVICE` on its operational axis. Either verdict increments both the order's `version` and the hull's `version`. Orders may be listed narrowed by `barge_id`, by `freight_contract_id`, by `required_for_commodity_id` or by `status`.

### 3.5. Building a Tow: Makeup, Draft Profile, and Light-Loading

A `TowboatDispatcher` opens a tow by naming the `towboat_name` pushing it, its `horsepower` greater than zero, the `origin_mile` it departs from, the `destination_mile` it is bound for, and the `controlling_gauge_station` whose readings will govern its draft restriction for the whole voyage. A new tow stands in status `BUILDING` with `barge_count` 0, `total_loaded_tons` 0 and `max_draft_feet` 0. Tows may be listed narrowed by `status`.

Barges join the makeup one at a time, each addition filed beneath the tow so that the assignment's `tow_id` comes from the tow it is filed under. The body names the `barge_id` joining, the `freight_contract_id` the cargo moves under, the `loaded_tons` greater than zero placed in that hull, and the `cut_position` greater than zero the barge occupies in the makeup. The assignment is created with `status` `PLANNED` on the makeup axis and `discharge_status` `NOT_ARRIVED` on the unload axis.

Several gates stand between the request and a new assignment, and they fire in the ladder order of §2.7. The named contract must stand in `movement_status` `ACTIVE` or `IN_TRANSIT`; a contract in any other movement state is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. A hull that is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` is refused with `ERR_BARGE_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The prior-cargo prohibition is then tested and, if the pair is prohibited, the request is refused with `ERR_CARGO_INCOMPATIBLE`; the hull's cleanliness rank is tested next against the required level and, if it falls short, the request is refused with `ERR_BARGE_CLEANLINESS_INSUFFICIENT`. Only then is `loaded_tons` compared against the hull's `capacity_tons`, exceeding it being refused with `ERR_BARGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Below that come the two structural gates: a `cut_position` already occupied by a non-`DROPPED` assignment of the same tow is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CUT_POSITION`, and a hull that already holds an assignment in `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `LOADED` status on any tow anywhere is refused with `ERR_BARGE_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`.

Every addition recomputes the tow's **draft profile**, and the arithmetic is pinned in both formula and order. For a single assignment, `barge_draft_feet` is the hull's `light_draft_feet` plus the quotient of `loaded_tons` divided by the product of the hull's `tons_per_inch_immersion` and the twelve inches in a foot, with the result rounded half up to two decimal places. The tow's rollup then runs over its **active** assignments only, meaning those whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `CONFIRMED` or `LOADED`; assignments that have been `DROPPED` or `DISCHARGED` never contribute. Over that active set, `barge_count` is the number of assignments, `total_loaded_tons` is the sum of their `loaded_tons`, and `max_draft_feet` is the greatest `barge_draft_feet` among them, taken as 0 when the active set is empty. The evaluation order matters: each barge's draft is rounded to two decimals *before* the maximum is taken, so the tow's `max_draft_feet` is the maximum of rounded values and never the rounding of an unrounded maximum. Finally the controlling gauge reading is resolved and the tow's `draft_restriction_band` and `allowed_draft_feet` are re-pinned from that band's permitted draft. The tow's `version` increments on every such addition.

Dropping a barge out of the makeup is available while its assignment stands in `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, and moves it to the terminal `DROPPED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_BARGE_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The drop removes that assignment's `loaded_tons` from the tow's `total_loaded_tons`, decrements the tow's `barge_count` by one, and recomputes `max_draft_feet` across the remaining active assignments — yielding 0 when none remain. The hull returns to `operational_status` `IDLE` if it had already been taken `IN_TOW`, and because no cargo ever moved, the hull's `cleanliness_status` and `last_cargo_commodity_id` are deliberately left untouched. Both the tow's `version` and the assignment's `version` increment.

When the river falls, the dispatcher **light-loads** the tow rather than rebuilding it. This operation is permitted only while the tow stands in `BUILDING`, requires a controlling gauge reading to exist for the tow's station at or before the logical clock — absent which it is refused with `ERR_NO_RIVER_STAGE_READING` — and requires `expected_tow_version` to match the tow's current `version` as described in §2.8. For each active assignment whose `barge_draft_feet` exceeds the controlling band's permitted draft, the hull's allowance is computed as the tow's `allowed_draft_feet` minus that hull's `light_draft_feet`, multiplied by twelve inches per foot, multiplied by that hull's `tons_per_inch_immersion`, then truncated downward to a whole ton. The assignment's `loaded_tons` is then set to the smaller of its current value and that allowance — light-loading only ever removes tonnage, never adds it. The assignments are processed in ascending `cut_position` order, after which every touched assignment's `barge_draft_feet` is recomputed and the tow's `total_loaded_tons` and `max_draft_feet` are rolled up again. The tow's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every assignment whose tonnage was rewritten. The operation is a self-loop: the tow remains in `BUILDING` while its payload changes, and it may be repeated against a freshly read `version` as many times as the river demands.

**Sealing** closes the makeup and is the moment every safety gate is re-examined against the state of the world right now. The tow must stand in `BUILDING`, and it must carry at least one active assignment — sealing a tow whose `barge_count` is 0 is refused with `ERR_TOW_INVALID_STATE`. A controlling gauge reading must exist for the tow's `controlling_gauge_station` at or before the logical clock, absent which the seal is refused with `ERR_NO_RIVER_STAGE_READING`. For every active assignment, the prior-cargo pair formed by that hull's last cargo group and that assignment's contract commodity group must not be prohibited, or the seal is refused with `ERR_CARGO_INCOMPATIBLE`; and each hull's cleanliness rank must be at least the rank the matrix requires, or the seal is refused with `ERR_BARGE_CLEANLINESS_INSUFFICIENT`. The tow's `max_draft_feet` must be at or below the controlling band's permitted `max_draft_feet`, failing which the seal is refused with `ERR_DRAFT_EXCEEDS_RESTRICTION`; and the tow's `barge_count` must be at or below that band's `tow_size_cap_barges`, failing which the seal is refused with `ERR_TOW_SIZE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A `CRITICAL` river therefore stops a seventh barge outright, while a `NORMAL` river tolerates forty.

On success the tow moves `BUILDING → SEALED`; every assignment on the tow standing in `PLANNED` advances to `CONFIRMED`; every hull named by those assignments moves to `operational_status` `IN_TOW` from either `IDLE` or `FLEETED`; and the tow's `draft_restriction_band`, `allowed_draft_feet` and `max_draft_feet` are re-pinned one final time. A hull that was `FLEETED` keeps its berth reservation standing in `RESERVED` — sealing does not release the harbor, because the barge has not left yet — and that reservation is settled automatically at departure. The tow's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every assignment and hull the seal touched. Assignments may be listed narrowed by `tow_id`, by `barge_id`, by `freight_contract_id`, by `status` or by `discharge_status`, and that listing is the only channel through which cascade-created assignment state becomes discoverable.

### 3.6. Lock Scheduling, Chamber Posture, and the Transit-Delay Cascade

A tow bound downriver must be scheduled through each chamber on its route. The dispatcher requests a slot by naming the `lock_id` of the chamber and the `tow_id` of the tow, the `requested_start_at` instant the tow asks to begin locking through, and the `expected_tow_version` described in §2.8. An optional `priority_class` declares the lockage priority as `PRIORITY`, `COMMERCIAL` or `RECREATIONAL`, defaulting to `COMMERCIAL` when omitted. The reservation is created in status `REQUESTED`.

The chamber window is computed at that moment. The `cut_count` is the tow's `barge_count` divided by the chamber's `chamber_capacity_barges`, rounded up to a whole number, with a floor of one cut — a tow smaller than one chamber-load still takes one cut. The `scheduled_start_at` is set equal to `requested_start_at`. The `scheduled_end_at` is `scheduled_start_at` advanced by `cut_count` multiplied by the chamber's `cycle_minutes` in minutes. The `occupied_until_at` is `scheduled_end_at` advanced by the chamber's `turnback_buffer_minutes`, and it is that buffered instant — not the end of locking — that marks the exclusive end of chamber occupation for conflict purposes.

Two reservations on the same chamber conflict when the first's `scheduled_start_at` falls strictly before the second's `occupied_until_at` **and** the second's `scheduled_start_at` falls strictly before the first's `occupied_until_at`. Because the interval is half-open, touching endpoints do not conflict: a tow whose `scheduled_start_at` equals another's `occupied_until_at` may proceed. Conflict is evaluated pairwise against reservations on the same chamber whose status is `GRANTED` or `IN_CHAMBER`; reservations still standing in `REQUESTED` never block anything, which is what makes a request queue possible. The test runs at request time and runs again at grant time, and a violation is refused with `ERR_LOCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A request against a chamber standing in `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_LOCK_CLOSED`. On success the tow's `pending_lock_reservation_count` increments by one and the tow's `version` increments as well — which is precisely why a dispatcher booking a second slot must re-read the tow first.

Granting a slot belongs to the chamber's own `LockMaster` and nobody else. The reservation must stand in `REQUESTED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_LOCK_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE`; the chamber must not be `CLOSED`; the tow must stand in `BUILDING`, `SEALED`, `UNDERWAY` or `DELAYED`; and the window must not conflict with any other `GRANTED` or `IN_CHAMBER` reservation on the chamber. A grant moves the reservation `REQUESTED → GRANTED` and increments the chamber's `granted_reservation_count` by one along with the reservation's `version`. Denying instead moves it `REQUESTED → DENIED`, records the optional `reason`, decrements the tow's `pending_lock_reservation_count` by one and increments the reservation's `version`; `DENIED` is terminal.

Rescheduling is the revert edge and is likewise reserved to the chamber's master: a granted slot returns to the request queue rather than being destroyed and rebuilt. It requires a new `requested_start_at`, moves the reservation `GRANTED → REQUESTED`, re-pins `scheduled_start_at` to the new instant and recomputes both `scheduled_end_at` and `occupied_until_at` from the tow's *current* cut count — which may differ from the cut count the original slot was sized with. The chamber's `granted_reservation_count` decrements by one and the reservation's `version` increments. The tow's `pending_lock_reservation_count` is unaffected, because the reservation is still pending.

Entering the chamber is the dispatcher's move: it requires an `actual_start_at`, demands that the reservation stand in `GRANTED`, that the tow stand in `UNDERWAY` or `DELAYED`, and that the chamber not be `CLOSED`; it moves the reservation `GRANTED → IN_CHAMBER`, stamps `actual_start_at`, and increments `version`. Clearing the chamber requires an `actual_end_at` that is at or after `actual_start_at` — an earlier instant is refused with `ERR_UNLOAD_SEQUENCE_INVALID` — and moves the reservation `IN_CHAMBER → CLEARED`, which is terminal. Clearing decrements both the chamber's `granted_reservation_count` and the tow's `pending_lock_reservation_count` by one, and increments the reservation's `version`.

The dispatcher may also cancel a slot from the tow's side while it stands in `REQUESTED` or in `GRANTED`, moving it to the terminal `CANCELLED`. Both branches decrement the tow's `pending_lock_reservation_count` by one and increment the reservation's `version`; the `GRANTED` branch additionally decrements the chamber's `granted_reservation_count` by one, since only a granted slot was ever counted there. Reservations may be listed narrowed by `lock_id`, by `tow_id` or by `status`.

Chamber posture is set by that chamber's `LockMaster` or by any `WaterwayAdmin`, supplying the new `status`. From `OPEN` the chamber may move to `RESTRICTED` or to `CLOSED`; from `RESTRICTED` it may return to `OPEN`; from `CLOSED` it may return to `OPEN`. `RESTRICTED` is purely advisory: it disturbs no reservation and does not block grants, and its only effect is to increment the chamber's `version`. `CLOSED` is severe. Closing a chamber cancels **every** reservation on it standing in `REQUESTED` or `GRANTED`, and charges each tow named by one of those cancelled reservations a flat closure delay of 240 minutes — four hours of lost transit. Re-opening a closed chamber restores nothing: cancelled reservations stay cancelled and the dispatcher must request fresh slots. Chambers may be listed narrowed by `status`.

**Delay attribution** is where the system's longest cascade lives, and it fires from two sources: a cleared lockage, and a chamber closure. On a clear, `delay_minutes` is the number of whole minutes by which `actual_end_at` exceeds `scheduled_end_at`, truncated toward zero and floored at 0 — a tow that clears early or on time accrues nothing. On a closure, each cancelled reservation contributes the flat 240-minute closure delay instead. Either way, the resulting delay figure then walks the following hops, each one caused by the hop before it.

Hop one always runs: the delay is added to the named tow's `accrued_delay_minutes`. The remaining hops run only if the tow stands in `UNDERWAY` and its `accrued_delay_minutes`, after that addition, has reached 120 minutes — two full hours of accumulated delay. When that threshold is met the tow moves `UNDERWAY → DELAYED`, and hop two adds the same delay figure to the `delay_attributed_minutes` of every assignment on that tow standing in `LOADED`, processed in ascending `cut_position` order. Hop three then adds the same figure to the `accrued_delay_minutes` of each distinct freight contract named by those assignments, each contract touched exactly once and processed in ascending contract `created_at` order. Hop four is the contractual remedy: any of those contracts whose `accrued_delay_minutes` has now reached 150 minutes — two and a half hours — and whose `delay_protection_applied` is still false has its `laytime_hours` increased by 12 additional free hours and its `delay_protection_applied` set to true. That extension is granted at most once per contract, no matter how many further delays the voyage suffers. The tow's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every assignment and every contract the cascade touched. When the tow is not `UNDERWAY` at the moment of the delay, only hop one runs and nothing downstream is disturbed.

Returning a delayed tow to service moves it `DELAYED → UNDERWAY` and deliberately leaves `accrued_delay_minutes` exactly where it stands, so that a later delay accumulates on top of the old total rather than starting fresh. The `UNDERWAY → DELAYED → UNDERWAY` cycle may repeat any number of times over a single voyage.

### 3.7. Under Way, Alongside, and Broken Up: the Discharge Axis and the Laytime Clocks

Departure is the single busiest cascade in the voyage. The dispatcher supplies `departed_at`, the tow moves `SEALED → UNDERWAY`, and four things happen at once. The tow's `departed_at` is stamped. Every assignment on the tow standing in `CONFIRMED` advances to `LOADED`. Every fleeting reservation standing in `RESERVED` whose barge holds a `CONFIRMED` assignment on this tow is driven `RESERVED → RELEASED` with its `released_at` set to the tow's `departed_at`, which fixes that reservation's `billable_days` and `fee_cents` by the same arithmetic an ordinary release would use and decrements the berth's `occupied_barge_count` — departure automatically clears the harbor, because a barge cannot be both under way and sitting on a fleeting berth. And every distinct freight contract named by those assignments whose `movement_status` stands in `ACTIVE` advances to `IN_TRANSIT`. The `version` of the tow, of every assignment, of every released reservation and of every contract moved is incremented.

Arrival requires an `arrived_at` instant at or after the tow's `departed_at`, an earlier instant being refused with `ERR_UNLOAD_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. The tow may arrive from either `UNDERWAY` or `DELAYED` — a delayed tow does not have to be resumed first — and both branches behave identically: the tow moves to `ARRIVED`, `arrived_at` is stamped, and the tow's `version` increments.

From that point the **discharge axis** of each assignment advances independently of the makeup axis, one barge at a time. Tendering the barge at the dock supplies its own `arrived_at` and moves `discharge_status` `NOT_ARRIVED → ARRIVED`; it requires that the assignment stand in makeup status `LOADED` and that its tow stand in `ARRIVED`. Starting the unload supplies `unload_started_at`, which must be at or after the assignment's `arrived_at`, and moves `discharge_status` `ARRIVED → UNLOADING`. Finishing the unload supplies `unload_finished_at`, which must be at or after `unload_started_at`, together with `discharged_tons`, the outturn weight actually weighed out at the dock; that weight must be at least zero and must not exceed the assignment's `loaded_tons`, and a value outside that range is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Any of these three called out of sequence on the discharge axis is refused with `ERR_BARGE_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`, while an out-of-order instant is refused with `ERR_UNLOAD_SEQUENCE_INVALID`.

Finishing the unload moves `discharge_status` `UNLOADING → COMPLETED` and closes both laytime clocks permanently. The `gross_laytime_hours` is the elapsed time from the barge's `arrived_at` to its `unload_finished_at`, divided into hours of 3,600 seconds and rounded **up** to a whole hour — this is the tender-to-finish clock, and it runs while the barge waits in line. The `net_laytime_hours` is the elapsed time from `unload_started_at` to `unload_finished_at`, likewise divided into whole hours and rounded up — this is the working clock, and it runs only while cargo is actually moving. Both are fixed at this moment and never recomputed. Rounding up happens per barge, before any comparison against a free allowance is made.

That same completion carries a cross-entity consequence: when **every** non-`DROPPED` assignment of the named freight contract has reached `discharge_status` `COMPLETED`, that contract advances on its movement axis from `IN_TRANSIT` to `DISCHARGED`. A contract with one barge still `UNLOADING` stays `IN_TRANSIT`. The assignment's `version` increments, and so does the contract's when it moved.

Breaking up the tow closes the voyage. It requires the tow to stand in `ARRIVED` and requires that every assignment on it standing in `LOADED` has reached `discharge_status` of either `COMPLETED` or `DISPUTED` — a disputed barge, one whose cargo is under an approved claim, does not hold the tow together. Break-up moves the tow to the terminal `BROKEN_UP`, advances every `LOADED` assignment to the terminal `DISCHARGED`, and returns every hull named by those assignments from `operational_status` `IN_TOW` to `IDLE`. Each of those hulls also has its `cleanliness_status` driven to `DIRTY` on the cleanliness axis, its `last_cargo_commodity_id` set to the `commodity_id` of the freight contract its assignment was moving, and its `last_cleaning_certificate_number` cleared — the certificate the hull held is void the moment it carries cargo, which is exactly what makes the next voyage's compatibility check meaningful. The tow's `version` increments together with the `version` of every assignment and hull touched.

### 3.8. Pricing the Move: the Charge Breakdown, the Invoice, and Payment

The freight bill is built from five buckets, and any authorized reader may ask for the breakdown at any time without changing anything. That read is a pure computation: nothing is persisted and no `version` anywhere is incremented. It accepts an optional `variant` selecting `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, defaulting to `PROVISIONAL` when omitted, and it answers with a payload carrying `variant`, `billable_line_count`, `linehaul_cents`, `fuel_surcharge_cents`, `fleeting_fee_cents`, `cleaning_fee_cents`, `demurrage_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `volume_rebate_cents`, `claim_credit_cents` and `total_cents`. The two variants read different assignment sets, different tonnage fields and different demurrage algorithms; they legitimately disagree, and the disagreement is the point — the provisional view is what the line expects to bill from bill-of-lading weights, and the final view is what it actually bills from outturn weights.

**Linehaul** is the freight itself. The rate is the pinned filing's `base_rate_cents_per_ton_mile`, and the distance is `distance_miles`, the absolute difference between the contract's `destination_mile` and its `origin_mile`. Each billable line contributes its tonnage multiplied by `distance_miles` multiplied by the rate, rounded half up to whole cents — **each line individually, before any summation**. Summing raw line values and rounding once at the end is expressly not the rule and yields a different total. The rounded lines are then summed, enumerated in ascending `cut_position` order so that the audit trail is stable even though the order cannot change the sum. Finally, and only when at least one billable line exists, the contract-level floor applies: `linehaul_cents` is the greater of that sum and the pinned filing's `min_charge_cents`. A contract with no billable lines at all yields `linehaul_cents` of 0, and the minimum charge is not applied to it. The minimum is a floor on the total, applied strictly after the summation, never per line. Under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, the billable lines are the contract's assignments whose makeup `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `LOADED` or `DISCHARGED`, and each contributes its `loaded_tons`, the bill-of-lading weight. Under the `FINAL` variant, the billable lines are the contract's assignments whose `discharge_status` is `COMPLETED` or `DISPUTED`, and each contributes its `discharged_tons`, the outturn weight. Assignments standing in `DROPPED` are excluded from both variants.

**The fuel surcharge** rides on the linehaul and on nothing else — never on demurrage, never on fleeting, never on cleaning, and never on the rebate. It is the `linehaul_cents` figure multiplied by the contract's `pinned_fuel_surcharge_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Because the percentage was pinned at activation from the then-controlling diesel posting, a later spike in `diesel_price_cents_per_gallon` has no effect on an already-active contract.

**Demurrage** is where the two variants diverge most sharply, and both are defensible readings of the same charter language producing different money from identical timestamps. The rate is the pinned filing's `demurrage_rate_cents_per_hour`, and in both variants the lines considered are the contract's assignments whose `discharge_status` is `COMPLETED` or `DISPUTED`; assignments still `UNLOADING`, `ARRIVED` or `NOT_ARRIVED` contribute nothing either way. Under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, each barge carries its own free-laytime allowance and its clock starts the moment it is tendered: for each line, the chargeable hours are that line's `gross_laytime_hours` minus the contract's `laytime_hours`, floored at zero; those chargeable hours are summed across the lines and the sum is multiplied by the rate. A barge that finishes early cannot lend its unused hours to a sister barge. Under the `FINAL` variant, free laytime is pooled across the contract's discharged barges and the clock runs only while cargo is actually moving, plus a fixed notice grace of 6 hours per barge: the hours used are the sum, over the lines, of each line's `net_laytime_hours` plus 6; the pooled allowance is the contract's `laytime_hours` multiplied by the number of lines; and `demurrage_cents` is the used hours minus the pooled allowance, floored at zero, multiplied by the rate. Unused hours on one barge absorb overrun on another. Remember that `laytime_hours` may itself have been increased by 12 hours by the delay-protection remedy of §3.6 before the bill is ever cut.

**Fleeting and cleaning** are pass-through harbor costs. The `cleaning_fee_cents` bucket is the sum of `fee_cents` over the cleaning orders that name this contract in their `freight_contract_id` and stand in status `COMPLETED`; orders that failed inspection carry a `fee_cents` of 0 and therefore contribute nothing even before that filter applies. The `fleeting_fee_cents` bucket differs by variant. Under `PROVISIONAL`, it sums the fleeting reservations naming this contract whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `RELEASED`, taking `estimated_fee_cents` from the reserved ones and `fee_cents` from the released ones. Under `FINAL`, it sums only the reservations standing in `RELEASED`, taking `fee_cents`. Cancelled reservations never contribute to either variant.

**The waterfall** that turns five buckets into a bill has a pinned evaluation order, and getting it wrong is the classic failure here, because the volume rebate reads like a percentage of "the invoice total" — which would be circular — when in fact it is a percentage of the subtotal. Step one: `subtotal_cents` is `linehaul_cents` plus `fuel_surcharge_cents` plus `fleeting_fee_cents` plus `cleaning_fee_cents` plus `demurrage_cents`. Step two: the rebate tier is selected on `subtotal_cents` alone, taking the highest tier whose floor it reaches — a subtotal below 1,000,000 cents earns 0.000, a subtotal of at least 1,000,000 cents but below 5,000,000 earns 0.015, and a subtotal of at least 5,000,000 cents earns 0.030. Step three: `volume_rebate_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents, rounded exactly once after the subtotal is complete. Step four: `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` minus `volume_rebate_cents` minus `claim_credit_cents`, floored at zero. Note carefully that `claim_credit_cents` enters at step four **only**; an approved claim can never move the rebate tier, because `subtotal_cents` was frozen when the invoice was issued.

**Issuing the invoice** is the `ContractManager` operation that converts the `FINAL` variant into stored money. It moves the billing axis `NOT_BILLED → INVOICED` while leaving `movement_status` completely untouched. It requires that the contract's `billing_status` stand in `NOT_BILLED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_BILLING_INVALID_STATE`; that the contract's `movement_status` be `IN_TRANSIT` or `DISCHARGED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`; that `expected_shipper_version` match the shipper's current `version` as described in §2.8; and that at least one assignment of the contract have a `discharge_status` of `COMPLETED` or `DISPUTED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_LINES`. Issue runs every charge rule in its `FINAL` form and fixes `linehaul_cents`, `fuel_surcharge_cents`, `fleeting_fee_cents`, `cleaning_fee_cents`, `demurrage_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `volume_rebate_cents` and `total_cents` onto the contract, where they are never re-derived afterwards. It then increases the shipper's `outstanding_balance_cents` by `total_cents`, and if that balance now exceeds the shipper's `credit_limit_cents` the shipper moves `status` `ACTIVE → CREDIT_HOLD`, which will block that customer's next contract activation until the balance comes down. Both the contract's `version` and the shipper's `version` increment.

**Recording payment** closes the account. It supplies `paid_amount_cents`, which must equal the contract's `total_cents` exactly — partial and over payments alike are refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. The contract's `billing_status` must stand in `INVOICED` or `ADJUSTED`, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_BILLING_INVALID_STATE`, and its `movement_status` must stand in `DISCHARGED`. Payment moves the billing axis to the terminal `PAID` from either of its two legal predecessors and simultaneously moves the movement axis `DISCHARGED → CLOSED`. The shipper's `outstanding_balance_cents` decreases by `total_cents`, and when that leaves the balance at or below the `credit_limit_cents` a shipper standing in `CREDIT_HOLD` returns to `ACTIVE`. Both the contract's `version` and the shipper's `version` increment. `VOID` is the billing axis's other terminal value, reached only when a contract is cancelled before it was ever billed.

### 3.9. Cargo Claims: Assessment, Two Settlement Variants, and the Credit Cascade

When outturn comes back light, or the cargo arrives wet or contaminated, the customer files a **cargo claim** against exactly one discharged barge. A claim may be filed by a `ClaimsAdjuster` on anyone's behalf or by a `Shipper` on its own behalf — the latter only when the named contract's `shipper_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`, and otherwise refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The body names the `freight_contract_id` under which the cargo moved, the `barge_assignment_id` that carried it, a `claim_type` of `SHORTAGE`, `DAMAGE` or `CONTAMINATION`, the `claimed_tons` greater than zero the claimant asserts were lost or spoiled, and a `claimed_amount_cents` greater than zero that the claimant places as a cap on the settlement.

Four guards stand at filing, in ladder order. The claim must be filed within 30 days: if the logical clock stands more than 30 days after the named assignment's `unload_finished_at`, the filing is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. No claim that is neither `WITHDRAWN` nor `DENIED` may already exist for that same `barge_assignment_id`, a repeat being refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_FILED`; note that a withdrawn or denied claim leaves the barge open for a fresh one. The named assignment's own `freight_contract_id` must equal the claim's, a mismatch being refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CONTRACT_MISMATCH`. And for a `SHORTAGE` claim specifically, `claimed_tons` may not exceed the outturn shortfall — the assignment's `loaded_tons` minus its `discharged_tons` — and the assignment must have reached `discharge_status` `COMPLETED` or `DISPUTED`; either failure is refused with `ERR_SHORTAGE_NOT_SUPPORTED`. A successful filing creates the claim in status `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` 0 and increments the owning shipper's `open_claim_count` by one.

Review is the adjuster's cycle. Opening the file moves the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. From there the adjuster may suspend the file pending more information, recording an `info_request_note` and moving the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`; that suspension is permitted only while the claim's `revision_round` is below 2, and an attempt beyond that is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_REVISION_LIMIT`. When the claimant answers, the adjuster records a `response_note` and moves the claim `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, which increments `revision_round` by one and closes that round. The cycle is genuinely a cycle, and it terminates only because the third request for information is refused. Any of these called from the wrong status is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`.

**Assessment** moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → ASSESSED` and computes, in one pass, both settlement variants — because at assessment time the adjuster does not decide which one is paid. The tonnage basis, `shortage_tons`, depends on the claim type: for a `SHORTAGE` claim it is the named assignment's `loaded_tons` minus its `discharged_tons`, the measured outturn shortfall; for a `DAMAGE` or `CONTAMINATION` claim it is simply the `claimed_tons` the claimant asserted. The trade tolerance, `tolerance_tons`, is the assignment's `loaded_tons` multiplied by one half of one percent, rounded half up to three decimal places. The unit value is the contract's `declared_value_cents_per_ton`.

The two variants then treat that tolerance in opposite ways, and both are stored. Under the **deductible** reading, the tolerance is subtracted from the loss: the compensable tonnage is `shortage_tons` minus `tolerance_tons`, floored at zero, and `settlement_deductible_cents` is the smaller of the claimant's `claimed_amount_cents` and that compensable tonnage multiplied by the unit value, rounded half up to whole cents. Under the **franchise** reading, the tolerance is an all-or-nothing trigger: the compensable tonnage is the whole of `shortage_tons` when `shortage_tons` is strictly greater than `tolerance_tons`, and zero otherwise, and `settlement_franchise_cents` is likewise the smaller of `claimed_amount_cents` and that compensable tonnage multiplied by the unit value, rounded half up to whole cents. A loss that sits just above the tolerance therefore pays very differently under the two readings. Assessment also writes `assessed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and increments the claim's `version`; `settlement_cents` remains 0, because nothing has been decided yet.

**Approval** is where money moves, and it may not be performed by the operator who assessed the claim — an attempt by the same `X-User-ID` recorded in `assessed_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The claim must stand in `ASSESSED`, and the named contract's `billing_status` must stand in `INVOICED`; approving against a contract that has not been invoiced, or has already been adjusted or paid, is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_BILLING_INVALID_STATE`. On approval the claim moves `ASSESSED → APPROVED`, which is terminal, and a four-hop chain runs. Hop one selects the money: `settlement_cents` is set to `settlement_franchise_cents` when the contract's `claim_settlement_basis` is `FRANCHISE`, and to `settlement_deductible_cents` otherwise, and `approved_by_user_id` is written from the acting `X-User-ID`. Hop two credits the bill: the named contract adds that `settlement_cents` to its `claim_credit_cents`, recomputes `total_cents` as `subtotal_cents` minus `volume_rebate_cents` minus the new `claim_credit_cents` floored at zero, and moves its billing axis `INVOICED → ADJUSTED`. Hop three moves the ledger: the contract's shipper has its `outstanding_balance_cents` decreased by the same `settlement_cents`. Hop four releases the customer: that shipper returns from `CREDIT_HOLD` to `ACTIVE` when its `outstanding_balance_cents` now stands at or below its `credit_limit_cents`. Separately from that chain, the named barge assignment moves its `discharge_status` from `COMPLETED` to `DISPUTED`, which is why a disputed barge still permits its tow to break up. The shipper's `open_claim_count` decrements by one, and the `version` of the claim, the contract and the shipper all increment.

**Denial** is the mirror image and carries the same segregation-of-duties bar: the operator recorded in `assessed_by_user_id` may not deny either. The claim must stand in `ASSESSED`; denial records the supplied `denial_reason`, moves the claim to the terminal `DENIED`, leaves `settlement_cents` at 0, and leaves the contract's billing entirely untouched — no credit, no `ADJUSTED`, no ledger movement. The shipper's `open_claim_count` decrements by one and the claim's `version` increments.

**Withdrawal** is available to the claimant's side while the claim stands in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, and all three branches behave identically: the claim moves to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, the shipper's `open_claim_count` decrements by one, and the claim's `version` increments. A `ClaimsAdjuster` may withdraw any claim; a `Shipper` may withdraw only a claim whose contract carries a `shipper_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A claim already `ASSESSED` can no longer be withdrawn and is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. Claims may be listed narrowed by `freight_contract_id`, by `barge_assignment_id` or by `status`.
