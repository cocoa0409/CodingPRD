# Migrant Education Identification & Recruitment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state migrant education program lives or dies by the quality of its identification and recruitment record. Field recruiters travel to agricultural and fishing areas during the weeks when migratory families actually arrive, interview parents and guardians about the move that brought them, and translate what they hear into a certificate of eligibility that will survive a federal monitoring visit three years later. Today that work is carried in spreadsheets and paper interview forms: nobody can say how many certificates are awaiting state review, whether the recruiter deciding a quality-control re-interview is the same person who wrote the original certificate, or how many instructional hours a child has actually received against the hours the coordinator promised. When a family moves to a second state mid-season, the receiving state re-interviews from scratch because the prior record cannot be located, and secondary credits earned in the sending state are simply lost.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service is mounted under the `/mep` prefix and owns the whole chain: recruitment geography and recruiter rostering, seasonal coverage assignment, child identification and priority-for-services scoring, qualifying-move adjudication, interview documentation, the certificate of eligibility lifecycle including quality-control sampling and correction rounds, the thirty-six-month continuation-of-eligibility term and its reidentification grace period, record transfer through the national exchange with credit reconciliation, and service plan development with an auditable hour and credit ledger.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **every consequence is recorded, never inferred** — when the state office approves a certificate, the resulting eligibility term, the child's eligibility status, the child's priority score and the hour allocation on every open service plan are all written in the same request, so no reader ever has to reconstruct them. Second, **every reversal restores the exact prior value** — cancelling a coverage assignment returns the area's capacity slot, reversing a delivered session returns the child's hour ledger to the value it held before the session was posted, and rejecting a reconciled transfer returns the credit counters to their pre-transfer state, with no residual drift.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Caller Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. There are exactly five roles, and their spellings are fixed: `StateProgramAdmin`, `Recruiter`, `QualityControlReviewer`, `ServiceCoordinator`, and `ExchangeLiaison`.

If either header is absent — or if `X-User-ID` is present but empty or whitespace-only — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Authentication is also where entity resolution happens for the two roles that are backed by a persisted record: if `X-User-Role` is `Recruiter` or `QualityControlReviewer` and `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing recruiter row, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` as well — not with a 404. Once the caller is authenticated, the service checks whether the acting role appears in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called; a valid role calling an operation it does not own is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The authorized-role lists are given in §4.

The identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because two roles share one backing table:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Recruiter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Recruiter`. That identifier is stamped by the server into `InterviewRecord.recruiter_id` when interview documentation is filed and into `CertificateOfEligibility.initiating_recruiter_id` when a certificate is opened; in both cases a value supplied in the request body is ignored. Ownership checks compare a resource's recruiter reference to `X-User-ID` — a certificate may only be corrected or submitted by the recruiter whose id sits in its `initiating_recruiter_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `QualityControlReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Recruiter` — the same table backs both roles, so one human being can hold both hats. This is precisely why quality-control decisions carry a segregation-of-duties comparison: the deciding reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `initiating_recruiter_id` of the certificate under review. The reviewer's `id` is recorded on the review as `reviewer_recruiter_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `StateProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier for the state migrant education office and is not tied to any persisted entity. The server does not compare it to any record's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; plans and delivery records store no coordinator reference, so the value is never compared to an `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ExchangeLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; transfer requests store no liaison reference and the value is never compared to an `id`.

The `StateProgramAdmin` owns the area and recruiter registries, coverage assignment, move adjudication, certificate approval and invalidation, the quality-control sample run, the continuation-of-eligibility sweep, and the logical clock. The `Recruiter` identifies children, records qualifying moves, opens and corrects certificates, and performs reidentification. The `QualityControlReviewer` decides quality-control reviews and, alongside the `Recruiter`, may file interview documentation. The `ServiceCoordinator` drafts, activates and closes service plans and posts or reverses delivered sessions, and shares the student-profile amendment operation with the `Recruiter`. The `ExchangeLiaison` opens and drives national-exchange transfer requests and touches no certificate, plan or delivery. Every read endpoint in §4 is open to all five roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; the prefix is followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100, and a value outside that inclusive range is a shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and must not be negative.

List results are returned newest-first — ordered by `created_at` descending, with `id` ascending used as the tie-break when two rows share a `created_at`. This ordering is worth internalising because several calculations in this specification walk the same rows oldest-first: an hour-allocation run iterates a student's plans by `created_at` ascending, so a coordinator reading the plan list sees exactly the reverse of the allocation order.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses omit these four fields. Where a list endpoint declares several filters, the filters combine conjunctively — every supplied filter must match for a row to be returned. A filter whose value is drawn from a declared enumeration — a `status`, a `coverage_role`, a `plan_type` and so on — rejects a value outside that member set as a shape violation (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 422) rather than returning an empty page, whereas a filter carrying a free-form identifier or reference string simply matches nothing and returns an empty page. Boolean filters accept `true` or `false` in any letter case as well as `1` and `0`, and reject anything else as a shape violation. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client influence; a client-supplied `id` is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, stamped when the row is first written and never changed afterwards.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every accepted mutation of that row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation. `version` is the currency of the stale-write guards described in §2.7, so its increments are specified individually for every operation in §3: a mutation that touches three rows increments three counters. Two refinements follow from the way the service books those increments. A row the request itself created is exempt for that request: it is returned with `version` 1 and with `updated_at` still equal to `created_at` even when a later hop of the same request rewrites its fields — the freshly drafted service plan that the allocation run immediately re-costs is still read back at version 1. And a row that already existed when the request arrived advances by exactly 1 across the whole request however many hops touch it; the per-term steps of the continuation sweep of §3.7 are the single exception, since one sweep call can move several of one child's terms and increments that child's `version` once for each term it moves.

Both stamps are read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from the wall clock, and the service issues them from one shared sequence that never repeats and never regresses: a stamp is the current logical clock instant when that instant is later than the last stamp the service issued, and otherwise it is that last stamp advanced by one millisecond. The first row written after the clock has been moved forward past every stamp already issued therefore carries the new clock instant exactly, while further rows written without moving the clock again step forward in whole milliseconds — which is what keeps `created_at` an ordering over rows written in sequence. A row's `updated_at` is stamped once, equal to its `created_at`, when the row is first written; each later accepted mutation of that row draws a fresh stamp from the same sequence.

Two entities are deliberately immutable and therefore carry neither field. An `InterviewRecord` exposes `id` and `created_at` only — there is no `updated_at`, no `version`, no amendment operation and no delete; a mis-recorded interview is corrected by filing a new one. A `ServiceDelivery` is likewise exposed with `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`; a mis-posted session is corrected by posting a compensating reversal record, which is itself a new immutable row.

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

Nothing in this service reads the wall clock, save for the `meta.api_request_timestamp` of §2.2 — the real-world instant at which the server handled the request, and the only wall-clock read anywhere in the service. A single logical clock supplies the current instant to every date-sensitive rule: the 365-day move-recency window that feeds priority scoring, the future-date checks on reported moves, interviews and delivered sessions, the boundary comparisons of the continuation-of-eligibility sweep, the reidentification grace window, the `created_at` and `updated_at` row stamps under the sequencing rule of §2.4, and the `adjudicated_on` and `decided_on` stamps — the last two taken as the clock instant itself, with no sequencing adjustment.

The logical clock is initialised to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` at every server start; all state lives in process memory, so each start also begins from an entirely empty store with nothing seeded, preloaded or restored from disk. A `StateProgramAdmin` may set it to any full ISO 8601 UTC instant by supplying `to_iso8601`; the response `data` reports the resulting clock as `current_utc` and carries no other field. The supplied string is read generously: the date and time may be separated by `T` or by a space, the seconds and the fractional seconds are both optional, and the zone designator may be a trailing `Z`, an explicit `±HH:MM` offset (which is normalised to UTC) or absent altogether (in which case the instant is read as already being UTC). `current_utc`, like the instant-valued `adjudicated_on` and `decided_on`, is rendered in the same shape as a row stamp — exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, with any precision in the supplied instant finer than a millisecond truncated rather than rounded when it is rendered. Moving the clock backwards is permitted, so a single session can probe both sides of a boundary. Any behaviour that depends on time must be driven by setting the clock explicitly rather than by assuming a particular starting date.

Where a rule speaks of "the logical clock date", it means the calendar date in UTC of the current logical clock instant — the time-of-day component is discarded for date comparisons but preserved in the instant-valued fields `adjudicated_on` and `decided_on`.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision, Date Arithmetic and Rounding Discipline

All monetary-style quantities in this domain — instructional hours, allocated hours and secondary credits — are carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half up. Half up means a value whose third decimal digit is 5 or greater rounds the second decimal digit away from zero. Every hour and credit field returned by the API is rendered with two decimals, including whole values, and it is rendered as a JSON number rather than a string — `75.00`, never `"75.00"` and never `75.0`.

Where a rounding sequence matters, this document pins it, and implementations must not "simplify" the order. Two places are load-bearing: hour allocation rounds each plan's raw share to two decimals **before** the shares are summed (summing first and rounding afterwards produces a different distribution), and credit reconciliation applies rounding exactly twice — once when the capped transfer amount is produced and once when the awarded total is produced — never inside the tier lookup.

Priority scoring is integer arithmetic. Where the formula divides interrupted school days by five, the quotient is rounded **up** to the next whole number before it is multiplied by the per-block weight, and the caps and floors are applied in the written order.

Date arithmetic uses whole calendar dates in ISO 8601 `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no rounding. Adding a number of calendar months to a date keeps the day of the month; when the target month has no such day, the result is the last day of the target month. Adding a number of years is defined as adding twelve times that number of months. Every date interval in this specification is closed at both ends — first day and last day are both inside the interval — unless the text says otherwise, and the day-before-a-boundary constructions in the eligibility-window rules are stated explicitly rather than implied.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Capacity- and Plan-Bearing Writes

Two write operations reason about a parent record's aggregate state, and both require the client to prove it is reasoning about the state the server actually holds. Each takes a mandatory version field — it is not optional, and omitting it is a shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

A `POST /mep/service-plans` request must carry `expected_student_version` holding the `version` of the target `Student` as the client last observed it. If that number does not equal the stored `Student.version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The guard exists because a plan create legitimately displaces an earlier plan of the same `plan_type`, moving it to `SUPERSEDED`; a client working from an out-of-date view of the student would silently discard planning work it never saw. An accepted create advances `Student.version`, which means a second create replayed with the same `expected_student_version` will now be rejected — as will any create carrying a version read before an intervening student amendment.

A `POST /mep/recruiter-assignments` request must carry `expected_area_version` holding the `version` of the target `AgriculturalArea` as the client last observed it. If that number does not equal the stored `AgriculturalArea.version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AREA_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. Assignment creation consumes area coverage capacity and rewrites `AgriculturalArea.assigned_primary_slots_used`, so a client holding a stale view of the area is reasoning about a capacity picture the server has already left behind. Because every accepted assignment create — and every area amendment — advances the area's `version`, a client placing several assignments in one area must re-read the area between creates.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. Activation, closure, submission, approval, invalidation, the quality-control decision, the sweep, reidentification and every transfer step are guarded by state and business rules alone.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

When several checks could fail on the same request, the service always reports the failure that comes first on the following ladder. The ladder is total: rungs that share a position guard disjoint sets of endpoints, so exactly one error is possible for any given call. Implementations must evaluate in this order rather than in whatever order is convenient, because the observable error code is part of the contract.

1. **Caller identity** — headers present, role recognised, and for `Recruiter` and `QualityControlReviewer` the `X-User-ID` resolves to a recruiter row: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization** — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Path resource existence** — every path-templated identifier is resolved left to right, so in a nested path an unresolvable earlier segment reports before a later one. The code names the missing resource type: `ERR_AREA_NOT_FOUND` for `area_id`, `ERR_RECRUITER_NOT_FOUND` for `recruiter_id`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `assignment_id`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND` for `student_id`, `ERR_MOVE_NOT_FOUND` for `move_id`, `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for `interview_id`, `ERR_COE_NOT_FOUND` for `coe_id`, `ERR_QC_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for `review_id`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND` for `term_id`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND` for `request_id`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` for `plan_id`, and `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND` for `delivery_id` — all HTTP 404.
4. **Request shape** — on every endpoint that accepts a body or query parameters: required fields present, enum values inside their declared member sets, numbers inside their declared ranges, dates well-formed ISO 8601 `YYYY-MM-DD`, `limit` within 1 to 100 and `offset` at least 0, state codes exactly two uppercase letters, an area's `peak_season_start_date` no later than its `peak_season_end_date`, an assignment's `starts_on` no later than its `ends_on`, a transfer request's `holding_state_code` different from its `requesting_state_code`, and `coe_id` supplied on an interview record exactly when `interview_kind` is `QC_REINTERVIEW`. Failures are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). A body that is absent or empty is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose fields are all optional accepts a call with no body at all; a body that is not valid JSON, or is valid JSON that is not an object, fails on this rung too. Throughout the service an explicit `null` is treated exactly as an absent key — it neither satisfies a required field nor displaces a declared default; a field declared as an integer also accepts a numeric string or a float carrying no fractional part, so `"5"` and `5.0` are both read as 5; a field declared as a two-decimal quantity likewise accepts a numeric string such as `"1.25"`; and a field declared as a boolean accepts only a JSON `true` or `false` literal. An endpoint that declares no body fields whatever — certificate submission, certificate approval, the quality-control sample run, the continuation-of-eligibility sweep, a transfer request's receive and reconcile steps, plan activation and delivery reversal among them — never reads the payload at all, so anything sent as a body to one of those is ignored rather than validated, malformed JSON included.
5. **Body-referenced resource existence** — foreign keys named in the body are resolved in the order the endpoint declares them: `area_id`, then `recruiter_id`, then `student_id`, then `move_id`, then `plan_id`, then `coe_id`, then `reinterview_id`, then `matched_prior_student_id`, then `new_move_id`. The reported code is the same 404 code as the corresponding path check — `ERR_AREA_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable body `area_id`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `student_id` or `matched_prior_student_id`, `ERR_MOVE_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `move_id` or `new_move_id`, `ERR_COE_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `coe_id`, `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `reinterview_id`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `plan_id`, and `ERR_RECRUITER_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `recruiter_id`.
6. **State-machine position** — `ERR_COE_NOT_IN_DRAFT`, `ERR_COE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_QC_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_MOVE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TERM_INVALID_STATE` and `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`, all HTTP 409. The precise state sets each one guards are stated with the workflows in §3.
7. **Stale-version guards** — `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_AREA_VERSION`, both HTTP 412, as specified in §2.7. Note the consequence of this position: a plan create against a student in the wrong eligibility posture still reports the stale-version failure first if the version is also wrong.
8. **Segregation of duties** on a quality-control decision — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
9. **Reviewer credential** — `ERR_QC_CREDENTIAL_REQUIRED` (403). Segregation of duties therefore outranks the credential check: a reviewer who both initiated the certificate and lacks the quality-control credential is told about the conflict of interest, not about the credential.
10. **Qualifying-work check** on move adjudication and certificate creation — `ERR_MOVE_NOT_QUALIFYING` (422).
11. **Move verification** on certificate submission and reidentification — `ERR_MOVE_NOT_VERIFIED` (409).
12. **Supporting interview documentation** on certificate submission — `ERR_INTERVIEW_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING` (409).
13. **Re-interview linkage** on a quality-control decision — `ERR_REINTERVIEW_STUDENT_MISMATCH` (422).
14. **Duplicate live certificate** on certificate submission — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_COE` (409). Submission therefore reports its four business failures in this order: move not verified, then missing supporting interview, then — only after both pass — the duplicate-certificate conflict.
15. **Self-match** on a transfer match — `ERR_TRANSFER_SELF_MATCH` (422).
16. **Credit permissibility** on a delivered session — `ERR_CREDITS_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_PLAN_TYPE` (422).
17. **Future-dating** of `move_date`, `conducted_on` and `delivered_on` — `ERR_MOVE_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422).
18. **Residency ordering** on certificate creation, certificate correction and reidentification — `ERR_RESIDENCY_DATE_BEFORE_MOVE` (422).
19. **Peak-season containment** on assignment creation — `ERR_PEAK_SEASON_NOT_COVERED` (422).
20. **Recruiter interval conflict** on assignment creation — `ERR_RECRUITER_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP` (409).
21. **Reidentification grace window** — `ERR_REIDENTIFICATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
22. **Recruiter standing** on assignment creation and activation — `ERR_RECRUITER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
23. **Area standing** on assignment creation and activation — `ERR_AREA_NOT_OPEN` (409).
24. **Service eligibility** on plan activation — `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_SERVICES` (409).
25. **Out-of-school plan gate** — `ERR_OSY_PLAN_REQUIRES_OUT_OF_SCHOOL` (422).
26. **Enrolled plan gate** — `ERR_SUPPLEMENTAL_REQUIRES_ENROLLED` (422).
27. **Hour and credit grid** on a delivered session — `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_HOURS` (422).
28. **Reversibility** of a delivery record — `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_REVERSIBLE` (409).
29. **Area coverage capacity** on assignment creation — `ERR_AREA_COVERAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). This is the last of the five assignment-creation business checks, so an assignment that both fails peak-season containment and would exceed capacity reports the peak-season failure.
30. **Non-empty quality-control pool** — `ERR_NO_QC_CANDIDATES` (409).

### 2.9. Cascades, Conserved Quantities and Derived Mirrors

Three structural habits recur throughout §3 and are stated once here.

**Cascades are chains, not single writes.** Certificate approval and certificate invalidation each set off a multi-hop chain in which each hop is caused by the hop before it, and every hop's writes are part of the same request. The specification names the hops explicitly because the later hops are the ones an implementer is most likely to miss: approval runs four hops, ending in a re-run of priority scoring and hour allocation, and invalidation runs three, ending in the closure of every open service plan. Each row a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1 — a hop that writes one field on four plans increments four plan versions.

**Conserved quantities return to their prior value exactly.** Three counters in this system are defined as aggregates over a filtered row set rather than as running totals nudged by each operation, and each has a compensating action that restores it precisely: `AgriculturalArea.assigned_primary_slots_used` against assignment cancellation, `Student.total_service_hours_ledger` against session reversal, and `Student.transferred_credits_count` against transfer rejection. Implementations that maintain these as increment/decrement counters must produce the same values as the aggregate definition in every case.

**Derived mirrors are recomputed, never edited.** Several fields exist only to hold the result of a calculation — the two counters just named, `Recruiter.active_assignment_count`, `Student.pfs_score`, `Student.pfs_variant`, `Student.pfs_flag`, `Student.eligibility_expires_on`, `Student.locally_accrued_credits`, `Student.total_awarded_credits`, `ServicePlan.priority_tier`, `ServicePlan.allocated_hours`, `ServicePlan.expected_service_hours`, `ServicePlan.delivered_hours`, `CertificateOfEligibility.risk_flag_count` and the certificate's four window fields, `QualifyingMove.student_age_at_move`, `ServiceDelivery.is_reversed`, and the term's `term_start_on`, `term_end_on` and `reidentification_due_on`. None of them is writable from a request body. Supplying one is ignored rather than rejected, and the same is true of the two student state dimensions `eligibility_status` and `service_status`: an amendment carrying `eligibility_status`, `service_status`, `pfs_score` or `total_service_hours_ledger` succeeds and simply drops those keys.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Recruitment Geography and the Recruiter Roster

Before any child can be identified, the state office has to describe where recruitment happens. An **agricultural area** is a named recruitment territory: it carries a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `state_code` of the state that owns it as exactly two uppercase letters, and a `primary_activity` naming the qualifying-work sector that dominates it — either `AGRICULTURE` or `FISHING`. Because migratory arrival is seasonal, each area also declares the calendar window in which its qualifying work peaks: `peak_season_start_date` is the first day of that window and `peak_season_end_date` is the last, both inclusive, and an area whose start date falls after its end date is refused as a shape violation.

Two further attributes govern how much coverage an area can absorb. `recruiter_capacity` is the ceiling on how many coverage assignments of **any** kind may be in force on any single calendar date once the handoff buffer of §3.2 has been applied; it accepts whole numbers from 1 through 12 and, when omitted at creation, defaults to 1. `status` records whether the area currently accepts new coverage and is either `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, defaulting to `OPEN`. Alongside these sits a derived counter, `assigned_primary_slots_used`, defined as the number of coverage assignments for this area whose `coverage_role` is `PRIMARY` and whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE`; it starts at 0 and is republished on every assignment create, activation and closure. Note the deliberate asymmetry: the capacity ceiling counts assignments of both coverage roles, while this counter counts only the primary ones, so an area at its capacity ceiling may still report a small number of used primary slots.

Only the state office may open or amend an area, and amendment is narrow: `status`, `recruiter_capacity`, `peak_season_start_date` and `peak_season_end_date` are the only writable keys, and any other key in the body — including `name`, `state_code`, `primary_activity` or `assigned_primary_slots_used` — is silently ignored rather than rejected. When an amendment supplies only one of the two peak-season dates, the ordering rule is applied to the effective pair — the supplied date measured against the stored one — and a violation is the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` shape failure as at creation. Every accepted amendment increments the area's `version` by 1 and refreshes `updated_at`, and that increment is what invalidates any `expected_area_version` a client was holding; an amendment that carries no writable key at all is still accepted and still takes that increment. Lowering `recruiter_capacity` below the number of slots already used is explicitly allowed; existing assignments are never retro-rejected, and the reduced ceiling simply governs future creates. Reading a single area returns the live `assigned_primary_slots_used` and the current `version`, which is how a client obtains the value it must echo back when placing an assignment; the counter is recomputed while that read is served, and the read itself never increments `version` and never refreshes `updated_at`.

The **recruiter** roster is equally thin. A recruiter carries `full_name` (1 to 120 characters, and the name printed on certificates), the `state_code` of the employing state as two uppercase letters, a `credential_level` of `BASIC`, `CERTIFIED` or `QC_CERTIFIED` defaulting to `BASIC`, and an employment `status` of `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` defaulting to `ACTIVE`. The derived `active_assignment_count` counts the recruiter's assignments in `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` status and starts at 0; reading a single recruiter recomputes it in the same way an area read recomputes its slot counter, and likewise moves neither `version` nor `updated_at`. `credential_level` is not decoration: only a recruiter holding `QC_CERTIFIED` may decide a quality-control review, and `BASIC` or `CERTIFIED` reviewers are turned away with `ERR_QC_CREDENTIAL_REQUIRED`. Amendment by the state office is limited to `status`, `credential_level` and `full_name`, any other key being silently ignored, and each accepted amendment bumps `version` by 1 — including an amendment that carries no writable key at all. Suspending a recruiter blocks *new* coverage but leaves existing `PLANNED` and `ACTIVE` assignments exactly as they stand — suspension is prospective, not retroactive — and it does not touch that recruiter's own access to the API: a `SUSPENDED` recruiter still resolves successfully at rung 1 of §2.8 and may still file interviews, open and correct certificates, and decide quality-control reviews.

### 3.2. Placing Seasonal Coverage

A **coverage assignment** joins one recruiter to one area for a closed interval of calendar dates. The payload names the `recruiter_id` and the `area_id`, declares a `coverage_role` of `PRIMARY` or `BACKUP`, and supplies `starts_on` and `ends_on` — both dates inside the coverage period, with `starts_on` no later than `ends_on`. It must also echo `expected_area_version`, the guard described in §2.7. A new assignment always begins in `PLANNED` status, and `cancelled_reason` — a free-text field of up to 240 characters — starts null.

The two coverage roles carry genuinely different obligations. A `PRIMARY` assignment is the area's season-long anchor and its interval must **contain** the area's whole peak season: `starts_on` must be on or before `peak_season_start_date` and `ends_on` must be on or after `peak_season_end_date`. An assignment that starts even one day into the peak season, or ends one day before it closes, is refused with `ERR_PEAK_SEASON_NOT_COVERED`. A `BACKUP` assignment skips that containment rule entirely — and *only* that rule. It is still checked for recruiter conflict and still consumes area capacity.

Recruiter conflict is a pairwise interval test with no buffer applied, and it governs both coverage roles. Consider the candidate interval against every other assignment of the same recruiter in `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` status, in any area and under either coverage role: the two conflict when the candidate's `starts_on` is on or before the existing `ends_on` **and** the existing `starts_on` is on or before the candidate's `ends_on`. Any such intersection is refused with `ERR_RECRUITER_ASSIGNMENT_OVERLAP`. Because both interval ends are inclusive and no buffer is involved, back-to-back coverage is legal: an assignment beginning on the day immediately after an existing assignment's last day does not conflict. Assignments in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` status are invisible to this test.

Area capacity is a different geometry, and the difference is the point rather than an accident. Here every existing assignment of the same area in `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` status — again of either coverage role — is widened by a **three-day handoff buffer on each side**, reflecting the days a departing recruiter spends briefing an arriving one. For each calendar date from the candidate's `starts_on` through its `ends_on` inclusive, count how many widened intervals cover that date, meaning the date falls on or after the existing `starts_on` minus three days and on or before the existing `ends_on` plus three days. Add one for the candidate itself, take the largest such count across all the candidate's dates, and if that maximum exceeds the area's `recruiter_capacity` the create is refused with `ERR_AREA_COVERAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The buffer is what makes near-adjacency expensive: a candidate that begins exactly four days after an existing interval's last day escapes the buffered overlap and passes, while one beginning three days after does not. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` assignments are excluded from this count too.

Two more gates apply at creation, and the same two are re-applied later at activation: the target recruiter's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RECRUITER_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the target area's `status` must be `OPEN`, or it is refused with `ERR_AREA_NOT_OPEN`. An accepted create republishes `AgriculturalArea.assigned_primary_slots_used` — which increases by one only when the new assignment is `PRIMARY` — increments the area's `version` by 1, and republishes `Recruiter.active_assignment_count`, which increases by one for either coverage role. That last republication refreshes the recruiter's `updated_at` but deliberately leaves the recruiter's `version` exactly where it was — in contrast to the activation and closure steps below, which do increment it.

Activating an assignment moves it `PLANNED → ACTIVE`. Only the state office may do it, the assignment must currently be `PLANNED` (otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`), and the two standing gates are re-evaluated against live state rather than the state that existed when the assignment was created: a recruiter suspended in the interim blocks activation with `ERR_RECRUITER_NOT_ACTIVE`, and an area closed in the interim blocks it with `ERR_AREA_NOT_OPEN`. Activation then recomputes both counters — and because `PLANNED` and `ACTIVE` are both inside each counter's filter, neither value changes — yet the area's `version` and the recruiter's `version` are still each incremented by 1, along with the assignment's own.

Closing an assignment takes an `outcome` and, optionally, a `cancelled_reason`. An `outcome` of `COMPLETED` requires the assignment to be `ACTIVE` and produces the transition `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`; a `cancelled_reason` supplied alongside it is ignored. An `outcome` of `CANCELLED` is permitted from either `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE`, produces `PLANNED → CANCELLED` or `ACTIVE → CANCELLED`, and stores the supplied `cancelled_reason` on the row. Attempting `COMPLETED` on a `PLANNED` assignment, or any closure of an assignment already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. All three closing transitions have the same effect on the surrounding records: `assigned_primary_slots_used` decreases by exactly one when the closed assignment was `PRIMARY` and is unchanged when it was `BACKUP`, restoring the value the area held before that assignment existed; `active_assignment_count` decreases by exactly one regardless of coverage role; and the area's `version`, the recruiter's `version` and the assignment's own `version` are each incremented by 1. Assignments can be surveyed with filters on `recruiter_id`, `area_id`, `coverage_role` and `status`.

### 3.3. Identifying a Child and Scoring Priority for Services

Identification is a `Recruiter` activity. The recruiter records the child's `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), a `birth_date` that must be strictly earlier than the logical clock date, the `current_state_code` of the state now holding the record as two uppercase letters, and the `area_id` of the recruitment area the family currently resides in. A `birth_date` falling on or after the logical clock date is a shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — not as a future-dating failure — and being a shape check it is reported before the `area_id` is resolved. The academic profile follows: `schooling_status` is either `ENROLLED` or `OUT_OF_SCHOOL` and defaults to `ENROLLED`; `grade_level` accepts whole numbers from 0 through 12 and defaults to null, which is the expected value for out-of-school youth; `school_days_interrupted` records instructional days missed because of qualifying moves in the current program year, accepts 0 through 180 and defaults to 0; `credit_deficit` records whole secondary credits short of the on-track total, accepts 0 through 40 and defaults to 0; and four booleans — `math_below_grade`, `reading_below_grade`, `has_hs_credential` and `primary_language_not_english` — each default to false. The record's `prior_record_student_id` starts null and is written only by the national exchange, never by a recruiter. A newly identified child also starts with `eligibility_expires_on` null and with all four derived ledger figures — `total_service_hours_ledger`, `transferred_credits_count`, `locally_accrued_credits` and `total_awarded_credits` — at 0.00.

A child's record carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and they never read each other. `eligibility_status` tracks the identification and certification story and takes one of `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `PENDING_DETERMINATION`, `ELIGIBLE`, `CONTINUATION` or `TERM_EXPIRED`, starting at `NOT_ELIGIBLE`; it is advanced only by certificate and eligibility-term events. `service_status` tracks the delivery story and takes one of `UNSERVED`, `PLAN_DRAFTED`, `RECEIVING_SERVICES` or `EXITED`, starting at `UNSERVED`; it is a derived aggregate over the child's service plans. A newly identified child therefore reads `NOT_ELIGIBLE` and `UNSERVED` simultaneously, and each dimension advances on its own triggers — a child can be `RECEIVING_SERVICES` while their eligibility reads `CONTINUATION`, and the two are never reconciled against each other.

**Priority for services** is the scored judgement the program uses to decide who gets scarce instructional hours. Three fields hold it: `pfs_score`, a whole number from 0 through 100; `pfs_variant`, recording which of the two scoring pathways produced it, either `INTERRUPTION_WEIGHTED` or `OSY_PATHWAY`; and `pfs_flag`, true exactly when `pfs_score` is 60 or greater — the threshold is inclusive, so a score of exactly 60 raises the flag. All three are computed and persisted at exactly five moments: at identification, when the profile is amended, when a qualifying move is adjudicated, as the final hop of the approval cascade, and as part of the invalidation cascade. The continuation sweep of §3.7 does **not** recompute them, so a term that expires, warns or lapses leaves the child's priority score, variant and flag exactly as they stood.

Scoring begins with the same shared term in both pathways. Count the child's qualifying moves that are in `VERIFIED` status and whose `move_date` falls within the 365-day window ending on the logical clock date, inclusive at both ends — a move dated exactly 365 days before the clock counts, and so does one dated on the clock date itself. Moves in `RECORDED` or `REJECTED` status never count. That count is reported as `recent_verified_moves`, and it converts into `move_band_points` on a step scale: zero recent verified moves scores 0 points, one scores 18, two scores 26, and three or more scores 30.

The `INTERRUPTION_WEIGHTED` pathway applies when `schooling_status` is `ENROLLED`. `interruption_points` are earned in five-day blocks: divide `school_days_interrupted` by five, round that quotient **up** to the next whole number, multiply by 4, and take whichever is smaller — that product or 24. `academic_points` add 14 when `math_below_grade` is true and a further 14 when `reading_below_grade` is true, for a maximum of 28. `credit_points` are `credit_deficit` multiplied by 3, or 18, whichever is smaller. This pathway consults neither the high-school credential nor the home-language flag, so `credential_points` and `language_points` are both 0. The `raw_score` is the sum of `move_band_points`, `interruption_points`, `academic_points` and `credit_points`; `pfs_score` is that raw sum or 100, whichever is smaller. This pathway has no floor, so `floor_applied` is always false under it.

The `OSY_PATHWAY` applies when `schooling_status` is `OUT_OF_SCHOOL`, and it re-weights the same child. `interruption_points` again work in five-day blocks — `school_days_interrupted` divided by five and rounded up, then multiplied by 6 — capped at 30 rather than 24. The placement flags are not consulted at all under this pathway, so `academic_points` is 0, and neither is `credit_deficit`, so `credit_points` is 0. In their place, `credential_points` contribute 20 when `has_hs_credential` is false and 0 when the child already holds a diploma or equivalency, and `language_points` contribute 10 when `primary_language_not_english` is true and 0 otherwise. The `raw_score` is the sum of `move_band_points`, `interruption_points`, `credential_points` and `language_points`. This pathway then applies a floor before the ceiling: take whichever is larger, the raw sum or 55, then take whichever is smaller, that result or 100. `floor_applied` is true exactly when the raw sum was below 55. The consequence is that one stored profile scores differently under the two pathways, and flipping `schooling_status` on an existing child can move `pfs_flag` in either direction.

Amending a child's profile is shared between the `Recruiter` and the `ServiceCoordinator`, and the writable set is `current_state_code`, `area_id`, `schooling_status`, `grade_level`, `school_days_interrupted`, `math_below_grade`, `reading_below_grade`, `credit_deficit`, `has_hs_credential` and `primary_language_not_english`. Neither state dimension nor any derived field is writable, as §2.9 sets out. `grade_level` is the one field in this service for which an explicit `null` is meaningful rather than ignored: sending `grade_level` as `null` clears the stored grade back to null, which is how a child who has left school is recorded, whereas every other writable key sent as `null` is simply skipped. No amendment is gated on the child's existing plans either — a child holding an `ACTIVE` `SUPPLEMENTAL_INSTRUCTION` plan may be amended to `OUT_OF_SCHOOL`, and that plan is left exactly as it stands. Every accepted amendment recomputes and persists `pfs_score`, `pfs_variant` and `pfs_flag`; when — and only when — `pfs_flag` actually changed value, the hour-allocation rule of §3.9 is re-run across the child's plans in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status, because the budget those plans divide depends on the flag. The amendment increments the child's `version` by 1 — including an amendment that carries no writable key at all — which invalidates any `expected_student_version` a coordinator was holding.

A separate read exposes the whole scoring derivation without persisting anything. It returns `student_id`, `pfs_variant`, `pfs_score`, `pfs_flag`, `recent_verified_moves`, `move_band_points`, `interruption_points`, `academic_points`, `credential_points`, `language_points`, `credit_points`, `raw_score` and `floor_applied`, evaluated against the current logical clock. The components the active pathway does not consume come back as 0 — `academic_points` and `credit_points` under `OSY_PATHWAY`, `credential_points` and `language_points` under `INTERRUPTION_WEIGHTED` — which is what lets a single response distinguish the two pathways. This read never writes and never touches `version`.

The remaining derived fields on a child's record are ledgers rather than scores, and each is described where it is written: `eligibility_expires_on` mirrors the child's live term (§3.7), `total_service_hours_ledger` accumulates delivered instruction (§3.9), and `transferred_credits_count`, `locally_accrued_credits` and `total_awarded_credits` hold the credit picture assembled from the national exchange and from credit-accrual sessions (§3.8 and §3.9). Children can be surveyed with filters on `area_id`, `prior_record_student_id`, `eligibility_status`, `service_status`, `schooling_status` and `pfs_flag`.

### 3.4. Recording, Documenting and Adjudicating the Qualifying Move

Eligibility rests on a **qualifying move**, and a recruiter records one for the child by naming the `student_id`, the `from_state_code` the family departed and the `to_state_code` they arrived in — each two uppercase letters — the `move_date` on which the move completed, the `work_type` sought or obtained, the `worker_relationship` describing who performs that work relative to the child, the `purpose` of the move, and the `distance_miles` travelled in a straight line, which must be greater than zero, has no upper bound, and is rounded half up to two decimals on the way in and rendered as a two-decimal JSON number just like the hour and credit fields of §2.6. `work_type` is one of `AGRICULTURAL`, `FISHING` or `NONE`; `worker_relationship` is one of `SELF`, `PARENT`, `GUARDIAN` or `SPOUSE`; and `purpose` is either `SEEK_OR_OBTAIN_QUALIFYING_WORK` or `ACCOMPANY_WORKER`. A `move_date` later than the logical clock date is refused with `ERR_MOVE_DATE_IN_FUTURE`.

A recorded move begins in `RECORDED` status with `adjudicated_on` null, and while it is `RECORDED` it contributes nothing whatsoever to priority scoring. At creation the server computes `student_age_at_move` — the number of whole years between the child's `birth_date` and the move's `move_date`, a figure that later feeds the certificate risk assessment of §3.6. The `move_date` is never compared with the child's `birth_date`, so a move dated before the child was born is accepted and simply yields a negative `student_age_at_move`. Recording a move writes nothing at all on the child: no scoring field is refreshed and `Student.version` does not move, precisely because a `RECORDED` move scores as nothing. A move may be recorded with `work_type` of `NONE`: the fact is preserved, but such a move can never be verified and can never carry a certificate.

Adjudication belongs to the state office. The payload carries a single `decision`, either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, and the move must still be `RECORDED` — a second adjudication of the same move, whether it landed on `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, is refused with `ERR_MOVE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. A `decision` of `VERIFIED` against a move whose `work_type` is `NONE` is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_QUALIFYING`; the same code is what turns away a certificate opened on such a move. An accepted adjudication moves the move `RECORDED → VERIFIED` or `RECORDED → REJECTED` and stamps `adjudicated_on` with the logical clock instant. Because verification changes the child's `recent_verified_moves` count, the adjudication also recomputes and persists the child's `pfs_score`, `pfs_variant` and `pfs_flag` as a cross-entity effect, and when that recomputation flips `pfs_flag` the hour-allocation rule is re-run over the child's `DRAFT` and `ACTIVE` plans. That recomputation, and the `Student.version` increment that accompanies it, happen on both decision branches — a `REJECTED` decision writes the child exactly as a `VERIFIED` one does, and so does a recomputation that leaves all three scoring fields at the values they already held. Moves can be surveyed with filters on `student_id`, `status` and `work_type`.

**Interview documentation** is the evidentiary spine of the whole program, and it is immutable by design. Both a `Recruiter` and a `QualityControlReviewer` may file one. The payload names the `student_id` and the `move_id` being documented — the move must belong to that same child, and a mismatch is refused as a shape violation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and declares an `interview_kind` of either `INITIAL`, documenting the original eligibility determination, or `QC_REINTERVIEW`, documenting a quality-control re-contact. The `coe_id` linking the record to a certificate is required exactly when `interview_kind` is `QC_REINTERVIEW` and must be absent when it is `INITIAL`; getting that pairing wrong is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` shape failure, and a `coe_id` that does not resolve reports `ERR_COE_NOT_FOUND`. The move-belongs-to-child test is applied only once `student_id`, `move_id` and any `coe_id` have all resolved, so a request that both names an unresolvable reference and mismatches the move reports the 404 first; and the certificate named by `coe_id` is otherwise unexamined here — it need not belong to the same child, and any such discrepancy surfaces only when the quality-control review is decided (§3.6). The record also carries `conducted_on`, the calendar date the interview took place, which may not be later than the logical clock date on pain of `ERR_MOVE_DATE_IN_FUTURE`; an `informant_relationship` of `PARENT`, `GUARDIAN`, `SELF`, `EMPLOYER` or `OTHER` naming who supplied the information; a `corroboration_count` of independent confirming sources, accepting 0 through 10 and defaulting to 0; an `outcome` of `SUPPORTS_ELIGIBILITY`, `CONTRADICTS_ELIGIBILITY` or `INCONCLUSIVE`; and `documentation_notes`, a required narrative of 1 to 2000 characters.

The filing recruiter is never taken from the body. The server stamps `recruiter_id` from `X-User-ID`, and a `recruiter_id` supplied in the payload is ignored. Nothing about an interview record can be edited afterwards — there is no amendment operation and no deletion, and, as §2.4 notes, the row carries no `updated_at` and no `version`. Interview records can be surveyed with filters on `student_id`, `move_id`, `recruiter_id`, `coe_id`, `interview_kind` and `outcome`, which is how a submission's supporting documentation is located.

### 3.5. The Certificate of Eligibility: Drafting, Correction and Submission

The **certificate of eligibility** is the artifact a federal monitor eventually reads, and its lifecycle is the most intricate in this service. Its status takes one of seven values — `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `QC_SELECTED`, `QC_PASSED`, `APPROVED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` or `INVALIDATED` — and the shape is neither linear nor acyclic: `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → QC_SELECTED → RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION → DRAFT` is a closed loop that may repeat any number of times, while `SUBMITTED` forks into either a direct approval or the quality-controlled leg. `INVALIDATED` is the only terminal status.

A recruiter opens a certificate by naming the `student_id` being certified, the `move_id` the certificate rests on, and the `residency_date` on which the family established residency in the receiving area; `qc_hold_exempt` may also be supplied and defaults to false. The referenced move must belong to the same child — a mismatch is refused as a shape violation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it must not be a `work_type` of `NONE`, a certificate opened on a non-qualifying move being refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_QUALIFYING`. The `residency_date` must fall on or after the move's `move_date`; an earlier residency date is refused with `ERR_RESIDENCY_DATE_BEFORE_MOVE`. The server stamps `initiating_recruiter_id` from `X-User-ID` and ignores any value supplied for it in the body. A new certificate begins in `DRAFT` with `correction_round_count` at 0, `risk_flag_count` at 0, `invalidation_reason` null, and all four eligibility-window fields — `provisional_eligibility_end_on`, `eligibility_start_on`, `eligibility_end_on` and `age_out_capped` — null and unwritten. Nothing about the window is knowable until the certificate is submitted.

Correction is the same recruiter's prerogative and nobody else's: only the recruiter whose id sits in `initiating_recruiter_id` may correct or submit a certificate, and any other caller — including another recruiter — is turned away with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). That ownership test runs as soon as the certificate itself has been resolved and before either the request body or the certificate's status is examined, so a non-owning recruiter is told `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` even when the body is malformed and even when the certificate sits in a status that could not have been corrected or submitted anyway. A correction may supply `residency_date`, `qc_hold_exempt`, or both, and it is accepted from exactly two statuses: from `DRAFT`, where the certificate stays `DRAFT`, and from `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, which drives the transition `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION → DRAFT` and closes the correction loop. From any other status the correction is refused with `ERR_COE_NOT_IN_DRAFT`. A supplied `residency_date` is re-validated against the linked move's `move_date` and again reports `ERR_RESIDENCY_DATE_BEFORE_MOVE` when it precedes it. Any other body key — `status`, `student_id`, `move_id`, `risk_flag_count` or any window field — is ignored. Crucially, `correction_round_count` is **not** reset when the loop closes, so the number of correction rounds a certificate has been through remains observable however many times it cycles. Every accepted correction increments the certificate's `version` by 1, including a correction that supplies neither writable key and therefore does nothing but close the loop.

Submission moves the certificate `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is where the program's evidentiary discipline bites. Five conditions must hold, and they are checked in the ladder order of §2.8. The certificate must be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_COE_NOT_IN_DRAFT`. The linked qualifying move must already be in `VERIFIED` status — a move still `RECORDED`, or one adjudicated `REJECTED`, is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_VERIFIED`. The move must be a qualifying one, its `work_type` being `AGRICULTURAL` or `FISHING` and its `purpose` being either `SEEK_OR_OBTAIN_QUALIFYING_WORK` or `ACCOMPANY_WORKER`; verification already guarantees this, since a `NONE` move can never reach `VERIFIED`. At least one interview record must exist for the same `student_id` and `move_id` whose `interview_kind` is `INITIAL` and whose `outcome` is `SUPPORTS_ELIGIBILITY` — an `INCONCLUSIVE` or `CONTRADICTS_ELIGIBILITY` initial interview does not satisfy this, and a certificate without qualifying documentation is refused with `ERR_INTERVIEW_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`. Finally, no sibling certificate for the same pairing of `student_id` and `move_id` may sit in `SUBMITTED`, `QC_SELECTED`, `QC_PASSED` or `APPROVED`; a second live certificate over the same move is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_COE`. Sibling certificates in `DRAFT`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` or `INVALIDATED` do not block submission.

An accepted submission writes two derived values and may nudge the child's eligibility dimension. First, the **provisional eligibility window** is computed and stored in `provisional_eligibility_end_on`. It is anchored on the qualifying move rather than on residency: take the move's `move_date`, add 36 calendar months to it under the day-of-month convention of §2.6, then step back one day; the result is the last day of the provisional window. This variant applies **no** age-out clip, so a child who will turn 22 inside the window still shows the full thirty-six-month provisional end date. Second, `risk_flag_count` is computed by the risk clause described in §3.6. Third, the child's `eligibility_status` advances `NOT_ELIGIBLE → PENDING_DETERMINATION`, and this write is conditional: it happens only when the child's `eligibility_status` is currently `NOT_ELIGIBLE` or `TERM_EXPIRED`, in which case `Student.version` is incremented by 1. A child already reading `PENDING_DETERMINATION`, `ELIGIBLE` or `CONTINUATION` is left entirely untouched and their `version` does not move. The certificate's own `version` is always incremented by 1. The final window fields remain null — submission never writes them.

Certificates can be surveyed with filters on `student_id`, `move_id`, `initiating_recruiter_id`, `status` and `qc_hold_exempt`, and reading one returns both window variants alongside `age_out_capped`, `risk_flag_count` and `correction_round_count`. That list is also how the fresh `DRAFT` certificate minted by a reidentification (§3.7) is discovered, since reidentification returns the term rather than the certificate.

### 3.6. Quality Control: Risk Flags, Sampling and the Re-Interview Decision

Federal monitoring expects a state to re-interview a share of its certified families, weighted toward the cases most likely to be wrong. This service implements that in two parts: a per-certificate risk score written at submission, and a state-wide sample run the state office triggers when it wants to open a batch of reviews.

`risk_flag_count` is a whole number from 0 through 3, computed at submission time from three independent indicators, each worth one flag. The first indicator fires when the certificate's linked move has a `work_type` of `FISHING`. The second fires when at least one interview record exists for the same `move_id` and `student_id` whose `interview_kind` is `INITIAL` and whose `outcome` is `INCONCLUSIVE` — note that this is compatible with submission, because submission requires *some* supporting `INITIAL` interview but does not forbid an additional inconclusive one. The third fires when the move's `worker_relationship` is anything other than `SELF` **and** the move's `student_age_at_move` is 18 or greater — an adult-aged child whose qualifying work was performed by somebody else. A certificate with one or more flags is a mandatory review candidate; a certificate with zero flags can still be drawn by quota.

The sample run is state-wide and takes no input. Its candidate pool is every certificate currently in `SUBMITTED` status whose `qc_hold_exempt` is false; certificates in `DRAFT`, `QC_SELECTED`, `QC_PASSED`, `APPROVED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` or `INVALIDATED` are excluded, as is every exempt certificate whatever its status. If that pool is empty the entire request is refused with `ERR_NO_QC_CANDIDATES` (HTTP 409) and nothing at all is written. Otherwise the run proceeds in a pinned order. The mandatory set is every candidate whose `risk_flag_count` is 1 or greater. The quota target, reported as `target_n`, is one tenth of the candidate count rounded **up** to the next whole number, or 1, whichever is larger — so a pool of a single candidate has a target of 1, a pool of ten has a target of 1, and a pool of eleven has a target of 2. The candidates are then ranked by `risk_flag_count` descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first `target_n` of that ranking are taken. The selected set is the union of the mandatory set and that quota slice, so a pool with more mandatory candidates than the quota target selects all of them and the selected count exceeds `target_n`.

Each selected certificate then moves `SUBMITTED → QC_SELECTED`, its `version` incremented by 1, and gains exactly one **quality-control review** row created in `OPEN` status with `coe_id` pointing at the certificate, `reviewer_recruiter_id` null, `reinterview_id` null, `discrepancy_codes` an empty list and `decided_on` null. The review's `selection_reason` records why the certificate was drawn: `MANDATORY_RISK_FLAG` when its `risk_flag_count` is 1 or greater, and `SAMPLE_QUOTA` otherwise. Certificates are processed in `created_at` ascending order so that the response arrays are deterministic. The response reports `candidate_count`, `target_n`, `selected_count`, `selected_coe_ids` and `review_ids`; the last two are index-aligned — the review at position *n* belongs to the certificate at position *n* — and both are ordered by the certificate's `created_at` ascending. A successful run answers 201, since it creates review resources.

Deciding a review is the exclusive province of a `QualityControlReviewer`, and it is where segregation of duties is enforced. The payload carries a `decision` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, a mandatory `reinterview_id`, and optionally `discrepancy_codes`, which defaults to an empty list. Five guards precede either branch. The review must still be `OPEN`; a review that has already been decided is refused with `ERR_QC_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED`, so the operation is deliberately not idempotent. The certificate under review must still be in `QC_SELECTED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_COE_INVALID_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the certificate's `initiating_recruiter_id` — a reviewer may not decide a certificate they themselves initiated, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The acting recruiter's `credential_level` must be `QC_CERTIFIED`, and a `BASIC` or `CERTIFIED` reviewer is refused with `ERR_QC_CREDENTIAL_REQUIRED` (403). And the record named by `reinterview_id` must have `interview_kind` of `QC_REINTERVIEW`, a `student_id` equal to the certificate's `student_id`, and a `coe_id` equal to the certificate's own `id`; an `INITIAL` record, or a re-interview belonging to a different child or a different certificate, is refused with `ERR_REINTERVIEW_STUDENT_MISMATCH` (422). A `reinterview_id` that resolves to nothing at all reports `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_FOUND` instead.

The two decision branches are symmetric in their guards and divergent in their consequences, and both answer with the review row itself rather than with the certificate. A `PASS` requires `discrepancy_codes` to be absent or an empty list; supplying codes alongside a pass is a shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An accepted pass sets the review's `status` to `PASSED`, writes `reviewer_recruiter_id` from `X-User-ID`, stores the supplied `reinterview_id`, stamps `decided_on` with the logical clock instant, leaves `discrepancy_codes` as an empty list, and increments the review's `version` by 1. It then drives the certificate `QC_SELECTED → QC_PASSED` and increments the certificate's `version` by 1. Nothing on the child changes.

A `FAIL` requires `discrepancy_codes` to contain at least one element; an empty or omitted list alongside a fail is likewise an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` shape violation. Each element must be one of `MOVE_DATE`, `WORK_TYPE`, `RESIDENCY_DATE`, `INFORMANT` or `DISTANCE`. An accepted fail sets the review's `status` to `FAILED`, writes `reviewer_recruiter_id` from `X-User-ID`, stores the supplied `reinterview_id`, stores the supplied `discrepancy_codes` verbatim, stamps `decided_on` from the logical clock, and increments the review's `version` by 1. It then drives the certificate `QC_SELECTED → RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, increments `correction_round_count` by 1, clears `provisional_eligibility_end_on` back to null, and increments the certificate's `version` by 1. That certificate transition in turn causes a second hop on the child, which is not a direct effect of the decision but of the certificate having left the review pipeline: the child's `eligibility_status` walks back `PENDING_DETERMINATION → NOT_ELIGIBLE`, but only when the child is in fact reading `PENDING_DETERMINATION` and holds no eligibility term in `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` status — a child with a live term from an earlier certificate keeps whatever eligibility posture they had. `Student.version` is incremented by 1 only when the value actually changes; when the child is left untouched, their `version` does not move. The failed certificate is now back at the top of the correction loop, and its recruiter can correct and resubmit it, whereupon a fresh provisional window and a fresh `risk_flag_count` are written and the cycle may repeat with `correction_round_count` climbing each time.

Reviews can be surveyed with filters on `coe_id`, `reviewer_recruiter_id`, `reinterview_id`, `status` and `selection_reason`, and reading one returns `selection_reason`, the re-interview link and the decision timestamp.

### 3.7. Approval, the Continuation-of-Eligibility Term, and Reidentification

Approval is the state office's act, and it is available from exactly two positions. A certificate in `SUBMITTED` may be approved, but only while no quality-control review for it is still `OPEN` — a certificate drawn into the sample must go through its review first. A certificate in `QC_PASSED` may be approved unconditionally. From anywhere else, including `DRAFT`, `QC_SELECTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, `APPROVED` or `INVALIDATED`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_COE_INVALID_STATE`. The two legs — `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` for a certificate never sampled, and `QC_PASSED → APPROVED` for the quality-controlled one — produce **identical** consequences; the only observable difference is that the quality-controlled leg leaves its `PASSED` review row completely untouched, so the review's `decided_on` continues to record the moment of the quality-control decision rather than the moment of approval.

Approval sets off a four-hop chain, and every hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the approval call directly. **Hop one** moves the certificate to `APPROVED` and writes the **final eligibility window**, which is a different variant of the same rule that produced the provisional window. `eligibility_start_on` is set equal to the certificate's `residency_date`. The uncapped end is obtained by adding 36 calendar months to `residency_date` under the day-of-month convention and stepping back one day. The age-out cap is obtained by adding 22 years to the child's `birth_date` and stepping back one day — the day before the child's twenty-second birthday. `eligibility_end_on` is whichever of those two dates is earlier, and `age_out_capped` is true exactly when the age-out date is strictly earlier than the uncapped end, and false when the two coincide or the uncapped end comes first. Because this variant anchors on `residency_date` — which is always on or after `move_date` — and applies the age-out clip that the provisional variant deliberately omits, the final window diverges from the provisional one whenever residency was established after the move or whenever the cap binds. The certificate's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Hop two**, caused by hop one, creates a new **eligibility term** for the child: `student_id` copied from the certificate, `coe_id` pointing at the approving certificate, `term_start_on` copied from `eligibility_start_on`, `term_end_on` copied from `eligibility_end_on`, `status` set to `ACTIVE`, and `reidentification_due_on`, `superseded_by_term_id` and `termination_reason` all null. `term_end_on` is the **inclusive** last eligible day throughout this specification.

**Hop three**, caused by the appearance of that new `ACTIVE` term, sets the child's eligibility dimension to `ELIGIBLE` and `eligibility_expires_on` to the new term's `term_end_on`. That is ordinarily the transition `PENDING_DETERMINATION → ELIGIBLE`, but the write is unconditional rather than guarded on the prior value: a child who is reading `CONTINUATION` or `TERM_EXPIRED` when a second certificate is approved is moved to `ELIGIBLE` just the same. This hop also repairs the reidentification chain: when the child holds any term in `REIDENTIFIED` status whose `superseded_by_term_id` is still null, the one with the greatest `term_end_on` — ties broken by the earliest `created_at` — has its `superseded_by_term_id` set to the new term's `id` and its own `version` incremented by 1, so a reidentified child's term history reads as a linked chain rather than a set of orphans. **Hop four**, caused by hop three's mutation of the child, re-runs priority scoring, persisting `pfs_score`, `pfs_variant` and `pfs_flag`, and then re-runs the hour-allocation rule over every service plan of that child in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status, rewriting each plan's `priority_tier`, `allocated_hours` and `expected_service_hours` and incrementing each touched plan's `version` by 1. Across hops three and four together the child's `version` is incremented by exactly 1, not twice.

A term's `status` takes one of six values: `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRING`, `EXPIRED`, `REIDENTIFIED`, `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED`, the last three being terminal. Terms are never created or edited directly; they appear through approval and advance only through the sweep, the reidentification action, or a certificate invalidation. They can be surveyed with filters on `student_id`, `coe_id`, `superseded_by_term_id` and `status`, which is how the term minted by an approval is discovered.

The **continuation sweep** is the state office's periodic pass over the whole term table, and it takes no input. Its batch is every term whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRING` or `EXPIRED`, processed in `term_end_on` ascending order with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; terms in `REIDENTIFIED`, `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED` are excluded from the batch entirely. Each term in the batch advances **at most one** status per call, and the three branches are evaluated in a fixed order against the logical clock date.

The first branch handles expiry and covers both `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and `EXPIRING → EXPIRED`: when a term is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` and the clock date is **strictly later** than its `term_end_on`, the term becomes `EXPIRED`. Because `term_end_on` is the inclusive last eligible day, a clock sitting exactly on `term_end_on` never expires the term. Entering `EXPIRED` writes `reidentification_due_on` as `term_end_on` plus forty-five days — the grace period during which the family can be reidentified — and drives the child's eligibility dimension to `TERM_EXPIRED` with `eligibility_expires_on` cleared to null. Both of those writes on the child are unconditional, so a child already reading `TERM_EXPIRED` from an earlier term's expiry is written again and still takes the `version` increment for this term's step. This is also the path a child takes straight from `ELIGIBLE` to `TERM_EXPIRED` when no sweep ever ran inside the continuation window. The `expired_count` is incremented and the term's `id` is appended to `expired_term_ids`.

The second branch handles the continuation warning, `ACTIVE → EXPIRING`: when a term is still `ACTIVE`, the clock date is on or after `term_end_on` minus sixty days, and the clock date is on or before `term_end_on`, the term becomes `EXPIRING` and the child's eligibility dimension advances `ELIGIBLE → CONTINUATION`. That second write is conditional on the child actually reading `ELIGIBLE`: a child in any other eligibility posture is left completely untouched and takes no `version` increment, while the term still moves. The window opening uses an inclusive comparison — a clock landing exactly sixty days before `term_end_on` does open the window — and `expiring_count` is incremented.

The third branch handles the lapse, `EXPIRED → LAPSED`: when a term is `EXPIRED` and the clock date is strictly later than its `reidentification_due_on`, the grace period has closed and the term becomes `LAPSED`. This is the sweep's most consequential branch, because it also closes out services: every service plan of that child in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status moves to `CLOSED` with `closure_reason` set to `REIDENTIFICATION_LAPSED` and its `version` incremented by 1, and the child's `service_status` is then re-derived by the rule in §3.9 — a child whose only plans were open therefore reads `EXITED` afterwards. On this branch the child's `version` moves only when that re-derivation actually changes `service_status`; the plans it closes each take their increment regardless, and the child's eligibility dimension is not touched at all here, so a child whose term lapses goes on reading `TERM_EXPIRED`. The `lapsed_count` is incremented.

Every term the sweep advances has its own `version` incremented by 1, and every child whose eligibility or service dimension the sweep rewrites has their `version` incremented by 1 for that term's step. The response reports `swept_count` — the size of the batch, whether or not any term moved — alongside `expiring_count`, `expired_count`, `lapsed_count` and `expired_term_ids`, the last listed in batch order. A sweep with nothing to do is not an error: it answers 200 with all four counters at zero and an empty `expired_term_ids`.

**Reidentification** is how a family whose term has run out re-enters the program, and it is a recruiter's action on the expired term — any `Recruiter` may perform it, since no ownership comparison applies here and the recruiter who initiated the certificate behind the term holds no special claim. The payload names a `new_move_id` and a `residency_date`. Four conditions apply: the term must be `EXPIRED`, and a term that is `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRING`, `REIDENTIFIED`, `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_TERM_INVALID_STATE`; the logical clock date must be on or before the term's `reidentification_due_on`, and a later clock is refused with `ERR_REIDENTIFICATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`; the move named by `new_move_id` must belong to the same child and already be in `VERIFIED` status, otherwise `ERR_MOVE_NOT_VERIFIED`; and the supplied `residency_date` must be on or after that move's `move_date`, otherwise `ERR_RESIDENCY_DATE_BEFORE_MOVE`. They are listed here in narrative order, not evaluation order: which failure is reported is decided by the ladder of §2.8, so a `new_move_id` that resolves to nothing reports `ERR_MOVE_NOT_FOUND` first of all, then the term-state check, then move verification, then residency ordering, and the grace-window check last of the four. An accepted reidentification drives the term `EXPIRED → REIDENTIFIED`, which is terminal, and increments its `version` by 1. It mints a fresh certificate in `DRAFT` status for the same child on the new move, carrying the supplied `residency_date`, `initiating_recruiter_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, and otherwise the §3.5 creation defaults: `qc_hold_exempt` false — the reidentification payload declares no such key, and nothing is inherited from the superseded term's own certificate — `correction_round_count` and `risk_flag_count` at 0, `invalidation_reason` null, and all four eligibility-window fields null. That certificate then walks the ordinary lifecycle of §3.5 from the beginning. And it returns the child's eligibility dimension `TERM_EXPIRED → PENDING_DETERMINATION`, incrementing `Student.version` by 1; that write is unconditional rather than guarded on the prior value, so a child who has meanwhile been certified afresh and reads `ELIGIBLE` or `CONTINUATION` is moved to `PENDING_DETERMINATION` just the same and still takes the increment. This is the cycle that makes reidentification observable: the same child can pass through `PENDING_DETERMINATION`, `ELIGIBLE`, `CONTINUATION` and `TERM_EXPIRED` more than once, and each pass leaves a term row behind. The response body is the term; the new certificate is found through the certificate list filtered by `student_id` and a `status` of `DRAFT`.

**Invalidation** is the destructive counterpart, reserved to the state office and available only from `APPROVED`; from any other status it is refused with `ERR_COE_INVALID_STATE`. It requires an `invalidation_reason` of 1 to 240 characters, and an absent or over-long reason is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` shape failure. Invalidation is terminal for the certificate and sets off a three-hop chain. Hop one moves the certificate to `INVALIDATED`, stores the supplied `invalidation_reason`, and increments its `version` by 1. Hop two, caused by hop one, finds every eligibility term whose `coe_id` is that certificate and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRING` or `EXPIRED`, moves each to `TERMINATED`, sets each one's `termination_reason` to `CERTIFICATE_INVALIDATED`, and increments each term's `version` by 1; terms already `REIDENTIFIED`, `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED` are left completely untouched. Hop three, caused by that termination rather than by the invalidate call, rewrites the child: `eligibility_status` becomes `TERM_EXPIRED`, `eligibility_expires_on` is cleared to null, every service plan of that child in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status moves to `CLOSED` with `closure_reason` set to `ELIGIBILITY_LOST` and its `version` incremented by 1, `service_status` is re-derived by the §3.9 rule, priority scoring is recomputed and persisted, and the child's `version` is incremented by exactly 1 for the whole hop. Hop three is therefore skipped entirely when hop two terminated nothing at all — a certificate all of whose terms are already `REIDENTIFIED`, `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED` still moves to `INVALIDATED` and still stores its reason, but the child is left completely untouched, no plan is closed, and `Student.version` does not move.

### 3.8. The National Record Exchange and Credit Reconciliation

When a family arrives from another state, the receiving state asks the national exchange for the child's prior record instead of re-interviewing from scratch. That conversation is modelled as a **record transfer request**, driven end to end by the `ExchangeLiaison` and by nobody else. Opening one names the `student_id` whose record is being assembled locally, the `requesting_state_code` of the state asking, the `holding_state_code` of the state believed to hold the predecessor record — which must differ from the requesting code, on pain of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and an `exchange_reference` correlation string of 1 to 64 characters. `reported_prior_credits` may also be supplied: it is the secondary credit total the holding state claims for the predecessor record *before* any cap is applied, accepts values from 0.00 through 40.00 — the range is checked against the value exactly as supplied, and a value carrying a third decimal is then rounded half up to two decimals on the way in rather than refused — and defaults to 0.00. A new request begins in `INITIATED` status with `matched_prior_student_id` null, `applied_transferred_credits` at 0.00 and `rejection_reason` null.

The exchange lifecycle runs `INITIATED`, then a fork, then `RECEIVED`, then either `RECONCILED` or `REJECTED`, with `NO_MATCH`, `RECONCILED` and `REJECTED` all terminal. Reporting the search result is the fork, and the two branches diverge observably. Supplying `matched_prior_student_id` drives `INITIATED → MATCHED`: the identifier is stored on the request, and — this is the branch's whole point — the child's `prior_record_student_id` is written to that same identifier, incrementing `Student.version` by 1. Omitting `matched_prior_student_id` drives `INITIATED → NO_MATCH`, which is terminal: the request's `matched_prior_student_id` stays null, **no** field on the child is written, and `Student.version` is **not** incremented. A liaison can therefore tell the branches apart purely by whether the child's version moved. Three guards apply: the request must be `INITIATED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`; a supplied `matched_prior_student_id` must resolve to an existing child, otherwise `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`; and it must differ from the request's own `student_id`, because a record cannot be its own predecessor — a self-match is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_SELF_MATCH`. Those three are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8 rather than in the order just listed, so an unresolvable `matched_prior_student_id` reports its 404 even on a request that has already left `INITIATED`, while the state check in turn precedes the self-match check.

Acknowledging receipt of the exchange payload drives `MATCHED → RECEIVED` and increments the request's `version` by 1. It is deliberately inert otherwise: no credits are posted at this step, and `applied_transferred_credits` is still 0.00. A receive attempted from any status other than `MATCHED` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`.

Accepting the received record drives `RECEIVED → RECONCILED` and is where credits actually move. The rule looks circular at first reading — the cap percentage depends on a credit total that itself includes the transferred credits — so the evaluation order is pinned and must be followed exactly. **First**, the child's locally accrued credits are totalled: sum `credits_awarded` over every delivered session attached to a service plan of that child whose `plan_type` is `CREDIT_ACCRUAL`, counting only forward records that have not been reversed. Plan status is deliberately **not** filtered — credits already earned survive plan closure and supersession. **Second**, the uncapped transfer amount is taken as the request's `reported_prior_credits`, unmodified. **Third**, the tier key is formed as the locally accrued total plus that full uncapped amount: this is the **pre-cap** total, and it is the only figure the tier lookup ever sees. **Fourth**, the cap percentage is chosen as the highest tier whose floor the key reaches — a key below 12.00 credits admits 50 percent, a key of at least 12.00 but below 24.00 admits 35 percent, and a key of 24.00 or more admits 25 percent. **Fifth**, the accepted transfer is the smaller of the uncapped amount and the cap percentage applied to the tier key, rounded half up to two decimals. **Sixth**, the awarded total is the locally accrued credits plus that accepted transfer, rounded half up to two decimals; the tier chosen in the third step is final and is never re-derived from this total, and re-deriving it produces a different, wrong answer.

The reconciliation writes four figures. `applied_transferred_credits` on the request receives the accepted transfer amount. On the child, `locally_accrued_credits` receives the local total rounded half up to two decimals, `transferred_credits_count` receives the accepted transfer amount, and `total_awarded_credits` receives the awarded total; the child's `version` is incremented by 1 and the request's `version` is incremented by 1. A reconcile attempted from any status other than `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`.

Rejecting the exchange result is permitted from `RECEIVED` and from `RECONCILED`, and from anywhere else it is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`. A `rejection_reason` of 1 to 240 characters is required. Rejection is the compensating action of the credit-conservation invariant, and it must land on exactly the values that existed before reconciliation: the accepted transfer amount is subtracted from the child's `transferred_credits_count`, the request's `applied_transferred_credits` returns to 0.00, `total_awarded_credits` is re-derived as `locally_accrued_credits` plus the now-reduced `transferred_credits_count`, and the child's `prior_record_student_id` is cleared to null. The child's `version` is incremented by 1 and the request's `version` is incremented by 1. Rejecting a request that had only reached `RECEIVED` has nothing to compensate — `applied_transferred_credits` was already 0.00 — but it still clears `prior_record_student_id` and still bumps both versions. Transfer requests can be surveyed with filters on `student_id`, `matched_prior_student_id`, `status` and `exchange_reference`.

### 3.9. Service Plans, Hour Allocation and the Delivery Ledger

A **service plan** is the coordinator's commitment to a child for a program year. Drafting one names the `student_id`, a `plan_type`, a `delivery_mode`, and the `expected_student_version` guard of §2.7. `plan_type` is one of `SUPPLEMENTAL_INSTRUCTION`, `SUMMER_PROGRAM`, `CREDIT_ACCRUAL` or `OSY_ALTERNATIVE`; `delivery_mode` is one of `IN_PERSON`, `VIRTUAL`, `HOME_BASED` or `WORKSITE`. Two plan types are gated on the child's `schooling_status`: an `OSY_ALTERNATIVE` plan requires `OUT_OF_SCHOOL` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_OSY_PLAN_REQUIRES_OUT_OF_SCHOOL`, while a `SUPPLEMENTAL_INSTRUCTION` plan requires `ENROLLED` and is otherwise refused with `ERR_SUPPLEMENTAL_REQUIRES_ENROLLED`. `SUMMER_PROGRAM` and `CREDIT_ACCRUAL` plans are open to either schooling status. A new plan starts in `DRAFT` with `closure_reason` and `superseded_by_plan_id` null, `priority_tier` at `NON_PFS`, and `allocated_hours`, `expected_service_hours` and `delivered_hours` all at 0.00 until the allocation run that immediately follows rewrites the first three.

Only one plan of a given `plan_type` may be live for a child at a time, and the newer one always wins. If that child already holds a plan of the same `plan_type` in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status, that older plan moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` or `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, its `closure_reason` is set to `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_plan_id` is pointed at the new plan's `id`, and its `version` is incremented by 1. A child can hold four live plans simultaneously — one per plan type — but never two of the same type. Every accepted create increments `Student.version` by 1, which is exactly why the stale-version guard exists.

Activation is the coordinator's second step and it is guarded on a completely different entity: the child's `eligibility_status` must currently be `ELIGIBLE` or `CONTINUATION`. A child reading `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `PENDING_DETERMINATION` or `TERM_EXPIRED` blocks activation with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_SERVICES`, so a plan drafted in anticipation of certification cannot be activated until the approval cascade has run, and a plan whose child's term has since expired cannot be activated at all. The plan itself must be `DRAFT`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`, and that state check outranks the eligibility check as §2.8 orders them. Those are the only two guards: unlike the recruiter and area standing gates of §3.2, the two `plan_type` gates of the draft step are **not** re-evaluated here, so a `SUPPLEMENTAL_INSTRUCTION` plan whose child has since been amended to `OUT_OF_SCHOOL`, and an `OSY_ALTERNATIVE` plan whose child has since been amended to `ENROLLED`, both still activate. An accepted activation drives `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, increments the plan's `version` by 1, re-runs the allocation rule, and re-derives the child's `service_status`.

Closing a plan takes a `closure_reason`, and the coordinator's vocabulary is narrower than the field's. A coordinator may supply only `COMPLETED` or `COORDINATOR_CLOSED`; the other three members — `ELIGIBILITY_LOST`, `REIDENTIFICATION_LAPSED` and `SUPERSEDED` — are written exclusively by the invalidation cascade of §3.7, by the lapse branch of the sweep, and by the supersession path above respectively, and supplying any of them by hand is refused as an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` shape violation. The plan must be in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, producing `DRAFT → CLOSED` or `ACTIVE → CLOSED`; a plan already `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`. Reason and starting status are independent here, unlike the assignment closure of §3.2: either permitted `closure_reason` is accepted from either permitted status, so a `DRAFT` plan may be closed as `COMPLETED`. Closure stores the supplied reason, increments the plan's `version` by 1, removes the plan from the allocation input set so the surviving plans are re-allocated over the whole budget, and re-derives the child's `service_status`. Closure never touches `delivered_hours` or the child's `total_service_hours_ledger` — hours already earned survive closure, exactly as earned credits do.

The child's `service_status` is not advanced by individual triggers but **re-derived from scratch after every plan mutation**, in this exact order of tests: if no service plan row has ever existed for that child, the status is `UNSERVED`; otherwise, if any plan of that child is in `ACTIVE` status, the status is `RECEIVING_SERVICES`; otherwise, if any plan is in `DRAFT` status, the status is `PLAN_DRAFTED`; otherwise the status is `EXITED`. The second test dominates the third, so a child holding one `ACTIVE` plan and one `DRAFT` plan reads `RECEIVING_SERVICES`, not `PLAN_DRAFTED`. The visible consequences are that a first draft moves the child `UNSERVED → PLAN_DRAFTED`, a first activation moves them `PLAN_DRAFTED → RECEIVING_SERVICES`, closing the last `ACTIVE` plan while a `DRAFT` plan remains walks them back `RECEIVING_SERVICES → PLAN_DRAFTED`, and closing out the last open plan of either kind moves them `RECEIVING_SERVICES → EXITED`. Each re-derivation that changes the value increments `Student.version` by 1.

**Hour allocation** decides how the program year's hours are divided among a child's live plans, and it runs on every plan create including the supersession path, on every activation, on every closure, as the fourth hop of the approval cascade, and after a profile amendment or a move adjudication that flipped `pfs_flag`. The budget depends on priority: a child whose `pfs_flag` is true divides 120.00 hours, and a child whose flag is false divides 75.00 hours. The input set is every plan of that child in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status, ordered by `created_at` ascending, with `id` ascending behind it as a tie-break that the never-repeating stamp sequence of §2.4 keeps unreachable because plan creates are serialised. Plans in `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED` status are excluded and keep whatever `allocated_hours` they last held.

Each plan type carries a weight: `SUPPLEMENTAL_INSTRUCTION` weighs 4, `SUMMER_PROGRAM` weighs 3, `OSY_ALTERNATIVE` weighs 3, and `CREDIT_ACCRUAL` weighs 2. Sum the weights of every plan in the input set to obtain the total weight. If the input set is empty the whole calculation is a no-op and nothing is written. If the total weight is zero — unreachable given the weight table, but specified for closure — every plan's `allocated_hours` is set to 0.00. Otherwise each plan's raw share is the budget multiplied by that plan's weight and divided by the total weight, and **each share is rounded half up to two decimals before any summing happens**. The rounded shares are then added together, and the residual is the budget minus that sum, itself rounded half up to two decimals; the residual may be positive or negative. That entire residual lands on one plan and one plan only — the first of the input set, meaning the plan with the earliest `created_at` — whose `allocated_hours` becomes its own rounded share plus the residual, re-rounded half up to two decimals. Rounding after summing instead of before produces a different distribution and is wrong. Finally, every plan in the input set has `priority_tier` set to `PFS` when the child's `pfs_flag` is true and `NON_PFS` when it is false, and `expected_service_hours` set equal to its freshly computed `allocated_hours`; each touched plan's `version` is incremented by 1. Plans can be surveyed with filters on `student_id`, `status`, `plan_type`, `superseded_by_plan_id` and `priority_tier`.

**Delivered sessions** are the immutable leaves of this tree. Posting one names the `plan_id`, the `delivered_on` date, the `hours` delivered, a `service_category` of `INSTRUCTION`, `COUNSELING`, `CREDIT_ACCRUAL_SESSION` or `SUPPORT_SERVICE`, and optionally `credits_awarded`, which defaults to 0.00. The referenced plan must be in `ACTIVE` status; posting against a `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED` plan is refused with `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`. `hours` must be strictly greater than 0.00, no more than 8.00, and an exact multiple of a quarter hour; `credits_awarded`, when present, must be at least 0.00, no more than 2.00, and likewise an exact multiple of 0.25 — a negative amount is off the grid exactly as a negative `hours` is, notwithstanding that it divides evenly by a quarter. A value off that grid — 0.00 hours, 8.25 hours, 1.10 hours, −0.25 credits — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_HOURS`. Credits are permitted only on a `CREDIT_ACCRUAL` plan: any `credits_awarded` greater than 0.00 against a `SUPPLEMENTAL_INSTRUCTION`, `SUMMER_PROGRAM` or `OSY_ALTERNATIVE` plan is refused with `ERR_CREDITS_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_PLAN_TYPE`. A `delivered_on` later than the logical clock date is refused with `ERR_MOVE_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. A forward record always carries `reverses_delivery_id` null — the field is never accepted from the body — and `is_reversed` false.

Correcting a session means compensating it, never editing it. Reversal creates a **new** immutable delivery record whose `reverses_delivery_id` points at the target and which copies the target's `plan_id`, `delivered_on`, `hours`, `service_category` and `credits_awarded` verbatim, and it flags the target's `is_reversed` as true. The reversal record's own `is_reversed` is false and never becomes true, so an `is_reversed=false` filter returns reversal records alongside unreversed forward ones. The response body is the newly created reversal record, not the target, and the operation answers 201 because it creates a resource. A record that is itself a reversal, or a forward record already flagged `is_reversed`, cannot be reversed and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_REVERSIBLE`. That reversibility test is the only gate on the operation: the target's plan is not re-examined, so unlike a forward post — which demands an `ACTIVE` plan — a reversal succeeds against a session whose plan has since moved to `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED`. Delivery records can be surveyed with filters on `plan_id`, `reverses_delivery_id`, `service_category` and `is_reversed`.

Every session post and every reversal republishes the ledger, and the definitions are pure aggregates. A plan's `delivered_hours` is the sum of `hours` over the delivery records attached to that plan that are forward records and are not themselves reversed, rounded half up to two decimals; the plan's `version` is incremented by 1 when the figure is republished. The child's `total_service_hours_ledger` is the sum of `delivered_hours` across **every** plan of that child regardless of plan status, rounded half up to two decimals. The child's `locally_accrued_credits` is the sum of `credits_awarded` over the same unreversed forward records, restricted to plans whose `plan_type` is `CREDIT_ACCRUAL`, rounded half up to two decimals; and `total_awarded_credits` is `locally_accrued_credits` plus `transferred_credits_count`, rounded half up to two decimals. The child's `version` is incremented by 1 when these figures are republished.

Because the reversal record is excluded from every sum by virtue of carrying a `reverses_delivery_id`, and the target is excluded by virtue of its `is_reversed` flag, the conservation invariant holds exactly: posting a session and then reversing it returns `total_service_hours_ledger` to precisely the value it held beforehand, and `locally_accrued_credits` likewise — no residual fraction, no drift. And because no plan-status filter appears anywhere in the ledger definitions, closing or superseding a plan never moves the ledger either.
