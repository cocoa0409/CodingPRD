# Vision Therapy & Developmental Optometry API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Developmental optometry practices sit awkwardly between two worlds. Clinically they behave like a rehabilitation service: a child arrives with convergence insufficiency, an accommodative dysfunction, an oculomotor problem or amblyopia, is measured on a battery of instruments, is put on a multi-phase therapy programme of in-office and at-home exercises, and is measured again every few weeks until the numbers either reach goal or stop moving. Financially they behave like two entirely different businesses at once: the same forty-minute encounter is a medical rehabilitation service when it is filed against a major-medical payer and a vision-plan materials-and-services benefit when it is filed against a routine vision payer, and the two produce completely different money. Practices that try to run both worlds on one general-purpose practice-management system end up reconciling visit pools by hand, discovering mid-episode that a prior authorization ran dry three appointments ago, and re-keying the same encounter twice because the billing rules diverged.

This document specifies the Vision Therapy & Developmental Optometry API, a single back-end service that owns both worlds. Every route is mounted beneath the `/vision-therapy` prefix. The service tracks patients from intake through the binocular evaluation, the signed therapy plan, the phase-by-phase progression of in-office and home sessions, periodic reassessment and progress scoring, occlusion (patching) prescriptions for amblyopia, the loan and recovery of activity equipment, referral traffic in and out of the practice, and finally discharge. Alongside that clinical spine it runs a prior-authorization visit pool that is drawn down by scheduling and refilled by cancellation, and a claim lifecycle whose adjudication forks on the payer's type.

Two structural commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **conservation**: the authorization visit pool, the physical kit inventory, the dollars on an adjudicated claim, and a vision plan's annual allowance are each governed by a named identity that must hold at every instant an API caller can observe, not merely at the end of a batch. The second is **ordered refusal**: when a request is bad in several ways at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by a single thirty-six-rung ladder that every write walks from the top. Implementers should treat that ladder — set out in §2.7 — as the backbone of the contract; almost every business rule described later in this document is a rung on it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Clinic Roles and the Identity Headers

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is any string other than one of the seven role names below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ClinicAdmin`, `Optometrist`, `VisionTherapist`, `TherapyAssistant`, `BillingSpecialist`, `FrontDesk` and `PatientUser`, spelled exactly as shown.

A **`ClinicAdmin`** is the operations administrator. The role owns configuration — payers, clinicians, therapy rooms and the equipment catalogue — plus the server's logical clock, and holds no clinical authority whatsoever. When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

An **`Optometrist`** is a licensed optometrist and is the only role that may sign an assessment, author, revise, suspend, resume or activate a therapy plan, append phases to a plan, prescribe or taper occlusion, and discharge a patient. When `X-User-Role` is `Optometrist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Therapist` row; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references a `Therapist` compare the resource's therapist-valued field to `X-User-ID`. By the role's definition that `Therapist` carries `credential` `OPTOMETRIST`.

A **`VisionTherapist`** is a certified vision therapist who runs sessions without supervision and records the instrument battery, but may not sign it. When `X-User-Role` is `VisionTherapist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Therapist` row, and by the role's definition that therapist carries `credential` `CERTIFIED_VISION_THERAPIST`.

A **`TherapyAssistant`** may deliver and complete sessions and record measurements and mastery, but a session an assistant runs is only completable when an on-site supervising optometrist is documented on it. When `X-User-Role` is `TherapyAssistant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Therapist` row, and by the role's definition that therapist carries `credential` `VISION_THERAPY_ASSISTANT`.

A **`BillingSpecialist`** is the revenue-cycle operator, owning coverages, authorizations and the entire claim lifecycle. When `X-User-Role` is `BillingSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server does record it — a claim submission stamps it onto `submitted_by_user_id` — but it is never resolved against an entity's `id`; it is only ever compared, as a string, against another recorded operator identifier.

A **`FrontDesk`** operator handles reception and scheduling: registering patients, booking rooms and clinicians, handing over and taking back equipment, and working referral traffic. Marking a session as a no-show is the one operation reserved to `FrontDesk` alone. When `X-User-Role` is `FrontDesk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`.

A **`PatientUser`** is the patient — or the guardian acting for a minor — working through the portal. The role self-logs home practice, attests to it, and logs patching adherence. When `X-User-Role` is `PatientUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient`; a `PatientUser` may only act on rows whose `patient_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and acting on another patient's row is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

For the three roles backed by a `Therapist` row and for `PatientUser`, `X-User-ID` must name an existing row of the backing entity; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` as part of the same authorization rung. That rung, as specified, tests existence of the backing row and — for a `PatientUser` — ownership of the row being acted on; the credential correspondences described above state how the clinic issues those identities, and no separate rejection is specified — nor performed — for a caller whose named `Therapist` carries a `credential` other than the one its role implies: rung 2 tests only that the backing row exists, so a `VisionTherapist` header naming a therapist whose `credential` is `OPTOMETRIST` is accepted everywhere.

Authorization is per-endpoint, and §4 lists the exact role set for each of the sixty-nine endpoints. Every `GET` endpoint in the service is readable by `ClinicAdmin`, `Optometrist`, `VisionTherapist`, `TherapyAssistant`, `BillingSpecialist` and `FrontDesk`; `PatientUser` additionally reads a patient record, a session, the session list, an occlusion prescription and the occlusion prescription list, and no other `GET`. On the three single-row reads the ownership test refuses another patient's row with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`; it can only fire on a row that exists, so an id naming nothing at all still reports the ordinary 404 rather than a 403. The two list reads behave differently: rather than refusing, they silently narrow to the calling patient's own rows, overriding any `patient_id` filter the caller supplied, so a `PatientUser` asking for somebody else's sessions receives an empty page rather than a 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Like every other instant the server writes, it is read from the logical clock of §2.5 and never from wall-clock time, so two calls made without moving the clock carry the same `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. A resource is projected identically wherever it appears — created, updated, read singly or read inside a list page: every field the entity declares is present on every representation, carrying JSON `null` when it has no value rather than being omitted, so the key set of a resource is fixed and "not set" is distinguishable only by the null and never by absence. Nothing beyond the declared field set plus the common properties of §2.4 is ever emitted, and no endpoint returns a partial or expanded projection — a plan never carries its phases inline, a claim never carries its coverage, and there is no field-selection parameter.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. Its wording is not part of the contract; only `error_id` and the HTTP status are. Two envelope-shaped failures fall outside the rung ladder entirely: a request to a path no endpoint claims answers `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and a request using an HTTP method the path does not support answers `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 400 — both decided before authentication, so both are returned even when the identity headers are absent.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest is 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0, and the smallest accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400), as is either parameter supplied as anything other than a whole number — `limit=1.5` and `limit=abc` are both `ERR_VALIDATION`. An explicitly empty value (`?limit=`) is likewise `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than a fall-back to the default.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` reports how many rows matched the filters before the page window was applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a specific list says otherwise, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending, with rows sharing a `created_at` — which is common, because the logical clock only moves when a caller moves it — broken by write order, the earlier-written row first. The one exception in this service is the per-plan phase list, which is returned sorted by `sequence_index` ascending and then, for phases sharing a `sequence_index`, by that same `created_at`-then-write-order rule.

Every filter parameter documented for a list endpoint in §3 and §4 is optional, and supplying several narrows the result set by conjunction. A filter whose value is an enum — any `status`, `schedule_status`, `billing_status`, `progress_state`, `modality`, `assessment_type`, `purpose`, `metric_code`, `direction`, `urgency`, `severity` or similar — is subject to enum-membership checking and a bad value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 400). A filter whose value is an identifier rather than an enum is never existence-checked: an id that matches no row simply yields an empty page with `total_count` 0, never a 404. A filter supplied with an empty value (`?patient_id=`) is treated as though it had not been supplied at all, and a query parameter the endpoint does not document is ignored without comment.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource is created with a server-minted `id`: a UUID string carrying no type prefix. Client-supplied identifiers are never honoured for `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, stamped from the logical clock described in §2.5 at the moment the row is written. That three-digit rendering applies to every instant the server itself writes and to those only. An instant the *caller* supplies — `scheduled_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at`, `performed_at`, `credential_expires_at`, `due_at` and the clock endpoint's `to_iso8601` — is stored and echoed back byte for byte exactly as it arrived and is never reformatted, so a `performed_at` sent as `2026-06-02T10:45:00.000000Z` reads back with six fractional digits and one sent as `2027-01-01T00:00:00+02:00` reads back carrying that offset, even though every temporal comparison in this document normalises it to UTC first.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Wherever a rule in this document says an operation "bumps" or "increments" a row's `version`, it also means `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock instant on that same row.

`BinocularMeasurement` is the single exception. A measurement row is an immutable instrument reading: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and nothing in the service ever bumps a measurement's version, even when finalization recomputes the row's derived fields. It is also the one resource whose projection (§2.2) omits keys rather than nulling them: `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a measurement, not null.

Because several operations in this service mutate more than one row, a caller must expect more than one version counter to advance from a single call. Those secondary bumps are stated explicitly in §3 wherever they occur, and a caller relying on optimistic concurrency (§2.9) must re-read the parent it pinned after any such call. The counter is per row and per request, not per write: a handler that writes the same row several times — a claim appeal writing its bookkeeping fields and then its status, a cascade reaching one row down two different legs — advances that row's `version` by exactly 1 for the whole call, while refreshing its `updated_at` on each write. A call that is refused leaves every version untouched, with the two deliberate exceptions of rungs 33 and 36, whose committed bookkeeping write does bump the row it writes.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a single logical UTC clock which, before any caller has set it, reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. It must not fall back to real time under any circumstance. Because server-written instants render with three fractional digits (§2.4), a row created before the clock has ever been moved carries `created_at` `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Every `signed_at`, `checked_out_at`, `attested_at`, `recalled_at`, `discharged_at`, `sla_due_at`, `last_reauthorized_at`, `last_appeal_attempt_at`, `prescription_changed_at`, `started_at`, `mastered_at`, `returned_at`, `recall_due_at`, `responded_at`, `created_at` and `updated_at` value the server writes is taken from that clock, and every "now" in every temporal rule in this document means that clock's current reading.

A `ClinicAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the endpoint answers with a body of the shape `{ current_utc: string }` carrying the new reading, echoed back as the exact string the caller supplied rather than re-rendered to three digits — this is the caller-supplied-instant rule of §2.4, and it means `current_utc` may not look like the `created_at` values the same clock reading produces. The clock accepts a `to_iso8601` earlier than its current reading and simply moves backwards. Moving the clock is not a passive act. Two sweeps run as part of the same call. First, every `Authorization` whose `window_end_date` now lies strictly in the past and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` moves to `EXPIRED`, bumping that authorization's `version`. Second, every `Coverage` whose `effective_end_date` now lies strictly in the past and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED`, bumping that coverage's `version` — which means moving the clock can invalidate an `expected_coverage_version` a biller was holding, and a claim create pinned to the pre-sweep value then fails at rung 13. Both comparisons are on the calendar date, and both windows are inclusive of their end date: a clock sitting exactly on `window_end_date` has not passed it and expires nothing.

### 2.6. Units, Money and Rounding Discipline

Money is expressed in dollars with two decimal places, and every monetary result is rounded half-up to two decimal places. Percentages — `home_compliance_pct`, `adherence_pct`, `symptom_improvement_pct`, `composite_progress_score` and its component percentages — are likewise carried to two decimal places, rounded half-up, on a scale of `0.00` to `100.00`.

Where a calculation names the step at which rounding happens, that instruction is binding and rounding happens there and nowhere else. Three patterns recur and must not be confused with one another:

- **Round at every named step.** The claim adjudication waterfall in §3.10 rounds each of its seven numbered steps to two decimal places before the next step consumes the result. The total is not rounded once at the end; per-step rounding is precisely what makes the claim-dollar identity exact.
- **Round the components, then round the sum.** The composite progress score in §3.7 rounds each of its component percentages to two decimal places first, then multiplies each by its weight, sums the weighted terms without rounding them, and rounds the resulting sum once.
- **Round once at the very end.** The equipment damage fee in §3.9 carries the depreciated value and the salvage floor unrounded through the whole computation and rounds only the final fee.

Cumulative weighted therapy minutes are carried to one decimal place, rounded half-up. Unit counts — authorization visits, billable units, claim units, kit quantities, mastery tallies, patching hours, whole months and whole calendar years — are integers and are never rounded to a fraction. Near-point-of-convergence distances in centimetres are compared exactly as supplied, at the one decimal place the instrument reports, with no rounding applied to the comparison.

### 2.7. The Order of Refusal

When a single request violates several rules at once, the service returns exactly one error, and which one is fully determined. Every request walks the following thirty-six rungs from rung 1 downward and stops at the first rung whose condition is violated. A rung that does not apply to the endpoint being called is skipped without changing the order of the rungs that follow. Two rungs — 33 and 36 — commit a bookkeeping write before they refuse; that behaviour is described where it occurs and does not change their position on the ladder.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present, and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven role names. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** The supplied role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4. For the roles backed by an entity, `X-User-ID` names an existing row of that backing entity, and a `PatientUser` may act only on rows whose `patient_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Shape and range.** Required body fields are present and well typed. Numbers that must not be negative are not negative — this covers `timed_minutes`, `quantity`, `visits_authorized`, `additional_visits`, `logged_days_delta`, `adherent_days_delta`, `corrected_units` and `mastered_delta`. `scheduled_end_at` is strictly after `scheduled_start_at`. `effective_end_date` is on or after `effective_start_date`. `window_end_date` is on or after `window_start_date`. `useful_life_months` is at least 1. A `ciss_score` is between 0 and 60 inclusive and a `coinsurance_rate` is between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive. A `REASSESSMENT` supplies a `plan_id`, a `THERAPY_SESSION` claim supplies a `session_id` and an `EVALUATION` claim supplies an `assessment_id`. Finalizing an assessment requires at least one child measurement whose `metric_code` is `NPC`. A measurement supplies the value field its `metric_code` requires. Pagination `limit` is between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is at least 0. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400. Applies to every write and every list endpoint. Three of these checks cannot physically be run at the top of the ladder, and their position is pinned accordingly: the `NPC` precondition needs the battery row resolved first, so it runs after rung 7 but still ahead of rungs 12 and 14; the metric value-field check needs `metric_code` resolved first, so it runs after rung 4 but still ahead of rungs 7 and 12; and the claim's `session_id`/`assessment_id` requirement needs `service_class` resolved first, so it too runs after rung 4 but still ahead of every existence rung. Nothing else about the order changes. No other numeric field is range-checked at all — a negative `replacement_cost`, `allowed_amount_*`, `npc_break_cm`, `amplitude_d`, `initial_qty`, `prescribed_home_sessions_per_week` or `activities_mastered_delta` is accepted as supplied.
4. **Enum membership.** This is the exhaustive list of fields that route to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`: `diagnosis_category`, `discharge_reason`, `payer_type`, `assessment_type`, `purpose`, `metric_code`, `eye`, `discharge_criteria_mode`, `procedure_code`, `cancel_reason`, `item_class`, `returned_condition`, `service_class`, `direction`, `urgency`, `response_outcome`, `severity`, `close_reason`, `suspension_reason`, `equipment_class`, `credential`, the `outcome` supplied on a claim adjudication, and every status-valued filter on a list endpoint. No other field routes here; any other unacceptable value is `ERR_VALIDATION`. The split is between *membership* and *shape*: a well-formed string that is not a member of the declared set yields `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, HTTP 400, while a value that is missing altogether, null, or of the wrong JSON type is a rung-3 `ERR_VALIDATION` even in one of the fields listed above — so a `severity` of `"HUGE"` is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` and a `severity` of `7` or an absent `severity` is `ERR_VALIDATION`. Applies to every write carrying one of those enum fields and every list endpoint carrying an enum filter.
5. **Patient existence.** The `Patient` named in the path, or in a `patient_id` field, exists. Otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint whose path or body names a patient.
6. **Coverage existence.** The `Coverage` named in the path or in `coverage_id` exists. Otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to reading one coverage, creating an authorization, and creating a claim.
7. **Assessment existence.** The `Assessment` named in the path, or in `source_assessment_id`, `assessment_id`, `triggering_assessment_id` or `reassessment_id`, exists. Otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to every assessment endpoint and to therapy-plan creation, claim creation, referral creation, occlusion-prescription creation and reauthorization.
8. **Plan existence.** The `TherapyPlan` named in the path or in `plan_id` exists. Otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to every therapy-plan endpoint and to kit-loan creation and referral creation.
9. **Session existence.** The `TherapySession` named in the path or in `session_id` exists. Otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to every session endpoint and to claim creation.
10. **Claim existence.** The `Claim` named in the path exists. Otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to every claim endpoint except claim creation.
11. **Other references.** Every other referenced row exists — a `Payer`, a `Therapist` named by `therapist_id`, `performed_by_therapist_id` or `supervising_optometrist_id`, a `TherapyRoom`, an `Authorization`, a `PlanPhase`, a `KitItem`, a `KitLoan`, a `Referral` or an `OcclusionPrescription`. Otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint naming one of those rows.
12. **The discharge write gate.** When the patient the request touches has `clinical_status` `DISCHARGED` and the endpoint belongs to the clinical write family, the request is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED`, HTTP 409. The family and, just as importantly, the endpoints deliberately left outside it are enumerated exhaustively in §2.8.
13. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_authorization_version` equals the `Authorization`'s current `version`, `expected_plan_version` equals the `TherapyPlan`'s current `version`, and `expected_coverage_version` equals the `Coverage`'s current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412. Applies to in-office session scheduling, plan revision and claim creation, as detailed in §2.9.
14. **Transition legality.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, and every state precondition on a directly referenced row holds: the source assessment behind a new plan is `SIGNED` and of `assessment_type` `EVALUATION`; the coverage behind a claim submission has `status` `ACTIVE`; the authorization named at plan activation, or carried by a session, has `status` `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` — `EXHAUSTED` is deliberately an open state here, which is what keeps rung 23 reachable; a kit loan being fulfilled has `status` `REQUESTED`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, HTTP 409. Applies to every state-changing endpoint.
15. **The plan-active gate.** The `TherapyPlan` the request depends on has `status` `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 409. Applies to exactly six operations: scheduling an in-office session, logging a home session, advancing a phase, checking a session in, recording phase mastery, and creating a kit loan.
16. **Credential currency.** The `Therapist` named in `therapist_id` has a `credential_expires_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at` when the session is scheduled, and strictly after `performed_at` when the session is completed. An expiry exactly equal to the instant in question fails. Otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to in-office session scheduling and session completion.
17. **Segregation of duties.** Signing a `REASSESSMENT` requires `X-User-ID` to differ from the assessment's `performed_by_therapist_id`; correcting a claim requires `X-User-ID` to differ from the claim's `submitted_by_user_id`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 409. Applies to assessment signature and claim correction.
18. **Supervision.** When the session's `therapist_id` names a `Therapist` whose `credential` is `VISION_THERAPY_ASSISTANT`, completion requires `supervising_optometrist_id` to be supplied, to name a `Therapist` whose `credential` is `OPTOMETRIST`, and for that supervisor's `credential_expires_at` to be strictly after `performed_at`. Otherwise `ERR_SUPERVISION_REQUIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to session completion.
19. **Authorization window.** The UTC calendar date of `scheduled_start_at` lies inside the plan's authorization window, inclusive of both `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`. Otherwise `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_AUTH_WINDOW`, HTTP 422. Applies to in-office session scheduling.
20. **Room occupancy.** The requested room is free once a fifteen-minute turnover buffer is applied on both sides. Otherwise `ERR_ROOM_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Applies to in-office session scheduling. The exact interval arithmetic is in §3.5.
21. **Therapist occupancy.** The requested clinician has no overlapping booking under unbuffered, half-open interval arithmetic. Otherwise `ERR_THERAPIST_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Applies to in-office session scheduling.
22. **Therapist daily capacity.** The clinician's already-scheduled minutes on that UTC date plus the requested minutes do not exceed one hundred and eighty. Otherwise `ERR_THERAPIST_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409. Applies to in-office session scheduling.
23. **Visit pool.** The plan's authorization has at least one visit remaining. Otherwise `ERR_AUTH_VISITS_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409. Applies to in-office session scheduling.
24. **Reassessment currency for progression.** When the phase being entered has a `sequence_index` of 3 or higher, the plan has a `SIGNED` assessment of `assessment_type` `REASSESSMENT` whose `signed_at` is at or after the instant twenty-one days before the current logical clock reading. Otherwise `ERR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to phase advancement.
25. **Activity mastery.** The `IN_PROGRESS` phase has an `activities_mastered_count` at least equal to its `required_mastery_count`. Otherwise `ERR_MASTERY_NOT_MET`, HTTP 422. Applies to phase advancement.
26. **Home compliance.** The plan's `home_compliance_pct`, recomputed at the moment of the call, is at least 75.00. Exactly 75.00 passes. Otherwise `ERR_COMPLIANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, HTTP 422. Applies to phase advancement.
27. **Equipment recovery.** No `KitLoan` for this patient is in `status` `CHECKED_OUT` or `RECALLED`. Otherwise `ERR_LOAN_OUTSTANDING`, HTTP 409. Applies to patient discharge.
28. **Stock availability.** The `KitItem`'s `on_hand_qty` is at least the loan's `quantity`, and a loan against a `LOANABLE_SERIALIZED` item has a `quantity` of exactly 1. Otherwise `ERR_KIT_STOCK_DEPLETED`, HTTP 409. Applies to kit-loan fulfilment.
29. **Claim uniqueness.** At most one claim exists per `session_id` and at most one per `assessment_id`, regardless of the existing claim's status. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, HTTP 409. Applies to claim creation.
30. **Billability.** A claim whose `service_class` is `THERAPY_SESSION` requires the named session to be in `billing_status` `BILLABLE`; a claim whose `service_class` is `EVALUATION` requires the named assessment to be in `status` `SIGNED`. Otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_BILLABLE`, HTTP 422. Applies to claim creation.
31. **Vision frequency limit.** When the coverage's payer is of `payer_type` `VISION` and the stored last-paid service date matching this `service_class` is not null, the whole months from that date to the claim's `service_date` are at least the payer's `frequency_limit_months`. Otherwise `ERR_VISION_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`, HTTP 422. Applies to claim creation.
32. **Reauthorization look-back.** The `reassessment_id` supplied names a `SIGNED` assessment of `assessment_type` `REASSESSMENT` belonging to the same patient, whose `signed_at` is at or after the instant thirty days before the current logical clock reading. Otherwise `ERR_REAUTH_REASSESSMENT_STALE`, HTTP 422. Applies to reauthorization.
33. **Referral retry cap.** A re-referral is refused once the referral's `attempt_count` has reached 3; the referral is first written to `ABANDONED` and the call then fails with `ERR_REFERRAL_ATTEMPT_LIMIT`, HTTP 409. Applies to re-referral.
34. **Taper gate.** The prescription's `adherence_pct` is at least 70.0 and the acuity gain from `baseline_acuity_logmar` is at least 0.20 logMAR. Otherwise `ERR_TAPER_GATE_NOT_MET`, HTTP 422. Applies to occlusion tapering.
35. **Discharge reason precondition.** The precondition attached to the supplied `discharge_reason` holds; there is one precondition per reason, set out in §3.11. Otherwise `ERR_DISCHARGE_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`, HTTP 422. Applies to patient discharge.
36. **Appeal cap.** An appeal is refused once the claim's `appeal_count` has reached 2; the attempt counter and its timestamp are committed first and the call then fails with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, HTTP 409. Applies to claim appeal.

Two ordering consequences deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. First, rung 12 sits above every business rung, so on a discharged patient a gated endpoint reports `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED` and never the business failure underneath it — which is exactly why the exclusion list in §2.8 matters. Second, rung 14 sits above rung 36: once any later call has moved a claim out of `DENIED`, an appeal attempt is refused by rung 14 as an illegal transition, and the rung-36 bookkeeping write does not happen at all.

### 2.8. The Discharge Write Gate and Its Exclusions

Once a patient reaches `clinical_status` `DISCHARGED`, the clinical record freezes. Any request in the **clinical write family** that touches that patient — whether the patient is named in the path, named in the body, or reached indirectly through a plan, phase, session, assessment, loan, referral or prescription that belongs to them — is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED` (HTTP 409) at rung 12, before any business rule below it is evaluated.

The clinical write family is exactly these twenty-nine endpoints:

`POST /vision-therapy/assessments`, `POST /vision-therapy/assessments/{id}/measurements`, `POST /vision-therapy/assessments/{id}/finalize`, `POST /vision-therapy/assessments/{id}/sign`, `POST /vision-therapy/assessments/{id}/amend`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/activate`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/advance-phase`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/revise`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/suspend`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/resume`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/phases`, `POST /vision-therapy/phases/{id}/mastery`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/sessions`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapy-plans/{id}/home-sessions`, `POST /vision-therapy/sessions/{id}/check-in`, `POST /vision-therapy/sessions/{id}/complete`, `POST /vision-therapy/sessions/{id}/cancel`, `POST /vision-therapy/sessions/{id}/no-show`, `POST /vision-therapy/sessions/{id}/attest`, `POST /vision-therapy/occlusion-prescriptions`, `POST /vision-therapy/occlusion-prescriptions/{id}/adherence`, `POST /vision-therapy/occlusion-prescriptions/{id}/taper`, `POST /vision-therapy/kit-loans`, `POST /vision-therapy/kit-loans/{id}/fulfill`, `POST /vision-therapy/authorizations`, `POST /vision-therapy/authorizations/{id}/reauthorize`, `POST /vision-therapy/referrals`, and `POST /vision-therapy/referrals/{id}/re-refer`.

The gate deliberately has holes, and widening it beyond the twenty-nine endpoints above is a specification error rather than a harmless extra safeguard. The clinic still has to collect its money, recover its equipment, answer the referrals it sent, tidy up its visit pools and read the chart after a patient leaves. These sixteen write endpoints sit **outside** the gate and operate normally on a discharged patient:

`POST /vision-therapy/claims`, `POST /vision-therapy/claims/{id}/submit`, `POST /vision-therapy/claims/{id}/adjudicate`, `POST /vision-therapy/claims/{id}/appeal`, `POST /vision-therapy/claims/{id}/correct`, `POST /vision-therapy/kit-loans/{id}/return`, `POST /vision-therapy/referrals/{id}/respond`, `POST /vision-therapy/authorizations/{id}/close`, `POST /vision-therapy/coverages`, `POST /vision-therapy/patients/{id}/discharge`, `POST /vision-therapy/patients`, `POST /vision-therapy/payers`, `POST /vision-therapy/therapists`, `POST /vision-therapy/rooms`, `POST /vision-therapy/kit-items`, and `POST /vision-therapy/_clock/set_current_utc`.

Every one of the twenty-four `GET` endpoints is likewise outside the gate; a discharged patient's entire record remains readable.

Discharge itself being outside the gate is load-bearing. A second discharge attempt on an already-discharged patient is refused by rung 14 as an illegal transition, reporting `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and not by rung 12. Equally load-bearing is that the non-gated write surface keeps rungs 13 through 36 observable on a discharged patient: a stale coverage version on a claim still reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, a duplicate claim still reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, an over-capped appeal still reports `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, and an over-capped re-referral — which *is* gated — is the case where rung 12 pre-empts rung 33.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`. In each case the version field is a required input, not an optional one, and omitting it is a rung-3 `ERR_VALIDATION` failure. In each case a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is rejected at rung 13 with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field.

Scheduling an in-office session must carry `expected_authorization_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Authorization` bound to the plan. Scheduling draws one unit out of that pool; a caller whose pinned version no longer matches is reasoning from a stale view of the pool, because some other writer has drawn, released or reauthorized units since the caller last read it, and the draw is refused.

Opening a plan revision must carry `expected_plan_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `TherapyPlan` named in the path. A revision legitimately supersedes the plan's prior prescription, so a second revision against the same observed version is a perfectly valid product operation rather than an illegal state change; it is refused purely because `expected_plan_version` is stale relative to the plan's current `version`.

Creating a claim must carry `expected_coverage_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Coverage` named in `coverage_id`. A claim is filed against that coverage's accumulator snapshot — `deductible_met` under a medical payer, `vision_allowance_remaining` and the last-paid service dates under a vision payer. If the coverage's `version` has advanced since the biller read it, the accumulators the biller reasoned about are no longer the ones adjudication will consume, and the create is refused.

### 2.10. Conservation Identities

Four named identities must hold at every instant an API caller can observe, on every row in scope, and not merely at the end of some batch.

**The visit-unit identity.** On every `Authorization` row, `visits_authorized` plus `visits_released` equals `visits_consumed` plus `visits_remaining`. It holds after a consume by in-office scheduling, after a release by any session cancellation including both cascade-driven ones, after a no-show — which changes nothing, because the unit stays consumed — after a reauthorization, in which `visits_authorized` and `visits_remaining` both rise by the same `additional_visits`, and after a close, which leaves all four counters untouched.

**The kit-unit identity.** On every `KitItem` row, `initial_qty` equals `on_hand_qty` plus `on_loan_qty` plus `dispensed_qty` plus `retired_qty`. It holds at catalogue creation, where `on_hand_qty` takes the whole `initial_qty` and the other three are 0; after fulfilment on either arm of the serialized/consumable fork; and after a return on both the recovered arm and the lost arm.

**The claim-dollar identity.** On every adjudicated `Claim` row, `billed_amount` equals `plan_paid_amount` plus `patient_responsibility_amount` plus `contractual_adjustment_amount` plus `denied_amount`. It holds after an adjudication with outcome `PAID` under either adjudication variant, and after an adjudication with outcome `DENIED` under either variant.

**The vision-allowance identity.** On every `Coverage` row whose payer is of `payer_type` `VISION`, `vision_allowance_total` equals `vision_allowance_used` plus `vision_allowance_remaining`. It holds at coverage creation, where `vision_allowance_used` is `0.00` and `vision_allowance_remaining` takes the whole `vision_allowance_total`, and after every `PAID` adjudication under the vision variant.

### 2.11. Cascade Chains

Two multi-hop cascades run in this service, and both are named here because their later hops are the ones implementers most often miss.

**CASCADE-A**, the red-flag chain, begins when an assessment is finalized with `red_flag_present` true. Hop 1 creates an outbound urgent referral. Hop 2 suspends the plan that referral names, and is a property of *creating* an outbound urgent referral rather than of hop 1 specifically — a referral posted directly to `POST /vision-therapy/referrals` with `direction` `OUTBOUND`, `urgency` `URGENT` and a `plan_id` naming an `ACTIVE` plan fires hops 2 through 5 exactly as a cascade-created one does. Hop 3 cancels that plan's future sessions. Hop 4 releases an authorization unit for each cancelled in-office session. Hop 5 recalls the plan's checked-out equipment. Each hop's exact preconditions and side effects are given in §3.2, §3.4, §3.5, §3.9 and §3.11.

**CASCADE-B**, the discharge chain, begins at patient discharge. Hop 1 moves the patient's open plans to `DISCHARGED`. Hop 2 cancels those plans' scheduled and checked-in sessions. Hop 3 releases an authorization unit for each cancelled in-office session. Hop 4 is the equipment-recall leg shared with CASCADE-A, and it never fires during a discharge: the recall condition requires the releasing session's plan to be `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL`, whereas hop 1 has already moved that plan to `DISCHARGED`, and independently rung 27 refuses the discharge outright if any loan is still `CHECKED_OUT` or `RECALLED`. Hop 5 closes the authorizations those plans were bound to, reaching each one only through the `authorization_id` of a plan hop 1 discharged, visiting an authorization named by two such plans only once, and skipping any pool not currently `ACTIVE`, `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` — an already-`CLOSED` pool keeps the `close_reason` it was closed with and is not touched or re-bumped. A separate fan-out, not numbered as part of CASCADE-B, discontinues the patient's active and tapered occlusion prescriptions.

Every row a cascade writes has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed exactly as if the change had been made by a direct call.

### 2.12. Nested Path Resolution

Several resources are addressed through a parent: measurements under an assessment, phases and sessions under a therapy plan. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. Appending a measurement to an assessment that does not exist is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` and appending a phase to a plan that does not exist is `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` — but only once the body has survived rungs 3 and 4, which sit above every existence rung and are not suspended for nested paths. A measurement appended to a missing assessment with a body that omits its metric's value field reports `ERR_VALIDATION`, and one whose `metric_code` is not a declared member reports `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`; only a well-formed body reaches `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Where a child's parent identifier also exists as a field on the child — `assessment_id` on a measurement, `plan_id` on a phase or a session — the value is always taken from the path and never from the request body, and a body attempting to set it is ignored rather than honoured.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Patient and Opening the Binocular Battery

Reception or the clinic administrator registers a patient with a `full_name`, a `date_of_birth` given as an ISO 8601 calendar date, and a `diagnosis_category` drawn from `CONVERGENCE_INSUFFICIENCY`, `ACCOMMODATIVE_DYSFUNCTION`, `OCULOMOTOR_DYSFUNCTION` and `AMBLYOPIA`. A `guardian_name` may be supplied for a minor and defaults to null when it is not. The new record begins life in `clinical_status` `INTAKE`, with `discharge_reason`, `discharged_at` and `discharged_by_user_id` all null. The `diagnosis_category` chosen here is not cosmetic: it selects which weight set the composite progress score uses much later in the episode, and changing the answer changes the number.

A binocular battery is opened by an optometrist or a vision therapist against a `patient_id`, and is created in `status` `DRAFT`. Four further inputs define what the battery is. `assessment_type` is either `EVALUATION`, the intake battery, or `REASSESSMENT`, a progress battery run against an existing plan — and a `REASSESSMENT` requires a `plan_id` naming that plan — a `REASSESSMENT` without one is a rung-3 `ERR_VALIDATION` — while the initial `EVALUATION` leaves `plan_id` null: an `EVALUATION` that supplies a `plan_id` anyway has it forced to null and discarded without ever being existence-checked, so naming a plan that does not exist there is not an error. `purpose` is `DIAGNOSTIC`, `PROGRESS` or `DISCHARGE_READINESS`. `encounter_date` is the ISO 8601 calendar date the battery was run. `performed_by_therapist_id` names the clinician who performed it, and is the value the segregation-of-duties rule will later compare against the signer.

Age is fixed at the moment the battery is opened, not read live afterwards. `patient_age_years` is the number of whole calendar years from the patient's `date_of_birth` to the `encounter_date`: take the encounter year minus the birth year, and subtract one more year when the encounter's month-and-day pair falls before the birth date's month-and-day pair. It is a whole number and is never derived by dividing days by 365.25. Every age-derived reference band in the service reads this stored value.

Instrument readings are appended to the battery one row at a time by an optometrist, a vision therapist or an assistant. Each row's `assessment_id` is taken from the path and never from the body. Every row declares a `metric_code` — `NPC`, `ACCOMMODATIVE_AMPLITUDE`, `VERGENCE_BASE_OUT`, `VISUAL_ACUITY` or `SYMPTOM_SURVEY` — and an `eye` of `OD`, `OS` or `OU`. Each `metric_code` requires its own value field and omitting it is a shape failure: an `NPC` row requires both `npc_break_cm` and `npc_recovery_cm` in centimetres, because a single near-point reading carries two independent thresholds; an `ACCOMMODATIVE_AMPLITUDE` row requires `amplitude_d` in dioptres; a `VERGENCE_BASE_OUT` row requires `vergence_bo_break_pd` in prism dioptres; a `VISUAL_ACUITY` row requires `acuity_logmar`; and a `SYMPTOM_SURVEY` row requires a `ciss_score`, the raw Convergence Insufficiency Symptom Survey total, which must be a whole number from 0 to a maximum of 60 and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` outside that range. The value fields of the other four metrics are plain numbers with no range check of their own, so a negative `npc_break_cm` or `amplitude_d` is accepted and simply flows into the comparisons below. Because the required value field is decided by `metric_code`, that shape check runs after `metric_code` has cleared enum membership: a row carrying both an unrecognised `metric_code` and no value field reports `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`. Any row may carry `red_flag`, a clinician-marked warning such as acute-onset diplopia or suspected papilloedema, defaulting to false. Measurement rows are immutable readings: they carry an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else from the common property set.

Writing an accommodative-amplitude row computes its age-derived reference band immediately, and which band is computed is decided by the parent battery's `purpose` — this is the first of the service's data-driven forks. When the `purpose` is `DIAGNOSTIC` or `PROGRESS`, the row's `reference_variant` is `STANDARD`: `expected_min_value` is 15.0 dioptres less one quarter of a dioptre — 0.25 — for each whole year of `patient_age_years`, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and `expected_band_high` is that minimum plus a band width of 2.00 dioptres, again rounded half-up to two decimals. When the `purpose` is `DISCHARGE_READINESS`, the row's `reference_variant` is `FUNCTIONAL` and the practice's narrower functional band applies: `expected_min_value` is 16.0 dioptres less 0.28 dioptres for each whole year of age, rounded half-up to two decimals, and `expected_band_high` is that minimum plus a band width of only 1.00 dioptre. In both variants `within_reference_band` is true when `amplitude_d` is at or above `expected_min_value` and at or below `expected_band_high`. The two variants really do disagree: at age 10 the standard minimum is 12.50 dioptres and the functional minimum is 13.20, so an amplitude of 13.00 sits inside the standard band and below the functional one.

Both `expected_min_value` and `expected_band_high` are null on every row whose `metric_code` is not `ACCOMMODATIVE_AMPLITUDE`, and `reference_variant` is null there too. Where the band does not apply, `within_reference_band` is false rather than null. A row's own `diagnostic_flag` records whether that single reading breaches a diagnostic threshold on its own; for an amplitude row that means `amplitude_d` strictly below `expected_min_value`, evaluated against whichever variant's minimum the row carries.

Batteries and their readings are readable individually and as lists. The battery list can be narrowed by `patient_id`, `plan_id`, `performed_by_therapist_id`, `signed_by_therapist_id`, `assessment_type`, `purpose` and `status`; the reading list for one battery can be narrowed by `metric_code`. A patient record is readable on its own, and is the one patient-scoped read a `PatientUser` may perform.

### 3.2. Finalizing, Signing and Amending the Battery

Finalization is where the raw readings become clinical conclusions. An optometrist or a vision therapist finalizes a battery that is still in `status` `DRAFT`, and the battery must already carry at least one `NPC` reading — a finalize without one is refused as a shape failure with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Finalizing a battery that is already `FINALIZED` or `SIGNED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, but the `NPC` requirement is checked first, so a battery that is both already finalized and short of an `NPC` reading reports `ERR_VALIDATION`, and so does one belonging to a discharged patient. The transition is `DRAFT → FINALIZED`, and it bumps the battery's `version`.

The near-point evaluation reads exactly one row: the `NPC` measurement on this battery with the greatest `created_at`, with the last one created winning any tie. That single reading is tested against two independent threshold pairs. It is a **diagnostic breach** when `npc_break_cm` is strictly greater than 6.0 centimetres or `npc_recovery_cm` is strictly greater than 9.0 centimetres; the row's `diagnostic_flag` is set to exactly that answer. It is a **discharge pass** when `npc_break_cm` is at most 5.0 centimetres *and* `npc_recovery_cm` is at most 7.0 centimetres. Distances are compared as supplied, at one decimal place, with no rounding.

Two battery-level booleans follow from that, and where the battery carries more than one accommodative-amplitude reading both consult exactly one of them: the amplitude row with the greatest `created_at`, with the last one created winning any tie, in the same way the near-point evaluation picks its `NPC` row. `therapy_indicated` is true when there is a diagnostic breach, or when an accommodative-amplitude row exists on this battery and that row's `diagnostic_flag` is true — which, since `diagnostic_flag` is measured against whichever variant's minimum that row carries, means a `DISCHARGE_READINESS` battery judges its amplitude against the functional minimum here and not against the standard one. `discharge_metric_met` is true when there is a discharge pass *and* either no accommodative-amplitude row exists on the battery or that row's `amplitude_d` is at or above its own `expected_min_value`. A third boolean, `red_flag_present`, is true when any child reading on the battery carries `red_flag` true.

Finalization also refreshes the derived fields of every child reading — each row's `diagnostic_flag` and, for amplitude rows, `reference_variant`, `expected_min_value`, `expected_band_high` and `within_reference_band` — and then recomputes `discharge_ready` as described in §3.7 on the one plan named by this battery's own `plan_id` — never on a plan that merely cites this battery as its `source_assessment_id`, and not at all when `plan_id` is null, which is always the case for an `EVALUATION`. Finalizing an `EVALUATION` therefore recomputes no plan's `discharge_ready`. When the battery is a `REASSESSMENT`, finalization additionally computes `composite_progress_score`, `symptom_improvement_pct`, `improvement_delta_points` and `plateau_flag`, updates the plan's `progress_state` and `consecutive_low_delta_count`, and may drive a phase regression; all of that is set out in §3.7. On an `EVALUATION` those four reassessment fields stay null — `composite_progress_score`, `symptom_improvement_pct` and `improvement_delta_points` are null and `plateau_flag` is false.

Finally, when `red_flag_present` is true, finalization opens CASCADE-A: the service creates a referral for the same patient with `direction` `OUTBOUND`, `urgency` `URGENT`, `status` `SENT`, `triggering_assessment_id` naming this battery and `plan_id` copied from the battery's own `plan_id`. Because no caller supplied them, the cascade-created referral's `referring_provider_name`, `target_provider_name` and `reason` are all null; its `attempt_count` is 1 and its `sla_due_at` is the urgent twenty-four-hour figure. What that referral does to the plan is described in §3.11.

Signing is reserved to an optometrist and moves the battery `FINALIZED → SIGNED`. The signature stamps `signed_by_therapist_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `signed_at` with the current clock instant, and bumps `version`. On a `REASSESSMENT` the signer must not be the performer: when `X-User-ID` equals the battery's `performed_by_therapist_id` the signature is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. That restriction applies only to a `REASSESSMENT`; an `EVALUATION` may be signed by whoever performed it. Signing an `EVALUATION` for a patient still in `clinical_status` `INTAKE` is what activates the patient: the patient moves `INTAKE → ACTIVE` and the patient's `version` is bumped. A second signed `EVALUATION` for a patient who is already `ACTIVE` leaves `clinical_status` untouched.

A signed battery can be re-opened for correction, but only twice. Amendment is an optometrist operation, takes the battery `SIGNED → DRAFT`, increments `amendment_count` by 1, clears `signed_by_therapist_id` and `signed_at` back to null, bumps `version`, and accepts an optional free-text `amendment_reason` that is validated as a string and then discarded — no field on the battery holds it and nothing later reads it. It is permitted only while `amendment_count` is still below 2, so the loop `DRAFT → FINALIZED → SIGNED → DRAFT` can be walked twice; a third amendment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as is any amendment of a battery that is not currently `SIGNED`.

### 3.3. Payers, Coverages and the Prior-Authorization Visit Pool

Before anyone can be billed, the clinic administrator configures the payers it contracts with. A payer carries a trade `name` and a `payer_type` of either `MEDICAL` or `VISION`, and that single enum decides which of the two adjudication arms in §3.10 will run for every claim filed against it. A payer also carries the three contracted allowed amounts a medical adjudication draws on — `allowed_amount_timed_unit` per timed therapeutic-activity unit, `allowed_amount_flat_session` for one flat orthoptic-training session and `allowed_amount_evaluation` for one evaluation or reassessment encounter, each to two decimal places — a `coinsurance_rate` expressed as a fraction between 0.00 and 1.00 that is applied to what survives the deductible — a rate outside that closed interval is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` — and a `frequency_limit_months` giving the minimum whole months a vision plan requires between two paid services of the same class. All seven values are supplied at creation whatever the `payer_type`; the arm that does not run simply ignores the ones it does not need. The three allowed amounts are stored rounded half-up to two decimal places, while `coinsurance_rate` is stored exactly as supplied, and neither the allowed amounts nor `frequency_limit_months` is checked for sign.

A billing specialist or the clinic administrator then attaches a coverage to a patient by naming a `patient_id`, a `payer_id` and the `member_number` printed on the card, together with an inclusive `effective_start_date` and `effective_end_date`. A coverage opens in `status` `ACTIVE` and moves to `TERMINATED` when the logical clock passes its `effective_end_date`, as described in §2.5. Creation never consults the clock: a coverage whose window has already closed relative to the current reading is still created `ACTIVE`, and only the next clock move terminates it. Four accumulators may be seeded at creation and are otherwise defaulted: `deductible_total` and `deductible_met`, both `0.00` by default and both meaningful only under a medical payer; `vision_allowance_total`, `0.00` by default; and the two last-paid service dates, `last_paid_service_date_therapy` and `last_paid_service_date_evaluation`, both null by default, which record the service date of the most recent paid claim in each frequency bucket. Two further accumulators are computed rather than supplied: `vision_allowance_used` starts at `0.00` and `vision_allowance_remaining` starts equal to `vision_allowance_total`, which is how the vision-allowance identity holds from the very first instant. All three seeded money figures are stored rounded half-up to two decimal places and none of them is checked for sign. Coverages are readable individually and as a list narrowed by `patient_id`, `payer_id` and `status`. Coverage creation sits outside the discharge write gate, so a coverage may be attached to a discharged patient's record for late billing.

The prior-authorization pool is the quota at the heart of the clinic's scheduling. A billing specialist grants one by naming the `patient_id` and the `coverage_id` it is granted under, recording the payer-issued `auth_number`, and declaring `visits_authorized` in-office visit units together with an inclusive `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`. The pool is created with `visits_consumed` at 0, `visits_released` at 0 and `visits_remaining` equal to `visits_authorized`, in `status` `ACTIVE`, with `reauthorization_count` 0, `last_reauthorized_at` null and `close_reason` null. The two identifiers are checked only for existence and never against each other: a pool may be granted naming a `coverage_id` that belongs to some other patient, and `visits_authorized` may be 0, which creates a pool that is `ACTIVE` yet already has nothing to give. Every in-office session scheduled against the pool costs exactly one unit; there is no operation anywhere in the service that consumes two units at once or fractions of one.

Four counters, one identity. `visits_remaining` is always `visits_authorized` less `visits_consumed` plus `visits_released`. `visits_consumed` and `visits_released` are cumulative tallies and are never decremented — a release does not undo a consume, it records a second, opposite movement — which is exactly why the visit-unit identity of §2.10 is stable rather than merely arithmetically convenient.

The pool's own state axis has a cycle in it. Scheduling a session while the pool is `ACTIVE` consumes a unit and leaves it `ACTIVE`; scheduling the session that takes the last unit consumes it, drives `visits_remaining` to 0 and moves the pool `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`. A cancellation that releases a unit back into an `ACTIVE` pool leaves it `ACTIVE`, and a cancellation that releases a unit back into an `EXHAUSTED` pool moves it `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE`, closing the cycle. `EXHAUSTED` is deliberately treated as an *open* state, not an illegal one: a scheduling attempt against an exhausted pool is refused as a business failure with `ERR_AUTH_VISITS_EXHAUSTED`, never as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Independently of all of this, the clock sweep moves a pool that is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` to `EXPIRED` once the clock passes `window_end_date`.

Reauthorization is a billing specialist's operation and is the pool's refill. It takes `additional_visits`, a `new_window_end_date`, and a `reassessment_id` that must name a `SIGNED` battery of `assessment_type` `REASSESSMENT` belonging to the same patient, signed no earlier than thirty days before the current clock reading; a citation older than that, or one that names anything other than a signed reassessment for that patient, is refused with `ERR_REAUTH_REASSESSMENT_STALE`. A pool may be reauthorized while it is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`. On success, `visits_authorized` and `visits_remaining` each rise by `additional_visits`, so the identity survives the refill untouched; `window_end_date` is rewritten to `new_window_end_date` — which is validated only as a calendar date, never against `window_start_date`, so a refill may legitimately shorten the window or move its end before its start; `reauthorization_count` increments by 1; `last_reauthorized_at` is stamped with the clock; the pool's `status` becomes `ACTIVE`; and the pool's `version` is bumped.

Closing a pool is terminal. A billing specialist closes an `ACTIVE`, `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` pool with a `close_reason` of `PAYER_TERMINATED` or `EPISODE_COMPLETE`, and the discharge cascade closes pools with the third value, `PATIENT_DISCHARGED`; that third value is not reserved, and a direct close supplying it is accepted like any other member of the enum. Closing writes `status` `CLOSED` and the reason, bumps `version`, and touches none of the four counters, so the identity continues to hold on a closed pool. Closing is outside the discharge write gate. Pools are readable individually — the read exposes all four counters — and as a list narrowed by `patient_id`, `coverage_id` and `status`.

### 3.4. Building and Activating a Therapy Plan

Only an optometrist authors a therapy plan, and only from a battery that has been signed. Creation names the `patient_id` and a `source_assessment_id`, which must point at a `SIGNED` battery whose `assessment_type` is `EVALUATION`; anything else — an unsigned battery, a reassessment — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Two further inputs shape the whole episode. `discharge_criteria_mode` is `METRIC` or `GOAL`, and it is the data-driven fork that decides which discharge-readiness test the plan will be judged by, as §3.7 explains. `prescribed_home_sessions_per_week` is the home-practice prescription, and it becomes the denominator source for the compliance metric in §3.6.

Creation snapshots the plan's five baselines from the source battery's readings, and those baselines are what every later progress percentage is measured against: `baseline_npc_break_cm` from the near-point break, `baseline_amplitude_d` from the accommodative amplitude, `baseline_vergence_bo_break_pd` from the base-out vergence break, `baseline_acuity_logmar` from the best-corrected acuity, and `baseline_ciss_score` from the symptom survey. Where the source battery carries several readings of one metric, the baseline is taken from the row with the greatest `created_at`, the last one created winning any tie. Any baseline whose metric has no row on the source battery is null, and a null baseline is not an error — it simply makes that metric contribute nothing later.

A new plan opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `authorization_id` null, `current_phase_index` 1, `revision_count` 0, `consecutive_low_delta_count` 0, `cumulative_therapy_minutes` 0.0, `home_compliance_pct` `0.00`, `discharge_ready` false, `suspension_reason` null, `previous_prescribed_home_sessions_per_week` null and `prescription_changed_at` null. It also opens on a second, entirely independent state axis: `progress_state`, which starts at `ON_TRACK` and evolves separately from `status` — a single plan row therefore always carries two current states at once, and a transition on one axis never implies a transition on the other except where this document says so explicitly.

Phases are the plan's ladder, appended by an optometrist one at a time. A phase carries a 1-based `sequence_index`, a human `name`, and a `required_mastery_count` giving how many activities must be mastered before the plan may leave it. The `sequence_index` is expected to be unique within the plan, but uniqueness is advisory rather than enforced: nothing rejects a second phase at the same index, and where two exist the ladder machinery — activation, advancement and regression — resolves that index to whichever of them was written first. Nor is there any precondition on the plan's own `status`: a phase may be appended to a plan in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `UNDER_REVISION`, `COMPLETED` or `DISCHARGED` alike. Every phase is created in `status` `LOCKED` with `activities_mastered_count` 0 and both `started_at` and `mastered_at` null. Phases are walked strictly in ascending `sequence_index`; there is no skipping and no re-ordering. A plan's phases are readable as a list, narrowable by `status`, returned in ascending `sequence_index` rather than the usual creation order.

Activation is the plan's start button and also the return arm of its revision cycle. An optometrist supplies an `authorization_id`; the named pool must belong to the same patient and must be in `status` `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`, and a pool in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Activating a plan that is in `DRAFT` requires at least one phase with `sequence_index` 1 to exist, moves the plan `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, binds `authorization_id`, sets `current_phase_index` to 1, moves that first phase `LOCKED → IN_PROGRESS` with `started_at` stamped from the clock, and bumps both the plan's and the phase's `version`. Activating a plan that is in `UNDER_REVISION` instead moves it `UNDER_REVISION → ACTIVE`, rebinds `authorization_id` to the supplied pool, and additionally resets `progress_state` from `REVISION_PENDING` back to `ON_TRACK` when it was sitting at `REVISION_PENDING`; it does not disturb the phase ladder.

Revision is how a prescription is legitimately superseded, and it is the one plan operation guarded by optimistic concurrency. An optometrist may revise a plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, supplying `expected_plan_version` equal to the plan's current `version` — a stale value is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — and may attach a free-text `revision_reason`, which, like the battery's `amendment_reason`, is validated as a string and then discarded rather than stored. The plan moves to `UNDER_REVISION`, `revision_count` increments by 1, and `version` is bumped. When the call also supplies a new `prescribed_home_sessions_per_week`, the old figure is copied into `previous_prescribed_home_sessions_per_week`, the new figure replaces it, and `prescription_changed_at` is stamped with the clock; when it does not, all three of those fields are left exactly as they were. That timestamp is not bookkeeping — §3.6 uses it to decide which of the two prescriptions a compliance window is measured against.

Suspension takes an `ACTIVE` plan to `SUSPENDED` and records why, in `suspension_reason`. An optometrist suspends with an explicit reason of `CLINICIAN_HOLD`, and the red-flag cascade suspends with `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL`; `suspension_reason` is a required rung-3 field on the direct call and both members of the enum are accepted there, so an optometrist who supplies `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL` by hand produces a suspension that behaves in every later respect — including the equipment-recall leg — exactly like a cascade-driven one. Suspension is never a quiet flag: as CASCADE-A hop 3, every session on the plan whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN` and whose `scheduled_start_at` is at or after the current clock reading is cancelled with `cancel_reason` `PLAN_SUSPENDED`, and each cancelled in-office session releases its authorization unit as described in §3.5. Sessions already in the past are left alone. Resuming reverses it, moving `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and clearing `suspension_reason` to null, but a plan suspended for `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL` may not be resumed until every referral on that plan with `direction` `OUTBOUND` and `urgency` `URGENT` has reached `ACCEPTED` or `ABANDONED`; while any of them is still `SENT` or `DECLINED`, the resume is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A plan suspended for `CLINICIAN_HOLD` carries no such precondition.

Mastery is recorded against the phase that is currently `IN_PROGRESS` — but that is a description of how the clinic uses the endpoint, not a precondition it enforces. An optometrist, a vision therapist or an assistant supplies a non-negative `mastered_delta`, which is added to the `activities_mastered_count` of whichever phase the path names, whatever that phase's `status`, and bumps that phase's `version`; a `LOCKED` or `MASTERED` phase accepts the delta and keeps its status. The only state precondition is on the plan behind the phase, which must be `ACTIVE`; a mastery record against a plan in any other status is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and a phase whose plan row is missing entirely is refused the same way.

Plans are readable individually — the read exposes both state axes and every derived field — and as a list narrowed by `patient_id`, `source_assessment_id`, `authorization_id`, `status` and `progress_state`.

### 3.5. Scheduling and Delivering In-Office Sessions

Two configuration records underpin scheduling, both owned by the clinic administrator. A clinician record carries a `full_name`, a `credential` of `OPTOMETRIST`, `CERTIFIED_VISION_THERAPIST` or `VISION_THERAPY_ASSISTANT`, a `credential_expires_at` instant, and an optional `npi` national provider identifier that defaults to null. A therapy room carries a `name` and an `equipment_class` of `STANDARD`, `VECTOGRAM_SUITE` or `COMPUTER_ORTHOPTICS`.

Booking an in-office session is the single most heavily guarded operation in the service, because it simultaneously books a clinician, books a room, and draws a unit out of a finite pool. Reception, an optometrist or a vision therapist books one against a plan, supplying a `therapist_id`, a `room_id`, a `scheduled_start_at` and a `scheduled_end_at` — the end instant is exclusive and must be strictly after the start — a `procedure_code`, and `expected_authorization_version` pinning the plan's authorization pool. The `procedure_code` is `TIMED_THERAPEUTIC_ACTIVITY` or `FLAT_ORTHOPTIC_TRAINING` for an in-office booking. The created row has `modality` `IN_OFFICE`, `schedule_status` `SCHEDULED` and `billing_status` `PENDING`; its `patient_id` and its `authorization_id` are copied from the plan rather than supplied, `performed_at`, `timed_minutes`, `supervising_optometrist_id`, `cancel_reason`, `attested_at`, `attestation_statement` and `activity_notes` are null, and `billable_units`, `activities_mastered_delta`, `charge_amount` and `weighted_minutes` are all zero — `charge_amount` at `0.00` and `weighted_minutes` at `0.0`, the latter staying there until the session is completed.

Seven checks stand between the request and the booking, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.7, so the first one to fail is the one reported. The pinned `expected_authorization_version` must match the pool's current `version`, or `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, and the pool must be `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both of those checks need a pool to test: a plan with no `authorization_id` bound yet has neither evaluated against it and falls straight through to the next check, which it always fails, because only a `DRAFT` plan can be unbound. The plan must be in `status` `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The clinician's `credential_expires_at` must be strictly after `scheduled_start_at` — an expiry landing exactly on the start instant fails — or `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`. The UTC calendar date of `scheduled_start_at` must fall inside the pool's window, inclusive of both `window_start_date` and `window_end_date`, or `ERR_SESSION_OUTSIDE_AUTH_WINDOW`. Then the room, then the clinician, then the clinician's day, then the pool.

Room and clinician contention use deliberately different interval arithmetic, and confusing the two is the classic implementation error here. A **room** carries a fifteen-minute turnover buffer applied on both sides: the booking is refused when some other session on the same `room_id`, in `schedule_status` `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`, satisfies both of two conditions at once — the new session's `scheduled_start_at` is earlier than that session's `scheduled_end_at` advanced by fifteen minutes, and that session's `scheduled_start_at` is earlier than the new session's `scheduled_end_at` advanced by fifteen minutes. A gap of exactly fifteen minutes between two bookings in the same room is therefore accepted; a gap of fourteen minutes is refused with `ERR_ROOM_CONFLICT`. A **clinician** carries no buffer at all — the turnover allowance for a clinician is zero minutes — and plain half-open overlap decides: the booking is refused when some other session on the same `therapist_id`, in `schedule_status` `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`, has the new session starting strictly before that session ends and that session starting strictly before the new session ends. Exact adjacency, where a booking starts at the very instant the previous one ends, is accepted; a genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_THERAPIST_CONFLICT`.

Aggregate clinician capacity is checked next, over a different row set again. Take the UTC calendar date of the requested `scheduled_start_at`; sum, over every in-office session for that same clinician whose `scheduled_start_at` falls on that date and whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`, the whole minutes between its `scheduled_start_at` and its `scheduled_end_at`; add the whole minutes of the requested booking. Cancelled sessions are excluded from that sum, though no-shows are counted. If the total is strictly greater than one hundred and eighty minutes the booking is refused with `ERR_THERAPIST_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`; a total of exactly one hundred and eighty is accepted.

Last comes the money. The plan's pool must have at least one visit remaining, or the booking is refused with `ERR_AUTH_VISITS_EXHAUSTED`. On success the pool gives up exactly one unit: `visits_consumed` rises by 1, `visits_remaining` falls by 1, the pool's `version` is bumped, and if `visits_remaining` reaches 0 the pool moves `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED` in the same call.

Arrival is recorded by reception, an optometrist, a vision therapist or an assistant, taking an in-office session `SCHEDULED → CHECKED_IN` and bumping its `version`. Check-in requires the plan to still be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and it applies only to in-office sessions.

Completion is where clinical delivery becomes money. An optometrist, a vision therapist or an assistant completes a `CHECKED_IN` session, supplying the `performed_at` instant the work actually happened and the `timed_minutes` of direct therapeutic activity delivered, optionally an `activities_mastered_delta` of activities mastered during the session, which defaults to 0 and, unlike the phase endpoint's `mastered_delta`, is not one of the fields rung 3 checks for sign, and optionally a `supervising_optometrist_id`. Whatever is supplied for the supervisor — including nothing — is written onto the session, so completing without one leaves `supervising_optometrist_id` null. The running clinician's `credential_expires_at` must be strictly after `performed_at`, or `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`. When the running clinician's `credential` is `VISION_THERAPY_ASSISTANT`, `supervising_optometrist_id` is mandatory, must name a clinician whose `credential` is `OPTOMETRIST`, and that supervisor's own `credential_expires_at` must also be strictly after `performed_at`; a missing, wrongly-credentialled or expired supervisor is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISION_REQUIRED`. Sessions run by an optometrist or a certified vision therapist need no supervisor.

The session then moves `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED` and is priced. Billable units follow a fork on `procedure_code`. A `FLAT_ORTHOPTIC_TRAINING` session always bills exactly 1 unit no matter how many minutes were delivered. A `TIMED_THERAPEUTIC_ACTIVITY` session is mapped through a stepped table of delivered `timed_minutes`: 7 minutes or fewer bills 0 units; 8 through 22 minutes bills 1 unit; 23 through 37 bills 2; 38 through 52 bills 3; 53 through 67 bills 4; 68 through 82 bills 5; and anything beyond 82 minutes bills the per-session cap of 6 units. The step edges matter — 22 minutes bills 1 unit and 23 minutes bills 2 — and so does the fork, since 45 delivered minutes bills 1 unit under the flat code and 3 units under the timed code. A `HOME_PRACTICE` session always bills 0 units. `charge_amount` is then `billable_units` multiplied by the clinic rate for the code, rounded half-up to two decimal places: 42.00 dollars per timed therapeutic-activity unit, 155.00 dollars per flat orthoptic-training unit, and 210.00 dollars per evaluation-encounter unit, with any other code priced at 0.00.

Completion writes several rows. On the session, `schedule_status` becomes `COMPLETED`, `performed_at` and `timed_minutes` are recorded, `billable_units` and `charge_amount` are written, and the independent billing axis moves off `PENDING` — to `BILLABLE` when `billable_units` is at least 1, and to `NON_BILLABLE` when it is 0. That zero-unit landing is a real and reachable outcome: an in-office session delivering 7 minutes or fewer completes normally, sits at `NON_BILLABLE`, and any later attempt to bill it is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_BILLABLE`. On the plan, `cumulative_therapy_minutes` rises by the session's `weighted_minutes`, which for an in-office session is simply its `timed_minutes`; it is in fact recomputed from scratch as the sum defined in §3.6, and the plan's `version` is bumped on every completion whether or not that sum changed. On the phase that is currently `IN_PROGRESS`, `activities_mastered_count` rises by the session's `activities_mastered_delta` — but only when that delta is non-zero, so a completion delivering no mastery leaves the phase row, and its `version`, entirely alone, as does a completion on a plan that has no `IN_PROGRESS` phase. The session's `version` is bumped. Completion does not recompute `home_compliance_pct`.

Cancellation and no-show are deliberately asymmetric about the visit unit. Reception, an optometrist or a vision therapist cancels a session that is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN`, supplying a `cancel_reason` of `PATIENT_REQUEST` or `CLINIC_REQUEST` — the two remaining values, `PLAN_SUSPENDED` and `PLAN_DISCHARGED`, are the ones the cascades write, but they are not reserved and a direct cancellation supplying either is accepted like any other member of the enum. Cancellation sets `schedule_status` `CANCELLED`, forces `billing_status` to `NON_BILLABLE`, and, when the session's `modality` is `IN_OFFICE`, returns its unit to the pool: `visits_released` rises by 1, `visits_remaining` rises by 1, the pool's `version` is bumped, and an `EXHAUSTED` pool becomes `ACTIVE` again. That release is also the trigger point for the equipment-recall leg described in §3.9. A no-show, by contrast, is reception's operation alone, applies only to an in-office session still in `SCHEDULED`, and is permitted only once the logical clock has reached or passed the session's `scheduled_start_at` — an attempt made before that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, the same rung-14 error a session in the wrong `schedule_status` gets. It sets `schedule_status` `NO_SHOW` and `billing_status` `NON_BILLABLE` and bumps the session's `version`, and it deliberately does **not** release the unit: `visits_consumed` and `visits_remaining` are left exactly as they were, because a burned appointment burns the visit.

Sessions are readable individually — the read exposes both the scheduling axis and the billing axis — and as a list narrowed by `plan_id`, `patient_id`, `therapist_id`, `room_id`, `authorization_id`, `supervising_optometrist_id`, `modality`, `schedule_status` and `billing_status`.

### 3.6. Home Practice, Compliance and Phase Progression

Home practice is logged by the patient, not by the clinic. Acting through the portal, a `PatientUser` records a home session against a plan by supplying the `performed_at` instant and the `timed_minutes` practised, optionally with `activity_notes` describing what was done. The row is created with `modality` `HOME`, `procedure_code` `HOME_PRACTICE`, `schedule_status` `LOGGED` and `billing_status` `NON_BILLABLE`, and with `therapist_id`, `room_id`, `authorization_id`, `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` all null. Its `weighted_minutes` is computed and stored at that moment, at the half credit described below, even though a `LOGGED` session contributes nothing to any aggregate until it is attested. A home session enters the scheduling axis at `LOGGED` and never at `SCHEDULED`, never consumes an authorization unit, and is never billable. The plan must be `ACTIVE`, or the log is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and a `PatientUser` may only log against a plan whose patient is themselves — that ownership test belongs to rung 2 and is applied before the body is validated, so a patient posting a malformed body against somebody else's plan is told `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, while the same body against a plan id that exists nowhere is told `ERR_VALIDATION` first and `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` only once the body is clean.

A logged session counts for nothing until the patient attests to it. Attestation is again a `PatientUser` operation, requires `X-User-ID` to equal the session's `patient_id` — again a rung-2 test applied ahead of the body — takes a free-text `attestation_statement` that is required rather than optional and is stored on the session, and moves the session `LOGGED → ATTESTED` with `attested_at` stamped from the clock and the session's `version` bumped. It then rewrites the session's `weighted_minutes`, recomputes the plan's `cumulative_therapy_minutes` and `home_compliance_pct`, and bumps the plan's `version` — every time, whether or not either aggregate moved.

Attestation is what feeds the two plan-level aggregates. A session's `weighted_minutes` is its `timed_minutes` outright when the `modality` is `IN_OFFICE`, and is its `timed_minutes` multiplied by a home weighting of 0.5 — half credit, rounded half-up to one decimal place — when the `modality` is `HOME`. The plan's `cumulative_therapy_minutes` is the sum of `weighted_minutes` over every session on the plan whose `schedule_status` is `COMPLETED` or `ATTESTED`; sessions sitting at `SCHEDULED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `LOGGED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` contribute nothing, which means a home session that is logged but never attested adds no minutes at all.

Compliance is a trailing-window ratio, recomputed at attestation and again at the moment a phase advance is requested — though only a *successful* advance stores what it recomputed. An advance refused at the compliance gate, or at either of the two gates above it, leaves `home_compliance_pct` on the plan reading whatever the last attestation or last successful advance left there. The window ends at the current clock reading and begins twenty-eight days earlier. The numerator is the count of home sessions on this plan whose `schedule_status` is `ATTESTED` and whose `performed_at` lies inside that window, treated as half-open and left-exclusive: strictly after the window start and at or before the window end. The denominator is the prescribed weekly figure multiplied by twenty-eight and divided by seven — four weeks of prescription — held as an exact unrounded quantity. `home_compliance_pct` is that numerator divided by that denominator, multiplied by 100, capped at 100, and rounded half-up to two decimal places; when the denominator works out to zero, `home_compliance_pct` is `0.00`.

Which prescription supplies the denominator is deliberately pinned, because the dependency would otherwise be circular: advancing a phase can change `prescribed_home_sessions_per_week`, and compliance against that very figure is one of the gates on the advance. The rule is that the denominator is always the prescription that was in force at the *start* of the window, never the current one. So when the plan's `prescription_changed_at` is not null and is later than the window start, the denominator uses `previous_prescribed_home_sessions_per_week`; in every other case — no prescription change has ever happened, or the change happened at or before the window start — it uses the current `prescribed_home_sessions_per_week`.

Advancing the ladder is an optometrist's or a vision therapist's operation on an `ACTIVE` plan, and it is gated by three checks evaluated in this order. **First, reassessment currency.** When the phase about to be entered has a `sequence_index` of 3 or higher, the plan must hold a `SIGNED` battery of `assessment_type` `REASSESSMENT` whose `signed_at` is at or after the instant twenty-one days before the current clock reading; if it does not, the advance is refused with `ERR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`. Advancing into phase 1 or phase 2 carries no such requirement, and neither does the advance that leaves the ladder altogether: when the phase currently `IN_PROGRESS` is the highest on the plan there is no phase to enter, so this gate is skipped entirely. **Second, activity mastery.** The phase currently `IN_PROGRESS` must have an `activities_mastered_count` at least equal to its own `required_mastery_count`, or the advance is refused with `ERR_MASTERY_NOT_MET`. **Third, home compliance.** The plan's `home_compliance_pct`, recomputed right then, must be at least 75.00, or the advance is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; exactly 75.00 passes. A plan that is not `ACTIVE` never reaches any of the three and is refused earlier with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. An `ACTIVE` plan with no phase in `IN_PROGRESS` at all — one whose ladder was never appended to, or whose current phase was moved by hand — has nothing to advance and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, checked after the plan-active gate and before all three of the gates above.

When all three gates pass, the phase currently `IN_PROGRESS` moves `IN_PROGRESS → MASTERED` with `mastered_at` stamped from the clock. If a phase with the next `sequence_index` exists, it moves `LOCKED → IN_PROGRESS` with `started_at` stamped, and the plan's `current_phase_index` is set to that phase's `sequence_index`; both phase rows and the plan have their `version` bumped. If instead the phase just mastered held the highest `sequence_index` on the plan, there is nothing left to enter: the plan itself moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` and, on its second axis, `progress_state` is set to `DISCHARGE_PENDING` in the same call — written unconditionally from whatever the axis was reading, so a plan sitting at `PLATEAU` or `REVISION_PENDING` lands at `DISCHARGE_PENDING` just as an `ON_TRACK` one does. `current_phase_index` is left pointing at the phase just mastered.

### 3.7. Reassessment, Progress Scoring and Discharge Readiness

A reassessment is a battery like any other — opened in `DRAFT`, filled with readings, finalized, signed — but its finalization does considerably more work, because it is the moment the plan learns whether therapy is working.

**Symptom improvement.** `symptom_improvement_pct` compares the plan's `baseline_ciss_score` against the symptom-survey reading on this reassessment with the greatest `created_at`. When the baseline is null, when the baseline is zero, or when this reassessment carries no symptom-survey reading at all, the result is `0.00`. Otherwise it is the baseline less the current `ciss_score`, divided by the baseline, multiplied by 100, clamped to lie between 0 and 100, and rounded half-up to two decimal places. A worsening score therefore reads as `0.00` improvement rather than as a negative number.

**Component percentages.** The composite score is built from five percent-of-goal components, each rounded half-up to two decimal places *before* it is weighted. A component whose metric has no reading on this reassessment, or whose plan baseline is null, contributes exactly `0.00`.

- The near-point component measures how far the break distance has closed toward its discharge target. It is the plan's `baseline_npc_break_cm` less the current `npc_break_cm`, divided by the plan's `baseline_npc_break_cm` less 5.0 centimetres — the discharge break target — with that divisor floored at 0.1 so it can never be zero or negative, then multiplied by 100 and clamped between 0 and 100.
- The amplitude component measures gain toward the age-appropriate minimum. It is the current `amplitude_d` less the plan's `baseline_amplitude_d`, divided by this reassessment's own `expected_min_value` for the amplitude reading less the plan's `baseline_amplitude_d`, with that divisor floored at 0.1, then multiplied by 100 and clamped between 0 and 100.
- The vergence component measures gain toward a fixed goal of 20.0 prism dioptres. It is the current `vergence_bo_break_pd` less the plan's `baseline_vergence_bo_break_pd`, divided by 20.0 less that same baseline, with the divisor floored at 0.1, then multiplied by 100 and clamped between 0 and 100.
- The acuity component measures reduction toward a fixed goal of 0.10 logMAR. It is the plan's `baseline_acuity_logmar` less the current `acuity_logmar`, divided by that baseline less 0.10, with the divisor floored at 0.01 — a tighter floor than the others, because logMAR moves in much smaller numbers — then multiplied by 100 and clamped between 0 and 100.
- The symptom component is simply `symptom_improvement_pct`, already carried at two decimal places.

Where a metric has more than one reading on the reassessment, the one with the greatest `created_at` is used.

**Weighting, forked on diagnosis.** Which components are used, and how heavily, is decided by the patient's `diagnosis_category`, and this is a genuine fork rather than a re-weighting: the amblyopia arm uses a different input set entirely. For a patient whose `diagnosis_category` is `AMBLYOPIA`, the score is three terms — 0.55 times the acuity component, plus 0.25 times the near-point component, plus 0.20 times the symptom component — and the amplitude and vergence components are not used at all. For every other diagnosis the score is four terms over near-point, amplitude, vergence and symptoms, with the weight set chosen by family: `CONVERGENCE_INSUFFICIENCY` weights them 0.40, 0.15, 0.25 and 0.20 respectively; `ACCOMMODATIVE_DYSFUNCTION` weights them 0.20, 0.45, 0.15 and 0.20; and `OCULOMOTOR_DYSFUNCTION` weights them 0.25, 0.25, 0.30 and 0.20. The weighted terms are summed without any intermediate rounding, and the sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places once, producing `composite_progress_score` on a scale of `0.00` to `100.00`. The same battery genuinely scores differently under different diagnoses — a reference set of readings scoring 59.31 under `CONVERGENCE_INSUFFICIENCY` scores 60.02 under `ACCOMMODATIVE_DYSFUNCTION`, and 59.09 under `AMBLYOPIA` when acuity is included in place of amplitude and vergence.

**Plateau detection.** `improvement_delta_points` compares this reassessment against the plan's history. The prior reassessment is the `SIGNED` battery of `assessment_type` `REASSESSMENT` on the same plan, other than this one, with the greatest `signed_at`, with the row written last breaking a tie. When there is no such prior battery, `improvement_delta_points` is null. Otherwise it is this reassessment's `composite_progress_score` less the prior one's, rounded half-up to two decimal places. When `improvement_delta_points` is not null and is strictly below 5.0 points, the plan's `consecutive_low_delta_count` increments by 1; in every other case — including the case where there is no prior and the delta is null — it is reset to 0. `plateau_flag` on the reassessment is true exactly when `consecutive_low_delta_count` has reached 2 or more, meaning two consecutive reassessments have each failed to gain five points.

**The progress axis.** The plan's `progress_state` axis moves only at reassessment finalization, and only along these edges. From `ON_TRACK`, a reassessment whose `plateau_flag` is true moves the plan `ON_TRACK → PLATEAU`. From `PLATEAU`, three outcomes are possible and they are evaluated in a fixed order. The recovery arm is tested first: a reassessment whose `improvement_delta_points` is at or above 5.0 points moves the plan `PLATEAU → ON_TRACK` and resets `consecutive_low_delta_count` to 0, and such a reassessment never routes to either of the other two. Only if the recovery arm does not apply does the plateau fork run: the plan moves `PLATEAU → DISCHARGE_PENDING` when `discharge_ready` is true after this finalization, and `PLATEAU → REVISION_PENDING` when it is false. Separately from all of this, `progress_state` also moves `ON_TRACK → DISCHARGE_PENDING` when a phase advance completes the plan's final phase, as described in §3.6. Every one of these transitions bumps the plan's `version`.

**Phase regression.** A reassessment that scores badly walks the ladder backwards. When a reassessment is finalized with a `composite_progress_score` strictly below 40.0 points and the plan's `current_phase_index` is greater than 1, and the phase one step below the current index is in `status` `MASTERED`, the phase at the current index moves back to `LOCKED` with its `started_at` cleared to null, the phase one step below moves `MASTERED → IN_PROGRESS` with `mastered_at` cleared to null and `started_at` re-stamped from the clock, and the plan's `current_phase_index` decreases by 1. Both phase rows have their `version` bumped. This is the closing edge of the phase cycle `IN_PROGRESS → MASTERED → IN_PROGRESS`.

**Discharge readiness, forked on the plan.** `discharge_ready` is recomputed on the plan at every assessment finalization, and which test is applied depends on the plan's `discharge_criteria_mode`. Under `METRIC` the service looks only at instrument evidence: it finds the battery on this plan whose `purpose` is `DISCHARGE_READINESS` and whose `status` is `SIGNED`, taking the greatest `signed_at` with the row written last as tie-break, and `discharge_ready` is true when such a battery exists and its `discharge_metric_met` is true — that is, a near-point break at or below 5.0 centimetres, a recovery at or below 7.0 centimetres, and an amplitude at or above the functional minimum. Under `GOAL` the service looks only at goal evidence: it finds the battery on this plan whose `assessment_type` is `REASSESSMENT` and whose `status` is `SIGNED`, again by greatest `signed_at` with the row written last as tie-break, and `discharge_ready` is true when such a battery exists, both of those two numbers are non-null on it, its `composite_progress_score` is at least 80.0, and its `symptom_improvement_pct` is at least 50.0. The raw instrument thresholds are ignored entirely in `GOAL` mode, and the composite score is ignored entirely in `METRIC` mode, so the very same signed battery can leave one plan ready for discharge and another not. Note that both arms consider only `SIGNED` batteries: a battery being finalized has not yet been signed and therefore does not count toward the recomputation triggered by its own finalization. The plan recomputed is the one the finalizing battery names in its own `plan_id`, so an `EVALUATION` — which always carries a null `plan_id` — recomputes nothing, and neither does a `REASSESSMENT` pointed at a plan other than the one being assessed. Signing a battery does not recompute `discharge_ready`; the flag therefore lags its own evidence by one finalization, and only the *next* finalization on that plan sees the battery just signed.

### 3.8. Occlusion Prescriptions for Amblyopia

Patching runs alongside therapy rather than inside it, and only an optometrist prescribes it. A prescription names the `patient_id`, a `source_assessment_id` pointing at the signed battery that justified patching, a `severity` of `MILD`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, and the `baseline_acuity_logmar` of the amblyopic eye at the moment of prescribing — the reading the taper gate will later measure improvement against. The battery being signed is clinical convention rather than an enforced precondition: the source assessment is checked only for existence, its `status` and `assessment_type` are never inspected, and it is not required to belong to the named patient. The prescription itself carries no age field; the ladder below reads the age stored on that source battery.

The initial dose comes from a two-axis ladder. The first axis is an `age_band` derived from the source battery's `patient_age_years`: `YOUNG` when the child is under 4 years old, `SCHOOL` when the age is 4 through 7 inclusive, and `OLDER` from 8 years upward. The second axis is `severity`. A `YOUNG` child is prescribed 2 hours a day when `MILD`, 3 when `MODERATE` and 4 when `SEVERE`. A `SCHOOL`-age child is prescribed 2 hours when `MILD`, 4 when `MODERATE` and 6 when `SEVERE`. An `OLDER` child is prescribed 3 hours when `MILD`, 5 when `MODERATE` and 8 when `SEVERE`. Whatever the ladder yields is then capped at a maximum of 7 hours a day, which is why the single most aggressive cell — an older child with severe amblyopia, whose ladder value is 8 — is actually prescribed 7. The capped figure is written to both `prescribed_daily_hours` and `current_daily_hours`, in whole hours. A new prescription opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `taper_step_count` 0, `logged_days` 0, `adherent_days` 0 and `adherence_pct` `0.00`.

Adherence is logged by the patient. A `PatientUser` supplies two non-negative whole numbers, `logged_days_delta` and `adherent_days_delta`, which are added to `logged_days` and `adherent_days` respectively. `adherence_pct` is then `adherent_days` divided by `logged_days`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half-up to two decimal places; when `logged_days` is 0 the result is `0.00` rather than an error or a null. The prescription's `version` is bumped. Adherence carries no state precondition of its own: days may still be logged against a prescription that has already reached `COMPLETED` or `DISCONTINUED`, and doing so recomputes `adherence_pct` on that terminal row.

Tapering is an optometrist's operation and steps the dose down by 2 hours at a time. The call supplies `current_acuity_logmar`, the amblyopic eye's acuity today. Two conditions form a single gate. Adherence must be at least 70.0 percent, and the acuity gain — the prescription's `baseline_acuity_logmar` less `current_acuity_logmar`, rounded half-up to two decimal places — must be at least 0.20 logMAR. If either half fails, the taper is refused with `ERR_TAPER_GATE_NOT_MET`; both halves are reported through that one error.

When the gate passes, `current_daily_hours` is reduced by 2 hours but never below zero, `taper_step_count` increments by 1, and the prescription's `version` is bumped. Where that leaves the prescription's `status` depends on what remains. A prescription in `ACTIVE` or `TAPERED` whose reduced dose is still strictly above zero lands in `TAPERED` — from `ACTIVE` that is the transition `ACTIVE → TAPERED`, and from `TAPERED` it is a self-loop that can be walked down repeatedly for as long as the gate keeps passing. A prescription in `ACTIVE` or `TAPERED` whose reduced dose would reach zero or below instead has `current_daily_hours` set to exactly 0 and moves to `COMPLETED`, which is terminal. The one remaining edge is written by the discharge cascade: a prescription in `ACTIVE` or `TAPERED` when its patient is discharged moves to `DISCONTINUED`, also terminal, with its `version` bumped.

Prescriptions are readable individually and as a list narrowed by `patient_id`, `source_assessment_id`, `severity` and `status`. Both reads are among the handful a `PatientUser` may perform, subject to the patient owning the row.

### 3.9. The Activity and Equipment Kit

The clinic lends and dispenses the physical apparatus therapy depends on — vectograms, Brock strings, red-green glasses, printed activity packs. The clinic administrator maintains the catalogue. Each catalogue item carries a `name`, an `item_class` of either `LOANABLE_SERIALIZED` for things that come back or `CONSUMABLE_STOCK` for things that do not, a `replacement_cost` in dollars to two decimal places, a `useful_life_months` of at least 1 whole month for straight-line depreciation, an `in_service_date` from which months in service are counted, and an `initial_qty` received. Creation seeds the four stock counters so that the kit-unit identity holds immediately: `on_hand_qty` takes the whole `initial_qty`, while `on_loan_qty`, `dispensed_qty` and `retired_qty` are all 0.

Reception or a vision therapist requests an item for a patient by naming the `kit_item_id`, the `patient_id`, the `plan_id` the kit supports and a `quantity`. The plan must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`; the plan is not required to belong to the named patient. `quantity` must merely be a non-negative whole number here — the serialized-item rule that a loan must be for exactly 1 is a fulfilment check and not a creation one, so a request for three units of a `LOANABLE_SERIALIZED` item, or for zero units of anything, is created without complaint and fails later. A request is created in `status` `REQUESTED`, and no stock moves at that point — `checked_out_at`, `due_at`, `returned_at`, `returned_condition`, `recalled_at`, `recall_due_at`, `issued_by_user_id` and `returned_at` are all null and `damage_fee_amount` is `0.00`.

Fulfilment is the hand-over, and it forks on the item's class with genuinely different consequences on each arm. Either way the loan must still be in `status` `REQUESTED`, the item's `on_hand_qty` must be at least the loan's `quantity`, and a `LOANABLE_SERIALIZED` item additionally requires a `quantity` of exactly 1; a shortfall on either count is refused with `ERR_KIT_STOCK_DEPLETED`. Fulfilling a `LOANABLE_SERIALIZED` item moves the loan `REQUESTED → CHECKED_OUT`, moves `quantity` units out of `on_hand_qty` and into `on_loan_qty`, and leaves the loan open — an optional `due_at` may be supplied to record when the item is expected back, stored verbatim as supplied and left null when it is not. Fulfilling a `CONSUMABLE_STOCK` item instead moves the loan `REQUESTED → DISPENSED`, which is terminal, and moves `quantity` units out of `on_hand_qty` and into `dispensed_qty` where they are never expected back. Both arms stamp `checked_out_at` with the clock and `issued_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and bump both the loan's and the item's `version`.

Recall is not an operation anyone calls; it is the last hop of the red-flag cascade. When a session cancellation releases an authorization unit and that session's plan is `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL`, every loan on that plan sitting in `status` `CHECKED_OUT` moves to `RECALLED`, with `recalled_at` stamped from the clock and `recall_due_at` set to seven days after that instant, and each loan's `version` bumped. Stock counters do not move on recall: the unit is still out of the building, so it stays counted in `on_loan_qty`.

Taking equipment back is reception's or a vision therapist's operation, and it sits outside the discharge write gate precisely so that a discharged patient's apparatus can still be recovered. A loan in `status` `CHECKED_OUT` or `RECALLED` is returned with a `returned_condition` of `GOOD`, `FAIR`, `DAMAGED` or `LOST`; the loan moves to `RETURNED`, `returned_at` is stamped, `damage_fee_amount` is computed, and both the loan's and the item's `version` are bumped. Where the units land depends on the grade: a `GOOD`, `FAIR` or `DAMAGED` return moves `quantity` units out of `on_loan_qty` and back into `on_hand_qty`, while a `LOST` return moves them out of `on_loan_qty` and into `retired_qty`. Either way the kit-unit identity holds. The same operation applied to a loan still in `status` `REQUESTED` is a withdrawal rather than a return: the loan moves `REQUESTED → CANCELLED` with `returned_at` stamped and the supplied `returned_condition` still recorded on the row, `damage_fee_amount` stays at `0.00` whatever grade was named, and no stock counter moves and the catalogue item's `version` is not bumped, because nothing ever left the shelf. A loan in any other status — `DISPENSED`, `RETURNED` or `CANCELLED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The damage fee is a depreciation calculation with a salvage floor, and it rounds exactly once, at the very end. Count the whole calendar months elapsed from the item's `in_service_date` to the return date — the UTC calendar date of the logical clock at the moment of the call, never a caller-supplied one — flooring the count and never letting it fall below zero, so that a partial month does not count and a return landing on the same day of the month completes that month. Cap that figure at the item's `useful_life_months`. The straight-line value is the `replacement_cost` multiplied by one minus the ratio of those capped months to `useful_life_months`. The salvage floor is the `replacement_cost` multiplied by 0.20. The depreciated value is whichever of the two is larger, and both are carried unrounded to this point. That depreciated value is then multiplied by the condition multiplier — 0.00 for a `GOOD` return, 0.25 for `FAIR`, 0.60 for `DAMAGED` and 1.00 for `LOST` — and only that final product is rounded half-up to two decimal places into `damage_fee_amount`. A `GOOD` return therefore always yields a fee of exactly `0.00`, and a fully depreciated item still bills a fifth of its replacement cost when it is lost.

Catalogue items are readable individually with all four stock counters exposed. Loans are readable individually and as a list narrowed by `kit_item_id`, `patient_id`, `plan_id` and `status`.

### 3.10. Claims: The Medical and Vision Adjudication Fork

Claims are the billing specialist's exclusive territory, and every claim endpoint sits outside the discharge write gate so the revenue cycle can be worked to completion long after a patient has left.

Opening a claim names the `patient_id`, the `coverage_id` it is filed against, a `service_class` of either `THERAPY_SESSION` or `EVALUATION`, the `service_date` of the encounter, and `expected_coverage_version` pinning the coverage's accumulators. A `THERAPY_SESSION` claim also names the `session_id` it bills and leaves `assessment_id` null; an `EVALUATION` claim names the `assessment_id` and leaves `session_id` null. The identifier the `service_class` requires is a rung-3 `ERR_VALIDATION` when it is missing, and the identifier it does not require is forced to null and discarded without ever being existence-checked — an `EVALUATION` claim that also names a `session_id` that exists nowhere is created cleanly with `session_id` null. Neither identifier is cross-checked against the claim's `patient_id`, and neither is the coverage. Four checks apply beyond the ladder's structural rungs. The pinned coverage version must match, or `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. At most one claim may exist for any given `session_id`, and at most one for any given `assessment_id`, regardless of what state the existing claim is in — a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`. The thing being billed must be billable: a `THERAPY_SESSION` claim requires the named session to be in `billing_status` `BILLABLE`, and an `EVALUATION` claim requires the named battery to be in `status` `SIGNED`, with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_BILLABLE` covering both failures. And when the coverage's payer is of `payer_type` `VISION` and the stored last-paid date for this class — `last_paid_service_date_therapy` for a `THERAPY_SESSION` claim, `last_paid_service_date_evaluation` for an `EVALUATION` claim — is not null, the whole months from that date to the claim's `service_date` must be at least the payer's `frequency_limit_months`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_VISION_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`.

A created claim opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `appeal_count` 0, `appeal_attempt_log_count` 0, `correction_count` 0, `adjudication_variant` null, `submitted_by_user_id` null, `denial_reason` null, `last_appeal_attempt_at` null, and every money field at `0.00`. Its `procedure_code`, `units` and `billed_amount` are snapshotted rather than supplied. A `THERAPY_SESSION` claim copies the session's `procedure_code` and takes `units` from the session's `billable_units`; an `EVALUATION` claim fixes `procedure_code` to `EVALUATION_ENCOUNTER` and `units` to 1. `billed_amount` is then `units` multiplied by the clinic rate for that code — 42.00 dollars for `TIMED_THERAPEUTIC_ACTIVITY`, 155.00 for `FLAT_ORTHOPTIC_TRAINING`, 210.00 for `EVALUATION_ENCOUNTER` — rounded half-up to two decimal places. Creating a `THERAPY_SESSION` claim also moves the named session's billing axis `BILLABLE → CLAIMED`, bumping that session's `version`. Creating a claim of *either* class bumps the coverage's `version`, which is what makes a second create against a version the caller has not re-read fail at rung 13.

Submission moves the claim `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. The coverage behind the claim must be in `status` `ACTIVE` at that moment, or the submission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — a coverage the clock has already terminated cannot be billed against.

Adjudication records the payer's answer and takes an `outcome`, optionally with a `denial_reason` narrative. From `SUBMITTED`, an outcome of `ACCEPTED` moves the claim to `ACCEPTED` and an outcome of `REJECTED` moves it to `REJECTED`, recording `denial_reason`; neither computes money, neither writes `adjudication_variant`, which stays null until a money outcome runs, and only the `REJECTED` arm stores the narrative — a `denial_reason` sent alongside an `ACCEPTED` outcome is discarded. From `ACCEPTED`, an outcome of `PAID` or `DENIED` runs the money waterfall and moves the claim to `PAID` or `DENIED` respectively; both of those arms store a `denial_reason` when one is supplied, so a paid claim may legitimately carry one. Every other pairing of current status and outcome is refused at rung 14 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every adjudication bumps the claim's `version`.

**The waterfall, forked on payer type.** Which arm runs is decided by the `payer_type` of the payer behind the claim's coverage, and the arm that ran is stamped onto `adjudication_variant`. Every numbered step below is rounded half-up to two decimal places before the next step consumes it; the total is never rounded once at the end, and it is this per-step rounding that makes the claim-dollar identity exact.

Under the **`MEDICAL`** arm, the payer's contracted per-unit figure is chosen by the claim's `procedure_code`: `allowed_amount_timed_unit` for `TIMED_THERAPEUTIC_ACTIVITY`, `allowed_amount_flat_session` for `FLAT_ORTHOPTIC_TRAINING`, and `allowed_amount_evaluation` otherwise. Step one, `allowed_amount` is the claim's `units` multiplied by that per-unit figure. Step two, `contractual_adjustment_amount` is `billed_amount` less `allowed_amount`, floored at `0.00`. Step three, `deductible_applied_amount` is the smaller of `allowed_amount` and the coverage's remaining deductible — its `deductible_total` less its `deductible_met` — floored at `0.00`. Step four, the post-deductible balance is `allowed_amount` less `deductible_applied_amount`. Step five, `coinsurance_amount` is that balance multiplied by the payer's `coinsurance_rate`. Step six, `plan_paid_amount` is that balance less `coinsurance_amount`. Step seven, `patient_responsibility_amount` is `deductible_applied_amount` plus `coinsurance_amount`. Worked through: three timed units billed at 126.00 against a 34.00 per-unit allowed figure, with 50.00 of deductible left and a coinsurance rate of 0.20, yields a plan payment of 41.60 and a patient responsibility of 60.40.

Under the **`VISION`** arm there is no deductible and no coinsurance, and the allowance is a hard cap. Step one, `allowed_amount` is the smaller of the claim's `billed_amount` and the coverage's `vision_allowance_remaining`. Step two, `contractual_adjustment_amount` is `0.00`. Step three, `deductible_applied_amount` is `0.00`. Step four, the post-deductible balance is simply `allowed_amount`. Step five, `coinsurance_amount` is `0.00`. Step six, `plan_paid_amount` is `allowed_amount`. Step seven, `patient_responsibility_amount` is the claim's `billed_amount` less `allowed_amount` — every dollar above the remaining allowance falls on the patient. The same 126.00 encounter against a remaining allowance of 250.00 yields a plan payment of 126.00 and a patient responsibility of 0.00.

An outcome of `PAID` leaves `denied_amount` at `0.00`. An outcome of `DENIED` overrides the arm's last three results whichever arm ran: `denied_amount` becomes the `allowed_amount` computed above, `plan_paid_amount` becomes `0.00`, `patient_responsibility_amount` becomes `0.00`, and `contractual_adjustment_amount` becomes `billed_amount` less `allowed_amount` rounded half-up to two decimals — note that under the vision arm this last override replaces the `0.00` step two produced. The two intermediate medical figures are *not* overridden: `deductible_applied_amount` and `coinsurance_amount` keep the values the waterfall computed, so a denied medical claim can show a non-zero deductible applied against a zero patient responsibility. Neither figure participates in the claim-dollar identity, which is why it still holds. The claim-dollar identity holds on both outcomes and under both arms.

A `PAID` adjudication also writes outside the claim. Under the medical arm, the coverage's `deductible_met` rises by the `deductible_applied_amount` just applied. Under the vision arm, the coverage's `vision_allowance_used` rises by `plan_paid_amount`, `vision_allowance_remaining` falls by the same figure, and the last-paid date matching the claim's `service_class` — `last_paid_service_date_therapy` or `last_paid_service_date_evaluation` — is set to the claim's `service_date`. Either way the coverage's `version` is bumped. And when the claim names a `session_id`, that session's `billing_status` is written to `SETTLED` and its `version` bumped — written outright from whatever the axis was reading, not conditioned on its being `CLAIMED`. A `DENIED` adjudication writes nothing outside the claim at all.

**Appeals are capped, and the cap leaves a trace.** A `DENIED` claim may be appealed, optionally with an `appeal_reason`. Every appeal call, successful or not, first increments `appeal_attempt_log_count` by 1, stamps `last_appeal_attempt_at` with the clock and bumps the claim's `version`; those three writes are committed before the cap is evaluated. If `appeal_count` is still below 2, the appeal proceeds: `appeal_count` increments by 1 and the claim moves `DENIED → SUBMITTED` for a second look. A successful appeal is nevertheless one mutation of one row, so it advances `version` by exactly 1 in total and not by 2, per §2.4. If `appeal_count` has already reached 2, the call fails with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` — and the three bookkeeping writes still stand, so a refused appeal is durably recorded. That behaviour depends on the claim actually being `DENIED` when the call arrives: if any earlier call has already moved the row out of `DENIED`, the attempt is refused one rung higher as an illegal transition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and nothing is written at all.

**Corrections close the other loop.** A claim in `REJECTED` or `DENIED` may be corrected back to `DRAFT`, optionally supplying a `correction_reason` — validated as a string and then discarded, like the battery's `amendment_reason` and the plan's `revision_reason` — and `correction_count` increments by 1. The operator correcting the claim must not be the operator who submitted it: when `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `submitted_by_user_id` the correction is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, checked after the status rung, so a correction attempted by the submitter against a claim in the wrong status reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The money is not re-adjudicated by a correction: `allowed_amount` and the six figures below it keep whatever the last adjudication left on the row, and `adjudication_variant` and `denial_reason` are likewise not cleared. When the correction supplies `corrected_units`, the claim's `units` is overwritten with that figure and `billed_amount` is recomputed as the new `units` multiplied by the same clinic rate for the claim's `procedure_code`, rounded half-up to two decimals; when it does not, both are left alone. This closes the cycle `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REJECTED → DRAFT`.

Claims are readable individually, exposing the full money breakdown, and as a list narrowed by `patient_id`, `coverage_id`, `session_id`, `assessment_id` and `status`.

### 3.11. Referrals, the Red-Flag Pathway and Discharge

Referral traffic runs in both directions. Reception or an optometrist records one by naming the `patient_id`, a `direction` of `INBOUND` for a referral the clinic receives or `OUTBOUND` for one it sends, an `urgency` of `ROUTINE` or `URGENT`, the `referring_provider_name` of the sending provider, the `target_provider_name` of the receiving one, and a free-text `reason` — all three required on this endpoint, and all three left null on the referral CASCADE-A creates. A `plan_id` may be attached when the referral concerns a specific plan, and a `triggering_assessment_id` when it arose from a specific battery; both default to null on a manually created referral, and neither is required to belong to the named patient. Every referral opens in `status` `SENT` with `attempt_count` 1, `responded_at`, `response_outcome` and `decline_reason` null, and `sla_breached` false.

The response-due clock is set by `urgency`. `sla_due_at` is the creation instant advanced by twenty-four hours for an `URGENT` referral and by one hundred and twenty hours for a `ROUTINE` one. `sla_breached` is true when `responded_at` ends up strictly after `sla_due_at`, and also when the logical clock has already passed `sla_due_at` while the referral is still sitting in `SENT`. Those two arms differ in kind. The first is written onto the row when the answer is recorded and stays there. The second is evaluated afresh every time the row is read, so a `SENT` referral's `sla_breached` can flip from false to true purely because the clock moved, with no write, no `updated_at` refresh and no `version` bump; the moment the referral leaves `SENT` the stored value takes over again.

Recording the answer is reception's or an optometrist's operation and sits outside the discharge write gate, so an outbound referral can still be answered after the patient has been discharged. It takes a `response_outcome` of `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED`, with a `decline_reason` narrative recorded when the outcome is `DECLINED` and discarded when it is `ACCEPTED`. `response_outcome` is written to both `response_outcome` and `status`, which is why those two fields always agree once a referral has been answered. A referral in `status` `SENT` moves to `ACCEPTED` or to `DECLINED` accordingly, `responded_at` is stamped from the clock, `sla_breached` is recomputed, and the referral's `version` is bumped.

A declined referral can be re-sent to somebody else, three times in total. Re-referral takes a new `target_provider_name`. While `attempt_count` is still below 3, the referral moves `DECLINED → SENT`, `attempt_count` increments by 1, `target_provider_name` is rewritten, `responded_at`, `response_outcome` and `decline_reason` are all cleared back to null, `sla_due_at` is re-stamped as the current clock instant advanced by the urgency's SLA hours — the urgency itself is never re-supplied and never changes — `sla_breached` is written back to false, and the `version` is bumped — the capped retry loop `SENT → DECLINED → SENT`. Once `attempt_count` has reached 3, the loop closes differently: the referral is written to `ABANDONED` and its `version` bumped, and *then* the call fails with `ERR_REFERRAL_ATTEMPT_LIMIT`. The status change persists even though the caller receives an error, which is the point — a referral that has exhausted its attempts must not be left looking retryable. Note that re-referral is inside the discharge write gate, so on a discharged patient this attempt is refused by `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED` before the cap is ever reached and nothing is written.

**The red-flag pathway.** CASCADE-A is what turns a clinician's warning marker into a coordinated clinical stop, and it runs five hops deep. Hop 1: finalizing a battery whose `red_flag_present` is true creates an outbound urgent referral for the same patient, in `status` `SENT`, with `triggering_assessment_id` naming that battery and `plan_id` copied from it. Hop 2: the creation of an outbound urgent referral that names a plan currently in `status` `ACTIVE` suspends that plan, moving it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL`. This hop hangs off referral *creation* and not off hop 1, so a referral posted directly by reception or an optometrist with `direction` `OUTBOUND`, `urgency` `URGENT` and a `plan_id` naming an `ACTIVE` plan fires it too, and the whole of hops 2 through 5 with it. It does not fire when the referral names no plan — which is the case for every red flag raised on an `EVALUATION`, whose `plan_id` is always null — nor when the named plan is in any status other than `ACTIVE`. Re-referral does not re-fire it either. Hop 3: the suspension cancels every session on that plan whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN` and whose `scheduled_start_at` is at or after the current clock reading, with `cancel_reason` `PLAN_SUSPENDED` and `billing_status` forced to `NON_BILLABLE`. Hop 4: each cancelled in-office session releases its unit back to the pool, raising `visits_released` and `visits_remaining` by 1 and returning an `EXHAUSTED` pool to `ACTIVE`. Hop 5: because that release came from a session whose plan is `SUSPENDED` for `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL`, every loan on that plan in `status` `CHECKED_OUT` moves to `RECALLED` with `recalled_at` stamped and `recall_due_at` set seven days out. A loan already sitting in `RECALLED`, or one that was `DISPENSED`, `RETURNED`, `CANCELLED` or still merely `REQUESTED`, is left alone, and the leg is re-evaluated for each releasing session, so two cancellations on one plan run it twice and the second finds nothing left to recall. Every row touched along the chain has its `version` bumped. A plan suspended for `CLINICIAN_HOLD` runs hops 3 and 4 but not hop 5: the recall leg tests the suspension reason, and only `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL` satisfies it.

Referrals are readable individually and as a list narrowed by `patient_id`, `plan_id`, `triggering_assessment_id`, `direction`, `urgency` and `status`.

**Discharge.** Only an optometrist discharges a patient, and only a patient in `clinical_status` `ACTIVE` — attempting to discharge a patient still in `INTAKE`, or one already `DISCHARGED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because discharge sits outside the write gate and so never reports `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED` against itself. Before anything else, the patient's equipment must be back: if any loan for this patient is in `status` `CHECKED_OUT` or `RECALLED`, the discharge is refused with `ERR_LOAN_OUTSTANDING`.

The call supplies a `discharge_reason`, and each of the five values carries its own precondition, checked against the patient's most recently created therapy plan — the plan with the greatest `created_at`, the row written last winning a tie, whatever `status` it is in. The three reasons that read that plan fail outright when the patient has no plan at all: with no plan on file, `GOALS_MET`, `PLATEAU` and `NON_COMPLIANCE` are all refused with `ERR_DISCHARGE_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`, while `TRANSFERRED` and `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`, which read referrals and sessions instead, are unaffected. `GOALS_MET` requires that plan's `discharge_ready` to be true. `PLATEAU` requires that plan's `progress_state` to be `PLATEAU`. `NON_COMPLIANCE` requires that plan's `home_compliance_pct` to be strictly below 75.0 — the same threshold that gates phase advancement, read here in the opposite direction. `TRANSFERRED` requires at least one referral for this patient with `direction` `OUTBOUND` and `status` `ACCEPTED`. `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP` requires at least one session for this patient in `schedule_status` `NO_SHOW`, and no session for this patient in `schedule_status` `COMPLETED` or `ATTESTED` whose `performed_at` is at or after the instant sixty days before the current clock reading. A reason whose precondition does not hold is refused with `ERR_DISCHARGE_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`.

On success the patient moves `ACTIVE → DISCHARGED`, `discharge_reason` is recorded, `discharged_at` is stamped from the clock, `discharged_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the patient's `version` is bumped. CASCADE-B then runs. Hop 1: every therapy plan of this patient whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `UNDER_REVISION` moves to `DISCHARGED`. Hop 2: on each of those plans, every session whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_IN` is cancelled with `cancel_reason` `PLAN_DISCHARGED` and `billing_status` forced to `NON_BILLABLE` — note that this hop, unlike hop 3 of the red-flag chain, does not filter on whether the appointment lies in the future. Hop 3: each cancelled in-office session releases its unit back to its pool, raising `visits_released` and `visits_remaining` by 1 and returning an `EXHAUSTED` pool to `ACTIVE`. Hop 4, the equipment-recall leg shared with CASCADE-A, does not fire on a discharge: it requires the releasing session's plan to be `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `RED_FLAG_REFERRAL`, and hop 1 has already moved that plan to `DISCHARGED` — and in any case the equipment-recovery precondition guarantees there is no `CHECKED_OUT` loan left to recall. Hop 5: the authorization each discharged plan was bound to, reached through that plan's `authorization_id`, moves to `CLOSED` with `close_reason` `PATIENT_DISCHARGED`, its four counters left untouched so the visit-unit identity still holds; a plan that never left `DRAFT` has a null `authorization_id` and so closes nothing, and an authorization no discharged plan references is not reached by this path. Two further filters bound the hop. One authorization named by two of the discharged plans is closed once, not twice, and bumped once. And a pool that is not currently `ACTIVE`, `EXHAUSTED` or `EXPIRED` is skipped entirely — an authorization already `CLOSED`, whether by a billing specialist or by an earlier discharge, keeps the `close_reason` it already carries and is neither rewritten nor version-bumped.

Separately from CASCADE-B's numbered hops, discharge also fans out to patching: every occlusion prescription of this patient in `status` `ACTIVE` or `TAPERED` moves to `DISCONTINUED`. Every row any of this touches has its `version` bumped and its `updated_at` refreshed.
