# Obstacle Course Race Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An obstacle course race is not a race held at a venue so much as a race *built* on one, and then taken away again. Our producer leases a field, a forest, a quarry floor or an out-of-season ski slope for a fortnight, obtains a temporary land-use permit from the municipality, hammers thirty walls and rope climbs and water crossings into the ground, funnels six thousand participants through a start corral in fifteen-minute heats, and then — because the parcel must be handed back — reseeds, decompacts and de-silts every square metre it disturbed in order to recover the restoration bond it posted before the first stake went in. Today all of this lives in a filing cabinet of spreadsheets: one for permits, one for course design, one for the wave grid, one for marshal shifts, and a separate one per race day for timing disputes. The spreadsheets do not talk to each other, which is why a marshal walking off a station at 07:40 does not stop the 08:00 heat, and why a denied permit does not stop the crew from continuing to sell festival-area tickets.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes those gaps. It is mounted under `/ocr` and it treats the parcel as the root of authority: a venue's land-use posture gates course design, course design gates heat scheduling, live marshal staffing gates the start line, and the settled restoration bond closes the parcel out. The design intent throughout is that a *physical* fact — a station with nobody standing at it, a permit that expired at midnight, a water obstacle on a parcel where water may not be entered — propagates automatically into every downstream operational decision rather than waiting for someone to notice.

Two properties of the domain drive most of the complexity below and are worth naming up front. First, almost every number the system reports exists in two readings: a *design* reading computed from the drawing (what the course could clear if fully staffed) and an *effective* reading computed from who is actually on shift, plus a *provisional* race result the participant sees at the finish line and an *official* result produced once protests are settled. The two readings never overwrite each other; both are stored and both are returned. Second, the timeline matters more than the wall clock: heats occupy a shared corral for a fixed window, staff cannot be in two places within travel time of each other, permits expire at an instant, and remediation is either before or after a deadline. The server therefore runs on an explicit logical clock rather than on real time, so that a whole race weekend can be driven deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that actor is operating. Both must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must be one of exactly seven declared roles — `PermitOfficer`, `CourseDesigner`, `RaceDirector`, `SafetyOfficer`, `SiteOpsLead`, `ObstacleMarshal`, `Participant`. A request missing either header, carrying an empty value in either header — each header value is trimmed of surrounding whitespace before it is examined, so a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty — or naming a role outside that set of seven is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose headers are well formed but whose role is not among the roles authorised for the operation being invoked is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The authorised roles for each operation are listed in the endpoint table of §4.

The seven roles divide the production along the lines of real accountability. A `PermitOfficer` is the land-authority side of the operation: it registers candidate parcels, rules on land-use applications, inspects remediation work and settles the restoration bond. A `CourseDesigner` draws the course and files the land-use application, but never rules on one. A `RaceDirector` owns race-day operations — divisions, the wave grid, the start line, results and adjudication — and is the broadest operational role, yet it cannot design a course, rule on a permit, or roster staff. A `SafetyOfficer` owns staffing and medical coverage: it rosters marshals, medics, water-safety swimmers and sweeps, releases people from shifts, and may hold a heat at the start line, but may never release or start one. A `SiteOpsLead` runs the front of house and the festival area: enrolling humans, registering them into heats, checking them in at bag check, installing festival services and opening remediation tasks. An `ObstacleMarshal` stands at a station and records what happens there. A `Participant` is the human running the race.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the participant role, and the distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `PermitOfficer`, `CourseDesigner`, `RaceDirector`, `SafetyOfficer`, `SiteOpsLead`, or `ObstacleMarshal`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It does, however, record it where a transition demands an audit trail: a permit submission stores the acting `X-User-ID` in `submitted_by_user_id`, a permit decision stores it in `decided_by_user_id`, and a recorded obstacle attempt stores it in `recorded_by_user_id` together with the acting `X-User-Role` in `recorded_by_role`. When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a participant compare the resource's participant reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Participant` may read only an `Entry` whose `participant_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`, may file a protest only on such an entry, and may sign a waiver only on the `Participant` row whose own `id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`; any other target yields `ERR_NOT_ENTRY_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Two operator identities are additionally held apart from each other by a segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.7: the operator identifier that submitted a permit may not decide it, and the operator identifier that recorded an obstacle attempt may not adjudicate a protest lodged against that attempt.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; when supplied it must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0; when supplied it must be an integer of at least 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something that is not a base-10 integer, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422); either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted and takes its default.

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first, newest last — so that pagination is stable while new rows are being written. Because the logical clock of §2.5 moves only when it is set, rows created between two clock movements share one `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were inserted, oldest insertion first. The two chronological attempt listings (an entry's own attempt card, and every attempt recorded at one obstacle across all entries) present the same ascending order, keyed on `recorded_at` rather than on `created_at`, with ties broken by ascending `created_at` and then by insertion order.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter before the limit and offset window is taken, and `has_more` is true when rows matching the filter remain beyond the returned window. Single-resource responses carry no pagination fields in `meta`. A list filter that names an id no row carries — a `course_id`, `obstacle_id`, `paired_obstacle_id`, `wave_id`, `division_id`, `participant_id`, `staff_person_id` or `disputed_attempt_id` filter value that resolves to nothing — is not a path-resolution failure and is never a 404: such a filter simply matches no rows, so `data` is an empty array and `total_count` is 0.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Versioning

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T07:15:00.000Z`). Every instant the server renders carries exactly those three digits; input is more permissive than output, so an instant supplied by a client is accepted with a trailing `Z`, with an explicit `+00:00`, with some other numeric offset that is then converted to UTC, or with no offset at all in which case it is read as already UTC, and its fractional digits may be absent, fewer than three, or finer than three — finer precision being truncated, not rounded, when the instant is rendered back. Clients never supply `id`; a client-supplied `id` is ignored.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and a `version` integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations caused by a cascade from another entity rather than by a request addressed to the row directly. Whenever `version` advances, `updated_at` is refreshed in the same operation. A write that recomputes a row's derived fields but leaves every stored value identical to what it already held is not a mutation of that row: `version` and `updated_at` both stand still, so an idempotent recomputation — a readiness refresh that lands on the posture already stored, a cascade link that finds its target already in the state the link would impose — never advances the row. `Venue`, `LandUsePermit`, `Course`, `Obstacle`, `Wave`, `Participant`, `Entry`, `StaffAssignment`, `FestivalService` and `RestorationTask` all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

Two resources are deliberately immutable records rather than living rows, and they carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`: a `Division` is fixed configuration created once and never edited, and an `ObstacleAttempt` is a marshal's observation that is never rewritten — an adjudication suppresses an attempt's penalty contribution rather than editing or deleting the attempt. For those two entity types, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response body entirely; only `id` and `created_at` are present.

### 2.5. The Logical Race Clock

The server does not read wall-clock time. Every instant the server stamps — a wave's gun time, a finish, an attempt's recording instant, a wave close — and every deadline comparison the server makes reads a single logical clock. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. The world the server boots with is likewise empty: no venue, permit, course, division or row of any other kind is seeded or preloaded, every row that exists was created through an endpoint of §4, and nothing survives a process restart — a fresh process begins again with no rows at all and with the clock back at that same epoch.

An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant, carried in the request field `to_iso8601`. The supplied instant may lie before the clock's current value as readily as after it: the clock is simply set to whatever is asked for, with no monotonicity rule and no error for moving it backwards. Its response still travels inside the standard success envelope of §2.2 with a `response_type` of `object`, but what sits in `data` is not an entity row — it is a small object carrying exactly `current_utc`, the clock value now in force, and `expired_permit_ids`, an array of the ids of every permit that this clock movement expired, listed in the order those permits were created. Setting the clock is the only way an `APPROVED` permit reaches `EXPIRED`: every approved permit whose `valid_until_utc` is strictly before the new clock value expires, and each such expiry runs the full parcel-freeze cascade described in §2.10 against that permit's own parcel — so when two approved permits on one parcel expire in a single movement, the second cascade finds its work already done and writes nothing further. Lateness of site remediation is likewise measured against the clock, never against real elapsed time.

### 2.6. Request Evaluation Order

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response is decided by the *first* violated rule in the following fixed ladder, evaluated from the top. This ordering is normative: it is what makes error responses predictable, and it means a later, more specific business complaint never masks an earlier, more structural one.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears among the roles authorised for the endpoint in §4 — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path resolution.** Every id embedded in the URL resolves, checked left to right: the venue first, then the permit, course, obstacle, wave, participant, entry, staff assignment or restoration task. The applicable code is `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSTACLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RESTORATION_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Because resolution is left to right, a request naming a non-existent venue and a non-existent course under it reports the venue failure.
4. **Body and query schema.** Required fields present, types correct, enum members drawn from the declared sets, and numeric bounds respected — `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, `offset` at least 0, `difficulty_rating` between 1 and 10 inclusive, `lane_count` at least 1, a wave's `capacity` at least 1, `area_sqm` strictly greater than 0, and each quiet-hour field between 0 and 23 inclusive. A shift whose `shift_end_utc` is not strictly after its `shift_start_utc` fails here, and so does a `MEDIC` or `SWEEP` shift that supplies `obstacle_id` at all. The code is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every write endpoint and to every list endpoint that accepts query parameters.
5. **Body foreign keys.** Every reference carried in the request body resolves — `division_id`, `participant_id`, `obstacle_id`, `paired_obstacle_id`, `target_wave_id` and `disputed_attempt_id` — reported as `ERR_DIVISION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSTACLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAVE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. This rung guards heat scheduling, obstacle placement, heat registration, attempt recording, protest filing, entry reassignment and staff rostering.
6. **Participant ownership.** A `Participant` may act only on its own rows — otherwise `ERR_NOT_ENTRY_OWNER` (403). This rung guards exactly three operations: reading a single entry, filing a protest on an entry, and signing a participant's waiver.
7. **Segregation of duties.** Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This rung guards exactly two operations: recording a permit decision, and adjudicating a protest.
8. **Expected-version match.** The supplied `expected_entry_version` or `expected_venue_version` equals the parent row's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_ENTRY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_VENUE_VERSION` (412). This rung guards exactly two operations: adjudicating a protest, and settling a restoration bond.
9. **Transition admissibility.** The target row is in a state that admits the requested transition, per the lifecycles in §3. The applicable code is one of `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN`, `ERR_PERMIT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PERMIT_ALREADY_APPROVED`, `ERR_COURSE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_OBSTACLE_SET_FROZEN`, `ERR_WAVE_NOT_RELEASABLE`, `ERR_WAVE_ROSTER_NOT_LOCKED`, `ERR_ROSTER_CLOSED`, `ERR_WAVE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_PROTESTABLE`, `ERR_PROTEST_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_BOND_ALREADY_SETTLED`, each HTTP 409. Applies to every transition endpoint.
10. **Cross-entity gates.** `ERR_COURSE_NOT_PUBLISHED` when a heat is released while its course has not been published, and `ERR_WAVE_COURSE_MISMATCH` when an entry is reassigned to a heat that is not on the same course carrying the same division, each HTTP 409. These two guard heat release and entry reassignment respectively.
11. **Medical coverage.** Confirmed medic headcount sufficient for the scheduled load — otherwise `ERR_MEDICAL_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (409). Guards heat release.
12. **Course readiness.** The course must read as `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_COURSE_UNSAFE` (409). Guards three operations: heat release, heat start, and returning a held heat to the schedule. Course publication is guarded by the same code under a weaker test of its own — its readiness need only not read `FROZEN` — evaluated after publication's state rung and parcel-posture rung.
13. **Waiver signed.** Otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED` (409). Guards heat registration.
14. **Medical clearance.** Otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_CLEARED` (409). Guards entry check-in.
15. **Marshaling truth.** Otherwise `ERR_OBSTACLE_UNMARSHALED` or `ERR_OBSTACLE_MARSHALED` (409). Guards attempt recording.
16. **Division shape rules.** Otherwise `ERR_DIVISION_RULE_VIOLATION` (409). Guards two operations: heat registration, and staff rostering.
17. **Bypass permission.** Otherwise `ERR_BYPASS_NOT_ALLOWED` (409). Guards attempt recording.
18. **Team roster minimum.** Otherwise `ERR_TEAM_INCOMPLETE` (409). Guards heat start.
19. **Quiet hours.** Otherwise `ERR_QUIET_HOURS_VIOLATION` (409). Guards heat scheduling.
20. **Staff shift collision.** Otherwise `ERR_STAFF_SHIFT_CONFLICT` (409). Guards staff rostering.
21. **Start-corral collision.** Otherwise `ERR_CORRAL_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Guards heat scheduling.
22. **Uniqueness.** `ERR_STATION_INDEX_CONFLICT`, `ERR_BIB_NUMBER_TAKEN` or `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT` (409). Guards five operations: obstacle placement, heat registration, heat scheduling, permit drafting and participant enrolment.
23. **Governing permit present.** Otherwise `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Guards three operations: course validation, installing a festival service that draws water, and bond settlement.
24. **Water permission.** Otherwise `ERR_WATER_USE_NOT_PERMITTED` (409). Guards two operations: course validation, and festival-service installation.
25. **Course design gates.** `ERR_COURSE_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_PROGRESSION_TOO_FLAT` or `ERR_PROGRESSION_REGRESSION`, each HTTP 422. Guards course validation.
26. **Age eligibility.** Otherwise `ERR_AGE_INELIGIBLE` (409). Guards heat registration.
27. **Remediation area budget.** Otherwise `ERR_RESTORATION_AREA_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards opening a remediation task.
28. **Remediation completeness.** Otherwise `ERR_RESTORATION_INCOMPLETE` (409). Guards a final bond settlement.
29. **Disturbed-area cap.** Otherwise `ERR_DISTURBED_AREA_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards course validation.
30. **Design capacity.** Otherwise `ERR_DESIGN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards heat scheduling.
31. **Roster capacity.** Otherwise `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards two operations: heat registration, and entry reassignment.
32. **Live bottleneck capacity.** Otherwise `ERR_BOTTLENECK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards heat release.
33. **Non-empty roster.** Otherwise `ERR_WAVE_ROSTER_EMPTY` (409). Guards heat release.

Note the practical consequences of this ladder on heat release, which is guarded by seven separate rungs. A heat that is `HELD` rather than `SCHEDULED`, on an unpublished course, with an empty roster, reports `ERR_WAVE_NOT_RELEASABLE` — the state rung sits above the cross-entity and capacity rungs. A locked, `SCHEDULED` heat on a published course with too few medics reports `ERR_MEDICAL_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` even when the course is also `RESTRICTED` for an unstaffed obstacle, because medical coverage is evaluated one rung above readiness posture. And a heat that clears readiness but has both an oversized capacity and an empty roster reports `ERR_BOTTLENECK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, because the bottleneck rung precedes the empty-roster rung.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties Across the Producer's Paper Trail

Two decisions in this system may never be taken by the same operator identifier that created the thing being decided. The land authority's decision on a permit application may not be recorded by the operator identifier stored in that permit's `submitted_by_user_id`; a `PermitOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` matches the submitter is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), which is what prevents one human wearing both the `CourseDesigner` and `PermitOfficer` hats from rubber-stamping their own application. Likewise, an adjudication of a protest may not be recorded by the operator identifier stored in `recorded_by_user_id` on the disputed attempt; the marshal who wrote down the disputed outcome cannot also rule on the complaint about it, and an attempt to do so is refused with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Both checks compare `X-User-ID` values as opaque strings. Neither check inspects `X-User-Role`: it is the identifier, not the hat, that is disqualified. The comparison for the adjudication case reads through the entry's `disputed_attempt_id` to find the attempt whose `recorded_by_user_id` matters.

### 2.8. Superseding Writes and Expected-Version Guards

Two operations in the system are legitimately repeatable, which means the server cannot use state alone to tell a well-informed writer from a stale one. Both therefore carry an explicit expected-version field, and both are the only endpoints in the API that do so. Every other write endpoint takes no version field at all and must not be sent one; a version field sent to one of them anyway is silently ignored rather than rejected, as is any other unrecognised body field on any endpoint.

Adjudicating a protest requires the non-optional field `expected_entry_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Entry` as the client last observed it. An adjudication genuinely supersedes the entry's prior result reading: an upheld-with-adjustment decision voids one attempt's penalty and returns the entry to `PROVISIONAL`, from which a second protest and a second adjudication may legitimately follow, so a second adjudication is not by itself a state violation. If `expected_entry_version` does not equal the entry's current `version` — because another writer advanced the entry since the client read it — the adjudication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENTRY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), which stops the client from ruling on a results card it has not seen.

Settling a restoration bond requires the non-optional field `expected_venue_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Venue` as the client last observed it. A progressive settlement recomputes and supersedes the parcel's released-and-held split from the currently verified remediation tasks, so repeating it is a valid operation rather than a state error. If `expected_venue_version` does not equal the venue's current `version` — because task verifications or clawbacks have moved the ledger since the client read it — the settlement is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VENUE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The field is a JSON integer with no declared bounds of its own, so a zero or negative value clears the schema rung and is simply reported as a version mismatch rather than as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.9. Rates, Roundings and the Conserved Bond Ledger

Three distinct numeric regimes coexist in this API and must not be mixed up. Clearance rates and the difficulty progression score are decimal quantities carried to **2 decimal places, rounded half up**; this applies to `service_rate_per_min` and `corral_release_rate_per_min` as supplied, and to `design_clearance_rate_per_min`, `effective_clearance_rate_per_min` and `difficulty_progression_score` as computed. Throughput capacities in participants per hour — `design_capacity_pph` and `bottleneck_capacity_pph` — and every capacity ceiling derived from them are **integers obtained by discarding any fractional part** (rounding down). Money is held in **whole cents, rounded half up**, and elapsed race times are held in **whole seconds** taken as exact differences between two instants and never rounded at all. In all three regimes a numeric field is carried in JSON as a number and never as a quoted string: a decimal rate sent as `"3.50"` or a cent amount sent as `"25000"` is a schema violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a number written with a decimal point where an integer is declared, `3.0` being refused there exactly as `3.5` is.

Where a threshold comparison is made against a rounded value, the comparison uses the rounded value: the design gate on `difficulty_progression_score` compares the value already rounded to two decimal places, so a raw mean of 0.495 rounds to 0.50 and passes a minimum of 0.50.

The bond ledger on a venue is a conserved quantity, and this is the invariant the whole restoration workflow is built to preserve. `restoration_bond_cents` is supplied when the parcel is registered and is immutable thereafter. Three computed columns partition it: `bond_released_cents` is what has been handed back to the producer, `bond_forfeited_cents` is what the land authority has kept as a late-remediation penalty, and `bond_held_cents` is the residual, always equal to `restoration_bond_cents` minus `bond_released_cents` minus `bond_forfeited_cents`. At registration the whole bond is held, so `bond_held_cents` equals `restoration_bond_cents` while the other two are zero. At every subsequent point the three columns sum back to `restoration_bond_cents` exactly.

### 2.10. Parcel Posture and the Freeze Cascade

A venue carries `site_posture`, the top-level gate over everything built on the parcel: `OPEN` while the parcel may be worked, `FROZEN` once the land authority has withdrawn its consent, and `CLOSED_OUT` once the bond has been finally settled. `CLOSED_OUT` is terminal. Any design, scheduling, permit-submission, festival-installation or inspection operation attempted against a parcel that is `FROZEN` or `CLOSED_OUT` is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409).

Withdrawal of consent propagates as a four-link chain, and every link must run in the same operation as the one that triggered it. Link one is the permit itself reaching `DENIED` (by decision) or `EXPIRED` (by clock movement past `valid_until_utc`). Link two, caused by link one, moves the parcel's `site_posture` from `OPEN` to `FROZEN`; this link is deliberately unconditional, so a denial or an expiry freezes the parcel even when a *different* permit on the same parcel still reads `APPROVED`. Link three, caused by link two, moves every `Course` on that parcel whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `PUBLISHED` to `SUSPENDED` and forces its `readiness_posture` to `FROZEN`. Link four, caused by link three, moves every `Wave` on those suspended courses whose `release_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` to `HELD` with `hold_reason` set to the string `VENUE_FROZEN`. Fanning out from link two, every `FestivalService` on the parcel whose `status` is `OPEN` moves to `CLOSED`; note that closing an installation does *not* reduce `disturbed_area_sqm`, because a footprint is physical ground disturbance and is counted regardless of whether the installation is still serving anyone. Every row this chain actually modifies has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed; a link whose target already stands exactly as the link would leave it — a parcel already `FROZEN` or `CLOSED_OUT`, a course already `SUSPENDED` with `readiness_posture` of `FROZEN`, a heat already `HELD` with `hold_reason` of `VENUE_FROZEN`, an installation already `CLOSED` — writes nothing, so that row's `version` and `updated_at` stand still.

The same discipline governs the staffing chain described in §3.3 and the wave-close bubble-up described in §3.6: a cascade is never partial, and a cascade never skips the version bump on a row it modified.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Securing the Parcel: Registration and the Land-Use Application Loop

Everything begins with a parcel. A `PermitOfficer` registers a candidate venue by supplying its trade `name` (1 to 120 characters), the dominant `terrain_type` — one of `FIELD`, `FOREST`, `QUARRY` or `SKI_SLOPE` — the `water_access` available on it, which is `NONE`, `POND` or `RIVER`, the municipal `land_use_zone` code as a string of 1 to 40 characters (both are descriptive columns that no gate reads: water permission is decided by the governing permit's `water_use_allowed` alone, so a parcel registered with `water_access` of `NONE` may still carry water obstacles under a permit that allows water), the `protected_acreage_sqm` of habitat that is fenced off and excluded from any course footprint (an integer of at least 0, recorded for the land authority's file only — no gate in this document compares a footprint, a disturbed-area total or a remediation area against it), and the `restoration_bond_cents` posted with the land authority (an integer of at least 0, immutable once the parcel exists). A freshly registered parcel reads `site_posture` of `OPEN`, `disturbed_area_sqm` of 0, `bond_released_cents` and `bond_forfeited_cents` of 0, and `bond_held_cents` equal to the whole `restoration_bond_cents`. Parcels can be listed with an optional filter on `terrain_type` and an optional filter on `site_posture`, and each parcel can be read individually with its live disturbed-area total and all three bond ledger columns.

`disturbed_area_sqm` is not supplied and is never edited directly; it is recomputed as the total ground disturbance physically present on the parcel. It is the sum of `footprint_sqm` over every `Obstacle` standing on any `Course` of that parcel — regardless of whether that course reads `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `PUBLISHED`, `SUSPENDED` or `COMPLETED` — plus the sum of `footprint_sqm` over every `FestivalService` installed on the parcel, with no status filter at all. Placing an obstacle raises it; installing a festival service raises it; closing a festival service does not lower it. Every permit application, course, festival installation and remediation task created under a parcel records that parcel in `venue_id`, and every aggregate and every cascade described in this document walks that reference; in the same way an obstacle, a heat and a shift record their course in `course_id`, an entry records its heat in `wave_id`, and an attempt records its entry in `entry_id`.

A `CourseDesigner` drafts the land-use application against the parcel. The application carries the authority-issued `permit_number`, which must be unique across every application ever filed anywhere in the system — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`. It carries `water_use_allowed`, the boolean stating whether water may be drawn or entered on the parcel at all; `max_disturbed_area_sqm`, a hard cap given as an integer strictly greater than 0 on how much of the parcel may be disturbed while this application governs; the nightly quiet window as `quiet_hours_start_hour_utc` and `quiet_hours_end_hour_utc`, each a UTC hour from 0 to 23 inclusive, where the start hour is the first hour inside the window and the end hour is the first hour after it — and where the two hours being equal describes an empty window that no hour ever falls inside, rather than one covering the whole day; `valid_until_utc`, the instant after which an approved application stops governing; and `restoration_deadline_utc`, the instant by which site remediation must be settled before late penalties begin to accrue. A new application reads `status` of `DRAFT` with `revision_rounds` of 0, `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` both null, and `decision_note` null. Applications on a parcel can be listed with an optional filter on `status`, and any single application can be read to see its lifecycle state, its revision count and both operator ids. Drafting is the one operation in the parcel workflow that is *not* gated on `site_posture`: an application may be drafted against a `FROZEN` or `CLOSED_OUT` parcel, and it is only submission that requires the parcel to read `OPEN`.

Submission is the `CourseDesigner`'s move and is admissible from exactly two states. A `DRAFT` application advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and an application that came back for rework advances `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`; in both directions the parcel must read `site_posture` of `OPEN`, and in both directions the acting `X-User-ID` is written into `submitted_by_user_id`, overwriting whatever was there from an earlier round. Submission from any other state is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_STATE_INVALID`, and submission against a frozen or closed-out parcel is refused with `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN`. When both faults are present the state refusal is the one reported, because the application's own state is examined before the parcel's posture.

The land authority's decision is the `PermitOfficer`'s move, admissible only from `SUBMITTED`, and it carries the `decision` field — one of `APPROVED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `DENIED` — plus an optional free-text `decision_note` that is stored on the application; the empty string is an acceptable note, while omitting the field or sending it as null leaves whatever note the application already carried untouched. Every decision writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `decided_by_user_id`, and every decision is blocked when that identifier equals `submitted_by_user_id`. A decision of `REVISION_REQUESTED` moves `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and increments `revision_rounds` by 1; the loop `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` may run any number of times and `revision_rounds` simply records how many times it has, with no ceiling. A decision of `APPROVED` moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` but only when no *other* application on the same parcel already reads `APPROVED`; if one does, the approval is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_ALREADY_APPROVED`. A successful approval also recomputes `readiness_posture` on every course of that parcel, because the presence of a governing application is one of the inputs to that posture. A decision of `DENIED` moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and is the head of the four-link freeze chain of §2.10: the parcel goes `OPEN → FROZEN`, its `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` and `PUBLISHED` courses go to `SUSPENDED` with `readiness_posture` forced to `FROZEN`, the `SCHEDULED` and `HELD` heats on those courses go to `HELD` with `hold_reason` of `VENUE_FROZEN`, and every `OPEN` festival installation on the parcel goes to `CLOSED`. A decision on an application that is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_STATE_INVALID`.

Throughout this document, **the governing permit** of a parcel means the single application on that parcel whose `status` is `APPROVED`. Approval enforces uniqueness of that role, so at most one can exist at a time. Expiry is the fourth and last way an application leaves the live set: when the logical clock is moved to an instant strictly after an approved application's `valid_until_utc`, that application advances `APPROVED → EXPIRED` and runs the identical freeze chain, and its id is reported back in `expired_permit_ids`. `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal — nothing moves an application out of them.

### 3.2. Laying Out the Course: Stations, Bypass Lanes and the Design Gate

A `CourseDesigner` lays a temporary course on an `OPEN` parcel by supplying the course `name` printed on the participant map (1 to 120 characters), the advertised `total_distance_m` in metres (an integer strictly greater than 0), the `corral_release_rate_per_min` — the upper bound, carried to two decimal places and strictly greater than 0, on how many participants the start corral can push onto the course each minute — and `corral_throughput_capacity`, an integer strictly greater than 0 giving how many participants may occupy the start-corral window simultaneously. A new course reads `status` of `DRAFT` and `readiness_posture` of `RESTRICTED`, with `obstacle_count`, `difficulty_progression_score`, `design_capacity_pph`, `bottleneck_capacity_pph`, `medic_headcount`, `water_safety_headcount` and `scheduled_capacity_total` all at 0. Courses on a parcel can be listed with an optional filter on `status`, and one course can be read to see its derived capacities, progression score, readiness posture, staffing headcounts and scheduled capacity total.

Stations go in while the course is still `DRAFT`. Each obstacle carries its map `name` (1 to 80 characters), a 1-based `station_index` giving its position along the course, a designer-assigned `difficulty_rating` on the producer's scale of 1 to 10 inclusive, a `lane_count` of at least 1 parallel lanes, a `service_rate_per_min` (two decimal places, strictly greater than 0) for how many participants a *single* lane clears per minute when fully marshaled, the `marshals_required` headcount of at least 1 needed for full staffing, its `footprint_sqm` of ground disturbance strictly greater than 0, the boolean `water_contact` stating whether participants enter water there, a `bypass_mode`, a `penalty_seconds` count of at least 0, and optionally a `paired_obstacle_id`. `station_index` is unique per course across *every* obstacle row standing on it, bypass lanes included — a lane carries a `station_index` of its own and may not reuse the index of the primary it relieves — and reusing one is refused with `ERR_STATION_INDEX_CONFLICT`. Once the course leaves `DRAFT`, the station set is frozen and any further placement is refused with `ERR_OBSTACLE_SET_FROZEN`, which is also the code reported when the course has left `DRAFT` *and* the parcel is frozen, the station-set state being examined before the parcel's posture.

`bypass_mode` is the designer's statement of how a participant may skip the station, and it takes one of four values. `NONE` means no bypass exists. `PENALTY_LOOP` means the participant may run a loop instead of attempting the obstacle. `TIMED_PENALTY` means the participant may accept added seconds instead of attempting it. `MANDATORY` means completion is required and bypassing is physically impossible. An obstacle whose `paired_obstacle_id` is null is a **primary** obstacle — a real station on the course. An obstacle whose `paired_obstacle_id` names another obstacle on the same course is a **bypass lane** that relieves that primary station; a `paired_obstacle_id` that does not resolve on the addressed course is refused with `ERR_OBSTACLE_NOT_FOUND`. The reference is checked only for resolving on that course: naming an obstacle that is itself a bypass lane is accepted rather than refused, so the primary-versus-lane distinction rests entirely on whether a row's own `paired_obstacle_id` is null. Bypass lanes are never counted as stations in any course-level aggregate: their relief is folded into their primary's clearance rate instead. Obstacles may be listed with optional filters on `course_id`, on `paired_obstacle_id` (to find the lanes relieving a given primary) and on `staffing_state`, and a single obstacle may be read to see its live marshal count, staffing state and both clearance rates.

Placing an obstacle immediately computes its `design_clearance_rate_per_min`, the paper design rate that deliberately ignores staffing, because the wave grid must be gated before a single shift has been rostered. Start from the **lane rate**: `lane_count` multiplied by `service_rate_per_min`. Then take a **design relief** term: it is 0.00 when `bypass_mode` is `NONE` or `MANDATORY`, and otherwise — that is, when `bypass_mode` is `PENALTY_LOOP` or `TIMED_PENALTY` — it is one half of the lane rate, capped at 8.00 participants per minute, so whichever of those two is smaller. Add the design relief to the lane rate, take whichever is smaller of that sum and the parent course's `corral_release_rate_per_min`, and round the result half up to two decimal places. The corral rate is a ceiling on every station: no obstacle is credited with clearing people faster than the corral can feed them.

Placing an obstacle also computes that obstacle's `effective_clearance_rate_per_min` by the live rule of §3.3 — which, for a station that has just been placed and therefore has a `confirmed_marshal_count` of 0 and a `staffing_state` of `UNSTAFFED`, evaluates to 0.00 — and it recomputes the parent course's `obstacle_count`, `difficulty_progression_score`, `design_capacity_pph` and `bottleneck_capacity_pph` along with the parcel's `disturbed_area_sqm`.

From the stations, two course-level numbers follow. `obstacle_count` is simply how many obstacle rows stand on the course, bypass lanes included. `design_capacity_pph` is the staffing-independent throughput of the course in participants per hour: consider only the primary obstacles, take the smallest `design_clearance_rate_per_min` among them, multiply by 60 to convert per-minute into per-hour, and discard any fractional part; when the course has no primary obstacle at all the value is 0. The course is only as fast as its slowest station, which is the whole point of bottleneck modelling.

`difficulty_progression_score` measures whether the course actually gets harder as it goes. List the `difficulty_rating` values of the primary obstacles in ascending `station_index` order — the index is unique per course, so this ordering is total and needs no tie-break. Walk the consecutive pairs from first to last. For each pair, take the rise: the later station's rating minus the earlier station's rating when that difference is positive, and 0 when the later station is the same or easier. Sum those rises, divide by the number of consecutive pairs (one fewer than the number of primary stations), and round half up to two decimal places. When the course has fewer than two primary stations the score is 0.00. Separately, count the **regressions**: the consecutive pairs where the later station's rating is lower than the earlier station's rating by more than 2 points. A drop of exactly 2 is a permitted breather; a drop of 3 is a regression.

Validation is the design gate, run by a `CourseDesigner` on a `DRAFT` course, and it advances `DRAFT → VALIDATED`. It recomputes `obstacle_count`, `difficulty_progression_score`, `design_capacity_pph` and `readiness_posture`, and it freezes the station set. Eight conditions must hold. The course must be `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COURSE_STATE_INVALID`. The parcel must read `site_posture` of `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN`. The parcel must have exactly one governing permit — that is, exactly one application reading `APPROVED` — or the request is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`. No obstacle on the course may have `water_contact` set to true unless the governing permit has `water_use_allowed` set to true, or the request is refused with `ERR_WATER_USE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The parcel's `disturbed_area_sqm` must not exceed the governing permit's `max_disturbed_area_sqm`, where equality passes and only a strict excess fails with `ERR_DISTURBED_AREA_EXCEEDED`. The course must have at least 3 primary obstacles, or it is refused with `ERR_COURSE_TOO_SHORT`. Its `difficulty_progression_score`, as rounded, must be at least 0.50, or it is refused with `ERR_PROGRESSION_TOO_FLAT`. And its regression count must be exactly zero, or it is refused with `ERR_PROGRESSION_REGRESSION`. The three design-gate refusals are HTTP 422; the rest are HTTP 409 as catalogued in §5.

Publication is the `RaceDirector`'s move and advances `VALIDATED → PUBLISHED`, which is the state a course must be in before any of its heats can be released. The course must be `VALIDATED` — publishing from any other state is refused with `ERR_COURSE_STATE_INVALID` — the parcel must read `OPEN`, and the course's `readiness_posture` must not be `FROZEN` — a test applied to the posture as recomputed by this operation rather than to the value the course last stored. Publication recomputes `readiness_posture` and stores it. A course leaves the design track in one of two ways: `SUSPENDED`, imposed by the parcel freeze chain from `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `PUBLISHED` alike, or `COMPLETED`, reached automatically from `PUBLISHED` when the last of its heats closes. Both are terminal.

### 3.3. Marshaling the Stations, Covering the Course, and the Staffing Cascade

A `SafetyOfficer` rosters people onto the course. Each shift names the `staff_person_id` — an opaque identifier of 1 to 40 characters for the human being scheduled, and the resource whose time must not be double-booked — the `staff_role` performed, which is one of `MARSHAL`, `MEDIC`, `WATER_SAFETY` or `SWEEP`, the `certification_level` that person holds, which is one of `BASIC`, `ADVANCED`, `PARAMEDIC` or `LIFEGUARD`, and the shift bounds `shift_start_utc` (inclusive) and `shift_end_utc` (inclusive, and required to be strictly after the start). A `MARSHAL` or `WATER_SAFETY` shift covers a specific station and must name it with `obstacle_id`; omitting it is refused with `ERR_DIVISION_RULE_VIOLATION`. A supplied `obstacle_id` must resolve to an obstacle standing on the addressed course, and one that does not resolve at all or resolves to an obstacle on some other course is alike refused with `ERR_OBSTACLE_NOT_FOUND` at the body-foreign-key rung — which sits above the rung that complains about a missing station, so a `MARSHAL` shift naming another course's obstacle reports the 404 rather than `ERR_DIVISION_RULE_VIOLATION`. A `MEDIC` or `SWEEP` shift covers the whole course and must omit `obstacle_id` entirely; supplying it is a schema violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, though an explicit null counts as omitting it and is accepted. Every new shift reads `status` of `CONFIRMED` with `vacate_reason` null. The `certification_level` is recorded on the roster and is never checked against the `staff_role`: no rule anywhere in this API requires a particular certification for a particular role, so a `MEDIC` shift held at `BASIC` is accepted exactly as one held at `PARAMEDIC` is. Rostering is likewise not gated on the parcel's `site_posture` or on the course's own `status` — the `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN` gate of §2.10 does not reach this operation, so a shift may be rostered onto a course reading any status on a parcel reading any posture. Shifts may be listed with optional filters on `course_id`, `obstacle_id`, `staff_person_id` and `status`, and any single shift may be read.

One human cannot be at two stations at once, and cannot teleport between them either, so shift intervals are compared after being inflated by a travel buffer of 15 minutes on both sides. Gather every shift already reading `CONFIRMED` for the same `staff_person_id`, across every course and every obstacle in the system — shifts reading `VACATED` are ignored entirely, which is precisely what makes a vacated slot immediately re-bookable. A collision exists with such a peer when both of the following hold: the candidate's `shift_start_utc` falls strictly before the peer's `shift_end_utc` advanced by 15 minutes, and the peer's `shift_start_utc` falls strictly before the candidate's `shift_end_utc` advanced by 15 minutes. Instants are compared exactly, at the full precision the client supplied, because the truncation described in §2.4 applies only when an instant is rendered back into a response and never to the stored value a comparison reads. A single collision with a single peer is fatal and yields `ERR_STAFF_SHIFT_CONFLICT`; there is no notion of aggregate capacity here. The boundary is exact: a shift beginning exactly 15 minutes after a peer's shift ends is accepted, and one beginning one second earlier than that is rejected.

Confirming a `MARSHAL` shift raises the station's `confirmed_marshal_count`, which is the number of shifts reading `CONFIRMED` with `staff_role` of `MARSHAL` at that obstacle, and recomputes the station's `staffing_state`: `UNSTAFFED` when the confirmed marshal count is zero, `PARTIAL` when it is greater than zero but still below `marshals_required`, and `MARSHALED` when it is `marshals_required` or more. Confirming a `MEDIC` shift raises the course's `medic_headcount`, the number of shifts on that course reading `CONFIRMED` with `staff_role` of `MEDIC`; confirming a `WATER_SAFETY` shift raises `water_safety_headcount`, defined the same way for that role. Confirming a shift of any role also recomputes the course's `bottleneck_capacity_pph` and its `readiness_posture`, but it never touches a heat: the 45-minute holdback that forms link four of the vacate cascade below has no counterpart here, so a course that still reads `RESTRICTED` after a shift is confirmed leaves every `SCHEDULED` heat exactly as it stood.

Live staffing produces the second of each obstacle's two clearance readings. `effective_clearance_rate_per_min` starts from a **staffing factor** that steps on the confirmed marshal headcount: the factor is 1.00 when `confirmed_marshal_count` is `marshals_required` or greater; otherwise it is 0.60 when `confirmed_marshal_count` is at least half of `marshals_required` rounded up to a whole number *and* is also at least 1; and otherwise it is 0.00, which is the case whenever the station has no confirmed marshal at all. Multiply `lane_count` by `service_rate_per_min` by that staffing factor to get the **staffed lane rate**. Take an **effective relief** term that is 0.00 when `bypass_mode` is `NONE` or `MANDATORY`, and otherwise one half of the staffed lane rate capped at 8.00 participants per minute, whichever of the two is smaller. Add the effective relief to the staffed lane rate, take whichever is smaller of that sum and the parent course's `corral_release_rate_per_min`, and round half up to two decimal places. Because the staffing factor multiplies before the relief is computed, an understaffed station loses throughput twice over — once on its lanes and once on its bypass relief.

The course-level companion is `bottleneck_capacity_pph`, the live staffing-aware throughput in participants per hour: consider only the primary obstacles, take the smallest `effective_clearance_rate_per_min` among them, multiply by 60 and discard any fractional part; when there is no primary obstacle the value is 0. A single primary station with nobody on it therefore drives the entire course's live capacity to zero, since its staffing factor is 0.00 and so is its effective rate.

`readiness_posture` is the safety gate that decides whether the start line may operate at all, and it is recomputed whenever a permit is approved, denied or expired, whenever a course is validated or published, whenever a shift is confirmed or vacated, and whenever a heat is scheduled. It is a stored field on the course rather than a value derived afresh at read time, and the list of triggers above is complete: each trigger rewrites the posture only when the operation it belongs to actually succeeds, and no other operation rewrites it at all. A refused request therefore leaves the stored posture exactly as it stood — a validation turned away at the permit rung of §2.6 writes nothing, so the course still carries the posture it last stored, which for a course that has not yet met one of those triggers is the `RESTRICTED` it was created with. Equally, an operation that is not a trigger leaves the posture alone even when it moves one of the posture's own inputs: placing a station, closing a heat, and closing the parcel out with a `FINAL` bond settlement each write their own derived fields without refreshing it. A permit denial or expiry, by contrast, reaches every course on the parcel, so the courses the freeze chain of §2.10 suspends and any course already reading `SUSPENDED` or `COMPLETED` alike end up with `readiness_posture` of `FROZEN`. The three operations that §2.6 gates on the posture at rung 12 — heat release, heat start and returning a held heat to the schedule — read that stored value as it stands. It reads `FROZEN` when the parcel's `site_posture` is anything other than `OPEN`, or when the parcel has no governing permit reading `APPROVED`. Failing that, it reads `RESTRICTED` when any of three conditions holds: the course's `medic_headcount` is below the required medic count, which is the course's `scheduled_capacity_total` divided by 250 participants per medic and rounded up to a whole number; or its `water_safety_headcount` is below the required water-safety count, which is 2 swimmers for each obstacle on the course whose `water_contact` is true, counting every obstacle including bypass lanes; or any obstacle of the course, again including bypass lanes, has a `staffing_state` of `UNSTAFFED`. Otherwise it reads `OPEN`. Note carefully that `PARTIAL` staffing does **not** restrict a course — it only lowers `bottleneck_capacity_pph`. Only a station with zero confirmed marshals restricts it.

Releasing a person from a shift is the `SafetyOfficer`'s move and is the head of a four-link cascade that reaches all the way to the start line. The shift must read `CONFIRMED`; vacating an already-vacated shift is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The request carries a `reason`, which is stored on the shift as `vacate_reason`. Link one moves the shift `CONFIRMED → VACATED`. Link two, caused by link one, applies when the vacated shift had `staff_role` of `MARSHAL`: the station's `confirmed_marshal_count` decrements and its `staffing_state` recomputes, falling to `UNSTAFFED` at zero or to `PARTIAL` while still below `marshals_required`; when instead the vacated shift had `staff_role` of `MEDIC` or `WATER_SAFETY`, the course's `medic_headcount` or `water_safety_headcount` decrements. Link three, caused by link two, recomputes the course's `readiness_posture` — which becomes `RESTRICTED` as soon as any station of the course reads `UNSTAFFED`, or as soon as a headcount minimum stops being met. Link four, caused by link three leaving the posture `RESTRICTED`, moves every heat on that course whose `release_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls at or before the logical clock advanced by the 45-minute holdback window to `HELD`, with `hold_reason` set to the string `COURSE_RESTRICTED`. Heats further out than three quarters of an hour are left alone, on the assumption that the gap can still be re-staffed. The same operation also recomputes the affected obstacle's `effective_clearance_rate_per_min` and the course's `bottleneck_capacity_pph`, and increments the `version` of every row it touched.

### 3.4. Divisions and the Wave Grid: Quiet Hours, the Corral Budget and Design Capacity

Before any heat can be scheduled, a `RaceDirector` defines the divisions participants run in. A division is immutable configuration once created. It carries the `name` printed on results (1 to 80 characters), a `division_type`, a `bypass_policy`, a `scoring_mode`, a `min_age` between 0 and 99 inclusive, and the team roster bounds `team_size_min` (at least 1) and `team_size_max` (at least `team_size_min`). `division_type` is one of three rule families: `COMPETITIVE` is chip-timed and bypassing disqualifies; `OPEN` is gun-timed and bypassing costs seconds; `TEAM` is gun-timed with every obstacle mandatory and a team roster minimum enforced at the gun. `bypass_policy` states what a bypassed attempt actually does in this division and is one of `ALLOWED_WITH_PENALTY`, `DQ_ON_BYPASS` or `MANDATORY_COMPLETION`. `scoring_mode` states which start instant the official elapsed time is measured from and is either `GUN_TIME` or `CHIP_TIME`. The three enumerations are supplied independently and are never cross-validated: the pairings named above are the producer's convention, not a constraint, so a `COMPETITIVE` division carrying `GUN_TIME` and `ALLOWED_WITH_PENALTY` is accepted exactly as given. Every downstream rule reads the field that governs it — bypassing consults `bypass_policy`, official timing consults `scoring_mode`, and `division_type` is consulted by exactly two rules and no others: the team roster minimum enforced at the gun, and the `team_code` shape rule of §3.5 that requires a `team_code` under `TEAM`, forbids one under any other type, and caps a single `team_code` at `team_size_max` within one heat. Divisions may be listed with an optional filter on `division_type`, and one division's rule set may be read on its own.

Scheduling a heat is also the `RaceDirector`'s move. Each heat names the `division_id` of the single division it carries, a `wave_code` label such as `A1` that must be unique among the heats of that course (a duplicate is refused with `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`), the planned gun instant `scheduled_start_utc`, and a `capacity` of at least 1 giving how many entries may be assigned to it. The course's own `status` is not consulted when a heat is scheduled: a heat may be scheduled on a course reading `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `PUBLISHED` or `COMPLETED` alike, publication being required only later, at release. A newly scheduled heat reads `release_status` of `SCHEDULED`, `roster_status` of `OPEN`, `assigned_count` of 0, and `hold_reason`, `actual_start_utc` and `closed_at_utc` all null. Creating a heat recomputes the course's `scheduled_capacity_total` — the sum of `capacity` over the heats of that course whose `release_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `RELEASED` or `STARTED` — and therefore also recomputes the course's `readiness_posture`, because the medic requirement scales with that total. Heats may be listed with optional filters on `course_id`, on `division_id`, and on either state dimension via `release_status` and `roster_status`; a single heat may be read with both dimensions, its roster count and its stamped gun and close instants.

Three independent gates guard heat scheduling, and they are checked in the ladder order of §2.6. The first is the nightly quiet window imposed by the land authority. When the parcel has a governing permit reading `APPROVED`, the UTC hour of the heat's `scheduled_start_utc` must fall outside the half-open window that begins at `quiet_hours_start_hour_utc` and ends just before `quiet_hours_end_hour_utc` — the start hour itself is inside the window and forbidden, the end hour itself is outside the window and allowed. When the start hour is numerically greater than the end hour, the window wraps past midnight and covers the hours from the start hour through 23 together with the hours from 0 up to but not including the end hour. When the two hours are numerically equal the window is empty rather than a whole day, so no start hour can violate it and the check passes for every heat. A heat whose start hour lands inside that window is refused with `ERR_QUIET_HOURS_VIOLATION`. When the parcel has no application reading `APPROVED`, this check is skipped entirely rather than treated as a failure.

The second gate is the start corral, which every heat on the course shares. Each heat occupies the corral for a half-open window of 6 minutes beginning at its `scheduled_start_utc` and ending just before the instant 6 minutes later. Gather the peer heats on the same course whose `release_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `RELEASED` or `STARTED` — a heat reading `CLOSED` has vacated the corral and is excluded — and keep those whose own 6-minute window overlaps the candidate's. Two half-open windows overlap when the peer's start falls strictly before the candidate's end and the candidate's start falls strictly before the peer's end; windows that merely touch at an endpoint do not overlap, so a heat scheduled exactly 6 minutes after a peer is always free of it. Sum the `capacity` of the overlapping peers, add the candidate's own `capacity`, and require that total to be at most the course's `corral_throughput_capacity`; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_CORRAL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Unlike the pairwise staff-shift geometry, this is an aggregate-capacity rule: many heats may share the corral window at once so long as their summed capacity fits inside the budget.

The third gate is the staffing-independent design capacity, which is why `design_capacity_pph` exists at all. Heats are planned on a 15-minute grid, so the most a single heat may carry is the course's `design_capacity_pph` scaled from an hour down to a quarter of an hour — that is, multiplied by 15 and divided by 60, with any fractional part discarded. A `capacity` above that ceiling is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because this gate reads the *paper* rate, the wave grid can be built weeks before the first marshal is rostered; the live counterpart is applied later, at release time.

### 3.5. Enrolment, Waivers, Bib Assignment and the Bag-Check Line

Humans are enrolled once and then registered into heats. Enrolment is performed by a `SiteOpsLead` or a `RaceDirector` and records the participant's `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), their four-digit `birth_year` between 1900 and 2026 inclusive, a contact `email` that must be unique across every enrolled human — uniqueness is the only test applied to it, since no address format is validated and any non-empty string is accepted — and the `chip_id` serial of the timing chip bonded to them, likewise unique across every enrolled human; a duplicate on either is refused with `ERR_UNIQUENESS_CONFLICT`. Enrolment deliberately does not sign the waiver: a new participant reads `waiver_signed` of false, `clearance_status` of `PENDING` and an empty `medical_flags` array. Participants may be listed with an optional filter on `clearance_status`, and one participant may be read to see waiver and clearance state.

Signing the waiver is the participant's own act, and a `RaceDirector` may also record it on their behalf; a `Participant` may sign only their own row. The request carries `signature_name`, which must be a non-empty string but is validated only and never persisted on the participant, and an optional `medical_flags` array of self-declared conditions. Signing always sets `waiver_signed` to true and always overwrites the stored `medical_flags` with the array exactly as declared — the array is free-form rather than an enumeration, so any string is accepted and only the three blocking values named below carry any effect, and omitting the field altogether stores an empty array — but it resolves `clearance_status` in one of two directions from `PENDING`. When none of the three blocking conditions `CARDIAC`, `PREGNANCY` or `RECENT_SURGERY` appears in `medical_flags`, the participant advances `PENDING → CLEARED`. When at least one of those three appears, the participant advances `PENDING → MEDICALLY_HELD` instead. There is no transition out of `MEDICALLY_HELD` — the producer's medical director handles those cases off-system — so a medically held human cannot take the start line at all. Signing a waiver that has already been signed is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) at the transition rung, since neither transition is admissible once `clearance_status` has left `PENDING`.

Registering a participant into a heat is done by a `SiteOpsLead` or a `RaceDirector` and carries the `participant_id`, the printed `bib_number` (an integer of at least 1, unique among the entries of that heat — a reuse is refused with `ERR_BIB_NUMBER_TAKEN`), and optionally a `team_code`. Registration is only accepted while the heat's `roster_status` is `OPEN`; a heat already `LOCKED` or `SEALED` refuses it with `ERR_ROSTER_CLOSED`. The participant must have signed the waiver, or the registration is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. Registration reads `waiver_signed` and nothing else about the human's medical state: `clearance_status` is not consulted here, so a participant reading `MEDICALLY_HELD` — who has by definition signed — registers successfully and is stopped later, at check-in. Nor is registration gated on the parcel's `site_posture` or on the course's own `status`. Division shape rules apply: `team_code` is required when the heat's division has `division_type` of `TEAM` and is forbidden when it has any other `division_type`, and a single `team_code` may not accumulate more than the division's `team_size_max` entries inside one heat; any of these is refused with `ERR_DIVISION_RULE_VIOLATION`. Age eligibility is computed as a pure calendar-year difference — the calendar year of the heat's `scheduled_start_utc` minus the participant's `birth_year`, with month and day deliberately ignored so that the boundary is exact and independent of the clock — and that age must be at least the division's `min_age`, or the registration is refused with `ERR_AGE_INELIGIBLE`. Finally the heat must have room: the heat's `assigned_count` plus one must be at most its `capacity`, or the registration is refused with `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A new entry reads `progress_status` of `REGISTERED` and `result_status` of `PENDING`, with `bag_tag_code` null, `chip_start_offset_seconds` of 0, `chip_start_utc` and `finish_recorded_utc` null, `provisional_penalty_seconds` and `official_penalty_seconds` of 0, `provisional_elapsed_seconds` and `official_elapsed_seconds` null, `disputed_attempt_id` null, `protest_rounds` of 0, an empty `voided_attempt_ids` array and `dq_reason` null. The heat's `assigned_count` — which is simply the number of entry rows currently pointing at that heat — is recomputed. Entries are never deleted; a reassignment moves the row rather than creating a second one. Entries may be listed with optional filters on `wave_id`, `participant_id`, `progress_status`, `result_status` and `disputed_attempt_id`, and a single entry may be read with both readings, penalty totals, protest count and voided attempt ids — though a `Participant` may read only their own.

Check-in happens at bag check on race morning and is performed by a `SiteOpsLead` or a `RaceDirector`. It advances the entry `REGISTERED → CHECKED_IN` and carries two fields that are stored on the entry: the `bag_tag_code` issued at the bag drop, which is stored exactly as supplied and is not required to be unique across entries, and `chip_start_offset_seconds`, the number of seconds after the gun at which this participant is expected to cross the start mat (an integer of at least 0). Four conditions gate it. The entry must still read `REGISTERED`, or it is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`. The participant's `clearance_status` must read `CLEARED` — a `PENDING` or `MEDICALLY_HELD` human is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_CLEARED`. The heat's `roster_status` must be `OPEN` or `LOCKED`; once it is `SEALED`, check-in is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_CLOSED`. And the heat's `release_status` must be `SCHEDULED`, `HELD` or `RELEASED`; a heat already `STARTED` or `CLOSED` refuses check-in with `ERR_WAVE_STATE_INVALID`.

Moving an entry between heats is a `RaceDirector` capability and carries `target_wave_id`. The entry must still read `REGISTERED` — an entry that has already checked in or run cannot be moved, and is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`. Both the source heat's and the target heat's `roster_status` must still be `OPEN`; a locked or sealed roster on either side refuses the move with `ERR_ROSTER_CLOSED`. The target heat must belong to the same course *and* carry the same division as the entry's current heat, or the move is refused with `ERR_WAVE_COURSE_MISMATCH`. The target heat must have room by the same rule as registration — its `assigned_count` plus one at most its `capacity` — or the move is refused with `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Roster capacity is the *only* registration gate the move re-applies: bib uniqueness inside the target heat, age eligibility against the target heat's own `scheduled_start_utc`, and the division's `team_size_max` for the entry's `team_code` are all left unchecked, so a move may legitimately leave two entries carrying the same `bib_number` in one heat. A `target_wave_id` naming the entry's current heat is not refused as such: it satisfies the course-and-division test trivially and is then held to the same capacity test as any other target, so it is refused with `ERR_WAVE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when that heat is already full and otherwise leaves the entry and both `assigned_count` values exactly as they stood, which by the rule of §2.4 advances no `version` at all. On success the entry's `wave_id` becomes the `target_wave_id` and `assigned_count` is recomputed on both heats, so the total roster across the course is conserved.

### 3.6. The Start Line: Two Orthogonal Dimensions, the Gun and the Close

A heat carries two state fields that advance on separate triggers and must be reasoned about independently. `release_status` is the start-line dimension, running `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `RELEASED`, `STARTED` and terminally `CLOSED`. `roster_status` is the roster dimension, running `OPEN`, `LOCKED` and terminally `SEALED`. A heat may be locked while it is `SCHEDULED` or while it is `HELD`; it may sit `RELEASED` only after it has been locked. Neither dimension is derivable from the other.

Holding is available to a `SafetyOfficer` and to a `RaceDirector`, and the request carries the boolean `held` plus an optional `reason`. With `held` set to true the heat advances `SCHEDULED → HELD` and the supplied `reason` is stored as `hold_reason`; the `reason` is genuinely optional, so omitting it leaves `hold_reason` null, and an empty string is accepted and stored as one. With `held` set to false the heat returns `HELD → SCHEDULED` and `hold_reason` is cleared back to null; un-holding additionally requires the course to read `readiness_posture` of `OPEN`, refusing with `ERR_COURSE_UNSAFE` when the course is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, and requires the course's own `status` to be `VALIDATED` or `PUBLISHED`, refusing with `ERR_COURSE_STATE_INVALID` otherwise. `SCHEDULED` and `HELD` form a two-state loop that may be traversed any number of times, and `roster_status` is untouched in both directions. Invoking either direction from a `release_status` that does not admit it is refused with `ERR_WAVE_STATE_INVALID`. The same `HELD` state is also imposed by the system: the parcel-freeze chain of §2.10 sets `hold_reason` to `VENUE_FROZEN`, and the staffing cascade of §3.3 sets it to `COURSE_RESTRICTED`.

Locking the roster is a `RaceDirector` move that advances `OPEN → LOCKED` and leaves `release_status` completely untouched. From `LOCKED` onward no further registration and no inbound reassignment is accepted, both refused with `ERR_ROSTER_CLOSED`. Locking an already locked or sealed roster is refused with `ERR_WAVE_STATE_INVALID`.

Releasing a heat to the corral is the most heavily gated operation in the system, and it advances `SCHEDULED → RELEASED` under a `RaceDirector`. Seven conditions must all hold, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.6. The heat's `release_status` must be `SCHEDULED`; a heat that is `HELD`, `RELEASED`, `STARTED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_WAVE_NOT_RELEASABLE`. Its `roster_status` must be `LOCKED` or `SEALED`; a still-`OPEN` roster is refused with `ERR_WAVE_ROSTER_NOT_LOCKED`. The course's `status` must be `PUBLISHED`, or the release is refused with `ERR_COURSE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Medical coverage must be sufficient: the course's `medic_headcount` must be at least the course's `scheduled_capacity_total` divided by 250 participants per medic and rounded up to a whole number, or the release is refused with `ERR_MEDICAL_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. The course's `readiness_posture` must read `OPEN`, or the release is refused with `ERR_COURSE_UNSAFE`. The heat must have at least one entry assigned — an `assigned_count` of 0 is refused with `ERR_WAVE_ROSTER_EMPTY`. And the heat's `capacity` must be at most the live bottleneck ceiling: the course's `bottleneck_capacity_pph` multiplied by 15 and divided by 60, with any fractional part discarded, exceeding which is refused with `ERR_BOTTLENECK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This last gate is the live twin of the design-capacity gate applied at scheduling time: a heat that fitted the paper design can still be refused at the start line once a marshal has walked off.

Firing the gun advances the heat `RELEASED → STARTED` under a `RaceDirector`. The course's `readiness_posture` must still read `OPEN`, or the start is refused with `ERR_COURSE_UNSAFE`. When the heat's division has `division_type` of `TEAM`, every distinct `team_code` present among the heat's entries must have at least `team_size_min` entries whose `progress_status` reads `CHECKED_IN`; a short team refuses the start with `ERR_TEAM_INCOMPLETE`. Starting from any `release_status` other than `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_WAVE_STATE_INVALID`. The gun does four things at once. It stamps `actual_start_utc` from the logical clock — this is the gun time, and it is the anchor for every subsequent timing computation. It advances the orthogonal roster dimension `LOCKED → SEALED`, after which check-in is refused. It advances every entry reading `CHECKED_IN` to `STARTED` and computes each one's `chip_start_utc` as `actual_start_utc` advanced by that entry's own `chip_start_offset_seconds`. And it advances every entry still reading `REGISTERED` — those who never showed at bag check — to `DNS`, setting their `result_status` to `NOT_CLASSIFIED`; a non-starter is given no start instant of its own, so its `chip_start_utc` stays null. The heat's `version` and the `version` of every entry touched are each incremented by 1.

Closing the heat advances `STARTED → CLOSED` under a `RaceDirector` and is refused from any other `release_status` with `ERR_WAVE_STATE_INVALID`. The close stamps `closed_at_utc` from the logical clock. Every entry still reading `progress_status` of `STARTED` — still out on the course when the heat closed — advances to `DNF` with `result_status` set to `NOT_CLASSIFIED`, and this reaches such an entry unconditionally, whatever its `result_status` held: an entry disqualified out on the course for bypassing in a `DQ_ON_BYPASS` division and never finished is overwritten `DISQUALIFIED → NOT_CLASSIFIED` by the close, with its `dq_reason` left standing as it was. Every entry reading `result_status` of `PROVISIONAL` advances `PROVISIONAL → OFFICIAL`, with `official_penalty_seconds` and `official_elapsed_seconds` computed by the official reading described in §3.8. Those promoted entries are the only ones the close computes a reading for: an entry the close moves to `DNF`, and a finished entry already reading `DISQUALIFIED`, are both left with `official_penalty_seconds` of 0 and `official_elapsed_seconds` of null however many penalties their attempt card carries. Entries reading `UNDER_PROTEST` are deliberately left `UNDER_PROTEST` — closing a heat does not resolve an open dispute. The heat's `version` and the `version` of every entry touched are incremented by 1.

Finally the close bubbles up. Every close, not only the one that completes the course, recomputes the parent course's `scheduled_capacity_total` — a heat reading `CLOSED` no longer counts toward that sum — and that recomputation on its own increments the course's `version` by 1 even when the course does not complete. When the heat that just closed leaves no heat of that course outside `CLOSED`, and the course has at least one heat, the course advances `PUBLISHED → COMPLETED` automatically, and its `scheduled_capacity_total` recomputes to 0 because no heat remains in `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `RELEASED` or `STARTED`. The course's `version` is incremented by 1 in the same operation.

### 3.7. Out on the Course: Attempt Cards, Marshaling Truth and the Provisional Reading

Every station keeps a card. An `ObstacleMarshal` — or a `RaceDirector` standing in — records what happened to one started entry at one obstacle by supplying the `obstacle_id` of the station and the `outcome` observed. The station must stand on the course the entry's own heat sits on: an `obstacle_id` that does not resolve at all, or that resolves to an obstacle belonging to some other course, is alike refused with `ERR_OBSTACLE_NOT_FOUND` at the body-foreign-key rung, ahead of every state and marshaling complaint below. The four outcomes are `COMPLETED` when the participant cleared the obstacle, `BYPASSED` when they took the bypass lane or the penalty loop, `FAILED_PENALTY` when they attempted it, failed, and accepted the penalty, and `SKIPPED_UNMARSHALED` when the station stood unstaffed and the field was simply waved through. The entry must read `progress_status` of `STARTED`; recording against an entry that has not started, or has already finished, is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`.

**Marshaling truth** is the rule that keeps the attempt card honest, and it cuts both ways. An outcome of `COMPLETED`, `BYPASSED` or `FAILED_PENALTY` asserts that somebody was standing there to observe it, so the obstacle's `staffing_state` must be anything other than `UNSTAFFED`; recording one of those three at a station with zero confirmed marshals is refused with `ERR_OBSTACLE_UNMARSHALED`. Conversely an outcome of `SKIPPED_UNMARSHALED` asserts the opposite, so the obstacle's `staffing_state` must be exactly `UNSTAFFED`; recording it at a station reading `PARTIAL` or `MARSHALED` is refused with `ERR_OBSTACLE_MARSHALED`. Because a station can lose its last marshal mid-heat, each attempt also snapshots the station's staffing at the moment of recording into `obstacle_staffing_state_at_record`, which holds `UNSTAFFED`, `PARTIAL` or `MARSHALED` and is never revised afterwards.

Bypassing is separately permitted or forbidden by two independent authorities, and either one can veto it. An outcome of `BYPASSED` is refused with `ERR_BYPASS_NOT_ALLOWED` when the obstacle's own `bypass_mode` reads `MANDATORY`, and it is refused with the same code when the heat's division has a `bypass_policy` of `MANDATORY_COMPLETION`. Where bypassing *is* permitted, the division's `bypass_policy` decides what it costs. Under `ALLOWED_WITH_PENALTY` it simply adds seconds. Under `DQ_ON_BYPASS` it is fatal: recording a `BYPASSED` outcome for an entry still reading `result_status` of `PENDING` advances that entry `PENDING → DISQUALIFIED` immediately, with `dq_reason` set to the string `BYPASS_IN_DQ_DIVISION`, and the entry's `version` incremented by 1.

Recording an attempt stamps `recorded_at` from the logical clock — this instant, not the insertion order, is the chronological key for penalty accumulation — together with `recorded_by_user_id` and `recorded_by_role` taken from the acting `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers. The attempt also records its own `provisional_penalty_seconds`: 0 when its `outcome` is `COMPLETED`, and the obstacle's `penalty_seconds` when its `outcome` is `FAILED_PENALTY`, `BYPASSED` or `SKIPPED_UNMARSHALED`. Note that under the provisional reading a wave-through at an unstaffed station is charged exactly like a bypass — that asymmetry is corrected only when results go official. Recording an attempt also recomputes the parent entry's own `provisional_penalty_seconds` as the running total. An entry's attempt card, and the roll of every attempt recorded at one station across all entries, are both readable in chronological order and both accept an optional filter on `outcome`. Attempts are immutable records: they are never edited and never deleted, and they carry no `version`.

Recording a finish advances the entry `STARTED → FINISHED` and is available to a `RaceDirector` or an `ObstacleMarshal`. The heat must still read `release_status` of `STARTED`, and the entry must read `progress_status` of `STARTED`, or the finish is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`. The finish stamps `finish_recorded_utc` from the logical clock and computes the **provisional reading**, which is the only result a participant sees before things go official. Take every attempt on the entry, in ascending `recorded_at` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and sum each attempt's provisional penalty as defined above; that total is the entry's `provisional_penalty_seconds`. Take the **gun base**, the exact number of whole seconds between the heat's `actual_start_utc` and the entry's `finish_recorded_utc`. `provisional_elapsed_seconds` is the gun base plus `provisional_penalty_seconds`. The provisional reading measures from the gun for *every* division, regardless of that division's `scoring_mode`, and it charges every recorded penalty without exception. Alongside the timing, the finish advances `result_status` from `PENDING` to `PROVISIONAL` — except that an entry already reading `DISQUALIFIED` stays `DISQUALIFIED`, since a runner disqualified at a station does not acquire a provisional result by crossing the line.

### 3.8. Protests, Adjudication and the Official Reading

A participant who disputes what a marshal wrote down may protest, and only the participant who owns the entry may do so. The protest names the `disputed_attempt_id` of the single attempt being challenged and carries a free-text `reason`. Four conditions gate it. The entry's `result_status` must read `PROVISIONAL`, or the protest is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_PROTESTABLE`. The entry's `protest_rounds` must be strictly fewer than 2 — a third protest against an entry already carrying two adjudicated rounds is refused with `ERR_PROTEST_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the entry's `participant_id`, or the protest is refused with `ERR_NOT_ENTRY_OWNER`. And `disputed_attempt_id` must name an attempt whose `entry_id` is this very entry; an id that does not resolve, or resolves to an attempt on somebody else's card, is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND`. On success the entry stores `disputed_attempt_id` and advances `PROVISIONAL → UNDER_PROTEST`, with its `version` incremented by 1.

Adjudication is a `RaceDirector` decision on the open protest. It carries the `outcome` of the ruling, the `expected_entry_version` guard described in §2.8, and an optional free-text `note` recording the reasoning. The entry must read `result_status` of `UNDER_PROTEST`, or the adjudication is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_STATE_INVALID`; `expected_entry_version` must equal the entry's current `version`, or it is refused with `ERR_STALE_ENTRY_VERSION`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `recorded_by_user_id` stamped on the disputed attempt, or it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Every one of the three possible rulings increments `protest_rounds` by 1 and increments the entry's `version` by 1.

The three rulings are `UPHELD_ADJUSTED`, `DENIED_OFFICIAL` and `DISQUALIFIED`, and each is specified in full. An `UPHELD_ADJUSTED` ruling agrees with the participant: the id held in `disputed_attempt_id` is appended to the entry's `voided_attempt_ids` array (appended unconditionally and without de-duplication, so a later round that protests the same attempt again and is again upheld records that id a second time), the official reading is recomputed with that attempt's penalty suppressed, and the entry returns `UNDER_PROTEST → PROVISIONAL`, from which a second protest naming a different attempt may legitimately follow — the cycle `PROVISIONAL → UNDER_PROTEST → PROVISIONAL` is bounded only by the requirement that `protest_rounds` be strictly fewer than 2 when a protest is filed. A `DENIED_OFFICIAL` ruling rejects the complaint and closes the matter: the official reading is computed and the entry advances `UNDER_PROTEST → OFFICIAL`, which is terminal. A `DISQUALIFIED` ruling finds against the participant on the merits: the entry advances `UNDER_PROTEST → DISQUALIFIED` with `dq_reason` set to the string `ADJUDICATED_DQ`, which is likewise terminal. This is the one ruling of the three that computes no reading at all: `official_penalty_seconds` and `official_elapsed_seconds` are left exactly as they stood — 0 and null, unless an earlier `UPHELD_ADJUSTED` round on the same entry had already computed them, in which case those earlier figures stand. `NOT_CLASSIFIED`, imposed on a no-show or a non-finisher, is the fourth and last terminal result state.

The **official reading** is computed whenever an entry reaches `OFFICIAL` — whether by a heat closing or by a `DENIED_OFFICIAL` ruling — and is recomputed by an `UPHELD_ADJUSTED` ruling. It walks the same attempts in the same ascending `recorded_at` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, but it suppresses two whole classes of penalty. An attempt whose id appears in the entry's `voided_attempt_ids` contributes 0 seconds. An attempt whose `outcome` is `SKIPPED_UNMARSHALED` also contributes 0 seconds, on the principle that the producer cannot penalise a runner for a station it failed to marshal. Every other attempt contributes exactly what it contributed provisionally: 0 for `COMPLETED`, and the obstacle's `penalty_seconds` for `FAILED_PENALTY` or `BYPASSED`. The sum is the entry's `official_penalty_seconds`.

The official clock base depends on the division. When the division's `scoring_mode` is `CHIP_TIME`, the base is the **chip base**: the exact number of whole seconds between the entry's own `chip_start_utc` and its `finish_recorded_utc`, so a runner who crossed the start mat forty seconds behind the gun is not charged for those forty seconds. When the division's `scoring_mode` is `GUN_TIME`, the base is the gun base, measured from the heat's `actual_start_utc` exactly as in the provisional reading. `official_elapsed_seconds` is that base plus `official_penalty_seconds`. Both bases are exact instant differences and are never rounded. The provisional fields are never overwritten by the official computation: `provisional_penalty_seconds` and `provisional_elapsed_seconds` remain readable alongside `official_penalty_seconds` and `official_elapsed_seconds` for the life of the entry, which is what lets the producer show a participant precisely how their result changed.

### 3.9. Festival Area, Land Restoration and Settling the Bond

The festival area is the other half of the physical footprint. A `SiteOpsLead` or a `RaceDirector` installs a festival service on the parcel by naming its `service_type` — one of `BAG_CHECK`, `RINSE_STATION` or `SPECTATOR_ZONE` — its `footprint_sqm` of ground disturbance strictly greater than 0, the `capacity` of people it serves at once (at least 1), and optionally its `water_draw_lpm`, the litres per minute it draws from the parcel's water source, which defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. Every installation reads `status` of `OPEN` on creation, and installing one recomputes the parcel's `disturbed_area_sqm`; because a footprint is always at least one square metre that recomputation is always a real change to the parcel, so the `Venue`'s `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshes in the same operation. A positive `water_draw_lpm` brings the land authority into it: the parcel must have exactly one governing permit reading `APPROVED`, or the installation is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`, and that permit's `water_use_allowed` must be true, or it is refused with `ERR_WATER_USE_NOT_PERMITTED`. An installation with a `water_draw_lpm` of 0 needs neither. Installations may be listed with optional filters on `service_type` and `status`. An installation leaves `OPEN` in exactly one way: the parcel-freeze fan-out of §2.10 moves it `OPEN → CLOSED`, which is terminal, increments its `version` by 1, and — as stressed earlier — leaves `disturbed_area_sqm` untouched.

When the race is over the parcel has to be handed back. A `PermitOfficer` or a `SiteOpsLead` opens a remediation task naming its `task_type` — one of `RESEED`, `DECOMPACT`, `SILT_REMOVAL` or `DEBRIS_SWEEP` — and the `area_sqm` it remediates, strictly greater than 0. That area is also the weight the task carries in the bond split, so a task may not claim ground that was never disturbed: the sum of `area_sqm` over every remediation task on the parcel, the candidate included, must not exceed the parcel's `disturbed_area_sqm`, and a task that would push the total past it is refused with `ERR_RESTORATION_AREA_EXCEEDED`. A new task reads `status` of `PENDING` with `bond_release_cents` of 0, `inspection_rounds` of 0 and `findings_note` null. Opening a task is deliberately *not* gated on the parcel's `site_posture` — the `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN` gate of §2.10 does not reach this operation, so remediation may be opened against a parcel reading `FROZEN` or `CLOSED_OUT` exactly as against an `OPEN` one, which matters because a permit expiry freezes the parcel at precisely the moment the ground still needs putting back. Tasks may be listed with optional filters on `status` and `task_type`, and a single task may be read with its inspection count and attributed bond cents.

Inspections are the `PermitOfficer`'s and carry the `outcome` of the visit — either `VERIFIED` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` — plus an optional `findings_note` that is stored on the task, overwriting whatever the previous visit wrote; the empty string is an acceptable note and overwrites as any other string does, while omitting the field or sending it as null leaves whatever note the task already carried untouched. Every inspection increments `inspection_rounds` by 1. The parcel must read `site_posture` of `OPEN`; a `FROZEN` or `CLOSED_OUT` parcel refuses the inspection with `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN`. An inspection whose outcome would not change the task's state — `VERIFIED` on a task already `VERIFIED`, or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` on a task already `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` — is refused with `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`. The two checks share rung 9 of §2.6 and the parcel posture is the one evaluated first, so a no-op outcome recorded against a task whose parcel reads `FROZEN` or `CLOSED_OUT` reports `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN` rather than `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`. Four transitions are therefore admissible, and each has its own ledger consequence.

A pass on a fresh task moves it `PENDING → VERIFIED`. It computes that task's provisional bond share and writes it to `bond_release_cents`: the parcel's `restoration_bond_cents` multiplied by the task's `area_sqm`, divided by the parcel's `disturbed_area_sqm`, and rounded half up to whole cents. It then syncs the parcel ledger by adding that same amount to `bond_released_cents` and subtracting the same amount from `bond_held_cents`, and increments the `version` of both the task and the parcel by 1 — save in the degenerate case where that share works out to 0, as it does on a parcel whose `restoration_bond_cents` is 0, in which event no money moves, the parcel row is not written at all, and its `version` and `updated_at` stand still while the task's still advance. A fail on a fresh task moves it `PENDING → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, increments `inspection_rounds` and the task's `version`, and moves no money at all. A fail on a task that had passed moves it `VERIFIED → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` and reverses the ledger exactly: the task's current `bond_release_cents` is subtracted from the parcel's `bond_released_cents` and added back to `bond_held_cents`, after which the task's `bond_release_cents` resets to 0; both `version` values increment. A re-inspection that passes moves it `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → VERIFIED` and applies precisely the same share computation and ledger sync as the first pass. The remediation cycle `VERIFIED → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → VERIFIED` may run any number of times; `inspection_rounds` is the only record of how many. Whichever of the four transitions runs, the response body carries the `RestorationTask` and not the parcel, so a client that needs the parcel's refreshed ledger columns must read the venue back.

Because each task's share is rounded to whole cents *independently* and the rounded amounts are then summed, the parcel ledger can sit a cent or two away from the posted bond during remediation. Three equal tasks of 1500 square metres each on a parcel with a `restoration_bond_cents` of 1,000,000 and a `disturbed_area_sqm` of 4500 each attribute 333,333 cents, so `bond_released_cents` stalls at 999,999 — one cent short — with the missing cent still sitting in `bond_held_cents`. That drift is expected, and closing the parcel out is what reconciles it.

Settling the bond is the `PermitOfficer`'s move and carries `settlement_type`, which is either `PROGRESSIVE` or `FINAL`, together with the `expected_venue_version` guard of §2.8. Both settlement kinds require the parcel to have exactly one governing permit reading `APPROVED`, or they are refused with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`, and both are refused with `ERR_BOND_ALREADY_SETTLED` when the parcel already reads `site_posture` of `CLOSED_OUT`. That `CLOSED_OUT` refusal is the *only* posture check either settlement makes: the `ERR_VENUE_FROZEN` gate of §2.10 does not reach this operation, so a parcel reading `FROZEN` may be settled progressively or finally exactly as an `OPEN` one may. Both kinds answer with the `Venue` in `data`, carrying the parcel's freshly written ledger columns.

A `PROGRESSIVE` settlement is a reconciliation snapshot that leaves the parcel open for more work. It recomputes the parcel's `bond_released_cents` as the sum of `bond_release_cents` over the remediation tasks currently reading `VERIFIED` — tasks reading `PENDING` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` contribute nothing — leaves `bond_forfeited_cents` as it stands, and sets `bond_held_cents` to `restoration_bond_cents` minus `bond_released_cents` minus `bond_forfeited_cents`. It never touches `site_posture` at all, so the parcel is left reading exactly the posture it already stood at — `OPEN` where it was open, `FROZEN` where a withdrawal of consent had frozen it — and the parcel's `version` increments by 1. That increment is *unconditional*, and this settlement is the one write in the API to which the idempotent-recomputation rule of §2.4 does not apply: a second progressive settlement that lands on ledger figures identical to the ones already stored still advances the parcel's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`. It may be repeated as often as the producer likes, which is exactly why it needs the expected-version guard rather than a state check — and why each repeat must quote the `version` the previous one produced.

A `FINAL` settlement closes the parcel out, writing `site_posture` to the terminal `CLOSED_OUT` from whatever it previously read — `OPEN` in the ordinary case, but `FROZEN` just as readily, since a parcel frozen after all its tasks had already been verified may still be closed out. It first demands that every remediation task on the parcel reads `VERIFIED` and that at least one such task exists; a parcel with an unverified task, or with no task at all, is refused with `ERR_RESTORATION_INCOMPLETE`. It then computes the settlement in a deliberately different rounding order from the per-task computation: sum the `area_sqm` of the verified tasks first, multiply the parcel's `restoration_bond_cents` by that summed verified area, divide by the parcel's `disturbed_area_sqm`, and round half up to whole cents *once*. The denominator is always the full `disturbed_area_sqm`, never the verified area, so a partial remediation releases only its proportional share of the bond. Call that once-rounded figure the **gross total**.

Lateness is then charged against the governing permit's `restoration_deadline_utc`, measured on the logical clock. When the clock reads at or before the deadline, the parcel is not late and the number of late days is 0. When the clock has passed the deadline, take the whole seconds elapsed from the deadline to the clock, divide by 3600 to obtain hours, divide that by 24, and round up to a whole number of days — so any part of a day counts as a whole day. The late penalty is 25,000 cents (250 currency units) for each late day, capped so that it can never exceed the gross total: the penalty is whichever is smaller of the per-day charge multiplied by the late-day count, and the gross total itself. The **net total** is the gross total minus that penalty.

The final settlement then reconciles the two rounding orders. Sum the `bond_release_cents` currently sitting on the verified tasks — the per-task figures — and subtract that sum from the net total; the difference, which may be positive or negative, is the **remainder**. The remainder is added to the `bond_release_cents` of exactly one task: the verified task with the greatest `area_sqm`; where two or more tie on area, the one with the earliest `created_at`; and where they share `created_at` too — as equal-area tasks opened between two clock movements always do — the one inserted first. That single task's `version` increments by 1 as the remainder lands on it, while the other verified tasks are not written and do not advance. No clamp is applied to the adjusted figure, so a remainder driven deeply negative by a large late penalty can leave that one task carrying a negative `bond_release_cents`, which is accepted rather than floored at zero. In the three-equal-tasks example above, the once-rounded gross total is 1,000,000 while the per-task sum is 999,999, so the remainder of one cent lands on a single task, which finishes carrying 333,334 while its two siblings stay at 333,333. Finally the parcel ledger is written in full: `bond_released_cents` becomes the net total, `bond_forfeited_cents` becomes the late penalty, and `bond_held_cents` becomes `restoration_bond_cents` minus the net total minus the late penalty. The invariant of §2.9 holds by construction — held plus released plus forfeited always equals `restoration_bond_cents` — and the parcel's `version` increments by 1 as it closes out.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/ocr` prefix. Operations that bring a brand-new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201 and return the created resource in `data`; operations that modify an existing resource, drive a state transition, or read data respond with HTTP 200 and return the affected or requested resource. The table gives only the HTTP contract and the authorised roles — the request payloads, the response side effects and the gating rules for each operation are specified in the narratives of §2 and §3 and must be read there. Alongside the table the service exposes one operational probe, `GET /ocr/_health`, which carries no role restriction and requires no identity headers at all; it answers HTTP 200 with the bare object `{"status": "ok", "current_utc": <the logical clock>}` rather than with the envelope of §2.2, and it is the only endpoint of either kind that behaves this way.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue
