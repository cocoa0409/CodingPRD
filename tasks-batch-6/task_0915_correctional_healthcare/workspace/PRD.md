# Correctional Healthcare API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every jail and prison in the country runs a small hospital inside a security perimeter, and the two institutions inside that perimeter — the clinic and the custody operation — answer to different masters. The clinic answers to constitutional standards of care, accreditation bodies and, when a patient dies, a statutory mortality-review clock. The custody operation answers to headcount, escort availability and lockdown. This document specifies the REST API for a correctional health services platform that has to serve both: it books a patient in, screens them on a legally-bounded clock, enrols them in chronic-disease management, receives their sick-call slips and triages them, administers their medication dose by dose under either directly-observed therapy or keep-on-person rules, sends them off-site for specialty care under a utilization-review gate and an armed-escort transport plan, charges them a co-pay their trust account may not be able to fund, hears their grievance when they think that charge was wrong, plans their release with a bridge supply of medication in hand, and — when the worst happens — freezes the record and opens the review.

The platform's discriminating requirement is that almost nothing in it is a single-step operation. Recording a death does not merely set a flag: it freezes the clinical record, discontinues every live medication order, voids every dose those orders had scheduled, recomputes adherence over the surviving denominator, cancels every open sick call and referral and transport, resolves every chronic-care enrollment, voids every release plan, and opens a mortality review with a statutory deadline — a five-level cascade from one request. Granting a billing grievance does not merely record an outcome: it reverses a co-pay charge, appends a compensating ledger entry, recomputes the trust balance and re-derives the indigency status that determined the charge's size in the first place. The specification below is written on the assumption that an implementer will build these cascades exactly as described, because partial cascades are indistinguishable from data corruption in an environment where the record is discoverable evidence.

Two further constraints shape the whole design. First, the system runs on a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that deadlines, lapse sweeps and filing windows are reproducible; every temporal rule in this document is measured against that clock. Second, every rule that can reject a request has a fixed place in a single, system-wide **validation priority ladder** — fourteen rungs, evaluated in order, first failure wins. Where several rules could plausibly fire on the same malformed request, the ladder, not the implementer's intuition, decides which error the caller sees.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Clinical Staff, Custody Staff and Patients: Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` names the role they are acting in. Both headers are mandatory on every endpoint without exception, including read-only ones. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the nine role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called (see the Authorized Role(s) column of §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The nine roles are `IntakeOfficer`, `Nurse`, `Provider`, `UtilizationReviewer`, `PeerReviewer`, `CustodyOfficer`, `HealthServicesAdmin`, `GrievanceCoordinator` and `Inmate`. Their responsibilities divide as follows. An `IntakeOfficer` works the booking desk and admits patients into the facility. A `Nurse` is licensed nursing staff: they dispose of intake screenings, triage sick call, record medication administrations and pill counts, and record chronic-care visits. A `Provider` is a physician, nurse practitioner or physician assistant: they prescribe, enrol patients in chronic care, write referrals and dispose of encounters. A `UtilizationReviewer` approves, denies or defers off-site care. A `PeerReviewer` is an independent clinician who resolves denials and may never be the same person who issued the denial under review. A `CustodyOfficer` builds, escorts, dispatches and closes out off-site transports. A `HealthServicesAdmin` handles custody events, litigation records freezes, the inmate trust account, release planning, mortality review, reporting and the logical clock. A `GrievanceCoordinator` staffs the grievance office. An `Inmate` is the patient.

Eight of the nine roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeOfficer`, `Nurse`, `Provider`, `UtilizationReviewer`, `PeerReviewer`, `CustodyOfficer`, `HealthServicesAdmin` or `GrievanceCoordinator`, the `X-User-ID` value is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server does record it where a state transition says so — as `prescriber_user_id` on a medication order, as `administered_by_user_id` on an administration, as `recorded_by_user_id` on a chronic-care visit, as `decided_by_user_id` and `peer_reviewer_user_id` on a utilization review — and those recorded values are what the segregation-of-duties rules later compare against, but the server never resolves an opaque `X-User-ID` to an entity.

The ninth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `Inmate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inmate` row. Ownership checks on any resource an `Inmate` addresses compare that resource's `inmate_id` — directly, or transitively through its parent chain, so that a grievance reached through its own identifier still resolves to the inmate who filed it — against `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This applies to every endpoint the `Inmate` role may call: submitting a sick-call slip, filing a grievance, escalating a grievance, withdrawing a grievance, reading their own inmate record, reading sick-call requests, reading co-pay charges, reading ledger entries, reading grievances and reading release plans. An `Inmate` reading a list endpoint sees only their own rows.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a payload-range failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 names a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Four endpoints order differently by design: the MAR rows of a medication order are returned by `scheduled_at` ascending, the visits of a chronic-care enrollment by `recorded_at` ascending, and the utilization reviews attached to a referral by `opened_at` ascending. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every persisted mutation of that row. Two resources are deliberately immutable audit records and carry neither: a `ChronicCareVisit` and a `LedgerEntry` have only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. They are written once and never modified.

The `version` integer matters beyond bookkeeping, because it is the value clients echo back on the two optimistic-concurrency-guarded writes described in §2.7. It is therefore important to know precisely which operations advance it. A read-time projection never does. The `indigent_flag`, `open_sick_call_count` and `no_show_count` fields on an inmate, the `sla_breached` flag on a sick-call request, the `response_breached` flag on a grievance and the `turnaround_breached` flag on a utilization review are all evaluated at the moment a response is built, from the rows and the clock as they stand at that moment, and are never persisted as stored inputs. A sick-call slip submitted for an inmate changes what `open_sick_call_count` reports on the next read without advancing the inmate's `version` at all.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not the host's wall clock. Before any call to the clock-setting capability the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. A `HealthServicesAdmin` moves it with an admin-only test-driving capability that takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the new instant. Setting the clock is not a passive act: it runs a two-step sweep in a fixed order. Step one transitions every `IntakeScreening` whose status is `PENDING` and whose `due_at` is strictly before the new clock instant into `OVERDUE` — a screening whose `due_at` equals the new clock instant exactly is still on time and stays `PENDING`. Step two, which runs only after step one has finished, transitions every `ChronicCareEnrollment` whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose `next_due_date` plus a fifteen-day grace period falls strictly before the new clock instant into `LAPSED`. Each sweep transition increments the swept row's own `version` by 1.

### 2.5. The Validation Priority Ladder

When a single request trips more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. All checks in this system belong to one global ladder of fourteen rungs, evaluated strictly in ascending order. The first rung that fails determines the response, and no rung below it is evaluated at all.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present, and `X-User-Role` naming one of the nine declared roles. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The named role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list. Failure yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Resource ownership.** An entity-backed actor — that is, the `Inmate` role — touches only rows whose `inmate_id` resolves to its own `X-User-ID`. Failure yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
4. **Existence.** Path and body references resolve, validated left to right along the URL hierarchy. A missing inmate yields `ERR_INMATE_NOT_FOUND` (404); a missing specialty referral, utilization review or transport order yields `ERR_REFERRAL_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (404); a missing intake screening, chronic-care enrollment, sick-call request, medication order, medication administration, co-pay charge, grievance, release plan or mortality review yields `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (404).
5. **The clinical-records write gate.** The addressed inmate's `records_state` must be `OPEN`. Failure yields `ERR_RECORDS_FROZEN` (409). **This rung sits deliberately high and pre-empts all nine rungs below it.** A frozen inmate returns `ERR_RECORDS_FROZEN` even when the very same request also carries a stale version, a malformed payload, an out-of-range enum, an illegal target state, an expired temporal window or a counter already at its cap. Litigation-hold integrity outranks every downstream correctness concern.
6. **The custody gate.** For custody-dependent operations the inmate's `custody_status` must still be `IN_CUSTODY`. Failure yields `ERR_INMATE_NOT_IN_CUSTODY` (409).
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** Where an endpoint requires an expected parent version, that value must equal the parent's current `version`. Failure yields `ERR_STALE_INMATE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_REFERRAL_VERSION` (both 412), as described in §2.7.
8. **Payload shape.** Required fields present and non-null, then declared JSON types and numeric ranges, then enum membership. The first two yield `ERR_VALIDATION` (400); enum membership yields `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (422). Enum membership is checked on every enum-typed field this API accepts, which is the full set `custody_level`, `admission_type`, `screening_type`, `severity`, `condition_code`, `acuity`, `encounter_type`, `drug_class`, `route_mode`, `urgency`, `specialty`, `decision`, `peer_decision`, `outcome`, `release_type`, `manner`, `entry_type`, `cancel_reason` and `escort_level`; a value outside the field's declared set is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` even though a missing or wrongly-typed value on the same field would have been `ERR_VALIDATION`.
9. **Target state.** The addressed row's own `status` or `outcome` is one from which the requested transition is declared. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This rung also carries three siblings: a re-triage that supplies the acuity the request already carries yields `ERR_VALIDATION` (400); a second death event on an inmate who already has a mortality review yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); and closing a mortality review that has not yet accumulated its minimum corrective action yields `ERR_MORTALITY_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE` (422).
10. **Cross-entity state.** A *different* entity's state must permit the transition. Recording an administration against a parent order that is not `ACTIVE` yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409); creating a transport or assigning an escort against a referral that is not `AUTHORIZED` yields `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (409); releasing an inmate with no `FINALIZED` release plan yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
11. **Clinical safety.** Directly-observed-therapy and keep-on-person observation discipline, and the intake-screening compliance gate. Failures yield `ERR_DOT_OBSERVATION_REQUIRED` (422), `ERR_KOP_OBSERVATION_FORBIDDEN` (422) and `ERR_INTAKE_SCREENING_OVERDUE` (422) respectively.
12. **Segregation of duties.** The acting user is not the same person who performed the conflicting earlier step. Failure yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
13. **Temporal windows.** Filing windows, escalation windows, transport lead times, cancellation cutoffs and release-plan lead time. Failures yield `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_GRIEVANCE_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_TRANSPORT_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT`, `ERR_CANCEL_CUTOFF_PASSED` and `ERR_RELEASE_PLAN_LEAD_TIME` (all 422).
14. **Caps, uniqueness, fiscal reversibility and interval occupancy — checked last of all.** Counter caps yield `ERR_MAX_RETRIAGE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_MAX_DEFERRALS_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_MAX_REMANDS_EXCEEDED` (all 409); an escort level that does not match the required level yields `ERR_ESCORT_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (422); uniqueness collisions yield `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409); an already-settled co-pay charge named by a granted grievance yields `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_REVERSIBLE` (409); and interval collisions on either the medication calendar or the transport calendar yield `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are counter-intuitive. First, because rung 5 outranks rung 8, a request with a genuinely broken payload against a frozen record reports the freeze, not the payload. Second, because rung 14 is last, a request that violates a uniqueness key *and* an earlier rung always reports the earlier rung; `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` and `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` are the quietest errors in the system and only surface when everything else about the request was correct.

### 2.6. The Clinical-Records Freeze and the Custody Gate

An inmate carries two independent status axes on the same row, and they advance separately. The custody axis is `custody_status`, which begins at `IN_CUSTODY` and moves to either `RELEASED` or `DECEASED`. The clinical-records axis is `records_state`, which begins at `OPEN` and moves between `OPEN` and `FROZEN`. A litigation hold may freeze the record of a patient who is very much still in custody, and a patient may be released while their record stays open — neither axis constrains the other in the general case. The single coupling runs one way only: entering `DECEASED` forces `records_state` to `FROZEN` on the same row, and while an inmate is `DECEASED` the unfreeze operation is refused, which makes a death-induced freeze permanent.

While `records_state` is `FROZEN`, the clinical write surface is closed. Rung 5 of the ladder guards, and rejects with `ERR_RECORDS_FROZEN` (HTTP 409), every mutating intake-screening, chronic-care, sick-call, medication, referral and co-pay operation. Concretely that is: opening an intake screening and recording its disposition; enrolling in chronic care, re-enrolling and recording a chronic-care visit; submitting, triaging, scheduling, seeing and closing a sick-call request; writing a medication order, holding or resuming it, discontinuing it, recording a pill count, scheduling a MAR row and recording an administration outcome; requesting a specialty referral and submitting it for review; deciding, supplying information to, escalating and peer-deciding a utilization review; creating a transport order and assigning, dispatching, closing out or cancelling a trip; and posting an ad-hoc co-pay charge. The freeze deliberately does **not** guard four families of operation: the inmate custody and records operations themselves (release, death event, records freeze and records unfreeze), trust-account ledger deposits and release-plan work, every grievance operation, and every mortality-review operation. A grievance must remain hearable and a mortality review must remain completable precisely when the record is frozen — those are the circumstances that produced the freeze.

The custody gate is narrower and sits one rung lower. Six operations require the inmate to be `IN_CUSTODY` at the moment of the call and reject with `ERR_INMATE_NOT_IN_CUSTODY` (HTTP 409) otherwise: submitting a sick-call request, enrolling an inmate in chronic care, writing a medication order, requesting a specialty referral, dispatching a transport, and holding or resuming a medication order. No other operation consults the custody axis at this rung.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Two Supersede-Bearing Writes

Two creates in this system silently destroy work if they are issued from a stale view, so both require the client to prove what it last saw.

Writing a medication order — the create that a `Provider` issues against `POST /medication-orders` — requires the body field `expected_inmate_version`, carrying the `version` integer of the target inmate as the client last observed it. This field is not optional. If it does not equal the inmate's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INMATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a new order for a `drug_name` that already has an `ACTIVE` order legitimately supersedes that prior order — the prior order is auto-discontinued — so a writer holding a stale view of the inmate would silently supersede an order it never saw. The version check makes that rejection deterministic rather than accidental.

Creating a transport — the create a `CustodyOfficer` issues against `POST /transport-orders` — requires the body field `expected_referral_version`, carrying the `version` integer of the parent specialty referral as the client last observed it. It too is mandatory, and a value that does not equal the referral's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REFERRAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the supersede is on the trip: creating a new trip cancels any `DRAFT` trip already hanging off that referral, so a stale writer would discard a trip it never saw.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field of any kind. Endpoints not named above must not be given one and must not require one.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Boundary Conventions

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents; there is no floating-point money anywhere in this system. Where a percentage of a monetary amount must be taken — the indigent lien rate — the product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Adherence percentages are numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up. The facility adherence index is likewise rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and is rounded twice by design (see §3.4). Day counts, minute counts and periodicity values are integers. Where a difference between two instants is converted to whole minutes, the conversion floors toward negative infinity, so a signed difference of minus ninety seconds becomes minus two minutes, not minus one.

Timestamps computed by adding a whole number of hours or days to an anchor are whole-second instants and are serialized as ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, which for such computed deadlines are always `.000`.

Because so many of this system's rules turn on an edge, the inclusivity convention of each is stated explicitly wherever the rule appears, and the following are the ones an implementer is most likely to get backwards. A screening is overdue only when the clock is **strictly after** its `due_at`; equality is still on time. A sick-call SLA is breached only when the resolution instant is **strictly after** the deadline; equality is not a breach. A utilization-review turnaround is breached only when the reference instant is **strictly after** the deadline; equality is not a breach. A chronic-care enrollment lapses only when the clock is **strictly after** the due date plus its grace period; equality is still active. A mortality review is late only when it closed **strictly after** its due date; closing exactly at the due instant is on time. Transport lead time is accepted at **exact equality** — the required minimum is a floor the appointment may sit exactly on. Transport cancellation is permitted up to and **including** the cutoff instant. Grievance filing and grievance escalation windows are **closed** at their far end: filing exactly at the last permitted instant is inside the window. The peer-review escalation window is likewise **closed** at forty-eight hours after the denial. A release plan's scheduled lead time is accepted at **exact equality**. The indigency threshold is a **strict** comparison in both of its parts. And two interval rules are deliberately opposite: medication order spans are **closed** intervals on calendar dates, so two orders sharing a single endpoint date do collide, while transport slots are **half-open** intervals on instants, so a trip starting exactly when another's slot ends does not collide.

### 2.9. Cascades, Version Propagation and Conservation

Several operations in this system fan out across entities, and every level of every fan-out is mandatory. When a cascade touches a row, that row's own `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshes; a cascade that visits five levels therefore leaves version bumps on every row at every level, not just on the row the caller addressed. The five named cascades are: **Cascade A**, originating at an in-custody death and running five levels deep; **Cascade B**, originating at a release and running four levels deep; **Cascade C**, originating at a witnessed diversion during a keep-on-person administration; **Cascade D**, originating at a transport no-show; and **Cascade E**, originating at a granted billing grievance and running five levels deep. Each is specified in full in the §3 subsection that owns its workflow.

Two conservation invariants hold at all times and are the strongest correctness statements in this specification.

**The money invariant.** For every inmate, the sum of `amount_cents` across all of that inmate's ledger entries equals that inmate's `account_balance_cents` exactly, and the `balance_after_cents` recorded on the chronologically last entry equals `account_balance_cents` exactly. The compensating pair is a co-pay charge posting — which appends either a `CHARGE` or a `LIEN` entry — followed by the grievance-granted reversal, which appends a `REVERSAL` entry. After both have run, `account_balance_cents` must equal its exact pre-charge value with zero drift in either direction.

**The dose-count invariant.** For every medication order, the five outcome buckets `SCHEDULED`, `ADMINISTERED`, `REFUSED`, `MISSED` and `VOIDED` partition that order's administration rows exactly: the sum of the five bucket counts always equals the total number of administration rows created under the order, with no row double-counted and none dropped. The compensating pair here is holding an order followed by resuming it. After both have run, every bucket count is identical to its pre-hold value, and `consecutive_missed_count` and `consecutive_refused_count` are likewise unchanged. Holding an order never touches a dose row's outcome; it only blocks new outcomes from being recorded while the hold is in force.

### 2.10. Nested Path Resolution

Many resources are addressed through their parent. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error, which means an inmate that does not exist is reported as `ERR_INMATE_NOT_FOUND` (404) before anything nested under that inmate is even looked up. The same left-to-right discipline governs body references: a body naming both an `inmate_id` and a sub-resource identifier reports the inmate first. Referral-family records — specialty referrals, utilization reviews and transport orders — report `ERR_REFERRAL_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (404) when missing; every other sub-resource — intake screenings, chronic-care enrollments, sick-call requests, medication orders, medication administrations, co-pay charges, grievances, release plans and mortality reviews — reports `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (404).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Booking, Receiving Screening and the Intake Clock

A patient enters the system when an `IntakeOfficer` books them in. The booking payload carries a `booking_number`, which must be non-empty and unique across every inmate ever booked — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409) — together with the patient's `full_name`, the `facility_code` of the holding facility, and a `custody_level` of `MINIMUM`, `MEDIUM` or `MAXIMUM`. The `booked_at` instant is supplied by the caller and anchors the receiving-screening clock. Three further fields are optional. `admission_type` says how the patient arrived and defaults to `NEW_BOOKING` when omitted, the alternative being `INTER_FACILITY_TRANSFER`. `prior_full_assessment_at` records the instant a full health assessment was completed at the sending facility and defaults to null, which is what it always is for a new booking. `scheduled_release_date` records the projected release instant and also defaults to null.

Booking initializes the row deterministically: `custody_status` is `IN_CUSTODY`, `records_state` is `OPEN`, `account_balance_cents` is 0, and `version` is 1. The `indigent_flag` that comes back on the response is not stored — it is derived at read time — and because a freshly booked patient has no ledger entries at all, the derivation falls back to a zero balance, which is below the indigency threshold, so a newly booked inmate always reads as indigent. Note that booking creates **no** screening rows: both intake instruments are opened explicitly afterwards. Reading an inmate back returns the current `account_balance_cents`, the read-time `indigent_flag`, the read-time `open_sick_call_count` (the number of that inmate's sick-call requests whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `TRIAGED` or `SCHEDULED`), the read-time `no_show_count` (the number of transport orders across all of that inmate's referrals whose status is `NO_SHOW`) and the `version` a medication-order writer will need.

Opening a screening is done by an `IntakeOfficer` or a `Nurse` and takes a single field, `screening_type`, which is either `RECEIVING` or `FULL_ASSESSMENT`. The screening is created with `status` `PENDING` and a `due_at` that is computed exactly once, at creation, from the parent inmate alone and is **never** recomputed afterwards no matter what changes later. The computation forks on both the instrument and the admission route, and this is the intake clock's central subtlety. For a `RECEIVING` screening on a `NEW_BOOKING` patient, `due_at` is `booked_at` plus 12 hours. For a `RECEIVING` screening on an `INTER_FACILITY_TRANSFER` patient the clock also restarts, but on a materially shorter fuse: `booked_at` plus 4 hours, because a transferred patient arrives with unknown en-route care. For a `FULL_ASSESSMENT`, a `NEW_BOOKING` patient — or any patient whose `prior_full_assessment_at` is null, regardless of admission type — gets `booked_at` plus 14 days. But an `INTER_FACILITY_TRANSFER` patient who arrives with a documented `prior_full_assessment_at` keeps the sending facility's clock rather than restarting it: their `due_at` is `prior_full_assessment_at` plus 14 days. Two patients booked at the very same instant can therefore hold very different full-assessment deadlines, and the transferred patient's may already be in the past.

The overdue determination is strict. Whenever a `HealthServicesAdmin` advances the logical clock, the first sweep step moves every `PENDING` screening whose `due_at` falls strictly before the new clock instant into `OVERDUE`; a screening whose `due_at` equals the new instant exactly is still on time. The transition never recomputes `due_at`. `OVERDUE` is not a terminal state — an overdue screening can still be disposed of.

A `Nurse` disposes of a screening with a `disposition` of either `COMPLETED` or `REFUSED`, and either disposition is legal from `PENDING` or from `OVERDUE`; from any other state the request fails at rung 9 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). A `COMPLETED` disposition additionally requires `findings_code` in the body — one of `NO_FINDINGS`, `CHRONIC_CONDITION`, `ACUTE_NEED` or `MENTAL_HEALTH_REFERRAL` — and omitting it on a completion is a missing-required-field failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) at rung 8. Completing sets `completed_at` to the current clock instant and then sets `completed_late` by comparing the two: true when `completed_at` is strictly after `due_at`, false when `completed_at` is at or before `due_at`. When the completed instrument was the `FULL_ASSESSMENT`, the parent inmate's `prior_full_assessment_at` is set to that same `completed_at`, so a full assessment done here becomes the anchor a subsequent transfer would inherit. Refusing, by contrast, leaves `completed_at` null and `completed_late` false. Both dispositions increment the screening's `version` by 1, and both require the parent inmate's record to be open.

An overdue receiving screening is not merely a reporting artefact — it is a compliance gate on downstream clinical ordering. While an inmate holds any intake screening whose `screening_type` is `RECEIVING` and whose status is `OVERDUE`, three operations are refused with `ERR_INTAKE_SCREENING_OVERDUE` (422) at rung 11: writing a medication order, requesting a specialty referral, and enrolling the patient in chronic care. Disposing of the overdue screening clears the gate. Screenings can be listed for one inmate with optional `screening_type` and `status` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Chronic-Care Enrollment, Periodicity and the Lapse Cycle

Chronic disease management is where the platform spends most of its clinical attention, and a `Provider` opens it by enrolling a patient against a `condition_code` — `DIABETES`, `HYPERTENSION`, `ASTHMA`, `SEIZURE_DISORDER` or `HIV` — with a clinical `severity` of `MILD`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`. Severity is not stored as the operative value; it is mapped immediately onto a management `tier`, and the tier onto a visit interval. A `SEVERE` patient is `TIER_1`, a `MODERATE` patient is `TIER_2`, and a `MILD` patient is `TIER_3`. The `periodicity_days` that follows is 30 days for `TIER_1`, 90 days for `TIER_2` and 180 days for `TIER_3` — the sicker the patient, the shorter the leash. Enrollment stamps `enrolled_at` with the current clock instant, leaves `last_visit_at` null, sets `missed_visit_count` to 0 and `escalated` to false, and initializes `status` to `ACTIVE`.

The `next_due_date` follows a single rule everywhere it is computed: take the enrollment's `last_visit_at` if it is not null, otherwise take `enrolled_at`, and add `periodicity_days` days to it. At the moment of first enrollment that anchor is necessarily `enrolled_at`. Only one `ACTIVE` enrollment may exist for a given inmate and condition at a time; a second request for the same `(inmate_id, condition_code)` pair while one is still active is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409). Enrolment is custody-gated and intake-gated: the patient must be `IN_CUSTODY` and must not be carrying an overdue receiving screening.

A `Nurse` records the result of each scheduled visit, supplying an `outcome` of `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `REFUSED`. Every such call creates one visit row against the parent enrollment. That row is a permanent audit artefact and carries only an `id` and a `created_at` — no `updated_at` and no `version` — together with its `enrollment_id`, a `recorded_at` stamped from the current clock instant, the supplied `outcome`, a `recorded_by_user_id` copied from the acting `X-User-ID`, and a `due_at_snapshot` that captures the enrollment's `next_due_date` exactly as it stood at the instant of recording, before any of the outcome's own effects are applied. Visits may only be recorded while the enrollment is `ACTIVE` and the parent inmate's record is `OPEN`, and each recording increments the enrollment's `version` by 1.

The three outcomes diverge sharply. A `COMPLETED` visit sets `last_visit_at` to the current clock instant, resets `missed_visit_count` to 0, and recomputes `next_due_date` from the freshly-set `last_visit_at` plus `periodicity_days`. A `MISSED` visit increments `missed_visit_count` by 1 and deliberately does **not** move `next_due_date` — the patient is not granted a fresh interval for failing to attend. A `REFUSED` visit changes no counter at all and likewise does not move `next_due_date`; a documented refusal is neither adherence nor neglect.

Missed visits escalate. When a `MISSED` outcome brings `missed_visit_count` to 2 or more and `escalated` is still false, the enrollment escalates one tier step toward the most intensive tier: `TIER_3` becomes `TIER_2`, `TIER_2` becomes `TIER_1`, and a patient already at `TIER_1` stays at `TIER_1`. The `periodicity_days` is then reset from the new tier, `escalated` is set true so the escalation fires once and only once, and `next_due_date` is recomputed as `last_visit_at` — or `enrolled_at` when no visit has ever completed — plus the *new*, shorter `periodicity_days`. A second missed visit at exactly two is the trigger; the threshold is met at two, not exceeded at three.

When the clock advances past an enrollment's tolerance, the enrollment lapses. The second sweep step, which runs only after the intake-screening sweep has completed, moves every `ACTIVE` enrollment whose `next_due_date` plus a 15-day grace period falls strictly before the new clock instant into `LAPSED`. Equality is still active: an enrollment sitting exactly 15 days past its due date at the moment the clock lands there survives.

Lapsing is recoverable, and this is the cycle edge in the chronic-care machine. A `Provider` re-enrolls a `LAPSED` enrollment by supplying a fresh `severity`, provided the patient is still `IN_CUSTODY` and their record is `OPEN`. Re-enrollment returns the status to `ACTIVE` and re-anchors everything: `enrolled_at` is reset to the current clock instant, `last_visit_at` is cleared back to null, `missed_visit_count` returns to 0 and `escalated` returns to false, `tier` and `periodicity_days` are recomputed from the newly supplied severity by the same severity-to-tier mapping, and `next_due_date` is recomputed from the re-anchored `enrolled_at`. The enrollment's `version` increments by 1. An enrollment can therefore cycle `ACTIVE` → `LAPSED` → `ACTIVE` indefinitely.

The terminal state is `RESOLVED`, and it is only ever reached by cascade. When an inmate is released, every enrollment of theirs that is `ACTIVE` becomes `RESOLVED`; when an inmate dies, every enrollment that is either `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` becomes `RESOLVED`. In both cases `next_due_date` is set to null and the enrollment's `version` increments by 1. Enrollments can be listed for one inmate with optional `status` and `condition_code` filters, read individually with their derived `tier`, `periodicity_days` and `next_due_date`, and their visits listed with an optional `outcome` filter.

### 3.3. Sick Call: Slip, Triage, Re-Triage and Encounter Disposition

Sick call is the patient's own door into the clinic. An `Inmate` submits their own slip, and a `Nurse` may submit one on a patient's behalf; either way the payload names the `inmate_id`, carries the free-text `complaint_text` written on the slip, and may carry an `encounter_type` that defaults to `SICK_CALL` when omitted. The permitted encounter classifications on a slip are `SICK_CALL`, `NURSE_TRIAGE`, `PROVIDER_VISIT`, `DENTAL`, `MENTAL_HEALTH`, `EMERGENCY`, `COMMUNICABLE_DISEASE`, `CHRONIC_FOLLOWUP`, `INTAKE_SCREENING` and `STAFF_INITIATED`; the classification chosen here is what the co-pay engine will later read. Submission stamps `submitted_at` with the current clock instant, sets `status` to `SUBMITTED`, and leaves `acuity`, `triaged_at`, `deadline_anchor_at`, `response_deadline_at`, `seen_at` and `copay_charge_id` all null with `retriage_count` at 0. The patient must be `IN_CUSTODY` and their record `OPEN`. Because `open_sick_call_count` on the inmate is a read-time projection, submitting a slip changes what subsequent inmate reads report without advancing the inmate's `version`.

A `Nurse` triages the slip by supplying an `acuity` of `EMERGENT`, `URGENT` or `ROUTINE`, and this is where the response clock is set. The first triage moves the request from `SUBMITTED` to `TRIAGED`, stamps `triaged_at` with the current clock instant, pins `deadline_anchor_at` to the request's own `submitted_at`, and leaves `retriage_count` at 0. The `response_deadline_at` is then the anchor plus the acuity's service-level allowance: one hour for `EMERGENT`, twenty-four hours for `URGENT`, and seventy-two hours for `ROUTINE`.

Re-triage is a self-loop — the request goes from `TRIAGED` back to `TRIAGED` — and it is the most consequential fork in this subsection. A re-triage is accepted only while `retriage_count` is strictly less than 2; a third attempt is rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_MAX_RETRIAGE_EXCEEDED` (409). The submitted `acuity` must differ from the one the request already carries; re-triaging to the identical acuity is a payload failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) at rung 9. When accepted, the re-triage increments `retriage_count` by 1, resets `triaged_at` to the current clock instant, and stores the new `acuity`. The anchor, however, depends entirely on the direction of the change, where urgency ranks `EMERGENT` as most urgent, then `URGENT`, then `ROUTINE` as least urgent:

- **An upgrade** — a move to a strictly more urgent acuity — re-pins `deadline_anchor_at` to the **original** `submitted_at`. Time already elapsed since the slip was written is not forgiven, so the recomputed `response_deadline_at` can and often does land in the past: a request submitted eight hours ago and upgraded from `ROUTINE` to `EMERGENT` acquires a deadline one hour after submission, which is seven hours overdue the moment it is written.
- **A downgrade** — a move to a strictly less urgent acuity — re-pins `deadline_anchor_at` to the **re-triage instant**, that is, the current clock. Elapsed time is forgiven, and the clock starts again from now.

In both cases `response_deadline_at` is the newly-pinned anchor plus the new acuity's allowance from the same one/twenty-four/seventy-two-hour table. Identical inputs under the two directions produce different deadlines, which is the whole point of distinguishing them.

Whether the response deadline was met is never stored. The `sla_breached` value on any read is derived on the spot: a `CANCELLED` request, or one whose `response_deadline_at` is still null because it was never triaged, always reads false. Otherwise the resolution instant is the request's `seen_at` when the patient has been seen, and the current clock instant when they have not, and the request is breached exactly when that resolution instant falls strictly after `response_deadline_at`. Equality is not a breach. Because `seen_at` is frozen at the moment the patient is seen and never changes afterwards, a request that was seen inside its window never becomes breached later no matter how far the clock advances.

Booking the triaged request into a clinic slot moves it from `TRIAGED` to `SCHEDULED` and takes no payload at all. This step explicitly does **not** recompute `response_deadline_at` — scheduling a patient is not the same as seeing them, and the SLA continues to run.

When a `Provider` records that the patient was seen, the request moves from `SCHEDULED` to `SEEN`, `seen_at` is stamped from the clock — freezing the SLA resolution instant — and the encounter bills. Exactly one co-pay charge is created, with its `encounter_type` copied from the sick-call request, its `source_sick_call_id` pointing back at this request, its `amount_cents`, `scheduled_amount_cents` and `charge_variant` produced by the co-pay engine of §3.7, and its `indigency_snapshot` taken from the indigency determination as it stood **immediately before** this charge posted. The new charge's identifier is written back onto the request as `copay_charge_id`. What follows depends on the variant: a `FUNDED` charge appends one ledger entry of type `CHARGE` whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the charge amount; an `INDIGENT` charge appends one ledger entry of type `LIEN`, again negated; and an `EXEMPT` charge appends nothing at all, because the charge is created already `WAIVED` at zero cents. In the first two cases the inmate's `account_balance_cents` is recomputed and `indigent_flag` re-derived, and the inmate's `version` increments by 1; in the exempt case the inmate's `version` is left completely untouched. The request's own `version` increments by 1 regardless.

Closing a seen encounter moves it from `SEEN` to `CLOSED` and increments the request's `version` by 1; it takes no payload and posts nothing. Note that the freeze gate is not consulted on this final step, whose only precondition is that the request is `SEEN`.

The remaining terminal state is `CANCELLED`, reached only by cascade. When an inmate is released or dies, every sick-call request of theirs whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `TRIAGED` or `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED` and its `version` increments by 1. No co-pay charge is ever created by that cancellation — a patient is not billed for an encounter the institution ended. Sick-call requests can be listed with optional `inmate_id`, `copay_charge_id`, `status` and `acuity` filters and read individually, and every such read evaluates `sla_breached` afresh.

### 3.4. Medication Orders, the Medication Administration Record and Adherence

Prescribing is the most tightly guarded write in the system, and it is the reason optimistic concurrency exists here at all. A `Provider` writes an order naming the `inmate_id`, the product `drug_name`, a formulary `drug_class` of `PSYCHOTROPIC`, `CONTROLLED`, `CHRONIC_MAINTENANCE` or `ACUTE_SHORT_COURSE`, a `route_mode` of `DOT` for directly-observed therapy or `KOP` for keep-on-person, a `doses_per_day` integer that must fall between 1 and 4 inclusive, a `start_date` and an `end_date` that must not precede the `start_date`, and the mandatory `expected_inmate_version` described in §2.7. An out-of-range `doses_per_day` or an `end_date` earlier than `start_date` is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).

The order in which the create's remaining work happens is fixed, because two of the steps interact. First the version check runs, rejecting a stale writer with `ERR_STALE_INMATE_VERSION` (412). Then the **supersede step**: any existing `ACTIVE` order for the same inmate with the same `drug_name` is transitioned to `DISCONTINUED`, which in turn voids that order's remaining scheduled doses exactly as an explicit discontinuation would. Only after the supersede has freed the calendar does the **overlap check** run. Two orders collide when they belong to the same inmate, share a `drug_class`, carry *different* `drug_name` values, the existing one is `ACTIVE`, and their calendar-date spans intersect — where each order's span runs from its `start_date` calendar date to its `end_date` calendar date as a **closed** interval on both ends. Two orders that share exactly one endpoint date, so that the new order begins on the day the existing order ends, therefore **do** collide; starting one day later does not. A collision is rejected with `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). Same-drug orders never reach this check, because the supersede rule has already retired the incumbent.

A surviving create initializes `status` to `ACTIVE`, sets `prescriber_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, zeroes `units_dispensed`, `units_returned`, `consecutive_missed_count` and `consecutive_refused_count`, sets `adherence_pct` to 0.00, and sets both `escalated_to_prescriber` and `forced_dot` to false. The inmate's `version` increments by 1, which means every successful order write invalidates any other client's cached inmate version. Order writing is custody-gated, intake-gated and freeze-gated. Orders can be listed with optional `inmate_id`, `status`, `route_mode` and `drug_class` filters and read individually with their live `adherence_pct`, consecutive counters and escalation flag.

Holding and resuming an order is a single operation driven by a boolean `held`. Passing true moves an `ACTIVE` order to `HELD`; passing false moves a `HELD` order back to `ACTIVE`, and the resume additionally requires the patient to be `IN_CUSTODY`. A hold is a pure gate and nothing more: doses already scheduled in the future stay `SCHEDULED` and are simply blocked from being acted on while the hold is in force, `adherence_pct` is not recomputed in either direction, and `consecutive_missed_count` and `consecutive_refused_count` are preserved across the hold with no reset. This is the compensating pair the dose-count invariant of §2.9 constrains. Each direction increments the order's `version` by 1.

Discontinuing an order moves it from `ACTIVE` or `HELD` to `DISCONTINUED`. Every administration under it whose outcome is still `SCHEDULED` becomes `VOIDED`; because those doses can no longer be acted on, `consecutive_missed_count` and `consecutive_refused_count` are reset to 0, `escalated_to_prescriber` is set back to false, and `adherence_pct` is recomputed over the now-reduced denominator with the voided rows excluded. The order's `version` increments by 1. The fourth order state, `COMPLETED`, is never reached by an explicit call — it exists only as the release cascade's landing state, described in §3.9.

A `Nurse` builds the medication administration record by scheduling dose rows one at a time against an order, supplying only the planned `scheduled_at` instant. Each row is created with `outcome` `SCHEDULED`, `acted_at` null and `timing_class` null, and carries its parent's identifier as `order_id`. The rows of an order are listed back in `scheduled_at` ascending order with an optional `outcome` filter.

Recording a dose is where the DOT/KOP discipline bites. The call supplies an `outcome` of `ADMINISTERED`, `REFUSED` or `MISSED`, may supply the boolean `observed_swallow` mouth-check result, and may supply the boolean `diversion_observed`, which defaults to false when omitted. The row itself must still be `SCHEDULED`, and the parent order must be `ACTIVE` — a `HELD`, `DISCONTINUED` or `COMPLETED` parent refuses the record with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) even though the dose row is perfectly legal in itself. On a `DOT` order, `observed_swallow` must be present in the body **when and only when** the recorded outcome is `ADMINISTERED`; a `REFUSED` or `MISSED` outcome never requires it, and an administered dose without it is rejected with `ERR_DOT_OBSERVATION_REQUIRED` (422). On a `KOP` order the rule inverts and broadens: `observed_swallow` must be absent from the body for **every** outcome, and supplying it is rejected with `ERR_KOP_OBSERVATION_FORBIDDEN` (422). Finally, the person recording the dose may not be the person who wrote the order: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the order's `prescriber_user_id`, the record is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). A successful record stamps `administered_by_user_id` with the acting user, increments the administration row's own `version` by 1, and increments the parent order's `version` by 1.

The three recordable outcomes carry deliberately divergent effects. `ADMINISTERED` stamps `acted_at` from the current clock, classifies the dose window as described below, and resets **both** consecutive counters to 0. `REFUSED` stamps `acted_at`, leaves `timing_class` null, increments `consecutive_refused_count` by 1 and leaves `consecutive_missed_count` untouched. `MISSED` stamps `acted_at`, forces `timing_class` to `MISSED_WINDOW` without consulting the window arithmetic at all, increments `consecutive_missed_count` by 1 and leaves `consecutive_refused_count` untouched. On either of the two failure outcomes, an incremented counter that reaches 3 or more sets `escalated_to_prescriber` to true. All three outcomes recompute `adherence_pct`. The fourth outcome, `VOIDED`, is never recorded by a nurse; it arrives only by cascade when the parent order is discontinued or completed, and it stamps `acted_at` from the clock, leaves `timing_class` null, and excludes the row from every adherence denominator thereafter.

The dose window is classified from the signed whole-minute difference between `acted_at` and `scheduled_at`, floored toward negative infinity. A dose given more than 30 minutes early — that is, a difference below minus thirty — is `MISSED_WINDOW`, but a dose given exactly 30 minutes early is still `ON_TIME`; one minute earlier than that, at minus thirty-one, is `MISSED_WINDOW`. From there up to and including 60 minutes late the dose is `ON_TIME`, so a difference of exactly 60 counts as on time and 61 does not. From 61 minutes through and including 120 minutes late the dose is `LATE`, so exactly 120 is `LATE` and 121 is `MISSED_WINDOW`. Anything beyond 120 minutes late is `MISSED_WINDOW`.

Adherence is computed by two entirely different formulas selected by the order's `route_mode`, and the two are not interchangeable.

- **The directly-observed variant** counts events. The denominator is the number of administration rows under the order whose outcome is `ADMINISTERED`, `REFUSED` or `MISSED`; rows still `SCHEDULED` and rows `VOIDED` are excluded from both numerator and denominator. If that denominator is zero, `adherence_pct` is exactly 0.00. Otherwise the numerator is the number of those rows whose outcome is `ADMINISTERED` **and** whose `timing_class` is either `ON_TIME` or `LATE` — an administered dose classified `MISSED_WINDOW` counts against the order in the denominator while contributing nothing to the numerator. The percentage is one hundred times the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.
- **The keep-on-person variant** counts pills against time. The last day of the measured span is the earlier of the current clock's calendar date and the order's `end_date` calendar date. If that last day precedes the order's `start_date` calendar date, the expected unit count is zero. Otherwise the expected unit count is `doses_per_day` multiplied by the number of whole days from `start_date`'s calendar date to that last day, **plus one**, because the span is inclusive of both endpoints — a one-day order at two doses per day expects two units, not zero. With an expected count of zero, `adherence_pct` is exactly 0.00. Otherwise consumption is `units_dispensed` minus `units_returned`, the raw percentage is one hundred times consumption divided by the expected count, and the result is clamped into the range 0 through 100 inclusive before being rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Identical dosing histories therefore yield different `adherence_pct` values under the two variants, which is exactly why the variant is pinned to the order rather than chosen per computation. A `Nurse` feeds the keep-on-person variant by recording pill counts, supplying `units_dispensed_delta` and `units_returned_delta`, which are added to the order's running `units_dispensed` and `units_returned` totals before adherence is recomputed and the order's `version` incremented by 1.

**Cascade C** connects the two variants. When a dose is recorded as `ADMINISTERED` with `diversion_observed` true on an order whose `route_mode` is `KOP`, the order's `route_mode` is forced to `DOT` and `forced_dot` is set true at level two of the cascade; and because the route mode has changed, at level three `adherence_pct` is recomputed using the directly-observed variant instead of the keep-on-person variant. A patient caught cheeking their medication loses self-administration privileges and is re-scored on the stricter formula from that moment on.

Finally, a `HealthServicesAdmin` can pull a facility-level adherence index for a `facility_code`. The candidate set is every medication order whose owning inmate's `facility_code` matches the requested code and whose status is `ACTIVE` or `HELD`, taken in `created_at` ascending order; discontinued and completed orders are excluded entirely. At least 1 candidate order is required to produce a real index; when the candidate count falls below that — that is, when the set is empty — the index is exactly 0.00 and the call still succeeds, since an empty facility is not an error. Otherwise the rounding order is pinned and must not be optimized away: each order's `adherence_pct` is **already** rounded to 2 decimal places by the per-order formula above, those already-rounded values are summed, the sum is divided by the number of candidate orders, and the quotient is rounded half up to 2 decimal places one further time. Pooling raw numerators and denominators across orders and rounding only at the end produces a different number and is wrong. The response body is a single object carrying the `facility_code` that was requested, the `order_count` of candidate orders, and the resulting `adherence_index`.

### 3.5. Off-Site Specialty Care and the Utilization-Review Gate

Care that cannot be delivered inside the walls begins with a `Provider` requesting a referral, naming the `inmate_id`, the `specialty` — `CARDIOLOGY`, `ONCOLOGY`, `ORTHOPEDICS`, `OPHTHALMOLOGY` or `DIALYSIS` — and an `urgency` of either `EMERGENT` or `ROUTINE`. The referral is created with `requested_at` stamped from the clock, `status` `REQUESTED`, `no_show_count` 0, `authorized_at` null and `version` 1. Requesting a referral is custody-gated, intake-gated and freeze-gated. Referrals can be listed with optional `inmate_id`, `status` and `urgency` filters, and read individually — that individual read is how a `CustodyOfficer` obtains the `version` the transport create will need.

Submitting a referral for review moves it from `REQUESTED` to `PENDING_UR` and creates exactly one utilization review as the response body, so this call returns 201 rather than 200. The optional boolean `retrospective` on the submission, defaulting to false, records that the care was already delivered before the review opened. The review is created with `status` `OPEN`, `referral_id` pointing at its parent, `opened_at` stamped from the clock, `decision`, `decided_at`, `decided_by_user_id` and `peer_reviewer_user_id` all null, and `defer_count` 0. The referral's `version` increments by 1.

The review's `turnaround_deadline_at` forks on the retrospective flag. A **prospective** review — `retrospective` false — takes its allowance from the parent referral's urgency: `opened_at` plus 4 hours for an `EMERGENT` referral, `opened_at` plus 120 hours for a `ROUTINE` one. A **retrospective** review takes a flat allowance of 720 hours from `opened_at` regardless of urgency, so an emergent case reviewed after the fact carries a deadline 716 hours later than the identical case reviewed before. Whether that deadline was met is derived at read time and never stored: `turnaround_breached` compares a reference instant — the review's `decided_at` when a decision has been recorded, otherwise the current clock — against `turnaround_deadline_at`, and is true only when the reference falls strictly after the deadline. Equality is not a breach.

A `UtilizationReviewer` records a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `DEFER`, and this three-way fan-out is the heart of the machine. All three branches require the review to be `OPEN`, the parent referral to be `PENDING_UR` and the inmate's record to be `OPEN`, and all three set `decision` to the supplied value, `decided_at` to the current clock instant and `decided_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, then increment the review's `version` by 1. Beyond that they diverge completely:

- **`APPROVE`** moves the review from `OPEN` to `APPROVED` and authorizes the care: the parent referral moves from `PENDING_UR` to `AUTHORIZED`, its `authorized_at` is stamped from the clock, and its `version` increments by 1.
- **`DENY`** moves the review from `OPEN` to `DENIED` and — importantly — leaves the parent referral sitting in `PENDING_UR`. A denial is not final until peer review has resolved it, so the referral is not yet denied.
- **`DEFER`** moves the review from `OPEN` to `DEFERRED`, increments `defer_count` by 1, and extends `turnaround_deadline_at` by a further 72 hours measured from its own previous value rather than from the clock. The parent referral again stays `PENDING_UR`. A deferral is accepted only while `defer_count` is strictly less than 1 — that is, only the very first deferral is permitted — and a second attempt is rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_MAX_DEFERRALS_EXCEEDED` (409).

A `DEFERRED` review is reopened by a `Provider` supplying the missing clinical justification as `information_text`. This is the cycle edge: the review returns from `DEFERRED` to `OPEN`, `decision` and `decided_at` are cleared back to null, and `turnaround_deadline_at` retains the extended value the deferral gave it rather than reverting. `decided_by_user_id` is deliberately **retained** through the reopening, because it is the value the segregation-of-duties comparison will later need. The review's `version` increments by 1.

A denial can be challenged. A `Provider` escalates a `DENIED` review to `PEER_REVIEW` within a closed 48-hour window measured from the review's `decided_at`: escalation at exactly 48 hours after the denial is still inside the window, and one second later is outside and is rejected with `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). The escalation takes no payload and increments the review's `version` by 1.

Resolution belongs to a `PeerReviewer`, who supplies a `peer_decision` of either `OVERTURN` or `UPHOLD` on a review in `PEER_REVIEW`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the review's `decided_by_user_id`; a reviewer resolving their own denial is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Both branches set `peer_reviewer_user_id` to the acting user and increment the review's `version` by 1, and both then move the parent referral out of `PENDING_UR`: `OVERTURN` moves the review to `OVERTURNED` and the referral from `PENDING_UR` to `AUTHORIZED` with `authorized_at` stamped and its `version` incremented; `UPHOLD` moves the review to `UPHELD` and the referral from `PENDING_UR` to `DENIED`, again incrementing the referral's `version`. `APPROVED`, `OVERTURNED` and `UPHELD` are the review's terminal states; `DENIED` and `DEFERRED` are way stations. The reviews attached to a referral are listed in `opened_at` ascending order with an optional `status` filter.

An `AUTHORIZED` referral is the only kind a trip can be built against, and its life from that point is a loop rather than a line: it moves to `SCHEDULED` when an escort is assigned, back to `AUTHORIZED` whenever that trip is cancelled or no-showed so a fresh trip can be built, and on to `COMPLETED` when a trip actually happens. It reaches `CANCELLED` either because the no-show ceiling was hit or because the patient died. The mechanics of all four of those edges live in §3.6.

### 3.6. Custody Transport: Escort, Lead Time, Slots and Outcomes

Moving a patient outside the perimeter is a security operation, and a `CustodyOfficer` owns every step of it. Building a trip requires the `referral_id` of the parent referral, the `appointment_at` instant of the off-site appointment, and the `expected_referral_version` described in §2.7. The parent referral must be `AUTHORIZED`; a referral in any other status refuses the create at rung 10 with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (409).

Two derived values are frozen onto the new trip. The `required_escort_level` comes straight from the patient's `custody_level` as it stands at creation time: a `MINIMUM` custody patient requires `ESCORT_1`, a `MEDIUM` custody patient requires `ESCORT_2`, and a `MAXIMUM` custody patient requires `ESCORT_2_ARMED`. The `lead_minutes` is the number of whole minutes from the current clock instant to `appointment_at`, floor-truncated.

The lead-time rule that `lead_minutes` is then tested against is built from three independent inputs — the referral's `urgency`, the escort level just derived, and the referral's accumulated `no_show_count` — and is deliberately not a simple sum. Start with an **escort-and-history component**: the escort surcharge, which is 0 minutes for `ESCORT_1`, 120 minutes for `ESCORT_2` and 480 minutes for `ESCORT_2_ARMED`, plus 720 minutes for each prior no-show on that referral. Cap that component at the routine floor of 2880 minutes by taking whichever of the two is smaller. Then take whichever is **larger** of that capped component and the urgency floor, which is 60 minutes for an `EMERGENT` referral and 2880 minutes for a `ROUTINE` one. The result is the required lead in minutes. The trip is accepted when `lead_minutes` is greater than or equal to that requirement — **exact equality is accepted**, since the requirement is a floor the appointment may sit precisely on. A shorter lead is rejected at rung 13 with `ERR_TRANSPORT_LEAD_TIME_TOO_SHORT` (422). A routine referral therefore always demands a full 2880 minutes of notice regardless of escort or history, while an emergent one can be moved on as little as an hour when the patient travels light.

Before the calendar is consulted, the supersede step runs: any existing trip on this same referral whose status is `DRAFT` is transitioned to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `CLINICAL_CHANGE`, which frees the slot that draft was holding so the new trip does not collide with the thing it replaces. Only then does the overlap check run. Every trip occupies a **half-open** 240-minute interval on the patient — beginning at its `appointment_at`, inclusive, and running to 240 minutes later, exclusive. Occupancy is counted across **all** referrals of the same inmate, not merely the one being booked, and only trips whose status is `DRAFT`, `ESCORT_ASSIGNED` or `DISPATCHED` occupy anything; a `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` trip releases its slot immediately. Two trips conflict when the new appointment falls strictly before the end of an occupied slot and that occupied appointment falls strictly before the end of the new slot. Because the interval is half-open, a trip beginning exactly 240 minutes after another begins does **not** conflict — adjacency is legal — while one beginning a single minute earlier does. A conflict is rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). Note the deliberate contrast with medication orders in §3.4, whose closed date spans make the analogous adjacency a collision; the two rules give opposite verdicts on the touching case, and both are intentional.

A surviving create initializes the trip with `status` `DRAFT`, `assigned_escort_level` null and `cancel_reason` null, and increments the parent referral's `version` by 1. Trips attached to a referral are listed in `created_at` ascending order with an optional `status` filter.

Assigning the escort detail supplies an `escort_level`, and it must equal the trip's `required_escort_level` **exactly** — not merely meet or exceed it. Assigning `ESCORT_2_ARMED` to a trip that requires `ESCORT_2` is as much a failure as assigning `ESCORT_1`, and both are rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_ESCORT_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (422). The assignment is also refused when the parent referral is no longer `AUTHORIZED`, with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (409). On success the trip moves from `DRAFT` to `ESCORT_ASSIGNED`, `assigned_escort_level` is stored, the parent referral moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `SCHEDULED` with its `version` incremented by 1, and the trip's own `version` increments by 1.

Dispatching moves the trip from `ESCORT_ASSIGNED` to `DISPATCHED` and takes no payload; it is one of the six custody-gated operations, so a patient who is no longer `IN_CUSTODY` blocks it with `ERR_INMATE_NOT_IN_CUSTODY` (409). The trip's `version` increments by 1.

Closing out a dispatched trip supplies an `outcome` of either `COMPLETED` or `NO_SHOW`, and the two branches could hardly be more different. A `COMPLETED` outcome moves the trip to `COMPLETED` and the parent referral from `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED`, which is the event that bills the off-site encounter: one co-pay charge with `encounter_type` `SPECIALTY_OFFSITE` is posted for the patient under exactly the same three-variant rules as a sick-call encounter, with its matching ledger entry appended unless the variant is exempt, and the inmate's `version` incremented by 1 when an entry was appended. The referral's `version` increments by 1 and the trip's `version` increments by 1.

A `NO_SHOW` outcome opens **Cascade D**. At level one the trip's status becomes `NO_SHOW`. At level two, because a no-show was recorded, the parent referral returns from `SCHEDULED` to `AUTHORIZED` and its `no_show_count` is recomputed as the number of trips under that referral whose status is `NO_SHOW`; the referral's `version` increments. At level three, if that recomputed `no_show_count` has reached 2, the referral moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `CANCELLED`, and every trip still under it whose status is `DRAFT`, `ESCORT_ASSIGNED` or `DISPATCHED` is cancelled with `cancel_reason` `CLINICAL_CHANGE`. The cap needs no validation rule of its own: once the referral is cancelled, the rung-10 authorization check refuses every subsequent trip against it. Below the cap, the referral simply returns to `AUTHORIZED` and a fresh trip can be built — with a lead-time requirement now 720 minutes longer for each no-show accumulated, which is the system's way of making a repeatedly-missed appointment progressively harder to rebook.

Cancelling a trip is legal from `DRAFT`, `ESCORT_ASSIGNED` or `DISPATCHED` and supplies a `cancel_reason` of `FACILITY_LOCKDOWN`, `VENDOR_CANCELLED`, `ESCORT_UNAVAILABLE` or `CLINICAL_CHANGE`. It is permitted only while the current clock instant is at or before `appointment_at` minus 120 minutes; at exactly that cutoff instant the trip is still cancellable, and strictly past it the cancellation is rejected at rung 13 with `ERR_CANCEL_CUTOFF_PASSED` (422). When the parent referral was `SCHEDULED`, cancelling returns it to `AUTHORIZED` — but crucially `no_show_count` is **not** incremented. A cancellation inside the cutoff is an institutional decision, not a patient failure, and it never counts against the patient's rebooking allowance.

### 3.7. The Inmate Trust Account: Co-Pay, Indigency and the Ledger

Correctional co-pay exists to deter frivolous sick call without denying care to patients who genuinely cannot pay, and the whole of this subsection is the machinery of that compromise. The trust account is an append-only ledger. Each ledger entry names its `inmate_id`, an `entry_type` of `DEPOSIT`, `CHARGE`, `LIEN` or `REVERSAL`, a signed `amount_cents` — deposits positive, charges and liens negative, and a reversal carrying whichever sign undoes the entry it compensates — a `balance_after_cents` running total, an optional free-text `memo` defaulting to the empty string, and a `source_copay_charge_id` that points at the charge that produced the entry or is null for operator deposits. Ledger entries are immutable: like chronic-care visits they carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

The balance is derived, never accumulated in place. Take every ledger entry for the inmate — with no filter on type, no filter on status and no time window whatsoever — sorted by `created_at` ascending, breaking ties between entries that share a `created_at` by the order in which the server appended them inside the request, earliest append first. Walk that list from a running total of zero, adding each entry's `amount_cents` to the running total and writing the running total into that entry's `balance_after_cents`. The inmate's `account_balance_cents` is the final running total. An inmate with no ledger entries at all has an `account_balance_cents` of exactly 0. This is the money invariant of §2.9 stated procedurally.

Indigency is a two-part test and both parts are strict. Take the window that begins 30 days before the current clock instant and ends at the current clock instant, **closed at both ends**, so an entry created exactly 30 days before the clock is still inside it. Collect the `balance_after_cents` values of every ledger entry of this inmate created inside that window, again with no filter on `entry_type`. The **peak** is the largest of those values; when the window contains no entries at all, the inmate's current `account_balance_cents` stands in as the peak. The patient is indigent when their current `account_balance_cents` is **strictly less than** 200 cents **and** the peak is likewise strictly less than 200 cents. A balance of exactly 200 cents is not indigent; 199 cents is. The lookback exists so that a patient who received money recently and spent it cannot present as indigent at the moment of the charge.

Posting a co-pay follows a pinned six-step order, and the order matters because the amount depends on indigency, indigency depends on the balance, and the charge itself changes the balance. The cycle is cut by evaluating indigency against the ledger **as it stands before this charge posts** — never after.

1. **The exemption test runs first, before any balance is read at all.** Six encounter classifications are exempt on public-health and constitutional grounds: `MENTAL_HEALTH`, `EMERGENCY`, `COMMUNICABLE_DISEASE`, `CHRONIC_FOLLOWUP`, `INTAKE_SCREENING` and `STAFF_INITIATED`. For any of these, `scheduled_amount_cents` and `amount_cents` are both 0, `charge_variant` is `EXEMPT`, the charge's `status` is created directly as `WAIVED`, `indigency_snapshot` is recorded as false because indigency was never evaluated, and **no ledger entry is appended**. Processing stops here. An indigent patient at a mental-health encounter therefore yields an `EXEMPT` charge, never an `INDIGENT` one.
2. Otherwise `scheduled_amount_cents` is looked up from the co-pay schedule: 500 cents for `SICK_CALL`, 300 cents for `NURSE_TRIAGE`, 800 cents for `PROVIDER_VISIT`, 1000 cents for `DENTAL`, and 1500 cents for `SPECIALTY_OFFSITE`.
3. `indigency_snapshot` is then set from the indigency determination above, evaluated on the pre-charge ledger.
4. **The funded variant** applies when `indigency_snapshot` is false: `amount_cents` equals `scheduled_amount_cents` in full, `charge_variant` is `FUNDED`, the charge's `status` is `POSTED`, and one ledger entry of type `CHARGE` is appended whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the charge amount.
5. **The indigent variant** applies when `indigency_snapshot` is true: `amount_cents` is 50 percent of `scheduled_amount_cents`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents; `charge_variant` is `INDIGENT`; the charge's `status` is `DEBT_LIEN`; and one ledger entry of type `LIEN` is appended, again with the negated amount. The lien is explicitly allowed to drive the balance negative — care is never refused for insufficient funds, the debt simply follows the patient.
6. Only now is the inmate's `account_balance_cents` recomputed and `indigent_flag` re-derived.

The same encounter classification therefore produces a different `amount_cents`, a different ledger `entry_type` and a different charge `status` depending purely on which side of the 200-cent line the patient sat immediately before the charge, and `indigency_snapshot` preserves that fact on the charge forever after.

A `HealthServicesAdmin` may post an ad-hoc charge outside the sick-call flow, supplying an `encounter_type` and optionally a `source_sick_call_id` linking it back to an encounter. The permitted classifications on a charge are the ten a sick-call slip may carry plus `SPECIALTY_OFFSITE`, which the transport-completion path uses. The same six steps run, the same ledger entry is or is not appended, and the inmate's `version` increments by 1 when an entry was appended and is left untouched when the charge came out exempt. Charges expose `reversal_grievance_id`, null until a granted grievance reverses them. Charges can be listed with optional `inmate_id`, `source_sick_call_id`, `reversal_grievance_id`, `status` and `charge_variant` filters, and read individually with their computed `amount_cents`, `charge_variant` and `indigency_snapshot`.

Money enters the account only one way. A `HealthServicesAdmin` posts an operator deposit supplying `entry_type`, `amount_cents` and an optional `memo`. Only `DEPOSIT` is accepted on this path — `CHARGE`, `LIEN` and `REVERSAL` entries are exclusively system-generated by the co-pay and grievance machinery, and supplying one of them here is rejected at rung 8 with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). The deposit appends the entry, sets its `balance_after_cents` from the running-balance walk, recomputes the inmate's `account_balance_cents`, re-derives `indigent_flag`, and increments the inmate's `version` by 1. Entries can be listed in `created_at` ascending order with optional `inmate_id`, `source_copay_charge_id` and `entry_type` filters, and read individually with their running `balance_after_cents`.

### 3.8. Grievances: The Ladder, the Remand Loop and the Fiscal Remedy

An `Inmate` who believes a charge or a clinical decision was wrong files a grievance, supplying the `grievance_text` statement, the `grieved_event_at` instant of the event complained of, and — for a billing grievance — the `subject_copay_charge_id` of the charge in dispute, which is null for everything else. Filing is bounded: a grievance is accepted only while the current clock instant is at or before `grieved_event_at` plus 15 days, with the boundary instant itself still inside the window. Filing later is rejected at rung 13 with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). A filed grievance starts at `stage` `INFORMAL` and `status` `FILED`, with `stage_entered_at` stamped from the clock, `remand_count` 0 and `outcome` null. Grievance operations are deliberately exempt from the clinical-records freeze — a frozen record is exactly when a patient most needs the grievance channel open.

The response window depends on the rung of the ladder the grievance currently occupies: 5 days at `INFORMAL`, 15 days at `FORMAL`, and 30 days at `APPEAL`, always measured from `stage_entered_at`. The `response_due_at` is recomputed from that table every time the stage or the stage-entry instant changes. Whether the institution blew the window is derived at read time: `response_breached` is true exactly when the grievance's status is `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REMANDED` **and** the current clock instant is strictly after `response_due_at`. Equality is not a breach.

A `GrievanceCoordinator` accepts a grievance for review, moving it from `FILED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`; the same acceptance operation also brings a `REMANDED` grievance back to `UNDER_REVIEW`. Either way `stage_entered_at` is reset to the current clock instant and `response_due_at` recomputed for whatever stage the grievance now sits at — for a remanded appeal, that is the `FORMAL` rung's 15 days. The grievance's `version` increments by 1.

Deciding a grievance supplies an `outcome` of `GRANTED` or `DENIED` and moves it from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DECIDED`. This is **Cascade E**, and it runs five levels when the grievance is a granted billing complaint. Level one records the outcome. Level two applies when the outcome is `GRANTED`, `subject_copay_charge_id` is not null, and that charge's `status` is either `POSTED` or `DEBT_LIEN`: the charge transitions to `REVERSED` and its `reversal_grievance_id` is set to this grievance. Level three follows from the reversal — one ledger entry of type `REVERSAL` is appended whose `amount_cents` is the exact negation of the `amount_cents` of the ledger entry that originally posted that charge, so a 500-cent charge that posted as minus 500 is undone by plus 500. Level four recomputes the inmate's `account_balance_cents` from the full entry walk, and level five re-derives the inmate's `indigent_flag` from the changed balance. The grievance's `version` increments by 1, and the inmate's `version` increments by 1 whenever the reversal actually fired. After the compensating pair has run, the balance must equal its exact pre-charge value with zero drift, per the money invariant. A granted grievance that names a charge already `WAIVED` or already `REVERSED` is refused at rung 14 with `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_REVERSIBLE` (409) — there is nothing left to reverse.

A denied grievance can be escalated by the grievant, and this is the ladder's cycle edge. Escalation is legal only from `DECIDED` with an `outcome` of `DENIED` and only while the stage is `INFORMAL` or `FORMAL`; an appeal has nowhere further to go. It is also time-bounded, against the filing-window table rather than the response-window table: escalation is accepted only while the current clock is at or before the prior decision instant plus the window belonging to the **next** stage — 10 days when escalating into `FORMAL`, 5 days when escalating into `APPEAL` — with the boundary instant itself inside the window. (The 15-day entry in that same table is the one governing the original filing.) Escalating advances the stage one rung, `INFORMAL` to `FORMAL` or `FORMAL` to `APPEAL`, returns the status to `FILED`, clears `outcome` back to null, resets `stage_entered_at` to the current clock instant and recomputes `response_due_at` for the new stage. The grievance's `version` increments by 1. A late escalation is rejected with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422).

The second cycle edge is the remand. A `GrievanceCoordinator` reviewing a grievance that is `UNDER_REVIEW` at stage `APPEAL` may remand it back for re-hearing: the status becomes `REMANDED`, `remand_count` increments by 1 and the stage drops back to `FORMAL`. A remand is accepted only while `remand_count` is strictly less than 1 — one remand per grievance, no more — and a second attempt is rejected at rung 14 with `ERR_MAX_REMANDS_EXCEEDED` (409). The remanded grievance is then re-accepted through the ordinary acceptance path, which restarts the `FORMAL` response window.

Two terminal states close the file. A `GrievanceCoordinator` closes a `DECIDED` grievance, moving it to `CLOSED`. And the grievant themselves may withdraw, which is legal from `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REMANDED`, moves the status to `WITHDRAWN` and sets `outcome` to `WITHDRAWN`; withdrawal is restricted to the inmate who filed it, so an acting `X-User-ID` that does not match the grievance's `inmate_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Both operations increment the grievance's `version` by 1. Grievances can be listed with optional `inmate_id`, `subject_copay_charge_id`, `stage` and `status` filters, and read individually with their stage, `response_due_at` and read-time `response_breached`.

### 3.9. Release Planning, Custody Termination and Mortality Review

Continuity of care at the gate is the single most-litigated failure point in correctional medicine, and the release plan exists to make it auditable. A `HealthServicesAdmin` opens a plan naming a `release_type` of `SCHEDULED` or `UNEXPECTED` and the `planned_release_at` instant. A `SCHEDULED` plan must be opened with at least 7 days of runway: `planned_release_at` must be at or later than the current clock instant plus 7 days, with exactly 7 days ahead **accepted**. A shorter runway is rejected at rung 13 with `ERR_RELEASE_PLAN_LEAD_TIME` (422). An `UNEXPECTED` plan — a court order, a sudden bond posting — is exempt from that rule entirely, which is precisely why the two release types exist. New plans start at `status` `DRAFT` with `records_packet_ready` false.

The bridge supply is sized at once. The eligible medication set is every order for that inmate whose status is `ACTIVE` or `HELD`, taken in `created_at` ascending order; discontinued and completed orders are excluded. Which day-table applies forks on `release_type`. A `SCHEDULED` release uses the generous table — 30 days for `PSYCHOTROPIC`, 7 days for `CONTROLLED`, 30 days for `CHRONIC_MAINTENANCE` and 10 days for `ACUTE_SHORT_COURSE`. An `UNEXPECTED` release uses the truncated table — 7 days for `PSYCHOTROPIC`, **zero** days for `CONTROLLED`, 7 days for `CHRONIC_MAINTENANCE` and 3 days for `ACUTE_SHORT_COURSE`. The same medication list therefore produces materially different totals under the two release types, with controlled substances dispensed on an unexpected release not at all.

For each eligible order in turn, compute how many days of therapy remain on the order itself: the number of whole days from the plan's `planned_release_at` calendar date to the order's `end_date` calendar date, **plus one** because the span is inclusive of both dates, floored at zero so an order that has already expired contributes nothing. The days actually dispensed for that order are the smaller of the table value for its `drug_class` and that remaining-days figure. Each order contributes one line to `bridge_supply_lines` carrying the `order_id`, the `drug_class` and the `days` granted, in the same `created_at` ascending order as the eligible set was taken. The `bridge_supply_total_days` is the sum of the `days` across all lines. Separately, `reentry_referral_required` is set true when the inmate has at least one `ACTIVE` chronic-care enrollment whose `tier` is `TIER_1` or `TIER_2` — the two tiers a community provider must pick up immediately — and false otherwise.

Finalizing a plan moves it from `DRAFT` to `FINALIZED` and recomputes `bridge_supply_lines`, `bridge_supply_total_days` and `reentry_referral_required` against the medication and enrollment lists as they stand at that later moment, so a drug discontinued between drafting and finalization drops out of the packet. The plan's `version` increments by 1. Plans can be listed for one inmate in `created_at` ascending order with an optional `status` filter.

Releasing the patient is the origin of **Cascade B**, and it requires two things: the patient must still be `IN_CUSTODY`, and there must exist a release plan for them whose status is `FINALIZED`. A release attempted with no finalized plan is refused at rung 10 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). On success `custody_status` moves from `IN_CUSTODY` to `RELEASED` while `records_state` is left exactly as it was — a released patient's record does not freeze. At level one that finalized plan transitions to `EXECUTED` and its `records_packet_ready` becomes true. At level two, because the plan executed, every medication order for the patient whose status is `ACTIVE` or `HELD` transitions to `COMPLETED`. At level three, because those orders completed, every administration under them whose outcome is still `SCHEDULED` transitions to `VOIDED`. At level four, because those doses were voided, each affected order has `consecutive_missed_count` and `consecutive_refused_count` reset to 0, `escalated_to_prescriber` set false, and `adherence_pct` recomputed over the surviving denominator with voided rows excluded. Alongside the cascade, every `ACTIVE` chronic-care enrollment transitions to `RESOLVED` with its `next_due_date` set null, and every sick-call request in `SUBMITTED`, `TRIAGED` or `SCHEDULED` transitions to `CANCELLED`. Every row touched increments its own `version` by 1, including the inmate's.

Recording an in-custody death is the origin of **Cascade A**, the deepest in the system, and it returns the newly created mortality review as its response body with status 201. The payload carries the `death_at` instant and a `manner` of `NATURAL`, `ACCIDENT`, `SUICIDE`, `HOMICIDE` or `UNDETERMINED`. The patient must still be `IN_CUSTODY`, and no mortality review may already exist for them — a second death event is refused at rung 9 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). The cascade then runs five levels. Level one sets `custody_status` to `DECEASED`. Level two, because custody became `DECEASED`, flips `records_state` from `OPEN` to `FROZEN` on the same row. Level three, because the record froze, transitions every medication order in `ACTIVE` or `HELD` to `DISCONTINUED` — note that a death discontinues while a release completes, and the two landing states are not interchangeable. Level four, because each of those orders was discontinued, transitions every administration under it whose outcome is `SCHEDULED` to `VOIDED`. Level five, because those doses were voided, resets each affected order's `consecutive_missed_count` and `consecutive_refused_count` to 0, sets `escalated_to_prescriber` false, and recomputes `adherence_pct` over the surviving denominator.

The death event carries four further consequences beyond that spine. Every sick-call request in `SUBMITTED`, `TRIAGED` or `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED`. Every specialty referral in `REQUESTED`, `PENDING_UR`, `AUTHORIZED` or `SCHEDULED` becomes `CANCELLED`, and every transport under each of those referrals whose status is `DRAFT`, `ESCORT_ASSIGNED` or `DISPATCHED` becomes `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `CLINICAL_CHANGE`. Every chronic-care enrollment in `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` becomes `RESOLVED`. Every release plan in `DRAFT` or `FINALIZED` becomes `VOIDED`. And one mortality review is created with `status` `OPEN`, `inmate_id` naming the decedent, the supplied `death_at` and `manner`, `corrective_action_count` 0, an empty `corrective_actions` list, `closed_at` null and `closed_late` false. Its `due_at` is the statutory deadline: `death_at` plus 30 days. The inmate's `version` increments by 1, as does the `version` of every row the cascade touched.

The two records-axis operations stand apart from all of this. A litigation hold moves `records_state` from `OPEN` to `FROZEN` and explicitly leaves `custody_status` untouched — this is the proof that the two axes are independent, since a freeze is entirely legal on a patient who is still in custody. Lifting the hold moves `records_state` from `FROZEN` back to `OPEN` and again leaves `custody_status` alone, but it is refused when the patient is `DECEASED`. A death-induced freeze is therefore permanent and unrecoverable by design. Both operations increment the inmate's `version` by 1, and neither is itself blocked by the freeze gate.

A mortality review is worked and closed by a `HealthServicesAdmin`. Attaching a corrective action supplies `action_text`; the action is appended to the review's `corrective_actions` array as an object carrying that `action_text` and a `recorded_at` stamped from the current clock, the array is kept sorted by `recorded_at` ascending, `corrective_action_count` increments, and the review moves from `OPEN` to `CORRECTIVE_ACTIONS_ISSUED`. The review's `version` increments by 1. Closing moves the review from `CORRECTIVE_ACTIONS_ISSUED` to `CLOSED`, but only when `corrective_action_count` is at least 1; a review with no corrective action attached is refused at rung 9 with `ERR_MORTALITY_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE` (422). Closing stamps `closed_at` from the clock and sets `closed_late` by comparing it to `due_at`: true only when `closed_at` is strictly after `due_at`, false when it lands at or before. A review closed exactly at its statutory deadline is on time. Mortality reviews are listed for one inmate in `created_at` ascending order with an optional `status` filter, and — like grievances — none of these operations is blocked by the records freeze that the death itself imposed.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence return HTTP 201 and carry the created resource in `data`; every other successful call returns HTTP 200 with the modified or requested resource. Three cases are worth calling out because the status follows the resource created rather than the resource addressed: recording a death returns 201 with the new mortality review, submitting a referral for review returns 201 with the new utilization review, and recording a chronic-care visit returns 201 with the new visit row. Attaching a corrective action returns 200 with the updated mortality review, because the review — not the action — is the resource. All paths below are mounted at the server root.
