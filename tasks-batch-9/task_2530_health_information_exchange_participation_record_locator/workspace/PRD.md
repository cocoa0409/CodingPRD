# Health Information Exchange Record Locator API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A statewide health information exchange sits between organizations that do not trust each other by default and patients who never consented to being a data-integration problem. Hospitals, clinics, laboratories, health plans and public health agencies each hold fragments of the same person's record under their own medical record numbers, with their own spelling of the same family name and their own appetite for sharing behavioral-health or reproductive-health data. The exchange's job is to make those fragments findable without making them freely available: to onboard participants under an enforceable legal agreement, to prove technically that each participant can speak the required interoperability profiles, to resolve demographic fragments into a single patient identity with a tunable and auditable algorithm, and to release the resulting record locations only to the extent that statewide consent policy and the individual patient's own directives permit.

This document specifies the API that operates that exchange. The system is deliberately built as an access-control matrix layered over a compliance audit trail: every locator query produces a permanent, per-record accounting of what was disclosed and what was withheld and why, and every emergency override of the consent model is reviewed after the fact by a privacy authority whose findings can sanction the invoking organization all the way into suspension. Four cross-cutting mechanics recur throughout: optimistic concurrency on the two parent entities whose shape other writes depend on, cascading side effects that propagate several entities deep from a single request, calculations that exist in two observably different variants, and rate-and-threshold triggers that fire state transitions without any operator asking for them.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **segregation of duties** is structural, not advisory: the administrator who countersigns a participation agreement may not certify that participant, the privacy officer who activated a matching-threshold generation may not adjudicate the links it scored, and the officer who decides an emergency-access review must be the same person who claimed it and must not be the clinician who invoked it. Second, **the exchange never silently overwrites a picture the caller has not seen** — writes that reshape identity clusters or retire feed generations carry the caller's last observed parent version and are refused outright when that version is stale. Implementers should read §2 as a contract and §3 as the domain narrative from which the behaviour of each endpoint must be reconstructed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The whole API is mounted under the `/hie` prefix. Everything in this section applies to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows it.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user or organization, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If `X-User-Role` is absent or empty the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `HieAdmin`, `ParticipantAdmin`, `Clinician`, `PrivacyOfficer` and `PublicHealthOfficer`. A role string outside that set, or a recognized role that is not listed among the authorized roles for the addressed endpoint, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Several endpoints are **multiplexed action endpoints**: a single path accepts an `action` (or `decision`, or `outcome`) discriminator and drives whichever state transition that value selects. For these, role authorization is evaluated twice — once for the endpoint and once for the specific action value. A caller whose role is permitted on the endpoint but not for the requested action is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The per-action split is as follows: only a `HieAdmin` may drive `EXECUTE_AGREEMENT`, `CERTIFY`, `REMEDIATE`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `TERMINATE` on a participant, and only a `HieAdmin` may `WRITE_OFF` an invoice; only a `ParticipantAdmin` may drive `SUBMIT_FOR_LEGAL_REVIEW`, `RESUME_CONFORMANCE` or `WITHDRAW`; and only a `PrivacyOfficer` may issue a match-link `REOPEN`.

`X-User-ID` means different things for different roles, and the difference is load-bearing:

- When `X-User-Role` is `ParticipantAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Participant` compare the resource's participant foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — the `participant_id` on the addressed patient record, conformance run, data feed or invoice, the `subscriber_participant_id` on the addressed subscription, or the `querying_participant_id` supplied in a locator-query payload.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PublicHealthOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Participant`, and the same ownership comparison applies to `querying_participant_id` and `subscriber_participant_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `HieAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands it — as `agreement_executed_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on `Participant`, as `executed_by_user_id` on `ConformanceTest`, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on `MatchLink` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PrivacyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `activated_by_user_id` on `ConsentPolicy` and `MatchThresholdConfig`, as `tuned_by_user_id` on a drafted threshold generation, as `recorded_by_user_id` on `ConsentDirective`, and as `review_claimed_by_user_id` and `reviewer_user_id` on a break-the-glass session; it is never compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Clinician`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `clinician_user_id` on a locator query and as `invoking_clinician_user_id` on an emergency session, and it participates in the emergency-access cooldown key and in the session-ownership checks described in §3.7; it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

Where the acting role is one of the two entity-backed roles (`ParticipantAdmin`, `PublicHealthOfficer`) and `X-User-ID` does not equal the participant that owns the addressed resource — or does not equal the `querying_participant_id` / `invoking_participant_id` named in the payload — the request fails with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This applies to contributing patient records, scheduling conformance runs, provisioning data feeds, creating notification subscriptions, submitting locator queries, opening emergency sessions, paying invoices, and reading a participant's scorecard.

Purpose assertion is also role-gated. On a record locator query the `purpose_of_use` value must lie inside the set permitted for the caller's role: a `Clinician` may assert `TREATMENT` only; a `ParticipantAdmin` may assert `PAYMENT` or `OPERATIONS` only; a `PublicHealthOfficer` may assert `PUBLIC_HEALTH` or `TREATMENT` only. A permitted role asserting a purpose outside its set is rejected with `ERR_PURPOSE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE` (HTTP 403). `PrivacyOfficer` and `HieAdmin` may not submit locator queries at all, which is already caught as an endpoint-level role failure.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. List results are returned in ascending `created_at` order unless a specific narrative in §3 pins a different ordering for an internal computation. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before pagination) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is less than `total_count`).

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix of any kind — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the logical clock on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, including mutations caused by a cascade the caller did not directly request.

Three resource families are deliberately immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — for them both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response entirely. Those are `RecordLocatorQuery` (an access event is a fact, not a mutable record), `AccessAuditEntry` (the per-record disclosure accounting), and `FeedQualitySample` (a measurement window). Every other entity in the system — `Participant`, `ConformanceTest`, `ConsentPolicy`, `MatchThresholdConfig`, `PatientRecord`, `IdentityCluster`, `MatchLink`, `ConsentDirective`, `BreakGlassAccess`, `NotificationSubscription`, `DataFeed` and `ParticipationInvoice` — carries the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201; every other successful operation, including all state transitions and all reads, returns HTTP 200.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. A single server-wide logical clock supplies every timestamp the server mints and every instant that any interval check, decay window or expiry comparison consults. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability lets a `HieAdmin` set the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant; the response body for that operation is the single field `current_utc` reporting the newly effective clock rather than an entity envelope payload.

Advancing the clock has observable consequences that are evaluated **lazily**: an emergency-access session whose `expires_at` is strictly earlier than the new clock is moved to the `EXPIRED` session state the next time any read or write touches that row, not eagerly at the moment the clock moves. Clock movement also re-windows the data-quality sample selection and the sanction-decay summation described in §3.9 and §3.8.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations reshape a parent entity that other rows depend on, and each of them therefore requires the caller to declare which version of that parent it last observed. In all three cases the version field is a **required, non-optional** member of the request body; endpoints not listed here take no version field at all and must not be given one.

- Recording a consent directive on an identity cluster (`POST /hie/identity-clusters/{id}/consent-directives`) requires `expected_cluster_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `IdentityCluster`. Recording consent is legitimately a create-with-supersede operation — a new directive sharing an incumbent's `(directive_type, scope, scoped_participant_id)` triple displaces that incumbent — so a caller working from a stale cluster version would be superseding a directive it never saw. When `expected_cluster_version` does not equal the cluster's current `version` the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLUSTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Recording a steward decision on a match link (`POST /hie/match-links/{id}/steward-decision`) requires `expected_cluster_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the cluster that currently holds the link's left record. Confirming a link absorbs one cluster into another, so a steward whose view of the cluster predates another confirmation would be merging a membership set that has already changed shape. A mismatch with that cluster's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLUSTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The compensating unlink operation (`POST /hie/match-links/{id}/unlink`) takes the same `expected_cluster_version` field, compared there against the `version` of the cluster the link currently points at, and fails the same way.
- Provisioning a data feed for a participant (`POST /hie/participants/{id}/data-feeds`) requires `expected_participant_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `Participant`. Provisioning is likewise a create-with-supersede operation — a new generation of the same `feed_type` retires the incumbent — so a stale participant version means the caller is about to retire a feed generation it has never observed. A mismatch with the participant's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. For a path such as `/hie/participants/{id}/participation-invoices`, the participant is resolved first, and a participant id that does not exist yields `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND` before any body field is inspected. Each entity family has its own not-found code: `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONFORMANCE_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_THRESHOLD_CONFIG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLUSTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATCH_LINK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIRECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BREAK_GLASS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DATA_FEED_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.

Identifiers supplied in a request **body** are resolved after path identifiers but before business rules, and they use the same codes: an unresolvable `querying_participant_id`, `invoking_participant_id` or `scoped_participant_id` yields `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `cluster_id` yields `ERR_CLUSTER_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `break_glass_id` yields `ERR_BREAK_GLASS_NOT_FOUND`. Body-identifier resolution applies to locator-query submission, emergency-session invocation, directive recording and subscription creation.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision

Three distinct precision regimes coexist and must not be mixed.

**Four decimal places, rounded half up**, governs everything the matching subsystem produces and consumes: `auto_link_threshold`, `review_lower_bound`, `partial_credit_factor`, the six field weights, `match_score`, `top_candidate_score`, a conformance run's `pass_rate`, a quality sample's `reject_rate`, and a data feed's `latest_reject_rate`.

**Two decimal places, rounded half up**, governs the scorecard: the average timeliness figure reported as `average_p95_seconds`, the intermediate `base_quality_score`, and the final `quality_score`.

**Whole cents** governs money. There is no fractional-cent field anywhere in the system: `provisional_monthly_fee_cents`, each entry of `tier_charge_breakdown`, `volume_fee_cents`, `base_platform_fee_cents`, `total_cents`, `amount_paid_cents` and `outstanding_balance_cents` are all integers. Rounding money is half up, and — critically — the *number of times* rounding is applied is part of the specification, not an implementation detail; §3.9 pins exactly where each rounding falls.

Wherever this document says "rounded half up", a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero (upward for the non-negative quantities this system deals in).

### 2.9. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response reports the violation with the lowest precedence number in the ordering below. The ordering is total and observable: a test that trips both a schema fault and a business fault must see the schema fault.

1. `X-User-Role` absent or empty, on any endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` unrecognized, or not authorized for the addressed endpoint, on any endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. On the five multiplexed action endpoints (participant onboarding transitions, match-link steward decision, data-feed status transitions, subscription lifecycle transitions, invoice administrative transitions), the caller's role is not permitted for the specific action value requested — `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE`.
4. An entity-backed caller's `X-User-ID` does not match the owning or named participant — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Applies to `POST /hie/participants/{id}/patient-records`, `POST /hie/participants/{id}/conformance-tests`, `POST /hie/participants/{id}/data-feeds`, `POST /hie/participants/{id}/notification-subscriptions`, `POST /hie/record-locator-queries`, `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses`, `POST /hie/participation-invoices/{id}/payment` and `GET /hie/participants/{id}/scorecard`.
5. `purpose_of_use` outside the caller role's permitted set, on `POST /hie/record-locator-queries` — `ERR_PURPOSE_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE`.
6. Schema faults on any write endpoint — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This covers a missing required field, a wrong JSON type, an enum value outside its declared domain, a numeric field outside its declared range (weights outside zero through one; thresholds outside zero through one; negative counts; an `ssn_last4` that is not exactly four digits; a `phone_digits` value that is not ten digits; a timestamp that is not ISO 8601 UTC), and any interval whose start is not strictly before its end (`period_start` at or after `period_end`, or `effective_from` at or after `effective_until`).
7. A path identifier that resolves to nothing, on any endpoint carrying a path id — the family-specific not-found code from §2.7.
8. A body identifier that resolves to nothing — the family-specific not-found code from §2.7, on locator-query submission, emergency-session invocation, directive recording and subscription creation.
9. A stale `expected_cluster_version` or `expected_participant_version` — `ERR_STALE_CLUSTER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION`, on the four endpoints named in §2.6.
10. The requested action is not an outgoing transition of the entity's current non-terminal state, or a declared precondition of the selected transition that carries no error code of its own is unsatisfied — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, on every action endpoint. The canonical example of the second clause is declaring an invoice delinquent while the logical clock is at or before its `due_date`.
11. The entity is already in a terminal state — `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, on every action endpoint. The terminal sets are: `Participant` in `WITHDRAWN` or `TERMINATED`; `ConformanceTest` in `PASSED` or `WAIVED`; `ConsentPolicy` in `SUPERSEDED`; `MatchThresholdConfig` in `RETIRED`; `MatchLink` in `REJECTED`; `ConsentDirective` in `SUPERSEDED` or `REVOKED`; `DataFeed` in `RETIRED`; `NotificationSubscription` in `TERMINATED`; `ParticipationInvoice` in `PAID`, `WRITTEN_OFF` or `VOID`; and for a `BreakGlassAccess`, a `session_status` of `CLOSED` or `EXPIRED` blocks closure while a `review_status` of `JUSTIFIED` or `UNJUSTIFIED` blocks both review actions.
12. The participant that owns or submits the operation is not `CERTIFIED` — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_CERTIFIED`, on `POST /hie/record-locator-queries`, `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses` and `POST /hie/notification-subscriptions/{id}/lifecycle-transitions`. Invoice issuance (`POST /hie/participants/{id}/participation-invoices`) is the conditional exception: it requires only that `onboarding_status` be one of `CONFORMANCE_TESTING`, `CERTIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, because billing begins once the participation agreement has been executed and continues after suspension. An invoice attempted before that point fails with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ONBOARDED_FOR_DATA`.
13. The querying participant has no `CCDA` data feed in `MONITORED` or `DEGRADED` status — `ERR_QUERY_FEED_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, on `POST /hie/record-locator-queries`.
14. The addressed identity cluster is `MERGED_AWAY` — `ERR_CLUSTER_MERGED_AWAY`, on `POST /hie/identity-clusters/{id}/consent-directives`, `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses` and `POST /hie/participants/{id}/notification-subscriptions`.
15. No consent policy is `ACTIVE` and governing the clock — `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CONSENT_POLICY`, on `POST /hie/record-locator-queries` and `POST /hie/identity-clusters/{id}/consent-directives`.
16. The certifying administrator is the agreement signatory — `ERR_SOD_CERTIFIER_IS_SIGNATORY`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/onboarding-transitions`.
17. A cited emergency session is not usable — `ERR_BREAK_GLASS_SESSION_NOT_OPEN`, on `POST /hie/record-locator-queries` and `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses/{id}/closure`.
18. The adjudicating steward tuned the pinned threshold generation — `ERR_SOD_STEWARD_IS_TUNER`, on `POST /hie/match-links/{id}/steward-decision`.
19. An emergency-access cooldown is still running — `ERR_BREAK_GLASS_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`, on `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses`.
20. A consent policy's effective interval overlaps an incumbent's — `ERR_CONSENT_POLICY_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, on `POST /hie/consent-policies`.
21. Subscription activation without a strictly passed publisher run — `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CONFORMANCE_MISSING`, on `POST /hie/notification-subscriptions/{id}/lifecycle-transitions`.
22. A public-health conduit subscription for a non-public-health organization — `ERR_CONDUIT_ORG_TYPE_INVALID`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/notification-subscriptions`.
23. A justification or unjustified-review note that is too short — `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_TOO_SHORT`, on `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses` and `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses/{id}/review-decision`.
24. An internally inconsistent or overlapping quality sample — `ERR_QUALITY_SAMPLE_INVALID`, on `POST /hie/data-feeds/{id}/quality-samples`.
25. A duplicate `(participant_id, source_mrn)` pair — `ERR_DUPLICATE_SOURCE_MRN`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/patient-records`.
26. A review action by someone other than the claimant, or a claim on an already-claimed review — `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLAIMED_BY_ACTOR`, on `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses/{id}/review-decision` and `POST /hie/break-glass-accesses/{id}/review-claim`.
27. `subscription_scope` and `cluster_id` disagree — `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/notification-subscriptions`.
28. A threshold generation whose weights, bounds or partial-credit factor are inadmissible — `ERR_THRESHOLD_CONFIG_INVALID`, on `POST /hie/match-threshold-configs`.
29. No threshold generation is `ACTIVE` at scoring time — `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_THRESHOLD_CONFIG`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/patient-records` and `POST /hie/record-locator-queries`.
30. A billing period overlapping a non-void invoice — `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/participation-invoices`.
31. A billing period that has not fully elapsed — `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, on the same endpoint.
32. The participant holds a `DELINQUENT` invoice — `ERR_PARTICIPATION_FEE_DELINQUENT` (HTTP 402), on `POST /hie/notification-subscriptions/{id}/lifecycle-transitions` and `POST /hie/participants/{id}/data-feeds`.
33. A payment that does not settle the invoice exactly — `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, on `POST /hie/participation-invoices/{id}/payment`.
34. A reconciled invoice with no qualifying quality samples — `ERR_NO_RECONCILIATION_BASIS`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/participation-invoices`.
35. A patient record contributed by an insufficiently onboarded participant — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ONBOARDED_FOR_DATA`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/patient-records`.
36. Certification attempted with incomplete profile coverage — `ERR_CERTIFICATION_PROFILES_INCOMPLETE`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/onboarding-transitions`.
37. A conformance run scheduled for an already-passed profile — `ERR_CONFORMANCE_PROFILE_ALREADY_PASSED`, on `POST /hie/participants/{id}/conformance-tests`.
38. A directive whose scope, scoped participant or restricted categories are incoherent — `ERR_DIRECTIVE_SCOPE_PARTICIPANT_REQUIRED`, on `POST /hie/identity-clusters/{id}/consent-directives`.

Note the consequence of placing the segregation-of-duties checks at 16 and 18 and the emergency-session check at 17: a certification attempt that is both by the signatory and lacking profile coverage reports the profile-coverage failure only if the coverage rule sits earlier — it does not, so the signatory conflict at 16 wins over the coverage failure at 36. Likewise a steward decision that carries a stale cluster version *and* is submitted by the tuner reports the stale version, because concurrency at 9 precedes the duties conflict at 18.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

A single request in this system routinely mutates four or five entities. Three rules govern all of them. First, **ordering is deterministic**: wherever a cascade iterates over a set of sibling rows, it processes them in ascending `created_at` order, and wherever a cascade touches the two clusters of a merge or an unmerge, it processes the winner first and the loser second. Second, **every mutated row advances**: each entity a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by exactly 1, including entities the caller never named. Third, **membership counters are recomputed, never adjusted**: after any change to cluster membership the four cluster counters (`member_record_count`, `participant_count`, `pending_review_link_count`, `active_directive_count`) and the `primary_record_id` pointer are recomputed from scratch over the current member set, rather than incremented or decremented in place.

The identity index maintains one global invariant that a correct implementation preserves through every merge and every compensating unmerge: the sum of `member_record_count` over all clusters whose `status` is `ACTIVE` always equals the total count of patient records whose `match_state` is `CLUSTERED`. A confirm-then-unlink pair must leave that total identical and must restore every re-pointed record's `cluster_id` from its `prior_cluster_id`.

The fee ledger maintains the parallel fiscal invariant: a participant's `outstanding_balance_cents` always equals the sum of `total_cents` over that participant's invoices whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `DELINQUENT`. Issuing an invoice and then voiding it returns the field to its exact pre-issue value.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Audit Permanence

Three duty separations are enforced as hard preconditions rather than as policy guidance, and each has its own error code: `ERR_SOD_CERTIFIER_IS_SIGNATORY` when the certifying administrator is the same user recorded in `agreement_executed_by_user_id`; `ERR_SOD_STEWARD_IS_TUNER` when the user adjudicating a match link is the same user recorded in the `activated_by_user_id` of the threshold generation pinned to that link; and `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLAIMED_BY_ACTOR` when an emergency-access review decision comes from anyone other than the `review_claimed_by_user_id` who claimed it. A fourth separation is expressed positively rather than as an error: a privacy officer may not claim the review of a session they themselves invoked, since the claim requires `X-User-ID` to differ from `invoking_clinician_user_id`.

Access audit entries are write-once. They are created only as a side effect of a locator query, they are never returned inside a write response, and the audit list and fetch reads are their only discovery channel. The sole permitted mutation of an existing audit entry is the `reviewed` flag being raised when a post-hoc review decision covers it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Participant Onboarding: From Application to Certification

Onboarding begins when a `HieAdmin` registers a candidate organization. The registration payload carries the organization's registered `legal_name` (one to two hundred characters, non-blank), its `org_type` — one of `HOSPITAL`, `CLINIC`, `LAB`, `HEALTH_PLAN` or `PUBLIC_HEALTH` — a self-declared `declared_annual_patient_volume` (at least zero and at most fifty million unique patients per year), and a `primary_contact_email` for the onboarding mailbox, which must contain an `@` character. The new organization is born with `onboarding_status` at `APPLIED`, `version` at 1, and every derived counter at its floor: `sanction_points_active`, `outstanding_balance_cents`, `active_subscription_count`, `conformance_attempt_count`, `consecutive_failed_attempts` and `remediation_count` all zero, and `passed_profiles` an empty list. Because no data feeds exist yet, `quality_score` is null and `quality_grade` is `UNGRADED`. Registration also runs the fee engine once, in its provisional form, writing `provisional_monthly_fee_cents` and `fee_tier`; §3.9 specifies exactly how.

The onboarding lifecycle runs through `APPLIED`, `LEGAL_REVIEW`, `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED`, `CONFORMANCE_TESTING`, `REMEDIATION`, `CERTIFIED` and `SUSPENDED`, terminating in either `WITHDRAWN` or `TERMINATED`. Each step is requested as an `action` value on a single multiplexed onboarding capability, and every step increments the participant's `version` by 1.

A `ParticipantAdmin` acting for its own organization opens the process by submitting for legal review, advancing `APPLIED → LEGAL_REVIEW`; the transition requires `onboarding_status` to be exactly `APPLIED` and requires `X-User-ID` to equal the participant's own `id`. A `HieAdmin` then executes the agreement, advancing `LEGAL_REVIEW → AGREEMENT_EXECUTED`. That request must carry both an `agreement_version` label and a `signed_at` instant; the server stores them as `agreement_version` and `agreement_signed_at` respectively and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `agreement_executed_by_user_id`. Execution does not stop there: **within the same request** the server performs the system transition `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED → CONFORMANCE_TESTING`, which resets `consecutive_failed_attempts` to 0, so the response body already reports `onboarding_status` as `CONFORMANCE_TESTING`. A client that expects to see `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED` in a response will never observe it.

Certification is the gate the whole exchange hangs on, and it is guarded by three independent conditions on top of the state requirement that `onboarding_status` be `CONFORMANCE_TESTING`. The participant's `passed_profiles` must cover, as a superset, the three required certification profiles — `ADT_PUBLISHER`, `DOCUMENT_SOURCE` and `QUERY_RESPONDER` — failing which the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_PROFILES_INCOMPLETE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `agreement_executed_by_user_id`, failing which the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SOD_CERTIFIER_IS_SIGNATORY`. And the participant must hold no invoice in `DELINQUENT` status, failing which the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPATION_FEE_DELINQUENT`. When all three hold, the participant advances `CONFORMANCE_TESTING → CERTIFIED`, with `certified_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` and `certified_at` set to the logical clock.

Remediation exists because conformance testing is iterative. A `HieAdmin` may push a participant `CONFORMANCE_TESTING → REMEDIATION` by supplying a `reason`; the transition increments `remediation_count` by 1. A `ParticipantAdmin` acting for its own organization closes the loop by resuming conformance, `REMEDIATION → CONFORMANCE_TESTING`, which resets `consecutive_failed_attempts` to 0 while deliberately preserving both `passed_profiles` and `conformance_attempt_count` — nothing already proven is thrown away. The cycle may repeat any number of times.

Withdrawal is available to a `ParticipantAdmin` acting for its own organization from any pre-certification state: the same withdraw action is accepted while `onboarding_status` is `APPLIED`, `LEGAL_REVIEW`, `AGREEMENT_EXECUTED`, `CONFORMANCE_TESTING` or `REMEDIATION`, in every case moving the participant to `WITHDRAWN` with identical side effects — every conformance run of that participant still in `RUNNING` is forced to `FAILED` with its `failure_categories` set to a single-member list containing `TRANSPORT`.

Participants are individually readable, and the participant list is filterable by `onboarding_status`, by `org_type` and by `fee_tier`.

### 3.2. Conformance Testing: Runs, Results, Failure Streaks and Waivers

A conformance run is booked against one participant and one `profile`, chosen from `QUERY_RESPONDER`, `ADT_PUBLISHER`, `DOCUMENT_SOURCE`, `ADT_SUBSCRIBER` and `PUBLIC_HEALTH_CONDUIT`. The booking payload supplies `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` for the sandbox window, and `scheduled_end` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start`. The created run has `status` `RUNNING`, its `participant_id` taken from the path, its `executed_by_user_id` recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`, and an `attempt_number` computed as one more than the count of that participant's prior runs for the same `profile` — equivalently, the run's one-based ordinal among same-profile runs ordered by ascending `created_at`. Booking also increments the participant's `conformance_attempt_count` by 1. A profile that already has a `PASSED` run for the same participant cannot be re-booked: the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CONFORMANCE_PROFILE_ALREADY_PASSED`. A `FAILED` or `WAIVED` profile may always be re-attempted.

Results are submitted by a `HieAdmin` against a run whose `status` is `RUNNING` and whose owning participant is in `CONFORMANCE_TESTING`. The payload carries `assertions_total` (at least 1 when results are submitted), `assertions_passed` (at least zero and no greater than `assertions_total`), and optionally `failure_categories`, whose members are drawn from `SECURITY`, `PATIENT_ID_INTEGRITY`, `VOCABULARY`, `TRANSPORT` and `PAYLOAD_STRUCTURE`. Two derived values are written. `pass_rate` is `assertions_passed` divided by `assertions_total`, rounded half up to four decimal places. `critical_failure` is true exactly when `failure_categories` has at least one member in common with the two categories the exchange treats as non-negotiable: `SECURITY` and `PATIENT_ID_INTEGRITY`.

The outcome branch is decided by both values together. The run advances `RUNNING → PASSED` when `pass_rate` is at least the pass-rate floor of 0.9500 **and** `critical_failure` is false; it advances `RUNNING → FAILED` when `pass_rate` is below 0.9500 **or** `critical_failure` is true. The floor is inclusive, which is observable at the boundary: 190 assertions passed out of 200 yields exactly 0.9500 and passes, while 189 out of 200 yields 0.9450 and fails. A run tagged with a critical failure category fails even at a perfect `pass_rate` of 1.0000. Only the counts submitted on this run participate; prior attempts on the same profile contribute nothing.

Each branch carries its own consequences, and they are not symmetric. On a pass, the run's `profile` is appended to the participant's `passed_profiles` with set semantics — deduplicated and re-sorted in ascending order — and `consecutive_failed_attempts` is reset to 0. On a failure, `consecutive_failed_attempts` is incremented by 1, and when it reaches the consecutive-failure limit of three the server immediately fires the system transition `CONFORMANCE_TESTING → REMEDIATION` on the owning participant, incrementing `remediation_count` by 1, leaving `suspension_reason` untouched and leaving `passed_profiles` intact. Either branch writes `pass_rate`, `critical_failure` and `executed_by_user_id` and increments the participant's `version` by 1.

A failed run is not always fatal. A `HieAdmin` may waive it, moving the run `FAILED → WAIVED` with a supplied `waiver_reason`; the waiver appends the `profile` to `passed_profiles` under the same deduplicate-and-sort rule and resets `consecutive_failed_attempts` to 0, exactly as a pass would. There is one hard limit: a run whose `critical_failure` is true is not waivable, and attempting to waive it fails the transition precondition. Note the deliberate asymmetry between certification and subscription activation that this creates — a waived `ADT_PUBLISHER` run counts toward certification but, as §3.9 explains, does **not** unlock event notification delivery.

Conformance runs are readable individually and as a per-participant list filterable by `profile` and by `status`.

### 3.3. Statewide Governance Generations: Consent Policy and Match Tuning

Two governance artifacts are versioned as generations, both administered by a `PrivacyOfficer`, and both following a `DRAFT` → activated → retired progression. Understanding them is a prerequisite for everything in §3.4 through §3.7, because every scored link and every disclosure decision is evaluated against whichever generation was in force at the time.

A consent policy generation is drafted with a human-readable `policy_version` label, unique across all policies; a statewide default `model` of either `OPT_IN` or `OPT_OUT`; a `sensitive_categories_segmented` list whose members are drawn from `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `SUD`, `HIV`, `REPRODUCTIVE` and `GENETIC`; a boolean `default_share_for_treatment` (defaulting to true) declaring whether treatment purposes inherit implied consent under an opt-out model; and the half-open governing interval `[effective_from, effective_until)` where `effective_from` is an inclusive lower bound and `effective_until` an exclusive upper bound. A null `effective_until` means the generation is open-ended. Drafting sets `status` to `DRAFT` and `directive_count` to 0, and immediately runs an interval-overlap check against every existing policy whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`: any overlap of the half-open intervals is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_POLICY_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Adjacency is explicitly permitted — a new generation whose `effective_from` is exactly an incumbent's `effective_until` does not overlap it and is accepted.

Activating a draft policy moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `activated_by_user_id`, and re-runs the overlap test against the currently `ACTIVE` set, again rejecting with `ERR_CONSENT_POLICY_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. The activation then supersedes history: every policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_until` is non-null and at or before the newly activated policy's `effective_from` is moved `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, each such row incrementing its own `version` by 1. A policy with a null `effective_until` is never superseded this way. Policies are readable individually and as a list filterable by `status` and by `model`.

A match tuning generation is a `MatchThresholdConfig`, drafted with a `config_label` unique across all generations. It declares the routing bounds — `auto_link_threshold`, an inclusive lower bound above zero and at most 1.0000 expressed to four decimal places, and `review_lower_bound`, an inclusive lower bound at least zero and strictly below `auto_link_threshold` — plus a `score_variant_mode` of either `DETERMINISTIC_FIRST` or `PROBABILISTIC_ONLY`, six probabilistic weights named `weight_family_name`, `weight_given_name`, `weight_birth_date`, `weight_ssn_last4`, `weight_postal_code` and `weight_phone_digits`, and a `partial_credit_factor` between zero and 1.0000 inclusive that is applied when a fuzzy-eligible field nearly agrees. Drafting sets `status` to `DRAFT`, `scored_link_count` to 0, and `tuned_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Three admissibility rules are checked at draft time, and any violation yields `ERR_THRESHOLD_CONFIG_INVALID`: the six weights must sum to 1.0000, tolerating an absolute difference of at most 0.0001; `auto_link_threshold` must be strictly greater than `review_lower_bound`; and `partial_credit_factor` must lie strictly inside the open interval between zero and one, so neither exactly zero nor exactly one is admissible at this gate.

Activating a tuning generation moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, records `activated_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` — the field that later powers the steward-versus-tuner duty separation — and moves the incumbent `ACTIVE` generation, if one exists, `ACTIVE → RETIRED`, incrementing that row's `version` by 1. Activation is deliberately **not** retroactive: match links already created keep the `threshold_config_id` pinned to them at proposal time and are never re-scored, so the exchange can always explain why a historical link was routed the way it was. At most one generation is `ACTIVE` at any instant, and when none is, scoring is impossible and the operations that need it fail with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_THRESHOLD_CONFIG`. Tuning generations are readable individually and as a list filterable by `status` and by `score_variant_mode`.

### 3.4. Contributing Patient Records and Resolving Identity

A participant contributes one patient record at a time. The contribution payload carries the contributor's local `source_mrn`, which must be unique within that contributor — a second record with the same `(participant_id, source_mrn)` pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SOURCE_MRN` — together with the demographic fields the matching algorithm consumes: `given_name`, `family_name`, `birth_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form, and `sex` from `M`, `F`, `O` or `U`. Three further demographics are optional and default to null: `ssn_last4`, which must be exactly four digits when present, `postal_code`, and `phone_digits`, which is a digits-only value. The payload may also declare `sensitive_categories`, a possibly empty list drawn from `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `SUD`, `HIV`, `REPRODUCTIVE` and `GENETIC`, describing what kinds of sensitive content this record contains. Contribution is only open to a participant whose `onboarding_status` is `CONFORMANCE_TESTING` or `CERTIFIED`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ONBOARDED_FOR_DATA`.

The stored record is born with `match_state` `CLUSTERED` — the other two states, `QUARANTINED` and `WITHDRAWN_FROM_INDEX`, are reached only through the withdrawal machinery of §3.9 — and with a `completeness_score` equal to the count of non-null values among exactly six fields: `given_name`, `family_name`, `birth_date`, `ssn_last4`, `postal_code` and `phone_digits`. The server then mints a fresh identity cluster in `ACTIVE` status holding this record alone, points the record's `cluster_id` at it, leaves `prior_cluster_id` null, and runs the membership recount described at the end of this subsection over that new cluster.

**Candidate selection.** Only cross-participant pairs are ever proposed. The candidate set for a newly contributed record consists of every stored patient record whose `participant_id` differs from the contributor's, whose `match_state` is `CLUSTERED`, and whose cluster's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Records from the same contributor are never paired, because `source_mrn` uniqueness already de-duplicates within a contributor. Candidates are evaluated in ascending `created_at` order, and **every** candidate produces exactly one `MatchLink` row — links are the exchange's permanent record of both agreement and disagreement. Each link records `left_record_id` as the pre-existing record (the earlier `created_at`) and `right_record_id` as the newly contributed one (the later `created_at`), and pins `threshold_config_id` to the single tuning generation whose `status` is `ACTIVE` at proposal time. If no generation is `ACTIVE`, the whole contribution is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_THRESHOLD_CONFIG`.

**Per-field agreement.** Before any comparison, both sides are normalized: text values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace and lowercased, and digit-bearing values are reduced to their digit characters only. The agreement value for a field is then determined as follows, and it is always one of three values, recorded per field in the link's `field_agreement_map`. If either side's value is null, agreement is zero. Otherwise, if the two normalized values are equal, agreement is one. Otherwise, if the field is one of the four fuzzy-eligible fields — `family_name`, `given_name`, `postal_code` and `phone_digits` — and the first three characters of the two normalized values are equal, agreement is the generation's `partial_credit_factor`. In every other case agreement is zero. The two strong identifiers, `birth_date` and `ssn_last4`, are exact-match-only and never earn partial credit no matter how close they come.

**The probabilistic score.** A raw total is formed by multiplying each of the six weights by its own field's agreement value and adding the six products: `weight_family_name` times the family-name agreement, plus `weight_given_name` times the given-name agreement, plus `weight_birth_date` times the birth-date agreement, plus `weight_ssn_last4` times the last-four agreement, plus `weight_postal_code` times the postal-code agreement, plus `weight_phone_digits` times the phone-digits agreement. That raw total is rounded half up to four decimal places. Two non-linear adjustments are then applied **in exactly this order**. First the *birth-date veto cap*: when the birth-date agreement is zero, the score is replaced by the lesser of itself and 0.6000 — two people who do not share a date of birth cannot be auto-linked on names alone. Second the *strong-identifier floor*: when the last-four agreement is one **and** the family-name agreement is one, the score is replaced by the greater of itself and 0.8000. The result is then rounded half up to four decimal places a second time. Both roundings are part of the specification; skipping either is observable at the fourth decimal.

**The deterministic score.** The deterministic variant is all-or-nothing. It yields exactly 1.0000 when either of two conjunctions holds: the last-four agreement is one and the birth-date agreement is one; or all four of the family-name, given-name, birth-date and postal-code agreements are one. Otherwise it yields exactly 0.0000. Because partial credit is irrelevant to a conjunction of exact matches, the deterministic variant ignores `partial_credit_factor` entirely.

**Variant selection.** The pinned generation's `score_variant_mode` decides which number becomes the link's `match_score` and what its `score_variant` reads. A mode of `DETERMINISTIC_FIRST` selects the deterministic score and sets `score_variant` to `DETERMINISTIC`; a mode of `PROBABILISTIC_ONLY` selects the probabilistic score and sets `score_variant` to `PROBABILISTIC`. The choice is observably consequential for the same pair of records: a pair agreeing on nothing but `ssn_last4` and `birth_date` earns a partial weighted total under the probabilistic variant, which may well fall below the review band, while the same pair earns a flat 1.0000 under the deterministic variant and is auto-linked outright.

**Routing.** Every link is born `PROPOSED` and is routed immediately, inside the same request, along exactly one of three branches, each with its own side-effect set. When `match_score` is at or above the pinned generation's `auto_link_threshold`, the link goes `PROPOSED → AUTO_LINKED`; this branch additionally requires that both records have `match_state` `CLUSTERED` and that both of their clusters have `status` `ACTIVE`. When `match_score` is at or above `review_lower_bound` and strictly below `auto_link_threshold`, the link goes `PROPOSED → PENDING_REVIEW`. When `match_score` is strictly below `review_lower_bound`, the link goes `PROPOSED → REJECTED`. Each branch's consequences differ sharply:

- `PROPOSED → AUTO_LINKED` performs the cluster merge immediately. The two clusters contest, and **the cluster with the earlier `created_at` wins**, ties broken by ascending `created_at` over the cluster rows themselves. The loser moves `ACTIVE → MERGED_AWAY` with its `merged_into_cluster_id` set to the winner's `id`; every patient record whose `cluster_id` was the loser has its `prior_cluster_id` set to the loser and its `cluster_id` set to the winner. The link records `cluster_id` as the winner and `merged_away_cluster_id` as the loser — the latter stays null when the incoming record had no cluster of its own to lose. The membership recount re-runs on the winner and then on the loser, and both clusters increment `version` by 1. Consent directives and cluster-scoped subscriptions belonging to the loser are re-pointed onto the winner under exactly the rules §3.5 sets out for a confirmed link, with duplicates superseded and terminated respectively.
- `PROPOSED → PENDING_REVIEW` mutates **no** cluster membership at all: both records keep the `cluster_id` they already had. What it does do is increment `pending_review_link_count` on both touched clusters and increment `version` by 1 on both.
- `PROPOSED → REJECTED` mutates nothing — no cluster change and no change to `pending_review_link_count`. The link is retained purely as negative-agreement evidence, and `REJECTED` is a terminal state for a link that reaches it this way.

Every link created by a contribution also advances the pinned generation's `scored_link_count`.

**Membership recount.** Whenever cluster membership can have changed — on record contribution, on every link transition into `AUTO_LINKED`, `LINKED` or `UNLINKED`, on a cluster moving to `MERGED_AWAY` and on its restoration, and on participant termination — five values are recomputed from scratch on each affected cluster. `member_record_count` is the count of patient records whose `cluster_id` is this cluster and whose `match_state` is `CLUSTERED`. `participant_count` is the count of distinct `participant_id` values over that same member set. `primary_record_id` points at the member with the highest `completeness_score`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`. `pending_review_link_count` is the count of match links in `PENDING_REVIEW` status whose `left_record_id` or `right_record_id` is a member of this cluster. `active_directive_count` is the count of consent directives on this cluster whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Records in `QUARANTINED` or `WITHDRAWN_FROM_INDEX` are excluded from all of these counters even though their `cluster_id` is deliberately retained for audit. When a merge or unmerge is in flight, the recount always runs on both clusters — winner first, loser second.

Patient records are individually readable and listable with filters on `participant_id`, `cluster_id`, `prior_cluster_id`, `match_state` and `source_mrn`, so both the current and the pre-merge cluster pointer are reverse-reachable. Clusters are readable individually and listable with filters on `primary_record_id`, `merged_into_cluster_id` and `status`. Links are readable individually and listable with filters on `left_record_id`, `right_record_id`, `threshold_config_id`, `cluster_id`, `merged_away_cluster_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Stewardship: Adjudication, Merge Cascades and Compensating Unlinks

A link sitting in `PENDING_REVIEW` waits for a human. Adjudication is expressed as a `decision` value of `CONFIRM`, `REJECT` or `REOPEN`, always accompanied by `expected_cluster_version` (see §2.6), and optionally by a `review_note` recording the steward's reasoning. Confirming and rejecting are open to both a `HieAdmin` and a `PrivacyOfficer`; reopening is reserved to a `PrivacyOfficer`. Every decision records the acting `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`, stores the `review_note`, and increments the link's `version` by 1 along with `version` on both touched clusters.

Confirmation drives `PENDING_REVIEW → LINKED` and is guarded by three preconditions beyond the state requirement. The supplied `expected_cluster_version` must equal the current `version` of the cluster holding the link's left record. Both records' clusters must have `status` `ACTIVE`, failing which the decision is rejected with `ERR_CLUSTER_MERGED_AWAY`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `activated_by_user_id` of the tuning generation pinned to this link, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_SOD_STEWARD_IS_TUNER` — the officer who tuned the thresholds does not get to adjudicate the links those thresholds produced.

A confirmation then fires a four-link cascade, each link caused by the one before it, and all four are observable:

1. The link's own `status` becomes `LINKED`, `reviewed_by_user_id` is set from `X-User-ID`, and `review_note` is stored.
2. Caused by step 1, the two clusters merge. The cluster with the earlier `created_at` wins; the loser moves `ACTIVE → MERGED_AWAY` with `merged_into_cluster_id` set to the winner. Every patient record of the loser has `prior_cluster_id` set to the loser and `cluster_id` set to the winner. The membership recount re-runs on the winner and then on the loser. The link's `cluster_id` becomes the winner and its `merged_away_cluster_id` becomes the loser.
3. Caused by step 2, every consent directive whose `cluster_id` was the loser is re-pointed at the winner. Where the winner already holds an `ACTIVE` directive sharing the same `(directive_type, scope, scoped_participant_id)` triple, the one with the **earlier** `effective_from` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and the survivor's `supersedes_directive_id` is set to the superseded row's `id`. The winner cluster's `active_directive_count` is recomputed accordingly.
4. Caused by step 3, every notification subscription whose `subscription_scope` is `CLUSTER` and whose `cluster_id` was the loser is re-pointed at the winner. Where the same `subscriber_participant_id` already holds a subscription on the winner in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` or `ACTIVE` status whose `event_types` overlap the re-pointed one's, the row with the **later** `created_at` moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to the literal `DUPLICATE_AFTER_CLUSTER_MERGE`, and the affected participant's `active_subscription_count` is recomputed.

Rejection is the quiet sibling. It drives `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED`, requires the same `expected_cluster_version` match and the same steward-versus-tuner separation with the same `ERR_SOD_STEWARD_IS_TUNER` code, and performs no merge whatsoever. Its one membership effect is to **decrement** `pending_review_link_count` on both clusters, since the link is no longer waiting on anyone. Both clusters and the link increment `version` by 1.

Unlinking is the compensating action, reserved to a `PrivacyOfficer`, and it is the only path back out of a merge. It accepts a link whose `status` is either `AUTO_LINKED` or `LINKED`, requires `expected_cluster_version` to equal the current `version` of the cluster the link points at, and requires an `unlink_reason` in the payload. The link moves to `UNLINKED` — the identical compensation set applies whether it arrived from `AUTO_LINKED` or from `LINKED` — and the merge is undone step for step: the cluster named by `merged_away_cluster_id`, when non-null, moves `MERGED_AWAY → ACTIVE` with its `merged_into_cluster_id` cleared; every patient record whose `prior_cluster_id` equals that restored cluster has its `cluster_id` set back to that value and its `prior_cluster_id` cleared to null; the membership recount re-runs on both clusters, restoring the conserved membership total described in §2.10; and the link's own `cluster_id` and `merged_away_cluster_id` are both cleared to null while `unlink_reason` is stored. Both clusters and the link increment `version` by 1.

Finally, a `PrivacyOfficer` may reopen an unlinked decision, moving the link `UNLINKED → PENDING_REVIEW`. Reopening requires `expected_cluster_version` to match the current `version` of the cluster holding the left record and requires both records' clusters to be `ACTIVE`; it increments `pending_review_link_count` on both clusters and `version` by 1 on both clusters and the link. This closes the link cycle `LINKED → UNLINKED → PENDING_REVIEW → LINKED`, which may be traversed repeatedly.

### 3.6. Patient Consent Directives and Their Precedence

Where §3.3 governs the statewide default, a consent directive is one patient's own instruction, recorded by a `PrivacyOfficer` against one identity cluster. Because a cluster *is* the patient as far as the exchange is concerned, a directive attaches to the cluster and follows it through merges. The addressed cluster must be `ACTIVE`; a cluster that has been absorbed by a merge rejects the recording with `ERR_CLUSTER_MERGED_AWAY`. There must also be a consent policy generation currently `ACTIVE` and governing the logical clock, failing which the recording is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CONSENT_POLICY`.

The payload declares a `directive_type` of `OPT_IN`, `OPT_OUT` or `SENSITIVE_CATEGORY_RESTRICTION`; a `scope` of either `ALL_PARTICIPANTS` or `SINGLE_PARTICIPANT`; the mandatory `expected_cluster_version` from §2.6; and, depending on those choices, `scoped_participant_id`, `restricted_categories` and `effective_from`. Three coherence rules apply, and any of them failing yields `ERR_DIRECTIVE_SCOPE_PARTICIPANT_REQUIRED`: a `SINGLE_PARTICIPANT` scope requires a non-null `scoped_participant_id`; an `ALL_PARTICIPANTS` scope requires `scoped_participant_id` to be null; and a `SENSITIVE_CATEGORY_RESTRICTION` requires a non-empty `restricted_categories` list, whose members are drawn from `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `SUD`, `HIV`, `REPRODUCTIVE` and `GENETIC`. `effective_from` is the instant from which the directive begins participating in disclosure evaluation; when the payload omits it, the server defaults it to the logical clock at recording time.

The stored directive is born with `status` `ACTIVE`, `cluster_id` taken from the path, `recorded_by_user_id` set from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `policy_id` pinned to the `id` of the consent policy generation that was `ACTIVE` and governing the clock at recording time. Recording is a create-with-supersede operation: any incumbent directive on the same cluster sharing the new one's `(directive_type, scope, scoped_participant_id)` triple moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, provided the incoming directive's `effective_from` is at or after the incumbent's, and the new row's `supersedes_directive_id` is set to the displaced row's `id`. The superseded row increments its own `version` by 1. The cluster's `active_directive_count` and the governing policy's `directive_count` are both refreshed, and the cluster's `version` increments by 1.

Revocation is the other exit. A `PrivacyOfficer` revokes an `ACTIVE` directive by supplying a `revocation_reason`, moving it `ACTIVE → REVOKED`. From that instant the directive stops participating in disclosure evaluation entirely — there is no grace period and no trailing effect. Revocation refreshes the owning cluster's `active_directive_count`, increments the cluster's `version` by 1 and the directive's `version` by 1. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `REVOKED` are terminal for a directive; neither can be revived, and a further attempt to act on such a row fails with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

Directives are readable individually and as a list filterable by `cluster_id`, `policy_id`, `scoped_participant_id`, `supersedes_directive_id`, `status` and `directive_type`, which makes both the governing policy and the displaced predecessor reverse-reachable from the audit side.

### 3.7. The Record Locator Query: Cluster Resolution, Disclosure and Audit

A locator query is the exchange's product. It is submitted by a `Clinician`, a `ParticipantAdmin` or a `PublicHealthOfficer` on behalf of a named `querying_participant_id`, with a `purpose_of_use` drawn from `TREATMENT`, `PAYMENT`, `OPERATIONS`, `PUBLIC_HEALTH` and `EMERGENCY`, and with the demographics to resolve: `family_name`, `given_name` and `birth_date` are required, while `sex`, `ssn_last4`, `postal_code` and `phone_digits` are optional and default to null. An emergency session may be cited through an optional `break_glass_id`. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded on the query as `clinician_user_id`.

Two gates precede any evaluation. The querying participant must be `CERTIFIED`, failing which the query is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_CERTIFIED`. And that participant must have at least one data feed of `feed_type` `CCDA` whose `status` is `MONITORED` or `DEGRADED`: a document feed still `PROVISIONING`, or one that is `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED`, cannot carry a locator response, and the query is refused with `ERR_QUERY_FEED_NOT_OPERATIONAL`.

**Step 1 — cluster resolution.** The submitted demographics are scored against every candidate patient record whose `match_state` is `CLUSTERED` and whose cluster's `status` is `ACTIVE`, using precisely the algorithm of §3.4 under whichever tuning generation is currently `ACTIVE`; when none is, the query fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_THRESHOLD_CONFIG`. The highest score achieved by any candidate is stored as `top_candidate_score`, or null when there are no candidates at all. The eligible set is every candidate scoring at or above the active generation's `auto_link_threshold`. If the eligible set is empty, the query terminates immediately: `cluster_id` stays null, `consent_decision` is `NO_MATCH`, `disclosed_record_count` and `withheld_record_count` are both zero, `withheld_categories` is empty, and — importantly — **no audit entry is written at all**, because there is no record whose disclosure could be audited. Otherwise the chosen record is the eligible member with the highest score, ties broken by ascending `created_at` over the record, and the query's `cluster_id` becomes that record's cluster.

**Step 2 — governing policy.** The governing policy is the consent policy generation whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock, and whose `effective_until` is either null or strictly after the clock. Its `id` is stored as `governing_policy_id`. When no such generation exists the query is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CONSENT_POLICY`.

**Step 3 — directive set and precedence.** The candidate directive set consists of the directives on the resolved cluster whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock. Revoked and superseded directives are ignored even when they are the most specific rows on the cluster. The set is ordered by a three-key precedence: first, directives whose `scope` is `SINGLE_PARTICIPANT` with `scoped_participant_id` equal to the `querying_participant_id` come ahead of those whose `scope` is `ALL_PARTICIPANTS`, while `SINGLE_PARTICIPANT` rows scoped to any *other* participant are dropped from the set entirely; second, descending `effective_from`; third, descending `created_at`. The **governing directive** is the first row of that ordered set whose `directive_type` is `OPT_IN` or `OPT_OUT`, and its `id` is recorded as `governing_directive_id`; when no such row exists, `governing_directive_id` stays null and the policy default governs instead. The base decision is the governing directive's `directive_type` when one was found; otherwise it is `OPT_IN` when the policy's `model` is `OPT_OUT` **and** its `default_share_for_treatment` is true, and `OPT_OUT` in every other case.

**Step 4 — overrides.** Two overrides are then evaluated. The *emergency* override applies when `break_glass_id` is non-null and every one of four conditions holds on the cited session: its `cluster_id` equals the resolved cluster, its `session_status` is `OPEN`, the logical clock is at or before its `expires_at`, and its `invoking_clinician_user_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`. A cited session that fails any of these — not open, expired, covering a different cluster, or belonging to a different clinician — causes the query to be rejected with `ERR_BREAK_GLASS_SESSION_NOT_OPEN` rather than silently ignored. The *conduit* override applies when `purpose_of_use` is `PUBLIC_HEALTH`, the querying participant's `org_type` is `PUBLIC_HEALTH`, and that participant holds a notification subscription whose `subscription_scope` is `PUBLIC_HEALTH_CONDUIT` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`.

**Step 5 — member set and suppression.** The member set is every patient record whose `cluster_id` is the resolved cluster and whose `match_state` is `CLUSTERED`, taken in ascending `created_at` order; `QUARANTINED` and `WITHDRAWN_FROM_INDEX` records are invisible to the locator and produce no audit entry. The suppressed-category set starts as the union of `restricted_categories` over those directives in the candidate set whose `directive_type` is `SENSITIVE_CATEGORY_RESTRICTION`, intersected with the policy's `sensitive_categories_segmented` — a patient cannot restrict a category the statewide policy does not segment. When `purpose_of_use` is `PAYMENT` or `OPERATIONS`, the whole of the policy's `sensitive_categories_segmented` is then added to that set, because non-clinical purposes never see segmented content regardless of what the patient said.

Each member is then classified, in ascending `created_at` order, by the first matching rule in this ordered chain:

1. If the member's contributing participant has an `onboarding_status` of `SUSPENDED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `TERMINATED`, the member is recorded with an `action` of `WITHHELD` and a `suppression_reason` of `CONTRIBUTOR_NOT_PARTICIPATING`. This test comes first, so a suspended contributor's records are withheld even under an emergency override.
2. Otherwise, if the emergency override applies, the member is disclosed with an `action` of `BREAK_GLASS_DISCLOSED` and a null `suppression_reason`. Emergency overrides both opt-out and segmentation.
3. Otherwise, if the base decision is `OPT_OUT` and the conduit override does not apply, the member is recorded with an `action` of `WITHHELD` and a `suppression_reason` of `OPT_OUT`.
4. Otherwise, if the member's `sensitive_categories` has at least one category in common with the suppressed-category set, the member is recorded with an `action` of `WITHHELD` — carrying a `suppression_reason` of `PURPOSE_RESTRICTED` when `purpose_of_use` is `PAYMENT` or `OPERATIONS`, and of `SENSITIVE_CATEGORY` otherwise.
5. Otherwise the member is disclosed with an `action` of `DISCLOSED` and a null `suppression_reason`.

**Step 6 — the decision label.** `disclosed_record_count` and `withheld_record_count` are the sizes of the two outcomes, and `withheld_categories` is the sorted union of every sensitive category that caused any suppression on this query. The query's `consent_decision` is then determined by the first matching clause: `PERMITTED_EMERGENCY` when the emergency override applied; otherwise `DENIED_OPT_OUT` when the base decision was `OPT_OUT`, the conduit override did not apply, and nothing was disclosed; otherwise `PERMITTED_PUBLIC_HEALTH` when the conduit override applied, the base decision was `OPT_OUT`, and nothing was withheld; otherwise `PARTIAL_SEGMENTED` when at least one member was disclosed and at least one was withheld; otherwise `DENIED_SEGMENTED` when nothing was disclosed and at least one member was withheld; otherwise `PERMITTED`.

**Step 7 — audit.** Exactly one access audit entry is written per cluster member, in the same ascending `created_at` order, and entries are written for withheld members just as for disclosed ones. Each entry records `query_id` pointing at the originating query, `record_id` pointing at the audited patient record, `disclosing_participant_id` naming that record's contributor, the `action` taken, the `suppression_reason` where one applies, and `sensitive_category_hit` — the sorted intersection of that record's `sensitive_categories` with the categories suppressed on this query. Each entry's `reviewed` flag starts false and is raised only by the post-hoc review of §3.8.

Queries are readable individually and as a list filterable by `querying_participant_id`, `cluster_id`, `break_glass_id`, `governing_policy_id`, `governing_directive_id` and `consent_decision`. Audit entries are readable individually and as a list filterable by `query_id`, `record_id`, `disclosing_participant_id` and `action`; as §2.11 notes, that list is their only discovery channel.

### 3.8. Break-the-Glass Emergency Access, Post-Hoc Review and Sanction Accrual

Emergency access exists because an unconscious patient cannot be asked to opt in. A `Clinician` opens a session over one identity cluster, naming that `cluster_id` and the `invoking_participant_id` of the organization they act for, asserting an `emergency_category` of `LIFE_THREATENING`, `UNCONSCIOUS_PATIENT` or `DISASTER`, and supplying a `justification_text` that must be at least forty characters after trimming — a shorter justification is refused with `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_TOO_SHORT`. The invoking participant must be `CERTIFIED` (`ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_CERTIFIED` otherwise) and the addressed cluster must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CLUSTER_MERGED_AWAY` otherwise).

The session is born with `session_status` `OPEN`, `review_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, `invoking_clinician_user_id` set from the acting `X-User-ID`, `opened_at` set to the logical clock, `expires_at` set to four hours after `opened_at`, and `disclosed_record_count`, `disclosed_sensitive_count` and `sanction_points_awarded` all zero.

**Cooldown.** Emergency access is not a channel a clinician may camp on. Each prior session defines an occupation interval for the pair `(cluster_id, invoking_clinician_user_id)` running from its `opened_at` to one hour after its `expires_at` — that is, five hours from opening in total. The interval is **closed at both ends**: a re-invocation landing exactly at the far endpoint still collides. If the logical clock falls inside any such interval for the same cluster and the same clinician, the new invocation is refused with `ERR_BREAK_GLASS_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. The interval survives expiry — an `EXPIRED` session still occupies its window for cooldown purposes.

**Two independent state axes.** A session carries two lifecycle dimensions on the same row, and no transition on one is a precondition of any transition on the other. `session_status` moves through `OPEN`, `CLOSED` and `EXPIRED` and answers only one question: may this session still authorize disclosure? `review_status` moves through `PENDING_REVIEW`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `JUSTIFIED` and `UNJUSTIFIED` and tracks the privacy authority's after-the-fact judgement. A review may be claimed and decided while the session is still `OPEN`, and a session may expire while its review is `UNDER_REVIEW`.

The invoking clinician closes their own session, driving `session_status` `OPEN → CLOSED`; the precondition is that `session_status` is `OPEN` and that the acting `X-User-ID` equals `invoking_clinician_user_id`, and a session that is not open is refused with `ERR_BREAK_GLASS_SESSION_NOT_OPEN`. Closure recomputes `disclosed_record_count` as the sum of `disclosed_record_count` over every locator query citing this session, recomputes `disclosed_sensitive_count` as the count of audit entries under this session whose `action` is `BREAK_GLASS_DISCLOSED` and whose `sensitive_category_hit` is non-empty, leaves `review_status` untouched, and increments `version` by 1. Separately, when the logical clock advances strictly past `expires_at`, the session moves `session_status` `OPEN → EXPIRED` — evaluated lazily, on the next read or write that touches the row, as §2.5 describes — again leaving `review_status` untouched and incrementing `version` by 1.

**Post-hoc review.** A `PrivacyOfficer` claims the review, driving `review_status` `PENDING_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW` and recording the acting `X-User-ID` as `review_claimed_by_user_id`. The claim requires that `X-User-ID` differ from `invoking_clinician_user_id`, and a claim attempted on a session whose `review_claimed_by_user_id` is already set to another user is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLAIMED_BY_ACTOR`. Claiming leaves `session_status` untouched — the session may still be `OPEN` — and increments `version` by 1.

The decision carries an `outcome` of either `JUSTIFIED` or `UNJUSTIFIED` together with a `review_note`. Only the claimant may decide: an acting `X-User-ID` other than `review_claimed_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLAIMED_BY_ACTOR`. When the `outcome` is `UNJUSTIFIED` the `review_note` is additionally held to the same forty-character minimum as the original justification, and a shorter note is refused with `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_TOO_SHORT`. Both outcomes record `reviewer_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the logical clock, raise the `reviewed` flag on every access audit entry belonging to every query that cited this session, and increment the session's `version` by 1.

The two outcomes then diverge sharply, and their specification density is deliberately equal. A `JUSTIFIED` finding sets `sanction_points_awarded` to 0; the invoking participant's `sanction_points_active` is recomputed and comes out unchanged. An `UNJUSTIFIED` finding computes `sanction_points_awarded` as the category's own weight — six points for `LIFE_THREATENING`, four for `UNCONSCIOUS_PATIENT`, three for `DISASTER` — plus a surcharge of two additional points when the session's `disclosed_sensitive_count` is greater than zero. A session whose `review_status` is anything other than `UNJUSTIFIED` contributes zero points.

**The active sanction total and its decay.** A participant's `sanction_points_active` is the sum of `sanction_points_awarded` over the break-the-glass sessions whose `invoking_participant_id` is that participant, whose `review_status` is `UNJUSTIFIED`, and whose `decided_at` is strictly later than one hundred eighty days before the logical clock. The lower bound is exclusive, which is observable: a decision exactly one hundred eighty days old has already decayed and contributes nothing. Decay is a step function, not a gradual taper — a qualifying row contributes its full points right up to the boundary and nothing at all afterwards. Only sessions invoked on behalf of the participant count; `JUSTIFIED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` and `PENDING_REVIEW` rows contribute zero, the invoking clinician's own identity is irrelevant to the sum, and the summation has no ordering dependence. Because the window is anchored on the logical clock, moving the clock alone can change the total.

**The sanction cascade.** When an `UNJUSTIFIED` decision pushes `sanction_points_active` to twelve points or more and the invoking participant's `onboarding_status` is `CERTIFIED`, a four-link cascade fires inside that same request. Link 1 writes `sanction_points_awarded` and recomputes `sanction_points_active`. Link 2, caused by link 1, fires the system transition `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` on the invoking participant with `suspension_reason` set to the literal `AUTOMATIC_SANCTION_THRESHOLD`. Link 3, caused by link 2, moves every data feed of that participant whose `status` is `PROVISIONING`, `MONITORED` or `DEGRADED` to `SUSPENDED` with `status_reason` set to the literal `PARTICIPANT_SUSPENSION_CASCADE`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Link 4, caused by link 3, re-runs the scorecard calculation over the now-shrunken qualifying sample set — those feeds have left the qualifying statuses — rewriting `quality_score` and `quality_grade`. As a separate fan-out that is not part of that depth chain, every notification subscription of the participant whose `status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION` or `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` in ascending `created_at` order and `active_subscription_count` is recomputed. The participant's `version` increments by 1 whenever the sanction sum changes. The threshold comparison is inclusive at twelve: eleven points do not suspend, twelve do.

Sessions are readable individually — exposing both state axes, both disclosure counters and the awarded points — and as a list filterable by `cluster_id`, `invoking_participant_id`, `session_status` and `review_status`.

### 3.9. Feed Quality, Event Notification and the Economics of Participation

Everything in this subsection describes the ongoing operating relationship between the exchange and a participant that has cleared onboarding: the pipes it feeds data through, the scorecard those pipes earn it, the alert subscriptions it consumes, the invoices it pays, and the enforcement actions available when any of these go wrong.

**Provisioning a feed.** A data feed is provisioned for one participant and one `feed_type` — `ADT`, `CCDA` or `LAB_RESULT` — and the request must carry `expected_participant_version` as described in §2.6. A participant holding any invoice in `DELINQUENT` status may not provision new feeds and is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPATION_FEE_DELINQUENT`. The new feed is born with `status` `PROVISIONING`, `sample_count` zero, `latest_reject_rate` null, `participant_id` taken from the path, and `generation` set to one more than the highest existing generation for the same participant and `feed_type` — equivalently its one-based ordinal among same-type feeds ordered by ascending `created_at`. Provisioning is a create-with-supersede operation: any incumbent feed of the same `feed_type` whose `status` is `PROVISIONING`, `MONITORED`, `DEGRADED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `RETIRED` with `status_reason` set to the literal `SUPERSEDED_BY_GENERATION`, and the scorecard is re-run with that retired feed's samples excluded. `RETIRED` is terminal for a feed. The owning participant's `version` increments by 1.

**Recording a measurement window.** A `HieAdmin` records immutable quality samples against a feed. Each sample declares the half-open window `[period_start, period_end)`, where `period_start` is the inclusive lower bound and `period_end` must be strictly later; `messages_received`, the count accepted at the interface engine during the window, at least zero; `messages_rejected`, at least zero and no greater than `messages_received`; `required_fields_missing_count`, the number of required demographic fields absent across accepted messages, at least zero; `duplicate_count`, at least zero; and `timeliness_p95_seconds`, the ninety-fifth-percentile lag in seconds between clinical event and message receipt, at least zero. Three further checks reject a sample with `ERR_QUALITY_SAMPLE_INVALID`: `messages_rejected` plus `required_fields_missing_count` exceeding `messages_received`; `duplicate_count` exceeding `messages_received`; and a window overlapping any existing sample on the same feed. Each accepted sample records the addressed feed as its `data_feed_id` and stores its own `reject_rate`, computed as `messages_rejected` divided by the greater of one and `messages_received`, rounded half up to four decimal places — the guard in the denominator means a window with no traffic yields a reject rate of exactly zero rather than an undefined value. Accepting a sample increments the feed's `sample_count` by 1 and writes the feed's `latest_reject_rate`.

**Feed monitoring states.** A feed's `latest_reject_rate` is always the `reject_rate` of its most recent accepted sample by descending `period_end`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`. The degradation boundary is a reject rate of 0.0500. The first accepted sample on a feed drives `PROVISIONING → MONITORED`, sets `latest_reject_rate`, and re-runs the scorecard because the feed has just entered the qualifying status set. If that very first sample's `reject_rate` already exceeds 0.0500, the feed continues along `MONITORED → DEGRADED` inside the same request, so the observable end state is `DEGRADED` with `status_reason` set to `REJECT_RATE_BREACH`. Thereafter an accepted sample whose `reject_rate` is strictly greater than 0.0500 drives `MONITORED → DEGRADED`, setting `status_reason` to `REJECT_RATE_BREACH`; an accepted sample whose `reject_rate` is at or below 0.0500 drives `DEGRADED → MONITORED`, clearing `status_reason`. Both directions re-run the scorecard and increment the feed's `version` by 1; note that because `DEGRADED` remains a qualifying status, the second of those transitions changes the sample set not at all. This closes the monitoring cycle `MONITORED → DEGRADED → MONITORED`, which may repeat indefinitely.

A `HieAdmin` may also suspend a feed directly by supplying a `reason`, from any of `PROVISIONING`, `MONITORED` or `DEGRADED` into `SUSPENDED`, with `status_reason` set to that `reason` and the scorecard re-run with this feed's samples now excluded from the input set. Restoring a suspended feed drives `SUSPENDED → MONITORED`, clears `status_reason`, and re-runs the scorecard with those samples back in; restoration requires that the owning participant's `onboarding_status` is not `SUSPENDED`, since a suspended organization cannot have a live pipe.

**The participant scorecard.** The scorecard is computed on demand and re-computed on every event that can change its inputs — a newly accepted sample, and every feed transition into or out of the qualifying statuses. The qualifying sample set is assembled in three steps. Start with every quality sample whose owning feed belongs to this participant and whose owning feed's `status` is `MONITORED` or `DEGRADED`; feeds in `PROVISIONING`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED` contribute nothing at all. Restrict to samples whose `period_end` is strictly later than ninety days before the logical clock — an exclusive lower bound — and at or before the logical clock, an inclusive upper bound. Then truncate to the newest six rows ordered by descending `period_end`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`. The resulting count is reported as `samples_counted`, and the window bounds themselves are reported as `window_start` and `window_end`.

Let the received total be the sum of `messages_received` across the qualifying set. Three ratio components are then formed, each expressed on a hundred-point scale: `completeness_component` is one hundred times the quantity one minus the sum of `required_fields_missing_count` divided by the greater of one and the received total; `rejection_component` is one hundred times the quantity one minus the sum of `messages_rejected` divided by the greater of one and the received total; and `duplicate_component` is one hundred times the quantity one minus the sum of `duplicate_count` divided by the greater of one and the received total. Timeliness is a step function rather than a ratio: `average_p95_seconds` is the sum of `timeliness_p95_seconds` across the qualifying set divided by the number of samples in it, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `timeliness_component` is one hundred when that average is at most three hundred seconds, eighty-five when it is at most nine hundred seconds, sixty when it is at most one hour, and thirty otherwise.

`base_quality_score` is the weighted blend of those four components — thirty hundredths of `completeness_component`, plus thirty hundredths of `rejection_component`, plus fifteen hundredths of `duplicate_component`, plus twenty-five hundredths of `timeliness_component` — rounded half up to two decimal places.

The grade and the penalty look circular and are resolved by a **pinned evaluation order** that implementers must follow exactly. First derive a `provisional_grade` from `base_quality_score`: `A` at ninety-two or above, `B` at eighty-four or above, `C` at seventy or above, and `D` below that. Second read `penalty_points` from the provisional grade alone — zero points for `A`, one for `B`, three for `C`, six for `D`. Third compute `quality_score` as `base_quality_score` minus `penalty_points`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Fourth derive the final `grade` from `quality_score` using the same four bands. The penalty is keyed on the provisional grade and is **never** recomputed from the final grade. Worked through: a `base_quality_score` of 84.60 yields a `provisional_grade` of `B`, hence one penalty point, hence a `quality_score` of 83.60, hence a final `grade` of `C`. Re-deriving the penalty from that final `C` would deduct three points for 81.60 and is wrong.

Two edge cases are specified separately because they behave differently. When the qualifying set is empty the participant is ungraded: `quality_score` is null, `grade` and `provisional_grade` are both `UNGRADED`, `penalty_points` is zero, and the four components, `average_p95_seconds` and `base_quality_score` are all reported as zero. When the qualifying set is non-empty but every one of its samples carries a `messages_received` of zero, the participant **is** graded — the guard in each denominator makes all three ratio components exactly one hundred, and only timeliness distinguishes the result. The scorecard read reports `participant_id` alongside every value named in this paragraph and the three before it, and the participant row itself carries the latest `quality_score` and `quality_grade`, the latter drawn from `A`, `B`, `C`, `D` or `UNGRADED`.

**Event notification subscriptions.** A subscription is created against one participant, becoming that organization's `subscriber_participant_id`. It declares a `subscription_scope` of `PATIENT_PANEL`, `CLUSTER` or `PUBLIC_HEALTH_CONDUIT`; a non-empty `event_types` list drawn from `ADMISSION`, `DISCHARGE` and `TRANSFER`; a `delivery_endpoint_url` that must begin with `https://`; and, for cluster scope only, a `cluster_id`. Two coherence rules apply. A `subscription_scope` of `CLUSTER` requires a non-null `cluster_id` and any other scope requires it to be null, with either violation refused as `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. And a `subscription_scope` of `PUBLIC_HEALTH_CONDUIT` requires the owning participant's `org_type` to be `PUBLIC_HEALTH`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CONDUIT_ORG_TYPE_INVALID`. A cluster-scoped subscription may only attach to an `ACTIVE` cluster; a merged-away one yields `ERR_CLUSTER_MERGED_AWAY`. The subscription is born with `status` `PENDING_ACTIVATION`.

Activation is where the asymmetry flagged in §3.2 bites. Driving `PENDING_ACTIVATION → ACTIVE` requires the subscriber participant to hold a conformance run whose `profile` is `ADT_PUBLISHER` and whose `status` is **strictly** `PASSED`; a `WAIVED` run satisfies certification but does not satisfy this gate, and its absence is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_CONFORMANCE_MISSING`. Activation additionally requires that the subscriber hold no `DELINQUENT` invoice (`ERR_PARTICIPATION_FEE_DELINQUENT` otherwise) and that its `onboarding_status` be `CERTIFIED` (`ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_CERTIFIED` otherwise). On success the subscription records `activating_conformance_test_id` as the `id` of that qualifying run — the earliest by `created_at` when several qualify — and the participant's `active_subscription_count` increases by 1.

The remaining lifecycle actions are `SUSPEND`, from either `PENDING_ACTIVATION` or `ACTIVE` into `SUSPENDED`; `REACTIVATE`, from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE`, which additionally requires the subscriber's `onboarding_status` to be `CERTIFIED` and closes the cycle `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`; and `TERMINATE`, available from `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, which writes `termination_reason` from the supplied `reason` and is terminal. All three recompute the participant's `active_subscription_count` and increment the subscription's `version` by 1. Two cascades reach the same terminal state with a system-chosen reason instead of an operator's: the cluster-merge duplicate rule of §3.5 writes `DUPLICATE_AFTER_CLUSTER_MERGE`, and participant termination writes `PARTICIPANT_TERMINATED`. Subscriptions are readable individually and as a list filterable by `subscriber_participant_id`, `cluster_id`, `activating_conformance_test_id`, `status` and `subscription_scope`.

**The participation fee.** Fees are computed by one engine with two variants. The tier ladder is fixed: the first 24,750 billable volume units fall in tier one, the next 75,000 units (carrying the ladder up to a cumulative 99,750 units) fall in tier two, and everything beyond 99,750 units falls in tier three. Given a billable volume, the tier-one quantity is the lesser of that volume and 24,750; the tier-two quantity is the lesser of 75,000 and the volume minus 24,750 with any negative result treated as zero; and the tier-three quantity is the volume minus 99,750, again floored at zero. The per-unit rates are 1.75 cents in tier one, 1.25 cents in tier two and 0.65 cents in tier three.

**The rounding order is pinned and observable to the cent.** Each tier's charge is computed as its quantity multiplied by its rate and **individually rounded half up to a whole cent**; only then are the three rounded charges added to give `volume_fee_cents`. Summing the unrounded tier charges and rounding once at the end is wrong. A billable volume of 30,001 units makes this concrete: tier one contributes 24,750 units at 1.75 cents for 43,312.5 cents, rounding to 43,313; tier two contributes 5,251 units at 1.25 cents for 6,563.75 cents, rounding to 6,564; the pinned order therefore yields 49,877 cents, whereas summing first and rounding once would yield 49,876. The three rounded per-tier charges are also exposed as `tier_charge_breakdown`, an object keyed `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` and `TIER_3`.

A flat `base_platform_fee_cents` of 250,000 cents is added to `volume_fee_cents`, and the sum is multiplied by the applicable `quality_multiplier`; that product is rounded half up to a whole cent **exactly once** to give `total_cents`. The `fee_tier` reached is `TIER_3` when the tier-three quantity is greater than zero, otherwise `TIER_2` when the tier-two quantity is greater than zero, otherwise `TIER_1`.

The two variants differ only in how the billable volume and the multiplier are obtained. Under the `PROVISIONAL` variant — used unconditionally at participant registration, and available at issuance by naming it as `basis_variant` — the billable volume is `declared_annual_patient_volume` divided by twelve and rounded **up** to a whole unit, and `quality_multiplier` is forced to 1.00 no matter what scorecard history exists. Under the `RECONCILED` variant the billable volume is the sum of `messages_received` over every quality sample whose window is fully contained in the billing period — that is, whose `period_start` is at or after `billing_period_start` and whose `period_end` is at or before `billing_period_end` — and whose owning feed's `status` at issuance is `MONITORED` or `DEGRADED`, with `PROVISIONING`, `SUSPENDED` and `RETIRED` feeds excluded. Samples are summed with no ordering dependence. The `RECONCILED` multiplier is read from the participant's `quality_grade` at issuance, captured on the invoice as `quality_grade_snapshot`: grade `A` multiplies by 0.95, `B` by 1.00, `C` by 1.08, `D` by 1.20, and `UNGRADED` by 1.00. When the qualifying sample set for a `RECONCILED` issuance is empty the issuance is refused with `ERR_NO_RECONCILIATION_BASIS`. The chosen volume is stored on the invoice as `billable_volume_units`. The same participant and period will generally produce a materially different figure under each variant, and the difference is intentional: the provisional basis bills a promise, the reconciled basis bills observed traffic adjusted for how clean it was.

**Issuing and settling invoices.** A `HieAdmin` issues an invoice for a fully elapsed period, supplying `billing_period_start`, `billing_period_end`, `basis_variant` and a `due_date`. `billing_period_end` must be strictly after `billing_period_start`, and it must be at or before the logical clock — a period that has not fully elapsed is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. The submitted half-open period must not overlap the period of any existing invoice for the same participant whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `PAID`, `DELINQUENT` or `WRITTEN_OFF`; `VOID` invoices are excluded from that test, so voiding an invoice frees its window for re-issue. An overlap is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Issuance requires only that `onboarding_status` be one of `CONFORMANCE_TESTING`, `CERTIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, per §2.9. The issued invoice carries `status` `ISSUED`, `amount_paid_cents` zero, `participant_id` from the path, and every computed field named above, and it increases the participant's `outstanding_balance_cents` by its `total_cents` while incrementing the participant's `version` by 1.

Payment is submitted with `amount_paid_cents` and is accepted from either `ISSUED` or `DELINQUENT`, driving the invoice to `PAID` in both cases with identical effects. The tendered figure must equal the invoice's `total_cents` minus its currently stored `amount_paid_cents` — which is `total_cents` itself for an invoice nothing has been tendered against — so both partial payment and overpayment are refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Settlement recomputes the participant's `outstanding_balance_cents`, from which this invoice has now departed, and increments both the invoice's and the participant's `version` by 1.

Three administrative actions remain, each writing `status_reason` from the supplied `reason`. Declaring an invoice delinquent drives `ISSUED → DELINQUENT` and requires the logical clock to be strictly past `due_date`; an attempt at or before `due_date` fails the transition precondition with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Delinquency leaves `outstanding_balance_cents` unchanged, because `DELINQUENT` invoices stay inside the balance sum. Writing off drives `DELINQUENT → WRITTEN_OFF` and recomputes the balance, which the invoice now leaves. Voiding drives `ISSUED → VOID` and is the compensating action: it recomputes the balance back to its exact pre-issue value and releases the billed window for re-invoicing. All three increment the invoice's `version` by 1, and write-off and void also increment the participant's `version` by 1. Invoices are readable individually and as a per-participant list filterable by `status` and `basis_variant`.

**Suspension, reinstatement and termination.** A `HieAdmin` suspends a certified participant by supplying a `reason`, driving `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to that `reason`. The cascade is exactly the one the sanction chain of §3.8 fires: every data feed whose `status` is `PROVISIONING`, `MONITORED` or `DEGRADED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `status_reason` set to `PARTICIPANT_SUSPENSION_CASCADE`, processed in ascending `created_at` order; because those feeds have left the qualifying statuses, the scorecard is re-run over the shrunken sample set and `quality_score` and `quality_grade` are rewritten; and as a separate fan-out, every notification subscription whose `status` is `PENDING_ACTIVATION` or `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` in ascending `created_at` order with `active_subscription_count` recomputed.

Reinstatement drives `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` and is gated on two things beyond the state: `sanction_points_active` must be strictly below twelve, so an organization cannot be reinstated while its sanctions still justify suspension, and it must hold no `DELINQUENT` invoice, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPATION_FEE_DELINQUENT`. Reinstatement clears `suspension_reason`, moves every feed whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` **and** whose `status_reason` is `PARTICIPANT_SUSPENSION_CASCADE` back to `MONITORED` in ascending `created_at` order — a feed an operator suspended for its own reason stays suspended until explicitly restored — re-runs the scorecard, and moves every `SUSPENDED` subscription back to `ACTIVE` in ascending `created_at` order.

Termination drives `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED` with a supplied `reason` and is the exchange's final act on a participant. Every patient record that participant contributed has its `match_state` set to `WITHDRAWN_FROM_INDEX` while its `cluster_id` is deliberately preserved for audit; every cluster those records touched has its `member_record_count` and `participant_count` recomputed by the membership recount of §3.4; and every notification subscription of the participant moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to the literal `PARTICIPANT_TERMINATED`. `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN` are both terminal for a participant.
