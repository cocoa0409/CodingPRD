# Buying Club & Food Co-op Order Platform API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A buying club is a group of households that pools its grocery demand so that it can buy at wholesale case prices from suppliers who will not sell a single jar of anything. The pooling is the whole product, and it is also the whole problem: a supplier ships whole cases, a member wants three units, a truck has a fixed number of refrigerated pallet slots, and a delivery route only runs at all if enough money is riding on it. This document specifies the server that turns those competing constraints into a single deterministic pipeline — from publishing a catalog price, through a timed pre-order cycle, through what the supplier actually put on the truck, all the way to a posted ledger for every drop point and a commission cheque for the volunteer who ran it.

The platform is organised around the **pre-order cycle**. A cycle opens, members place orders against the drop point they collect from, and at the cutoff instant the club stops accepting writes and performs a single aggregation pass that binds prices, rounds member demand up or down to whole cases, tests every drop point against its minimum, tests every route against its minimum, and unwinds the orders that no longer have a truck to ride on. Everything downstream — receiving, shortage allocation, substitution, pallet building, payment capture, reconciliation — is a consequence of what that one pass decided. Because the pass is cascading, and because a failure five levels up can reach back down and cancel an order that had already qualified, the ordering of its effects is specified here as precisely as the arithmetic.

Two further commitments shape the design. First, **every cent is accounted for**: freight, the reefer surcharge, the supplier invoice, store credit and coordinator commission are all allocated by explicit largest-remainder schemes whose conservation properties are stated as invariants, and a drop point's books do not post until its ledger identity closes exactly. Second, **the system is deterministic and testable**: it runs on a logical clock rather than wall-clock time, every list has a pinned ordering, every threshold has a pinned inclusivity, and every validation has a pinned position in a single global evaluation ladder. Where two readings of a business rule are possible, this document pins one; where an operation could be performed twice from a stale view of the world, an optimistic-concurrency token makes the second attempt fail rather than silently overwrite.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, read or write, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is missing or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same code and status are returned when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the five roles this platform recognises.

The five roles are `ClubAdmin`, `DropCoordinator`, `Member`, `WarehouseOperator` and `FinanceOfficer`, spelled exactly so. A `ClubAdmin` runs the club: the catalog, the route network, the drop points, membership administration and the cycle lifecycle. `ClubAdmin` is a superset operator and is additionally authorised for every write operation any other role may perform, so wherever another role appears in the authorisation list of a write endpoint, `ClubAdmin` appears beside it. A `DropCoordinator` is a member who runs a drop point on the club's behalf. A `Member` is an ordering household. A `WarehouseOperator` runs receiving and consolidation. A `FinanceOfficer` runs the money. When the acting role is not listed among the roles the endpoint admits, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Every read endpoint in this specification admits all five roles.

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters because ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against stored foreign keys rather than against anything derived from the role name.

- When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references a member compare that resource's member foreign key to `X-User-ID`: a member order is owned by the member named in its `member_id`, an order line is owned through the member order it belongs to, and a substitution offer is owned through the order line it was raised against.
- When `X-User-Role` is `DropCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Member` row. A coordinator may act only on drop runs whose `coordinator_member_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and only while that member's `tier_rank` is at least 2.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClubAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule says so — as `invoice_recorded_by_user_id`, `recorded_by_user_id`, `verified_by_user_id`, `prepared_by_user_id` or `posted_by_user_id` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `WarehouseOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it is stored as `recorded_by_user_id` on a receipt line and as `invoice_recorded_by_user_id` on a route run, and is not compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FinanceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is stored as `prepared_by_user_id` when a drop reconciliation is drafted and as `posted_by_user_id` when one is posted, and the only comparison ever made against it is a comparison between those two values: segregation of duties is enforced on `X-User-ID`, not on role, so the officer who drafted a reconciliation may not be the one who posts it even though both actions sit inside the same role.

Two ownership failures are distinguished. A `Member` reaching a member order, order line or substitution offer that belongs to someone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_ORDER_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `DropCoordinator` reaching a drop run whose `coordinator_member_id` is not their `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_NOT_DROP_COORDINATOR` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer of at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and must be an integer of at least 0. A `limit` outside that range, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`; `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

The default ordering of every list endpoint is `created_at` ascending. Because several rules in this specification create more than one row inside a single request, and rows written in one request share a `created_at` value to the millisecond, the writing order of every such rule is pinned explicitly so that the resulting list order is total:

- Case aggregates are written in ascending `sku` order of the item they aggregate, during the cycle lock.
- Pallets are written class by class in the fixed order `FROZEN`, then `CHILLED`, then `DRY`, and within a class in ascending `sequence_index`, during the pallet build.
- Substitution offers are written in ascending `created_at` order of the order lines they are raised against, during the recording of the receipt that introduced the substitute.
- Order lines are created one per request, so ascending `created_at` is already a total order.
- Within one capture, the store-credit payment record is written before the card payment record.
- Credit grants are created one per request, or one per verified volunteer shift, so ascending `created_at` is already a total order.
- Commission accruals are written in ascending `created_at` order of the drop runs they belong to, during delivery.

Filters offered by a collection endpoint are combined conjunctively, and any enum-typed filter value outside that field's declared member list is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 400).

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Versioning

Every stored resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. All resources except one additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock reading on every mutation that bumps `version`.

The single exception is the price version. A published price is immutable and append-only: a `PriceVersion` row carries `id` and `created_at` and nothing else of the common set — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and no endpoint mutates one once written.

Version increments are not confined to the row the caller named. Many operations in this system mutate several rows at once, and each mutated row's own `version` advances by 1. Where a rule below says that a cascade recomputes a figure on a parent, that recomputation is a mutation of the parent and bumps the parent's `version`. This matters directly, because the optimistic-concurrency tokens described in §2.10 are compared against exactly those numbers.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time for business purposes. The server maintains a single logical clock which, before any client has set it, reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant, carried in the field `to_iso8601`, and responds with the new reading under the key `current_utc`.

Every clock-dependent quantity is derived at read time from the current clock reading rather than being frozen when it was last written. Three quantities in particular behave this way: a cycle's `exception_state`, a credit grant's spendability, and a member's dues grace position. Setting the clock forward therefore changes what a subsequent read reports, without any intervening write.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and Allocation Determinism

All money in this system is expressed as an integer number of cents; there are no fractional-cent fields anywhere. Wherever a rule says an amount is rounded, the rounding is **round half up** — half away from zero — to the whole cent, and each rule below states exactly how many roundings occur and at which step, because several of them would give different answers if the roundings were reordered or merged. Where a rule instead says a quantity is taken as a **ceiling** or a **floor**, that is an exact integer operation and not a rounding.

Pro-rata splits of a money total across several rows follow one shared scheme, referred to below as the **largest-remainder** scheme. Each recipient's exact share is computed as a rational number; each recipient is first given the whole-cent floor of its exact share; the number of cents still unallocated is the total less the sum of those floors; and those leftover cents are handed out one each, in descending order of the fractional part that was discarded by the floor, with ties broken by ascending `created_at` of the recipient row. The scheme distributes the total exactly: never one cent more, never one cent fewer. The same descending-remainder-then-ascending-`created_at` ordering is used when whole units rather than cents are being split.

Where any rule in this document requires an ordering and does not name one, the ordering is ascending `created_at`; nothing in this system ever falls back to ordering by `id`.

### 2.7. Boundary Semantics

Thresholds in this domain are deliberately not uniform, and the inclusivity of each one is fixed here. A reader who assumes that all minimums behave alike will get the route qualification rule wrong.

- **Drop minimum.** A drop run qualifies when its `subtotal_cents` is greater than or equal to the drop point's `drop_minimum_cents`. The boundary is inclusive: a drop landing exactly on its minimum qualifies.
- **Route minimum.** A route run qualifies when its `aggregated_subtotal_cents` is strictly greater than the route's `route_minimum_cents`. The boundary is exclusive: a route landing exactly on its minimum is deferred.
- **Cutoff.** Member order and order-line writes are permitted while the clock is strictly before the cycle's `cutoff_at`. The bound is exclusive at the top: at the cutoff instant itself, writes are already refused.
- **Price binding.** The price version bound to a line is the one with the greatest `effective_from` among those whose `effective_from` is at or before the cycle's `cutoff_at`. The comparison is inclusive: a price effective exactly at the cutoff instant binds.
- **Exception window.** A cycle's `exception_state` is `OPEN` while the clock is at or after `received_at` and strictly before `exception_deadline_at`. The window is half-open: inclusive at the bottom, exclusive at the top.
- **Substitution response.** A member's response to a substitution offer is accepted while the clock is strictly before the offer's `respond_by`.
- **Substitution tolerance.** A substitute is within tolerance when its `offered_unit_price_cents` is less than or equal to the `original_unit_price_cents` multiplied by 1.10 and rounded half up to the whole cent. Inclusive.
- **Payment variance.** A capture is inside its band when the actual total less the authorized amount is less than or equal to the tolerance. Inclusive.
- **Reconciliation variance.** A reconciliation is `BALANCED` when the absolute value of its `variance_cents` is at most 200 cents, and `VARIANCE_HELD` otherwise. Inclusive at 200.
- **Pallet capacity.** A unit may join the pallet currently being built when the resulting `weight_grams` would be at most 900000 and the resulting `cube_cm3` would be at most 1600000. Both caps are inclusive, so a unit that lands exactly on a cap still fits; only when one of them would be exceeded is a new pallet of that temperature class opened.
- **Reefer capacity.** A pallet plan is accepted when the count of cold pallets is at most the route's `reefer_slots`. Inclusive.
- **Credit offset cap.** The credit applied to an order is at most the floor of half its `pre_credit_total_cents`. Inclusive.
- **Credit expiry.** A credit grant is spendable while the clock is strictly before its `expires_at`.
- **Dues grace.** A member remains eligible while the clock is at or before `dues_paid_through` plus 14 days. Inclusive at the end of the grace period.
- **Volunteer obligation.** A drop run's volunteer obligation is met when its `verified_volunteer_hours` is at least 4.0. Inclusive.
- **Commission bands.** Band one applies when the commission base is at most 100000 cents; band two applies when the base is above 100000 and at most 300000 cents; band three applies when the base is above 300000 cents. Both band ceilings are inclusive.
- **Route turnaround.** A route may host a second cycle when the new cycle's `opens_at` is at or after the existing cycle's `cutoff_at` plus 48 hours. Inclusive at exactly 48 hours of separation.
- **Order line quantity.** A line's `qty_units` must be at least 1 and at most 500. Both ends inclusive.

### 2.8. Path and Body Reference Resolution

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved left to right, and the first one that resolves to nothing terminates the request. Which not-found code is returned is fixed per path segment and admits no interpretation: `member_id` yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `drop_point_id` yields `ERR_DROP_POINT_NOT_FOUND`, `item_id` yields `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `price_version_id` yields `ERR_PRICE_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `cycle_id` yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `route_run_id` yields `ERR_ROUTE_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `drop_run_id` yields `ERR_DROP_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `member_order_id` yields `ERR_MEMBER_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `order_line_id` yields `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `case_aggregate_id` yields `ERR_CASE_AGGREGATE_NOT_FOUND`, `receipt_line_id` yields `ERR_RECEIPT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `substitution_offer_id` yields `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `pallet_id` yields `ERR_PALLET_NOT_FOUND`, `payment_id` yields `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `drop_reconciliation_id` yields `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `commission_accrual_id` yields `ERR_COMMISSION_ACCRUAL_NOT_FOUND`, `volunteer_shift_id` yields `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, and `credit_grant_id` yields `ERR_CREDIT_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`. All eighteen carry HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved next, in the order the fields appear in that endpoint's declared input list. The mapping is the same one the path segments use, extended to the keys that only ever appear in a body: `route_id` yields `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`; `home_drop_point_id` and `drop_point_id` yield `ERR_DROP_POINT_NOT_FOUND`; `coordinator_member_id` and `member_id` yield `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`; `item_id` and `substitute_item_id` yield `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`; `cycle_id` and `target_cycle_id` yield `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`; `route_run_id` yields `ERR_ROUTE_RUN_NOT_FOUND`; `drop_run_id` and `target_drop_run_id` yield `ERR_DROP_RUN_NOT_FOUND`; `member_order_id` yields `ERR_MEMBER_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; and `case_aggregate_id` yields `ERR_CASE_AGGREGATE_NOT_FOUND`. These too carry HTTP 404.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder

Validation is a single global ladder, evaluated strictly in ascending rung order on every request. The first rung that fails ends the request with that rung's error and status; no later rung runs, and no side effect of the operation is performed. Rungs 9 through 13 are **write gates**: they are positioned deliberately above the state, ownership, concurrency and fiscal rungs so that a gate condition surfaces as a gate error rather than being masked by a more specific check further down. Rung 9 is the global write gate.

1. **Rung 1** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401.
2. **Rung 2** — `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401.
3. **Rung 3** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised role list. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403.
4. **Rung 4** — every id embedded in the path resolves to a stored row, checked left to right, using the per-segment mapping of §2.8. Applies to every endpoint with a path id. Failure: the mapped not-found code, HTTP 404.
5. **Rung 5** — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, checked in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input list, using the mapping of §2.8. Applies to every endpoint with foreign-key fields in its input. Failure: the mapped not-found code, HTTP 404.
6. **Rung 6** — `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0. Applies to every list endpoint. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, HTTP 400.
7. **Rung 7** — every field the endpoint declares as required is present and of the declared type. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400.
8. **Rung 8** — every enum-typed field carries a value from that field's declared member list. This covers `tier`, `new_tier`, `temp_class`, `remainder_policy`, `substitution_pref`, `payout_mode`, `destination`, `simulated_outcome`, and every enum-valued filter on a list endpoint including `status`, `payment_status`, `kind` and `source`. Applies to every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, HTTP 400.
9. **Rung 9 — GLOBAL WRITE GATE.** The cycle that the target resource resolves to does not have status `CLOSED`. The target resolves to a cycle directly for a cycle action, and indirectly through a route run, drop run, member order, order line, case aggregate, receipt line, substitution offer, pallet, payment, drop reconciliation, commission accrual or volunteer shift. This rung pre-empts every rung from 10 downwards, including all state, ownership, concurrency and fiscal checks. Applies to every mutating endpoint whose target resolves to a cycle. Failure: `ERR_CYCLE_CLOSED`, HTTP 409.
10. **Rung 10 — MEMBERSHIP GATE.** The member in scope — the acting member for a member-owned write, or the `member_id` supplied in the body — does not have status `LAPSED`. It pre-empts every order, line and substitution rung below it. Applies to opening a member order, creating an order line, patching an order line, cancelling an order line, submitting a member order, and approving or declining a substitution offer. Failure: `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_LAPSED`, HTTP 403.
11. **Rung 11 — COORDINATOR GATE.** When the acting role is `DropCoordinator`, that member's `tier_rank` is at least 2. It pre-empts every coordinator-scoped rung below it, including the coordinator ownership check at rung 27. Applies to opening a drop run, merging a drop run, rolling a drop run forward, logging a volunteer shift, and verifying or rejecting a volunteer shift. Failure: `ERR_COORDINATOR_INELIGIBLE`, HTTP 403.
12. **Rung 12 — POSTED LEDGER GATE.** The drop run in scope has no drop reconciliation with status `POSTED`. It pre-empts the whole money family below it. Applies to refunding a member order, recording a receipt line, drafting a drop reconciliation, resolving a held variance, and verifying a volunteer shift. Failure: `ERR_RECONCILIATION_POSTED`, HTTP 409.
13. **Rung 13 — CYCLE LOCK GATE.** For member order and order-line writes, the parent cycle has status `OPEN`. Substitution responses are the single carve-out: approving or declining an offer is permitted while the cycle has status `RECEIVED` and its `exception_state` is `OPEN`. This gate pre-empts the cutoff rung at 14 and every ordering rung from 30 downwards. Applies to opening a member order, creating an order line, patching an order line, cancelling an order line, submitting a member order, and cancelling a member order. Failure: `ERR_CYCLE_LOCKED`, HTTP 409.
14. **Rung 14** — for member order and line writes reaching a cycle that is still `OPEN`, the clock is strictly before the cycle's `cutoff_at`. Applies to opening a member order, creating an order line, patching an order line, cancelling an order line, and submitting a member order. Failure: `ERR_CUTOFF_PASSED`, HTTP 409.
15. **Rung 15** — the cycle is in a from-state that the requested transition declares. Applies to the cycle lock, submit, receive, consolidate, deliver, reconcile, close and cancel actions. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`, HTTP 409.
16. **Rung 16** — the member order is in a from-state the requested transition declares, on both the fulfilment axis and the payment axis. Applies to submitting, cancelling, authorizing, capturing and refunding a member order. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, HTTP 409.
17. **Rung 17** — the order line is in a from-state the requested transition declares. Applies to patching and cancelling an order line. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_LINE_STATE`, HTTP 409.
18. **Rung 18** — the drop run is in a from-state the requested transition declares: `OPEN` for a merge with the target also `OPEN`, `DEFERRED` for a roll-forward, and any of `OPEN`, `QUALIFIED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED` or `SETTLED` for logging a volunteer shift. Applies to merging a drop run, rolling one forward, and logging a volunteer shift. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_DROP_RUN_STATE`, HTTP 409.
19. **Rung 19** — the route run has status `RECEIVED`. Applies to recording a receipt line, recording the supplier invoice, and building pallets. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_ROUTE_RUN_STATE`, HTTP 409.
20. **Rung 20** — the substitution offer has status `PENDING`. Applies to approving and declining an offer. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`, HTTP 409.
21. **Rung 21** — the drop reconciliation is in a from-state the requested transition declares: `BALANCED` for posting, `VARIANCE_HELD` for resolving a variance. Applies to posting a reconciliation and resolving a variance. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_RECONCILIATION_STATE`, HTTP 409.
22. **Rung 22** — the commission accrual has status `ACCRUED` or `ADJUSTED`, and the drop run it belongs to has a drop reconciliation with status `POSTED`. Applies to paying out a commission accrual. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_ACCRUAL_STATE`, HTTP 409.
23. **Rung 23** — the volunteer shift has status `LOGGED`. Applies to verifying and rejecting a shift. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_STATE`, HTTP 409.
24. **Rung 24** — the pallet has status `BUILDING`. Applies to sealing a pallet. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_PALLET_STATE`, HTTP 409.
25. **Rung 25** — the optimistic-concurrency field the endpoint declares equals the stored `version` of the named parent. Applies to creating an order line, patching an order line, cancelling an order line, recording a receipt line, drafting a drop reconciliation, minting a credit grant, merging a drop run, paying dues, and changing tier. Failure: `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412.
26. **Rung 26** — when the acting role is `Member`, `X-User-ID` equals the `member_id` of the member order the target resolves to. This rung is deliberately ordered after the state rungs and after the concurrency rung, so a member touching another member's already-terminal line sees the state error rather than the ownership error. Applies to creating an order line, patching an order line, cancelling an order line, submitting a member order, cancelling a member order, and approving or declining a substitution offer. Failure: `ERR_NOT_ORDER_OWNER`, HTTP 403.
27. **Rung 27** — when the acting role is `DropCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` equals the `coordinator_member_id` of the drop run the target resolves to. Applies to merging a drop run, rolling one forward, logging a volunteer shift, and verifying or rejecting a shift. Failure: `ERR_NOT_DROP_COORDINATOR`, HTTP 403.
28. **Rung 28 — COLD CHAIN.** Either the item's `temp_class` is `DRY`, or the route serving the order's drop point has `reefer_slots` greater than 0. This rung is deliberately placed ahead of the discontinued-item check, so a frozen line ordered onto an ambient route reports the cold-chain failure even when the item is also discontinued. Applies to creating and patching an order line. Failure: `ERR_ROUTE_NO_COLD_CHAIN`, HTTP 422.
29. **Rung 29** — the number of pallets requiring a reefer does not exceed the route's `reefer_slots`. Applies to building pallets. Failure: `ERR_REEFER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409.
30. **Rung 30** — the total pallet count on the run does not exceed the route's `max_pallets`. Applies to building pallets. Failure: `ERR_PALLET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409.
31. **Rung 31** — the item has status `ACTIVE`. Applies to creating and patching an order line. Failure: `ERR_ITEM_DISCONTINUED`, HTTP 409.
32. **Rung 32** — at least one price version exists for the item with `effective_from` at or before the cycle's `cutoff_at`. Applies to creating and patching an order line. Failure: `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_PRICE`, HTTP 409.
33. **Rung 33** — `qty_units` is an integer between 1 and 500 inclusive, and when the item has `split_allowed` false, `qty_units` is an exact whole multiple of the item's `case_pack_units`. Applies to creating and patching an order line. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`, HTTP 422.
34. **Rung 34** — no other order line whose status is anything other than `CANCELLED` exists on this member order for the same `item_id`. Applies to creating an order line. Failure: `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_FOR_ITEM`, HTTP 409.
35. **Rung 35** — no other member order whose status is anything other than `CANCELLED` exists for the same combination of `member_id` and `drop_run_id`. Applies to opening a member order. Failure: `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORDER_FOR_MEMBER`, HTTP 409.
36. **Rung 36 — POINT-COLLISION INTERVAL RULE.** No other price version for this `item_id` carries the same `effective_from` instant. An item's price history is a partition of half-open intervals running from each `effective_from` up to but excluding the next one, and two versions sharing a left endpoint would make that partition ill-defined. Applies to publishing a price version. Failure: `ERR_PRICE_VERSION_OVERLAP`, HTTP 409.
37. **Rung 37 — BUFFERED-INTERVAL RULE.** Creating the first drop run for a given cycle and route mints a route run only when the route hosts no other route run whose cycle window collides with the new one. Two cycle windows collide when the new cycle's `opens_at` is strictly before the other cycle's `cutoff_at` plus 48 hours **and** the other cycle's `opens_at` is strictly before the new cycle's `cutoff_at` plus 48 hours. Unlike the point-collision rule at rung 36, mere adjacency is not enough here: a 48-hour turnaround must separate the two closed windows. Applies to opening a drop run. Failure: `ERR_ROUTE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409.
38. **Rung 38** — the cycle's `exception_state` is `OPEN` and the clock is strictly before the offer's `respond_by`. Applies to approving and declining a substitution offer. Failure: `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, HTTP 409.
39. **Rung 39** — the clock is at or after the cycle's `exception_deadline_at`. Applies to consolidating a cycle. Failure: `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_OPEN`, HTTP 409.
40. **Rung 40** — `target_drop_run_id` names a drop run other than this one, sitting on the same `route_run_id`, whose status is `OPEN`. Applies to merging a drop run. Failure: `ERR_MERGE_TARGET_INVALID`, HTTP 422.
41. **Rung 41** — the drop run's `rollforward_count` is below 1, and `target_cycle_id` names a cycle with status `OPEN` whose `cutoff_at` is strictly after the source cycle's `cutoff_at`. Applies to rolling a drop run forward. Failure: `ERR_ROLLFORWARD_LIMIT`, HTTP 409.
42. **Rung 42 — AGGREGATE CAPACITY RULE.** The units implied by `cases_received`, added to the aggregate's existing `units_received`, do not push `units_received` above the aggregate's `covered_units`. Unlike the two interval rules at rungs 36 and 37, this one polices a running total against a capacity rather than a geometry. Applies to recording a receipt line. Failure: `ERR_RECEIPT_EXCEEDS_ORDERED`, HTTP 422.
43. **Rung 43** — `substitute_case_price_cents` is present and at least 1 whenever `substitute_item_id` is present, and is absent whenever `substitute_item_id` is absent. Applies to recording a receipt line. Failure: `ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400.
44. **Rung 44** — every pallet on every route run of the cycle has status `SEALED`. Applies to consolidating a cycle. Failure: `ERR_PALLETS_NOT_SEALED`, HTTP 409.
45. **Rung 45** — the route run's `supplier_invoice_cents` is greater than 0, meaning the supplier invoice has already been recorded. Applies to building pallets. Failure: `ERR_SUPPLIER_INVOICE_MISSING`, HTTP 409.
46. **Rung 46 — SEGREGATION OF DUTIES.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the reconciliation's `prepared_by_user_id`. Applies to posting a drop reconciliation. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403.
47. **Rung 47** — the parent cycle has status `DELIVERED`. Applies to posting a drop reconciliation. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`, HTTP 409.
48. **Rung 48** — `adjustment_cents` equals the reconciliation's current `variance_cents` exactly. Applies to resolving a held variance. Failure: `ERR_VARIANCE_UNRESOLVED`, HTTP 422.
49. **Rung 49** — at least one route run on the cycle has status `QUALIFIED`. Applies to submitting a cycle to the supplier. Failure: `ERR_ROUTE_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`, HTTP 409.
50. **Rung 50** — every drop run on the cycle with status `SETTLED` has a drop reconciliation with status `POSTED`. Applies to reconciling a cycle. Failure: `ERR_DROP_RECONCILIATION_INCOMPLETE`, HTTP 409.
51. **Rung 51** — `apply_credit_cents`, when supplied, is at most the floor of half the order's `pre_credit_total_cents`. Applies to capturing a member order. Failure: `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422.
52. **Rung 52** — the capture variance is inside the tolerance band. Applies to capturing a member order. Failure: `ERR_PAYMENT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409.
53. **Rung 53** — `amount_cents` is at least 1 and at most the order's `captured_cents` less its `refunded_cents`. Applies to refunding a member order. Failure: `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_CAPTURE`, HTTP 422.
54. **Rung 54** — when `payout_mode` is `STORE_CREDIT`, the drop run's `volunteer_obligation_met` is true. A `payout_mode` of `CASH` carries no such requirement, so a coordinator who missed the volunteer obligation can still be paid, at the halved accrual. Applies to paying out a commission accrual. Failure: `ERR_VOLUNTEER_OBLIGATION_UNMET`, HTTP 409.
55. **Rung 55** — for a dues payment, `amount_cents` equals the annual dues of the member's tier multiplied by `term_years`; for a tier change, `prorated_amount_cents` equals the figure the proration rule produces. Applies to paying dues and to changing tier. Failure: `ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Four write operations in this system are legitimate on their own terms — a strictly later request would be allowed to perform them too — and therefore no state precondition catches a caller who is working from a stale picture of the world. Those four carry a mandatory optimistic-concurrency token naming the client's last observed `version` of a parent entity. The token is a required input field, not an optional one; omitting it fails the required-field rung at 7 rather than the concurrency rung at 25.

- `POST /order-lines` requires `expected_member_order_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `MemberOrder`. Because that order's `version` advances on every line create, line cancel and quantity change, a request whose `expected_member_order_version` no longer equals the order's stored `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so the client cannot add a second line on top of a picture it never saw.
- `POST /receipt-lines` requires `expected_route_run_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `RouteRun`. Every receipt line advances the run's `version` through the allocation cascade, so a stale `expected_route_run_version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /drop-reconciliations` requires `expected_drop_run_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `DropRun`. Drafting a reconciliation legitimately supersedes and voids any existing non-posted draft, so nothing else would stop a duplicate; the guard is the drop run's `version`, which advances whenever a refund, a verified shift or a further receipt changes the numbers being reconciled. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /credit-grants` requires `expected_member_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Member`. The member's `version` advances on every dues payment, tier change and credit-expiry sweep, so a stale `expected_member_version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Three further endpoints declare an optimistic-concurrency token as part of their own contract and are checked at the same rung: `PATCH /order-lines/{order_line_id}` and `POST /order-lines/{order_line_id}/cancel` both require `expected_member_order_version` against the parent member order; `POST /drop-runs/{drop_run_id}/merge` requires `expected_drop_run_version` against the drop run being merged; and `POST /members/{member_id}/dues-payments` and `POST /members/{member_id}/tier-changes` both require `expected_member_version` against the member. Every other endpoint in this specification takes no version field at all, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Six quantities are conserved by construction, and each names a forward action and the action that compensates it. They are stated here because they are the properties an implementation is most likely to break by accident, and because several allocation rules below are only correct if they hold.

1. **Units conserved on allocation.** For every case aggregate, the sum over its lines of `allocated_units` plus `short_units` equals the sum of their `confirmed_units`, and the sum of `allocated_units` equals the smaller of the aggregate's `units_received` and the total confirmed demand. Recording a receipt is the forward action; a later receipt that tops the aggregate up to full demand is the compensating one. No unit may be invented or lost by the largest-remainder split.
2. **Aggregate demand conserved on line reversal.** Creating an order line and then cancelling that same line before the cycle locks must leave both the case aggregate's `requested_units` for that route-run-and-item pair and the drop run's `subtotal_cents` exactly where they started.
3. **Member credit conserved on authorization void.** A member's `credit_balance_cents`, together with the sum of `remaining_cents` over that member's grants that have not expired, must be identical before an authorization and after that authorization is voided by cancelling the order. An authorization reserves nothing permanently.
4. **Reefer surcharge fully allocated.** Summing `reefer_alloc_cents` across every member order on a route run yields the run's `reefer_surcharge_cents` exactly — never one cent more and never one cent fewer than the pallet build computed.
5. **Freight fully allocated at both levels.** Summing `freight_alloc_cents` across a route run's drop runs yields the run's `freight_cents` exactly, and summing `freight_alloc_cents` across the member orders inside each drop run yields that drop run's `freight_alloc_cents` exactly. Rounding once per level must not leak a cent at either level.
6. **Drop ledger balances at post.** After a held variance is resolved, the reconciliation's ledger identity holds exactly and its `variance_cents` is 0; `member_charges_cents` is then equal to the sum of `supplier_cost_cents`, `freight_alloc_cents`, `reefer_alloc_cents`, `commission_cents` and `club_margin_cents`.


## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Joining the Club: Membership, Dues and Store Credit

A household joins by being registered by a club administrator, who supplies an `email`, a `full_name` and the `home_drop_point_id` of the drop point the household normally collects from. The `email` must be non-empty and unique across all members. A `tier` may be supplied; when it is omitted the member is registered at `BASIC`. The three tiers are `BASIC`, `PLUS` and `PRO`, and each carries two derived attributes that the server writes rather than accepts: `tier_rank`, which is 1 for `BASIC`, 2 for `PLUS` and 3 for `PRO`, and `discount_pct`, which is 0.00 for `BASIC`, 0.05 for `PLUS` and 0.09 for `PRO`. A newly registered member has status `PENDING`, a `lifetime_dues_paid_cents` of 0, a `credit_balance_cents` of 0, an `is_coordinator` flag of false, and a null `dues_paid_through` and `dues_grace_until`. The `is_coordinator` flag is derived, not set by hand: it is true exactly when at least one drop point names this member as its `coordinator_member_id`.

Membership becomes real when dues are paid. A dues payment carries `amount_cents`, a `term_years` count and `expected_member_version`, and it is accepted only when `amount_cents` equals the annual dues of the member's current `tier` multiplied by `term_years`. The annual dues are 3000 cents at `BASIC`, 9000 cents at `PLUS` and 18000 cents at `PRO`; a two-year `PRO` payment is therefore exactly 36000 cents, and anything else is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. On acceptance the server sets `dues_paid_through` to the current clock reading plus 365 days multiplied by `term_years`, sets `dues_grace_until` to `dues_paid_through` plus a further 14 days, adds `amount_cents` to `lifetime_dues_paid_cents`, runs the credit expiry sweep for this member, and moves the membership status to `ACTIVE`.

The same operation drives two different transitions, and the axis is therefore cyclic rather than a one-way pipeline. A `PENDING` member paying dues moves `PENDING → ACTIVE`. A member who had fallen to `LAPSED` and now pays moves `LAPSED → ACTIVE` — this is the reinstatement leg, and the new `dues_paid_through` is measured from the current clock reading, not from the stale `dues_paid_through` the member was carrying, so a member who lapsed a year ago and reinstates today gets a full term from today and no back-credit for the gap.

Lapsing is an explicit administrative act, not an automatic one, and it is only available once the grace period has genuinely expired: a member may be moved `ACTIVE → LAPSED` only when `dues_grace_until` is not null and the clock is strictly after it. While the clock is at or before `dues_grace_until`, the member is still inside grace and the lapse is refused. Once the member is `LAPSED`, the membership gate described at rung 10 of §2.9 refuses every subsequent order, line and substitution write for that member with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_LAPSED` until dues are paid again.

Changing tier is a self-loop: the status axis does not move, but the tier axis does. The request carries `new_tier`, a `prorated_amount_cents` and `expected_member_version`, and `new_tier` must differ from the member's current `tier`. The proration is computed as follows. Take the annual dues of the current tier and the annual dues of `new_tier`. If `dues_paid_through` is null, or the clock is at or after `dues_paid_through`, the remaining-days count is 0. Otherwise the remaining-days count is the **ceiling** of the number of days between the clock and `dues_paid_through`, so a member 200.4 days from expiry counts as 201 remaining days and the day on which the change is made counts as remaining. If the new tier's annual dues are less than or equal to the current tier's, the prorated amount is 0 — downgrades are never refunded. Otherwise the prorated amount is the difference between the two annual dues figures, multiplied by the remaining-days count, divided by 365, and rounded half up to the whole cent. The request is accepted only when `prorated_amount_cents` matches that figure exactly; otherwise it fails with `ERR_VALIDATION`. On acceptance the server rewrites `tier`, `tier_rank` and `discount_pct` to the new tier's values and adds `prorated_amount_cents` to `lifetime_dues_paid_cents`.

Downgrading has a consequence outside the membership record. When the new `tier_rank` falls below 2 and this member coordinates at least one drop point, every coordinator action on that drop point's drop runs is thereafter refused by the coordinator gate at rung 11 with `ERR_COORDINATOR_INELIGIBLE`. Nothing is retroactively undone; the member simply stops being able to act as a coordinator until their rank returns to 2 or above.

Store credit is held as a set of grants rather than as a single balance. A grant records the `member_id` who holds it, an `amount_cents` face value of at least 1, a `remaining_cents` unspent portion, an `expires_at` instant, a `source` and a `status`. The `source` is one of `VOLUNTEER`, `REFUND`, `COMMISSION` or `ADJUSTMENT`; a grant minted by an administrator directly carries `ADJUSTMENT`, and the other three are minted by the rules described later in this document. A grant minted from a refund also records the `source_member_order_id` of the order whose refund produced it; grants from every other source leave that field null. The `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED`, `CONSUMED` and `EXPIRED`, and a freshly minted grant is `ACTIVE` with `remaining_cents` equal to its `amount_cents`. A member's `credit_balance_cents` is not stored independently; it is the sum of `remaining_cents` over that member's grants whose status is `ACTIVE` or `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` and whose `expires_at` the clock has not yet reached.

Expiry is swept rather than scheduled. Before an administrator mints a further grant, before a capture consumes credit, before a dues payment is applied, and before a commission payout is made, the server sweeps that member's grants: every grant whose `expires_at` the clock has reached or passed moves to `EXPIRED`. An expiring grant's `remaining_cents` is left untouched as a record of what was forfeited, but the grant is thereafter excluded from `credit_balance_cents` and from every future draw. A grant sweeps from `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` if it had never been drawn on, and from `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED → EXPIRED` if it had, in which case the unspent remainder is simply forfeited. Minting a grant runs the sweep first, then creates the grant, then raises the member's `credit_balance_cents`, and bumps the member's `version` — which is why the mint carries `expected_member_version` and why two clients cannot both mint against the same observed member state.

### 3.2. The Catalog and the Delivery Network

A route models a truck run. It carries a unique non-empty `code`, a human-readable `name`, a `reefer_slots` count of refrigerated pallet positions which defaults to 2 and may not be negative, a `route_minimum_cents` which defaults to 250000 and may not be negative, and a `max_pallets` total of pallet positions across all temperature classes which defaults to 12 and must be at least 1. From `reefer_slots` the server derives `cold_capable`, which is true exactly when `reefer_slots` is greater than 0; a route that is not cold-capable may not carry frozen or chilled goods at all.

Drop points hang off routes. Creating one requires the `route_id` it is served by and a `name`, and optionally a `coordinator_member_id` naming the member who will run it and a `drop_minimum_cents` which defaults to 50000. Naming a coordinator has a side effect on the member record: that member's `is_coordinator` becomes true. Drop points may be listed filtered by `route_id` or by `coordinator_member_id`.

Catalog items describe what can be bought. An item carries a unique non-empty `sku`, a display `name`, a `temp_class` of `FROZEN`, `CHILLED` or `DRY`, a `case_pack_units` count of at least 1 giving how many member-facing units the supplier packs into one case, a `unit_weight_grams` of at least 1, a `unit_cube_cm3` of at least 1, a `split_allowed` flag defaulting to true, and a `remainder_policy` defaulting to `SHORT_FILL`. The `remainder_policy` is one of `SHORT_FILL`, `ROUND_UP` and `DROP`, and it decides what the club does with the last partial case of aggregated demand — the three behaviours are specified in §3.4. An item's `status` starts at `ACTIVE`; retiring an item moves it `ACTIVE → DISCONTINUED`, which is terminal. Retirement is forward-looking only: order lines that have already reached `CONFIRMED` are untouched, and only *new* order lines against the item are refused, with `ERR_ITEM_DISCONTINUED`. Items may be listed filtered by `sku`, by `temp_class` or by `status`.

Prices are effective-dated and immutable. Publishing a price supplies the `item_id`, an `effective_from` instant and a `case_price_cents` of at least 1, and the server additionally derives `split_unit_base_cents` as the ceiling of `case_price_cents` divided by the item's `case_pack_units` — the per-unit price before any split-case fee, rounded **up** to the whole cent rather than to nearest. A price version, once written, is never mutated; a price change is a new row with a later `effective_from`. Two rows for the same item may not share an `effective_from` instant, because an item's price history is read as a partition of half-open intervals and a shared left endpoint would make the partition ambiguous; the duplicate is refused with `ERR_PRICE_VERSION_OVERLAP`. Price versions may be listed filtered by `item_id`, and each one is individually readable.

Field-level shape constraints in this section — a non-empty `code`, `sku` or `email`, a `case_pack_units` of at least 1, a non-negative `reefer_slots`, a `max_pallets` of at least 1, a `case_price_cents` of at least 1 — are enforced by the shape rung at position 7 and reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`.

### 3.3. Opening a Cycle and Taking Member Orders

A pre-order cycle is created with a unique non-empty `code`, an `opens_at` instant and a `cutoff_at` instant which must be strictly after `opens_at`; a `cutoff_at` at or before `opens_at` is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new cycle starts in status `OPEN`, with `locked_at`, `received_at` and `exception_deadline_at` all null, and with `exception_state` initialised to `NOT_STARTED`. The nine cycle statuses are `OPEN`, `LOCKED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED`, `CONSOLIDATED`, `DELIVERED`, `RECONCILED`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. The `exception_state` is derived from the clock on every read and takes one of three values: `NOT_STARTED` before `received_at` exists, `OPEN` while the clock sits inside the half-open window running from `received_at` up to but excluding `exception_deadline_at`, and `CLOSED` once the clock reaches `exception_deadline_at`.

A drop point participates in a cycle through a **drop run**, opened by naming the `cycle_id` and the `drop_point_id`. Opening the first drop run for a given cycle and route also mints the cycle's **route run** for that route, with status `PLANNED` — but only if the route's calendar allows it. Two cycles collide on a route when the new cycle's `opens_at` falls strictly before the other cycle's `cutoff_at` plus 48 hours and, symmetrically, the other cycle's `opens_at` falls strictly before the new cycle's `cutoff_at` plus 48 hours; a collision is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A truck needs 48 hours of turnaround, so two windows that merely abut are still a conflict; only a genuine 48-hour separation clears the rule. The new drop run starts in status `OPEN` with a `subtotal_cents` of 0, a `rollforward_count` of 0, a null `merged_into_drop_run_id`, and a `coordinator_member_id` that is a **snapshot** of the drop point's `coordinator_member_id` taken at drop-run creation — later changes to the drop point do not rewrite it, and it may be null if the drop point had no coordinator. Drop runs may be listed filtered by `cycle_id`, `drop_point_id`, `route_run_id`, `coordinator_member_id`, `merged_into_drop_run_id` or `status`; route runs may be listed by `cycle_id`, `route_id` or `status`.

The nine drop-run statuses are `OPEN`, `QUALIFIED`, `MERGED`, `DEFERRED`, `ROLLED_FORWARD`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED`, `SETTLED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `MERGED`, `ROLLED_FORWARD`, `SETTLED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. A route run's seven statuses are `PLANNED`, `QUALIFIED`, `DEFERRED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED`, `CONSOLIDATED` and `DELIVERED`, of which `DEFERRED` and `DELIVERED` are terminal.

A coordinator whose drop is not going to make its minimum may fold it into a neighbour while the cycle is still `OPEN`. Merging supplies a `target_drop_run_id` and `expected_drop_run_version`, and requires that the acting member is this drop run's `coordinator_member_id` with a `tier_rank` of at least 2, and that the target names a *different* drop run with status `OPEN` sitting on the same `route_run_id` — a target that is the source itself, sits elsewhere, or is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_MERGE_TARGET_INVALID`. The merge is a four-hop cascade, each hop caused by the one above it. First, the source moves `OPEN → MERGED`, its `merged_into_drop_run_id` is set to the target and its own `subtotal_cents` is zeroed. Second, because the source has emptied, every member order that pointed at it is repointed to the target drop run. Third, because the target has gained those orders, the target's `subtotal_cents` and `discounted_subtotal_cents` are recomputed. Fourth, because the target's subtotal moved, the route run's `aggregated_subtotal_cents` is recomputed. Both the source and the target have their `version` incremented by 1.

Members order against a drop run. Opening an order supplies the `member_id` and the `drop_run_id`; the order is created with status `DRAFT` on the fulfilment axis and `UNPAID` on the money axis, and both axes move independently thereafter. The eight fulfilment statuses are `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `LOCKED`, `FILLED`, `SHORT_FILLED`, `DELIVERED`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, with `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` terminal; the seven payment statuses are `UNPAID`, `AUTHORIZED`, `AUTH_FAILED`, `CAPTURED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, `REFUNDED` and `VOIDED`, with `VOIDED` and `REFUNDED` terminal. A member may hold only one non-cancelled order per drop run: a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORDER_FOR_MEMBER`. Orders may be listed filtered on either axis, by `member_id`, `drop_run_id`, `status` or `payment_status`.

Lines are added to an order one item at a time. A line supplies its `member_order_id`, the `item_id`, a `qty_units` between 1 and 500 inclusive, and `expected_member_order_version`; it may also supply `rollover_opt_in`, which defaults to false and decides whether unfilled units may be carried into a later cycle, and `substitution_pref`, which defaults to `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` and is one of `NONE`, `ANY_SUBSTITUTE` and `APPROVAL_REQUIRED`. When the item has `split_allowed` false, `qty_units` must additionally be an exact whole multiple of the item's `case_pack_units`; a quantity outside the range or violating the multiple rule is refused with `ERR_INVALID_QUANTITY`. Only one non-cancelled line per item is permitted on an order — a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_FOR_ITEM` — and a member wanting more of the same item raises the quantity on the existing line instead. Cold goods may not be ordered onto an ambient route: unless the item's `temp_class` is `DRY`, the route serving the order's drop point must have a positive `reefer_slots`, and a violation is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_NO_COLD_CHAIN`. An item that has no price version effective at or before the cycle's `cutoff_at` cannot be ordered at all, and the attempt fails with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_PRICE`.

A new line is created in status `REQUESTED` with `bound_case_price_cents`, `unit_price_cents`, `split_fee_per_unit_cents`, `confirmed_units`, `allocated_units`, `short_units`, `allocation_remainder_rank` and `line_gross_cents` all at 0, `is_split_unit` false, and a null `rolled_from_order_line_id`. Prices are deliberately *not* bound at line creation — they bind at the cycle lock — but the drop run's `subtotal_cents` is nevertheless kept live at the prices currently in force, so a coordinator watching the drop's progress toward its minimum sees the effect of each line immediately. The eight line statuses are `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `FILLED`, `SHORT_FILLED`, `SUBSTITUTED`, `DROPPED`, `ROLLED_FORWARD` and `CANCELLED`, with `FILLED`, `DROPPED`, `ROLLED_FORWARD` and `CANCELLED` terminal. Lines may be listed filtered by `member_order_id`, `item_id`, `rolled_from_order_line_id` or `status`.

While the cycle is `OPEN` and the clock is strictly before `cutoff_at`, a member may amend a `REQUESTED` line — changing its `qty_units`, its `rollover_opt_in` or its `substitution_pref` — or withdraw it entirely, moving it `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`. Both operations carry `expected_member_order_version`, both recompute the drop run's `subtotal_cents`, and both bump the versions of the line and of its owning order. A cancellation returns the drop run's `subtotal_cents` to precisely the value it held before the line existed. Committing a draft moves the order `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and requires that the acting member owns it, that their membership status is `ACTIVE`, and that the order carries at least one line in status `REQUESTED`; submission recomputes the drop run's `subtotal_cents` from the order's `REQUESTED` lines at the prices currently in force. A member may also withdraw the whole order while the cycle is still `OPEN`, from either `DRAFT → CANCELLED` or `SUBMITTED → CANCELLED`; in both cases every line on the order moves `REQUESTED → CANCELLED` and the drop run's `subtotal_cents` is recomputed. Withdrawing a `SUBMITTED` order additionally voids any successful authorization on it, as described in §3.8.

### 3.4. The Cutoff: Locking a Cycle and Aggregating It Into Whole Cases

Locking is the pivot of the whole system. It is available to a club administrator while the cycle has status `OPEN`; attempting it from any other status fails with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. The cycle moves `OPEN → LOCKED`, `locked_at` is stamped with the current clock reading, and a six-hop cascade runs. Each hop is caused by the hop above it rather than by the lock itself, and the hops must be applied in order, because every one of them consumes a figure the previous hop wrote.

**Hop 1 — orders settle.** Every member order on this cycle with status `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → CANCELLED`, and every one of its lines moves `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`; a member who filled a basket and never committed it simply does not participate. Every member order with status `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → LOCKED`.

**Hop 2 — lines confirm and prices bind.** Because an order moved to `LOCKED`, each of its lines with status `REQUESTED` moves `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`. Confirming binds the price. Among all price versions for the line's item, consider those whose `effective_from` is at or before the cycle's `cutoff_at`; the bound one is whichever of those has the greatest `effective_from`. Because `effective_from` is unique per item, no tie-break is needed. If no such price version exists the confirmation cannot proceed and `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_PRICE` is raised. Note carefully that the comparison instant is the cycle's `cutoff_at` and **not** the moment the member added the line: a price published after a member created their line, but before the cutoff, still binds that line. The bound version's `case_price_cents` is written to the line as `bound_case_price_cents`.

From that bound case price the unit price is derived. First take the **base**, the ceiling of `bound_case_price_cents` divided by the item's `case_pack_units` — a ceiling to the whole cent, applied *before* anything else. If the line's `qty_units` is an exact whole multiple of `case_pack_units`, then `is_split_unit` is false, `split_fee_per_unit_cents` is 0, and `unit_price_cents` is the base. Otherwise the member is buying a broken case: `is_split_unit` is true, `split_fee_per_unit_cents` is the greater of 25 cents and the base multiplied by 0.12 rounded half up to the whole cent, and `unit_price_cents` is the base plus that fee. The order of operations is pinned and is not commutative — the ceiling on the base happens first, the twelve-percent fee is computed from the already-ceilinged base, and only then are the two added. Computing the fee against the unrounded per-unit figure in a single step gives a different answer: for a case price of 10000 cents over a three-unit case, the pinned order yields 3734 and the collapsed one yields 3733. Finally, `confirmed_units` is set equal to `qty_units`, subject to the remainder policy in hop 3.

**Hop 3 — demand is rounded into whole cases.** Because lines confirmed, the server creates one **case aggregate** per combination of route run and item. The lines feeding an aggregate are the `CONFIRMED` lines for that item whose member order sits on a drop run with status `OPEN` belonging to that route run, taken in ascending `created_at` order; lines on deferred or merged drop runs are excluded, because a merge repointed their orders before the lock. The aggregate records its `route_run_id`, its `item_id`, the `requested_units` as the sum of `qty_units` over those lines, the `case_price_cents` the price binding produced, the `policy_applied` copied from the item's `remainder_policy` as it stood at this moment, and a `units_received` that starts at 0. The whole-case count is `requested_units` divided by `case_pack_units` discarding any fraction, and `remainder_units` is what is left over — recorded on the aggregate before the policy resolves it.

When `remainder_units` is 0 the demand divides evenly: `cases_ordered` is the whole-case count, `overage_units` is 0, `dropped_units` is 0, and no line is touched. Otherwise the item's `policy_applied` decides between three behaviours, and each is specified to the same depth:

- Under `SHORT_FILL`, `cases_ordered` stays at the whole-case count, `overage_units` is 0 and `dropped_units` is 0. Every line keeps its `confirmed_units` equal to its `qty_units`, so the aggregate is deliberately short of member demand; the shortfall is resolved later, at receiving, by the shortage rule described in §3.6.
- Under `ROUND_UP`, `cases_ordered` is the whole-case count plus one, `overage_units` is the item's `case_pack_units` less `remainder_units`, and `dropped_units` is 0. The overage is not spread: it is charged whole to exactly one line, namely the line with the greatest `qty_units`, with ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. That line's `confirmed_units` becomes its own `qty_units` plus the whole `overage_units`, and the aggregate's `overage_order_line_id` records which line it was. Every other line is untouched.
- Under `DROP`, `cases_ordered` stays at the whole-case count, `overage_units` is 0 and `dropped_units` equals `remainder_units`. The units are taken back from members starting at the **latest** line: walking the feeding lines in descending `created_at` order, each line surrenders the smaller of its own `qty_units` and the shortfall still outstanding, its `confirmed_units` becomes its `qty_units` less what it surrendered, the outstanding shortfall falls by the same amount, and the walk stops as soon as the shortfall reaches 0. Any line whose `confirmed_units` is reduced to 0 by this walk moves `CONFIRMED → DROPPED`, with its `line_gross_cents` also at 0.

In every branch, the aggregate's `covered_units` is `cases_ordered` multiplied by the item's `case_pack_units`. Case aggregates are readable individually and listable filtered by `route_run_id`, `item_id` or `overage_order_line_id`.

**Hop 4 — drops qualify.** Because `confirmed_units` has settled, each member order's `estimated_total_cents` is recomputed and each drop run's `subtotal_cents` and `discounted_subtotal_cents` are recomputed. A drop run's `subtotal_cents` is the sum, over its member orders with status `LOCKED` and over their lines with status `CONFIRMED`, of `confirmed_units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`; orders cancelled in hop 1 contribute nothing. Its `discounted_subtotal_cents` is that figure less the sum of `tier_discount_cents` across those same orders. Qualification then reads the **pre-discount** figure: a drop run with status `OPEN` moves `OPEN → QUALIFIED` when its `subtotal_cents` is greater than or equal to its drop point's `drop_minimum_cents`, and `OPEN → DEFERRED` otherwise. A drop sitting exactly on its minimum in list terms therefore qualifies, even if a `PLUS` member's five-percent discount would push the discounted figure below it. A drop run that defers takes its orders down with it: every member order on it moves `LOCKED → CANCELLED` and every line on those orders moves `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, and its subtotal is excluded from the route aggregate computed in the next hop.

**Hop 5 — routes qualify.** Because drop qualification has settled, each route run's `aggregated_subtotal_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `subtotal_cents` over the drop runs on it whose status is `OPEN` or `QUALIFIED`. Before the lock this same field is maintained live so that a merge is immediately observable; at hop 5 no drop run on the run is still `OPEN`, so in practice the sum reads exactly the qualified set. The route run then moves `PLANNED → QUALIFIED` when `aggregated_subtotal_cents` is **strictly greater** than the route's `route_minimum_cents`, and `PLANNED → DEFERRED` otherwise. The asymmetry with hop 4 is deliberate and is the single most common misreading of this system: a *drop* landing exactly on its minimum qualifies, while a *route* landing exactly on its minimum is deferred. Deferred drop runs contribute nothing to the sum, and a merged drop run contributes nothing of its own — its orders are already counted inside the run it merged into. A qualifying route run keeps its qualified drop runs and their orders exactly as they stand.

**Hop 6 — a deferred route reaches back down.** Because a route run moved to `DEFERRED`, every drop run on it that had already reached `QUALIFIED` reverts `QUALIFIED → DEFERRED`, every member order on those drop runs moves `LOCKED → CANCELLED`, and every line on those orders moves `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`. This is the revert leg: a drop that made its own minimum is pulled back because a sibling aggregate failed. No payment can exist at this point on this path — an authorization requires the order to already be `LOCKED`, which only this same request achieves — so no authorization is voided here. The authorization-voiding leg belongs to cycle cancellation, described in §3.5, and not to the lock.

The cycle's own `version` is incremented by 1 by the lock, as is the `version` of every route run, drop run, member order, order line and case aggregate the cascade touched.

### 3.5. When a Drop Misses: Deferral, Roll-Forward and Abandoning a Cycle

A deferred drop run is not necessarily a lost one. Its coordinator — or an administrator — may carry the opted-in demand into a later cycle. Rolling forward requires that the drop run has status `DEFERRED`, that the acting member is its `coordinator_member_id` with a `tier_rank` of at least 2, that the drop run's `rollforward_count` is still below 1, and that the supplied `target_cycle_id` names a cycle with status `OPEN` whose `cutoff_at` is strictly after the source cycle's `cutoff_at`. A violation of either the count limit or the target-cycle condition is refused with `ERR_ROLLFORWARD_LIMIT`.

The roll-forward is a five-hop cross-cycle cascade. First, the source drop run's `rollforward_count` is incremented by 1 and it moves `DEFERRED → ROLLED_FORWARD`. Second, because the run rolled, a new drop run with status `OPEN` is created on the target cycle for the same drop point, attaching to the target cycle's route run for that drop point's route, or minting one if the target cycle has none yet; the new drop run **inherits** `rollforward_count` from the source rather than starting at 0, so a chain of deferrals can be rolled forward only once in total and never repeatedly. Third, because the new drop run exists, one new member order with status `DRAFT` is created on it for each member whose cancelled order on the source run carried at least one line with `rollover_opt_in` true. Fourth, because those orders exist, one new order line with status `REQUESTED` is created for each such line, with `rolled_from_order_line_id` pointing at the line it came from. The new line's `qty_units` is the source line's `short_units` when the source line was `SHORT_FILLED`, and the source line's own `qty_units` otherwise. Nothing about the old price is carried across: the new line will be priced at the target cycle's cutoff, under the new cycle's prices. Fifth, because the source lines have been consumed, each source line with `rollover_opt_in` true moves to `ROLLED_FORWARD` — from `SHORT_FILLED → ROLLED_FORWARD` for a line that was partially filled, and from `CANCELLED → ROLLED_FORWARD` for a line that the drop's deferral had already cancelled. The deferral cancels lines first, and the roll-forward then promotes exactly the opted-in ones back out of `CANCELLED`.

Abandoning a cycle outright is available to a club administrator while the cycle has status `OPEN` or `LOCKED`, and the two paths carry an identical set of consequences. The cycle moves to `CANCELLED`. Every route run on it moves to `DEFERRED` — from `PLANNED → DEFERRED` on the open path, and from either `PLANNED → DEFERRED` or `QUALIFIED → DEFERRED` on the locked path. Every drop run whose status is `OPEN` or `QUALIFIED` moves to `CANCELLED`. Every member order whose status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `LOCKED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and every line on those orders whose status is `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED`. Finally — and this is the part the lock cascade cannot reach — every successful authorization payment on those orders moves to `VOIDED` and the order's `payment_status` becomes `VOIDED`.

Closing a cycle is the opposite end of the same lifecycle. Once a cycle has reached `RECONCILED`, a club administrator closes it: every member order with status `DELIVERED` moves `DELIVERED → CLOSED`, and the global write gate at rung 9 activates, so that from then on every mutating request whose target resolves to this cycle fails with `ERR_CYCLE_CLOSED` before any other business rule is even considered.

### 3.6. Sending, Receiving, Allocating Shortages and Handling Substitutes

Submission sends the qualified route runs to the supplier. It requires the cycle to be `LOCKED` and at least one route run on it to have status `QUALIFIED`; a cycle whose every route run was deferred at the route minimum cannot be submitted and fails with `ERR_ROUTE_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. On success the cycle moves `LOCKED → SUBMITTED`, every route run with status `QUALIFIED` moves `QUALIFIED → SUBMITTED`, and every drop run with status `QUALIFIED` moves `QUALIFIED → SUBMITTED`.

Arrival is recorded against the cycle as a whole. Receiving requires the cycle to be `SUBMITTED`; it moves the cycle `SUBMITTED → RECEIVED`, stamps `received_at` with the clock reading, sets `exception_deadline_at` to `received_at` plus 48 hours, and thereby flips the derived `exception_state` to `OPEN`, because the clock now sits inside the half-open exception window. Every route run with status `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → RECEIVED` and every drop run with status `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → RECEIVED`. Receipt lines, the supplier invoice and pallet building all become available on a run only once it is `RECEIVED`; attempting any of them earlier or later fails with `ERR_INVALID_ROUTE_RUN_STATE`.

A **receipt line** records what the supplier physically delivered against one aggregate. It carries the `route_run_id`, the `case_aggregate_id`, a `cases_received` count of at least 0, and `expected_route_run_version`; it may also carry a `substitute_item_id` when the supplier shipped something else, in which case a `substitute_case_price_cents` of at least 1 must accompany it — the pairing is strict in both directions, and a substitute price supplied without a substitute item, or a substitute item supplied without its price, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The server derives `units_received` on the receipt line as `cases_received` multiplied by the `case_pack_units` of the item actually received, which is the substitute when one is named and the ordered item otherwise, and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `recorded_by_user_id`. The aggregate's own `units_received` is the running sum of `units_received` over the receipt lines against it, and no receipt may push that running total above the aggregate's `covered_units`; an over-receipt is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_EXCEEDS_ORDERED`. Receipt lines may be listed filtered by `route_run_id`, `case_aggregate_id` or `substitute_item_id`, and each is individually readable.

Recording a receipt runs the **shortage allocation** over the aggregate's lines. The participating lines are those feeding the aggregate whose status is `CONFIRMED` or `SHORT_FILLED` and whose `confirmed_units` is above 0; dropped and cancelled lines are excluded from the demand entirely and receive nothing. Total demand is the sum of `confirmed_units` across those lines, and supply is the aggregate's cumulative `units_received`. When supply is at least total demand, every line receives `allocated_units` equal to its `confirmed_units` and an `allocation_remainder_rank` of 0. When supply falls short, each line's exact share is its `confirmed_units` multiplied by supply and divided by total demand, treated as an exact rational; each line first receives the whole-unit floor of that share; the leftover is supply less the sum of those floors; the lines are ranked in descending order of the fractional part discarded by the floor, with ties broken by ascending `created_at`; and the first however-many lines in that ranking, one for each leftover unit, each receive one extra unit. Each line's `allocation_remainder_rank` is its one-based position in that ranking. In every case `short_units` is `confirmed_units` less `allocated_units`, and the sum of `allocated_units` across the aggregate equals the smaller of supply and total demand exactly — no unit is invented and none is lost. The allocation deliberately spreads the pain rather than filling early lines to the brim and starving late ones.

Allocation moves the lines and, through them, the orders. A line whose `allocated_units` reached its `confirmed_units` moves `CONFIRMED → FILLED`, with `short_units` at 0 and `line_gross_cents` written as `allocated_units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`. A line allocated more than 0 but fewer than its `confirmed_units` moves `CONFIRMED → SHORT_FILLED`. A line allocated 0 moves `CONFIRMED → DROPPED`, with `short_units` equal to its `confirmed_units` and `line_gross_cents` at 0. A later receipt line topping the aggregate up can move a line back `SHORT_FILLED → FILLED`, setting `short_units` to 0. Correspondingly, a member order moves `LOCKED → FILLED` when every one of its lines has `allocated_units` equal to `confirmed_units`, and `LOCKED → SHORT_FILLED` when at least one line falls short; and an order can move `SHORT_FILLED → FILLED` when a later receipt raises every line to its full `confirmed_units`. The fulfilment axis can therefore move *backwards* toward `FILLED` as later receipts arrive. Every one of these transitions recomputes the order's `gross_cents`, `split_fee_cents`, `tier_discount_cents` and `pre_credit_total_cents`, and the affected drop runs and route run recompute their `received_subtotal_cents` — the pre-discount value of what was actually allocated, summed as `allocated_units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`.

When a receipt names a `substitute_item_id`, the server raises one **substitution offer** per affected line, written in ascending `created_at` order of those lines. An offer records the `order_line_id` it applies to, the `receipt_line_id` that introduced the substitute, the `substitute_item_id`, the `offered_units` of the substitute allocated to that line, the line's `original_unit_price_cents` as bound before substitution, and an `offered_unit_price_cents` derived from `substitute_case_price_cents` by exactly the same base-then-split-fee rule that priced the original line. It also records `within_tolerance`, which is true when `offered_unit_price_cents` is at most `original_unit_price_cents` multiplied by 1.10 and rounded half up to the whole cent, and a `respond_by` instant set to the offer's `created_at` plus 24 hours. Offers may be listed filtered by `order_line_id`, `receipt_line_id`, `substitute_item_id` or `status`.

Most offers never dwell. The line's own `substitution_pref` decides. If the preference is `ANY_SUBSTITUTE`, or it is `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` and `within_tolerance` is true, the offer resolves `PENDING → AUTO_APPROVED` inside the very request that created it, and the line moves `CONFIRMED → SUBSTITUTED` at the offered price. If the preference is `NONE`, the offer resolves `PENDING → AUTO_DECLINED` in the same request: the substitute units are not allocated at all, the line keeps its original price, and it is short by the `offered_units`. Only an `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` line facing an out-of-tolerance price leaves an offer sitting in `PENDING` for the member to answer. When that happens the allocation's arithmetic on that line stands — its `allocated_units`, `short_units`, `allocation_remainder_rank` and `line_gross_cents` keep the values the shortage allocation just wrote, so the aggregate's unit conservation is undisturbed — but the *status* the allocation wrote does not stand: because `FILLED` is terminal and the member's answer is declared as a move out of `CONFIRMED`, a line carrying a pending offer is put back to the status it held immediately before this receipt's allocation pass, which is `CONFIRMED` for a line no earlier receipt had part-filled and `SHORT_FILLED` for one that an earlier receipt had. A line sitting at `CONFIRMED` with a pending offer is treated as having allocated nothing by the figures that value what was received: it contributes 0 to its order's `gross_cents` and `split_fee_cents` exactly as §3.8 already provides for `CONFIRMED` lines, it contributes nothing to any drop run's or route run's `received_subtotal_cents`, and it puts no units into the pallet plan or the reefer split of §3.7 — its units enter those figures only once the offer is answered and the line lands in `SUBSTITUTED`, `SHORT_FILLED` or `DROPPED`. Its `allocated_units` are nonetheless real elsewhere: the line still participates in a later receipt's shortage allocation, and the owning order's fulfilment status is settled from `allocated_units` against `confirmed_units` in the ordinary way, so an order all of whose lines are fully allocated reaches `FILLED` even while one of them is holding a pending offer. A line put back to `SHORT_FILLED` is subject to none of these exclusions and counts at its new `allocated_units` throughout.

A member answers inside the exception window. Approving requires that the acting member owns the offer's line through its member order, that the cycle's `exception_state` is `OPEN`, and that the clock is strictly before the offer's `respond_by`; a response arriving outside either bound is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and an offer that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE`. Approval moves the offer `PENDING → APPROVED`, moves the line `CONFIRMED → SUBSTITUTED` at `offered_unit_price_cents` with `allocated_units` set to the offer's `offered_units`, and recomputes the owning order's `gross_cents`, `tier_discount_cents` and `pre_credit_total_cents`. Declining moves the offer `PENDING → DECLINED`, removes the `offered_units` from the line's `allocated_units`, moves the line to `SHORT_FILLED` or to `DROPPED` according to whether anything is left allocated to it, and recomputes the owning order's `gross_cents` and `pre_credit_total_cents`. Both responses are permitted even though the cycle is no longer `OPEN`: this is the single carve-out in the cycle-lock gate at rung 13, and it holds only while the cycle is `RECEIVED` with its `exception_state` `OPEN`.

Silence declines. When the exception window closes and the cycle is consolidated, every offer still in `PENDING` moves `PENDING → AUTO_DECLINED`, its `offered_units` are removed from the line's `allocated_units`, and the line moves to `SHORT_FILLED` or `DROPPED` accordingly — exactly as an explicit decline would have done.

### 3.7. Consolidation: The Invoice, the Pallet Plan and Delivery

Once a run has been received, the warehouse records what the supplier billed. The invoice submission carries `supplier_invoice_cents`, the whole-case cost the supplier charged for this run, and `freight_cents`, the freight billed for it; both are written onto the route run along with `invoice_recorded_by_user_id`, taken from the acting `X-User-ID`. Recording the invoice immediately allocates the freight down two levels.

At **level one**, the route run's `freight_cents` is split across its drop runs whose status is `RECEIVED`, weighted by each drop run's `subtotal_cents`. Each drop run's exact share is `freight_cents` multiplied by its own `subtotal_cents` and divided by the sum of those subtotals; each takes the whole-cent floor of that share; the leftover cents are the route total less the sum of the floors; the drop runs are ranked in descending order of the discarded fraction with ties broken by ascending `created_at`; and the first however-many drop runs in that ranking, one for each leftover cent, each take one extra cent. The result is written as each drop run's `freight_alloc_cents`.

At **level two**, each drop run's `freight_alloc_cents` is split across its member orders whose status is `FILLED` or `SHORT_FILLED`, weighted by each order's `gross_cents`, using the identical floor-then-largest-remainder scheme with ascending member-order `created_at` as the tie-break. The result is written as each order's `freight_alloc_cents`. The rounding happens **once per level**: level two divides the already-rounded integer that level one wrote onto the drop run, and does not go back and re-divide the route total. Computing each order's share directly from the route total in a single pass gives a different answer whenever a drop-level remainder was carried, and that reading is not the one this system implements. Both levels conserve exactly: the drop runs' `freight_alloc_cents` sum to the run's `freight_cents`, and inside each drop run the orders' `freight_alloc_cents` sum to that drop run's `freight_alloc_cents`.

Pallet building comes next, and it is refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_INVOICE_MISSING` unless the run's `supplier_invoice_cents` is already greater than 0. The plan is built one temperature class at a time, in the fixed order `FROZEN`, then `CHILLED`, then `DRY`. Within a class, the server takes every **allocated** unit on the run belonging to a line whose item sits in that class — a line's `short_units` contribute nothing, and dropped or cancelled lines contribute nothing — expands them one row per unit, and orders them by ascending `sku` of the item and then by ascending `created_at` of the order line. It opens a pallet with `sequence_index` 1, `weight_grams` 0 and `cube_cm3` 0, and walks the units. A unit joins the current pallet when adding it would leave `weight_grams` at most 900000 **and** `cube_cm3` at most 1600000; both caps are inclusive, so a unit landing exactly on a cap still fits. Otherwise nothing is sealed — the server simply opens the next pallet of that class with `sequence_index` incremented by one and puts the unit there. A class with no units produces no pallets at all. Each pallet records its `route_run_id`, its `temp_class`, its running `weight_grams` and `cube_cm3`, a `unit_count` of the units loaded onto it, a `requires_reefer` flag that is true exactly when its `temp_class` is `FROZEN` or `CHILLED`, and a `status` that starts at `BUILDING`.

Two capacity checks then guard the plan. If the count of pallets requiring a reefer exceeds the route's `reefer_slots`, the build is refused with `ERR_REEFER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; if the total pallet count across all classes exceeds the route's `max_pallets`, it is refused with `ERR_PALLET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the run's `cold_pallet_count` is written as the number of pallets requiring a reefer, `dry_pallet_count` as the number of ambient ones, `reefer_slots_used` as exactly the `cold_pallet_count` (each cold pallet occupies one slot on the truck), and `reefer_surcharge_cents` as `reefer_slots_used` multiplied by 4500 cents per slot.

That surcharge is then pushed down onto the members who caused it, and the weight is money rather than mass. For each member order on the run with status `FILLED` or `SHORT_FILLED`, the server computes that order's cold value as the sum, over its lines whose item is `FROZEN` or `CHILLED`, of `allocated_units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`; cancelled orders are excluded. The run's `reefer_surcharge_cents` is then split across those orders in proportion to their cold values, by the same floor-then-largest-remainder scheme, ranking on the discarded fraction descending with ascending member-order `created_at` as the tie-break, and each order's share is written as its `reefer_alloc_cents`. Each drop run's `reefer_alloc_cents` is the sum of its orders' shares. A member ordering cheap but heavy frozen goods therefore pays less than one ordering expensive light frozen goods, and the split distributes every cent of `reefer_surcharge_cents` exactly.

Pallets are sealed individually by the warehouse while the parent cycle is `RECEIVED`, moving each `BUILDING → SEALED`; sealing anything not in `BUILDING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PALLET_STATE`. Pallets may be listed filtered by `route_run_id`, `temp_class` or `status`, and each is individually readable.

Consolidation closes the exception window. It requires the cycle to be `RECEIVED`, requires the clock to be at or after `exception_deadline_at` — an attempt before then is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_OPEN` — and requires every pallet on every route run of the cycle to have status `SEALED`, failing with `ERR_PALLETS_NOT_SEALED` otherwise. On success every still-`PENDING` substitution offer auto-declines as described in §3.6, the cycle moves `RECEIVED → CONSOLIDATED`, and every route run with status `RECEIVED` moves `RECEIVED → CONSOLIDATED`.

Delivery is the settlement event. From `CONSOLIDATED`, the cycle moves `CONSOLIDATED → DELIVERED`; every pallet with status `SEALED` moves `SEALED → DELIVERED`; every route run with status `CONSOLIDATED` moves `CONSOLIDATED → DELIVERED`; every drop run with status `RECEIVED` moves `RECEIVED → SETTLED`; every member order with status `FILLED` or `SHORT_FILLED` moves to `DELIVERED`; and one commission accrual is created for each settled drop run that has a non-null `coordinator_member_id`, as described in §3.9.

### 3.8. The Money Axis: Authorization, Capture, Credit and Refunds

The payment axis of a member order runs independently of its fulfilment axis, and every movement on it mints a `Payment` record. A payment carries the `member_order_id` it belongs to, a `kind` of `AUTHORIZATION`, `CAPTURE` or `REFUND`, a `method` of `CARD` or `STORE_CREDIT` which defaults to `CARD`, a positive `amount_cents`, and a `status` of `SUCCEEDED`, `FAILED` or `VOIDED`. Payments are minted directly into `SUCCEEDED` or `FAILED` by the operations below; the only transition a payment ever undergoes afterwards is `SUCCEEDED → VOIDED`, and only an `AUTHORIZATION` is eligible for it. Payments may be listed filtered by `member_order_id`, `kind` or `status`, and each is individually readable.

**Authorizing** is available once the order's fulfilment status is `LOCKED`, `FILLED` or `SHORT_FILLED`. The first successful authorization on an order — the leg that moves the payment axis `UNPAID → AUTHORIZED` — additionally requires the parent cycle's status to be `LOCKED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED`, `CONSOLIDATED` or `DELIVERED`; the failing leg `UNPAID → AUTH_FAILED` and the retry leg `AUTH_FAILED → AUTHORIZED` declare no cycle-status condition of their own beyond the global write gate. The request may carry `simulated_outcome`, which is either `SUCCEEDED` or `FAILED` and defaults to `SUCCEEDED`; it exists so that a failed instrument can be exercised deterministically. The amount is a forward-looking estimate. The estimated gross is the sum, over the order's lines with status `CONFIRMED`, `FILLED`, `SHORT_FILLED` or `SUBSTITUTED`, of `confirmed_units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`. The estimated discount is that gross multiplied by the member's `discount_pct` and rounded half up to the whole cent. `estimated_total_cents` is the estimated gross less that discount, and `authorized_amount_cents` is `estimated_total_cents` multiplied by 1.10 — a ten-percent buffer — rounded half up to the whole cent. The estimate is built on `confirmed_units`, which is what the club intends to deliver; freight and the reefer surcharge are unknown at authorization time and are deliberately left out, and the buffer is what covers them.

On a `SUCCEEDED` outcome the server creates a payment of kind `AUTHORIZATION`, method `CARD`, status `SUCCEEDED` and `amount_cents` equal to `authorized_amount_cents`, writes `authorized_amount_cents` onto the order, and moves the payment axis `UNPAID → AUTHORIZED`. On a `FAILED` outcome it creates a payment of kind `AUTHORIZATION` with status `FAILED` and `amount_cents` equal to the same computed figure, leaves the order's `authorized_amount_cents` at 0, and moves the axis `UNPAID → AUTH_FAILED`. A retry after a failure is explicitly supported: a second successful attempt creates a second `AUTHORIZATION` payment with status `SUCCEEDED`, writes `authorized_amount_cents`, and moves the axis `AUTH_FAILED → AUTHORIZED`, which is what makes this axis cyclic rather than a one-way pipeline.

An authorization reserves nothing permanently. Whenever the order moves to `CANCELLED` — by the member withdrawing a submitted order, or by the lock's hop 6, or by cycle cancellation — every successful `AUTHORIZATION` payment on it moves `SUCCEEDED → VOIDED`, the order's `payment_status` becomes `VOIDED`, and its `authorized_amount_cents` returns to 0, releasing anything the authorization had notionally held. A member's credit position must be byte-identical before the authorization and after the void.

**Capturing** happens after receiving, and is available while the order's fulfilment status is `FILLED`, `SHORT_FILLED` or `DELIVERED` and the parent cycle's status is `CONSOLIDATED` or `DELIVERED`. The capture computes the order's money in a pinned seven-step sequence which is not commutative, because the credit cap depends on a total that itself depends on the discount:

1. `gross_cents` is the sum, over the order's lines with status `FILLED`, `SHORT_FILLED` or `SUBSTITUTED`, of `allocated_units` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`. Lines in `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `DROPPED`, `ROLLED_FORWARD` or `CANCELLED` contribute 0.
2. `split_fee_cents` is the sum over those same lines of `allocated_units` multiplied by `split_fee_per_unit_cents` — the portion of `gross_cents` that is split-case fee rather than product.
3. `tier_discount_cents` is `gross_cents` multiplied by the member's `discount_pct`, rounded half up to the whole cent. There is exactly **one** rounding, applied to the order-level gross, not one rounding per line. On a `PLUS` order of three lines at 3734, 3734 and 3735 cents, the order-level rule discounts 560 while a per-line rule would discount 561; the order-level figure is the correct one.
4. `pre_credit_total_cents` is `gross_cents` less `tier_discount_cents`, plus `freight_alloc_cents`, plus `reefer_alloc_cents`.
5. The credit cap is the floor of `pre_credit_total_cents` multiplied by 0.50 — store credit may never cover more than half an order.
6. The amount wanted is `apply_credit_cents` when the capture request supplies it, and the credit cap otherwise. `credit_applied_cents` is the smallest of the amount wanted, the credit cap, and the credit the member can actually produce. Supplying an `apply_credit_cents` above the cap is not silently clamped — it is rejected at rung 51 with `ERR_CREDIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
7. `actual_total_cents` is `pre_credit_total_cents` less `credit_applied_cents`, and that is the amount the card capture attempts.

The credit the member can produce is drawn by a **soonest-expiry-first** rule, run immediately after the credit expiry sweep. The eligible grants are those of this member whose status is `ACTIVE` or `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` and whose `expires_at` the clock has not yet reached; expired and fully consumed grants are skipped. They are ordered by ascending `expires_at` and then by ascending `created_at`, so the credit closest to being lost is spent first — draining by grant age instead would spend different grants and leave a different `remaining_cents` behind whenever an older grant expires later than a newer one. Walking that order, each grant surrenders the smaller of its own `remaining_cents` and the amount still wanted; its `remaining_cents` falls by what it surrendered; it moves `ACTIVE → CONSUMED` or `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED → CONSUMED` when nothing is left, and `ACTIVE → PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` when something is; and the walk stops as soon as the wanted amount has been taken. The total taken is `credit_applied_cents`. Across a member's whole history, the sum of grant `amount_cents` always equals the sum of `remaining_cents` plus everything ever drawn plus everything forfeited on expiry.

Before any of this is committed, the capture is tested against its **variance band**. The variance is `actual_total_cents` less `authorized_amount_cents`. The tolerance is the greater of 500 cents and `authorized_amount_cents` multiplied by 0.15 and rounded half up to the whole cent. The capture proceeds when the variance is less than or equal to the tolerance, and is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` otherwise. A negative variance — the club capturing less than it authorized — is always inside the band. This band exists precisely because the authorization figure and the capture figure are computed from different inputs: the authorization uses `confirmed_units` and excludes freight and the reefer surcharge, while the capture uses `allocated_units` and includes both, so identical order-time inputs legitimately produce two different numbers and the band polices the gap between them.

On success the capture creates up to two payment records: one of kind `CAPTURE` with method `STORE_CREDIT` and `amount_cents` equal to `credit_applied_cents` when that figure is above 0, written **first**, and one of kind `CAPTURE` with method `CARD` and `amount_cents` equal to `actual_total_cents` when that figure is above 0, written second. The order's `captured_cents` becomes `credit_applied_cents` plus `actual_total_cents`, the consumed grants are moved as described above, and the payment axis moves `AUTHORIZED → CAPTURED`.

**Refunding** carries an `amount_cents` and a `destination` of either `CARD` or `STORE_CREDIT`. It is available while the order's `payment_status` is `CAPTURED` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, and only while the drop run's reconciliation has not been posted — once a reconciliation is `POSTED`, rung 12 refuses the refund with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_POSTED`. The `amount_cents` must be at least 1 and at most `captured_cents` less `refunded_cents`; outside that range the refund fails with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_CAPTURE`. A refund of strictly less than the outstanding captured balance moves the axis `CAPTURED → PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`; a refund of exactly the outstanding balance moves it `CAPTURED → REFUNDED`, and a later refund clearing the remainder moves it `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED → REFUNDED`.

The refund is a four-hop cascade. First, a payment of kind `REFUND` and status `SUCCEEDED` is created for `amount_cents`. Second, because that payment exists, the order's `refunded_cents` rises by the same amount and its `payment_status` moves as just described; when the `destination` is `STORE_CREDIT` a credit grant with `source` `REFUND` is minted for `amount_cents`, expiring 180 days after the current clock reading, its `source_member_order_id` set to this order, and the member's `credit_balance_cents` rises accordingly. Third and fourth, because the member's net charge has fallen, the coordinator's commission and the drop's ledger both move — those two hops are specified in §3.9.

### 3.9. Volunteers, Coordinator Commission and Closing the Books

Running a drop point is unpaid work done by members, and the club recognises it twice — once as store credit for the volunteer, and once as a multiplier on the coordinator's commission. A shift is logged against a `drop_run_id` for a `member_id` with an `hours` figure, which must be greater than 0 and is recorded in quarter-hour increments. Logging is permitted while the drop run's status is `OPEN`, `QUALIFIED`, `SUBMITTED`, `RECEIVED` or `SETTLED`, and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DROP_RUN_STATE` in any other state. A logged shift starts in status `LOGGED`, with a null `verified_by_user_id` and a null `credit_grant_id`; logging alone mints no credit and leaves the drop run's `verified_volunteer_hours` untouched. Shifts may be listed filtered by `drop_run_id`, `member_id`, `credit_grant_id` or `status`.

Verification is the act that counts, and it is a four-hop cascade. First, the shift moves `LOGGED → VERIFIED` and `verified_by_user_id` is written from the acting `X-User-ID`. Second, because a shift became verified, the drop run's `verified_volunteer_hours` is recomputed as the sum of `hours` over its shifts with status `VERIFIED`, and `volunteer_obligation_met` is re-evaluated as true exactly when that sum is at least 4.0; the same hop mints a credit grant for the volunteering member with `source` `VOLUNTEER`, an `amount_cents` equal to the shift's `hours` multiplied by 1200 cents per hour and rounded half up to the whole cent, and an `expires_at` 180 days after the current clock reading, links it back through the shift's `credit_grant_id`, and raises the member's `credit_balance_cents`. Third, because `volunteer_obligation_met` may have flipped to true, any commission accrual on that drop run with status `ACCRUED` recomputes its `volunteer_multiplier_applied` to 1.00, recomputes `accrued_cents` and `net_cents`, and moves `ACCRUED → ADJUSTED`. Fourth, because `net_cents` changed, any drop reconciliation on that drop run that is not `POSTED` recomputes its `commission_cents` and `variance_cents`, and moves `BALANCED → VARIANCE_HELD` when the absolute value of `variance_cents` now exceeds 200 cents. Verification is only permitted while the acting member is the drop run's `coordinator_member_id` with a `tier_rank` of at least 2, or the actor is a club administrator, and only while the drop run's reconciliation has not been posted.

Rejecting a shift is the sibling operation and is specified to the same depth. It requires the same actor conditions and the same `LOGGED` starting status, moves the shift `LOGGED → REJECTED`, and writes `verified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. No credit grant is minted, the drop run's `verified_volunteer_hours` is unchanged, `volunteer_obligation_met` is not re-evaluated, and no commission or reconciliation recompute follows.

Commission itself is accrued at delivery, one accrual per settled drop run carrying a non-null `coordinator_member_id`, written in ascending `created_at` order of the drop runs. The accrual records its `drop_run_id`, its `coordinator_member_id`, and a `base_subtotal_cents` copied from the drop run's `received_subtotal_cents` at accrual time — the value of what was actually delivered, at list prices, **before** any tier discount. The band is selected by that base and applied **flat**, not marginally: a base of at most 100000 cents takes a `rate_applied` of 0.03, a base above 100000 and at most 300000 cents takes 0.05, and a base above 300000 cents takes 0.07, and the whole base is then multiplied by that single rate. The `volunteer_multiplier_applied` is 1.00 when the drop run's `volunteer_obligation_met` is true and 0.50 when it is not — a drop that never mustered four verified volunteer hours halves its coordinator's pay. `accrued_cents` is `base_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `rate_applied` multiplied by `volunteer_multiplier_applied`, with exactly one rounding half up applied to the whole product rather than one per factor. `clawback_cents` starts at 0 and `net_cents` is `accrued_cents` less `clawback_cents`. Because the base is what was delivered rather than what was ordered, a shortage shrinks the commission automatically and the clawback stays at 0 unless a refund lands afterwards. New accruals start in status `ACCRUED`; the three statuses are `ACCRUED`, `ADJUSTED` and `PAID`, with `PAID` terminal, and there is no path that voids an accrual because an accrual is only ever created after delivery. Accruals may be listed filtered by `drop_run_id`, `coordinator_member_id` or `status`.

This is where hops three and four of the refund cascade land. Because the member's net charge fell, the drop run's commission accrual recomputes `clawback_cents` as the refunded `amount_cents` multiplied by the accrual's `rate_applied` multiplied by its `volunteer_multiplier_applied`, rounded half up to the whole cent, recomputes `net_cents` as `accrued_cents` less `clawback_cents`, and moves `ACCRUED → ADJUSTED`. Then, because `net_cents` changed, the drop run's drop reconciliation — provided it is not `POSTED` — recomputes `commission_cents` and `member_charges_cents`, recomputes `variance_cents`, and moves `BALANCED → VARIANCE_HELD` when the absolute value of `variance_cents` now exceeds 200 cents.

Paying a coordinator is available only once the drop run's reconciliation has status `POSTED` and the accrual is in `ACCRUED` or `ADJUSTED`; it moves the accrual to `PAID` from either state, computing the payout on whichever `net_cents` is current. The request carries `payout_mode`, which is `STORE_CREDIT` or `CASH`, and the two modes are genuinely different offers. Under `STORE_CREDIT`, `payout_value_cents` is `net_cents` multiplied by 1.10, rounded half up to the whole cent — a ten-percent bonus — and a credit grant with `source` `COMMISSION` is minted for that value, expiring 180 days after the current clock reading. Under `CASH`, a fee is computed as the greater of 100 cents and `net_cents` multiplied by 0.02 rounded half up to the whole cent, `payout_value_cents` is `net_cents` less that fee, and no credit grant is minted. On a `net_cents` of 3600, the two modes yield 3960 and 3528 from identical inputs. The store-credit mode is permitted only when the drop run's `volunteer_obligation_met` is true, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_OBLIGATION_UNMET`; the cash mode carries no such condition, so a coordinator who missed the obligation can still be paid, at the halved accrual. The payout also runs the credit expiry sweep for the coordinator first.

A **drop reconciliation** is the drop point's ledger. Drafting one names the `drop_run_id` and carries `expected_drop_run_version`. Drafting first voids any existing reconciliation on the same drop run whose status is `BALANCED` or `VARIANCE_HELD`, moving it to `VOID`; superseding is a normal operation, which is exactly why the drop run's `version` is the only guard against a caller working from a stale picture. The new draft records `prepared_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, leaves `posted_by_user_id` and `posted_at` null, and computes six figures:

- `member_charges_cents` is the sum, over the drop run's member orders whose `payment_status` is `CAPTURED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` or `REFUNDED`, of `captured_cents` less `refunded_cents`. Orders that are `UNPAID`, `AUTHORIZED`, `AUTH_FAILED` or `VOIDED` contribute 0.
- `supplier_cost_cents` is this drop run's share of its route run's `supplier_invoice_cents`, allocated by the same floor-plus-largest-remainder scheme that level one of the freight allocation uses, weighted by `subtotal_cents` across the route run's `RECEIVED` drop runs.
- `freight_alloc_cents` and `reefer_alloc_cents` are copies of the drop run's own values at draft time.
- `commission_cents` is the `net_cents` of this drop run's commission accrual, or 0 when there is none.
- `club_margin_cents` is `member_charges_cents` less `supplier_cost_cents`, less `freight_alloc_cents`, less `reefer_alloc_cents`, less `commission_cents`, plus any adjustment later absorbed by a variance resolution.
- `variance_cents` is `member_charges_cents` less the sum of `supplier_cost_cents`, `freight_alloc_cents`, `reefer_alloc_cents`, `commission_cents` and `club_margin_cents`.

The draft never rests in `DRAFT`. Inside the very request that creates it, it auto-branches: `DRAFT → BALANCED` when the absolute value of `variance_cents` is at most 200 cents, and `DRAFT → VARIANCE_HELD` otherwise. A held reconciliation can return to balance without being recreated: resolving a variance carries an `adjustment_cents` which must equal the current `variance_cents` exactly — anything else is refused with `ERR_VARIANCE_UNRESOLVED` — adds it to `club_margin_cents` so that `variance_cents` becomes 0, and moves the reconciliation `VARIANCE_HELD → BALANCED`. The reverse direction also exists: a refund or a verified volunteer shift can push a `BALANCED` reconciliation back to `VARIANCE_HELD`, as described above.

Posting closes the drop's books. It requires the reconciliation to be `BALANCED`, requires the parent cycle to have status `DELIVERED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` — and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the `prepared_by_user_id` that drafted it, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when it does not. Posting moves the reconciliation `BALANCED → POSTED`, writes `posted_by_user_id` and stamps `posted_at` with the clock reading, and activates the posted-ledger gate at rung 12 for this drop run: refunds, receipt lines, commission payouts, volunteer verification and further reconciliation drafts on it are all thereafter refused with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_POSTED`. The five reconciliation statuses are `DRAFT`, `BALANCED`, `VARIANCE_HELD`, `POSTED` and `VOID`, with `POSTED` and `VOID` terminal, and reconciliations may be listed filtered by `drop_run_id` or `status`.

The cycle's own books close last. A finance officer or administrator reconciles a `DELIVERED` cycle, moving it `DELIVERED → RECONCILED`, but only once every drop run on it with status `SETTLED` carries a reconciliation with status `POSTED`; otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_DROP_RECONCILIATION_INCOMPLETE`. From `RECONCILED` the administrator closes the cycle, as described at the end of §3.5, and the global write gate takes effect.
