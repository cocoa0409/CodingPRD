# Building Trades Hiring Hall Dispatch API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A local building-trades union operates a hiring hall: the single lawful doorway between out-of-work members and signatory contractors who need manpower. The hall's authority rests entirely on the credibility of its books. When a member signs an out-of-work list, that signature buys a position in a queue, and the member's willingness to keep answering the phone depends on believing that the queue is real — that the dispatcher cannot quietly reach past twelve people to place a friend, that a traveler from a sister local cannot leapfrog a local journeyworker on a long call, and that an apprentice cannot be sent out without the journeyworker headcount the agreement requires alongside. Today this trust is maintained on paper, in a bound register and a drawer of carbon-copy dispatch slips. Every grievance over an improper dispatch turns into an argument about handwriting.

This document specifies the API for replacing that register. The system is mounted under `/hiring-hall` and it is, at heart, an allocation engine wrapped in an audit trail. The allocation half computes who is next: it filters the registrants who are actually dispatchable, ranks them under the ordering rule the list group carries, and refuses to issue a referral to anyone other than the top-ranked candidate unless the dispatcher writes down why. The audit half retains a snapshot of that ranked candidate list at the moment each referral leaves the counter, so that when a member grieves five weeks later the business manager can reconstruct the decision instead of relitigating it. Two consequences of retention are load-bearing rather than incidental: a grievance filed too late is refused, and a grievance whose underlying dispatch record has aged out of the retention period cannot be sustained on an out-of-order theory at all.

The domain is unforgiving about time and about arithmetic. Acceptance windows close, re-sign intervals lapse, recall windows expire, refusal strikes roll off after a year, and re-registration after a removal is barred for a cooling-off period. Every one of those deadlines is evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that the hall's behaviour is reproducible and testable. Money and hours are integers throughout — cents and whole hours, never floating point — and the order in which rounding is applied is part of the specification, not an implementation detail. Readers should treat every threshold, boundary inclusivity, and rounding step in this document as contractual.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. There is no session, no token exchange, and no login endpoint; the headers are the whole of authentication. If either header is absent, or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not spell one of the five recognized roles exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check runs before anything else — before body parsing, before resource lookup, before any business rule.

The five roles are `HallAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, `BusinessManager`, `ContractorRep`, and `Member`. A `HallAdmin` is the hall's office administrator: it maintains locals, list groups, the member books, skill endorsements, contractor records and the apprentice-ratio schedule, drives the logical clock, and runs the housekeeping sweep. A `Dispatcher` works the out-of-work lists and is the only role permitted to issue a referral. A `BusinessManager` is the elected officer who reviews and decides grievances, awards make-whole remedies, and rules on member standing and contractor signatory status; the role never issues referrals, which is what makes adjudication structurally separable from dispatch. A `ContractorRep` acts for one signatory employer. A `Member` is a registered journeyworker, apprentice, or traveler acting on their own book. Once the caller is authenticated, the acting role must appear in the authorized-role list published for the requested endpoint in §4; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403.

Two of the five roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is a real primary key and ownership is checked against it. When `X-User-Role` is `ContractorRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Contractor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a representative may open, cancel, lay off against, and recall against its own job requests and dispatches only. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a member may sign the list, re-sign, and answer a referral only for itself. A violation of either ownership rule yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403. Grievance filing is the deliberate exception: a grievant challenges a referral issued to somebody else, so no ownership comparison is applied at filing time, and the grievance's `grievant_member_id` is taken from `X-User-ID` rather than from the request body.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `HallAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Dispatcher`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity — the server records it as `issued_by_user_id` on each `Referral` the dispatcher issues, but it does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `BusinessManager`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity — the server records it as `decided_by_user_id` on each `Grievance` the manager decides, but it does not compare it to any entity's `id`. Those two recorded identifiers exist for one purpose, described in §2.10: a small local's officers wear several hats, and the hall must be able to prove that the person who decided a grievance was not the person who issued the referral being challenged. When they coincide, adjudication is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 further characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`, giving a 24-character value overall.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the only instant in the system read from real wall-clock time: it records when the response was built and is deliberately **not** taken from the logical hall clock of §2.5, so it does not move when a `HallAdmin` sets that clock and it does not match the `created_at` or `updated_at` values in the same response.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses — 4xx and 5xx alike — follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. Its wording is never contractual; only `error_id` and the HTTP status are.
- Failures raised by the transport layer before any endpoint body runs are shaped in this same envelope rather than in framework-native HTML: a request whose path matches no endpoint answers HTTP 404, and a request whose method the matched path does not accept answers HTTP 405, each carrying `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with the transport layer's own explanatory sentence as `message`. Both are decided by routing alone, so they are returned even when the two authentication headers of §2.1 are absent, and they keep their own 404 or 405 status rather than the 422 that §5 lists against `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A path that differs from a documented path only by a trailing slash is routed to that documented path instead of being refused. Any unexpected server fault answers HTTP 500 in the same envelope, again with `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound of any kind — not even the size of the collection. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 422 rather than being silently clamped. Either parameter supplied as an empty string — `?limit=` — is treated as though it had not been supplied at all and takes its default; any other value that is not a plain optionally-signed run of digits, including a decimal such as `5.0` or a value carrying a leading `+`, is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Surrounding whitespace is trimmed before the digits are read. An `offset` at or beyond the number of matching rows is perfectly legal and answers HTTP 200 with an empty `data` array, `total_count` reporting the true row count and `has_more` false.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two collections carry a documented exception, stated where they are discussed: out-of-work list groups are returned in `priority_rank` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and retention-ledger entries are returned in `recorded_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Because `created_at` is read from the logical hall clock of §2.5, every row created without moving the clock carries the same `created_at` to the millisecond, so ties on it are ordinary rather than exceptional. Wherever two rows tie on every sort component this document names — in a list page, in the candidate rankings of §3.2, in the ordered cascades of §3.1, §3.4, §3.5 and §3.7, and in the schedule selection of §3.6 — the tie is broken deterministically and finally by the order in which the hall recorded the rows, earliest recorded first. That recording order is an internal sequence, not a field of any resource, and it is never the row's `id`: identity is not a sort component anywhere in this system.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns, and the Two Reduced-Shape Records

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted random UUID, rendered in the canonical lowercase 36-character hyphenated form, with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a creation body is not honoured. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional digits, fixed at the moment of creation and never rewritten.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation that changes any stored field, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. At creation `updated_at` is present and equal to `created_at` rather than null, and every refresh of it reads the logical hall clock of §2.5, so a mutation applied without moving the clock leaves `updated_at` numerically unchanged while still incrementing `version`. The resources carrying the full set of four — `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version` — are `Local`, `Member`, `OutOfWorkList`, `Registration`, `Contractor`, `JobRequest`, `Referral`, `Dispatch`, `Grievance`, and `RatioPolicy`. Representations are of fixed shape: every field §3 names for a resource, whether stored or computed, appears on every read and every write response for that resource, and a field that has no value at the moment of serialization is emitted as JSON `null` rather than omitted from the object. Nothing beyond the fields §3 names for a resource is ever emitted, so a value the client supplied only as a precondition or only as free-text commentary does not reappear on the resource.

Two record types are deliberately reduced. A skill endorsement carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because an endorsement is a sighting of a certification card rather than a mutable object. A retention-ledger entry likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is an append-only audit row. The one field a ledger entry ever loses is its `snapshot_json` payload, which the housekeeping sweep nulls out when the row passes its retention horizon; that purge does not create an `updated_at` or a `version` on the row.

### 2.5. The Logical Hall Clock, and How Windows Close

Every deadline, expiry, window, retention horizon and ordering comparison in this system reads a single server-held logical UTC clock. The clock never falls back to real wall-clock time. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `HallAdmin` may move it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting operation, which stores that instant and answers HTTP 200 with a `data` object holding exactly one key, `current_utc`, echoing the instant as stored; the call changes no stored entity and creates none, and it is accepted whether the supplied instant lies ahead of or behind the clock's present reading — the clock may be wound backwards as freely as forwards — but every subsequent request evaluates every temporal rule against the new instant. All instants are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, all comparisons are exact to the millisecond, and all day-denominated durations in this document are exact multiples of twenty-four hours. Reading an instant out of a request is deliberately tolerant, and the tolerance is contractual: the value must be a JSON string, but it may end in `Z`, may carry a numeric UTC offset which the server converts to UTC before storing, or may carry no zone designator at all, in which case it is read as already being UTC; a date-only value such as `2026-08-01` is read as midnight UTC on that date. Precision finer than a millisecond is **truncated** toward zero rather than rounded, so a supplied `2026-08-02T03:04:05.6789Z` is stored, compared and echoed as `2026-08-02T03:04:05.678Z`. A string that cannot be read as an instant at all is a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Boundary inclusivity differs between windows and is never incidental. **The acceptance window** on a referral runs for 24 hours from issuance and is open while the clock is at or before the referral's `acceptance_deadline`; an answer arriving exactly at the deadline is still in time, and expiry requires the clock to be strictly greater than the deadline. **A registration's outstanding-referral occupation** is the closed interval from `issued_at` through `acceptance_deadline` with capacity one, so an attempt to issue a second referral against the same registration at the instant the clock exactly equals the existing `acceptance_deadline` is refused. **The recall window** on a laid-off dispatch is the closed interval from the dispatch's `ended_at` through its `recall_window_ends_at`, seven days later, inclusive at both ends. **A member's employment interval** is half-open — from `start_at` up to but not including `ended_at`, or from `start_at` forward without end while the dispatch is `WORKING` — which means a new acceptance whose reporting instant is exactly equal to a prior dispatch's `ended_at` is permitted as adjacency rather than blocked as overlap. **The grievance filing window** is open while the clock is at or before the challenged referral's `issued_at` plus 10 days. **The refusal-strike window** is a rolling 365 days closed at its left edge, so a referral issued exactly 365 days before the current instant still counts against the member.

The day-denominated constants that govern the hall are: 24 hours to answer a referral; 30 days between required re-signs of the out-of-work list; 7 days of recall rights after a layoff; 5 days after a layoff during which a re-registration may claim the laid-off member's old list position; 10 days to file a grievance after the challenged referral was issued; 1,825 days — five years — of dispatch-record retention; and a 5-day ceiling on expected job duration that separates a short call from a long call.

### 2.6. Nested Paths and Referential Existence

Several read and write operations address a child resource through its parent — a member's skill endorsements, a job request's referrals, a job request's computed dispatch order. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A member id that matches no `Member` yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`; a job request id that matches no `JobRequest` yields `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. All existence failures carry HTTP 404, and the family of codes is one per resource type: `ERR_LOCAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRIEVANCE_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.

Identifiers that arrive in a request body or a query string are checked the same way and with the same code family. Member creation resolves `home_local_id`; signing the out-of-work list resolves `member_id`, `list_id`, `predecessor_registration_id` and every `sister_local_id` inside the declared `reciprocity_credits`; job-request intake resolves `contractor_id` and every entry of `named_call_member_ids`; referral issuance resolves an explicitly named `member_id`; grievance filing resolves `referral_id`. A missing sister local is reported as `ERR_LOCAL_NOT_FOUND`, a missing named-call member as `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing predecessor registration as `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, and so on for the rest.

### 2.7. The Order in Which the Hall Says No

A single request can violate several rules at once, and the hall must always report the same one. Checks are therefore evaluated in a fixed precedence, lowest number first, and evaluation stops at the first failure. This ordering is contractual: a request that trips both a late check and an early one is reported under the early one.

1. Header authentication — both headers present, non-empty, and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles — on every endpoint, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role permission — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list — on every endpoint, failing with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Request shape, on every endpoint: required fields present, JSON types correct, enum members recognized, integers inside their declared ranges, at least one worker requested across the two headcount fields, `apprentice_period` present exactly when `classification` is `APPRENTICE` and absent otherwise, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0. Failures are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Several parts of that check are worth stating exactly, because they are visible in every endpoint. A body that is absent, empty, or nothing but whitespace is read as an empty JSON object, so the failure reported is the first missing required field rather than a complaint about the body itself — and an endpoint that declares no required field accepts such a request. A body that is not parseable JSON, a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object such as an array or a bare scalar, and a body sent without a JSON content type are each themselves `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field explicitly present as JSON `null` is treated exactly as an absent field: it fails if the field is required and otherwise takes the field's documented default. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, which is also why a client-supplied `id` is simply discarded. A JSON boolean is never an acceptable integer; a string field whose documented minimum is 1 character rejects the empty string; an array-valued field must be a JSON array, and where it holds identifiers every element must be a non-empty string. Query-string filters are held to the same standard as body fields: a filter naming an unrecognized enum member is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a filter that quietly matches nothing, and a boolean filter accepts only `true`, `false`, `1`, or `0`, case-insensitively and after trimming surrounding whitespace.
4. Existence of path-templated resources, resolved left to right, on every endpoint that has a path parameter, failing with the matching not-found code.
5. Existence of body and query foreign keys — `home_local_id`, `list_id`, `member_id`, `contractor_id`, `referral_id`, `predecessor_registration_id`, every `sister_local_id` inside `reciprocity_credits`, and every entry of `named_call_member_ids` — on member creation, list-signing, job-request intake, referral issuance, and grievance filing, failing with the matching not-found code.
6. Ownership for the two entity-backed roles, failing with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, on list-signing, re-signing, referral acceptance, referral refusal, job-request intake, job-request cancellation, layoff, and recall. Grievance filing is exempt by design.
7. Segregation of duties on grievance adjudication, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. The job request is not suspended by contractor delinquency, on referral issuance, failing with `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_SUSPENDED`.
9. Job-request openness: for referral issuance the request's `status` is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` and the open-slot counter matching the requested `slot_kind` is greater than zero; for cancellation the `status` is `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_FILLED` or `SUSPENDED`. Failures are `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN`.
10. The referral is still outstanding — its `status` is `ISSUED` — on acceptance, refusal, and withdrawal, failing with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_PENDING`.
11. The named member holds no outstanding referral, on referral issuance, failing with `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_OUTSTANDING`. Like the checks at 17, 18, 19 and 30, this one is evaluated only when the dispatcher names a `member_id` explicitly, because the ranked selector of §3.2 never returns a registration that would trip it. This check sits deliberately ahead of the registration-state check at position 30, so that a member who is already out on a referral is reported as such rather than as the holder of an inactive registration.
12. The dispatch being laid off is `WORKING`, failing with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_WORKING`.
13. The dispatch being recalled is `LAID_OFF`, failing with `ERR_RECALL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
14. Grievance state — `FILED` for review, `UNDER_REVIEW` for adjudication — failing with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`.
15. The supplied `expected_job_request_version` equals the job request's current `version`, on referral issuance, failing with `ERR_STALE_JOB_REQUEST_VERSION`.
16. The contractor behind a new job request has `signatory_status` of `SIGNATORY`, failing with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_SIGNATORY`.
17. The named member has `standing` of `GOOD_STANDING` and is `dispatch_eligible`, on referral issuance and only when a `member_id` is named explicitly, failing with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`.
18. The named member's `classification` matches the requested `slot_kind`, on referral issuance and only when a `member_id` is named explicitly, failing with `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_MISMATCH`.
19. A special-skill referral that names a `member_id` explicitly targets a member holding a matching endorsement that is verified and unexpired, failing with `ERR_SKILL_ENDORSEMENT_MISSING`. On the unnamed path the endorsement is a condition of the candidate filter instead, so its absence empties the pool rather than raising this code.
20. List-group eligibility on list-signing — the member's local hours reach the group's floor and travelers are admissible — failing with `ERR_LIST_GROUP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
21. Reciprocity eligibility on list-signing, failing with `ERR_TRAVELER_NOT_RECIPROCAL`.
22. A supplied `predecessor_registration_id` belongs to the same member as the new registration, failing with `ERR_REREGISTRATION_PREDECESSOR_MISMATCH`.
23. A named-call referral targets a member the contractor actually declared, failing with `ERR_NAMED_CALL_NOT_DECLARED`.
24. Sustaining an out-of-order claim requires the challenged referral's issuance record to be unpurged, failing with `ERR_DISPATCH_RECORD_PURGED`.
25. The member's re-registration cooling-off period has elapsed, on list-signing, failing with `ERR_REREGISTRATION_TOO_EARLY`.
26. A claim to preserve the predecessor's list position is inside the post-layoff window and rests on a dispatch that ended in layoff, failing with `ERR_REREGISTRATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
27. The acceptance window is still open, on acceptance and refusal, failing with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
28. The recall window is still open, failing with `ERR_RECALL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
29. The grievance filing window is still open, failing with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
30. The target registration's `status` is `REGISTERED`, on referral issuance, re-signing, and removal, failing with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. On referral issuance this is checked only when a `member_id` is named explicitly, because the ranked selector never returns a registration that is not `REGISTERED`.
31. No employment overlap at the reporting instant, on referral acceptance and on recall, failing with `ERR_OVERLAPPING_DISPATCH`.
32. The member holds at most one registration in `REGISTERED` or `REFERRED` status across all lists, on list-signing, failing with `ERR_MEMBER_ALREADY_REGISTERED`.
33. The declared named-call count is within the request's computed allowance, on job-request intake, failing with `ERR_NAMED_CALL_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`.
34. The declared special-skill slot count is within the request's computed allowance, on job-request intake, failing with `ERR_SPECIAL_SKILL_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`.
35. A governing apprentice-ratio schedule exists whenever an apprentice slot is referred or accepted, failing with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATIO_POLICY`.
36. Committing one more apprentice stays within the ratio cap, on referral issuance and on acceptance, failing with `ERR_APPRENTICE_RATIO_EXCEEDED`.
37. A list-order or special-skill referral that names an explicit member names the top-ranked candidate, unless a non-empty `override_reason` is supplied, failing with `ERR_OUT_OF_ORDER_DISPATCH`.
38. The ranked candidate pool is non-empty, on referral issuance and on the dispatch-order read, failing with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REGISTRANT`.
39. Reported hours at layoff do not exceed twelve hours per elapsed calendar day, failing with `ERR_HOURS_EXCEED_ELAPSED`.
40. Remedy applicability at adjudication, failing with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

### 2.8. Version Pinning on Referral Issuance

Referral issuance is the one write in this system guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and it is guarded because it is legitimately repeatable. A dispatcher may issue referral after referral against the same job request while slots remain open, so no state precondition stops a second issuance — which means the dispatcher's view of how many slots remain, which apprentice-ratio cap currently applies, and which referrals are already outstanding must be pinned to the snapshot the dispatcher actually read.

Accordingly, a `POST /hiring-hall/job-requests/{id}/referrals` request must carry `expected_job_request_version` as a required, non-optional field whose value is the client's last observed `version` of the target `JobRequest`. If that value does not equal the job request's current `version` — because any other writer has advanced it since the client last read the request — the issuance is rejected with `ERR_STALE_JOB_REQUEST_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written. No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field of any kind; `expected_job_request_version` appears on referral issuance and nowhere else. It is a precondition only: it is not stored on the referral and the `Referral` representation carries no field echoing it. Being a `version`, it must be a non-negative integer, and a negative value is a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at precedence 3 rather than a stale-version failure.

Because so many operations bump a job request's `version` — acceptance, refusal, withdrawal, expiry during the sweep, suspension, reinstatement, cancellation, and each prior issuance — a dispatcher working a large call must re-read the request between issuances. That is the intended cost of the guarantee.

### 2.9. Basis Points, Integer Money, and Pinned Rounding Order

Nothing in this system is stored as a floating-point number. Wage rates are whole cents in `hourly_rate_cents`. Awards are whole cents in `back_pay_amount_cents`. Hours are whole hours, in `local_hours_in_trade`, `credited_seniority_hours`, `hours_worked`, `back_pay_hours` and `hours_delta`. Proportions are basis points — hundredths of one percent on a scale of 10,000, so 5,000 basis points means one half and 10,000 basis points means the whole — in `reciprocity_factor_bp` and in the wage factors behind an apprentice's make-whole award.

Where a proportional computation must produce an integer, the rule is round half up: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole unit. Where a computation counts or divides counts, the rule is floor: integer division discards the remainder. List positions and candidate ranks are integer ordinals, one-based, with no rounding involved at all.

The **order** of rounding is pinned in two places, and in both places the alternative order would produce a different integer. When a traveler's reciprocity credits are converted into credited seniority hours, each declared credit is rounded to whole hours individually and only then are the rounded results summed; summing the raw products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule, and the two orders diverge whenever two or more credits land on a half-hour boundary. When a make-whole award is projected forward over scheduled days, each day's money is rounded to whole cents individually and only then are the days summed; when the same award is computed from actual reported hours instead, the whole award is rounded exactly once. §3.3 and §3.9 give the two computations in full.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and the Five-Year Dispatch Record

Every consequential event in the hall appends a row to an append-only dispatch record: a retention-ledger entry carrying an `event_code`, the `member_id` the record is about, an optional `referral_id`, the instant it fired in `recorded_at`, a computed `retention_until` horizon, a `purge_state`, a `snapshot_json` payload holding the ranked candidate list and decision inputs that were live at the time, and an `hours_delta` that is zero on every event except the two that carry hours — a make-whole credit (§3.9) and a layoff (§3.8), the latter recording the reported `hours_worked`. The recognized `event_code` values are `REGISTRATION_CREATED`, `REGISTRATION_RENEWED`, `REGISTRATION_REMOVED`, `REGISTRATION_EXPIRED`, `REFERRAL_ISSUED`, `REFERRAL_ACCEPTED`, `REFERRAL_REFUSED`, `REFERRAL_EXPIRED`, `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN`, `DISPATCH_LAID_OFF`, `DISPATCH_RECALLED`, `GRIEVANCE_DECIDED`, `MAKE_WHOLE_CREDITED`, and `POSITION_RESTORED`. The `referral_id` is null on exactly five kinds of entry — `REGISTRATION_CREATED`, `REGISTRATION_RENEWED`, `REGISTRATION_EXPIRED`, and the `REGISTRATION_REMOVED` and `POSITION_RESTORED` entries that list-signing and manual removal write — and is populated on every other event, including the `REGISTRATION_REMOVED` entry the three-strike cascade of §3.7 writes and the `POSITION_RESTORED` entry a withdrawal writes, both of which carry the referral that provoked them. `snapshot_json` is a JSON object whose key set is fixed by `event_code` and which carries nothing beyond those keys. For the events that registration and list housekeeping write, the key sets are: `REGISTRATION_CREATED` → `registration_id`, `list_id`, `credited_seniority_hours`; `REGISTRATION_RENEWED` → `registration_id`; a manual `REGISTRATION_REMOVED` → `registration_id`, `removal_reason`; `REGISTRATION_EXPIRED` → `registration_id`, `removal_reason`; and the `POSITION_RESTORED` a preserved re-registration writes → `registration_id`, `predecessor_registration_id`. For the events that answering or retracting a referral writes: a no-cause `REFERRAL_REFUSED` or `REFERRAL_EXPIRED` → `good_cause`, always false, and `strike_count`; the `REGISTRATION_REMOVED` of the three-strike cascade → `removal_reason`, always `THREE_REFUSALS`, and `registration_id`; `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN` → `reason` and `referral_kind`; and the `POSITION_RESTORED` that follows a withdrawal → `registration_id`. The remaining key sets complete the catalogue: `REFERRAL_ISSUED` → the five keys §3.6 names; a good-cause `REFERRAL_REFUSED` → `good_cause`, always true, and `refusal_good_cause_code`; `REFERRAL_ACCEPTED` → `dispatch_id` and `slot_kind`; `DISPATCH_LAID_OFF` → `layoff_reason` and `dispatch_id`; `DISPATCH_RECALLED` → `recall_of_dispatch_id`, naming the laid-off record, and `dispatch_id`, naming the new one the recall creates; `GRIEVANCE_DECIDED` → `grievance_id`, `decision`, `remedy`, and `note`, that last present as null whenever the adjudicator supplied none; `MAKE_WHOLE_CREDITED` → `grievance_id`, `back_pay_variant`, and `back_pay_amount_cents`; and the `POSITION_RESTORED` that a sustained next-available-priority remedy writes → `grievance_id` and `remedy`. Each entry's `retention_until` is its `recorded_at` plus 1,825 days — the single exception being the `REGISTRATION_CREATED` entry of §3.3, whose horizon is measured from the registration's `registered_at` instead — and its `purge_state` is `RETAINED` until the housekeeping sweep flips it to `PURGED` and clears `snapshot_json` to null. A purged entry is still readable: it returns with `purge_state` of `PURGED` and a null `snapshot_json`. Only a `HallAdmin` or a `BusinessManager` may read the ledger at all.

Retention is what makes adjudication possible, and it is also what limits it. When a member grieves an out-of-order dispatch, the manager's finding rests on the `REFERRAL_ISSUED` entry for the challenged referral — specifically on the ordered candidate snapshot it holds. If that entry has already been purged, an `OUT_OF_ORDER_DISPATCH` claim cannot be sustained and adjudication is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_RECORD_PURGED`.

Separation of the two hats is enforced at the moment of decision rather than at the moment of appointment. At a small local the same person may legitimately hold a dispatcher's job and be elected business manager, so the system does not forbid one human from holding both roles; it forbids one `X-User-ID` from deciding a grievance about a referral that the same `X-User-ID` issued. The referral's `issued_by_user_id` and the adjudicator's `X-User-ID` are compared directly, and equality is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Hall's Books: Locals, Members, and Certifications

Before anything can be dispatched, a `HallAdmin` establishes the locals the hall recognizes. Each `Local` record carries a `local_number` — the union local number printed on every dispatch slip, an integer from 1 through 9999 — and a `name` of 1 to 120 characters for display. Its `kind` is either `HOME` or `SISTER`: exactly one local, the `HOME` local, operates this hiring hall, and every other recognized local is a `SISTER` local whose members may travel here for work. A sister local's `reciprocity_factor_bp` records, in basis points, how much of the hours a traveler earned under that local's agreement this hall will credit when the traveler signs the book here — 5,000 basis points credits half of them. The field defaults to 0, accepts values from 0 through 10,000, and a value of 0 means there is no reciprocity agreement with that local at all. Neither `local_number` nor `kind` is constrained to be unique: the hall's single `HOME` local is a convention of the data rather than an invariant the system enforces, so a second `HOME` local, or two locals sharing a `local_number`, is accepted without complaint. Locals may be listed, optionally narrowed by `kind`.

Entering a worker on the books creates a `Member` with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `home_local_id` naming the local that holds the worker's book. The member's `classification` is either `JOURNEYWORKER` or `APPRENTICE`, and it determines which dispatch slots the member may ever fill. An apprentice must also declare an `apprentice_period`, an integer from 1 through 4 identifying the wage period; a journeyworker must leave `apprentice_period` null. Supplying a period for a journeyworker, or omitting it for an apprentice, is a shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The member's `local_hours_in_trade` records hours already worked under the `HOME` local's agreement and must be zero or greater; that figure is what qualifies a worker for the higher-precedence list groups. A member's `standing` with the local is `GOOD_STANDING` or `DELINQUENT` and defaults to `GOOD_STANDING`. Members may be listed and filtered by `home_local_id`, by `classification`, and by `standing`.

Three member attributes are derived rather than supplied, and one is derived from another. `is_traveler` is true exactly when the member's home local has `kind` of `SISTER`. `re_registration_eligible_at` is null for a member in ordinary standing, and is set to the current instant plus 30 days when one of the member's registrations is removed for three refusals — a cooling-off period described in §3.7. `refusal_strike_count` exposes the member's current effective refusal strikes, counted over a rolling window as §3.7 specifies. Finally, `dispatch_eligible` is true when the member's `standing` is `GOOD_STANDING` and either `re_registration_eligible_at` is null or the current instant has reached or passed it; it is false otherwise, and a member who is not `dispatch_eligible` is invisible to every ranked candidate set the hall computes.

Placing a member in or out of good standing is a `HallAdmin` or `BusinessManager` action that sets `standing` and increments the member's `version` by 1. Two things follow. First, `dispatch_eligible` is recomputed, which silently removes a newly delinquent member from every candidate pool. Second — and this is the consequence dispatchers most often forget — a change to `DELINQUENT` reaches into work already in flight: every referral of that member still in `ISSUED` status becomes `WITHDRAWN`, and each linked registration returns from `REFERRED` to `REGISTERED` with its original ordinal and its accrued `demotion_count` restored and no refusal strike charged, exactly as if the hall had withdrawn the referral itself. Those referrals are processed in `acceptance_deadline` ascending order, and each one appends a `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN` entry whose recorded `reason` is the literal `MEMBER_DELINQUENT` followed by a `POSITION_RESTORED` entry, and increments its parent job request's `version` by 1 — so a member carrying outstanding referrals against several requests moves every one of them. The cascade turns on the value being written rather than on any change in it: writing `DELINQUENT` over a standing that is already `DELINQUENT` runs it again, and writing `GOOD_STANDING` runs nothing at all and reissues none of the referrals the hall pulled back. The member's own `version` increments by 1 either way.

Special skills live in a separate record because they lapse. A `SkillEndorsement` attaches to one member through `member_id` and names a `skill_code` from `WELDING_6G`, `RIGGING`, `INSTRUMENTATION`, and `FIRE_WATCH`. Its `certified_until` is the instant at which the certification lapses, and the endorsement counts only while the clock is strictly before that instant. Its `verified` flag, which defaults to false, records whether the hall has physically sighted the certification card; an unverified endorsement never satisfies a special-skill referral no matter how current it is. A member's endorsements can be listed from the member's own record and narrowed by `skill_code`, and any single endorsement can be read directly; because the reduced-shape endorsement record has no not-found code of its own in the family of §2.6, an endorsement id that matches nothing is reported as `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404. A `certified_until` already behind the clock is accepted at creation without complaint — a lapsed card is a fact the hall may legitimately record; it simply never satisfies a special-skill referral. Endorsements are additive: the hall records a new one rather than editing an old one, which is why they carry no `version`.

### 3.2. List Groups and the Question of Who Is Next

An `OutOfWorkList` is a group on the hall's out-of-work register, not a single queue. Its `group_code` is one of `GROUP_1`, `GROUP_2`, `GROUP_3`, or `GROUP_4`, and its `priority_rank`, an integer from 1 through 4, states its dispatch precedence — rank 1 is worked first. Its `requires_local_hours` sets the minimum `local_hours_in_trade` a member must have accumulated to sign that group, and its `accepts_travelers` flag decides whether a member whose `is_traveler` is true may sign it at all. Both fields are required at creation, and `requires_local_hours` is any integer of 0 or greater with no upper bound. Neither `group_code` nor `priority_rank` is constrained to be unique, so two groups may legitimately share a `priority_rank` and be worked as a single precedence band. By convention the hall's groups run from a well-qualified local group at rank 1 down to a traveler-admitting group at rank 4, but nothing in the system hard-codes that arrangement; the ranks and the flags are data. A group's `registered_count` is computed as the number of registrations currently on it whose `status` is `REGISTERED`. Both `registered_count` and every registration's `position_number` are derived at read time from the registrations' current `status` and are never stored, so wherever this document says a group's `registered_count` is incremented or decremented, or that a list is renumbered and its ordinal gap closed, it is describing the arithmetic consequence of a status change rather than a separate write. No ordinal is ever persisted, none can drift, and there is no moment at which a list is observably un-renumbered. When list groups are listed they come back in `priority_rank` ascending order, optionally filtered by `ordering_policy` and by `accepts_travelers`.

The group's `ordering_policy` fixes how its own registrants are ordered against each other. Under `SENIORITY` the group ranks by each registration's `credited_seniority_hours` from highest to lowest. Under `FIRST_REGISTERED` the group ranks by each registration's `registered_at` from earliest to latest — the book-signing rule. A hall commonly runs its journeyworker groups on seniority and its traveler group on first-registered, but again, this is data. Because two groups may share a `priority_rank`, the strict-group-order variant of the ranking can reach its `ordering_policy` component holding candidates drawn from groups governed by *different* policies, and the two policies are then compared on one numeric axis: a `SENIORITY` candidate's key is the negation of its `credited_seniority_hours`, and a `FIRST_REGISTERED` candidate's key is its `registered_at` expressed as seconds since the Unix epoch. The practical consequence is fixed rather than incidental — at equal `priority_rank`, `priority_flag` and `demotion_count`, a candidate from a `SENIORITY` group precedes a candidate from a `FIRST_REGISTERED` group, whatever either registration's hours or signing instant.

Whenever the hall needs to know who is next, it computes a **ranked candidate pool** for a specific job request and a specific `slot_kind`. The pool is built in two steps: first a filter that decides who is even a candidate, then a sort key that orders them. The filter admits a registration only if all of the following hold. The registration's `status` is `REGISTERED`. The registrant member's `classification` equals `JOURNEYWORKER` when the slot being filled is a journeyworker slot, and equals `APPRENTICE` when it is an apprentice slot. The member's `standing` is `GOOD_STANDING`. The member's `re_registration_eligible_at` is either null or at or before the current instant. No referral exists against that same registration whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `acceptance_deadline` is at or after the current instant. No dispatch exists for that member whose `status` is `WORKING`. And when the referral being issued is a special-skill referral, the member must hold a skill endorsement whose `skill_code` equals the job request's `required_skill_code`, whose `verified` is true, and whose `certified_until` is strictly after the current instant. If the filter admits nobody, the operation fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REGISTRANT`. Registrations in `REFERRED`, `DISPATCHED_OUT`, `REMOVED`, or `EXPIRED` status are never candidates. The filter reads nothing beyond those conditions. In particular it does not consult `renewal_due_at`, so a `REGISTERED` registration whose monthly re-sign has already lapsed remains a candidate — and may be dispatched — right up until the housekeeping sweep of §3.4 expires it; and it does not re-check the list group's `requires_local_hours` or `accepts_travelers`, which are conditions of signing the group rather than of being dispatched from it. When a special-skill referral is worked against a job request whose `required_skill_code` is null, no endorsement can match and the filter therefore admits nobody at all.

The sort key comes in **two variants, and which one governs is decided entirely by the length of the job**. For a long call, the *strict group order* variant applies, and it sorts the surviving candidates by six components in this exact sequence: first by the list group's `priority_rank` ascending, so that every eligible rank-1 registrant outranks every rank-4 registrant regardless of when either signed; then by `priority_flag`, with a flagged registration ahead of an unflagged one; then by `demotion_count` ascending, so a registration carrying fewer charged demotions ranks ahead of one carrying more; then by that group's own `ordering_policy` key, which is `credited_seniority_hours` descending under `SENIORITY` and `registered_at` ascending under `FIRST_REGISTERED`; then, as a tie-break, by `registered_at` ascending; and finally, as the last tie-break, by `created_at` ascending. Record identity is never a tie-break; candidates tied on all six components are ordered by the recording sequence of §2.3, earliest recorded first.

For a short call, the *short-call interfiled* variant applies instead, and it deliberately discards both the group precedence and the group's `ordering_policy`. All groups are interfiled into one pool and sorted by four components: `priority_flag` first, with a flagged registration ahead of an unflagged one; then `demotion_count` ascending; then `registered_at` ascending; then `created_at` ascending — and, for candidates still tied on all four, the recording sequence of §2.3. Note that this variant reaches neither the list group's `priority_rank` nor its `ordering_policy`, so `credited_seniority_hours` has no bearing on a short call whatsoever. The practical effect is the one the agreement intends: on a two-day call, a traveler sitting on the rank-4 group who signed the book in March outranks a high-hours local on the rank-1 group who signed in June. Identical registration data therefore yields a different top-ranked registration under the two variants, and the variant that was actually used is stamped onto every referral as `variant_applied`, taking the value `STRICT_GROUP_ORDER` or `SHORT_CALL_INTERFILED`. A named call consults no ranking at all and records `variant_applied` as `NOT_APPLICABLE`.

Ranks are one-based integer ordinals with no rounding, and every datetime comparison inside the ranking is exact to the millisecond. A `HallAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, or `BusinessManager` may inspect the computed pool for a job request before committing to anything: supplying the `slot_kind` of interest returns the governing variant in `variant`, the size of the surviving pool in `candidate_count`, and a description of the top-ranked candidate in `top_registration_id`, `top_member_id`, `top_list_group_code`, `top_position_number`, and `top_credited_seniority_hours`. Those seven keys are the whole of the payload and nothing else is returned. The `slot_kind` query parameter is required on this read, and omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a default to either bucket; the read also accepts an optional `referral_kind` query parameter, which defaults to `LIST_ORDER` when omitted and which selects the same filter issuance would apply — so `SPECIAL_SKILL` narrows the pool to holders of a verified, unexpired endorsement for the request's `required_skill_code`, while `NAMED_CALL` is accepted here and computes exactly the `LIST_ORDER` pool, since the ranking a named call bypasses is precisely what this read exists to show. The read is a pure projection of the ranking and of nothing else: it never consults the request's `status`, so a `SUSPENDED`, `FILLED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` request answers just as an `OPEN` one does, and it never consults the open-slot counters either, so it answers for a `slot_kind` whose slots are all already reserved. `variant` is always the variant the job's `duration_type` selects, so this read never answers `NOT_APPLICABLE`. `top_position_number` is the top-ranked candidate's own ordinal *within its own list*, which under the interfiled variant is frequently not 1 — the candidate ranked first across the interfiled pool may sit third on the list it actually signed. The ordered candidate list itself is not returned by this read; it is what the `REFERRAL_ISSUED` snapshot of §3.6 retains for adjudication, and there each candidate is described, in rank order, by `rank`, `registration_id`, `member_id`, `list_id`, `list_group_code`, `list_priority_rank`, `priority_flag`, `demotion_count`, `credited_seniority_hours`, and `position_number`. That read is computed live and stores nothing, and it fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REGISTRANT` when the pool is empty, exactly as issuance would.

### 3.3. Signing the Book: Registration, Reciprocity, and Preserved Position

Signing onto an out-of-work list creates a `Registration`. A `Member` may sign for itself; a `HallAdmin` or `Dispatcher` may sign a member on at the counter. The request names the `member_id` being registered and the `list_id` of the group being signed, and may additionally declare `reciprocity_credits`, a `predecessor_registration_id`, which defaults to null, and `claim_position_preservation`, which defaults to false.

Group eligibility is checked before anything is written. The member's `local_hours_in_trade` must be at least the group's `requires_local_hours`, and if the member's `is_traveler` is true the group's `accepts_travelers` must be true; failing either is `ERR_LIST_GROUP_NOT_ELIGIBLE` with HTTP 422. A member may hold only one live registration at a time — at most one whose `status` is `REGISTERED` or `REFERRED`, counted across every list — and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_ALREADY_REGISTERED`. A member who was removed for three refusals cannot sign again until the cooling-off period has elapsed: the current instant must have reached or passed `re_registration_eligible_at`, or that field must be null, and an early attempt is refused with `ERR_REREGISTRATION_TOO_EARLY`. That cooling-off period is the only condition of the member's own record that bars a signature. In particular `standing` is not consulted here: a `DELINQUENT` member may sign the book and hold a perfectly valid registration, and is simply invisible to every candidate pool until standing is restored. Nor is present employment consulted: because the one-live-registration rule counts only `REGISTERED` and `REFERRED` rows, a member whose previous registration has gone to the terminal `DISPATCHED_OUT` may sign again even while still working that dispatch — though a `WORKING` dispatch keeps them out of every candidate pool regardless.

**Reciprocity credits are how a traveler brings hours with them.** The `reciprocity_credits` field is an array — empty by default — of objects, each carrying a `sister_local_id` and a `raw_hours` integer; each entry must be a JSON object whose `sister_local_id` is a non-empty string and whose `raw_hours` is an integer of 0 or greater, and any entry failing that shape is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every cited local must have `kind` of `SISTER` and a `reciprocity_factor_bp` strictly greater than 0, and a traveler's own home local must likewise carry a non-zero reciprocity factor; a credit citing a home local, or a sister local with which the hall has no agreement, is refused with `ERR_TRAVELER_NOT_RECIPROCAL`. The registration's `credited_seniority_hours` — the value the `SENIORITY` ordering policy sorts on — is then computed as follows: start from the member's `local_hours_in_trade`, which are hours under the home local's own agreement and are always credited in full; then walk the declared credits in the order they were supplied in the array, and for each one multiply its `raw_hours` by that sister local's `reciprocity_factor_bp`, divide by 10,000, and round the result half up to a whole hour; finally add those individually rounded per-entry results to the base. The rounding happens once per array entry, before the summation, never once over the total. Entries are not deduplicated: two credits citing the same `sister_local_id` are rounded and credited independently, exactly as supplied. A non-traveler may simply supply an empty array, in which case `credited_seniority_hours` equals `local_hours_in_trade` exactly.

**Position preservation is the layoff courtesy, and it is narrow.** A member laid off through no fault of their own should not lose a year of book seniority to a two-week job, so a new registration may name the member's prior registration in `predecessor_registration_id` and set `claim_position_preservation` to true. That predecessor must belong to the same member as the new registration; a mismatch is `ERR_REREGISTRATION_PREDECESSOR_MISMATCH` with HTTP 422. The claim is honoured only when a dispatch arising from one of the predecessor's referrals actually ended in `LAID_OFF` status and the current instant is at or before that dispatch's `ended_at` plus 5 days. A claim outside that five-day window, or resting on a dispatch that ended in `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE` or `QUIT` instead, is refused with `ERR_REREGISTRATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Where more than one dispatch arising from the predecessor's referrals stands at `LAID_OFF`, the window is measured against the most recently created of them — which includes a recall dispatch, since a recall carries forward the `referral_id` of the record it recalls. `claim_position_preservation` set to true with no `predecessor_registration_id` supplied is refused under that same code rather than as a shape violation, and a claim left false is never evaluated at all, even when a predecessor is named: the predecessor is then merely recorded, and nothing is inherited from it.

When preservation succeeds, the new registration's `registered_at` is inherited verbatim from the predecessor's `registered_at` and its `demotion_count` is inherited from the predecessor's `demotion_count` — the member returns to the book exactly as they left it, demotions and all. Nothing else is inherited: `priority_flag` starts false even where the predecessor carried it, and `credited_seniority_hours` is always recomputed from the new registration's own `local_hours_in_trade` and its own declared credits rather than copied across. When preservation does not apply, or is not claimed, `registered_at` is the current instant and `demotion_count` starts at 0. In either case `last_renewed_at` is set to the current instant and is never inherited from a predecessor, and `renewal_due_at` is computed as `last_renewed_at` plus 30 days. The new registration's `position_number` is not assigned at creation but computed, then and on every later read, as the registration's rank among its group's `REGISTERED` rows under the intra-list key given below; the group's `registered_count` rises by one purely as a consequence of the new row existing. Under a `FIRST_REGISTERED` group an unpreserved registration therefore does land at the tail, at `registered_count`, because its `registered_at` is the current instant and so the latest on the list — but under a `SENIORITY` group it lands wherever its `credited_seniority_hours` put it, which is `position_number` 1 if it brings more hours than anybody already signed. A preserved registration lands wherever its inherited `registered_at` and `demotion_count` put it, which reproduces the predecessor's standing rather than literally restoring a remembered ordinal.

Creating a registration appends a `REGISTRATION_CREATED` entry to the dispatch record whose `recorded_at` is the current instant but whose `retention_until` is set to `registered_at` plus 1,825 days — so a preserved registration inherits its predecessor's retention horizon and can be created already nearer to purge than a fresh signature would be — and appends a second entry with `event_code` of `POSITION_RESTORED` when a predecessor's ordinal was in fact preserved. Both carry a null `referral_id` and the `snapshot_json` key sets §2.10 fixes for them. Registrations may be listed and filtered by `member_id`, by `list_id`, by `status`, and by `predecessor_registration_id`, and any single registration can be read with its computed `position_number`, `credited_seniority_hours`, `demotion_count`, and `priority_flag`. The registration representation also retains and echoes the two declaration fields: `claim_position_preservation` exactly as supplied (false when omitted), and `reciprocity_credits` as the array that was declared, preserved in the order supplied but normalized so that each entry holds only `sister_local_id` and `raw_hours` — any other key inside a supplied credit object is discarded and never echoed. A registration created with no credits echoes an empty array rather than null.

A registration's `status` takes one of five values — `REGISTERED`, `REFERRED`, `DISPATCHED_OUT`, `REMOVED`, or `EXPIRED` — beginning at `REGISTERED`, with the last three terminal. Its `position_number` is the one-based rank of the registration within its own list among the rows whose `status` is `REGISTERED`, computed under the same intra-list key the strict-group-order variant uses, and it is null whenever the `status` is anything other than `REGISTERED`. Its `removal_reason` explains why the registration left the book and is null while `status` is `REGISTERED`, `REFERRED`, or `DISPATCHED_OUT`; when set it is one of `THREE_REFUSALS`, `VOLUNTARY`, `ADMINISTRATIVE`, or `RENEWAL_LAPSED`. Its `priority_flag` defaults to false and is raised only by a sustained grievance, as §3.9 describes.

### 3.4. Staying on the Book: Re-signing, Removal, and the Housekeeping Sweep

The hall requires a monthly re-sign. A registration's `renewal_due_at` is always its `last_renewed_at` plus 30 days, and re-signing — available to the member itself, to a `Dispatcher`, or to a `HallAdmin`, and taking no request body at all — sets `last_renewed_at` to the current instant and recomputes `renewal_due_at` as that instant plus 30 days. There is no deadline on the act of re-signing itself: `renewal_due_at` gates the housekeeping sweep below, not this operation, so a registration still standing at `REGISTERED` may be re-signed at any time — including long after its `renewal_due_at` has passed — and re-signing before the next sweep runs is precisely what saves it from expiry. A member acting for themselves must be the registration's own member; another member's `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Only a registration whose `status` is `REGISTERED` may be re-signed, and any other status is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`.

What re-signing pointedly does **not** do is move anybody. `registered_at`, `credited_seniority_hours`, `demotion_count`, `priority_flag`, and `position_number` are all left exactly as they were — a monthly re-sign is a statement that the member is still looking for work, not a fresh signature. The operation appends a `REGISTRATION_RENEWED` ledger entry, whose `snapshot_json` names the `registration_id` alone, and increments the registration's `version` by 1.

A registration can also be taken off the book deliberately. A `Dispatcher` or `HallAdmin` supplies a `reason`, which is recorded verbatim as the registration's `removal_reason` and must be either `VOLUNTARY`, when the member is withdrawing from the list, or `ADMINISTRATIVE`, when the hall is removing the registration on its own motion. The registration's `status` becomes `REMOVED` and its `position_number` becomes null; the group's `registered_count` is decremented and the ordinal gap is closed by shifting every later `position_number` on that list down by one, so the surviving rows are renumbered 1 through `registered_count` with no holes. A `REGISTRATION_REMOVED` ledger entry is appended, carrying a null `referral_id` and a `snapshot_json` of `registration_id` and `removal_reason`, and the registration's `version` increments by 1. Only a `REGISTERED` registration may be removed this way, and only those two reasons are accepted here: naming `THREE_REFUSALS` or `RENEWAL_LAPSED`, which the hall alone writes, is a shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Everything the hall would otherwise do on a timer is instead driven explicitly by a housekeeping sweep, which a `HallAdmin` or `Dispatcher` runs against the current logical clock. The sweep takes no input and performs four steps **in this fixed order**.

Step one expires stale referrals: every referral whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `acceptance_deadline` is strictly earlier than the current instant becomes `EXPIRED`. A referral sitting exactly at its deadline is untouched — expiry needs the clock strictly past the deadline. The stale referrals are visited in `acceptance_deadline` ascending order, and step one writes every one of those terminal statuses before step two charges anything to anybody, which is why the strike count each expiry is charged against already includes every other expiry of the very same run. Step two applies the consequences of those expiries to each affected registration, using the same strike arithmetic a no-cause refusal uses: the registration returns from `REFERRED` to `REGISTERED` with its `demotion_count` incremented by 1, unless the expiry brings the member's effective refusal strikes to the removal threshold, in which case the registration is instead removed with `removal_reason` of `THREE_REFUSALS`. §3.7 gives that arithmetic in full. Step three expires lapsed registrations: every registration whose `status` is `REGISTERED` and whose `renewal_due_at` is strictly earlier than the current instant becomes `EXPIRED` with `removal_reason` of `RENEWAL_LAPSED` and a null `position_number`, its list is renumbered, and the group's `registered_count` is decremented; these are processed in `renewal_due_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and each appends a `REGISTRATION_EXPIRED` entry with a null `referral_id` and a `snapshot_json` of `registration_id` and `removal_reason`. Step four purges the dispatch record: every ledger entry whose `retention_until` is strictly earlier than the current instant has its `purge_state` flipped from `RETAINED` to `PURGED` and its `snapshot_json` cleared to null. Only entries still standing at `RETAINED` are considered, so a purge is performed once and never repeated, and the purged row gains neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`.

The ordering of steps one and three has a consequence worth stating outright: because referral expiries are processed before renewal expiries, a registration that step one returns to `REGISTERED` is itself a candidate for renewal expiry in step three of the very same run, and can therefore leave the book as `EXPIRED` in the same sweep that brought it back. Along the way the sweep recomputes each affected job request's open-slot counters, re-derives the status of any request standing at `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` — a request already at `FILLED`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is never re-derived by the sweep and keeps whatever status it holds — and increments that request's `version` by 1 once for each of its own referrals that expired, so a request that lost three referrals in one run advances three versions. It appends `REFERRAL_EXPIRED`, `REGISTRATION_REMOVED`, and `REGISTRATION_EXPIRED` ledger entries as it goes.

The sweep reports what it did: `run_at` echoes the instant the run was evaluated against, `expired_referral_count` the number of referrals expired in step one, `demoted_registration_count` the number of registrations returned to the book with a demotion charged, `removed_registration_count` the number removed for a third strike, `expired_registration_count` the number expired for a lapsed re-sign, and `purged_ledger_entry_count` the number of ledger snapshots purged. Those six keys are the whole of the payload — `run_at` an instant, the other five integers — the sweep answers HTTP 200 and reports nothing else, and a run that found no work to do answers with five zeroes rather than an error. Because step two disposes of each expired referral as either a demotion or a removal and never as neither or both, `demoted_registration_count` and `removed_registration_count` always sum to `expired_referral_count`; they count expiries charged rather than distinct registrations.

### 3.5. The Contractor Side: Intake, Allowances, and Delinquency

A `Contractor` is a signatory employer, entered by a `HallAdmin` with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `agreement_expires_at` instant recording when its collective bargaining agreement lapses — a required field, accepted whether it lies ahead of or behind the clock, and one no rule in this document afterwards consults: a lapsed agreement bars nothing by itself, and `signatory_status` alone decides whether the contractor may open work — and a `signatory_status` that defaults to `SIGNATORY` and is otherwise `DELINQUENT_TRUST_FUNDS` or `TERMINATED`. Contractors may be listed and filtered by `signatory_status`.

Manpower requests are opened either by the contractor's own representative or by a `Dispatcher` taking the call at the counter. A `JobRequest` names its `contractor_id`, a `job_site` of 1 to 160 characters that is the reporting location printed on the dispatch slip, and an `hourly_rate_cents` from 1 through 99999 giving the journeyworker rate in whole cents — apprentice pay is derived from that figure by wage-period factor when a make-whole award is computed. The headcount is declared as `journeyworkers_requested` and `apprentices_requested`, each an integer from 0 through 40, with the additional requirement that the two together come to at least 1; a request for nobody is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `expected_duration_days` is an integer from 1 through 3650 and `report_at` is the instant the dispatched worker must appear at the site; `report_at` is accepted whether it lies ahead of or behind the clock, and the hall never compares it to `expected_duration_days` or to the contractor's `agreement_expires_at`. A contractor whose `signatory_status` is not `SIGNATORY` at the moment of intake cannot open work at all: intake is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_SIGNATORY`.

Duration decides almost everything about how the request will be dispatched. The computed `duration_type` is `SHORT_CALL` when `expected_duration_days` is 5 or fewer, and `LONG_CALL` otherwise. A short call is a favour the hall does the industry — a couple of days of work handed out without disturbing the seniority queue — and it therefore carries lighter consequences at every turn: it is ranked under the interfiled variant, it grants no named-call rights, and a refusal of it charges no refusal strike.

**The two allowances are computed at intake and frozen there.** They are never recomputed, precisely so that a later change in the agreement cannot retroactively legalize a named call that was illegal when it was made. Both are derived from the request's own input fields plus the computed `duration_type`; no other record participates. The `named_call_allowance` is 0 for a short call. For a long call, take the total headcount requested — journeyworkers plus apprentices — divide it by 5 discarding any remainder, take the greater of that quotient and 1, and then take the lesser of the result and a hard ceiling of 4. So a long call for any number of workers from 1 through 9 permits a single named call; ten through fourteen permit two; and no request, however large, ever permits more than four. The `special_skill_allowance` is 0 whenever `required_skill_code` is null. Otherwise it is half the journeyworkers requested rounded up — computed as the journeyworker count multiplied by 5,000 basis points and divided by 10,000 discarding the remainder, plus 1 when the journeyworker count is odd — and then capped at the journeyworker count itself.

The two allowances gate intake in a fixed order. First, if the number of entries in `named_call_member_ids` exceeds `named_call_allowance`, the intake is refused with `ERR_NAMED_CALL_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. Only then, if `special_skill_slots_requested` exceeds `special_skill_allowance`, the intake is refused with `ERR_SPECIAL_SKILL_ALLOWANCE_EXCEEDED`. The `named_call_member_ids` array defaults to empty and holds member identifiers the contractor names by right of the agreement; each must resolve to a real member, and every named-call referral the hall later issues must target somebody in that array. Its entries are neither deduplicated nor required to be distinct, so a member named twice takes up two of the allowance's places, and the count compared against `named_call_allowance` is simply the number of entries supplied. The `special_skill_slots_requested` count defaults to 0, is an integer from 0 through 40, and declares how many of the journeyworker slots the contractor wants filled by special-skill referral rather than by plain list order; `required_skill_code` defaults to null and, when present, is one of `WELDING_6G`, `RIGGING`, `INSTRUMENTATION`, or `FIRE_WATCH`.

On successful intake the request's `open_journeyworker_slots` and `open_apprentice_slots` are initialized to the two requested counts, its `accepted_journeyworker_count` and `accepted_apprentice_count` begin at 0, and its `max_apprentices_allowed` is initialized from the ratio computation of §3.6 — which yields 0 until a journeyworker referral has actually been accepted. Thereafter `open_journeyworker_slots` is always the journeyworkers requested minus the number of journeyworker referrals whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED`, and `open_apprentice_slots` is the apprentices requested minus the number of apprentice referrals whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED`; slots are reserved at issuance, not at acceptance. The acceptance counters, by contrast, count only referrals whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Requests may be listed and filtered by `contractor_id`, by `status`, and by `duration_type`.

A request's `status` begins at `OPEN` and is derived from its children's acceptances rather than set by hand. When an acceptance leaves the accepted headcount — journeyworkers plus apprentices — greater than zero but still short of the total requested, an `OPEN` request advances to `PARTIALLY_FILLED`. When an acceptance brings the accepted headcount level with the total requested, the request advances to `FILLED`, whether it was `OPEN` or already `PARTIALLY_FILLED` beforehand. Issuance itself never changes `status`, because status derives from accepted referrals only. Once a request is `FILLED` and every dispatch belonging to it has left `WORKING` status, the request bubbles up to the terminal `CLOSED` state with its `version` incremented — a parent's completion inferred from the aggregate condition of its children, re-evaluated after every dispatch status change on the request.

**Contractor delinquency reaches into live work in four hops.** When a `HallAdmin` or `BusinessManager` changes a contractor's `signatory_status` to `DELINQUENT_TRUST_FUNDS` or `TERMINATED`, the first hop moves every `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` job request of that contractor to `SUSPENDED`, recording the status it held in `suspended_from_status` — a field that is null unless the request is suspended and otherwise holds `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` — and incrementing each request's `version`. The contractor's requests are visited in `created_at` ascending order, and a request of that contractor standing at any other status — including one already `SUSPENDED` from an earlier delinquency — is passed over untouched. The second hop withdraws every referral whose `status` is `ISSUED` on the requests the first hop has just suspended, and on those alone, taking those requests one at a time in the same `created_at` ascending order and, within each request, processing its referrals in `acceptance_deadline` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — unlike the member-delinquency cascade of §3.1, whose referrals are drawn from every request at once and so are ordered by `acceptance_deadline` across all of them; each withdrawal records the literal `CONTRACTOR_DELINQUENT` as its `reason`, which is what the `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN` entry of the fourth hop carries. The third hop returns each withdrawn referral's registration from `REFERRED` to `REGISTERED` with its `registered_at`, `credited_seniority_hours`, `demotion_count`, and `priority_flag` all unchanged and no refusal strike charged, after which the affected lists are renumbered and their `registered_count` values raised. The fourth hop appends a `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN` entry and a `POSITION_RESTORED` entry to the dispatch record for each affected registration. The contractor's own `version` increments by 1. Each affected job request's `version`, by contrast, rises by exactly 1 across the whole cascade — the increment of the first hop — however many of its referrals the second hop withdraws: the withdrawals of a cascade do not each bump the parent request the way a hand-driven withdrawal of §3.7 does. Like the member-delinquency cascade of §3.1, this one turns on the value being written rather than on any change in it: writing `DELINQUENT_TRUST_FUNDS` over a status that already reads `DELINQUENT_TRUST_FUNDS`, or `TERMINATED` over `DELINQUENT_TRUST_FUNDS`, runs the four hops again and simply finds no `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED` request left to suspend, so nothing further is withdrawn. Referrals already `ACCEPTED`, and the dispatches behind them, are left entirely alone — the members are on the job and stay there.

Reinstatement is not symmetric, and unlike the delinquency cascade it turns on the change rather than on the value: it fires only when `SIGNATORY` is written over a `signatory_status` that read something else, so writing `SIGNATORY` over `SIGNATORY` restores nothing at all. When the contractor's `signatory_status` does return to `SIGNATORY` this way, each `SUSPENDED` request of that contractor — visited in `created_at` ascending order — returns to whichever value its `suspended_from_status` recorded, `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, falling back to `OPEN` should that field somehow be null; that field is cleared back to null, the open-slot counters are recomputed, and `version` increments by 1. The contractor's own `version` increments by 1 on every accepted write of `signatory_status`, including one that writes back the value already standing and therefore restores and suspends nothing. The withdrawn referrals are **not** reissued; the reopened slots must be dispatched from the list again, which means the members who were pulled back take their chances in the ranking exactly like everybody else. A referral issued against a suspended request is refused with `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_SUSPENDED`.

A contractor's representative, or a `HallAdmin`, may also cancel a request outright while its `status` is `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, or `SUSPENDED`, supplying a short written `reason` of 1 to 500 characters. The request's `status` becomes the terminal `CANCELLED`, its `suspended_from_status` is cleared back to null whether or not it was suspended, and its `version` increments by exactly 1; every referral on it whose `status` is `ISSUED` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, processed in `acceptance_deadline` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and each affected registration returns to `REGISTERED` with its ordinal and `demotion_count` restored and no strike charged, appending a `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN` and a `POSITION_RESTORED` entry per affected registration — the same third and fourth hops the delinquency cascade performs. Where that cascade records a fixed literal, cancellation records the caller's own free-text `reason` verbatim as the `reason` of every `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN` entry it writes; and as in that cascade the request's `version` rises by 1 in total rather than once more per referral withdrawn. Cancelling a request that is already `FILLED`, `CLOSED`, or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN`.

### 3.6. Issuing the Referral: Ratio, Order, and the Dispatcher's Discretion

Apprentices are never dispatched on their own account; the agreement pairs them to journeyworkers actually on the job. The schedule that fixes the pairing is published by a `HallAdmin` as a `RatioPolicy`, carrying a `policy_code` of 1 to 40 characters as the human handle for the schedule in the agreement, a `first_apprentice_at_jw_count` from 1 through 10 giving the accepted journeyworker count at which the first apprentice becomes allowable, a `then_every_n_jw` from 1 through 10 giving how many further accepted journeyworkers each additional apprentice requires, and an `effective_from` instant from which the schedule may be selected. The common agreement language permits the first apprentice as soon as a single journeyworker is on the job, which is the default shape of the schedule. A policy's `status` is `ACTIVE` on creation; publishing a new policy sets the `status` of any previously `ACTIVE` policy whose `effective_from` is *strictly* earlier to `SUPERSEDED`, incrementing each superseded policy's `version` by 1. A standing policy whose `effective_from` equals or follows the new one's is left `ACTIVE`, so several `ACTIVE` policies can legitimately coexist and step one below is what chooses between them; and the new policy never supersedes itself. Policies may be listed and filtered by `status`, and a policy id that matches nothing is reported as `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, since `RatioPolicy` has no not-found code of its own in the family of §2.6.

Selecting the governing schedule is a three-step computation, always evaluated in this order. **Step one** picks the policy: among policies whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_from` is at or before the current instant, rank by `effective_from` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and take the first row. If no such row exists, the operation is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATIO_POLICY`. **Step two** computes the cap, `max_apprentices_allowed`, from **accepted journeyworker referrals only**: count the referrals on the request whose `slot_kind` is `JOURNEYWORKER` and whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`; if that count is zero, the cap is zero; otherwise subtract the schedule's `first_apprentice_at_jw_count` from the count, floor the difference at zero, divide by `then_every_n_jw` discarding the remainder, and add one. Read as a field on a job request, `max_apprentices_allowed` never raises an error: it reports 0 both when no journeyworker referral has yet been accepted and when step one finds no governing schedule at all, and `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATIO_POLICY` is raised only by the two operations that actually commit an apprentice. **Step three** applies the consumption test, which counts apprentice referrals more broadly: at **issuance**, count the referrals on the request whose `slot_kind` is `APPRENTICE` and whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED` — at **acceptance** that same count reaches only the `ACCEPTED` ones, for the reason the next paragraph gives — add one for the apprentice about to be committed, and compare against the lesser of `max_apprentices_allowed` and the request's `apprentices_requested`. If the committed count plus one exceeds that limit, the operation is refused with `ERR_APPRENTICE_RATIO_EXCEEDED`.

The asymmetry between steps two and three is the rule, not an oversight: an outstanding journeyworker referral does **not** raise the cap, because a journeyworker who has not accepted may yet refuse, while an outstanding apprentice referral **does** consume the cap, because the hall must not promise the same apprentice slot twice. Both the ratio check and the schedule selection run at referral issuance for an apprentice slot and again at acceptance of an apprentice slot. The consumption count of step three is nevertheless read differently by those two operations, and deliberately so: at **issuance** it counts the request's apprentice referrals standing at `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED`, none of which is the referral about to be created, whereas at **acceptance** it counts only those standing at `ACCEPTED`, because the referral being answered is itself still `ISSUED` at the moment the test runs and counting it would charge one slot twice. Either way the count is raised by one for the apprentice being committed and compared against the lesser of `max_apprentices_allowed` and `apprentices_requested`.

Only a `Dispatcher` issues referrals. The request declares which bucket the referral consumes in `slot_kind`, either `JOURNEYWORKER` or `APPRENTICE`; on what basis the referral is being made in `referral_kind`, one of `LIST_ORDER`, `NAMED_CALL`, or `SPECIAL_SKILL`; and the pinned `expected_job_request_version` of §2.8. The dispatcher may additionally supply an explicit `member_id` and an `override_reason`.

The three referral kinds differ in how the recipient is chosen. A `LIST_ORDER` referral goes to the top-ranked candidate in the pool of §3.2, under the variant the job's `duration_type` selects. A `SPECIAL_SKILL` referral goes to the top-ranked candidate in that same pool narrowed to members holding a verified, unexpired endorsement for the request's `required_skill_code`; an explicitly named target without such an endorsement is refused with `ERR_SKILL_ENDORSEMENT_MISSING`, while on the unnamed path the narrowing simply leaves the pool empty. A `NAMED_CALL` referral bypasses the ranking entirely and goes to the member the contractor named — which must be a member appearing in the request's `named_call_member_ids`, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_NAMED_CALL_NOT_DECLARED` — and records `variant_applied` as `NOT_APPLICABLE` with a null `list_position_at_issue`. Because it names its recipient by definition, a `NAMED_CALL` request that carries no `member_id` at all is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; that particular requirement is evaluated only after the path-templated job request has been resolved, so an issuance quoting an unknown job-request id and omitting `member_id` together is reported as `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. A named call also never reaches the empty-pool test at precedence 38, so it succeeds against a request for which the ranked pool would have been empty; and because it computes no pool, the ordered candidate list in its dispatch-record snapshot is an empty array rather than null.

Neither of the two intake allowances of §3.5 is re-consulted at issuance. `named_call_allowance` bounds the size of `named_call_member_ids` at intake and nothing afterwards, so a member the contractor did declare may be named-called again and again — after refusing, after a withdrawal, after being laid off — with no running tally of named calls issued. `special_skill_slots_requested` likewise bounds nothing the dispatcher does: any number of `SPECIAL_SKILL` referrals may be issued against a request that carries a `required_skill_code`, whatever that declaration said, and a request declaring 0 special-skill slots may still be worked by special-skill referral. The only counters issuance respects are the two open-slot counters and the apprentice-ratio cap.

When `member_id` is omitted, the hall selects the rank-one registration itself and no question of order arises. When a dispatcher names a `member_id` explicitly on a `LIST_ORDER` or `SPECIAL_SKILL` referral, that member must be the rank-one candidate under the governing variant — **unless** the dispatcher supplies a non-empty `override_reason`, a written justification for skipping the top of the list. Naming somebody other than rank one without a justification is refused with `ERR_OUT_OF_ORDER_DISPATCH`. `override_reason` is a string of at most 500 characters carrying no minimum length of its own, so an empty or whitespace-only value is not a shape violation: it is accepted, stored verbatim, and simply fails to justify anything, leaving the out-of-order refusal to fire exactly as an omitted reason would. Conversely a justification supplied where none was needed — alongside a target who is in fact rank one, or on a named call — is likewise stored verbatim on the referral while `out_of_order_override` stays false. When a justification is supplied and is needed, the referral is issued and its `out_of_order_override` flag — which is false by default and on every other referral — is set to true, which is precisely what makes the referral grievable: the written reason and the ranked snapshot both go into the dispatch record, and a passed-over member can later ask a business manager to read them.

Issuance carries a substantial set of preconditions beyond order and ratio. The target registration's `status` must be `REGISTERED`, failing with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The member must be `dispatch_eligible` — in `GOOD_STANDING` and past any cooling-off period — failing with `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. The member's `classification` must match the requested `slot_kind`, a journeyworker for a journeyworker slot and an apprentice for an apprentice slot, failing with `ERR_CLASSIFICATION_MISMATCH` and HTTP 422. The member must hold no outstanding referral — no referral of any of that member's registrations may have `status` of `ISSUED` with an `acceptance_deadline` at or after the current instant — failing with `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_OUTSTANDING`. The job request's `status` must be `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, and the open-slot counter matching the requested `slot_kind` must be at least 1 — `open_journeyworker_slots` for a journeyworker slot, `open_apprentice_slots` for an apprentice slot — failing with `ERR_JOB_REQUEST_NOT_OPEN`.

Those five member-facing preconditions — precedences 11, 17, 18, 19 and 30 — are evaluated **only when the dispatcher names a `member_id` explicitly**. When `member_id` is omitted the hall takes the pool's rank-one registration, and the filter of §3.2 has already excluded every registration that would have tripped any of them, so none of those five codes can arise on that path. The registration the hall tests against a named `member_id` is that member's live registration — the one whose `status` is `REGISTERED` or `REFERRED`, and the most recently created of them should more than one somehow stand — falling back to the member's most recently created registration of any status when none is live; a member who has never signed the book at all is reported the same way as one whose registration has gone terminal, namely `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. A member's want of a `WORKING` dispatch, by contrast, is **not** a precondition of issuance in its own right and has no error code: it is enforced solely through the candidate filter of §3.2. A named member who is out on a `WORKING` dispatch therefore cannot be rank one, so a `LIST_ORDER` or `SPECIAL_SKILL` referral to them is refused as `ERR_OUT_OF_ORDER_DISPATCH` unless the dispatcher writes an `override_reason` — and with one, it is issued — while a `NAMED_CALL` reaches no pool at all and is issued to a working member without complaint.

A successful issuance creates a `Referral` in `ISSUED` status against the request named in `job_request_id`, recording the chosen `registration_id`, the `member_id` denormalized from that registration so record-retention lookups survive the registration's later removal, `issued_at` as the current instant, `acceptance_deadline` as `issued_at` plus 24 hours, `issued_by_user_id` as the dispatcher's `X-User-ID`, `list_position_at_issue` as a snapshot of the selected registration's `position_number` taken while it still stands at `REGISTERED` — so it is a real ordinal of 1 or more on every list-order and special-skill referral, and null only on a named call — and `variant_applied` as the variant that produced the ranking. The `refusal_good_cause_code` field starts null, and `override_reason` is null unless the dispatcher supplied one. On the list side, the selected registration moves from `REGISTERED` to `REFERRED`, its own `position_number` becomes null, and every remaining `REGISTERED` registration on the same list is renumbered from 1 through the group's `registered_count` under the strict-group-order intra-list key; the group's `registered_count` is decremented by 1. On the request side, the matching open-slot counter is decremented to reserve the slot, the counters are recomputed, and the request's `version` increments by 1 — while its `status` is untouched, because status follows acceptances. Finally a `REFERRAL_ISSUED` entry is appended to the dispatch record, whose `snapshot_json` carries exactly five top-level keys — `variant_applied`, `override_reason`, `referral_kind`, `slot_kind`, and `candidates`, the last being the full ordered candidate list in the row shape §3.2 gives, and `override_reason` present as null when the dispatcher supplied none — and whose `hours_delta` is 0. A referral's `status` runs over five values — `ISSUED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REFUSED`, `EXPIRED`, `WITHDRAWN` — beginning at `ISSUED`, with the other four terminal. Referrals may be read individually and listed with filters on `job_request_id`, `registration_id`, `member_id`, `status`, and `referral_kind`.

### 3.7. Twenty-Four Hours to Answer: Acceptance, Refusal, and the Three-Strike Rule

A referral is an offer with a deadline. From issuance the member has 24 hours to answer, and the window is open while the current instant is at or before the `acceptance_deadline`; an answer arriving strictly after the deadline is refused with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Either the member themselves or a `Dispatcher` taking a phoned-in answer may respond, but a member acting for someone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and any referral not in `ISSUED` status is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_PENDING`. Neither answer declares a single required field: acceptance reads nothing at all out of the body and refusal reads only the optional `refusal_good_cause_code`, so an absent, empty or whitespace-only body is a perfectly good answer either way, and any further key the caller sends is ignored. Only acceptance consults the parent job request's `status`; a refusal is accepted against a request standing at `FILLED`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` exactly as against an open one, since declining work the hall can no longer give still has to be recorded against the member's book.

**Accepting** moves the referral from `ISSUED` to `ACCEPTED` and the registration from `REFERRED` to the terminal `DISPATCHED_OUT`, and it creates the employment record: a `Dispatch` in `WORKING` status whose `start_at` is the job request's `report_at`, whose `hours_worked` begins at 0, and whose `ended_at`, `layoff_reason`, `recall_of_dispatch_id`, and `recall_window_ends_at` all begin null. Acceptance is guarded against double-booking: the member must have no dispatch whose employment interval contains the job request's `report_at`, where that interval runs from `start_at` up to but not including `ended_at`, or from `start_at` forward without end while the dispatch is still `WORKING`. A collision is refused with `ERR_OVERLAPPING_DISPATCH`; a reporting instant exactly equal to an earlier dispatch's `ended_at` is adjacency and is allowed. The job request must still be `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_FILLED`. For an apprentice slot, the ratio arithmetic of §3.6 is applied again at this moment, and its consumption count is read here as §3.6 specifies for acceptance: only the request's apprentice referrals already standing at `ACCEPTED` are counted — not the ones still `ISSUED`, and in particular not the referral being answered, which is itself still `ISSUED` while the test runs — so that accepted count plus one must stay within the lesser of `max_apprentices_allowed` and `apprentices_requested`.

Acceptance also settles two bookkeeping matters. Any `priority_flag` on the accepted registration is cleared to false — a next-available-priority remedy is consumed by the referral the member actually takes. And on the request side, the matching acceptance counter is incremented, `max_apprentices_allowed` is re-derived from the new accepted journeyworker count, the open-slot counters are recomputed, the group's `registered_count` is decremented, the request's `status` is re-derived from the acceptance counts, and the request's `version` increments by 1 — once for the whole acceptance, whatever else it moved. A `REFERRAL_ACCEPTED` entry is appended to the dispatch record. The payload the call answers with is the newly created `Dispatch`, at HTTP 201, and not the answered `Referral`; a caller who wants to see the referral in its `ACCEPTED` state must read it back.

**Refusing with good cause costs the member nothing.** The refusal declares a `refusal_good_cause_code` of `MEDICAL`, `TEMPORARILY_EMPLOYED`, or `OUT_OF_AREA_TRAVEL`; the referral becomes `REFUSED` with that code recorded, and the registration returns from `REFERRED` to `REGISTERED` with its `registered_at`, `credited_seniority_hours`, `demotion_count`, and `priority_flag` every one of them unchanged. The list is renumbered so that the registration reoccupies the very ordinal it held at the moment of issuance, the group's `registered_count` is incremented by 1, the request's open-slot counters are recomputed and its `version` increments by 1 — its `status` is unaffected, since a refused referral was never accepted — and a `REFERRAL_REFUSED` entry is appended whose `snapshot_json` records that good cause was claimed and whose `hours_delta` is 0.

**Refusing without good cause costs the member position, and eventually the book.** Omitting `refusal_good_cause_code` leaves it null and makes the refusal a bare turn-down. The referral becomes `REFUSED` with a null cause code, the registration's `demotion_count` is incremented by 1 — which ranks it behind every peer on its list carrying a lower `demotion_count`, since `demotion_count` sorts ahead of the ordering-policy key in both variants — the registration and every peer on the list have their `position_number` recomputed, the request's open-slot counters are recomputed and its `version` increments by 1, and a `REFERRAL_REFUSED` entry is appended whose `snapshot_json` records `good_cause` as false alongside the `strike_count` the answer produced — the count as it stands *after* this refusal, so the entry that removes a member for a third strike carries a `strike_count` of 3. This is the outcome whenever the member's strike count after the refusal stands at 1 or 2.

The strike count itself — surfaced on the member as `refusal_strike_count` — is computed as follows. Count the referrals belonging to that member, across **all** of the member's registrations including registrations that have already gone terminal, where the referral either has `status` of `REFUSED` with a null `refusal_good_cause_code`, or has `status` of `EXPIRED`; where the referral's job request has `duration_type` of `LONG_CALL`; and where the referral's `issued_at` is at or after the current instant minus 365 days. The window is closed at its left edge, so a referral issued exactly 365 days ago still counts. Because the count reaches across registrations, a member cannot shed strikes by removing themselves and signing the book again. Good-cause refusals never count. Withdrawn referrals never count. And **every short-call referral is excluded from the count entirely** — though a short-call no-cause refusal still increments `demotion_count`, so the member loses list position without accruing a strike.

When that count stands at 3 or more, the consequence cascades through four hops — the test is against the removal threshold of 3 rather than equality with it, so a fourth bare turn-down still inside the rolling window removes exactly as the third did. First, the referral becomes `REFUSED` with a null `refusal_good_cause_code`. Second, because that refusal is the third bare turn-down inside the rolling window, the registration's `demotion_count` is incremented and its `status` becomes `REMOVED` with `removal_reason` of `THREE_REFUSALS` and a null `position_number`. Third, because that registration was removed, the member's `re_registration_eligible_at` is set to the current instant plus 30 days — with the member's own `version` incrementing by 1 — and the group's `registered_count` is decremented. Fourth, because `re_registration_eligible_at` now lies in the future, the member's `dispatch_eligible` becomes false, which removes the member from every candidate pool the hall computes until the cooling-off period elapses. The request's open-slot counters are recomputed and its `version` increments by 1. Two ledger entries are appended in this order: `REFERRAL_REFUSED` first, then `REGISTRATION_REMOVED`, both stamped with the same `recorded_at`.

**An unanswered referral is charged exactly like a bare refusal.** When the housekeeping sweep of §3.4 finds a referral whose `acceptance_deadline` has strictly passed, the referral's `status` becomes `EXPIRED` and a `REFERRAL_EXPIRED` entry is appended, whose `snapshot_json` carries the same two keys a no-cause refusal writes — `good_cause` false and the resulting `strike_count`. If the member's strike count after that expiry is below 3, the registration returns from `REFERRED` to `REGISTERED` with `demotion_count` incremented by 1, its list and the request's counters are recomputed, the group's `registered_count` is incremented, and the request's `version` increments. If the expiry brings the count to exactly 3, the identical four-hop cascade fires — with the referral ending in `EXPIRED` rather than `REFUSED` — and the two ledger entries are appended as `REFERRAL_EXPIRED` followed by `REGISTRATION_REMOVED`. Silence, in other words, is treated as a refusal without cause.

**Withdrawal makes the member whole.** A `Dispatcher` or `HallAdmin` may retract the hall's own referral — for a dispatcher's error, or because the contractor pulled the call back — supplying a short written `reason` of 1 to 500 characters. Withdrawal is the one answer to a referral that carries no ownership check of its own, since neither role that may perform it is entity-backed, and it consults neither the acceptance window nor the parent request's `status`: a referral may be withdrawn after its deadline has passed, so long as the housekeeping sweep has not yet moved it to `EXPIRED`. The referral's `status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, and the registration returns from `REFERRED` to `REGISTERED` with its `registered_at`, `credited_seniority_hours`, `demotion_count`, and `priority_flag` all unchanged, reoccupying its pre-referral ordinal; the list is renumbered, the group's `registered_count` is incremented by 1, the matching open-slot count on the request is restored — the only counter a referral ever consumed, since neither intake allowance is charged at issuance — and the request's `version` increments by 1. That per-withdrawal bump is the rule for a withdrawal driven by hand at this endpoint, and for the member-delinquency cascade of §3.1, which withdraws each outstanding referral the same way; the contractor-delinquency and cancellation cascades of §3.5 are the two exceptions, and there the request's `version` moves once in total however many referrals are pulled back. Two ledger entries are appended in order: `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN`, whose `snapshot_json` carries the `reason` given and the withdrawn referral's `referral_kind`, then `POSITION_RESTORED`, whose `snapshot_json` names the `registration_id` alone; both carry the referral's id. No strike is charged and no demotion is recorded — a withdrawal is the hall's mistake, not the member's.

### 3.8. On the Job: Layoff, Recall, and Hours Back to the Book

A `Dispatch` is the employment record that an accepted referral produces. It carries the `referral_id` that produced it, the `job_request_id` and `member_id` denormalized from that referral, a `start_at` instant, and a `status` that begins at `WORKING` and ends in one of three terminal states — `LAID_OFF`, `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE`, or `QUIT`. Dispatches may be read individually and listed with filters on `member_id`, `referral_id`, `job_request_id`, `recall_of_dispatch_id`, and `status`.

When the job ends, the contractor's representative — or a `HallAdmin` acting on a phoned-in report — closes the record by supplying `hours_worked` and a `layoff_reason`. Only a dispatch whose `status` is `WORKING` may be closed, and any other status is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_WORKING`; the caller must be the representative of the contractor behind the dispatch's job request, and any other `X-User-ID` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Reported hours are sanity-checked against elapsed time: `hours_worked` may not exceed twelve hours for each elapsed calendar day since the dispatch began, where the number of elapsed days is the difference between the current instant and `start_at` divided by twenty-four hours and rounded up to a whole day. A report of 40 hours against a dispatch that started thirty hours ago is therefore refused with `ERR_HOURS_EXCEED_ELAPSED` and HTTP 422, because two elapsed days permit at most 24 hours. `hours_worked` is an integer of 0 or greater and carries no ceiling of its own in the request shape; the elapsed-time test is the only thing that bounds it, and it is a ceiling rather than a strict bound, so a report of exactly 24 hours against two elapsed days is accepted. An elapsed span of zero or less counts as **zero** elapsed days rather than one, so while the clock stands at or before the dispatch's `start_at` — which is the ordinary case for a dispatch whose reporting instant still lies ahead — the only report that can be accepted is 0 hours.

The `layoff_reason` decides which terminal state the record lands in, and with it whether the member keeps any rights. `REDUCTION_IN_FORCE`, `JOB_COMPLETE`, and `LACK_OF_WORK` are all no-fault endings: the `status` becomes `LAID_OFF`, `ended_at` is set to the current instant, the reported `hours_worked` is recorded, and `recall_window_ends_at` is set to the current instant plus 7 days. Those endings also open the five-day post-layoff window in which the member may sign the book again and claim the list position they held before the job, as §3.3 describes. `FOR_CAUSE` sends the record to `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE` and `QUIT` sends it to `QUIT`; in both cases `ended_at` is set to the current instant but `recall_window_ends_at` stays null, no recall is ever possible, and no position-preserving re-registration is available — a later claim resting on such a dispatch is refused with `ERR_REREGISTRATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Whichever ending applies, the reported `hours_worked` is added to the member's `local_hours_in_trade`, which is how work under the home local's agreement accumulates toward the higher list groups and toward seniority on the next registration; that write increments the member's own `version` by 1, and it happens on every ending, a termination for cause and a quit included — the hours were worked either way. Adding hours never disturbs the `credited_seniority_hours` of any registration already standing, since that figure was computed once, at signing. A `DISPATCH_LAID_OFF` ledger entry is appended carrying `hours_delta` equal to the reported hours, with the reason recorded in its `snapshot_json`, and the dispatch's `version` increments by 1. Because a dispatch has just left `WORKING`, the parent job request's bubble-up to `CLOSED` is re-evaluated at this point.

**Recall is the one path onto a job that bypasses the out-of-work lists entirely**, which is why it is short and tightly bounded. Only the contractor's own representative may recall, and only a dispatch whose `status` is `LAID_OFF` is recallable — a `WORKING`, `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE`, or `QUIT` record is refused with `ERR_RECALL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The recall window is the closed interval from the dispatch's `ended_at` through its `recall_window_ends_at`, inclusive at both ends; a recall arriving strictly after that horizon is refused with `ERR_RECALL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and so — since the clock may be wound backwards — is one arriving with the clock strictly before the `ended_at` at which the window opens. The member must hold no dispatch whose `status` is `WORKING`, and the supplied `report_at` must not fall inside any existing employment interval of that member; the two are tested in that order and both report `ERR_OVERLAPPING_DISPATCH`. The laid-off record the recall springs from is itself among the intervals tested, so a `report_at` falling inside the span that record already covers is refused, while a `report_at` exactly equal to its `ended_at` is adjacency and is allowed. Nothing exhausts a recall right: the laid-off record stays `LAID_OFF`, and the same contractor may recall against it again later in the window — each time creating a further dispatch — provided the member holds no `WORKING` dispatch at that moment.

A successful recall leaves the laid-off record exactly where it is — that row stays `LAID_OFF` — and creates a **new** `Dispatch` in `WORKING` status whose `recall_of_dispatch_id` points back at the laid-off record, whose `start_at` is the supplied `report_at`, and whose `referral_id`, `job_request_id` and `member_id` are all carried over unchanged from the record being recalled — which is why §3.3 can measure a position-preservation claim against a recall dispatch. The new row begins its own life exactly as an accepted referral's dispatch does: `hours_worked` at 0, `ended_at`, `layoff_reason` and `recall_window_ends_at` all null, and `version` at 1. "Leaves the laid-off record exactly where it is" is literal: that row's fields are not rewritten and its `version` is **not** incremented. If the member had signed the book again in the meantime and holds a registration whose `status` is `REGISTERED`, that registration — the earliest-created one, and only that one, however many the member somehow holds — becomes `DISPATCHED_OUT` and its list is renumbered. Otherwise the member's out-of-work position is untouched: a recall consumes no list ordinal and no named-call allowance. Nor does a recall touch the parent job request in any way: its `status` is not re-derived and its `version` does not move, so a request that had already bubbled up to `CLOSED` stays `CLOSED` even though the recall has put a member back to work on it. A `DISPATCH_RECALLED` entry is appended to the dispatch record, carrying the recalled record's `referral_id` and an `hours_delta` of 0.

### 3.9. Grieving the Dispatch: Review, Remedy, and Make-Whole Pay

A member who believes the hall dispatched out of order may file a `Grievance`, naming the challenged `referral_id`, a `claim_type`, and a `narrative` of 1 to 2,000 characters stating the case. The recognized claim types are `OUT_OF_ORDER_DISPATCH`, when the referral went to someone other than the top-ranked candidate; `IMPROPER_NAMED_CALL`, when a named call exceeded what the agreement permitted; `RATIO_VIOLATION`, when apprentices were dispatched without the journeyworker headcount to support them; and `RECORD_UNAVAILABLE`, when the hall cannot produce the record of a dispatch at all. The grievance's `filed_at` is the current instant, and filing is only open while that instant is at or before the challenged referral's `issued_at` plus 10 days; a later filing is refused with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Either the member or a `BusinessManager` filing on a member's behalf may open the grievance, and `grievant_member_id` is always taken from `X-User-ID` rather than from the body — which is also why no ownership check is applied here, since the whole point of the filing is to challenge somebody else's referral. Filing appends nothing at all to the dispatch record — until it is decided the grievance is its own record — and nothing limits how many grievances may stand against one referral or how many one member may file: no uniqueness rule is applied, no `claim_type` is checked for plausibility against the referral's actual history, and the hall never asks whether the grievant has any connection to the referral beyond having named it. Grievances may be read individually and listed with filters on `referral_id`, `grievant_member_id`, `status`, and `claim_type`.

A grievance's `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` are both null until it is adjudicated. Its `status` begins at `FILED`, and the business manager opens it for consideration, moving it to `UNDER_REVIEW` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Only a `FILED` grievance may be opened this way and only an `UNDER_REVIEW` grievance may be decided; either mismatch is refused with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, so a grievance that has already reached `SUSTAINED` or `DENIED` can be neither reopened nor decided again. Both operations belong to the `BusinessManager` alone. Opening for review declares no field and takes no body, writes nothing beyond the `status`, `updated_at` and `version` of the grievance itself, and appends nothing to the dispatch record; it is also the one of the two that is **not** subject to segregation of duties, so the very `X-User-ID` recorded as the challenged referral's `issued_by_user_id` may open the file even though it may not decide it.

Deciding a grievance takes a `decision` of `SUSTAINED` or `DENIED`, a `remedy`, and an optional `note` of up to 2,000 characters. The `note` is written into the `snapshot_json` of the `GRIEVANCE_DECIDED` dispatch-record entry and is not a field of the `Grievance` resource, which never carries a `note`. It carries no minimum length either, so an empty string is a perfectly good note and is recorded verbatim as the empty string, while an omitted note — or one sent explicitly as JSON `null` — is recorded as null. The `remedy` defaults to `NONE` and is one of `NONE`, `MAKE_WHOLE_HOURS`, or `NEXT_AVAILABLE_PRIORITY`. Three guards precede the decision. The adjudicator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the challenged referral's `issued_by_user_id`, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. For an `OUT_OF_ORDER_DISPATCH` claim, the `REFERRAL_ISSUED` ledger entry of the challenged referral must still have `purge_state` of `RETAINED`, failing with `ERR_DISPATCH_RECORD_PURGED` — a claim whose evidence has aged out of the five-year retention period cannot be sustained. That guard is reached **only when the decision being recorded is `SUSTAINED`**: an `OUT_OF_ORDER_DISPATCH` claim may always be denied, whatever became of the evidence. It is satisfied when at least one `REFERRAL_ISSUED` entry naming that referral still stands at `RETAINED`, and it fails identically in the two cases where no such unpurged entry can be found — every one of them purged, or none ever written. No other `claim_type` consults the ledger at all. And a `MAKE_WHOLE_HOURS` remedy requires that the challenged referral actually produced a dispatch, while a `DENIED` decision requires `remedy` to be `NONE`; either violation is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_APPLICABLE`. The dispatch consulted — both by that guard and by the make-whole computation below — is the one the challenged referral produced directly: the earliest-created dispatch on that referral whose `recall_of_dispatch_id` is null, falling back to the earliest-created dispatch on the referral of any kind should the referral somehow have left only recall records behind.

Sustaining sets `status` to `SUSTAINED`, records `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and sets `decided_at` to the current instant. **Where the remedy is `NEXT_AVAILABLE_PRIORITY`**, the grievant's registration whose `status` is `REGISTERED` — the earliest-created one, and only that one, should the grievant hold several — has its `priority_flag` raised to true and its own `version` incremented by 1, its list is renumbered so that registration holds `position_number` 1 on that list unless a peer there already carries the flag, and a `POSITION_RESTORED` entry is appended to the dispatch record. That ledger entry is written on the strength of the remedy alone, so it is appended even when the search finds nothing to flag — because the grievant holds no `REGISTERED` registration just then, or because `grievant_member_id` is the opaque identifier of a `BusinessManager` who filed on a member's behalf and so names no member at all. Because `priority_flag` is the second component of the strict-group-order key and the first component of the interfiled key, the flagged registration ranks ahead of every **unflagged** registration it is compared against, and it keeps that advantage until it is consumed — cleared back to false by the next referral the member accepts. The remedy is a promotion within the ordering rather than a guarantee of rank one: a peer on the same list already carrying the flag is still separated from it by `demotion_count` and the components below, and under the strict-group-order variant of a long call the group's `priority_rank` is compared first, so a flag raised on a low-ranked group does not lift the registration past unflagged candidates sitting on a higher-ranked one.

**Where the remedy is `MAKE_WHOLE_HOURS`**, the hall computes what the member lost, and the computation comes in two variants selected not by the adjudicator but by the current condition of the improperly dispatched worker's own dispatch. First a wage factor is fixed from the grievant's own classification: a journeyworker is credited at the full 10,000 basis points of the job request's `hourly_rate_cents`, while an apprentice is credited at the basis-point factor for their `apprentice_period` — 5,625 basis points in period 1, 6,875 in period 2, 8,125 in period 3, and 9,375 in period 4. The full 10,000 basis points are the fallback wherever that lookup cannot be made: where `grievant_member_id` resolves to no `Member` at all — the `BusinessManager`-filed case, since the field is always taken from `X-User-ID` — and where a member somehow carries no `apprentice_period` to look up. The `hourly_rate_cents` the factor is applied to is always read live from the job request behind the challenged referral, never from any figure frozen at issuance.

If the improperly dispatched worker's dispatch is still `WORKING` at the moment of adjudication, the **scheduled-hours** variant applies and `back_pay_variant` records `SCHEDULED_HOURS`. Count the calendar dates falling in the half-open interval that begins on the date of that dispatch's `start_at` and runs up to but not including the date of the grievance's `decided_at`, keeping only those dates that are Monday through Friday. That weekday count multiplied by 8 standard hours per day gives `back_pay_hours`. For the money, first compute one day's award — 8 hours multiplied by `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by the wage factor, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents — and only then multiply that rounded per-day figure by the weekday count to obtain `back_pay_amount_cents`. Weekend dates are excluded from the projection and holidays are not modelled. The two endpoints are the **UTC calendar dates** of those two instants, and the time of day within either is discarded, so a dispatch that started late on a Friday evening UTC and a decision taken early the following Monday span the single weekday Friday. Because the interval is half-open, an adjudication falling on the same UTC date as `start_at` counts zero weekdays, and so does one taken with the clock wound back before that date; the award is then 0 `back_pay_hours` and 0 `back_pay_amount_cents`, and `back_pay_variant` still records `SCHEDULED_HOURS`. Because the interval ends at the adjudication date, the award grows the longer the hall takes to decide.

If the dispatch has already left `WORKING` — `LAID_OFF`, `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE`, or `QUIT` — the **actual-hours** variant applies and `back_pay_variant` records `ACTUAL_HOURS`. Then `back_pay_hours` is simply the contractor-reported `hours_worked` on that dispatch, and `back_pay_amount_cents` is that hours figure multiplied by `hourly_rate_cents` multiplied by the wage factor, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up to whole cents exactly once over the whole award rather than per day. The same grievance therefore yields both a different hours figure and a different cents figure depending on whether the improperly dispatched worker was still on the job when the manager ruled. A `MAKE_WHOLE_CREDITED` ledger entry is appended with `hours_delta` equal to `back_pay_hours`.

Denying is deliberately inert. `status` becomes `DENIED`, `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` are recorded, `back_pay_variant` stays `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and `back_pay_hours` and `back_pay_amount_cents` both stay 0. No registration, referral, or dispatch field changes anywhere — apart from the dispatch record itself, the denial branch has no side effects at all. In both branches a `GRIEVANCE_DECIDED` entry is appended to the dispatch record, recorded after any remedy entry, and the grievance's `version` increments by 1.
