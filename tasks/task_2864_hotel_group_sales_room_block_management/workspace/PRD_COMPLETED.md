# Hotel Group Sales API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A hotel group sales office lives or dies by a single question that nobody can answer from a spreadsheet: *is this piece of group business worth the transient rooms it will push out of the house?* Today the answer is assembled by hand. A sales manager writes a lead into one system, a revenue manager eyeballs a demand forecast in another, a contract administrator types allotments into a Word template, the reservations desk keeps the rooming list in a shared inbox, and three months after the group has departed an accounting clerk tries to reconstruct what was actually picked up so that attrition can be billed and a rebate paid. Every hand-off loses a number, and the numbers that get lost — displaced transient revenue, the pinned comp-room ratio, the waived sub-block — are exactly the ones that decide whether the deal made money.

This document specifies the Hotel Group Sales API, a single transactional service that carries a piece of group business from the first inbound enquiry to the last settlement cent. The service owns the house inventory calendar, scores and routes inbound leads, runs displacement studies under two competing demand series, prices concession packages, papers and countersigns contracts, mints and manages room blocks and their sub-blocks, tracks rooming-list pickup night by night, releases unsold rooms at cutoff, captures banquet, audiovisual, parking and internet revenue, and finally reconciles the event — assessing attrition damages, paying the earned rebate, and advancing the client's repeat-business standing so that the next enquiry from the same organization is priced with its history attached.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as constraints rather than aspirations. First, **every quantity is either an input or derived from inputs by a rule stated here** — there are no discretionary numbers, and where an order of operations changes the answer by a cent, that order is pinned normatively. Second, **the service is the referee between the group sales office and the revenue management office**, which means it enforces separation of duties (the person who requests a displacement study may not clear it; the clerk who prepares a settlement may not finalize it) and it enforces the house's group ceiling even when each individual deal looks admissible in isolation. All business resources are mounted under `/group-sales`; a single operations-only endpoint controls the service's logical clock and sits outside that mount.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — without exception, including read-only requests — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` names the capability set that user is acting under. If either header is absent, blank, or if `X-User-Role` names anything outside the seven roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before request-body parsing, so a syntactically broken payload sent without headers still comes back as `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rather than as a schema complaint. Once the headers are accepted, the role named in `X-User-Role` is matched against the roles authorized for the target endpoint in §4; a role that is not listed there is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- **`SalesManager`** — the group sales manager. Owns the lead from capture through qualification, holds meeting space, drafts and revises rate quotes, and sends them to the client.
- **`RevenueManager`** — revenue management. Owns the house inventory calendar and the concession-package catalogue, requests and decides displacement studies, and waives attrition exposure on a block.
- **`ContractAdmin`** — contract administration. Papers an accepted quote, issues it, countersigns it, cancels it, and carves sub-blocks out of a block.
- **`ReservationsAgent`** — the reservations desk. Manages the rooming list and the block's day-to-day pickup, and advances a guest through the stay.
- **`AccountingClerk`** — group accounting. Quotes and posts ancillary revenue, opens the post-event settlement, and finalizes billing.
- **`GroupContact`** — the client-side signatory acting for exactly one client organization. Counters and accepts quotes and manages its own rooming list.
- **`SystemOperator`** — a test and operations identity that owns only the logical clock. It holds no business permissions whatsoever; every business endpoint rejects a `SystemOperator` with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `SalesManager`, `RevenueManager`, `ContractAdmin`, `ReservationsAgent`, `AccountingClerk`, or `SystemOperator`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it on rows where a workflow step demands an audit stamp — as `owner_user_id` on a lead, `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a displacement analysis, `prepared_by_user_id` on a rate quote and on a reconciliation, `signed_by_user_id` on a contract, `waived_by_user_id` on a room block, and `finalized_by_user_id` on a reconciliation — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

`GroupContact` is the one exception and it is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `GroupContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting client organization — a `GroupAccount` row. Ownership checks on any resource a `GroupContact` touches resolve that resource back to its owning account and compare the result to `X-User-ID`: a `GroupAccount` — and the reverse listing of its leads — resolves to itself; a lead resolves through its `account_id`; a rate quote resolves through its `lead_id` to that lead's `account_id`; a contract resolves through its `quote_id` to the quote's `lead_id` and then to the lead's `account_id`; a reservation resolves through its `block_id` to the block's `contract_id` and then along the same contract chain. If the resolved account is not the one named in `X-User-ID`, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) — not with a 404. Ownership is deliberately checked *after* path existence, so a `GroupContact` probing an identifier that does not exist at all receives the relevant not-found error, while probing a real row belonging to a different organization receives 403.

Separation of duties is a third, independent gate that runs after both the role gate and the ownership gate. It rejects the right role held by the wrong person: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id` when deciding a displacement analysis, must differ from `prepared_by_user_id` when finalizing a reconciliation, and must differ from the quote's `prepared_by_user_id` when accepting a quote. Any of these three collisions is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that this applies even where one role legitimately covers both halves of the pair — a `RevenueManager` may both request and decide displacement studies in general, just never the same one; and a `GroupContact` whose account `id` happens to equal a quote's `prepared_by_user_id` cannot accept that quote.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.4 and never from the host's wall clock, so two requests separated by no clock movement carry the same `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0 when omitted. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp, `created_at`, ascending. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock, every row written without an intervening clock move carries an identical value; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were written, earliest first, and that same tie-break settles equal sort keys under the overriding orderings too. Two endpoints override this: the inventory calendar listing is ordered by `stay_date` ascending, a block's nights are ordered by `stay_date` ascending, and a lead's function-space holds are ordered by `start_at` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the pagination window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this service reads wall-clock time. Every deadline, expiry, cutoff, lead-time bucket, lapse test and damage tier is evaluated against a **logical clock** that the service owns. Before any client moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this is a mandated startup default and the service never falls back to the host's real time. A `SystemOperator` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the clock's new position as `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no business row — but because so many rules are clock-derived, the same read of the same account can legitimately return a different `rebooking_incentive_pct` before and after the clock advances.

Two conventions govern date arithmetic throughout. First, **stay ranges are half-open**: a program described by `arrival_date` and `departure_date` occupies every calendar date from `arrival_date` up to but excluding `departure_date`, and its `nights_count` is the number of whole days between the two. A group departing on the same date another group arrives therefore never collides on inventory. Second, where a rule compares a clock instant to a calendar date, the clock's UTC calendar date is the value used.

Date-typed fields (`stay_date`, `arrival_date`, `departure_date`, `cutoff_date`, `last_completed_departure_date`, and the `from_date` / `to_date` window filters) are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings. Instant-typed fields (`decision_deadline_at`, `expires_at`, `start_at`, `end_at`, `signed_at`) are ISO 8601 UTC instants; instants the server mints are rendered with three fractional digits. A client-supplied instant is accepted liberally — with or without the seconds component, with anywhere from zero to nine fractional digits, and with a `Z` designator, no designator at all (read as UTC), or a numeric `±HH:MM` / `±HHMM` offset that is converted to UTC — but whatever form arrives is normalized on storage, so a read of the row renders it with exactly three fractional digits and a `Z`.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource in this service — `GroupAccount`, `Lead`, `InventoryDay`, `ConcessionPackage`, `DisplacementAnalysis`, `RateQuote`, `GroupContract`, `RoomBlock`, `BlockNight`, `Reservation`, `AncillaryCharge`, `Reconciliation` and `FunctionSpaceHold` — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** — a server-minted UUID string. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply it on creation.
- **`created_at`** — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time.
- **`updated_at`** — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the last mutation of the row. Any change to any property refreshes it, including changes the server makes to derived properties on its own initiative during a cascade.
- **`version`** — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that only touch derived properties.

The `version` property is what makes cascades observable and what the version guards of §2.7 compare against. It is important to read it literally: when a cascade touches five rows, all five rows increment their own `version` and refresh their own `updated_at`. When a lead's negotiation stage does not move but the lead is nonetheless touched — for instance because an additional displacement study was requested against it — the lead's `version` still increments, and any client holding the pre-request value is now stale.

### 2.6. Money, Room Nights and Rounding Discipline

All money in this service is expressed in **whole cents as JSON integers**. Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer, and a client that sends money as a decimal number or as a quoted string is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The same rule applies to room counts and room-night counts: `peak_rooms`, `total_requested_room_nights`, `rooms_allotted`, `contracted_room_nights` and every sibling are integers, never strings.

Percentages and ratios are the exception: `room_rate_discount_pct`, `banquet_discount_pct`, `av_discount_pct`, `parking_discount_pct`, `max_discount_stack_pct`, `discount_stack_pct`, `attrition_threshold_pct`, `rebooking_incentive_pct`, `incentive_rate_snapshot` and `discount_pct` are JSON numbers expressing a fraction of one — a five per cent discount is `0.05`, not `5`.

Wherever a monetary result must be rounded, the method is **round half up**: a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude. Rounding is never applied speculatively — it is applied only at the points the calculation rules of §3 name explicitly, and the *number of rounding points and their order are normative*, because a rule that rounds two components separately and then subtracts them produces a different cent total than one that subtracts in exact arithmetic and rounds once at the end. Where a rule rounds a commitment *up to the next whole room night* rather than half-up, it says so; that operation always rounds up, so a commitment of 40.1 room nights becomes 41. Every product, quotient and sum that feeds one of those rounding points is formed in **exact decimal arithmetic** on the values exactly as they were written, never in binary floating point, so a quantity that lands precisely on a boundary is rounded from that boundary rather than from a representation error near it: 1,050 cents at a `room_rate_discount_pct` of 0.15 is exactly 157.5 and therefore rounds half up to 158, and a 100-room-night commitment at an `attrition_threshold_pct` of 0.8 is exactly 80 and therefore rounds up to 80 rather than to 81.

Integer-only quantities — room counts, room nights, scores, negotiation rounds — are exact and are never rounded at all. Where a quantity is defined as an exact rational (for example the ratio of requested meeting-space square footage to peak rooms), it is compared against its thresholds at full precision, not after being rounded to an integer.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Parent Records

Four write operations in this service change a picture that a second writer may have already changed. Each of them therefore requires the client to declare which version of the **parent** record it was reasoning about, and each rejects the write if that declaration no longer matches the parent's current `version`. The declaration field is mandatory on those four operations — it is not an optional optimistic-locking opt-in — and no other endpoint in the service accepts a version field at all.

- **Drafting a rate quote** (`POST /group-sales/rate-quotes`) requires `expected_lead_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Lead`. If it does not equal the lead's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEAD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A newly drafted quote legitimately supersedes a `DRAFT` quote already sitting on the lead, so a writer working from a stale view of the lead would silently discard a sibling quote it never saw.
- **Deciding a displacement analysis** (`POST /group-sales/displacement-analyses/{id}/decide`) requires `expected_lead_version`, again carrying the client's last observed `version` of the analysis's `Lead`. A mismatch against the lead's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEAD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Requesting an additional study under the other series variant advances the lead's `version`, and clearing the lead from a stale view would approve a displacement picture the decider never inspected.
- **Creating a sub-block** (`POST /group-sales/room-blocks`) requires `expected_parent_block_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `RoomBlock`. A mismatch against the parent block's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every sub-block carves rooms out of the parent's per-night allotment and bumps the parent's `version`, so a writer holding a stale parent version is reasoning about unallocated rooms that are already taken.
- **Opening a reconciliation** (`POST /group-sales/contracts/{id}/reconciliations`) requires `expected_contract_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `GroupContract`. A mismatch against the contract's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new draft legitimately supersedes an open draft, and an attrition waiver or a newly posted ancillary charge bumps the contract's `version`, so a stale writer would settle on numbers that no longer hold.

The version guard is evaluated early — ahead of every state check and every business rule — so a client holding a stale parent version learns that fact first, even when the operation it attempted would also have been rejected on state grounds.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

A single request can violate several rules at once, and which error the client sees is part of the contract. The service evaluates the following ladder in order and returns the **first** violation it finds; lower-numbered rules win. Priorities 1 through 4 apply to every endpoint that can reach them.

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` present and non-blank, `X-User-Role` present and one of the seven declared roles. Applies to all endpoints, and runs before request-schema parsing. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role gate** — `X-User-Role` is listed among the endpoint's authorized roles. Applies to all endpoints. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This is the role check only; ownership and separation of duties are separate, later gates.
3. **Request schema** — the body parses, every non-optional field named by the endpoint is present, types match, enum values are members of their declared sets, money and room fields are JSON integers rather than strings, date fields are `YYYY-MM-DD`, and instant fields are ISO 8601 UTC. Applies to all write endpoints, and to the query parameters of every list endpoint (a `limit` outside 1 through 100 or a negative `offset` lands here too). Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Every per-field numeric floor and ceiling and every string-length bound that §3 or §2.9 declares is enforced at this same gate and reports the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) — a `peak_rooms` of 0, a `room_rate_discount_pct` of 0.7, a `group_ceiling_rooms` above the same day's `total_rooms`, an over-long `name` — so none of them is a 422. This gate inspects request shape only and performs no cross-row lookups; fields whose values the server computes or stamps are ignored when a client supplies them, never rejected.
4. **Path existence** — every identifier embedded in the URL resolves, validated left to right, and the error names the entity of the leftmost segment that failed. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter. Violation is one of `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.
5. **Client ownership** — when the acting role is `GroupContact`, `X-User-ID` must equal the owning account resolved as described in §2.1. Applies to countering a quote, accepting a quote, creating a reservation, cancelling a reservation, and every single-resource or parent-anchored read a `GroupContact` performs. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Where the owning account is reached through a **body** foreign key rather than a path identifier — creating a reservation, whose owner resolves through `block_id` — that key is resolved to its row before this gate can run, so an unresolvable `block_id` reports `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND` (404) and only a resolvable one belonging to another organization reports 403. The one filtered peer listing a `GroupContact` may reach — the rate-quotes listing — has nothing to reject and is instead silently narrowed: only quotes resolving to `X-User-ID` are returned, and `total_count` counts only those.
6. **Body foreign keys** — every foreign key supplied in the body resolves: `account_id`, `lead_id`, `concession_package_id`, `quote_id`, `parent_block_id`, `contract_id`, `block_id`. They are checked in the order the endpoint lists them. Applies to creating a lead, a displacement analysis, a rate quote, a contract, a room block, a reservation, an ancillary charge and a function-space hold. Violation is one of `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.
7. **Intra-request date sanity** — `departure_date` strictly after `arrival_date` on leads and reservations; `end_at` strictly after `start_at` on function-space holds; `expires_at` strictly after the current logical clock on quote creation and revision; and a nightly allotment plan whose `stay_date` values are all distinct and contiguous. Applies to creating a lead, creating a reservation, creating a function-space hold, creating a rate quote, revising a rate quote, and creating a contract. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (422). No other row is consulted at this level.
8. **Parent version guard** — the `expected_lead_version`, `expected_parent_block_version` or `expected_contract_version` in the body equals the named parent's current `version`, exactly as specified in §2.7. Applies to creating a rate quote, deciding a displacement analysis, creating a room block, and opening a reconciliation. Violation: `ERR_STALE_LEAD_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (412).
9. **Separation of duties** — the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the user recorded for the preceding step: `requested_by_user_id` on deciding an analysis, `prepared_by_user_id` on finalizing a reconciliation, `prepared_by_user_id` on accepting a quote. Applies to those three operations. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. **Declared transition exists** — the target row is in a state from which the requested trigger has a declared transition. Applies to every action endpoint, and includes terminal-state lockdown: any trigger aimed at a terminal state fails here. Violation is one of `ERR_LEAD_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_QUOTE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_HOLD_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_CHARGE_INVALID_STATE`, all HTTP 409.
11. **One-shot replay guard** — a step that may only be consumed once is being repeated: an analysis whose status is no longer `PENDING` is being decided, a reconciliation whose status is no longer `DRAFT` is being finalized, or a block whose `billing_status` is already `WAIVED` or `SETTLED` is having attrition waived. Applies to those three operations. Violation is `ERR_ANALYSIS_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_RECONCILIATION_ALREADY_FINALIZED` or `ERR_BLOCK_ALREADY_BILLED` (409), each distinguishable from an ordinary wrong-state rejection. Two of the three invert the order against priority 10: finalizing a reconciliation tests the settlement's own `DRAFT` status before the contract's `SIGNED` status, and waiving attrition tests the block's already-billed `billing_status` before the settlement conditions the two source states impose. Only deciding an analysis keeps the declared order, and §3.3 spells that case out.
12. **Block open for pickup** — the block a rooming-list entry is being written into has `inventory_status` equal to `OPEN`; `SETUP`, `CUT_OFF`, `RELEASED` and `CLOSED` all reject. Applies to creating a reservation. Violation: `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_OPEN` (409). This sits deliberately **above** the per-night allotment ceiling at priority 29, so a request that both targets a `SETUP` block and asks for more rooms than remain returns `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_OPEN`.
13. **Quote still live** — the quote's `expires_at` is strictly after the current logical clock. Applies to countering a quote, to accepting a quote and to creating a contract; revising a quote is not gated here, so a `COUNTERED` quote may be re-priced after its `expires_at` has passed. Violation: `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` (422). This sits **above** the approved-displacement gate at priority 18, so a quote that is both expired and lacking an approved study returns `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`.
14. **Before cutoff** — the current logical clock date is strictly before the block's `cutoff_date`. Applies to creating a reservation. Violation: `ERR_PAST_CUTOFF_DATE` (422). Rooming-list additions stop at the cutoff date even when the block has not yet been mechanically cut off.
15. **At or after cutoff** — the current logical clock date is on or after the block's `cutoff_date`. Applies to cutting a block off. Violation: `ERR_CUTOFF_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (422). Together with priority 14 this makes the cutoff date a hard partition of the block's timeline.
16. **Before arrival** — the current logical clock date is strictly before the reservation's `arrival_date`. Applies to cancelling a reservation. Violation: `ERR_CANCEL_AFTER_ARRIVAL` (422). After arrival a guest may only be advanced or marked a no-show; the cancellation channel is closed.
17. **Event concluded** — the current logical clock date is on or after the contract's `departure_date`. Applies to opening a reconciliation. Violation: `ERR_EVENT_NOT_CONCLUDED` (422). Settlement cannot open while the group is still in house.
18. **Displacement cleared** — the quote's lead carries at least one displacement analysis in `APPROVED` status, and no analysis of that lead is still `PENDING`. Applies to accepting a quote and to creating a contract. Proposing a quote is gated here too but on the weaker half of the test only: it requires an `APPROVED` analysis and is indifferent to a sibling study still sitting `PENDING`. Violation: `ERR_DISPLACEMENT_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
19. **Calendar seeded** — an inventory day exists for every date in the half-open range the operation must walk. Applies to qualifying a lead, creating a displacement analysis, proposing a quote, and signing a contract. Violation: `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_MISSING` (422). A gap in the calendar is a data error, never a zero.
20. **Sub-block dates inside parent** — every `stay_date` in a sub-block's allotment plan has a corresponding night on the parent block, checked in ascending `stay_date` order so the first offending date decides the error. Applies to creating a room block. Violation: `ERR_SUB_BLOCK_DATES_OUTSIDE_PARENT` (422).
21. **Stay inside block** — the reservation's half-open stay range is fully covered by nights of the target block. Applies to creating a reservation. Violation: `ERR_STAY_DATE_OUTSIDE_BLOCK` (422). A guest may not shoulder a night the block does not hold, even when the contract's own date range covers it.
22. **Blocks cut off** — every room block of the contract has `inventory_status` in `CUT_OFF`, `RELEASED` or `CLOSED`. Applies to opening a reconciliation. Violation: `ERR_BLOCKS_NOT_CUT_OFF` (409). Attrition cannot be assessed while any sub-block can still take pickup.
23. **Allotment plan agrees with the headline** — the plan's room counts sum to `contracted_room_nights`, its largest nightly figure equals `contracted_peak_rooms`, and its dates are exactly the lead's half-open stay range. Applies to creating a contract. Violation: `ERR_ALLOTMENT_PLAN_MISMATCH` (422).
24. **No duplicate rooming-list entry** — no other non-cancelled reservation on the same contract already carries this `guest_name` with this `arrival_date`. Applies to creating a reservation. Violation: `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOMING_LIST_ENTRY` (409).
25. **Function space free** — no other held or confirmed hold on the same `space_code` overlaps the requested interval once each existing interval is extended by the turnover buffer at its end. Applies to creating a function-space hold. Violation: `ERR_FUNCTION_SPACE_CONFLICT` (409).
26. **Concession eligibility** — the quote's `quoted_peak_rooms` is at least the package's `min_peak_rooms` and its `quoted_room_nights` is at least the package's `min_total_room_nights`; both floors are inclusive. Applies to creating a rate quote and to revising one. Violation: `ERR_CONCESSION_INELIGIBLE` (422).
27. **Discount stack within cap** — the package's four discount fractions sum to no more than its `max_discount_stack_pct`, the comparison being inclusive at the cap. Applies to creating a concession package and to creating a rate quote. Violation: `ERR_DISCOUNT_STACK_EXCEEDED` (422).
28. **Group ceiling** — for every date in the program's half-open stay range, the inventory day's `committed_group_rooms` plus `held_group_rooms` plus the rooms this request would add is no more than that day's `group_ceiling_rooms`. Applies to proposing a quote and to signing a contract. Violation: `ERR_GROUP_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (409). This is an aggregate rule: two quotes can each be individually admissible while their sum breaches the ceiling.
29. **Per-night allotment** — for every stay date of the reservation, the block's night for that date has at least one room open. Applies to creating a reservation. Violation: `ERR_BLOCK_ALLOTMENT_EXCEEDED` (409).
30. **Sub-block per-night allotment** — every requested nightly figure is no greater than the parent night's unallocated rooms. Applies to creating a room block. Violation: `ERR_SUB_BLOCK_ALLOTMENT_EXCEEDED` (409). Sub-blocks may collectively carve up the master but may never overdraw a single night of it.
31. **Net benefit or written override** — the analysis's `net_benefit_cents` is zero or greater, or the approving call supplies a non-blank `override_reason`. Applies to approving a displacement analysis. Violation: `ERR_DISPLACEMENT_NET_BENEFIT_NEGATIVE` (422). A rejection decision never needs an override.
32. **Account credit standing** — the contract's client organization has `credit_hold` false. Applies to signing a contract and to posting an ancillary charge. Violation: `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 402). This is the last gate of all: financial standing is checked only after every other reason to reject has been exhausted.

One further rejection sits outside the ladder because it guards a single compensating action: voiding a posted ancillary charge whose contract already carries a finalized reconciliation is rejected with `ERR_RECEIVABLE_ALREADY_SETTLED` (409), because a settled receivable cannot be unwound.

### 2.9. House Inventory Conservation and the Group Ceiling

The house inventory calendar is the shared resource that every deal competes for, and it is represented one calendar date at a time. Each `InventoryDay` row describes a single `stay_date` and carries the house's sellable `total_rooms`, the `group_ceiling_rooms` that caps how much of that date may be held plus committed to group business, a forward-looking `transient_demand_rooms` and `transient_adr_cents` forecast pair, and a same-date-last-year `historical_pickup_rooms` and `historical_adr_cents` realized pair. `group_ceiling_rooms` is bounded below by zero and above by `total_rooms`; the demand and rate figures are all zero or greater; `total_rooms` is at least 1. A `stay_date` is unique across the calendar, and re-posting a date that already exists replaces those seven input figures while preserving that date's two derived occupancy counters. That replacement is an update of the row already standing on the date — its `id` and `created_at` are unchanged, its `version` increments by one and its `updated_at` refreshes — and the response is still HTTP 201 carrying that same row.

Those two counters are the conserved quantities. `held_group_rooms` is the number of rooms on that date **softly held** by rate quotes whose status is `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`. `committed_group_rooms` is the number of rooms on that date **hard-committed** by master room blocks — blocks with no parent — whose night for that date has not yet been released. A soft hold becomes a hard commitment at exactly one moment, when a contract is countersigned; at that moment the quote's hold comes off `held_group_rooms` and the allotment plan's rooms go onto `committed_group_rooms`. Every other movement is a pure increase or a pure decrease. Neither counter is ever set directly by a client.

The group ceiling itself is an **aggregate** rule, not a per-deal rule: for each date in the range under consideration, the sum of that date's `held_group_rooms`, its `committed_group_rooms`, and the rooms the request in hand would add must not exceed that date's `group_ceiling_rooms`. Because the test is aggregate, two quotes that are each individually admissible can be jointly inadmissible, and the second one to be proposed is the one that fails with `ERR_GROUP_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. Because the stay range is half-open, a group departing on the date another group arrives never contends for the same night.

### 2.10. Cascade Integrity Across the Contract Tree

Many operations in this service touch more than one row, and the specification treats a cascade as a **single atomic step with a normative hop order** rather than as a bag of eventual side effects. Three consequences follow, and they hold everywhere a cascade is described in §3.

First, **every row a cascade moves records the touch**: its `updated_at` refreshes and its `version` increments by one, whether the change was to a status field, to a stamped user identifier, or only to a derived roll-up — except where §3 says explicitly that a particular row's `version` is left alone. A cascade that flows from a reconciliation into a contract and from the contract into every block of its tree bumps the `version` of each of those rows. The client organization is not among them: its repeat-business history is re-derived on every read rather than stored, so settlement finalization changes what a read of the account returns without moving that account's own `version` or `updated_at`.

Second, **derived quantities are recomputed rather than adjusted by inference** wherever a rule says so. A room block's pickup roll-up, a contract's receivable roll-up, and the comp designations across a contract's reservations are all re-derived from their inputs after the change that disturbed them, which is why cancelling one guest can move the complimentary designation onto a completely different guest.

Third, **a block carries two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `inventory_status` tracks the block's relationship with the house's rooms and moves through `SETUP`, `OPEN`, `CUT_OFF`, and then either `RELEASED` or `CLOSED`. `billing_status` tracks the block's relationship with the settlement and moves through `UNBILLED`, then `ATTRITION_ASSESSED` or `WAIVED`, and finally `SETTLED`. Neither dimension constrains the other's transitions except where a rule explicitly says so; a block can be `CUT_OFF` and still `UNBILLED`, or `CUT_OFF` and `WAIVED`.

### 2.11. Nested and Filtered Resource Retrieval

Several read paths address a child collection through its parent, and several more expose a filtered view of a peer collection. Where a path contains more than one identifier, or where an identifier precedes a sub-resource segment, identifiers are resolved strictly **left to right** and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error — so a request for the nights of a block that does not exist returns `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND` and never attempts to read nights.

Child collections are reached in one of two ways and never in a third. A **reverse-lookup listing** hangs off the parent: a client organization's leads, a lead's displacement analyses, a lead's function-space holds, a contract's reconciliations, a contract's ancillary charges, a block's nights, and a block's own reservations. A **filtered peer listing** takes the foreign key as a query parameter: rate quotes filterable by `lead_id` and `concession_package_id`, contracts filterable by `quote_id`, and room blocks filterable by `contract_id`, by `parent_block_id`, and by `is_master` — the last taking `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` case-insensitively, any other value being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Most listings additionally accept a `status` filter, the analyses listing accepts `analysis_variant`, the holds listing accepts `space_code`, the charges listing accepts `category`, the blocks listing accepts `inventory_status`, and the date-indexed listings — the inventory calendar and a block's nights — accept a `from_date` window bound that is inclusive and a `to_date` window bound that is exclusive. A foreign-key query filter is matched by string equality and is never resolved as a row: a `lead_id`, `concession_package_id`, `quote_id`, `contract_id` or `parent_block_id` naming nothing that exists is not a 404 but simply an empty page with `total_count` 0.

With one deliberate exception, resources are **not created through a nested path**. A sub-block names its parent in the body, a reservation names its block in the body, an ancillary charge names its contract in the body. The exception is the post-event settlement, which is opened against a contract identified in the path. Two further resources are never created by a client at all: the master room block and its nights are minted by the countersignature cascade.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Client Organizations, the House Calendar, and Concession Design

Everything begins with a **client organization**, recorded as a `GroupAccount`. Either a `SalesManager` or a `RevenueManager` may register one, supplying the organization's legal `name` (a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters), its `market_segment`, and the `billing_contact_email` to which attrition invoices and rebate advices will be sent — a non-blank string of 1 to 320 characters that must contain an `@`. The `market_segment` is one of `CORPORATE`, `ASSOCIATION`, `GOVERNMENT`, or `SMERF`, and it is not a label: it is the multiplier that scales every lead this organization ever sends. A `credit_hold` flag may be supplied at registration and defaults to false when omitted; while it is true the organization may not have a contract countersigned and may not have ancillary revenue posted.

Four properties of an organization are the service's own memory of the relationship and are never written by a client. `repeat_group_count` counts the contracts belonging to this organization — reachable from a contract through its quote, that quote's lead, and that lead's `account_id` — whose status has reached `COMPLETED`; it starts at 0. `lifetime_actualized_room_nights` sums the `actualized_room_nights` of every finalized settlement whose contract resolves to this organization; it also starts at 0. `last_completed_departure_date` is the latest `departure_date` among that organization's completed contracts, and is null while none has completed. And `rebooking_incentive_pct` is the organization's standing on the repeat-business ladder, starting at `0.01`.

That ladder is worth spelling out because it is **re-derived on every single read of the organization**, against the logical clock, rather than being cached at the moment a contract completes. The base standing is five per cent (`0.05`) when the organization has completed at least 3 contracts **or** has accumulated at least 1500 lifetime actualized room nights; failing that it is three per cent (`0.03`) when the organization has completed at least 1 contract **or** has accumulated at least 400 lifetime actualized room nights; failing both it is one per cent (`0.01`). A relationship that has gone quiet then decays: if `last_completed_departure_date` is not null and the number of whole days from that date to the logical clock's current date is more than 730 — that is, more than two years have passed since the organization's last group departed — the base standing drops exactly one rung, so five per cent becomes three per cent and three per cent becomes one per cent. One per cent is the floor and cannot decay further. The resulting `rebooking_incentive_pct` is therefore always exactly `0.01`, `0.03`, or `0.05`, with no rounding involved, and advancing the logical clock past the two-year mark visibly changes what a read of the organization returns.

The house calendar is seeded by a `RevenueManager`, one `stay_date` at a time, with the seven figures described in §2.9. Nothing in the service invents a calendar date: qualification, displacement study, quote proposal and countersignature all walk a date range, and any gap in that range stops the operation with `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_MISSING` rather than being treated as an empty day.

Concession design is also a `RevenueManager` responsibility. A **concession package** is a reusable bundle of everything a hotel is willing to give away, and it is defined once and then pinned onto individual quotes. It carries a `name` of 1 to 60 characters (`PLATINUM_CITYWIDE` is a typical label), a `room_rate_discount_pct` taken off the quoted nightly rate, a `comp_room_ratio` recording how many paid room nights earn one complimentary room night, a `rebate_per_room_night_cents` figure rebated to the client per billable room night at settlement, and three category discounts — `banquet_discount_pct`, `av_discount_pct`, and `parking_discount_pct`. Each of the four discount fractions lies between 0 and 0.5 inclusive; `comp_room_ratio` is at least 1; `rebate_per_room_night_cents` is zero or greater. Two eligibility floors, `min_peak_rooms` and `min_total_room_nights`, are each zero or greater and gate which programs may use the package at all. Finally, `max_discount_stack_pct` is greater than 0 and no more than 1.

On creation the service computes the package's `discount_stack_pct` as the sum of exactly four fractions — the room-rate discount, the banquet discount, the audiovisual discount, and the parking discount. That sum is formed in the exact decimal arithmetic of §2.6 and stored as the exact decimal total, so a package combining 0.1 and 0.2 reads back a `discount_stack_pct` of exactly `0.3`. If that sum exceeds `max_discount_stack_pct` the package is refused with `ERR_DISCOUNT_STACK_EXCEEDED`; a sum landing exactly on the cap is accepted, because the comparison is inclusive. The same stack check is re-run when a quote is drafted against the package, so a package that was edited into an inadmissible state cannot be quoted from.

### 3.2. Lead Capture, Qualification Scoring, and Meeting Space

A `SalesManager` captures an inbound enquiry as a **`Lead`**, naming the client organization through `account_id` and supplying the client-facing `program_name` (1 to 120 characters), the program's `arrival_date` and `departure_date`, the `peak_rooms` requested on the busiest night, the `total_requested_room_nights` across the whole pattern, the `meeting_space_sqft_needed`, and the client's stated `decision_deadline_at`. `departure_date` must be strictly later than `arrival_date`; `peak_rooms` and `total_requested_room_nights` are each at least 1; `meeting_space_sqft_needed` is zero or greater; `decision_deadline_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC instant and three fractional digits are accepted. The service stamps `owner_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, derives `nights_count` as the number of whole days between the two dates — which is exactly the count of occupied stay dates in the half-open range — and starts the lead's `status` at `NEW`. Both `qualification_score` and `estimated_room_night_value_cents` are null until the lead is qualified.

**Qualification** is the sales manager's decision point, and it is a scoring exercise with a hard fork. It may only be performed on a lead whose `status` is `NEW`, and it requires an inventory day for every date in the lead's half-open stay range even though the score itself reads no inventory figure — a missing date stops the operation with `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_MISSING`. The score is built from four additive components and then scaled:

- **Room-night volume** contributes the smaller of 40 and the `total_requested_room_nights` divided by 10 with any fraction discarded. A 250-room-night program earns 25 points; a 700-room-night program earns 40, the cap.
- **Peak size** contributes the smaller of 25 and `peak_rooms` divided by 4 with any fraction discarded. A 60-room peak earns 15 points; anything from 100 rooms up earns the 25-point cap.
- **Meeting-space intensity** works off an exact ratio: `meeting_space_sqft_needed` divided by the larger of 1 and `peak_rooms`, kept as an exact rational and never rounded. When that ratio is 30.0 or less the program contributes 15 points; when it is greater than 30.0 but 60.0 or less it contributes 8 points; when it is greater than 60.0 it contributes nothing. A program asking for a great deal of function space per guest room is space-hungry and scores worse.
- **Lead time** works off the number of whole days from the logical clock's current date to the lead's `arrival_date`. Six months or more of lead time — 180 days or more — contributes 20 points; three months or more but less than six — 90 days or more — contributes 12; anything shorter contributes 5.

Those four components are summed into a raw score. That raw score is then multiplied by the segment factor for the organization's `market_segment` — 1.20 for `CORPORATE`, 1.00 for `ASSOCIATION`, 0.85 for `GOVERNMENT`, 0.75 for `SMERF` — and the product is rounded half up to a whole number **once**, after which the result is capped at 100. The capping happens after the rounding, and the rounding happens after the multiplication; a raw score of 88 in the corporate segment therefore yields 100, not 105.

The resulting integer is written to `qualification_score`, and the same call writes `estimated_room_night_value_cents` regardless of which way the fork goes. That value is the projected group rooms revenue and is computed in a strictly pinned two-stage rounding. First, the transient average daily rates of every inventory day in the half-open stay range are summed and divided by the number of those dates, and that average is rounded half up to whole cents. Second, the rounded average is multiplied by the group-rate factor of 0.82 — group business is priced at eighty-two per cent of transient — and that product is rounded half up to whole cents again. Only then is the twice-rounded nightly figure multiplied by `total_requested_room_nights`, and that final multiplication is **not** rounded because both operands are already integers. Reversing the order — averaging, applying the factor, and rounding once at the end — is not equivalent and is not permitted.

Then the fork. A `qualification_score` of 30 or more moves the lead `NEW → QUALIFIED`; a score below 30 moves it `NEW → TURNED_DOWN`. The threshold is inclusive, so a score of exactly 30 qualifies. The two branches are not symmetric in their consequences: on the turned-down branch the service additionally **releases the lead's meeting space**, moving every function-space hold of that lead whose status is `HELD` to `RELEASED`. Both branches increment the lead's `version`. `TURNED_DOWN` is terminal — a turned-down lead can never be re-qualified, and a second qualification attempt against any lead that is no longer `NEW` is refused with `ERR_LEAD_INVALID_STATE`.

Meeting space is held independently of the lead's stage. A `SalesManager` creates a **`FunctionSpaceHold`** naming the `lead_id`, the `space_code` — one of `GRAND_BALLROOM`, `SALON_A`, `SALON_B`, or `BOARDROOM` — the `start_at` and `end_at` instants bounding the occupation, and the `attendee_count` expected in the set, which must be at least 1. `end_at` must be strictly after `start_at`, and the occupied interval is half-open: it includes `start_at` and excludes `end_at`. A new hold starts in `HELD`.

Conflict detection on function space accounts for **turnover**. Before comparing intervals, every existing hold on the same `space_code` whose status is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` is notionally extended by 45 minutes at its end; holds already in `RELEASED` are ignored entirely. If the requested interval overlaps any of those extended intervals, the request is refused with `ERR_FUNCTION_SPACE_CONFLICT`. Because the comparison is half-open on the extended interval, a request starting exactly at an existing hold's `end_at` collides — the room is still being reset — while a request starting exactly 45 minutes after that `end_at` does not.

A hold leaves `HELD` in one of two directions. It is **confirmed** as a consequence of the contract being countersigned, moving `HELD → CONFIRMED`, after which the buffered occupation stays in force for future overlap checks. Or it is **released**, moving either `HELD → RELEASED` or `CONFIRMED → RELEASED`, which a `SalesManager` or a `RevenueManager` may do directly and which also happens as a consequence of the lead being turned down at qualification or lost at a displacement rejection. Releasing frees the buffered interval immediately, so a later hold may take the slot. Releasing a hold that is already `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_HOLD_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.3. Displacement Review and the Clearing Decision

No qualified lead may be quoted until revenue management has looked at what the group would push out of the house. That examination is a **`DisplacementAnalysis`**, requested by a `RevenueManager` against a `lead_id` under one of two `analysis_variant` values: `FORECAST_BASED` or `HISTORICAL_BASED`. The study is admissible when the lead's `status` is `QUALIFIED`, `CLEARED`, `QUOTED`, or `DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW`; any other stage — a `NEW` lead not yet scored, a `TURNED_DOWN` one, a `WON` or `LOST` one — is refused with `ERR_LEAD_INVALID_STATE`. The service stamps `requested_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, leaves `decided_by_user_id` null, starts the analysis in `PENDING`, and requires an inventory day for every date of the lead's half-open stay range.

The study walks that half-open date range in ascending order and accumulates two figures. For each date it first establishes how many rooms transient demand could still have: the day's `total_rooms` less its `committed_group_rooms` less its `held_group_rooms`. It then takes a demand figure and an average-daily-rate figure for that date **from the series the variant selects**, and computes the rooms displaced on that date as the larger of zero and the following quantity: the variant's demand figure plus the lead's `peak_rooms`, less the rooms transient could still have had. Those per-date displaced rooms are summed into `displaced_room_nights`, and each date's displaced rooms multiplied by that date's variant rate is summed into `displaced_revenue_cents`. Both per-date products are exact integers, so no intermediate rounding exists anywhere in the accumulation.

The two variants differ only in which pair of columns the series comes from, and that single difference is enough to flip the study's conclusion:

- Under **`FORECAST_BASED`**, the demand figure for a date is that inventory day's `transient_demand_rooms` and the rate figure is its `transient_adr_cents` — the revenue-management forward forecast.
- Under **`HISTORICAL_BASED`**, the demand figure for a date is that inventory day's `historical_pickup_rooms` and the rate figure is its `historical_adr_cents` — the same-date-last-year realized series.

The same lead run against the same calendar therefore produces a different `displaced_room_nights`, a different `displaced_revenue_cents`, and can produce the opposite verdict under the two variants; running both is precisely how the office argues about a marginal piece of business.

Three more figures complete the study. `group_revenue_cents` is copied straight from the lead's `estimated_room_night_value_cents` as a snapshot at study creation and is never recomputed inside the study. `ancillary_projection_cents` projects the banquet, audiovisual and parking spend as the lead's `peak_rooms` multiplied by its `nights_count` multiplied by 4,500 cents — forty-five dollars per room night — rounded half up to whole cents. And `net_benefit_cents` is `group_revenue_cents` plus `ancillary_projection_cents` less `displaced_revenue_cents`. From that, `verdict` is `ACCEPT` when `net_benefit_cents` is zero or greater, and `OVERRIDE_REQUIRED` when it is negative; the boundary is inclusive, so a study that exactly breaks even accepts.

Requesting a study also moves the lead — but only the **first** one moves its negotiation stage. A study requested against a `QUALIFIED` lead advances it `QUALIFIED → DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW`. A study requested against a lead that has already been past that gate leaves the stage exactly where it stands: `CLEARED → CLEARED` for a re-study of already-cleared business, `QUOTED → QUOTED` for a mid-negotiation re-study, and `DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW → DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW` for a second study run under the other variant while the first is still open. In every one of those cases an additional analysis is created in `PENDING` and any earlier `PENDING` analysis is left `PENDING` — the service does not supersede studies. This is what makes "a `PENDING` analysis exists" independently observable from the lead's stage. Every one of these creations increments the lead's `version`, which is the bump that strands a client still holding the pre-creation lead version when it later tries to decide a study.

While a re-study sits `PENDING` against a `QUOTED` lead, the outstanding quote keeps its `PROPOSED` status and keeps its inventory hold — but it **cannot be accepted**, because acceptance requires that no study of the lead is still open.

**Deciding** a study is the `RevenueManager`'s clearing act. The payload carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, an `expected_lead_version` matching the lead's current `version`, and, when needed, an `override_reason` of up to 500 characters. The lead must be in `DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW`, `CLEARED`, or `QUOTED`, and on this operation that lead-stage gate is the priority-10 check of §2.8, so it is evaluated **ahead** of the one-shot replay guard at priority 11: re-deciding a study that was already `REJECTED`, whose lead has consequently moved to `LOST`, is refused with `ERR_LEAD_INVALID_STATE` and not with `ERR_ANALYSIS_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The study must then be `PENDING`; a second decision against an already-decided study whose lead still stands in one of those three stages is refused with `ERR_ANALYSIS_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the study's `requested_by_user_id`, or the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the person who asked the question may not answer it, even though one role covers both operations.

On **`APPROVE`**, one further gate applies: either the study's `verdict` is `ACCEPT`, or the payload supplies a non-blank `override_reason`. Approving a study whose `net_benefit_cents` is negative without that written justification is refused with `ERR_DISPLACEMENT_NET_BENEFIT_NEGATIVE`. When the gate passes, the analysis moves `PENDING → APPROVED`, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID`, and the supplied `override_reason` is recorded on the analysis whenever it arrives non-blank — whether or not the negative-benefit gate actually needed it — while an absent, null or all-whitespace value leaves `override_reason` null. The lead then moves according to where it stood: a **first** study clears the lead `DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW → CLEARED`, while a **re-study** approval leaves the negotiation stage untouched, `CLEARED → CLEARED` or `QUOTED → QUOTED`, because the lead was already past the first gate. In the `QUOTED → QUOTED` case the practical effect is that the outstanding quote becomes acceptable again, since no study of the lead remains open. Either way the lead's `version` increments.

On **`REJECT`**, no override is ever required — a rejection is always permitted on a `PENDING` study. The analysis moves `PENDING → REJECTED`, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped, an `override_reason` sent alongside a rejection is ignored so the analysis's `override_reason` stays null, and the deal dies: the lead moves to `LOST` from whichever of `DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW`, `CLEARED`, or `QUOTED` it occupied. That death carries a two-part cascade. Every rate quote of the lead whose status is `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED`, or `COUNTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; each quote that was sitting in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` was holding rooms, so its hold is released — `hold_placed` becomes false and the `held_group_rooms` of every inventory day in the lead's half-open stay range decreases by that quote's `quoted_peak_rooms`. A quote withdrawn out of `DRAFT` moves no inventory at all, because a draft quote never held rooms. Separately, every function-space hold of the lead still in `HELD` moves to `RELEASED`. The lead's `version` increments. `LOST` is terminal.

### 3.4. Rate Quoting and the Negotiation Cycle

A **`RateQuote`** is drafted by a `SalesManager` against a lead that has been cleared. The payload names the `lead_id` and the `concession_package_id` whose terms will govern, supplies the rack-side starting `base_rate_cents` (at least 1), the `quoted_peak_rooms` this quote will hold on the peak night and the `quoted_room_nights` it prices (each at least 1), the `expires_at` instant after which the quote may no longer be countered or accepted, and the `expected_lead_version` guard described in §2.7. `expires_at` must be strictly later than the current logical clock. The quote starts in `DRAFT` with `negotiation_round` at 1, `hold_placed` false, `prepared_by_user_id` stamped from `X-User-ID`, and both `countered_base_rate_cents` and `counter_note` null.

Two package terms are **pinned onto the quote at the moment it is created**: `comp_room_ratio` and `rebate_per_room_night_cents` are copied from the concession package onto the quote itself. Later edits to the package never move an existing quote's pinned copies — this is deliberate, because the comp ratio governs complimentary rooms months later at the rooming-list stage and the rebate governs cents at settlement. The package's eligibility floors are checked at draft time: `quoted_peak_rooms` must be at least `min_peak_rooms` and `quoted_room_nights` must be at least `min_total_room_nights`, both inclusive, or the draft is refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_INELIGIBLE`. The package's discount stack is re-checked here too, against `max_discount_stack_pct`, with `ERR_DISCOUNT_STACK_EXCEEDED` on breach.

The quote's **effective net rate** is where the concession package turns into money, and the order of operations is normative. Three components are computed and each is rounded independently to whole cents *before* any subtraction happens:

1. The **rate-discount component** is the quote's `base_rate_cents` multiplied by the package's `room_rate_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents.
2. The **comp-amortization component** is the quote's `base_rate_cents` divided by one more than the quote's pinned `comp_room_ratio`, rounded half up to whole cents. The "one more than" is the point: a ratio of 40 means one free room night for every 40 paid, so the giveaway is spread across 41 room nights, not 40.
3. The **rebate component** is simply the quote's pinned `rebate_per_room_night_cents`, which is already a whole-cent figure and needs no rounding.

`effective_net_rate_cents` is then the `base_rate_cents` less the rate-discount component, less the comp-amortization component, less the rebate component — a subtraction of four already-rounded integers. `total_quote_value_cents` is `effective_net_rate_cents` multiplied by `quoted_room_nights`, with no rounding, because both operands are integers. Computing the same expression in exact arithmetic and rounding only once at the end is **not** equivalent and is not permitted: whenever the two fractional components each round down but their sum would round up, the two approaches differ by one cent. No floor is applied to the subtraction — a `base_rate_cents` smaller than the sum of its three concession components yields a negative `effective_net_rate_cents`, and `total_quote_value_cents` carries that sign through.

Drafting a quote does not touch inventory. **Proposing** it does. A `SalesManager` sends a quote to the client, which moves it `DRAFT → PROPOSED`, and this is the moment rooms are softly held. The proposal is admissible only when the quote is in `DRAFT`, the lead's `status` is `CLEARED`, and the lead carries a displacement analysis in `APPROVED` status; and it is subject to the group ceiling — for every date in the program's half-open stay range, that day's `held_group_rooms` plus `committed_group_rooms` plus this quote's `quoted_peak_rooms` must not exceed the day's `group_ceiling_rooms`, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_GROUP_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. On success `hold_placed` becomes true, `held_group_rooms` increases by `quoted_peak_rooms` on every occupied date, and the lead advances `CLEARED → QUOTED`, incrementing its `version`. A missing inventory day anywhere in the range stops the proposal with `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_MISSING`.

The **client counters**. A `GroupContact` — whose `X-User-ID` must be the account that owns the quote's lead — supplies a `countered_base_rate_cents` (at least 1) and an optional `counter_note` of up to 500 characters explaining the counter-offer. This moves the quote `PROPOSED → COUNTERED` and records both fields. The counter is admissible only while the logical clock is strictly before the quote's `expires_at`. Countering does **not** disturb the inventory hold: `hold_placed` stays true and the rooms stay in `held_group_rooms` on every occupied date, because a countered quote is still a live negotiation and must keep contending for the group ceiling. Countering does not advance `negotiation_round` either; that counter moves only when the hotel re-prices.

**Revising** a quote is the sales manager's answer, and it is a fork on the direction of the peak-rooms change. A revise supplies a fresh `base_rate_cents`, `quoted_peak_rooms`, `quoted_room_nights`, and `expires_at`, and is admissible only from `COUNTERED` while the lead is `QUOTED`. Both branches increment `negotiation_round` by one, recompute `effective_net_rate_cents` and `total_quote_value_cents` from the new `base_rate_cents` — reading `room_rate_discount_pct` live off the concession package, exactly as at draft time, while using the quote's own pinned `comp_room_ratio` and `rebate_per_room_night_cents` — and re-check the package's eligibility floors: a revise that shrinks the program below `min_peak_rooms` or `min_total_room_nights` is refused with `ERR_CONCESSION_INELIGIBLE`. The package's discount stack is *not* re-checked on a revise, and `countered_base_rate_cents` and `counter_note` are left standing, still recording the client's last counter.

- When the revision leaves `quoted_peak_rooms` the same or lower, the quote returns `COUNTERED → PROPOSED` and stays in the client's court. The hold is adjusted rather than dropped: `held_group_rooms` moves by the difference between the new and old `quoted_peak_rooms` on every occupied date, and the ceiling is re-tested against that adjusted figure — for every occupied date, the day's `held_group_rooms` with the old peak rooms removed and the new peak rooms added, plus its `committed_group_rooms`, must not exceed `group_ceiling_rooms`.
- When the revision **raises** `quoted_peak_rooms`, the deal has materially grown and revenue management must look again. The quote drops all the way back to `DRAFT`, `hold_placed` becomes false, and `held_group_rooms` decreases by the *pre-revision* `quoted_peak_rooms` on every occupied date. The lead moves `QUOTED → DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW`, incrementing its `version`. No analysis is created by this transition: a fresh displacement study must be requested and approved before the quote can be proposed again. This is what closes the negotiation cycle `QUOTED → DISPLACEMENT_REVIEW → CLEARED → QUOTED`.

**Acceptance** is the client's signature of intent. A `GroupContact` accepts a quote that is in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` — both are acceptable, and `countered_base_rate_cents` is advisory throughout: acceptance re-prices nothing, so the `effective_net_rate_cents` standing on the quote, derived from the hotel's own `base_rate_cents`, is what the contract will pin. A client's counter reaches the money only if the hotel adopts it through a revise. Acceptance requires that the logical clock is strictly before `expires_at`, that the lead is `QUOTED`, that the lead carries an `APPROVED` displacement analysis with no analysis of the lead still `PENDING` (otherwise `ERR_DISPLACEMENT_NOT_APPROVED`), that `X-User-ID` is the owning account, and that `X-User-ID` differs from the quote's `prepared_by_user_id`.

Acceptance then fires a **four-hop cascade** in this order. First, the accepted quote moves to `ACCEPTED`. Second, because the quote is accepted, the lead moves `QUOTED → WON`. Third, because the lead is won, every **sibling** quote of that lead whose status is `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED`, or `COUNTERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Fourth, because each sibling that was in `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` with `hold_placed` true was holding rooms, each such sibling releases its hold — `held_group_rooms` decreases by that sibling's `quoted_peak_rooms` on every date of the program's half-open range and that sibling's `hold_placed` becomes false. Siblings withdrawn out of `DRAFT` move no inventory. The lead's `version` increments. The **accepted** quote keeps its own hold: those rooms stay in `held_group_rooms` until the contract is countersigned, at which point they convert to committed inventory.

Two further paths take a quote out of play. A quote can be **superseded**: creating a newer quote for the same lead withdraws a `DRAFT` quote already sitting there, again with no inventory movement. And a quote can **expire lazily**. Expiry is not swept on a timer; it is evaluated when the quote is next read for writing. If a counter or an acceptance is attempted at or after the quote's `expires_at`, the quote moves from `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` to `EXPIRED`, `hold_placed` becomes false, `held_group_rooms` decreases by `quoted_peak_rooms` on every occupied date, **and the triggering request is itself rejected** with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. `ACCEPTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, and `EXPIRED` are all terminal; countering a `DRAFT` quote, reviving a `WITHDRAWN` one, or acting on any terminal quote is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.5. Papering and Countersigning the Group Contract

A `ContractAdmin` papers an accepted quote into a **`GroupContract`**. The payload names the `quote_id` and then supplies the commercial terms that a quote does not carry: the `attrition_threshold_pct` the group must actualize to avoid damages (a fraction between 0.5 and 1.0 inclusive), the `attrition_basis` on which that test will be measured (`ROOM_NIGHTS` or `PEAK_NIGHT`), the `force_majeure_clause` strength (`NONE`, `STANDARD`, or `EXPANDED`), the `cutoff_days_before_arrival` at which the block will cut off (a whole number from 1 to 120 inclusive), the headline `contracted_room_nights` and `contracted_peak_rooms`, and the night-by-night `nightly_allotments` plan.

The `nightly_allotments` plan is an array of 1 to 60 entries, each pairing a `stay_date` with a `rooms` count of at least 1. The `stay_date` values must be distinct and contiguous, and every one of them must fall inside the program's half-open stay range; a plan with a duplicate or a gap is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, while a plan that is itself distinct and contiguous but does not sit on the program's dates passes this gate and is caught by the exact-range test below as `ERR_ALLOTMENT_PLAN_MISMATCH`. Beyond that, the plan and the headline figures must agree exactly: the plan's `rooms` values must sum to `contracted_room_nights`, the largest single `rooms` value must equal `contracted_peak_rooms`, and the plan's dates must be exactly the lead's half-open stay range — no more and no less. Any disagreement is refused with `ERR_ALLOTMENT_PLAN_MISMATCH`. `contracted_room_nights` must additionally be at least 1 and must not exceed the quote's `quoted_room_nights`, and `contracted_peak_rooms` must be at least 1. The two floors are per-field bounds and land at the schema gate as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, but the ceiling against the quote is a cross-row comparison and is reported as one more plan disagreement, `ERR_ALLOTMENT_PLAN_MISMATCH`. However the request ordered its entries, the plan the contract stores and echoes back on every read is sorted by `stay_date` ascending. This agreement is what makes the room-night conservation invariant of §3.6 checkable at all.

Papering is admissible only when the quote's status is `ACCEPTED` (otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_INVALID_STATE`), while the logical clock is strictly before the quote's `expires_at` (otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`), and while the quote's lead carries an `APPROVED` displacement analysis with none still `PENDING` (otherwise `ERR_DISPLACEMENT_NOT_APPROVED`). Nothing else is checked: a quote may be papered more than once, so a second contract drafted from the same `ACCEPTED` quote is created normally rather than refused. On success the contract is created in `DRAFT` with three values frozen off the upstream chain: `contracted_rate_cents` is a pinned copy of the quote's `effective_net_rate_cents`, and `arrival_date` and `departure_date` are copied from the quote's lead, from which `nights_count` is derived as the number of whole days between them. `signed_by_user_id`, `signed_at`, `signature_name` and `cancellation_reason` are all null; `assessed_damages_cents`, `ancillary_posted_cents`, `rebate_paid_cents` and `receivable_total_cents` all start at 0.

**Issuing** the contract moves it `DRAFT → SENT`, and is admissible only while the underlying quote is still `ACCEPTED` — a quote that has left `ACCEPTED` refuses the issue with `ERR_QUOTE_INVALID_STATE` (409), tested after the contract's own `DRAFT` check. Nothing else changes; the contract's `version` increments.

**Countersignature** is the single most consequential step in the whole workflow, because it is where soft holds become hard commitments and where the room block tree comes into existence. A `ContractAdmin` countersigns a `SENT` contract, supplying the printed `signature_name` for the signature line — a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters. Four preconditions apply. The contract must be `SENT`. The client organization reached through the contract's quote, that quote's lead, and that lead's `account_id` must have `credit_hold` false, or the countersignature is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 402). An inventory day must exist for every `stay_date` in the allotment plan, or `ERR_INVENTORY_DAY_MISSING`. And the group ceiling must accommodate the conversion: for every allotment entry, that date's `held_group_rooms` **with the accepted quote's own `quoted_peak_rooms` removed** — removed only while that quote still shows `hold_placed` true, so a second contract papered off the same quote and signed after the first has no hold of its own left to remove and tests the full `held_group_rooms` — plus that date's `committed_group_rooms`, plus that entry's `rooms`, must not exceed the date's `group_ceiling_rooms`. Removing the quote's own hold first is what lets a contract sign against a ceiling that the quote itself was already occupying; a breach is `ERR_GROUP_CEILING_EXCEEDED`.

When those pass, the countersignature cascade runs as one atomic step:

1. The contract moves `SENT → SIGNED`, and `signed_by_user_id`, `signature_name` and `signed_at` are stamped — the last from the logical clock's current instant.
2. The **master room block** is minted: a `RoomBlock` with `parent_block_id` null, `name` set to `MASTER`, `is_master` true, `inventory_status` `SETUP` and `billing_status` `UNBILLED`, belonging to this contract. Its `cutoff_date` is derived as the contract's `arrival_date` less `cutoff_days_before_arrival` whole days.
3. One `BlockNight` is created on that master block for each `nightly_allotments` entry, carrying that entry's `stay_date` and its `rooms` as `rooms_allotted`, with `rooms_sub_allocated`, `rooms_picked_up` and `rooms_released` all starting at 0.
4. Each allotment date's `committed_group_rooms` increases by that entry's `rooms`.
5. The accepted quote's soft hold is released: its `hold_placed` becomes false and each occupied date's `held_group_rooms` decreases by the quote's `quoted_peak_rooms`. The quote's own `version` increments, and it does so even in the corner where `hold_placed` was already false — a second contract papered off the same quote — in which case no `held_group_rooms` figure moves at all.
6. Every function-space hold of the contract's lead that is still `HELD` moves to `CONFIRMED`.
7. The contract's `version` increments.

Signing a contract that is not `SENT` — a `DRAFT` one, or one already signed — is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.6. Room Blocks, Sub-Blocks, and Rooming-List Pickup

A contract's inventory lives in a small tree of **`RoomBlock`** rows. Exactly one of them is the master, minted by the countersignature; the rest are sub-blocks carved out of it. Every block carries the `contract_id` it belongs to, a `name` of 1 to 60 characters, an `is_master` flag that is true precisely when `parent_block_id` is null, the two independent status dimensions described in §2.10, and a `cutoff_date`. Every block also carries five derived roll-ups: `allotted_room_nights` (the sum of its own nights' `rooms_allotted`), `picked_up_room_nights`, `released_room_nights` (the sum of its own nights' `rooms_released`), `peak_night_rooms`, and `comp_room_nights` (the room nights on its own reservations that are complimentary and not cancelled). Two more fields, `waived_by_user_id` and `waiver_reason`, stay null unless attrition on that block is waived.

Each block's nights are `BlockNight` rows, one per `stay_date`, unique within a block. A night carries its `block_id`, its `rooms_allotted`, and four derived counters: `rooms_sub_allocated` (rooms carved out to sub-blocks that are not `RELEASED`), `rooms_picked_up`, `rooms_released` (rooms handed back to the house), and `rooms_open`, which is `rooms_allotted` less `rooms_sub_allocated` less `rooms_picked_up` less `rooms_released`. That identity is the **conserved quantity of a night**: allotted rooms are always exactly the sum of sub-allocated, picked up, released and open rooms, and every operation in this section preserves it.

**Carving a sub-block** is performed by a `ContractAdmin` or a `ReservationsAgent`. The payload names the `parent_block_id`, a `name`, a `nightly_allotments` plan, and the `expected_parent_block_version` guard from §2.7. The master block is never created this way, but `parent_block_id` may name either the master or an existing sub-block, and neither the parent's own `inventory_status` and `billing_status` nor the contract's status gates the carve. The plan's `stay_date` values must be distinct — a duplicate is a shape error and is refused at the schema gate with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), not with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` — but, unlike a contract's allotment plan, they need not be contiguous. The plan is then validated **in ascending `stay_date` order** and applied atomically — a single failing entry rejects the whole sub-block, leaving the parent untouched. Validation is two complete passes over the sorted plan rather than one interleaved walk, which is what keeps it aligned with the ladder of §2.8. The first pass requires the parent to hold a night for every entry's `stay_date`, refusing with `ERR_SUB_BLOCK_DATES_OUTSIDE_PARENT` and naming the first offending date; only once every date has been found does the second pass look at rooms, computing each parent night's **unallocated** rooms as its `rooms_allotted` less `rooms_sub_allocated` less `rooms_picked_up` less `rooms_released` and requiring the entry's `rooms` not to exceed that figure, or the request is refused with `ERR_SUB_BLOCK_ALLOTMENT_EXCEEDED` naming the first offending night. So a plan that both overdraws an early night and names a late date the parent does not hold reports `ERR_SUB_BLOCK_DATES_OUTSIDE_PARENT`. On success, each parent night's `rooms_sub_allocated` increases by the entry's `rooms` — so the parent night's `rooms_open` shrinks by exactly the same amount — and a `BlockNight` is created on the new sub-block with `rooms_allotted` set to the entry's `rooms`. The sub-block inherits its `contract_id` and its `cutoff_date` from the parent, has `is_master` false, and starts in `inventory_status` `SETUP` with `billing_status` `UNBILLED`. The parent block's `version` increments — and it is the only row of the parent side that is stamped: because a night's `rooms_sub_allocated` is re-derived from the sub-blocks standing on it rather than stored, the parent's own `BlockNight` rows keep the `version` and `updated_at` they had before the carve even though their `rooms_sub_allocated` and `rooms_open` now read differently. Sub-blocks whose `inventory_status` has reached `RELEASED` no longer contribute to a parent night's `rooms_sub_allocated`, which returns those rooms to the parent's open pool.

**Opening a block** for pickup moves it `SETUP → OPEN` and may be done by a `ContractAdmin` or a `ReservationsAgent`. It requires, in this order, the block to be in `SETUP`, the contract to be `SIGNED` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` (409) — and the logical clock's current date to be on or before the block's `cutoff_date`, a date already past being refused with `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than with `ERR_PAST_CUTOFF_DATE`, which belongs to the rooming list alone. Sub-blocks are opened independently of their master — a master may be open while a sub-block is still in setup, and vice versa. Opening a block that is not in `SETUP` is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`.

**Rooming-list entries** are `Reservation` rows, created by a `ReservationsAgent` or by the `GroupContact` that owns the contract. The payload names the `block_id` the room night is drawn from, the `guest_name` (1 to 80 characters), the `arrival_date` and `departure_date` of the stay, and the `source` — `ROOMING_LIST` for a name delivered on the group's list, or `INDIVIDUAL_CALL` for a guest who called in against the block. `departure_date` must be strictly after `arrival_date`; the reservation occupies the half-open range between them, and `room_nights` is derived as the number of whole days between them. A new reservation starts in `PENDING` with `disposition_reason` null.

Five gates guard a rooming-list write, and their relative order matters. The target block's `inventory_status` must be exactly `OPEN` — `SETUP`, `CUT_OFF`, `RELEASED` and `CLOSED` all reject with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_OPEN`, and this gate sits **above** the allotment check, so a request that both targets a `SETUP` block and overdraws a night reports `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_OPEN`. The logical clock's current date must be strictly before the block's `cutoff_date`, or `ERR_PAST_CUTOFF_DATE` — rooming-list additions stop at the cutoff date even when the block has not yet been mechanically cut off. The reservation's half-open stay range must be fully covered by nights of **that** block, or `ERR_STAY_DATE_OUTSIDE_BLOCK`; a guest may not shoulder a night the block does not hold, even when the contract's own dates cover it. No other non-cancelled reservation on the same contract may already carry the same `guest_name` with the same `arrival_date` — the name being matched by exact, case-sensitive string equality, and a `NO_SHOW` row counting as non-cancelled and therefore still blocking the duplicate — or `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOMING_LIST_ENTRY` — double-booking one guest would inflate pickup and corrupt attrition, so this is treated as a data-integrity rule rather than a capacity rule. And finally every night of the stay must have at least one room open on that block, or `ERR_BLOCK_ALLOTMENT_EXCEEDED`.

**Pickup** is the running measure of how much of a block has sold, and it is derived, never stored by a client. A night's `rooms_picked_up` is the number of that block's **own** reservations whose status is `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `CHECKED_OUT` and whose half-open stay range covers that date — a reservation counts on a date when its `arrival_date` is on or before that date and its `departure_date` is strictly after it. `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` reservations leave the pickup set entirely. A block's `picked_up_room_nights` is the sum of its own nights' `rooms_picked_up`, **plus**, when the block is the master, the sum of every sub-block's `picked_up_room_nights` — every sub-block standing beneath it at any depth of the tree, not only its direct children, each contributing its own nights' pickup exactly once. A sub-block's pickup therefore bubbles up into its master's roll-up but never into the master's own `rooms_picked_up` figures, because those rooms were already carved out of the master's allotment as `rooms_sub_allocated`. A block's `peak_night_rooms` is the largest `rooms_picked_up` across that block's own nights, and is 0 when the block has no nights at all.

**Complimentary rooms** are earned, not requested, and the designation is re-derived across the contract's *entire* reservation set — every block in the contract's tree — whenever a reservation is created, cancelled or marked a no-show through the rooming-list endpoints; the one cancellation that does not re-derive it is the contract-cancellation cascade of §3.9. The governing ratio is the `comp_room_ratio` pinned on the contract's quote. The derivation walks the contract's non-cancelled reservations in ascending `created_at` order (the row `id` is never consulted), ties on an identical `created_at` — the ordinary case, since the logical clock rarely moves between two rooming-list writes — being broken by the order in which the rows were written, earliest first, exactly as §2.3 breaks them. It carries a running count of complimentary rooms already awarded and a running total of paid room nights. For each reservation in turn: if the paid room nights accumulated so far divided by the ratio, with any fraction discarded, is **greater than** the number of complimentary rooms awarded so far, then this reservation is the earned freebie — its `is_comp` becomes true, its `rate_cents` becomes 0, and the complimentary count advances by one; otherwise its `is_comp` becomes false, its `rate_cents` becomes the contract's `contracted_rate_cents`, and its `room_nights` are added to the paid total. The invariant this maintains is that after any create or cancel, the number of complimentary reservations equals the paid room nights divided by the ratio with the fraction discarded. Because the walk restarts from scratch every time, cancelling one guest can move the complimentary designation onto a completely different guest, flipping that guest's `rate_cents` from the contracted rate to zero. Only the rows whose designation actually moves are stamped: a reservation the walk re-derives to the `is_comp` and `rate_cents` it already carried has not been mutated, so its `version` and `updated_at` stand exactly where they were. The one row the walk never counts as mutated is the reservation being created: the walk that runs inside a creation may designate that brand-new entry the earned freebie and set its `rate_cents` to 0, and the 201 response shows exactly that, but the row is still returned with `version` 1 and with `updated_at` equal to its `created_at`.

A guest is **advanced** along the stay by a `ReservationsAgent`, who supplies the `to_status` the reservation should reach — one of the six reservation statuses, anything else being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Four advances are declared, and a declared-but-unreachable target such as `CANCELLED`, which is a member of the status set yet has no advance, lands on `ERR_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE` like any other undeclared pair. `PENDING → CONFIRMED` requires the reservation to be `PENDING` and its block's `inventory_status` to be `OPEN` or `CUT_OFF`, otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE` (409); the pickup counters do not move, because a `PENDING` reservation was already counted. `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN` requires the logical clock's current date to be on or after the reservation's `arrival_date`. `CHECKED_IN → CHECKED_OUT` requires the logical clock's current date to be on or after the reservation's `departure_date`. And `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW` requires the logical clock's current date to be on or after the `arrival_date`; each of those three clock gates reports `ERR_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE` (409) when the clock has not reached the required date, and none of the three inspects the block's `inventory_status` at all. A no-show **leaves the pickup set**, so every date of its stay has `rooms_picked_up` reduced by one, and the contract's complimentary designations are re-derived — but the no-show's room nights stay inside the actualized set that attrition is measured against. No `rooms_released` figure is adjusted to compensate, so on a block that has already been cut off each of those nights simply shows one open room again. Every advance increments the reservation's `version`, and any `to_status` for which no transition is declared from the reservation's current status is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE`.

**Cancelling** a reservation may be done by a `ReservationsAgent` or by the owning `GroupContact`, and it requires a `disposition_reason` — a non-blank string of 1 to 500 characters. It is admissible from `PENDING` or from `CONFIRMED`, only while the block's `inventory_status` is `OPEN` or `CUT_OFF` (otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`, 409), and only while the logical clock's current date is strictly before the reservation's `arrival_date` — after arrival the cancellation channel is closed with `ERR_CANCEL_AFTER_ARRIVAL` and the guest can only be advanced or marked a no-show. Both `PENDING → CANCELLED` and `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` carry identical compensation: on every date in the reservation's half-open stay range, that night's `rooms_picked_up` decreases by one, and where the block is still `OPEN` that night's `rooms_open` increases by one, returning the room to the pool. Where the block has already been cut off the room is not returned to the pool: that night's `rooms_released` rises by one instead, holding `rooms_open` at 0 and adding the room to the block's `released_room_nights` roll-up. The contract's complimentary designations are then re-derived from scratch, so a surviving reservation may gain or lose `is_comp` and see its `rate_cents` flip accordingly. Finally the block's `picked_up_room_nights`, `peak_night_rooms` and `comp_room_nights` are recomputed. `CHECKED_OUT`, `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` are terminal; cancelling a checked-out guest, or checking in a `PENDING` one, is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.7. Cutoff, Release, and Attrition Exposure

The **cutoff** is the moment the hotel stops holding rooms it has not sold. A `ReservationsAgent` or a `ContractAdmin` cuts off a block that is `OPEN`, and only when the logical clock's current date is on or after the block's `cutoff_date` — an attempt before that date is refused with `ERR_CUTOFF_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, the exact mirror of the rule that stops rooming-list additions on or after the same date. Together the two rules make `cutoff_date` a hard partition of the block's timeline.

Cutoff runs as a four-hop cascade with a normative order:

1. The targeted block's `inventory_status` moves `OPEN → CUT_OFF`.
2. Because that block is cut off, every sub-block whose `parent_block_id` is this block and whose `inventory_status` is `OPEN` also moves `OPEN → CUT_OFF`.
3. Because each of those blocks reached `CUT_OFF`, each of their nights hands back what it never sold: that night's `rooms_released` becomes its `rooms_allotted` less its `rooms_sub_allocated` less its `rooms_picked_up`, and its `rooms_open` becomes 0.
4. Because **master** nights released rooms, each affected `stay_date`'s `committed_group_rooms` decreases by the rooms that master night released — the house gets them back to sell to transient. A **sub-block** release moves no inventory-day figure at all and does not restore the master's `rooms_open`, because those rooms were already carved out of the master's allotment as `rooms_sub_allocated`.

Every block the cascade touches increments its own `version`, and each touched block's `released_room_nights` and `picked_up_room_nights` roll-ups are recomputed. Cutting off a block that is not `OPEN` — one still in `SETUP`, one already `CUT_OFF`, or one `RELEASED` — is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE`.

Before committing to an attrition basis, the office wants to see both. A **pickup summary** is a read-only projection on a single block that returns the block's `block_id`, its `allotted_room_nights`, `picked_up_room_nights`, `released_room_nights` and `peak_night_rooms`, the contract's `commitment_base_room_nights`, and then **both** attrition projections side by side: `room_nights_basis_shortfall` with its `room_nights_basis_damages_cents`, and `peak_night_basis_shortfall` with its `peak_night_basis_damages_cents`. Only the first five figures belong to the addressed block; the commitment base and both projections are the **contract's**, computed across its whole block tree, so a sub-block's pickup summary reports the same commitment base and the same two projections as its master's. Nothing is persisted by this read; it exists so that the `ROOM_NIGHTS` and `PEAK_NIGHT` bases can be compared on real pickup before a contract commits to one.

**Attrition liability** is computed from the *actualized* set, which is deliberately different from the pickup set of §3.6. A reservation actualizes when its status is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `CHECKED_OUT`, or `NO_SHOW`. `PENDING` rows never actualize — a name on the list that was never confirmed does not count toward the commitment — and `CANCELLED` rows are excluded. The set spans every reservation in the contract's whole block tree, master and sub-blocks alike. From it, `actualized_room_nights` is the sum of `room_nights` over the actualized reservations, and `billable_room_nights` is the same sum restricted to those whose `is_comp` is false — the complimentary nights the hotel gave away are not billable and do not earn a rebate.

The commitment the group is measured against can be reduced. `waived_room_nights` is the sum of `allotted_room_nights` over the contract's blocks whose `billing_status` is `WAIVED`, and `commitment_base_room_nights` is the contract's `contracted_room_nights` less that waived total. Of the two, only `commitment_base_room_nights` is ever surfaced, and only by the pickup summary above; neither is a property of the `GroupContract` and no response field carries `waived_room_nights` at all. Damages, whatever the basis, are then the shortfall in room nights multiplied by the contract's `contracted_rate_cents` multiplied by the attrition damage factor of 0.85 — the hotel recovers eighty-five per cent of the room revenue it lost — with the product rounded half up to whole cents once.

The two bases produce different shortfalls from identical reservations, and which one applies is fixed by the contract's `attrition_basis`:

- Under **`ROOM_NIGHTS`**, the commitment is `commitment_base_room_nights` multiplied by the contract's `attrition_threshold_pct`, **rounded up to the next whole room night**. `attrition_shortfall_room_nights` is then the larger of zero and that commitment less `actualized_room_nights`. The threshold test is inclusive: actualized room nights exactly equal to the commitment produce a shortfall of zero and therefore zero damages.
- Under **`PEAK_NIGHT`**, the commitment is the contract's `contracted_peak_rooms` multiplied by its `attrition_threshold_pct`, again **rounded up to the next whole room**. The group's actual peak is the largest number, across all of the contract's stay dates, of actualized reservations covering that date — the same half-open coverage test used for pickup. The shortfall in *rooms* is the larger of zero and the peak commitment less that actual peak, and `attrition_shortfall_room_nights` is that shortfall in rooms multiplied by the contract's `nights_count`. Whenever the pickup pattern is uneven across dates, this produces a materially different shortfall than the room-nights basis on exactly the same reservations.

`attrition_damages_cents` is the damages figure computed from whichever shortfall the contract's basis produced.

**Waiving attrition** on a block is a `RevenueManager`'s concession, and it takes a written `waiver_reason` — a non-blank string of 1 to 500 characters. It moves the block's `billing_status` from `UNBILLED` to `WAIVED`, or from `ATTRITION_ASSESSED` to `WAIVED`, stamping `waived_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and recording the reason. The `inventory_status` dimension is untouched. Waiving a block whose `billing_status` is already `WAIVED` or already `SETTLED` is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_ALREADY_BILLED`, so the concession cannot be replayed. The two source states differ in what else must be true and in what else happens:

- From **`UNBILLED`**, the waiver is admissible when no settlement exists for the contract yet, so there is nothing to recompute. "Yet" is literal: any `Reconciliation` at all on the contract — `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED` or `FINALIZED` — closes this branch with `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE` (409), on the ground that a block reached by a settlement should already have been assessed. The consequence is forward-looking: the block's `allotted_room_nights` will already be outside the commitment base of whatever settlement is opened later.
- From **`ATTRITION_ASSESSED`**, the waiver is admissible only while the contract's newest settlement is still `DRAFT` — a missing, `SUPERSEDED` or `FINALIZED` newest settlement is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_INVALID_STATE` (409) — and it triggers a recomputation **in place**: the waived block's `allotted_room_nights` leaves the commitment base, which re-derives `attrition_shortfall_room_nights` and `attrition_damages_cents`, and from those re-derives every downstream step of the settlement waterfall on that draft. The recomputation is a full re-derivation of the draft rather than a patch of two fields: `actualized_room_nights`, `billable_room_nights` and `ancillary_net_cents` are re-read from the contract's current actualized set and its currently `POSTED` charges as well, and `incentive_rate_snapshot` is the one figure left frozen at the value the draft was opened with. The recomputed draft's own `version` increments too.

Either way the block's `version` increments, and so does the contract's — this is the "attrition waiver" bump that §2.7 names, and it strands a settlement writer still holding the pre-waiver `expected_contract_version`.

### 3.8. Ancillary Revenue and the Contract Receivable

Group business is not only rooms. A `ContractAdmin` or an `AccountingClerk` quotes an **`AncillaryCharge`** against a signed contract, naming the `contract_id`, the `category`, and the menu-price `gross_amount_cents` (at least 1). A `SIGNED` parent is a creation precondition, tested once the `contract_id` has resolved: quoting a charge against a contract in any other status — `DRAFT`, `SENT`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `TERMINATED_FORCE_MAJEURE` — is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` (409). The `category` is one of `BANQUET`, `AUDIOVISUAL`, `PARKING`, or `INTERNET`, and it selects which of the concession package's discounts applies. The package in question is the one reached from the contract through its `quote_id` to that quote's `concession_package_id`, read at the moment the charge is created.

The charge's `discount_pct` is taken directly from that package: the `banquet_discount_pct` for a `BANQUET` charge, the `av_discount_pct` for an `AUDIOVISUAL` charge, the `parking_discount_pct` for a `PARKING` charge, and exactly `0.0` for an `INTERNET` charge — internet is never discounted, whatever the package says. The charge's `net_amount_cents` is then `gross_amount_cents` multiplied by one less `discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents in a single rounding step. A new charge starts in `QUOTED`, which means it sits outside the settlement input set until it is posted.

**Posting** a charge is an `AccountingClerk` action that moves it `QUOTED → POSTED` and admits it into the receivable. It requires the charge to be `QUOTED` and the contract to be `SIGNED`, and it is subject to the account's credit standing — a client organization with `credit_hold` true cannot have revenue posted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 402). On success the contract's `ancillary_posted_cents` increases by the charge's `net_amount_cents`, the contract's receivable is recomputed, and the contract's `version` increments — this is the "newly posted ancillary charge" bump that §2.7 names, and it is what strands a settlement writer holding the pre-posting `expected_contract_version`. Posting a charge that is not `QUOTED` is refused with `ERR_CHARGE_INVALID_STATE`; the charge's own status is inspected before the contract's, so a charge still `QUOTED` whose contract has meanwhile left `SIGNED` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` (409).

The contract's receivable is a **conserved ledger quantity** and is always re-derived rather than nudged. `ancillary_posted_cents` is the sum of `net_amount_cents` over the contract's charges whose status is `POSTED`; `QUOTED` and `VOIDED` charges are excluded. `receivable_total_cents` is then `ancillary_posted_cents` plus the contract's `assessed_damages_cents` less its `rebate_paid_cents`. That identity is recomputed on every event that can disturb it: posting a charge, voiding a charge, cancelling or terminating the contract, and finalizing the settlement.

**Voiding** a charge is also an `AccountingClerk` action, and the two source states behave differently. Voiding a charge that is still `QUOTED` moves it `QUOTED → VOIDED` and moves no money at all — the charge simply leaves the settlement input set, because it was never posted. Voiding a charge that is `POSTED` moves it `POSTED → VOIDED` as a **compensating action**: the contract's `ancillary_posted_cents` decreases by that charge's `net_amount_cents` and `receivable_total_cents` is recomputed. That compensation is only available while the money is still unsettled: voiding a posted charge whose contract already carries a `FINALIZED` settlement is refused with `ERR_RECEIVABLE_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Voiding an already-`VOIDED` charge is refused with `ERR_CHARGE_INVALID_STATE`, and the contract's own status is never gated on a void at all. Either successful void increments the charge's `version` — and, on this endpoint, only the charge's. Unlike posting, a void asked for directly leaves the contract's `version` and `updated_at` exactly where they stood, even though the contract's `ancillary_posted_cents` and `receivable_total_cents` read differently on the very next read; nothing in §2.7 or §2.10 is stranded by a void. `VOIDED` is terminal.

Charges are also voided **as a consequence** of a force-majeure termination, and there the two source states again diverge. A charge that was `POSTED` moves `POSTED → VOIDED` and the contract's `ancillary_posted_cents` decreases by its `net_amount_cents`, returning to 0 once every charge is voided, with `receivable_total_cents` recomputed. A charge that was merely `QUOTED` moves `QUOTED → VOIDED` with no ledger movement whatsoever. The purpose of that asymmetry is a strict conservation guarantee: posting charges and then terminating the contract under force majeure with a `STANDARD` or `EXPANDED` clause voids every charge and zeroes the damages, so `receivable_total_cents` returns to exactly its pre-charge value of 0 with no drift.

### 3.9. Post-Event Settlement, Cancellation, and Force Majeure

When the group has gone home, an `AccountingClerk` opens the settlement. Opening one creates a `Reconciliation` carrying its `contract_id`, a `status` that starts at `DRAFT`, and a `prepared_by_user_id` stamped from `X-User-ID`; `finalized_by_user_id` stays null while the row is `DRAFT`. The request body carries `expected_contract_version`, which must equal the contract's current `version` or the attempt is refused with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the settlement numbers were read from a contract, and if that contract moved in the meantime the figures are stale. Behind that guard the contract must itself be `SIGNED`: opening a settlement against a `DRAFT`, `SENT`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `TERMINATED_FORCE_MAJEURE` contract is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` (409), and that check runs ahead of both timing gates below.

Two timing gates protect the settlement. The current logical clock date must be on or after the contract's `departure_date`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_CONCLUDED` — settlement cannot open while the group is still in house. And every room block of the contract must have left the sellable states: a contract with any block still in `SETUP` or `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_BLOCKS_NOT_CUT_OFF`, because attrition cannot be measured while a sub-block can still take pickup.

Opening a settlement runs the whole computation eagerly and freezes one input. `actualized_room_nights` and `billable_room_nights` are taken from the actualized set of §3.7, `attrition_shortfall_room_nights` and `attrition_damages_cents` from the contract's chosen `attrition_basis`, and `ancillary_net_cents` from the sum of `net_amount_cents` over the contract's `POSTED` charges. `incentive_rate_snapshot` is read from the account's `rebooking_incentive_pct` **at creation and frozen there**; the finalize cascade must not re-read it, so re-tiering the account between draft and finalize cannot change what the draft promised. Opening a settlement also increments the contract's `version`, and any earlier `DRAFT` settlement of the same contract moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, so a contract has at most one open draft; the superseded row stops being the contract's newest settlement and can no longer be finalized. Opening a settlement is also what moves each of the contract's blocks whose `billing_status` is `UNBILLED` to `ATTRITION_ASSESSED` — but only those whose `inventory_status` is `CUT_OFF` or `CLOSED`. A block that reached `RELEASED` instead is skipped and left `UNBILLED`; it does not reject the settlement.

The **settlement waterfall** has six steps, and the order is normative — each step consumes the previous step's output, so a reordering changes the answer:

1. `gross_settlement_cents` is `attrition_damages_cents` plus `ancillary_net_cents`.
2. The rebate is earned but capped. The cap is `gross_settlement_cents` multiplied by the rebate cap percentage of 0.25, rounded half up to whole cents. The earned rebate is the quote's `rebate_per_room_night_cents` multiplied by `billable_room_nights` — complimentary nights earn nothing. `rebate_payable_cents` is the **smaller** of the earned rebate and the cap.
3. `net_before_incentive_cents` is `gross_settlement_cents` less `rebate_payable_cents`.
4. The rebooking incentive rate is assembled from the frozen snapshot plus a size bonus, then capped. The size bonus is 0.02 when `net_before_incentive_cents` is greater than or equal to the size-bonus floor of 2,000,000 cents, and 0.0 otherwise — note that the floor is tested against the **post-rebate** figure from step 3, not against `gross_settlement_cents`. The applied rate is the smaller of the incentive rate cap of 0.05 and the sum of `incentive_rate_snapshot` and the size bonus.
5. `rebooking_incentive_cents` is `net_before_incentive_cents` multiplied by that applied rate, rounded half up to whole cents.
6. `total_settlement_cents` is `net_before_incentive_cents` less `rebooking_incentive_cents`.

Because step 2's cap is a percentage of the gross, a generous per-room-night rebate on a small settlement is trimmed to the cap while the same rebate on a large settlement is paid in full; and because step 4 reads step 3's output, a rebate large enough to push the net below the size-bonus floor also removes the bonus.

**Finalizing** the settlement is the money-moving act, and it belongs to a different person than the one who prepared it: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the settlement's `prepared_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), even though both halves are `AccountingClerk` work. The settlement must still be `DRAFT`; finalizing one that is already `FINALIZED`, or one that has been `SUPERSEDED`, is refused with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_ALREADY_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409), which makes finalization a one-shot step. On this operation that replay guard is evaluated **ahead** of the contract-state check, so re-finalizing a settlement whose contract has by then moved to `COMPLETED` reports `ERR_RECONCILIATION_ALREADY_FINALIZED` and not `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`. The contract must be `SIGNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` (409).

Finalization then runs as a three-hop cascade:

1. The settlement's `status` becomes `FINALIZED` and `finalized_by_user_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID`.
2. Because the settlement is `FINALIZED`, the contract moves `SIGNED → COMPLETED`; its `assessed_damages_cents` is set to the settlement's `attrition_damages_cents`, its `rebate_paid_cents` to the settlement's `rebate_payable_cents`, and its `receivable_total_cents` is recomputed from those two together with `ancillary_posted_cents`. Every room block of the contract whose `billing_status` is `ATTRITION_ASSESSED` moves to `SETTLED`, and blocks already `WAIVED` also move to `SETTLED` — the waived block contributes zero damages because its allotment had already left the commitment base. On the independent inventory axis, each of those blocks moves `CUT_OFF → CLOSED`; a block that had already been `RELEASED` keeps that `inventory_status` and only its `billing_status` moves. The contract's `version` increments.
3. Because the contract is `COMPLETED`, the client organization's repeat-business history advances: `repeat_group_count` increases by one, `lifetime_actualized_room_nights` increases by the settlement's `actualized_room_nights`, `last_completed_departure_date` is updated, and `rebooking_incentive_pct` is re-tiered from the new figures. All four are derived on read rather than stored, so this hop writes nothing to the account row itself: the next read of the organization reports the advanced history while the account's own `version` and `updated_at` stand exactly where they did before finalization.

That re-tiering is a **decay function** with a lapse rule, and it is also evaluated freshly on every read of the client organization, not only at finalization. The base rate is 0.05 when `repeat_group_count` is at least 3 **or** `lifetime_actualized_room_nights` is at least 1,500; otherwise 0.03 when `repeat_group_count` is at least 1 **or** `lifetime_actualized_room_nights` is at least 400; otherwise 0.01. The account is then treated as lapsed when `last_completed_departure_date` is not null and the whole days from that date to the current logical clock date exceed 730. A lapsed account decays exactly one tier — 0.05 becomes 0.03, 0.03 becomes 0.01, and 0.01 stays 0.01 — so a long-dormant account loses standing without ever falling below the floor. The result is always exactly one of 0.01, 0.03 or 0.05, with no rounding. Because the evaluation is clock-relative, moving the logical clock forward past the lapse window changes the rate a read reports without any write having occurred.

**Cancelling a contract** is the alternative to settling it. A `ContractAdmin` cancels a contract that is `SENT` or `SIGNED`, naming a `cancellation_reason` of `CLIENT_CANCEL` or `FORCE_MAJEURE` and optionally a free-text `note` of up to 1000 characters, which may be blank and which the service validates but never persists — no contract property carries it back. The current logical clock date must be strictly before the contract's `arrival_date`; once the group has begun arriving there is no declared cancellation transition and the request is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE`, as is any attempt against a contract that is `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED`, or already terminal.

Damages are tiered by how much notice the hotel got, and mitigated by what the group actually sold. The notice is the whole days from the current logical clock date to the contract's `arrival_date`. The tier percentage is 0.25 at 180 days or more, else 0.50 at 90 days or more, else 0.75 at 30 days or more, else 1.00 — every boundary inclusive, so exactly 180 days out pays the cheapest tier and exactly 179 pays the next one up. The mitigated base is the contract's `contracted_room_nights` less the room nights the master block actually picked up (zero when no block exists yet, as on a `SENT` contract), multiplied by `contracted_rate_cents`, and floored at zero so that a group which over-picked owes nothing. The three branches then diverge:

- `FORCE_MAJEURE` where the contract's `force_majeure_clause` is `STANDARD` or `EXPANDED` yields damages of exactly 0 — the negotiated clause is honoured in full and the tier is never applied.
- `FORCE_MAJEURE` where the clause is `NONE` yields the mitigated base multiplied by the tier percentage multiplied by the no-clause factor of 0.50, rounded half up to whole cents: half the ordinary figure, because the event was genuinely beyond control but the contract bought no protection.
- `CLIENT_CANCEL` yields the mitigated base multiplied by the tier percentage, rounded half up to whole cents.

The result is written to the contract's `assessed_damages_cents` and `receivable_total_cents` is recomputed.

Both cancellation branches then release everything the contract was holding. Every room block of the contract whose `inventory_status` is `SETUP`, `OPEN` or `CUT_OFF` moves to `RELEASED`; each of their nights hands back all remaining allotment, with `rooms_released` absorbing the unpicked and the picked rooms alike and `rooms_picked_up` returning to 0. For every **master** night released, that stay date's `committed_group_rooms` decreases by that night's remaining committed rooms, returning the house inventory. Every reservation of those blocks whose status is `PENDING` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED`. Those cascade cancellations are the one place a reservation reaches `CANCELLED` **without** the contract's complimentary designations being re-derived: each cancelled row keeps the `is_comp` and `rate_cents` it was carrying, and no surviving reservation's designation moves. The contract's `version` increments. `CANCELLED` and `TERMINATED_FORCE_MAJEURE` are both terminal, as is `COMPLETED`.

A `CLIENT_CANCEL` stops there: posted ancillary revenue stands, and the contract keeps its `ancillary_posted_cents`. A `FORCE_MAJEURE` termination goes further, voiding every ancillary charge of the contract whose status is `QUOTED` or `POSTED` and driving `ancillary_posted_cents` to 0, with `receivable_total_cents` recomputed. Where the clause was `STANDARD` or `EXPANDED`, that combination — zero damages, zero posted ancillary, zero rebate paid — returns `receivable_total_cents` to exactly 0, the pre-charge value, with no residue.
